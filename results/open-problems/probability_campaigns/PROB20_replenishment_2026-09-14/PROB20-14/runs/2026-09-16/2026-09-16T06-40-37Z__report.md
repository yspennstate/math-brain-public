# 3. NEW: variance-sensitive anti-concentration for the conditioned marginal

Because all offspring values lie in \(h\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), it is convenient in this section to divide by the lattice span. Let
\[
\bar\xi:=\xi/h,
\]
let \(\bar\pi\) and \(\bar\mu_n\) be the corresponding laws, and define
\[
V(R):=\mathbb E_{\bar\pi}[\bar\xi^2;\bar\xi\le R].
\tag{3.1}
\]
Since \(\operatorname{Var}\xi=\infty\),
\[
V(R)\uparrow\infty.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Relative entropy is unchanged by this deterministic rescaling.

Fix once and for all
\[
0<\gamma<\frac14.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Define the self-adaptive truncation radius
\[
\boxed{
R_n:=\left(\frac{n}{x_n+1}\right)^\gamma.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Since \(x_n\le\lambda_n=o(n)\), we have \(R_n\to\infty\).

### Theorem 3.1 — variance-sensitive concentration bound

There are constants \(c,C\in(0,\infty)\), depending only on the offspring law and \(\gamma\), such that for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_m \bar\mu_n^{*n}(m)
\le
\frac{C}{\sqrt{nV(R_n)}}
+
C\exp\!\left(-c\frac{n}{R_n^2}\right).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_m \bar\mu_n^{*n}(m)
\le
\frac{C'}{\sqrt{nV(R_n)}}
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\).

### Proof

Let
\[
\varphi_n(t):=\mathbb E_{\bar\mu_n}e^{it\bar\xi},\qquad -\pi\le t\le\pi.
\]
We use two lower bounds on \(1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2\).

First, Pinsker and (2.1) give
\[
\|\bar\mu_n-\bar\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le \sqrt{d_n/2}.
\tag{3.7}
\]
Because \(\bar\pi(0)=\pi_0>0\), for all large \(n\),
\[
\bar\mu_n(0)\ge \pi_0/2.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Let \(X,X'\) be iid with law \(\bar\mu_n\). Then
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2
=\mathbb E[1-\cos(t(X-X'))].
\tag{3.9}
\]
Restricting in (3.9) to the ordered pairs with exactly one coordinate equal to zero yields
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2
\ge
2\bar\mu_n(0)
\mathbb E_{\bar\mu_n}[1-\cos(tX)].
\tag{3.10}
\]
If \(|t|\le R^{-1}\), then for \(X\le R\) we have \(|tX|\le1\), and hence
\[
1-\cos(tX)\ge c_0t^2X^2.
\]
Thus
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2
\ge
c_1 t^2 V_n(R),
\qquad
V_n(R):=\mathbb E_{\bar\mu_n}[\bar\xi^2;\bar\xi\le R].
\tag{3.11}
\]

Second, we need a uniform bound away from the origin. Since the span after rescaling is one, choose a finite set
\[
F\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\bar\pi)
\]
containing zero and with \(\gcd F=1\). Because \(d_n\to0\), Pinsker implies that every mass \(\bar\mu_n(a)\), \(a\in F\), is eventually bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(F\) and \(\bar\pi\). Therefore
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2
\ge
G(t):=
\sum_{a,b\in F}c_ac_b(1-\cos(t(a-b))),
\tag{3.12}
\]
with fixed \(c_a>0\). On \([-\pi,\pi]\), \(G(t)=0\) only at \(t=0\), because the differences generated by \(F\) have gcd one. Moreover \(G(t)/t^2\) has a positive finite limit at zero. Compactness therefore gives
\[
G(t)\ge c_2t^2,
\qquad -\pi\le t\le\pi.
\tag{3.13}
\]

We now show that the truncated second moment under \(\bar\mu_n\) is asymptotically as large as the corresponding truncated second moment under \(\bar\pi\) at the adaptive radius \(R_n\). Since the function
\[
f_R(x)=x^2\mathbf1_{\{x\le R\}}
\]
takes values in \([0,R^2]\), (3.7) gives
\[
V_n(R)\ge V(R)-R^2\sqrt{d_n/2}.
\tag{3.14}
\]
At \(R=R_n\), using \(d_n=x_n/n\),
\[
R_n^2\sqrt{d_n}
=
\left(\frac{n}{x_n+1}\right)^{2\gamma}
\sqrt{\frac{x_n}{n}}
\le
\left(\frac{x_n+1}{n}\right)^{1/2-2\gamma}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{3.15}
\]
because \(x_n=o(n)\) and \(1/2-2\gamma>0\). Hence
\[
V_n(R_n)\ge V(R_n)-o(1),
\tag{3.16}
\]
and because \(R_n\to\infty\) and \(V(R)\to\infty\), eventually
\[
V_n(R_n)\ge \tfrac12 V(R_n).
\tag{3.17}
\]

Fourier inversion for the span-one lattice gives
\[
\sup_m\bar\mu_n^{*n}(m)
\le
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt.
\tag{3.18}
\]
For \(|t|\le R_n^{-1}\), (3.11), (3.17), and \(u\le e^{-(1-u^2)/2}\) for \(0\le u\le1\) give
\[
|\varphi_n(t)|^n
\le
\exp\{-c nV(R_n)t^2\}.
\tag{3.19}
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_{|t|\le R_n^{-1}}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt
\le
\frac{C}{\sqrt{nV(R_n)}}.
\tag{3.20}
\]
For \(R_n^{-1}<|t|\le\pi\), (3.13) gives
\[
|\varphi_n(t)|^n
\le
\exp(-cn t^2)
\le
\exp\!\left(-c\frac{n}{R_n^2}\right).
\tag{3.21}
\]
This proves (3.5).

Finally,
\[
\frac{n}{R_n^2}
=n^{1-2\gamma}(x_n+1)^{2\gamma}
\ge n^{1-2\gamma}\to\infty,
\tag{3.22}
\]
while \(V(R_n)\le R_n^2\). Hence the exponential term in (3.5) is eventually smaller than the first term, proving (3.6). \(\square\)

No local limit theorem, regular variation, stable-domain hypothesis, or finite variance is used.

&\ge

&\ge
\frac12\log n
+

-C.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}

\[
\boxed{

+

\right)
\le
\lambda_n-rac12\log n+C.

\tag{4.2}

This is the main new quantitative conclusion of the run.

\boxed{

In particular

\tag{4.6}

This is a useful denominator fact in its own right.

(n/(x_n+1))^\gamma\ge(n/(U_n+1))^\gamma,

\le

V\!\left(

# 6. Consequences for the root entropy window and mark certificates

\[

\le

+

\sqrt{\frac{x_n}{n}B^qv^*(K)},
\tag{6.1}
\]
where
\[
v^*(K)=\sup_{t\ge K}t^{q-2}\mathbb E[\xi^2;\xi\le t]\downarrow0.
\]
Therefore every valid upper budget \(U_n\ge x_n\) gives a root low-window cutoff
\[
B_n=(n/U_n)^{1/q}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Replacing the old \(U_n^{(0)}\) by the variance-corrected bound (5.4) strictly enlarges this window whenever the new bound is positive.

# 7. Why the new tax is not an unverified local limit theorem

The only ingredients in Theorem 3.1 are:

1. the exact entropy decomposition (2.2);
2. Pinsker’s inequality;
3. the existence of a finite aperiodic support core after division by the lattice span;
4. the elementary identity
   \[
   1-|\varphi(t)|^2=\mathbb E[1-\cos(t(X-X'))];

No asymptotic expansion of \(\Pr(S_n=m)\), no stable-domain hypothesis, and no strong renewal theorem is used. The proof is a direct anti-concentration bound for the **moving marginal \(\mu_n\)**.

# 10. Next attack

The highest-value next step is to combine the new self-consistent entropy budget with the exact single-mark and replicated-mark lower bounds **without replacing the truncated variance by an arbitrary slow divergence**.

A promising route is to keep the three law-dependent moduli coupled:
\[
V(R)=\mathbb E[\xi^2;\xi\le hR],
\qquad
R_q(K)=\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi>K],
\qquad
H(y)=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta y-\Lambda(\theta)\}.
\]
For a hypothetical anomalous shell at scale \(d\), the new correlation tax charges \(\log V((n/(x_n+1))^\gamma)\), the ordinary tail amplification charges \(\log(1/R_q(K))\), and the exact-sum displacement pays \(nH(d/n)\) and \(nH(c/(d^{q-1}J))\). These quantities are not independent: finite \(q\)-moment couples \(V\) and \(R_q\) through
\[
R^{q-2}V(R)\to0
\]
and through dyadic integration by parts. The next proof attempt should exploit that coupling quantitatively rather than bounding the three moduli separately.

On the counterexample side, any viable lacunary construction must now arrange simultaneously:

- a sufficiently deep central atom valley \(a_n=e^{-\lambda_n}\);
- amplified conditioned \(q\)-mass on a moving scale;
- enough ordinary tail sparsity to make \(R_q(K)\) small;
- yet sufficiently slow truncated-variance growth that the new \(\frac12\log V\) correlation tax does not consume the available marginal entropy budget.

A construction failing any one of those checks is not a counterexample.
