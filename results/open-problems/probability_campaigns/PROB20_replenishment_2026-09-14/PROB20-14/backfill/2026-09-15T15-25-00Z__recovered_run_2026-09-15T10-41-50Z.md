## Target

Let \(\xi\) be a nondegenerate nonnegative-integer offspring variable with
\[
\mathbb E\xi=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty,\qquad q:=1+r\in(1,2).
\]
Let \(T_n\) be the Galton–Watson tree with offspring law \(\xi\), conditioned on total size \(n\), along admissible \(n\). The full target is UI of \(\{\ell_k(T_n)^r\}_n\) for every fixed \(k\). This checkpoint does not close that target.

Write
\[
a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),
\]
where \(S_n\) is the sum of \(n\) iid copies of \(\xi\). Earlier work, using Abraham–Delmas–Guo's strong-ratio framework at the critical mean, had established only
\[
-\log a_n=o(n).
\tag{1}
\]
The main result of this recovered run is a strictly sharper universal rate that uses a fixed bounded aperiodic component of the offspring law and no infinite-variance local limit theorem.

## 1. Fixed finite core and mixture decomposition

Let
\[
h:=\gcd\{d\ge1:\Pr(\xi=d)>0\}.
\]
Choose a finite set \(F\subset\operatorname{supp}(\xi)\) containing 0 and finitely many positive support values whose gcd is \(h\). Such an \(F\) exists because successive gcds of an enumeration of the positive support can strictly decrease only finitely many times.

Let \(\nu\) be the conditional offspring law on \(F\). It is finite-support, nondegenerate, and has lattice span \(h\). Choose a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) small enough that
\[
\pi=\varepsilon\nu+(1-\varepsilon)\rho
\tag{2}
\]
for another probability law \(\rho\), where \(\pi\) is the law of \(\xi\). Because \(\rho(x)\le (1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\pi(x)\),
\[
\mathbb E_\rho X^q<\infty.
\tag{3}
\]

Represent each offspring variable by first choosing a Bernoulli indicator \(I_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)\). If \(I_i=1\), draw from \(\nu\); otherwise draw from \(\rho\). Let
\[
M_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n I_i.
\]
Conditionally on \(M_n=m\),
\[
S_n\stackrel d=A_m+B_{n-m},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(A_m\) is a sum of \(m\) iid \(\nu\)-variables and \(B_{n-m}\) a sum of \(n-m\) iid \(\rho\)-variables, independent.

## 2. Residual concentration at the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund scale

Let \(m_n\) be an integer nearest \(\varepsilon n\). Since \(\rho\) has finite \(q\)-moment, the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund law yields
\[
\frac{B_{n-m_n}-(n-m_n)\mu_\rho}{n^{1/q}}\to0
\]
in probability (and in the usual centered form almost surely). Therefore there exists a deterministic \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
\Pr\left(
\left|B_{n-m_n}-(n-m_n)\mu_\rho\right|
\le \eta_n n^{1/q}
\right)\to1.
\tag{5}
\]
Because
\[
\varepsilon\mu_\nu+(1-\varepsilon)\mu_\rho=1
\]
and \(m_n-\varepsilon n=O(1)\), on the event in (5) the core sum required for \(S_n=n-1\) differs from its own mean \(m_n\mu_\nu\) by at most
\[
O(1)+\eta_n n^{1/q}.
\tag{6}
\]

## 3. Finite-support local Cramér lower bound

For the fixed law \(\nu\), after dividing the lattice by \(h\), the law is aperiodic with finite support. There are constants \(c,C,\delta>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(m\) and all admissible lattice points \(x\) satisfying
\[
|x-m\mu_\nu|\le\delta m,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(A_m=x)
\ge c m^{-1/2}
\exp\left[-C\frac{(x-m\mu_\nu)^2}{m}\right].
}
\tag{7}
\]
A standard proof exponentially tilts the bounded law so that \(x/m\) is the tilted mean, uses the lattice local CLT uniformly over the resulting compact family of tilt parameters, and then reverses the tilt. The finite-support Cramér rate is quadratic in a fixed neighborhood of the original mean.

The lattice admissibility required in (7) is satisfied for the conditional target: all offspring values lie in \(h\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), and the target \(n-1\) is in the admissible tree-size lattice. Since 0 lies in \(F\) and the scaled positive core has gcd one, every sufficiently central point in the correct lattice is attainable for large \(m\).

## 4. Quantitative central-atom lower bound

The binomial local estimate gives
\[
\Pr(M_n=m_n)\ge c_0 n^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
On the residual event (5), the required core target obeys (6), so (7) applies and, because \(m_n\asymp n\),
\[
\Pr(A_{m_n}=n-1-B_{n-m_n})
\ge c_1n^{-1/2}
\exp\left[-C_1\eta_n^2 n^{2/q-1}ight].
\tag{9}
\]
The residual good event has probability tending to one. Combining (4), (5), (8), and (9),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(S_n=n-1)
\ge c n^{-1}
\exp\left[-C\eta_n^2 n^{2/q-1}ight].
}
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(\eta_n\to0\) and \(\log n=o(n^{2/q-1})\),
\[
\boxed{
-\log\Pr(S_n=n-1)
=o\left(n^{2/q-1}\right)
=o\left(n^{(1-r)/(1+r)}\right).
}
\tag{11}
\]
This is a genuine improvement over the previously available universal \(o(n)\) exponent. It uses only the finite \(q\)-moment and a fixed finite-support component, not a stable domain of attraction.

## 5. Consequence for the large-level escape scale

Earlier work proved a one-sided lower-deviation estimate: with
\[
p:=\frac q{q-1},
\]
for sublinear lower deviations \(\ell\),
\[
\Pr(S_m-m\le-\ell)
\le
\exp\left[-c\ell^p m^{-(p-1)}\right],
\tag{12}
\]
with a fixed exponential estimate for linear deviations.

Let
\[
\lambda_n:=-\log a_n,
\qquad A_n:=\lambda_n+\log n.
\]
Choose \(\omega_n\to\infty\) so slowly that
\[
\omega_n A_n=o(n^{2/q-1})
\]
and set
\[
c_n:=\left[\omega_n A_n n^{p-1}\right]^{1/p}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{c_n^p}{n^{p-1}}=\omega_nA_n\gg A_n,
\]
so the earlier prefix proof applies unchanged and gives, for every fixed level \(k\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0.
}
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover (11) implies the power-scale improvement
\[
\boxed{c_n=o(n^{E_r}),}
\tag{15}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
E_r
=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2}
=1-\frac{2r^2}{(1+r)^2}.
}
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed \(E_r=(2/q-1+p-1)/p\). For \(0<r<1\),
\[
\frac1{1+r}<E_r<1,
\qquad
E_r-\frac1{1+r}
=\frac{r(1-r)}{(1+r)^2}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus possible UI failure is pushed below a universal power \(n^{E_r+o(1)}\), although this is still above the natural \(n^{1/q}\) scale.

## 6. Scope and limitations

This recovered theorem does **not** settle the full problem. It strengthens the denominator control and the arbitrary-law large-width exclusion, but it does not provide the moving local-probability ratio needed in the remaining mesoscopic window.

In particular:
- no unverified infinite-variance local limit theorem is used;
- no claim is made that the central atom is polynomially large;
- the result applies to every fixed level only through the previously proved prefix escape argument;
- it does not by itself give root UI, much less every-level UI.
