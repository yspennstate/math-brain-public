## Target

Let \(q=1+r\in(1,2)\). The full target remains uniform integrability of \(\{\ell_k(T_n)^r\}\) for every fixed \(k\) for every critical nondegenerate integer offspring law with \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\) and \(\operatorname{Var}\xi=\infty\), along all admissible sizes.

This recovered run developed a quantitative lower bound for the central lattice atom using a fixed bounded aperiodic component of the offspring law. It was not a full solution.

## 1. Finite-core mixture

Let
\[
h=\gcd\{d\ge1:\pi_d>0\}.
\]
Choose a finite set \(F\subset\operatorname{supp}(\pi)\) containing \(0\) and finitely many positive support values whose positive gcd is \(h\). Let \(\nu=\pi(\cdot\mid F)\). Then \(\nu\) has finite support, is nondegenerate, and has lattice span \(h\).

Choose any fixed \(0<\varepsilon<\pi(F)\) and define
\[
\rho(x)=\frac{\pi(x)-\varepsilon\nu(x)}{1-\varepsilon}.
\]
Because \(\nu(x)=\pi(x)/\pi(F)\) on \(F\), this is nonnegative. Thus
\[
\boxed{\pi=\varepsilon\nu+(1-\varepsilon)\rho.}\tag{1.1}
\]
The residual law \(\rho\) inherits a finite \(q\)-moment.

Represent iid offspring variables by independent selectors \(I_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)\): on \(I_i=1\) sample \(Y_i\sim\nu\), otherwise sample \(R_i\sim\rho\). Put
\[
M_n=\sum_{i=1}^n I_i,
\qquad A_m=Y_1+\cdots+Y_m,
\qquad B_N=R_1+\cdots+R_N.
\]
Conditionally on \(M_n=m\),
\[
S_n\stackrel d=A_m+B_{n-m}.\tag{1.2}
\]

## 2. Residual concentration at a scale \(o(n^{1/q})\)

Let \(m_n\) be an integer nearest \(\varepsilon n\). Since \(\rho\) has finite \(q\)-moment, the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund law gives
\[
\frac{B_{n-m_n}-(n-m_n)\mu_\rho}{n^{1/q}}\to0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
Hence there is a deterministic \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
\Pr\!\left(
\left|B_{n-m_n}-(n-m_n)\mu_\rho\right|
\le \eta_n n^{1/q}
\right)\to1.\tag{2.1}
\]
Because
\[
\varepsilon\mu_\nu+(1-\varepsilon)\mu_\rho=1,
\]
and \(m_n-\varepsilon n=O(1)\), on the event in (2.1) the value required of the bounded core in order to realize \(S_n=n-1\) differs from \(m_n\mu_\nu\) by at most
\[
O(1)+\eta_n n^{1/q}.\tag{2.2}
\]
The lattice is compatible: every offspring value is a multiple of \(h\) except for the zero value, \(n-1\) is an admissible multiple of \(h\), and the residual sum is also a multiple of \(h\).

## 3. Uniform bounded-core local lower bound

For the fixed finite-support span-\(h\) law \(\nu\), there are constants \(c,C,\delta>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(m\) and every attainable lattice point \(x\) satisfying
\[
|x-m\mu_\nu|\le\delta m,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(A_m=x)
\ge c m^{-1/2}\exp\!\left[-C\frac{(x-m\mu_\nu)^2}{m}\right].
}\tag{3.1}
\]
One proof is exponential tilting: tilt the fixed bounded law so that \(x/m\) is the tilted mean, apply the uniform lattice local CLT on the resulting compact family of tilted finite-support laws, and undo the tilt. The finite-support Cramér rate is quadratic in a fixed neighborhood of \(\mu_\nu\).

The binomial local estimate also gives
\[
\Pr(M_n=m_n)\ge c_0n^{-1/2}.\tag{3.2}
\]

## 4. Quantitative central-atom lower bound

Let
\[
a_n=\Pr(S_n=n-1).
\]
On the residual event (2.1), the core displacement in (2.2) is \(o(n)\), so (3.1) applies with \(m=m_n\asymp n\). Combining (1.2), (2.1), (3.1), and (3.2),
\[
\boxed{
a_n
\ge c n^{-1}
\exp\!\left[-C\eta_n^2 n^{2/q-1}\right]}
\tag{4.1}
\]
for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\). Since \(\eta_n\to0\) and \(2/q-1>0\),
\[
\boxed{-\log a_n=o\!\left(n^{2/q-1}\right).}\tag{4.2}
\]
Equivalently, with \(q=1+r\),
\[
2/q-1=\frac{1-r}{1+r}.
\]
This strictly strengthens the earlier universal conclusion \(-\log a_n=o(n)\) without invoking an infinite-variance local limit theorem.

## 5. Consequence for the universal large-level escape scale

Let
\[
p=\frac q{q-1}.
\]
The earlier one-sided lower-deviation argument kills level sizes \(L\) whenever
\[
L^p n^{-(p-1)}\gg -\log a_n+\log n.
\]
Using (4.2), one may choose a deterministic cutoff \(c_n\) with
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0
\]
for every fixed \(k\), and moreover
\[
\boxed{c_n=o(n^{E_r}),
\qquad
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2}
=1-\frac{2r^2}{(1+r)^2}.}\tag{5.1}
\]
Here
\[
E_r-\frac1{1+r}=\frac{r(1-r)}{(1+r)^2}>0,
\]
so this narrows the unresolved mesoscopic window but does not reach the natural \(n^{1/(1+r)}\) moment scale.

## 6. Status and limitation

This is a partial quantitative smoothing result, not a proof of uniform integrability. It does not control the ratio of a downward-shifted local atom to the central atom at the natural mesoscopic scale. Later canonical-recentering work showed that the residual conditional problem can be represented as exact mean conditioning of \(W_q\)-stable triangular laws; that later work supersedes the original run's broader heuristic discussion but not the denominator estimate (4.2).

**Verification level:** author-side derivation. The bounded-core local estimate (3.1) is a standard finite-support exponential-tilt/local-CLT fact; no independent/formal verification is claimed.
