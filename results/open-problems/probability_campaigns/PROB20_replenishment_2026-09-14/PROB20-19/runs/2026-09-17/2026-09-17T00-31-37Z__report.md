# 1. Source target and current literature check

For a centered Gaussian `Y ~ N(0,Lambda)` with diagonal eigenvalue matrix

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p),
\]

and an orthogonal basis change `Q in O(p)`, write

\[
F_d(Q;\Lambda)
=\mathbb E\,\operatorname{Top}_d\big((QY)_1^2,\ldots,(QY)_p^2\big).
\]

Mallat--Zeitouni Conjecture 1 asks whether

\[
F_d(Q;\Lambda)\le F_d(I;\Lambda)
\qquad(1\le d<p)
\tag{1.1}
\]

for every spectrum and every orthogonal `Q`.

# 4. B18: rewrite `d=p-1` as a Gaussian-minimum problem

Let

\[
C=Q\Lambda Q^T,
\qquad X\sim N(0,C),
\]

and define

\[
M_C:=\min_{1\le i\le p}|X_i|.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because the sum of all squared coordinates has expectation `tr(C)=tr(Lambda)`, the `d=p-1` retained-energy objective is exactly

\[
F_{p-1}(C)
=\operatorname{tr}\Lambda-\mathbb E M_C^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the source conjecture in this regime is equivalent to

\[
\mathbb E M_{Q\Lambda Q^T}^2
\ge
\mathbb E M_\Lambda^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the precise point at which Kunisky's spherical-zone language is relevant, while his 2026 counterexample to Litvak's different equal-diagonal correlation-matrix conjecture does **not** refute (4.3): here the full covariance spectrum is frozen.

The tail integral is

\[
\mathbb E M_C^2
=2\int_0^\infty t\,\Pr\{M_C>t\}\,dt.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore a retained-energy counterexample is possible only if its minimum tail is sufficiently *smaller* than the KL minimum tail after this `2t` weighting.

# 5. B12: two invariants at the two threshold ends

Two quantities are preserved by orthogonal conjugation and control opposite tail scales:

1. the eigenvalue vector `lambda`, which Schur--Horn compares to the diagonal variances at `t downarrow 0`;
2. the precision trace

\[
\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1})
=\sum_i\lambda_i^{-1},
\tag{5.1}
\]

which controls a sign-vector large-deviation comparison at `t to infinity`.

These lead to the following theorem.

# 6. Main theorem: two-ended KL dominance for `d=p-1`

Assume first that every `lambda_i>0`. Let

\[
T_C(t):=\Pr\{M_C>t\}
=\Pr\{|X_i|>t\ \text{for every }i\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

## Theorem 6.1

Let `C=Q Lambda Q^T` be any covariance in the orthogonal orbit of a positive diagonal `Lambda`.

### (a) Small-threshold asymptotic

If `v_i=C_{ii}`, then

\[
T_C(t)
=1-\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,t\sum_{i=1}^p v_i^{-1/2}+O(t^2)
\qquad(t\downarrow0).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
\sum_{i=1}^p v_i^{-1/2}
\le
\sum_{i=1}^p \lambda_i^{-1/2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If `C` is genuinely non-diagonal (equivalently, the basis mixes at least two unequal eigenspaces), then the inequality in (6.3) is strict. Consequently there exists `epsilon(C)>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
T_C(t)>T_\Lambda(t)
\quad\text{for }0<t<\epsilon(C).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### (b) Large-threshold large-deviation rate

Define

\[
I(C)
:=
\inf_{|x_i|\ge1\ \forall i}
 x^T C^{-1}x.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Then

\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1{t^2}\log T_C(t)
=-\frac12 I(C).
\tag{6.6}
\]

For the KL covariance,

\[
I(\Lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^p\lambda_i^{-1}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

For every `C` in the same orthogonal orbit,

\[
I(C)
\le
\min_{s\in\{\pm1\}^p}s^TC^{-1}s
\le
\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1})
=
\sum_i\lambda_i^{-1}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

If `C` is non-diagonal, the second inequality is strict. Hence

\[
I(C)<I(\Lambda),
\tag{6.9}
\]

and therefore there exists `T(C)<infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
T_C(t)>T_\Lambda(t)
\quad\text{for all }t>T(C).
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

So the KL minimum tail is strictly smaller at **both** ends of the threshold axis for every genuine non-eigenbasis covariance in the fixed-spectrum orbit.

## Proof of (a)

Set

\[
A_i(t)=\{|X_i|\le t\}.
\]

Since `X_i~N(0,v_i)`, Taylor expansion of the one-dimensional Gaussian density at zero gives

\[
\Pr(A_i(t))
=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\frac{t}{\sqrt{v_i}}+O(t^3).
\tag{6.11}
\]

Because `C` is positive definite, every two-coordinate principal covariance is positive definite, so its density is bounded on a fixed neighborhood of the origin. Hence for `i ne j`,

\[
\Pr(A_i(t)\cap A_j(t))=O(t^2).
\tag{6.12}
\]

Bonferroni therefore yields

\[
\Pr\Big(\bigcup_iA_i(t)\Big)
=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,t\sum_i v_i^{-1/2}+O(t^2),
\tag{6.13}
\]

which is (6.2).

Now

\[
v_i=\sum_j q_{ij}^2\lambda_j.
\tag{6.14}
\]

The matrix `(q_ij^2)` is doubly stochastic, so Schur--Horn gives

\[
(v_1,\ldots,v_p)\prec(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p).
\tag{6.15}
\]

The function `x -> x^{-1/2}` is strictly convex on `(0,infinity)`. Karamata therefore proves (6.3). If unequal eigenspaces are genuinely mixed, the diagonal vector is not merely a permutation of the eigenvalue vector, so strict convexity makes (6.3) strict. Comparing (6.2) for `C` and `Lambda` proves (6.4).

## Proof of (b)

The set

\[
A=\{x\in\mathbb R^p:|x_i|\ge1\ \forall i\}
\]

is closed and bounded away from the origin. The standard finite-dimensional Gaussian Laplace principle applied to `tA` gives

\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-2}\log\Pr\{X\in tA\}
=-\frac12\inf_{x\in A}x^TC^{-1}x,
\]

which is (6.6). For completeness, the upper bound follows by bounding the Gaussian density by its largest exponential value on `tA` and absorbing the remaining polynomial-volume factor into `o(t^2)`; the lower bound follows by choosing an almost-minimizer `x_* in A`, scaling it by `1+delta` into the interior, integrating over a small ball around `t(1+delta)x_*`, and then sending `delta downarrow0`.

For diagonal `Lambda`, each constraint `|x_i|>=1` separates, so

\[
I(\Lambda)=\inf_{|x_i|\ge1}\sum_i\frac{x_i^2}{\lambda_i}
=\sum_i\lambda_i^{-1}.
\tag{6.16}
\]

For general `C`, every sign vector `s in {+/-1}^p` belongs to `A`, so

\[
I(C)\le\min_s s^TC^{-1}s.
\tag{6.17}
\]

Averaging over all sign vectors annihilates all off-diagonal terms:

\[
2^{-p}\sum_s s^TC^{-1}s
=\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}).
\tag{6.18}
\]

Hence the minimum sign energy is at most the average, proving (6.8). If `C^{-1}` is non-diagonal, the Walsh polynomial

\[
s\mapsto 2\sum_{i<j}(C^{-1})_{ij}s_is_j
\]

is nonconstant but has mean zero over the hypercube. Its minimum is therefore strictly negative, so

\[
\min_s s^TC^{-1}s<\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}).
\tag{6.19}
\]

Orthogonal conjugation preserves the eigenvalues of `C^{-1}`, hence its trace is `sum lambda_i^{-1}`. Equations (6.16)--(6.19) give (6.9). The strict inequality of exponential rates implies (6.10).

QED.

# 7. Singular spectra

If at least one eigenvalue is zero and `d=p-1`, the KL basis has an identically zero coordinate. Therefore

\[
M_\Lambda=0\quad\text{a.s.},
\qquad
F_{p-1}(I;\Lambda)=\operatorname{tr}\Lambda,
\]

which is the absolute maximum possible retained energy. Thus the `d=p-1` source conjecture is trivial on the singular boundary. The nontrivial endpoint theorem above therefore loses no source-valid case by assuming positive definiteness.

# 8. B30: the exact intermediate-scale obligation

From (4.4),

\[
F_{p-1}(C)-F_{p-1}(\Lambda)
=-2\int_0^\infty t\,[T_C(t)-T_\Lambda(t)]\,dt.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Theorem 6.1 says that for every genuine non-eigenbasis `C`,

\[
T_C(t)-T_\Lambda(t)>0
\]

on a nonempty interval adjacent to zero and again for all sufficiently large `t`.

Therefore a counterexample `F_{p-1}(C)>F_{p-1}(Lambda)` can exist only if there is a bounded intermediate threshold interval on which

\[
\boxed{T_C(t)<T_\Lambda(t)}
\tag{8.2}
\]

and the negative `2t`-weighted area there exceeds the positive area from both endpoint regions.

Because both tails are continuous for positive-definite Gaussian covariances, any such counterexample must have at least **two threshold crossings** of

\[
T_C(t)-T_\Lambda(t).
\tag{8.3}
\]

This is the main search reduction of the run.

For the smallest unresolved complementary case

\[
p=3,\qquad d=2,
\tag{8.4}
\]

an efficient counterexample search should therefore no longer optimize only the final expectation. It should search directly for a certified intermediate-scale crossing of the full three-coordinate minimum tail, then integrate the signed area. Near-zero or far-tail candidates are structurally impossible.

# 9. Quantitative invariants exposed by the proof

The two endpoint mechanisms yield explicit diagnostics for a candidate covariance `C`.

## Small-threshold slope gap

\[
\Delta_0(C)
:=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}
\left(
\sum_i\lambda_i^{-1/2}
-
\sum_i C_{ii}^{-1/2}
\right)>0
\tag{9.1}
\]

for every genuine non-eigenbasis covariance in the orbit.

## Large-threshold sign-energy gap

\[
\Delta_\infty(C)
:=
\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1})
-
\min_{s\in\{\pm1\}^p}s^TC^{-1}s
>0.
\tag{9.2}
\]

The true large-deviation gap can only be larger, because `I(C)` may be below the minimum sign-vector energy.

For `p=3`, if the off-diagonal precision entries are `a,b,c`, then the four distinct sign corrections are

\[
2(a+b+c),\quad
2(a-b-c),\quad
2(-a+b-c),\quad
2(-a-b+c).
\tag{9.3}
\]

Their mean is zero. Unless `a=b=c=0`, at least one is strictly negative. Thus the far-tail safe direction is completely explicit in the first unresolved dimension.

# 10. Relation to the 2026 Gaussian-minima paper

Kunisky's May 2026 paper is directly useful here as a warning and a language change, not as a proof of the source conjecture. It records that a stronger Gaussian-minimum principle with **fixed marginal variances** is false: van Handel's three-variable example already beats the independent identity covariance, and Kunisky gives a separate rank-two cosine-covariance counterexample to Litvak's simplex conjecture at `n=4,p=2`.

Theorem 6.1 avoids that false route. Its small-threshold comparison uses the fact that under a **fixed spectrum**, nontrivial rotation changes the marginal variances by Schur--Horn majorization; its large-threshold comparison uses the independent invariant `tr(C^{-1})`. Neither mechanism is available in the same-marginals problem.

This explains precisely why the fixed-spectrum Mallat--Zeitouni problem can remain plausible even though stronger dependence-erasing or equal-diagonal minima conjectures fail.
