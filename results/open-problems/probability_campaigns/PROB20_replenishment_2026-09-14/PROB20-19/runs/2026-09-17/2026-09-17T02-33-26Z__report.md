## 4. A closed trivariate positive-orthant second moment

Let `U~N(0,K)` in `R^3`, with `K` positive definite. Write

\[
a=\frac{k_{12}}{\sqrt{k_{11}k_{22}}},\qquad
b=\frac{k_{13}}{\sqrt{k_{11}k_{33}}},\qquad
c=\frac{k_{23}}{\sqrt{k_{22}k_{33}}}.
\]

Define

\[
P(K)=\Pr(U_1\ge0,U_2\ge0,U_3\ge0)
=\frac18+\frac{\arcsin a+\arcsin b+\arcsin c}{4\pi}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1

For every positive-definite `K`,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Psi(K)
&:=\mathbb E\bigl[U_1^2\mathbf 1_{\{U_1,U_2,U_3\ge0\}}\bigr]\\
&=k_{11}\Bigg[
P(K)+\frac1{4\pi}\Bigg(
 a\sqrt{1-a^2}+b\sqrt{1-b^2}
 +\frac{2ab-c(a^2+b^2)}{\sqrt{1-c^2}}
\Bigg)\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Let `A=K^{-1}` and perturb the precision matrix by

\[
A(t)=A+tE_{11},\qquad K(t)=A(t)^{-1}.
\]

Differentiate the orthant probability using the Gaussian density. Since

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log\det(A+tE_{11})\bigg|_{t=0}=k_{11},
\]

we have

\[
P'(0)=\frac{k_{11}}2P(K)-\frac12\Psi(K).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\Psi(K)=k_{11}P(K)-2P'(0).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Also

\[
K'(0)=-KE_{11}K,
\]

so direct differentiation of the three correlations gives

\[
a'=-\frac{k_{11}}2a(1-a^2),
\qquad
b'=-\frac{k_{11}}2b(1-b^2),
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
c'=k_{11}\left(\frac{c(a^2+b^2)}2-ab\right).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Differentiating (4.1),

\[
P'(0)=\frac1{4\pi}
\left(
\frac{a'}{\sqrt{1-a^2}}+
\frac{b'}{\sqrt{1-b^2}}+
\frac{c'}{\sqrt{1-c^2}}
\right).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Substitution of (4.5)--(4.7) into (4.4) is exactly (4.2). QED.

## 5. B25: the 24-cone identity

Let `X~N(0,C)` with arbitrary positive-definite `3 x 3` covariance `C`. For each candidate minimum index `i in {1,2,3}` and each sign vector

\[
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3)\in\{\pm1\}^3,
\]

let `{j,k}={1,2,3}\setminus\{i\}` and define

\[
B_{i,\varepsilon}x=
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon_i x_i\\
\varepsilon_j x_j-\varepsilon_i x_i\\
\varepsilon_k x_k-\varepsilon_i x_i
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The cone

\[
\mathcal C_{i,\varepsilon}
=
\{x:B_{i,\varepsilon}x\ge0\}
\tag{5.2}
\]

is precisely the set on which the signs are `epsilon` and coordinate `i` has the smallest absolute value:

\[
\varepsilon_i x_i=|x_i|,
\qquad |x_j|\ge|x_i|,
\qquad |x_k|\ge|x_i|.
\]

The 24 cones partition `R^3` up to the Gaussian-null tie hyperplanes. On `C_{i,epsilon}`,

\[
\min_r X_r^2=X_i^2=(B_{i,\varepsilon}X)_1^2.
\]

Because each `B_{i,epsilon}` is invertible and `C` is positive definite,

\[
K_{i,\varepsilon}:=B_{i,\varepsilon}CB_{i,\varepsilon}^\top
\]

is positive definite.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact finite evaluator for the complete `p=3,d=2` objective

For every positive-definite `3 x 3` covariance `C`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\min\{X_1^2,X_2^2,X_3^2\}
=
\sum_{i=1}^3\ \sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^3}
\Psi\!\left(B_{i,\varepsilon}CB_{i,\varepsilon}^\top\right),
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

where `Psi` is the explicit elementary expression in (4.2). Consequently

\[
\boxed{
F_2(C)
=\operatorname{tr}C-
\sum_{i=1}^3\ \sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^3}
\Psi\!\left(B_{i,\varepsilon}CB_{i,\varepsilon}^\top\right).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

Integrate `min_r X_r^2` over the 24 disjoint cones (modulo null boundaries). On each cone use the invertible linear change `U=B_{i,epsilon}X`. The event becomes `U>=0`, the covariance becomes `K_{i,epsilon}`, and the integrand becomes `U_1^2`. Lemma 4.1 evaluates that orthant moment. Summing all cones gives (5.3). Since the sum of all three squares has expectation `tr C`, retaining the largest two squares is the trace minus the smallest square, giving (5.4). QED.

This is B3's stronger statement: instead of proving one selected two-angle family, it gives a closed finite formula for **every** covariance in the complete `p=3,d=2` source orbit. It does not by itself prove that the formula is minimized at the diagonal covariance; that remaining finite-dimensional extremization is still the source-level obligation.
