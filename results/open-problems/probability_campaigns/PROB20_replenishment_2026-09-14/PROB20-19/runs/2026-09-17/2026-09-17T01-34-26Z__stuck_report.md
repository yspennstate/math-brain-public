## 3. The exact statement that blocks

The preceding run reduced the complementary case `d=p-1` to a Gaussian-minimum problem and proved that every genuine non-eigenbasis fixed-spectrum covariance has the correct KL-favoring sign in the minimum tail both for sufficiently small thresholds and for sufficiently large thresholds. The first unresolved source-valid case is therefore already

\[
p=3,\qquad d=2.
\]

Let

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3),
\qquad \lambda_1>\lambda_2>\lambda_3>0,
\qquad C=Q\Lambda Q^\top,
\qquad Q\in O(3),
\]

and let `Z` be standard Gaussian in `R^3`. Because retaining the largest two squares is the trace minus the smallest square, the source inequality in this case is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\min_{1\le i\le3}
\bigl(e_i^\top Q\Lambda^{1/2}Z\bigr)^2
\;\ge\;
\mathbb E\min\{\lambda_1Z_1^2,\lambda_2Z_2^2,\lambda_3Z_3^2\}
\quad\text{for every }Q\in O(3).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
T_C(t)=\Pr_C\{|X_1|>t,|X_2|>t,|X_3|>t\},
\]

one must prove

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty 2t\,[T_C(t)-T_\Lambda(t)]\,dt\ge0.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The preceding run proves `T_C(t)-T_Λ(t)>0` near `t=0` and again for all sufficiently large `t`, for every genuine non-diagonal orbit point. Hence a source-valid counterexample to (3.1), if one exists, must contain a **bounded intermediate interval of negative tail difference with enough weighted area to defeat both positive ends**, and necessarily at least two threshold crossings.

That intermediate signed area is the current blocker.

## 4. Planning reformulation: three linked spherical zones

This subsection records a standard radial restatement for planning; it is **not promoted as a new theorem claim**.

Write `Z=RU`, with `U` uniform on `S^2`, `R>=0` independent, and `E R^2=3`. Let `a_i^T` be the rows of

\[
A=Q\Lambda^{1/2}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb E\min_i(a_i\cdot Z)^2
=3\,\mathbb E_{U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^2)}\min_i(a_i\cdot U)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

By layer cake,

\[
\mathbb E_U\min_i(a_i\cdot U)^2
=
\int_0^\infty
\sigma\bigl\{u\in S^2: |a_i\cdot u|>\sqrt{s}\ \forall i\bigr\}\,ds,
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `σ` is normalized spherical area. For a fixed `s`, the excluded set for row `a_i` is a symmetric spherical zone about the great circle orthogonal to `a_i`; its half-width is

\[
\arcsin\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{s}}{\|a_i\|}\right)
\]

when `s<=||a_i||^2`.

The crucial point is that the three zone normals and their widths are **not free**. They come from one matrix `A` whose singular values are fixed at `sqrt(lambda_1),sqrt(lambda_2),sqrt(lambda_3)`. Thus the problem is an integrated uncovered-area comparison for three *linked, generally unequal* zones under a fixed-singular-value constraint.

This language makes precise why the existing covering theorem is close but insufficient: covering tells us when uncovered area has reached zero, whereas (3.2)/(4.2) needs the entire area profile, with exact weights, across the intermediate thresholds.
