> M_3(P):=\mathbb E_{\theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^{p-1})}

## 3. Recovered exact cubic formula

The archived `11-32-02Z` report derived the exact sphere representation for positive two-eigenvalue spikes and the exact invariant formula for the cubic moment. The backfilled `12-31-55Z` run proved the key coefficient signs and solved all ranks `r>=3`.

For completeness, use
\[
X_i=\theta_i^2,
\]
\[
A_3=\mathbb E[f_d X_1^3],\qquad
B_3=\mathbb E[f_d X_1^2X_2],\qquad
C_3=\mathbb E[f_d X_1X_2X_3].
\]
For a rank-`r` projection `P`, write
\[
d_i=P_{ii},\quad
S_2=\sum_i d_i^2,\quad
S_3=\sum_i d_i^3.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
M_3(P)
=C_3(r^3+6r^2+8r)
+3(r+4)(B_3-3C_3)S_2
+(A_3-15B_3+30C_3)S_3.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The recovered pair-conditioning proof gives the strict inequalities
\[
D_1:=B_3-3C_3>0,
\qquad
D_2:=A_3-5B_3>0.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Indeed, conditioning on `S=X_1+X_2` and all outside squared coordinates, with
\[
Q=\frac{|X_1-X_2|}{S}=|\cos2\phi|,
\]
one has
\[
B_3-3C_3
=\mathbb E\left[f_d X_3S^2(Q^2-1/2)\right],
\]
\[
A_3-5B_3
=\mathbb E\left[f_d S^3(Q^2-1/2)\right].
\]
For fixed `S` and outside squares, `f_d` is nondecreasing in `Q`, while `E[Q^2]=1/2`; strictness holds on a positive-probability region where `f_d=constant+max(X_1,X_2)`.

For a coordinate projection, `S_2=S_3=r`. Set
\[
\Delta_2=r-S_2=\sum_i d_i(1-d_i),
\]
\[
\Delta_3=r-S_3=\sum_i d_i(1-d_i)(1+d_i).
\]
Subtracting (3.1) from the coordinate value gives the exact gap
\[
\boxed{
M_3(P_{coord})-M_3(P)
=3(r+4)D_1\Delta_2+(D_2-10D_1)\Delta_3.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
The recovered run used the crude universal bound `Delta_3<=2 Delta_2` to prove positivity for every `r>=3`. The only open cubic case was rank two.

## 4. New diagonal inequality for rank two

The exact rank-two problem in (3.3) reduces to a deterministic inequality on a projection diagonal.

### Lemma 4.1

If
\[
0\le x_i\le1,\qquad \sum_i x_i=2,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_3:=\sum_i x_i(1-x_i)(1+x_i)
\le \frac53\sum_i x_i(1-x_i)=\frac53\Delta_2.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
3\sum_i x_i^3-5\sum_i x_i^2+4\ge0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Because `sum x_i=2`, define
\[
g(x)=3x^3-5x^2+2x.
\]
Then the left side of (4.2) is exactly `sum_i g(x_i)`.

We prove its minimum on the compact capped simplex is nonnegative.

**Boundary `x_i=0`.** Drop that coordinate; nothing changes.

**Boundary `x_i=1`.** The remaining coordinates `y_j` satisfy `sum y_j=1`. For `a,b>=0` with `a+b<=1`,
\[
g(a)+g(b)-g(a+b)
=ab\bigl(10-9(a+b)\bigr)\ge0.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Iteratively merging the `y_j` therefore gives
\[
\sum_jg(y_j)\ge g(1)=0.
\]
Since `g(1)=0`, every boundary point with a unit coordinate is nonnegative.

It remains to consider an interior minimizer with all active coordinates in `(0,1)`. By Lagrange multipliers,
\[
g'(x_i)=\lambda,
\qquad
g'(x)=9x^2-10x+2.
\]
Hence the active coordinates take at most two distinct values.

**One interior value.** If there are `N` active coordinates, each equals `2/N`, with `N>=3`. Then
\[
N g(2/N)
=\frac{4(N-2)(N-3)}{N^2}\ge0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

**Two distinct interior values.** Let the values be `a!=b`, with multiplicities `m,n`. Since `g'(a)=g'(b)`,
\[
(a-b)\bigl(9(a+b)-10\bigr)=0,
\]
so
\[
a+b=\frac{10}{9}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
If both multiplicities were at least two, the total sum would be at least
\[
2(a+b)=\frac{20}{9}>2,
\]
a contradiction. Thus one value occurs once. Let the total active count be `N>=3`, with singleton `a` and repeated value `b`. Solving
\[
a+(N-1)b=2,
\qquad a+b=10/9
\]
gives
\[
b=\frac{8}{9(N-2)},
\qquad
a=\frac{10N-28}{9(N-2)}.
\]
Direct substitution yields
\[
g(a)+(N-1)g(b)
=
\frac{8(59N^2-356N+540)}{243(N-2)^2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
The quadratic numerator is strictly positive for all real `N` because its discriminant is
\[
356^2-4\cdot59\cdot540=-704<0.
\]
Thus every interior critical candidate is nonnegative. Together with the boundary analysis this proves (4.1). QED.

The constant `5/3` is sharp: equality is attained by `(2/3,2/3,2/3,0,...)` and also in the limiting coordinate-projection boundary.

## 5. Rank-two cubic gap is strictly positive

Take `r=2` in (3.3):
\[
G(P):=M_3(P_{coord})-M_3(P)
=18D_1\Delta_2+(D_2-10D_1)\Delta_3.
\tag{5.1}
\]
If `D_2-10D_1>=0`, positivity is immediate because `D_1>0` and `Delta_2>=0`.

If `D_2-10D_1<0`, Lemma 4.1 may be used with the inequality direction reversed after multiplication by the negative coefficient:
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(P)
&\ge
18D_1\Delta_2
+\frac53(D_2-10D_1)\Delta_2\\
&=
\frac{4D_1+5D_2}{3}\,\Delta_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Because `D_1,D_2>0`, the coefficient is strictly positive. Therefore
\[
\boxed{M_3(P)<M_3(P_{coord})}
\]
for every noncoordinate rank-two projection.

This closes the exact gap left by the backfilled `12-31-55Z` run without needing the previously proposed, stronger and unnecessary sign
\[
A_3-6B_3+3C_3\ge0.
\]
That sign may or may not hold; it is no longer required for cubic optimality.

## 6. New rank-one diagonal lemma and a self-contained cubic proof

For completeness, cubic maximality can also be proved directly for rank one from (3.3), without importing the stronger all-orders one-spike theorem.

### Lemma 6.1

If `0<=x_i<=1` and `sum x_i=1`, then
\[
\boxed{\Delta_3\le\frac32\Delta_2.}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
2\sum_i x_i^3-3\sum_i x_i^2+1\ge0.
\]

Set
\[
h(x)=2x^3-3x^2+x.
\]
For `a,b>=0`, `a+b<=1`,
\[
h(a)+h(b)-h(a+b)
=6ab(1-a-b)\ge0.
\]
Repeated merging gives
\[
\sum_i h(x_i)\ge h(1)=0.
\]
This proves (6.1).

Now put `r=1` in (3.3). If `D_2-10D_1>=0`, positivity is immediate. Otherwise,
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(P)
&\ge15D_1\Delta_2
+\frac32(D_2-10D_1)\Delta_2\\
&=\frac32D_2\Delta_2>0
\end{aligned}
\]
for every noncoordinate rank-one projection.

## 8. Consequence for the two-eigenvalue series

For
\[
C=aI+cP,\qquad a>0,c\ge0,
\]
the exact spherical representation gives, up to an orientation-independent positive factor,
\[
J_\kappa(P)
=\mathbb E\left[
f_d(\theta)(1-\kappa\theta^TP\theta)^{-\alpha}\right],
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{p+2}{2},\quad
\kappa=\frac{c}{a+c}.
\]
Its binomial expansion is
\[
J_\kappa(P)
=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(\alpha)_k}{k!}\kappa^kM_k(P),
\]
with positive coefficients.

The exact ledger is now:

- `M_0`: orientation-independent;
- `M_1`: orientation-independent;
- `M_2`: coordinate projection uniquely maximizes for every rank (archived `11-32-02Z` report);
- `M_3`: coordinate projection uniquely maximizes for every rank (this run + recovered backfill);
- `M_k`, `k>=4`: open in general.

Therefore the first two nonconstant orientation coefficients are globally aligned with the KL/coordinate eigenspace for **all** projection ranks.

This strengthens the weak-spike theorem and substantially narrows any possible two-eigenvalue counterexample: it must arise from a genuinely nonperturbative accumulation of quartic-and-higher terms, not from quadratic or cubic orientation effects.
