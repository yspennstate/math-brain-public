# PROB20-19 — Full nonlinear Gaussian PCA conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.**

The universal Mallat–Zeitouni/Bandeira conjecture remains open. This run obtains a new exact projection-moment theorem for the full `Top_d` objective and uses it to isolate the first nontrivial orientation coefficient for arbitrary-rank two-eigenvalue spikes.

The main exact result is: for every rank-`r` orthogonal projection `P`, the second spherical moment

\[
M_2(P):=\mathbb E_{\theta\sim \mathrm{Unif}(S^{p-1})}
\Big[f_d(\theta)(\theta^T P\theta)^2\Big],
\qquad
f_d(\theta)=\operatorname{Top}_d(\theta_1^2,\ldots,\theta_p^2),
\]

is maximized by a coordinate projection. The gap has the exact form

\[
\boxed{
M_2(P_{\rm coord})-M_2(P)
=(A_2-3B_2)\Big(r-\sum_i P_{ii}^2\Big),
}
\]

with a strictly positive coefficient

\[
A_2=\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_1^4],
\qquad
B_2=\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_1^2\theta_2^2],
\qquad
A_2-3B_2>0.
\]

Since

\[
r-\sum_iP_{ii}^2=2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2,
\]

the orientation gap is exactly proportional to the off-diagonal Frobenius mass of `P`.

This identifies the first orientation-sensitive coefficient in the complete positive-series expansion for every positive rank-`r` spike `aI+cP`, not merely rank one. It also yields a short independent proof that coordinate eigenspaces are uniformly optimal for sufficiently weak two-eigenvalue spikes. The full finite-spike problem remains open because higher moment coefficients are not all reduced to one positive invariant.

## 2. Exact sphere representation

For any positive-definite covariance `C`, degree-two homogeneity of `f_d` gives

\[
\mathcal F_d(C)
:=\mathbb E_{X\sim N(0,C)}f_d(X)
=
\frac{\Gamma((p+2)/2)}{\pi^{p/2}\sqrt{\det C}}
\int_{S^{p-1}}
 f_d(\theta)
 (\theta^TC^{-1}\theta)^{-(p+2)/2}
\,dS(\theta).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed in polar coordinates `x=r theta`,

\[
\int_0^\infty r^{p+1}e^{-qr^2/2}\,dr
=2^{p/2}\Gamma((p+2)/2)q^{-(p+2)/2}.
\]

For a positive rank-`r` spike

\[
C=aI+cP,
\qquad P^2=P=P^T,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}P=r,
\qquad a>0,c\ge0,
\]

we have

\[
C^{-1}=a^{-1}(I-\kappa P),
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{c}{a+c}\in[0,1),
\]

so, up to an orientation-independent positive constant,

\[
\mathcal F_d(aI+cP)
\propto
J_\kappa(P)
:=\mathbb E_{\theta}
\left[f_d(\theta)(1-\kappa\,\theta^TP\theta)^{-\alpha}\right],
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{p+2}{2}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The binomial expansion has strictly positive coefficients:

\[
(1-\kappa q)^{-\alpha}
=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(\alpha)_k}{k!}\kappa^k q^k.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the two-eigenvalue problem reduces to the hierarchy

\[
M_k(P):=\mathbb E\left[f_d(\theta)(\theta^TP\theta)^k\right].
\]

The `k=0` term is constant. The `k=1` term is also orientation-independent. The first informative term is `k=2`, and it can be solved exactly.

## 3. First moment is orientation-independent

Write `d_i=P_{ii}`. Because `f_d(theta)` is invariant under independent sign flips,

\[
\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_i\theta_j]=0
\quad(i\ne j),
\]

while permutation symmetry gives

\[
\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_i^2]=C_1
\]

independent of `i`. Therefore

\[
M_1(P)
=\sum_i d_i C_1
=C_1\operatorname{tr}P
=C_1r.
\tag{3.1}
\]

So no orientation information appears at first order in spike strength.

## 4. Exact second-moment identity

Set

\[
A_2:=\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_1^4],
\qquad
B_2:=\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_1^2\theta_2^2].
\]

Expand

\[
Q:=\theta^TP\theta
=\sum_i d_i\theta_i^2
+2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}\theta_i\theta_j.
\]

After squaring, every term with an odd power of some coordinate vanishes against `f_d` by sign symmetry. Hence

\[
M_2(P)
=A_2\sum_i d_i^2
+2B_2\sum_{i<j}d_id_j
+4B_2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since `P` is a projection,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(P^2)=\operatorname{tr}P=r,
\]

so

\[
\sum_i d_i^2+2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2=r.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Also

\[
2\sum_{i<j}d_id_j=r^2-\sum_i d_i^2.
\]

Substitution into (4.1) yields the exact invariant formula

\[
\boxed{
M_2(P)
=B_2r^2+2B_2r+(A_2-3B_2)\sum_i d_i^2.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For a coordinate rank-`r` projection, `d_i in {0,1}` and

\[
\sum_i d_i^2=r.
\]

For any projection, `0<=d_i<=1` and `sum d_i=r`, so

\[
\sum_i d_i^2\le r,
\]

with equality iff every `d_i` is `0` or `1`, equivalently iff `P` is diagonal, hence a coordinate projection up to permutation.

It remains only to prove

\[
A_2-3B_2>0.
\]

## 5. Strict positivity of the coefficient

By exchange symmetry of coordinates `1,2`,

\[
A_2-3B_2
=\mathbb E\left[
f_d(\theta)
\left(
\frac{\theta_1^4+\theta_2^4}{2}
-3\theta_1^2\theta_2^2
\right)
\right].
\tag{5.1}
\]

Condition on

\[
S=\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2
\]

and on all outside squared coordinates. Conditional on these quantities, the angle in the first two coordinates is uniform. Put

\[
Q_*:=\frac{|\theta_1^2-\theta_2^2|}{S}=|\cos 2\phi|.
\]

For fixed `S` and fixed outside squares, `f_d(theta)` is a nondecreasing function `h(Q_*)`: making the first two squared coordinates more unequal while preserving their sum can only increase the sum of the largest `d` coordinates.

Moreover

\[
\frac{\theta_1^4+\theta_2^4}{2}
-3\theta_1^2\theta_2^2
=S^2\left(Q_*^2-\frac12\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The uniform angle satisfies

\[
\mathbb E[Q_*^2]=\frac12.
\]

Therefore the conditional contribution to (5.1) is

\[
S^2\operatorname{Cov}(h(Q_*),Q_*^2)\ge0,
\]

because both factors are nondecreasing functions of the same scalar variable.

The inequality is strict on a positive-probability set: choose `S` and outside squared coordinates so that exactly `d-1` outside coordinates exceed `S` and every remaining outside square is smaller than `S/4`. Then

\[
f_d=\text{constant}+\max(\theta_1^2,\theta_2^2)
=\text{constant}+\frac S2(1+Q_*),
\]

which is strictly increasing in `Q_*`. Hence

\[
\boxed{A_2-3B_2>0.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Combining (4.3) and (5.3):

### Theorem 5.1 — exact rank-r second-moment extremizer

For every `p>=2`, `1<=d<p`, and every rank-`r` orthogonal projection `P`,

\[
\boxed{
M_2(P_{\rm coord})-M_2(P)
=(A_2-3B_2)
\left(r-\sum_iP_{ii}^2\right)
=2(A_2-3B_2)\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus coordinate eigenspaces uniquely maximize the first orientation-sensitive moment.

## 6. Consequence for weak positive two-eigenvalue spikes

From (2.3),

\[
J_\kappa(P)
=M_0+\alpha\kappa M_1
+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}2\kappa^2M_2(P)
+O(\kappa^3).
\]

The first two terms are orientation-independent, while Theorem 5.1 gives a strict second-order advantage to coordinate projections.

For a fixed non-coordinate `P`, this already proves coordinate optimality for all sufficiently small positive `kappa`.

A uniform statement over the entire rank-`r` Grassmannian also follows by a relative-remainder argument. For each `k>=3`, `M_k(P)` is smooth in `P`. The finite set of coordinate rank-`r` projections are fixed by diagonal sign conjugations; therefore all first derivatives of `M_k` in tangent directions vanish there. Hence near the coordinate set,

\[
|M_k(P_{\rm coord})-M_k(P)|
\le C_k\,\operatorname{dist}(P,\mathcal C_r)^2.
\]

Meanwhile

\[
r-\sum_iP_{ii}^2
=2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2
\]

is comparable to that squared distance near the coordinate set, and is bounded away from zero on the compact complement of any such neighborhood. Consequently the full analytic remainder obeys, for `kappa` in a fixed small interval,

\[
|R_3(P_{\rm coord},\kappa)-R_3(P,\kappa)|
\le C\kappa^3
\left(r-\sum_iP_{ii}^2\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Combining (5.4) and (6.1) gives:

### Corollary 6.1 — uniform weak-spike theorem

For every fixed triple `(p,d,r)` with `1<=d<p` and `1<=r<p`, there exists `kappa_0(p,d,r)>0` such that for every rank-`r` projection `P` and every `0<=kappa<=kappa_0`,

\[
J_\kappa(P)\le J_\kappa(P_{\rm coord}).
\]

Equivalently, every sufficiently weak positive two-eigenvalue spike

\[
aI+cP
\]

is exactly KL-optimal, uniformly over all orientations of the rank-`r` eigenspace.

This is still only a neighborhood result in spike strength; the finite-spike rank-`r` problem is open.

## 7. Exact third moment: the next obstruction

The positive-series strategy would settle every positive two-eigenvalue spectrum if coordinate projections maximized every `M_k`. The next term already has richer structure.

For `p>=3`, define

\[
A_3=\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_1^6],
\quad
B_3=\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_1^4\theta_2^2],
\quad
C_3=\mathbb E[f_d(\theta)\theta_1^2\theta_2^2\theta_3^2].
\]

Let

\[
S_2(P)=\sum_iP_{ii}^2,
\qquad
S_3(P)=\sum_iP_{ii}^3.
\]

A complete sign-parity expansion of `Q^3=(theta^T P theta)^3`, followed by the projection identities `P^2=P` and `tr P^3=r`, gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
M_3(P)
={}&C_3(r^3+6r^2+8r)\\
&+3(r+4)(B_3-3C_3)S_2(P)\\
&+(A_3-15B_3+30C_3)S_3(P).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For a coordinate projection, `S_2=S_3=r`, recovering directly

\[
rA_3+3r(r-1)B_3+r(r-1)(r-2)C_3.
\]

Thus the third term is no longer governed by a single positive coefficient. A proof via coefficient-by-coefficient positivity now requires nontrivial inequalities among `A_3,B_3,C_3` and the feasible pair `(S_2,S_3)` for projection diagonals. This is the first exact obstruction to the naive moment-hierarchy route.

The derivation of (7.1) is also useful diagnostically: it reduces all orientation dependence at order three to the two scalar diagonal invariants `S_2,S_3`; triangle products and off-diagonal squares cancel through projection identities.

## 8. Derivation of the third-moment formula

Write

\[
Q=D+O,
\qquad
D=\sum_i d_i\theta_i^2,
\qquad
O=2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}\theta_i\theta_j.
\]

Only even coordinate-parity monomials survive against `f_d`.

The diagonal cube contributes

\[
A_3\sum_i d_i^3
+3B_3\sum_{i\ne j}d_i^2d_j
+6C_3\sum_{i<j<k}d_id_jd_k.
\]

The `3DO^2` term contributes

\[
12B_3\sum_{i<j}(d_i+d_j)P_{ij}^2
+12C_3\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2\sum_{\ell\ne i,j}d_\ell.
\]

The `O^3` term survives only on triangles and contributes

\[
48C_3\sum_{i<j<k}P_{ij}P_{jk}P_{ki}.
\]

Now use

\[
\sum_{i<j}(d_i+d_j)P_{ij}^2=S_2-S_3,
\]

\[
\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2=\frac{r-S_2}{2},
\]

and, from `tr P^3=r`,

\[
6\sum_{i<j<k}P_{ij}P_{jk}P_{ki}
=r-3S_2+2S_3.
\]

Together with the elementary symmetric identities for the diagonal entries, these simplify exactly to (7.1).

## 10. Failed / blocked route clarified

The hope that every positive-series coefficient in (2.3) would have an orientation gap proportional to the single invariant `r-S_2` fails structurally at `k=3`: the exact expression contains both `S_2` and `S_3` with distinct coefficient combinations. Thus a complete two-eigenvalue proof needs either:

- sharp inequalities among the coefficient combinations `B_3-3C_3` and `A_3-15B_3+30C_3` together with the feasible `(S_2,S_3)` region, and their higher-order analogues; or
- a nonperturbative rearrangement/polarization theorem that controls the full weight `(1-kappa Q)^(-alpha)` at once.

No sign claim for the third-order combinations is made here.

## 11. Next attack

The next mathematically sharp target is the finite-strength positive two-eigenvalue family.

### Route A: solve the `k=3` coefficient geometry exactly

For projection diagonals `0<=d_i<=1`, `sum d_i=r`,

\[
r-S_3=\sum_i d_i(1-d_i)(1+d_i),
\]

so

\[
r-S_2\le r-S_3\le2(r-S_2).
\]

Combine this feasible relation with (7.1). It is enough at order three to prove the appropriate coefficient inequality depending on the sign of `A_3-15B_3+30C_3`. These coefficient inequalities may be approachable by the same two-coordinate angular conditioning used to prove `A_2-3B_2>0`.

### Route B: full spherical polarization

Prove directly that for every increasing convex weight

\[
W(q)=(1-\kappa q)^{-\alpha},
\]

the integral

\[
\int_{S^{p-1}}f_d(\theta)W(\theta^TP\theta)dS(\theta)
\]

is maximized when `P` is a coordinate projection. A two-plane polarization of the subspace, if monotone, would bypass the coefficient hierarchy entirely.

### Route C: certified counterexample search

If Route B is false, use deterministic sphere quadrature plus interval arithmetic on the Grassmannian for the first nontrivial rank-2 case. Any candidate must then be certified by interval enclosures, not by reusing Monte Carlo optimization samples.
