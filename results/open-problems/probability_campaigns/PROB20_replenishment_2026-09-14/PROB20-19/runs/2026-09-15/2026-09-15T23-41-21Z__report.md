## 3. Occupancy formulation of the missing top-three facet

Let `P` be a rank-`r` orthogonal projection in `R^p`. Write

\[
P=QQ^T,
\qquad Q^TQ=I_r,
\]

and let `a_i in R^r` denote the rows of `Q`. Put

\[
d_i=\|a_i\|^2=P_{ii},
\qquad q_{ij}=P_{ij}^2=(a_i\cdot a_j)^2.
\]

Then

\[
0\le d_i\le1,
\qquad \sum_i d_i=r,
\qquad \sum_j q_{ij}=d_i.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `U` uniform on `S^{r-1}`, define

\[
p_i(U)=(a_i\cdot U)^2,
\qquad \sum_i p_i(U)=1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The previous checkpoint identifies the degree-five geometric partition law with the sorted occupancy partition of five iid categorical draws from this random probability vector.

The first three quintic partitions

\[
(5)\succ(4,1)\succ(3,2)
\]

are exactly the event

\[
K_5\le2,
\tag{3.3}
\]

where `K_5` is the number of distinct observed categories among the five draws.

Thus the missing top-three stochastic facet asks whether the coordinate projection maximizes

\[
\Pr\{K_5\le2\}.
\]

## 4. Exact pair formula for `Pr(K_5<=2)`

For a deterministic probability vector `p=(p_i)`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{K_5\le2\mid p\}
=\sum_{i<j}(p_i+p_j)^5-(p-2)\sum_i p_i^5.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, a sample using exactly two categories is counted once by the unique supporting pair, whereas a sample using only category `i` is counted `p-1` times in the pair sum and must be corrected down to one copy.

For each pair define the rank-at-most-two PSD matrix

\[
A_{ij}=a_i a_i^T+a_j a_j^T.
\]

Its two possibly nonzero eigenvalues have

\[
t_{ij}:=\operatorname{tr}A_{ij}=d_i+d_j,
\qquad
\delta_{ij}:=\det\operatorname{Gram}(a_i,a_j)
=d_i d_j-q_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For any rank-at-most-two PSD matrix with nonzero eigenvalues `lambda_1,lambda_2`, Gaussian/spherical moment conversion gives

\[
T_{r,5}\,\mathbb E_U(U^TAU)^5
=\mathbb E(\lambda_1Z_1^2+\lambda_2Z_2^2)^5,
\]

where

\[
T_{r,5}=r(r+2)(r+4)(r+6)(r+8).
\]

Expanding the Gaussian moment and rewriting in `t=lambda_1+lambda_2`, `delta=lambda_1lambda_2` gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
T_{r,5}\,\mathbb E_U(U^TAU)^5
=945t^5-4200\delta t^3+3600\delta^2t.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Define

\[
S_k:=\sum_i d_i^k,
\]

and the two exterior-pair quantities

\[
J:=\sum_{i<j}\delta_{ij}d_i d_j(d_i+d_j),
\qquad
K:=\sum_{i<j}\delta_{ij}^2(d_i+d_j).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Because the wedge vectors `a_i\wedge a_j` form a Parseval frame in `Lambda^2 R^r`, equivalently by a direct Cauchy--Binet computation,

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{ij}=(r-1)d_i.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Using (4.1)–(4.5), together with

\[
\sum_{i<j}\big[(d_i+d_j)^5-d_i^5-d_j^5\big]
=5(rS_4-S_5)+10(S_2S_3-S_5),
\]

one obtains the exact normalized numerator

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_3(P)
:=T_{r,5}\Pr_P\{K_5\le2\}
={}&9450S_2S_3+525(r+8)S_4-13230S_5\\
&-12600J+3600K.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

For a coordinate rank-`r` projection,

\[
\boxed{C_3(P_{\rm coord})=975r^2-30r.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

## 5. Rewriting the exterior terms through `P` and `I-P`

Put

\[
u_{ij}=d_i d_j,
\qquad q_{ij}=P_{ij}^2,
\qquad \delta_{ij}=u_{ij}-q_{ij}.
\]

Define

\[
L:=\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}d_i d_j(d_i+d_j),
\qquad
M:=\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}^2(d_i+d_j).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since

\[
\sum_{i<j}d_i^2d_j^2(d_i+d_j)=S_2S_3-S_5,
\]

we have

\[
J=S_2S_3-S_5-L,
\qquad
K=S_2S_3-S_5-2L+M.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Substituting (5.2) into (4.6) gives the exact alternative form

\[
\boxed{
C_3(P)
=450S_2S_3+525(r+8)S_4-4230S_5+5400L+3600M.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Now use the projection identities

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}=d_i(1-d_i).
\tag{5.4}
\]

First,

\[
\begin{aligned}
L
&=\sum_{i\ne j}q_{ij}d_i^2d_j\\
&\le\sum_{i\ne j}q_{ij}d_i^2\\
&=\sum_i d_i^3(1-d_i)
=S_3-S_4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Second, because `I-P` is PSD,

\[
q_{ij}=P_{ij}^2\le(1-d_i)(1-d_j).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
M
&\le\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}(d_i+d_j)(1-d_i)(1-d_j)\\
&=\sum_i d_i(1-d_i)\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}(1-d_j)\\
&\le\sum_i[d_i(1-d_i)]^2\\
&=S_2-2S_3+S_4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Combining (5.3), (5.5), and (5.7),

\[
\boxed{
C_3(P)
\le
450S_2S_3+3600S_2-1800S_3+(525r+2400)S_4-4230S_5.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is the new key bound.
