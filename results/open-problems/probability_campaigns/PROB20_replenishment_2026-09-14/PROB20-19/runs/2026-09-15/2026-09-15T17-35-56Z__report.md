# PROB20-19 — Full nonlinear Gaussian PCA conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.** The universal Mallat–Zeitouni/Bandeira conjecture remains open. This run resumes the exact projection-moment program from the newest verified GitHub checkpoint and proves a new exact quartic theorem for the first unresolved rank-three case: **rank `r=3` in ambient dimension `p=4`**. Equivalently, this is the quartic coefficient for a codimension-one positive eigenspace, i.e. a covariance with one smaller eigenvalue and three equal larger eigenvalues.

The new exact conclusion is:

> Let `P` be any rank-three orthogonal projection in `R^4`, let
> \[
> f_d(\theta)=\operatorname{Top}_d(\theta_1^2,\ldots,\theta_4^2),
> \qquad 1\le d<4,
> \]
> and define
> \[
> M_4(P)=\mathbb E_{\theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^3)}
> \left[f_d(\theta)(\theta^TP\theta)^4\right].
> \]
> Then
> \[
> \boxed{M_4(P)\le M_4(P_{\rm coord})}
> \]
> with strict inequality for every noncoordinate `P`.

The proof closes the only geometric coefficient still open at rank three, `lambda_1`, in the codimension-one case. It yields the explicit lower bound
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1(P)\ge 4e_2>0
}
\]
for every noncoordinate rank-three projection in `R^4`, where `e_2` is the second elementary symmetric polynomial of the squared coordinates of the one-dimensional complementary vector.

This run also derives a new set of exact **complement-transfer identities** connecting the quartic geometric coefficients of a rank-three projection to those of its complement. These identities reduce future rank-three work to quantitative relations among already studied lower-rank coefficients rather than a fresh quartic expansion.

This is still only a quartic coefficient theorem for a special two-eigenvalue multiplicity pattern. It does not prove the full finite-spike two-eigenvalue theorem and does not resolve arbitrary spectra.

## 4. New analytic hierarchy identities

This subsection records an exact algebraic bridge between the quartic analytic contrasts and the lower-degree contrasts used in the quadratic and cubic projection-moment theorems. It is useful for complement arguments below.

Let the five degree-four permutation moment types be
\[
A=\mathbb E[f_dX_1^4],
\quad
B=\mathbb E[f_dX_1^3X_2],
\quad
C=\mathbb E[f_dX_1^2X_2^2],
\]
\[
D=\mathbb E[f_dX_1^2X_2X_3],
\quad
E=\mathbb E[f_dX_1X_2X_3X_4],
\qquad X_i=\theta_i^2.
\]
The verified quartic positive contrasts are
\[
I_1=D-3E,
\qquad
I_2=C-3D,
\qquad
I_3=B-5D,
\qquad
I_4=A-4B-5C.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Because `sum_i X_i=1` on the sphere, the degree-three moment types satisfy
\[
A_3=A+(p-1)B,
\]
\[
B_3=B+C+(p-2)D,
\]
\[
C_3=3D+(p-3)E.
\]
Therefore the two cubic positive contrasts reduce exactly to
\[
\boxed{
B_3-3C_3=(p-3)I_1+I_2+I_3,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
\[
\boxed{
A_3-5B_3=(p-2)I_3+I_4.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Similarly the degree-two contrast from the exact quadratic theorem is
\[
A_2-3B_2,
\]
where
\[
A_2=A_3+(p-1)B_3,
\qquad
B_2=2B_3+(p-2)C_3.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\boxed{
A_2-3B_2
=(p-2)(p-3)I_1
+(p-2)I_2
+2(p-2)I_3
+I_4.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Thus every lower-order analytic contrast is a nonnegative linear combination of the quartic positive contrasts. These identities are exact, require no approximation, and are independent of projection orientation.

## 5. New complement-transfer identities for rank-three projections

Let `P` have rank three in `R^p` and let
\[
Q=I-P,
\qquad
s=\operatorname{rank}Q=p-3.
\]
For the coordinate reference projections choose `Q_0=I-P_0`. Put
\[
G_k(Q)=M_k(Q_0)-M_k(Q).
\]
Since
\[
\theta^TP\theta=1-\theta^TQ\theta,
\]
we have pointwise
\[
(\theta^TP\theta)^4
=(1-q)^4
=1-4q+6q^2-4q^3+q^4,
\qquad q=\theta^TQ\theta.
\]
The zeroth and first projection moments are orientation-independent, so
\[
\boxed{
M_4(P_0)-M_4(P)
=6G_2(Q)-4G_3(Q)+G_4(Q).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Using (4.2)--(4.4), together with the exact quadratic, cubic and quartic geometric gap formulas, and comparing coefficients of `I_1,...,I_4`, gives the following transfer law.

Let
\[
W=s-\sum_iQ_{ii}^2,
\qquad
\Delta_3(Q)=s-\sum_iQ_{ii}^3,
\]
and define
\[
H=40\Delta_3(Q)-6(s+7)W.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1(P)=\lambda_1(Q)+sH,
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_2(P)=\lambda_2(Q)+H,
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_3(P)=\lambda_3(Q)
+4\big[(9-s)\Delta_3(Q)-9W\big],
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_4(P)=\lambda_4(Q)+6W-4\Delta_3(Q).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1(P)-s\lambda_2(P)
=
\lambda_1(Q)-s\lambda_2(Q).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
This exact complement invariant is a useful new diagnostic for the remaining rank-three problem.

If
\[
\mu_P=
\frac{\sum_iP_{ii}(1-P_{ii})P_{ii}}
     {\sum_iP_{ii}(1-P_{ii})},
\]
then `mu_Q=1-mu_P` and (5.3) may equivalently be written
\[
\frac{\lambda_1(P)-\lambda_1(Q)}{W}
=2s\big(19-3s-20\mu_P\big).
\tag{5.8}
\]
This shows exactly why complement positivity alone is insufficient in general: the transfer term changes sign as the weighted diagonal mean varies. No circular inference is made from (5.8).

## 6. Codimension-one specialization `p=4`, `r=3`

Now let `p=4`, so the complement has rank one. Write
\[
Q=zz^T,
\qquad
\|z\|_2=1,
\]
and define
\[
e_i=z_i^2,
\qquad
e_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^4e_i=1.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
P=I-zz^T,
\qquad
d_i=P_{ii}=1-e_i,
\]
and for `i ne j`,
\[
P_{ij}^2=e_ie_j.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Let the elementary symmetric polynomials of `e_1,...,e_4` be
\[
e_1^{\rm elem}=\sum_i e_i=1,
\]
\[
e_2=\sum_{i<j}e_ie_j,
\quad
e_3=\sum_{i<j<k}e_ie_je_k,
\quad
e_4=e_1e_2e_3e_4.
\]
(Here the superscript on the first elementary symmetric polynomial is only to distinguish it from the coordinate `e_1`.)

Substitute (6.1)--(6.2) into the exact rank-three formula (3.4). A direct symmetric-polynomial reduction gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1(P)
=24\big(e_2-5e_3+35e_4\big).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
The full unspecialized symmetric reduction before imposing `sum e_i=1` is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_1={}&-105s_1^4+60s_1^3+420s_1^2s_2+126s_1^2
-360s_1s_2-840s_1s_3\\
&-12s_1-36s_2+720s_3+840s_4-69,
\end{aligned}
\]
where `s_j` are the elementary symmetric polynomials. Setting `s_1=1` reduces exactly to (6.3).

## 7. Strict positivity of the codimension-one coefficient

It remains to prove
\[
e_2-5e_3+35e_4\ge0.
\]
In fact a stronger elementary bound holds.

First, because `sum_i e_i=1`,
\[
1=\sum_i e_i^2+2e_2.
\]
Cauchy gives
\[
\sum_i e_i^2\ge\frac14,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{e_2\le\frac38.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Second, Newton's inequality for four nonnegative variables gives
\[
\left(\frac{e_2}{\binom42}\right)^2
\ge
\left(\frac{e_1^{\rm elem}}{\binom41}\right)
\left(\frac{e_3}{\binom43}\right).
\]
Since `e_1^{elem}=1`,
\[
\left(\frac{e_2}{6}\right)^2\ge\frac{e_3}{16},
\]
therefore
\[
e_3\le\frac49e_2^2.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Combining with (7.1),
\[
\boxed{
e_3\le\frac16e_2.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Hence, using `e_4>=0`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_1(P)
&=24(e_2-5e_3+35e_4)\\
&\ge24\left(e_2-\frac56e_2\right)\\
&=4e_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{\lambda_1(P)\ge4e_2\ge0.}
\tag{7.5}
\]
Moreover `e_2=0` for a nonnegative vector of sum one iff exactly one `e_i=1` and the others vanish. That is precisely the case in which `Q` and `P` are coordinate projections. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1(P)>0
\quad\text{for every noncoordinate rank-three }P\subset\mathbb R^4.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

## 8. Quartic theorem for rank three in `R^4`

The recovered checkpoint already proves
\[
I_1,I_2,I_3,I_4>0

for the `Top_d` sphere functional and
\[
\lambda_2,\lambda_3,\lambda_4\ge0

for every rank-three projection. Section 7 proves `lambda_1>0` for every noncoordinate rank-three projection in `R^4`.

Therefore:

### Theorem 8.1 — exact quartic codimension-one theorem

For every `1<=d<4` and every rank-three orthogonal projection `P` in `R^4`,
\[
\boxed{
M_4(P)\le M_4(P_{\rm coord}),
}
\]
with equality iff `P` is coordinate.

This includes the nonlinear retained dimensions and is independent of numerical optimization or Monte Carlo.

It is important not to overstate the conclusion. The theorem proves the **quartic coefficient** in the positive-series orientation expansion for this two-eigenvalue multiplicity pattern. It does not sum the full series at finite spike strength and therefore does not establish the complete negative-one-spike covariance theorem.

## 10. Failed / rejected shortcuts

1. **Complement positivity alone is insufficient.** Formula (5.8) has a sign-changing transfer term. Even when the complement rank is in a previously solved quartic range, one cannot simply infer `lambda_1(P)>=0` without a quantitative lower bound.

2. **The generic compression lower bound remains too weak at rank three.** Reusing only the PSD compression estimate from the preceding checkpoints leaves a negative scalar relaxation in a midrange of the diagonal variance. The codimension-one proof succeeds because rank-one complement structure gives the exact off-diagonal squares `P_ij^2=e_i e_j`.

3. **No finite-spike conclusion is inferred from quartic positivity.** Higher coefficients can in principle change the finite-`kappa` comparison. The positive-series program still requires all higher moments or a direct whole-kernel polarization theorem.

4. **No claim is made that the exact codimension-one formula extends to higher codimension.** For complement rank at least two, extra Pluecker/cycle invariants survive and require additional control.
