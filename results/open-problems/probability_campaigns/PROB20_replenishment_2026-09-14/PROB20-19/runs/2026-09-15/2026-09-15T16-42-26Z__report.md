## 5. Integer-sum lemma I: no coordinate can carry more than half the total Bernoulli variance

### Lemma 5.1

Let `x_1,...,x_n in [0,1]` and assume
\[
\sum_i x_i=m\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Put
\[
W_x=\sum_i x_i(1-x_i).
\]
Then for every `i`,
\[
\boxed{
x_i(1-x_i)\le\frac{W_x}{2}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\sum_i[x_i(1-x_i)]^2\le\frac{W_x^2}{2}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Fix `x_i=t`. The remaining coordinates have sum
\[
m-t.
\]
Consider the concave function
\[
\phi(x)=x(1-x)
\]
on `[0,1]`. The minimum of
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}\phi(x_j)
\]
over the polytope
\[
0\le x_j\le1,
\qquad
\sum_{j\ne i}x_j=m-t
\]
is attained at an extreme point. At such an extreme point all but at most one coordinate are in `{0,1}`. Since `m` is integer, the unique possible fractional coordinate has value `1-t` when `0<t<1`. Therefore
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}x_j(1-x_j)
\ge t(1-t).
\]
Adding the `i`th contribution gives
\[
W_x\ge2t(1-t),
\]
which is (5.1). Then
\[
\sum_i[x_i(1-x_i)]^2
\le
\left(\max_i x_i(1-x_i)\right)W_x
\le\frac{W_x^2}{2}.
\]
QED.

Applied to the projection diagonal, Lemma 5.1 gives
\[
\boxed{q=Q/W\le W/2.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

## 6. Integer-sum lemma II: small-variance third cumulant

Define the Bernoulli third-cumulant sum
\[
K=\sum_i x_i(1-x_i)(1-2x_i).
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Lemma 6.1

If `x_i in [0,1]`, `sum_i x_i` is an integer, and
\[
0<W_x<\frac12,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{|K|\le W_x^2.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Split the fractional coordinates according to the nearest endpoint. For coordinates `x_i<=1/2`, write
\[
a_i=x_i.
\]
For coordinates `x_j>1/2`, write the deficit
\[
b_j=1-x_j.
\]
Thus every `a_i,b_j` lies in `[0,1/2]`.

Let
\[
A=\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
B=\sum_j b_j.
\]
Because
\[
\sum_k x_k
=\#\{x_j>1/2\}+A-B
\]
is an integer,
\[
A-B\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Also, for `0<=t<=1/2`,
\[
t(1-t)\ge t/2.
\]
Hence
\[
A+B
\le2W_x
<1.
\]
Therefore
\[
|A-B|\le A+B<1.
\]
Together with (6.3), this forces
\[
A=B=:A_0.
\tag{6.4}
\]
In particular `A_0<1/2`.

Define
\[
f(t)=t(1-t)(1-2t)=t-3t^2+2t^3.
\]
Then
\[
K=\sum_i f(a_i)-\sum_j f(b_j).
\tag{6.5}
\]
Set
\[
Q_a=\sum_i a_i^2,
\quad R_a=\sum_i a_i^3,
\qquad
Q_b=\sum_j b_j^2,
\quad R_b=\sum_j b_j^3.
\]
Using the equal sums (6.4),
\[
K=3(Q_b-Q_a)+2(R_a-R_b),
\tag{6.6}
\]
and
\[
W_x=2A_0-Q_a-Q_b.
\tag{6.7}
\]

We prove `K<=W_x^2`; the opposite inequality follows by interchanging the `a` and `b` families.

Since every `a_i<=A_0`,
\[
R_a\le A_0Q_a.
\tag{6.8}
\]
By Cauchy,
\[
Q_b^2
=\left(\sum_j b_j^2\right)^2
\le\left(\sum_jb_j\right)
\left(\sum_jb_j^3\right)
=A_0R_b,
\]
so
\[
R_b\ge Q_b^2/A_0.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Thus
\[
K
\le
3Q_b-\frac{2Q_b^2}{A_0}-(3-2A_0)Q_a.
\tag{6.10}
\]
Let
\[
q_b=Q_b,
\qquad x=Q_a.
\]
The difference between the desired right side and the upper bound (6.10) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(x)
={}&(2A_0-q_b-x)^2\\
&-\left[
3q_b-\frac{2q_b^2}{A_0}-(3-2A_0)x
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.11}
\]
As a polynomial in `x`,
\[
D'(x)
=2x+3+2q_b-6A_0.
\]
Because `A_0<1/2`,
\[
D'(x)>0
\]
for every `x>=0`. Hence
\[
D(x)\ge D(0).
\]
Since `Q_b<=A_0^2`, write
\[
Q_b=A_0^2t,
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
Then
\[
D(0)
=A_0^2
\left[
4-(3+4A_0)t+(A_0^2+2A_0)t^2
\right].
\tag{6.12}
\]
The bracket is decreasing on `[0,1]`, because its derivative is at most
\[
-(3+4A_0)+2(A_0^2+2A_0)
=-3+2A_0^2<0.
\]
Therefore its minimum occurs at `t=1`, where it equals
\[
(1-A_0)^2>0.
\]
Thus `D(x)>=0`, proving
\[
K\le W_x^2.
\]
Interchanging `a` and `b` gives `-K<=W_x^2`, completing (6.2). QED.

## 11. Why this does not solve the original conjecture

For a positive two-eigenvalue covariance
\[
C=aI+cP,
\]
the exact spherical representation gives a positive series
\[
J_\kappa(P)
=
\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(\alpha)_k}{k!}\kappa^kM_k(P).
\]
The cumulative program has now proved coordinate maximality of:

- `M_2` for every rank;
- `M_3` for every rank;
- `M_4` for rank one and every rank at least four.

But `M_k` for `k>=5` remain uncontrolled, and rank-two/rank-three quartic geometry is not fully closed. Therefore no finite-strength rank-`r>1` two-eigenvalue theorem, and certainly no arbitrary-spectrum theorem, is claimed here.

## 12. Failed / rejected shortcuts

1. **Using only `nu>=mu^2`.** This is exactly the relaxation that left rank four unresolved in the previous checkpoint. Near coordinate projections the weighted distribution of the `d_i` is bimodal, and `nu` is much larger than `mu^2`; the integer-sum lemmas recover that lost information.

2. **Bounding `q` only by `1/4`.** The trivial pointwise inequality `w_i<=1/4` gives `q<=1/4`, which is too weak when `W` is small. Lemma 5.1 sharpens this to `q<=W/2` using the integer sum.

3. **Assuming `|K|<=W^2` without proof.** This bound is not a generic consequence of `0<=x_i<=1`; the integer-sum condition is essential. Section 6 gives a complete proof in the only range used here, `W<1/2`.

4. **Treating quartic coefficient optimality as finite-spike optimality.** Still invalid because all higher positive-series coefficients remain.
