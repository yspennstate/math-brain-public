# PROB20-19 — Full nonlinear Gaussian PCA conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.** The universal Mallat–Zeitouni/Bandeira conjecture remains open. This run closes the remaining quartic projection-moment obstruction for **every rank-three projection in every ambient dimension**.

The new exact theorem is:

> Let `P` be any rank-three orthogonal projection in `R^p`, `p>=4`, let
> \[
> f_d(\theta)=\operatorname{Top}_d(\theta_1^2,\ldots,\theta_p^2),
> \qquad 1\le d<p,
> \]
> with `theta` uniform on `S^{p-1}`, and define
> \[
> M_4(P)=\mathbb E\left[f_d(\theta)(\theta^TP\theta)^4\right].
> \]
> Then
> \[
> \boxed{M_4(P)\le M_4(P_{\rm coord})}
> \]
> with equality iff `P` is a coordinate projection.

Cumulatively, the quartic coefficient `M_4` is now exactly KL-aligned for rank one, rank three, and every rank `r>=4`. Thus **rank two is the only unresolved quartic projection rank**. The quadratic and cubic coefficients `M_2,M_3` were already solved for every rank.

The proof is elementary once the exact quartic coefficient decomposition from the previous checkpoints is used. Its new ingredient is an exact edge-sum representation of the last rank-three geometric coefficient `lambda_1`, followed by two complementary global lower bounds whose validity intervals overlap.

This is still a coefficient theorem for the positive two-eigenvalue expansion. It does not sum the full finite-spike series and does not prove or refute the arbitrary-spectrum conjecture.

## 3. Exact rank-three coefficient and projection edge variables

Let `P` be a rank-three orthogonal projection. Put
\[
d_i=P_{ii},
\qquad
q_{ij}=P_{ij}^2\quad(i<j),
\]
and
\[
w_i=d_i(1-d_i).
\]
Because `P^2=P`, taking the `ii` entry gives
\[
\boxed{
w_i=\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Also
\[
W:=3-\sum_i d_i^2
=\sum_iw_i
=2\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Write
\[
Q:=\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}=\frac W2,
\qquad
E:=\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The exact geometric coefficient from the quartic decomposition is, at rank `r=3`,
\[
\lambda_1
=378\Delta_2-360\Delta_3+114\Delta_4
-9(9-S_2^2)+48V,
\tag{3.4}
\]
where
\[
S_k=\sum_i d_i^k,
\quad
\Delta_k=3-S_k,
\quad
V=E+3\sum_{i<j}d_id_jq_{ij}.
\]

Projection identity (3.1) implies
\[
S_k-S_{k+1}
=\sum_i d_i^k(1-d_i)
=\sum_{i<j}(d_i^k+d_j^k)q_{ij}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Hence
\[
\Delta_2=2\sum q_{ij},
\]
\[
\Delta_3=\sum_{i<j}
(2+d_i+d_j)q_{ij},
\]
\[
\Delta_4=\sum_{i<j}
(2+d_i+d_j+d_i^2+d_j^2)q_{ij}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Finally
\[
9-S_2^2=(3-S_2)(3+S_2)=W(6-W).
\tag{3.7}
\]
Substitute (3.6)--(3.7) into (3.4), collect the `d_id_j` term from `48V`, and use `W=2Q`. This gives the exact new edge representation
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1
=\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}c(d_i,d_j)
+36Q^2+48E,
}
\tag{3.8}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
c(a,b)
=156-246(a+b)+114(a^2+b^2)+144ab.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]
No inequality has yet been used in (3.8).

## 4. First global lower bound: high-mixing regime

The Hessian of `c` is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}228&144\\144&228\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is positive definite. The unique unconstrained minimizer is
\[
a=b=\frac{41}{62},
\]
and it lies in `[0,1]^2`. Direct substitution gives
\[
\boxed{
\min_{0\le a,b\le1}c(a,b)
=-\frac{207}{31}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Therefore from (3.8), discarding the positive `48E` term,
\[
\lambda_1
\ge
-\frac{207}{31}Q+36Q^2.
\]
Using `Q=W/2`,
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1
\ge
W\left(9W-\frac{207}{62}\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
W\ge\frac{23}{62}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_1\ge0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
For `W>23/62` this lower bound is strict; at the endpoint the omitted `48E` is strictly positive for every noncoordinate projection.

## 5. Second global lower bound: low-mixing regime

The preceding bound is deliberately crude because it ignores how a negative value of `c(a,b)` forces the two diagonal entries away from `0,1`. A sharper inequality couples `c` to the Bernoulli variances
\[
w(a)=a(1-a),\qquad w(b)=b(1-b).
\]
Put
\[
s=a+b,
\qquad
t=ab,
\qquad
w_{ab}=w(a)+w(b)=s-s^2+2t.
\]
Equation (3.9) becomes
\[
c(a,b)
=156-246s+114s^2-84t.
\]
Eliminating `t` using `w_ab` gives
\[
c(a,b)
=156-204s+72s^2-42w_{ab}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The scalar quadratic satisfies
\[
156-204s+72s^2
\ge
\frac{23}{2},
\tag{5.2}
\]
with equality at `s=17/12`. Hence for every `a,b in [0,1]`,
\[
\boxed{
c(a,b)
\ge
\frac{23}{2}-42\big(a(1-a)+b(1-b)\big).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
This inequality is sharp as a two-variable bound. Equivalently, the smallest possible pair variance on the zero level `c=0` is `23/84`.

Multiply (5.3) by `q_ij` and sum. By (3.1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}(w_i+w_j)
&=\sum_iw_i\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}\\
&=\sum_iw_i^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]
Let
\[
R=\sum_iw_i^2.
\]
Then (3.8) and (5.3)--(5.4) imply
\[
\lambda_1
\ge
\frac{23}{2}Q-42R+36Q^2+48E.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Now use the previously archived integer-sum lemma (1.1). Since the diagonal of a rank-three projection obeys
\[
0\le d_i\le1,
\qquad
\sum_i d_i=3\in\mathbb Z,
\]
we have
\[
R\le\frac{W^2}{2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Substituting `Q=W/2` in (5.5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_1
&\ge
\frac{23}{4}W-42\frac{W^2}{2}+9W^2+48E\\
&=
\boxed{
W\left(\frac{23}{4}-12W\right)+48E.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
0<W\le\frac{23}{48}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_1>0.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
Strictness at `W=23/48` follows from `E>0` whenever `W>0`.

## 6. The two regimes overlap: full rank-three closure

The two exact thresholds satisfy
\[
\frac{23}{62}<\frac{23}{48}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Thus every `W>0` belongs to at least one of the two proved regimes:

- if `W<=23/48`, use (5.7);
- if `W>=23/62`, use (4.2) together with the positive omitted `48E` at the boundary.

A rank-three projection has `W=0` iff every diagonal entry is `0` or `1`. Since `P` is a projection, this is equivalent to `P` being diagonal, hence coordinate up to permutation.

Therefore:

### Theorem 6.1 — complete quartic rank-three theorem

For every ambient dimension `p>=4`, every `1<=d<p`, and every rank-three orthogonal projection `P`,
\[
\boxed{
M_4(P)\le M_4(P_{\rm coord}).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Equality holds iff `P` is a coordinate projection.

Indeed `I_1,I_2,I_3,I_4>0`; the recovered results already give `lambda_2,lambda_3,lambda_4>=0` at rank three; Sections 3--6 prove `lambda_1>0` for every noncoordinate rank-three projection.

This strictly extends the preceding codimension-one `p=4,r=3` theorem to **all ambient dimensions without using complement rank**.

## 8. Failed or rejected routes

1. **Using the global minimum of `c` alone.** This proves only `W>=23/62` and leaves a neighborhood of the coordinate set untreated. The second variance-coupled bound is essential.

2. **Using only the variance-coupled lower bound.** After `R<=W^2/2`, it proves only `W<=23/48`. It is not globally nonnegative for large `W`. The high-mixing bound is essential.

3. **Complement positivity.** The previous checkpoint derived exact complement-transfer identities, but the transfer term can change sign. The present direct edge proof avoids that obstruction entirely and works uniformly in ambient dimension.

4. **Numerical optimization.** No numerical search is used as a proof. Randomized checks may be useful for algebra verification, but the theorem above is analytic.

5. **Inferring finite-spike optimality from `M_2,M_3,M_4`.** Invalid. Higher coefficients remain uncontrolled.

## 10. Next precise attack

The quartic program is now reduced to **rank two only**. For a rank-two projection choose an orthonormal-column matrix `U in R^{p x 2}` with row vectors `(a_i,b_i)` and define
\[
z_i=a_i+ib_i,
\qquad
w_i=z_i^2,
\qquad
|w_i|=d_i=P_{ii}.
\]
The Parseval conditions imply
\[
\sum_iw_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i d_i=2.
\]
Writing
\[
A=\sum_i d_iw_i,
\qquad
B=\sum_iw_i^2,
\]
a direct frame calculation gives the useful identities
\[
U_{\rm geom}:=\sum_{i<j}d_id_jP_{ij}^2
=\frac{S_2^2+|A|^2-2S_4}{4},
\tag{10.1}
\]
\[
T_4:=\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^4
=\frac{3S_2^2+4|A|^2+|B|^2-8S_4}{16},
\tag{10.2}
\]
so
\[
V=T_4+3U_{\rm geom}
=\frac{15S_2^2+16|A|^2+|B|^2-32S_4}{16}.
\tag{10.3}
\]
Substitution into the unresolved quartic coefficients yields
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_2
=16-18S_2^2+30S_4-16|A|^2-|B|^2,

\tag{10.4}

\[
\boxed{
