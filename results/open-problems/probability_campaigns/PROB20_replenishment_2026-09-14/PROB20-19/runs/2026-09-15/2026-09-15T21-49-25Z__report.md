# PROB20-19 — Full nonlinear Gaussian PCA conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.** The universal Mallat–Zeitouni/Bandeira conjecture remains open. This run does not claim a full proof or refutation. It produces two structural theorems that substantially simplify the positive two-eigenvalue moment program:

1. **Universal even-coefficient positivity:** every even monomial coefficient of every power of **every PSD quadratic form, of arbitrary rank**, is nonnegative. The previous checkpoint had proved this only for rank at most two.
2. **Normalized analytic Schur-convexity:** after dividing the `Top_d` spherical moments by their raw Dirichlet moments, the resulting score is monotone under majorization of the exponent partition.

Together these give an exact probabilistic reduction of the order-`k` projection-moment problem to a stochastic-order question on integer partitions of `k`. The coordinate projection induces an explicit Dirichlet-multinomial partition law; an arbitrary projection induces a different but still honest probability law because of theorem 1. If the coordinate law majorizes the latter law in first-order stochastic order on the dominance poset, then the coordinate projection maximizes `M_k`.

This run also proves three geometric stochastic-order facets for **every order `k` and every projection rank**: the top partition `(k)`, the cumulative top two partitions `(k),(k-1,1)`, and the bottom all-distinct partition `(1^k)`. At quintic order, where the dominance poset is a chain, only the three middle cumulative facets remain for arbitrary rank. A crude but rigorous estimate additionally proves the third cumulative facet for rank `r>=18`.

The immediately preceding checkpoint already proves the full quintic theorem for rank two. The new results here are not a re-proof of that theorem; they generalize its key coefficient-positivity input to all ranks and replace the ad hoc analytic contrast bookkeeping by a majorization-normalized framework.

## 3. Universal even-coefficient positivity for arbitrary PSD rank

The rank-two Fourier argument in the preceding checkpoint is a special case of a simpler arbitrary-rank fact.

### Theorem 3.1 — all even coefficients of a PSD quadratic power are nonnegative

Let `R` be any real positive-semidefinite matrix, with no rank restriction. Let

\[
m_1,\ldots,m_s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},
\qquad
m_1+\cdots+m_s=k.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
[x_1^{2m_1}\cdots x_s^{2m_s}](x^TRx)^k\ge0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

If every active diagonal entry `R_{ii}` with `m_i>0` is positive, the coefficient is strictly positive.

### Proof

Factor `R` as a Gram matrix. Thus there are vectors

\[
r_i\in\mathbb R^q
\]

such that

\[
R_{ij}=r_i\cdot r_j.
\]

Put

\[
y(x)=\sum_i r_i x_i.
\]

Then

\[
x^TRx=\|y(x)\|^2.
\]

Let `Z~N(0,I_q)`. For every deterministic `y`,

\[
\mathbb E(Z\cdot y)^{2k}=(2k-1)!!\,\|y\|^{2k}.
\]

Therefore

\[
(x^TRx)^k
=\frac1{(2k-1)!!}
\mathbb E_Z\left(\sum_i (Z\cdot r_i)x_i\right)^{2k}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Extracting the target coefficient gives

\[
\boxed{
[x_1^{2m_1}\cdots x_s^{2m_s}](x^TRx)^k
=
\frac{(2k)!}{(2k-1)!!\prod_i(2m_i)!}
\mathbb E_Z\prod_i(Z\cdot r_i)^{2m_i}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The integrand is pointwise nonnegative, proving (3.1).

If every active `R_{ii}=\|r_i\|^2` is positive, no active linear form `Z\mapsto Z\cdot r_i` is identically zero. Their zero sets are finitely many proper hyperplanes, hence have Gaussian measure zero. The product in (3.3) is strictly positive off their union, so the coefficient is strictly positive.

QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — all sign-even partition weights are nonnegative at every rank

Let `P` be any orthogonal projection of any rank and expand

\[
(\theta^TP\theta)^k.
\]

After grouping terms by even exponent partition `lambda` of `k`, let `W_lambda(P)` denote the total geometric coefficient multiplying the symmetric analytic moment of type `lambda`. Then

\[
\boxed{W_\lambda(P)\ge0\quad\text{for every partition }\lambda\vdash k.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This removes the rank-two restriction from the strongest algebraic positivity input in the previous checkpoint.

## 4. Analytic moments normalized by the sphere law

Let

\[
X_i=\theta_i^2,
\qquad
\theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^{p-1}),
\qquad
f_d(X)=\operatorname{Top}_d(X_1,\ldots,X_p).
\]

For a partition

\[
\lambda=(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_\ell>0),
\qquad |\lambda|=k,
\]
define

\[
a_\lambda
=
\mathbb E\left[f_d(X)\prod_{j=1}^\ell X_j^{\lambda_j}\right],
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the raw spherical moment

\[
b_\lambda
=
\mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^\ell X_j^{\lambda_j}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because `(X_1,...,X_p)` is Dirichlet with parameters `(1/2,...,1/2)`,

\[
\boxed{
b_\lambda
=
\frac{c_\lambda}{D_{p,k}},
\qquad
c_\lambda=\prod_{j=1}^\ell(2\lambda_j-1)!!,
\qquad
D_{p,k}=p(p+2)\cdots(p+2k-2).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Define the normalized analytic score

\[
R_\lambda:=\frac{a_\lambda}{b_\lambda}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For constant `f`, all `R_lambda` are equal. The nontrivial question is how `Top_d` changes them.

## 5. New normalized Schur-convexity theorem

### Theorem 5.1 — partition majorization increases the normalized `Top_d` moment

If partitions `lambda,mu` of the same integer `k` satisfy

\[
\lambda\succeq\mu
\]

in dominance/majorization order, then

\[
\boxed{R_\lambda\ge R_\mu.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, the normalized analytic moment is Schur-convex as a function of the integer exponent partition.

### Proof by one-unit transfers

Integer majorization is generated by elementary reverse Robin-Hood transfers. It is therefore enough to compare two exponent vectors that differ only by

\[
(a,b)\longmapsto(a+1,b-1),
\qquad a\ge b\ge1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

All outside exponents are kept fixed.

Condition on the outside squared coordinates and on

\[
S=X_1+X_2.
\]

Given this conditioning,

\[
T:=\frac{X_1}{S}\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1/2,1/2)
\]

and is independent of the outside variables and of `S`. Write

\[
q=|2T-1|\in[0,1].
\]

For fixed `S` and fixed outside squares, `f_d` is a nondecreasing function `h(q)`. Indeed, increasing `q` makes the pair `(X_1,X_2)` more unequal at fixed sum, hence majorizes the old pair; the sum of the largest `d` coordinates is Schur-convex on the nonnegative orthant.

By exchange symmetry, the angular weight for exponents `(a,b)` may be symmetrized:

\[
w_{a,b}(T)
=\frac12\left[T^a(1-T)^b+T^b(1-T)^a\right].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Let

\[
\delta=a-b\ge0,
\qquad
u=\operatorname{arctanh}(q),
\qquad
\frac{T}{1-T}=e^{2\nu}.
\]

A direct calculation gives the exact likelihood ratio

\[
\boxed{
\frac{w_{a+1,b-1}(T)}{w_{a,b}(T)}
=
\frac{\cosh((\delta+2)\nu)}{\cosh(\delta\nu)}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This ratio is increasing in `q`. Indeed

\[
\frac{d}{d\nu}\log\frac{\cosh((\delta+2)\nu)}{\cosh(\delta\nu)}
=(\delta+2)\tanh((\delta+2)\nu)-\delta\tanh(\delta\nu)>0
\tag{5.5}
\]

for `nu>0`, since `c\mapsto c\tanh(c\nu)` is strictly increasing for `c>=0`.

Thus the transferred angular measure has a monotone likelihood ratio with respect to the original one. Since `h(q)` is nondecreasing,

\[
\frac{\mathbb E_T[h(q)w_{a+1,b-1}(T)]}
{\mathbb E_T[w_{a+1,b-1}(T)]}
\ge
\frac{\mathbb E_T[h(q)w_{a,b}(T)]}
{\mathbb E_T[w_{a,b}(T)]}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The pair total exponent `a+b` is unchanged, so the factors involving `S` and the outside coordinates are identical on the two sides. The denominators in (5.6) are exactly the corresponding angular factors in the raw Dirichlet moments. Integrating the conditional inequality therefore gives

\[
R_{(a+1,b-1,\ldots)}
\ge
R_{(a,b,\ldots)}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Iterating unit transfers proves (5.1).

QED.

## 6. Exact partition-law reduction for every projection rank

Expand

\[
M_k(P)
:=
\mathbb E\left[f_d(X)(\theta^TP\theta)^k\right]
=
\sum_{\lambda\vdash k}W_\lambda(P)a_\lambda.
\tag{6.1}
\]

By Theorem 3.1, every `W_lambda(P)>=0`.

For a rank-`r` projection, the unweighted spherical moment is orientation-independent:

\[
\mathbb E(\theta^TP\theta)^k
=
\frac{T_{r,k}}{D_{p,k}},
\qquad
T_{r,k}=r(r+2)\cdots(r+2k-2).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Using (4.3), this is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\lambda\vdash k}c_\lambda W_\lambda(P)=T_{r,k}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\pi_P(\lambda)
:=
\frac{c_\lambda W_\lambda(P)}{T_{r,k}}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

is an honest probability distribution on the partitions of `k`, for every projection rank.

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
M_k(P)
=
\frac{T_{r,k}}{D_{p,k}}
\mathbb E_{\lambda\sim\pi_P}R_\lambda.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This turns the analytic/geometric separation into an exact finite probability problem.

### Coordinate law

For a coordinate rank-`r` projection and a partition `lambda` of length `ell`,

\[
\boxed{
W_\lambda^{\rm coord}
=
\frac{k!}{\prod_j\lambda_j!}
\frac{r^{\underline\ell}}
{\prod_m n_m!},
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

where `n_m` is the multiplicity of the part size `m` in `lambda`.

Thus `pi_coord` is explicit.

### Probabilistic interpretation

The Gaussian proof of Theorem 3.1 yields more. Let the projection be represented by a Parseval frame

\[
P_{ij}=r_i\cdot r_j,
\qquad
\sum_i r_ir_i^T=I_r.
\]

Take `U` uniform on `S^{r-1}` and define

\[
p_i(U)=(r_i\cdot U)^2.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i p_i(U)=1.
\]

Conditional on `U`, draw `k` iid categorical samples with probabilities `p(U)` and sort the nonzero occupancy counts into a partition of `k`. The resulting partition has exactly the law `pi_P`.

Indeed, multiplying the coefficient formula (3.3) by `c_lambda` cancels the even factorials and leaves the multinomial occupancy coefficient

\[
\frac{k!}{\prod_i m_i!}\prod_i p_i(U)^{m_i}.
\]

For a coordinate projection, `p(U)=(U_1^2,\ldots,U_r^2)` and is Dirichlet `(1/2,...,1/2)`.

This is the main conceptual reduction of the run.

## 7. A clean sufficient geometric conjecture

Because `R_lambda` is increasing in partition majorization, (6.5) immediately gives:

> **Sufficient stochastic-dominance principle.** If
> \[
> \pi_{P_{\rm coord}}\succeq_{\rm st}\pi_P
> \]
> for the dominance order on partitions of `k`, then
> \[
> M_k(P)\le M_k(P_{\rm coord}).
> \]

If this stochastic dominance held for every `k` and every rank `r`, then every positive two-eigenvalue spike `aI+cP` would be exactly KL-optimal at **all spike strengths**, because the binomial sphere expansion has positive coefficients at every order.

This stochastic statement is a sufficient route, not claimed to be equivalent to the full Mallat–Zeitouni conjecture.

## 8. Three geometric dominance facets proved for every order

The stochastic conjecture is not left completely abstract. Three extremal facets can be proved exactly at every order.

Let

\[
d_i=P_{ii},
\qquad 0\le d_i\le1,
\qquad \sum_i d_i=r,
\qquad S_j=\sum_i d_i^j.
\]

### 8.1 Top partition `(k)`

The coefficient of `X_i^k` is `d_i^k`, so

\[
W_{(k)}(P)=S_k\le r=W_{(k)}(P_{\rm coord}).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus the probability of the maximal partition `(k)` is largest for the coordinate projection.

### 8.2 Cumulative top two partitions `(k)` and `(k-1,1)`

For an ordered pair `i!=j`, the coefficient of

\[
x_i^{2(k-1)}x_j^2
\]

in `(x^TPx)^k` is

\[
k d_i^{k-1}d_j
+2k(k-1)d_i^{k-2}P_{ij}^2.
\]

Using

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}P_{ij}^2=d_i-d_i^2,
\]

we get the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
W_{(k-1,1)}(P)
=
k\left[(r+2k-2)S_{k-1}-(2k-1)S_k\right].
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The raw Dirichlet factors are

\[
c_{(k)}=(2k-1)!!,
\qquad
c_{(k-1,1)}=(2k-3)!!.
\]

After factoring `(2k-3)!!`, the coordinate-minus-general cumulative gap is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\rm top2}
={}&r(kr+k-1)
-k(r+2k-2)S_{k-1}
+(k-1)(2k-1)S_k\\
={}&\sum_i d_i H_{k,r}(d_i),
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.3}
\]

where

\[
H_{k,r}(x)
=kr+k-1
-k(r+2k-2)x^{k-2}
+(k-1)(2k-1)x^{k-1}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Put

\[
A=k(r+2k-2),
\qquad
B=(k-1)(2k-1).
\]

Then

\[
H_{k,r}(x)
=(A-B)-x^{k-2}(A-Bx).
\]

For `k>=4,r>=2`, the function

\[
x^{k-2}(A-Bx)
\]

is increasing on `[0,1]`, because its derivative has minimum bracket at `x=1` equal to

\[
(k-2)A-(k-1)B
=k^2r-k^2-2kr+1\ge0.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus `H_{k,r}(x)>=0`. The case `k=3,r>=3` is the same because the last quantity becomes `3r-8>=1`. For `k=3,r=2`, the coordinate law has no three-distinct occupancy, so the top-two cumulative probability is the total probability and the inequality follows directly from nonnegativity of the omitted all-distinct weight. `k=2` is tautological, and rank one is trivial.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\pi_{\rm coord}\bigl(\{(k),(k-1,1)\}\bigr)
\ge
\pi_P\bigl(\{(k),(k-1,1)\}\bigr)
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

for every `k>=2` and every projection rank.

### 8.3 Bottom all-distinct partition `(1^k)`

The purely diagonal contribution to the all-distinct coefficient is

\[
k!\,e_k(d_1,\ldots,d_p).
\]

All other even contributions are nonnegative by Theorem 3.1. Hence

\[
W_{(1^k)}(P)\ge k!e_k(d).
\tag{8.7}
\]

For `0<=d_i<=1` with integer sum `r`,

\[
e_k(d)\ge\binom rk.
\tag{8.8}
\]

A direct proof uses pair compression. Holding all other coordinates fixed,

\[
e_k
=e_k(\text{rest})+(a+b)e_{k-1}(\text{rest})+ab\,e_{k-2}(\text{rest}).
\]

At fixed `a+b`, pushing `(a,b)` toward the nearest boundary pair `(0,a+b)` or `(a+b-1,1)` weakly decreases `ab`, hence weakly decreases `e_k`. Iterating reaches the vector with `r` ones and the rest zeros. This proves (8.8).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
W_{(1^k)}(P)
\ge
r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)
=W_{(1^k)}(P_{\rm coord}).
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

Thus the coordinate law has the smallest probability of the least-concentrated partition.

## 9. Quintic consequence and one additional high-rank facet

At `k=5`, the dominance order is the total chain (5.8). Hence stochastic dominance is equivalent to six cumulative inequalities.

Sections 8.1–8.3 prove, for every rank:

- cumulative level 1: `(5)`;
- cumulative level 2: `(5),(4,1)`;
- cumulative level 6, equivalently the bottom `(1^5)` inequality.

Only the three middle cumulative levels remain for the general-rank quintic theorem in this stochastic framework.

One of them can already be proved for sufficiently large rank.

### Proposition 9.1 — the top-three quintic cumulative facet for `r>=18`

Let `A,B,C` denote the partition types `(5),(4,1),(3,2)` geometrically. The exact first two weights are

\[
W_A=S_5,
\qquad
W_B=5(r+8)S_4-45S_5.
\tag{9.1}
\]

For the `(3,2)` weight, writing `q_{ij}=P_{ij}^2`, direct two-coordinate extraction gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_C={}&10(S_2S_3-S_5)
+120\sum_{i\ne j}d_i^2d_jq_{ij}
+80\sum_{i\ne j}d_iq_{ij}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Using `d_j<=1`, the row identity `sum_{j!=i}q_{ij}=d_i(1-d_i)`, and `sum q_{ij}^2 <= (sum q_{ij})^2`,

\[
\boxed{
W_C
\le
10S_2S_3+200S_3-280S_4+70S_5.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

The weighted top-three mass uses raw factors `(945,105,45)`. Substituting (9.1)–(9.3), and defining

\[
\Delta_j=r-S_j,
\]

shows that the coordinate-minus-general gap is at least `15 G`, where

\[
G
=30r(\Delta_2+\Delta_3)-30\Delta_2\Delta_3
+600\Delta_3+(35r-560)\Delta_4-42\Delta_5.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Now

\[
30r\Delta_2-30\Delta_2\Delta_3
=30\Delta_2(r-\Delta_3)\ge0,
\]

and pointwise on `[0,1]`,

\[
1-x^4\le\frac43(1-x^3),
\]

so

\[
\Delta_5\le\frac43\Delta_4.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
(35r-560)\Delta_4-42\Delta_5
\ge
(35r-616)\Delta_4\ge0
\]

for `r>=18`. Every remaining term in (9.4) is nonnegative. Therefore the top-three cumulative stochastic facet holds for every rank `r>=18`.

This does **not** yet prove the full quintic theorem for `r>=18`, because the two middle-lower cumulative facets are still open.

## 10. Failed shortcut and obstruction retained

A tempting route is to prove the lower cumulative facets using only the diagonal contribution and elementary symmetric polynomials of `d=diag(P)`. This is not sufficient.

For example, the diagonal lower bound for the quintic bottom-two weighted mass involves

\[
H(d)=3r\,e_4(d)-13e_5(d).
\]

For the coordinate diagonal this equals its coordinate value, but `H` is not minimized there in general. A reproducible random rank-10 projection in ambient dimension 15 with seed `10000` gives

\[
H(d)\approx2964.28
\]

versus the coordinate value `3024`.

Thus off-diagonal positive coefficient mass is genuinely needed; a proof based solely on the projection diagonal cannot close the remaining stochastic facets.

This is useful negative information: the next attack should exploit the Parseval-frame occupancy representation, not only Schur-Horn constraints on `diag(P)`.
