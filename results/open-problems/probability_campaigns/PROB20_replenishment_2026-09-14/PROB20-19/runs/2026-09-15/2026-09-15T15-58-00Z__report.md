## 3. Recalled exact quartic gap

Let
\[
f_d(\theta)=\operatorname{Top}_d(\theta_1^2,\ldots,\theta_p^2),
\qquad \theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^{p-1}),
\]
and let `P` be a rank-`r` orthogonal projection. Define
\[
M_4(P)=\mathbb E\left[f_d(\theta)(\theta^TP\theta)^4\right].
\]
The previous verified checkpoint proves the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{
M_4(P_{\rm coord})-M_4(P)
=\lambda_1I_1+\lambda_2I_2+\lambda_3I_3+\lambda_4I_4,
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
where all four analytic coefficients are strictly positive:
\[
I_1=D-3E>0,
\qquad
I_2=C-3D>0,
\]
\[
I_3=B-5D>0,
\qquad
I_4=A-4B-5C>0.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Here `A,B,C,D,E` are the five degree-four permutation moment types of the `Top_d` sphere functional.

Write
\[
d_i=P_{ii},\qquad S_k=\sum_i d_i^k,
\qquad \Delta_k=r-S_k,
\]
\[
U=\sum_{i<j}d_id_jP_{ij}^2,
\qquad
T_4=\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^4,
\qquad
V=T_4+3U.
\]
Then
\[
\lambda_4=\Delta_4,
\tag{3.3}
\]
\[
\lambda_3=4\big[(r+6)\Delta_3-6\Delta_4\big],
\tag{3.4}
\]
\[
\lambda_2=3(r^2-S_2^2)+2\Delta_4-16V,
\tag{3.5}
\]
and
\[
\lambda_1=
6(r+4)(r+6)\Delta_2
-40(r+6)\Delta_3
+114\Delta_4
-9(r^2-S_2^2)
+48V.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The previous run proved `lambda_3>=0` for `r>=3`, `lambda_2>=0` for `r>=6`, and `lambda_1>=0` for `r>=9`. The present run improves all three low-rank thresholds.

## 4. New rank-two theorem for `lambda_3`

For `r=2`, equation (3.4) becomes
\[
\frac{\lambda_3}{8}
=4\Delta_3-3\Delta_4
=2-4S_3+3S_4.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Thus it is enough to prove the following scalar hypersimplex inequality.

### Lemma 4.1

If `0<=x_i<=1` and
\[
\sum_i x_i=2,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\sum_i\left(x_i-4x_i^3+3x_i^4\right)\ge0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Put
\[
h(x)=x-4x^3+3x^4.
\]
If `a,b>=0` and `a+b<=1`, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(a)+h(b)-h(a+b)
&=-6ab\left(2a^2+3ab-2a+2b^2-2b\right)\\
&=-6ab\left(2(a+b)^2-ab-2(a+b)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
For `a+b<=1`,
\[
2(a+b)^2-2(a+b)\le0,
\]
and subtracting `ab` makes the bracket still smaller. Hence
\[
h(a)+h(b)\ge h(a+b).
\tag{4.3}
\]
Therefore one may repeatedly merge any positive pair whose sum is at most one, replacing `(a,b)` by `(a+b,0)`, without increasing the total `sum h(x_i)`.

At a terminal configuration, every two positive coordinates have sum strictly bigger than one. Since the total sum is two, there can be at most three positive coordinates.

- With two positive coordinates, both must equal one, and the total `h`-sum is zero.
- With three positive coordinates `a,b,c in (0,1)` satisfying `a+b+c=2`, put
  \[
  x=1-a,\quad y=1-b,\quad z=1-c.
  \]
  Then `x,y,z>=0` and `x+y+z=1`. Direct expansion gives
  \[
  h(a)+h(b)+h(c)
  =6\left[(xy+yz+zx)^2-2xyz\right].
  \tag{4.4}
  \]
  Since
  \[
  (xy+yz+zx)^2\ge3xyz(x+y+z)=3xyz\ge2xyz,
  \]
  the expression is nonnegative.

Thus every terminal configuration has nonnegative total, and each merge could only decrease that total. Hence the original configuration also satisfies (4.2). QED.

Applying Lemma 4.1 to `d_i=P_{ii}` proves
\[
\boxed{\lambda_3\ge0\quad\text{for rank }r=2.}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Together with the previous `r>=3` theorem, `lambda_3>=0` now holds for every `r>=2`.

## 5. Sharper projection bounds for `V=T_4+3U`

Put
\[
q_{ij}=P_{ij}^2.
\]
For a projection,
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}=d_i-d_i^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Also the `2 x 2` principal minors of `P` and `I-P` are nonnegative, so
\[
q_{ij}\le d_id_j,
\qquad
q_{ij}\le(1-d_i)(1-d_j).
\tag{5.2}
\]

First,
\[
\begin{aligned}
U
&=\sum_{i<j}d_id_jq_{ij}\\
&\le\frac12\sum_{i<j}(d_i^2+d_j^2)q_{ij}\\
&=\frac12\sum_i d_i^2(d_i-d_i^2)\\
&=\frac12(S_3-S_4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Using the first bound in (5.2),
\[
T_4=\sum q_{ij}^2\le U,
\]
so
\[
V\le4U\le2(S_3-S_4).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Using instead the second bound in (5.2),
\[
T_4
\le\sum_{i<j}(1-d_i)(1-d_j)q_{ij}.
\]
Now
\[
\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}=\frac{r-S_2}{2},
\qquad
\sum_{i<j}(d_i+d_j)q_{ij}=S_2-S_3,
\]
so
\[
T_4
\le\frac{r-S_2}{2}-(S_2-S_3)+U.
\]
Together with (5.3),
\[
V\le
2(S_3-S_4)
+\left[\frac{r-S_2}{2}-(S_2-S_3)\right].
\tag{5.5}
\]
Combining (5.4) and (5.5),
\[
\boxed{
V\le2(S_3-S_4)
+\min\left\{0,\frac{r-S_2}{2}-(S_2-S_3)\right\}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
This is the key improvement over the preceding run's upper bound.

## 6. `lambda_2>=0` for every rank `r>=3`

Set
\[
w_i=d_i(1-d_i),
\qquad
W=\sum_iw_i=r-S_2.
\]
For `W>0`, define the weighted moments
\[
\mu=\frac{\sum_iw_id_i}{W}
=\frac{S_2-S_3}{W},
\qquad
\nu=\frac{\sum_iw_id_i^2}{W}
=\frac{S_3-S_4}{W}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Since `0<=d_i<=1`,
\[
0\le\nu\le\mu\le1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz gives another useful constraint:
\[
W^2
=\left(\sum_i d_i(1-d_i)\right)^2
\le\left(\sum_i d_i\right)
\left(\sum_i d_i(1-d_i)^2\right).
\]
But
\[
\sum_i d_i(1-d_i)^2=W-(S_2-S_3)=W(1-\mu),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{W\le r(1-\mu).}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The sign in the minimum of (5.6) is exactly the sign of
\[
\frac W2-W\mu=W(1/2-\mu).
\]

### Case 1: `mu<=1/2`

Use (5.4), i.e. `V<=2(S_3-S_4)=2Wnu`. Substitution in (3.5) gives exactly
\[
\lambda_2
\ge
W\left[-3W+2\mu-30\nu+6r+2\right].
\tag{6.4}
\]
Using `nu<=mu` and (6.3), the bracket is at least
\[
3r+2+(3r-28)\mu.
\tag{6.5}
\]
If `3r-28>=0`, this is obviously positive. If `3r-28<0`, its minimum on `0<=mu<=1/2` occurs at `mu=1/2` and equals
\[
\frac{9r-24}{2}>0
\]
for every integer `r>=3`.

### Case 2: `mu>1/2`

Use the sharper branch (5.5). Substitution gives
\[
\lambda_2
\ge
3W\left[-W+6\mu-10\nu+2r-2\right].
\tag{6.6}
\]
Again using `nu<=mu` and (6.3), the bracket is at least
\[
r-2+(r-4)\mu.
\tag{6.7}
\]
For `r=3`, this is `1-mu>0`; for `r=4`, it equals `2`; and for every `r>4` it is positive.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{\lambda_2\ge0\quad\text{for every rank }r\ge3.}
\tag{6.8}
\]
For every noncoordinate projection the inequalities above are strict except possibly at intermediate relaxations; nonnegativity is all that is required for the quartic theorem below.

Rank two remains open for `lambda_2`; no sign is claimed there.

## 7. A compression-trace lower bound for `U`

The `lambda_1` problem benefits from a lower rather than upper bound on `V`.

Let `D=diag(d_1,...,d_p)` and consider the positive semidefinite compression
\[
A=PDP
\]
on the rank-`r` range of `P`. Then
\[
\operatorname{tr}A
=\operatorname{tr}(DP)=S_2,
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
=\operatorname{tr}(PDPDP)
=S_4+2U.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Since `A` has at most `r` nonzero eigenvalues, Cauchy on those eigenvalues gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
\ge\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{r}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
U\ge\frac12\left(\frac{S_2^2}{r}-S_4\right).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
This lower bound is useful precisely because `lambda_1` contains `+48V`.

## 8. `lambda_1>=0` for every rank `r>=5`

From `V=T_4+3U>=3U` and (7.2),
\[
48V\ge144U
\ge72\left(\frac{S_2^2}{r}-S_4\right).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Apply this to (3.6). Using the same `W,mu,nu` from Section 6, a direct simplification gives
\[
\lambda_1
\ge
W\left[
6r^2+2r-54
+\left(9+\frac{72}{r}\right)W
-(40r+54)\mu
+186\nu
\right].
\tag{8.2}
\]
Weighted Cauchy gives
\[
\nu\ge\mu^2.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Dropping the nonnegative `W` term in the bracket,
\[
\frac{\lambda_1}{W}
\ge
6r^2+2r-54
+186\mu^2-(40r+54)\mu.
\tag{8.4}
\]
The quadratic in `mu` is bounded below on all of `R` by its unconstrained minimum:
\[
186\mu^2-(40r+54)\mu
\ge
-\frac{(40r+54)^2}{744}.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{\lambda_1}{W}
\ge
\frac{716r^2-708r-10773}{186}.
\tag{8.5}
\]
The numerator is already positive at `r=5`:
\[
716\cdot25-708\cdot5-10773=3587>0,
\]
and its derivative as a real quadratic is positive for all `r>=5`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\lambda_1>0\quad\text{for every noncoordinate projection of rank }r\ge5.}
\tag{8.6}
\]
At a coordinate projection `W=0` and all quartic gap coefficients vanish, as they should.

This lowers the previous threshold for `lambda_1` from `r>=9` to `r>=5`.

## 11. Failed / rejected shortcuts

1. **Using the previous crude upper bound on `U`.** The old estimate `U<=(S_2-S_3)/2` loses too much. The sharper bound (5.3), `U<=(S_3-S_4)/2`, is essential for the new `lambda_2` range.

2. **Using only `T_4<=U`.** That proves `V<=2(S_3-S_4)` but does not exploit the sign of `1/2-mu`. The second `I-P` principal-minor bound in (5.2) is what yields the two-case improvement.

3. **Dropping `+48V` in `lambda_1`.** This is the main reason the previous proof needed large rank. The compression inequality (7.2) converts part of `+48V` into a quantitative positive term and lowers the threshold to five.

4. **Assuming the rank-two `lambda_3` summand is pointwise nonnegative.** It is not: `x-4x^3+3x^4` is negative on part of `(0,1)`. The proof requires the global sum-two constraint and the merging reduction in Lemma 4.1.

5. **Treating `M_4` optimality as a finite-strength two-eigenvalue theorem.** Invalid. Higher positive-series coefficients `M_k`, `k>=5`, remain uncontrolled.
