## 4. Hadamard coordinates and the exact sign decomposition

Use the Hadamard coordinates from the previous report:

\[
D=\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4}{2},\quad
A=\frac{x_1+x_2-x_3-x_4}{2},
\]

\[
B=\frac{x_1-x_2+x_3-x_4}{2},\quad
C=\frac{x_1-x_2-x_3+x_4}{2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

At the balanced point,

\[
D=sX,\qquad s=\sqrt\lambda,
\qquad X,A,B,C\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The orientation-sensitive part of `Top_2` is

\[
m=\max\{|DA+BC|,|DB+AC|,|DC+AB|\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Condition on the four magnitudes

\[
r_D=|D|,\ r_A=|A|,\ r_B=|B|,\ r_C=|C|,
\]

and let

\[
T=r_{(1)}r_{(2)},\qquad U=r_{(3)}r_{(4)},
\tag{4.4}
\]

where `r_(1)>=...>=r_(4)` are the decreasingly sorted magnitudes. Let

\[
\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(DABC).
\]

The sign argument in the preceding report can be sharpened from its averaged form to the pointwise conditional identity

\[
\boxed{m=T+\sigma U.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed, for `sigma=+1` all three pairing expressions have their two products with the same relative sign and the maximal absolute value is `T+U`; for `sigma=-1` the relative sign is opposite and the maximal absolute value is `T-U`.

Consequently, for any monomial in the coordinate signs:

- the sign-trivial character sees only `T`;
- the full parity character `sigma` sees only `U`;
- every other nontrivial sign character averages to zero.

This is what makes the cubic coefficient exactly tractable.

## 5. The `3+1` tangent and the third density derivative

Choose the unit `3+1` tangent

\[
h=\frac{1}{2\sqrt3}(3,-1,-1,-1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Under the Hadamard transform this is

\[
W=\frac{A+B+C}{\sqrt3}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Along the geodesic

\[
u_\theta=u_*\cos\theta+h\sin\theta,
\tag{5.3}
\]

the small-eigenvalue direction rotates from `D` toward `W`.

Put

\[
\alpha=\frac{1-s^2}{s^2}>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Relative to the balanced Gaussian density, the likelihood ratio is

\[
L_\theta=
\exp\left[-\frac\alpha2
\left((D\cos\theta+W\sin\theta)^2-D^2\right)\right].
\tag{5.5}
\]

Writing `r(theta)=log L_theta`, at `theta=0` one has

\[
r_1=-\alpha DW,
\qquad
r_2=-\alpha(W^2-D^2),
\qquad
r_3=4\alpha DW.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
L_0'''=r_3+3r_1r_2+r_1^3
\]

and, because the trace part of the objective is constant along the orbit,

\[
\frac{d^3}{d\theta^3}F_2(C_{\lambda,u_\theta})\Big|_{0}
=
\mathbb E[mL_0'''].
\tag{5.7}
\]

The terms proportional to `DW` and `D^3W` vanish by the conditional sign-character rule from Section 4. Only the `DW^3` and `D^3W^3` terms survive.

Since

\[
W^3=\frac{(A+B+C)^3}{3\sqrt3},
\]

the only surviving full-parity monomial is

\[
\frac{2}{\sqrt3}ABC.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathbb E[mDW^3]
=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,
\mathbb E[U|DABC|],
\tag{5.8}
\]

\[
\mathbb E[mD^3W^3]
=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\,
\mathbb E[U|DABC|D^2].
\tag{5.9}
\]

## 6. A positive integral quantity and a scale-derivative bound

Define

\[
H(s):=\mathbb E[U|DABC|].
\tag{6.1}
\]

Because `U>0` almost surely away from a null set,

\[
H(s)>0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

There is also a one-dimensional integral representation. Let `f` and `F` denote the standard half-normal density and CDF. If

\[
Y_1\ge Y_2\ge Y_3
\]

are the decreasing order statistics of `|A|,|B|,|C|`, then direct rank splitting of `s|X|` gives

\[
\boxed{
H(s)=6s^2\int_0^\infty
 y f(y)^2\,[F(y)-y f(y)]\,F(y/s)\,dy.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The integrand is strictly positive for `y>0`.

More importantly, no closed form is needed for the sign argument. Pointwise, for fixed positive magnitudes of `X,A,B,C`, the quantity

\[
U|DABC|=T U^2
\]

is a monomial in the scale `s` of degree exactly `1` when `s|X|` is one of the two largest magnitudes and degree exactly `2` when it is one of the two smallest. Rank-switching points form a null set. Therefore

\[
\boxed{H(s)\le sH'(s)\le2H(s).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This elementary inequality is the key sign certificate.

The standard Gaussian scale-score identity gives

\[
\mathbb E[U|DABC|D^2]
=s^2\big(H+sH'\big).
\tag{6.5}
\]

## 7. Strict positivity of the cubic coefficient for every strength

Combining Sections 5 and 6 yields

\[
\boxed{
 c_3(s):=
\frac{d^3}{d\theta^3}F_2(C_{\lambda,u_\theta})\Big|_{0}
=
\frac{2\alpha^2}{\sqrt3}
\left[(2+s^2)H(s)-(1-s^2)sH'(s)\right].
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Using `sH'<=2H`,

\[
(2+s^2)H-(1-s^2)sH'
\ge
(2+s^2)H-2(1-s^2)H
=3s^2H.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 c_3(s)\ge2\sqrt3\,\alpha^2s^2H(s)>0
 \qquad (0<s<1).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is an exact theorem, not numerical evidence.

In particular,

\[
\boxed{c_3(s_*)>0}
\]

at the first balanced curvature bifurcation.

As a non-rigorous magnitude check only, high-precision quadrature at the previously located

\[
s_*\approx0.5046113125617359603
\]

gives approximately

\[
H(s_*)\approx0.08996571514,
\qquad
c_3(s_*)\approx0.7676185666.
\]

The analytic proof of positivity does not depend on these decimals.

## 8. Consequence: the balanced crossing is cubic, not pitchfork-even

In local tangent coordinates `z in E`, `S_4` invariance gives the Taylor form

\[
F(z,\lambda)
=F(0,\lambda)
+\frac{\kappa(\lambda)}2\|z\|^2
+a(\lambda)\sum_i z_i^3
+O(\|z\|^4),
\tag{8.1}
\]

where Section 7 proves

\[
a(\lambda_*)\ne0.
\]

For the normalized `3+1` vector `h` in (5.1),

\[
\sum_i h_i^3=\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\]

Since the third directional derivative equals `c_3`,

\[
a(\lambda_*)=\frac{c_3(s_*)}{2\sqrt3}>0.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Therefore at `lambda=lambda_*` the balanced point is already a degenerate **saddle**, not a flat local extremum: the cubic term changes sign between `+h` and `-h`.

## 9. The `3+1` critical branch exists and is a saddle on both sides

Restrict to the `S_3`-fixed geodesic (5.3). Write

\[
\phi(\theta,\lambda)
=F_2(C_{\lambda,u_\theta}).
\]

The balanced point is stationary for every `lambda`, so

\[
\phi_\theta(\theta,\lambda)
=\theta\left[
\kappa(\lambda)+\frac{c_3(s_*)}{2}\theta
+O(\theta^2+|\lambda-\lambda_*|\,|\theta|)
\right].
\tag{9.1}
\]

Because `c_3(s_*)>0`, the implicit-function theorem gives a unique nearby nonzero `3+1` branch

\[
\boxed{
\theta_{31}(\lambda)
=-\frac{2\kappa(\lambda)}{c_3(s_*)}
+O(\kappa(\lambda)^2).
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

By permutation symmetry, criticality on this one-dimensional fixed-point set implies full criticality.

The axial Hessian eigenvalue on the branch is

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{\parallel}=-\kappa+O(\kappa^2).
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

To compute the transverse pair, use the cubic invariant. For any unit transverse vector `e` supported in the three equal coordinates and summing to zero,

\[
D^3F(0)[h,e,e]= -\frac12 c_3(s_*).
\tag{9.4}
\]

Substituting (9.2),

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{\perp}=2\kappa+O(\kappa^2)
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

with multiplicity two.

Thus, for all sufficiently small nonzero `|lambda-lambda_*|`, the nonzero `3+1` branch has Hessian signature

- one sign from `-kappa`,
- the opposite sign, twice, from `2kappa`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{the `3+1` symmetry-breaking branch is a saddle on both sides of the first balanced crossing.}
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

This excludes the most immediate symmetry-breaking branch as a local-max counterexample mechanism.

## 10. What remains locally unresolved

The tangent representation also has a `2+2` fixed direction, for example

\[
h_{22}=(1,1,-1,-1)/2.
\]

Along this direction

\[
\sum_i h_{22,i}^3=0,
\]

and in fact the cubic gradient vanishes on the `2+2` axis because all four squared coordinates are equal. Therefore its bifurcation is controlled by **quartic** order, not by the cubic theorem above.

This is now the precise unresolved local obligation. One must compute and sign the quartic coefficient in the `2+2` fixed-point sector at `lambda_*`, and then classify the corresponding transverse Hessian if a nonzero `2+2` branch exists.

A separate high-precision derivation performed during this run suggests the `2+2` fourth directional derivative at the crossing is positive (approximately `1.30`), but that sign has **not** been promoted to a certified theorem in this checkpoint and is therefore recorded only as a lead, not as a proved claim.
