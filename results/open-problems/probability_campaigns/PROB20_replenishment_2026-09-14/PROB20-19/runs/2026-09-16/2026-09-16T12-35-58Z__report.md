## 5. New exact two-scale top-two formula

Introduce a second scale `t>0`. Let

\[
Y_1=s|Z_1|,\quad Y_2=t|Z_2|,\quad
Y_3=|Z_3|,\quad Y_4=|Z_4|,
\]

for independent standard Gaussians, and let `T_{s,t}` be the product of the two largest of `Y_1,...,Y_4`.

Define

\[
G(s,t)=\mathbb E T_{s,t}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — exact closed form

Put

\[
R=\sqrt{s^2t^2+s^2+t^2},\qquad
r_s=\sqrt{1+2s^2},\qquad
r_t=\sqrt{1+2t^2}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
G(s,t)=\frac4{\pi^2}
\left[
2R\arctan\frac{st}{R}
+r_s\arctan\frac{s}{t r_s}
+r_t\arctan\frac{t}{s r_t}
\right].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

For four independent nonnegative variables, the product of the top two can be decomposed over the unordered pair that occupies the top two ranks. For half-normal scale `sigma`,

\[
f_\sigma(y)=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac1\sigma e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2)},
\qquad
F_\sigma(y)=\operatorname{erf}\frac{y}{\sqrt2\sigma},
\]

and

\[
\int_y^\infty x f_\sigma(x)\,dx
=\sigma\sqrt{\frac2\pi}e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Splitting each pair contribution according to which member is larger reduces it to integrals of the form

\[
I(a,b,c)=\int_0^\infty y e^{-ay^2}
\operatorname{erf}(by)\operatorname{erf}(cy)\,dy.
\]

An integration by parts gives

\[
\boxed{
I(a,b,c)=\frac1{a\pi}
\left[
\frac{b}{\sqrt{a+b^2}}\arctan\frac{c}{\sqrt{a+b^2}}
+
\frac{c}{\sqrt{a+c^2}}\arctan\frac{b}{\sqrt{a+c^2}}
\right].
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Substituting the six unordered pairs for scales `(s,t,1,1)` and simplifying yields (5.2).

As an exact consistency check,

\[
G(s,1)=g(s),
\tag{5.5}
\]

where `g(s)` is the closed balanced-score formula proved in the `10-30-13Z` checkpoint.

## 9. The `2+2` critical branch exists and is a saddle

Let

\[

=F_2(C_{\lambda,u_\theta})
\]

for (7.1). Exact pair-exchange symmetry gives

\[

\tag{9.1}

\[

\qquad
\kappa<0\text{ immediately below }\lambda_*,
\qquad
\kappa>0\text{ immediately above }\lambda_*.
\tag{9.2}
\]

At the crossing,

\[
\phi_{22}(\theta,\lambda)
=\phi_{22}(0,\lambda)
+\frac{\kappa(\lambda)}2\theta^2
+\frac{q_4(s_*)}{24}\theta^4
+O(\theta^6+|\lambda-\lambda_*|\theta^4).
\tag{9.3}
\]

Because `q_4(s_*)>0`, apply the implicit-function theorem to `rho=theta^2`. There are nonzero `2+2` critical points on the `lambda<lambda_*` side satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\theta_{22}^2
=-\frac{6\kappa(\lambda)}{q_4(s_*)}
+O(\kappa(\lambda)^2).
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

The axial Hessian eigenvalue there is

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{\parallel}
=-2\kappa+O(\kappa^2)>0.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

Thus the branch is a minimum along its symmetry axis. To classify it in the full tangent space, use the strictly positive cubic invariant coefficient from the preceding checkpoint.

Take the two orthonormal transverse modes

\[
e_+=(1,-1,0,0)/\sqrt2,
\qquad
e_-=(0,0,1,-1)/\sqrt2.
\tag{9.6}
\]

The local cubic term is

\[
a(\lambda_*)\sum_i z_i^3,
\qquad
a(\lambda_*)=\frac{c_3(s_*)}{2\sqrt3}>0.
\]

Since

\[
\sum_i h_{22,i}e_{+,i}^2=\frac12,
\qquad
\sum_i h_{22,i}e_{-,i}^2=-\frac12,
\]

the cubic tensor gives

\[
D^3F(0)[h_{22},e_+,e_+]
=+3a=+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}c_3(s_*),
\]

\[
D^3F(0)[h_{22},e_-,e_-]
=-3a=-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}c_3(s_*).
\tag{9.7}
\]

Therefore the two transverse Hessian eigenvalues on the nonzero branch obey

\[
\boxed{
\mu_\pm
=
\kappa
\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}c_3(s_*)\theta_{22}
+O(\theta_{22}^2+|\lambda-\lambda_*|).
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

Because `theta_22` is of order `sqrt(-kappa)` while `kappa` is of smaller order, the opposite linear terms dominate. For every sufficiently nearby nonzero branch point, one transverse eigenvalue is positive and the other is negative. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{the bifurcating `2+2` branch is a saddle.}
}
\tag{9.9}
\]

The sign conclusion holds for either member `+theta_22` or `-theta_22`; the two transverse labels simply interchange.
