# 4. Recalled exact radial formula for `p=4,d=2`

Let `Q in O(3)` act on the first three eigen-directions and leave the fourth fixed. Put

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1},\sqrt{\lambda_2},\sqrt{\lambda_3}),
\qquad
b_i(u)=(e_i^TQDu)^2,\quad u\in S^2,
\]

and write the increasing order statistics as

\[
b_{(1)}(u)\le b_{(2)}(u)\le b_{(3)}(u).
\]

If the fourth eigenvalue is `lambda>0`, then the expected bottom-two energy is exactly

\[
B_Q(\lambda)
=3\,\mathbb E b_{(1)}
+4\lambda\,\mathbb E J\!\left(\frac{b_{(2)}}{\lambda}\right),
\tag{4.1}
\]

where, for `q ~ Beta(3/2,1/2)`,

\[
J(x)=\mathbb E\min\{qx,1-q\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because retained top-two energy equals `tr Lambda - B_Q(lambda)`, proving `B_Q(lambda)>B_I(lambda)` is exactly proving strict KL dominance.

The previous checkpoint established

\[
J''(x)=-\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{x}(1+x)^3}<0,
\qquad
J(0)=0,
\qquad
J'(0)=\mathbb E q=\frac34.
\tag{4.3}
\]

# 5. B7: a compact-window lemma for the fourth eigenvalue

Define

\[
K_\lambda(b):=4\lambda J(b/\lambda).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Two elementary upper bounds follow directly from the definition of `J`:

\[
J(x)\le \mathbb E(1-q)=\frac14,
\qquad
J(x)\le x\mathbb E q=\frac{3x}{4},
\]

hence

\[
0\le K_\lambda(b)\le \min\{\lambda,3b\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For a lower bound, (4.3) gives

\[
J''(x)
=-\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{x}(1+x)^3}
\ge -\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{x}}.
\]

Integrating first from `0` to `x` and then once more,

\[
J'(x)\ge \frac34-\frac{4}{\pi}\sqrt{x},
\]

and therefore

\[
J(x)
\ge \frac{3x}{4}-\frac{8}{3\pi}x^{3/2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
R_\lambda(b):=3b-K_\lambda(b),
\]

then

\[
0\le R_\lambda(b)
\le \frac{32}{3\pi}\frac{b^{3/2}}{\sqrt\lambda}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For a fixed `Q`, define the exact angular gaps

\[
C_Q:=\mathbb E b_{(1)}^Q-\mathbb E b_{(1)}^I,
\tag{5.5}
\]

\[
A_Q:=\mathbb E(b_{(1)}^Q+b_{(2)}^Q)
     -\mathbb E(b_{(1)}^I+b_{(2)}^I),
\tag{5.6}
\]

and

\[
M_Q:=\mathbb E[(b_{(2)}^Q)^{3/2}].
\tag{5.7}
\]

Using (5.2) in (4.1),

\[
B_Q(\lambda)-B_I(\lambda)
\ge 3C_Q-\lambda.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Using `K_lambda(b)=3b-R_lambda(b)`, (5.4), and only the nonnegativity of the KL remainder,

\[
B_Q(\lambda)-B_I(\lambda)
\ge
3A_Q-\frac{32}{3\pi}\frac{M_Q}{\sqrt\lambda}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Therefore, whenever `C_Q>0` and `A_Q>0`, any counterexample for this fixed `Q` must lie inside the explicit compact interval

\[
\boxed{
3C_Q\le \lambda\le
\left(\frac{32M_Q}{9\pi A_Q}\right)^2.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

If the lower endpoint exceeds the upper endpoint, this fixed rotation is KL-safe for **every** positive fourth eigenvalue. This is a theorem about the exact correlated Gaussian objective; no independence-replacement inequality is used.

# 6. B16: the balanced-spike family

For an integer `n>=7`, define

\[
L_n=2n^2-2,
\qquad
\Lambda_n(\lambda)=\operatorname{diag}(1,L_n,1,\lambda),
\quad \lambda>0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Use the fixed algebraic orthogonal matrix

\[
Q_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt3&1/\sqrt6\\
-1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt3&1/\sqrt6\\
0&1/\sqrt3&-2/\sqrt6
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Q_4=Q_3\oplus1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The second column of `Q_3` is the balanced vector `(1,1,1)/sqrt(3)`. Hence the rotated first-three covariance is

\[
Q_3\operatorname{diag}(1,L_n,1)Q_3^T
=I_3+\frac{L_n-1}{3}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus all three off-diagonal covariances are nonzero. This is a connected three-direction overlap and is not covered by the earlier matching-supported theorem.

Let `u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)` be uniform on `S^2`, and set `t=|u_2|`. In dimension three, `u_2` is uniform on `[-1,1]`, so `t` is uniform on `[0,1]`.

Each of the three rotated linear forms has a common spike term of magnitude

\[
a t,\qquad a=\sqrt{L_n/3},
\]

plus a low-eigenvalue term whose absolute value is at most

\[
b\sqrt{1-t^2},\qquad b=\sqrt{2/3}.
\]

Since

\[
\frac ab=\sqrt{L_n/2}=\sqrt{n^2-1}=:s,
\]

the reverse triangle inequality gives the pointwise bound

\[
b_{(1)}^{Q_3}(u)
\ge
b^2\left(s t-\sqrt{1-t^2}\right)_+^2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The sign switch occurs exactly at

\[
t=\frac1n,
\tag{6.5}
\]

because `s^2=n^2-1`. Integrating (6.4) exactly over the uniform variable `t` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E b_{(1)}^{Q_3}
\ge \frac{2(n-1)^2}{9}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

For the KL orientation, the three angular squares are

\[
u_1^2,\quad L_nu_2^2,\quad u_3^2.
\]

The smallest is at most `u_1^2`, hence

\[
\mathbb E b_{(1)}^I\le \frac13.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Likewise the sum of the two smallest is at most `u_1^2+u_3^2`, so

\[
\mathbb E(b_{(1)}^I+b_{(2)}^I)\le \frac23.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Since `b_(1)+b_(2)>=2b_(1)`, define

\[
D_n:=2n^2-4n-1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Then (6.6)--(6.8) imply

\[
\boxed{C_{Q_3}\ge \frac{D_n}{9}},
\qquad
\boxed{A_{Q_3}\ge \frac{2D_n}{9}}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

It remains to control `M_Q`. The ordinary triangle inequality gives, for every rotated linear form,

\[
|\ell_i(u)|
\le b\left(s t+\sqrt{1-t^2}\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
(b_{(2)}^{Q_3})^{3/2}
\le
b^3\left(s t+\sqrt{1-t^2}\right)^3.
\]

Using

\[
\int_0^1t^3dt=\frac14,
\quad
\int_0^1t^2\sqrt{1-t^2}\,dt=\frac\pi{16},
\]

\[
\int_0^1t(1-t^2)dt=\frac14,
\quad
\int_0^1(1-t^2)^{3/2}dt=\frac{3\pi}{16},
\]

one obtains the explicit bound

\[
\boxed{
M_{Q_3}\le U_n
:=\sqrt6\left[
\frac{\sqrt{n^2-1}(n^2+2)}{18}
+\frac{\pi n^2}{24}
\right].
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Combining (5.8), (5.9), (6.10), and (6.11),

\[
B_{Q_3}(\lambda)-B_I(\lambda)
\ge \frac{D_n}{3}-\lambda,
\tag{6.12}
\]

and

\[
B_{Q_3}(\lambda)-B_I(\lambda)
\ge
\frac{2D_n}{3}
-\frac{32U_n}{3\pi\sqrt\lambda}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
H_n:=\left(\frac{16U_n}{\pi D_n}\right)^2
<\frac{D_n}{3},
\tag{6.14}
\]

then (6.12) handles every `0<lambda<D_n/3`, while (6.13) is strictly positive for every `lambda>=D_n/3`; hence KL is strictly optimal for this `Q_3` for all `lambda>0`.

# 9. Counterexample-first interpretation and failed/closed routes

The previous run had found a rigorous threshold-tail reversal for the second spherical order statistic. The present balanced-spike family was chosen to try to amplify that adverse effect by tuning the fourth variance `lambda`. Instead, the limit analysis shows that the first-order-statistic penalty is too large for every `lambda` once `n>=7`.

The outcome sharpens the counterexample search:

1. large variance flattening by a balanced spike is not enough by itself;
2. the fourth-scale tuning can only matter in a compact interval when both angular KL gaps `C_Q,A_Q` are positive;
3. a plausible counterexample should therefore seek rotations for which at least one of these angular gaps is nonpositive, or where `M_Q` is sufficiently large that the compact window does not collapse.

I did not retain Monte Carlo candidates or sampled signs as proof. No invalid comparison to independent coordinates with the same marginal variances is used anywhere in this run.
