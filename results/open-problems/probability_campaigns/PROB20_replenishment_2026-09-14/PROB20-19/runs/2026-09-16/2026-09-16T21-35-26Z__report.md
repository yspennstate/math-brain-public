# 6. B30: the frozen metric is degenerate

## Proposition 6.1 — dimension-free near-identity continuity bound

For every orthogonal `Q`, every positive-semidefinite diagonal `Lambda`, and every `1<=d<p`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{|F_d(Q;\Lambda)-F_d(I;\Lambda)|}{\operatorname{tr}\Lambda}
\le 2\|Q-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

For nonnegative vectors `a,b`, the sum of the largest `d` coordinates has the variational representation

\[
\operatorname{Top}_d(a)=\max_{|S|=d}\sum_{i\in S}a_i.
\]

Hence

\[
|\operatorname{Top}_d(a)-\operatorname{Top}_d(b)|
\le \|a-b\|_1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For a deterministic vector `y`, put `x=Qy`. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i|x_i^2-y_i^2|
&=\sum_i |(x_i-y_i)(x_i+y_i)|\\
&\le \|x-y\|_2\,\|x+y\|_2\\
&\le \|Q-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|y\|_2\cdot 2\|y\|_2\\
&=2\|Q-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|y\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Combining (6.2)--(6.3), taking `Y~N(0,Lambda)`, and using `E||Y||^2=tr Lambda` gives (6.1). QED.

For a single Givens rotation by angle `theta`,

\[
\|Q-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}=2|\sin(\theta/2)|,
\]

so

\[
\frac{|F_d(Q;\Lambda)-F_d(I;\Lambda)|}{\operatorname{tr}\Lambda}
\le4|\sin(\theta/2)|.
\tag{6.4}
\]

# 7. B8/B19: exact chamber structure on the sphere

Write the rows of `A=QD` as `a_1,a_2,a_3`. Then

\[
b_i(u)=(a_i\cdot u)^2.
\]

For `i != j`,

\[
b_i(u)-b_j(u)
=((a_i-a_j)\cdot u)((a_i+a_j)\cdot u).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore each ordering boundary `b_i=b_j` is the union of two great circles. There are three unordered pairs, so at most six great circles determine every region on which the identities of `b_(1),b_(2),b_(3)` are fixed. A generic arrangement of `N` great circles has at most

\[
N^2-N+2
\]

regions; for `N=6` this is at most `32`. Degeneracies can only reduce the count.

Thus the median-order statistic is not an arbitrary nonsmooth function: its ordering part has a finite, explicitly algebraic chamber decomposition. Adding a threshold `b_i=t` contributes small-circle boundaries. This converts threshold probabilities into a finite spherical-cap/lens problem, or equivalently a rigorous adaptive-cell problem.

# 8. Certified failure of threshold-by-threshold median domination

\[
\Pr_Q\{b_{(2)}>t\}
\ge
\Pr_{KL}\{b_{(2)}>t\}
\quad\text{for every }t,
\tag{8.1}
\]

because a larger second-order statistic would increase the bottom-two penalty in (5.1). **This stronger route is false.**

Take the three-dimensional spectrum

\[

\[

with exact rational angles

\[

\sin\alpha=\frac{272}{353},
\tag{8.2}

\tag{8.3}

At the exact threshold

<

\tag{8.5}

## Rigorous enclosure method

Use the equal-area parameterization

\[
u(z,q)=\left(\sqrt{1-z^2}\cos(\pi q),
\sqrt{1-z^2}\sin(\pi q),z\right),
\]

with

\[
z\in[-1,1],\qquad q\in[0,2].
\]

Uniform spherical probability is exactly `dz dq / 4`. Dyadic rectangles in `(z,q)` therefore have exact rational probability masses.

On each cell, outward interval arithmetic encloses each linear form `a_i dot u`, then its square. A cell is certified **inside** `{b_(2)>19/20}` if at least two squared-form lower bounds exceed `19/20`; it is certified **outside** if fewer than two squared-form upper bounds can exceed `19/20`; otherwise it is subdivided. No Monte Carlo probability estimate enters the certificate.

The executed enclosure produced

\[

\in
\left[

\frac{623215}{33554432}
\right],
\tag{8.6}

while

\[

\in
\left[
\frac{44523}{1048576},
\frac{364375}{8388608}
\right].
\tag{8.7}
\]

The rigorous separation is

\[
\boxed{

-

\ge
\frac{801521}{33554432}
>0.02388.

\tag{8.8}

The unresolved boundary-cell mass was at most
