# PROB20-19 — Full nonlinear Gaussian PCA conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.** The universal Mallat–Zeitouni/Bandeira conjecture remains open. This run does not claim a full proof or refutation. It establishes a rigorous obstruction to the most immediate continuation of the previous one-plane/local-Hessian program: **pairwise covariance diagonalization is not, in general, a monotone ascent step for the nonlinear top-`d` objective once the selected pair is correlated with the remaining coordinates.**

More precisely, there is an explicit centered positive-definite Gaussian in dimension `p=3`, with `d=2`, whose `(1,2)` covariance block is already diagonal with distinct eigenvalues, but the nonlinear retained-energy objective has a strictly nonzero first derivative under a `(1,2)` Givens rotation. Reversing that infinitesimal rotation gives a covariance with a nondiagonal `(1,2)` block for which the standard Jacobi diagonalization step **strictly decreases** the nonlinear objective.

This does **not** refute Mallat–Zeitouni: the starting basis in the example is not a full covariance eigenbasis. It refutes a stronger auxiliary statement that would have enabled a simple iterative Jacobi proof.

## 1. Exact target and source status

For `X ~ N(0,C)` in `R^p`, define

\[
F_d(C):=\mathbb E\,\operatorname{Top}_d(X_1^2,\ldots,X_p^2),
\]

and, equivalently for a fixed covariance `Sigma` and orthogonal basis `U`,

\[
F_\Sigma(U)=F_d(U\Sigma U^T).
\]

The universal target is to prove or refute that a covariance eigenbasis maximizes this quantity for every covariance and every `1<d<p`.

## 3. General exact first-variation formula

Let `C` be positive definite and let `X~N(0,C)`. Fix `i<j`, and rotate only coordinates `i,j` by

\[
R_{ij}(\theta):
\qquad
Y_i(\theta)=\cos\theta\,X_i+\sin\theta\,X_j,
\qquad
Y_j(\theta)=-\sin\theta\,X_i+\cos\theta\,X_j.
\]

Put

\[
\Phi_{ij}(\theta)
=\mathbb E\,\operatorname{Top}_d(Y_1(\theta)^2,\ldots,Y_p(\theta)^2).
\]

Because `C` is positive definite, ties between distinct squared coordinates occur with probability zero. Let

\[
I_k(X)=\mathbf 1\{X_k^2\text{ belongs to the }d\text{ largest of }X_1^2,\ldots,X_p^2\}.
\]

Away from the null tie set, the selected set is locally constant at `theta=0`, and

\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}Y_i(\theta)^2\bigg|_{0}=2X_iX_j,
\qquad
\frac{d}{d\theta}Y_j(\theta)^2\bigg|_{0}=-2X_iX_j.
\]

The samplewise derivative is bounded by `2|X_iX_j|`, which is integrable. Dominated convergence therefore gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{ij}'(0)
=2\,\mathbb E\left[X_iX_j\bigl(I_i(X)-I_j(X)\bigr)\right].
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus every local extremum satisfies the nonlinear stationarity equations

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[X_iX_j\bigl(I_i-I_j\bigr)\right]=0
\quad\text{for every }i<j.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

At a covariance eigenbasis these equations hold by independent sign symmetry, consistent with the previous checkpoint. But `C_{ij}=0` alone is not sufficient, because selection can correlate the sign product `X_iX_j` with the remaining coordinates.

## 4. Exact singular model exposing the obstruction

First consider the rank-two Gaussian model

\[
U,V\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\qquad
X_1=2U,
\quad
X_2=V,
\quad
X_3=U+V.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The `(1,2)` covariance block is exactly

\[
\begin{pmatrix}4&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]

so the first two covariance coordinates are already the distinct eigenvectors of their marginal `2 x 2` block.

Take `p=3,d=2`. Since `Top_2` equals total energy minus the smallest squared coordinate and the `(1,2)` rotation preserves `X_1^2+X_2^2`, formula (3.1) becomes

\[
\Phi_{12}'(0)
=4\,\mathbb E\left[UV\left(\mathbf 1_A-\mathbf 1_B\right)\right],
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
A=\{|V|<2|U|,\ |V|<|U+V|\},
\]

and

\[
B=\{2|U|<|V|,\ 2|U|<|U+V|\}.
\]

Because `(U,V)` is rotationally invariant, write

\[
U=r\cos\phi,
\qquad
V=r\sin\phi.
\]

Both events depend only on the angle. On one projective period let `t=tan(phi)`. Then

\[
A:\quad -\frac12<t<2,
\tag{4.3}
\]

because `|t|<2` and `|t|<|1+t|` is equivalent to `t>-1/2`. Likewise

\[
B:\quad t\in(-\infty,-3)\cup(2,\infty).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The radial integral is

\[
\int_0^\infty r^3e^{-r^2/2}\,dr=2,
\]

so

\[
\mathbb E[UV(\mathbf 1_A-\mathbf 1_B)]
=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\phi\sin\phi\,(\mathbf 1_A-\mathbf 1_B)\,d\phi.
\]

Using the projective symmetry and `d phi = dt/(1+t^2)`, this angular integral equals

\[
2\left[
\int_{-1/2}^{2}\frac{t}{(1+t^2)^2}\,dt
-\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{t}{(1+t^2)^2}\,dt
-\int_{-\infty}^{-3}\frac{t}{(1+t^2)^2}\,dt
\right].
\]

Since

\[
\int \frac{t}{(1+t^2)^2}\,dt=-\frac1{2(1+t^2)},
\]

the three integrals are respectively

\[
\frac3{10},\qquad \frac1{10},\qquad -\frac1{20}.
\]

Therefore the bracket is `1/4`, the doubled angular integral is `1/2`, and

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{12}'(0)=\frac{2}{\pi}>0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

So even though the selected pair's covariance block is diagonal, rotating away from its covariance eigenvectors immediately **increases** the nonlinear retained-energy objective.

## 5. Robust positive-definite version with an explicit parameter

The preceding example is singular, but the obstruction persists quantitatively inside the positive-definite cone.

Let

\[
U,V,W\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\]

and for `epsilon>0` set

\[
X_1=2U,
\qquad
X_2=V,
\qquad
X_3=U+V+\epsilon W.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
C_\epsilon=
\begin{pmatrix}
4&0&2\\
0&1&1\\
2&1&2+\epsilon^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The leading principal minors are `4`, `4`, and

\[
\det C_\epsilon=4\epsilon^2>0,
\]

so `C_epsilon` is positive definite for every `epsilon>0`. Its `(1,2)` principal block remains exactly `diag(4,1)`.

Let `D_epsilon=Phi_12'(0)`. We now give an explicit perturbation bound from the exact singular value `D_0=2/pi`.

Only comparisons involving `X_3` can change between `epsilon=0` and `epsilon>0`. A comparison between `|z|` and a threshold `a>=0` can change after replacing `z` by `z+epsilon W` only if the original `z` lies within `epsilon|W|` of one of the two roots `+a,-a`.

Here `z=U+V`. For the threshold `|V|`, the two root distances reduce to the linear forms

\[
U,
\qquad U+2V.
\]

For the threshold `2|U|`, they reduce to

\[
V-U,
\qquad 3U+V.
\]

Hence

\[
|D_\epsilon-D_0|
\le
4\sum_{L\in\{U,U+2V,V-U,3U+V\}}
\mathbb E\left[|UV|\,\mathbf 1\{|L|\le\epsilon|W|\}\right].
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `L~N(0,sigma^2)`, independent of `W`, then by Cauchy--Schwarz and `E[U^2V^2]=1`,

\[
\mathbb E\left[|UV|\,\mathbf 1\{|L|\le\epsilon|W|\}\right]
\le
\sqrt{\mathbb P(|L|\le\epsilon|W|)}.
\]

Because the ratio of two independent standard normals is Cauchy,

\[
\mathbb P(|L|\le\epsilon|W|)
=
\frac2\pi\arctan\left(\frac\epsilon\sigma\right)
\le
\frac{2\epsilon}{\pi\sigma}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The four standard deviations are

\[
1,\quad\sqrt5,\quad\sqrt2,\quad\sqrt{10}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|D_\epsilon-D_0|
\le
4\sqrt{\frac{2\epsilon}{\pi}}
\left(1+5^{-1/4}+2^{-1/4}+10^{-1/4}\right).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Take explicitly

\[
\epsilon=10^{-3}.
\]

Using the elementary estimates

\[
1+5^{-1/4}+2^{-1/4}+10^{-1/4}<3.1,
\]

`pi>25/8`, and `pi<22/7`, one obtains

\[
|D_{10^{-3}}-D_0|<0.314,
\qquad
D_0=\frac2\pi>\frac7{11}>0.636.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
D_{10^{-3}}>0.322.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is a completely explicit centered **positive-definite** Gaussian counterexample to the auxiliary assertion that a diagonal marginal covariance block must be stationary, let alone locally optimal, for the nonlinear objective.

## 6. Consequence for Jacobi-type proof strategies

Let

\[
C_*:=C_{10^{-3}}.
\]

At `theta=0`, its `(1,2)` principal covariance block is `diag(4,1)`, yet

\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}F_2(R_{12}(\theta)C_*R_{12}(\theta)^T)\bigg|_{\theta=0}>0.
\]

Therefore for all sufficiently small positive `theta`,

\[
F_2(R_{12}(\theta)C_*R_{12}(\theta)^T)>F_2(C_*).
\tag{6.1}
\]

But the `(1,2)` principal block of the rotated covariance is exactly

\[
R_{12}(\theta)\operatorname{diag}(4,1)R_{12}(\theta)^T,
\]

so the standard Jacobi step that diagonalizes that block rotates it back to `diag(4,1)` and returns the objective to the smaller value `F_2(C_*)`.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A covariance-Jacobi diagonalization step need not increase the nonlinear top-}d\text{ objective.}
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Equivalently, the hoped-for extension of the previous one-plane theorem from an actual global eigenbasis to an arbitrary basis with a locally diagonalized `2 x 2` marginal block is false.

The mechanism is exactly the one anticipated by the conditional-mean warning in the previous checkpoint: correlations with the outside coordinates make the adaptive selection event carry sign information about `X_iX_j`, so the centered marginal pair symmetry is not enough.

## 8. Failed approach and revised next attack

### Failed route isolated

A natural continuation of the previous full-tangent theorem was:

1. take an arbitrary current covariance matrix in the working basis;
2. choose a pair `(i,j)`;
3. diagonalize its `2 x 2` principal covariance block;
4. prove this cannot decrease the nonlinear retained energy;
5. iterate Jacobi rotations to a global eigenbasis.

The explicit positive-definite example above proves step 4 false.

A still stronger hoped-for conditional lemma—uniform optimality of covariance-axis alignment for a noncentral two-dimensional Gaussian hinge after conditioning on the outside variables—is likewise impossible in this unrestricted form. The conditional mean induced by outside correlations is precisely what generates the nonzero first variation.

### Next attack

The next useful obligation should avoid pointwise Jacobi monotonicity. Two more plausible directions are now isolated:

1. **Stationary-point route.** Use (3.2) simultaneously for all pairs and attempt to prove that every local maximum must commute with the original covariance, even though a single equation does not follow from `C_ij=0`. The global coupling of all stationarity equations may be much stronger than any one Jacobi step.
2. **Averaged multi-plane interpolation.** Seek a global path or heat-flow/random-rotation interpolation for which the *sum* of pairwise first variations has a sign, even though individual Jacobi steps can be adverse. The exact counterexample tells us any successful flow must exploit cancellation or a global Lyapunov quantity, not marginal block diagonalization alone.

A useful immediate calculation for the next run is to rewrite the full gradient in matrix form and study the commutator stationarity condition for `F_d(C)`, then test whether sign/majorization properties of the Gaussian selection matrix can force commutation at local maxima.
