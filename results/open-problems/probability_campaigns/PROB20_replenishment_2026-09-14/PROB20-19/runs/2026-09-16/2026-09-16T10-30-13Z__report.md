## 3. The family and the balanced stationary point

Fix

\[
C_{\lambda,u}=I_4-(1-\lambda)uu^T,
\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1,
\qquad \|u\|=1,
\tag{3.1}
\]

whose spectrum is `(1,1,1,lambda)`.

Take the fully balanced direction

\[
u_*:=\frac12(1,1,1,1).
\tag{3.2}
\]

By permutation symmetry, `u_*` is an exact stationary point for every `lambda`. The stabilizer acts on the tangent space `T_{u_*}S^3` as the irreducible three-dimensional standard representation of `S_4`, so the tangent Hessian at `u_*` is a scalar multiple of the identity. It therefore suffices to compute curvature in one unit tangent direction, for example

\[
h:=\frac12(1,1,-1,-1),
\qquad
u_\theta=u_*\cos\theta+h\sin\theta.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The sign of that one curvature classifies the balanced point as a strict local maximum or minimum whenever the curvature is nonzero.

## 4. Hadamard coordinates and the exact top-two identity

Introduce the orthogonal Hadamard coordinates

\[
D=\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4}{2},\qquad
A=\frac{x_1+x_2-x_3-x_4}{2},
\]

\[
B=\frac{x_1-x_2+x_3-x_4}{2},\qquad
C=\frac{x_1-x_2-x_3+x_4}{2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For every deterministic `x in R^4`, direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Top}_2(x_1^2,x_2^2,x_3^2,x_4^2)
=
\frac{D^2+A^2+B^2+C^2}{2}
+
\max\{|DA+BC|,|DB+AC|,|DC+AB|\}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

At the balanced point `u_*`, these coordinates diagonalize the covariance:

\[
D\sim N(0,\lambda),\qquad
A,B,C\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\tag{4.3}
\]

independently.

Set

\[
s:=\sqrt\lambda,
\qquad D=sX,
\qquad X,A,B,C\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The trace term in (4.2) has expectation `(3+s^2)/2`; all nontrivial balanced-point information is contained in

\[
m:=\max\{|DA+BC|,|DB+AC|,|DC+AB|\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

## 5. New exact sign-averaging lemma

### Lemma 5.1

Let `r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4>=0` be fixed and let `eps_i` be independent Rademacher signs. Then

\[
\mathbb E_{\varepsilon}
\max\Big\{
|\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2 r_1r_2+\varepsilon_3\varepsilon_4 r_3r_4|,
|\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_3 r_1r_3+\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_4 r_2r_4|,
|\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_4 r_1r_4+\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3 r_2r_3|
\Big\}

\[
\boxed{=r_{(1)}r_{(2)},}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `r_(1)>=r_(2)>=r_(3)>=r_(4)` are the decreasing rearrangement of the four magnitudes.

### Proof

The relative sign in each of the three pairings is the same product

\[
\sigma:=\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_3\varepsilon_4,
\]

which equals `+1` and `-1` with probability `1/2` each.

After sorting `r_(1)>=...>=r_(4)`:

- if `sigma=+1`, rearrangement gives
  \[
  \max = r_{(1)}r_{(2)}+r_{(3)}r_{(4)};
  \]
- if `sigma=-1`, elementary pairwise comparison gives
  \[
  \max = r_{(1)}r_{(2)}-r_{(3)}r_{(4)}.
  \]

Averaging the two cases yields (5.1). `square`

Applied to (4.5), conditional on the four absolute values,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[m\mid |X|,|A|,|B|,|C|]
=
\text{product of the two largest of }
\{s|X|,|A|,|B|,|C|\}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the key simplification of the run.

## 6. Exact closed form for the balanced objective

Let

\[
f(y)=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}e^{-y^2/2},
\qquad
F(y)=\operatorname{erf}(y/\sqrt2),
\qquad y\ge0,
\tag{6.1}
\]

be the half-normal density and CDF. Let

\[
Y_{(1)}\ge Y_{(2)}\ge Y_{(3)}
\]

be the decreasing order statistics of three independent half-normal variables, independent of `X>=0` half-normal. From (5.2),

\[
g(s):=\mathbb E m
=
\mathbb E\big[Y_{(1)}\max\{Y_{(2)},sX\}\big].
\tag{6.2}
\]

The joint density of `(Y_(1),Y_(2))` is

\[
6f(y_1)f(y_2)F(y_2),\qquad y_1\ge y_2\ge0.
\]

Since

\[
\int_y^\infty t f(t)\,dt=f(y)
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E\max\{y,sX\}
=yF(y/s)+s f(y/s),
\]

we obtain the one-dimensional integral

\[
\boxed{
 g(s)=6\int_0^\infty
 f(y)^2F(y)\big[yF(y/s)+s f(y/s)\big]\,dy.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Use the elementary integral

\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-a y^2}\operatorname{erf}(by)\,dy
=
\frac1{\sqrt{\pi a}}
\arctan\frac{b}{\sqrt a},
\qquad a>0,\ b\ge0,
\tag{6.4}
\]

and one integration by parts in the term containing
`y e^{-y^2}F(y)F(y/s)`. After simplification,

\[
\boxed{
 g(s)=\frac{12}{\pi^2}
 \left[
 \frac1{\sqrt3}\arctan\frac1{\sqrt3\,s}
 +\sqrt{1+2s^2}\,
 \arctan\frac{s}{\sqrt{1+2s^2}}
 \right].
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore the exact full nonlinear objective at the balanced point is

\[
\boxed{
F_{\rm bal}(\lambda)
=\frac{3+\lambda}{2}
+\frac{12}{\pi^2}
\left[
\frac1{\sqrt3}\arctan\frac1{\sqrt{3\lambda}}
+\sqrt{1+2\lambda}\,
\arctan\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{1+2\lambda}}
\right]
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

for `0<lambda<=1`, with the continuous endpoint value at `lambda=0`.

Consistency checks:

\[
g(0)=\frac{2\sqrt3}{\pi},
\qquad
F_{\rm bal}(0)=\frac32+\frac{2\sqrt3}{\pi},
\tag{6.7}
\]

matching the exact singular calculation in the `08-45-00Z` checkpoint.

## 7. Exact all-strength balanced Hessian

Let

\[
\kappa(\lambda)
:=
\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}
F_2(C_{\lambda,u_\theta})\Big|_{\theta=0}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Write `epsilon=1-lambda=1-s^2`. At `theta=0`, the covariance in `(D,A,B,C)` coordinates is `diag(s^2,1,1,1)`. Its inverse is

\[
K_\theta=I+\alpha u_\theta u_\theta^T,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{1-s^2}{s^2}.
\]

Because the determinant is constant along the orbit, the Gaussian density score gives

\[
\frac{p_\theta''}{p_0}\Big|_{0}
=\alpha(D^2-A^2)+\alpha^2D^2A^2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The trace term in (4.2) has constant expectation on the orbit, so only `m` contributes:

\[
\kappa
=
\alpha\,\mathbb E[m(D^2-A^2)]
+\alpha^2\,\mathbb E[mD^2A^2].
\tag{7.3}
\]

Now use symmetry among `A,B,C`. Put

\[
S=A^2+B^2+C^2.
\]

Because `m` is homogeneous of degree two in the standardized Gaussian vector `(X,A,B,C)`, radial-angular independence gives

\[
\mathbb E[m(X^2+S)]=6g(s),
\tag{7.4}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E[mX^2(X^2+S)]=8\,\mathbb E[mX^2].
\tag{7.5}
\]

Differentiating the variance parameter, or equivalently differentiating (6.3), gives

\[
\mathbb E[mX^2]=g+s g',
\tag{7.6}
\]

\[
\mathbb E[mX^4]=s^2g''+5s g'+3g.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbb E[mS]=5g-sg',
\qquad
\mathbb E[mX^2S]=5g+3sg'-s^2g''.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Substitution into (7.3) collapses the full Hessian to

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(\lambda)
=\frac{2(1-s^2)}{3}
\left[
-g(s)+\frac{2}{s}g'(s)
-\frac{1-s^2}{2}g''(s)
\right],
\qquad s=\sqrt\lambda.
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

The derivatives of the closed form simplify strongly. If

\[
r:=\sqrt{1+2s^2},
\qquad
A_s:=\arctan\frac{s}{r},
\qquad
B_s:=\arctan\frac1{\sqrt3\,s},
\]

then

\[
g'(s)=\frac{24s}{\pi^2r}A_s,
\tag{7.10}
\]

\[
g''(s)=\frac{24}{\pi^2}
\left[
\frac{A_s}{r^3}
+\frac{s}{r^2(1+3s^2)}
\right].
\tag{7.11}
\]

Define the sign function

\[
Q(s):=-g(s)+\frac{2}{s}g'(s)-\frac{1-s^2}{2}g''(s).
\tag{7.12}
\]

Then `sign(kappa)=sign(Q)` on `0<s<1`. Multiplying by the positive factor `pi^2/12`, set

\[
\widehat Q(s):=\frac{\pi^2}{12}Q(s).
\]

The exact elementary expression is

\[
\boxed{
\widehat Q(s)
=
\frac{-4s^4+5s^2+2}{(1+2s^2)^{3/2}}A_s
-\frac1{\sqrt3}B_s
-\frac{(1-s^2)s}{(1+2s^2)(1+3s^2)}.
}
\tag{7.13}
\]

Thus the balanced-point local geometry is reduced to the sign of one explicit one-variable elementary function.

## 8. Endpoint checks and an exact closed form for `Delta_{4,2}`

From (7.13),

\[
\widehat Q(0+)=-\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt3},
\]

\[
\boxed{

}
\tag{8.1}

At the isotropic endpoint, both `Q(1)=0` and the prefactor `1-s^2` vanish. Dividing once more by `1-s^2` gives

\[
\lim_{s\uparrow1}
\frac{\widehat Q(s)}{1-s^2}
=
\frac{\sqrt3\pi}{9}-\frac14.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\kappa(\lambda)

\left(
\frac{8\sqrt3}{9\pi}-\frac{2}{\pi^2}

>0.

\tag{8.4}

Numerically this is approximately `1.1497110479165584`, but the positivity and formula are exact.
