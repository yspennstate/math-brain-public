# 4. New exact theorem: KL optimality for every matching-supported orthogonal mixing

The old single-Givens theorem has a stronger consequence that was not stated in the recovered report and that subsumes the entire continuous version of the previous run's separable two-block search.

Let

\[
Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_p),\qquad Y_i\sim N(0,\lambda_i)
\]

be independent eigen-coordinates. Let `Q` be an orthogonal matrix which, after row/column permutations and sign changes, is a direct sum of blocks of sizes `1` and `2`:

\[
Q\sim Q_1\oplus\cdots\oplus Q_m\oplus(\pm1)\oplus\cdots,
\qquad Q_r\in O(2),
\]

with the `2x2` blocks acting on pairwise disjoint sets of eigen-coordinates. Equivalently, `Q` is a product of Givens rotations with pairwise disjoint supports, up to signed permutations.

Define

\[
F_d(Q)=\mathbb E\operatorname{Top}_d\big((QY)_1^2,\ldots,(QY)_p^2\big).
\]

> **Theorem 4.1 (matching-supported KL optimality).** For every covariance spectrum, every `1<=d<p`, and every such matching-supported `Q`,
> \[
> \boxed{F_d(Q)\le F_d(I).}
> \]
> If the covariance is positive definite and at least one nontrivial `2x2` block mixes two unequal eigenvalues through an angle not in `(pi/2)Z`, then the inequality is strict. Thus equality in this class can occur only through signed permutations and rotations wholly inside equal-eigenvalue subspaces.

This is still a restricted basis class, hence **PARTIAL**, but it is a continuous all-spectrum theorem rather than a finite search.

## 4.1 Independent-rest strengthening of the recovered one-plane lemma

The key observation is that the proof at commit `bde4ff915784b49f3e07c365ced43388a449c6be` never uses mutual independence of the coordinates *outside* the rotated pair. It uses only:

1. the chosen pair `(Y_i,Y_j)` is an independent diagonal Gaussian pair;
2. that pair is independent of the rest of the vector;
3. after conditioning on the rest, the global top-`d` score depends on the pair only through its fixed sum and its maximum.

For completeness, if the outside squared coordinates have decreasing partial sums `B_k`, and the chosen pair has squared sum `S` and larger member `M_theta`, then pointwise

\[
\operatorname{Top}_d
=
\max\{B_d,\ B_{d-1}+M_\theta,\ B_{d-2}+S\},
\]

with invalid indices omitted. Conditional on `S` and the outside vector, this is a nondecreasing stop-loss function of `M_theta`.

For pair variances `a>= b>0`, polar coordinates give the conditional angular density

\[
f_s(\phi)\propto e^{\kappa_s\cos2\phi},
\qquad
\kappa_s=\frac{s}{4}\left(\frac1b-\frac1a\right)\ge0.
\]

After the quarter-turn symmetrization forced by the pair maximum, the weight on the circle of length `pi/2` is

\[
w_s(u)=\cosh(\kappa_s\cos2u),
\]

which is symmetric decreasing in circular distance from the eigen-axis. The conditional top-`d` profile is also symmetric decreasing. The circular layer-cake/rearrangement lemma proved in the recovered report therefore puts the maximum at zero rotation. Its Stieltjes layer-cake representation includes any selection-boundary atoms exactly; no derivative across a top-`d` kink is silently discarded.

Because the rest was conditioned upon, its internal dependence is irrelevant. Hence:

> **Independent-rest lemma.** If an unequal diagonal Gaussian pair is independent of an otherwise arbitrary random rest vector, rotating only that pair cannot increase expected retained top-`d` energy; the decrease is strict for a nontrivial angle whenever the full covariance is positive definite and `d<p`.

The zero-variance endpoint follows by the same continuity/majorization argument already archived in the one-plane proof.

## 4.2 Proof of Theorem 4.1

Write the nontrivial pair blocks of `Q` as commuting rotations

\[
Q=G_1(\theta_1)\cdots G_m(\theta_m)
\]

on pairwise disjoint supports, absorbing signs/permutations into harmless endpoint symmetries.

Start with

\[
X^{(m)}=G_1(\theta_1)\cdots G_m(\theta_m)Y.
\]

Consider the pair supporting `G_m`. No other `G_r` touches this pair. Therefore its two entries are still the original independent diagonal Gaussian eigen-pair before `G_m` is applied, and they are independent of the entire transformed rest vector produced by `G_1,\ldots,G_{m-1}`. By the independent-rest lemma,

\[
F_d(G_1\cdots G_mY)
\le
F_d(G_1\cdots G_{m-1}Y).
\]

Repeat for `G_{m-1}`, then `G_{m-2}`, and so on. This yields the chain

\[
F_d(QY)
\le F_d(G_1\cdots G_{m-1}Y)
\le\cdots\le
F_d(Y),
\]

proving the theorem.

If one of the blocks genuinely mixes unequal positive variances, the corresponding independent-rest comparison is strict on a positive-probability set of outside configurations (exactly as in the recovered one-plane strictness argument), so the full chain is strict.

## B20 — find why the obvious algorithm fails

**Outcome: exact obstruction identified.** A generic orthogonal matrix is a product of Givens rotations, but the chaining argument works only while the support graph is a matching. The first forbidden pattern is two rotations sharing a coordinate, for example

\[
G_{12}(\alpha)G_{23}(\beta).
\]

Thus “Givens rotations generate `O(p)`” does **not** glue the one-plane theorem into a universal proof.

A structural corollary is immediate:

> **Necessary mixing complexity for a counterexample.** Any counterexample basis must have, after removing signed permutations and rotations inside equal-eigenvalue subspaces, a mixing-support component involving at least three eigen-directions. No basis whose nontrivial support components all have size at most two can beat KL.

This isolates the next exact target: the smallest overlapping-Givens component rather than another disjoint two-block quadrature family.
