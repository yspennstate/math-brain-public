## 4. Parseval-frame notation and exact spherical moments

Let `P` be a rank-`r` orthogonal projection and write

\[
P=QQ^T,
\]

with row vectors `a_i\in\mathbb R^r`. Thus

\[
\sum_i a_i a_i^T=I_r.
\]

Put

\[
d_i=\|a_i\|^2=P_{ii},\qquad q_{ij}=P_{ij}^2=(a_i\cdot a_j)^2,
\]

and

\[
S_k=\sum_i d_i^k.
\]

For `U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^{r-1})`, the occupancy probabilities are

\[
p_i(U)=(a_i\cdot U)^2.
\]

Define the four off-diagonal invariants

\[
A=\sum_{i<j}d_i d_j q_{ij},
\qquad
B=\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}^2,
\tag{4.1}

\[
L=\sum_{i<j}d_i d_j(d_i+d_j)q_{ij},
\qquad
M=\sum_{i<j}(d_i+d_j)q_{ij}^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
T_{r,k}=r(r+2)\cdots(r+2k-2).

## 7. New integer-hypersimplex cumulant lemma

### Theorem 7.1

Let `x_i\in[0,1]` be finitely many numbers with integer sum

\[
\sum_i x_i=m\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Define

\[
W_x=\sum_i x_i(1-x_i),
\]

and

\[
K_x=\sum_i x_i(1-x_i)(1-2x_i).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{K_x\ge-W_x^2.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently, if `W_x>0` and

\[
\mu_x=\frac{\sum_i x_i^2(1-x_i)}{W_x},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\mu_x\le\frac{1+W_x}{2}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This extends the earlier archived small-`W` cumulant estimate to a one-sided inequality valid on every integer hypersimplex.

### 7.1 Pair merging

Write

\[
w(t)=t(1-t),\qquad k(t)=t(1-t)(1-2t).
\]

Suppose `a,b>=0` and `s=a+b<=1`. Let `R` be the contribution to `W_x` from all other coordinates. Compare the functional

\[
F_-(x)=W_x^2+K_x
\]

before and after replacing `(a,b)` by `(s,0)`. Exact algebra gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_-^{\rm before}-F_-^{\rm after}
&=2ab\left[2R+3-s-2s^2+2ab\right]\\
&=2ab\left[2R+(1-s)(2s+3)+2ab\right]\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus merging a pair whose sum is at most one cannot increase the difficulty of proving `F_-\ge0`.

Repeat this operation until every pair of positive coordinates satisfies

\[
x_i+x_j>1.
\tag{7.4}
\]

If the terminal positive support has size `n`, then summing (7.4) over all pairs gives

\[
(n-1)m>{n\choose2},
\]

hence

\[
n<2m
\tag{7.5}
\]

unless the vector is already a `0/1` vertex.

On the positive support define

\[
y_i=1-x_i.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i y_i=n-m\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
y_i+y_j<1,
\tag{7.6}
\]

and

\[
W_y=W_x,
\qquad
K_y=-K_x.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Therefore it remains to prove

\[
W_y^2-K_y\ge0
\tag{7.8}
\]

for an integer-sum vector whose positive coordinates have pairwise sums below one.

### 7.2 Terminal complement with sum one

If

\[
\sum_i y_i=1,
\]

then, writing `e_j` for the elementary symmetric polynomials,

\[
W_y=2e_2,
\qquad
K_y=6e_3.
\tag{7.9}
\]

For `n>=3`, Newton's inequality gives

\[
e_2^2
\ge
\frac{3(n-1)}{2(n-2)}e_3
\ge\frac32e_3.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Hence

\[
W_y^2-K_y
=4e_2^2-6e_3\ge0.
\tag{7.11}
\]

The cases with fewer than three positive coordinates are immediate.

### 7.3 Terminal complement with integer sum at least two

Now suppose

\[
s:=\sum_i y_i\ge2.
\]

Let

\[
M=\max_i y_i.
\]

If `M<=1/2`, then

\[
S_2(y)\le M\sum_i y_i\le\frac s2,
\]

so

\[
W_y=s-S_2(y)\ge\frac s2\ge1.
\tag{7.12}
\]

If `M>1/2`, pairwise sums below one imply every other coordinate is at most `1-M`. Thus

\[
S_2(y)
\le M^2+(1-M)(s-M).
\]

For `s>=2`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
s-1-S_2(y)
&\ge M(s+1-2M)-1\\
&\ge M(3-2M)-1\\
&=(2M-1)(1-M)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.13}
\]

Again

\[
W_y=s-S_2(y)\ge1.
\tag{7.14}
\]

Finally, pointwise

\[
y_i(1-y_i)(1-2y_i)\le y_i(1-y_i),
\]

so

\[
K_y\le W_y.
\]

Together with `W_y>=1`,

\[
W_y^2-K_y\ge W_y^2-W_y\ge0.
\tag{7.15}
\]

This proves Theorem 7.1.

Applied to the projection diagonal, (7.2) gives

\[
\boxed{W\ge2\mu-1}
\tag{7.16}
\]

whenever the right-hand side is positive.

## 13. Why the present relaxation stops at rank eight

The proof above uses the robust bounds

\[
B\le A\le\frac12(S_2-S_3)
\]

and discards the favorable positive terms `16200L+10800M` from the exact numerator (4.8). This relaxation is strong enough at `r>=9` but not uniformly at `r=4,...,8`.

Indeed, the resulting diagonal lower bound `G_r` can be negative on valid integer-hypersimplex diagonals in those ranks. This does **not** give a counterexample to the stochastic facet, because the dropped `L,M` terms and the sharper relationship among `A,B,L,M` for a genuine Parseval frame can compensate the negative diagonal relaxation.

Thus the next proof obligation is genuinely geometric, not merely a stronger scalar inequality on the projection diagonal.

Promising exact routes are:

- retain the edgewise combination
  \[
  -2160(r+8)A-720(r+8)B+16200L+10800M
  \]
  instead of bounding its negative and positive pieces separately;
- use both principal-minor constraints
  \[
  q_{ij}\le d_id_j,
  \qquad
  q_{ij}\le(1-d_i)(1-d_j),
  \]
  together with `sum_{j ne i}q_{ij}=d_i(1-d_i)`;
- exploit exterior-square/Plücker relations at the finite ranks `4,...,8`;
- search for a sum-of-squares decomposition of the exact coordinate gap in frame invariants, rather than of the diagonal relaxation.

A counterexample to the stronger partition-stochastic-order facet would not by itself refute the original Mallat–Zeitouni conjecture; it would only show that this sufficient route is too strong. A certified negative value of the actual `M_5` gap, however, would refute coefficientwise positive-spike optimality at quintic order and would be materially stronger evidence.
