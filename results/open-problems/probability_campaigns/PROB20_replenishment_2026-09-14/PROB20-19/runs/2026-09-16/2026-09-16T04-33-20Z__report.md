# PROB20-19 — Full nonlinear Gaussian PCA conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.** The universal Mallat–Zeitouni/Bandeira conjecture remains open. This run proves a new exact arbitrary-spectrum local theorem for the full orthogonal group: for every positive-definite covariance with simple spectrum, a covariance eigenbasis is a **nondegenerate strict local maximizer** of the nonlinear retained top-`d` energy, simultaneously against arbitrary infinitesimal combinations of Givens rotations. More precisely, the Hessian at an eigenbasis is diagonal in the natural two-plane rotation coordinates and is strictly negative in every coordinate. With repeated eigenvalues, the only Hessian-null directions are rotations internal to equal-eigenvalue eigenspaces.

This strengthens the previous run's global one-plane theorem. The previous result ruled out improving a KL basis by one isolated Givens rotation. The present result rules out an improvement by **any sufficiently small simultaneous multi-plane perturbation** when the spectrum is simple.

It still does not prove the global conjecture: a remote basis could in principle have larger objective, and the argument does not give a Jacobi iteration that monotonically diagonalizes an arbitrary correlated basis.

No numerical optimization is used as a proof.

## 3. New theorem: full-tangent strict local optimality

Assume first that

\[
\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p),
\qquad \lambda_i>0.
\]

For `A` skew-symmetric, write

\[
U(t)=e^{tA}.
\]

Let

\[
E^{ij}=e_i e_j^T-e_j e_i^T,
\qquad i<j,
\]

so every tangent vector is uniquely

\[
A=\sum_{i<j} a_{ij}E^{ij}.
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — diagonal Hessian and local KL maximum

For every `1<d<p`, `F_Sigma` is `C^\infty` in a neighborhood of the identity and

\[
DF_\Sigma(I)=0.
\]

Its Hessian on the tangent space of `O(p)` has the exact diagonal form

\[
\boxed{
D^2F_\Sigma(I)[A,A]
=-\sum_{i<j} c_{ij}\,a_{ij}^2,
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
c_{ij}\ge0,
\qquad
c_{ij}=0 \iff \lambda_i=\lambda_j,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and therefore `c_ij>0` whenever `lambda_i != lambda_j`.

Consequences:

1. If the spectrum is simple, the Hessian is negative definite on the whole tangent space. Hence every eigenbasis is a **nondegenerate strict local maximizer** of `F_Sigma` on `O(p)`.
2. With repeated eigenvalues, the Hessian is strictly negative in every direction that mixes distinct eigenspaces and vanishes exactly on infinitesimal rotations internal to equal-eigenvalue blocks. Those null directions are genuine covariance symmetries.
3. For a fixed positive-definite simple spectrum, any counterexample to the global Mallat–Zeitouni conjecture must lie at positive distance from every eigenbasis; it cannot bifurcate infinitesimally from a KL basis.

The rest of the report proves this theorem.

## 4. Smoothness despite the nonsmooth `Top_d`

Although `Top_d` is nonsmooth on tie surfaces, the Gaussian expectation is smooth as a function of a positive-definite covariance.

Put

\[
f(x)=\operatorname{Top}_d(x_1^2,\ldots,x_p^2).
\]

For `C` positive definite,

\[
\mathcal F(C)=\int_{\mathbb R^p} f(x)\,\varphi_C(x)\,dx,
\]

where `varphi_C` is the centered Gaussian density with covariance `C`. Since

\[
0\le f(x)\le \|x\|^2,
\]

and every covariance derivative of `varphi_C` is a polynomial in `x` times a Gaussian density, all derivatives are locally dominated on compact subsets of the positive-definite cone by an integrable function of the form

\[
C_m(1+\|x\|^{m+2})e^{-c\|x\|^2}.
\]

Differentiation under the integral is therefore valid to every order. Thus `mathcal F` is `C^\infty` on the positive-definite cone, and

\[
F_\Sigma(U)=\mathcal F(U\Sigma U^T)
\]

is `C^\infty` on `O(p)`.

This removes any concern that switching of the selected top-`d` coordinates might invalidate a Hessian argument.

## 5. Sign symmetry forces the Hessian to be diagonal in Givens coordinates

Let `D=diag(epsilon_1,...,epsilon_p)` with each `epsilon_i in {+1,-1}`.

Two independent symmetries hold:

- left multiplication by `D` only changes output signs, hence leaves all output squares unchanged;
- right multiplication by `D` changes signs of the independent eigen-coordinates, hence preserves their joint law.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
F_\Sigma(DUD)=F_\Sigma(U)
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

for every diagonal sign matrix `D`.

At `U=I`, conjugation fixes the base point and acts on tangent basis vectors by

\[
D E^{ij} D=(\epsilon_i\epsilon_j)E^{ij}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### 5.1 Gradient vanishes

Let `L(A)=DF_Sigma(I)[A]`. Invariance gives

\[
L(A)=L(DAD).
\]

For a fixed pair `(i,j)`, choose `D` with `epsilon_i epsilon_j=-1`. Then

\[
L(E^{ij})=-L(E^{ij}),
\]

so

\[
L(E^{ij})=0.
\]

Since these vectors span the tangent space,

\[
\boxed{DF_\Sigma(I)=0.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### 5.2 Mixed Hessian entries vanish

Let `H(A,B)=D^2F_Sigma(I)[A,B]`. Again

\[
H(A,B)=H(DAD,DBD).
\]

For two distinct unordered pairs `{i,j}!={k,l}`, choose a sign pattern such that

\[
(\epsilon_i\epsilon_j)(\epsilon_k\epsilon_l)=-1.
\]

This is always possible because the symmetric difference of two distinct edges is nonempty. Hence

\[
H(E^{ij},E^{kl})=-H(E^{ij},E^{kl}),
\]

which forces

\[
\boxed{
H(E^{ij},E^{kl})=0
\quad\text{for }\{i,j\}\ne\{k,l\}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus no dangerous second-order cross term between simultaneous Givens rotations exists at an eigenbasis. It remains only to determine the sign of each diagonal entry.

## 6. Exact conditional curvature for one pair

Fix a pair `(i,j)` and abbreviate

\[
a=\lambda_i,
\qquad b=\lambda_j.
\]

Assume `a>b>0`; the case `b>a` is the same after relabeling. Let

\[
\phi_{ij}(\theta)=F_\Sigma(e^{\theta E^{ij}}).
\]

The previous checkpoint proved globally that `phi_ij(theta)<=phi_ij(0)`. Here we need the stronger local statement

\[
\phi_{ij}''(0)<0.
\]

### 6.1 Stop-loss reduction

Condition on all coordinates outside the chosen pair and on

\[
S=Y_i^2+Y_j^2=s.
\]

Let `B_m` be the sum of the `m` largest squared outside coordinates. Exactly as in the previous checkpoint, the top-`d` retained energy is

\[
\operatorname{Top}_d
=
\max\{B_d,\ B_{d-1}+M_\theta,\ B_{d-2}+s\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

with invalid constant terms omitted, where `M_theta` is the larger squared coordinate of the rotated pair.

Hence, for a constant `C` and threshold `tau` determined by `s` and the outside coordinates,

\[
\operatorname{Top}_d
=C+(M_\theta-\tau)_+.
\tag{6.2}
\]

### 6.2 Folded angular density

Write

\[
(Y_i,Y_j)=\sqrt s(\cos\Phi,\sin\Phi).
\]

Conditioned on `S=s`, the angular density is proportional to

\[
\exp(\kappa_s\cos2\phi),
\qquad
\kappa_s=\frac{s}{4}\left(\frac1b-\frac1a\right)>0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The pair maximum after rotating by `theta` is

\[
M_\theta
=
\frac{s}{2}\left(1+|\cos2(\Phi-\theta)|\right).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Because the hinge in (6.2) has period `pi/2`, fold the angular density onto the fundamental circle

\[
-\frac\pi4\le u\le\frac\pi4.
\]

After adding the four quarter-turns, the normalized folded density is

\[
q_{s}(u)
=
\frac{\cosh(\kappa_s\cos2u)}{Z_s},
\qquad
Z_s=\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4}\cosh(\kappa_s\cos2u)\,du.
\tag{6.5}
\]

On this fundamental interval define

\[
h_{s,\tau}(u)
=
\left[
\frac{s}{2}(1+\cos2u)-\tau
\right]_+.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The conditional orientation-sensitive expectation is a circular convolution of `h` and a shift of `q`. Its second derivative at alignment is therefore

\[
J_{s,\tau}''(0)
=
\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4}h_{s,\tau}(u)q_s''(u)\,du.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Since `h` is absolutely continuous and periodic on the folded circle, integration by parts gives

\[
J_{s,\tau}''(0)
=-\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4}h_{s,\tau}'(u)q_s'(u)\,du.
\tag{6.8}
\]

For `0<u<pi/4`, almost everywhere,

\[
h_{s,\tau}'(u)
=-s\sin2u\;\mathbf 1\left\{\frac{s}{2}(1+\cos2u)>\tau\right\},
\tag{6.9}
\]

while

\[
q_s'(u)
=-\frac{2\kappa_s\sin2u\,\sinh(\kappa_s\cos2u)}{Z_s}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Both derivatives have the same sign on each side of zero. Using evenness,

\[
\boxed{
J_{s,\tau}''(0)
=-\frac{4s\kappa_s}{Z_s}
\int_0^{\pi/4}
\sin^2(2u)\,
\sinh(\kappa_s\cos2u)\,
\mathbf 1\left\{\frac{s}{2}(1+\cos2u)>\tau\right\}
\,du.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Therefore

\[
J_{s,\tau}''(0)\le0,
\]

and it is strictly negative exactly when the hinge is active on a set of positive angular measure, in particular whenever

\[
\tau<s.
\tag{6.12}
\]

## 7. The strict-curvature event has positive probability

It remains to rule out the possibility that `tau>=s` almost surely.

Let the outside squared coordinates, in decreasing order, be

\[
z_1\ge z_2\ge\cdots\ge z_{p-2}>0
\]

almost surely; positivity follows from positive definiteness.

Because `1<d<p`, `B_{d-1}` is always valid.

### Case 1: `d<=p-2`

Then

\[
B_d=B_{d-1}+z_d,
\qquad
B_{d-1}=B_{d-2}+z_{d-1}.
\]

From the definition of `C` and `tau`,

\[
\tau
=\max\{z_d,\ s-z_{d-1}\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Since `z_{d-1}>0`,

\[
\tau<s
\iff
z_d<s.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The pair radius `S` is independent of the outside coordinates and has unbounded support, while `z_d<infinity` almost surely. Hence

\[
\mathbb P(S>z_d)>0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Case 2: `d=p-1`

The term `B_d` is invalid, and

\[
\tau=s-z_{p-2}<s
\tag{7.4}
\]

almost surely.

Thus for every `1<d<p`, if `a!=b`, the conditional curvature (6.11) is strictly negative on an event of positive probability. Averaging yields

\[
\boxed{
\phi_{ij}''(0)<0
\quad\text{whenever }\lambda_i\ne\lambda_j.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

If `lambda_i=lambda_j`, the two-dimensional Gaussian block is isotropic, so `phi_ij` is constant and

\[
\phi_{ij}''(0)=0.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Define

\[
c_{ij}:=-\phi_{ij}''(0).
\]

Then (3.2) follows.

## 8. Completion of the Hessian theorem

By Section 5, the Hessian has no mixed entries in the basis `{E^{ij}}`. By Section 7,

\[
H(E^{ij},E^{ij})=-c_{ij},
\qquad
c_{ij}>0 \text{ iff }\lambda_i\ne\lambda_j.
\]

Therefore for

\[
A=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}E^{ij},
\]

\[
D^2F_\Sigma(I)[A,A]
=
-\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}a_{ij}^2.
\]

If the spectrum is simple, this quadratic form is negative definite. Since `F_Sigma` is `C^2` and has vanishing gradient at `I`, the ordinary second-derivative test on the smooth manifold `O(p)` gives a strict local maximum at `I`.

Output sign changes and coordinate permutations preserve `Top_d`, so the same conclusion holds at every signed/permuted eigenbasis.

For repeated eigenvalues, the kernel consists exactly of rotations internal to equal-eigenvalue blocks. These are genuine symmetries because such rotations commute with `Sigma` and do not change the Gaussian law. Every tangent direction with any component mixing distinct eigenspaces has strictly negative second variation.

## 9. What this does and does not settle

### Newly proved in this run

- For every positive-definite covariance and every `1<d<p`, a KL basis is a stationary point of the full nonlinear objective on `O(p)`.
- The Hessian at a KL basis is exactly diagonal in Givens-rotation coordinates.
- Each diagonal curvature is nonpositive, and is strictly negative precisely for a pair of unequal eigenvalues.
- Therefore a simple-spectrum KL basis is a nondegenerate strict local maximizer against **arbitrary simultaneous infinitesimal rotations**, not merely against one coordinate plane at a time.

### Previously proved and reused

- Exact global one-plane optimality from `runs/2026-09-16/2026-09-16T03-38-54Z__report.md` at commit `bde4ff915784b49f3e07c365ced43388a449c6be`.
- Coordinate maximality of positive-spike coefficients `M_2,M_3,M_4,M_5` for every projection rank from earlier archived checkpoints.

### Still open

- Global optimality over all `U in O(p)` for arbitrary covariance spectra.
- A monotone Jacobi theorem starting from an arbitrary correlated basis.
- Positive-spike coefficients `M_k` for `k>=6` if the coefficientwise route is resumed.
- A certified counterexample, if the conjecture is false.

The present theorem means that a counterexample for a simple positive-definite spectrum, if one exists, must be genuinely nonlocal: it cannot arise as an arbitrarily small perturbation of a covariance eigenbasis.

## 10. Failed shortcut and why it fails

A tempting argument is: every orthogonal matrix is a product of Givens rotations, and the previous checkpoint shows each Givens rotation away from the eigenbasis is unfavorable, so iterate the one-plane theorem.

This is invalid. After the first nontrivial rotation, the working covariance is no longer diagonal. The pair being rotated can then be correlated with the untouched coordinates, and the conditional angular density used in the one-plane proof is no longer the isolated `exp(kappa cos 2phi)` law.

The new Hessian theorem avoids this circularity only infinitesimally, where sign symmetries at the diagonal covariance force all mixed second-order terms to vanish. It does not establish monotonicity of finite Jacobi steps away from the diagonal point.

## 11. Next attack

The sharp next proof obligation is a **Jacobi/block extension** of the one-plane theorem.

Let a general covariance be partitioned as

\[
C=
\begin{pmatrix}
A & R\\
R^T & B
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where `A` is a `2x2` covariance block to be diagonalized by a Givens rotation and `R` contains cross-covariances from that pair to the remaining coordinates. The desired step would compare the exact top-`d` objective before and after diagonalizing `A` while keeping the rest of the basis fixed.

The current theorem says that the first nontrivial term in `R` cannot create an improving direction at `R=0`: the full Hessian is already negative in every normal direction. The next question is whether one can control the finite-`R` correction, perhaps by conditioning on the outside Gaussian vector. Conditionally, the pair is Gaussian with a mean linear in the outside vector and a Schur-complement covariance. The central obstacle is therefore a **noncentral two-dimensional stop-loss rearrangement inequality**.

A precise next subproblem is:

> For a two-dimensional Gaussian `X~N(mu,A)` and fixed `s`/outside threshold data, determine whether diagonalizing `A` in the coordinate system that diagonalizes its covariance maximizes the expected hinge of `max(X_1^2,X_2^2)` uniformly in the noncentral mean `mu`, or characterize exactly the means for which it fails.

A positive theorem stable under the conditional means generated by `R` could enable a genuine Jacobi iteration. A failure would identify the mechanism required for a global counterexample.
