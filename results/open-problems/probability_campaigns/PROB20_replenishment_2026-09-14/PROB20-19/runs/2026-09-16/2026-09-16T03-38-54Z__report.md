# PROB20-19 — Full nonlinear Gaussian PCA conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.** The universal Mallat–Zeitouni/Bandeira conjecture remains open. This run proves a new exact arbitrary-spectrum, finite-strength theorem: starting from a covariance eigenbasis, **any single Givens rotation mixing two eigenvectors cannot improve the expected retained top-`d` energy**. Thus a KL basis is globally optimal along every coordinate two-plane orbit through the eigenbasis, for every `1<=d<p` and every covariance spectrum.

This does **not** imply the full conjecture, because a product of Givens rotations leaves the eigenbasis after the first factor, and the proof below uses the independence/block-diagonal structure present at the eigenbasis. No claim of literature novelty is made; a bounded source search did not locate this exact one-plane statement.

No numerical optimization is used as a proof.

## 3. New theorem: exact one-plane rotation optimality

Let

\[
Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_p),\qquad Y_k\sim N(0,\lambda_k)
\]

have independent coordinates, with `lambda_k>=0`. Fix two indices `i!=j`. Let `G_{ij}(theta)` be the Givens rotation by angle `theta` in the `(i,j)` coordinate plane, leaving all other coordinates fixed. Define

\[
\mathcal R_d(\theta)
=
\mathbb E\,\operatorname{Top}_d\left(
(G_{ij}(\theta)Y)_1^2,\ldots,(G_{ij}(\theta)Y)_p^2
\right).
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — one-plane KL optimality

For every `1<=d<p`, every covariance spectrum, every pair `i!=j`, and every real `theta`,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_d(\theta)\le \mathcal R_d(0).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since `theta=pi/2` only swaps/sign-flips the two eigen-coordinates, equality also holds at multiples of `pi/2`. If `lambda_i=lambda_j`, the entire two-dimensional covariance block is isotropic and equality holds for every `theta`.

For positive definite covariance, `lambda_i!=lambda_j`, and `theta` not a multiple of `pi/2`, the inequality is strict.

This is an exact finite-strength statement for an arbitrary spectrum; it is not a perturbative or low-order result.

## 4. Deterministic reduction of top-`d` to a stop-loss function of the pair maximum

Relabel the chosen pair as coordinates `1,2`. Put

\[
X=Y_1^2,\qquad Y=Y_2^2,
\qquad S=X+Y.
\]

After a Givens rotation, write the two squared coordinates as `X_theta,Y_theta`, so

\[
X_\theta+Y_\theta=S
\]

pointwise. Set

\[
M_\theta=\max(X_\theta,Y_\theta).
\]

Condition on all other squared coordinates. Let

\[
B_m=\text{sum of the }m\text{ largest among }Y_3^2,\ldots,Y_p^2,
\]

with `B_0=0`, and omit an expression whenever its index is outside `0,...,p-2`.

The global top-`d` sum can select zero, one, or two coordinates from the chosen pair. Therefore exactly

\[
\operatorname{Top}_d
=
\max\{B_d,\ B_{d-1}+M_\theta,\ B_{d-2}+S\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

with invalid terms omitted.

Since `1<=d<p`, the middle term is always valid. Let

\[
C=\max\{B_d,\ B_{d-2}+S\}
\]

over the valid constant terms, and define

\[
\tau=C-B_{d-1}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Top}_d
=C+(M_\theta-\tau)_+.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus, conditional on `S` and on the other coordinates, the only orientation-sensitive part is a nondecreasing stop-loss function of the pair maximum.

## 5. Polar form of the pair

First assume

\[
a:=\lambda_1>0,\qquad b:=\lambda_2>0.
\]

Write the original pair in polar coordinates

\[
(Y_1,Y_2)=\sqrt S(\cos\Phi,\sin\Phi).
\]

After rotating the coordinate axes by `theta`,

\[
\boxed{
M_\theta
=
\frac S2\left(1+\left|\cos 2(\Phi-\theta)\right|\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence the orientation-sensitive term in (4.2) is

\[
H_{S,\tau}(\Phi-\theta)
:=
\left[
\frac S2\left(1+|\cos 2(\Phi-\theta)|\right)-\tau
\right]_+.
\tag{5.2}
\]

As a function of its angular argument, `H_{S,tau}` has period `pi/2`, is even, and on `[0,pi/4]` is nonincreasing.

## 6. Exact conditional angular density

The joint density of `(Y_1,Y_2)` is proportional to

\[
\exp\left[-\frac12\left(\frac{y_1^2}{a}+\frac{y_2^2}{b}\right)\right].
\]

Conditioning on `S=s` gives an angular density

\[
f_s(\phi)
\propto
\exp\left[-\frac s4\left(\frac1a-\frac1b\right)\cos2\phi\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]

After swapping labels if necessary, assume `a>=b` and set

\[
\kappa_s=\frac s4\left(\frac1b-\frac1a\right)\ge0.
\]

Then

\[
f_s(\phi)\propto e^{\kappa_s\cos2\phi}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Because `H_{S,tau}` is `pi/2`-periodic, group the four quarter-turns. On one fundamental circle of length

\[
L=\frac\pi2,
\]

the relevant symmetrized density is

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_s(u)
&:=\sum_{m=0}^3 f_s(u+mL)\\
&\propto
2e^{\kappa_s\cos2u}+2e^{-\kappa_s\cos2u}\\
&=
\boxed{4\cosh(\kappa_s\cos2u)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Viewed on the circle `R/LZ`, `G_s` is even and symmetric decreasing in circular distance from `0`: on `0<=u<=L/2=pi/4`, `cos2u` decreases from `1` to `0`, and `cosh` is increasing on the nonnegative axis.

The same is true of `H_{s,tau}(u)` because it is a nondecreasing function of `|cos2u|`.

## 7. Self-contained circular rearrangement lemma

### Lemma 7.1

Let `F,H` be nonnegative functions on a circle of length `L`, both symmetric decreasing about `0`. Then for every shift `theta`,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^L F(u)H(u-\theta)\,du
\le
\int_0^L F(u)H(u)\,du.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof

Use the layer-cake representations

\[
F(u)=\int_0^\infty 1_{\{F>t\}}(u)\,dt,
\qquad
H(u)=\int_0^\infty 1_{\{H>s\}}(u)\,ds.
\]

Because `F,H` are symmetric decreasing, every superlevel set is a centered arc on the circle (up to null endpoint choices). Therefore

\[
\int F(u)H(u-\theta)du
=
\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
\left|
A_t\cap(B_s+\theta)
\right|\,ds\,dt,
\]

where `A_t,B_s` are centered arcs. For two arcs of fixed lengths on a circle, their intersection length is maximized when their centers coincide. Hence

\[
|A_t\cap(B_s+\theta)|
\le
|A_t\cap B_s|
\]

for every `s,t`. Integrating gives (7.1). QED.

This is the only rearrangement input required below.

## 8. Proof of Theorem 3.1

Condition on

1. `S=s`, and
2. all squared coordinates outside the chosen pair.

By (4.2), the only angle-dependent conditional contribution is

\[
H_{s,\tau}(\Phi-\theta).
\]

Using the `pi/2` periodicity and (6.3), its conditional expectation is proportional to

\[
\int_0^L G_s(u)H_{s,\tau}(u-\theta)\,du.
\]

Both factors are symmetric decreasing on the circle of length `L`. Lemma 7.1 therefore gives

\[
\mathbb E\left[H_{s,\tau}(\Phi-\theta)\mid S=s,\text{others}\right]
\le
\mathbb E\left[H_{s,\tau}(\Phi)\mid S=s,\text{others}\right].
\tag{8.1}
\]

The constant term `C` in (4.2) is independent of `theta`. Thus the entire conditional expected top-`d` retained energy is maximal at `theta=0` modulo `pi/2`.

Finally integrate over `S` and all other coordinates. This proves

\[
\mathcal R_d(\theta)\le\mathcal R_d(0).
\]

If one of `a,b` is zero, the result follows either by taking `b downarrow 0` in the proved positive-variance case (the retained-energy functional is continuous under `L^2` convergence of the Gaussian pair), or directly: the unrotated squared pair is `(S,0)`, which majorizes every split `(S\cos^2\theta,S\sin^2\theta)` pointwise, while `Top_d` is symmetric convex/Schur-convex in the vector of squared coordinates.

This completes the proof. QED.

## 9. Strictness in the positive-definite unequal-variance case

Assume all covariance eigenvalues are positive, `a!=b`, and `theta` is not a multiple of `pi/2`.

For every `s>0`, `kappa_s>0`, so `G_s` is strictly decreasing on `(0,pi/4)`. Because `1<=d<p`, there is a positive-probability set of outside-coordinate configurations for which exactly one member of the chosen pair is selection-relevant; equivalently the stop-loss profile `H_{s,tau}` is nonconstant. For such configurations, the layer-cake proof is strict for a nontrivial shift. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_d(\theta)<\mathcal R_d(0)
}
\]

for nontrivial `theta` modulo `pi/2`.

If `a=b`, `kappa_s=0`, the symmetrized angular density is constant and every rotation within that equal-eigenvalue plane has the same law, as required.

## 10. Interpretation and relation to the cumulative coefficient program

This theorem is qualitatively different from the archived `M_2,M_3,M_4,M_5` results.

The earlier program fixes a positive two-eigenvalue spike family and proves low-order Taylor coefficients are KL-aligned. The theorem here:

- uses the **full nonlinear top-`d` objective**, not a series coefficient;
- allows an **arbitrary covariance spectrum**;
- is **finite-strength/exact**;
- controls an arbitrary angle in one eigen-coordinate plane, not only a local derivative.

It proves that no counterexample to Mallat–Zeitouni can be obtained by taking an eigenbasis and rotating only one pair of eigenvectors.

However it does **not** prove the full conjecture. A generic orthogonal matrix is a product of Givens rotations, but after the first nontrivial rotation the covariance is no longer diagonal in the working coordinate system. The conditional angular law then couples to the remaining coordinates, and the proof above cannot simply be iterated. Treating “Givens rotations generate `O(p)`” as a chaining argument would therefore be circular/invalid.

## 12. Failed/invalid shortcut explicitly excluded

It is tempting to combine Theorem 3.1 with a Givens factorization

\[
U=G_1G_2\cdots G_m
\]

and conclude global optimality. This is not valid: Theorem 3.1 compares `I` to a one-plane rotation while the covariance is diagonal. After applying `G_1`, the next pair generally is correlated with the current coordinates and the conditional angular density no longer has the isolated form (6.2). A valid global proof needs either:

- a rearrangement theorem stable under those induced correlations, or
- a monotone diagonalization procedure that proves each *reverse* Jacobi step improves the objective in a non-diagonal covariance.

Neither is proved here.
