## 3. Signed-permutation equivariance

Let `Q` be any signed permutation matrix. Since `f_d(Qx)=f_d(x)`,

\[
\boxed{F_d(QCQ^T)=F_d(C).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Differentiating this covariance equivariance, or using the explicit Gaussian-gradient formula from the preceding checkpoint, gives

\[
\boxed{J(QCQ^T)=QJ(C)Q^T,}
\qquad
\boxed{K(QCQ^T)=QK(C)Q^T.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the stabilizer of a covariance forces the same symmetries on its nonlinear selection-gradient operator.

A useful abstract form is:

### Lemma 3.1 — stabilizer principle

If a subgroup `H` of signed permutations satisfies

\[
QCQ^T=C\qquad(Q\in H),
\]

then

\[
QJ(C)Q^T=J(C)\qquad(Q\in H).
\]

Hence, whenever the symmetric matrices fixed by `H` commute with `C`, the covariance is stationary on its orthogonal orbit.

This is a direct consequence of (3.2). The balanced-spike family below can be proved either from this lemma or directly from the pairwise gradient formula.

## 4. Canonical balanced-support rank-one spikes

Fix

\[
a>0,\qquad c>0,
\]

and the rank-one spike spectrum

\[
(a+c,a,\ldots,a).
\]

For any support `S subset {1,...,p}` with `|S|=m` and signs `sigma_i in {+1,-1}`, define

\[
u_{S,\sigma}
=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{i\in S}\sigma_i e_i,
\qquad
C_{S,\sigma}
=aI+c\,u_{S,\sigma}u_{S,\sigma}^T.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `m=1`, these are exactly coordinate/KL covariances. For every `m>=2`, they are non-diagonal in the coordinate basis and hence non-KL.

### Theorem 4.1 — every balanced-support spike is stationary

For every `m=1,...,p`, every support `S`, and every sign pattern `sigma`,

\[
\boxed{K(C_{S,\sigma})=0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{[C_{S,\sigma},M(C_{S,\sigma})]=0.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

So the exact coupled stationarity equation has non-KL solutions for every support size `m>=2`.

### Proof

By signed-coordinate conjugation it suffices to take all active signs positive and `S={1,...,m}`. Use the exact pair-gradient identity from the previous checkpoint,

\[
K_{ij}(C)=2\mathbb E[X_iX_j(I_i-I_j)],
\tag{4.4}
\]

where `I_k` is the indicator that `X_k^2` belongs to the top `d` squares.

There are three cases.

**Case 1: `i,j in S`.** The law of `X` is invariant under swapping coordinates `i,j`. Under that swap, `X_iX_j` is unchanged while `I_i-I_j` changes sign. Hence the expectation in (4.4) is zero.

**Case 2: `i,j notin S`.** The same swap symmetry holds on the isotropic background block, so again `K_{ij}=0`.

**Case 3: `i in S`, `j notin S`.** Coordinate `X_j` is an independent centered `N(0,a)` variable, independent of all active spike coordinates and all other background coordinates. The selection indicators depend on `X_j` only through `X_j^2`. Therefore

\[
X_iX_j(I_i-I_j)
\]

is odd in `X_j`, and its expectation is zero.

Thus every off-diagonal entry of `K` vanishes. Since `K` is skew-symmetric, `K=0`.

The same conclusion can be read from the stabilizer principle: permutations within `S`, permutations outside `S`, and independent sign flips outside `S` force `J(C)` to be block diagonal, with its active block of the form `alpha I + beta 11^T`, which commutes with the active block of `C`.

## 5. Exact landscape classification for positive one-spike spectra

The archived positive one-spike theorem proves more than KL maximality. Write a general spike direction `u` through its squared coordinates

\[
y(u)=(u_1^2,\ldots,u_p^2)\in\Delta_{p-1}.
\]

For `c>0`, the objective

\[
\mathcal G(y)
:=F_d(aI+c\,uu^T)
\]

is a **strictly Schur-convex symmetric function of `y`**. This follows from the archived conditional shifted-Gaussian theorem: after conditioning on the scalar spike Gaussian, every genuine two-coordinate equalization strictly lowers the conditional objective on a positive-probability set, and integrating preserves strictness.

For the balanced-support vector, define

\[
y^{(m)}=
(\underbrace{1/m,\ldots,1/m}_{m\text{ entries}},0,\ldots,0).
\tag{5.1}
\]

These form the strict majorization chain

\[
y^{(1)}\succ y^{(2)}\succ\cdots\succ y^{(p)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
F_d(C^{(1)})>
F_d(C^{(2)})>
\cdots>
F_d(C^{(p)})
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

for every `a>0`, `c>0`, and `1<=d<p`, where `C^{(m)}` denotes any balanced-support covariance of support size `m`.

This gives a complete landscape classification of these canonical stationary points.

### 5.1 `m=1`: strict global maxima

Every simplex point `y` is majorized by a vertex `y^{(1)}`. Strict Schur-convexity therefore gives

\[
\mathcal G(y)\le \mathcal G(y^{(1)}),
\]

with equality only at a coordinate spike up to signed permutation. Thus `m=1` is exactly the KL/global-maximal class, recovering the archived positive one-spike theorem.

### 5.2 `m=p`: strict global minima

The uniform vector

\[
y^{(p)}=(1/p,\ldots,1/p)
\]

is majorized by every point of the simplex. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G(y^{(p)})<\mathcal G(y)
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

for every nonuniform `y`. Therefore the fully spread compound-symmetric spike

\[
C=aI+\frac cp\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T
\tag{5.5}
\]

is a **strict global minimum** of the nonlinear retained-energy objective on the rank-one spike orbit, even though it exactly satisfies the coupled commutator stationarity equation.

By signed conjugation there are `2^{p-1}` projectively distinct equal-magnitude sign patterns with the same strict-minimum value.

### 5.3 `1<m<p`: genuine saddles

Fix `1<m<p`. There are arbitrarily nearby spike directions with both larger and smaller objective.

For an ascent perturbation, choose small `epsilon>0` and make two active weights less equal:

\[
y^+
=\left(
\frac1m+\epsilon,
\frac1m-\epsilon,
\frac1m,\ldots,\frac1m,
0,\ldots,0
\right).
\]

Then

\[
y^+\succ y^{(m)},
\]

strictly, so

\[
\mathcal G(y^+)>\mathcal G(y^{(m)}).
\]

For a descent perturbation, move a small mass from one active coordinate to one inactive coordinate:

\[
y^-
=\left(
\frac1m-\epsilon,
\frac1m,\ldots,\frac1m,
\epsilon,0,\ldots,0
\right),
\qquad 0<\epsilon<\frac1{2m}.
\]

After sorting,

\[
y^{(m)}\succ y^-,
\]

strictly, hence

\[
\mathcal G(y^{(m)})>\mathcal G(y^-).
\]

Taking componentwise square roots gives actual unit spike directions converging to the balanced-support point as `epsilon -> 0`. Therefore every canonical stationary point with

\[
1<m<p
\]

is a genuine saddle on the orthogonal orbit.

## 6. Consequence for the commutator/double-bracket program

The previous checkpoint proposed classifying or ruling out non-KL solutions of

\[
[C,M(C)]=0.
\]

Theorem 4.1 shows that the literal statement “all stationary points are KL” is false, already on the completely solved positive rank-one spike orbit.

The corrected landscape target is:

> **Non-KL maximum-exclusion problem.** Prove that every non-KL solution of `[C,M(C)]=0` has a direction of strictly positive second or higher variation, so it cannot be a local maximum.

The positive rank-one orbit now provides an exact model of the desired picture:

- KL points are the strict global maxima;
- fully balanced points are strict global minima;
- partially balanced support points are saddles;
- all of these classes satisfy the same exact commutator stationarity equation.

This also sharpens the interpretation of the monotone double-bracket flow

\[
\dot C=[[J(C),C],C].
\]

The flow cannot decrease `F_d`, but it can be exactly stationary at a non-KL strict minimum or saddle if initialized there. Thus convergence merely to the stationary set is insufficient; any global proof through the flow needs a landscape/instability theorem for non-KL equilibria.

## 7. Extension to negative one-spike spectra: proved stationarity, open landscape

The stationarity proof in Section 4 is purely symmetry-based and does not use `c>0`. Therefore it extends verbatim to

\[
C=aI+c\,uu^T,
\qquad a>0,\qquad a+c>0,
\]

including `c<0`.

Hence every balanced-support direction is also an exact stationary point for a one-*small*-eigenvalue spectrum.

What does **not** extend is the Schur-convex landscape classification, because the archived positive one-spike proof uses the representation

\[
X=\sqrt a\,Z+\sqrt c\,G u,
\]

which requires `c>=0`.

This exposes a concrete unresolved family that is structurally much smaller than the full conjecture:

> For `c<0`, classify the balanced-support stationary points `y^{(m)}` as maxima, minima, or saddles, and compare their values with the KL point `m=1`.

A non-KL local/global maximum in this family would be a serious counterexample candidate. Failure to find one, together with a proof of saddle/minimum structure, would strengthen the maximum-exclusion route.

No numerical evidence is promoted to a theorem here.
