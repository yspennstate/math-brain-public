## 5. New hypersimplex theorem

### Theorem 5.1

For every finite vector `x=(x_i)` satisfying

\[
0\le x_i\le1,
\qquad
\sum_i x_i=3,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\sum_i\phi(x_i)\ge0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equality occurs at the coordinate extreme `(1,1,1,0,...)`; for the rank-three application this is the only equality configuration relevant after the reduction below.

### 5.1 Pair-merging inequality

First prove that whenever

\[
a,b\ge0,\qquad a+b\le1,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\phi(a)+\phi(b)\ge\phi(a+b).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Set

\[
s=a+b,\qquad p=ab.
\]

Exact factorization gives

\[
\phi(a)+\phi(b)-\phi(s)
=-10ab\,B(s,p),
\tag{5.3}
\]

where

\[
B(s,p)
=141s^3-106s^2+9s-48+(53-141s)p.
\tag{5.4}
\]

It remains to show `B<=0` for `0<=s<=1` and `0<=p<=s^2/4`.

If

\[
s\ge\frac{53}{141},
\]

then the coefficient of `p` is nonpositive, so

\[
B(s,p)
\le141s^3-106s^2+9s-48.
\]

Because `s^3<=s^2` on `[0,1]`,

\[
B(s,p)
\le35s^2+9s-48
\le35+9-48=-4<0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

If

\[
0\le s\le\frac{53}{141},
\]

then the coefficient of `p` is nonnegative and `p<=s^2/4`, hence

\[
4B(s,p)
\le423s^3-371s^2+36s-192.
\]

Since `s<=53/141`,

\[
423s^3
\le159s^2,
\]

so

\[
4B(s,p)
\le-212s^2+36s-192<0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This proves (5.2).

### 5.2 Reduction to at most five positive coordinates

Starting from any feasible vector `x`, whenever two positive coordinates satisfy `a+b<=1`, replace them by `(a+b,0)`.

This preserves the total sum and the box constraints. By (5.2), the replacement does not increase `sum phi(x_i)`. Repeating finitely many times yields a terminal vector with `n` positive coordinates such that every positive pair satisfies

\[
x_i+x_j>1.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Summing (5.7) over all pairs gives

\[
(n-1)\sum_i x_i
>
{n\choose2}.
\]

Since `sum x_i=3`,

\[
3(n-1)>\frac{n(n-1)}2,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{n\le5.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Also `n>=3` because every coordinate is at most one. Thus only `n=3,4,5` remain.

### 5.3 Terminal case `n=3`

With three positive coordinates, each at most one and total sum three, necessarily

\[
(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(1,1,1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_i\phi(x_i)=0.
\tag{5.9}
\]

### 5.4 Terminal case `n=4`

Put

\[
y_i=1-x_i.
\]

Then

\[
y_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^4 y_i=1.
\]

Let `e_j` be the elementary symmetric polynomials of `(y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4)`. Exact Newton reduction of

\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\phi(1-y_i)
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^4\phi(x_i)
=10\left[
88e_2^2+(141e_2-220)e_3+4e_2-317e_4
\right].
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Maclaurin/Newton inequalities for four nonnegative numbers of sum one give

\[
e_2\le\frac38,
\qquad
e_3\le\frac49e_2^2,
\qquad
e_4\le\frac1{36}e_2^2.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Because `141e_2-220<0`, substituting the upper bounds in the negative terms yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{10}\sum_i\phi(x_i)
&\ge
88e_2^2+4e_2
+(141e_2-220)\frac49e_2^2
-317\frac1{36}e_2^2\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{e_2}{12}
(752e_2^2-223e_2+48).
}
\tag{5.12}
\end{aligned}
\]

The quadratic factor has discriminant

\[
223^2-4\cdot752\cdot48=-94655<0
\]

and positive leading coefficient. Thus (5.12) is nonnegative, and it is positive whenever `e_2>0`.

### 5.5 Terminal case `n=5`

Let

\[
m=\min_i x_i.
\]

By the terminal property (5.7), every other coordinate is greater than `1-m`. Hence

\[
3=\sum_i x_i
>m+4(1-m)=4-3m,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{x_i>\frac13\quad\text{for all }i.}
\tag{5.13}
\]

On the interval `[1/3,1]` we use the pointwise affine minorant

\[
\boxed{
\phi(x)\ge60-100x.
}
\tag{5.14}
\]

For completeness, set

\[
x=\frac13+\frac23t,
\qquad0\le t\le1,
\]

and define

\[
P(x)=\phi(x)+100x-60.
\]

Its exact degree-five Bernstein coefficients on this interval are

\[
\boxed{
10,
\quad\frac{10592}{405},
\quad\frac{4018}{135},
\quad\frac{308}{15},
\quad\frac{94}{15},
\quad40.
}
\tag{5.15}
\]

All are strictly positive, so `P(x)>0` on `[1/3,1]`. Summing (5.14) over the five coordinates gives

\[
\sum_{i=1}^5\phi(x_i)
\ge5\cdot60-100\cdot3=0.
\tag{5.16}
\]

This completes the proof of Theorem 5.1.
