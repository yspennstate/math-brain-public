## 5. Method B18 — say the problem in threshold / stop-loss language

For `a=(a_1,...,a_p) in R_+^p`, write

\[
T_d(a)=\max_{|S|=d}\sum_{i\in S}a_i,
\]

the sum of the `d` largest coordinates. For every deterministic threshold `tau>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
T_d(a)\le d\tau+\sum_{i=1}^p(a_i-\tau)_+.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof of (5.1)

Let `S_*` index the `d` largest coordinates. For each `i in S_*`,

\[
a_i\le \tau+(a_i-\tau)_+.
\]

Summing over `S_*` gives

\[
T_d(a)
\le d\tau+\sum_{i\in S_*}(a_i-\tau)_+
\le d\tau+\sum_i(a_i-\tau)_+.
\]

No probabilistic independence is used.

Now let `X ~ N(0,C)` and put `mu_i=C_{ii}`. Each marginal satisfies `X_i \overset d= \sqrt{\mu_i} Z`, `Z~N(0,1)`, regardless of the correlations. Define

\[
h(v,\tau)=\mathbb E[(vZ^2-\tau)_+].
\]

Taking expectations in (5.1) gives the correlation-blind upper bound

\[
\boxed{
F_d(C)\le d\tau+\sum_{i=1}^p h(\mu_i,\tau).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the useful B18 reformulation: the dependence structure is retained in `F_d`, but the upper envelope depends only on the diagonal variance vector.

## 6. Method B11 — dual/relaxation bound and a one-Lipschitz fact

For fixed `tau`, the function `v -> h(v,tau)` is 1-Lipschitz on `v>=0`.

Indeed, for every fixed `z`,

\[
|(vz^2-\tau)_+-(wz^2-\tau)_+|
\le |v-w|z^2,
\]

because `x -> x_+` is 1-Lipschitz. Averaging and using `E Z^2=1`,

\[
\boxed{|h(v,\tau)-h(w,\tau)|\le |v-w|.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
m=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}{p},
\qquad
\mu(Q)=\operatorname{diag}(Q\Sigma Q^T).
\]

By trace conservation, `sum_i mu_i=pm`. From (6.1),

\[
\sum_i h(\mu_i,\tau)
\le p h(m,\tau)+\|\mu-m\mathbf1\|_1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Choose `tau=m`. We can evaluate the scalar stop-loss exactly:

\[
h(m,m)=m\,\mathbb E[(Z^2-1)_+].
\]

Since

\[
\mathbb E[(Z^2-1)_+]
=2\int_1^\infty(z^2-1)\varphi(z)\,dz
=2\varphi(1),
\]

where the last equality follows by integration by parts from

\[
\int_1^\infty z^2\varphi(z)\,dz
=\varphi(1)+\int_1^\infty\varphi(z)\,dz,
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{h(m,m)=2m\varphi(1).}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Combining (5.2), (6.2), and (6.3) yields the central new inequality

\[
\boxed{
F_d(Q\Sigma Q^T)
\le
m\,[d+2p\varphi(1)]
+\|\operatorname{diag}(Q\Sigma Q^T)-m\mathbf1\|_1.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This holds for **every** orthogonal `Q`, including dense products of overlapping Givens rotations. It uses only the correct Gaussian marginal laws and never replaces dependent coordinates by independent ones.

## 7. Method B29 — close the rotation search inside the Schur--Horn polytope

Let the eigenvalues be ordered

\[
\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_p\ge0
\]

and write

\[
S_d=\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_d.
\]

At the KL basis, samplewise `T_d` dominates the sum on the fixed set of the first `d` eigen-coordinates. Therefore

\[
\boxed{F_d(\Sigma_{\rm diag})\ge S_d.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The diagonal vector

\[
\mu(Q)=\operatorname{diag}(Q\Sigma Q^T)
\]

runs over the orthostochastic part of the compact Schur--Horn permutahedron and has barycenter `m 1`.

Equations (6.4) and (7.1) give the following theorem.

### Theorem 7.1 — variance-flattening safe ball

For every covariance `Sigma`, every `1<=d<p`, and every orthogonal `Q`, if

\[
\boxed{
\|\operatorname{diag}(Q\Sigma Q^T)-m\mathbf1\|_1
<
S_d-m[d+2p\varphi(1)],
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{F_d(Q\Sigma Q^T)<F_d(\Sigma_{\rm diag}).}
\tag{7.3}
\]

When the right-hand side of (7.2) is positive, this certifies an explicit open `l^1` neighborhood of the barycenter in diagonal-variance space in which **every** rotation, regardless of its correlation pattern or Givens-overlap graph, is strictly worse than KL.

### Proof

By (6.4), the left side of (7.3) is at most

\[
m[d+2p\varphi(1)]+\|\mu(Q)-m\mathbf1\|_1.
\]

Under (7.2), this is strictly smaller than `S_d`, while (7.1) gives `S_d <= F_d(Sigma_diag)`. Hence (7.3).

This is a complete proof of the stated restricted theorem; it is **not** a proof of the universal Mallat--Zeitouni conjecture because (7.2) need not hold for an arbitrary rotation.

## 10. What the three methods accomplished

### B18 — say it in another language: SUCCESS

The top-`d` functional admits a deterministic stop-loss envelope. This converts a hard correlated Gaussian selection problem into an upper bound depending only on the marginal variance vector and a scalar half-normal tail.

### B11 — dualize / relax: SUCCESS WITH A SHARP LIMITATION

The threshold envelope gives a correlation-blind certificate. It is strong for sufficiently flattened diagonal variances, but by construction it can have a relaxation gap. It does **not** decide rotations whose diagonal variance vector remains close to an extreme eigenvalue permutation while correlations are highly nontrivial.

### B29 — compactify / add the boundary: SUCCESS

Schur--Horn places every possible diagonal variance vector in one compact permutahedron. The theorem certifies an explicit central `l^1` safe region around its barycenter. Therefore a source counterexample, if it exists, cannot be sought indiscriminately among strongly variance-flattening rotations when the safe radius is positive.

Combined with the previous matching-support theorem, a viable counterexample mechanism must involve a genuine overlap component on at least three eigendirections **and** avoid this certified central variance-flattening region. This narrows, but does not eliminate, the search space.

## 11. Why the universal proof is still open

The missing inequality is global. The stop-loss upper bound discards correlation information and can be too weak in the intermediate region between near-eigenbasis rotations and strongly flattened diagonals. Nothing here proves that every point of the Schur--Horn permutahedron satisfies (7.2), and nothing here excludes a dense overlapping basis whose marginal variances remain too extreme for the safe-ball certificate.

The exact next obstruction can be stated as follows. For an overlapping three-direction family such as

\[
Q(\alpha,\beta)=G_{12}(\alpha)G_{23}(\beta),
\]

one needs either

\[
F_d(Q(\alpha,\beta)\Lambda Q(\alpha,\beta)^T)
\le F_d(\Lambda)
\quad\text{for the entire parameter rectangle},
\]

or a source-valid parameter point with rigorously positive excess. The single-plane theorem cannot simply be iterated because the second plane no longer acts on a Gaussian pair independent of the rest.
