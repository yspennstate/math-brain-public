## 3. Smooth covariance formulation

Fix `C \succ 0`. Although `f_d` is nonsmooth on the tie hyperplanes `x_i^2=x_j^2`, it has quadratic growth and the Gaussian density is smooth in `C`. Therefore `F_d(C)` is smooth on the positive-definite cone by differentiation of the density under the integral sign.

Put

\[
F:=F_d(C),
\qquad
M(C):=\mathbb E\big[f_d(X)XX^T\big].
\]

For a symmetric perturbation `H`, differentiating the Gaussian density gives

\[
D\phi_C(x)[H]
=\frac12\phi_C(x)
\left(
 x^TC^{-1}HC^{-1}x-\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}H)
\right).
\]

Hence

\[
DF_d(C)[H]
=\frac12\operatorname{tr}\!\left(
H\left[C^{-1}MC^{-1}-FC^{-1}\right]
\right).
\]

Define the symmetric matrix

\[
\boxed{
J(C):=C^{-1}M(C)C^{-1}-F_d(C)C^{-1}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then the exact covariance gradient is

\[
\boxed{
\nabla_C F_d(C)=\frac12J(C).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

No independence or special spectrum is used here.

## 4. Orthogonal-orbit gradient is an exact commutator

Let `A^T=-A` and move along the fixed-spectrum orthogonal orbit

\[
C(\theta)=e^{\theta A}Ce^{-\theta A}.
\]

Then

\[
C'(0)=[A,C]:=AC-CA.
\]

Using (3.2),

\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}F_d(C(\theta))\bigg|_{0}
=\frac12\operatorname{tr}(J[A,C]).
\]

Define the skew-symmetric matrix

\[
\boxed{
K(C):=[J(C),C]=J(C)C-CJ(C).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since `K` and `A` are skew,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{d\theta}F_d(e^{\theta A}Ce^{-\theta A})\bigg|_{0}
=\frac12\langle K(C),A\rangle_F.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus `K/2` is the exact Lie-algebra gradient in these coordinates.

Substituting (3.1) gives another exact formula with no distributional derivatives:

\[
\boxed{
K(C)=C^{-1}M(C)-M(C)C^{-1}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore, because `C` is invertible,

\[
\boxed{
K(C)=0
\iff [C,M(C)]=0.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the promised self-consistent stationarity equation.

## 5. Equivalent selection-moment formula

Because `C \succ 0`, ties between distinct squared coordinates have probability zero. Let

\[
I_i(X)=\mathbf 1\{X_i^2\text{ is among the top }d\text{ squared coordinates}\}.
\]

Almost everywhere,

\[
\nabla f_d(X)=2D(X)X,
\qquad
D(X)=\operatorname{diag}(I_1,\ldots,I_p).
\]

For the elementary Givens generator `E^{ij}` with `(i,j)` entry `+1` and `(j,i)` entry `-1`, direct sample differentiation yields

\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}
F_d(e^{\theta E^{ij}}Ce^{-\theta E^{ij}})\bigg|_0
=2\mathbb E\left[X_iX_j(I_i-I_j)\right].
\]

Comparing with (4.2),

\[
\boxed{
K_{ij}(C)
=2\mathbb E\left[X_iX_j(I_i-I_j)\right].
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus the first-variation formula proved in the preceding checkpoint is exactly the coordinate form of the global commutator gradient.

Equation (5.1) is particularly useful for certified computation: an interval enclosure for one expectation that excludes zero is already a certificate that the tested basis is not stationary.

## 6. Energy-tilted covariance interpretation

Define the probability measure

\[
\frac{dQ_C}{dP_C}(x)
=\frac{f_d(x)}{F_d(C)},
\]

where `P_C=N(0,C)`. Since both the Gaussian density and `f_d` are invariant under the global sign flip `x -> -x`, `Q_C` is centered. Its covariance is

\[
\widetilde C(C)
:=\mathbb E_{Q_C}[XX^T]
=\frac{M(C)}{F_d(C)}.
\]

Therefore (4.4) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\text{stationarity on the orthogonal orbit}
\iff
[C,\widetilde C(C)]=0.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

In words: **at every local extremum, the original covariance and the covariance obtained by tilting Gaussian samples in proportion to their retained top-`d` energy must have the same eigenspaces.**

This is a stronger coupled condition than any one marginal covariance-block equation and survives the Jacobi obstruction from the previous run.

There is also an exact radial normalization. Write `X=C^{1/2}RU`, with `U` uniform on `S^{p-1}` and `R^2 ~ chi^2_p`, independent. Since `f_d` is homogeneous of degree two,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}M)
=\mathbb E[f_d(X)X^TC^{-1}X]
=(p+2)F_d(C).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}\left(C^{-1}\widetilde C(C)\right)=p+2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Equivalently, the energy tilt raises the mean squared Mahalanobis radius from `p` to exactly `p+2`.

## 7. Distributional Hessian and boundary decomposition

The matrix `J(C)` also has a geometric representation. The function

\[
f_d(x)=\max_{|S|=d}\sum_{i\in S}x_i^2
\]

is convex. Its distributional Hessian is therefore a positive-semidefinite matrix-valued measure.

Away from ties, its ordinary Hessian is simply

\[
2D(X).
\]

The only codimension-one singular sets are the hyperplanes `x_i=x_j` and `x_i=-x_j`. On either hyperplane, a singular contribution occurs only when exactly `d-1` of the remaining `p-2` coordinates have squared magnitude strictly larger than the common squared magnitude. Define

\[
G_{ij}(t,x_{-ij})
:=\mathbf 1\left\{
\#\{k\ne i,j:x_k^2>t^2\}=d-1
\right\}.
\]

For `i<j`, define nonnegative boundary weights

\[
\beta_{ij}^{-}
:=2\int_{\mathbb R}|t|
\int_{\mathbb R^{p-2}}
G_{ij}(t,x_{-ij})
\phi_C(x_i=t,x_j=t,x_{-ij})
\,dx_{-ij}\,dt,
\]

and

\[
\beta_{ij}^{+}
:=2\int_{\mathbb R}|t|
\int_{\mathbb R^{p-2}}
G_{ij}(t,x_{-ij})
\phi_C(x_i=t,x_j=-t,x_{-ij})
\,dx_{-ij}\,dt.
\]

Then Gaussian averaging of the distributional Hessian gives

\[
\boxed{
J(C)
=2\operatorname{diag}(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_p)
+\sum_{i<j}
\left[
\beta_{ij}^{-}(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T
+\beta_{ij}^{+}(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^T
\right],
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

where

\[
\pi_i=\mathbb P(I_i=1).
\]

The coefficient `2|t|` follows from the exact two-variable identity

\[
\max(x_i^2,x_j^2)
=\frac{x_i^2+x_j^2+|x_i^2-x_j^2|}{2}
\]

and the distributional rule `D^2|g|` has singular part `2 delta(g) grad(g)grad(g)^T`.

Every term in (7.1) is positive semidefinite. Moreover, for a positive-definite Gaussian every coordinate has positive probability of entering the top `d`, so every `pi_i>0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{J(C)\succ0.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This explicit decomposition identifies the nonlinear selection operator as a positive diagonal term plus a signed-hyperplane graph contribution. At an actual KL basis, coordinate sign symmetry forces `beta^-_{ij}=beta^+_{ij}`, so all off-diagonal entries of `J` vanish, consistent with stationarity.

A standard mollification of `f_d` justifies the identification of (3.1) with the Gaussian average of this distributional Hessian; alternatively, (3.1) itself can be taken as the smooth definition of `J` and (7.1) as its distributional representation.

## 8. Exact energy identity

Euler's identity for the degree-two homogeneous function `f_d` gives almost everywhere

\[
X^T\nabla f_d(X)=2f_d(X).
\]

Gaussian integration by parts, or equivalently (3.1) plus the radial identity above, yields

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(C J(C))=2F_d(C).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus the objective itself is the covariance-weighted trace of the nonlinear selection Hessian.

## 9. A monotone nonlinear double-bracket flow

The previous checkpoint showed that *naive covariance-Jacobi diagonalization* can decrease the objective. The exact commutator gradient gives a replacement that is monotone by construction.

Let

\[
K(C)=[J(C),C]
\]

and evolve

\[
\boxed{
\dot C=[K(C),C].
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Because `K(C)` is skew-symmetric, (9.1) is an isospectral flow: if `C(0)=U_0\Lambda U_0^T`, then it is generated by an orthogonal evolution `\dot U=K(C)U`, so the eigenvalues of `C` are exactly preserved.

Using (4.2) with `A=K(C)`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}F_d(C(t))
=\frac12\|K(C(t))\|_F^2
\ge0.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Equality holds exactly at stationary points.

Therefore this is an exact nonlinear analogue of a double-bracket ascent flow: it follows the selection-weighted commutator, not the covariance's own off-diagonal entries. On the compact orthogonal orbit, `F_d` is bounded above by `tr(C)`, so `F_d(C(t))` converges and

\[
\int_0^\infty \|K(C(t))\|_F^2\,dt<\infty.
\]

Every accumulation point of the flow is stationary. This does not show that the stationary set consists only of KL bases, but it gives a canonical coupled dynamics for both proof attempts and certified counterexample searches.

## 10. Quantitative application to the previous Jacobi obstruction

For the explicit positive-definite covariance `C_*` recovered from the preceding checkpoint, that report proved analytically that

\[
K_{12}(C_*)
=2\mathbb E[X_1X_2(I_1-I_2)]
>0.322.

Therefore `C_*` is certainly not stationary for the nonlinear double-bracket flow, and (9.2) gives the rigorous instantaneous lower bound

\[
\frac{d}{dt}F_2(C(t))\bigg|_{C(0)=C_*}
=\frac12\|K(C_*)\|_F^2
\ge K_{12}(C_*)^2
>0.322^2
=0.103684.
\]

So the same covariance that defeats naive Jacobi diagonalization admits a certified *coupled* ascent direction with an explicit positive rate.

This sharply distinguishes the two mechanisms:

- covariance-Jacobi uses the off-diagonal entries of `C` and can move downhill;
- the nonlinear double-bracket flow uses the off-diagonal commutator between `C` and its energy-tilted covariance / selection Hessian and always moves uphill.

## 12. Failed / superseded route and what changed

The immediately preceding run showed that an arbitrary `2 x 2` covariance block can already be diagonal while its nonlinear Givens derivative is nonzero due to correlations with outside coordinates. Therefore ordinary Jacobi diagonalization cannot be the monotone mechanism.

The present derivation explains the precise replacement. The correct object to diagonalize *against* `C` is not `C` itself but the nonlinear operator

\[
J(C)=C^{-1}M(C)C^{-1}-F(C)C^{-1}
\]

or, equivalently for stationarity, the energy-tilted covariance `M(C)/F(C)`. The commutator `[J(C),C]` automatically includes all outside-coordinate selection effects that the local Jacobi block misses.

No claim is made that following this flow proves convergence to a KL basis; only monotone ascent and stationarity of accumulation points are proved.
