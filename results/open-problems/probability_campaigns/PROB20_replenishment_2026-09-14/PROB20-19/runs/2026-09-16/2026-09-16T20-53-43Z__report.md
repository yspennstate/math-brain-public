# 6. New theorem: exact `3+1` radial/Beta elimination for `p=4,d=2`

The result below is broader than the selected two-Givens word. It permits an **arbitrary** orthogonal mixing of three eigen-directions, including fully connected overlapping Givens words, while leaving the fourth eigenvector fixed.

## Theorem 6.1

Let

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,\lambda_4),
\qquad \lambda_i>0,
\]

and let `Q in O(3)`. Consider the `4 x 4` orthogonal basis change `Q \oplus 1`. Put

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1},\sqrt{\lambda_2},\sqrt{\lambda_3}).
\]

For `u in S^2` let

\[
b_i(u)=\big(e_i^TQD u\big)^2,\qquad i=1,2,3,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and write their increasing rearrangement as

\[
b_{(1)}(u)\le b_{(2)}(u)\le b_{(3)}(u).
\]

Define for `x>=0`

\[
J(x)=\mathbb E\min\{q x,1-q\},
\qquad q\sim\operatorname{Beta}(3/2,1/2).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then the exact retained top-two energy is

\[
\boxed{
F_2(Q\oplus1;\Lambda)
=\operatorname{tr}\Lambda
-3\,\mathbb E_{u\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^2)}b_{(1)}(u)
-4\lambda_4\,\mathbb E_{u\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S^2)}
J\!\left(\frac{b_{(2)}(u)}{\lambda_4}\right).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the original four-dimensional Gaussian integral has been reduced **exactly** to a two-dimensional spherical integral with an elementary scalar kernel.

## Proof

Let `Z_1,Z_2,Z_3,Z_4` be independent standard normals and write

\[
Z_{1:3}=R U,
\]

where `U` is uniform on `S^2`, `R^2\sim\chi_3^2`, and `R` is independent of `U`. Let

\[
V=Z_4^2\sim\chi_1^2,
\]

independent of `(R,U)`. The transformed Gaussian coordinates are

\[
X_{1:3}=QD Z_{1:3},\qquad X_4=\sqrt{\lambda_4}Z_4.
\]

Hence, conditional on `U=u`,

\[
X_i^2=R^2 b_i(u),\quad i\le3,
\qquad
X_4^2=\lambda_4 V.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Now use the standard independent chi-square decomposition

\[
T=R^2+V\sim\chi_4^2,
\qquad
q=\frac{R^2}{R^2+V}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(3/2,1/2),
\tag{6.5}
\]

with `T`, `q`, and `U` mutually independent. Also

\[
\mathbb E T=4.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Therefore the four squared coordinates can be written as

\[
Tq b_1,\quad Tq b_2,\quad Tq b_3,\quad T\lambda_4(1-q).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Because the first three entries share the same nonnegative factor `Tq`, their order is exactly the order of the `b_i`. The sum of the two smallest among the four entries is consequently

\[
T\left[q b_{(1)}+\min\{q b_{(2)},\lambda_4(1-q)\}\right].
\tag{6.8}
\]

For `p=4,d=2`, retained top-two energy equals total energy minus the sum of the bottom two squared coordinates. Taking expectations in (6.8), using independence and

\[
\mathbb E q=\frac{3/2}{3/2+1/2}=\frac34,
\]

gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\text{bottom two}]
&=4\,\mathbb E_U\left[\frac34 b_{(1)}
+\mathbb E_q\min\{q b_{(2)},\lambda_4(1-q)\}\right]\\
&=3\,\mathbb E_U b_{(1)}
+4\lambda_4\,\mathbb E_U J\left(\frac{b_{(2)}}{\lambda_4}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Since total expected energy is `tr Lambda`, subtracting (6.9) proves (6.3). QED.

# 7. Closed elementary form of the scalar kernel

The Beta density in (6.2) is

\[
f(q)=\frac2\pi\sqrt{\frac{q}{1-q}},\qquad 0<q<1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For `x>0`, the two terms in the minimum cross at

\[
q_x=\frac1{1+x}.
\]

Set

\[
\theta_x=\arcsin\frac1{\sqrt{1+x}}
=\arctan\frac1{\sqrt{x}}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

With `q=\sin^2\theta`, the Beta measure becomes

\[
f(q)dq=\frac4\pi\sin^2\theta\,d\theta.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
J(x)=\frac4\pi\left[
 x\int_0^{\theta_x}\sin^4\theta\,d\theta
+\int_{\theta_x}^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta
\right].
\tag{7.4}
\]

Using

\[
\int\sin^4\theta\,d\theta
=\frac{3\theta}{8}-\frac{\sin2\theta}{4}+\frac{\sin4\theta}{32},
\]

and

\[
\int\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta
=\frac{\theta}{8}-\frac{\sin4\theta}{32},
\]

we obtain the explicit elementary formula

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
J(x)=\frac4\pi\Bigg[&x\left(
\frac{3\theta_x}{8}-\frac{\sin2\theta_x}{4}
+\frac{\sin4\theta_x}{32}\right)\\
&+\frac\pi{16}-\frac{\theta_x}{8}
+\frac{\sin4\theta_x}{32}\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The continuous value is `J(0)=0`.

Differentiating under the integral in (6.2), with the moving-boundary contribution cancelling because the two branches agree at `q_x`, gives

\[
J'(x)=\int_0^{1/(1+x)}qf(q)\,dq>0,
\tag{7.6}
\]

and then

\[
\boxed{
J''(x)=-\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{x}(1+x)^3}<0
\qquad(x>0).
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

So `J` is increasing and strictly concave.

# 8. B27 quantifier swap: positive mixture of truncated second spherical order statistics

Because `J(0)=0`, `J'(\infty)=0`, and (7.7) gives a positive second-derivative measure, define

\[
\rho(s)=\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{s}(1+s)^3},\qquad s>0.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
J(x)=\int_0^\infty \min\{x,s\}\,\rho(s)\,ds.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Indeed differentiating the right-hand side yields `\int_x^\infty rho(s)ds=J'(x)`, and both sides vanish at zero.

Substituting (8.2) into (6.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\text{bottom two}]
=3\,\mathbb E b_{(1)}
+4\int_0^\infty
\mathbb E\min\{b_{(2)},\lambda_4 s\}\,\rho(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This is the useful quantifier swap. The first term is exactly the radial form of the `p=3,d=2` bottom-one problem for the first three eigenvalues: if `X=QD Z_{1:3}`, then

\[
\mathbb E\min_{i\le3}X_i^2=3\,\mathbb E b_{(1)}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

The **new obstruction introduced by the fourth coordinate** is the positive mixture of truncated second spherical order statistics in the second term of (8.3). This explains cleanly why solving `(p,d)=(3,2)` does not by itself settle this `p=4,d=2` family.

A sufficient route for the selected recovery family is now explicit: prove, for the relevant `Q(\alpha,\beta)`,

\[
\mathbb E b_{(1)}(Q)\ge \mathbb E b_{(1)}(I)
\tag{8.5}
\]

and simultaneously for every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\min\{b_{(2)}(Q),t\}
\ge
\mathbb E\min\{b_{(2)}(I),t\}.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

Equation (8.6) is stronger than strictly necessary but, together with (8.5), it would prove KL optimality for the full fixed-eigenvector class (6.3).

Finally,

\[
\mathbb E\min\{b_{(2)},t\}
=\int_0^t
\mathbb P\{b_{(2)}>s\}\,ds,
\tag{8.7}
\]

and `b_(2)>s` means that at least two of the three quantities

\[
|(e_i^TQD)U|^2
\]

exceed `s`. Thus the remaining strong form (8.6) is a **spherical two-of-three cap-intersection problem**. The Gaussian radial variables and the fourth-coordinate tail have disappeared completely.
