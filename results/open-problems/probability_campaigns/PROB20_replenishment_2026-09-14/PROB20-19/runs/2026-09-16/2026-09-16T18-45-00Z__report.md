# 3. Idea before code

The previous exact one-Givens theorem cannot be naively iterated: after a first rotation, the coordinate used by a second overlapping rotation is correlated with another coordinate, and the earlier proof used an independent centered pair. The previous Jacobi-obstruction report proves that this is a real difficulty, not just a technical inconvenience.

The new route is to condition on the already-entangled coordinates and ask what remains of the active coordinate. For a Gaussian vector, conditional on the rest of its current block, a single coordinate is still one-dimensional Gaussian with an arbitrary conditional mean and a deterministic residual variance. If the other endpoint of the next Givens rotation is a **fresh, untouched eigen-coordinate**, it remains independent and centered. This suggests proving a stronger two-variable rearrangement lemma that allows an arbitrary mean in the correlated endpoint.

That is the B10 derandomization step. B13 asks for a monotone quantity controlling whether the step remains safe; it turns out to be the **conditional residual variance**, which is a weighted harmonic mean of the eigenvalues already present in the active block. B23 then attacks the hoped-for counterexample mechanism directly: an overlapping Givens word can beat this argument only after it violates that residual-variance ordering or revisits two already-entangled coordinates.

# 4. Shifted anisotropic pair-alignment lemma

## Lemma 4.1

Let

\[
U\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2),\qquad V\sim N(0,\tau^2)
\]

be independent, with

\[
\sigma^2\ge \tau^2>0.
\]

Fix any deterministic values of all other coordinates, and let the full retained objective be the sum of the `d` largest squared coordinates, for any valid `d`. Rotate only `(U,V)` by an angle `theta`. Then the conditional expected retained energy is maximized at `theta=0` modulo the quarter-turn/sign symmetries of the two coordinate squares.

This statement allows an arbitrary shift `mu`; it is therefore strictly stronger than the centered pair lemma needed at an eigenbasis.

## Proof

Fix the values of the outside squared coordinates and condition further on the pair radius

\[
r^2=U^2+V^2.
\]

Write

\[
U=r\cos\phi,\qquad V=r\sin\phi.
\]

Let `B_k` be the sum of the `k` largest outside squared coordinates, with invalid indices omitted. Exactly as in the archived one-Givens proof, the full top-`d` sum is

\[
\operatorname{Top}_d
=\max\{B_d,\;B_{d-1}+M(\phi-\theta),\;B_{d-2}+r^2\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
M(t)=\frac{r^2}{2}\bigl(1+|\cos 2t|\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore, on the circle of circumference `pi/2`, the angle-dependent selection kernel

\[
h(t):=\max\{B_d,\;B_{d-1}+M(t),\;B_{d-2}+r^2\}
\]

is nonnegative, even, `pi/2`-periodic, and nonincreasing on `[0,pi/4]`.

Now compute the conditional angular weight of the independent shifted anisotropic Gaussian pair. Up to a factor independent of `phi`,

\[
\exp\left[-\frac{(r\cos\phi-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}
          -\frac{r^2\sin^2\phi}{2\tau^2}\right]
=
\exp\{\kappa\cos2\phi+\eta\cos\phi\},
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
\kappa=\frac{r^2}{4}\left(\frac1{\tau^2}-\frac1{\sigma^2}\right)\ge0,
\qquad
\eta=\frac{r\mu}{\sigma^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Because `h` is `pi/2`-periodic, fold the density over the four quarter-turns. Replacing `eta` by `|eta|` does not change the folded function. For `0\le\phi\le\pi/4`, set

\[
c=\cos\phi,\qquad s=\sin\phi,\qquad q=\cos2\phi\ge0.
\]

The folded angular density is proportional to

\[
\boxed{
K(\phi)
=2e^{\kappa q}\cosh(\eta c)
 +2e^{-\kappa q}\cosh(\eta s).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

It is even and `pi/2`-periodic. Differentiate on `(0,pi/4)`:

\[
\frac{K'(\phi)}2
=
2\kappa\sin2\phi
\Big[e^{-\kappa q}\cosh(\eta s)-e^{\kappa q}\cosh(\eta c)\Big]
\]

\[
\qquad
+\eta\Big[e^{-\kappa q}c\sinh(\eta s)-e^{\kappa q}s\sinh(\eta c)\Big].
\tag{4.6}
\]

Both square brackets are nonpositive. The first is immediate from `q>=0`, `kappa>=0`, and `c>=s>=0`. For the second, the function

\[
x\mapsto\frac{\sinh(\eta x)}x
\]

is nondecreasing for `x>0` and `eta>=0`, hence

\[
c\sinh(\eta s)\le s\sinh(\eta c).
\]

The exponential factors only strengthen this inequality because `e^{-\kappa q}\le e^{\kappa q}`. Thus

\[
K'(\phi)\le0\qquad(0<\phi<\pi/4).
\tag{4.7}
\]

So both `K` and `h` are symmetric decreasing functions on the circle of circumference `pi/2`. The circular layer-cake rearrangement lemma already proved in the archived one-Givens checkpoint gives

\[
\int K(\phi)h(\phi-\theta)\,d\phi
\le
\int K(\phi)h(\phi)\,d\phi.
\tag{4.8}
\]

This proves the conditional claim after integrating over the pair radius. QED.

### Why this is not the forbidden independence replacement

No dependent Gaussian vector is replaced by independent coordinates with the same marginal variances. The independence in Lemma 4.1 is an **exact conditional statement** obtained from Gaussian regression in the theorem below: after conditioning on the already-entangled block, the fresh eigen-coordinate is genuinely independent, and the other active coordinate has its exact conditional Gaussian law.

# 5. First overlapping-Givens theorem

Start from independent eigen-coordinates

\[
Y_1\sim N(0,\lambda_1),\quad
Y_2\sim N(0,\lambda_2),\quad
Y_3\sim N(0,\lambda_3),
\]

independent also of any remaining eigen-coordinates. First apply a Givens rotation in the `(1,2)` plane:

\[
A=cY_1+sY_2,
\qquad
B=-sY_1+cY_2,
\qquad c^2+s^2=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then apply an arbitrary second Givens rotation to the **overlapping** pair `(B,Y_3)`.

The exact conditional variance of `B` given `A` is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(B\mid A)
=
\frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}
     {\lambda_1c^2+\lambda_2s^2}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed, with

\[
a=\lambda_1c^2+\lambda_2s^2,
\]

one has

\[
\operatorname{Var}(B)=\lambda_1s^2+\lambda_2c^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)=(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)sc,
\]

and Gaussian regression gives (5.2) after direct simplification.

## Theorem 5.1 — exact safe region for two overlapping rotations

If

\[
\frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}
     {\lambda_1c^2+\lambda_2s^2}
\ge \lambda_3,
\tag{5.3}
\]

then, for every second-rotation angle and every `1\le d<p`, the second overlapping Givens rotation cannot increase the expected retained top-`d` energy. Consequently the two-Givens product cannot beat the KL basis.

### Proof

Condition on `A` and on every coordinate outside `(B,Y_3)`. Gaussian regression gives

\[
B\mid A\sim N(\mu(A),v),
\qquad
v=\frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}{\lambda_1c^2+\lambda_2s^2}.
\]

The untouched eigen-coordinate `Y_3` remains independent of this sigma-field and is exactly `N(0,lambda_3)`. Under (5.3), Lemma 4.1 applies conditionally to `(B,Y_3)`, so the second rotation cannot improve the objective. Integrating the conditioning proves the first inequality. The first Givens rotation itself cannot improve over KL by the archived exact one-Givens theorem. QED.

Because the right-hand side of (5.2) is a weighted harmonic mean of `lambda_1,lambda_2`,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(B\mid A)\ge\min(\lambda_1,\lambda_2).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore a simple sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{\lambda_3\le\min(\lambda_1,\lambda_2).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the first exact connected three-eigendirection class beyond matching-supported rotations in which overlapping Givens factors are rigorously known to remain KL-safe.

# 6. Descending fresh-leaf Givens forest theorem

The preceding argument iterates much farther.

Consider a Givens construction starting from a diagonal eigenbasis. At some stage let `B` be one already-formed transformed block supported on eigenvalue indices `S`, and choose one current coordinate `i` in this block. Let `j` be a **fresh eigen-coordinate** that has never been rotated before, with variance `lambda_j`. Apply an arbitrary Givens rotation to current coordinate `i` and fresh coordinate `j`.

## Theorem 6.1

Assume at every such attachment step that

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_j\le\min_{k\in S}\lambda_k.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then every attachment step is nonincreasing for the nonlinear retained-energy objective. Hence any Givens forest grown by attaching fresh leaves in nonincreasing eigenvalue order satisfies

\[
\boxed{F_d(Q\Lambda Q^T)\le F_d(\Lambda)}
\tag{6.2}
\]

for every retained dimension `1\le d<p` and every choice of Givens angles. The connected support of `Q` may be arbitrarily large.

### Proof

Write the covariance on the current transformed block as

\[
C_B=Q_B\,\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k:k\in S)\,Q_B^T.
\]

Condition on all current block coordinates except coordinate `i`, and on all coordinates outside the active pair. The conditional distribution of coordinate `i` is Gaussian with some mean `mu` and residual variance

\[
\sigma_i^2
=\frac1{(C_B^{-1})_{ii}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Since

\[
C_B^{-1}
=Q_B\,\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_k^{-1}:k\in S)\,Q_B^T,
\]

we have

\[
(C_B^{-1})_{ii}
=\sum_{k\in S}\frac{q_{ik}^2}{\lambda_k},
\qquad
\sum_{k\in S}q_{ik}^2=1.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus `sigma_i^2` is the weighted harmonic mean

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_i^2
=\left(\sum_{k\in S}\frac{q_{ik}^2}{\lambda_k}\right)^{-1}
\ge\min_{k\in S}\lambda_k.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

By the attachment hypothesis (6.1), `sigma_i^2>=lambda_j`. The fresh eigen-coordinate `j` is independent of the entire existing block and remains centered `N(0,lambda_j)` under the conditioning. Therefore Lemma 4.1 applies exactly to the active pair and says that rotating them cannot increase the conditional expected top-`d` energy. Integrate the conditioning.

Inducting over all fresh-leaf attachments proves (6.2). QED.

## Corollary 6.2 — tree-supported mixing in decreasing spectral order is safe

Take any rooted forest on the eigen-directions, orient every edge away from the root, and require eigenvalues to be nonincreasing from each root toward its descendants. Any Givens word that builds each tree by attaching a previously untouched child along a parent-block coordinate is KL-safe, for arbitrary angles.

This strictly extends the prior matching-support theorem: matching support only allowed connected components of size at most two, whereas Theorem 6.1 permits connected mixing components of arbitrary size.

# 7. What a counterexample must now do

The new theorem isolates a sharper gluing obstruction. A source-valid counterexample cannot be generated solely by descending fresh-leaf Givens attachments. A minimal bad Givens word must eventually do at least one of the following:

1. **revisit active support:** rotate two coordinates that are both already entangled in nontrivial transformed blocks; or
2. **violate the residual-variance order:** attach a fresh eigen-coordinate whose variance exceeds the conditional residual variance of the chosen parent coordinate.

For the simplest two-overlapping-Givens word, the exact obstruction is the reverse of (5.3):

\[
\lambda_3>
\frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}{\lambda_1c^2+\lambda_2s^2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is a materially smaller counterexample-search domain than the full two-angle rectangle.
