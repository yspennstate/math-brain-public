## 3. New theorem: exact global KL optimality on every fixed spectral ray near isotropy

Let

\[
H=\operatorname{diag}(h_1,\dots,h_p)
\]

be a fixed real diagonal matrix. The scalar case `H=cI` is trivial because the covariance is isotropic for every `t`. Assume henceforth that `H` is not scalar.

For real `t` sufficiently close to zero so that

\[
I+tH\succ0,
\]

define the fixed-spectrum orbit objective

\[
\Phi_t(U)
:=F_d\!\left(U(I+tH)U^T\right),
\qquad U\in O(p).
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — spectral-ray neighborhood theorem

For every fixed `p`, every `1<d<p`, and every fixed non-scalar diagonal `H`, there exists

\[
t_0=t_0(H,p,d)>0
\]

such that for every nonzero `t` satisfying `|t|<t_0` and `I+tH\succ0`, one has

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t(U)\le \Phi_t(I)
\qquad\text{for every }U\in O(p).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equality holds exactly when

\[
UHU^T
\]

is diagonal. Thus the maximizing covariance is expressed in a coordinate eigenbasis. If `H` has simple spectrum, the equality cases are precisely signed coordinate permutations of the eigenbasis. Repeated-eigenvalue rotations do not change the covariance and are included in the same statement.

This is an exact theorem for a nonzero open interval of anisotropy along every fixed spectral direction; it is not merely an asymptotic comparison of objective values.

## 4. Gaussian density expansion around isotropy

Let `G~N(0,I_p)` and let `A` be any symmetric matrix in the compact orbit

\[
\mathcal O_H=\{UHU^T:U\in O(p)\}.
\]

For `I+tA\succ0`, the density ratio of `N(0,I+tA)` against the standard Gaussian density is

\[
R_{t,A}(x)
=
\det(I+tA)^{-1/2}
\exp\left[
-\frac12x^T\big((I+tA)^{-1}-I\big)x
\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Uniformly for `A\in\mathcal O_H`, expand

\[
(I+tA)^{-1}
=I-tA+t^2A^2+O(t^3),
\]

and

\[
\log\det(I+tA)
=t\operatorname{tr}A
-\frac{t^2}{2}\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
+O(t^3).
\]

Therefore

\[
\log R_{t,A}(x)
=tL_1(A,x)+t^2L_2(A,x)+O(t^3(1+\|x\|^2)),
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
L_1(A,x)
=
\frac12\big(x^TAx-\operatorname{tr}A\big),
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
L_2(A,x)
=
\frac14\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
-\frac12x^TA^2x.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Exponentiating,

\[
R_{t,A}
=
1+tL_1
+t^2\left(L_2+\frac12L_1^2\right)
+O(t^3P_H(x)),
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `P_H` can be chosen as a fixed polynomial times a Gaussian-dominable exponential for all `A` on the orbit and all sufficiently small `|t|`.

Since

\[
0\le f_d(x)\le\|x\|^2,
\]

the expansion may be integrated term-by-term. Moreover, because `A=UHU^T` depends smoothly on `U` and the orbit is compact, the same domination applies after differentiating up to two times in any local coordinates on the orbit. Hence the expansion below is **uniform in `C^2` on the orbit**, a fact used in the global stability step.

## 5. Hyperoctahedral symmetry collapses the orientation dependence to one scalar

Set

\[
m_0=\mathbb E f_d(G),
\qquad
m_2=\mathbb E[f_d(G)G_1^2].
\]

Because `f_d` is invariant under all coordinate permutations and independent sign flips,

\[
\mathbb E[f_d(G)G_iG_j]=0\quad(i\ne j),
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E[f_d(G)G_i^2]=m_2
\quad\text{for every }i.
\]

Therefore the first-order term

\[
\mathbb E[f_d(G)L_1(A,G)]
=
\frac12(m_2-m_0)\operatorname{tr}A
\tag{5.1}
\]

depends only on the spectrum and is orientation-independent.

At second order define

\[
a_{p,d}
:=\mathbb E[f_d(G)G_1^4],
\qquad
b_{p,d}
:=\mathbb E[f_d(G)G_1^2G_2^2],
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{p,d}:=a_{p,d}-3b_{p,d}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The terms coming from `L_2`, from `(tr A)x^TAx`, and from `(tr A)^2` again depend only on `tr A` and `tr A^2`, hence are fixed on the orbit. The only nontrivial calculation is

\[
\mathbb E\left[f_d(G)(G^TAG)^2\right].
\]

Write `A=(A_ij)`. Sign symmetry kills every monomial with an odd power of some Gaussian coordinate, leaving

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[f_d(G)(G^TAG)^2]
={}&
 a_{p,d}\sum_i A_{ii}^2
+2b_{p,d}\sum_{i<j}A_{ii}A_{jj}
+4b_{p,d}\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Use

\[
(\operatorname{tr}A)^2
=
\sum_iA_{ii}^2+2\sum_{i<j}A_{ii}A_{jj},
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
=
\sum_iA_{ii}^2+2\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}^2.
\]

Then (5.4) becomes the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[f_d(G)(G^TAG)^2]
=
b_{p,d}(\operatorname{tr}A)^2
+2b_{p,d}\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
+\Delta_{p,d}\sum_iA_{ii}^2.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Substituting into the density expansion yields

\[
\boxed{
F_d(I+tA)
=C_H(t)
+\frac{\Delta_{p,d}}8t^2D(A)
+O_{C^2}(t^3),
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

uniformly for `A\in\mathcal O_H`, where

\[
D(A):=\sum_iA_{ii}^2,
\tag{5.7}
\]

and `C_H(t)` contains only orientation-independent terms through order two. The notation `O_{C^2}(t^3)` means that the remainder and its first two orbit derivatives are bounded by `C|t|^3` uniformly on the compact orbit.

Thus the entire leading orientation question is reduced to the sign of the single universal coefficient `Delta_{p,d}`.

## 7. Schur–Horn geometry of the leading orbit functional

On the fixed orbit `A=UHU^T`,

\[
\|A\|_F^2
=\operatorname{tr}(H^2)
\]

is constant. Therefore

\[
D(A)
=
\sum_iA_{ii}^2
=
\operatorname{tr}(H^2)
-2\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
D(A)\le\operatorname{tr}(H^2),
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

with equality if and only if `A` is diagonal. This is the squared-Euclidean strict-convex instance of Schur–Horn, but here it follows directly from Frobenius norm conservation.

Thus, because `Delta_{p,d}>0`, the `t^2` limit functional in (5.6) has exactly the KL covariances as its global maximizers.

The local geometry at such a maximizer is also explicit. If

\[
A_0=\operatorname{diag}(h_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,h_{\sigma(p)})
\]

and one rotates coordinates `i,j` by `theta`, then

\[
D(A_\theta)
=D(A_0)
-2(h_{\sigma(i)}-h_{\sigma(j)})^2
\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}D(A_\theta)\Big|_{0}
=-4(h_{\sigma(i)}-h_{\sigma(j)})^2.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Every genuine tangent direction to the orbit mixes unequal eigenspaces, so the Hessian of `D` is negative definite on the orbit tangent space at each diagonal orbit point. Rotations inside a repeated-eigenvalue eigenspace do not move `A` at all and are not genuine orbit directions.

## 8. Stability upgrades the asymptotic expansion to an exact global theorem

Define the normalized orbit functional after subtracting the orientation-independent terms in (5.6):

\[
\Psi_t(A)
:=
\frac{F_d(I+tA)-C_H(t)}{t^2},
\qquad A\in\mathcal O_H.
\tag{8.1}
\]

By (5.6),

\[
\Psi_t
\xrightarrow[t\to0]{C^2}
\Psi_0(A)
:=\frac{\Delta_{p,d}}8D(A)
\tag{8.2}
\]

uniformly on the compact orbit.

The maximizer set of `Psi_0` consists of finitely many diagonal covariance matrices obtained by permuting the multiset of eigenvalues of `H`. Choose pairwise disjoint small orbit neighborhoods around these diagonal points such that the Hessian of `Psi_0` is uniformly negative definite throughout each local coordinate chart. This is possible by (7.4) and continuity.

For sufficiently small nonzero `t`, `C^2` convergence implies the Hessian of `Psi_t` remains negative definite in those same neighborhoods. Each diagonal covariance is an exact critical point of `Psi_t` for every `t`: when the covariance is diagonal, the sign-flip symmetry from the archived local-Hessian report forces the orbital gradient to vanish. Therefore each neighborhood contains exactly its diagonal covariance as the local maximizer.

Outside the union of these neighborhoods, compactness and (7.2) give a fixed gap

\[
\max\Psi_0-\Psi_0\ge\eta>0.
\]

Uniform `C^0` convergence then gives, for all sufficiently small nonzero `t`,

\[
\Psi_t(A)
<
\Psi_t(A_0)
\]

for every `A` outside those neighborhoods and every diagonal maximizer `A_0`.

Finally, all diagonal permutations have exactly the same retained-energy expectation because `f_d` is coordinate-permutation invariant. Hence the global maximizers of the exact `Psi_t`, and therefore of the exact original `F_d`, are precisely the diagonal/eigenbasis covariances.

This proves Theorem 3.1.

### Important scope point

The theorem is **not** merely the statement that KL wins to second order. The second-order calculation identifies a nondegenerate global limit landscape, and uniform `C^2` stability plus compactness upgrades that limit to exact global optimality for every sufficiently small but nonzero `t` on the fixed spectral ray.

The theorem does not currently provide a useful explicit numerical value of `t_0`; it is an existence theorem depending on the fixed spectral shape.

## 9. Corollary for the negative one-spike family

Take

\[
H=-uu^T
\]

with `u` varying on the sphere. Equivalently, for

\[
\lambda=1-\varepsilon,
\qquad
C_{\lambda,u}=I-\varepsilon uu^T,
\]

the spectrum is `(1,\ldots,1,lambda)`.

For rank one,

\[
D(-uu^T)
=\sum_i u_i^4.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Therefore the exact near-isotropy expansion is

\[
F_d(I-\varepsilon uu^T)
=C(\varepsilon)
+\frac{\Delta_{p,d}}8\varepsilon^2\sum_i u_i^4
+O_{C^2}(\varepsilon^3).
\tag{9.2}

\]

Since `Delta_{p,d}>0`, the coordinate directions are the limiting global maxima.

For `p=4,d=2`, at the balanced point

\[
u_*=\frac12(1,1,1,1),
\]

and a unit tangent `h\perp u_*`, the geodesic `u_theta=u_* cos theta+h sin theta` satisfies

\[
\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}
\sum_i u_{\theta,i}^4\Big|_{0}=2.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hess}\mathcal F_{1-\varepsilon}(u_*)
=
\frac{\Delta_{4,2}}4\varepsilon^2 I_{T_{u_*}S^3}
+O(\varepsilon^3).
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

Thus, for all sufficiently small positive `epsilon`, i.e. all `lambda<1` sufficiently close to `1`, the balanced non-KL stationary point is a **strict local minimum**.

This sharply complements the preceding checkpoint:

- at the singular endpoint `lambda=0`, the balanced point is a nondegenerate strict local maximum with exact curvature `-4sqrt(3)/(3pi)`;
- suboptimal non-KL local maxima persist for sufficiently small positive `lambda`;
- near the isotropic endpoint `lambda=1`, the balanced point is instead a strict local minimum, and KL is globally optimal on the entire one-small-eigenvalue orbit.

Therefore the non-KL local-max landscape created near `lambda=0` must undergo a genuine bifurcation before the isotropic endpoint. This is a structural conclusion, not a Monte Carlo observation.

For the limiting rank-one landscape `sum_i u_i^4`, the only local maxima are coordinate directions; equal-magnitude support sizes `m=2,...,p-1` are saddles and the fully balanced support `m=p` is a strict minimum. By the same `C^2` stability argument, the negative one-spike orbit sufficiently near isotropy has no non-KL local maxima in neighborhoods of those balanced-support critical classes.

## 11. Failed / rejected shortcuts

1. **Second-order asymptotics alone are not enough.** A pointwise expansion does not prove exact finite-`t` optimality because competitors may approach the KL set as `t->0`. The proof therefore uses uniform `C^2` convergence and nondegenerate orbit geometry near the diagonal set.
2. **Uniform `C^0` convergence alone is not enough near KL.** The leading Schur–Horn gap vanishes quadratically as a competitor approaches an eigenbasis. Local Hessian stability is needed to exclude nearby competitors.
3. **Stationarity is not uniqueness.** Earlier checkpoints already exhibit non-KL stationary points and non-KL local maxima away from isotropy, so the argument is explicitly global-in-value near isotropy rather than a claim that all stationary points are KL.
4. **The theorem does not chain Jacobi steps.** The earlier exact Jacobi obstruction remains valid; the new proof works by perturbation of the entire compact isospectral orbit around the isotropic covariance.
