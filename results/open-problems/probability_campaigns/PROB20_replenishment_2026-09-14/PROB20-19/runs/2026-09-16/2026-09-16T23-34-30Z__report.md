# 5. Spectator-stable shift rearrangement lemma

The key point is stronger than the previously archived isotropic one-spike theorem.

Fix a block size `m>=2`, an outside length `r>=0`, and `p=m+r`. Let `a>0`. Let

\[
Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,a).
\]

For an arbitrary deterministic outside squared-energy vector

\[
w=(w_1,\ldots,w_r)\in[0,\infty)^r
\]

and a mean vector `mu in R^m`, define

\[
H_{d,w}(\mu)
=
\mathbb E_Z\,\operatorname{Top}_d
\big((Z_1+\mu_1)^2,\ldots,(Z_m+\mu_m)^2,w_1,\ldots,w_r\big).
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Lemma 5.1

For every `1<=d<p`, the function

\[
(y_1,\ldots,y_m)
\longmapsto
H_{d,w}(\sqrt{y_1},\ldots,\sqrt{y_m})
\tag{5.2}
\]

is Schur-convex on `[0,\infty)^m` for every fixed spectator vector `w`.

In particular, if `y` and `y'` have the same sum and `y'` majorizes `y`, then

\[
H_{d,w}(\sqrt y)\le H_{d,w}(\sqrt{y'}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Signed-coordinate symmetry shows that `H_{d,w}` depends on `mu` only through `mu_i^2`. Symmetry under permutations of the `m` iid Gaussian block coordinates is also immediate. It therefore suffices to prove the two-coordinate Robin-Hood inequality.

Fix all block coordinates except coordinates `1,2`, and also fix every spectator value `w_j`. All those frozen squared values may be combined into one list

\[
q_1,\ldots,q_{p-2}\ge0.
\]

For `0<=k<=p-2`, let `C_k` be the sum of the `k` largest `q_j`. Set `C_k=-\infty` when `k` is outside this feasible range. This convention simply deletes an impossible selection case below.

Now integrate the first two actual Gaussian coordinates in polar form

\[
x_1=\rho\cos\phi,
\qquad
x_2=\rho\sin\phi.
\]

At fixed `rho` and fixed frozen coordinates, exactly zero, one, or two of the pair can enter the retained set. Therefore the samplewise top-`d` functional is exactly

\[
h_\rho(\phi)
=
\max\left\{
C_d,
C_{d-1}+M_\rho(\phi),
C_{d-2}+\rho^2
\right\},
\tag{5.4}
\]

where infeasible terms have value `-infinity`, and

\[
M_\rho(\phi)
=
\max\{x_1^2,x_2^2\}
=
\frac{\rho^2}{2}\bigl(1+|\cos2\phi|\bigr).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus `h_rho` is even, `pi/2`-periodic, and nonincreasing on `[0,pi/4]`.

Next fix the pair mean norm. By sign symmetry take

\[
\mu_1=s\cos\theta,
\qquad
\mu_2=s\sin\theta,
\qquad
0\le\theta\le\pi/4.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The Gaussian pair density at fixed radius has, up to factors independent of `phi`, angular factor

\[
\exp\left(A\cos(\phi-\theta)\right),
\qquad
A=\frac{\rho s}{a}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Because `h_rho` is `pi/2`-periodic, periodize the density over this symmetry. The resulting kernel is

\[
K_A(t)
=
\sum_{k=0}^3
\exp\left(A\cos(t+k\pi/2)\right)
=
2\cosh(A\cos t)+2\cosh(A\sin t).
\tag{5.8}
\]

For `0<t<pi/4`,

\[
K_A'(t)
=
2A\left[
-\sin t\,\sinh(A\cos t)
+\cos t\,\sinh(A\sin t)
\right].
\tag{5.9}
\]

If `A>0`, the map

\[
x\mapsto\frac{\sinh(Ax)}{x}
\]

is strictly increasing for `x>0`. Since `cos t>sin t>0`, this gives

\[
\frac{\sinh(A\cos t)}{\cos t}
>
\frac{\sinh(A\sin t)}{\sin t},
\]

hence

\[
K_A'(t)<0.
\tag{5.10}
\]

For `A=0`, `K_A` is constant. Thus both `h_rho` and `K_A` are symmetric decreasing functions on the circle of circumference `pi/2`.

For completeness, the circular rearrangement fact needed here follows directly from layer cake: every superlevel set of an even decreasing function on this circle is a centered interval, and for two centered intervals the overlap length is nonincreasing as one interval is translated from shift `0` toward shift `pi/4`. Integrating the overlap lengths over the two layer parameters shows that the convolution of two nonnegative even decreasing functions is itself even and nonincreasing on `[0,pi/4]`.

Therefore the fixed-`rho`, fixed-frozen-coordinate angular expectation

\[
\int h_\rho(\phi)K_A(\phi-\theta)\,d\phi
\tag{5.11}
\]

is nonincreasing as `theta` moves from `0` to `pi/4`. Integrating over `rho` and then over all the other iid Gaussian block coordinates preserves the inequality.

Moving `theta` toward `pi/4` keeps

\[
\mu_1^2+\mu_2^2=s^2
\]

fixed while equalizing the pair `(mu_1^2,mu_2^2)`. Hence every two-coordinate Robin-Hood equalization weakly decreases (5.1). By the standard finite characterization of majorization by such transforms, (5.2) is Schur-convex. QED.

### Why this is genuinely stronger than the archived lemma

The earlier positive one-spike report stated Schur-convexity for the fully isotropic Gaussian shift functional. The proof above makes explicit a feature not promoted there: **the outside coordinates are arbitrary fixed spectators throughout the two-coordinate rearrangement.** They need not share the block variance, need not be exchangeable with the block, and need not even be random. This is exactly what allows the source-valid covariance extension below.

# 6. Main theorem: positive one-spike block with arbitrary diagonal spectators

Let `B subset {1,...,p}` have cardinality `m>=2`. Let

\[
D_0=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_p)
\tag{6.1}
\]

with

\[
\beta_i=a>0\quad(i\in B),
\qquad
\beta_j=\tau_j>0\quad(j\notin B),
\tag{6.2}
\]

where the spectator variances `tau_j` are otherwise arbitrary. Let `u` be any unit vector supported on `B`, and let `c>=0`. Define

\[
\Sigma_u=D_0+c\,uu^T.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For a covariance `C`, write

\[
\mathcal F_d(C)
=
\mathbb E_{X\sim N(0,C)}
\operatorname{Top}_d(X_1^2,\ldots,X_p^2).
\tag{6.4}
\]

## Theorem 6.1 — spectator-stable positive one-spike optimality

For every `1<=d<p`, every unit `u` supported on `B`, and every coordinate vector `e_k` with `k in B`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_d(\Sigma_u)
\le
\mathcal F_d(\Sigma_{e_k}).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

All covariances `Sigma_u` have the same spectrum. In particular, `Sigma_{e_k}` is diagonal and therefore represents a covariance eigenbasis. Thus KL is globally optimal over every rotation that only changes the orientation of the positive one-spike direction inside the repeated-`a` block, **regardless of the variances of the untouched spectator eigendirections**.

### Proof

Let

\[
G,Z_1,\ldots,Z_p
\]

be independent standard normals. Realize `X~N(0,Sigma_u)` as follows. For `i in B`,

\[
X_i=\sqrt a\,Z_i+\sqrt c\,G u_i,
\tag{6.6}
\]

and for `j notin B`,

\[
X_j=\sqrt{\tau_j}\,Z_j.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Condition on `G=g` and on all spectator values `(X_j)_{j notin B}`. Their squared values now form a deterministic vector `w`. The conditional block mean is

\[
\mu_i=\sqrt c\,g u_i,
\qquad i\in B,
\tag{6.8}
\]

so its squared mean vector is

\[
(cg^2u_i^2)_{i\in B}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Because `u` is a unit vector,

\[
(cg^2,0,\ldots,0)
\succ
(cg^2u_i^2)_{i\in B}
\tag{6.10}
\]

in the majorization order. Lemma 5.1 therefore gives, pointwise in the conditioning variables,

\[
\mathbb E\left[
\operatorname{Top}_d(X_1^2,\ldots,X_p^2)
\mid G=g, X_{B^c}
\right]
\le
\mathbb E\left[
\operatorname{Top}_d(\widetilde X_1^2,\ldots,\widetilde X_p^2)
\mid G=g, X_{B^c}
\right],
\tag{6.11}
\]

where `widetilde X` is the same construction but with block spike direction `e_k`. Integrating over `G` and the spectators proves (6.5).

Finally, because `u` is supported on `B`, the block covariance

\[
aI_B+c\,u_Bu_B^T
\]

has eigenvalues `a+c,a,...,a`, while all spectator eigenvalues remain `tau_j`. Thus every `Sigma_u` has the same full spectrum and `Sigma_{e_k}` is diagonal. QED.

## Boundary extension

If `a=0` or some spectator variances vanish, the same non-strict conclusion follows either by continuity from positive variances or directly. For `a=0`, after conditioning on `G` and the spectators the block squared vector is

\[
cg^2(u_i^2)_{i\in B}.
\]

For fixed spectator values, `Top_d` is a symmetric convex function of the nonnegative block squared coordinates because it is a maximum of linear subset sums. Hence it is Schur-convex, and the coordinate vector `(cg^2,0,...,0)` again dominates. No positive-definiteness boundary is needed for the main source-valid corollaries below.

# 7. Equivalent orthogonal-block formulation

The theorem can be stated directly in the basis-optimization language.

Let

\[
\Lambda
=
\operatorname{diag}
(a+c,\underbrace{a,\ldots,a}_{m-1},\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_r),
\qquad p=m+r,
\tag{7.1}
\]

with `a,c>=0` and `tau_j>=0`. Let `Q_B in O(m)` and embed it as

\[
Q=Q_B\oplus I_r.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then

\[
Q_B\operatorname{diag}(a+c,a,\ldots,a)Q_B^T
=aI_m+c\,uu^T,
\qquad u=Q_Be_1.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Theorem 6.1 gives

\[
\boxed{
F_d(Q;\Lambda)\le F_d(I;\Lambda)
\quad\text{for every }1\le d<p.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is a continuous `O(m)` family, not a finite set of rational rotations.

# 8. Consequence for the current `p=4,d=2` frontier

Take

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(L,1,1,\lambda),
\qquad L\ge1,\quad \lambda\ge0,
\tag{8.1}
\]

and let **any** `Q_3 in O(3)` act on the first three eigendirections while the fourth eigenvector is fixed. Then

\[
\boxed{
F_2(Q_3\oplus1;\Lambda)
\le
F_2(I;\Lambda)
\quad\text{for every }Q_3,L,\lambda.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Indeed, the mixed block is exactly a rank-one positive spike above the repeated background `I_3`.

This completely subsumes the previous report's balanced family
