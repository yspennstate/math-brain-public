## 9. Section-I(e): nearest solved analogue and the missing ingredient

The nearest solved *concentration* analogue cited by the source is the Erdős–Rényi logarithmic regime `G(n,p)` with `p=t log n/n`. In ER, each degree is a binomial sum of independent Bernoulli edge indicators, so classical Chernoff concentration can be applied directly and then union-bounded when the desired uniform regime permits it. Draief–Massoulié's book explicitly develops Chernoff/Azuma concentration tools for these random-graph arguments.

`LK(n,k)` lacks the key ER ingredient: edge independence after bilateral rank selection. The historical threshold embedding is exactly the device that replaces this missing independence for a one-sided uniform lower bound, while the deterministic ceiling `D_v<=k` supplies the upper bound.

The nearest solved **exact-model** result is Dabirian–Subramanian's Theorem 8, which gives detailed mean-degree asymptotics under `k=o(n^{1/3})`, `k->infinity`. It is one-vertex expectation information, not an all-vertex tail theorem.
