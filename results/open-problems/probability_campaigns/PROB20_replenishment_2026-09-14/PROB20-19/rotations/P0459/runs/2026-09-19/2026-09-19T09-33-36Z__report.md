\[
1\le k(n)\le n-1,\qquad \frac{k(n)}{\log n}\to\infty,
\]

then

\[
\frac{\max_{1\le v\le n}|D_v-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.

\[
\frac{\max_v|D_v-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}

## 3. Adversarial mathematical interface check

No new claim is promoted here; this section checks the four interfaces named in the installed gate prompt.

### 3.1 Threshold-subgraph implication

On the global event that every threshold degree `B_v<=k`, every edge of weight at most the threshold is among the `k` lowest-weight incident edges at each endpoint. Continuous iid edge weights eliminate ties almost surely. Therefore every threshold edge is bilaterally preferred and the threshold graph is a subgraph of `LK(n,k)`. No endpoint-independence assumption is used.

### 3.2 Chernoff/union constants

For `B_v~Bin(n-1,p)` with mean `k-delta`, `delta=sqrt(6k log n)`, the standard Bernstein/Chernoff upper-tail denominator

\[
2(k-\delta)+2\delta/3=2k-4\delta/3\le2k
\]

gives exponent at least `delta^2/(2k)=3 log n`; the lower-tail denominator `2(k-delta)<=2k` gives the same. Thus each tail is at most `n^{-3}` and a union bound over `n` vertices and two tails gives `2/n^2`. Independence between vertex degrees is unnecessary.

### 3.3 Expectation bridge

Let `A_n` denote the global event. Since `D_1>=0` always and `D_1>=k-2delta` on `A_n`,

\[
E[D_1]\ge (k-2\delta)P(A_n).
\]

This is deterministic expectation arithmetic; no independence between `D_1` and `A_n` is assumed. Together with deterministic `D_1<=k`, it yields the mean-deficit bound used in the proof.

### 3.4 Centering/quantifiers

The conclusion is simultaneous over every vertex and centered at the deterministic symmetry mean `mu_n=E[D_v]`. It is therefore strictly stronger than merely saying a typical vertex is near its mean. The exact source model and bilateral-preference rule are unchanged.

**Outcome:** no concrete mathematical defect was identified at these four interfaces.
