## 2. Exact model

Assign iid `Uniform(0,1)` weights to the edges of `K_n`. Vertex `v` prefers its `k=k(n)` incident edges with the smallest weights. An undirected edge is present in `LK(n,k)` iff both endpoints prefer it. Let

\[
D_v=\deg_{LK(n,k)}(v),\qquad \mu_n:=\mathbb E D_v,
\]

where symmetry makes `mu_n` independent of `v`. Deterministically,

\[
0\le D_v\le k.
\]

Throughout the positive theorem take integer `1<=k<=n-1` and assume

\[
\frac{k}{\log n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

This implies `k -> infinity` and, for all sufficiently large `n`, `k>2 sqrt(6k log n)`.

## 3. B2 — attack the negation first: relative simultaneous concentration fails below the logarithmic threshold

The source proves that when

\[
k=\lfloor t\log n\rfloor,\qquad 0<t<1,
\]

there is an isolated vertex with probability tending to one (this is part of its disconnectedness result; the paper explicitly obtains isolated vertices by a second-moment argument). In this regime Theorem 8 applies because `k -> infinity` and `k=o(n^{1/3})`, giving in particular

\[
\mu_n=k-\sqrt{k/\pi}+o(\sqrt{k}),
\]

so `mu_n>0` for all sufficiently large `n`.

On every realization containing an isolated vertex `v`,

\[
\frac{\max_u|D_u-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}
\ge \frac{|D_v-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac{\max_u|D_u-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}\ge1\right)\to1
\qquad (k=\lfloor t\log n\rfloor,\ 0<t<1).
\]

So a relative all-vertex concentration theorem cannot extend through the whole logarithmic region. This does **not** prove sharpness of the positive condition `k/log n -> infinity`; it only supplies a source-valid obstruction on the subcritical side.

## 4. B4 — prove something weaker completely: superlogarithmic simultaneous concentration around the mean

### Theorem 4.1 (source-valid sufficient condition)

Let `G_n ~ LK(n,k(n))` and let `D_v` be the degree of vertex `v`. If

\[
1\le k(n)\le n-1,\qquad \frac{k(n)}{\log n}\to\infty,
\]

then, with `mu_n=E[D_v]`,

\[
\boxed{\frac{\max_{1\le v\le n}|D_v-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.}
\]

More quantitatively, put

\[
\delta_n:=\sqrt{6k\log n},\qquad
r_n:=\frac{2\delta_n+2k/n^2}{(k-2\delta_n)(1-2/n^2)}.
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb P\!\left(\frac{\max_v|D_v-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}\le r_n\right)\ge1-\frac{2}{n^2},}
\]

and `r_n -> 0`.

### Proof

The historical comparison supplies the right finite-probability event; I reproduce the short argument here so that the source-level theorem is self-contained.

Set

\[
\delta:=\sqrt{6k\log n},\qquad
p:=\frac{k-\delta}{n-1}.
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`, `k>2 delta`, hence `0<p<1`. Form the threshold graph `H_n` by retaining every complete-graph edge whose uniform weight is at most `p`. Let `B_v` be its degree at `v`. Then

\[
B_v\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,p),\qquad
\mathbb E B_v=k-\delta.
\]

For the upper tail, the standard Bernstein/Chernoff bound gives

\[
\mathbb P(B_v\ge k)
=\mathbb P(B_v-\mathbb EB_v\ge\delta)
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\delta^2}{2(k-\delta)+2\delta/3}\right)
\le e^{-3\log n}=n^{-3},
\]

because the denominator is at most `2k` and `delta^2=6k log n`. Likewise, for the lower tail,

\[
\mathbb P(B_v\le k-2\delta)
=\mathbb P(B_v-\mathbb EB_v\le-\delta)
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\delta^2}{2(k-\delta)}\right)
\le n^{-3}.
\]

Hence the union bound over all `n` vertices gives the event

\[
A_n:=\{k-2\delta\le B_v\le k\ \text{for every }v\}
\]

with

\[
\mathbb P(A_n)\ge1-2n^{-2}. \tag{4.1}
\]

On `A_n`, the threshold graph is a subgraph of `LK(n,k)`: if a threshold edge `(u,v)` has weight at most `p`, then at endpoint `u` there are at most `B_u<=k` threshold edges total, so `(u,v)` is among the `k` smallest incident weights at `u`; the same holds at `v`. Thus on `A_n`, for every vertex,

\[
D_v\ge B_v\ge k-2\delta.
\]

The bilateral construction always gives `D_v<=k`. Therefore

\[
A_n\subseteq\{k-2\delta\le D_v\le k\ \text{for all }v\}. \tag{4.2}
\]

The historical memo stopped after (4.2), describing concentration around the ceiling `k`. The following expectation bridge is the missing source-centered step.

Fix a vertex, say `v=1`. Since `D_1>=0` always and `D_1>=k-2delta` on `A_n`, (4.1) yields

\[
\mu_n=\mathbb E D_1
\ge (k-2\delta)\mathbb P(A_n)
\ge (k-2\delta)(1-2n^{-2}). \tag{4.3}
\]

Also `mu_n<=k`. Consequently

\[
0\le k-\mu_n
\le k-(k-2\delta)(1-2n^{-2})
=2\delta+\frac{2(k-2\delta)}{n^2}
\le2\delta+\frac{2k}{n^2}. \tag{4.4}
\]

Now work on `A_n`. From (4.2), for every `v`,

\[
\mu_n-D_v\le k-(k-2\delta)=2\delta,
\]

while from `D_v<=k` and (4.4),

\[
D_v-\mu_n\le k-\mu_n\le2\delta+2k/n^2.
\]

Therefore, simultaneously for all vertices,

\[
\max_v|D_v-\mu_n|\le2\delta+2k/n^2. \tag{4.5}
\]

Combining (4.3) and (4.5), on `A_n`,

\[
\frac{\max_v|D_v-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}
\le
\frac{2\delta+2k/n^2}{(k-2\delta)(1-2/n^2)}
=:r_n. \tag{4.6}
\]

Write

\[
\eta_n:=\frac{\delta}{k}=\sqrt{\frac{6\log n}{k}}.
\]

The assumption `k/log n -> infinity` gives `eta_n -> 0`, and therefore

\[
r_n=\frac{2\eta_n+2/n^2}{(1-2\eta_n)(1-2/n^2)}\longrightarrow0. \tag{4.7}
\]

Finally (4.1), (4.6), and (4.7) imply that for every `epsilon>0`, for all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac{\max_v|D_v-\mu_n|}{\mu_n}>\epsilon\right)
\le\mathbb P(A_n^c)\le2n^{-2}\to0.
\]

This proves the theorem. `\square`

### Why this genuinely answers the source's second additional question

The theorem stays in the exact `LK(n,k)` model, is simultaneous over **all** vertices, is centered at the deterministic source mean `E[D]`, states an explicit sufficient growth condition, and gives a quantitative high-probability bound. It therefore meets the frozen metric exactly. No assertion of optimality or sharpness is made.

## 5. B18 — say it in another language: maximum deficit is the exact order parameter

Define the maximum degree deficit from the deterministic ceiling

\[
M_n:=\max_v(k-D_v)=k-\min_vD_v,
\]

and the deterministic mean deficit

\[
a_n:=k-\mu_n\ge0.
\]

Let

\[
Z_n:=\max_v|D_v-\mu_n|.
\]

For every realization and every vertex,

\[
k-D_v=(k-\mu_n)+(\mu_n-D_v),
\]

so

\[
M_n\le a_n+Z_n. \tag{5.1}
\]

Conversely,

\[
|D_v-\mu_n|=|(k-D_v)-a_n|\le(k-D_v)+a_n\le M_n+a_n,
\]

hence

\[
Z_n\le M_n+a_n. \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{|Z_n-M_n|\le a_n.} \tag{5.3}
\]

Whenever `a_n/k ->0` (and hence `mu_n/k->1`), simultaneous relative mean-centered concentration is therefore equivalent to

\[
M_n/k\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

The proof above gives both ingredients directly:

\[
a_n/k\le2\sqrt{6\log n/k}+2/n^2\to0
\]

and, on `A_n`,

\[
M_n/k\le2\sqrt{6\log n/k}\to0.
\]

This reformulation isolates the real global object as the **minimum degree / maximum deficit**, rather than a variance calculation. It also explains why isolated vertices give the clean obstruction in B2.
