## 4. B3 — prove something stronger: normalized Sine spectral measure is injective with the bounded-type hypothesis discharged

### Proposition 4.1

Let

\[
T=\operatorname{Dir}(R,\infty,u_1),
\qquad
\widetilde T=\operatorname{Dir}(\widetilde R,\infty,\widetilde u_1)
\]

be zero-potential weighted Dirac/canonical systems in the standard Sine normalization on `[0,1)`, with

\[
\det R(t)=\det\widetilde R(t)=\frac14,
\qquad
R(0)=\widetilde R(0)=\frac12I,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and common regular left boundary line `span(e_1)`. If their **left scalar spectral measures are equal**, then the normalized operators are identical (up to the operator-trivial global sign of a fundamental solution).

### Step 1. The spectral-weight convention is the inverse-theory convention

Normalize the regular-left solution `H(t,lambda)` by

\[
H(0,\lambda)=e_1.
\]

At an eigenvalue `lambda_j`, the left spectral atom is

\[
\mu_j
=\frac{|H_1(0,\lambda_j)|^2}{\|H(\cdot,\lambda_j)\|_{L_R^2}^2}
=\frac1{\|H(\cdot,\lambda_j)\|_{L_R^2}^2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is exactly the usual norming-constant convention used in the scalar spectral measure of the weighted Dirac inverse theorem. Thus equality of the Sine left marked spectral measures is equality of the EKT scalar spectral measures, not merely equality of spectra.

### Step 2. The bounded-type hypothesis is automatic here

The left endpoint `t=0` is regular for the standard Sine canonical system; in particular it is limit-circle. EKT Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.2 give canonical real entire left solutions whose components, and hence the associated de Branges functions, lie in the Cartwright class at a limit-circle endpoint.

EKT's discussion after Corollary 4.6 notes that the quotient bounded-type requirement in Theorem 4.1 is automatic when the relevant de Branges functions are Cartwright. Therefore the bounded-type condition needed for the inverse uniqueness theorem is **not an extra random hypothesis** in the standard Sine class: it follows from the regular/limit-circle left endpoint.

This closes the previously identified audit gap.

### Step 3. Apply the zero-potential inverse theorem

EKT Corollary 4.6 yields an increasing locally absolutely continuous reparameterization `eta` and a constant matrix

\[
\Gamma_0\in SL(2,\mathbb R)
\]

such that almost everywhere

\[
\eta'(t)\,\widetilde R(\eta(t))
=
\Gamma_0^{\!T}R(t)\Gamma_0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Step 4. Determinant normalization kills the reparameterization

Taking determinants in (4.3), using `det Gamma_0=1` and (4.1), gives

\[
(\eta'(t))^2\frac14=\frac14.
\]

Because `eta` is increasing,

\[
\eta'(t)=1\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

An increasing bijection of `[0,1)` onto itself with derivative one a.e. and the same regular left endpoint has

\[
\eta(t)=t.
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Step 5. Left-end normalization kills the constant gauge

Letting `t downarrow 0` in (4.3) and using continuity at the regular endpoint gives

\[
\frac12 I
=
\Gamma_0^{\!T}\frac12I\Gamma_0,
\]

so

\[
\Gamma_0\in SO(2).
\tag{4.6}
\]

The EKT unitary equivalence also transports the left boundary condition. Both normalized systems use the same left line `span(e_1)`, hence

\[
\Gamma_0e_1\parallel e_1.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The only rotations in `SO(2)` preserving that line are `I` and `-I`. Therefore

\[
\Gamma_0=\pm I.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Substituting back into (4.3) yields

\[
\widetilde R(t)=R(t)\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

The global sign acts only on a fundamental solution and is operator-trivial. Equality of the operator and its normalized endpoint data follows.

`QED`.

## 5. B7 — take the limit only after an exact deterministic finite operator lift

The continuum coupling should not be an artifact of infinite-dimensional spectral inversion. We now construct a source-to-target operator map at every finite circular size.

Fix `k>=2` and `N>=1`. Start from a standard finite circular `beta_-=2/k` operator realization with `kN` eigenangles. Its marked spectral measure has

1. support distributed as `CE^0_{2/k,kN}`;
2. norming weights
   \[
   P^-=(P^-_1,\ldots,P^-_{kN})\sim
   \operatorname{Dirichlet}(a,\ldots,a),
   \qquad a=\frac1k,
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
   independent of the support.

Forrester gives the exact support relation (2.1). It remains to transform the source weights deterministically to the target finite spectral-weight law

\[
P^+\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(k,\ldots,k)
\quad\text{on }N\text{ coordinates}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Stick-breaking coordinates for the source

For `j=1,...,kN-1`, define

\[
V_j^-=
\frac{P_j^-}{1-\sum_{r<j}P_r^-}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The standard Dirichlet stick-breaking identity gives independent variables

\[
V_j^-\sim
\operatorname{Beta}\!\left(a,(kN-j)a\right).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Use only the first `N-1` of these independent source coordinates and set

\[
U_j
=
F_{\operatorname{Beta}(a,(kN-j)a)}(V_j^-),
\qquad 1\le j\le N-1.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because the Beta laws are continuous, the `U_j` are independent `Uniform(0,1)` variables. They are deterministic functions of the source norming weights and remain independent of the entire source eigenangle support.

Now define target sticks

\[
V_j^+
=
F^{-1}_{\operatorname{Beta}(k,(N-j)k)}(U_j),
\qquad 1\le j\le N-1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then the `V_j^+` are independent with exactly the target Dirichlet stick-breaking laws. Reconstruct

\[
P_1^+=V_1^+,
\tag{5.7}
\]

\[
P_j^+
=V_j^+\prod_{r<j}(1-V_r^+),
\qquad 2\le j\le N-1,
\tag{5.8}
\]

and

\[
P_N^+
=\prod_{r=1}^{N-1}(1-V_r^+).
\tag{5.9}
\]

By the inverse stick-breaking theorem,

\[
P^+\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(k,\ldots,k),
\tag{5.10}
\]

still independent of the decimated support.

### Reconstruct the finite target operator

Keep the Forrester support

\[
\Theta^+
=D_k(\Theta^-)
\tag{5.11}
\]

pathwise and decorate it with `P^+`. Valkó--Virág's finite circular reconstruction / modified-Verblunsky map is deterministic and invertible from this marked measure to the finite Dirac operator. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D_{k,N}^{\rm fin}(T^-)
:=
\operatorname{Rec}_N\bigl(D_k(\operatorname{spec}T^-),\ P^+(P^-)\bigr)
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

is a deterministic measurable map of the **source finite operator** satisfying

\[
\mathfrak D_{k,N}^{\rm fin}(T^-)
\stackrel d=
\operatorname{Circ}_{2k,N},
\tag{5.13}
\]

with exact pathwise support decimation.

This is stronger than merely saying the finite eigenvalue law decimates: it is a finite operator-level lift driven entirely by information already present in the source operator. No new randomness is introduced.

The finite maps are not yet claimed to converge samplewise to the continuum map `mathfrak D_k^{op}`. What is established is cleaner: the finite and limiting problems each possess a deterministic marked-spectral operator lift, and the finite target marginals converge to the standard Sine target under the existing circular-to-Sine operator theory.

**B7 outcome:** operator-level lifting is already exact at finite `N`; the continuum construction is not exploiting a peculiarity absent from Forrester's finite models.

## 6. B16 — compute the smallest case: support decimation alone cannot determine the target operator

A tempting stronger interpretation of Virág's question would demand that the target limiting operator be a deterministic function of the **decimated eigenvalues alone**. The finite operator model rules this out immediately in the smallest nontrivial case.

Take `k=2`, so `beta_-=1` and `beta_+=4`, and take target size `N=2`. Fix two distinct target support points `z_1,z_2` on the unit circle. Write the target marked spectral measure as

\[
\mu_p=p\,\delta_{z_1}+(1-p)\,\delta_{z_2},
\qquad 0<p<1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a two-atom probability measure, the degree-one monic orthogonal polynomial is

\[
\Phi_1(z)
=z-\bigl(pz_1+(1-p)z_2\bigr).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The OPUC recurrence gives

\[
\overline{\alpha_0}
=pz_1+(1-p)z_2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

At the first modified-Verblunsky step the same first moment determines the finite operator coefficient (up to the fixed convention phase). Thus, after the support is fixed, changing `p` changes the operator.

Under the standard finite circular `beta_+=4` operator law,

\[
p\sim\operatorname{Beta}(2,2)
\tag{6.4}
\]

conditional on the support, so this coefficient is nondegenerate whenever `z_1\ne z_2`.

For the exact real witness `z_1=1`, `z_2=-1`,

\[
\overline{\alpha_0}=2p-1.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Hence

\[
p=\frac14\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\overline{\alpha_0}=-\frac12,
\]

while

\[
p=\frac34\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\overline{\alpha_0}=+\frac12.
\]

The support is identical but the finite operator is different. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No deterministic function of the decimated support alone can have the full target finite operator law.}
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Any source-valid operator decimation coupling must use additional operator information -- norming weights, coefficient-path data, or fresh randomness. The D0044 construction uses the source operator's own norming weights and therefore supplies exactly the missing information without violating pathwise spectral decimation.
