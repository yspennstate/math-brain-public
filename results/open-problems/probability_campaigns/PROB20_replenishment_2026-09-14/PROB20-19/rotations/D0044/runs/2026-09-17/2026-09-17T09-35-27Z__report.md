## 4. B30 — localize and glue: exact bulk scaling and a compactness lift to joint limiting operators

Set

\[
\beta_- = \frac{2}{k},\qquad \beta_+=2k,\qquad k\ge 2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the finite circular relation take `kN` source eigenangles

\[
0\le \theta^-_{1,N}<\cdots<\theta^-_{kN,N}<2\pi
\]

with CbetaE(`beta_-`) law. Forrester's circular identity selects every `k`th angle (with the source's prescribed cyclic convention), giving `N` angles

\[
\theta^+_{j,N}=\theta^-_{kj+r_N,N},
\tag{4.2}
\]

for a residue/cut convention `r_N in {0,...,k-1}`, and the selected set has CbetaE(`beta_+`) law.

### 4.1 The missing factor `1/k`

In the circular-operator bulk scaling used for Sine-beta, spectral locations are proportional to matrix size times angle. Write, suppressing an immaterial common normalization constant,

\[
\xi^-_{i,N}=kN\,\theta^-_{i,N},
\qquad
\xi^+_{j,N}=N\,\theta^+_{j,N}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For the same retained angle (4.2), therefore,

\[
\boxed{
\xi^+_{j,N}=\frac1k\,\xi^-_{kj+r_N,N}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the source-valid infinite bulk relation is **not** the unscaled schematic `D_k(Sine_{2/k})=Sine_{2k}`. In the operator normalization it is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal S_{2k})
=
\frac1k D_{k,U}\bigl(\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal S_{2/k})\bigr)
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

inside an appropriate joint coupling, where `U` records the limiting cyclic color/residue inherited from the finite cut convention.

This factor matters: Sine-beta has beta-independent bulk intensity, whereas keeping one point out of `k` without the spatial compression would divide the intensity by `k`. The `1/k` compression restores the target intensity.

### 4.2 Exact finite operator pair

For each `N`, construct a pair `(\mathcal C_N^-,\mathcal C_N^+)` as follows.

**Source.** Sample the standard finite circular `beta_-` spectral measure on `kN` points: CbetaE(`beta_-`) support together with independent Dirichlet(`beta_-/2`,...,`beta_-/2`) norming weights, and apply the standard deterministic modified-Verblunsky / finite-Dirac reconstruction. Then `\mathcal C_N^-` is the standard finite circular-beta-minus operator.

**Target.** Apply Forrester's every-`k`th rule to the source support. Independently attach target weights

\[
(w^+_{1,N},\ldots,w^+_{N,N})
\sim \operatorname{Dirichlet}(k,\ldots,k),
\tag{4.6}
\]

because `beta_+/2=k`, and apply the same deterministic finite reconstruction. Then `\mathcal C_N^+` has **exactly** the standard finite circular-beta-plus operator law, and (4.4) holds pathwise for its spectrum after the standard source/target bulk scalings.

This is the B4 finite lift from the preceding report, now with the required scaling relation written explicitly.

### 4.3 Compactness-lift theorem

**Proposition (operator coupling exists as a joint subsequential bulk limit).**
For every integer `k>=2`, there exists a coupling of random limiting bulk operators `(\mathcal S^-,\mathcal S^+)` with marginals

\[
\mathcal S^-\stackrel d=\mathtt{Sine}_{2/k},
\qquad
\mathcal S^+\stackrel d=\mathtt{Sine}_{2k},
\tag{4.7}
\]

and a cyclic residue/color `U in Z/kZ` such that, with the spectra ordered using the inherited origin convention,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal S^+)
=
\frac1k D_{k,U}(\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal S^-))
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

almost surely.

**Proof.**

1. For each `N`, take the exact finite operator pair `(\mathcal C_N^-,\mathcal C_N^+)` above together with the finite cyclic residue `r_N`.
2. By the Valkó-Virág circular-beta operator-limit theorem, the marginal law of the properly rescaled source operator converges to the Sine-`2/k` operator law, and the marginal law of the properly rescaled target operator converges to the Sine-`2k` operator law.
3. Work in the operator/canonical-system state space used for the circular-to-Sine convergence (equivalently one can retain the resolvent representation used in their convergence theorem). Convergence of each marginal law implies tightness of each marginal family. Therefore the joint laws of the pairs are tight: for any `epsilon>0`, choose compact marginal sets `K_-` and `K_+` with probabilities at least `1-epsilon/2`; then `K_- x K_+` has joint probability at least `1-epsilon` by the union bound.
4. The residue variables live in the finite compact space `Z/kZ`, so adjoining `r_N` preserves tightness. Extract a subsequence along which
   \[
   (\mathcal C_N^-,\mathcal C_N^+,r_N)
   \Rightarrow
   (\mathcal S^-,\mathcal S^+,U).
   \tag{4.9}
   \]
5. The two marginals of the limit are forced to be the unique marginal limits in (4.7).
6. In the circular-to-Sine operator topology, isolated simple eigenvalues converge locally under the corresponding resolvent convergence. The Sine-beta spectrum is almost surely simple and locally finite, and almost surely the anchoring point is not itself an eigenvalue. Hence, on every bounded window whose endpoints avoid the limiting spectra, the ordered finite eigenvalues converge to the ordered limiting eigenvalues. The exact pathwise relation (4.4) is therefore closed under this local convergence. Passing to the limit yields (4.8) on each such window; a countable exhaustion of the line gives the full relation.

This proves the proposition. `square`

### What this proposition does and does not answer

It is genuinely an **operator-level coupling existence statement**, not merely a point-process equality: both random variables in (4.9) are limiting operators with the correct Sine-beta marginal laws, and their spectra obey the source decimation relation.

It is nevertheless not promoted to `E_decim=1` because the source asks whether the limiting operators can be coupled **explicitly**. The proof above chooses an abstract subsequential joint weak limit of already explicit finite pairs. It does not yet produce a direct continuum transform of the source hyperbolic-Brownian/canonical-system path, nor prove uniqueness of the joint limit. Calling this the requested explicit coupling would overstate what has been constructed.

### Why one spectral measure still does not automatically close the source

A tempting statement would be:

> reconstruct the target operator uniquely from `mu_+^{dec}`.

General inverse spectral theory is subtler. Eckhardt-Kostenko-Teschl prove that a spectral measure determines a weighted Dirac operator **up to a gauge transformation**. In canonical-system language, a full Weyl/Herglotz function determines a trace-normalized Hamiltonian under standard hypotheses, but a measure by itself is only the measure part of a Herglotz representation; real affine normalization data / boundary gauge must also be pinned down.

For the standard Sine-beta realization there are extra deterministic normalizations available. In the Valkó-Virág Dirac representation the left boundary convention is fixed, the canonical determinant normalization is fixed, and in the standard Brownian parametrization the terminal coefficient is normalized. These facts make it plausible that the gauge orbit has a unique standard representative. But I did **not** prove in this run that (5.3), together with a short deterministic list of Sine normalizations, selects that representative uniquely and measurably for the singular Sine-beta canonical system.

That is now the exact B11 obstruction:

\[

\mathtt{Sine}_{2k}\ \text{operator}.

\tag{5.5}

## 6. B19 — make the inverse an algorithm: finite reconstruction followed by a canonical-system limit

The finite inverse is completely explicit. This suggests defining the continuum inverse rather than invoking abstract inverse uniqueness.

Given the candidate limiting target measure `mu_+^{dec}` in (5.3), the algorithmic route is:

1. choose increasing finite spectral windows `[-L,L]` and retain the atoms in the window, with a boundary completion/periodization chosen to match the circular normalization;
2. normalize the positive weights to a finite probability vector;
3. map finite support + weights to ordinary Verblunsky coefficients, then to modified Verblunsky coefficients;
4. map those coefficients to the finite hyperbolic path / finite Dirac coefficient path exactly as in the circular-beta construction;
5. impose the same endpoint normalization used for `Circ_{2k,n}`;
6. send `L -> infinity` (or, more naturally, use the actual finite decimated CbetaE measures along `N -> infinity`).

At finite `N` this algorithm gives exactly the target circular-beta-plus operator from Section 4. Therefore every finite stage has the correct law and its spectrum is exactly the decimated source support.

The missing theorem is a **pathwise inverse-continuity statement**:

> If the weighted spectral measures generated by this construction converge vaguely to `mu_+^{dec}` with the Sine growth/normalization, then the reconstructed normalized canonical systems converge, without subsequence and independently of the admissible finite approximation, to a uniquely determined canonical system.

Modern canonical-system transition papers establish the useful forward direction—coefficient convergence gives Weyl/spectral convergence—while de Branges-type inverse theory provides uniqueness after the correct normalization. What is needed here is to combine those facts into a measurable inverse on the Sine spectral-measure support, with the exact endpoint/gauge convention used by the random operator.

If this theorem is established, then the construction

\[
(\mathcal S_{2/k},U,(G_m))
\mapsto
\mu_+^{\mathrm{dec}}
\mapsto
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Sine\,norm}}(\mu_+^{\mathrm{dec}})
\tag{6.1}
\]

is an explicit continuum operator coupling and would move `E_decim` from `0` to `1` in the **bulk** regime.
