The standard Sine-beta operator already carries, in its left spectral measure, an iid sequence of Gamma norming weights independent of its Sine-beta eigenvalues. Therefore:

1. decimate the **eigenvalues** exactly as Forrester dictates;
2. deterministically transport the corresponding retained **source spectral weights** from the Gamma law of shape `1/k` to the Gamma law of shape `k`;
3. apply the unique normalized inverse spectral transform for the Sine Dirac gauge.

This produces the target operator as a deterministic measurable function of the source operator itself.

The resulting operator map is

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D_k^{\rm op}(T_-)
=
\Phi^{-1}\!\left(
\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}
\mathcal Q_k(w^-_{km})\,
\delta_{\xi^-_{km}/k}
\right),
}
\]

where `Phi` is the left-spectral-measure map in the standard normalized Sine Dirac gauge and `Q_k` is an explicit one-dimensional Gamma quantile transport defined below.

The rest of this report proves that this formula is well-defined almost surely and sends

T_-\sim\mathtt{Sine}_{2/k}

## 3. B30 — localize the finite identity and pass the anchored decimation to the bulk

Fix an integer `k>=2` and set

\[
\beta_-:=\frac{2}{k},\qquad \beta_+:=2k.
\]

### 3.1 Exact finite input

Forrester's Theorem 2, final circular identity, is

\[
D_k\!\left(CE^0_{2/k,kN}\right)=CE^0_{2k,N}.
\]

Here the circular angles are ordered relative to the cut at zero,

\[
0<\theta_1<\theta_2<\cdots<\theta_{kN}<2\pi,
\]

and `D_k` keeps

\[
\theta_k,\theta_{2k},\ldots,\theta_{Nk}.
\]

Thus the residue class is not an unspecified random choice: the finite theorem itself supplies the anchor through the fixed cut.

### 3.2 Correct bulk scaling

Valkó–Virág's circular operator normalization scales a size-`n` circular angle by `n` (up to the common convention-dependent `2pi` intensity factor, which is the same on both sides). Therefore a retained source angle has source bulk coordinate

\[
(kN)\theta_{km,N}^{-},
\]

while the same angle, viewed as a target size-`N` point, has target bulk coordinate

\[
N\theta_{km,N}^{-}
=\frac1k\big((kN)\theta_{km,N}^{-}\big).
\]

Hence the limiting decimation necessarily includes the spatial compression `1/k`.

### 3.3 Anchored infinite indexing

For a locally finite simple configuration with no point at zero, index it by

\[
\cdots<\xi^-_{-1}<\xi^-_0<0<\xi^-_1<\xi^-_2<\cdots.
\]

The finite circular cut has the following two-sided limit: the positive retained points are the `k,2k,...` points to the right of the cut, while the point just to the left of zero comes from the last retained finite circular point, and the remaining negative retained points step backwards in increments of `k`. Thus the anchored limit is

\[
D_k^0(\Xi^-)
:=
\left\{\frac{\xi^-_{km}}{k}:m\in\mathbb Z\right\}.
\]

### 3.4 Distributional identity for the limiting support

Use the Valkó–Virág coupling of the finite circular `beta_-` operators to the standard `Sine_{beta_-}` operator. Their operator convergence implies pointwise convergence of the indexed finite spectra on every fixed compact spectral window. The map taking an anchored simple indexed configuration to its every-`k`th subconfiguration is continuous at configurations with no point at the cut, and a Sine-beta process has probability zero of a point exactly at zero.

Therefore the decimated finite target supports converge under this coupling to `D_k^0(Xi^-)`. But for every finite `N`, Forrester's identity says that the decimated support has exactly the size-`N` circular `beta_+` law. Its bulk limit is `Sine_{beta_+}`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
D_k^0(\operatorname{Sine}_{2/k})
\overset d=
\operatorname{Sine}_{2k}.
}
\]

This is a point-process statement only, so by itself it would remain PARTIAL. It is used as one ingredient in the operator construction below.

## 4. B10 — derandomize the norming weights

The decisive extra structure comes from Valkó–Virág's 2022/2023 spectral-measure result.

For their `tau_beta` representation, the weights of the right spectral measure are independent of one another and independent of the eigenvalue sequence, and each has Gamma distribution with shape `beta/2` and mean `2`. Their Corollary 40 identifies the right spectral measure of `tau_beta` in law with the **left** spectral measure of the standard `Sine_beta` operator. Hence the standard Sine operator may be written spectrally as

\[
\Phi(T_\beta)
=
\mu_\beta
=
\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}w_j\,\delta_{\xi_j},
\]

where

\[
(\xi_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\sim\operatorname{Sine}_\beta,
\]

and, independently,

\[
w_j\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}
\Gamma\!\left(\frac\beta2,\ \text{mean }2\right).
\]

For the source beta `2/k`,

\[
w_j^-\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}\Gamma(1/k,\text{ mean }2).
\]

For the target beta `2k`, the required weights are

\[
w_m^+\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}\Gamma(k,\text{ mean }2).
\]

### 4.1 A local deterministic Gamma transport

Let

\[
F_-:=F_{\Gamma(1/k,\text{ mean }2)},
\qquad
F_+:=F_{\Gamma(k,\text{ mean }2)}.
\]

Both distributions have strictly positive continuous densities on `(0,infinity)`, so `F_-` and `F_+` are continuous and strictly increasing. Define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_k(x):=F_+^{-1}(F_-(x)),\qquad x>0.
}
\]

By the probability integral transform,

\[
X\sim\Gamma(1/k,\text{ mean }2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal Q_k(X)\sim\Gamma(k,\text{ mean }2).
\]

Because `Q_k` is applied coordinatewise to an iid sequence, the transformed sequence stays iid; because the source weights are independent of the entire source spectrum, the transformed weights remain independent of the entire decimated support.

Thus define

\[
\boxed{
w_m^+:=\mathcal Q_k(w^-_{km}).}
\]

No fresh randomness has been added. Each target spectral weight is a deterministic function of the spectral weight attached to the retained source eigenvalue itself.

### 4.2 Elementary alternative without inverse CDFs

There is also an elementary nonlocal deterministic transport. A Gamma variable of shape `1/k` and mean `2` has scale `2k`. Therefore, for disjoint blocks

\[
B_m=\{k^2m,k^2m+1,\ldots,k^2m+k^2-1\},
\]

the variables

\[
\widetilde w_m^+
:=
\frac1{k^2}\sum_{j\in B_m}w_j^-
\]

are iid Gamma with shape

\[
k^2\cdot\frac1k=k
\]

and scale

\[
\frac{2k}{k^2}=\frac2k,
\]

hence mean `2`. They are again independent of the whole source spectrum. This gives a second completely elementary deterministic mark coupling. The local quantile coupling is used in the main theorem because it follows the retained eigenvalue index directly.

## 5. B11 — dualize back from the marked spectral measure to the operator

The remaining issue is whether the marked target measure determines a **standard normalized limiting Dirac operator**, rather than merely a point process plus decorations.

It does.

### 5.1 The normalized Sine Dirac class

Use the standard `Sine_beta` representation of Valkó–Virág on `[0,1)`,

\[
T=\mathtt{Dir}(R_\cdot,e_1,u_1),
\]

with zero potential, `det R(t)=1/4`, fixed left boundary direction `e_1`, and standard Sine gauge. At the regular left endpoint one has

\[
R(0)=\frac12 I.
\]

(The Brownian driving point starts at `i`, so this follows directly from the usual half-plane parametrization.)

Let `Phi(T)` denote the left spectral measure in this gauge.

### 5.2 Normalized inverse uniqueness lemma

**Lemma.** Within this standard normalized class, `Phi(T)` determines `T` uniquely.

**Proof.** Suppose `T=Dir(R,e_1,u_1)` and `T~=Dir(R~,e_1,u~_1)` have the same left spectral measure.

The left endpoint is regular, hence limit-circle. In the Eckhardt–Kostenko–Teschl inverse uniqueness theorem the bounded-type hypothesis is therefore available via the standard Cartwright/limit-circle normalization. Since the potential is identically zero, their canonical-system refinement (Corollary 4.6) gives an increasing locally absolutely continuous reparameterization `eta` and a constant matrix `Gamma_0 in SL(2,R)` such that

\[
\eta'(t)\,\widetilde R(\eta(t))
=
\Gamma_0^T R(t)\Gamma_0
\quad\text{a.e.}
\]

Take determinants. Both Hamiltonians have determinant `1/4`, and `det Gamma_0=1`, so

\[
\frac14\eta'(t)^2=\frac14.
\]

Since `eta` is increasing,

\[
\eta'(t)=1\quad\text{a.e.}
\]

Both systems live on the same normalized interval with the same regular left endpoint, so `eta(t)=t`.

Now evaluate the coefficient relation at the regular left normalization `R(0)=R~(0)=I/2`:

\[
\frac12 I
=
\Gamma_0^T\frac12 I\Gamma_0.
\]

Thus `Gamma_0 in SO(2)`. The unitary equivalence in the inverse theorem must also carry the fixed left boundary line to itself. A rotation preserving the line spanned by `e_1` is only `+I` or `-I`. Therefore

\[
\widetilde R(t)=R(t)
\quad\text{a.e.}
\]

and the boundary lines are identical. Hence the operators coincide. `QED`

### 5.3 Existence and measurability of the inverse on the target law

Let `M_+` be the set of spectral measures arising from the normalized class. The spectral-measure map `Phi` is Borel in the standard canonical-system/operator topology (equivalently, it can be read through the Weyl/spectral transform), and the normalized coefficient-path space is standard Borel. By the lemma, `Phi` is injective. Hence the Lusin–Souslin theorem gives a Borel inverse on `Phi`'s image.

For the random measure constructed below, existence is automatic almost surely because its law is exactly the law of the left spectral measure of a standard `Sine_{2k}` operator. Thus

\[
\Phi^{-1}(\mu^+)
\]

is a well-defined measurable random operator.

This is not a choice of an arbitrary operator with the desired spectrum; it is the unique standard normalized Sine-gauge inverse spectral reconstruction from the full eigenvalue-plus-norming data.

## 6. Main theorem — deterministic operator-level beta decimation in the bulk

**Theorem (bulk operator decimation).** Let `k>=2` be an integer. There is a deterministic measurable map

\[
\mathfrak D_k^{\rm op}:\mathcal S_{2/k}\to\mathcal S_{2k}
\]

on a full-probability set of standard Sine Dirac operators with the following property. If

\[
T_-\sim\mathtt{Sine}_{2/k},
\]

then

\[
T_+:=\mathfrak D_k^{\rm op}(T_-)
\sim\mathtt{Sine}_{2k},
\]

and, pathwise,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}(T_+)
=
\left\{\frac{\xi^-_{km}}{k}:m\in\mathbb Z\right\},
}
\]

where

\[
\cdots<\xi^-_0<0<\xi^-_1<\cdots
\]

is the anchored ordered spectrum of `T_-`.

More explicitly, if

\[
\Phi(T_-)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}w_j^-\delta_{\xi_j^-},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D_k^{\rm op}(T_-)
:=
\Phi^{-1}\!\left(
\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}
\mathcal Q_k(w^-_{km})
\,\delta_{\xi^-_{km}/k}
\right),
}
\]

with

\[
\mathcal Q_k
=F_{\Gamma(k,\text{mean }2)}^{-1}
\circ
F_{\Gamma(1/k,\text{mean }2)}.
\]

### Proof

Take `T_-~Sine_{2/k}` in the standard operator representation and write its left spectral measure as

\[
\mu_-=
\sum_j w_j^-\delta_{\xi_j^-}.
\]

By Valkó–Virág, `(w_j^-)` is iid Gamma(shape `1/k`, mean `2`) and independent of the entire eigenvalue process.

By the finite Forrester circular identity followed through the anchored circular-to-Sine bulk scaling,

\[
\eta_m:=\frac{\xi^-_{km}}{k}
\]

has the joint point-process law `Sine_{2k}`.

Define

\[
v_m:=\mathcal Q_k(w^-_{km}).
\]

The probability integral transform gives

\[
v_m\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}\Gamma(k,\text{ mean }2).
\]

Moreover `(v_m)` is independent of the whole sequence `(eta_m)` because the entire source weight sequence is independent of the entire source spectrum.

Therefore the random marked measure

\[
\mu_+
:=
\sum_m v_m\delta_{\eta_m}
\]

has exactly the law of the left spectral measure of the standard `Sine_{2k}` operator: its support has the `Sine_{2k}` law and, independently, its weights are iid Gamma(shape `k`, mean `2`), exactly as required by Valkó–Virág Proposition 3 plus Corollary 40.

By the normalized inverse uniqueness lemma, `mu_+` has a unique standard Sine-gauge operator inverse almost surely. Define

\[
T_+:=\Phi^{-1}(\mu_+).
\]

Since `mu_+` has exactly the target spectral-measure law and `Phi^{-1}` is deterministic and measurable on that law's support,

\[
T_+\overset d=\mathtt{Sine}_{2k}.
\]

Finally, by construction the support of `mu_+`, hence the spectrum of `T_+`, is precisely

\[
\{\xi^-_{km}/k:m\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]

Thus the two limiting **operators** are coupled on one probability space, indeed deterministically from the source operator, and the coupling realizes the beta `2/k -> 2k` decimation pathwise in the bulk. `QED`
