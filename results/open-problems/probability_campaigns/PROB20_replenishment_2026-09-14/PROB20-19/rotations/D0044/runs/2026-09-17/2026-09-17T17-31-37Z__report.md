## 3. Primary-source evidence: Virág uses “coupling” for both spectral-measure and path-level constructions

Fresh primary-source read dated **2026-09-17** used the ICM/arXiv PDF itself.

### 3.1 The review explicitly treats spectral measure as operator data

In the opening tridiagonal discussion Virág writes the spectral measure

\[
\sigma=\sum_i q_i\,\delta_{\lambda_i},
\]

explains that the Jacobi matrix `T` **encodes the spectral measure**, and states that the map from Jacobi coefficients to eigenvalues plus logarithmic weights is a bijection. Thus the review itself treats eigenvalues plus norming weights as complete coordinates for the finite operator representative, not merely as a point-process statistic.

This matters for D0044: the current map does not stop after decimating support. It transforms the complete marked spectral measure and then reconstructs the uniquely normalized limiting operator.

### 3.2 Theorem 4.1 is literally titled “Coupling of the beta-Hermite ensembles” and is spectral-measure based

Section 4 gives a particularly direct source-internal precedent. Virág observes that the finite beta-Hermite matrices are minors of one semi-infinite Jacobi matrix and that, conversely, a self-adjoint Jacobi matrix recovers its measure as the spectral measure at the first coordinate. He then states:

**Theorem 4.1 (Coupling of the beta-Hermite ensembles).** There exists a random measure `mu_beta` such that, for every `n`, the zeros of the orthogonal polynomial for `mu_beta` have the law of the `n`-point beta-Hermite eigenvalues.

Immediately afterward Question 2 asks whether other Dyson beta ensembles can be **“coupled this way.”**

The important point is structural: in Virág's own terminology, a construction organized through a common spectral measure is explicitly called a **coupling**. It is not described as invalid merely because it is not a local transformation of a Brownian coefficient path.

### 3.3 The same review also uses “coupling” for a direct Brownian-path construction

Section 11 gives the other style. For circular beta, Virág writes that, given a hyperbolic Brownian motion path `B`, the discrete hyperbolic walk construction suggests an **efficient coupling**, and Theorem 11.1 then couples finite circular operators to their limiting operator with norm-resolvent convergence.

So the review itself uses “coupling” in at least two mathematically different modes:

1. a spectral-measure/Jacobi construction (Theorem 4.1 and Question 2), and
2. a direct common-Brownian/path construction (Section 11).

This source evidence substantially weakens the claim that the bare word “coupled” in Question 6 secretly *must* mean a local coefficient/Brownian transformation. It does **not** by itself decide how much explicitness Virág intended in the adjective “explicitly.”
