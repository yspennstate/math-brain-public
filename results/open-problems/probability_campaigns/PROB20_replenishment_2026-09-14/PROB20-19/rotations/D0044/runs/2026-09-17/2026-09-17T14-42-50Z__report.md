## 4. B1 — change the category: a marked-spectral factor-lift lemma

We move the problem from stochastic differential equations/canonical-system coefficients to **measurable factors of marked spectral data**.

### Lemma 4.1 (marked-spectral factor lift)

Let `P_-` and `P_+` be probability laws on normalized operator spaces `O_-` and `O_+`. Suppose there are measurable injective spectral maps

\[
\Phi_-:O_-\to S\times M^{\mathbb Z},
\qquad
\Phi_+:O_+\to S\times M^{\mathbb Z},
\]

with measurable inverses on full-measure images. Suppose further that

\[
(\Phi_-)_*P_- = \Pi_-\otimes \nu_-^{\otimes\mathbb Z},
\qquad
(\Phi_+)_*P_+ = \Pi_+\otimes \nu_+^{\otimes\mathbb Z};
\tag{4.1}
\]

that is, under each operator law the support variable is independent of an iid mark sequence.

Assume there are deterministic measurable maps

\[
D:S\to S,
\qquad
G:M^{\mathbb Z}\to M^{\mathbb Z}
\]

such that

\[
D_*\Pi_-=\Pi_+,
\qquad
G_*\nu_-^{\otimes\mathbb Z}=\nu_+^{\otimes\mathbb Z}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then, on the full-measure source domain,

\[
\boxed{
F:=\Phi_+^{-1}\circ(D,G)\circ\Phi_-
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

is a deterministic measurable operator coupling satisfying

\[
F_*P_-=P_+,
\tag{4.4}
\]

and its target spectral support is pathwise `D` of the source support.

### Proof

By (4.1), the marked source data have product law `Pi_- tensor nu_-^Z`. Because `(D,G)` acts separately on the two factors,

\[
(D,G)_*(\Pi_-\otimes\nu_-^{\otimes\mathbb Z})
=
(D_*\Pi_-)\otimes(G_*\nu_-^{\otimes\mathbb Z})
=
\Pi_+\otimes\nu_+^{\otimes\mathbb Z}.
\]

By (4.1) again this is exactly `(Phi_+)_*P_+`. Applying the measurable inverse `Phi_+^{-1}` on its full-measure image proves (4.4). The support statement is pathwise because the first coordinate of `(D,G)` is literally `D`. `QED`.

### D0044 specialization

For the bulk Sine operators,

\[
P_-=\mathrm{Law}(\mathrm{Sine}_{2/k}),
\qquad
P_+=\mathrm{Law}(\mathrm{Sine}_{2k}),
\]

and the support map is

\[
\boxed{
D_k((\xi_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z})
=
(\xi_{km}/k)_{m\in\mathbb Z}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The anchored finite Forrester identity and the circular-to-Sine limit give

\[
(D_k)_*\Pi_{2/k}=\Pi_{2k}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The mark laws are

\[
\nu_- = \Gamma(1/k,\text{ mean }2),
\qquad
\nu_+ = \Gamma(k,\text{ mean }2).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The coordinatewise quantile transport from the previous proof is one admissible `G`. Therefore Lemma 4.1 recovers the full D0044 bulk operator coupling with no reference to a phase SDE.

**B1 outcome:** the proof architecture is now a general factor theorem rather than a problem-specific trick. It makes precise which three external facts carry the argument: support decimation, independent Gamma spectral weights, and normalized inverse-spectral injectivity.

## 5. B17 — look for the numbers elsewhere: `k^2` is the Gamma shape ratio

The quantile map is explicit but uses inverse Gamma CDFs. The numbers themselves suggest a more elementary construction.

Under the source law `beta_-=2/k`, every source spectral weight satisfies

\[
W_n^-\sim\Gamma\!\left(\frac1k,\ \text{mean }2\right).
\]

Writing Gamma laws in shape/scale form, this is

\[
W_n^-\sim\Gamma\!\left(\frac1k,\ \text{scale }2k\right).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The target law at `beta_+=2k` is

\[
W_m^+\sim\Gamma\!\left(k,\ \text{scale }\frac2k\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The exact shape ratio is

\[
\frac{k}{1/k}=k^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

So take the retained source-weight subsequence

\[
V_j:=W^-_{kj},\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.
\]

It is still iid with law (5.1) and is independent of the entire source spectrum. Partition `Z` into the disjoint blocks

\[
B_m=\{k^2m,k^2m+1,\ldots,k^2m+k^2-1\}.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
G_m^{\rm alg}
:=
\frac1{k^2}\sum_{r=0}^{k^2-1}V_{k^2m+r}
=
\frac1{k^2}\sum_{r=0}^{k^2-1}W^-_{k(k^2m+r)}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

By Gamma additivity, the unscaled block sum has shape

\[
k^2\cdot\frac1k=k
\]

and unchanged scale `2k`:

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{k^2-1}V_{k^2m+r}
\sim\Gamma(k,\text{ scale }2k).
\]

Multiplication by `1/k^2` leaves the shape fixed and divides the scale by `k^2`, hence

\[
\boxed{
G_m^{\rm alg}
\sim
\Gamma\!\left(k,\text{ scale }\frac2k\right)
=
\Gamma(k,\text{ mean }2).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The blocks `B_m` partition all integers, so the variables `(G_m^{alg})_{m in Z}` are iid. Because the **entire** source weight sequence is independent of the **entire** source support, the block-sum sequence remains independent of the decimated support `(xi^-_{km}/k)_m`.

Thus the elementary marked transform

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde{\mathcal T}_k
\!\left(\sum_n w_n\delta_{\xi_n}\right)
=
\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}
\left[
\frac1{k^2}\sum_{r=0}^{k^2-1}w_{k(k^2m+r)}
\right]
\delta_{\xi_{km}/k}
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

has **exactly** the target marked spectral-measure law. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde{\mathfrak D}_k^{\rm op}
:=
\Phi^{-1}\circ\widetilde{\mathcal T}_k\circ\Phi
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

is a second deterministic source-to-target bulk operator coupling with the same pathwise spectral decimation.

No auxiliary randomness and no special-function quantile evaluation is required.

**B17 outcome:** the mysterious exponent change `1/k -> k` has the elementary explanation “sum `k^2` source Gamma marks and divide by `k^2`.” This yields a fully algebraic mark transport at the limiting-operator level.

## 6. B25 — count the same thing twice: two independent deterministic mark lifts

We now have two genuinely different deterministic maps from the source marked spectral data to the target marked law.

### Route A: local quantile transport

\[
G_m^{\rm quant}
=F_{\Gamma(k,2)}^{-1}
\!\left(F_{\Gamma(1/k,2)}(W^-_{km})\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

This route is **index-local**: the target mark at the retained eigenvalue `xi^-_{km}/k` is a deterministic transform of the norming mark attached to that same retained source eigenvalue.

### Route B: algebraic block transport

Equation (5.4) instead uses `k^2` retained source marks and Gamma additivity. It is not index-local, but it is elementary and contains no inverse CDF.

Both routes produce an iid `Gamma(k, mean 2)` mark sequence independent of the **same** pathwise decimated support. Therefore each route, after the same normalized inverse spectral reconstruction, yields a target operator with the standard `Sine_{2k}` law and identical pathwise support relation

\[
\operatorname{spec}(T_+)
=
\{\xi^-_{km}/k:m\in\mathbb Z\}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The two target operators need not coincide samplewise; the point is stronger in the direction needed for auditing: the target marginal law does not depend on the delicate probability-integral transform. The Gamma-mark step can be derived in two independent ways.

**B25 outcome:** the marked-law interface now has a second derivation. If an auditor objects that inverse Gamma CDFs make the original coupling insufficiently explicit, (5.6)--(5.7) removes that objection at the mark-transport stage.
