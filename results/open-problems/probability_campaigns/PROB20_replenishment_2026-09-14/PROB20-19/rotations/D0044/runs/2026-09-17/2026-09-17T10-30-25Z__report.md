## 5. B12 — normalized inverse uniqueness for the tau class

### Proposition (right spectral measure is injective on the tau-normalized class)

Consider Dirac operators

`T = Dir(R, e_1, u_q)` on `(0,1]`,

where:

- `Q=0` in the weighted-Dirac notation;
- `R(t)` is positive definite and locally regular with `det R(t)=1/4`;
- `R` extends continuously to the regular endpoint `t=1` and `R(1)=I/2`;
- the left boundary direction at `t=0` is the fixed line `R e_1`;
- the right boundary direction at `t=1` is `u_q=(-q,-1)^T`, with `q in R union {infinity}`.

Within this class, equality of the **right spectral measures with their standard endpoint normalization** implies equality of the operators (including the boundary parameter `q`), modulo the harmless global sign `±I` on eigenfunctions.

### Proof

Take two such operators `T_1,T_2` with the same right spectral measure.

**Step 1: reverse the interval.** By Valkó–Virág Lemma 20, the path reversal/conjugation

`T -> T^rev = rho^{-1} S T S rho`

turns the original right spectral measure into the reversed left spectral measure. Therefore `T_1^rev,T_2^rev` have equal left spectral measures. Their reversed left endpoint is the original regular endpoint `t=1`, so both reversed Hamiltonians satisfy

`R_1^rev(0)=R_2^rev(0)=I/2`.

The reversed right boundary is obtained from the original fixed left boundary `e_1`; hence the two reversed operators have the same right boundary line.

**Step 2: apply inverse uniqueness.** The reversed left endpoint is regular, hence limit-circle. The bounded-type condition required by Eckhardt–Kostenko–Teschl Corollary 4.6 is therefore available by their Cartwright/limit-circle discussion. Since both potentials are zero and the left spectral measures agree, Corollary 4.6 yields an increasing locally absolutely continuous bijection `eta:(0,1)->(0,1)` and a constant `Gamma in SL(2,R)` such that

`eta'(t) R_2^rev(eta(t)) = Gamma^T R_1^rev(t) Gamma` a.e.

**Step 3: determinant normalization kills time change.** Taking determinants and using `det R_i^rev=1/4`, `det Gamma=1`, gives

`(eta'(t))^2 /4 = 1/4`.

As `eta` is increasing, `eta'(t)=1` a.e. Since it is a bijection of the same interval, `eta(t)=t`.

**Step 4: endpoint normalization reduces the constant gauge to SO(2).** Letting `t downarrow 0` in the weight identity and using `R_i^rev(0)=I/2` gives

`I/2 = Gamma^T (I/2) Gamma`,

so `Gamma^T Gamma=I`. Together with `det Gamma=1`, this says `Gamma in SO(2)`.

**Step 5: the common right boundary kills the rotation.** The unitary equivalence supplied by Corollary 4.6 maps the domain/boundary condition of one reversed operator to the other. Their reversed right boundary line is the same line `R e_1` (up to the fixed `S` sign convention, which does not change the line). Thus `Gamma e_1` must be collinear with `e_1`. The only rotations in `SO(2)` with this property are `Gamma=I` and `Gamma=-I`.

Therefore the Hamiltonians agree, the reversed left boundary lines agree, and consequently the original right boundary parameters agree. The remaining `-I` changes every eigenfunction sign and does not change the operator. This proves injectivity.

### Why the tau_beta operators lie in this class

For Valkó–Virág's `tau_beta`,

`R = X^T X/(2 det X)`, `X=[[1,-x],[0,y]]`.

Hence `det R=1/4`. At `t=1`, the logarithmic Brownian time is `u(1)=0`; standard Brownian normalization gives `x_1=0`, `y_1=1`, and therefore `R(1)=I/2` deterministically. The left boundary is `[1,0]^T`; the right boundary is `[-q,-1]^T`. Thus the proposition applies almost surely.

`mu_k^dec =_d mu_right,tau_{2k}`.

By the injectivity proposition above, there is at most one tau-normalized operator producing each measure in the image. Since `mu_k^dec` has exactly the law of a genuine target right spectral measure, it belongs to that image almost surely.
