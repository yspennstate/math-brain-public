## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The historical scalar Brownian-carousel transform is too rigid: it tries to manufacture the beta change by altering one phase process. The 2023 Sine-beta Palm/spectral-measure theorem suggests a different category entirely.

A one-boundary Dirac/canonical operator is encoded not merely by its eigenvalue support but by a spectral measure consisting of eigenvalues **and norming weights**. For Sine_beta, the right spectral weights are independent iid Gamma(beta/2) variables. Therefore finite spectral decimation can be lifted from "delete points" to "delete points, then attach the target-beta norming weights", after which inverse spectral / canonical-system machinery can in principle reconstruct the target operator.

This produces two complementary attacks in one run:

## 4. B20 — exact no-go theorem for deterministic phase division / time change

Use Virág's Brownian-carousel phase SDE after logarithmic time change. In the standard notation one may write the beta-dependent family as

\[
d\alpha^{(\beta)}_\lambda(t)
=\lambda f_\beta(t)\,dt
+\Re\!\left((e^{-i\alpha^{(\beta)}_\lambda(t)}-1)dZ_t\right),
\qquad
f_\beta(t)=\frac\beta4 e^{-\beta t/4}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently the one-dimensional marginal noise coefficient is

\[
2\sin\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)dW_t.
\]

Fix integer `k>=2`, source beta

\[
\beta_- = \frac2k,
\]

and target beta

\[
\beta_+=2k.
\]

Consider the broad deterministic scalar ansatz containing the old attempted idea:

\[
\widetilde\alpha_\lambda(t)
=\frac1k\alpha^{(\beta_-)}_{c\lambda}(\tau(t)),
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `c>0` is an arbitrary fixed spectral rescaling and `tau` is any deterministic increasing C1 time change.

Applying deterministic time change to (4.1) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
d\widetilde\alpha_\lambda(t)
={}&\frac{c}{k}\lambda f_{\beta_-}(\tau(t))\tau'(t)\,dt\\
&+\frac{\sqrt{\tau'(t)}}{k}
\Re\!\left((e^{-ik\widetilde\alpha_\lambda(t)}-1)d\widetilde Z_t\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For this process to have the target beta-plus phase diffusion law, equality of the phase-dependent quadratic-variation coefficient would require, for every phase value `theta`,

\[
\frac{\tau'(t)}{k^2}|e^{-ik\theta}-1|^2
=|e^{-i\theta}-1|^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\frac{\tau'(t)}{k^2}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{k\theta}{2}\right)
=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Taking `theta -> 0` forces

\[
\tau'(t)=1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

But at

\[
\theta=\frac{2\pi}{k}
\]

the left side of (4.5) is zero whereas the right side equals

\[
\sin^2(\pi/k)>0.
\]

This is a contradiction.

### B20 theorem

For every integer `k>=2`, **no transform of the form**

\[
\widetilde\alpha_\lambda(t)=k^{-1}\alpha^{(2/k)}_{c\lambda}(\tau(t))
\]

with deterministic time change `tau` and fixed spectral scaling `c` can have the Brownian-carousel phase diffusion law for beta `2k`.

There is a second independent mismatch already infinitesimally: local phase diffusion matching forces `tau'=1`, while drift matching would require

\[
\frac{c}{k} f_{2/k}(t)=f_{2k}(t)
\quad\text{for all }t,
\]

which is impossible for constant `c` because the ratio of these two exponential functions depends nontrivially on `t` whenever `k>=2`.

This is stronger than merely observing that one attempted time change gives wrong coefficients. It rules out the entire deterministic scalar phase-division/time-change class. It is still only an obstruction to one class of couplings, not an impossibility theorem for Question 6 itself.

## 5. B21 — transfer canonical-system machinery through the spectral measure

The key later theorem is the 2023 Sine-beta spectral-measure description. For a standard Sine-beta Dirac operator, its right spectral measure can be represented as

\[
\mu_\beta^R=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}G_n\,\delta_{\xi_n},
\tag{5.1}
\]

where

- the support `(xi_n)` has Sine_beta law;
- conditional on the support, the `G_n` are iid Gamma variables;
- the Gamma shape parameter is `beta/2` (with the paper's mean-two normalization);
- the weights are independent of the eigenvalues.

For the target beta `beta_+=2k`, the target right spectral weights therefore have shape

\[
\frac{\beta_+}{2}=k.
\]

Now suppose we have a stationary/anchored every-kth decimation operation `D_{k,U}` on the source Sine process, with residue `U` chosen as needed to make the infinite indexing translation-covariant, and suppose the point-process limit of Forrester's finite relation gives

\[
D_{k,U}(\operatorname{Sine}_{2/k})\ \stackrel d=\ \operatorname{Sine}_{2k}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then, **on the same probability space**, start with the source points `(xi_n^-)`, retain `(xi_{km+U}^-)`, and attach fresh iid weights

\[
G_m\sim \mathrm{Gamma}(k,\text{mean }2)
\]

independently of the source process. Define

\[
\boxed{
\mu_+^{\mathrm{dec}}
=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}G_m\,\delta_{\xi^-_{km+U}}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

By (5.2) and the 2023 independence theorem, this random measure has **exactly the target right-spectral-measure law** of the standard Sine_{2k} operator.

This is a genuine strengthening over a point-process identity. It upgrades support decimation to full one-boundary spectral data: locations plus independent target-beta norming constants.

General de Branges and weighted-Dirac inverse spectral theorems give uniqueness up to the appropriate gauge/reparametrization under their hypotheses, but this run has not yet checked every singular Sine-beta normalization, boundary convention, measurability clause, and infinite-volume growth condition needed to turn (5.3) into the exact standard normalized Sine_{2k} operator as a measurable explicit transform.

## 6. B4 — exact finite circular operator lift of Forrester decimation

The simplest source-valid setting is the circular/bulk finite approximation because both Forrester's finite decimation and the Valkó--Virág finite Dirac realization are explicit there.

Let

\[
\beta_-=\frac2k,
\qquad
\beta_+=2k,
\]

and take the finite circular beta-minus Dirac operator of size `kN`. The finite Killip--Nenciu spectral-measure description gives a measure

\[
\mu^-_N=\sum_{j=1}^{kN}w^-_j\,\delta_{z^-_j},
\tag{6.1}
\]

whose support `(z_j^-)` has the circular beta-minus ensemble law and whose weights are independent Dirichlet variables with parameter beta-minus/2.

Forrester's finite circular decimation relation gives, with the exact source's appropriate every-kth/alternating convention,

\[
X^+_N:=D_k(\{z^-_j\}_{j=1}^{kN})
\stackrel d=
\mathrm{C}\beta\mathrm{E}_{\beta_+,N}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Now, **on the same finite probability space**, discard the old weights and independently attach

\[
(w^+_1,\ldots,w^+_N)
\sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(k,\ldots,k),
\tag{6.3}
\]

because

\[
\beta_+/2=k.
\]

Set

\[
\mu^+_N=\sum_{m=1}^{N}w^+_m\,\delta_{x^+_m}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

By (6.2)--(6.3), `mu_N^+` has **exactly** the finite Killip--Nenciu spectral-measure law for circular beta-plus. Apply the deterministic modified-Verblunsky / finite-Dirac reconstruction map from the circular-beta operator construction. The resulting operator `Circ^+_{beta_+,N}` therefore has exactly the standard finite beta-plus Dirac-operator law, while its spectrum is **literally the decimated support** of the coupled beta-minus finite operator.

### B4 finite theorem

For every integer `k>=2` and finite `N`, the circular Forrester decimation relation admits an exact finite **operator-level spectral lift**:

1. sample the standard finite circular beta=`2/k` Dirac model;
2. keep every kth spectral point according to Forrester's rule;
3. attach independent Dirichlet `(k,...,k)` target norming weights;
4. reconstruct the finite target Dirac operator through the standard deterministic modified-Verblunsky map.

The output is exactly the standard finite circular beta=`2k` Dirac model in law, and its spectrum is exactly the selected source spectrum.

This statement is stronger than finite point-process decimation but still does not by itself answer Virág's Question 6, which asks about **limiting operators**.
