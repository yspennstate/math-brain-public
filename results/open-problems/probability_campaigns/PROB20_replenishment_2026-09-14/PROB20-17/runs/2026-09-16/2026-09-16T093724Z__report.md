## 2. Literature/status check

Primary matrix source:

* A. Israel, F. Krahmer, R. Ward, *An arithmetic–geometric mean inequality for products of three matrices*, Linear Algebra Appl. 488 (2016), 1–12, arXiv:1411.0333, DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2015.09.013`.

Their norm-inside conjecture is stated for unitarily invariant norms; they prove product length at most three and explicitly leave extension to `m>=4` open. A bounded fresh search did not locate a later full resolution.

New combinatorial source used in this run:

* S. Moran, M. Snir, U. Manber, *Applications of Ramsey's Theorem to Decision Tree Complexity*, J. ACM 32(4) (1985), 938–949, DOI `10.1145/4221.4259`.

Their Lemma 4.4 states that a graph with `n` vertices and `m` edges has at most
\[
n\left(\frac{m}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}
\]
Hamiltonian paths. Their proof is a rooted induction: if the starting vertex has degree `d`, delete that vertex, apply induction to the residual graph with `m-d` edges, and maximize
\[
d\left(\frac{m-d}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}
\]
at `d=m/n`.

The theorem below is the exact nonnegative-edge-weight extension of that argument.

# 3. Weighted Moran--Snir--Manber Hamiltonian-path theorem

Let \(x_{ij}=x_{ji}\ge0\) for \(i\ne j\), and put \(x_{ii}=0\). Define the total edge mass
\[
W:=\sum_{1\le i<j\le n}x_{ij}.
\]

Define the **directed Hamiltonian-path polynomial**
\[
D_n(x):=
\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}x_{\sigma(r),\sigma(r+1)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
For a fixed starting vertex \(v\), define
\[
D_{n,v}(x):=
\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in S_n\\ \sigma(1)=v}}
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}x_{\sigma(r),\sigma(r+1)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — rooted weighted path bound

For every \(n\ge2\), every nonnegative symmetric edge-weight array, and every starting vertex \(v\),
\[
\boxed{
D_{n,v}(x)\le
\left(\frac{W}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
D_n(x)\le
n\left(\frac{W}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

We prove (3.3) by induction on \(n\).

For \(n=2\), there is one possible directed Hamiltonian path from the chosen start, with weight equal to the only edge weight. Thus \(D_{2,v}=W\), exactly the right side of (3.3).

Assume the result for every weighted graph on \(n\) vertices. Consider \(n+1\) vertices and fix a starting vertex \(v\). Let
\[
d_v:=\sum_{u\ne v}x_{vu}
\]
be its weighted degree. Deleting \(v\) leaves a weighted graph on \(n\) vertices whose total edge mass is exactly
\[
W-d_v.
\]

Every Hamiltonian path starting at \(v\) first takes some edge \(v\to u\), and is then a Hamiltonian path in the residual graph starting at \(u\). Therefore, exactly,
\[
D_{n+1,v}(x)
=
\sum_{u\ne v}x_{vu}\,
D^{(V\setminus\{v\})}_{n,u}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
By the induction hypothesis, each residual rooted sum is at most
\[
\left(\frac{W-d_v}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
\]
Hence
\[
D_{n+1,v}(x)
\le
d_v\left(\frac{W-d_v}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

It remains to maximize the scalar function
\[
f(d)=d(W-d)^{n-1},\qquad 0\le d\le W.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
f'(d)
=
(W-d)^{n-2}(W-nd),
\]
so the maximum occurs at \(d=W/n\). Substituting into (3.6),
\[
D_{n+1,v}(x)
\le
\frac{W}{n}
\left(
\frac{W-W/n}{n-1}
\right)^{n-1}
=
\left(\frac{W}{n}\right)^n.
\]
This is precisely (3.3) with \(n+1\) in place of \(n\). The induction is complete.

Finally,
\[
D_n(x)=\sum_{v=1}^nD_{n,v}(x),
\]
so summing (3.3) over starts proves (3.4). \(\square\)

### Remarks

1. Setting all nonzero edge weights equal to `1` recovers Moran--Snir--Manber Lemma 4.4.
2. No Gram-matrix condition is used. The theorem is valid for arbitrary nonnegative symmetric edge weights.
3. Zeros are allowed, so sparse supports are included without a limiting argument.

# 4. Degree-sensitive refinement

The first induction step retains more information than the global theorem.

Let
\[
d_v=\sum_{u\ne v}x_{vu}.
\]
For \(n\ge3\), applying Theorem 3.1 to the residual graph after deleting \(v\) gives
\[
D_{n,v}(x)
\le
d_v
\left(
\frac{W-d_v}{n-2}
\right)^{n-2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
D_n(x)
\le
\sum_{v=1}^n
d_v
\left(
\frac{W-d_v}{n-2}
\right)^{n-2}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is always at least as strong as the global bound (3.4): for fixed \(W\), the scalar function
\[
d\left(\frac{W-d}{n-2}\right)^{n-2}
\]
is maximized at \(d=W/(n-1)\), where its value is
\[
\left(\frac{W}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
\]
Thus summing the individual maxima recovers (3.4).

Equation (4.2) is useful when the overlap graph is inhomogeneous.

# 5. Application to the exact rank-one reduction

Let
\[
A_i=a_i u_i u_i^*,
\qquad
a_i>0,\qquad
\|u_i\|=1,
\]
and define the absolute Gram kernel
\[
g_{ij}=|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|,
\qquad
g_{ii}=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every word,
\[
\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n}\|
=
\left(\prod_{r=1}^na_{i_r}\right)
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}g_{i_ri_{r+1}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

On the all-distinct side,
\[
L_n
=
\left(\prod_i a_i\right)H_n(G),
\tag{5.3}
\]
where
\[
H_n(G)
=
\frac1{n!}
\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}g_{\sigma(r),\sigma(r+1)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Put
\[
W:=\sum_{i<j}g_{ij},
\qquad
\bar g:=\frac{W}{\binom n2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Applying Theorem 3.1 to the off-diagonal edge weights \(g_{ij}\) gives
\[
\boxed{
H_n(G)
\le
\frac1{(n-1)!}
\left(\frac{W}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Since
\[
\frac{W}{n-1}=\frac n2\,\bar g,
\]
this is equivalently
\[
\boxed{
H_n(G)
\le
C_n^{\rm path}\,\bar g^{\,n-1},
\qquad
C_n^{\rm path}
=
\frac{(n/2)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The degree-sensitive version is
\[
\boxed{
H_n(G)
\le
U_{\deg}(G)
:=
\frac1{n!}
\sum_{v=1}^n
d_v
\left(
\frac{W-d_v}{n-2}
\right)^{n-2},
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
where \(d_v=\sum_{u\ne v}g_{vu}\).

# 7. Improved all-scale rank-one closure theorem

Recall the direct Blakley--Roy lower bound from the previous verified checkpoint.

Let
\[
S=\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
\mu_a=\frac{a^TGa}{S^2},
\qquad
\kappa(a)=
\frac{(S/n)^n}{\prod_i a_i}\ge1.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Then
\[
R_n
:=
\frac1{n^n}
\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}
\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n}\|
\ge
\left(\frac Sn\right)^n
\mu_a^{\,n-1}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Combining (5.3), (5.7), and (7.2) gives:

## Theorem 7.1

The full owner inequality holds for a scaled rank-one PSD family whenever
\[
\boxed{
C_n^{\rm path}\bar g^{\,n-1}
\le
\kappa(a)\mu_a^{\,n-1},
\qquad
C_n^{\rm path}
=
\frac{(n/2)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\bar g
\le
\frac{
\kappa(a)^{1/(n-1)}\mu_a
}{
(C_n^{\rm path})^{1/(n-1)}
}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The degree-sensitive sufficient condition
\[
\boxed{
U_{\deg}(G)
\le
\kappa(a)\mu_a^{\,n-1}
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
is at least as strong.

# 9. Strict worked example: `n=5` two-cluster rank-one family

Take equal scales and five rank-one directions arranged in two duplicate clusters of sizes \(2\) and \(3\). Within each cluster the absolute overlap is \(1\), while every cross-cluster overlap is
\[
c=\frac13.
\]
This is realizable: take two unit vectors with absolute inner product \(1/3\), duplicate the first twice and the second three times.

There are four within-cluster edges of weight \(1\) and six cross edges of weight \(1/3\). Therefore
\[
W=4+6\cdot\frac13=6,
\qquad
\bar g=\frac6{10}=0.6.
\tag{9.1}
\]

The previous mean-overlap spanning-tree threshold at \(n=5\) was
\[
0.4982490195796578,
\]
so it does **not** certify this family.

The new path threshold is
\[
0.6069756595508077,
\]
so the new theorem **does** certify it.

The older global overlap-band theorem also fails badly:
\[
g_+=1,\qquad
g_-=\frac13,
\]
while that theorem requires
\[
g_+
\le
\frac{1+4g_-}{5}
=
\frac7{15}
\approx0.4667.
\]

Direct enumeration gives
\[
H_5
=
0.11604938271604921.
\tag{9.2}
\]

The new global weighted-path bound gives
\[
H_5
\le
\frac1{4!}\left(\frac64\right)^4
=

\tag{9.3}
\]

The weighted degrees are
\[

\]
and the degree-sensitive bound sharpens (9.3) to
\[

\le
0.17046181984453587.
\tag{9.4}
\]

For equal scales,
\[
\mu=\frac1{25}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}=0.68,
\qquad

\tag{9.5}

The direct Blakley--Roy theorem therefore gives

R_5\ge\mu^4.

Direct transfer evaluation gives

\tag{9.6}
\]

Thus the fully certified chain is
\[
0.1160493827
=

\le
0.1704618198
\le

<
0.21381376
\le

\tag{9.7}
\]

This is a strict example newly covered by the weighted-path/Blakley--Roy certificate but not by either the preceding tree-threshold certificate or the older overlap-band theorem.
