# 3. Exact rank-one reduction recalled

Let
\[
A_i=a_i u_i u_i^*,\qquad a_i>0,\qquad \|u_i\|=1,
\]
and define the absolute Gram kernel
\[
g_{ij}:=|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|,\qquad g_{ii}=1.
\tag{3.1}
\]
For every word,
\[
\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n}\|
=
\left(\prod_{r=1}^n a_{i_r}\right)
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}g_{i_r i_{r+1}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Set
\[
q_i:=\sqrt{a_i},\qquad
D_q:=\operatorname{diag}(q),\qquad
G:=(g_{ij}),\qquad
M:=D_q G D_q.
\tag{3.3}
\]
The preceding verified checkpoint established exactly
\[
\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}
\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n}\|
=q^T M^{n-1}q.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Thus the with-replacement average is
\[
R_n=rac1{n^n}q^TM^{n-1}q.
\tag{3.5}
\]
The distinct average is
\[
L_n=\left(\prod_i a_i\right)H_n(G),
\tag{3.6}
\]
where
\[
H_n(G):=rac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}g_{\sigma(r),\sigma(r+1)}
\tag{3.7}
\]
is the mean weight of Hamiltonian paths in the complete weighted graph with edge weights `g_ij`.

A key difficulty is that the **absolute** Gram matrix `G` need not be PSD, even though the ordinary complex Gram matrix is PSD. Hence one cannot in general apply spectral Jensen directly to `M`. The next theorem gets around exactly that obstruction.

# 4. New PSD-minorant theorem

Let `H=(h_ij)` be any real symmetric matrix satisfying
\[
\boxed{
H\succeq0,\qquad
0\le h_{ij}\le g_{ij}\quad\text{for every }i,j.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Define
\[
N:=D_q H D_q.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Then `N` is PSD and entrywise nonnegative. Moreover, because `H<=G` entrywise,
\[
0\le N\le M
\quad\text{entrywise}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

## Lemma 4.1 — powers preserve this entrywise order
For every integer `k>=1`,
\[
0\le N^k\le M^k
\quad\text{entrywise}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof
For `k=1` this is (4.3). If `0<=N^k<=M^k`, then for every `i,j`,
\[
(N^{k+1})_{ij}
=\sum_\ell (N^k)_{i\ell}N_{\ell j}
\le
\sum_\ell (M^k)_{i\ell}M_{\ell j}
=(M^{k+1})_{ij},
\]
because every factor is nonnegative and each factor from `N` is bounded by the corresponding factor from `M`. QED.

Since `q>=0`, Lemma 4.1 implies
\[
q^TM^{n-1}q\ge q^TN^{n-1}q.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Put
\[
S:=\sum_i a_i=\|q\|_2^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Because `N` is PSD, write its spectral decomposition and normalize the spectral weights induced by `q`. The function `x -> x^{n-1}` is convex on `[0,infinity)` for every `n>=2`, so scalar Jensen gives
\[
\frac{q^TN^{n-1}q}{S}
\ge
\left(\frac{q^TNq}{S}\right)^{n-1}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
But
\[
q^TNq
=q^T D_q H D_q q
=a^T H a,
\tag{4.8}
\]
where `a=(a_1,...,a_n)^T`. Therefore
\[
q^TM^{n-1}q
\ge
S\left(\frac{a^THa}{S}\right)^{n-1}
=S^{2-n}(a^THa)^{n-1}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Dividing by `n^n` proves the main lower bound.

## Theorem 4.2 — PSD-minorant walk lower bound
For every feasible `H` satisfying (4.1),
\[
\boxed{
R_n
\ge
\left(\frac Sn\right)^n
\left(\frac{a^THa}{S^2}\right)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

This theorem does **not** assume that the absolute Gram matrix `G` is PSD.

# 5. SDP certificate for the full owner inequality in the rank-one class

Define the semidefinite minorant value
\[
\boxed{
\Delta(G,a)
:=
\max\left\{
\frac{a^THa}{S^2}:
H\succeq0,\ 0\le H\le G\ \text{entrywise}
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is a semidefinite program: the objective is linear in `H`, the PSD condition is an LMI, and the entrywise bounds are linear inequalities. The feasible set is nonempty because `H=0` is feasible; in fact `H=I` is feasible since `g_ii=1` and off-diagonal entries of `I` vanish.

Theorem 4.2 immediately gives
\[
\boxed{
R_n
\ge
\left(\frac Sn\right)^n
\Delta(G,a)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Define the scale-dispersion factor
\[
\kappa(a):=
\frac{(S/n)^n}{\prod_i a_i}\ge1.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Combining (3.6) with (5.2) gives the new exact closure criterion.

## Theorem 5.1 — rank-one SDP certificate
The full norm-inside-average inequality holds for the scaled rank-one PSD family whenever
\[
\boxed{
H_n(G)
\le
\kappa(a)\,\Delta(G,a)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is a deterministic finite-dimensional certificate. It requires no commutativity, no equiangularity, no global coherence bound, and no positivity of the absolute Gram matrix itself.

Two important points:

1. `H_n(G)` is the **exact** distinct-word rank-one average after removing the common scale product.
2. `Delta(G,a)` is obtained from a convex SDP, so failure of a particular hand-chosen minorant does not imply failure of the certificate.

This theorem is a sufficient condition, not a proof that the SDP condition always holds.

# 6. Closed-form product minorants

The SDP has a useful explicit feasible family. Choose numbers
\[
0\le c_i\le1
\tag{6.1}
\]
satisfying
\[
\boxed{c_i c_j\le g_{ij}\quad(i\ne j).}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Define
\[
H_c:=\operatorname{diag}(1-c_1^2,\ldots,1-c_n^2)+cc^T.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Then
\[
(H_c)_{ii}=1,
\qquad
(H_c)_{ij}=c_i c_j\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{6.4}
\]
Hence `0<=H_c<=G` entrywise, while
\[
H_c\succeq0
\tag{6.5}
\]
by construction as a sum of a nonnegative diagonal matrix and a rank-one PSD matrix.

For this minorant,
\[
a^TH_c a
=
\sum_i a_i^2
+2\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j c_i c_j.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Define
\[
d_c(a):=
\frac{\sum_i a_i^2+2\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j c_i c_j}{S^2}.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
R_n\ge\left(\frac Sn\right)^n d_c(a)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
H_n(G)\le\kappa(a)d_c(a)^{n-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_n\le R_n.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]
A still simpler, but weaker, condition uses
\[
g_+:=\max_{i\ne j}g_{ij},
\qquad H_n(G)\le g_+^{n-1},
\]
and yields
\[
\boxed{
g_+\le d_c(a)\,\kappa(a)^{1/(n-1)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L_n\le R_n.}
\tag{6.10}
\]

### Relation to the preceding global overlap-band theorem
Take
\[
c_i=\sqrt{g_-},
\qquad
g_-:=\min_{i\ne j}g_{ij}.
\]
Then (6.2) is automatic and
\[
d_c(a)
=g_-+(1-g_-)\frac{\sum_i a_i^2}{S^2}
\ge
\frac{1+(n-1)g_-}{n}.
\tag{6.11}
\]
Thus the previous overlap-band theorem is recovered by this single constant choice, followed by the coarser inequality `sum a_i^2 >= S^2/n`. The new PSD-minorant formulation is therefore a genuine extension, not an unrelated special case.

A completely explicit heterogeneous feasible choice is also available: put
\[
h_i:=\min_{j\ne i}g_{ij},\qquad c_i:=\sqrt{h_i}.
\tag{6.12}
\]
Then, for every `i!=j`, both `h_i<=g_ij` and `h_j<=g_ij`, so
\[
c_i c_j=\sqrt{h_i h_j}\le g_{ij}.
\tag{6.13}
\]
This gives a no-optimization row-minorant certificate. It can be weaker than the SDP optimum, but it retains heterogeneity that the global `g_-` bound discards.

# 7. Concrete strict extension beyond the old global band

Consider four real unit vectors in `R^2` at angles
\[
0^\circ,\quad60^\circ,\quad75^\circ,\quad105^\circ,
\tag{7.1}
\]
and take equal scales `a_i=1`.

Their absolute Gram kernel is
\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2&\sin15^\circ&\sin15^\circ\\
1/2&1&\cos15^\circ&1/\sqrt2\\
\sin15^\circ&\cos15^\circ&1&\sqrt3/2\\
\sin15^\circ&1/\sqrt2&\sqrt3/2&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Here
\[
g_-=\sin15^\circ\approx0.2588190451,
\qquad
g_+=\cos15^\circ\approx0.9659258263.
\tag{7.3}
\]
The preceding scale-free overlap-band theorem would require
\[
g_+\le\frac{1+3g_-}{4}
\approx0.4441142838,
\tag{7.4}
\]
so it fails by a wide margin.

Now choose
\[
c=
\left(
\sin15^\circ,
\cos15^\circ,
1,
\sqrt3-1
\right).
\tag{7.5}
\]
The six constraints `c_i c_j<=g_ij` are immediate; three useful equalities are
\[
c_1c_3=\sin15^\circ=g_{13},
\quad
c_2c_3=\cos15^\circ=g_{23},
\quad
c_2c_4=1/\sqrt2=g_{24}.
\tag{7.6}
\]
Thus `H_c` is a valid PSD minorant.

For equal scales,
\[
d_c
=\frac1{16}\left(\frac52+2\sqrt3+3\sqrt2\right)
=
\frac5{32}+\frac{\sqrt3}{8}+\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}
\approx0.6379213939.
\tag{7.7}
\]
A direct enumeration of the 24 Hamiltonian paths gives
\[
H_4(G)
=\frac{\sqrt3}{96}+\frac1{32}+\frac{\sqrt2}{12}
\approx0.1671433261.
\tag{7.8}
\]
while
\[
d_c^3\approx0.2595980954.
\tag{7.9}
\]
Hence the new certificate closes the owner inequality for this family.

The margin can be checked without relying on high-precision floating point: using
\[
1.414<\sqrt2<1.415,
\qquad
1.732<\sqrt3<1.733,
\]
one gets
\[
d_c>0.637875,
\qquad d_c^3>0.25954,
\qquad H_4(G)<0.16722.
\tag{7.10}
\]
So the certificate gap is robust.

For reference, direct exact rank-one evaluation gives
\[
L_4=H_4(G)\approx0.1671433261,
\qquad
R_4\approx0.3559136278,
\tag{7.11}
\]
with `R_4/L_4 approx 2.12939`.

This example proves that the PSD-minorant theorem is not merely a reformulation of the old `g_-`/`g_+` band: it certifies a highly heterogeneous configuration that the old band cannot approach.

# 10. Failures and boundaries

The new theorem does **not** prove the full rank-one problem. The obstacle has moved to a clean deterministic comparison:
\[
H_n(G)
\stackrel?\le
\kappa(a)\Delta(G,a)^{n-1}.
\tag{10.1}
\]
The SDP only supplies a lower bound on the repeated walk moment through a PSD kernel lying entrywise below `G`; it can discard correlations in `G` that matter to Hamiltonian paths.

Also, replacing the exact Hamiltonian mean by the crude bound `g_+^{n-1}` can lose most of the gain. The strict example in Section 7 is certified by the exact `H_4(G)` condition but not by the crude `g_+` version. Therefore future use should preserve the exact Hamiltonian-path statistic whenever `n` is computationally manageable.

No attempt was made to infer missing transient work from prior sandboxes. No unverified historical claim was promoted.
