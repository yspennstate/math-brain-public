# 4. New endpoint-symmetrization lemma

## Lemma 4.1
Let `A` be PSD, let `M=M^*` be arbitrary Hermitian, and fix `s>=0`. Then
\[
f(u):=\|A^u M A^{s-u}\|,\qquad 0\le u\le s,
\]
is log-convex and symmetric under `u -> s-u`. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\|A^uMA^{s-u}\|\ge\|A^{s/2}MA^{s/2}\|.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof
First take `A>0`. For `u_0,u_1 in [0,s]`, put `delta=u_1-u_0` and
\[
F(z)=A^{u_0+z\delta} M A^{s-u_0-z\delta}.
\]
On the vertical line `z=it`,
\[
F(it)=A^{it\delta}\,[A^{u_0}MA^{s-u_0}]\,A^{-it\delta}.
\]
The outer factors are unitary, so the operator norm is constant on that boundary line. The same is true on `Re z=1`. Hadamard three-lines, applied to scalar matrix coefficients and then maximized over unit vectors, proves log-convexity of `f`.

Because `M=M^*`,
\[
f(u)=\|(A^uMA^{s-u})^*\|=f(s-u).
\]
A symmetric convex function of `u` in logarithmic scale has its minimum at `s/2`, proving (4.1). For merely PSD `A`, replace it by `A+epsilon I` and let `epsilon downarrow 0`. QED.

This lemma is distinct from the preceding joint-exponent theorem: here the middle factor is fixed and only the two endpoint powers of the same PSD matrix are shifted.

# 5. A four-run spectral-radius theorem

Fix an **even** integer `m>=4` and set
\[
q=m/2.
\]
For `1<=a<=m-1`, consider the four-run balanced word
\[
W_a=A^aB^qA^{m-a}B^q.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Theorem 5.1
For every such `a`,
\[
\boxed{\rho(W_a)\ge p^m t^{-(m-4)}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]
The same bound holds after interchanging `A,B`, i.e. for every word
\[
A^qB^bA^qB^{m-b}.
\]

### Proof
Define
\[
X=A^{(m-a)/2}B^qA^{a/2}.
\]
Using `UV`/`VU` spectral invariance,
\[
W_a=A^aB^qA^{m-a}B^q
\]
has the same characteristic polynomial as
\[
A^{a/2}B^qA^{m-a}B^qA^{a/2}=X^*X.
\]
Hence
\[
\rho(W_a)=\|X\|^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Apply Lemma 4.1 with total endpoint exponent `s=m/2` and middle matrix `M=B^q`:
\[
\|X\|
\ge
\|A^{m/4}B^{m/2}A^{m/4}\|.
\tag{5.4}
\]
The matrix on the right is PSD and equals `Y^*Y` for
\[
Y=B^{m/4}A^{m/4}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|A^{m/4}B^{m/2}A^{m/4}\|
=\|A^{m/4}B^{m/4}\|^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Since `m/4>=1`, (3.1) with `s=m/4` gives
\[
\|A^{m/4}B^{m/4}\|
\ge
\frac{p^{m/2-1}}{r^{m/4-1}}.
\]
Combining with (5.3)–(5.5),
\[
\rho(W_a)
\ge
\frac{p^{2m-4}}{r^{m-4}}
=p^m t^{-(m-4)}.
\]
QED.

For `m=4`, the spectral-radius floor becomes especially clean:
\[
\boxed{\rho(A^aB^2A^{4-a}B^2)\ge\|AB\|^4.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

# 6. Counting two disjoint four-run necklace families

Let `F_B` be the union of cyclic orbits of
\[
A^aB^qA^{m-a}B^q,
\qquad 1\le a\le q.
\]
For `a<q`, the cyclic run-length pattern has unequal `A`-runs `a,m-a`, hence is primitive and its orbit has size `2m`. For `a=q`,
\[
A^qB^qA^qB^q=(A^qB^q)^2
\]
has cyclic orbit size `m`. Distinct `a<q` give distinct unordered `A`-run pairs, so the orbits are disjoint. Thus
\[
|F_B|=(q-1)2m+m=m(m-1).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define `F_A` symmetrically by fixing the two `A`-runs to length `q` and varying the two `B`-runs. Again
\[
|F_A|=m(m-1).
\]
The intersection consists exactly of the central orbit
\[
(A^qB^q)^2,
\]
whose size is `m`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{|F_A\cup F_B|=m(2m-3).}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Every word in this union has norm at least its spectral radius and hence, by Theorem 5.1,
\[
\|W\|\ge p^m t^{-(m-4)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
These are all four-run words, so this union is disjoint from the block orbit `A^mB^m` (two runs) and, for `m>=4`, from the two alternating words (2m runs).

# 7. Improved balanced collision theorem for every even m>=4

Retain three disjoint sets of words:

1. the `2m` cyclic rotations of the block word `A^mB^m`, each paying at least `p^m t^{-(m-2)}` by (3.2);
2. the `m(2m-3)` four-run words in `F_A union F_B`, each paying at least `p^m t^{-(m-4)}` by (6.3);
3. the two alternating words, each paying at least `p^m t^m` by (3.3).

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
C_{m,m}^{\{A,B\}}
\ge
p^m\Big[
2m t^{-(m-2)}
+m(2m-3)t^{-(m-4)}
+2t^m
\Big].
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let the scalar bracket be `F_m(t)`. For `0<t<=1`,
\[
F_m'(t)=
-2m(m-2)t^{-(m-1)}
-m(2m-3)(m-4)t^{-(m-3)}
+2m t^{m-1}.
\]
The first negative term alone has magnitude at least `2m(m-2)`, whereas the positive term is at most `2m`. Since `m>=4`, `F_m'(t)<0` throughout `(0,1]`. Hence the minimum occurs at `t=1`, giving
\[
\boxed{
C_{m,m}^{\{A,B\}}
\ge
(2m^2-m+2)\|AB\|^m,
\qquad m\ge4\text{ even}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This strictly improves the preceding checkpoint's coefficient `2(m+1)` for every even `m>=4`.

Concrete cases:
\[
\boxed{C_{4,4}\ge30\|AB\|^4,}
\]
\[
C_{6,6}\ge68\|AB\|^6,
\qquad
C_{8,8}\ge122\|AB\|^8.
\]

The coefficient `30` is not claimed sharp; the complete `4+4` class contains `binom(8,4)=70` words. The new proof certifies only 30 strategically chosen words at the scalar worst point `t=1`.

# 8. Aggregate consequence for the owner problem

For `2m` PSD matrices define
\[
P_m:=\sum_{i<j}\|A_iA_j\|^m,
\]
and let `Q_{m,m}` be the complete occupancy layer consisting of words supported on two labels, each appearing `m` times. Summing (7.2) over unordered pairs gives
\[
\boxed{
Q_{m,m}\ge(2m^2-m+2)P_m,
\qquad m\ge4\text{ even}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The earlier independently proved perfect-matching estimate is
\[
S_{2m}\le2(2m-2)!P_m.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
Q_{m,m}
\ge
\frac{2m^2-m+2}{2(2m-2)!}
S_{2m},
\qquad m\ge4\text{ even}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For eight matrices (`m=4`),
\[
\boxed{Q_{4,4}\ge\frac1{48}S_8.}
\tag{8.4}
\]
The preceding checkpoint gave `Q_{4,4}>=S_8/144`, so (8.4) is a factor-three improvement for this collision layer.

This is not a proof of the full `n=8` inequality: it controls one occupancy layer only. It is, however, a dimension-free higher-length contribution in exactly the owner norm placement and PSD setting.

# 11. Failed / bounded routes and next attack

This run also tested the stronger heuristic that every balanced two-letter word might satisfy
\[
\rho(W)\ge\rho(AB)^m.
\]
Broad random tests for `m=3,4,5` did not find a violation, but no proof was obtained and the statement is **not promoted**. General balanced words need not be cyclically similar to a positive sandwich, so the new theorem deliberately restricts to certified necklace families where an exact `X^*X` representation is available.

The next high-value direction is to classify more balanced necklaces admitting fractional endpoint symmetrization. For `m=4`, there are still four-run necklaces whose two `A`-runs and two `B`-runs are both unequal; controlling even one additional orbit at a comparable `p^4` scale would improve the coefficient `30`. A second direction is to construct analogous reflection-symmetric cyclic families for **odd** `m`, where an exact half-split of one letter is impossible at the integer run level. Both routes directly extend the new mechanism without assuming the unresolved four-letter pair conjecture.
