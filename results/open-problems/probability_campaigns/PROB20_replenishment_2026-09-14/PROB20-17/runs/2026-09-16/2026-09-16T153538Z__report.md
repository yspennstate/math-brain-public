# 4. After the failed search: full rank-one equality-manifold Hessian, including scales

The previous run proved negative quadratic curvature only for common-scale projective perturbations. Its proposed next question was whether **scale perturbations or scale/direction cross terms** could reverse that sign. They cannot at quadratic order.

## Theorem 4.1 -- universal second variation along smooth rank-one paths

Let `2<=m<=n`. Let `A_i(t)` be `C^2` rank-one PSD paths for `i=1,...,n` such that
\[
A_i(0)=ee^*
\]
for one unit vector `e`. Write locally
\[
A_i(t)=s_i(t)u_i(t)u_i(t)^*,
\qquad s_i(t)>0,
\qquad \|u_i(t)\|=1,
\]
and choose the harmless phase gauge with `u_i(0)=e` and `\langle e,u_i(t)\rangle` real positive near zero. Expand
\[
\log s_i(t)=a_i t+b_i t^2+O(t^3),
\qquad
v_i=u_i'(0)\in e^\perp.
\]
Set
\[
\bar a=\frac1n\sum_i a_i,
\quad
\sigma_a^2=\frac1n\sum_i(a_i-\bar a)^2,
\quad
\bar b=\frac1n\sum_i b_i,
\]
\[
\bar v=\frac1n\sum_i v_i,
\qquad
V=\frac1n\sum_i\|v_i-\bar v\|^2.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{WR}_m(t)
=1+m\bar a\,t+
\left[
 m\bar b+\frac{m}{2}\sigma_a^2+\frac{m^2}{2}\bar a^2-(m-1)V
\right]t^2+O(t^3),
\tag{4.1}
\]
while
\[
\operatorname{WOR}_m(t)
=1+m\bar a\,t+
\left[
 m\bar b+
 \frac{m}{2}\frac{n-m}{n-1}\sigma_a^2
 +\frac{m^2}{2}\bar a^2
 -(m-1)\frac{n}{n-1}V
\right]t^2+O(t^3).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{WOR}_m(t)}{\operatorname{WR}_m(t)}
=
1-
\frac{m-1}{n-1}
\left(\frac m2\sigma_a^2+V\right)t^2
+O(t^3).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
In particular, if either the first-order logarithmic scales are not all equal (`sigma_a^2>0`) or the first-order projective tangents are not all equal (`V>0`), then
\[
\operatorname{WOR}_m(t)<\operatorname{WR}_m(t)
\]
for all sufficiently small nonzero `t`.

### Proof

For rank-one matrices,
\[
\|A_{i_1}(t)\cdots A_{i_m}(t)\|
=
\left(\prod_{r=1}^m s_{i_r}(t)\right)
\left(\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}
|\langle u_{i_r}(t),u_{i_{r+1}}(t)\rangle|
\right).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Write
\[
u_i(t)=e+t v_i+\tfrac12t^2w_i+O(t^3).
\]
The phase gauge gives `\langle e,v_i\rangle=0`. Differentiating `\|u_i(t)\|^2=1` twice yields
\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle e,w_i\rangle=-\|v_i\|^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\langle u_i(t),u_j(t)\rangle|
=1-\frac12\|v_i-v_j\|^2t^2+O(t^3).
\tag{4.5}
\]

For a fixed word `I=(i_1,...,i_m)`, define
\[
S_a(I)=\sum_{r=1}^m a_{i_r},
\qquad
S_b(I)=\sum_{r=1}^m b_{i_r},
\]
\[
D(I)=\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}\|v_{i_r}-v_{i_{r+1}}\|^2.
\]
Substitution into (4.4) gives uniformly over the finite word set
\[
\|A_{i_1}(t)\cdots A_{i_m}(t)\|
=
1+tS_a+t^2\left(S_b+\frac12S_a^2-\frac12D\right)+O(t^3).
\tag{4.6}
\]

For sampling with replacement, each position is iid uniform, hence
\[
\mathbb E_{wr}S_a=m\bar a,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{wr}S_b=m\bar b,
\]
\[
\mathbb E_{wr}S_a^2=m\sigma_a^2+m^2\bar a^2.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Also a uniform ordered pair `(I,J)` has
\[
\mathbb E\|v_I-v_J\|^2=2V,
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E_{wr}D=2(m-1)V.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Equations (4.6)--(4.8) give (4.1).

For sampling without replacement, every position is still marginally uniform, so the first two linear expectations are unchanged. For two distinct sampled positions,
\[
\mathbb E_{i\ne j}[a_i a_j]
=\bar a^2-\frac{\sigma_a^2}{n-1}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E_{wor}S_a^2
=m^2\bar a^2+
 m\frac{n-m}{n-1}\sigma_a^2.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Similarly, a consecutive pair is uniform over ordered distinct indices and
\[
\mathbb E_{i\ne j}\|v_i-v_j\|^2
=\frac{2n}{n-1}V,
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E_{wor}D
=2(m-1)\frac{n}{n-1}V.
\tag{4.11}
\]
Equations (4.6), (4.9)--(4.11) give (4.2). Since (4.1) and (4.2) have identical constant and linear terms, division yields (4.3). This proves the theorem.

## 4.2 Interpretation

The quadratic form in (4.3) is negative semidefinite and its only null directions are common first-order scale motion plus common first-order projective motion. Thus the possible scale/direction cross term suggested after the previous run does **not** exist at second order. A rank-one counterexample cannot bifurcate from the common-matrix equality family through any first-order relative scale or projective splitting.

This is a campaign theorem, not a literature-priority claim. It does not rule out a counterexample along a path flat to first order (`sigma_a=V=0` but differing at higher order), away from the equality manifold, or among full-rank matrices where the operator norm at the common identity has a degenerate top singular subspace.
