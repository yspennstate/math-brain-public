## 2. Literature/status check

Primary matrix source:

* A. Israel, F. Krahmer, R. Ward, *An arithmetic–geometric mean inequality for products of three matrices*, Linear Algebra Appl. 488 (2016), 1–12, arXiv:1411.0333, DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2015.09.013`.

Their norm-inside conjecture is stated for unitarily invariant norms; they prove product length at most three and explicitly leave `m>=4` open. A bounded fresh search still finds the norm-inside `m>=4` problem listed as open in 2025/2026 summaries.

Important current distinction: a 2026 formal/certified counterexample concerns **Bandeira 0.2(a)** / the Recht–Ré norm-outside formulation. That does not settle the present 0.2(b) norm-inside-average task.

Classical walk inequality used below:

* G. R. Blakley and P. Roy, *A Hölder type inequality for symmetric matrices with nonnegative entries*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 16 (1965), 1244–1245, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9939-1965-0184950-9` (also JSTOR `10.2307/2035908`).

The exact hypothesis needed here is the standard Blakley–Roy matrix inequality: if `M` is real symmetric and entrywise nonnegative and `x` is an entrywise nonnegative unit vector, then for each positive integer `k`,

\[
\langle M^k x,x\rangle\ge \langle Mx,x\rangle^k.
\tag{BR}
\]

No PSD assumption on `M` is required. That distinction is the key new observation for this task.

# 3. Exact rank-one reduction recalled

Let

\[
A_i=a_i u_i u_i^*,\qquad a_i>0,\qquad \|u_i\|=1,
\]

and define

\[
g_{ij}=|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|,\qquad g_{ii}=1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For every word,

\[
\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n}\|
=
\left(\prod_{r=1}^n a_{i_r}\right)
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}g_{i_r i_{r+1}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Put

\[
q_i=\sqrt{a_i},\qquad D_q=\operatorname{diag}(q),\qquad G=(g_{ij}),\qquad M=D_qGD_q.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The already-verified transfer identity is

\[
\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n}\|
=q^TM^{n-1}q.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus

\[
R_n:=\frac1{n^n}\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_n}\|
=\frac1{n^n}q^TM^{n-1}q.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The distinct side is

\[
L_n=\left(\prod_i a_i\right)H_n(G),
\tag{3.6}
\]

where

\[
H_n(G)=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}g_{\sigma(r),\sigma(r+1)}
\tag{3.7}
\]

is the mean Hamiltonian-path weight.

The absolute Gram matrix `G` can be indefinite; that blocked direct spectral Jensen in the previous run. But `M` is always **symmetric and entrywise nonnegative**, exactly the Blakley–Roy hypothesis.

# 4. New direct Blakley–Roy lower bound

Let

\[
S=\sum_i a_i=\|q\|_2^2,
\qquad
\mu_a:=\frac{a^TGa}{S^2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Apply (BR) to

\[
x=\frac q{\sqrt S}
\]

and `k=n-1`. Since

\[
\langle Mx,x\rangle
=\frac{q^TMq}{S}
=\frac{a^TGa}{S},
\]

we get

\[
\frac{q^TM^{n-1}q}{S}
\ge
\left(\frac{a^TGa}{S}\right)^{n-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
q^TM^{n-1}q
\ge
S^{2-n}(a^TGa)^{n-1}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Dividing by `n^n` gives

\[
\boxed{
R_n
\ge
\left(\frac Sn\right)^n\mu_a^{\,n-1}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is valid for every scaled rank-one PSD family, every `n`, and every ambient dimension.

## 4.1 Strict logical improvement over the previous PSD-minorant theorem

The preceding report allowed any PSD entrywise minorant `0<=H<=G` and obtained

\[
R_n\ge
\left(\frac Sn\right)^n
\left(\frac{a^THa}{S^2}\right)^{n-1}.
\]

But `a>=0` and `H<=G` entrywise imply

\[
a^THa\le a^TGa.
\]

Hence (4.3) dominates **every** PSD-minorant bound simultaneously, including the SDP optimum from the preceding checkpoint. The SDP certificate remains correct, but it is no longer the strongest campaign lower bound for rank-one families.

Define

\[
\kappa(a):=\frac{(S/n)^n}{\prod_i a_i}\ge1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Combining (3.6) and (4.3) yields the new exact sufficient condition

\[
\boxed{
H_n(G)\le \kappa(a)\mu_a^{n-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_n\le R_n.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The unresolved question is whether (4.5) might in fact hold automatically for every absolute Gram kernel. I did not prove that, so it remains a target rather than a theorem.

# 5. New Hamiltonian-path upper bound from the weighted matrix-tree theorem

Write

\[
w:=\sum_{i<j}g_{ij},
\qquad
N:=\binom n2,
\qquad
\bar g:=\frac{w}{N}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `P_H(G)` be the sum of products of edge weights over **undirected** Hamiltonian paths on the `n` labeled vertices. Since each undirected path has exactly two orientations,

\[
H_n(G)=\frac{2}{n!}P_H(G).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every Hamiltonian path is a spanning tree, so

\[
P_H(G)\le \tau(G),
\tag{5.3}
\]

where `tau(G)` is the weighted spanning-tree polynomial

\[
\tau(G)=\sum_{T\text{ spanning tree}}\prod_{e\in T}g_e.
\]

Let `L` be the weighted graph Laplacian with off-diagonal weights `g_ij`, and let its eigenvalues be

\[
0=\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_n.
\]

By the weighted matrix-tree theorem,

\[
\tau(G)=\frac1n\prod_{r=2}^n\lambda_r.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Also

\[
\sum_{r=2}^n\lambda_r=\operatorname{tr}L=2w.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Scalar AM–GM therefore gives

\[
\tau(G)
\le
\frac1n\left(\frac{2w}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.2), (5.3), and (5.6),

\[
H_n(G)
\le
\frac{2}{n!n}
\left(\frac{2w}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}.
\]

Since `2w/(n-1)=n\bar g`, this is

\[
\boxed{
H_n(G)
\le
C_n\,\bar g^{\,n-1},
\qquad
C_n:=\frac{2n^{n-3}}{(n-1)!}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

No Gram-positivity property is needed for (5.7); it holds for arbitrary nonnegative symmetric edge weights.

# 6. New all-scale rank-one average-overlap certificate

Combining the direct Blakley–Roy lower bound (4.3) with (5.7) gives:

## Theorem 6.1
For a scaled rank-one PSD family, the full owner inequality holds whenever

\[
\boxed{
C_n\bar g^{\,n-1}
\le
\kappa(a)\mu_a^{\,n-1},
\qquad
C_n=\frac{2n^{n-3}}{(n-1)!}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\bar g
\le
\frac{\kappa(a)^{1/(n-1)}\mu_a}{C_n^{1/(n-1)}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This uses the **mean** off-diagonal absolute overlap rather than the maximum coherence and can therefore certify families containing a few very large overlaps.

### Equal scales
If `a_i=1`, then `S=n`, `kappa=1`, and

\[
\mu_a
=
\frac{1+(n-1)\bar g}{n}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Hence the conjecture is proved whenever

\[
C_n^{1/(n-1)}\bar g
\le
\frac{1+(n-1)\bar g}{n}.
\]

Solving gives

\[
\boxed{
\bar g\le
\tau_n^{\rm tree}:=
\frac{1}{nC_n^{1/(n-1)}-(n-1)}.

\tag{6.4}

# 7. Sharper four-vertex Hamiltonian-path polynomial bound

The spanning-tree estimate can be sharpened for four vertices.

Let the six off-diagonal edge weights of `K_4` be

\[
a,b,c,d,e,f,
\]

with opposite pairs

\[
(a,f),\qquad(b,e),\qquad(c,d).
\]

Let

\[
w=a+b+c+d+e+f.
\]

Every three-edge Hamiltonian path contains **exactly one** opposite pair. Therefore its undirected weighted path sum is

\[
P_4
=
af(b+c+d+e)
+be(a+c+d+f)
+cd(a+b+e+f).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Put

\[
x=a+f,\qquad y=b+e,\qquad z=c+d,
\qquad x+y+z=w.
\]

Using `af<=x^2/4`, `be<=y^2/4`, `cd<=z^2/4`,

\[
P_4
\le
\frac14\bigl[x^2(y+z)+y^2(x+z)+z^2(x+y)\bigr].
\tag{7.2}
\]

Normalize `X=x/w`, `Y=y/w`, `Z=z/w`; then `X+Y+Z=1`. The bracket divided by `w^3` equals

\[
X^2+Y^2+Z^2-(X^3+Y^3+Z^3)
=s_2-3s_3,
\]

where `s_2=XY+YZ+ZX` and `s_3=XYZ`. Schur's degree-three inequality gives

\[
1+9s_3\ge4s_2.
\]

Hence

\[
s_2-3s_3
\le
\frac14-rac34s_3
\le\frac14.
\]

Substitution into (7.2) yields

\[
\boxed{P_4\le \frac{w^3}{16}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since `H_4=P_4/12` and `bar g=w/6`,

\[
\boxed{
H_4(G)\le\frac98\,\bar g^3.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

For equal scales, direct Blakley–Roy gives

\[
R_4\ge\left(\frac{1+3\bar g}{4}\right)^3.
\]

Thus this particular proof certifies the rank-one `n=4` owner inequality whenever

\[
\boxed{
\bar g\le
\frac{1}{4(9/8)^{1/3}-3}
=0.8619443951555629\ldots .
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The campaign already has an exact rank-one `n=4` theorem, so (7.5) is not a new closure of that class. Its value is methodological: it demonstrates that Hamiltonian-path polynomials can be bounded materially below the full spanning-tree polynomial, suggesting sharper all-`n` path-polynomial estimates.
