## 5. Method B18 — say the `2 x 2` case in another language

For an arbitrary complex `2 x 2` matrix `P`, put

\[
\tau(P)=\operatorname{tr}(P^*P)=\|P\|_F^2,
\qquad
\delta(P)=|\det P|^2.
\]

The squared singular values are the roots of

\[
x^2-\tau(P)x+\delta(P)=0,
\]

so exactly

\[
\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
=
\frac{\tau(P)+\sqrt{\tau(P)^2-4\delta(P)}}{2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This gives a useful reformulation of (B). For a without-replacement word `P_\sigma=A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(4)}`,

\[
\delta(P_\sigma)
=
\prod_{r=1}^4 |\det A_r|^2
=:D_0,
\]

independently of the permutation. Therefore every WOR norm is the same increasing scalar function of the single order-sensitive quantity `\tau(P_\sigma)` with the **same** determinant parameter `D_0`.

That simplification disappears on the with-replacement side. For a word `w=(i,j,k,\ell)`,

\[
\delta(P_w)
=|\det A_i\det A_j\det A_k\det A_\ell|^2,
\]

which varies with the multiplicity pattern. Thus the source inequality becomes a comparison between

\[
\frac1{24}\sum_{\sigma} F_{D_0}(\tau(P_\sigma))
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac1{256}\sum_w F_{D_w}(\tau(P_w)),
\]

where

\[
F_D(t)=\sqrt{\frac{t+\sqrt{t^2-4D}}{2}}.
\]

### Outcome of B18

This language isolates a precise obstruction rather than solving it. The WOR side has a common determinant parameter, but replacement changes **both** the Frobenius invariant `\tau` and the determinant parameter `D`. Consequently, a Jensen argument in `\tau` at fixed `D` cannot compare the two sampling laws. Any successful `2 x 2` proof using singular-value algebra has to control the joint pair `(\tau,D)` under replacement, not only a trace/Frobenius moment.

This also explains why several previous scalarizations were too weak: they discarded the multiplicity-sensitive determinant information that the operator norm retains through the square-root discriminant.

A second useful language is the Bloch/Lorentz parametrization of PSD `2 x 2` Hermitian matrices,

\[
A_i=a_i I+x_i\cdot\sigma,
\qquad a_i\ge \|x_i\|.
\]

It converts positivity into the Lorentz-cone condition. But multiplication is noncommutative and leaves the Hermitian cone after two distinct factors, so Lorentz-cone order alone does not propagate through a four-factor word. The representation is a dimension reduction, not a replacement inequality.

## 6. Method B14 — identify the symmetry group, and identify the symmetry that is *not* available

The ratio in (B) is invariant under simultaneous unitary conjugation

\[
A_i\mapsto U A_i U^*,
\]

because every word is conjugated by `U`, and it is invariant under one common positive scalar multiplication

\[
A_i\mapsto c A_i
\]

because both averages acquire the same factor `c^4`.

In Bloch coordinates, simultaneous unitary conjugation rotates all Bloch vectors by one common `SO(3)` rotation. Thus, away from degeneracies, one can place the first Bloch vector on an axis and the second in a coordinate plane. This removes genuine gauge freedom from the `d=2` search/proof problem.

The tempting stronger normalization is **not valid**: one cannot independently rescale each matrix to determinant one, trace one, or operator norm one without changing the source ratio. Under independent scalings `A_i\mapsto c_iA_i`, every WOR term receives the common factor

\[
\prod_{i=1}^4 c_i,
\]

but a WR word receives

\[
\prod_{r=1}^4 c_{i_r},
\]

which depends on the multiplicities in the word. Independent scale normalization therefore destroys exactly the replacement structure being compared.

### Outcome of B14

The quotient by simultaneous unitary conjugation and common scaling is legitimate and should be used in the next focused `2 x 2` attack. The much larger quotient by four independent scalings is illegitimate. This sharply limits how far symmetry reduction can go and identifies scale imbalance as a structural variable rather than nuisance normalization.

## 7. Method B11 — dualize the operator norm, and locate the wrong inequality direction

For each word product `P`,

\[
\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=
\max_{\|x\|=\|y\|=1}|y^*Px|.
\tag{7.1}
\]

A natural replacement proof would try to average the bilinear forms first and then take one norm/support functional. But

\[
\max_{x,y}\mathbb E\,|y^*P_wx|
\le
\mathbb E\max_{x,y}|y^*P_wx|
=
\mathbb E\|P_w\|,
\tag{7.2}
\]

so exchanging the maximum and expectation gives a **lower** bound on the average of norms. It does not provide the upper bound on WOR needed for the conjecture.

Worse, the maximizing left/right singular vectors generally depend on the word. A proof that moves the norm outside the average erases this word-by-word adaptation and changes the mathematical statement into a norm-of-an-average problem of the Recht–Ré type. That is precisely the neighboring conjecture known to have different behavior.

### Outcome of B11

The obvious dual/symmetrization algorithm fails for a structural reason: there is no common dual witness. A successful dual argument would need an additional mechanism, for example a coupling that assigns each WOR word's maximizing singular-vector pair to repeated WR words with enough aggregate weight, or a stronger PSD-specific inequality that controls the collection of word-dependent support functionals simultaneously. None of the archived approaches currently supplies such a mechanism.

This is the clearest formulation this run of **why the plausible replacement argument fails** without invoking a false independence inequality.

## 8. Routes already tried and the precise reason each has not closed the source

The campaign has accumulated many useful restricted results, but their failure modes are now separable.

1. **`m<=3` Araki–Lieb–Thirring route.** Israel–Krahmer–Ward's proof exploits a three-factor permutation/ALT structure. Their key inequality is specific to three operators. At four factors new alternating/repeated order types appear; the archive has no four-factor ALT analogue in the required direction.

2. **Rank-one Gram reduction.** For rank-one PSD matrices, word norms factor into products of absolute inner products, enabling Hamiltonian-path/walk methods and complete equal-scale results in several small `n`. Full-rank `2 x 2` matrices do not admit this scalar path factorization.

3. **Near-equality rank-one deformation.** The previous two source-correct runs proved that relative projective and first-order scale perturbations of the common-projector equality family bend `WOR/WR` below one at quadratic order. This makes that local rank-one equality manifold an unfavorable place to search for a counterexample; it does not control full-rank nonsmooth perturbations near a matrix with repeated top singular value.

4. **Exact rational finite search.** The archive contains large exact/certified rank-one and rational `3 x 3` searches, including all `9^4` words for a noncommuting rational PSD library and 382 source-valid `m=4` subsets. No ratio above one was found. A finite library search cannot prove the universal inequality.

5. **Repeated-word/collision lower bounds.** The campaign obtained nontrivial arbitrary-matrix lower bounds and an unrestricted `n=4` normalized guarantee below one. They do not bridge the final gap to the source constant one.

6. **Pairwise four-letter inequalities.** Candidate inequalities involving terms such as `\|A^2B^2\|` and `\|ABAB\|` remain unproved or insufficient to aggregate all word classes with the exact source weights.

7. **PSD/Loewner order.** Products of noncommuting PSD matrices are not generally Hermitian or PSD, so there is no termwise Loewner comparison that survives multiplication through a four-letter word.

8. **Naive replacement independence / Jensen.** Sampling without replacement is dependent, and the map from the tuple of factors to `\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_m}\|` is neither separately linear nor a scalar convex function for which standard sampling-with/without-replacement convex-order results apply. Treating the factors as independent or moving the norm outside the sum changes the problem.

9. **Independent normalization.** As B14 makes explicit, rescaling individual matrices changes WR multiplicity weights differently from WOR. It is not a harmless normalization.

10. **Noncommutative Positivstellensatz as used for Recht–Ré.** Lai–Lim's method handles a polynomial matrix inequality after the norm is outside a sum. Here each word has its own operator norm/maximizing singular directions. A direct port would introduce word-specific spectral constraints and does not yield the same polynomial positivity problem.

## 9. What a successful argument must contain that no current route contains

For a **proof** of the simplest case (B), the missing ingredient is a replacement-sensitive control of word-adapted singular vectors. In the `2 x 2` scalarized language, that means an inequality that compares the joint order statistics

\[
(\tau(P_w),\delta(P_w))
\]

under WOR and WR with the exact function `F_D(\tau)`, while respecting multiplicity-dependent determinants. A trace-only or determinant-only argument loses essential information.

Equivalently in the dual language, a proof needs a way to aggregate the different maximizing singular-vector pairs of all 24 permutation words into the 256 repeated words with the correct weights. No current route has that coupling/certificate.

For a **counterexample**, the accumulated negative evidence says where not to look: not infinitesimally along the common rank-one projector manifold, and not in the rational libraries already exhausted. A plausible counterexample must exploit full-rank noncommutativity, scale imbalance, and word-dependent singular-vector rotation far enough from equality that the quadratic rank-one stability calculation is irrelevant.
