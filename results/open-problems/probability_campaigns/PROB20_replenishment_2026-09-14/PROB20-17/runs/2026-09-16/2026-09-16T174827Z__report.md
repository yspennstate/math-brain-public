# 2. Source and literature checks, dated 2026-09-16

## 2.1 Original source: Duchi 2012

John C. Duchi, *Commentary on “Towards a Noncommutative Arithmetic-Geometric Mean Inequality” by B. Recht and C. Ré*, COLT 2012, pp. 11.25–11.27.

Direct PDF checked:

`https://proceedings.mlr.press/v23/duchi12/duchi12.pdf`

Duchi defines without- and with-replacement expectations and then explicitly proposes the scalar-valued choice

\[
f(A_1,\ldots,A_k)=\|A_1A_2\cdots A_k\|,
\]

which places the operator norm **inside** the expectation. This is distinct from the Recht–Ré norm-of-the-average statement.

## 2.2 Israel–Krahmer–Ward source statement

Arie Israel, Felix Krahmer and Rachel Ward, *An arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for products of three matrices*, arXiv:1411.0333 / Linear Algebra and its Applications 488 (2016), 1–12.

Direct arXiv record checked:

`https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.0333`

Their Conjecture 1.3 is, for PSD `A_1,...,A_n`, every `m<=n`, and any unitarily invariant norm,

\[
\frac1{n^m}\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_m=1}^n
\|A_{j_1}\cdots A_{j_m}\|
\ge
\frac{(n-m)!}{n!}
\sum_{\substack{j_1,\ldots,j_m=1\\\text{all distinct}}}^{n}
\|A_{j_1}\cdots A_{j_m}\|.
\tag{2.1}
\]

They prove `m<=3`; the paper explicitly says extension to `m>=4` remains open. Operator norm is included among their UI norms.

## 2.3 Separate Bandeira Open Problem 0.2(b) alias

The direct Bandeira/MIT source was checked rather than trusting the malformed old E0044 intake row:

`https://people.math.ethz.ch/~abandeira/TenLecturesFortyTwoProblems.pdf`

and the MIT Open Problem 0.2 handout. Part **(b)** is exactly the `m=n`, spectral-norm, norm-inside specialization of (2.1): average of the individual product norms under permutations is at most the with-replacement average. Part **(a)** is the distinct Recht–Ré norm-outside statement. Thus `Open Problem 0.2(b)` is a valid provenance alias for the diagonal specialization, not a separate reverse inequality.

# 4. B1 — use Gram geometry as a constraint, not another blind rank-one search

The rank-one reduction itself is old campaign machinery, so I did **not** repeat the large rational-pool search from `T145229Z`. The new use of B1 here is to identify an exact **realizability obstruction** that an abstract replacement/symmetrization proof would otherwise miss.

For rank-one PSD matrices

\[
A_i=a_i u_i u_i^*,\qquad a_i\ge0,\quad \|u_i\|=1,
\]

one has exactly

\[
\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_m}\|
=
\left(\prod_{r=1}^m a_{i_r}\right)
\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}g_{i_ri_{r+1}},
\qquad
g_{ij}=|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The important point for this run is that a nonnegative symmetric matrix with diagonal one is **not** automatically an absolute Gram kernel. For any three unit vectors `u_i,u_j,u_k`, writing the latter two as their component along `u_i` plus an orthogonal component gives

\[
\boxed{
 g_{jk}
 \ge
 \max\left\{0,
 g_{ij}g_{ik}
 -\sqrt{(1-g_{ij}^2)(1-g_{ik}^2)}
 \right\}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Proof: rephase `u_j,u_k` so their inner products with `u_i` have moduli `g_{ij},g_{ik}` and are nonnegative. Write

\[
u_j=\alpha u_i+y,\qquad u_k=\beta u_i+z,
\]

with `y,z` orthogonal to `u_i`, `|\alpha|=g_{ij}`, `|\beta|=g_{ik}`. Then

\[
|\langle u_j,u_k\rangle|
\ge |\alpha\beta|-|\langle y,z\rangle|
\ge g_{ij}g_{ik}-\sqrt{(1-g_{ij}^2)(1-g_{ik}^2)}.
\]

The trivial lower bound zero gives (4.2).

In particular,

\[
g_{ij}=g_{ik}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_{jk}=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This will matter in B20: the cleanest abstract-kernel failure of a support-monotonicity proof is **forbidden by Gram geometry**. Thus any proof which relaxes absolute Gram kernels to arbitrary nonnegative transition kernels loses a real structural inequality.

# 5. B23 — actively seek the opposite in a new structured rank-one family

The previous exact rank-one searches already stressed many rational directions and small scale grids. Rather than repeat that exhausted move, I attacked a closed-form family designed to maximize two competing effects: exact duplicates create strong repeated-word terms, while a tunable cross angle creates alternating-word losses.

Take `n=m=4` and

\[
A_1=A_2=aP,\qquad A_3=A_4=bQ,
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `P=uu^*`, `Q=vv^*` are rank-one orthogonal projectors, `a,b\ge0`, and

\[
c=|\langle u,v\rangle|\in[0,1].
\]

This family deliberately breaks the all-distinct geometry by putting two duplicate matrices in each of two clusters and allows arbitrary scale imbalance `a/b`.

## 5.1 Exact without-replacement average

Every WOR word contains two `P`-type and two `Q`-type factors. Among the six cluster-type sequences, two have one transition, two have two transitions, and two have three transitions. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{WOR}_4
=
\frac{a^2b^2}{3}(c+c^2+c^3).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

## 5.2 Exact with-replacement average

Under WR sampling from the four labeled matrices, the cluster type is iid Bernoulli `1/2`. Enumerating the 16 cluster-type words gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{WR}_4
=
\frac1{16}\Big[
 a^4+b^4
 +2a^3b(c+c^2)
 +2ab^3(c+c^2)
 +2a^2b^2(c+c^2+c^3)
\Big].
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

## 5.3 The attempted counterexample fails for every parameter

For `a,b>0`, subtracting (5.2) from (5.3) and multiplying by 48 gives

\[
48(\operatorname{WR}_4-\operatorname{WOR}_4)
=
3a^4+3b^4
+6a^3b(c+c^2)
+6ab^3(c+c^2)
-10a^2b^2(c+c^2+c^3).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Put

\[
t=a/b,\qquad x=t+t^{-1}\ge2.
\]

After division by `a^2b^2`, the right side is

\[
F(x,c)
=
3(x^2-2)+6(c+c^2)x-10(c+c^2+c^3).
\tag{5.5}
\]

For `x\ge2` and `0\le c\le1`,

\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}=6x+6(c+c^2)>0,
\]

so the minimum occurs at `x=2`, i.e. equal scales. At that point

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(2,c)
&=6+2c+2c^2-10c^3\\
&=2(1-c)(5c^2+4c+3)\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{WOR}_4\le \operatorname{WR}_4
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

for the entire two-duplicate-cluster rank-one family, with arbitrary nonnegative scales. For positive scales, equality in this family requires `a=b` and `c=1`, the common-matrix equality configuration. If `a=0` or `b=0`, WOR is zero and the inequality is immediate.

This is a complete restricted theorem, not a source closure and not metric movement. It is useful mainly as a **failed refutation route**: even strong scale imbalance does not overturn the conjecture in this highly clustered rank-one family; in fact the scale imbalance only increases the safety margin because (5.5) is increasing in `x=t+t^{-1}`.

A bounded repository search found older equal-scale cluster examples and the general rank-one transfer machinery, but did not surface this exact arbitrary-scale `2+2` duplicate-cluster calculation. I make no external novelty claim.

# 6. B20 — exact failure of the tempting support-by-support replacement proof

A very natural way to try to prove Duchi's comparison is to condition a WR word on how many distinct indices it uses and hope that the conditional expected product norm decreases as the support size increases. If this were true, the all-distinct stratum would be smallest and the source inequality would follow from the mixture over support sizes.

That **proof strategy is false already for a four-state nonnegative transition kernel**.

Consider

\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&0\\
1&1&0&1\\
1&0&1&1\\
0&1&1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{6.1}
\]

and assign a length-four word `w=(i_1,i_2,i_3,i_4)` the weight

\[
W(w)=K_{i_1i_2}K_{i_2i_3}K_{i_3i_4}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Exact enumeration of all `4^4=256` words gives the following support strata:

| number of distinct symbols | total weight | number of words | conditional mean |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4 | 4 | `1` |
| 2 | 56 | 84 | `2/3` |
| 3 | 40 | 144 | `5/18` |
| 4 | 8 | 24 | `1/3` |

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[W\mid |\mathrm{supp}|=4]
=\frac13
>
\frac5{18}
=
\mathbb E[W\mid |\mathrm{supp}|=3].
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

So the conditional mean is **not** monotone in the number of distinct sampled indices. Any proof based on a one-step principle saying “replace a repetition by a new symbol and the conditional average can only go down” is invalid at this level of generality.

This relaxed kernel is **not** a counterexample to the source inequality. Its total averages are

\[
\operatorname{WOR}=\frac13,
\qquad
\operatorname{WR}=\frac{108}{256}=\frac{27}{64},
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{WOR}}{\operatorname{WR}}=\frac{64}{81}<1.
\tag{6.4}
\]

More importantly, `K` is **not an absolute Gram kernel**. For example `K_{01}=K_{02}=1` would force, by (4.3), `K_{12}=1`, whereas (6.1) has `K_{12}=0`.

This cleanly isolates the missing ingredient in the naive symmetrization route:

> support-count combinatorics alone is too weak; a successful rank-one replacement proof must use Gram-realizability inequalities such as (4.2), and a full-rank proof must use an analogous PSD/singular-value structure rather than an arbitrary nonnegative-kernel relaxation.

The exact finite claim (6.3) and the arithmetic in (6.4) were checked by an executed exact-`Fraction` artifact archived with this run.

# 7. How the new Alaifari–Cheng–Pierce–Steinerberger theorem changes the `d=2` blocker

The prior run identified `(n,m,d)=(4,4,2)` as the simplest genuinely unproved source frontier. The 2019/2020 rearrangement theorem provides a useful partial decomposition of that case.

For any two real symmetric PSD `2x2` matrices `A,B`, every word with fixed multiplicities satisfies

\[
\|W(A,B)\|\le \|A^pB^q\|.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Their proof works because, for `2x2`, the top singular value is determined by the trace and determinant of `W^TW`; an Ando–Hiai–Okubo trace inequality supplies the trace comparison while multiplicativity supplies equal determinants.

For Duchi's WR average, this can control the entire **two-symbol support stratum** after conditioning on multiplicities. But it does not compare the support-two stratum to support-three or support-four words with the exact sampling coefficients. Section 6 shows that such cross-stratum monotonicity is not a purely combinatorial truth even in a benign nonnegative-kernel relaxation.

Thus the literature theorem sharpens, rather than removes, the blocker: the unresolved `2x2` issue is genuinely about **three- and four-symbol interaction**, or about finding a multi-matrix analogue of the trace/determinant mechanism that survives the replacement mixture.
