# PROB20-17 — norm-inside-average noncommutative AM–GM

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.**

The owner target remains, for arbitrary `n` and arbitrary PSD `d x d` matrices `A_1,...,A_n`,
\[
\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(n)}\|
\le
\frac1{n^n}\sum_{k_1,\dots,k_n\in[n]}\|A_{k_1}\cdots A_{k_n}\|,
\]
with the spectral norm **inside** both averages.  The Recht–Ré norm-outside counterexample is a different statement.  No full proof or certified refutation is claimed here.

This run extends the previous `n=4` power-interpolation mechanism to **every even product length** and obtains a new dimension-free lower bound for the full balanced two-label collision class.  The central new results are:

1. for every real `s>=1` and PSD `A,B`,
\[
\boxed{\rho(AB)^{s-1}\,\|A^sB^s\|\ge \|AB\|^{2s-1}};
\tag{0.1}
\]
2. for every integer `m>=3`, if `C_{m,m}^{\{A,B\}}` is the sum of the norms of **all** length-`2m` words containing exactly `m` copies of `A` and `m` copies of `B`, then
\[
\boxed{C_{m,m}^{\{A,B\}}\ge 8\|AB\|^m};
\tag{0.2}
\]
3. for a family `A_1,...,A_{2m}` and
\[
P_m:=\sum_{i<j}\|A_iA_j\|^m,
\]
the distinct-word sum
\[
S_{2m}:=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2m}}\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(2m)}\|
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{S_{2m}\le 2(2m-2)!\,P_m};
\tag{0.3}
\]
4. consequently, if `Q_{m,m}` denotes the complete occupancy layer consisting of all length-`2m` words supported on two labels with multiplicities `(m,m)`, then for `m>=3`,
\[
\boxed{Q_{m,m}\ge 8P_m\ge \frac{4}{(2m-2)!}S_{2m}}.
\tag{0.4}
\]

For `m=2`, the same framework recovers the previous `2+2` mechanism and the uniform pair coefficient
\[
2+3\,2^{1/3}=5.779763149684619\ldots,
\]
while the latest checkpoint's weighted/Jensen refinement remains stronger for the `n=4` scalar optimization.  Thus the strongest unrestricted `n=4` normalized factor remains
\[

The new contribution of this run is the extension beyond `n=4` to the balanced collision layers at every even length.

# 3. Equal-power interpolation hierarchy

Let
\[
g(s):=\|A^sB^s\|,\qquad s\ge0.
\]
The previous checkpoint proved that `log g(s)` is convex.  For completeness, for positive-definite `A,B` one applies Hadamard's three-lines theorem to
\[
F(z)=A^zB^z.
\]
On every vertical line,
\[
F(s+it)=A^{it}A^sB^sB^{it},
\]
and the two imaginary powers are unitary, so
\[
\|F(s+it)\|=\|A^sB^s\|.
\]
The PSD case follows by `epsilon I` regularization.

Also
\[
g(1/2)^2
=\|B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\|
=\rho(AB).
\tag{3.1}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — all-power spectral-radius bridge

For every real `s>=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\rho(AB)^{s-1}\,\|A^sB^s\|
\ge
\|AB\|^{2s-1}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
Interpolate the point `1` between `1/2` and `s`.  Put
\[
\theta=\frac{1}{2s-1},
\qquad
1=(1-\theta)\frac12+\theta s.
\]
Log-convexity gives
\[
\|AB\|
\le
\|A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\|^{1-\theta}
\|A^sB^s\|^\theta.
\]
Using (3.1),
\[
\|AB\|
\le
\rho(AB)^{(s-1)/(2s-1)}
\|A^sB^s\|^{1/(2s-1)}.
\]
Raise to the power `2s-1`.  QED.

For integer `s=m`,
\[
\boxed{
\|A^mB^m\|
\ge
\frac{\|AB\|^{2m-1}}{\rho(AB)^{m-1}}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
At `m=2` this is exactly the previous checkpoint's theorem.

# 4. Ordered-versus-alternating lower-sum hierarchy

Fix an integer `m>=2` and abbreviate
\[
p:=\|AB\|,
\qquad r:=\rho(AB),
\qquad t:=\frac rp\in(0,1]
\]
when `p>0`.  Put
\[
X_m:=\|A^mB^m\|,
\qquad
Y_m:=\|(AB)^m\|.
\]
By (3.3),
\[
X_m\ge p^m t^{-(m-1)},
\tag{4.1}
\]
while spectral radius gives
\[
Y_m\ge r^m=p^m t^m.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
X_m+Y_m
\ge
p^m\big(t^{-(m-1)}+t^m\big).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The scalar function
\[
f_m(t)=t^{-(m-1)}+t^m
\]
has its unique minimum at
\[
t_m=\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{1/(2m-1)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
X_m+Y_m\ge c_m p^m,
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
c_m=
\frac{2m-1}{m}
\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\right)^{(m-1)/(2m-1)}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Examples are
\[
c_2=1.8898815748423097\ldots,
\quad
c_3=1.9601317042077893\ldots,
\quad
c_4=1.9796263300525183\ldots,
\]
and `c_m -> 2` as `m -> infinity`.

There is also a norm-sensitive piecewise refinement.  Let
\[
a=\|A\|,\qquad b=\|B\|,
\qquad
z=\frac{p^2}{a^2b^2}\in[0,1].
\]
The elementary spectral-radius floor from the previous checkpoint,
\[
r\ge\frac{p^2}{ab},
\]
says `t>=sqrt(z)`.  Thus, if
\[
z_m=t_m^2=
\left(\frac{m-1}{m}\right)^{2/(2m-1)},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{X_m+Y_m}{p^m}
\ge
\psi_m(z):=
\begin{cases}
 c_m, & 0\le z\le z_m,\\[4pt]
 z^{-(m-1)/2}+z^{m/2}, & z_m\le z\le1.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
This specializes exactly to the previous run's `m=2` ordered/alternating estimate.

# 5. Four additional sandwich words

The key step beyond the previous `n=4` calculation is that when `m>=3`, the balanced `(m,m)` occupancy class contains four further distinct words whose norms are controlled by the same interpolation hierarchy.

Define
\[
H_m:=\rho(A^mB^m).
\]
Since `A^mB^m` has the same nonzero spectrum as the PSD matrix
\[
B^{m/2}A^mB^{m/2},
\]
we have
\[
H_m
=\|B^{m/2}A^mB^{m/2}\|
=\|A^{m/2}B^{m/2}\|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Apply Theorem 3.1 at the real exponent `s=m/2`:
\[
\|A^{m/2}B^{m/2}\|
\ge
\frac{p^{m-1}}{r^{m/2-1}}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
H_m\ge p^m t^{-(m-2)}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `m>=3`, the following four length-`2m` words are distinct:
\[
A^{m-1}B^mA,
\qquad
AB^mA^{m-1},
\]
\[
B^{m-1}A^mB,
\qquad
BA^mB^{m-1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Each contains exactly `m` copies of each letter.  The first is a cyclic product partner of `A^mB^m`:
\[
(A^{m-1}B^m)A
\quad\text{and}\quad
A(A^{m-1}B^m)=A^mB^m
\]
have the same nonzero spectrum.  Thus its spectral radius is `H_m`, and its operator norm is at least `H_m`.  The second word is its adjoint.  The two `B`-endpoint words are treated identically, using the common nonzero spectrum of `A^mB^m` and `B^mA^m`.  Therefore the four words in (5.3) contribute at least
\[
4H_m\ge4p^m t^{-(m-2)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

# 6. New balanced two-label collision theorem

Let `C_{m,m}^{\{A,B\}}` denote the sum of the operator norms of **all** `binom(2m,m)` words of length `2m` containing exactly `m` copies of `A` and `m` copies of `B`.

For `m>=3`, this class contains the two ordered words `A^mB^m,B^mA^m`, the two alternating words `(AB)^m,(BA)^m`, and the four sandwich words (5.3), all distinct.  Combining (4.1), (4.2), and (5.4),
\[
C_{m,m}^{\{A,B\}}
\ge
2p^m\left(
 t^{-(m-1)}+t^m+2t^{-(m-2)}
\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

## Lemma 6.1 — the scalar bracket is at least four
For every integer `m>=3` and every `0<t<=1`,
\[
\boxed{
 t^{-(m-1)}+t^m+2t^{-(m-2)}\ge4.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof
Set `x=1/t>=1`.  The left side is
\[
F_m(x)=x^{m-1}+x^{-m}+2x^{m-2}.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
F_m'(x)
=(m-1)x^{m-2}-mx^{-m-1}+2(m-2)x^{m-3}.
\]
For `x>=1`,
\[
F_m'(x)
\ge
(m-1)-m+2(m-2)
=2m-5>0.
\]
Thus `F_m` is increasing on `[1,infinity)`, so
\[
F_m(x)\ge F_m(1)=4.
\]
QED.

Substituting into (6.1) proves the new theorem:
\[
\boxed{
C_{m,m}^{\{A,B\}}\ge8\|AB\|^m,
\qquad m\ge3.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This theorem is arbitrary-dimensional and requires only PSD.  No commutativity, rank restriction, invertibility, or projection hypothesis is used.

### The exceptional first case `m=2`
For `m=2` the four sandwich words in (5.3) collapse pairwise to the two palindromes `AB^2A` and `BA^2B`; each has norm exactly `p^2`.  Therefore the six-word `2+2` class obeys
\[
C_{2,2}^{\{A,B\}}
\ge
2p^2\big(t^{-1}+t^2+1\big).
\]
Minimizing over `t` yields
\[
C_{2,2}^{\{A,B\}}
\ge
\left(2+3\,2^{1/3}\right)p^2,
\tag{6.4}
\]
which is the uniform tail of the stronger piecewise/Jensen `Q_22` theorem from the latest checkpoint.

# 7. A general even-length distinct-word bound

Now let the owner-diagonal case have `n=2m` PSD matrices
\[
A_1,\dots,A_{2m},
\qquad
p_{ij}:=\|A_iA_j\|=\|A_jA_i\|,
\]
and define
\[
P_m:=\sum_{1\le i<j\le2m}p_{ij}^m.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Let
\[
S_{2m}:=
\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2m}}
\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(2m)}\|.
\]

## Theorem 7.1 — perfect-matching split
For every integer `m>=1`,
\[
\boxed{
S_{2m}\le2(2m-2)!\,P_m.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof
For each permutation `sigma`, repeated submultiplicativity across adjacent pairs gives
\[
\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(2m)}\|
\le
\prod_{r=1}^m
p_{\sigma(2r-1),\sigma(2r)}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Every permutation determines a perfect matching of the `2m` labels.  Conversely, a fixed undirected perfect matching is represented by exactly
\[
2^m m!
\]
permutations: `m!` orders of its pairs and two orientations for each pair.  Hence
\[
S_{2m}
\le
2^m m!
\sum_{M\in\mathcal M_{2m}}
\prod_{e\in M}p_e,
\tag{7.4}
\]
where `mathcal M_{2m}` is the set of perfect matchings of `K_{2m}`.

For one matching, scalar AM–GM gives
\[
\prod_{e\in M}p_e
\le
\frac1m\sum_{e\in M}p_e^m.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Each edge belongs to exactly `(2m-3)!!` perfect matchings, so
\[
\sum_{M\in\mathcal M_{2m}}
\prod_{e\in M}p_e
\le
\frac{(2m-3)!!}{m}P_m.
\tag{7.6}
\]
Finally,
\[
2^m m!\frac{(2m-3)!!}{m}
=2(2m-2)!,
\]
which proves (7.2). QED.

For `m=2`, (7.2) is exactly the previously used `S_4<=4P`.

# 8. Consequence for the balanced collision layer in every even owner case

Let `Q_{m,m}` be the sum over all length-`2m` words whose multiplicity pattern is exactly `(m,m)`: choose an unordered pair `{i,j}` and sum all words containing `m` copies of `A_i` and `m` copies of `A_j`.

For `m>=3`, Theorem 6.3 gives pairwise
\[
C_{m,m}^{\{A_i,A_j\}}\ge8p_{ij}^m.
\]
Summing over all unordered pairs,
\[
\boxed{
Q_{m,m}\ge8P_m.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Together with Theorem 7.1,
\[
\boxed{
Q_{m,m}
\ge
\frac{4}{(2m-2)!}\,S_{2m},
\qquad m\ge3.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The first new concrete owner-diagonal instance is `n=6` (`m=3`):
\[
S_6\le48\sum_{i<j}\|A_iA_j\|^3,
\]
while the complete `(3,3)` collision layer satisfies
\[
Q_{3,3}\ge8\sum_{i<j}\|A_iA_j\|^3,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{Q_{3,3}\ge\frac16 S_6.}
\tag{8.3}
\]
This is only one occupancy layer and is far from the full `n=6` coefficient required by the owner inequality, but it is a rigorous dimension-free collision contribution beyond the previously concentrated `n=4` work.

# 10. Failed routes / obstructions this run

1. **No unrestricted cubic alternating proof.**  For positive contractions `A,B`, writing `T=AB` and taking a top singular pair `Tv=pu`, `T^*u=pv` yields useful structural inequalities such as `<u,Av>=<u,ABA? >`-type lower overlaps after the standard contraction comparisons, but the attempted vector-geometric closure loses too much in orthogonal components.  No such argument is used above.

2. A purely spectral condition `rho(T)>=||T||^2` is not sufficient by itself to force `||T^2||>=||T||^3` for a general nonnormal matrix with positive spectrum; the special product-of-positive structure matters.

3. The new even-length theorem controls only one occupancy family `(m,m)`.  It does not by itself supply the enormous total collision mass required for the full `n=2m` owner inequality.  Additional unbalanced multiplicity layers must be coupled rather than counted independently without proof.
