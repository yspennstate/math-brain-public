# 3. A sharp weighted Hamiltonian-path inequality for \(K_5\)

Let \(x_{ij}=x_{ji}\ge0\), \(x_{ii}=0\), on five labeled vertices. Put
\[
W:=\sum_{1\le i<j\le5}x_{ij}.
\]
Define the directed Hamiltonian-path polynomial
\[
D_5(x)
:=
\sum_{\sigma\in S_5}
\prod_{r=1}^{4}x_{\sigma(r),\sigma(r+1)}.
\]

## Theorem 3.1

For every nonnegative symmetric five-vertex edge-weight array,
\[
\boxed{
D_5(x)\le \frac{2}{125}W^4.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Equivalently, for the mean directed Hamiltonian-path weight
\[
H_5(x)=\frac{D_5(x)}{120},
\]
\[
\boxed{
H_5(x)\le\frac{W^4}{7500}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The constant is sharp. Equality is attained by an equal-weight 5-cycle: assign weight \(W/5\) to the five cycle edges and zero to the five chords. There are exactly ten directed Hamiltonian paths, each of weight \((W/5)^4\), so
\[
D_5=10(W/5)^4=2W^4/125.
\]

### Proof

Let
\[
d_v:=\sum_{u\ne v}x_{vu}
\]
be the weighted degree of vertex \(v\). The degree-sensitive theorem from the previous verified checkpoint gives
\[
D_5(x)
\le
\sum_{v=1}^5
 d_v\left(\frac{W-d_v}{3}\right)^3.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Indeed, after choosing the first edge from \(v\), delete \(v\); the residual graph has four vertices and edge mass \(W-d_v\), and the rooted weighted Moran--Snir--Manber theorem bounds each residual rooted path sum by \(((W-d_v)/3)^3\).

If \(W=0\), the result is trivial. Otherwise set
\[
y_v:=\frac{d_v}{W}.
\]
Then
\[
0\le y_v\le1,
\qquad
\sum_{v=1}^5 y_v=2,
\]
because the sum of weighted degrees is \(2W\). Equation (3.3) becomes
\[
D_5(x)
\le
\frac{W^4}{27}
\sum_{v=1}^5 y_v(1-y_v)^3.
\tag{3.4}
\]

It remains to prove the finite-dimensional scalar lemma
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{v=1}^5 y_v(1-y_v)^3
\le
\frac{54}{125}
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
under \(0\le y_v\le1\) and \(\sum y_v=2\).

Write
\[
f(y)=y(1-y)^3.
\]
We have
\[
f''(y)=6(1-y)(2y-1),
\]
so \(f\) is concave on \([0,1/2]\).

First consider a boundary maximizer. If some \(y_i\in\{0,1\}\), then \(f(y_i)=0\), while
\[
\max_{0\le y\le1}f(y)=f(1/4)=\frac{27}{256}.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_i f(y_i)\le4\cdot\frac{27}{256}
=\frac{27}{64}
=\frac{54}{125}-\frac{81}{8000}
<
\frac{54}{125}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus any maximizer attaining or exceeding \(54/125\) is interior. At an interior constrained local maximum, the second-order necessary condition along every tangent direction \(e_i-e_j\) gives
\[
f''(y_i)+f''(y_j)\le0.
\tag{3.7}
\]
Consequently at most one coordinate can exceed \(1/2\), because \(f''(y)>0\) for \(y>1/2\).

If no coordinate exceeds \(1/2\), Jensen on the concavity interval gives
\[
\sum_i f(y_i)
\le
5f(2/5)
=
\frac{54}{125}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

If exactly one coordinate, say \(y_1=t\), exceeds \(1/2\), then the other four lie in \((0,1/2)\) and have sum \(2-t\). Jensen gives
\[
\sum_i f(y_i)
\le
f(t)+4f\left(\frac{2-t}{4}\right).
\]
A direct expansion gives the exact identity
\[
\frac{54}{125}
-
\left[
f(t)+4f\left(\frac{2-t}{4}\right)
\right]
=
\frac{(5t-2)^2(325t^2-680t+364)}{8000}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
The quadratic factor is positive for all real \(t\), since its discriminant is
\[
680^2-4\cdot325\cdot364=-10800<0.
\]
Thus (3.5) holds.

Substitution into (3.4) yields
\[
D_5(x)
\le
\frac{W^4}{27}\frac{54}{125}
=
\frac{2W^4}{125}.
\]
This proves Theorem 3.1. \(\square\)

# 4. Low-overlap rank-one bound

Now let
\[
A_i=a\,u_i u_i^*,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,5,
\]
where \(a\ge0\) is common to all five matrices and \(\|u_i\|=1\). By homogeneity it suffices to take \(a=1\).

Define
\[
g_{ij}:=|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|,
\qquad
g_{ii}=1,
\]
and
\[
\bar g:=\frac1{10}\sum_{i<j}g_{ij}.
\]

For rank-one projectors,
\[
\|A_{i_1}\cdots A_{i_5}\|
=
\prod_{r=1}^4 g_{i_r i_{r+1}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Hence the all-distinct side is exactly
\[
L_5=H_5(G).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Theorem 3.1 with \(W=10\bar g\) gives
\[
\boxed{
L_5\le\frac43\,\bar g^4.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The previously verified rank-one transfer identity gives
\[
R_5
=
\frac1{5^5}\mathbf1^T G^4\mathbf1.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Blakley--Roy, applied to the symmetric entrywise-nonnegative matrix \(G\) and \(5^{-1/2}\mathbf1\), gives
\[
\boxed{
R_5
\ge
\mu^4,
\qquad
\mu:=\frac{1+4\bar g}{5}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

For \(0<\bar g\le18/25\), the ratio
\[
\frac{\mu}{\bar g}
=
\frac{1+4\bar g}{5\bar g}
\]
is decreasing in \(\bar g\). At \(\bar g=18/25\),
\[
\left(\frac{\mu}{\bar g}\right)^4
=
\left(\frac{97}{90}\right)^4
>
\frac43,
\]
because
\[
3\cdot97^4-4\cdot90^4=3147843>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\bar g\le\frac{18}{25}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_5\le R_5.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This already improves the preceding global path threshold \(0.6069\ldots\) to at least \(0.72\).

# 5. High-overlap complementary bound

The low-overlap estimate loses a factor because it ignores the constraint \(0\le g_{ij}\le1\). Near the all-ones kernel, it is better to work with deficits
\[
h_{ij}:=1-g_{ij}\in[0,1],
\qquad
T:=\sum_{i<j}h_{ij}=10(1-\bar g).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Choose a uniformly random directed Hamiltonian path \(P\) on the five vertices. Its weight is
\[
\prod_{e\in P}(1-h_e).
\]
For four numbers in \([0,1]\),
\[
\prod_{r=1}^4(1-h_r)
=
1-s_1+s_2-s_3+s_4
\le
1-s_1+s_2,
\tag{5.2}
\]
because \(s_3\ge s_4\).

Each edge of \(K_5\) belongs to a random Hamiltonian path with probability \(2/5\), so
\[
\mathbb E s_1=\frac25T.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For \(s_2\), two specified edges occur together with probability \(1/10\) if they share a vertex and \(1/5\) if they are disjoint. Let
\[
A:=\sum_{\substack{e<f\\ e,f\text{ adjacent}}}h_eh_f,
\qquad
D:=\sum_{\substack{e<f\\ e,f\text{ disjoint}}}h_eh_f.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E s_2=\frac1{10}A+\frac15D.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Let
\[
r_v:=\sum_{e\ni v}h_e
\]
be the deficit degree at vertex \(v\), and let
\[
Q:=\sum_e h_e^2.
\]
Since each adjacent pair of edges has a unique common endpoint,
\[
\sum_v r_v^2=2Q+2A.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Also
\[
T^2=Q+2A+2D.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Eliminating \(A,D,Q\) gives the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E s_2
=
\frac1{10}T^2-\frac1{20}\sum_v r_v^2.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Because
\[
\sum_v r_v=2T,
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
\sum_v r_v^2\ge\frac{4T^2}{5}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E s_2\le\frac{3}{50}T^2.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Combining (5.2), (5.3), and (5.8),
\[
\boxed{
L_5
\le
1-\frac25T+\frac{3}{50}T^2.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Meanwhile
\[
\mu
=
\frac{1+4\bar g}{5}
=
1-\frac{2T}{25},
\]
so Blakley--Roy still gives
\[
R_5\ge\left(1-\frac{2T}{25}\right)^4.
\tag{5.10}
\]

The difference factors as
\[
\left(1-\frac{2T}{25}\right)^4
-
\left(1-\frac25T+\frac{3}{50}T^2\right)
=
\frac{T}{781250}
\left(
32T^3-1600T^2-16875T+62500
\right).
\tag{5.11}
\]
Let
\[
P(T)=32T^3-1600T^2-16875T+62500.
\]
For \(0\le T\le14/5\),
\[
P'(T)=96T^2-3200T-16875<0,
\]
and
\[
P(14/5)=\frac{426058}{125}>0.
\]
Hence \(P(T)>0\) throughout this interval. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
T\le\frac{14}{5}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bar g\ge\frac{18}{25}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_5\le R_5.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

# 6. New full \(n=5\) equal-scale rank-one theorem

The low- and high-overlap regions meet exactly at the convenient rational split \(\bar g=18/25\).

## Theorem 6.1

Let
\[
A_i=a\,u_i u_i^*,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,5,
\]
where \(a\ge0\), \(\|u_i\|=1\), and the ambient real or complex dimension is arbitrary. Then
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{5!}
\sum_{\sigma\in S_5}
\|A_{\sigma(1)}A_{\sigma(2)}A_{\sigma(3)}A_{\sigma(4)}A_{\sigma(5)}\|
\le
\frac1{5^5}
\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_5=1}^5
\|A_{i_1}A_{i_2}A_{i_3}A_{i_4}A_{i_5}\|.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

If \(a=0\), both sides vanish. Otherwise divide by \(a^5\) and use the normalized projectors.

If \(\bar g\le18/25\), apply (4.6). If \(\bar g\ge18/25\), apply (5.12). These cases cover \(0\le\bar g\le1\). \(\square\)

No equiangularity, coherence bound, dimension restriction, reality assumption, or genericity assumption is used. The only extra hypothesis beyond rank one is the common scale \(\|A_i\|=a\).

This is the first complete \(n=5\) rank-one closure recorded in the campaign. The arbitrary-scale rank-one \(n=5\) problem remains open.

# 7. Method ceiling and failed extension

The natural conjectural all-\(n\) strengthening
\[
P_H(x)\stackrel{?}{\le}\frac{W^{n-1}}{n^{n-2}}
\tag{7.1}
\]
is now proved here for \(n=5\) (and the prior \(K_4\) calculation is exactly its \(n=4\) case).

The present degree-optimization proof does **not** extend directly to \(n\ge6\). For general \(n\), the degree-sensitive bound reduces to maximizing
\[
\sum_{v=1}^n
 y_v(1-y_v)^{n-2},
\qquad
0\le y_v\le1,\qquad
\sum_v y_v=2.
\tag{7.2}
\]
For \(n=5\), the uniform point \(y_v=2/5\) is the global maximizer, which is the scalar lemma above. For \(n\ge6\), this scalar optimization is no longer maximized at the uniform point; numerical optimization already produces one-large-degree patterns with a larger value. Thus the same one-step degree relaxation cannot prove (7.1) for \(n\ge6\), even though direct optimization of the actual Hamiltonian-path polynomial continues to favor the equal-weight cycle in tests.

This is a **failure of the proof relaxation**, not a counterexample to (7.1).
