# 3. New singular-value/eigenvalue constraint for products of PSD matrices

Let `A,B` be PSD.  Write their operator norms
\[
a:=\|A\|,
\qquad
b:=\|B\|.
\]
Let
\[
s_1(AB)\ge\cdots\ge s_d(AB)\ge0
\]
be the singular values and let
\[
\lambda_1(AB)\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d(AB)\ge0
\]
be the eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity.  The latter are nonnegative because `AB` has the same nonzero spectrum as the PSD matrix `B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}`.

## Lemma 3.1
For every `j`,
\[
\boxed{
s_j(AB)^2
\le
ab\,\lambda_j(AB).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
In particular, for positive contractions,
\[
\boxed{s_j(AB)^2\le\lambda_j(AB).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
Scale first to `a=b=1`, so `0<=A,B<=I`.

Because `A^2<=A`,
\[
(AB)^*(AB)=BA^2B\preceq BAB.
\]
By eigenvalue monotonicity for PSD matrices,
\[
s_j(AB)^2
=\lambda_j(BA^2B)
\le
\lambda_j(BAB).
\tag{3.3}
\]
Now set
\[
C:=B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\succeq0.
\]
The eigenvalues of `C` are exactly the eigenvalues of `AB`, including zeros.  Also
\[
BAB=B^{1/2}CB^{1/2}
=(C^{1/2}B^{1/2})^*(C^{1/2}B^{1/2}).
\]
Hence
\[
\lambda_j(BAB)
=s_j(C^{1/2}B^{1/2})^2.
\]
The standard singular-value product bound
\[
s_j(XY)\le\|Y\|s_j(X)
\]
gives, since `||B^{1/2}||<=1`,
\[
\lambda_j(BAB)
\le
s_j(C^{1/2})^2
=\lambda_j(C)
=\lambda_j(AB).
\tag{3.4}
\]
Combining (3.3) and (3.4) proves (3.2).

For general `a,b`, apply the normalized result to `A/a` and `B/b`; after rescaling, (3.1) follows.  QED.

### Remarks

* The `j=1` case recovers the useful inequality
  \[
  \rho(AB)\ge\frac{\|AB\|^2}{\|A\|\|B\|}.
  \]
* For a product of two orthogonal projections, equality holds at each nonzero singular/eigenvalue pair: the singular values are cosines of principal angles while the eigenvalues are their squares.
* This lemma by itself only gives the previously known quartic alternating lower bound through spectral radius; it does **not** prove the desired cubic bound in general dimension.

# 4. Exact metric normal form for invertible products of two positive contractions

The next reformulation isolates the remaining two-matrix difficulty.

## Lemma 4.1
Let `T` be invertible.  Then `T` is a product of two positive definite contractions if and only if it is unitarily similar to
\[
\boxed{G^{1/2}DG^{-1/2}}
\tag{4.1}
\]
for a positive diagonal matrix `D` and a positive definite matrix `G` satisfying
\[
\boxed{D\preceq G\preceq I.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Forward proof
Suppose
\[
T=AB,
\qquad
0<A\preceq I,
\quad
0<B\preceq I.
\]
Equip the space with the weighted inner product
\[
\langle x,y\rangle_{A^{-1}}:=x^*A^{-1}y.
\]
Then
\[
\langle Tx,y\rangle_{A^{-1}}
=x^*By
=
\langle x,Ty\rangle_{A^{-1}},
\]
so `T` is self-adjoint in this inner product; moreover
\[
\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A^{-1}}=x^*Bx\ge0.
\]
Hence choose an `A^{-1}`-orthonormal eigenbasis, written as the columns of an invertible matrix `V`, so
\[
TV=VD,
\qquad
V^*A^{-1}V=I,
\tag{4.3}
\]
with `D` diagonal and positive.

Set
\[
G:=V^*V.
\]
Since `A<=I`, we have `A^{-1}>=I`, and (4.3) gives
\[
G=V^*V\preceq V^*A^{-1}V=I.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Also, for every coefficient vector `c`, letting `x=Vc`,
\[
c^*Dc
=
\langle Tx,x\rangle_{A^{-1}}
=x^*Bx
\le x^*x
=c^*Gc,
\]
so
\[
D\preceq G.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Finally polar-decompose `V=UG^{1/2}` with `U` unitary.  From `T=VDV^{-1}` we obtain
\[
U^*TU=G^{1/2}DG^{-1/2}.
\]

### Reverse proof
Conversely, suppose `D<=G<=I` and put
\[
T:=G^{1/2}DG^{-1/2}.
\]
Set
\[
A:=G,
\qquad
B:=G^{-1/2}DG^{-1/2}.
\]
Then `A` is a positive contraction, while `D<=G` implies `0<B<=I`.  Moreover
\[
AB=G^{1/2}DG^{-1/2}=T.
\]
Thus `T` is a product of two positive contractions.  QED.

### Reduction of the cubic alternating problem
Under this normal form, the conjectural all-dimensional two-matrix cubic inequality becomes the purely metric statement
\[
\boxed{
\|G^{1/2}D^2G^{-1/2}\|
\stackrel{?}{\ge}
\|G^{1/2}DG^{-1/2}\|^3
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
for every
\[
0<D\preceq G\preceq I.
\]
The earlier `2 x 2` proof verifies (4.6) in dimension two.  This is now a sharply isolated subproblem for future runs.

For singular factors/products one can pass by standard positive regularization for inequalities continuous in the entries; no singular case is needed in the quadratic consequence below because that consequence is obtained directly from the Li–Tsai block classification.

# 5. Cubic alternating-word theorem for quadratic products in arbitrary dimension

Recall that an operator `T` is **quadratic** if
\[
(T-\alpha I)(T-\beta I)=0
\]
for some scalars `alpha,beta`; equivalently, its minimal polynomial has degree at most two.  For a product of PSD matrices its spectrum is nonnegative, so the relevant roots lie in `[0,infinity)`.

The Li–Tsai classification says that a quadratic operator which is a product of two positive contractions is unitarily similar to an orthogonal direct sum of scalar blocks and blocks of the form
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha I & P\\
0 & \beta I
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{5.1}
\]
where `P` is positive and satisfies the corresponding positive-contraction factorization bound.  Diagonalizing `P` unitarily on the common multiplicity space decomposes (5.1) into `2 x 2` upper-triangular blocks
\[
\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&p_j\\0&\beta\end{pmatrix}
\tag{5.2}
\]
which are themselves products of two positive contractions.

The previous durable checkpoint `runs/2026-09-15/2026-09-15T142426Z__report.md` proves for **every `2 x 2` product of two positive contractions** `X` that
\[
\|X^2\|\ge\|X\|^3.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Scalar contraction blocks also satisfy `x^2>=x^3` for `0<=x<=1`.

Therefore direct sums preserve the cubic inequality.

## Theorem 5.1
Let `A,B` be PSD matrices and suppose `AB` is quadratic.  Then
\[
\boxed{
\|ABAB\|
\ge
\frac{\|AB\|^3}{\|A\|\|B\|}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
The same bound holds for `BABA`.

### Proof
If `A=0` or `B=0`, trivial.  Normalize
\[
\widehat A=A/\|A\|,
\qquad
\widehat B=B/\|B\|,
\qquad
T:=\widehat A\widehat B.
\]
Then `T` is a quadratic product of two positive contractions.  By the Li–Tsai block form and the preceding direct-sum argument,
\[
\|T^2\|\ge\|T\|^3.
\]
Rescaling gives
\[
\frac{\|ABAB\|}{\|A\|^2\|B\|^2}
\ge
\left(
\frac{\|AB\|}{\|A\|\|B\|}
\right)^3,
\]
which is exactly (5.4).  The `BABA` statement follows either identically or by taking adjoints.  QED.

### Scope
This is a genuine arbitrary-dimensional extension of the previous `2 x 2` lemma, but only for quadratic pair products.  It does **not** prove the all-dimensional cubic bound for an arbitrary pair `A,B`.

A convenient sufficient condition is: `AB` has at most two distinct eigenvalues.  Since a product of two PSD matrices is diagonalizable on its nonzero spectral subspace and has nonnegative spectrum, that condition makes the minimal polynomial quadratic (with the usual harmless zero block included only when it is one of the two eigenvalues).

\[
