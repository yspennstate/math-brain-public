# PROB20-17 — norm-inside-average noncommutative AM–GM

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.**

The full Bandeira/Duchi norm-inside-average conjecture remains open.  The target is, for arbitrary `n` and arbitrary PSD `d x d` matrices `A_1,...,A_n`,
\[
\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(n)}\|
\le
\frac1{n^n}\sum_{k_1,\dots,k_n\in[n]}\|A_{k_1}\cdots A_{k_n}\|,
\]
with spectral norm inside both averages.

This run continues the first unresolved diagonal case `n=m=4`.  It does **not** improve the strongest durable unrestricted quantitative factor
\[
\frac{R/256}{S/24}\ge 0.7303902120286716\ldots,
\]
but it proves a new exact obstruction that materially changes the proof strategy: the natural sharp-looking standalone occupancy bound
\[
Q_{211}\stackrel{?}{\ge}6S
\]
is false even for `2 x 2` rank-one PSD matrices.  In fact an explicit exact rational example has
\[
\frac{Q_{211}}S=\frac{21179}{4000}=5.29475<6,
\]
and a two-parameter rank-one family shows that no universal pure linear bound `Q_211 >= c S` can have
\[
c>1+2\sqrt3=4.464101615\ldots.
\]
Thus a proof of sharp `n=4` cannot close by independently forcing every occupancy layer to its equal-matrix coefficient; it must exploit quantitative tradeoffs between layers (notably the compensation by `Q_4`, `Q_22`, or scale statistics when `Q_211` is small).

A second exact contribution is a top-singular-vector reduction of the still-open two-matrix `2+2` product inequality, together with a positive `2 x 2` Gram constraint on the singular pair.  This isolates a more structured vector inequality for future attacks.

# 3. Rank-one bookkeeping

Let
\[
A_i=s_i u_i u_i^*,
\qquad
s_i>0,
\qquad
\|u_i\|=1.
\]
Put
\[
g_{ij}:=|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|.
\]
For every word `w=(i_1,i_2,i_3,i_4)`, rank one gives the exact formula
\[
\boxed{
\|A_{i_1}A_{i_2}A_{i_3}A_{i_4}\|
=
\left(\prod_{r=1}^4s_{i_r}\right)
 g_{i_1i_2}g_{i_2i_3}g_{i_3i_4}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For an all-distinct word the scale product is always `s_1s_2s_3s_4`.  For a `2+1+1` word whose repeated index is `r` and whose missing index is `m`, the scale product equals
\[
(s_1s_2s_3s_4)\frac{s_r}{s_m}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
This makes exact occupancy calculations possible using only a four-vertex weighted Gram graph.

# 4. An exact rational counterexample to `Q_211 >= 6S`

Work in `R^2` and define unit vectors
\[
u_1=e_1,
\qquad
u_2=\frac7{25}e_1+\frac{24}{25}e_2,
\qquad
u_3=e_2,
\qquad
u_4=e_1.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Take scales
\[
(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4)=\left(1,\frac1{10},2,1\right)
\tag{4.2}
\]
and set `A_i=s_i u_i u_i^T`.

Equivalently,
\[
A_1=A_4=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_3=
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
A_2=\frac1{6250}
\begin{pmatrix}
49&168\\168&576
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Every matrix is rational, PSD, rank one, and `2 x 2`.

The nontrivial absolute Gram entries are
\[
g_{12}=g_{24}=\frac7{25},
\qquad
g_{23}=\frac{24}{25},
\qquad
g_{14}=1,
\]
while
\[
g_{13}=g_{34}=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

## 4.1 Exact distinct-word sum

Among the 24 Hamiltonian paths, the total Gram edge weight is
\[
4\left(\frac7{25}\right)\left(\frac{24}{25}\right).
\]
The common all-distinct scale product is `1*(1/10)*2*1=1/5`.  Hence
\[
\boxed{
S=\frac{672}{3125}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

## 4.2 Exact `2+1+1` sum

For completeness, introduce the general family
\[
u_1=u_4=e_1,
\qquad
u_2=a e_1+b e_2,
\qquad
u_3=e_2,
\qquad
a^2+b^2=1,
\tag{4.6}
\]
with scales
\[
(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4)=(1,\varepsilon,t,1).
\tag{4.7}
\]
For repeated index `r` and missing index `m`, let `F_{rm}` denote the sum of the Gram edge products over the 12 permutations of the corresponding multiset.  Direct enumeration from (3.1) gives
\[
(F_{rm})_{r\ne m}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
- &0&6a(a+1)&2ab\\
2ab(a+b+1)&-&4a(a^2+a+1)&2ab(a+b+1)\\
2ab&0&-&2ab\\
2ab&0&6a(a+1)&-
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Because of (3.2),
\[
Q_{211}
=(\varepsilon t)
\sum_{r\ne m}\frac{s_r}{s_m}F_{rm}.
\]
Substitution of (4.8) yields the exact closed form
\[
\boxed{
\frac{Q_{211}}S
=
1+t+\frac{3(a+1)}{bt}
+\varepsilon(a+b+1)
+\varepsilon\frac{a^2+a+1}{bt}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

For the rational choice
\[
a=\frac7{25},
\qquad
b=\frac{24}{25},
\qquad
t=2,
\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac1{10},
\]
(4.9) gives
\[
\boxed{
Q_{211}=\frac{444759}{390625},
\qquad
\frac{Q_{211}}S=\frac{21179}{4000}=5.29475<6.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{Q_{211}\ge6S\quad\text{is false}.}
\tag{4.11}
\]
This is an exact rational certificate, not a floating-point counterexample.

# 5. A sharp obstruction to any layerwise coefficient

Formula (4.9) gives more than one isolated example.  For fixed `a,b`, let `epsilon -> 0+`.  The limiting expression is
\[
1+t+\frac{3(a+1)}{bt}.
\]
Its minimum over `t>0` occurs at
\[
t_*(a)=\sqrt{\frac{3(a+1)}b}
\]
and equals
\[
1+2\sqrt{\frac{3(a+1)}b}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Since `b=sqrt(1-a^2)`, letting `a -> 0+` gives
\[
\boxed{
\liminf\frac{Q_{211}}S
\le
1+2\sqrt3
=4.464101615\ldots.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Rational points on the unit circle are dense, and rational choices of the scales approximate the minimizing parameters, so the same upper barrier is obtained by rational rank-one PSD examples.

Consequently, if a universal pure linear inequality
\[
Q_{211}\ge cS
\]
holds for all PSD quadruples, then necessarily
\[
\boxed{c\le1+2\sqrt3<6.}
\tag{5.3}
\]
No claim is made that `1+2sqrt(3)` is the optimal universal coefficient; (5.3) is an upper bound on what any such coefficient could be.

# 6. Structural consequence for the `n=4` proof strategy

At the equal-matrix configuration, the occupancy ratios are
\[
Q_{211}/S=6,
\qquad
Q_{22}/S=\frac32,
\qquad
Q_{31}/S=2,
\qquad
Q_4/S=\frac16,
\]
and these sum with the distinct layer `S` to the sharp target
\[
1+6+\frac32+2+\frac16=\frac{32}{3}.
\]
It was therefore natural to try to prove each collision layer independently at its equal-matrix coefficient.

The exact example above proves that this strategy is impossible already for the `2+1+1` layer: its equal-matrix coefficient `6` is not a valid universal lower bound, even in dimension two and rank one.

Hence any sharp proof of `3R>=32S` must use **cross-layer compensation**.  Small `Q_211` is necessarily accompanied by other scale/geometry information, and that information must be charged to `Q_4`, `Q_22`, `Q_31`, or a coupled statistic.  This is consistent with the current durable lower bound
\[
Q_{211}\ge2S+\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{PE}},
\]
which deliberately retains the scale statistic `E` instead of asserting a fixed multiple of `S`.

The counterexample family is very far from a counterexample to the full AMGM inequality: the scale imbalance making `Q_211/S` small makes the pure fourth-power layer large.  Thus (4.11) refutes a proof route, **not** the owner target.

# 7. New top-singular-vector reduction for the open `2+2` pair product target

The other principal unresolved local target is, for PSD `A,B`,
\[
\boxed{
\|A^2B^2\|\,\|ABAB\|
\stackrel{?}{\ge}\|AB\|^4.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
It is proved in earlier checkpoints for several special classes, including when one factor is a scalar projection, but remains open here in general dimension.

Assume first `A>0`, put
\[
p:=\|AB\|>0,
\qquad
T:=\frac{AB}{p},
\]
and choose top singular vectors `u,v` with
\[
Tv=u,
\qquad
T^*u=v.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Because
\[
T^*=A^{-1}TA,
\tag{7.3}
\]
we have an exact reduction.

First,
\[
\|ABAB\|
=p^2\|T^2\|
\ge
p^2\|Tu\|.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Second, since `B=pA^{-1}T`,
\[
A^2B^2
=p^2 A T A^{-1}T,
\]
so applying this operator to `v` and using `Tv=u`,
\[
\|A^2B^2\|
\ge
p^2\|A T A^{-1}u\|.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Therefore the full pair product inequality (7.1) would follow from the vector statement
\[
\boxed{
\|Tu\|\,\|A T A^{-1}u\|\ge1,
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
for the top left singular vector `u` of a positive-symmetrizable contraction `T` satisfying (7.3).

This is an exact reduction, not a proof of (7.6).

There is, however, a useful exact scalar constraint.  From (7.2),
\[
BAu=pv,
\qquad
Bv=pA^{-1}u.
\tag{7.7}
\]
Define
\[
\alpha:=\langle Au,v\rangle,
\qquad
\beta:=\langle v,A^{-1}u\rangle.
\]
The `B`-Gram matrix of the vectors `Au` and `v` is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\langle Au,BAu\rangle & \langle Au,Bv\rangle\\
\langle v,BAu\rangle & \langle v,Bv\rangle
\end{pmatrix}
=
p
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&1\\1&\beta
\end{pmatrix}
\succeq0.
\tag{7.8}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\alpha\beta\ge1.}
\tag{7.9}
\]
Equivalently, this is the scalar moment log-convexity constraint obtained from the positive operator `B` on the top singular pair.  Bridging (7.9) to the vector product (7.6) is now one sharply isolated route to (7.1).

Fresh random tests supported (7.6), but numerical evidence is not promoted to a theorem.

# 8. Failed / obstructed routes retained

1. **Layerwise equal-matrix closure is impossible.**  The exact rational certificate (4.10) disproves `Q_211>=6S`.
2. **Pure three-lines similarity convexity remains too weak.**  For the similarity orbit used in the preceding checkpoint, standard log-convexity yields bounds in the wrong direction or only recovers known estimates; it does not prove (7.1).
3. **The projection moment proof does not directly extend to arbitrary `A`.**  Idempotence was essential in the `T215211Z` argument.
4. **No full counterexample was found.**  The new rank-one example violates only an intermediate occupancy conjecture and satisfies the full target with a very large margin.
