# PROB20-17 — norm-inside-average noncommutative AM–GM

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.**

The full Bandeira/Duchi norm-inside-average conjecture remains open.  The exact target is, for arbitrary `n` and arbitrary PSD `d x d` matrices `A_1,...,A_n`,
\[
\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(n)}\|
\le
\frac1{n^n}\sum_{k_1,\dots,k_n\in[n]}\|A_{k_1}\cdots A_{k_n}\|,
\]
with spectral norm **inside** both averages.  The Recht–Ré norm-outside counterexample is a different statement.

This run continues the first unresolved diagonal case `n=m=4`.  It proves a new exact two-matrix theorem for arbitrary dimension: the sharp unresolved `2+2` pair inequality is true whenever either PSD factor is a positive scalar multiple of an orthogonal projection.  As a consequence, for four arbitrary scaled projections the complete `2+2` collision layer has its sharp equal-matrix coefficient, and combining this with the durable general collision bounds gives a new quantitative `n=4` theorem for that broader scaled-projection class.

The general arbitrary-PSD constant is **not** changed by this run: its strongest durable value remains
\[
\frac{R/256}{S/24}\ge0.7303902120286716\ldots.
\]

# 3. Sharp pair target with one projection

## Theorem 3.1 — projection pair product inequality

Let `P` be an orthogonal projection on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and let `B>=0` be arbitrary.  Define
\[
p:=\|PB\|,
\qquad
x:=\|PB^2\|,
\qquad
y:=\|PBPB\|.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{xy\ge p^4.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{x+y\ge2p^2.}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Both conclusions hold in arbitrary dimension.

### Proof
Set
\[
K:=PB^2P
\]
viewed on `Ran P`.  Since
\[
(PB)(PB)^*=PB^2P,
\]
we have
\[
\|K\|=p^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Choose a unit vector `v in Ran P` satisfying
\[
Kv=p^2v.
\tag{3.4}
\]
For `r>=0` write the scalar moments
\[
m_r:=\langle v,B^r v\rangle.
\]
By (3.4),
\[
m_2=p^2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

First control the ordered-square word.  Since
\[
x^2
=\|(PB^2)(PB^2)^*\|
=\|PB^4P\|,
\]
we obtain by testing on `v`
\[
\boxed{x^2\ge m_4.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Now put
\[
C:=PBP
\]
on `Ran P`.  If `W=PBPB`, then
\[
WW^*=PBPB^2PBP=CKC,
\]
so
\[
y^2=\|CKC\|.
\tag{3.7}
\]
Evaluate on the same top eigenvector `v` of `K`:
\[
y^2
\ge\langle v,CKCv\rangle
=\langle Cv,K Cv\rangle.
\]
Expand `Cv` in an orthonormal eigenbasis of the PSD matrix `K` containing `v`.  Every spectral contribution is nonnegative, and the component along `v` alone contributes
\[
p^2|\langle v,Cv\rangle|^2.
\]
Because `Pv=v`,
\[
\langle v,Cv\rangle
=\langle v,Bv\rangle
=m_1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{y^2\ge m_2m_1^2.}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Finally use scalar moment log-convexity for the spectral measure of `B` at `v`.  Hölder gives
\[
m_2
=\int t^2\,d\mu(t)
=\int (t)^{2/3}(t^4)^{1/3}\,d\mu(t)
\le m_1^{2/3}m_4^{1/3},
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{m_2^3\le m_1^2m_4.}
\tag{3.9}
\]
Multiplying (3.6) and (3.8) and applying (3.9),
\[
x^2y^2
\ge m_4m_2m_1^2
\ge m_2^4
=p^8.
\]
All quantities are nonnegative, so taking square roots proves (3.1).  Scalar AM–GM then yields (3.2).  QED.

### Homogeneous extension
If `A=cP` for some `c>=0`, then by homogeneity
\[
\boxed{
\|A^2B^2\|\,\|ABAB\|\ge\|AB\|^4,
}
\tag{3.10}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\|A^2B^2\|+\|ABAB\|\ge2\|AB\|^2.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]
By swapping the two factors and taking adjoints, the same holds if **either** factor is a nonnegative scalar multiple of an orthogonal projection.

This class is substantially larger than the earlier rank-one or `2 x 2` classes: `P` may have arbitrary rank and `B` may have arbitrary dimension and spectrum.

# 4. Exact `2+2` layer for scaled projections

Suppose now
\[
A_i=c_iP_i,
\qquad c_i\ge0,
\]
with arbitrary orthogonal projections `P_i` of arbitrary ranks.

For an unordered pair `{i,j}`, set
\[
p_{ij}:=\|A_iA_j\|.
\]
Write
\[
x_{ij}:=\|A_i^2A_j^2\|,
\qquad
y_{ij}:=\|A_iA_jA_iA_j\|.
\]
The six `2+2` words formed from this pair have exact sum
\[
2(x_{ij}+y_{ij}+p_{ij}^2),
\tag{4.1}
\]
because the two palindromic words have norm exactly `p_ij^2` and adjoint pairs have equal norm.

Theorem 3.1 gives
\[
x_{ij}y_{ij}\ge p_{ij}^4,
\]
so
\[
x_{ij}+y_{ij}\ge2p_{ij}^2.
\]
Hence each pair contributes at least
\[
6p_{ij}^2.
\]
Summing over all six unordered pairs,
\[
\boxed{Q_{22}\ge6P.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
This is the sharp equal-matrix coefficient for the complete `2+2` occupancy layer.

# 5. Quantitative `n=4` theorem for arbitrary scaled projections

For four PSD matrices define
\[
S:=\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}\|A_{\sigma(1)}A_{\sigma(2)}A_{\sigma(3)}A_{\sigma(4)}\|,
\]
\[
R:=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell=1}^4\|A_iA_jA_kA_\ell\|.
\]
For scaled projections `A_i=c_iP_i`, combine:

\[
Q_{211}\ge2S+\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{PE}},
\tag{5.1}
\]
\[
Q_{22}\ge6P,
\tag{5.2}
\]
\[
Q_{31}\ge8P,
\tag{5.3}
\]
\[
Q_4=Q,
\tag{5.4}
\]
where
\[
E:=\sum_{i<j}\|A_i\|^2\|A_j\|^2,
\qquad
Q:=\sum_i\|A_i\|^4.
\]
Thus
\[
R
=S+Q_{211}+Q_{22}+Q_{31}+Q_4
\ge
3S+\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{PE}}+14P+Q.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Use the durable constraints
\[
S\le4P,
\qquad
P\le E,
\qquad
E\le\frac32Q.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Normalize
\[
s:=S/P,
\qquad e:=E/P,
\qquad q:=Q/P.
\]
Then
\[
0<s\le4,
\qquad e\ge1,
\qquad q\ge\frac{2e}{3}.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Dividing (5.5) by `S`,
\[
\frac RS
\ge
3+\frac{s}{2\sqrt e}+\frac{14+q}{s}.
\tag{5.8}
\]
For fixed `s,e`, minimize at `q=2e/3`.  For fixed `e>=1`, the right side is strictly decreasing on `0<s<=4`: indeed
\[
\partial_s
=\frac1{2\sqrt e}-\frac{14+2e/3}{s^2}
\le\frac12-\frac{14}{16}<0.
\]
Therefore set `s=4`.  This gives
\[
\frac RS
\ge
F(e)
:=\frac{13}{2}+\frac{2}{\sqrt e}+\frac e6,
\qquad e\ge1.
\tag{5.9}
\]
The unique minimizer solves
\[
e^{3/2}=6,
\qquad
e_*=6^{2/3}.
\]
At this point
\[
\boxed{
\frac RS
\ge
\frac{13}{2}+\frac{3}{6^{1/3}}
=8.150963624447314\ldots.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]
Consequently every four scaled projections satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\frac{R/256}{S/24}
=\frac{3R}{32S}
\ge
0.7641528397919357\ldots.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

This is **not** intended to replace the stronger archived `29/32` theorem for four actual orthogonal projections (`c_i=1`).  It applies to the broader class with arbitrary independent nonnegative scales `c_i`, where idempotence is lost at the matrix level.

# 6. Checks and failed/generalization routes

### Checks actually performed

The proof above was independently algebraically re-read in this run.  In addition, fresh complex-PSD numerical tests in dimensions `2,3,5,8`, with projections of varying ranks, checked
\[
\frac{\|PB^2\|\,\|PBPB\|}{\|PB\|^4}\ge1
\]
and
\[
\frac{\|PB^2\|+\|PBPB\|}{2\|PB\|^2}\ge1.
\]
The smallest sampled values were at floating-point distance from `1`, as expected from commuting/equality regimes.  These tests are corroborative only; the proof is the moment argument in Section 3.

### Failed/generalization routes retained

1. The same top-eigenvector moment proof does **not** directly extend from a projection `P` to arbitrary PSD `A`: the identities `A^2=A` and the compression `K=PB^2P` are used essentially.
2. A stronger cross-Gram attempt using
   \[
   \|(A^2B^2)^*(ABAB)\|
   \]
   as a lower certificate for `p^4` is false numerically in dimension `>=3`; it cannot prove the general pair product target by a single operator Cauchy step.
3. Simple spectral-radius conditions such as `x rho(AB)^2>=p^4` are false numerically and are not used.
4. The stronger oriented-sum candidate
   \[
   \|A^2B^2+ABAB\|\stackrel{?}{\ge}2\|AB\|^2
   \]
   continues to survive random stress tests, but no proof was found and it remains strictly diagnostic.

# 8. Next attack

The projection proof identifies a concrete mechanism worth trying to lift.  It succeeds because a top eigenvector of the compression
\[
K=PB^2P
\]
turns the difficult pair target into scalar moment log-convexity
\[
m_2^3\le m_1^2m_4.
\]
The next useful question is whether an arbitrary PSD `A` admits a weighted compression or metric in which the top singular pair of `AB` yields an analogous three-moment certificate.  The latest similarity-orbit reduction
\[
\|HXH^{-1}X\|\,\|X^2\|\stackrel{?}{\ge}\|X^*X\|^2,
\qquad X^*=H^{-1}XH,
\]
should be compared directly to the projection moment proof rather than attacked only by unconstrained interpolation.

A second route is to prove the oriented-sum inequality
\[
\|A^2B^2+ABAB\|\ge2\|AB\|^2,
\]
which would imply the desired pair sum immediately by the triangle inequality.  Numerical survival is not evidence sufficient for promotion; any future use requires a complete proof or a certified counterexample.

No terminal claim is made.
