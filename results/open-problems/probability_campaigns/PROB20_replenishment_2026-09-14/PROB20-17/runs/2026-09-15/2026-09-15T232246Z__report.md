# PROB20-17 — norm-inside-average noncommutative AM–GM

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.**

The full Bandeira/Duchi norm-inside-average conjecture remains open. The exact target is, for arbitrary `n` and arbitrary PSD `d x d` matrices `A_1,...,A_n`,
\[
\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(n)}\|
\le
\frac1{n^n}\sum_{k_1,\dots,k_n\in[n]}\|A_{k_1}\cdots A_{k_n}\|,
\]
with spectral norm **inside** both averages. The Recht–Ré norm-outside counterexample is a different statement.

This run continues the first unresolved diagonal case `n=m=4`. It proves a new exact arbitrary-dimensional extension of the sharp unresolved two-matrix `2+2` inequality. The pair product inequality
\[
\|A^2B^2\|\,\|ABAB\|\ge \|AB\|^4
\]
is proved whenever a top left singular vector of `AB` is an eigenvector of `A` (or, symmetrically, a top right singular vector of `AB` is an eigenvector of `B`). This strictly contains the previous projection-versus-arbitrary-PSD theorem as a special case and yields the same sharp `Q_22>=6P` collision coefficient for any four-matrix family satisfying this pairwise top-singular alignment condition.

The strongest durable unrestricted arbitrary-PSD `n=4` normalized factor is **not changed** by this run and remains
\[
\frac{R/256}{S/24}\ge0.7303902120286716\ldots.
\]
For the new aligned class the normalized factor is
\[
0.7641528397919357\ldots.
\]

# 3. New top-singular-alignment theorem

Fix arbitrary PSD matrices `A,B` on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. Put
\[
p:=\|AB\|,
\qquad
x:=\|A^2B^2\|,
\qquad
y:=\|ABAB\|.
\]
Let
\[
K:=AB^2A=(AB)(AB)^*\succeq0.
\]
Then
\[
\|K\|=p^2.
\]
A unit vector `v` satisfying
\[
Kv=p^2v
\]
is a top left singular vector of `AB`.

## Theorem 3.1 — aligned top singular vector

Assume there exists a unit top eigenvector `v` of `K` which is also an eigenvector of `A`:
\[
Kv=p^2v,
\qquad
Av=a v
\]
for some `a>=0`. Then
\[
\boxed{xy\ge p^4.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{x+y\ge2p^2.}
\tag{3.2}
\]
The conclusion is dimension-free.

### Proof
If `p=0`, the result is trivial. Hence assume `p>0`; then `a>0` because
\[
p^2=\langle v,AB^2Av\rangle.
\]
For `r>=0` define scalar moments of the spectral measure of `B` at `v` by
\[
m_r:=\langle v,B^r v\rangle.
\]
Using `Av=av`,
\[
p^2
=\langle v,Kv\rangle
=\langle Av,B^2Av\rangle
=a^2m_2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Ordered-square word
Since
\[
x^2
=\|(A^2B^2)(A^2B^2)^*\|
=\|A^2B^4A^2\|,
\]
testing this PSD matrix on `v` gives
\[
x^2
\ge
\langle v,A^2B^4A^2v\rangle
=a^4m_4.
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Alternating word
Let
\[
W:=ABAB,
\qquad
D:=AB.
\]
Then
\[
WW^*
=ABA B^2 ABA
=D K D^*.
\]
Therefore
\[
y^2=\|DKD^*\|.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Testing on `v` gives
\[
y^2
\ge
\langle D^*v,KD^*v\rangle.
\]
Expand `D^*v` in an orthonormal eigenbasis of the PSD matrix `K` containing `v`. Every spectral contribution is nonnegative. Retaining only the component in the top eigendirection `v`,
\[
y^2
\ge
p^2\,|\langle v,D^*v\rangle|^2.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Now `D^*=BA`, so the alignment hypothesis gives
\[
\langle v,D^*v\rangle
=\langle v,BAv\rangle
=a\langle v,Bv\rangle
=a m_1.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{y^2\ge p^2a^2m_1^2.}
\tag{3.7}
\]

### Scalar moment interpolation
Because `B>=0`, Hölder applied to its spectral measure at `v` gives
\[
m_2
=\int t^2\,d\mu(t)
=\int (t)^{2/3}(t^4)^{1/3}\,d\mu(t)
\le
m_1^{2/3}m_4^{1/3}.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{m_2^3\le m_1^2m_4.}
\tag{3.8}
\]
Multiplying (3.4) and (3.7), then using (3.8),
\[
\begin{aligned}
x^2y^2
&\ge a^4m_4\cdot p^2a^2m_1^2\\
&=a^6p^2m_1^2m_4\\
&\ge a^6p^2m_2^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
By (3.3), `m_2=p^2/a^2`, so
\[
a^6p^2m_2^3=p^8.
\]
Therefore
\[
x^2y^2\ge p^8,
\]
and all quantities are nonnegative, proving (3.1). Scalar AM–GM yields (3.2). QED.

## 3.1 Symmetric formulation

Swapping `A` and `B` and using adjoint invariance gives the same conclusion if a top left singular vector of `BA` is an eigenvector of `B`.

Equivalently, for `T=AB`, the pair theorem holds if either

* a top **left** singular vector of `T` is an eigenvector of `A`; or
* a top **right** singular vector of `T` is an eigenvector of `B`.

Only one of the two conditions is needed.

---

## 3.2 Why this strictly extends the projection theorem

If `A=cP` with `c>=0` and `P` an orthogonal projection, then for `p>0`
\[
K=c^2PB^2P
\]
has range contained in `Ran P`. Any eigenvector `v` with positive eigenvalue `p^2` therefore lies in `Ran P`, so
\[
Av=cv.
\]
Theorem 3.1 immediately recovers the previous projection-versus-arbitrary-PSD theorem.

The new hypothesis is weaker: `A` need not be a scaled projection and may have arbitrary additional positive spectral values. For positive-definite `A`, the alignment condition forces the dominant direction to be a common eigenvector of `A` and `B`, but the matrices may still be noncommuting on the orthogonal complement. For singular `A`, the condition permits additional leakage of `B^2v` into `ker A`, so it includes genuinely projection-like noncommutative geometry as well.

A simple noncommuting positive-definite example is a block direct sum in which the one-dimensional dominant block is common while a lower block is noncommuting. For instance
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1,1/2),
\qquad
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
3&0&0\\
0&1&0.4\\
0&0.4&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here `[A,B] != 0`, the top singular direction is `e_1`, and the theorem applies.

# 4. Consequence for the complete `2+2` layer

For four PSD matrices `A_1,...,A_4`, define
\[
p_{ij}:=\|A_iA_j\|,
\qquad
P:=\sum_{i<j}p_{ij}^2.
\]
For one unordered pair `{i,j}`, set
\[
x_{ij}:=\|A_i^2A_j^2\|,
\qquad
y_{ij}:=\|A_iA_jA_iA_j\|.
\]
The six `2+2` words from this pair have exact sum
\[
Q_{22}^{\{i,j\}}
=2(x_{ij}+y_{ij}+p_{ij}^2),
\tag{4.1}
\]
because the two palindromic words have norm exactly `p_ij^2` and the remaining words occur in adjoint pairs.

Assume the following **pairwise top-singular alignment condition**:

> For every unordered pair `{i,j}`, at least one of the two equivalent orientations satisfies Theorem 3.1; i.e. either a top left singular vector of `A_iA_j` is an eigenvector of `A_i`, or a top right singular vector of `A_iA_j` is an eigenvector of `A_j`.

Then for every pair
\[
x_{ij}+y_{ij}\ge2p_{ij}^2,
\]
so (4.1) gives
\[
Q_{22}^{\{i,j\}}\ge6p_{ij}^2.
\]
Summing over all six unordered pairs,
\[
\boxed{Q_{22}\ge6P.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
This is the sharp equal-matrix coefficient for the full `2+2` occupancy layer.

# 5. Quantitative `n=4` theorem for pairwise-aligned families

Let
\[
S:=\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}\|A_{\sigma(1)}A_{\sigma(2)}A_{\sigma(3)}A_{\sigma(4)}\|,
\]
\[
R:=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell=1}^4\|A_iA_jA_kA_\ell\|.
\]
For a pairwise-aligned family, combine the new (4.2) with the durable arbitrary-PSD bounds
\[
Q_{211}\ge2S+\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{PE}},
\tag{5.1}
\]
\[
Q_{31}\ge8P,
\tag{5.2}
\]
\[
Q_4=Q,
\qquad
E:=\sum_{i<j}\|A_i\|^2\|A_j\|^2,
\qquad
Q:=\sum_i\|A_i\|^4.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Then
\[
R
=S+Q_{211}+Q_{22}+Q_{31}+Q_4
\ge
3S+\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{PE}}+14P+Q.
\tag{5.4}
\]
The durable scalar constraints are
\[
S\le4P,
\qquad
P\le E,
\qquad
E\le\frac32Q.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Normalize
\[
s:=S/P,
\qquad e:=E/P,
\qquad q:=Q/P.
\]
Thus
\[
0<s\le4,
\qquad e\ge1,
\qquad q\ge\frac{2e}{3}.
\]
Dividing (5.4) by `S`,
\[
\frac RS
\ge
3+\frac{s}{2\sqrt e}+\frac{14+q}{s}.
\tag{5.6}
\]
For fixed `s,e`, minimize at `q=2e/3`. For fixed `e>=1`, the right side is strictly decreasing over `0<s<=4`, since
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
\le
\frac12-\frac{14}{16}<0.
\]
Hence set `s=4`:
\[
\frac RS
\ge
F(e)
:=
\frac{13}{2}+\frac2{\sqrt e}+\frac e6.
\tag{5.7}
\]
The unique minimizer satisfies
\[
e^{3/2}=6,
\qquad
e_*=6^{2/3}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\frac RS
\ge
\frac{13}{2}+\frac3{6^{1/3}}
=8.150963624447314\ldots.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{R/256}{S/24}
=\frac{3R}{32S}
\ge
0.7641528397919357\ldots.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This strictly enlarges the class covered by the previous scaled-projection theorem while preserving the same quantitative collision consequence. It does not improve the unrestricted arbitrary-PSD constant.

# 6. Further structural observation

The alignment condition exposes precisely why the projection moment argument worked. If `p>0`, `Kv=p^2v`, and `Av=av`, then
\[
A B^2v=\frac{p^2}{a}v.
\]
If `A` is invertible, this forces
\[
B^2v=\frac{p^2}{a^2}v,
\]
so positivity of `B` implies
\[
Bv=\frac pa v.
\]
Thus in the positive-definite case the aligned dominant singular direction is actually a common eigenvector. The genuinely new noncommutative freedom arises when `A` has kernel: `B^2v` may differ from `(p^2/a^2)v` by a vector in `ker A` without affecting the top-eigenvector equation.

This suggests that the remaining unrestricted pair problem is fundamentally about how the top singular vector mixes multiple positive spectral subspaces of `A`, not merely about noncommutativity elsewhere in the matrices.

A deterministic noncommuting block example was evaluated numerically:
\[

\quad
B=
\begin{pmatrix}3&0&0\\0&1&0.4\\0&0.4&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It has `||[A,B]||_F>0`, top aligned direction `e_1`, and satisfies equality in the pair product/sum bound because the dominant one-dimensional block is scalar. This check is corroborative only.

## Failed/generalization routes retained

1. The moment proof does **not** immediately extend to a generic top eigenvector `v` of `K`. Without `Av=av`, the lower bound for `x^2` involves `A^2v` while the useful top-eigenspace contribution to `y^2` involves the mixed scalar `\langle v,BAv\rangle`; these no longer arise from one positive scalar spectral measure of `B`.
2. Testing `WW^*=(AB)K(BA)` gives the always-valid bound
   \[
   y\ge p\,\|ABv\|
   \]
   for a top eigenvector `v` of `K`, but no matching lower bound on `x` was found that closes the product inequality.
3. A direct Bhatia–Davis factor choice yielding the desired pair sum `x+y>=2p^2` was searched algebraically but no valid factorization was found. The earlier Heinz bridge remains a distinct promising route.
4. The general product target
   \[
   \|A^2B^2\|\,\|ABAB\|\stackrel{?}{\ge}\|AB\|^4
   \]
   remains **unproved**. Extensive earlier random evidence is not a proof and is not promoted here.

# 9. Next attack

The new proof identifies the exact obstruction to extending the projection moment method: mixing of the top singular vector across more than one positive eigenspace of `A`.

The next high-value route is therefore to decompose a top eigenvector `v` of
\[
K=AB^2A
\]
into spectral components of `A` and seek an **aggregate moment inequality** that replaces the scalar identity `Av=av`. Two concrete formulations are:

1. derive a two-measure or matrix-valued analogue of
   \[
   m_2^3\le m_1^2m_4
   \]
   that simultaneously controls the `A^2v` term appearing in `x` and the mixed `\langle v,BAv\rangle` term appearing in `y`;
2. return to the recovered similarity-orbit formulation
   \[
   \|H X H^{-1}X\|\,\|X^2\|
   \stackrel{?}{\ge}
   \|X^*X\|^2,
   \qquad X^*=H^{-1}XH,
   \]
   and interpret the alignment theorem as the case where a top singular direction of `X` is an eigenvector of the metric `H`. A quantitative inequality in terms of the spectral variance of `H` on that direction could interpolate from the new theorem toward the full pair target.

In parallel, the independent aggregate target `C_23>=S^2/(4P)` remains worth pursuing because it attacks a different occupancy deficit and does not rely on solving the pair problem.
