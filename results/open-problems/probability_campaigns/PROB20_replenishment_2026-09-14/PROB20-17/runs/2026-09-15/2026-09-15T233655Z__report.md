# PROB20-17 — norm-inside-average noncommutative AM–GM

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL.**

The full Bandeira/Duchi norm-inside-average conjecture remains open. The exact owner target is, for arbitrary `n` and arbitrary PSD `d x d` matrices `A_1,...,A_n`,
\[
\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(n)}\|
\le
\frac1{n^n}\sum_{k_1,\dots,k_n\in[n]}\|A_{k_1}\cdots A_{k_n}\|,
\]
with spectral norm **inside** both averages. The Recht–Ré norm-outside counterexample is a different statement.

This run makes a substantive unrestricted arbitrary-dimensional advance for the first unresolved diagonal case `n=m=4`. The main new theorem is the two-matrix interpolation inequality
\[
\boxed{
\rho(AB)\,\|A^2B^2\|\ge \|AB\|^3
}
\tag{0.1}
\]
for arbitrary PSD `A,B`. Combining it with the spectral-radius lower bound
\[
\rho(AB)\ge\frac{\|AB\|^2}{\|A\|\,\|B\|}
\]
and `||ABAB||>=rho(AB)^2` yields a new piecewise lower bound for the full `2+2` collision layer. After weighted Jensen and the previously proved independent `2+1+1` and `3+1` estimates, the unrestricted arbitrary-PSD `n=4` normalized factor improves from
\[

to
\[
\boxed{
0.7589910386126688\ldots
}.
\tag{0.2}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{

\frac{11}{2}+\frac34\,2^{1/3}+\frac{3}{6^{1/3}}

# 3. A power-interpolation theorem for two PSD matrices

## Lemma 3.1 — log-convexity of equal powers

For PSD matrices `A,B`, define
\[
g(s):=\|A^sB^s\|,\qquad s\ge0.
\]
Then `log g(s)` is convex wherever `g(s)>0`; equivalently, for `s_0,s_1>=0` and `0<=theta<=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\|A^{(1-\theta)s_0+\theta s_1}B^{(1-\theta)s_0+\theta s_1}\|
\le
\|A^{s_0}B^{s_0}\|^{1-\theta}
\|A^{s_1}B^{s_1}\|^\theta.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof
First assume `A,B>0`. On the strip `s_0<=Re z<=s_1`, consider the analytic matrix-valued function
\[
F(z):=A^zB^z.
\]
On a vertical line `Re z=s`, functional calculus and commutation of powers of the same matrix give
\[
F(s+it)
=A^{it}A^sB^sB^{it}.
\]
Both `A^{it}` and `B^{it}` are unitary, so operator norm is unchanged by the left and right factors:
\[
\|F(s+it)\|=\|A^sB^s\|=g(s).
\tag{3.2}
\]
Apply the scalar Hadamard three-lines theorem to
\[
z\mapsto \langle u,F(z)v\rangle
\]
for arbitrary unit vectors `u,v`, and then take the supremum over `u,v`. Since the boundary suprema are exactly `g(s_0)` and `g(s_1)` by (3.2), this yields (3.1).

For merely PSD `A,B`, replace them by `A+epsilon I` and `B+epsilon I`, apply the positive-definite result, and let `epsilon downarrow 0`. Positive real powers and operator norm are continuous in this limit. QED.

## Theorem 3.2 — new spectral-radius/order-word bridge

For arbitrary PSD `A,B`,
\[
\boxed{
\rho(AB)\,\|A^2B^2\|\ge\|AB\|^3.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof
Apply Lemma 3.1 with
\[
s_0=\frac12,
\qquad
s_1=2,
\qquad
1=\frac23\cdot\frac12+\frac13\cdot2.
\]
Then
\[
\|AB\|
\le
\|A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\|^{2/3}
\|A^2B^2\|^{1/3}.
\]
Cubing,
\[
\|AB\|^3
\le
\|A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\|^2\,\|A^2B^2\|.
\tag{3.4}
\]
But
\[
\|A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\|^2
=
\|B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\|.
\]
The matrix `B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}` is PSD and has the same nonzero spectrum as `AB`; hence
\[
\|B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\|=\rho(AB).
\]
Substitute into (3.4). QED.

### Equality check
If `A=B=cI` on the active subspace, then both sides of (3.3) are `c^6`, so the coefficient is sharp.

# 4. A piecewise lower bound for the unresolved `2+2` pair sum

Fix PSD `A,B` and set
\[
a:=\|A\|,
\qquad
b:=\|B\|,
\qquad
p:=\|AB\|,
\qquad
r:=\rho(AB),
\]
\[
x:=\|A^2B^2\|,
\qquad
y:=\|ABAB\|.
\]
If `p=0`, all bounds below are trivial, so assume `p>0`.

Theorem 3.2 gives
\[
x\ge\frac{p^3}{r}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Also
\[
y=\|(AB)^2\|\ge\rho((AB)^2)=r^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

We need one further elementary relation.

## Lemma 4.1 — spectral-radius floor

\[
\boxed{
r\ge\frac{p^2}{ab}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
Because `p=||AB||=||BA||`,
\[
p^2=\|BA^2B\|.
\]
Since `A^2<=aA` in Loewner order,
\[
BA^2B\preceq aBAB,
\]
so
\[
p^2\le a\|BAB\|.
\]
Let `C=B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}`. Then `C>=0`, `||C||=rho(AB)=r`, and
\[
BAB=B^{1/2}CB^{1/2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|BAB\|\le b r,
\]
which proves (4.3). QED.

Now put
\[
t:=\frac rp\in(0,1],
\qquad
z:=\frac{p^2}{a^2b^2}\in(0,1].
\]
Lemma 4.1 says
\[
t\ge\sqrt z.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Combining (4.1) and (4.2),
\[
\boxed{
x+y\ge p^2\left(\frac1t+t^2\right).}
\tag{4.5}
\]
The scalar function
\[
f(t)=t^{-1}+t^2
\]
has its unique minimum at
\[
t_0=2^{-1/3},
\qquad
f(t_0)=\frac{3}{2^{2/3}}.
\]
Let
\[
z_0:=t_0^2=2^{-2/3}.
\]
Minimizing (4.5) over `sqrt(z)<=t<=1` yields the exact proved piecewise estimate
\[
\boxed{
\frac{x+y}{p^2}\ge
\psi(z):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{3}{2^{2/3}}, & 0\le z\le2^{-2/3},\\[6pt]
\displaystyle z^{-1/2}+z, & 2^{-2/3}\le z\le1.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
At `z=1`, the right side is exactly `2`, matching the desired sharp pair coefficient. Uniformly, (4.6) gives
\[
x+y\ge\frac{3}{2^{2/3}}p^2
=1.8898815748423097\ldots p^2.
\tag{4.7}
\]
This still falls short of the conjectured universal `2p^2`, but it is strong enough to improve the global `n=4` constant.

# 5. Convex aggregation of the entire `2+2` layer

For four PSD matrices write
\[
a_i:=\|A_i\|,
\qquad
p_{ij}:=\|A_iA_j\|,
\qquad
w_{ij}:=a_i^2a_j^2,
\]
with
\[
P=\sum_{i<j}p_{ij}^2,
\qquad
E=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}.
\]
For `w_ij>0` set
\[
z_{ij}:=\frac{p_{ij}^2}{w_{ij}}\in[0,1],
\]
and set `z_ij=0` when `w_ij=0` (then necessarily `p_ij=0`). Thus
\[
P=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}z_{ij}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For one unordered pair `{i,j}`, let
\[
x_{ij}:=\|A_i^2A_j^2\|,
\qquad
y_{ij}:=\|A_iA_jA_iA_j\|.
\]
Its six `2+2` words have exact total
\[
Q_{22}^{\{i,j\}}
=2(x_{ij}+y_{ij}+p_{ij}^2).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Define
\[
\Phi(z):=z(1+\psi(z)).
\]
By (4.6),
\[
Q_{22}^{\{i,j\}}\ge2w_{ij}\Phi(z_{ij}).
\tag{5.3}
\]
Explicitly,
\[
\Phi(z)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \left(1+\frac3{2^{2/3}}\right)z,
&0\le z\le2^{-2/3},\\[6pt]
\displaystyle z+\sqrt z+z^2,
&2^{-2/3}\le z\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.4}
\]

## Lemma 5.1 — `Phi` is convex on `[0,1]`

On the first interval it is linear. On the second,
\[
\Phi''(z)=2-\frac1{4z^{3/2}}.
\]
For `z>=2^{-2/3}`, one has `z^{3/2}>=1/2`, so
\[
\Phi''(z)\ge\frac32>0.
\]
At `z_0=2^{-2/3}`, the two values agree and the derivatives agree:
\[
1+\frac3{2^{2/3}}
=
1+\frac1{2\sqrt{z_0}}+2z_0.
\]
Hence `Phi` is globally convex. QED.

Weighted Jensen applied to (5.3) and (5.1) now gives
\[
\boxed{
Q_{22}\ge2E\,\Phi(P/E).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Let
\[
e:=\frac EP\ge1.
\]
Then (5.5) becomes the explicit new layer bound
\[
\boxed{
\frac{Q_{22}}P\ge h(e):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle 2\left(1+\sqrt e+\frac1e\right),
&1\le e\le2^{2/3},\\[8pt]
\displaystyle 2+3\,2^{1/3},
&e\ge2^{2/3}.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
The constant tail is
\[
2+3\,2^{1/3}=5.779763149684619\ldots.
\]
This materially improves the previous general `2+2` estimate used in the `0.730390...` checkpoint.

# 6. New unrestricted arbitrary-PSD `n=4` constant

For four PSD matrices decompose all 256 words by occupancy:
\[
R=S+Q_{211}+Q_{22}+Q_{31}+Q_4.
\]
Use the independent durable inequalities (1.2)–(1.4), the new (5.6), and `Q_4=Q`:
\[
R
\ge
3S+\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{PE}}
+P\,[8+h(e)]
+Q.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Normalize
\[
s:=\frac SP,
\qquad
e:=\frac EP,
\qquad q:=\frac QP.
\]
From (1.1) and (1.4),
\[
0<s\le4,
\qquad e\ge1,
\qquad q\ge\frac{2e}{3}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Divide (6.1) by `S`:
\[
\frac RS
\ge
3+\frac{s}{2\sqrt e}
+\frac{8+h(e)+q}{s}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
For fixed `s,e`, minimize at `q=2e/3`.

For fixed `e`, the right side is strictly decreasing throughout `0<s<=4`: indeed
\[
\partial_s
=
\frac1{2\sqrt e}
-
\frac{8+h(e)+2e/3}{s^2}
\le
\frac12-
\frac{8+(2+3\,2^{1/3})}{16}<0.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Thus the relaxed minimum occurs at
\[
s=4.
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Low-`e` branch
For `1<=e<=2^{2/3}`,
\[
F_{\rm low}(e)
=
\frac{11}{2}
+\frac2{\sqrt e}
+\frac12\sqrt e
+\frac1{2e}
+\frac e6.
\tag{6.6}
\]
This is decreasing on the whole interval. To see this, write `x=sqrt(e)` and multiply the derivative by `12e^2`; its sign is the sign of
\[
H(x):=2x^4+3x^3-12x-6.
\]
On `1<=x<=2^{1/3}`, one has
\[
H''(x)=24x^2+18x>0,
\qquad H'(1)=5>0,
\]
so `H` is increasing there, while at the right endpoint
\[
H(2^{1/3})=-8\,2^{1/3}<0.
\]
Hence `H<0` throughout, proving the claimed decrease.

### High-`e` branch
For `e>=2^{2/3}`,
\[
F_{\rm high}(e)
=
\frac{11}{2}
+\frac34\,2^{1/3}
+\frac2{\sqrt e}
+\frac e6.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Its unique minimum satisfies
\[
-e^{-3/2}+\frac16=0,
\qquad
\boxed{e_*=6^{2/3}},
\tag{6.8}
\]
which lies strictly above `2^{2/3}`. Since the two branches join continuously and the low branch decreases up to the join, the global relaxed minimum is at `e_*`.

Using `e_*^{3/2}=6`,
\[
\frac2{\sqrt{e_*}}+\frac{e_*}{6}
=\frac3{6^{1/3}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\frac RS
\ge
\frac{11}{2}+\frac34\,2^{1/3}+\frac3{6^{1/3}}
=8.095904411868467\ldots .
}
\tag{6.9}
\]
Finally,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{R/256}{S/24}
=\frac{3R}{32S}
\ge
0.7589910386126688\ldots .
}
\tag{6.10}
\]
This is a new unrestricted arbitrary-dimensional `n=4` guarantee. It strictly improves the previous durable `0.7303902120286716...` factor.

# 7. What this does and does not settle

The new interpolation theorem does **not** prove the sharp two-matrix pair conjecture
\[
\|A^2B^2\|+\|ABAB\|\stackrel{?}{\ge}2\|AB\|^2.
\]
It proves the best lower bound obtainable from the pair of scalar facts
\[
\|A^2B^2\|\ge p^3/r,
\qquad
\|ABAB\|\ge r^2,
\]
namely `p^2(1/t+t^2)` with `t=r/p`. The residual gap occurs around
\[
t=2^{-1/3},
\]
where the scalar minimum is `3/2^{2/3}=1.88988...` rather than `2`.

Thus any proof of the sharp pair inequality must use additional structure coupling the two words beyond their separate spectral-radius lower bounds.

# 9. Failed / obstructed routes retained

1. Termwise repeated-word domination is false; repeated-index payments have to be aggregate.
2. The projection moment proof depends essentially on alignment/idempotence and does not directly lift to general PSD pairs.
3. The earlier similarity-orbit / naive three-lines path had uncontrolled boundary norms; the successful interpolation here instead uses the simpler analytic family `A^zB^z`, whose vertical-line norms are exactly constant.
4. The new result exhausts the information in the separate lower bounds `x>=p^3/r` and `y>=r^2`; their scalar minimum is strictly below the target coefficient `2`, so a sharp proof needs a new coupling inequality rather than further scalar optimization of those two facts alone.

# 10. Next attack

The most focused next routes are now:

1. **Couple the interpolation extremizers.** Equality or near-equality in
   \[
   p^3\le r x
   \]
   comes from the three-lines interpolation between powers `1/2,1,2`. Equality or near-equality in `y>=r^2` means `(AB)^2` is close to normal on a top spectral direction. Prove these two near-equality regimes cannot coexist around the scalar worst point `r/p=2^{-1/3}` unless the pair is close to commuting, in which case the sharp coefficient approaches `2`.
