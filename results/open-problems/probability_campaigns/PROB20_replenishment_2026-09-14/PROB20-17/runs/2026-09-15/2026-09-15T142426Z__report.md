## 0. Full target and this run's advance

For arbitrary `n` and arbitrary positive semidefinite `d x d` matrices `A_1,...,A_n`, the full target is
\[
\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\|A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(n)}\|
\le
\frac1{n^n}\sum_{k_1,\dots,k_n\in[n]}\|A_{k_1}\cdots A_{k_n}\|,
\]
with spectral norm **inside** both averages.  The norm-outside Recht–Ré counterexample is a different statement.

This iteration continues the first unresolved diagonal case `n=4`.  Define
\[
S:=\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}\|A_{\sigma(1)}A_{\sigma(2)}A_{\sigma(3)}A_{\sigma(4)}\|,

\]
The sharp `n=4` conjecture is `3R>=32S`.

**New exact theorem in this run.**  When the matrices are `2 x 2` PSD, the two alternating `2+2` words satisfy the stronger cubic lower bound
\[
\boxed{
\|ABAB\|,\ \|BABA\|
\ge
\frac{\|AB\|^3}{\|A\|\,\|B\|}.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]
This improves the cumulative `n=4,d=2` approximation to

\boxed{
\frac{R/256}{S/24}
\ge

}
\tag{0.2}
\]
This is a special-case theorem only.  It does **not** resolve arbitrary dimension, arbitrary `n`, or the sharp `n=4` case.

# 4. New `2 x 2` cubic alternating-word lemma

## Lemma 4.1
Let `A,B` be `2 x 2` PSD matrices.  Then
\[
\boxed{
\|ABAB\|
\ge
\frac{\|AB\|^3}{\|A\|\,\|B\|},
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
and the same bound holds for `BABA`.

### Proof
If `A=0` or `B=0`, the statement is trivial.  Put
\[
a:=\|A\|,
\qquad b:=\|B\|,
\qquad
\widehat A:=A/a,
\qquad
\widehat B:=B/b.
\]
It suffices to prove the normalized assertion for
\[
0\preceq \widehat A,\widehat B\preceq I.
\]
Drop hats and write
\[
T:=AB,
\qquad
p:=\|T\|\le1.
\]

Because `A,B` are PSD, `T=AB` has real nonnegative eigenvalues.  Denote them
\[
\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge0.
\]
Let the singular values of `T` be
\[
p\ge s\ge0.
\]

### Step 1: the leading eigenvalue obeys `lambda_1 >= p^2`

We have
\[
p^2
=\|T^*T\|
=\|BA^2B\|.
\]
Since `0<=A<=I`,
\[
A^2\preceq A,
\]
therefore
\[
BA^2B\preceq BAB
\]
and hence
\[
p^2\le\|BAB\|.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Now
\[
BAB
=B^{1/2}\bigl(B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\bigr)B^{1/2}.
\]
The PSD matrix `B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}` has the same nonzero eigenvalues as `AB`, so
\[
\lambda_1
=\|B^{1/2}AB^{1/2}\|.
\]
Because `||B||<=1`,
\[
\|BAB\|\le\lambda_1.
\]
Together with (4.2),
\[
\boxed{\lambda_1\ge p^2.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Step 2: the smaller singular value obeys `s <= lambda_2`

For a `2 x 2` matrix, the product of singular values equals the absolute determinant.  Here the determinant is nonnegative, so
\[
ps
=\det T
=\lambda_1\lambda_2.
\]
Since spectral radius never exceeds operator norm,
\[
\lambda_1\le p.
\]
Thus
\[
s=\frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}{p}\le\lambda_2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Step 3: Cayley–Hamilton converts (4.3) and (4.4) into a cubic power bound

For a `2 x 2` matrix,
\[
T^2-(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)T+\lambda_1\lambda_2I=0.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Choose a unit right singular vector `x` for the largest singular value, so
\[
\|Tx\|=p.
\]
Using (4.5) and the reverse triangle inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|T^2x\|
&=\|(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)Tx-\lambda_1\lambda_2x\|\\
&\ge p(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)-\lambda_1\lambda_2\\
&=p\left(\lambda_1+\lambda_2-\frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2}{p}\right)\\
&=p(\lambda_1+\lambda_2-s)\\
&\ge p\lambda_1\\
&\ge p^3,
\end{aligned}
\]
where (4.4) is used in the penultimate inequality and (4.3) in the last.
Hence
\[
\|T^2\|\ge p^3.
\tag{4.6}
\]
That is,
\[
\|ABAB\|\ge\|AB\|^3
\]
for normalized contractions.  Restoring `a,b`,
\[
\|ABAB\|
=a^2b^2\|\widehat A\widehat B\widehat A\widehat B\|
\ge
a^2b^2\left(\frac{\|AB\|}{ab}\right)^3
=
\frac{\|AB\|^3}{ab}.
\]
The proof for `BABA` is identical, since `BA` has the same eigenvalues and operator norm as `AB`.  QED.

### Scope
The Cayley–Hamilton step is specifically two-dimensional.  Extensive numerical experiments in earlier work suggested (4.1) might remain true in higher dimension, but **no general-dimensional proof is claimed** here.

# 5. Consequence for the `2+2` collision layer when `d=2`

Fix an unordered pair `{i,j}` and abbreviate
\[
a=a_i,\quad b=a_j,\quad p=p_{ij}.
\]
Among the six `2+2` words:

* the palindromic words `A_iA_j^2A_i` and `A_jA_i^2A_j` have norm exactly `p^2`;
* the words `A_i^2A_j^2` and `A_j^2A_i^2` have norm at least their spectral radius `p^2`;
* by Lemma 4.1, the alternating words contribute at least `p^3/(ab)` each.

Therefore
\[
Q_{22}
\ge
4P
+2\sum_{i<j}\frac{p_{ij}^3}{a_i a_j}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Set
\[
w_{ij}:=a_i^2a_j^2,
\qquad
r_{ij}:=\frac{p_{ij}}{a_i a_j}\in[0,1].
\]
Then
\[
P=\sum w_{ij}r_{ij}^2,
\qquad
E=\sum w_{ij},
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i<j}\frac{p_{ij}^3}{a_i a_j}
=\sum w_{ij}r_{ij}^3.
\]
Weighted power mean (equivalently Hölder) gives
\[
\sum w_{ij}r_{ij}^3
\ge
\frac{P^{3/2}}{E^{1/2}}.
\]
Hence for `2 x 2` PSD matrices,
\[
\boxed{
Q_{22}
\ge
4P+\frac{2P^{3/2}}{\sqrt E}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

# 6. Improved `n=4,d=2` global factor

Combine (3.2), (3.3), (5.2), and `Q_4=Q`:
\[
R
\ge
3S
+\frac{S^2}{2\sqrt{PE}}
+4P
+\frac{10P^{3/2}}{\sqrt E}
+Q.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Normalize
\[
s:=\frac SP,
\qquad
e:=\frac EP,
\qquad q:=\frac QP.
\]
From (3.1),
\[
0<s\le4,
\qquad e\ge1,
\qquad q\ge\frac{2e}{3}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Dividing (6.1) by `S`,
\[
\frac RS
\ge
G(s,e,q)
:=
3+\frac{s}{2\sqrt e}
+
\frac{4+10/\sqrt e+q}{s}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
For fixed `s,e`, this is increasing in `q`, so the relaxed minimum occurs at
\[
q=\frac{2e}{3}.
\]
For fixed `e`, (6.3) is strictly decreasing in `0<s<=4`: at `s=4`,
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial s}
\le
\frac1{2\sqrt e}
-
\frac{4+10/\sqrt e+2e/3}{16}
=
-\frac{4+2/\sqrt e+2e/3}{16}<0.
\]
Thus the relaxed minimum has `s=4`, and
\[
\frac RS
\ge
F_2(e)
:=
4+\frac{9}{2\sqrt e}+\frac e6,
\qquad e\ge1.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Differentiate:
\[
F_2'(e)
=-\frac94e^{-3/2}+\frac16.
\]
The unique minimizer satisfies
\[
e^{3/2}=\frac{27}{2},
\qquad
\boxed{e_*=(27/2)^{2/3}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]
At the minimizer,
\[
\frac{9}{2\sqrt{e_*}}=\frac{e_*}{3},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\frac RS
\ge
4+\frac12\left(\frac{27}{2}\right)^{2/3}
=6.8348223622634645\ldots .
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Multiplying by `3/32` gives (0.2):
\[
\boxed{
\frac{R/256}{S/24}
\ge
0.6407645964621997\ldots .
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

\[
0\preceq A,B\preceq I,
\qquad T=AB,
