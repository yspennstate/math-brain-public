## 2. Literature/dependency check

The primary target remains Israel–Krahmer–Ward, arXiv:1411.0333 / Linear Algebra Appl. 488 (2016), which formulates the norm-inside conjecture for arbitrary unitarily invariant norms and proves it for product length `m<=3`; the full higher-product statement is explicitly left conjectural.

The new proof below uses the primary Bhatia–Davis matrix AGM inequality, *More Matrix Forms of the Arithmetic-Geometric Mean Inequality*, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 14 (1993), 132–136, DOI `10.1137/0614012`:
\[
2\,|||A^*XB|||\le |||AA^*X+XBB^*|||
\tag{2.1}
\]
for arbitrary square `A,B,X` and every unitarily invariant norm.  In operator norm, the triangle inequality then gives a two-term upper bound by the norms of the two summands.

A second relevant primary reference checked this run is S. W. Drury, *Operator norms of words formed from positive-definite matrices*, Electronic J. Linear Algebra 18 (2009), 13–20, DOI `10.13001/1081-3810.1290`.  In particular Drury records the general ordered-vs-alternating comparison
\[
\|(AB)^k\|\le\|A^kB^k\|,
\qquad k\in\mathbb N,
\tag{2.2}
\]
so for `k=2`, `||ABAB||<=||A^2B^2||`.  This is useful structural information but does not imply the lower-sum conjecture (0.2).

No literature source located in the bounded current check supplied the exact coupling (0.1) or resolved the full norm-inside target.

# 3. Exact two-matrix bookkeeping for the `2+2` layer

Fix PSD matrices `A,B`, and abbreviate
\[
p:=\|AB\|,
\qquad
x:=\|A^2B^2\|=\|B^2A^2\|,
\qquad
y:=\|ABAB\|=\|BABA\|.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The two palindromic `2+2` words satisfy
\[
\|AB^2A\|=\|BA\|^2=p^2,
\qquad
\|BA^2B\|=\|AB\|^2=p^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Therefore the six `2+2` words built from the unordered pair `{A,B}` have the exact sum
\[
\boxed{
Q_{22}^{\{A,B\}}=2x+2y+2p^2=2(x+y+p^2).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
This identity is only bookkeeping plus adjoint invariance; no inequality is used.

# 4. New Bhatia–Davis collision coupling

Let `Q_31^{\{A,B\}}` denote the sum of the eight `3+1` words from the same unordered pair `{A,B}`:
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{31}^{\{A,B\}}
:=&\ \|A^3B\|+\|A^2BA\|+\|ABA^2\|+\|BA^3\|\\
&+\|B^3A\|+\|B^2AB\|+\|BAB^2\|+\|AB^3\|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

## Lemma 4.1 — ordered-square words are paid by endpoint `3+1` words
Apply (2.1) with the Bhatia–Davis matrices equal to the PSD pair `A,B` and with `X=AB`.  Since `A^*=A`, `B^*=B`,
\[
2\|A(AB)B\|
\le
\|A^2(AB)+(AB)B^2\|
\le
\|A^3B\|+\|AB^3\|.
\]
The left side is `2x`, so
\[
2x\le \|A^3B\|+\|AB^3\|.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Swap `A,B` and use adjoint invariance to obtain
\[
2x\le \|B^3A\|+\|BA^3\|.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Adding,
\[
4x\le
\|A^3B\|+\|AB^3\|+\|B^3A\|+\|BA^3\|.
\tag{4.4}
\]

## Lemma 4.2 — alternating words are paid by interior `3+1` words
Apply (2.1) with `X=BA`.  Then
\[
2\|A(BA)B\|
\le
\|A^2BA+BAB^2\|
\le
\|A^2BA\|+\|BAB^2\|.
\]
The left side is `2y`, hence
\[
2y\le\|A^2BA\|+\|BAB^2\|.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Swapping `A,B` gives
\[
2y\le\|B^2AB\|+\|ABA^2\|.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Thus
\[
4y\le
\|A^2BA\|+\|BAB^2\|+\|B^2AB\|+\|ABA^2\|.
\tag{4.7}
\]

## Theorem 4.3 — exact pairwise cross-layer coupling
Adding (4.4) and (4.7),
\[
\boxed{
Q_{31}^{\{A,B\}}\ge4(x+y).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]
Use the exact identity (3.3):
\[
x+y=\frac12Q_{22}^{\{A,B\}}-p^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
Q_{31}^{\{A,B\}}
\ge
2Q_{22}^{\{A,B\}}-4\|AB\|^2.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]
No previously proved campaign lemma is used in deriving (4.9); its only substantive dependency is the published Bhatia–Davis inequality (2.1).

Summing (4.9) over the six unordered pairs yields the new dimension-free occupancy relation
\[
\boxed{
Q_{31}\ge2Q_{22}-4P,
\qquad
P=\sum_{i<j}\|A_iA_j\|^2.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Together with the previous exact theorem `Q_31>=8P`, this can be recorded compactly as
\[
\boxed{
Q_{31}\ge\max\{8P,\ 2Q_{22}-4P\}.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]
This is a genuine feasible-region restriction: a configuration with unusually large `Q_22` must pay proportionally in `Q_31`; the two layers cannot be minimized independently.

# 6. The sharp pairwise `2+2` target and current evidence

The most valuable immediate pair theorem remains
\[
\boxed{x+y\stackrel{?}{\ge}2p^2.}
\tag{6.1}
\]
By (3.3), this is equivalent to
\[
Q_{22}^{\{A,B\}}\stackrel{?}{\ge}6p^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Summed over all pairs it would yield
\[
Q_{22}\ge6P\ge\frac32S,
\]
which is exactly the equal-matrix occupancy coefficient for the `2+2` layer.

A stronger-looking equivalent route tested numerically is the product inequality
\[
\boxed{xy\stackrel{?}{\ge}p^4,}
\tag{6.3}
\]
which would imply (6.1) by scalar AM–GM.  This run performed fresh random searches with real and complex PSD matrices through dimension six, including strongly ill-conditioned spectra.  No violation of (6.3) was observed; the smallest sampled ratios `xy/p^4` approached `1` from above.  This is **diagnostic evidence only**.

Drury's theorem gives the opposite-order comparison
\[
y\le x,
\tag{6.4}
\]
but (6.4) plus the elementary `x>=p^2` is insufficient to prove (6.1).

An additional singular-vector diagnostic was explored: if `u,v` are top left/right singular vectors of `AB`, then fresh tests also suggested
\[
|\langle u,A^2B^2v\rangle|\,|\langle u,ABABv\rangle|\ge p^4.
\]
No proof was found, so this is not promoted beyond a computational lead.

Drury's explicit `3x3` noncommutative family used in his necessity arguments was also tested for (6.1)/(6.3) over small parameter values; it did not yield a counterexample.
