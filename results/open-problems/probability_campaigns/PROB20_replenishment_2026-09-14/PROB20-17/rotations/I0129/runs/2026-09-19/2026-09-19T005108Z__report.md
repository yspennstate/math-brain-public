# 4. B21 — neighboring shape: exact exponent transfer from t to an actual top gap

The previous report separated large gap primes into primes dividing `t` and primes external to `t`. The external part already pays a polynomial Diophantine deficit. The missing question was whether a prime dividing both `t` and an actual gap could hide much more p-adic mass in `t` than in the gap. The square lift shows that it cannot.

## 4.1 Localization at one large prime

Suppose first that
\[
\binom{N+d}{k}=\binom{N}{k+1}=t,
\qquad d>0,
\tag{4.1}
\]
and let
\[
p>2k+1,\qquad p\mid d,\qquad p\mid t.
\]
Write
\[
f=v_p(t),\qquad e=v_p(d),\qquad Z=p^f.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Because `p>k+1`, the factorial denominators in both binomial coefficients are p-adic units and each numerator block, having length `<p`, contains at most one multiple of `p`.

Since `p|d`, the two top indices are congruent modulo `p`. Because `p|t`, the row-`k` representation forces a unique residue
\[
r\equiv N\equiv N+d\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le r<k.
\tag{4.3}
\]
The unique p-divisible numerator factors are `N-r` and `N+d-r`, and each has valuation exactly `f`; hence
\[
p^f\mid d,
\qquad e\ge f.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Moreover `N mod Z=(N+d) mod Z=r`, so the square lift (3.11) applies with the **same** residue in both rows.

Subtracting the two square lifts gives
\[
Z^2\mid d-t\Delta_{k,r},
\qquad
\Delta_{k,r}:=\kappa_{k,r}-\kappa_{k+1,r}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
An exact simplification is
\[
\Delta_{k,r}
=(-1)^{k-1-r}(2k+1-r)\binom{k}{r}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
All factors on the right are p-adic units because `0<=r<k` and `p>2k+1`. Therefore
\[
v_p(t\Delta_{k,r})=f.
\tag{4.7}
\]
If `e>f`, then `v_p(d)>f` while (4.7) has valuation exactly `f`; the ultrametric inequality would give
\[
v_p(d-t\Delta_{k,r})=f<2f,
\]
contradicting (4.5). Hence `e<=f`, and together with (4.4):

### Theorem 4.1 — adjacent-gap exponent rigidity
Under (4.1), if `p>2k+1` divides both `d` and `t`, then
\[
\boxed{v_p(d)=v_p(t).}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is stronger than the previous dichotomy, which only said that a large prime of the gap is either supported on `t` or on an explicit exceptional factor.

## 4.2 The two-row jump has the same rigidity

Now suppose
\[
\binom{N+d}{k}=\binom{N}{k+2}=t,
\tag{4.9}
\]
and
\[
p>2k+2,\qquad p\mid d,\qquad p\mid t.
\]
Again write `f=v_p(t)`, `e=v_p(d)`, `Z=p^f`. The same unique-factor argument gives `e>=f` and a common residue `0<=r<k` modulo `Z`. Subtracting the square lifts gives
\[
Z^2\mid d-t\Delta^{(2)}_{k,r},
\qquad
\Delta^{(2)}_{k,r}:=\kappa_{k,r}-\kappa_{k+2,r}.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Here the two signs agree, and direct algebra gives
\[
\Delta^{(2)}_{k,r}
=(-1)^{k-1-r}\binom{k}{r}
\frac{(r+1)(r-2k-2)}{k+1-r}.
\tag{4.11}
\]
Since `p>2k+2` and `0<=r<k`, every numerator and denominator factor in (4.11) is a p-adic unit. Thus `Delta^(2)` is a p-unit. The same valuation comparison as above proves:

### Theorem 4.2 — two-row-gap exponent rigidity
Under (4.9), if `p>2k+2` divides both `d` and `t`, then
\[
\boxed{v_p(d)=v_p(t).}
\tag{4.12}
\]

## 4.3 Application to the plastic quartet

Take the harmless common threshold
\[
Y=4m+8.
\tag{4.13}
\]
For every prime `p>Y`:

- if `p|t` and `p|a`, Theorem 4.1 with `k=m` gives
  \[
  v_p(a)=v_p(t);
  \tag{4.14}
  \]
- if `p|t` and `p|b`, Theorem 4.1 with `k=m+1` gives
  \[
  v_p(b)=v_p(t);
  \tag{4.15}
  \]
- if `p|t` and `p|c`, Theorem 4.2 with `k=m+2` gives
  \[
  v_p(c)=v_p(t).
  \tag{4.16}
  \]

Define the high `t`-supported gap kernels
\[
A_t=\prod_{\substack{p>Y\\p\mid a,\ p\mid t}}p^{v_p(t)},
\quad
B_t=\prod_{\substack{p>Y\\p\mid b,\ p\mid t}}p^{v_p(t)},
\quad
C_t=\prod_{\substack{p>Y\\p\mid c,\ p\mid t}}p^{v_p(t)}.
\tag{4.17}
\]
Equations (4.14)--(4.16) imply
\[
A_t\mid a,\qquad B_t\mid b,\qquad C_t\mid c.
\tag{4.18}
\]
Thus
\[
\log A_t,\log B_t,\log C_t\le d_0m+O(\log m).
\tag{4.19}
\]
In particular, if `B_gap` denotes the product of **distinct** high rough components `p^{v_p(t)}` whose base prime divides at least one of `a,b,c`, then
\[
B_{\rm gap}\mid abc
\tag{4.20}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\log B_{\rm gap}\le 3d_0m+O(\log m)=O(m).
}
\tag{4.21}
\]
Also every such component has size at least `Y`, so
\[
\boxed{
\#\{p>Y:p\mid t,\ p\mid abc\}=O(m/\log m)=o(m).
}
\tag{4.22}
\]

**B21 outcome: positive.** The previously unresolved high-prime part of an actual adjacent gap supported on the common binomial value is now completely exponent-matched to `t` and has only `O(m)` total logarithmic capacity. Fixed-S Thue–Mahler machinery is no longer needed merely to control that part. The new obstruction must live predominantly on rough components that do **not** divide any actual adjacent top gap.
