# 4. B7 — take the limit: a finite entropy-phase arrangement and a positive-mass phase-separated core

\[

\tag{4.1}

\tag{4.2}

The key fact is elementary but decisive:
\[
H(x)=H(y)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x=y\ \text{ or }\ x+y=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Thus all leading phase ties live on finitely many equality/complement hyperplanes.

## 4.1 Logarithmic capacity of one residue-difference or residue-sum class

Fix distinct rows `h,j`.

\[
r_h-r_j=q,
\]

\[

\tag{4.4}

# 5. B28 — replace “for all” with “for typical”: exact parabolic dynamics and what the 2026 factorial literature can and cannot supply

The recurrence of the signed-Pascal coefficient has a more rigid form than the previous moving-degree polynomial presentation suggests.

Fix one row index `k` and put

## 5.1 Exact ratio and second-order transition

For `0<=r<k-1`,
\[
q_r:=\frac{a_{r+1}}{a_r}
=-\frac{k-1-r}{r+1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Solving for the next ratio gives
\[
\boxed{
q_{r+1}=T_k(q_r)
:=\frac{(k-1)q_r+1}{k+1-q_r}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Equivalently, if `x=a_r`, `y=a_{r+1}`, then
\[
\boxed{
 a_{r+2}
 =y\frac{x+(k-1)y}{(k+1)x-y}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
All denominators here are nonzero on the legal orbit because these identities come from the explicit unit ratios (5.1).

The Möbius matrix of `T_k` is
\[
M_k=
\begin{pmatrix}
k-1&1\\
-1&k+1
\end{pmatrix}
=kI+N,
\qquad
N=
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&1\\
-1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad N^2=0.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Hence the projective dynamics is **parabolic**. For every legal shift `s`,
\[
M_k^s=k^{s-1}(kI+sN),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
T_k^s(q)
=
\frac{(k-s)q+s}{-sq+k+s}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Thus a `Theta(m)` displacement does not create complicated projective dynamics: the ratio coordinate is still a single Möbius transformation whose coefficients depend only linearly on the displacement.

The real difficulty is the amplitude cocycle
\[
\frac{a_{r+s}}{a_r}
=
\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}T_k^j(q_r),
\tag{5.6}
\]
which is exactly the long factorial/Pochhammer product seen in the previous cleared-denominator degree. The high algebraic complexity sits in this multiplicative cocycle, not in the projective orbit itself.

\tag{6.4}

## 6.1 Constant-sparse filter factorization

\[

=(x-1)^{m-1}\,\mathcal B_{m,\mathbf c}(x),
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{m,\mathbf c}(x)
={}&m
-(m+1)x^{-c_1}(x-1)\\
&-(m+2)x^{-c_2}(x-1)^2
+(m+4)x^{-c_4}(x-1)^4.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Before coincident monomials are combined, (6.6) contains at most
\[
1+2+3+5=11
\tag{6.7}
\]
Laurent monomials. The apparent `Theta(m)` degree of a macroscopic strand is therefore generated solely by **where** this constant-size filter is placed, not by an increasing number of filter terms.

Moreover
\[
\mathcal B_{m,\mathbf c}(1)=m.
\tag{6.8}
\]
For every clean prime `p>4m+8`, `m` is a unit modulo `p`, so the factor `(x-1)^{m-1}` in (6.5) has **exactly** multiplicity `m-1` modulo `p`; the sparse filter does not add another root at `x=1`.

## 6.2 Sparse-multiplicity lemma

I use the following elementary finite-field fact.

**Lemma 6.1.** Let `P(x) in F_p[x]` have degree `<p`. If `x=1` is a root of multiplicity at least `q`, then `P` has at least `q+1` nonzero monomial coefficients.

**Proof.** Suppose `P=sum_{i=1}^s a_i x^{e_i}` with distinct `0<=e_1<...<e_s<p` and `s<=q`. Vanishing of the first `s` derivatives at `1` gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^s a_i(e_i)_j=0,
\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,s-1,
\]
where `(e)_j=e(e-1)...(e-j+1)`. The determinant of this system is the Vandermonde product
\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le s}(e_j-e_i),
\]
which is nonzero in `F_p` because all exponents are distinct and less than `p`. Hence all `a_i=0`, contradiction. ∎

## 6.3 Offset-span root theorem

Let
\[
W(\mathbf c)
:=
\max\{0,1-c_1,2-c_2,4-c_4\}
-
\min\{0,-c_1,-c_2,-c_4\}.
\tag{6.9}
\]
The Laurent support of `B` is contained in an interval of exponent length `W(c)`. If the strand is legal for at least one base residue `r`, then the legality inequalities imply
\[
W(\mathbf c)\le m+7.
\tag{6.10}
\]
Indeed `0<=r+c_h<=m+h-1` implies
\[
r-m-3\le -c_h\le r
\]
and hence every exponent `-c_h+j`, `0<=j<=h<=4`, lies between `r-m-3` and `r+4`.

Shift (6.5) by a monomial so that it becomes an ordinary polynomial. If the actual sparse-filter support width is `w<=W(c)`, the shifted polynomial has degree
\[
m-1+w\le2m+6<p.
\]
By (6.8), its root at `1` has exact multiplicity `m-1`. Lemma 6.1 therefore forces at least `m` nonzero coefficients. There are only `m+w` coefficient positions in its full exponent interval, so at most `w` coefficients vanish.

Since every legal base residue root of the signed-Pascal congruence gives one such zero coefficient, we obtain the exact uniform bound
\[
\boxed{
\#\{r\ \text{legal}:\Phi_{m,\mathbf c}(r)\equiv0\pmod p\}
\le W(\mathbf c).
}
\tag{6.11}
\]
This is valid for every clean prime `p>4m+8`.

In the macroscopic variables `delta=d/m`, `epsilon=e/m`, one has
\[

=
\operatorname{range}\{0,\delta,\delta+\epsilon,2\delta+\epsilon\}

\tag{6.12}
\]
because
\[
c_1=-d,
\qquad c_2=-d-e,
\qquad c_4=X-2d-e.
\]
Thus the modular fiber size is controlled by a **geometric offset width**, rather than by the previous bound `7+|c1|+|c2|+|c4|`.

This still does not close the source: on the hard macroscopic family `W(c)` can itself be a positive fraction of `m`, so (6.11) need not be `O(1)`. But it identifies the true one-dimensional complexity parameter and removes an artificial sum-of-offset loss.
