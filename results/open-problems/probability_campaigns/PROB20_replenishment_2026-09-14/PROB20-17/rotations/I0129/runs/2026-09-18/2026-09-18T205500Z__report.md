# 4. B3 — prove something stronger: a common high Kummer-carry core gives `X=O(1)`

The obstruction in the previous weighted proof was the `O(L/log m)` logarithmic mass carried by prime bases at most `m+4`. Treating all of that mass as adversarial and then multiplying by a residue of size `O(m)` lost the factor `m/log m`. Kummer’s theorem contains substantially more structure: a large `p`-adic valuation is a stack of carry levels `p^\ell`, and above the row length those carry levels occupy one fixed numerator slot. The four rows have the same total number of `p`-carry levels because they represent the same integer `t`.

Set
\[
B:=4m+8.
\tag{4.1}
\]
For a prime `p`, write
\[
a_p:=v_p(t).
\]
For row `j` and level `\ell>=1`, put `q=p^\ell` and define the Kummer carry indicator
\[
\epsilon_{j,p,\ell}
:=
\left\lfloor\frac{n_j}{q}\right\rfloor
-\left\lfloor\frac{k_j}{q}\right\rfloor
-\left\lfloor\frac{n_j-k_j}{q}\right\rfloor
\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Kummer’s theorem says, for every `j`,
\[
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\epsilon_{j,p,\ell}=a_p.
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.1 Above `B`, the carry levels form one prefix

Let `\ell_p` be the least positive integer with
\[
p^{\ell_p}>B.
\tag{4.4}
\]
For `\ell>=\ell_p`, we have `q>B>k_j` for all four rows. In that range
\[
\epsilon_{j,p,\ell}=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r_{j,p,\ell}:=n_j\bmod q<k_j.
\tag{4.5}
\]
If (4.5) fails at one such level, it fails at every larger level: passing from modulus `q` to `pq` can only add a nonnegative digit multiple of `q` to the residue, and `q>k_j`. If it holds at consecutive levels, the residue stays unchanged. Therefore for each row the carry levels above `B` are an initial interval
\[
\ell_p,\ell_p+1,\ldots,\ell_p+h_{j,p}-1
\tag{4.6}
\]
for some `h_{j,p}>=0`.

Let
\[
\ell^{\rm low}_{j,p}:=\sum_{\ell<\ell_p}\epsilon_{j,p,\ell}.
\]
By (4.3),
\[
h_{j,p}=a_p-\ell^{\rm low}_{j,p}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Define the common high-carry length
\[
h_p:=\min_{j\in J} h_{j,p}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Then the levels
\[
\ell_p\le\ell<\ell_p+h_p
\tag{4.9}
\]
are carries in **all four** representations.

## 4.2 The common high-carry mass is `L-O(m)`

Define its logarithmic mass
\[
\Lambda:=\sum_p h_p\log p.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Since `h_p<=a_p`, certainly `\Lambda<=L`. Conversely, from (4.7),
\[
a_p-h_p=\max_j\ell^{\rm low}_{j,p}\le\ell_p-1.
\tag{4.11}
\]
If `p>B`, then `\ell_p=1`, so there is no loss at all. For `p<=B`,
\[
(\ell_p-1)\log p=\lfloor\log_p B\rfloor\log p.
\]
Therefore
\[
0\le L-\Lambda
\le
\sum_{p\le B}\lfloor\log_p B\rfloor\log p
=\psi(B),
\tag{4.12}
\]
where `\psi` is Chebyshev’s second function. The elementary Chebyshev bound `\psi(x)=O(x)` gives
\[
\boxed{\Lambda=L-O(m).}
\tag{4.13}
\]
In particular `\Lambda=(1-o(1))L` because `L\asymp m^2`.

## 4.3 Every common high carry obeys the exact four-row residue relation

Take one common level `q=p^\ell>B`. For each row, (4.5) gives a unique residue
\[
0\le r_j:=n_j\bmod q<k_j
\]
and an integer `Q_j` with `n_j=qQ_j+r_j`. Hence
\[
X=q(Q_0-Q_1-Q_2+Q_4)+(r_0-r_1-r_2+r_4).
\tag{4.14}
\]
The signed residue satisfies
\[
|r_0-r_1-r_2+r_4|\le2m+2.
\tag{4.15}
\]
Combining (3.4), (4.15), and `q>B=4m+8`, the multiple of `q` in (4.14) has absolute value strictly smaller than `q`. It must vanish. Thus at **every** common high carry level,
\[
\boxed{r_0-r_1-r_2+r_4=X.}
\tag{4.16}
\]
This is the small-prime analogue of the rough quotient collapse, but it applies level-by-level and costs only `O(m)` logarithmic mass in total.

## 4.4 Prime-power slot moments

For any row `(n,k)` and prime power `q=p^\ell`, define
\[
S_q(n,k):=\sum_{\substack{0\le r<k\\q\mid n-r}}r.
\tag{4.17}
\]
Prime factorization gives the exact identity
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1} r\log(n-r)
=
\sum_p\log p\sum_{\ell\ge1}S_{p^\ell}(n,k).
\tag{4.18}
\]
For a common high carry level, `q>k` and the sum in (4.17) has exactly one term, namely the residue in (4.16).

Let
\[
T:=\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_j\sum_{r=0}^{k_j-1}r\log(n_j-r).
\tag{4.19}
\]
Let `E` be the signed contribution in (4.18) from every prime-power level **except** the common high levels (4.9). Summing (4.16) with weight `\log p` gives the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{T=X\Lambda+E.}
\tag{4.20}
\]
The point is that `E` is only `O(m^2)`, not `O(mL/log m)`.

### Lemma 4.1 — low prime-power levels contribute only `O(m^2)` after the four-row sign pattern

Fix `q=p^\ell<=B` and write `s=n\bmod q`, `a=\lfloor k/q\rfloor`. The slots in (4.17) form one arithmetic progression. Its length is either `a` or `a+1`, so
\[
\left|
S_q(n,k)-\frac{q a(a-1)}2
\right|\le3k.
\tag{4.21}
\]
Put
\[
A_q(k):=\frac{q\lfloor k/q\rfloor(\lfloor k/q\rfloor-1)}2.
\]
When `k` increases by one, `A_q(k)` either does not change or changes by at most `k`. Consequently
\[
|A_q(m)-A_q(m+1)-A_q(m+2)+A_q(m+4)|=O(m)
\tag{4.22}
\]
uniformly in `q`. Combining (4.21) and (4.22),
\[
\left|
S_q(n_0,m)-S_q(n_1,m+1)-S_q(n_2,m+2)+S_q(n_4,m+4)
\right|=O(m).
\tag{4.23}
\]
Summing with weight `\log p` over all prime powers `p^\ell<=B` costs
\[
O(m)\sum_{p^\ell\le B}\log p
=O(m\psi(B))
=O(m^2).
\tag{4.24}
\]

**Proof of (4.21).** If the progression has `a` terms, its sum differs from `q a(a-1)/2` by `a s`, which is at most `k`. If it has `a+1` terms, the extra contribution is at most another `2k`; the case `q>k` has `a=0` and is even smaller. This proves the uniform bound. Equation (4.22) follows by writing the four-term expression as
`[A_q(m+4)-A_q(m+2)]-[A_q(m+1)-A_q(m)]` and bounding the three one-step increments. `QED`

### Lemma 4.2 — exceptional high carry levels contribute only `O(m^2)`

For `p>B`, `\ell_p=1`, so all four high-carry prefix lengths equal the common valuation `a_p`; there are no exceptional levels. For `p<=B`, the number of high carries present in one row but not all four is at most a constant multiple of `\ell_p-1`. Each such level contributes one residue of size at most `m+3`. Therefore their total weighted contribution is
\[
O(m)\sum_{p\le B}(\ell_p-1)\log p
=O(m\psi(B))
=O(m^2).
\tag{4.25}
\]
Combining Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2,
\[
\boxed{E=O(m^2).}
\tag{4.26}
\]

## 4.5 The deterministic slot moment is also `O(m^2)`

For completeness, the previous weighted calculation can be written in one line here. Put
\[
C_j=\frac{k_j(k_j-1)}2.
\]
Because `n_j` is exponential in `m`,
\[
\sum_{r<k_j}r\log(n_j-r)=C_j\log n_j+o(1).
\]
An exact calculation gives
\[
C_0-C_1-C_2+C_4=m+5.
\]
Using (3.3),
\[
T=O(m^2)=O(L).
\tag{4.27}
\]

### Theorem 4.3 — bounded signed top-index defect on the plastic branch

Every sufficiently large exact common-binomial realization of the surviving `(0,1,2,4)` plastic branch satisfies
\[
\boxed{|X|=O(1).}
\tag{4.28}
\]

**Proof.** From (4.20), (4.26), and (4.27),
\[
|X|\Lambda\le O(m^2).
\]
By (4.13) and (3.2), `\Lambda=(d_0+o(1))m^2`. Divide. `QED`

This is a genuine strengthening of the previous `O(m/log m)` result. It still does **not** prove eventual `X=0`; the implied absolute constant is not optimized or made explicit here.

**B3 outcome: positive.** The removed-rough/small-prime slack is not intrinsically `O(L/log m)` adversarial mass. At the Kummer carry-level resolution, all but `O(m)` log mass synchronizes across the four rows, and the low-level remainder has only `O(m^2)` signed first moment.

## 6.1 One rough component square-lifts the slot congruence

The preserved exact localization says that for a representation `t=binom(n,k)`, a rough component `Z=p^{v_p(t)}` with `p>k`, residue `r=n mod Z<k`, quotient `Q=(n-r)/Z`, and `U=t/Z`,
\[
Q\equiv U\kappa_{k,r}\pmod Z,
\qquad
\kappa_{k,r}=(-1)^{k-1-r}k\binom{k-1}{r}.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Multiplying by `Z` gives the exact square lift
\[
\boxed{Z^2\mid (n-r)-t\kappa_{k,r}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]
Because distinct rough components are coprime, both components sharing one cycle slot can be combined by CRT. Put
\[
\kappa_i:=\kappa_{m+i,r_i}\qquad(i=0,1,2).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
(xz)^2\mid A_0-t\kappa_0,\qquad
(xy)^2\mid A_1-t\kappa_1,\qquad
(yz)^2\mid A_2-t\kappa_2.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
This is stronger than merely saying the pair product divides the slot value.

## 6.2 Degree-two slots give a cyclic moving-S congruence system

In the degree-two case, write
\[
t=xyz\,T.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Substitute (6.3) into (6.6) and divide by the corresponding pair product. One obtains
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
xz&\mid H_0-yT\kappa_0,\\
xy&\mid H_1-zT\kappa_1,\\
yz&\mid H_2-xT\kappa_2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.8}
\]
Here every `H_i` and every `\kappa_i` has prime support at most `m+4`, while `x,y,z` are powers of three distinct primes exceeding `m+4`.

Eliminating `T` on the pairwise overlaps gives, exactly,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
x&\mid z\kappa_1H_0-y\kappa_0H_1,\\
y&\mid x\kappa_2H_1-z\kappa_1H_2,\\
z&\mid y\kappa_0H_2-x\kappa_2H_0.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.9}
\]
The elementary difference triangle is simultaneously
\[
x\mid A_0-A_1,\qquad y\mid A_1-A_2,\qquad z\mid A_2-A_0
\tag{6.10}
\]
after cyclic relabeling of the joining edge if desired. Thus a fixed `{0,1,2}` survivor is a **moving-S cyclic Thue–Mahler system**: three variable rough prime powers, three smooth cofactors, and one common multiplier `T`, coupled by both (6.8) and (6.10).
