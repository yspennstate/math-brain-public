# B21 — neighboring shape: what Tovey’s Pell/Fibonacci mechanism actually needs

The useful part of Tovey’s 1985 construction is not merely “a recurrence appears”; it is an enormous overlap of falling-factorial factors. The plastic branch loses exactly that feature.

### Lemma 1 (falling-factorial overlap degree)

Let \(d,r,k\) be positive integers. The exact collision
\[
{n\choose k}={n-d\choose k+r}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
(k+1)(k+2)\cdots(k+r)\,(n)_k=(n-d)_{k+r},                 \tag{1}
\]
where \((x)_j=x(x-1)\cdots(x-j+1)\). As polynomials in \(n\),
\[
\gcd\big((n)_k,(n-d)_{k+r}\big)
\]
has degree \(\max(0,k-d)\). After cancelling the polynomial gcd, the right side of (1) has degree
\[
r+\min(d,k).                                                \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** The roots of \((n)_k\) are \(0,1,\ldots,k-1\). The roots of \((n-d)_{k+r}\) are \(d,d+1,\ldots,d+k+r-1\). Their intersection is \(\{d,d+1,\ldots,k-1\}\) if \(d<k\), containing \(k-d\) points, and is empty when \(d\ge k\). Both polynomials split into distinct linear factors, so this is exactly the gcd degree. The uncancelled right degree is \(k+r-\max(0,k-d)=r+\min(d,k)\). ∎

For Tovey’s special case \(r=d=1\), (2) is degree two; explicit cancellation gives
\[
(k+1)n=(n-k)(n-k-1),
\]
which is precisely the Pell/Fibonacci-friendly reduction.

Now suppose the plastic branch were realized by four exact common-binomial rows
\[
t={n_0\choose m}={n_1\choose m+1}={n_2\choose m+2}={n_4\choose m+4}.
\]
Set
\[
a=n_0-n_1,\qquad b=n_1-n_2,\qquad c=n_2-n_4.
\]
The plastic ratios imply \(a,b,c\asymp n_0\), while \(n_0=\exp(d_0m+O(\log m))\). Hence eventually \(a,b,c>m+4\). Lemma 1 therefore says the first two exact collision equations have **no falling-factor overlap at all**; their remaining polynomial degrees grow like \(m\), rather than collapsing to a fixed quadratic/cubic. The two-step third collision has the same no-overlap feature. Thus the identity \(\rho^3=\rho+1\) does **not** port Tovey’s fixed Pell/Thue mechanism by itself.

The exact system that any branch realization must solve is
\[
\begin{aligned}
(m+1)(n_0)_m&=(n_1)_{m+1},\\
(m+2)(n_1)_{m+1}&=(n_2)_{m+2},\\
(m+3)(m+4)(n_2)_{m+2}&=(n_4)_{m+4}.                       \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
The signed four-row form simplifies exactly to
\[
X=n_0-n_1-n_2+n_4=a-c.                                     \tag{4}
\]
Consequently a rough-core band hit requires
\[
|a-c|\le 2(M-1),                                            \tag{5}
\]
although each of \(a,c\) is exponentially large. This is a useful reformulation: the first and last enormous top gaps must agree to polynomial accuracy.

There are also exact gap divisibilities obtained directly from (3). Reducing the three equations modulo their respective top gaps gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&\mid (n_0)_m\,(n_0-2m-1),\\
b&\mid (n_1)_{m+1}\,(n_1-2m-3),\\
c&\mid (n_2+1)_{m+3}\,(n_2-2m-6).                          \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
For example, modulo \(c\), replace \(n_4\) by \(n_2\) in the third equation and factor
\[
(n_2-m-2)(n_2-m-3)-(m+3)(m+4)=(n_2+1)(n_2-2m-6).
\]
Equations (5)–(6) are the concrete Tovey-style arithmetic left after the Pell overlap disappears. They do not yet close the branch.

**B21 outcome.** The nearest successful construction technique fails at a specific algebraic step: its fixed-degree Pell reduction is caused by falling-factor overlap, whereas every plastic-branch top gap is exponentially larger than the lower index. Any successful construction/exclusion must handle a growing-degree system, not merely a cubic number field.

# B6 — deform: fixed recurrences and finite-order `1/m` corrections are too rigid

The previous report showed that the undeformed Padovan/Perrin shadow has the wrong curvature. The following strengthens that to every fixed linear recurrence with one dominant root.

### Theorem 2 (no fixed-LRS realization of the plastic branch)

Let \((A_j)\) be a fixed constant-coefficient linear recurrence with a unique positive dominant characteristic root \(\rho>1\), allowing arbitrary fixed multiplicity of that root. Suppose eventually \(A_j>0\). Then there is no sequence \(j=j(m)\) for which
\[
A_j=n_0,\quad A_{j-1}=n_1,\quad A_{j-2}=n_2
\]
and the four rows lie on the exact plastic common-binomial branch for all sufficiently large \(m\).

**Proof.** Standard linear-recurrence asymptotics with a unique dominant root give
\[
A_j=\rho^j P(j)(1+O(\theta^j)),\qquad 0<\theta<1,
\]
where \(P\) is a fixed nonzero polynomial. If its degree is \(s\), positivity gives
\[
\log A_j=j\log\rho+s\log j+C+O(j^{-1})+O(\theta^j).          \tag{7}
\]
The plastic tube gives
\[
\log n_0=m\log\rho+\log m+O(1).
\]
Comparing with (7) forces \(j=m+O(\log m)\). Taking the second discrete difference of (7),
\[
\log\frac{A_jA_{j-2}}{A_{j-1}^2}=O(j^{-2})=O(m^{-2}),        \tag{8}
\]
because the linear term has zero second difference, \(s\log j\) contributes \(O(j^{-2})\), and the remaining fixed asymptotic expansion is smaller. But every exact common-binomial plastic quartet must satisfy
\[
\log\frac{n_0n_2}{n_1^2}=\frac{2\log\rho}{m}+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right), \tag{9}
\]
which is positive and of order \(1/m\). Equations (8) and (9) contradict each other for large \(m\). ∎

So the obstacle is not specific to the Padovan initial values: **no single fixed constant-coefficient recurrence with plastic dominant root can supply the required deformation**, even if the dominant root is repeated and supplies a polynomial prefactor.

There is a second quantitative obstruction to a naive asymptotic deformation.

### Lemma 3 (finite-order deformation cannot meet the rough band)

Normalize the signed row form by \(n_0\). Suppose an attempted `1/m` deformation has been matched through order \(R\), and its first nonzero omitted term is
\[
\frac{X}{n_0}=c_Rm^{-(R+1)}+O(m^{-(R+2)}),\qquad c_R\ne0.     \tag{10}
\]
If it is to lie in the rough band \(|X|\le2(M-1)\), then necessarily
\[
R\ge \frac{d_0m}{\log m}-O(1).                              \tag{11}
\]

**Proof.** From `M log M=o(L)` and \(L=d_0m^2+O(m)\), one has \(M=o(m^2/\log m)\), hence \(\log M=O(\log m)\). Also \(n_0=\exp(d_0m+O(\log m))\). Therefore the normalized band has width
\[
\frac{2M}{n_0}=\exp(-d_0m+O(\log m)).                       \tag{12}
\]
For fixed nonzero \(c_R\), (10) and (12) imply
\[
(R+1)\log m\ge d_0m-O(\log m),
\]
which is (11). ∎

This lemma is deliberately conditional on a genuine first nonzero power-series coefficient; it is not a universal theorem about all possible deformations. Its consequence is nevertheless sharp for the current route: **any fixed finite-order `1/m` correction is exponentially too coarse**. A realization requires an exact identity, a trans-asymptotic mechanism, or an expansion whose depth grows on the order of \(m/\log m\).

**B6 outcome.** The previous “slow `1/m` deformation” language was still too permissive. Fixed recurrences fail by curvature, and finite-order asymptotic corrections fail by exponential band width. Only genuinely arithmetic/non-LRS deformations remain.
