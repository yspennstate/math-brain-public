# 4. B18 — another language: the cubic branch is an inverse-jet collinearity Wronskian

The first task is to stop treating `G_I` as an opaque determinant.

Fix one row `k` and put `N=k-1`. For `0<=r<=N`, define
\[
p_r:=H_{N-r}-H_r,
\qquad
a_{2,r}:=H_{N-r}^{(2)}+H_r^{(2)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Here `H_j^(s)=sum_{u=1}^j u^{-s}`.

## Theorem 4.1 — exact normalized second and third inverse jets

At a root `r`,
\[
v_r=p_r\kappa_r,
\tag{4.2}
\]
\[
w_r=\frac{3p_r^2+a_{2,r}}2\,\kappa_r^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
Write the local factorization
\[
F_k(r+z)=\frac z{\kappa_r}
\prod_{u\ne r}\left(1+\frac z{r-u}\right).
\]
If
\[
F_k(r+z)=az+bz^2+cz^3+O(z^4),\qquad a=\kappa_r^{-1},
\]
then
\[
\frac ba=\sum_{u\ne r}\frac1{r-u}=H_r-H_{N-r}=-p_r,
\]
and
\[
\frac ca=\frac12\left((H_r-H_{N-r})^2-a_{2,r}\right).
\]
For the inverse series
\[
z=\kappa y+\alpha y^2+\beta y^3+O(y^4),
\]
coefficient comparison gives
\[
\alpha=-b\kappa^3,
\qquad
\beta=2b^2\kappa^5-c\kappa^4.
\]
Substitution gives `alpha/kappa=p_r kappa_r` and (4.3). ∎

Thus every row/root supplies a point
\[
P_{k,r}:=(v_{k,r},w_{k,r})
\tag{4.4}
\]
in an affine inverse-jet plane.

So the singular branch `Z|G_I` is a modular collinearity problem for three explicit inverse-jet points, not a generic cubic congruence.

# 5. B20 — why the lift algorithm still fails: polynomial degree versus rational denominator

A singular clean component satisfies
\[
Z\mid G_I(\mathbf r(Z)).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Since `r_h congruent n_h (mod Z)`, the most direct global strategy is to lift the residue function `G_I` to one rational or polynomial expression in the fixed integers `n_h`. Two natural lifts exist. They fail for opposite height reasons.

## 5.1 Low-denominator polynomial interpolation lift

For one row let `V_k(x)` and `W_k(x)` denote the unique polynomials of degree at most `N=k-1` with
\[
V_k(r)=v_{k,r},\qquad W_k(r)=w_{k,r}
\quad(0\le r\le N).
\tag{5.2}
\]
All denominators of the data are supported on primes `<=k`; after a denominator of size `exp(O(k log k))` these interpolation polynomials are integral, because the Lagrange denominators divide `N!`. Therefore clean primes `p>4m+8` see these polynomials as congruence-preserving.

The degree cost is genuinely macroscopic.

## Lemma 5.1 — exact degree of the second-jet interpolant

For `k>=3`,
\[
\deg V_k=N-1=k-2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof
Reflection gives
\[
\kappa_{N-r}=(-1)^N\kappa_r,
\qquad p_{N-r}=-p_r,
\]
so
\[
v_{N-r}=(-1)^{N+1}v_r.
\]
Uniqueness of interpolation gives
\[
V_k(N-x)=(-1)^{N+1}V_k(x).
\tag{5.4}
\]
The degree-`N` coefficient is therefore zero, so `deg V_k<=N-1`.

The coefficient of `x^(N-1)` is, up to the nonzero factor `1/N!`,
\[
\sum_{r=0}^N(-1)^{N-r}\binom Nr r v_r
=(N+1)\sum_{r=0}^N r\binom Nr^2p_r.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Pair `r` with `N-r`. For `r<N/2`, `p_r>0` and
\[
r p_r+(N-r)p_{N-r}=(2r-N)p_r<0.
\]
The central term, when present, has `p=0`. Hence the sum in (5.5) is strictly negative. Thus the `x^(N-1)` coefficient is nonzero. ∎

The reflection symmetry of `w` gives `W_k(N-x)=W_k(x)`, so `deg W_k<=N` (and often lower, but no stronger degree statement is needed here).

The natural branch polynomial
\[
\widehat\Gamma_I^{\rm poly}(x_i,x_j,x_k)
=\det[W_{k_h}(x_h),1,V_{k_h}(x_h)]
\tag{5.6}
\]
therefore has total degree `O(m)` and terms involving two independent row variables with combined degree asymptotically `2m`. At the plastic-scale collision points
\[
\log n_h=d_0m+O(\log m),
\]
a direct coefficient/degree bound after the small-prime denominator clearing gives only
\[
\log|\operatorname{num}\widehat\Gamma_I^{\rm poly}(n_i,n_j,n_k)|
\le 2L+o(L).
\tag{5.7}
\]
This is already larger than the full clean rough scale `log W=L-o(L)`. The polynomial lift is congruence-friendly but not height-friendly.

## 5.2 Low-value rational derivative lift

There is a completely different lift. For any `x` with `F_k'(x) != 0`, define
\[
\mathcal V_k(x)=-\frac{F_k''(x)}{2F_k'(x)^2},
\tag{5.8}
\]
\[
\mathcal W_k(x)=
\frac{F_k''(x)^2}{2F_k'(x)^4}
-\frac{F_k'''(x)}{6F_k'(x)^3}.
\tag{5.9}
\]
At every root `r`, these equal the inverse-jet data:
\[
\mathcal V_k(r)=v_{k,r},\qquad
\mathcal W_k(r)=w_{k,r}.
\tag{5.10}
\]
For a clean `p`, `F_k'(r)` is a `p`-adic unit because `k<p` and all factorial factors are units. Since `n_h congruent r_h (mod Z)`, the rational functions are `p`-integral and
\[
\mathcal V_{k_h}(n_h)\equiv v_{k_h,r_h}\pmod Z,
\qquad
\mathcal W_{k_h}(n_h)\equiv w_{k_h,r_h}\pmod Z.
\tag{5.11}
\]
Thus the single rational number
\[
\widehat\Gamma_I^{\rm rat}
:=\det[\mathcal W_{k_h}(n_h),1,\mathcal V_{k_h}(n_h)]
=\frac{A_I}{B_I}
\quad(\gcd(A_I,B_I)=1)
\tag{5.12}
\]
has
\[
\gcd(B_I,W)=1,
\tag{5.13}
\]
and every singular component divides the one global numerator:
\[
\prod_{Z:\,Z\mid G_I(\mathbf r(Z))} Z\ \mid\ A_I.
\tag{5.14}
\]
This is a genuine cross-component aggregation theorem; it avoids CRT idempotents entirely.

Unfortunately the numerator height is too large.

## Proposition 5.2 — explicit rational-lift numerator bound

In the plastic regime,
\[
\log|A_I|\le 9L+O(m\log m).
\tag{5.15}
\]

### Proof
Put
\[
P_k(x)=\prod_{u=0}^{k-1}(x-u)=k!F_k(x)\in\mathbb Z[x].
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal V_k(x)=-\frac{k!P_k''(x)}{2P_k'(x)^2},
\]
\[
\mathcal W_k(x)=
\frac{(k!)^2(3P_k''(x)^2-P_k'(x)P_k'''(x))}{6P_k'(x)^4}.
\tag{5.16}
\]
At each integer `n_h`, multiply the whole determinant row by
\[
d_h=6P_{k_h}'(n_h)^4.
\]
The three row entries become the integers
\[
(k_h!)^2(3P_h''{}^2-P_h'P_h'''),
\qquad 6P_h'^4,
\qquad -3k_h!P_h''P_h'^2.
\tag{5.17}
\]
Let `A_raw` be the determinant of these integer rows. The reduced numerator `A_I` divides `A_raw` up to sign, so `|A_I|<=|A_raw|`.

For `n_h>>k_h`, write `P=P_h(n_h)=k_h!t`. The logarithmic-derivative identities give
\[
|P'|\ll P\frac{k_h}{n_h-k_h},\quad
|P''|\ll P\left(\frac{k_h}{n_h-k_h}\right)^2,
\quad
|P'''|\ll P\left(\frac{k_h}{n_h-k_h}\right)^3.
\]
Every determinant term in (5.17) is therefore at most
\[
O(1)\,t^9\prod_{h\in I}(k_h!)^4
\prod_{h\in I}\left(\frac{k_h}{n_h-k_h}\right)^4.
\]
Taking logarithms and using `log(k!)=O(m log m)` and (3.1) yields (5.15). ∎

So the rational lift is value-small but denominator-expensive; after exact denominator clearing, its numerator can cost nine source scales. The polynomial lift has small-prime denominators but macroscopic degree and costs about two source scales. Neither reaches the required `<(1-c)L` scale.

**B20 outcome.** The branch section does admit natural global lifts, but the simplest interpolation and rational-derivative constructions expose an explicit degree-versus-denominator tradeoff rather than a saving. A successful auxiliary object must exploit cross-row cancellation *before* the lift, not interpolate each row independently.
