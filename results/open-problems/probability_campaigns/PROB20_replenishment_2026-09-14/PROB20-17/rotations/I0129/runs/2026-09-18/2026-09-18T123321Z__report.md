## 3. B5 — edge parameter and uniform derivative asymptotics

The useful object is the real inverse-binomial row curve. For real `x>=2`, let `f_t(x)=y` be the unique solution on the large-`y` branch of

\[
\frac{\Gamma(y+1)}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(y-x+1)}=t.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For all represented left-edge integer rows, `y=n` and `x=m`. Put

\[
g(x):=\log\Gamma(x+1),
\qquad
s(x):=\log f_t(x),
\qquad
\lambda(x):=\frac{L}{x},
\qquad
\lambda_0:=\frac{L}{M}.
\]

The ultraflat hypothesis gives

\[
\frac{\lambda_0}{\log M}=\frac{L}{M\log M}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The key point is that, uniformly for `2<=x<=M`, the inverse-binomial branch is exponentially far from the diagonal and its logarithm is governed by the elementary normal form

\[
h(x):=\frac{L+g(x)}x.
\]

### Lemma 3.1 — uniform logarithmic normal form, with fixed-order derivatives

Fix an integer `J>=1`. In the ultraflat regime, uniformly for `2<=x<=M`,

\[
s^{(j)}(x)=h^{(j)}(x)+o(M^{-J})
\qquad(0\le j\le J),
\tag{3.3}
\]

after increasing the asymptotic threshold if necessary. In particular, for every fixed `j>=1`,

\[
s^{(j)}(x)
=
(-1)^j j!\frac{L}{x^{j+1}}
+
O_j\!\left(\frac{\log(x+1)}{x^j}\right),
\tag{3.4}
\]

and hence

\[
s^{(j)}(x)
=
(-1)^j j!\frac{\lambda(x)}{x^j}
\left(1+o(1)\right)
\tag{3.5}
\]

uniformly on `[2,M]`.

#### Proof

Write

\[
R(y,x):=\log\Gamma(y+1)-\log\Gamma(y-x+1).
\]

Using the digamma function,

\[
R(y,x)=\int_0^x \psi(y+1-u)\,du.
\]

For `y>=2M` and `2<=x<=M`, the standard polygamma estimates

\[
\psi(z)=\log z+O(z^{-1}),
\qquad
\psi^{(r)}(z)=O_r(z^{-r})\quad(r\ge1)
\]

give

\[
R(y,x)=x\log y+E(y,x),
\tag{3.6}
\]

where, for each fixed total differentiation order, every mixed partial of `E` is bounded by a fixed polynomial in `M` times `1/y` (and additional `y` derivatives only improve that decay). For example,

\[
E=O(M^2/y),\qquad
E_x=O(M/y),\qquad
E_{xx}=O(1/y),\qquad
E_{xxx}=O(1/y^2),
\]

and similarly for the mixed `y` derivatives needed below.

We first justify that the branch is indeed enormous. Since `\psi(u)<=\log u` for `u>0`, (3.1) implies

\[
L+g(x)=R(f_t(x),x)
\le x\log(f_t(x)+1),
\]

so

\[
f_t(x)+1\ge \exp(L/x)\ge \exp(\lambda_0).
\tag{3.7}
\]

By (3.2), `exp(lambda_0)` dominates every fixed power of `M`. Thus throughout the interval the error term in (3.6), together with every fixed collection of its mixed partial derivatives, is super-polynomially small in `M`.

Set `y=e^s` and `\widetilde E(s,x)=E(e^s,x)`. Equation (3.1) becomes

\[
x s-g(x)+\widetilde E(s,x)=L.
\tag{3.8}
\]

All fixed-order partial derivatives of `\widetilde E` are super-polynomially small because each differentiation in `s` contributes factors of `y` that are canceled by the corresponding additional `y` decay of the mixed derivatives of `E`. Also

\[
\partial_s\bigl(xs-g(x)+\widetilde E(s,x)\bigr)
=x+o(1),
\]

uniformly, hence is bounded away from zero. Equation (3.8) first gives

\[
s=h+o(M^{-J}).
\]

Differentiating (3.8) repeatedly, and inducting on the derivative order, gives (3.3): at each step the coefficient of the highest derivative of `s` is `x+o(1)`, while every term containing a derivative of `\widetilde E` is super-polynomially small and all lower derivatives of `s` have already been controlled.

Finally, Stirling/polygamma bounds give

\[
g(x)=O(x\log(x+1)),
\quad
g'(x)=O(\log(x+1)),
\quad
g^{(r)}(x)=O_r(x^{1-r})\ (r\ge2).
\]

Differentiating `h=(L+g)/x` yields (3.4). Since `lambda(x)>=lambda_0>>log M`, the error in (3.4) is `o(lambda/x^j)`, proving (3.5). `square`

### Lemma 3.2 — fixed-order complete-monotonicity scale

For every fixed integer `k>=1`, uniformly for `2<=x<=M`,

\[
f_t^{(k)}(x)
=
(-1)^k f_t(x)
\left(\frac{\lambda(x)}x\right)^k
\left(1+o(1)\right).
\tag{3.9}
\]

Consequently, for each fixed `k`, for all sufficiently large `t` in the ultraflat regime,

\[
\boxed{
(-1)^k f_t^{(k)}(x)>2^{k-1}M
\qquad(2\le x\le M).
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

#### Proof

Since `f_t=e^s`, Faà di Bruno / the complete Bell polynomial identity gives

\[
\frac{f_t^{(k)}}{f_t}=B_k(s',s'',\ldots,s^{(k)}).
\]

By (3.5), the monomial `(s')^k` equals

\[
(-1)^k\left(\frac{\lambda}x\right)^k(1+o(1)).
\]

Every other Bell-polynomial monomial has total differential weight `k` but strictly fewer than `k` factors. Each factor `s^{(j)}` contributes one power of `lambda` and `x^{-j}`, so every other monomial is

\[
O_k\!\left(\frac{\lambda^{k-1}}{x^k}\right),
\]

which is smaller than `(s')^k` by `O_k(1/lambda_0)=o(1)`. This proves (3.9).

From (3.3) and `g(x)>=0` for `x>=2`,

\[
f_t(x)=\exp(s(x))\ge \tfrac12 e^{L/x}\ge \tfrac12 e^{\lambda_0}
\]

for all sufficiently large `t`. Therefore (3.9) gives, uniformly,

\[
|f_t^{(k)}(x)|
\ge
\frac14 e^{\lambda_0}
\left(\frac{\lambda_0}{M}\right)^k.
\tag{3.11}
\]

For fixed `k`, the logarithm of the right-hand side divided by `M` is dominated by `lambda_0`, while (3.2) says `lambda_0>>log M`. Hence

\[
\frac{e^{\lambda_0}(\lambda_0/M)^k}{M}\to\infty.
\]

In particular (3.11) eventually exceeds `2^{k-1}M`, and its sign is `(-1)^k` by (3.9). `square`

For SC-4 we use `k=3`: the third derivative is eventually negative throughout `[2,M]` and satisfies

\[
\boxed{|f_t'''(x)|>4M.}
\tag{3.12}
\]

This is the B5 scale comparison that the recovery report asked for.

## 4. B11 — dualize to divided differences and Vandermonde cofactors

Let

\[
2\le x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_k\le M
\]

be distinct integer lower-index nodes and

\[
P(X)=\prod_{i=0}^k(X-x_i),
\qquad
D_i:=|P'(x_i)|.
\]

The previously proved primitive-annihilator formula says that the unique primitive integer relation annihilating moments `0,1,...,k-1` is a scalar primitive multiple of

\[
\frac1{P'(x_i)}.
\]

If `Lambda=lcm(D_0,...,D_k)`, then one can take

\[
c_i=\frac{\Lambda}{P'(x_i)},
\qquad
A=\|c\|_1=\Lambda\sum_i\frac1{D_i},
\]

and its row form is

\[
X=\sum_i c_i f_t(x_i)
=\Lambda\, f_t[x_0,\ldots,x_k].
\]

Hence the exact rough-core band-entry test is scale-free:

\[
\boxed{
|f_t[x_0,\ldots,x_k]|
\le
\frac{M-1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^k\frac1{D_i}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The arithmetic integer spacing gives a sharp universal denominator majorant. For sorted distinct integers,

\[
x_i-x_j\ge i-j\quad(j<i),
\qquad
x_j-x_i\ge j-i\quad(j>i),
\]

so

\[
D_i
=\prod_{j\ne i}|x_i-x_j|
\ge i!\,(k-i)!.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^k\frac1{D_i}
\le
\sum_{i=0}^k\frac1{i!(k-i)!}
=\frac{2^k}{k!}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equality holds for consecutive nodes, so no hidden irregular-gap configuration makes the right side larger.

The generalized mean-value theorem for divided differences supplies some

\[
\xi\in(x_0,x_k)
\]

with

\[
f_t[x_0,\ldots,x_k]
=\frac{f_t^{(k)}(\xi)}{k!}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (3.10), (4.2), and (4.3) immediately compares the real curve geometry with the arithmetic residue band.

## 5. B4 — SC-4 completely, and the fixed-support extension

### Theorem 5.1 — SC-4

Assume

For all sufficiently large `t`, for every four distinct integer nodes

the primitive integer relation annihilating moments `0,1,2` **fails** the rough-core band-entry criterion. Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
|f_t[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]|
>
\frac{M-1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^3\frac1{|P'(x_i)|}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

Take `k=3` in (4.3). By (3.12),

\[
|f_t[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]|
=\frac{|f_t'''(\xi)|}{6}
>\frac{4M}{6}
=\frac{2M}{3}.
\]

On the other hand, (4.2) for `k=3` gives

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3\frac1{D_i}\le\frac{8}{6}=\frac43,
\]

so the right side of the band criterion is at most

\[
\frac{M-1}{2}\cdot\frac43
=\frac{2(M-1)}3
<\frac{2M}{3}.
\]

Thus (4.1) is impossible. `square`

### Theorem 5.2 — every fixed-support full moment annihilator is excluded

Fix any integer `k>=2`. Under the same ultraflat hypothesis `M log M=o(log t)`, for all sufficiently large `t` (with the threshold allowed to depend on fixed `k`) and every `k+1` distinct integer nodes in `[2,M]`, the primitive relation annihilating moments `0,1,...,k-1` fails its rough-core band-entry criterion:

\[
\boxed{
|f_t[x_0,\ldots,x_k]|
>
\frac{M-1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^k\frac1{|P'(x_i)|}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

By (4.3) and (3.10),

\[
|f_t[x_0,\ldots,x_k]|
=\frac{|f_t^{(k)}(\xi)|}{k!}
>
\frac{2^{k-1}M}{k!}.
\]

By (4.2),

\[
\frac{M-1}{2}\sum_i\frac1{D_i}
\le
\frac{M-1}{2}\frac{2^k}{k!}
=
\frac{2^{k-1}(M-1)}{k!}
<
\frac{2^{k-1}M}{k!}.
\]

Hence band entry is impossible. `square`
