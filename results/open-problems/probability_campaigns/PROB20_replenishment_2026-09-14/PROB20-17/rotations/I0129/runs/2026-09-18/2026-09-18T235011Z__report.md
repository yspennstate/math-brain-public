# 5. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: the defect is the unique fixed leading linear invariant

The previous report suggested searching for a second short integer linear form in the four center coordinates. The plastic algebra itself shows a sharp limitation on that route.

Let

\tag{5.1}

The leading plastic vector is
\[
(1,q_0,q_0^2,q_0^4).

### Theorem 5.1 — uniqueness of the fixed rational annihilator
If
\[
Q_a(q_0)=0,
\tag{5.2}
\]
then `a` is a rational scalar multiple of
\[
(1,-1,-1,1).
\tag{5.3}
\]

**Proof.** The number `q_0` has irreducible cubic minimal polynomial
\[
M(q)=q^3+q^2-1.
\]
A polynomial `Q_a` of degree at most four that vanishes at `q_0` must be divisible by `M`. Hence
\[
Q_a(q)=(uq+v)(q^3+q^2-1)
\]
for rational `u,v`. Expanding,
\[
Q_a(q)=u q^4+(u+v)q^3+v q^2-uq-v.
\]
Our support has no `q^3` term, so `u+v=0`. Thus
\[
Q_a(q)=u(q^4-q^2-q+1)=uP(q),
\]
which is exactly (5.3). `QED`

### Corollary 5.2 — no independent fixed-coefficient bounded linear conservation law
For any fixed rational vector `a` not proportional to `(1,-1,-1,1)`, and uniformly for bounded defect `x`,
\[
\sum_h a_hM_h(x;m)
=\Theta_a(m\rho^m).
\tag{5.4}
\]
In particular it cannot remain `O(m^C)` for any fixed `C`.

**Proof.** The fixed-offset plastic ratios give
\[
M_h(x;m)=M_0(x;m)q_0^h(1+O(1/m)),
\]
and `M_0\asymp m\rho^m`. By Theorem 5.1 the leading coefficient `Q_a(q_0)` is nonzero, yielding (5.4). `QED`

So the exact affine defect relation
\[
M_0-M_1-M_2+M_4=x
\tag{5.5}
\]
is not one of several comparable fixed linear invariants: it is the **only** one visible at the plastic leading scale on these four rows. A second scalar obstruction must use either coefficients depending nontrivially on `m`, nonlinear resultants/gcds, or the exact p-adic/binomial structure.

There is also an elementary exact gcd conservation which becomes stronger now that `X` is bounded. Write the actual top gaps
\[

\qquad
b=n_1-n_2,
\qquad
c=n_2-n_4.
\tag{5.6}

Then
\[
a-c=X,
\tag{5.7}

so
\[
\boxed{\gcd(a,c)\mid X.}
\tag{5.8}

For every fixed nonzero `X`, all prime divisors of `a` and `c` exceeding `|X|` are therefore disjoint. This will combine with the exact collision factorization below.

**B12 outcome: mixed.** The defect gcd is a genuine conserved arithmetic invariant, but the leading plastic vector admits no second independent fixed rational annihilator. This closes off one tempting dual-linear route and forces the next obstruction to be nonlinear or p-adic.

# 6. B26 — HUNT THE SPORADIC CASE: exact large-prime classification for the actual top gaps

The previous B21 diagnosis identified a precise missing bridge: fixed-gap methods would become relevant if one controlled the rough prime kernels of the **actual** gaps `a` and `c`, rather than only residue-shifted gaps. There is an exact elementary collision identity that supplies the first such bridge.

## 6.1 General collision-gap lemma

Use falling factorial notation
\[
(x)_r=x(x-1)\cdots(x-r+1).
\]
Suppose
\[
\binom{N+d}{k}=\binom{N}{k+s}=t,
\qquad d>0,\ s\ge1.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
(k+1)_s(N+d)_k=(N)_k(N-k)_s.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Let `p^e|d` with `p>k+s`. Reducing (6.2) modulo `p^e`, using `(N+d)_k congruent (N)_k mod p^e`, gives
\[
(N)_k\big((N-k)_s-(k+1)_s\big)\equiv0\pmod{p^e}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — large gap-prime dichotomy
Under (6.1), let `p^e|d` and `p>k+s`.

1. If `p` does **not** divide `t`, then
\[
\boxed{
p^e\mid (N-k)_s-(k+1)_s.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
2. If `p` divides `t`, then
\[
\boxed{N\bmod p<k.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

**Proof.** If `p` does not divide `t`, then because `p>k+s`, the denominator `(k+s)!` is a `p`-adic unit and none of the numerator factors in `(N)_{k+s}` is divisible by `p`. In particular `(N)_k` is invertible modulo `p^e`, so (6.3) gives (6.4).

If `p|t`, use the other representation `t=binom(N+d,k)`. Since `p>k`, its denominator is a unit and an interval of `k<p` consecutive numerator factors contains a multiple of `p` exactly when `(N+d) mod p<k`. Since `p|d`, `(N+d) mod p=N mod p`, proving (6.5). `QED`

This is an exact dichotomy between a prime already belonging to the common binomial value and a prime forced onto a low-degree exceptional polynomial in the top index.

## 6.2 Specialization to the three successive plastic gaps

For
\[
a=n_0-n_1,
\]
use `N=n_1`, `k=m`, `s=1`. Then
\[
(N-k)_1-(k+1)_1=N-(2k+1),
\]
so for `p^e|a`, `p>2m+7` and `p not dividing t`,
\[
\boxed{p^e\mid n_1-(2m+1).}
\tag{6.6}
\]
If instead `p|t`, then `n_1 mod p<m`.

For
\[
b=n_1-n_2,
\]
use `N=n_2`, `k=m+1`, `s=1`. Thus for `p^e|b`, `p>2m+7` and `p not dividing t`,
\[
\boxed{p^e\mid n_2-(2m+3).}
\tag{6.7}
\]

For
\[
c=n_2-n_4,
\]
use `N=n_4`, `k=m+2`, `s=2`. Here
\[
(N-k)_2-(k+1)_2
=(N+1)(N-(2k+2)),
\]
so
\[
(N-k)_2-(k+1)_2=(n_4+1)(n_4-(2m+6)).
\tag{6.8}
\]
If `p>2m+7`, the two factors in (6.8) are incongruent modulo `p`, since their difference is `2m+7`. Therefore every external prime power goes wholly into one factor:
\[
\boxed{
p^e\mid n_4+1
\quad\text{or}\quad
p^e\mid n_4-(2m+6).
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

This is the first exact classification in this track of the large-prime powers of the **unshifted actual top gaps**.

## 6.3 Polynomial loss for the external rough kernel of the adjacent gaps

Define the large-prime part of `a` not already supported on the common binomial value:
\[
A_{\rm ext}
:=\prod_{\substack{p^e\parallel a\\ p>2m+7\\ p\nmid t}}p^e.
\tag{6.10}

By (6.6),
\[
A_{\rm ext}\mid \gcd\big(a,n_1-(2m+1)\big).
\tag{6.11}

On the corrected plastic branch,
\[

Put

Substituting `q=alpha/(1+alpha)` into `q^3+q^2-1=0` shows that `alpha` is the cubic algebraic irrational root of
\[
f(z)=z^3-2z^2-3z-1.
\tag{6.13}
\]
Let
\[
G=\gcd\big(a,n_1-(2m+1)\big),
\qquad
q=\frac aG,
\qquad
p=\frac{n_1-(2m+1)}G,

\qquad

The effective `1/3` statement (6.15)--(6.16) is sufficient as a completely elementary recorded gain.

## 6.4 Fixed nonzero defect separates the two actual gap kernels

From (5.8), if `X` is a fixed nonzero integer and `m>|X|`, then no prime `p>2m+7` can divide both `a` and `c`. Thus the large-prime supports of the two exponentially large, bounded-distance integers

\tag{6.19}

\boxed{
p^e\mid n_1-n_4+5
\quad\text{or}\quad
p^e\mid n_1-n_4-(2m+2).

\tag{6.20}

This gives an explicit exceptional-line condition for every large external prime power in a zero-defect survivor.

**B26 outcome: positive arithmetic bridge, no source closure.** The missing “rough kernel of the actual gaps” problem is no longer completely opaque: the large external part of the adjacent gaps is forced onto explicit linear factors and is polynomially deficient. # 7. Why these gains still do not move `rho_SM`

The new results are source-relevant but branch-restricted.

1. The exact defect deformation reduces an exact fixed-`X` quartet to three simultaneous integer coordinates, but it does not prove those lattice hits are finite.
2. The unique-annihilator theorem shows why a second fixed short linear form does not exist, but a negative route diagnosis is not a multiplicity bound.
3. The gap-prime lemma controls actual large-prime factors, but the polynomial `m^{-1/3}` loss is only logarithmic on the `log` scale. By itself it is far smaller than the `L asymp m^2` mass needed for a fixed improvement of `rho_SM`.
4. Large prime factors of the gaps that also divide `t` remain governed by the moving Kummer core. The present run does not yet prove that this `t`-supported part is small enough.

So the exact remaining arithmetic bottleneck is sharper:

> **Actual-gap core problem.** For a deterministic exact nonlinear lattice hit `M_h(X;m) in Z`, show that the large-prime part of at least one adjacent top gap supported on primes dividing the common value `t` is too small to complement the external deficit (6.15)--(6.16), or derive a resultant/cycle contradiction from that support.

This is a different target from generic Gamma-center fractional-part estimates: it directly couples the deterministic lattice hit to the exact prime support of the common binomial value.
