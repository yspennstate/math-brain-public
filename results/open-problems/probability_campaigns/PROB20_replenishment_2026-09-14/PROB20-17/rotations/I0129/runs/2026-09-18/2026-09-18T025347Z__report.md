## 2. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: a rough-core linear-form principle

The first goal was to stop treating prime-power components independently and force the **whole profile** through any low-height relation among represented rows.

### Lemma 2.1 — finite-linear-form rough-core divisibility

Let `(n_i,m_i)`, `1<=i<=k`, be represented nontrivial left-half rows of the same `t`, with every `m_i<=M`. Let `c_1,...,c_k` be integers and put

\[
E:=\sum_{i=1}^k c_i n_i,
\qquad
C:=\sum_{i=1}^k|c_i|,
\qquad
S:=C(M-1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For each rough prime-power component `Z_ell` define

\[
s_\ell:=\sum_{i=1}^k c_i r_{i,\ell}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Then `|s_ell|<=S` and

\[
Z_\ell\mid E-s_\ell.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For each integer `s in [-S,S]`, define

\[
W_s:=\prod_{\substack{\ell>M\\ s_\ell=s}}Z_\ell.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The `Z_ell` are pairwise coprime, so

\[
\boxed{W_s\mid E-s\quad\text{for every }s.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
W\mid\prod_{s=-S}^{S}(E-s).
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

If `|E|>S`, no factor on the right is zero, hence

\[
\boxed{
W\le (|E|+S)^{2S+1},
\qquad
|E|+S\ge W^{1/(2S+1)}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

If instead `E` is an integer in `[-S,S]`, the zero factor in (2.6) can be isolated. Writing `W_E` for the subproduct whose exact residue linear form equals `E`, one gets

\[
\boxed{
\frac{W}{W_E}
\le
\prod_{\substack{-S\le s\le S\\s\ne E}}|E-s|
\le (2S)^{2S}.
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

Thus a genuinely small Archimedean integer relation among rows forces almost all of a sufficiently large rough core into prime-power components satisfying the **same exact residue relation**, not merely congruent relations modulo unrelated primes.

#### Proof

By (1.2), for each `ell>M` we can write

\[
n_i=Z_\ell q_{i,\ell}+r_{i,\ell}.
\]

Multiplying by `c_i` and summing gives

\[
E-s_\ell
=Z_\ell\sum_i c_iq_{i,\ell},
\]

which proves (2.3). Since `0<=r_{i,ell}<=m_i-1<=M-1`, (2.2) gives `|s_ell|<=S`. Components with the same `s` are powers of distinct primes, hence pairwise coprime, proving (2.5). Multiplying (2.5) over `s` proves (2.6). If `|E|>S`, then `|E-s|<=|E|+S` for all `2S+1` values of `s`, giving (2.7). If `E in [-S,S]`, discard the group `s=E`; the remaining `2S` nonzero factors have magnitude at most `2S`, giving (2.8). `QED`

This is stronger than the earlier one-prime Hensel formulation because it constrains the complete common rough profile at once. It still does not by itself beat Kane: for low-order finite differences of the real inverse-binomial curve, `|E|` is generally exponentially large on the natural `L/M` scale, so (2.7) is not yet contradictory.

### Theorem 2.2 — optimized two-row rough-core transition inequality

The linear-form lemma can be sharpened substantially for a transition between two represented rows because a prime-power component has only two possibilities: it remains in an old slot or moves into one of the newly opened slots.

Let

\[
{n\choose m}={n'\choose m'}=t,
\qquad 2\le m<m'\le M,
\qquad n\ge2m,\ n'\ge2m'.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Then necessarily `n>n'`. Put

\[
D:=n-n'>0,
\qquad
h:=m'-m.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Assume in addition that

\[
n'\ge2(m')^2.
\tag{2.11}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
W\le (n')^h\,(D+m-1)^{2m-1}.
}
\tag{2.12}
\]

Equivalently, whenever `log W>h log n'`,

\[
\boxed{
D+m-1
\ge
\exp\!\left(
\frac{\log W-h\log n'}{2m-1}
\right).
}
\tag{2.13}
\]

#### Proof, step 1: the row separation is at least `m'`

Suppose toward contradiction that `D<=m'-1`. Then

\[
n\le n'+m'-1,
\qquad
m\le m'-1.
\]

Since binomial coefficients increase in both the top argument and, up to the middle, the lower argument,

\[
t={n\choose m}
\le {n'+m'-1\choose m'-1}.
\tag{2.14}
\]

Compare this with `t={n'\choose m'}`. Their ratio is

\[
R:=
\frac{{n'+m'-1\choose m'-1}}{{n'\choose m'}}
=
\frac{m'}{n'}
\prod_{j=1}^{m'-1}\frac{n'+j}{n'-j}.
\tag{2.15}
\]

For `0<x<1`,

\[
\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}\le\frac{2x}{1-x}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\log R
\le
\log\frac{m'}{n'}
+
\frac{m'(m'-1)}{n'-m'+1}.
\tag{2.16}
\]

Under (2.11),

\[
\log\frac{m'}{n'}\le-\log(2m'),
\qquad
\frac{m'(m'-1)}{n'-m'+1}<\frac12.
\]

For `m'>=2`, `-log(2m')+1/2<0`; hence `R<1`, contradicting (2.14). Thus

\[
\boxed{D\ge m'.}
\tag{2.17}
\]

#### Proof, step 2: split the common rough core by placement type

For each `Z_ell|W`, let

\[
r_\ell:=n\bmod Z_\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\},
\qquad
r'_\ell:=n'\bmod Z_\ell\in\{0,\ldots,m'-1\}.
\tag{2.18}
\]

Split

\[
W=W_{\rm new}W_{\rm old},
\]

where `W_new` is the product of those components with `r'_ell>=m` and `W_old` the product of those with `r'_ell<m`.

For a component in `W_new`, `Z_ell` divides one of the `h` integers

\[
n'-m,\ n'-(m+1),\ldots,n'-(m'-1).
\]

Components are pairwise coprime, so grouping by their slot gives

\[
\boxed{
W_{\rm new}
\mid
\prod_{j=m}^{m'-1}(n'-j),
\qquad
W_{\rm new}\le(n')^h.
}
\tag{2.19}
\]

For a component in `W_old`, both residues are below `m`. Since the component divides both `n-r_ell` and `n'-r'_ell`, it divides

\[
D-(r_\ell-r'_\ell).
\]

Here

\[
-(m-1)\le r_\ell-r'_\ell\le m-1.
\]

By (2.17), every integer `D-s` in this range is positive. Grouping components by `s=r_ell-r'_ell` and using coprimality gives

\[
\boxed{
W_{\rm old}
\mid
\prod_{s=-(m-1)}^{m-1}(D-s),
\qquad
W_{\rm old}\le(D+m-1)^{2m-1}.
}
\tag{2.20}
\]

Multiplying (2.19) and (2.20) proves (2.12), and taking logarithms gives (2.13). `QED`

### Corollary 2.3 — most transitions in a deep-edge chain must pay a large Archimedean gap

In the unresolved deep edge, earlier work gives

\[
\log W=(1-o(1))L,
\tag{2.21}
\]

while for a represented row

\[
\log n'\le \frac{L}{m'}+\log m'.
\tag{2.22}
\]

Suppose along a sequence that `m,m'->infinity`, `m' log m'/L->0`, and for some fixed `epsilon in (0,1)`

\[
\frac{m}{m'}\ge\epsilon.
\tag{2.23}
\]

Then `h<= (1-epsilon)m'`, so

\[
h\log n'
\le
(1-\epsilon)L+o(L).
\]

Substituting into (2.13) yields

\[
\boxed{
\log(D+m)
\ge
\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}-o(1)\right)\frac{L}{m}.
}
\tag{2.24}
\]

If more strongly `h=o(m')`, then `m/m'->1` and

\[
\boxed{
\log(D+m)
\ge
\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)\frac{L}{m}.
}
\tag{2.25}
\]

Now order represented lower indices increasingly,

\[
m_1<m_2<\cdots<m_R\le M.
\]

For fixed `epsilon`, a transition with `m_i/m_{i+1}<epsilon` multiplies the lower index by more than `1/epsilon`; there can be only `O_epsilon(log M)` such transitions. Hence, apart from logarithmically many coarse multiplicative jumps and any bounded-index fringe, a long unresolved chain is forced into the large-gap alternative (2.24).

**B3 outcome.** The diffuse rough core is not merely “reusable.” Across two rows it must be paid for in one of two currencies: newly opened lower-index slots (`h`) or a large Archimedean row displacement (`D`). This replaces the previous qualitative simultaneous-placement obligation by the exact inequality (2.12).

## 3. B13 — FIND WHAT ONLY INCREASES: quotient-drop energy and why it is not yet enough

The split in Theorem 2.2 suggests a natural monotone object. Fix one rough component `Z_ell` and follow represented rows in increasing lower index, hence decreasing row top `n_i`. Write

\[
n_i=Z_\ell q_{i,\ell}+r_{i,\ell},
\qquad 0\le r_{i,\ell}<m_i.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because `n_i` strictly decreases, the quotient `q_{i,ell}=floor(n_i/Z_ell)` is nonincreasing.

More is true. In a transition `m<m'`, if the component lies in `W_new`, then `r_ell<m` but `r'_ell>=m`. If its quotient did not drop, `q'_ell=q_ell`, then `n>n'` would force `r_ell>r'_ell`, impossible. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Z_\ell\mid W_{\rm new}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q'_{\ell}<q_{\ell}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define the weighted quotient-drop energy of a transition by

\[
\Delta_i:=
\sum_{\ell>M}
(\log Z_\ell)
\bigl(q_{i,\ell}-q_{i+1,\ell}\bigr).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then `Delta_i>=0` and, by (3.2),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_i\ge \log W_{{\rm new},i}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus cumulative quotient-drop energy is a genuine monovariant. If we put

\[
\theta_i:=\frac{\log W_{{\rm new},i}}{\log W},
\]

Theorem 2.2 becomes the exact placement tradeoff

\[
\boxed{
\theta_i\log W\le h_i\log n_{i+1},
\qquad
(1-\theta_i)\log W
\le (2m_i-1)\log(D_i+m_i-1).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

So a transition can avoid a large displacement only by moving a large fraction of the rough log-mass into newly opened slots, and every such move consumes quotient-drop energy.

### Why this monovariant does not close the source

Summing (3.3) telescopes componentwise:

\[
\sum_i\Delta_i
=
\sum_{\ell>M}(\log Z_\ell)
\bigl(q_{1,\ell}-q_{R,\ell}\bigr).
\tag{3.6}
\]

But the initial quotients `q_{1,ell}` can be enormous in precisely the ultraflat regime. There is no source-valid bound of order `O(log W)` or even a fixed power of `M` on the right side of (3.6). The same component may therefore pay for many strict quotient drops before its budget is exhausted.

I also tested a Shannon-entropy variant. For a row `(n,m)`, assign each rough component to its residue slot and define normalized bin masses

\[
b_{m,j}:=
\frac{1}{\log W}
\sum_{\substack{\ell>M\\r_{m,\ell}=j}}
\log Z_\ell,
\qquad 0\le j<m.
\tag{3.7}
\]

These bin masses are nearly uniform in the deep edge (proved precisely in Section 4), so their entropy is asymptotically maximal. However, the partitions for successive rows are not refinements of one another: prime-power atoms can jump between slots after quotient drops. Hence the entropy is **not monotone across represented rows**. High entropy is a static description, not a consumable resource.

**B13 outcome.** The quotient vector provides a real monotone state, and (3.5) converts it into a placement/displacement tradeoff. The failure is equally precise: its available budget is uncontrolled because the quotients can start huge. A successful monovariant would need a bounded *renormalized* quotient budget or another quantity that charges repeated repartitioning independently of absolute row height.

## 4. B7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: the diffuse profile is too flexible by itself

The previous report showed that an exponent-one extremizing sequence must have

\[
\log Y_{\max}=M^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The present run asks whether the normalized rough prime-power mass profile itself has an impossible limit.

### 4.1 Atom masses become diffuse

Normalize every rough component by

\[
x_\ell:=\frac{\log Z_\ell}{\log W},
\qquad
\sum_{\ell>M}x_\ell=1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Along an exponent-one extremizing sequence, `N(t)<=2M+2` forces

\[
M=L^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Together with (4.1) and `log W=(1-o(1))L`, this gives

\[
\boxed{
\max_{\ell>M}x_\ell
\le\frac{\log Y_{\max}}{\log W}
=M^{-1+o(1)}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus every exponent-one extremizing profile is necessarily **diffuse**: no prime-power atom retains positive normalized log mass.

### 4.2 Every represented row is an asymptotic equipartition of that same diffuse mass

For a represented row in the quadratic edge, let

\[
A_{m,j}:=
\prod_{\substack{\ell>M\\ r_{m,\ell}=j}}Z_\ell,
\qquad
b_{m,j}:=\frac{\log A_{m,j}}{\log W}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then `A_{m,j}|n-j`, so

\[
0\le b_{m,j}\le c_{m,j}:=\frac{\log(n-j)}{\log W},
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}b_{m,j}=1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{m,j}
=
\frac{\log (n)_m}{\log W}
=
\frac{L+\log(m!)}{\log W}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

In the remaining edge `m log m=o(L)` and `log W=(1-o(1))L`, so

\[
\sum_j(c_{m,j}-b_{m,j})=o(1).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Because `n>=2m^2`, uniformly in `0<=j<m`,

\[
\log(n-j)=\log n+O(m/n),
\]

and summing this over the block together with

\[
\log(n)_m=L+\log(m!)
\]

shows

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left|c_{m,j}-\frac1m\right|=o(1).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Combining (4.8) and (4.9),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left|b_{m,j}-\frac1m\right|=o(1).
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus each represented deep-edge row repartitions essentially the **same diffuse probability mass** `(x_ell)` into `m` bins that are asymptotically equal in total log mass.

### 4.3 The limiting mass problem has trivial countermodels

This limiting statement by itself cannot prove Singmaster. An atomless probability space admits an exact partition into `m` equal-measure pieces for **every** positive integer `m`. Therefore after throwing away the arithmetic labels of the prime-power atoms and retaining only their normalized log masses, the limiting object permits arbitrarily many simultaneous equipartitions.

Equivalently, the entropy of the bin vector in (4.10) satisfies

\[
H(b_m)=(1-o(1))\log m,
\]

but maximal entropy at many different `m` is not contradictory. The partitions need not refine each other, and the atomless limit has enough flexibility to realize all of them.

**B7 outcome.** The only surviving extremal profile is diffuse, but the diffuse continuum limit is **too flexible**. A proof cannot be based solely on prime-power log-mass distribution, support size, or partition entropy. It must retain the arithmetic labels/residue placements of the discrete atoms, or couple those placements to the Archimedean inverse-binomial trajectory. This is a useful negative result: it explains exactly why the B3 profile strengthening needs the transition arithmetic of Theorem 2.2 rather than a mass-only compactness argument.
