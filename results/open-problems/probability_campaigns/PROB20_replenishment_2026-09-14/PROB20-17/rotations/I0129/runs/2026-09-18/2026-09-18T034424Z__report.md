## 2. Preserved rough transition theorem

Put `L=log t`, let `M` be the largest represented nontrivial left-half lower index, and
\[
W=W_M(t):=\prod_{p\mid t,\ p>M}p^{v_p(t)}.
\]
The preceding report proved: if
\[
\binom nm=\binom{n'}{m'}=t,\quad 2\le m<m'\le M,\quad n'\ge2(m')^2,
\]
with `D=n-n'>0`, `h=m'-m`, then `D>=m'` and
\[
\boxed{W\le(n')^h(D+m-1)^{2m-1}.}\tag{2.1}
\]
Hence when `W>(n')^h`,
\[
\boxed{D+m-1\ge\exp\!\left(\frac{\log W-h\log n'}{2m-1}\right).}\tag{2.2}
\]
In the surviving deep-edge regime `log W=(1-o(1))L`.

## 3. B11 — exact inverse-binomial transition ceiling

### Theorem 3.1
Suppose
\[
\binom{N+D}{m}=\binom N{m+h}=t,\qquad m,h,D\ge1,\ N\ge m+h.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{m\log\frac{N+D+1}{N+1}\le h\log\frac{N-m}{m+1}}\tag{3.1}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{D\le U(N,m,h):=(N+1)\left[\left(\frac{N-m}{m+1}\right)^{h/m}-1\right].}\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
Divide the common value by `C(N,m)`. Exactly,
\[
\frac{\binom{N+D}{m}}{\binom Nm}=\prod_{k=1}^D\frac{N+k}{N-m+k},\qquad
\frac{\binom N{m+h}}{\binom Nm}=\prod_{j=1}^h\frac{N-m-j+1}{m+j}.\tag{3.3}
\]
For the first product,
\[
\log\frac{N+k}{N-m+k}=\log\left(1+\frac m{N-m+k}\right)\ge\frac m{N+k},
\]
using `log(1+x)>=x/(1+x)`. Thus
\[
\log\frac{\binom{N+D}{m}}{\binom Nm}
\ge m\sum_{k=1}^D\frac1{N+k}
\ge m\log\frac{N+D+1}{N+1}.\tag{3.4}
\]
For the second product each factor is at most `(N-m)/(m+1)`, so
\[
\log\frac{\binom N{m+h}}{\binom Nm}\le h\log\frac{N-m}{m+1}.\tag{3.5}
\]
The ratios in (3.3) are equal, proving (3.1). Exponentiating gives (3.2). `QED`

This is an exact elementary upper description of a transition on the real inverse-binomial trajectory; no Gamma interpolation error term is needed.

## 4. B8 — deterministic rough/Archimedean pruning

Set `N=n'` and `m'=m+h` in (2.1). Under `N>=2(m+h)^2`, Theorem 3.1 and (2.2) imply the necessary interval

\boxed{\left(\frac W{N^h}\right)^{1/(2m-1)}\le D+m-1\le U(N,m,h)+m-1}\tag{4.1}

whenever `W>N^h`. Equivalently,

\boxed{W\le N^h[U(N,m,h)+m-1]^{2m-1}.}\tag{4.2}

This is the requested deterministic transition-pruning inequality.

## 5. Exact diagnosis: pairwise curvature loses by a factor two

For close deep-edge transitions `h=o(m)` with `m log m/L ->0` and `log W=(1-o(1))L`, the rough theorem gives on its effective range
\[
\log(D+m)\ge\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)\frac Lm.\tag{5.1}
\]
The exact ceiling is
\[
D\le(N+1)\left[\exp\!\left(\frac hm\log\frac{N-m}{m+1}\right)-1\right].\tag{5.2}
\]
The inverse-binomial row itself lies on `log N=(1+o(1))L/m` up to the standard lower-order `log m` contribution. Therefore the pairwise real geometry naturally permits gaps on essentially the `exp(L/m)` row scale, whereas (5.1) only forces a square-root scale `exp(L/(2m))`.

The factor two is not coming from a sloppy real estimate. It is already present in the arithmetic proof through the `2m-1` possible old-slot differences

## 6. B28 — typical transitions and global telescoping

Order represented deep-edge left-half rows by
\[
m_1<\cdots<m_R\le M,\qquad n_1>\cdots>n_R,
\]
and write `h_i=m_{i+1}-m_i`, `D_i=n_i-n_{i+1}`.

### Lemma 6.1 — every rough quotient strictly drops
Fix a rough component `Z=p^{v_p(t)}` with `p>M`. For each row there is a unique residue `0<=r_i<m_i` with
\[
n_i=Zq_i+r_i.
\]
The preceding report gives `D_i>=m_{i+1}`. Since
\[
r_i-r_{i+1}\le m_i-1<m_{i+1}\le D_i,
\]
we have
\[
Z(q_i-q_{i+1})=D_i-(r_i-r_{i+1})>0.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{q_i-q_{i+1}\ge1}\tag{6.1}
\]
for **every** rough component at **every** deep-edge transition, and so `q_1-q_R>=R-1`. This strengthens the previous new-slot-only monovariant, but its initial budget `q_1~n_1/Z` can be enormous.

### Lemma 6.2 — the new-slot cost telescopes
Multiplying (2.1) over the chain gives
\[
(R-1)\log W\le\sum_i h_i\log n_{i+1}+\sum_i(2m_i-1)\log(D_i+m_i-1).\tag{6.2}
\]
Since `C(n,m)>=(n/m)^m`, any represented row obeys
\[
n\le m t^{1/m},\qquad \log n\le L/m+\log m.\tag{6.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_i h_i\log n_{i+1}\le L\sum_i\frac{h_i}{m_{i+1}}+\sum_i h_i\log m_{i+1}.\tag{6.4}
\]
But
\[
\frac{h_i}{m_{i+1}}=1-\frac{m_i}{m_{i+1}}\le\log\frac{m_{i+1}}{m_i},
\]
so
\[
\sum_i\frac{h_i}{m_{i+1}}\le\log\frac M{m_1},
\]
and `sum h_i=M-m_1`. Hence
\[
\boxed{\sum_i h_i\log n_{i+1}\le L\log\frac M{m_1}+(M-m_1)\log M.}\tag{6.5}
\]
Thus all new-slot costs telescope globally.

The previous report already showed that only `O_epsilon(log M)` transitions can have `m_i/m_{i+1}<epsilon`. Deleting those coarse jumps and using (6.5) still leaves the second sum of (6.2), whose coefficient is `2m_i-1`. Plugging the exact B11 ceiling into typical transitions leaves a leading capacity of about `2L` per transition against only about `L` per transition on the left. So coarse jumps are not the source of the obstruction. The old-slot factor two survives on the typical transitions.
