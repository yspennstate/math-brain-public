\[

n_i=Z_pq_{i,p}+r_{i,p},\qquad 0\le r_{i,p}<m_i\le M.

## 3. B21 — neighboring shape: the annihilator problem is exactly Prouhet–Tarry–Escott

### 3.1 Moment annihilation equals a high-order zero at 1

Let `x_1,...,x_s` be distinct nonnegative integer nodes and let `c_1,...,c_s` be integers. Define

\[
F(z):=\sum_{i=1}^{s} c_i z^{x_i}.
\]

For an integer `k>=1`, the following are equivalent:

\[
\sum_i c_i x_i^r=0\qquad(0\le r<k),
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
(z-1)^k\mid F(z)\quad\hbox{in }\mathbf Z[z].
\tag{3.2}
\]

Indeed, `F^(r)(1)=sum_i c_i (x_i)_r`, and the falling factorials `(x)_0,...,(x)_{k-1}` and ordinary powers `1,x,...,x^{k-1}` are related by an integral triangular change of basis. Thus (3.1) is equivalent to `F^(r)(1)=0` for `r<k`, which is exactly (3.2).

This translation is important because it shows that the desired low-height irregular-node annihilator is not a new isolated linear-algebra problem. It is a classical constrained-polynomial / Prouhet–Tarry–Escott shape.

### 3.2 Literature barrier, not a black-box solution

A fresh 2026-09-18 literature check found the standard formulation in the constrained-polynomial literature: the classical Prouhet–Tarry–Escott problem can be rephrased as asking for the maximal order of vanishing at `1` of a nonzero integer polynomial with prescribed `l1` coefficient norm `2n`; the familiar extremal conjecture is that this order is at most `n`. See P. Borwein–C. Ingalls, *The Prouhet, Tarry, Escott Problem* (Enseign. Math. 40 (1994), 3–27), the surveys of T. Erdélyi on constrained polynomials, and the 2019 PTE review. Recent work such as Coppersmith–Mossinghoff–Scheinerman–VanderKam, arXiv:2304.11254, continues to search for ideal PTE solutions.

So a general theorem saying “high moment order forces very large coefficient `l1` norm” would run directly into a difficult neighboring problem. In particular, the hoped-for superlinear coefficient-norm obstruction is not something this track may assume. Even the conjectural PTE scale is only linear: order `k` corresponds naturally to `l1` norm about `2k`.

Bombieri–Vaaler’s Siegel-lemma refinement is also the correct nearby geometry-of-numbers language for an underdetermined moment matrix: small integer null vectors are controlled by a determinant divided by the gcd of maximal minors. That gcd term is not cosmetic here; the exact one-dimensional computation below shows it can cancel most of the Vandermonde size.

**B21 outcome:** the neighboring machinery clarifies the route but does not solve it. The missing statement is not a routine Siegel-lemma bound; it needs information special to the represented inverse-binomial nodes.

## 4. B24 — extremal object: exact primitive annihilator on `k+1` nodes

This part can be solved completely.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact Vandermonde-cofactor formula

Let

\[
x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_k
\]

be distinct integers. Consider the integer solutions `c=(c_0,...,c_k)` of

\[
\sum_{i=0}^k c_i x_i^r=0,
\qquad 0\le r<k.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Put

\[
P(X)=\prod_{j=0}^k(X-x_j),
\qquad
D_i:=|P'(x_i)|=\prod_{j\ne i}|x_i-x_j|,
\]

and

\[
\Lambda:=\operatorname{lcm}(D_0,\ldots,D_k).
\]

Then the integer kernel of (4.1) has rank one, and its primitive generator is, up to overall sign,

\[
\boxed{
 c_i=\frac{\Lambda}{P'(x_i)}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently its exact primitive `l1` height is

\[
\boxed{
A(x_0,\ldots,x_k)
=\Lambda\sum_{i=0}^k\frac1{D_i}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

The `k x (k+1)` moment matrix `(x_i^r)_{0<=r<k}` has rank `k` by the Vandermonde determinant, so its kernel is one-dimensional. For every polynomial `f` of degree at most `k-1`, Lagrange interpolation gives

\[
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^k f(x_i)\frac{P(X)}{(X-x_i)P'(x_i)}.
\]

Comparing the coefficient of `X^k` gives

\[
\sum_{i=0}^k\frac{f(x_i)}{P'(x_i)}=0.
\]

Taking `f(X)=X^r`, `0<=r<k`, proves that `(1/P'(x_i))_i` spans the rational kernel. Multiplying by `Lambda` makes every entry integral. It is primitive because

\[
\gcd_i\frac{\Lambda}{D_i}=1:
\]

for every prime `ell`, some `D_i` attains the maximal `ell`-adic valuation occurring in the lcm, so `Lambda/D_i` is not divisible by `ell`. This proves (4.2) and (4.3). `QED`

Equivalently, if `Delta=prod_{a<b}(x_b-x_a)` is the full Vandermonde product and `d_i` is the signed maximal minor obtained by deleting column `i`, then the primitive vector is `(d_i/g)_i`, where `g=gcd_i d_i`. Formula (4.2) is the same statement after using `d_i=±Delta/D_i` and `g=Delta/Lambda`. Thus the decisive arithmetic quantity is the **gcd of the Vandermonde cofactors**, exactly the feature suggested by Bombieri–Vaaler.

### 4.2 Literal arithmetic progressions are not coefficient-height extremizers

For an arithmetic progression `x_i=a+di`, (4.2) gives the usual binomial vector

\[
c_i=(-1)^{k-i}\binom{k}{i},
\qquad
A=2^k.
\]

But this is not minimal among irregular integer nodes. Already for `k=3`, the nodes

\[
(0,1,3,4)
\]

have

\[
(D_0,D_1,D_2,D_3)=(12,6,6,12),\qquad \Lambda=12,
\]

so the primitive relation is

\[
(1,-2,2,-1),
\qquad A=6<8.
\]

In fact `6` is the exact minimum for four distinct integer nodes annihilating moments `0,1,2`. Every primitive kernel vector has four nonzero alternating-sign integer entries. Because its entries sum to zero, its `l1` norm is even and at least `4`. Equality `A=4` would force all four absolute values to be one; then the first-moment relation would be, up to sign,

\[
x_0-x_1+x_2-x_3=0,
\]

which is impossible because both `x_0-x_1` and `x_2-x_3` are strictly negative. Hence `A>=6`, and the displayed example attains it.

Thus a literal-AP extremal conjecture is false even in the first nontrivial cubic-moment case. The improvement is exactly the sparse polynomial

\[
1-2z+2z^3-z^4=-(z-1)^3(z+1),
\]

which is consistent with the neighboring constrained-polynomial literature.

### 4.3 A sharper decomposition: analytic band entry versus arithmetic mass retention

There is an important cancellation that was not explicit in the previous run. Let `f_t(x)` denote the smooth left-half inverse-binomial row function, so that `f_t(m_i)=n_i` on represented lower indices. For the primitive `k+1`-node vector (4.2),

\[
X:=\sum_i c_i n_i
=\Lambda\sum_i\frac{f_t(x_i)}{P'(x_i)}
=\Lambda\,f_t[x_0,\ldots,x_k],
\tag{4.4}
\]

where the last term is the exact divided difference. Meanwhile

\[
A=\Lambda\sum_i\frac1{D_i}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore the condition that the row form enter its natural local residue band (replace `M` by `u=max x_i` in the residue estimate)

\[
|X|\le\frac{A}{2}(u-1)
\]

is **equivalent** to

\[
\boxed{
|f_t[x_0,\ldots,x_k]|
\le
\frac{u-1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^k\frac1{D_i}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The lcm `Lambda` cancels completely from the band-entry test. It reappears only after entry, because the collective rough-core theorem loses at most

\[
A(u-1)\log(A(u-1))
\]

of logarithmic rough mass.

This separates the obstruction cleanly:

- **analytic entry:** a normalized divided-difference inequality (4.6), depending on node geometry but not primitive scaling;
- **arithmetic retention:** once inside, the primitive `l1` height (4.3) must be small enough that the rough-core loss is nonvacuous.

So “find a low-height null vector” by itself was too coarse a description of the live obligation.

**B24 outcome:** exact primitive height solved for `k+1` nodes; AP extremality refuted; the correct arithmetic statistic is the gcd/lcm profile of Vandermonde cofactors; and height is shown to govern retention, not band entry.

## 5. B10 — derandomize: a deterministic irregular-node Prouhet extraction, and its exact entropy failure

A natural attempt is to force a signed moment relation directly from a long represented set by pigeonhole/discrepancy. This can be done exactly, but the row-value coordinate costs too much entropy.

### Theorem 5.1 — subset-signature annihilator

Let

\[
(m_i,n_i),\qquad 1\le i\le s,
\]

be `s` points with distinct integer lower indices

\[
1\le m_i\le u
\]

and row indices

\[
0\le n_i\le N.
\]

Fix an integer `k>=2` and a bin width `R>=1`. Define

\[
Q(s,u,N,k,R)
:=(s+1)
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{sN}{R}\right\rfloor+1\right)
\prod_{r=1}^{k-1}(su^r+1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

If

\[
2^s>Q(s,u,N,k,R),
\tag{5.2}
\]

then there is a nonzero vector

\[
c=(c_1,\ldots,c_s)\in\{-1,0,1\}^s
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_i c_i m_i^r=0\quad(0\le r<k)
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\left|\sum_i c_i n_i\right|<R.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

#### Proof

For each subset `S subset {1,...,s}`, record the signature

\[
\Phi(S)=
\left(
|S|,
\sum_{i\in S}m_i,
\ldots,
\sum_{i\in S}m_i^{k-1},
\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i\in S}n_i}{R}\right\rfloor
\right).
\]

There are at most `s+1` choices for the first coordinate, at most `su^r+1` for the `r`th moment coordinate, and at most `floor(sN/R)+1` for the last coordinate. Thus the number of signatures is at most (5.1). Under (5.2), two distinct subsets `S,T` have the same signature. Set

\[
c_i=1_S(i)-1_T(i).
\]

Equal subset cardinalities give the `r=0` equation; the moment coordinates give the remaining equations in (5.3); and equality of the final floor bin gives (5.4). `QED`

Because the `m_i` are distinct and `k>=2`, the resulting vector cannot have exactly one `+1` and one `-1`: that would make the first-moment equality force the same lower index. Hence

\[
A:=\|c\|_1\ge4.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Taking `R=u-1` for `u>=2`, (5.4) lies strictly inside the local residue band

\[
B=\frac A2(u-1)\ge2(u-1).
\]

Therefore Theorem 3.1 of the previous run applies to this completely irregular-node relation. The bad rough logarithmic mass is bounded by

\[
\log\frac{W}{W_0}
\le
A(u-1)\log(A(u-1))
\le
s u\log(su).
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is a genuine deterministic extraction, requiring no AP assumption.

### 5.2 Why this blind extraction does not move the source metric

The obstruction is visible directly in the same count. Condition (5.2) in particular implies

\[
2^s>rac{sN}{R},
\]

up to harmless integer endpoints, so any guaranteed application satisfies

\[
s\log 2\gtrsim\log(N/R).
\tag{5.7}
\]

In a close edge block with all lower indices comparable to `u`, the elementary binomial bounds

\[
\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^m
\le \binom nm
\le
\left(\frac{en}{m}\right)^m
\]

show that represented row indices satisfy

\[
\log N=\frac{L}{u}+O(\log u)
\tag{5.8}
\]

at this scale. In the surviving extremal regime `u log u=o(L)`, and for `R asymp u`, (5.7) therefore places the blind subset collision on the scale

\[
s\gtrsim \frac{L}{u}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

But the only coefficient-height guarantee supplied by this construction is `A<=s`. Feeding that guarantee into (5.6) gives an upper bound of scale

\[
su\log(su),
\]

which is already at least of order `L log(su)` when `s` is on the collision scale (5.9). Thus the collective relation-collapse estimate is **vacuous as a certification of surviving rough mass** at the point where the blind pigeonhole principle is guaranteed to produce a row-small relation.

This is not a proof that every collision has large support — an unusually low-support collision could still be useful. It is the exact failure of the generic derandomization: it pays the full entropy of the inverse-binomial row coordinate and gives no simultaneous control of the support/`l1` norm of the colliding subsets.

Adding more moment coordinates only increases `Q`; using a code to force large symmetric difference can enlarge the admissible row bin but simultaneously enlarges `A` and therefore worsens the rough-core retention budget. So the missing object is specifically a **low-support additive collision aligned with the inverse-binomial row values**, not an arbitrary Prouhet relation.
