## 4.1 Hypergeometric tilt of the squared-binomial selector

Let
\[
B_N:=\sum_{u=0}^Nw_u=\binom{2N}{N}.
\]
Introduce the one-parameter tilted Stieltjes numerator
\[
P_N(x;\theta)
:=D_N(x)\sum_{u=0}^N\frac{w_u e^{\theta u}}{x-u}
\]
and the tilted selector
\[
H_{m,\theta}(x)
:=\frac{m^2}{(m!)^2}D_N(x)P_N(x;\theta).
\]
At `theta=0` this is exactly `H_m`.

### Theorem 4.1 — normalized curvature has nonzero tilt derivative at every root

For every root `r=0,...,N`, write
\[
H_{m,\theta}(r+z)
=e^{\theta r}z+e^{\theta r}c_{m,r}(\theta)z^2+O(z^3).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 c_{m,r}(\theta)
 =2(H_r-H_{N-r})
 +\frac1{w_r}\sum_{u\ne r}
 \frac{w_u e^{\theta(u-r)}}{r-u}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
In particular
\[
\boxed{
 c_{m,r}'(0)=1-\frac{B_N}{w_r}\ne0,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
 c_{m,r}''(0)=\left(r-\frac N2\right)\frac{B_N}{w_r}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Equivalently the Wronskian of the root slope `s_r(theta)=e^{theta r}` and quadratic coefficient `q_r(theta)=e^{theta r}c_{m,r}(theta)` is
\[
s_rq_r'-q_rs_r'=e^{2\theta r}c_{m,r}'(\theta),
\]
so at `theta=0` it is nonzero at every root.

**Proof.** Put `C=m^2/(m!)^2`. At a root `r`,
\[
D_N'(r)^2w_r=(N!)^2,
\qquad C(N!)^2=1.
\]
Since
\[
P_N(r;\theta)=w_re^{\theta r}D_N'(r),
\]
one obtains `H'_{m,theta}(r)=e^{theta r}`. As in the untitled calculation,
\[
\frac{H_{m,\theta}''(r)}{2H_{m,\theta}'(r)}
=H_r-H_{N-r}+\frac{P_N'(r;\theta)}{P_N(r;\theta)}.
\]
Expanding `P_N` at the simple node gives
\[
\frac{P_N'(r;\theta)}{P_N(r;\theta)}
=H_r-H_{N-r}
+\frac1{w_r}\sum_{u\ne r}
\frac{w_ue^{\theta(u-r)}}{r-u},
\]
which is (4.1). Differentiating,
\[
c_{m,r}'(0)
=-\frac1{w_r}\sum_{u\ne r}w_u
=1-\frac{B_N}{w_r},
\]
proving (4.2). A second derivative gives
\[
c_{m,r}''(0)
=-\frac1{w_r}\sum_u w_u(u-r).
\]
Symmetry `w_u=w_{N-u}` implies `sum u w_u=(N/2)B_N`, giving (4.3). QED.

This proves genuine local transversality: the second coefficient is not a frozen artifact of the unweighted selector.

# 5. B5 payoff — an exact discrete Stein/contiguity law

The squared-binomial weights satisfy the exact Pearson relation
\[
(u+1)^2w_{u+1}=(N-u)^2w_u.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let
\[
S_N(x)=\sum_{u=0}^N\frac{w_u}{x-u},
\qquad P_N(x)=D_N(x)S_N(x).
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — Stieltjes contiguity identity

Exactly as rational functions,
\[
\boxed{
 x^2S_N(x)-(N+1-x)^2S_N(x-1)=(2N+1)B_N.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Equivalently, as polynomials,
\[
\boxed{
 xP_N(x)+(N+1-x)P_N(x-1)
 =(2N+1)B_N\prod_{u=1}^N(x-u).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

**Proof.** Expand
\[
\frac{x^2}{x-u}=(x-u)+2u+\frac{u^2}{x-u}.
\]
With `v=u+1`, similarly
\[
\frac{(N+1-x)^2}{x-1-u}
=(x-v)-2(N-u)+\frac{(N-u)^2}{x-v}.
\]
After shifting the singular part of the second sum by `v=u+1`, (5.1) identifies its residue at `x=v` with `v^2w_v`, exactly the residue from the first sum. All poles cancel. The remaining affine pieces differ by `(2N+1)sum w_u=(2N+1)B_N`, proving (5.2). Since
\[
D_N(x)=x\prod_{u=1}^N(x-u),
\quad
D_N(x-1)=-(N+1-x)\prod_{u=1}^N(x-u),
\]
clearing denominators gives (5.3). QED.

### Theorem 5.2 — exact adjacent-gap law for the second coefficient

For every `1<=r<=N`,
\[
\boxed{
 c_{m,r}-c_{m,r-1}
 =\frac{(2N+1)B_N}{r^2w_r}
 =\frac{(2m-1)\binom{2m-2}{m-1}}
 {r^2\binom{m-1}{r}^2}>0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
Moreover, for every clean prime `p>4m+8`, the right side is a `p`-adic unit. Hence
\[
\boxed{c_{m,r}\not\equiv c_{m,r-1}\pmod p}
\tag{5.5}
\]
for all adjacent root indices.

**Proof.** Differentiate (5.3) and evaluate at `x=r`, `1<=r<=N`. Since the right side of (5.3) vanishes at `r`, the undifferentiated identity gives
\[
rP_N(r)+(N+1-r)P_N(r-1)=0.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Let `alpha_r=P_N'(r)/P_N(r)`. Dividing the differentiated identity by `rP_N(r)` and using (5.6) gives
\[
\alpha_r-\alpha_{r-1}
+\frac1r+\frac1{N+1-r}
=\frac{(2N+1)B_NR'(r)}{rP_N(r)},
\]
where `R(x)=prod_{u=1}^N(x-u)`. Now
\[
P_N(r)=w_rD_N'(r)=w_rrR'(r),
\]
so the right side is `(2N+1)B_N/(r^2w_r)`. Finally
\[
(H_r-H_{N-r})-(H_{r-1}-H_{N-r+1})
=\frac1r+\frac1{N+1-r},
\]
and `c_{m,r}=H_r-H_{N-r}+alpha_r`, giving (5.4).

For a clean prime, `p>4m+8>2N+1`; all prime factors of `B_N=binom(2N,N)` and of `r^2w_r` are `<p`, so both numerator and denominator of (5.4) are `p`-units. QED.

This is considerably more rigid than the previous sign argument: the full second-coefficient sequence is governed by an explicit first-order hypergeometric forcing law.

b_{h,r}:=c_{m,r}\kappa_{m+h,r}^2,

C_m:=m^2(2m-1)\binom{2m-2}{m-1}.

Using

r^2\kappa_{m+h,r}^2=(m+h-r)^2\kappa_{m+h,r-1}^2,

\boxed{
 r^2b_{h,r}-(m+h-r)^2b_{h,r-1}

\tag{5.7}

Every factor on the right is a clean-prime unit. In the base row `h=0`, this simplifies to the constant-forcing law

# 7. B26 — HUNT THE SPORADIC CASE

The exact gap law (5.4) and the preserved antisymmetry now classify every exact one-row symmetry of the second coefficient.

### Theorem 7.1 — exact zero/equality/opposite-value classification

The sequence
\[
r\mapsto c_{m,r},\qquad 0\le r\le N,
\]
is strictly increasing over the rationals and satisfies
\[
c_{m,N-r}=-c_{m,r}.
\]
Consequently:

1. `c_{m,r}=c_{m,s}` iff `r=s`;
2. `c_{m,r}=-c_{m,s}` iff `s=N-r`;
3. `c_{m,r}=0` iff `N` is even and `r=N/2`.

**Proof.** Strict increase is (5.4). Antisymmetry was proved in the previous report. If two values are equal, strict increase gives equal indices. If `c_s=-c_r=c_{N-r}`, injectivity gives `s=N-r`. \sum_{Z:r_h=q}\log Z\le\log|n_h-q|=d_0m+O(\log m).
\tag{7.1}

There are at most four central-row events.

For a fixed unordered row pair `{h,j}`, every component with `r_h+r_j=N` divides

\le\log|n_h+n_j-N|=d_0m+O(\log m).
\tag{7.2}

There are six row pairs. Summing finitely many `O(m)` bounds proves the theorem. QED.
