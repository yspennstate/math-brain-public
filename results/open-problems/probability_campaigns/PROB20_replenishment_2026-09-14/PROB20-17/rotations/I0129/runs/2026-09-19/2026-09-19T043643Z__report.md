# 5. B29 — add the point at infinity: fixed-offset strands have bounded algebraic degree and no exceptional infinity component

The transfer correspondence looks high-degree only because its residue offsets are allowed to move with `m`. On each **fixed offset strand** it is actually a bounded-degree one-variable rational equation. This is an exact statement.

Fix integers
\[
c_1,c_2,c_4
\]
with
\[
c_4=X+c_1+c_2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let the row residues be parametrized by a base residue `r`:
\[
r_0=r,
\qquad r_1=r+c_1,
\qquad r_2=r+c_2,
\qquad r_4=r+c_4.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Equation (5.1) is exactly the residue-defect law
\[
r_0-r_1-r_2+r_4=X.
\]
For legal residues define
\[
\Phi_{m,\mathbf c}(r)
:=
\kappa_{m,r}
-\kappa_{m+1,r+c_1}
-\kappa_{m+2,r+c_2}
+\kappa_{m+4,r+c_4}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Every clean component whose offset vector equals `c` has base prime `p>4m+8` and must satisfy
\[
\Phi_{m,\mathbf c}(r)\equiv0\pmod p.
\tag{5.4}
\]

## Theorem 5.1 — fixed-offset strand root bound

For `h in {1,2,4}` and an integer offset `c`, put
\[
d_{h,c}:=\max(c,0)+\max(h-c,0).
\tag{5.5}
\]
For every fixed legal offset triple `(c_1,c_2,c_4)`, after clearing denominators in (5.3) one obtains a nonzero polynomial in `r` of degree at most
\[
D(\mathbf c)
:=d_{1,c_1}+d_{2,c_2}+d_{4,c_4}
\le 7+|c_1|+|c_2|+|c_4|.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Its leading coefficient is a unit modulo every prime `p>4m+8`. Hence (5.4) has at most `D(c)` legal base residues modulo `p`.

In particular, if all offsets have absolute value at most a fixed `C`, then the local transfer on that strand has at most
\[
7+3C
\tag{5.7}
\]
legal base residues for every clean prime.

### Proof

From (3.7), for `h>0`,
\[
\frac{\kappa_{m+h,r+c}}{\kappa_{m,r}}
=
(-1)^{h-c}
\frac{(m+h)!}{m!}
\frac{r!}{(r+c)!}
\frac{(m-1-r)!}{(m+h-1-r-c)!}.
\tag{5.8}
\]
This is an exact rational function of `r`.

If `c>=0`, the first factorial ratio in `r` contributes denominator
\[
(r+1)(r+2)\cdots(r+c),
\]
of degree `c`; if `c<0`, it contributes instead a numerator of degree `-c`. Likewise, if `h-c>=0`, the last ratio contributes a denominator of degree `h-c`; if `h-c<0`, it contributes a numerator of degree `c-h`. Therefore the denominator degree of (5.8) is exactly
\[
\max(c,0)+\max(h-c,0)=d_{h,c}.
\]
This proves (5.5), and the elementary case split `c<0`, `0<=c<=h`, `c>h` gives
\[
d_{h,c}\le h+|c|.
\]
Summing over `h=1,2,4` gives (5.6) for the product common denominator `Q_{m,c}(r)`.

More importantly, the numerator/denominator degree difference in (5.8) is always `-h`. Thus, as a rational function at infinity,
\[
\frac{\kappa_{m+h,r+c}}{\kappa_{m,r}}
=O(r^{-h}).
\tag{5.9}
\]
Divide (5.3) by `kappa_{m,r}`. Equations (5.8)--(5.9) give
\[
\frac{\Phi_{m,\mathbf c}(r)}{\kappa_{m,r}}
=1+O(r^{-1}).
\tag{5.10}
\]
Consequently, after multiplication by `Q_{m,c}(r)`, the cleared numerator polynomial has the **same degree and same leading coefficient** as `Q_{m,c}`. Choosing the linear denominator factors with their natural monic signs, that leading coefficient is `+1` or `-1`; in particular it is never zero modulo `p`.

Finally, for a legal residue and `p>4m+8`, every denominator factor appearing above is a nonzero integer of absolute value less than `p`, hence is invertible modulo `p`. Therefore clearing denominators is legitimate and a nonzero degree-`D(c)` polynomial has at most `D(c)` roots in `F_p`. All legal base residues lie below `m<p` and are distinct modulo `p`. This proves the theorem. ∎

So bounded branching is completely true on every bounded-offset strand. The obstruction can only come from offset vectors whose size grows with `m`.

\[

\tag{6.1}

\[
\boxed{

\tag{6.2}

### Proof
