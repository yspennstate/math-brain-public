## 2. Exact residue decomposition for the largest prime factor

Work on the left half `2<=m<=n/2`; the `m=1` occurrence and symmetry will be restored at the end.

Fix a representation

\[
t={n\choose m}.
\]

Let

\[
p=P(t),\qquad a=v_p(t),\qquad Y=p^a,\qquad T=t/Y,\qquad L=\log t.
\]

The classical Sylvester–Schur theorem applied to the numerator block of length `m` gives a prime divisor of the binomial coefficient exceeding `m`; hence

\[
\boxed{p>m.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because `p>m`, the denominator `m!` is a `p`-adic unit. For each `j>=1`, the block `(n-m,n]` contains at most one multiple of `p^j`, and

\[
v_p\!\left({n\choose m}\right)
=\sum_{j\ge1}
\left(
\left\lfloor{n\over p^j}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor{n-m\over p^j}\right\rfloor
\right).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Each summand is `0` or `1`; it equals `1` exactly when `n mod p^j < m`. These indicators are nested: if the condition holds at `p^{j+1}`, then the residue modulo `p^j` is the same small residue and the condition holds there too. Since the sum equals `a`, the first `a` indicators are `1`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{r:=n\bmod Y<m.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Write uniquely

\[
\boxed{n=Yq+r,\qquad q\ge1,\qquad 0\le r<m.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

(The earlier prime-power profile argument gives `Y<=n`, hence `q>=1`.) Dividing the binomial identity by `Y` and reducing modulo `Y` yields the exact first-digit congruence already derived in the preceding track history:

\[
\boxed{
q\equiv(-1)^{m-1-r}T\,m{m-1\choose r}\pmod Y.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

The present question is not whether this congruence has residues. It is whether the extra freedom `0<r<m` can let many *different rows* share the same quotient `q` and thereby defeat the injection used in the `r=0` theorem.

## 3. B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: fixed-quotient uniqueness in the quadratic edge

### Theorem 3.1 — nonzero residues do not create quotient multiplicity once `n>=2m^2`

Suppose two left-half representations of the same `t` have decompositions

\[
n=Yq+r,\qquad n'=Yq+r',
\]

with the **same positive quotient `q`**, with

\[
0\le r<m,\qquad 0\le r'<m',
\]

and both satisfy the quadratic-edge condition

\[
n\ge2m^2,\qquad n'\ge2(m')^2.
\]

Then the two representations are identical. Equivalently, among all left-half representations in the quadratic edge, each quotient `q=floor(n/Y)` occurs at most once.

### Proof

Assume for contradiction that the representations are distinct. If `m=m'`, strict monotonicity of `{n\choose m}` in `n` forces `n=n'`, hence `r=r'`, contradiction. We may therefore suppose

\[
m<m'.
\tag{3.1}
\]

There are two residue-order cases.

**Case 1: `r'>=r`.** Then `n'>=n`. Because `m<m'<=n'/2`, the binomial coefficients in row `n'` are strictly increasing from index `m` through index `m'`, hence

\[
{n'\choose m'}>{n'\choose m}\ge {n\choose m}=t,
\]

contradicting `{n'\choose m'}=t`.

**Case 2: `r'<r`.** Since `0<=r<m`,

\[
1\le r-r'\le m-1,
\]

so

\[
n'=n-(r-r')\ge n-m+1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Also `m'>=m+1`. Since `m'<=n'/2`, row monotonicity gives

\[
{n'\choose m'}\ge {n'\choose m+1}
\ge {n-m+1\choose m+1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

It remains to compare the last quantity with `{n\choose m}`. An exact cancellation gives

\[
R:={ {n-m+1\choose m+1}\over {n\choose m}}
=
{n-m\over m+1}
\prod_{i=0}^{m-2}
{n-2m+1+i\over n-m+2+i}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Every factor in the product is at least `(n-2m+1)/n`. Thus

\[
R\ge {n-m\over m+1}
\left(1-{2m-1\over n}\right)^{m-1}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

By Bernoulli's inequality,

\[
\left(1-{2m-1\over n}\right)^{m-1}
\ge 1-{(m-1)(2m-1)\over n}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Using `n>=2m^2` in both factors,

\[
{n-m\over m+1}\ge {m(2m-1)\over m+1}
\tag{3.7}
\]

and

\[
1-{(m-1)(2m-1)\over n}
\ge {3m-1\over 2m^2}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
R\ge
{(2m-1)(3m-1)\over 2m(m+1)}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

The numerator minus the denominator in (3.9) equals

\[
4m^2-7m+1>0
\]

for every `m>=2`. Hence `R>1`. Equations (3.3) and (3.4) give

\[
{n'\choose m'}>{n\choose m}=t,
\]

again a contradiction. Both cases are impossible, so fixed-`q` uniqueness holds. QED.

### What B15 learned

The `r!=0` freedom is **not** by itself a many-row mechanism in the difficult large-row regime. Once `n>=2m^2`, moving the residue inside its entire legal interval cannot compensate for increasing the lower index while keeping the same quotient block `Yq`.

This changes the immediate obstruction materially: the task is no longer “control all nonzero residues separately.” The remaining issue is to control how many different quotient blocks `q` can occur when `Y` is extremely small on a logarithmic scale.

## 4. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: endpoint residue intervals

For fixed positive `q`, put `Q=Yq`. At lower index `m`, all legal residue rows lie in

\[
Q\le n=Q+r\le Q+m-1.
\]

Since `{n\choose m}` is increasing in `n`, the possible coefficient values lie in the interval

\[
I_{q,m}:=
\left[{Q\choose m},{Q+m-1\choose m}\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

The residue configuration most favorable to a collision between adjacent lower indices is therefore the **largest** lower residue `r=m-1` at level `m` against the **smallest** residue `r'=0` at level `m+1`.

Let

\[
n=Q+m-1.
\]

Then the upper endpoint at level `m` is `{n\choose m}`, while the lower endpoint at level `m+1` is

\[
{Q\choose m+1}={n-m+1\choose m+1}.
\]

The ratio computation (3.4) shows immediately that if

\[
Q+m-1\ge2m^2,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
{Q+m-1\choose m}<{Q\choose m+1}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus adjacent residue intervals are strictly disjoint in the quadratic regime:

\[
\boxed{I_{q,m}<I_{q,m+1}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The central residues are not extremal for collision; the endpoints are. Once the endpoint pair separates, *every* internal residue pair separates. This is the interval version of Theorem 3.1 and explains geometrically why residue freedom disappears from the count.

## 5. All-residue parameter theorem by gluing B15 to the interior theorem

The previous run's `r=0` bound can now be lifted to all residues in a source-valid statement.

Let

\[
H(t):=\#\{(n,m):{n\choose m}=t,\ 2\le m\le n/2\}
\]

be the nontrivial left-half count, and define

\[
\boxed{
m_0:=\max\left\{4,\left\lceil{L\over\log Y}\right\rceil\right\}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — all-residue modulus-scale bound

For all sufficiently large `t`,

\[
\boxed{H(t)\le2m_0+2,}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{N(t)\le4m_0+6.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently:

1. if `Y>=t^delta` for a fixed `delta>0`, then `N(t)=O_delta(1)`;
2. if `log Y >= (log t)^eta` for a fixed `0<eta<=1`, then
   \[
   N(t)=O\big((\log t)^{1-\eta}\big).
   \tag{5.4}
   \]

These are conditional structural consequences for the full source multiplicity, not an unconditional improvement of `rho_SM`.

### Proof

Partition the left-half representations counted by `H(t)` into

\[
\mathcal A:=\{(n,m):n\ge2m^2\}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal B:=\{(n,m):n<2m^2\}.
\]

#### Step A1: real row bound

As in the preceding run, each factor satisfies

\[
{n-i\over m-i}\ge {n\over m},
\]

so

\[
t={n\choose m}\ge(n/m)^m,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{n\le m e^{L/m}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For `m>=4`, left-halfness gives `n>=2m`, so

\[
t\ge{2m\choose m}>e^m,
\]

and therefore every represented `m>=4` satisfies

\[
m<L.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The function

\[
g(x)=xe^{L/x}
\]

is strictly decreasing on `(0,L)`.

#### Step A2: head of the quadratic edge

There are fewer than `m_0` possible lower indices with

\[
2\le m<m_0,
\]

and for fixed `m`, `{n\choose m}` is strictly increasing in `n`. Hence the head contributes fewer than `m_0` elements of `\mathcal A`.

#### Step A3: tail of the quadratic edge

If `m_0>=L`, (5.6) makes the tail empty. Otherwise, for every tail representation `m>=m_0`, (5.5) and monotonicity of `g` give

\[
q={n-r\over Y}\le {n\over Y}
\le {g(m)\over Y}
\le {g(m_0)\over Y}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Since `m_0>=L/log Y`,

\[
e^{L/m_0}\le Y,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{q\le m_0.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

By Theorem 3.1, each positive quotient `q` supports at most one representation in `\mathcal A`. Hence the tail contributes at most `m_0` more elements. Therefore

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal A|\le2m_0.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

#### Step B: the non-quadratic region is already in the proved interior

If `(n,m) in \mathcal B`, then

\[
n<2m^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m>\sqrt{n/2}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Fix, for example, `epsilon=1/4` in Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Shao–Tao–Teräväinen (2022). Their interior threshold is

\[
\exp\big((\log n)^{2/3+\epsilon}\big)
=
\exp\big((\log n)^{11/12}\big).
\]

As `n->infinity`,

\[
\sqrt{n/2}>
\exp\big((\log n)^{11/12}\big),
\]

and left-halfness gives

\[
n-m\ge n/2>
\exp\big((\log n)^{11/12}\big).
\]

Thus, for sufficiently large `t` (hence sufficiently large represented `n`), every element of `\mathcal B` lies in the MRSTT interior. Their theorem gives at most **two** solutions in either half of Pascal's triangle. Therefore

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal B|\le2.}
\tag{5.11}
\]

Combining (5.9) and (5.11) proves (5.2).

Finally, the `m=1` left-half edge contributes exactly one occurrence `(t,1)`. Reflecting the left half across Pascal symmetry can only double the count (and merely overcounts a possible central occurrence), so

\[
N(t)\le2(H(t)+1)\le4m_0+6,
\]

which is (5.3). The two parameter consequences follow immediately from (5.1). QED.

### Why this does not move `rho_SM`

The theorem is source-valid for every sufficiently large `t`, but its right side contains `Y= P(t)^{v_{P(t)}(t)}`. In the prime-power-flat branch one can have only

\[
\log Y=(\log t)^{o(1)},
\]

in which case (5.3) is merely

\[
N(t)\le(\log t)^{1-o(1)}.
\]

That does not imply `N(t)<=C(log t)^rho` for any fixed `rho<1`. So the frozen global metric honestly remains `rho_SM=1`.

## 6. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: a proof-based pruning invariant

The quotient-separation theorem yields a constructive enumeration rule without requiring a generic brute-force checker.

For each positive quotient `q`, the legal residue rows at index `m` form the interval `I_{q,m}` in (4.1). In the quadratic regime these intervals are strictly ordered in `m`. Thus:

- if `t < {Yq\choose m}`, then every later eligible `m'` with separated intervals is already too large;
- if `t > {Yq+m-1\choose m}`, then every earlier eligible `m'` with separated intervals is already too small;
- after locating the unique possible `m`, strict monotonicity of `{Yq+r\choose m}` in `r` permits a second binary search over `0<=r<m`.

For the large-`m` tail, (5.8) reduces the quotient search to

\[
1\le q\le m_0.
\]

So the proof itself supplies the pruning invariant: **one candidate lower index per quotient block in the quadratic edge**, followed by one monotone residue search. I did not create a finite artifact because no finite-range claim is needed to establish the theorem; manufacturing a checker here would violate the run's new-method priority without moving the source metric.

**B19 outcome:** success as a constructive reformulation, but not as a source-closing algorithm. The number of quotient blocks remains too large in the prime-power-flat regime.

## 7. What failed / what remains

The important failed hypothesis from the previous run was that nonzero residues might themselves be the obstruction. B15/B24 show that this is false in the quadratic edge: endpoint separation eliminates multiple hits inside one quotient block.

The remaining hard branch is now narrower and cleaner:

\[
\boxed{
\log\big(P(t)^{v_{P(t)}(t)}\big)=(\log t)^{o(1)}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

combined with the need to control **many different quotients `q`**, not many residues for one quotient. The current theorem only counts those blocks one by one. To obtain a fixed exponent improvement in the source metric, the next argument must exploit arithmetic coupling *across quotient blocks*.

The exact congruence available for that purpose is

\[
q\equiv
T\,(-1)^{m-1-r}m{m-1\choose r}\pmod{Y}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus a high-multiplicity coefficient forces many quotient classes to be represented by signed Pascal-row coefficients modulo the same prime-power modulus. This is the new object to study.
