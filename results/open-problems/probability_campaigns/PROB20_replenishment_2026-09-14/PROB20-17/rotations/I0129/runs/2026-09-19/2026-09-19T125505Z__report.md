# 5. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: the exact first Newton selector

Let `I_m=J_{m,1}` be the degree `<m` polynomial satisfying
\[
I_m(r)=\kappa_{m,r}=\frac1{F_m'(r)}
\qquad(0\le r<m).
\]
The previous reports proved integer-valuedness abstractly. Here is an exact Newton formula that makes the common-base arithmetic transparent.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact Newton formula

For all `m>=2`,
\[
\boxed{
I_m(x)=(-1)^{m-1}m
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^j
\binom{m+j-1}{j}\binom{x}{j}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
In particular `I_m(n)\in Z` for every integer `n>=0`, and for every prime `p>m` its coefficients are `p`-integral.

**Proof.** Put `a_r=\kappa_{m,r}=(-1)^{m-1-r}m\binom{m-1}{r}`. Newton interpolation gives
\[
I_m(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(\Delta^j a)_0\binom{x}{j}.
\]
Now
\[
(\Delta^j a)_0
=\sum_{i=0}^j(-1)^{j-i}\binom ji(-1)^{m-1-i}m\binom{m-1}{i}
\]
\[
=(-1)^{m-1+j}m
\sum_{i=0}^j\binom ji\binom{m-1}{i}
=(-1)^{m-1+j}m\binom{m+j-1}{j},
\]
by Vandermonde. This is (5.1). The integer-valued claim follows because every `binom(n,j)` is integral; for `p>m`, all denominators `j!`, `j<m`, are `p`-units. QED.

There is also a useful discrete-Stieltjes form. Set `N=m-1`,
\[
D_N(x)=\prod_{u=0}^N(x-u),
\quad
w_u=\binom Nu^2,
\]
and
\[
P_N(x):=D_N(x)\sum_{u=0}^N\frac{w_u}{x-u}
=\sum_{u=0}^Nw_u\prod_{v\ne u}(x-v).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Then the exact positive formula from the preceding work simplifies to
\[
\boxed{
I_m(x)=\frac{m^2}{m!}P_{m-1}(x),
\qquad
F_m(x)I_m(x)=\frac{m^2}{(m!)^2}D_{m-1}(x)P_{m-1}(x).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Because all `w_u>0`, the rational Stieltjes transform in (5.2) is strictly decreasing between adjacent nodes and runs from `+infinity` to `-infinity`; hence `P_N` has exactly one simple real zero in every interval `(u,u+1)`. This interlacing will be the natural language for the next-order coefficient below.

Define the base-row Newton selector
\[
H_m(x):=F_m(x)I_m(x)=x-T_{m,2}(x).
\tag{5.4}
\]
At every root `r<m`,
\[
H_m(r)=0,
\qquad
H_m'(r)=F_m'(r)I_m(r)=1.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Thus `H_m` is tangent to the identity map at every base-row root.

# 7. The second osculatory coefficient and an exact pure-residue obstruction

### Theorem 7.1 — exact second coefficient

Put
\[

\[

\tag{7.1}

Equivalently, with `N=m-1` and `P_N` from (5.2),
\[
\boxed{

\tag{7.2}

For every prime `p>m`, `c_{m,r}` is `p`-integral.

**Proof.** Write `F=F_m`, `I=I_m`. At a simple root `r`,
\[
\frac{F''(r)}{2F'(r)}=\sum_{u\ne r}\frac1{r-u}=h_{m,r}.
\tag{7.3}

Using the Lagrange basis `L_u`, `I=\sum_u\kappa_{m,u}L_u`. One has `L_r'(r)=h_{m,r}` and, for `u\ne r`,
\[
L_u'(r)=\frac{F'(r)}{(r-u)F'(u)}
=\frac{\kappa_{m,u}}{\kappa_{m,r}(r-u)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
I'(r)=\kappa_{m,r}h_{m,r}
+\frac1{\kappa_{m,r}}
\sum_{u\ne r}\frac{\kappa_{m,u}^2}{r-u}.
\]
Since `H=FI`,
\[
c_{m,r}=\frac{H''(r)}2
=F'(r)I'(r)+\frac{F''(r)}2I(r),
\]
and `F'(r)=1/\kappa_{m,r}`, `I(r)=\kappa_{m,r}` give (7.1). Formula (7.2) follows from `H=(m^2/(m!)^2)D_NP_N`: at a zero `r` of `D_N`, the quadratic coefficient is `D_N''(r)/(2D_N'(r))+P_N'(r)/P_N(r)=h_{m,r}+P_N'/P_N`. All denominators in (7.1) are products of nonzero integers of absolute value `<m`, while every `kappa` is a `p`-unit for `p>m`, proving p-integrality. QED.

The coefficient has a rigid sign pattern.

### Theorem 7.2 — antisymmetry and sign

For `0<=r<m`,
\[
\boxed{c_{m,m-1-r}=-c_{m,r}.}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Moreover
\[
r<\frac{m-1}{2}\implies c_{m,r}<0,
\qquad
r>\frac{m-1}{2}\implies c_{m,r}>0,
\tag{7.5}
\]
and when `m-1` is even the central coefficient is zero.

**Proof.** Let `N=m-1` and `w_u=binom(N,u)^2`. Then (7.1) is
\[
c_{m,r}=2(H_r-H_{N-r})+\frac1{w_r}\sum_{u\ne r}\frac{w_u}{r-u}.
\tag{7.6}
\]
The substitution `(r,u)->(N-r,N-u)` negates both terms, proving (7.4). Suppose `r<N/2`. The harmonic term is strictly negative. In the second sum, pair `u=r-j` with `u=r+j` for `1<=j<=r`. Symmetry and strict unimodality of `binom(N,u)` imply `w_{r+j}>w_{r-j}` because `r+j` is strictly closer to the center than `r-j`; hence the paired contribution `(w_{r-j}-w_{r+j})/j` is negative. Every unpaired term lies to the right of `r` and therefore also has negative denominator. Thus the second term in (7.6) is strictly negative. This proves the left half of (7.5); the right half follows from antisymmetry, and the center from (7.4). QED.
