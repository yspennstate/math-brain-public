# 4. B25 — double count: a local rough core forces `X = O(m/log m)`

## 4.1 The local rough core still has almost all logarithmic mass

Define
\[
\widehat W:=\prod_{\substack{p\mid t\\ p>R}}p^{v_p(t)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
For `p<=R`, Kummer’s theorem gives
\[
v_p\!\binom{n_0}{m}\le 1+\lfloor\log_p n_0\rfloor.
\]
Therefore
\[
\log\frac{t}{\widehat W}
=\sum_{p\le R}v_p(t)\log p
\le \pi(R)(\log n_0+\log R).
\tag{4.3}
\]
Using `R=m+4`, the standard Chebyshev bound `pi(R)=O(R/log R)`, and (3.3),
\[
\boxed{\log(t/\widehat W)=O(m^2/\log m)=O(L/\log m).}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Hence
\[
\log\widehat W=L-O(L/\log m).
\tag{4.5}
\]

## 4.2 The preserved collective collapse theorem applies at this local threshold

For the coefficient vector `(1,-1,-1,1)`, its `l1` height is `A=4`. With all four lower indices at most `R`, the natural residue-error band in the preserved collective theorem is
\[
B=\frac{A(R-1)}2=2(R-1)=2m+6.
\tag{4.6}
\]
If `|X|>B`, the collective product bound gives
\[
\log\widehat W\le(2B+1)\log(|X|+B).
\tag{4.7}
\]
The original band hit has `|X|<=2(M-1)`. Since `m<=M` and the surviving extremal regime has `M log M=o(L)`, the right side of (4.7) is
\[
O(m\log M)=o(L),

contradicting (4.5). Thus every sufficiently large surviving plastic-branch hit actually satisfies
\[
|X|\le2m+6.
\tag{4.8}

holds. The preserved in-band loss estimate gives

\log(\widehat W/\widehat W_*)\le2B\log(2B)=O(m\log m).

Combining (4.5) and (4.10),

\boxed{\log\widehat W_*=L-O(L/\log m).}

## 4.3 Weighted slot-mass identity

For each row `j in J` and slot `0<=r<k_j`, define
\[
w_{j,r}:=\sum_{\substack{Z\mid\widehat W_*\\ r_j(Z)=r}}\log Z.
\tag{4.13}
\]
Multiply (4.12) by `log Z` and sum over all components. Because every component is assigned to exactly one slot in each row,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_j\sum_{r=0}^{k_j-1}r\,w_{j,r}
=X\log\widehat W_*.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]
This is an exact real identity, not an asymptotic estimate.

The pairwise-coprime components assigned to a slot all divide the corresponding numerator factor, hence
\[
w_{j,r}\le\log(n_j-r).
\tag{4.15}
\]
Define the nonnegative slot slack
\[
h_{j,r}:=\log(n_j-r)-w_{j,r}\ge0.
\tag{4.16}
\]
The product of the `k_j` numerator factors is exactly `k_j! t`, so
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{k_j-1}h_{j,r}
=L+\log(k_j!)-\log\widehat W_*.
\]
Using (4.11) and `log(k_j!)=O(m log m)`, while `L asymp m^2`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_r h_{j,r}=O(L/\log m)
}
\tag{4.17}
\]
uniformly for the four fixed rows.

Insert `w=log(n_j-r)-h` in (4.14):
\[
X\log\widehat W_*=T-H,
\tag{4.18}
\]
where
\[
T:=\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_j\sum_{r=0}^{k_j-1}r\log(n_j-r),
\qquad
H:=\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_j\sum_{r=0}^{k_j-1}r h_{j,r}.
\tag{4.19}
\]
From (4.17),
\[
|H|\le(m+3)\sum_{j\in J}\sum_r h_{j,r}
=O\!\left(\frac{mL}{\log m}\right).
\tag{4.20}
\]

It remains to bound `T`. Since `n_j` is exponential in `m`,
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{k_j-1}r\bigl(\log(n_j-r)-\log n_j\bigr)
=O(m^3/n_j)=o(1).
\]
Put
\[
C_j:=\sum_{r=0}^{k_j-1}r=\frac{k_j(k_j-1)}2.
\]
Then
\[
T=\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_j C_j\log n_j+o(1).
\tag{4.21}
\]
An exact calculation gives
\[
C_0-C_1-C_2+C_4=m+5.
\tag{4.22}
\]
Using `log n_j-log n_0=O(1)` from (3.3),
\[
T=(m+5)\log n_0+
\sum_{j\in\{1,2,4\}}\sigma_jC_j(\log n_j-\log n_0)+o(1)
=O(m^2)=O(L).
\tag{4.23}
\]
Finally (4.18), (4.20), (4.23), and `log \widehat W_*=(1-o(1))L` yield
\[
|X|
\le O(1)+O\!\left(\frac{m}{\log m}\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{|X|=O(m/\log m).}
\tag{4.24}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (local rough-core weighted-defect compression)

# 5. B24 — extremal short-cycle classification: 4-cycles are penalized; 6-cycles are first scale-saturated survivors

The preceding graph theorem gives many independent cycles, but not their lengths. I therefore classified the two shortest possible incidence-cycle types.

## 5.1 A 4-cycle means two rough components share two row slots

Suppose distinct rough prime-power components `Z,Z'` form a simple 4-cycle. Then there are distinct row colors `j!=k` and residues `r,s` such that both components are assigned to the same two slots `(j,r)` and `(k,s)`. Thus
\[
P:=ZZ'\mid n_j-r,\qquad P\mid n_k-s,
\]
and hence
\[
P\mid G:=\gcd(n_j-r,n_k-s).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let
\[
A=n_j-r,\qquad B=n_k-s,\qquad g=\gcd(A,B),
\]
and write `B/g=p`, `A/g=q` in lowest terms. From the plastic asymptotics,
\[
\frac BA=q_0^{\,k-j}+O((\log m)/m).
\tag{5.2}
\]
For every fixed nonzero offset `k-j` occurring here, the algebraic number
\[
\alpha=q_0^{\,k-j}
\]
has degree at most three and is irrational. Its fixed minimal polynomial gives the elementary Liouville lower bound
\[
\left|\alpha-\frac pq\right|\ge \frac{c_{j,k}}{q^3}
\tag{5.3}
\]
for some positive constant depending only on the row pair. Combining (5.2) and (5.3),
\[
q\gg\left(\frac{m}{\log m}\right)^{1/3}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Since `q=A/g`,
\[
g\ll n_j\left(\frac{\log m}{m}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
With `P<=g`, every 4-cycle obeys
\[
\boxed{
ZZ'\ll n_j\left(\frac{\log m}{m}\right)^{1/3}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus a 4-cycle pays a genuine polynomial gcd penalty relative to the raw slot capacity `asymp n_j`. This is stronger than the magnitude-only cycle certificate from the previous run.

It is not yet enough globally: the factor graph can have linear cycle rank while avoiding all 4-cycles, and even linearly many independent penalties of size `~(1/3)log m` would produce only `O(m log m)` logarithmic loss, not automatically a fixed fraction of `L~m^2`.

## 5.2 Six cycles are the first extremal survivors

A simple incidence 6-cycle has three slot vertices. Consecutive slot vertices cannot have the same row color, because one rough component has only one slot in any fixed row. With three slots in a cyclic word, all three colors are therefore distinct. The only possible color sets are
\[
\{0,1,2\},\quad \{0,1,4\},\quad \{0,2,4\},\quad \{1,2,4\}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

At the pure scale level there is **no obstruction** for any of these four triples. If the three cycle components have logarithmic sizes `y_1,y_2,y_3` and the three shared slots have capacities
\[
\lambda_i=\log(n_{j_i}-r_i)=d_0m+\log m+O(1),
\]
the saturated degree-two equations are
\[
y_1+y_2=\lambda_1,\qquad
y_2+y_3=\lambda_2,\qquad
y_3+y_1=\lambda_3.
\tag{5.7}
\]
They have the unique solution
\[
2y_1=\lambda_1-\lambda_2+\lambda_3,
\]
and cyclically. Since all `lambda_i=d_0m+log m+O(1)`, every solution has
\[
y_i=\frac{d_0}{2}m+\frac12\log m+O(1)>0.
\tag{5.8}
\]
So a 6-cycle can live exactly at the average plastic rough-component scale. This shows why cycle rank plus magnitude cannot close the branch: the shortest 4-cycle has an arithmetic penalty, but the next cycle type has no exponential-scale loss.

### Proposition 5.2 (short-cycle extremal language)
Among simple factor-graph cycles, 4-cycles are the first arithmetically penalized type via a shared two-row gcd, while 6-cycles with three distinct row colors are the first types that remain fully feasible at the plastic exponential scale.

**B24 outcome:** positive classification, negative closure. Any proof based only on cycle length / product magnitude will fail on the 6-cycle triangle model.

# 6. B30 — localize to one 6-cycle: exact rough-square triangle certificate

Take a simple 6-cycle with three rough components `Z_1,Z_2,Z_3` and three shared slot values
\[
A_i:=n_{j_i}-r_i\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
where the row colors `j_1,j_2,j_3` are distinct. Label cyclically so that
\[
Z_1Z_2\mid A_1,\qquad
Z_2Z_3\mid A_2,\qquad
Z_3Z_1\mid A_3.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Define exact positive integer cofactors
\[
H_1=\frac{A_1}{Z_1Z_2},\qquad
H_2=\frac{A_2}{Z_2Z_3},\qquad
H_3=\frac{A_3}{Z_3Z_1}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Then
\[
A_1A_3H_2=Z_1^2A_2H_1H_3,
\tag{6.3}
\]
and cyclically. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
Z_1^2=\frac{A_1A_3H_2}{A_2H_1H_3},\quad
Z_2^2=\frac{A_1A_2H_3}{A_3H_1H_2},\quad
Z_3^2=\frac{A_2A_3H_1}{A_1H_2H_3}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
These are exact square constraints, not asymptotics.

If a 6-cycle slot has degree exactly two in the **full local rough graph** (all prime-power components with prime base `>m+4`, not merely the collapsed subcore), then its `H_i` is `(m+4)`-smooth. In that degree-two case, (6.4) says that the rough part of each indicated ratio has even valuation; equivalently the `>m+4` prime-parity vector of `A_1A_3/A_2` is a square. Thus the localized obstruction is a squarefree-kernel / S-unit problem for three near-geometric integers.

There is also the elementary triangle-difference certificate. The component joining slots `i` and `i+1` divides their difference, so
\[
Z_2\mid A_1-A_2,\qquad
Z_3\mid A_2-A_3,\qquad
Z_1\mid A_3-A_1,
\tag{6.5}
\]
and therefore
\[
Z_1Z_2Z_3\mid(A_1-A_2)(A_2-A_3)(A_3-A_1).
\tag{6.6}
\]
Together with (6.1),
\[
(Z_1Z_2Z_3)^2\mid A_1A_2A_3.
\tag{6.7}
\]
The square bound (6.7) is asymptotically saturated by (5.8), so it is not itself a contradiction.

Finally, for the component joining two slots `(j,r)` and `(k,s)`, the exact signed-Pascal quotient congruences imply the two-row determinant
\[
Z^2\mid (n_j-r)\kappa_{k,s}-(n_k-s)\kappa_{j,r}.
\tag{6.8}
\]
Multiplying the three instances around the triangle gives an exact determinant product divisible by `(Z_1Z_2Z_3)^2`. Its crude magnitude has an extra central-binomial `2^{O(m)}` factor and is therefore too large; this reproduces, in the shortest surviving cycle, the same entropy barrier seen earlier in the one-component defect image.

### Local conclusion
The 6-cycle is now reduced to a concrete object:

1. three pairwise-coprime rough prime powers `Z_i` at scale `exp(d_0m/2+O(log m))`;
2. three near-geometric slot values `A_i` from distinct plastic rows;
3. the exact factorization system (6.1)–(6.4);
4. the shifted-difference divisibilities (6.5);
5. the signed-Pascal square congruences (6.8).

No contradiction follows from size alone. A successful next step must control the cofactor `H_i` (especially its rough part) or prove a squarefree-kernel obstruction for one fixed color triple.

**B30 outcome:** localized the first true extremal survivor to an explicit rough-square / S-unit triangle. The obstruction is now arithmetic rather than graph-theoretic.
