## 2. Preserved edge setup from the fixed-support moment theorem

Put

\[
L:=\log t
\]

and let `M` be the maximal represented nontrivial left-half lower index. The source-threatening branch surviving the earlier reductions is the ultraflat edge

\[
M\log M=o(L),
\qquad
\log W_M(t)=(1-o(1))L,
\]

with

\[
W_M(t)=\prod_{\substack{p\mid t\\p>M}}p^{v_p(t)}.
\]

Let `f_t(x)` denote the large real solution `y` of

\[
\frac{\Gamma(y+1)}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(y-x+1)}=t,
\qquad x\ge2.
\]

The preceding verified report proves, uniformly for `2<=x<=M` and each fixed `k`,

\[
f_t^{(k)}(x)
=(-1)^k f_t(x)\left(\frac{L}{x^2}\right)^k(1+o(1)),
\tag{2.1}
\]

and in particular every fixed derivative has the alternating sign expected from a locally completely monotone edge curve, with absolute magnitude super-polynomially larger than `M`.

That report used (2.1) to exclude every fixed-support **full moment annihilator**. The present question is different: can a bounded-support coefficient pattern that does *not* annihilate all lower moments achieve an accidental cancellation on the curve?

## 3. B18 — translate local row relations into a sparse polynomial

The correct local language is not another moment matrix. On the natural parabolic edge scale, a fixed node pattern becomes a one-variable sparse polynomial.

For clarity, let

\[
F_L(x):=f_{e^L}(x).
\]

Fix distinct nonnegative integer offsets

\[
0=h_0<h_1<\cdots<h_{s-1}=H
\]

and fixed integers `c_0,...,c_{s-1}`, not all zero. Define

\[
P_{c,h}(q):=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_j q^{h_j}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — parabolic local-resonance normal form

Fix a compact interval `K=[d_-,d_+]` contained in `(0,infinity)`. Uniformly for `d in K` as `x->infinity`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_j F_{d x^2}(x+h_j)}{F_{d x^2}(x)}
=P_{c,h}(e^{-d})+O_{c,h,K}\!\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus fixed local non-moment cancellation on the edge scale `L asymp x^2` is governed, to first order, by positive roots of the explicit integer polynomial `P_{c,h}` in `(0,1)`.

#### Proof

The previous report proves the uniform logarithmic normal form

\[
\log F_L(u)=\frac{L+\log\Gamma(u+1)}u+\varepsilon_L(u),
\tag{3.3}
\]

where on the present regime (`u=x+O(1)`, `L=d x^2`, `d in K`) the error and each fixed number of derivatives are smaller than every fixed power of `x^{-1}`. Indeed `L/u = d x+O(1)`, so the top index is exponentially large in `x`.

For fixed `h>=0`, subtract (3.3) at `u=x+h` and `u=x`. The `L`-part is exact:

\[
L\left(\frac1{x+h}-\frac1x\right)
=-\frac{d h x}{x+h}
=-dh+O_{h,K}(x^{-1}).
\tag{3.4}
\]

By Stirling,

\[
\frac{\log\Gamma(u+1)}u
=\log u-1+O\!\left(\frac{\log u}{u}\right),
\]

so changing `u` by a fixed amount changes this term by `O_h((log x)/x)`. Therefore

\[
\log\frac{F_{d x^2}(x+h)}{F_{d x^2}(x)}
=-dh+O_{h,K}\!\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right),
\]

uniformly for `d in K`. Exponentiating,

\[
\frac{F_{d x^2}(x+h)}{F_{d x^2}(x)}
=e^{-dh}\left(1+O_{h,K}\!\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right)\right).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Multiplying by the fixed coefficients and summing gives (3.2). `square`

### Corollary 3.2 — fixed-pattern scale quantization

Suppose the coefficient pattern has fixed `l1` height `A=sum |c_j|`, and consider a represented local block with base lower index `x` and `L/x^2` staying in a compact subinterval of `(0,infinity)`. If its row form enters the rough-core band

\[
\left|\sum_j c_jF_L(x+h_j)\right|\le \frac{A(M-1)}2,
\tag{3.6}
\]

then every subsequential limit `d` of `L/x^2` must satisfy

\[
P_{c,h}(e^{-d})=0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Indeed `F_L(x)` is `exp(d x+O(log x))`, while the right side of (3.6) is only polynomial in `M`; in the ultraflat regime their ratio tends to zero. Dividing (3.6) by `F_L(x)` and using (3.2) proves (3.7).

If `q_0=e^{-d_0}` is a simple root of `P_{c,h}` in `(0,1)`, then any band-entering sequence converging to that root satisfies

\[
\frac{L}{x^2}=d_0+O\!\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right)
\tag{3.8}
\]

provided it remains in a fixed small neighborhood of `d_0`; this follows from (3.2) and the nonzero derivative of `d -> P(e^{-d})` at `d_0`.

This is the useful B18 translation. A root at `q=1` is the moment-cancellation boundary: using `q d/dq`, multiplicity `r` at `q=1` is equivalent to annihilation of the first `r` power moments (equivalently the first `r` falling moments). Those boundary roots were excluded in the previous fixed-order derivative theorem. The genuinely new cases are **interior roots `0<q<1`**, which correspond to accidental non-moment cancellation at a positive parabolic scale.

## 4. B27 — fix a coefficient/gap pattern first

Theorem 3.1 gives an exact dichotomy for every fixed local pattern.

### Pattern without an interior positive root

If `P_{c,h}(q)` has no root on a compact interval `q in [q_-,q_+] subset (0,1)`, then its absolute value has a positive minimum there. Equation (3.2) then gives

\[
\left|\sum_jc_jF_{d x^2}(x+h_j)\right|
\asymp_{c,h,K} F_{d x^2}(x),
\]

uniformly for the corresponding `d`-interval. Since `F_{d x^2}(x)=exp(dx+O(log x))`, the row form is exponentially larger than its `O(M)` rough-core band and therefore cannot enter it.

### Pattern with a simple interior root

The opposite behavior is real and cannot be dismissed by total positivity alone.

Let `q_0 in (0,1)` be a simple root of `P` and `d_0=-log q_0`. Choose `d_-<d_0<d_+` so close that `P(e^{-d_-})` and `P(e^{-d_+})` have opposite signs and no other root lies between them. By (3.2), for all sufficiently large `x`, the normalized exact Gamma-inverse row form has the same opposite signs at `d_-` and `d_+`. Continuity in `d` therefore gives a real

\[
d_x=d_0+O\!\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right)
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_j c_j F_{d_xx^2}(x+h_j)=0.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
L_x=d_0x^2+O(x\log x).
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is an exact **continuous** cancellation on the real inverse-binomial curve. It is not a Singmaster counterexample: `e^{L_x}` need not be an integer and the values `F_{L_x}(x+h_j)` need not be integers. But it proves that the purely real Chebyshev/curvature route cannot uniformly exclude arbitrary fixed-support non-moment relations. The arithmetic integrality/common-binomial condition is essential.

### A discrete variation observation

There is still a useful one-dimensional rigidity. For any fixed coefficient vector and fixed first `s-1` integer nodes, the row form as a function of the final node is monotone because the final contribution is `c_{s-1}f_t(x)`. The previous derivative estimate gives

\[
\min_{2\le u\le M-1}|f_t(u+1)-f_t(u)|/M\to\infty.
\]

Hence, for every fixed coefficient height `A`, for sufficiently large `t` there is **at most one integer final node** that can put a fixed pattern into a band of total width `A(M-1)`. This does not bound multiplicity by itself, but it shows that bounded-height resonances are sporadic graphs over the preceding nodes rather than thick families.

## 5. B26 — the first sporadic case: minimal height four

Take four local nodes

\[
x_0=x,
\qquad
x_1=x+a,
\qquad
x_2=x+a+b,
\qquad
x_3=x+a+b+c,
\]

with positive integer gaps `a,b,c` fixed as `x->infinity`.

A nonzero integer coefficient vector with exact support four, zero total mass and minimal possible `l1` height `4` has two `+1` and two `-1` entries. Up to overall sign there are three ordered sign patterns.

### 5.1 The two nonresonant patterns

For `++--`,

\[
P(q)=1+q^a-q^{a+b}-q^{a+b+c}>0
\qquad(0<q<1),
\]

because `1>q^{a+b}` and `q^a>q^{a+b+c}`.

For `+-+-`,

\[
P(q)=1-q^a+q^{a+b}-q^{a+b+c}
=(1-q^a)+q^{a+b}(1-q^c)>0.
\]

Therefore neither pattern has an interior positive resonance scale.

### 5.2 Exact classification of the dangerous `+--+` pattern

For the remaining pattern `(1,-1,-1,1)`,

\[
P_{a,b,c}(q)
=1-q^a-q^{a+b}+q^{a+b+c}
=(1-q^a)-q^{a+b}(1-q^c).
\tag{5.1}
\]

**Proposition 5.1.** `P_{a,b,c}` has a root in `(0,1)` if and only if `c>a`. When `c>a`, that root is unique.

#### Proof

If `c<=a`, then for `0<q<1`,

\[
1-q^c\le1-q^a,
\]

and therefore

\[
q^{a+b}(1-q^c)<1-q^a.
\]

So (5.1) is strictly positive and there is no interior root.

Now suppose `c>a`. As `q->0+`, `P(q)->1>0`. As `q->1-`,

\[
\frac{P(q)}{1-q}\longrightarrow a-c<0,
\]

so `P(q)<0` immediately to the left of `1`. Hence an interior root exists.

The nonzero coefficient signs of `P` are `+,-,-,+`, so Descartes' rule of signs gives at most two positive roots counted with multiplicity. The root `q=1` is simple because

\[
P'(1)=c-a>0.
\]

Therefore there can be exactly one further positive root, and the root in `(0,1)` is unique. `square`

Thus the same outer-gap inequality that appeared qualitatively in the previous convexity analysis is exact at the parabolic model level: **a minimal-height accidental resonance exists precisely when the final outer gap is longer than the first, `c>a`.**

### 5.3 Explicit smallest local resonance

The first simple example is

\[
(a,b,c)=(1,1,2),
\qquad
(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)=(x,x+1,x+2,x+4).
\]

Then

\[
P(q)=1-q-q^2+q^4
=(q-1)(q^3+q^2-1).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The cubic has one root

\[
q_0=0.7548776662\ldots\in(0,1),
\]

hence

\[
d_0=-\log q_0=0.2811995743\ldots.
\tag{5.3}
\]

By Section 4, for all sufficiently large integer `x` there is a real parameter

\[
L_x=d_0x^2+O(x\log x)
\]

such that the **exact real Gamma-inverse curve** satisfies

\[
\boxed{
F_{L_x}(x)-F_{L_x}(x+1)-F_{L_x}(x+2)+F_{L_x}(x+4)=0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is not a numerical heuristic and no checker is needed: it follows from the on-page uniform asymptotic plus the intermediate-value theorem. It is also not a source refutation, because (5.4) does not assert integer `t=e^{L_x}` or integer top indices.

## 6. What this run rules out — and what it exposes

The intended B18 “total positivity will exclude all bounded-support non-moment relations” hope is **false as a real-geometric mechanism**. The edge curve itself has exact fixed-support accidental cancellations. The failure is structured rather than arbitrary:

- fixed local patterns reduce to the sparse polynomial `P(q)=sum c_jq^{h_j}`;
- roots at `q=1` are the already-excluded full-moment boundary mechanism;
- roots in `(0,1)` produce discrete parabolic resonance scales `L/x^2 -> -log q`;
- patterns without such roots are uniformly excluded on compact positive scales;
- at minimal height four, the only resonant sign pattern is `+--+`, and its gap criterion is exactly `c>a`.

The source-level gap is therefore now more arithmetic than geometric:

> Can an integer `t`, together with several *integer* inverse-binomial top indices representing that same `t`, shadow one of these algebraic sparse-polynomial resonance branches closely enough to enter the rough-core band?

If yes systematically, it is a plausible counterexample-building mechanism. If no, the obstruction should come from the common rough prime-power labels / Kummer structure, a fixed-difference or near-collision theorem, or a linear-form-in-logarithms statement tied to the algebraic resonance root.

The explicit example (5.2) suggests the natural asymptotic target

\[
t\approx \exp(d_0x^2)=q_0^{-x^2},
\]

where `q_0` is algebraic. This is precisely where pure calculus stops: an arithmetic theorem is needed to decide whether exact binomial values can lie on such a branch.
