## 2. Preserved extremal setup

Put

\[
L:=\log t,
\qquad
M:=\max\{m:2\le m\le n/2,\ \binom nm=t\},
\]

and define the common `M`-rough core

\[
W:=W_M(t):=\prod_{\substack{p\mid t\\p>M}}p^{v_p(t)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For each rough prime `p>M`, set

\[
Z_p:=p^{v_p(t)}.
\]

For every represented left-half row `(n_i,m_i)` with `m_i<=M`, there is a unique residue

\[
0\le r_{i,p}<m_i\le M,
\qquad
Z_p\mid n_i-r_{i,p},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and therefore a unique integer quotient

\[
n_i=Z_pq_{i,p}+r_{i,p}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The preceding report proved for standard finite differences that

\[
\operatorname{dist}(\Delta^k n_i,Z_p\mathbf Z)<2^kM,
\]

and that a sufficiently large *single* `Z_p` can force `Delta^k q_{i,p}=0`. The purpose of this run is to remove the single-large-modulus hypothesis by multiplying the information from all pairwise coprime rough components.

Whenever an exponent-one extremizing sequence is discussed below, the previously isolated surviving regime includes

\[
M\log M=o(L),
\qquad
\log W=(1-o(1))L.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The lemmas themselves are stated before taking this asymptotic specialization.

## 3. B20 — why the simultaneous-modulus recovery algorithm fails, exactly

### 3.1 General zero-sum integer linear form

Take represented rows `n_0,...,n_{s-1}` and integers `c_0,...,c_{s-1}` satisfying

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_j=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Put

\[
A:=\sum_j|c_j|,
\qquad
P:=\sum_{c_j>0}c_j=\frac A2,
\qquad
B:=P(M-1)=\frac A2(M-1),
\tag{3.2}
\]

and define the real/integer row form

\[
X:=\sum_jc_jn_j.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For every rough component `Z_p`, define

\[
e_p:=\sum_jc_jr_{j,p},
\qquad
Q_p:=\sum_jc_jq_{j,p}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then (2.3) gives the exact identity

\[
X=Z_pQ_p+e_p.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Because the positive and negative coefficient masses both equal `P` and every residue is in `[0,M-1]`,

\[
-B\le e_p\le B.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus every rough prime power divides one member of the same short integer family:

\[
Z_p\mid X-e_p,
\qquad e_p\in\{-B,-B+1,\ldots,B\}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is the approximate-common-divisor system in its exact form.

### Theorem 3.1 — collective rough-core relation-collapse lemma

With the notation above:

**(i) Outside the residue band.** If `|X|>B`, then

\[
\boxed{
W\mid\prod_{e=-B}^{B}(X-e)
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
\log W\le (2B+1)\log(|X|+B).
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\log(|X|+B)\ge
\frac{\log W}{A(M-1)+1}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

**(ii) Inside the residue band.** If `X in [-B,B]`, let

\[
W_0:=\prod_{\substack{p>M\\e_p=X}}Z_p
=\prod_{\substack{p>M\\Q_p=0}}Z_p.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac W{W_0}\mid
\prod_{\substack{-B\le e\le B\\e\ne X}}(X-e)
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

and, for `B>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac W{W_0}\le 2B\log(2B).
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

#### Proof

For each `p>M`, (3.7) says the full prime power `Z_p` divides the factor `X-e_p`. Distinct `Z_p` are powers of distinct primes, hence pairwise coprime. If `|X|>B`, none of the factors `X-e` vanishes, so multiplying one common list containing every possible selected factor proves (3.8). Since every factor has magnitude at most `|X|+B`, (3.9) follows, and `2B+1=A(M-1)+1` gives (3.10).

If `X in [-B,B]`, the components with `e_p=X` are exactly those for which (3.5) gives `Q_p=0`. Remove those components and remove the zero factor. Every remaining `Z_p` divides one of the `2B` nonzero factors in (3.12), again with coprime prime bases. Every remaining factor has magnitude at most `2B`, proving (3.13). `QED`

### Corollary 3.2 — almost-all rough mass obeys the quotient relation

Assume the extremal scale (2.4), and let the coefficient vector `c` have fixed `A=O(1)`. If

\[
\left|\sum_jc_jn_j\right|\le \frac A2(M-1),
\tag{3.14}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\log W_0=(1-o(1))L.
}
\tag{3.15}
\]

In words: **almost the entire rough logarithmic mass satisfies the exact quotient relation**

\[
\sum_jc_jq_{j,p}=0.
\tag{3.16}
\]

Indeed, (3.13) loses only `O(A M log(AM))=o(L)` logarithmic mass.

This is the key B20 diagnosis. The obstruction is *not* that every individual `Z_p` is too small: a product of many small coprime rough prime powers collectively enforces an exact quotient relation as soon as the real row form lies in its natural residue-error band. The actual failure occurs before modulus recovery — namely, current source-valid information does not force a useful high-order real row form into that band.

### 3.2 Standard finite differences: the exact exponential loss

For the standard `k`th difference, take

\[
c_j=(-1)^{k-j}\binom kj,
\qquad 0\le j\le k.
\tag{3.17}
\]

Then

\[
A=2^k,
\qquad
B_k=2^{k-1}(M-1).
\tag{3.18}
\]

(The previous report used the looser `<2^kM` residue bound; (3.18) is the exact positive/negative-mass improvement.) Therefore:

- if `|Delta^k n_i|<=B_k`, then all but `O(2^kM log(2^kM))` logarithmic rough mass satisfies
  \[
  \Delta^kq_{i,p}=0;
  \tag{3.19}
  \]
- if `|Delta^k n_i|>B_k`, the collective argument gives only
  \[
  \boxed{
  \log(|\Delta^k n_i|+B_k)
  \ge
  \frac{\log W}{2^k(M-1)+1}.
  }
  \tag{3.20}
  \]

So increasing the difference order makes the residue alphabet exponentially more expensive. This is the exact scale failure of the naive simultaneous-modulus recovery algorithm.

## 4. B3 — prove a stronger simultaneous-relations theorem

The preceding theorem can be intersected across many translated forms; this is stronger than the single-modulus statement from the previous run.

### Theorem 4.1 — many-form collective quotient rigidity

For `ell=1,...,H`, let `c^(ell)` be zero-sum integer coefficient vectors, with

\[
A_\ell:=\|c^{(\ell)}\|_1,
\qquad
B_\ell:=\frac{A_\ell}{2}(M-1),
\]

and suppose every associated row form satisfies

\[
|X_\ell|\le B_\ell.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then there is a subcore `W_*|W` such that every prime-power component of `W_*` satisfies **all** quotient relations

\[
\sum_jc_j^{(\ell)}q_{j,p}=0,
\qquad 1\le\ell\le H,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac W{W_*}
\le
\sum_{\ell=1}^{H}2B_\ell\log(2B_\ell).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

For each form `ell`, Theorem 3.1(ii) bounds the total logarithmic mass of components violating its quotient relation by `2B_ell log(2B_ell)`. The components violating at least one relation lie in the union of these bad sets. Since logarithmic rough mass is additive over the pairwise coprime prime-power components, the logarithmic mass of the union is at most the sum of the individual bounds. Remove this union to obtain `W_*`. `QED`

### Corollary 4.2 — polynomial quotient trajectories on a long block

Fix `k`. Suppose a block of represented rows has `H+k` entries and

\[
|\Delta^k n_i|\le B_k=2^{k-1}(M-1)
\qquad(0\le i<H).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then there is `W_*|W` with

\[
\log\frac W{W_*}
\le
2H B_k\log(2B_k)
=O_k(HM\log M),
\tag{4.5}
\]

such that for every component `Z_p|W_*`,

\[
\Delta^kq_{i,p}=0
\qquad(0\le i<H).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Hence the quotient trajectory `i -> q_{i,p}` agrees on that block with an integer-valued polynomial sequence of discrete degree at most `k-1`.

In particular, in the extremal regime (2.4), if

\[
H M\log M=o(L),
\tag{4.7}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\log W_*=(1-o(1))L.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus **simultaneous multi-modulus recovery itself works on blocks growing almost as fast as `L/(M log M)`**, provided the real finite differences fall into their natural residue band. This sharply relocates the obstacle to the real/irregular-node side.

This theorem is still PARTIAL: strict decrease of a quotient sequence is compatible with a low-degree polynomial, and current source-valid estimates do not provide (4.4) on a sufficiently long represented block.

## 5. B6 — deform the annihilator: low-height vanishing polynomials help, then fail at irregular nodes

The exponential `2^k` in (3.18) is not an unavoidable feature of integer annihilators on equally spaced samples.

Let

\[
C(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_jz^j\in\mathbf Z[z]
\]

have a zero of order at least `k` at `z=1`. Then `C(E)` annihilates every polynomial sequence in the chain index of degree `<k`. The rough residue-error band in Theorem 3.1 is controlled by

\[
\|C\|_1 M,
\]

not directly by the order `k`.

Already:

\[
(z-1)^3(z+1)=z^4-2z^3+2z-1
\tag{5.1}
\]

has order three but coefficient length `6<8`, and

\[
(z-1)^4(z+1)(z^2+z+1)
=z^7-2z^6+z^4+z^3-2z+1
\tag{5.2}
\]

has order four but coefficient length `8<16`.

More importantly, classical low-height-polynomial theory studies exactly this tradeoff. Borwein–Mossinghoff (Experimental Mathematics 9 (2000), 425–433) define the least degree `d(k)` of a polynomial with coefficients in `{-1,0,1}` and a zero of order `k` at one, and record the known bounds

\[
k^2\ll d(k)\ll k^2\log k.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus there exist order-`k` integer annihilators with coefficient height one, degree `O(k^2 log k)`, and hence

\[
\|C\|_1=O(k^2\log k),
\tag{5.4}
\]

replacing the standard difference's `2^k` residue-alphabet cost by a polynomial one in an **equally spaced chain-index model**.

That deformation does not yet solve the source, for two precise reasons.

### Failure 1: the first nonzero smooth term is not improved

Write

\[
C(z)=(z-1)^kQ(z)
\]

with exact vanishing order `k`. Since `Q(1)` is a nonzero integer,

\[
|Q(1)|\ge1,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
C^{(k)}(1)=k!Q(1),
\qquad |C^{(k)}(1)|\ge k!.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The standard `k`th difference has `Q(1)=1`, so no integer-coefficient order-`k` annihilator can reduce the leading Taylor coefficient below the ordinary finite difference. Low-height constructions buy a smaller residue alphabet by using a longer stencil; they do not magically improve the first surviving real derivative term.

### Failure 2: represented lower indices are not equally spaced

The inverse-binomial row is a smooth function of the **lower index** `m`, whereas `C(E)` cancels low-order Taylor terms only when the sampled lower indices form an arithmetic progression (or are controlled closely enough to one). The represented indices

\[
m_0<m_1<\cdots
\]

are not known to be affine in the chain index.

For arbitrary nodes, the natural exact annihilator is a divided-difference relation. Clearing its denominators introduces products of node spacings (Vandermonde-type factors). This is precisely the kind of denominator growth already visible in Kane's Gamma/divided-difference method; Kane's 2007 “Further Work” section explicitly diagnoses denominator growth as the reason that technique alone saturates.

So the B6 deformation succeeds in the equal-spacing model but fails at the first genuinely source-relevant step: **constructing a low-`l1` integer moment annihilator on the actual irregular represented lower-index set while retaining enough real smooth cancellation**.

## 6. What changed, and what did not

### What this run resolves

The previous live obligation asked whether many individually small coprime rough prime powers could collectively force low-degree quotient rigidity. The answer is now precise:

- **Yes, conditionally on a small real row form.** Theorem 3.1 uses the entire rough core at once and loses only `O(M log M)` mass for a fixed low-height form.
- **Yes, simultaneously over many forms.** Theorem 4.1 permits intersection of quotient relations; Corollary 4.2 gives a long-block polynomial quotient trajectory on `1-o(1)` of rough mass whenever the corresponding real finite differences enter the band.
- The exact failure of standard high-order differences is the coefficient-length/error-alphabet factor `2^k`, not a lack of total modulus.
- Low-height high-vanishing polynomials can reduce that exponential coefficient cost to polynomial cost on equally spaced samples, but the real represented `m`-nodes are irregular. Clearing arbitrary-node divided differences reintroduces denominator growth.

### What remains open

We still do **not** have an unconditional fixed exponent below one. The new live obstruction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Build or force a low-height integer annihilator on the actual represented }m\text{-nodes}
\text{ whose inverse-binomial row form is }O(M\|c\|_1).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently, prove a dichotomy: either a long represented lower-index set contains enough additive/near-arithmetic structure to admit a cheap annihilator, or its irregularity itself forces a source-valid sparsity bound.

That is materially narrower than “simultaneous modulus recovery.” The modulus side is now handled once the real form is small.
