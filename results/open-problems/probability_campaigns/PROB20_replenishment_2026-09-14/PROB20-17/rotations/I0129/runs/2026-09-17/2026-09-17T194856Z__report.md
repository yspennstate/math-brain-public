## 4. B2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

The first refutation attempt was to ask whether the one known infinite nontrivial collision mechanism can be concatenated to make collision chains of increasing length.

### 4.1 Exact failure of the naive Fibonacci-collision chain

The known Lind–Singmaster family is built from solutions of

\[
\binom{n+1}{m+1}=\binom n{m+2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The most direct way to turn such pairs into larger and eventually unbounded multiplicity would be to concatenate a translated copy through the middle coefficient:

\[
\binom{n+1}{m+1}
=\binom n{m+2}
=\binom{n-1}{m+3}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is impossible over the integers.

For the first equality, dividing both sides by `binom(n,m+1)` gives

\[
(n+1)(m+2)=(n-m)(n-m-1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

For the second equality, dividing by `binom(n-1,m+2)` gives

\[
n(m+3)=(n-m-2)(n-m-3).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Set `d=n-m`. Moving the two equations to the left gives respectively

\[
-d^2+dm+3d+m^2+3m+2=0,
\]

and

\[
-d^2+dm+8d+m^2+3m-6=0.
\]

Subtracting forces

\[
5d-8=0,
\]

hence `d=8/5`, contradiction.

**Outcome.** Consecutive translated copies of the classical infinite collision family cannot share their middle coefficient. This does **not** rule out other collision networks, but it kills the most literal mechanism for turning the Fibonacci pair family into an arbitrarily long chain. I make no novelty claim for this elementary observation.

### 4.2 A quantitative smoothness barrier for any unbounded-multiplicity counterexample

The more useful B2 result is an exact inequality relating multiplicity to the largest prime factor of the repeated integer.

Let `P(t)` denote the largest prime divisor of `t`. Then for every integer `t>1`,

\[
\boxed{
P(t)\le \frac{\log t}{\log 2}\, t^{2/N(t)}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

Let `R(t)` be the number of representations in the left half `1<=m<=n/2`. Symmetry gives

\[
N(t)=2R(t)-c(t),\qquad c(t)\in\{0,1\},
\]

where `c(t)=1` only if there is a central representation. Hence

\[
R(t)\ge \frac{N(t)}2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For fixed `m`, `n -> binom(n,m)` is strictly increasing, so distinct left-half representations have distinct lower indices. Let `M` be the largest such lower index. Then

\[
M\ge R(t)\ge \frac{N(t)}2.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Choose the corresponding representation `t=binom(n,M)`, with `n>=2M`. Pairing the factors in the usual product formula gives

\[
\binom nM
=\prod_{j=0}^{M-1}\frac{n-j}{M-j}
\ge \left(\frac nM\right)^M,
\]

because `(n-j)/(M-j) >= n/M` for every `j`. Therefore

\[
n\le M t^{1/M}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Also

\[
t\ge\binom{2M}{M}
=\prod_{j=0}^{M-1}\frac{2M-j}{M-j}
\ge 2^M,
\]

so

\[
M\le \frac{\log t}{\log 2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Every prime divisor of `binom(n,M)` is at most `n`, hence `P(t)<=n`. Combining this with (4.7)–(4.9) gives

\[
P(t)\le n\le M t^{1/M}
\le \frac{\log t}{\log 2}\,t^{2/N(t)},
\]

which proves (4.5). `QED`

A useful rearrangement, whenever the denominator is positive, is

\[
N(t)
\le
\frac{2\log t}
{\log P(t)-\log(\log t/\log2)}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

In particular, for every fixed `delta>0`,

\[
P(t)\ge t^\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N(t)\le \frac{2}{\delta}+o(1).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Therefore any source-level refutation sequence `t_j` with `N(t_j)->infinity` must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log P(t_j)}{\log t_j}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

So an unbounded-multiplicity construction is forced into increasingly **subpower-smooth** integers. This is a genuine restriction on the negation of Singmaster, but not a proof of boundedness. The proof is elementary; a bounded prior-art search did not locate this exact inequality, but **novelty is unverified and not claimed**.

## 5. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

Two translations sharpen what a successful proof now has to exploit.

### 5.1 Exact centered consecutive-product form

Write

\[
x=n-\frac{m-1}{2}.
\]

Then the binomial equation is exactly a symmetric polynomial equation.

If `m=2r`, then `x` is a half-integer and

\[
\boxed{
(2r)!\,t
=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(x^2-(j-\tfrac12)^2\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `m=2r+1`, then `x` is an integer and

\[
\boxed{
(2r+1)!\,t
=x\prod_{j=1}^{r}(x^2-j^2).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

These identities are obtained simply by pairing the factors in

\[
m!t=n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)
\]

around their midpoint.

Thus a collision at two lower indices `m,m'` is an integral point on

\[
\frac{F_m(x)}{m!}=\frac{F_{m'}(x')}{m'!},
\tag{5.3}
\]

where `F_m` is one of the centered polynomials above. For fixed `(m,m')`, this is the familiar arithmetic-geometry world in which Siegel-type finiteness can apply; Jenkins and the literature already exploit related curves. The source problem is harder because both degrees grow and one needs a **uniform** statement across an unknown set of many lower indices.

The centered form removes the degree-`m-1` term automatically and exposes the parity symmetry. That makes it a cleaner input for any future p-adic, S-unit, or height argument than the raw Gamma interpolation alone.

### 5.2 Why merely continuing Kane’s derivative/LCM route is unlikely to move `rho_SM`

Kane’s Section 8 is unusually explicit about the bottleneck. The finite-difference denominator `B(m_1,...,m_k)` can already have `log B=Omega(S)` once `k^2=Omega(S)`. In the relevant range the derivative comparison then yields only logarithmic inverse density in `log t`, and Kane notes that this mechanism alone cannot exclude on the order of

\[
\frac{\log t}{(\log\log t)^3}
\]

solutions.

That is still `(log t)^{1-o(1)}` and therefore leaves the frozen exponent at 1. So the next useful attack must use information not present in the spacing denominator alone: the common arithmetic value `t`, its prime-factor profile, positivity/integrality of the consecutive-product factors, or a genuinely new uniform arithmetic-geometric input.

## 6. B30 — ONE SCALE AT A TIME

Let

\[
L=\log t,\qquad \ell=\log L.
\]

Take `epsilon=1/4` in MRSTT Remark 1.5. For sufficiently large `t`, the only unbounded part of the left half lies in

\[
2\le m\le \frac{L}{\ell^{5/4}}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The complementary interior contributes at most two left-half solutions, so any power-saving theorem has to come from (6.1).

MRSTT Remark 1.2 records the sharper edge approximation

\[
n=(m!t)^{1/m}+\frac{m-1}{2}+O\!\left(m t^{-1/m}\right).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Set

\[
A_m=(m!t)^{1/m}.
\]

For every representation in the remaining edge (6.1),

\[
\frac{L}{m}\ge \ell^{5/4}.
\]

Hence (6.2) forces

\[
\left\|A_m+\frac{m-1}{2}\right\|_{\mathbb Z}
\le C m e^{-L/m}
\le C L e^{-\ell^{5/4}}
\le \exp\!\left(-\tfrac12\ell^{5/4}\right)
\tag{6.3}
\]

for all sufficiently large `t` (after absorbing the fixed implied constant).

So the unresolved global problem can be phrased as a **super-polylogarithmically thin near-integer problem**:

> How many `m <= L/ell^(5/4)` can make the specific algebraic number `(m!t)^(1/m)+(m-1)/2` lie within `exp(-c ell^(5/4))` of an integer, for one common integer `t`?

This is much sharper than merely saying “the edge remains.” It also dovetails with (4.12): if multiplicity were unbounded, then the common `t` would have to be increasingly subpower-smooth, so `m!t` is an increasingly smooth integer whose `m`-th root is forced extraordinarily close to the correct integer/half-integer lattice for many different `m`.

### Metric criterion exposed by the scale reduction

Any theorem of the form

\[
\#\left\{m\le L/\ell^{5/4}:\ \binom{n}{m}=t\text{ for some }n\right\}
=O(L^\rho),\qquad \rho<1,
\tag{6.4}
\]

uniformly in `t`, would immediately move `rho_SM` below 1 after adding the O(1) interior contribution. An `O(1)` bound in (6.4) would settle Singmaster.

At present I do not have such a uniform theorem, so **the frozen metric does not move**.

## 7. What the three methods say together

The first run narrows both sides of the problem:

- **Refutation side:** the obvious attempt to concatenate the one known infinite collision family fails algebraically, and any genuinely unbounded-multiplicity sequence must be subpower-smooth by (4.12).
- **Proof side:** the 2022 theorem removes the interior, leaving only a small-`m` edge in which every representation forces the near-integer condition (6.3).
- **Method-selection side:** Kane’s own analysis says that polishing the same finite-difference denominator cannot plausibly produce a fixed power saving; fixed-pair Diophantine finiteness is also nonuniform/ineffective. The missing ingredient must exploit the **common arithmetic value across many growing lower indices**.

This suggests a more concrete hard core than the original statement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{uniformly control many near-integer roots of the common smooth integer family }m!t
\text{ across }m\le L/\ell^{5/4}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

## 8. Failed routes and precise reasons

1. **Chain the Lind–Singmaster/Fibonacci collision.** Failed exactly: two consecutive translated collision equations force `5(n-m)=8`.
2. **Keep sharpening Kane’s spacing denominator.** The primary paper itself exhibits an `exp(Omega(S))` denominator obstruction and says the route saturates at only polylogarithmic density improvement; that cannot lower the frozen log-power exponent below 1.
3. **Use the MRSTT interior theorem plus trivial edge counting.** The remaining edge still has `L/ell^(5/4)=L^{1-o(1)}` possible lower indices, so this alone leaves `rho_SM=1`.
4. **Invoke fixed-pair Siegel finiteness.** It is nonuniform as `(m,m')` grows and the cutoff in MRSTT Remark 1.5 is explicitly ineffective; it does not control a growing collision network.
