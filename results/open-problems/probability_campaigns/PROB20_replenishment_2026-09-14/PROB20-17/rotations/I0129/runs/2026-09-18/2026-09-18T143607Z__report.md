# 2. B2 — attack the negation: sharpen the dangerous branch instead of assuming it is impossible

The first useful result is that the parabolic window can be sharpened by an entire order.

## Theorem 2.1 — two-term quantization of the `(0,1,2,4)` resonance branch

Assume `m -> infinity`, `M>=m+4`,

\[
M\log M=o(L),
\]

and `L/m^2` stays in a fixed compact subset of `(0,infinity)`. Put

\[
n_h=F_L(m+h),\qquad h\in\{0,1,2,4\}.
\]

Suppose the four-term row form enters the natural rough-core band,

\[
|n_0-n_1-n_2+n_4|\le 2(M-1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

If the sequence lies on the interior-root branch from the preceding report, then

\[
\boxed{
L=d_0m^2+\delta_0m+O(\log m),
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\delta_0
=1+d_0\,
\frac{15q_0+12q_0^2}{3q_0+2q_0^2}
=2.500136583258\ldots .
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
\log n_0
=d_0m+\log m+(\delta_0-1)
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m}\right),
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

so, writing `rho=exp(d_0)=q_0^{-1}`,

\[
\boxed{
n_0=e^{\delta_0-1}\,m\,\rho^m
\left(1+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m}\right)\right),
\qquad
e^{\delta_0-1}=4.4823012358\ldots .
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Finally the first three top indices have an unavoidable multiplicative curvature defect

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{n_0n_2}{n_1^2}
=\frac{2d_0}{m}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right).
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

### Proof

For `k=m+O(1)` and `n=F_L(k)` in this edge regime,

\[
L=\log\binom nk
=k\log n-\log\Gamma(k+1)+O(k^2/n).
\]

Because `L/m^2` is bounded below by a positive constant, `n=exp(Theta(m))`; hence `k^2/n` is exponentially small. Therefore

\[
\log F_L(k)
=\frac{L+\log\Gamma(k+1)}{k}+O(e^{-c m})
\tag{2.7}
\]

for some fixed `c>0` along the branch.

Write

\[
d_m=\frac{L}{m^2},\qquad q_m=e^{-d_m}.
\]

For every fixed integer `h`, Stirling's formula gives

\[
\frac{\log\Gamma(m+h+1)}{m+h}
-
\frac{\log\Gamma(m+1)}m
=
\frac{h}{m}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right).
\tag{2.8}
\]

Also

\[
\frac{L}{m+h}-\frac{L}{m}
=-hd_m+\frac{h^2d_m}{m}+O(m^{-2}).
\tag{2.9}
\]

Combining (2.7)--(2.9), uniformly for `h in {1,2,4}`,

\[
\log\frac{n_h}{n_0}
=-h d_m+
\frac{h+h^2d_m}{m}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right).
\tag{2.10}
\]

Exponentiating,

\[
\frac{n_h}{n_0}
=q_m^h\left[
1+\frac{h+h^2d_m}{m}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right)
\right].
\tag{2.11}
\]

The band error in (2.1), divided by `n_0`, is exponentially small. Indeed `M log M=o(L)=Theta(m^2)` implies `M=o(m^2)` after enlarging `m`, while `n_0=exp(Theta(m))`. Hence (2.1) and (2.11) imply

\[
0=P(q_m)+\frac{A(q_m)+d_mB(q_m)}{m}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right),
\tag{2.12}
\]

where

\[
A(q)=\sum_h c_h h q^h=qP'(q),
\qquad
B(q)=\sum_h c_h h^2q^h=(q\partial_q)^2P(q),
\]

for `(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_4)=(1,-1,-1,1)`.

At the interior root `q_0`, using `q_0^4=q_0+q_0^2`,

\[
A_0:=A(q_0)=3q_0+2q_0^2>0,
\]

and

\[
B_0:=B(q_0)=15q_0+12q_0^2.
\]

Because the root is simple, (2.12) first gives `d_m-d_0=O(1/m)`. Expanding

\[
P(e^{-d_m})=-A_0(d_m-d_0)+O((d_m-d_0)^2)
\]

inside (2.12) then yields

\[
d_m=d_0+\frac{1+d_0B_0/A_0}{m}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right).
\]

This is (2.2)--(2.3).

Setting `h=0` in (2.7) and applying Stirling gives

\[
\log n_0
=\frac{L}{m}+\log m-1+O(\log m/m),
\]

which, with (2.2), proves (2.4)--(2.5).

Finally subtract twice the `h=1` instance of (2.10) from the `h=2` instance. The terms depending linearly on `h` cancel, while the quadratic term leaves

\[
\log(n_0n_2/n_1^2)
=\frac{2d_m}{m}+O(\log m/m^2)
=\frac{2d_0}{m}+O(\log m/m^2).
\]

This proves (2.6). `square`

### Meaning

The dangerous branch is not merely `L=d_0m^2+O(m log m)`. A genuine band hit has to lie in a logarithmic-width tube around one explicit two-term scale. This is a substantial narrowing, but it is still not an integer realization or exclusion.

# 3. B17 — the resonance number is the plastic constant, and the obvious Pisot/Padovan construction both helps and fails

The cubic from the previous run has a classical identity hidden in it. Put

\[
\rho=q_0^{-1}.
\]

Then `q_0^3+q_0^2=1` is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{\rho^3=\rho+1.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus `rho=1.324717957244746...` is the **plastic constant**, the dominant root of the Padovan/Perrin recurrence.

This is not cosmetic. It changes the arithmetic interpretation of the branch.

## 3.1 Exact integer additive shadows exist automatically

Let `(A_j)` be any integer sequence satisfying

\[
A_{j+3}=A_{j+1}+A_j.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then, identically,

\[
\boxed{
A_j-A_{j-1}-A_{j-2}+A_{j-4}=0.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed subtract `A_{j-1}=A_{j-3}+A_{j-4}` from `A_j=A_{j-2}+A_{j-3}`.

For a nondegenerate Padovan/Perrin-type sequence the dominant-root expansion is

\[
A_j=a\rho^j+O(\rho^{-j/2}),
\tag{3.4}
\]

because the two conjugate roots of `X^3-X-1` have modulus `rho^{-1/2}`. Therefore the exact integer quartet

\[
(A_j,A_{j-1},A_{j-2},A_{j-4})
\]

has precisely the limiting ratio vector

\[
(1,q_0,q_0^2,q_0^4)
\]

and satisfies the dangerous `+--+` row relation **exactly**.

So top-index integrality by itself is not a plausible exclusion mechanism.

There is an even sharper Pisot observation. For the Perrin sequence,

\[
R_j=\rho^j+\beta^j+\gamma^j\in\mathbb Z,
\]

with `|beta|=|gamma|=rho^{-1/2}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}(\rho^j,\mathbb Z)
\le 2\rho^{-j/2}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus a strategy that tries to rule out the branch merely because algebraic powers should not lie near integers points in the wrong direction: the plastic constant is a Pisot number and has exponentially good integer shadows.

Fresh literature lookup identifies exactly this constant as the Padovan/Perrin limiting ratio and the smallest Pisot number. I found no source connecting this plastic/Padovan structure to Singmaster's conjecture in the bounded search; that is **not** a novelty certificate.

## 3.2 But the undeformed Padovan shadow cannot be an exact common-binomial quartet

The factorial correction in Theorem 2.1 creates a decisive first-order mismatch.

From (3.4), whenever `j=j(m)` satisfies `rho^{-3j/2}=o(1/m)`, any scalar multiple of four Padovan/Perrin entries obeys

\[
\log\frac{A_jA_{j-2}}{A_{j-1}^2}=o(1/m).
\tag{3.6}
\]

But any actual common-binomial quartet in the dangerous branch obeys (2.6), namely

\[
\log\frac{n_0n_2}{n_1^2}
=\frac{2\log\rho}{m}+o(1/m),
\tag{3.7}
\]

with a strictly positive coefficient. Therefore:

**Corollary 3.1.** No sufficiently large exact common-binomial quartet on lower indices `(m,m+1,m+2,m+4)` in this resonance tube can have its top-index vector equal to a common scalar multiple of

\[
(A_j,A_{j-1},A_{j-2},A_{j-4})
\]

from a fixed nondegenerate Padovan/Perrin recurrence whenever `rho^{-3j/2}=o(1/m)` (in particular on the natural `j asymp m` scale).

The real branch needs a **slow `1/m` deformation of a plastic/Pisot lattice shadow**, not the undeformed recurrence itself. More explicitly, (2.10) and (2.2) give

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{n_h}{n_0}
=-h\log\rho
+\frac{h(1-\delta_0)+d_0h^2}{m}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m^2}\right).
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

The quadratic-in-`h` first-order correction is exactly what a pure geometric/Padovan block lacks.

## 3.3 Relation to the historical Fibonacci mechanism

Craig Tovey's 1985 *Fibonacci Quarterly* paper, “Multiple Occurrences of Binomial Coefficients,” conjectured that for every prescribed number of proper representations there should be infinitely many integers having that many proper binomial representations. He proved the two-proper-representation case constructively through the classical Fibonacci/Pell family. That conjectural direction is the negation-side counterpart to universal bounded multiplicity.

The present branch gives a striking but still incomplete analogue:

- the classical two-representation construction is governed by the golden ratio / Fibonacci recurrence;
- the first local four-support `+--+` real resonance is governed by the plastic constant / Padovan recurrence;
- but Theorem 2.1 shows that the literal Padovan recurrence misses the factorial `1/m` curvature needed for exact common binomial values.

This makes a **deformed Padovan/Thue-type construction** a concrete counterexample-hunt route, while also identifying exactly what the naive construction gets wrong.

## 3.4 Why generic linear-forms-in-logs does not immediately close it

If exact representations at lower indices `m` and `m+h` exist, then with

\[
E_k(n):=-\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\log(1-r/n)\ge0
\]

one has the exact identity

\[
\Lambda_h
:=(m+h)\log n_h-m\log n_0
-\log\frac{(m+h)!}{m!}
=E_{m+h}(n_h)-E_m(n_0).
\tag{3.9}
\]

On the parabolic branch,

\[
|\Lambda_h|\ll \frac{m^2}{n_4}
=\exp(-d_0m+O(\log m)).
\tag{3.10}
\]

This is a genuine exponentially small logarithmic form. But the integer bases `n_h,n_0` themselves have logarithmic height `Theta(m)` and move with `m`; standard off-the-shelf linear-form lower bounds do not visibly beat the scale (3.10). The known binomial-collision literature instead repeatedly exploits special factorization / largest-prime-factor structure. So B17 supplies a useful route diagnosis rather than a closure theorem.

# 4. B30 — one rough prime-power scale: exact signed-Pascal congruence for a band hit

The common rough core gives an exact arithmetic condition which is stronger than a first-residue/Hensel digit calculation because it uses the **same normalized binomial value** across all four rows and the already-proved quotient relation.

## Lemma 4.1 — full rough-prime-power quotient congruence

Let

\[
t=\binom nk,
\qquad
Z=p^a,
\qquad
a=v_p(t),
\qquad p>k.
\]

Put `U=t/Z`. Then there is a unique `r in {0,...,k-1}` such that

\[
n=ZQ+r,
\qquad p\nmid Q,
\]

and, with

\[
\kappa_{k,r}
:=(-1)^{k-1-r}k\binom{k-1}{r},
\tag{4.1}
\]

one has the congruence modulo the **full prime power** `Z`,

\[
\boxed{
Q\equiv U\,\kappa_{k,r}\pmod Z.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Since `p>k`, the denominator `k!` is a `p`-adic unit. The `p`-valuation `a` of the numerator falling factorial therefore occurs in exactly one of its `k` consecutive factors, say `n-r`, because an interval of length `<p` contains at most one multiple of `p`. Thus `v_p(n-r)=a`, so `n-r=ZQ` with `p` not dividing `Q`. Since `0<=r<k<p<=Z`, `r` is also the remainder of `n` modulo `Z`.

Divide the binomial identity by `Z`:

\[
U
=Q\,\frac{\prod_{j\ne r}(n-j)}{k!}.
\]

Modulo `Z`, replace `n` by `r`. Then

\[
\prod_{j\ne r}(r-j)
=(-1)^{k-1-r}r!(k-1-r)!,
\]

so

\[
\frac{\prod_{j\ne r}(r-j)}{k!}
=\frac{(-1)^{k-1-r}}{k\binom{k-1}{r}}.
\]

The denominator is invertible modulo `Z`, and rearranging gives (4.2). `square`

## Corollary 4.2 — the `(0,1,2,4)` collapsed-core constraint

Assume four representations of the same `t` at lower indices

\[
k_h=m+h,
\qquad h\in\{0,1,2,4\},
\]

and a band hit

\[
X:=n_0-n_1-n_2+n_4,
\qquad |X|\le2(M-1).
\]

For every rough component `Z=p^{v_p(t)}` in the subcore on which the prior relation-collapse theorem gives

\[
Q_{0,Z}-Q_{1,Z}-Q_{2,Z}+Q_{4,Z}=0,
\tag{4.3}
\]

write `r_{h,Z}` for its residue slot. Then exactly

\[
\boxed{
X=r_{0,Z}-r_{1,Z}-r_{2,Z}+r_{4,Z},
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
Z\mid
\kappa_{m,r_{0,Z}}
-\kappa_{m+1,r_{1,Z}}
-\kappa_{m+2,r_{2,Z}}
+\kappa_{m+4,r_{4,Z}}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The common unit `U=t/Z` cancels in (4.5) because it is invertible modulo `Z`.

By the earlier collective theorem, the product of rough components satisfying (4.3) retains logarithmic mass `(1-o(1))L` in the ultraflat regime. Thus (4.4)--(4.5) are not a one-prime curiosity: almost the whole rough core must be routed through these coupled residue classes if the dangerous band hit is real.

## Why B30 still does not close the branch

For fixed `X`, (4.4) leaves at most `O(m^3)` residue triples, with the fourth residue determined. For each nonzero signed-Pascal defect in (4.5), all pairwise-coprime rough components assigned to the same residue class have product dividing that one integer. Its absolute value is at most `exp(O(m))`. The resulting crude product bound is only

\[
\log W_{\mathrm{nonzero}}=O(m^4),
\]

whereas the branch has `L=Theta(m^2)`. This is far too weak in the wrong direction; exact-zero defect classes are not controlled by it at all.

So the one-scale theorem isolates the arithmetic datum, but a source-useful argument must **compress the residue-class entropy from three free slot coordinates to essentially one scale**, or must show that exact-zero / low-defect signed-Pascal classes cannot carry substantial rough mass. Merely enumerating residues modulo one prime would not accomplish this.
