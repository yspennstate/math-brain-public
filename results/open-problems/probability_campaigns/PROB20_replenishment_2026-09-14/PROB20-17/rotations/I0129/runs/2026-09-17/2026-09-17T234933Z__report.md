## 2. Setup for the selected special case SC-r0

Work on the left half, `2 <= m <= n/2`; the `m=1` edge and its symmetric partner contribute only a universal constant to the full source problem.

Let

$$
p=P(t),\qquad a=v_p(t),\qquad Y=p^a,\qquad T=t/Y.
$$

For any left-half representation of `t`, the classical Sylvester–Schur theorem gives `p>m`: the numerator block contains a prime greater than `m`, and such a prime cannot be cancelled by `m!`. Hence every `1,...,m-1` is a `p`-adic unit.

SC-r0 restricts further to representations satisfying

$$
n\equiv0\pmod Y.
$$

Write `n=Yq`. Because `p>m` and `v_p(t)=a`, necessarily `p` does not divide `q`. Dividing the binomial identity by `Y` gives the exact equation selected in the recovery report:

$$
\boxed{
q\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(Yq-j)=m!T.
}
\tag{2.1}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{q{Yq-1\choose m-1}=mT.}
\tag{2.2}
$$

Define the restricted count

$$
R_0(t):=\#\left\{(n,m): {n\choose m}=t,\ 2\le m\le n/2,\ n\equiv0\pmod Y\right\}.
$$

This is a diagnostic subcount of `N(t)`, not a replacement for the source quantity.

## 3. B4 — PROVE SOMETHING WEAKER, COMPLETELY

### Theorem 3.1 — SC-r0 modulus-scale bound

Let `L=log t` and

$$
m_0:=\max\left\{4,\left\lceil {L\over\log Y}\right\rceil\right\}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{R_0(t)\le 2m_0.}
\tag{3.1}
$$

Consequently, for every fixed `delta>0`,

$$
Y\ge t^\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_0(t)\le 2\max\{4,\lceil1/\delta\rceil\}=O_\delta(1),
\tag{3.2}
$$

and for every fixed `0<eta<=1`,

$$
\log Y\ge (\log t)^\eta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_0(t)=O\big((\log t)^{1-\eta}\big).
\tag{3.3}
$$

This theorem is **PARTIAL**: it controls only the `r=0` residue branch. It does not change `rho_SM` for the full source.

### Proof

We use only elementary monotonicity plus the common modulus.

**Step 1: a uniform real upper bound for the row.** For every `0<=i<m`,

$$
{n-i\over m-i}-{n\over m}
=
{i(n-m)\over m(m-i)}\ge0.
$$

Therefore

$$
t={n\choose m}
=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}{n-i\over m-i}
\ge\left({n\over m}\right)^m,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{n\le m t^{1/m}.}
\tag{3.4}
$$

For an SC-r0 solution `n=Yq`, this yields

$$
q\le {m t^{1/m}\over Y}.
\tag{3.5}
$$

**Step 2: for `m>=4`, every represented lower index satisfies `m<L`.** Since we work on the left half, `n>=2m`, hence

$$
t={n\choose m}\ge {2m\choose m}.
$$

Now `{8\choose4}=70>e^4`. Also

$$
{{2m+2\choose m+1}\over {2m\choose m}}
=4-{2\over m+1}>e
$$

for every `m>=4`. Induction gives `{2m\choose m}>e^m` for every `m>=4`. Thus `L=log t>m`.

**Step 3: the tail has at most `m_0` possibilities.** Let

$$
g(x):=x e^{L/x}.
$$

For `0<x<L`,

$$
g'(x)=e^{L/x}\left(1-{L\over x}\right)<0,
$$

so `g` is strictly decreasing on the whole range containing every represented `m>=4`.

If `m_0>=L`, there is no represented `m>=m_0` by Step 2. Otherwise, for every SC-r0 solution with `m>=m_0`, (3.5) and monotonicity give

$$
q\le {g(m)\over Y}
\le {g(m_0)\over Y}
={m_0 e^{L/m_0}\over Y}.
$$

Since `m_0 >= L/log Y`, we have `e^{L/m_0}<=Y`, and hence

$$
\boxed{q\le m_0.}
\tag{3.6}
$$

For a fixed positive integer `q`, the row `n=Yq` is fixed. On the left half, `{n\choose m}` is strictly increasing in `m` up to the center, because

$$
{{n\choose m+1}\over {n\choose m}}={n-m\over m+1}>1
$$

whenever both `m` and `m+1` lie before the center. Hence a fixed `q` supports at most one left-half solution of `{n\choose m}=t`. By (3.6), the tail `m>=m_0` therefore contains at most `m_0` SC-r0 solutions.

**Step 4: the head has fewer than `m_0` possibilities.** There are fewer than `m_0` integers `m` with `2<=m<m_0`, and each `m` determines at most one `n` because `{n\choose m}` is strictly increasing in `n` for fixed `m`. Thus the head contributes fewer than `m_0` solutions.

Adding head and tail proves (3.1). Statements (3.2) and (3.3) follow immediately from the definition of `m_0`. QED.

1. This is a genuine complete theorem about the selected special case, but Kane's total bound is quantitatively stronger than the crude uniform consequence one obtains by forgetting `Y`. The value here is the **explicit dependence on the common prime-power modulus** and the exact identification of which modulus scales make SC-r0 bounded or power-saving.
2. Since `m<p<=Y`, one also has the trivial `R_0(t)<p<=Y`. Combining this with (3.1) gives

   R_0(t)\le \min\left(Y,\ 2\max\left\{4,\left\lceil {L\over\log Y}\right\rceil\right\}\right),
   $$
   and in particular an elementary `O(L/log L)` bound for SC-r0 alone. This is still weaker than Kane's known bound for the full problem, so it is not advertised as a global advance.

## 4. B6 — DEFORM TO A SOLVABLE MODULUS SCALE

The theorem above suggests that the useful deformation parameter is not merely `Y/t` but how many `Y`-adic digits are needed to locate the real integer root.

Factor (2.1) in `Z_p`. Let

$$
s:=(-1)^{m-1},\qquad H_{m-1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}{1\over j}\in\mathbb Z_p.
$$

Because `p>m`, all denominators are `p`-adic units. Equation (2.1) becomes

$$
sq\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(1-{Yq\over j}\right)=mT.
\tag{4.1}
$$

Modulo `Y`, this recovers the unique first Hensel digit

$$
\boxed{q\equiv s mT\pmod Y.}
\tag{4.2}
$$

Expanding one digit further,

$$
\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(1-{Yq\over j}\right)
\equiv 1-YqH_{m-1}\pmod{Y^2},
$$

and therefore

$$
q-Yq^2H_{m-1}\equiv smT\pmod{Y^2}.
$$

Using (4.2) inside the term already multiplied by `Y`, we obtain the explicit second digit

$$
\boxed{
q\equiv smT+Y m^2T^2 H_{m-1}\pmod{Y^2},
}
\tag{4.3}
$$

where `H_{m-1}` is interpreted modulo `Y` using the inverses of `1,...,m-1`.

There is also an exact convergent p-adic logarithmic normal form. From (4.1),

$$
{s q\over mT}
=
\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(1-{Yq\over j}\right)^{-1},
$$

so, since `p` is odd and each factor is in `1+pZ_p`, one has

$$
\boxed{
\log_p\left({s q\over mT}\right)
=
\sum_{r\ge1}{(Yq)^r\over r}
\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}{1\over j^r}.
}
\tag{4.4}
$$

This does not by itself count ordinary integer roots, but it makes the deformation failure quantitative. The real scale from (3.5) is approximately

$$
\log q\lesssim {L\over m}-\log Y+\log m.
$$

A modulus `Y^k` can pin down `q` uniquely only once `k log Y` overtakes this real search scale. Ignoring lower-order logarithms, the necessary depth is governed by

$$
k\gtrsim {L\over m\log Y}-1.
\tag{4.5}
$$

Thus bounded-depth Hensel information is effective when `m log Y/L` stays away from zero, and it necessarily loses uniform force when that ratio approaches zero. This is the precise version of the qualitative failure recorded in the stuck report.

A small coupling survives across different SC-r0 rows. If `(n_m,m)` and `(n_k,k)` are two such representations, with `n_m=Yq_m` and `n_k=Yq_k`, then (4.2) gives

$$
Y^2\mid k n_m-m n_k
$$

when `m,k` have the same parity, and

$$
Y^2\mid k n_m+m n_k
$$

when they have opposite parity. This is exact but, without a real separation estimate, is not yet strong enough to improve the count in Theorem 3.1.

**B6 outcome:** the large-modulus side of the deformation is solved by Theorem 3.1, and the Hensel expansion shows exactly why the argument degenerates as the prime-power scale shrinks. No global metric movement.

## 5. B29 — COMPACTIFY THE SCALE PARAMETERS

Let again `L=log t`. For asymptotic sequences with `Y>1`, define the logarithmic scale coordinate

$$
\eta(t):={\log\log Y\over\log L}
={\log\log Y\over\log\log t},
$$

whenever this ratio is convenient, and the local Hensel/real coordinate

$$
\lambda(t,m):={m\log Y\over L}.
$$

If

$$
\log Y=L^{\eta+o(1)}
$$

for some fixed `eta>0`, Theorem 3.1 gives the special-case exponent

$$
\boxed{R_0(t)\le L^{1-\eta+o(1)}.}
\tag{5.1}
$$

In particular, `eta=1` (equivalently a fixed positive power of `t` at the `Y` level) gives `O(1)` on SC-r0. The boundary on which this theorem alone ceases to give **any fixed logarithmic power saving** is

$$
\boxed{\eta\to0,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \log Y=L^{o(1)}.}
\tag{5.2}
$$

This is much thinner than the earlier global high-multiplicity condition `Y=t^{o(1)}`. It says that a genuinely difficult SC-r0 sequence must push the complete largest-prime power all the way to a double-logarithmically flat boundary, not merely to a subpower of `t`.

The second coordinate `lambda` records the same boundary locally. Formula (4.5) says the required Hensel depth is approximately `1/lambda`. The point `lambda=0` is therefore a genuine point at infinity for the bounded-digit argument: taking more routine Hensel digits does not yield a uniform theorem there.

This compactification also clarifies what this run did **not** solve. The source includes every residue

$$

and the nonzero `r` branches allow the row `n` to change even when `q` is fixed. The injection “one `q`, one left-half row” that closes the SC-r0 tail is lost. The next obligation is therefore not another digit of (4.4); it is to understand whether residue freedom can create many rows at a fixed or slowly varying quotient `q`.
