\[
L:=\log t,\qquad

\left|\sum_i c_i n_i\right|\le \frac{A}{2}(M-1)

## 3. B12 — invariant: exact three-node Vandermonde gcd profile

Write the consecutive node gaps

\[
a:=x_1-x_0\ge1,\qquad b:=x_2-x_1\ge1,
\]

and `g=gcd(a,b)`. The moment matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1\\
x_0&x_1&x_2
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has maximal minors, up to sign,

\[
b,\quad a+b,\quad a.
\]

Their gcd is exactly `g`. Hence the primitive integer kernel is

\[
\boxed{
 c=\left(\frac b g,-\frac{a+b}{g},\frac a g\right)
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

up to overall sign, and its exact `l1` height is

\[
\boxed{
A_3=\frac{2(a+b)}{g}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the `k=2` instance of the previous Vandermonde-cofactor/lcm formula, but here the arithmetic-conditioning invariant becomes completely transparent.

### Consequence 3.1 — bounded primitive height means finitely many reduced gap shapes

Put `a=ga_0`, `b=gb_0` with `gcd(a_0,b_0)=1`. Then

\[
A_3=2(a_0+b_0).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore `A_3<=H` if and only if the reduced adjacent-gap pair `(a_0,b_0)` lies in the finite set

\[
\{(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2:\gcd(u,v)=1,\ u+v\le H/2\}.
\]

So repeated small primitive height on consecutive triples forces the gap ratios to move in a finite rational alphabet. This is a genuine arithmetic rigidity statement, but by itself it does not create a source-closing row relation.

**B12 outcome:** the three-node gcd/lcm invariant is solved exactly. Small height means commensurable gaps with bounded reduced numerator/denominator. The decisive question remains analytic band entry.

## 4. B15 — break symmetry: quantitative convexity of the inverse-binomial row curve

### 4.1 Smooth row curve

For real `x>=2`, define `f_t(x)=n` implicitly by

\[
G(n,x):=\log\Gamma(n+1)-\log\Gamma(x+1)-\log\Gamma(n-x+1)-L=0,
\tag{4.1}
\]

on the left-edge branch `n>2x`. At every represented integer lower index `m`, this gives exactly `f_t(m)=n` when `binom(n,m)=t`.

Write `psi` and `psi_1` for the di- and trigamma functions. Then

\[
G_n=\psi(n+1)-\psi(n-x+1)>0,
\]

\[
G_x=\psi(n-x+1)-\psi(x+1)>0,
\]

and

\[
G_{nn}<0,\qquad G_{nx}=\psi_1(n-x+1)>0,
\qquad
G_{xx}=-\psi_1(x+1)-\psi_1(n-x+1)<0.
\]

Implicit differentiation gives

\[
f_t'(x)=-\frac{G_x}{G_n}<0
\]

and

\[
f_t''(x)
=-\frac{G_{xx}+2G_{nx}f_t'(x)+G_{nn}(f_t'(x))^2}{G_n}>0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the inverse-binomial row curve is strictly decreasing and strictly convex throughout this edge branch. More is true quantitatively.

### 4.2 Uniform lower bound for the curvature

The standard series for the polygamma functions give, for `s>1`,

\[
\psi_1(s)\ge \frac1s,
\qquad
\psi_1(s)\le \frac1{s-1},
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
-\psi_2(s)=2\sum_{j\ge0}(s+j)^{-3}\ge \frac1{s^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently, with `n=f_t(x)`,

\[
G_n
=\int_{n-x+1}^{n+1}\psi_1(s)\,ds
\le \frac{x}{n-x},
\tag{4.5}
\]

\[
G_x
=\int_{x+1}^{n-x+1}\psi_1(s)\,ds
\ge \log\frac{n-x+1}{x+1},
\tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
-G_{nn}
=\psi_1(n-x+1)-\psi_1(n+1)
=\int_{n-x+1}^{n+1}(-\psi_2(s))\,ds
\ge \frac{x}{(n+1)^2}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

In (4.2), each of the three terms inside the numerator is negative because `f_t'<0`. Keeping only the `G_nn(f_t')^2` term yields

\[
f_t''(x)
\ge
\frac{(-G_{nn})G_x^2}{G_n^3}
\ge
\frac{(n-x)^3}{x^2(n+1)^2}
\left(\log\frac{n-x+1}{x+1}\right)^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Whenever `n>=4x>=8`,

\[
\frac{(n-x)^3}{(n+1)^2}\ge \frac n4,
\qquad
\frac{n-x+1}{x+1}\ge\frac n{2x},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
 f_t''(x)
\ge
\frac{n}{4x^2}
\left(\log\frac n{2x}\right)^2.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

It remains to place this on the Singmaster scale. The standard gamma-ratio/Stirling inequalities

\[
\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n-x+1)}\le n^x,
\qquad
\Gamma(x+1)\ge (x/e)^x
\]

give from (4.1)

\[
n\ge \frac{x}{e}e^{L/x}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Put

\[
\lambda_M:=\frac{L}{M}.
\]

The surviving exponent-one regime `M log M=o(L)` is exactly

\[
\frac{\lambda_M}{\log M}\to\infty
\tag{4.11}
\]

along any unbounded-`M` extremizing sequence. Equations (4.9)-(4.10) therefore imply uniformly for `2<=x<=M`, for all sufficiently large members of such a sequence,

\[
f_t''(x)
\ge
\frac{e^{\lambda_M-1}}{4M}
(\lambda_M-1-\log2)^2
>2M.
\tag{4.12}
\]

(The bounded-`M` case is already bounded in multiplicity and is not the extremal obstruction.)

### Theorem 4.2 — no three-node moment annihilator enters the rough-core band

Let

\[
2\le x_0<x_1<x_2\le M
\]

be represented lower indices in the regime (2.2), and let `a=x_1-x_0`, `b=x_2-x_1`. By the divided-difference mean-value theorem there exists `xi in (x_0,x_2)` with

\[
f_t[x_0,x_1,x_2]=\frac{f_t''(\xi)}2>M.
\tag{4.13}
\]

On the other hand, for the three nodes

\[
D_0=a(a+b),\qquad D_1=ab,\qquad D_2=b(a+b),
\]

and hence

\[
\frac1{D_0}+\frac1{D_1}+\frac1{D_2}
=\frac2{ab}.
\tag{4.14}
\]

The exact band-entry criterion from the preceding report is

\[
|f_t[x_0,x_1,x_2]|
\le
\frac{x_2-1}{2}
\left(\frac1{D_0}+\frac1{D_1}+\frac1{D_2}\right)
=\frac{x_2-1}{ab}
\le M-1.
\tag{4.15}
\]

This contradicts (4.13). Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No primitive three-node relation annihilating moments }0,1
\text{ enters its rough-core residue band in the ultraflat extremal regime.}
}
\tag{4.16}
\]

This is a complete theorem for the intended three-node moment-annihilator route. It is still PARTIAL for Singmaster because a four-or-more-node relation or a non-moment arithmetic cancellation is not excluded.

**B15 outcome:** breaking the generic PTE symmetry by using the actual ordered inverse-binomial curve kills the entire support-three moment route. The obstruction is analytic curvature, not coefficient height.

## 5. B29 — compactify the triple gap simplex: there is no boundary escape

Normalize the triple gaps by their span

\[
H=a+b,
\qquad
\theta=\frac aH\in(0,1).
\]

The band side of (4.15) is

\[
\frac{x_2-1}{ab}
=
\frac{x_2-1}{\theta(1-\theta)H^2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

At first sight one might hope to evade an interior-shape obstruction by sending `theta->0` or `theta->1`, because the scale-free factor `1/[theta(1-theta)]` diverges. For integer nodes, however, `ab>=1`, so (5.1) is always at most `M-1`, regardless of whether the normalized shape lies in the interior or approaches either boundary face. The uniform curvature bound (4.12) gives the opposite side `>M` with no shape dependence.

Thus every point of the compactified one-dimensional gap simplex is excluded asymptotically:

- interior shapes `theta in [epsilon,1-epsilon]`: excluded;
- left boundary `theta->0`: excluded;
- right boundary `theta->1`: excluded;
- fixed tiny integer gap next to a macroscopic gap: excluded.

So support-three failure is not an artifact of having assumed comparable gaps. There is **no compactification boundary case left over**.

**B29 outcome:** the complete triple gap-shape space is eliminated. Any surviving low-support strategy begins at support four or leaves the moment-annihilator family.

## 6. What failed and what changed

What this does **not** prove:

- it does not rule out support `>=4` moment relations, where higher-order signs can cancel curvature;
- it does not rule out an accidental low-`l1` three-node zero-sum relation that is not a first-moment annihilator;
