# 4. B27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS: the jet order may grow arbitrarily

The previous proof used fixed-`s` denominator notation. The first task here is to make the construction explicit enough that `s` is allowed to depend on `m`.

Let
\[
F_k(x)=\binom{x}{k}=\frac1{k!}\prod_{u=0}^{k-1}(x-u).
\]
For `0<=r<k` define the cardinal polynomial
\[
L_{k,r}(x)=\prod_{u\ne r}\frac{x-u}{r-u}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Its signed denominator is
\[
D_{k,r}:=\prod_{u\ne r}(r-u)=(-1)^{k-1-r}r!(k-1-r)!,
\]
so
\[
\kappa_{k,r}=\frac{k!}{D_{k,r}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
For `x=r+z`, an exact identity is
\[
F_k(r+z)=\frac{z}{\kappa_{k,r}}L_{k,r}(r+z).
\tag{4.3}
\]
Therefore the local reciprocal germ from the previous report is simply
\[
\psi_{k,r}(z)=\frac{z}{F_k(r+z)}
=\frac{\kappa_{k,r}}{L_{k,r}(r+z)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For an arbitrary integer `s>=1`, let `[G(z)]_{<s}` denote truncation to degrees `0,...,s-1`.

### Theorem 4.1 — explicit cardinal formula for every jet order

The unique degree-`<sk` confluent interpolant `J_{k,s}` satisfying
\[
J_{k,s}(r+z)=\psi_{k,r}(z)+O(z^s)
\qquad(0\le r<k)
\]
is
\[
\boxed{
J_{k,s}(x)=
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}
L_{k,r}(x)^s
\left[
\kappa_{k,r}L_{k,r}(r+z)^{-(s+1)}
\right]_{<s,\ z=x-r}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

**Proof.** Fix a root `r`. The `r`-summand equals
\[
L_{k,r}(r+z)^s
[\kappa_{k,r}L_{k,r}(r+z)^{-(s+1)}]_{<s}
=\kappa_{k,r}L_{k,r}(r+z)^{-1}+O(z^s),
\]
which is `psi_{k,r}(z)+O(z^s)` by (4.4). Every `u!=r` summand contains `L_{k,u}(r+z)^s=O(z^s)`. Thus (4.5) has the required jets at all `k` roots. Its degree is `<sk`: `L^s` has degree `s(k-1)` and the truncated factor contributes at most `s-1`. Uniqueness follows because two such polynomials differ by a polynomial of degree `<sk` divisible by `prod_r(x-r)^s`, which has degree `sk`. QED.

The formula gives a denominator bound uniform in `s`.

### Corollary 4.2 — uniform denominator

For all `k>=2` and `s>=1`,
\[
\boxed{((k-1)!)^{2s-1}J_{k,s}(x)\in\mathbb Z[x].}
\tag{4.6}
\]
In particular every denominator prime of `J_{k,s}` is at most `k-1`, with no fixed-`s` hypothesis.

**Proof.** The denominator of `L_{k,r}(x)^s` divides `((k-1)!)^s`. In
\[
L_{k,r}(r+z)^{-(s+1)}
=
\prod_{u\ne r}\left(1+\frac{z}{r-u}\right)^{-(s+1)},
\]
the coefficient of `z^j` is an integer linear combination of products whose denominator is a product of `j` integers of absolute value at most `k-1`; hence it divides `((k-1)!)^j`. Since `j<=s-1` and `kappa_{k,r}` is integral, multiplying by `((k-1)!)^{2s-1}` clears every denominator in every summand. QED.

### Lemma 4.3 — uniform height

There is an absolute constant `C>0` such that, for all `k,s>=1`, if
\[
P_{k,s}(x):=((k-1)!)^{2s-1}J_{k,s}(x),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\log H(P_{k,s})\le Csk\log(k+s+2),
}
\tag{4.7}
\]
where `H` is the maximum absolute coefficient.

**Proof.** Use (4.5). The numerator polynomial of `L_{k,r}` has coefficient `l^1` norm at most
\[
\prod_{u\ne r}(1+u)\le k!,
\]
so its `s`-th power contributes `exp(O(sk log k))`. For `j<s`, the absolute coefficient of `z^j` in the reciprocal product is at most the coefficient of `z^j` in
\[
(1-z)^{-(s+1)(k-1)},
\]
namely
\[
\binom{(s+1)(k-1)+j-1}{j},
\]
whose logarithm is `O(s log(k+s+2))`. Also `|kappa_{k,r}|<=k2^{k-1}`, and replacing `z` by `x-r` costs at most `exp(O(s log k))` in coefficient norm. The common denominator clearing in (4.6) contributes `O(sk log k)`. Summing `k` roots changes the logarithm by only `O(log k)`. This proves (4.7). QED.

Now take the four row sizes `k_h=m+h` and the common denominator
\[
C_{m,s}:=((m+3)!)^{2s-1}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Set
\[
S_{m,s}:=
J_{m,s}(n_0)-J_{m+1,s}(n_1)-J_{m+2,s}(n_2)+J_{m+4,s}(n_4),
\]
\[
Q_{m,s}:=C_{m,s}S_{m,s}\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{4.9}
\]

### Theorem 4.4 — all-order clean-core power tower

For **every** integer `s>=1`, with no fixed-order restriction,
\[
\boxed{(W^\circ)^s\mid Q_{m,s}.}
\tag{4.10}
\]

**Proof.** Let `Z=p^f` be clean. Put `A_h=n_h-r_h`. The preserved exponent-rigidity statement gives `v_p(A_h)=f=v_p(t)`. By construction,
\[
J_{k_h,s}(n_h)
=

=
\frac{A_h}{t}+O_p(p^{sf}).

The exact quotient/residue collapse (3.2) gives

Hence `v_p(S_{m,s})>=sf`. The denominator `C_{m,s}` is a `p`-unit because every clean `p>4m+8>m+3`. Thus `p^{sf}|Q_{m,s}`. Multiplying over the pairwise-coprime clean prime powers proves (4.10). QED.

### Corollary 4.5 — growing-order Archimedean bound

There is an absolute `C>0` such that for all `s>=1`,

\boxed{

\le

\tag{4.11}

whenever `Q_{m,s}!=0`. Therefore

\tag{4.12}
\]
In particular, for every `s=s(m)` with `log(s+1)=o(m)`, the right side of (4.11) is `sL+o(sL)`.

**Proof.** Apply (4.7) at `k=m+h`, use `deg J<sk`, and evaluate at `n_h` with `log n_h=d_0m+log m+O(1)`. The degree term is

# 5. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: exact F-adic digit recurrence

The all-order tower has a much more rigid algebraic description.

### Theorem 5.1 — one-step digit recurrence

For every `k>=2` and `s>=1`, there is a unique polynomial `R_{k,s}(x) in Q[x]` with
\[
\deg R_{k,s}<k
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
J_{k,s+1}(x)-J_{k,s}(x)=F_k(x)^sR_{k,s}(x).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
All denominator primes of `R_{k,s}` are at most `k`, and a common denominator/height bound of the form
\[
\log D_{k,s}+\log H(D_{k,s}R_{k,s})
\le Csk\log(k+s+2)
\tag{5.2}
\]
holds for some absolute `C`.

**Proof.** The two interpolants have identical jets of order `<s` at every root, so their difference is divisible by `prod_r(x-r)^s=(k!F_k)^s`, hence by `F_k^s` in `Q[x]`. The difference has degree `<(s+1)k`, while `F_k^s` has degree `sk`, giving `deg R<k`. Uniqueness is immediate. The prime-support and crude height bound follow from Corollary 4.2, polynomial division by `F_k^s`, and Gauss-lemma clearing; every denominator introduced by `F_k` is supported on primes `<=k`. QED.

The recurrence is not accidental Hermite bookkeeping. Let
\[
\widehat A_k:=\varprojlim_s \mathbb Q[x]/(F_k^s)
\]
be the `F_k`-adic completion. The compatible system `J_{k,s}` converges to an element `mathcal J_k in widehat A_k`. Put
\[
\Theta_k:=x-F_k(x)\mathcal J_k.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Under the CRT identification with the product of the `k` local completions at `x=r`, formula (4.4) says `mathcal J_k=(x-r)/F_k(x)` in the `r`-factor. Hence `Theta_k=r` in that factor.

### Theorem 5.2 — explicit algebraic closure

The limit `mathcal J_k` is the unique `F_k`-adic solution, congruent to `J_{k,1}` modulo `F_k`, of
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_k(x,Y):=\frac{F_k(x-F_k(x)Y)}{F_k(x)}=0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
Moreover
\[
\Phi_k(x,Y)
=
1-F_k'(x)Y+
\sum_{j=2}^k
\frac{(-1)^j}{j!}F_k(x)^{j-1}F_k^{(j)}(x)Y^j,
\tag{5.5}
\]
and over `Q(x)` it factors as
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_k(x,Y)
=
\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}
\left(1-\frac{F_k(x)}{x-r}Y\right),
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
so its `Y`-roots are exactly
\[
Y_r=\frac{x-r}{F_k(x)}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

**Proof.** In each local factor, `Theta_k=r`, hence `F_k(Theta_k)=0`, proving (5.4). Expanding `F_k(x-F_kY)` in Taylor series gives (5.5). Direct factorization of the numerator gives (5.6). Modulo `F_k`, the derivative of `Phi_k` in `Y` is `-F_k'(x)`, which is invertible in `Q[x]/(F_k)` because `F_k` has simple roots. Hensel uniqueness gives the specified `F_k`-adic branch. QED.

Thus `R_{k,s}` are literally the `F_k`-adic digits of one explicit algebraic root-selector. There is no hidden infinite-dimensional local state.

Now define the global row sums
\[
S_s:=\sum_{h\in\{0,1,2,4\}}\sigma_hJ_{k_h,s}(n_h),
\qquad
E_s:=\sum_h\sigma_hR_{k_h,s}(n_h).
\tag{5.8}
\]
Since `F_{k_h}(n_h)=t` in every represented row, (5.1) gives the exact global recurrence
\[
\boxed{S_{s+1}=S_s+t^sE_s.}
\tag{5.9}
\]
Work in the localization obtained by inverting primes `<=m+3`, so every clean component is still nonunit. Put
\[
U:=t/W^\circ,
\qquad
H_s:=S_s/(W^\circ)^s.
\]
The all-order divisibility theorem makes `H_s` integral in this localization, and (5.9) becomes
\[
\boxed{
W^\circ H_{s+1}=H_s+U^sE_s.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

There is an even more revealing normalization. For `s>=1`, set
\[
A_s:=\frac{S_s}{t^{s-1}}.
\tag{5.11}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{A_{s+1}=E_s+\frac{A_s}{t}.}
\tag{5.12}
\]
At every clean `p^f`,
\[
v_p(A_s)\ge sf-(s-1)f=f.
\tag{5.13}
\]
So after removing the obvious `t^{s-1}` factor, **every jet level carries exactly the same forced first-power clean-core divisibility**:
\[
\boxed{W^\circ\mid A_s}
\tag{5.14}
\]
in the small-prime localization.

### B19 outcome

The tower is an `F_k`-adic Hensel expansion of the exact local root selector `(x-r)/F_k(x)`. # 6. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: what a generic Hermite–Padé relation can and cannot buy

The recurrence makes the natural Hermite–Padé object finite-dimensional. Define
\[
\mathbf R_s:=
(R_{m,s},R_{m+1,s},R_{m+2,s},R_{m+4,s}).
\]
It lies in
\[
V_m=
\mathbb Q[x]_{<m}
\oplus\mathbb Q[x]_{<m+1}
\oplus\mathbb Q[x]_{<m+2}
\oplus\mathbb Q[x]_{<m+4},
\]
whose dimension is
\[
\boxed{\dim V_m=4m+7.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 — exact finite-dimensional jet dependence

Among any `4m+8` digit vectors `mathbf R_s` there is a nontrivial rational relation
\[
\boxed{\sum_s\lambda_s\mathbf R_s=0.}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\sum_s\lambda_sE_s=0
\tag{6.3}
\]
for every exact represented quartet, and the normalized auxiliaries satisfy the exact recurrence
\[
\boxed{
t\sum_s\lambda_sA_{s+1}
=
\sum_s\lambda_sA_s.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

**Proof.** Equation (6.2) is linear dependence in the `(4m+7)`-dimensional space `V_m`. Evaluation at the four `n_h` and signed summation gives (6.3). Substitute `E_s=A_{s+1}-A_s/t` from (5.12) and multiply by `t`. QED.

This is the first exact multi-level compression, but the generic dependence is too expensive arithmetically. If one forms (6.2) from the first `4m+8` levels and clears denominators using the uniform bounds above, the coefficient matrix has logarithmic height `O(m^2 log m)`; a naive cofactor/Cramer relation therefore has coefficient logarithmic height as large as
\[
O(m^3\log m),
\tag{6.5}
\]
far beyond the natural source scale `L=Theta(m^2)`. Thus the existence of a dimension relation by itself gives no product-formula contradiction.

The same obstruction appears in a partial-degree cancellation strategy. To **guarantee by raw dimension count** cancellation of the top `d` coefficients in all four row polynomials requires solving up to `4d` scalar equations, hence at least `4d+1` jet levels. The uniform denominator/height bound for those levels grows with the largest level. For `d` proportional to `m` this crude cost is larger than the entire `Theta(m^2)` Archimedean degree gain; even for `d` near `m/log m`, the present generic height estimate is not competitive with the desired `Theta(L/log m)` saving.

This does **not** prove that a specially structured Hermite–Padé relation cannot work. It proves that a generic Siegel/dimension argument is insufficient. Any successful B24 relation must use the explicit algebraic equation (5.4) to obtain coefficients vastly smaller than generic minors.

### B24 outcome

The extremal object is no longer an arbitrary high-order interpolant. It is a **low-height structured relation among the `F_k`-adic digits of the explicit root-selector algebraic equation**. Generic multi-level Padé dependence exists exactly, but its naive height is too large.
