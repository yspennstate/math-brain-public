## 2. Preserved setup: the exact old-slot factor-two barrier

Put

\[
L:=\log t,
\qquad
M:=\max\{m:2\le m\le n/2,\ \binom nm=t\},
\]

and let the common `M`-rough core be

\[
W:=W_M(t)=\prod_{\substack{p\mid t\\p>M}}p^{v_p(t)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For every represented left-half row `(n_i,m_i)` and every complete rough prime-power component

\[
Z_p:=p^{v_p(t)}\mid W,
\]

there is a unique residue

\[
0\le r_{i,p}<m_i
\quad\text{such that}\quad
Z_p\mid n_i-r_{i,p}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Order consecutive represented deep-edge rows by

\[
m_i<m_{i+1},\qquad n_i>n_{i+1},
\]

and write

\[
h_i:=m_{i+1}-m_i,
\qquad
D_i:=n_i-n_{i+1}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The preceding report proves, in the regime `n_{i+1}>=2m_{i+1}^2`, that `D_i>=m_{i+1}` and that if a rough component stays in an old slot at row `i+1`, then its residue difference lies in

\[
-(m_i-1)\le r_{i,p}-r_{i+1,p}\le m_i-1.
\]

Grouping by this difference produced exactly `2m_i-1` possible old-slot bins. Pairwise arithmetic therefore permits the old rough mass to be spread across `2m_i-1` integers `D_i-s`, causing the previously isolated factor-two loss.

The present run asks whether near-saturation of all those bins can persist for the **same labeled rough components** over two consecutive transitions.

## 3. B9 RANDOMIZE — the discovery model says saturation is highly non-generic

This method is used only to discover the structure to prove; it is not evidence for the source theorem.

Take two independent uniformly random residues `R,S in {0,...,m-1}`. Their difference has the triangular law

\[
\Pr(R-S=d)=\frac{m-|d|}{m^2},
\qquad -(m-1)\le d\le m-1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\Pr(R-S=0)=\frac1m.
\tag{3.2}
\]

But if the exact arithmetic old-slot product bound is near saturation, the normalized log-mass allowed in each of the `2m-1` difference bins is only approximately

\[
\frac1{2m-1}\sim\frac1{2m}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus independent random placements overload the central difference bin by a factor asymptotic to **2**. Randomness does not explain saturation; saturation demands a strongly anti-correlated transport of the rough components between consecutive rows.

This points to the correct deterministic statistic: not entropy of the residue marginals alone, but the second moment of `R+S-(m-1)`. If the residue marginals are almost flat while the difference law is also almost flat, then the two residues must lie close to the anti-diagonal `R+S=m-1`.

No random simulation or artifact was needed: (3.1) is exact.

## 4. B10 DERANDOMIZE — a flat-difference transport rigidity lemma

### Lemma 4.1 — quantitative anti-diagonal rigidity

Let `mu` be a probability distribution on

\[
\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}^2,
\]

with coordinate marginals `a_r`, `b_s` and difference law

\[
p_d:=\mu\{(r,s):r-s=d\},
\qquad -(m-1)\le d\le m-1.
\]

Assume for some `alpha,beta>=0` that

\[
a_r\le\frac{1+\alpha}{m},
\qquad
b_s\le\frac{1+\alpha}{m},
\tag{4.1}
\]

for every `r,s`, and

\[
p_d\le\frac{1+\beta}{2m-1}
\tag{4.2}
\]

for every `d`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\mu\bigl(R+S-(m-1)\bigr)^2
\le
\frac{m-1}{3}+(\alpha+\beta)(m-1)^2.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular, if `m->infinity` and `alpha+beta->0`, then

\[
\frac1{m^2}\mathbb E_\mu\bigl(R+S-(m-1)\bigr)^2\to0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
c:=\frac{m-1}{2},\qquad X:=R-c,\qquad Y:=S-c.
\]

The uniform distribution on `0,...,m-1` has second centered moment

\[
V_m:=\frac1m\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(r-c)^2=\frac{m^2-1}{12}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

If a probability vector on `m` points is bounded above by `(1+alpha)/m`, its positive deviation from uniform has total mass at most `alpha`; hence its total-variation distance from uniform is at most `alpha`. Since `0<=X^2<=c^2`, (4.1) gives

\[
\mathbb E X^2\le V_m+\alpha c^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E Y^2\le V_m+\alpha c^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Likewise, the uniform law on the `2m-1` differences has second moment

\[
\frac1{2m-1}\sum_{d=-(m-1)}^{m-1}d^2
=\frac{m(m-1)}3.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Condition (4.2) places the difference law within total variation at most `beta` of this uniform law, so

\[
\mathbb E(X-Y)^2=\mathbb E(R-S)^2
\ge
\frac{m(m-1)}3-\beta(m-1)^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Using

\[
(X+Y)^2+(X-Y)^2=2X^2+2Y^2
\]

and (4.6)-(4.8),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(X+Y)^2
&\le4V_m+\alpha(m-1)^2-rac{m(m-1)}3+\beta(m-1)^2\\
&=\frac{m-1}{3}+(\alpha+\beta)(m-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since `X+Y=R+S-(m-1)`, this is (4.3). `QED`

The lemma is completely deterministic. The factor-two-capacity saturation forces anti-diagonal transport because simultaneous near-flatness of two marginals and their difference distribution leaves essentially no variance for their sum.

## 5. Arithmetic translation of Lemma 4.1

Fix one transition `(n,m)->(n',m')` with

\[
m'=m+h,\qquad D=n-n'>0.
\]

Let `O` be the set of rough prime-power components whose later residue remains in an old slot:

\[
\mathcal O:=\{p>M:r'_{p}<m\},
\qquad
W_{\rm old}:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal O} Z_p,
\qquad
A:=\log W_{\rm old}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Give each component `Z_p` normalized weight

\[
w_p:=\frac{\log Z_p}{A}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This makes the pair `(r_p,r'_p)` a probability distribution on `{0,...,m-1}^2`.

### Marginal caps

For each source residue `r`, the product of all old components placed at `r` divides `n-r`, hence

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal O:r_p=r}w_p\le\frac{\log n}{A}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Similarly, for every later old residue `s<m`,

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal O:r'_p=s}w_p\le\frac{\log n'}{A}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Define the marginal slack

\[
\alpha:=\max\left\{\frac{m\log n}{A}-1,\frac{m\log n'}{A}-1,0\right\}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Then (5.3)-(5.4) have exactly the form (4.1).

### Difference-bin cap

For a fixed difference `d=r_p-r'_p`, every corresponding component divides

\[
D-d.
\]

Components are pairwise coprime, so the product in that difference class divides `D-d`. Since `D>=m'`, all these factors are positive, and

\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal O:r_p-r'_p=d}w_p
\le
\frac{\log(D+m-1)}{A}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Define the **old-slot saturation excess**

\[
\boxed{
\sigma:=\frac{(2m-1)\log(D+m-1)}{A}-1\ge0.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Then (5.6) has exactly the form (4.2) with `beta=sigma`.

Consequently Lemma 4.1 gives the exact deterministic estimate

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{W_{\rm old}}
\bigl(r_p+r'_p-(m-1)\bigr)^2
\le
\frac{m-1}{3}+(\alpha+\sigma)(m-1)^2.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is the promised derandomization of the discovery model.

### Close deep-edge specialization

In the surviving extremal edge, prior reports give

\[
\log W=(1-o(1))L,
\qquad
m\log m=o(L),
\qquad
\log n=\frac Lm+O(\log m).
\tag{5.9}
\]

If additionally `h=o(m)`, the new-slot rough product satisfies

\[
\log W_{\rm new}\le h\log n'=o(L),
\tag{5.10}
\]

so

\[
A=\log W_{\rm old}=(1-o(1))L.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Because `m'=m+o(m)`, (5.9)-(5.11) imply

\[
\alpha=o(1).
\tag{5.12}
\]

Therefore a close transition with `sigma=o(1)` has

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{m^2}
\mathbb E_{W_{\rm old}}
\bigl(r_p+r'_p-(m-1)\bigr)^2=o(1).
}
\tag{5.13}
\]

Near saturation forces the rough profile to travel almost entirely along the anti-diagonal.

## 6. B26 SPORADIC CASE — two consecutive near-saturations are impossible

The anti-diagonal description becomes rigid when the same rough components are followed through a third represented row.

### Theorem 6.1 — no double saturation on consecutive close deep-edge transitions

Consider a sequence `t->infinity` admitting three consecutive represented nontrivial left-half rows

\[
(n_0,m_0),\quad(n_1,m_1),\quad(n_2,m_2),
\qquad
m_0<m_1<m_2,
\tag{6.1}
\]

with

\[
\binom{n_0}{m_0}=\binom{n_1}{m_1}=\binom{n_2}{m_2}=t.
\]

Assume the surviving deep-edge hypotheses

\[
n_j\ge2m_j^2,\qquad m_j\to\infty,\qquad m_j\log m_j=o(L),
\tag{6.2}
\]

and the close-transition hypotheses

\[
h_0:=m_1-m_0=o(m_0),
\qquad
h_1:=m_2-m_1=o(m_1).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Assume also the already-established extremal rough-core condition

\[
\log W=(1-o(1))L.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For each transition define the old-slot saturation excess `sigma_i` by (5.7), using its own `m_i,D_i,W_{{\rm old},i}`. Then it is impossible that

\[
\boxed{\sigma_0\to0\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_1\to0.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Equivalently: two consecutive close deep-edge transitions cannot both asymptotically attain the `2m-1` old-slot capacity.

### Proof

By (5.10), each transition loses only `o(L)` rough log-mass to newly opened slots. Hence each old set carries `1-o(1)` of the global rough log-mass. Put the global probability weight

\[
\omega_p:=\frac{\log Z_p}{\log W}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

From (5.13), and because the omitted new-slot mass is `o(1)` while every residue is `O(m_i)`, the first near-saturated transition gives, now under the **global** weight,

\[
\mathbb E_{\omega}
\bigl(r_{0,p}+r_{1,p}-(m_0-1)\bigr)^2=o(m_0^2).
\tag{6.7}
\]

The second gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\omega}
\bigl(r_{1,p}+r_{2,p}-(m_1-1)\bigr)^2=o(m_1^2)=o(m_0^2).
\tag{6.8}
\]

Subtract the two anti-diagonal errors. Since `m_1-m_0=h_0`, (6.7)-(6.8) imply

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega}
\bigl(r_{0,p}-r_{2,p}+h_0\bigr)^2=o(m_0^2).
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Choose a positive sequence `eta->0` slowly enough that the rough components satisfying

\[
|r_{0,p}-r_{2,p}+h_0|\le \eta m_0
\tag{6.10}
\]

carry total global weight `1-o(1)`; this follows from (6.9) and Markov's inequality. Let `G` denote this set and

\[
W_G:=\prod_{p\in G}Z_p.
\]

Then

\[
\log W_G=(1-o(1))\log W=(1-o(1))L.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Write

\[
H:=n_0-n_2=D_0+D_1.
\]

For every `p in G`, the component `Z_p` divides both `n_0-r_{0,p}` and `n_2-r_{2,p}`. Therefore

\[
Z_p\mid H-(r_{0,p}-r_{2,p}).
\]

Put

\[
e_p:=r_{0,p}-r_{2,p}+h_0.
\]

By (6.10), `|e_p|<=eta m_0`, and

\[
Z_p\mid H+h_0-e_p.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Group the pairwise coprime components in `G` by the integer `e_p`. Then

\[
\boxed{
W_G
\mid
\prod_{|e|\le \eta m_0}(H+h_0-e).
}
\tag{6.13}
\]

The previous deep-edge separation theorem gives `D_0>=m_1` and `D_1>=m_2`, so `H+h_0` is `>2m_0` for large `t`; no factor in (6.13) vanishes. Also `H<=n_0`, and all factors are at most `2n_0` eventually. Hence

\[
\log W_G
\le
(2\eta m_0+O(1))\log(2n_0).
\tag{6.14}
\]

But from the elementary binomial bounds

\[
\left(\frac{n_0}{m_0}\right)^{m_0}\le t\le\left(\frac{e n_0}{m_0}\right)^{m_0},
\]

we have

\[
\log n_0=\frac L{m_0}+O(\log m_0).
\tag{6.15}
\]

Using `m_0 log m_0=o(L)` and `eta->0`, (6.14)-(6.15) yield

\[
\log W_G=o(L),
\]

contradicting (6.11). Therefore (6.5) is impossible. `QED`

### What the theorem means

The pairwise `2m-1` bound was not merely loose bookkeeping: if one transition comes close to saturating all `2m-1` difference bins, its rough components are forced into an anti-diagonal residue transport. The next close transition would force another anti-diagonal transport. Composing them makes the two-step residue displacement nearly deterministic,

\[
r_{0,p}-r_{2,p}\approx-h_0,
\]

which collapses the rough core into only `o(m_0)` possible divisors of integers near `n_0-n_2+h_0`. That is arithmetically impossible because the rough core has logarithmic mass `(1-o(1))L` while `o(m_0)` such integers carry only `o(L)` logarithmic capacity.

Thus **persistent factor-two saturation is itself a sporadic rigid pattern, and that pattern cannot occur on two consecutive close transitions.**
