# 5. B25 — count the same thing twice: a positive-density distinct-offset matching

The divisibilities (3.7) give capacity bounds separately in the two offset coordinates. The previous work used those bounds only to remove small coordinate neighborhoods. Here I use both coordinates simultaneously.

## 5.1 Weighted degree bounds

Form a bipartite multigraph `G_m` as follows.

- The left vertices are the integers `d` occurring among components of `R_half`.
- The right vertices are the integers `e` occurring among components of `R_half`.
- Each distinguished component `Z` gives one edge from `d(Z)` to `e(Z)`, with weight
\[
w(Z)=\log Z.
\]

Different components have distinct prime bases and hence are pairwise coprime.

Fix a left vertex `d`. By (3.7), every incident component divides the same integer `a-d`. Hence the product of all incident components divides `a-d`, and therefore
\[
\sum_{Z:d(Z)=d}\log Z\le\log|a-d|.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Likewise, for every right vertex `e`,
\[
\sum_{Z:e(Z)=e}\log Z\le\log|b-e|.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Because all residue differences are `O(m)` while `a,b` are exponential in `m`, (3.4) gives a uniform bound
\[
M_m:=\max\{\log|a-d|,\log|b-e|\}
=d_0m+\log m+O(1).
\tag{5.3}
\]
Thus every vertex of `G_m` has total incident **weight** at most `M_m`.

## 5.2 Fractional matching and integrality

For each edge/component define
\[
x_Z:=\frac{w(Z)}{M_m}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Equations (5.1)--(5.3) imply that the sum of `x_Z` over all edges incident to any left or right vertex is at most one. Therefore `x` is a feasible fractional matching.

For completeness, the needed integrality is elementary. Direct every bipartite edge from its left endpoint to its right endpoint, add a source with capacity one to each left vertex and a sink edge of capacity one from each right vertex, and give each original edge capacity one. The fractional matching is a feasible fractional flow of value `sum_Z x_Z`. All capacities are integers, so the max-flow integrality theorem supplies an integral flow of at least the same value. Such an integral flow is exactly a matching.

Hence there exists a matching `M` with
\[
|M|\ge\sum_{Z\in R_{\rm half}}x_Z
=
\frac{\sum_{Z\in R_{\rm half}}\log Z}{M_m}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Using (4.8), (5.3), and `L=d_0m^2+O(m)`, we obtain
\[
\boxed{
|M|
\ge
\left(\frac{3\delta_{\rm cyc}}8-o(1)\right)m
=
(0.0154330329392367\ldots-o(1))m.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus a positive-density subfamily of distinguished independent circuits can be chosen so that
\[
\boxed{
d(Z)\text{ are pairwise distinct and }e(Z)\text{ are pairwise distinct.}
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
Every selected component still lies in the prime window (4.9), is cofactor-light, half-scale, entropy-separated, and has a one-dominant nonzero signed-Pascal defect.

## 5.3 Product-gcd packaging with no coordinate congestion

Write
\[
D_M=\{d(Z):Z\in M\},
\qquad
E_M=\{e(Z):Z\in M\},
\qquad
\mathcal P_M=\prod_{Z\in M}Z.

Because the `d`-coordinates are distinct and each `Z|a-d(Z)`, while the `e`-coordinates are distinct and each `Z|b-e(Z)`, one has the exact simultaneous divisibility

\boxed{
