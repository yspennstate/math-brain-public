# 4. B14 — find the group/action: signed-Pascal symmetry, and the scale mismatch it exposes

The first useful step is to write the exact action carried by the signed Pascal coefficients rather than treating (3.4) as a black-box four-variable table.

### Lemma 4.1 (reflection and Pascal transport)

Extend `kappa_{k,r}` by zero outside `0<=r<=k-1`. Then
\[
\kappa_{k,k-1-r}=(-1)^{k-1}\kappa_{k,r},
\tag{4.1}
\]
and
\[
\kappa_{k+1,r}=\frac{k+1}{k}\bigl(\kappa_{k,r-1}-\kappa_{k,r}\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** Reflection follows from binomial symmetry:
\[
\kappa_{k,k-1-r}=(-1)^r k\binom{k-1}{r}
=(-1)^{k-1}\kappa_{k,r}.
\]
For (4.2), Pascal's identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{k+1,r}
&=(-1)^{k-r}(k+1)\left(\binom{k-1}{r-1}+\binom{k-1}{r}\right)\\
&=\frac{k+1}{k}\left(\kappa_{k,r-1}-\kappa_{k,r}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The boundary cases are included by the zero extension. ∎

Thus reflection reduces absolute-value orbit types, while row transport is a discrete derivative. But the parity twist in (4.1) means reflecting different rows does not preserve `D_m` up to a single global sign: the `m,m+2,m+4` rows share one reflection sign and the `m+1` row has the opposite sign. So there is no fourfold reflection quotient that turns (3.4) into a one-quadrant positive equation.

The more important B14 consequence is a scale comparison that was missed in the previous run.

\tag{4.3}

Consequently the implication `Z>B_m => D_m=0` from the previous run has empty premise asymptotically on this source-relevant branch. In particular, the exact zero-defect scan, while a correct finite statement, does **not** by itself constrain the asymptotic plastic resonance.

**Proof.** Consider one representation `t=binom(n,k)` with `k<=M<p`. Since `p>k`, the denominator `k!` is coprime to `p`. Among the `k` consecutive numerator factors

# 5. B3 — prove something stronger: why pairwise signed-Pascal separation is false

A natural response to Proposition 4.2 would be to try to sharpen (3.5) drastically by proving that nearby signed-Pascal rows have no exact coincidences. That stronger route is false in an infinite Pell family.

### Proposition 5.1 (infinitely many adjacent-row signed-Pascal collisions)

For integers `m,r` with `1<=r<m`,
\[
\kappa_{m,r}=\kappa_{m+1,r-1}
\tag{5.1}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
r(m+1)=(m-r)(m-r+1).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Writing
\[
y^2=5r^2+2r+1,
\tag{5.3}
\]
this is equivalent to the negative Pell equation
\[
x^2-5y^2=-4,
\qquad x=5r+1,
\tag{5.4}
\]
with
\[
m=\frac{3r-1+y}{2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
There are infinitely many admissible solutions. One explicit infinite subfamily is generated by
\[
x_j+y_j\sqrt5=(11+5\sqrt5)(161+72\sqrt5)^j,
\qquad j\ge0,
\tag{5.6}
\]
for which `x_j ≡ 1 (mod 5)`, `r_j=(x_j-1)/5` and (5.5) is integral. The first terms are
\[
(r,m)=(2,5),(714,1869),(229970,602069),\ldots.
\]

**Proof.** The two signs in (5.1) agree, because their sign exponents differ by two. Taking the ratio of absolute values,
\[
\frac{|\kappa_{m+1,r-1}|}{|\kappa_{m,r}|}
=\frac{(m+1)\binom{m}{r-1}}{m\binom{m-1}{r}}
=\frac{(m+1)r}{(m-r)(m-r+1)}.
\]
Thus equality is exactly (5.2). Put `s=m-r`. Then (5.2) is
\[
s^2+(1-r)s-r(r+1)=0,
\]
whose discriminant must be
\[
y^2=(1-r)^2+4r(r+1)=5r^2+2r+1.
\]
The positive root gives (5.5). Multiplying (5.3) by five and completing the square yields
\[
(5r+1)^2-5y^2=-4,
\]
which is (5.4).

Finally `161^2-5\cdot72^2=1`, so multiplication by `161+72sqrt(5)` preserves norm `-4`. Starting from `(x_0,y_0)=(11,5)`, recurrence
\[
x_{j+1}=161x_j+360y_j,
\qquad y_{j+1}=72x_j+161y_j
\]
preserves `x_j≡1 (mod 5)`, oddness of `x_j,y_j`, positivity, and strict growth. Hence each gives an admissible positive integer `r_j`, and (5.5) is integral. ∎

This proposition does **not** produce a zero of the four-term defect `D_m`; it proves something more diagnostic: any B3 proof of `D_m != 0` cannot be based on pairwise separation of adjacent signed-Pascal rows. The coefficient array already contains infinite exact Pell-type cancellations.

A second tempting strengthening — “every nonzero defect is much smaller than the central binomial envelope” — is likewise unsupported: (4.2) shows that differences of neighboring rows naturally retain central-scale coefficients. A source-valid defect bound must use the **coupled residue constraints from a common binomial value**, not only the coefficient table.

**B3 outcome.** The desired all-`m` nonzero-defect statement remains unproved, and its simplest naive proof strategy is rigorously blocked by an infinite Pell family. No claim is made that four-term zero defects exist infinitely often.

### Lemma 6.1 (low-entropy residue slots have plastic-scale coefficients)

Fix `epsilon>0`. If
\[

\]
satisfies `H(alpha(r,k))<=d_0-epsilon`, then
\[
|\kappa_{k,r}|\le k\exp((k-1)(d_0-\epsilon)).
\tag{6.3}
\]
Consequently, if all four residues in `D_m` lie in such low-entropy slots (with the obvious row-specific normalization), then
\[
|D_m|\le \operatorname{poly}(m)\exp((d_0-\epsilon)m).
\tag{6.4}
\]
Thus any divisor `Z>=exp((d_0-\epsilon/2)m)` of such a nonzero defect is impossible for all sufficiently large `m`; a component at that near-maximal scale would force `D_m=0`.

**Proof.** The standard entropy bound
\[
\binom nr\le \exp(nH(r/n))
\]
and binomial symmetry give (6.3). Summing the four bounds, noting that the row lengths differ from `m` by at most four, yields (6.4) after absorbing fixed shifts and the leading `k` factors into `poly(m)`. The final statement follows by comparing logarithms. ∎

So there is a real “typical instead of all” mechanism if the common rough mass is forced into simultaneous edge slots. The obstruction is that the already available **one-coordinate slot marginals do not force such a four-way intersection**.

Indeed, at the critical entropy level the union of the two edge intervals occupies asymptotic slot fraction only

Even with perfectly uniform marginals in all four residue coordinates, the Fréchet lower bound for the probability that all four coordinates land in a set of mass `a` is only
\[
\max(0,4a-3).
\]
For `a=2alpha_0`, this is zero. One may explicitly couple four uniform coordinates by cyclic shifts so that at least one coordinate always lies outside the edge set. Therefore **marginal flatness/capacity alone cannot force positive rough mass into the simultaneous low-entropy region** needed by Lemma 6.1.

**B28 outcome.** Plastic-scale entropy localization is viable only after obtaining joint residue structure. “Typical slot” arguments based on four independent marginals can be coupled adversarially to avoid the useful region altogether.
