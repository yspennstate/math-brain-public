# 4. B11 — dualize: defect-value incidence and a squared quotient-gap determinant

## 4.1 One nonzero defect value has finite logarithmic capacity

Let the pairwise-coprime components of `W_*` be `Z_ell`. Associate to each its integer defect `D_ell` from (3.11), and let
\[
\Lambda_0:=\sum_{D_\ell=0}\log Z_\ell,
\qquad
\mathcal V:=\{D_\ell\ne0\}.
\]
Here `mathcal V` means the set of **distinct nonzero integer values**, not the multiset of components.

### Lemma 4.1 (dual defect-capacity inequality)
For every fixed nonzero integer `d`,
\[
\prod_{\ell:D_\ell=d}Z_\ell\mid d.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\log W_* -\Lambda_0
\le \sum_{d\in\mathcal V}\log|d|.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Using the preserved crude envelope `|D_m|<=(23m+74)2^(m-1)`, this gives
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal V|\ge
\left((1-\eta)\frac{d_0}{\log2}-o(1)\right)m
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
whenever `Lambda_0<=eta L`.

**Proof.** Every `Z_ell` assigned to `d` divides `d`, and distinct rough components are powers of distinct primes, hence pairwise coprime. Their product divides `d`, proving (4.1). Summing logarithms over distinct `d` proves (4.2). The envelope gives `log|d|<=(log2)m+O(log m)`, while (3.2) and (3.8) give `log W_*=(1-o(1))d_0m^2`. Rearrangement yields (4.3). ∎

Numerically
\[
\frac{d_0}{\log2}=0.4056852313\ldots .
\]
Thus a nonzero-defect compression theorem must do more than replace the raw `O(m^3)` residue count by `O(m)`: a linear number of distinct defect values is still compatible with essentially all rough logarithmic mass.

## 4.2 Eliminating the common unit gives a square-divisibility invariant

Set, for one rough component,
\[
d:=r_0-r_1,
\qquad e:=r_1-r_2,
\tag{4.4}
\]
and
\[
\delta_{01}:=\kappa_{m,r_0}-\kappa_{m+1,r_1},
\qquad
\delta_{12}:=\kappa_{m+1,r_1}-\kappa_{m+2,r_2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
From `n_j=ZQ_j+r_j`,
\[
a-d=Z(Q_0-Q_1),
\qquad
b-e=Z(Q_1-Q_2).
\tag{4.6}
\]
By (3.6),
\[
Q_0-Q_1\equiv U\delta_{01}\pmod Z,
\qquad
Q_1-Q_2\equiv U\delta_{12}\pmod Z.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Eliminate the same unit `U`.

### Lemma 4.2 (quotient-gap determinant)
For every rough component in the collapsed subcore,
\[
\boxed{
Z^2\mid R_m:=(a-d)\delta_{12}-(b-e)\delta_{01}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

**Proof.** Put `A=Q_0-Q_1=(a-d)/Z` and `B=Q_1-Q_2=(b-e)/Z`. From (4.7), `A delta_12-B delta_01` is divisible by `Z`. Multiplying by the outer factor `Z` gives (4.8). ∎

This is not a second Hensel digit. It is a two-row elimination identity: the extra power of `Z` comes from combining exact quotient gaps with the common residue unit.

If many components share one nonzero determinant value `R`, their product `P` satisfies `P^2|R`. Crude size control gives
\[
\log|R|\le(d_0+\log2)m+O(\log m).
\tag{4.9}
\]
Therefore, if the exact-zero `R`-mass is negligible, the same dual counting would require at least
\[
\left(\frac{2d_0}{d_0+\log2}-o(1)\right)m
=0.5772063650\ldots m
\tag{4.10}

distinct nonzero determinant values.

**B11 outcome.** Dualization gives honest capacity inequalities and a new square-divisibility invariant, but the central signed-Pascal scale still permits linearly many values. # 5. B12 — find what is conserved: shifted-gap gcds are continued-fraction objects

The exact congruences `Z|n_j-r_j` carry a simpler invariant that does not involve `kappa` at all.

For one component use `d,e` from (4.4). Then (4.6) gives
\[
Z\mid a-d,
\qquad Z\mid b-e.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Define the shifted-gap gcd
\[
G_{d,e}:=\gcd(a-d,b-e).
\tag{5.2}
\]
All rough components having the same ordered residue-gap pair `(d,e)` divide the same integer `G_{d,e}`. Because their prime bases are distinct,
\[
\boxed{
\prod_{\ell:(d_\ell,e_\ell)=(d,e)}Z_\ell\mid G_{d,e}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
So `log G_{d,e}` is the total capacity of one joint gap class.

The key new observation is that **large** `G_{d,e}` makes a rational approximation to the plastic ratio.

Write
\[
A':=\frac{a-d}{G_{d,e}},
\qquad
B':=\frac{b-e}{G_{d,e}},
\qquad \gcd(A',B')=1.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Since `|d|,|e|=O(m)` and `b/a` stays in a fixed compact subinterval of `(0,1)`,
\[
\left|\frac ba-\frac{B'}{A'}\right|
=
\frac{|eA'-dB'|}{aA'}
\le \frac{C m}{a}
\tag{5.5}
\]
for an absolute constant `C` and all sufficiently large `m`.

### Theorem 5.1 (large shifted-gap gcds lie on a sparse continued-fraction spine)
Fix `beta>1/2`. Let `mathcal P_beta` be the set of admissible residue-gap pairs `(d,e)` for which
\[
G_{d,e}>a^\beta.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Then every sufficiently large `m` satisfies
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal P_\beta|
\le
\left(\frac{(1-\beta)d_0}{\log\varphi}+o(1)\right)m,
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
where `varphi=(1+sqrt(5))/2`. Moreover the total logarithmic rough mass assigned to all these large-gcd pairs is at most
\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac{(1-\beta)d_0}{\log\varphi}+o(1)\right)L.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

**Proof.** If (5.6) holds, then by (5.4)
\[
A'\le 2a^{1-\beta}
\tag{5.9}
\]
for large `m`. Since `beta>1/2`, (5.5) is eventually smaller than `1/(2A'^2)`. Legendre's continued-fraction criterion therefore says `B'/A'` is a convergent of the rational number `b/a`.

We also need a lower bound on `A'`. The plastic number `q_0` is the unique root in `(0,1)` of
\[
P(x)=x^3+x^2-1.
\]
For a reduced rational `p/q in [0,1]`, `p/q != q_0`, the integer `q^3P(p/q)` is nonzero, while `|P'(x)|<=5` on `[0,1]`; hence
\[
\left|q_0-\frac pq\right|\ge\frac1{5q^3}.
\tag{5.10}
\]
By (3.5) and (5.5), `|q_0-B'/A'|=O(1/m)`, so (5.10) implies
\[
A'\gg m^{1/3}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Denominators of successive continued-fraction convergents grow at least as fast as Fibonacci numbers. Thus the number of convergents with denominator at most the bound (5.9) is
\[
\le\frac{(1-\beta)\log a}{\log\varphi}+O(1)
=
\left(\frac{(1-\beta)d_0}{\log\varphi}+o(1)\right)m.
\tag{5.12}
\]
For a fixed reduced convergent `p/q=B'/A'`, the condition `a-d=Gq` with `|d|=O(m)` leaves only `1+O(m/q)` possible integers `G`, and then `d,e` are determined. Using (5.11) and Fibonacci growth of successive convergent denominators, the total contribution of the `O(m/q)` terms over all relevant convergents is `o(m)`. This proves (5.7).

Finally, for a fixed pair `(d,e)`, (5.3) bounds its assigned logarithmic rough mass by
\[
\log G_{d,e}\le\log(a+O(m))=d_0m+O(\log m).
\]
Multiply this by (5.7) and use `L=d_0m^2+O(m)` to obtain (5.8). ∎

For orientation, letting `beta downarrow 1/2` gives the limiting coefficient
\[
\frac{d_0}{2\log\varphi}=0.2921785788\ldots .
\tag{5.13}
\]
The theorem is stated for each fixed `beta>1/2`; (5.13) is the limiting constant, not an endpoint assertion.

A useful auxiliary consequence is that every such large-gcd class has reduced denominator `A'>>m^(1/3)`. Equivalently, an extraordinarily large common divisor of the two shifted top gaps is forced onto a continued-fraction skeleton and cannot have bounded reduced denominator.

**B12 outcome.** The exact common-binomial geometry does compress one genuinely joint object: components with a large shared shifted-gap gcd can occupy only a linear continued-fraction spine, and their total rough mass has the explicit coefficient (5.8). This is the first compression in this branch that uses the real plastic gap ratio and the p-adic residue labels simultaneously.

# 6. B15 — break symmetry: almost two rough components per first-row slot

The B12 tail bound becomes stronger when combined with the fact that the first represented row has almost no unused logarithmic slot capacity.

For each residue slot `r=0,...,m-1`, let
\[
P_r:=\prod_{\ell:r_{0,\ell}=r}Z_\ell
\]
using only components of the collapsed core `W_*`. Pairwise coprimality and `Z_ell|n_0-r` give
\[
P_r\mid n_0-r.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\log(n_0-r)-\log W_*\ge0.
\tag{6.2}
\]
But
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\log(n_0-r)
=\log (n_0)_m
=L+\log(m!),
\]
while (3.1), the fixed-height relation-collapse loss, and (3.8) give `log W_*=(1-o(1))L`. Since `m log m=o(L)`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\bigl(\log(n_0-r)-\log P_r\bigr)=o(L).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Thus the first-row slots are asymptotically saturated in aggregate.

Fix `beta in (1/2,1)`. Call a rough component **beta-large** if
\[
Z>a^\beta.
\]
Every beta-large component has `G_{d,e}>=Z>a^beta`, so Theorem 5.1 applies. Its total logarithmic mass is at most the right side of (5.8). Since each beta-large component contributes more than `beta log a`, the number `K_beta` of beta-large components satisfies
\[
K_\beta
\le
\left(\frac{(1-\beta)d_0}{\beta\log\varphi}+o(1)\right)m.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Now consider a first-row slot containing no beta-large component and at most one smaller component. Its assigned product is at most `a^beta`, whereas uniformly in `0<=r<m`,
\[
\log(n_0-r)=\log a+O(1).
\]
Such a slot therefore contributes at least
\[
(1-\beta)\log a-O(1)
\tag{6.5}
\]
to the deficit (6.3). For fixed `beta<1`, (6.3) implies that only `o(m)` first-row slots can have this property.

Every other first-row slot not touched by a beta-large component contains at least **two distinct smaller rough components**. A beta-large component can touch only one first-row slot. Therefore, if `K` denotes the total number of distinct rough prime-power components in `W_*`,
\[
K\ge 2\bigl(m-K_\beta-o(m)\bigr)+K_\beta
=2m-K_\beta-o(m).
\]
Using (6.4),
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac Km
\ge
2-\frac{(1-\beta)d_0}{\beta\log\varphi}
\qquad(1/2<\beta<1).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Because this holds for every fixed `beta<1`, let `beta` tend to one **after** taking the liminf:
\[
\boxed{
K\ge(2-o(1))m.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

### Interpretation as a near-critical incidence graph

Build the bipartite multigraph whose left vertices are the `m` residue slots of the `m`-row, whose right vertices are the `m+1` residue slots of the `(m+1)`-row, and whose edges are the rough prime-power components, joining their two residue slots. Then (6.7) says the graph has
\[
E\ge2m-o(m)
\]
edges on `2m+1` vertices.

This is extremely close to the forest threshold `E<=V-1=2m`, but it does **not** yet force a cycle. An acyclic graph can still have exactly `2m` edges if it is a spanning tree, and the `o(m)` loss in (6.7) is much larger than the one-edge gap needed for a graph-theoretic contradiction. The same saturation argument on adjacent rows shows that an acyclic survivor would have to be a near-spanning forest with only `o(m)` low-degree defects — heuristically a small number of long path/tree pieces — but that last classification is not yet a theorem strong enough to move `rho_SM`.

**B15 outcome.** Breaking row symmetry and combining the continued-fraction tail with first-row capacity forces **near-two-component-per-slot density**. The generic nonzero-defect image problem has collapsed to a near-critical labeled bipartite-graph problem. This materially changes the remaining obligation, but it still falls one edge-scale short of a contradiction.
