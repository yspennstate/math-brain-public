# 4. B24 — normalize the cubic minor before measuring its height

The raw determinant (3.5) carries large powers of the signed-Pascal coefficients. Since every clean prime exceeds every row index, those coefficients are p-adic units and should be divided out *before* comparing p-adic gain with Archimedean size.

For a fixed triple `I={i,j,k}`, define
\[
u_h:=\frac{n_h-r_h}{\kappa_h},
\qquad
v_h:=\frac{\alpha_h}{\kappa_h}
=-(H_{r_h}-H_{k_h-1-r_h})\kappa_h,
\tag{4.1}
\]
and the normalized minor
\[
\widetilde{\mathfrak D}_I(Z)
:=\det\begin{pmatrix}
 u_i&1&v_i\\
 u_j&1&v_j\\
 u_k&1&v_k
\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{\mathfrak D_I(Z)}{\kappa_i\kappa_j\kappa_k}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Because `p>4m+8`, every `kappa_h` is a p-unit, so
\[
v_p\!\left(\widetilde{\mathfrak D}_I(Z)\right)
=v_p(\mathfrak D_I(Z))\ge3f.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Thus the cubic p-adic gain survives the normalization unchanged.

## Lemma 4.1 — one universal LCM clears all normalized entries

Let
\[

\tag{4.4}
\]
Then, for every `h in {0,1,2,4}` and every `0<=r<k_h`,
\[

\tag{4.5}
\]
Indeed Farhi's binomial-LCM identity gives
\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{0\le r<k}\binom{k-1}{r}
=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)}{k},
\]
so multiplying the row by `k` gives
\[
\operatorname{lcm}_{0\le r<k}|\kappa_{k,r}|
=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k).
\tag{4.6}
\]
Also every harmonic difference in (4.1) belongs to `Lambda^{-1} Z`, since all of its summand denominators are at most `m+3`. Consequently
\[
u_h\in\Lambda^{-1}\mathbb Z,
\qquad
v_h\in\Lambda^{-1}\mathbb Z.

Each determinant monomial contains one `u` and one `v`, so

# 6. B21 — closest literature and why it does not yet transplant

Three literature directions were checked specifically against the new aggregation problem.

### 6.1 Farhi/Nair binomial LCM identities

Bakir Farhi, *An Identity Involving the Least Common Multiple of Binomial Coefficients and Its Application*, *American Mathematical Monthly* 116 (2009), DOI `10.4169/000298909X474909`, proves
\[
\operatorname{lcm}\left\{\binom{k}{0},\ldots,\binom{k}{k}\right\}
=\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k+1)}{k+1}.
\]
Shaofang Hong, arXiv:0907.3401, explains its equivalence with Nair's earlier LCM identity. This supplies the exact normalization (4.5)-(4.7); the LCM step is **known machinery**, not a novelty claim.

### 6.2 Salberger's global determinant method

Per Salberger, *Counting rational points on projective varieties*, *Proc. London Math. Soc.* 126 (2023), DOI `10.1112/plms.12508`, develops a global version of Heath-Brown's p-adic determinant method. Its explicit novelty is to use congruences modulo many small primes simultaneously in one shared determinant. In the local determinant lemma, many rational points reducing to the same finite-field point contribute a p-adic determinant exponent that grows superlinearly with the number of points in that residue class.

That is exactly the kind of amplification one would like here, but the present geometry is different in a decisive way. A clean Singmaster component supplies one selected residue tuple attached to its *own* moving prime power `Z=p^f`. Across distinct components the prime bases are distinct. If one forms a matrix with one row per component, then at the prime attached to a given row there is no collection of many other component-rows lying in the same p-adic residue class. A direct Salberger/Heath-Brown determinant therefore gives no superlinear p-adic gain: it reduces to the product of the already-known scalar local divisibilities.

So the global determinant method identifies the missing structural ingredient rather than closing the problem: one needs a **single auxiliary object whose rows/columns are genuinely shared across many clean components**, or a cross-prime coupling that replaces the usual same-prime clustering.

### 6.3 Hypergeometric determinant calculus

Christian Krattenthaler's *Advanced Determinant Calculus* (arXiv:math/9902004; later published in *The Andrews Festschrift*) and its 2005 complement collect powerful exact determinant evaluations, including many binomial/hypergeometric matrices. No recovered identity directly aggregates the present moving selected-root minors with one different large prime modulus per row. These references remain useful for any future auxiliary determinant once a common matrix has actually been found.

### 6.4 The explicit cross-prime datum that remains unused

There is one exact coupling across the clean components which the scalar-minor product ignores. If
\[
W:=W^\flat,
\qquad U:=t/W,
\]
then for every component `Z|W`,
\[
\boxed{
T_Z:=\frac tZ=U\frac WZ.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
The global cofactor `U` has
\[
\log U=O(L/\log m),
\]
while `W/Z` is the complementary CRT cofactor. Thus the apparently independent local quotient units are not arbitrary: they are one small common factor times the canonical complementary cofactors of a single squarefree-in-components modulus `W`.

Plain CRT idempotent summation of (6.1) is tautological. But (6.1) is the most concrete surviving candidate for converting the one-point-per-prime family into a shared global object. The next run should use it together with the row-specific `kappa/alpha` data, rather than multiplying local minors independently.
