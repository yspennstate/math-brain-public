# 4. B12 — conserved quantity: exact signed-Pascal boundary charge on a fundamental circuit

Fix one simple fundamental incidence cycle. Enumerate its component vertices cyclically as
\[
Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_{\ell-1},
\]
and its shared slot vertices as
\[
s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_{\ell-1},
\]
where `s_i` is incident to `Z_i` and `Z_{i+1}` (indices modulo `ell`). The cycle uses two of the four row-slots at each component. Denote the other two, off-cycle slots of `Z_i` by `B_i`. For any slot `s=(h,r)` write
\[
K(s):=\sigma_h\kappa_{k_h,r}.
\]
Then
\[
D_i:=D(Z_i)
=K(s_{i-1})+K(s_i)+\sum_{b\in B_i}K(b),
\qquad Z_i\mid D_i.
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 4.1 Even circuits: all shared signed-Pascal terms cancel

Assume `ell` is even and put `epsilon_i=(-1)^i`. Summing (4.1) with these signs, each shared slot `s_i` appears with coefficient
\[
\epsilon_i+\epsilon_{i+1}=0,
\]
including the closing slot because `ell` is even. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\epsilon_iD_i
=
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\epsilon_i
\sum_{b\in B_i}K(b).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
This is the exact alternating **signed-Pascal boundary charge** of an even fundamental circuit.

The analogous residue identity is
\[
\sum_i\epsilon_i\sum_{b\in B_i}\sigma(b)r(b)=0,
\tag{4.3}
\]
because the component residue relation is `sum_h sigma_h r_h=X`, the shared terms cancel in exactly the same way, and `sum_i epsilon_i=0`.

## 4.2 Odd circuits: one seam survives

If `ell` is odd, choose the same `epsilon_i=(-1)^i` and take `s_{ell-1}` as the seam between `Z_{ell-1}` and `Z_0`. Every other shared term cancels, whereas the seam coefficient is `epsilon_{ell-1}+epsilon_0=2`. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\sum_i\epsilon_iD_i
=
2K(s_{\ell-1})
+
\sum_i\epsilon_i\sum_{b\in B_i}K(b).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
The residue analogue has right-hand total defect `X` rather than zero.

Thus parity is the first exact split in the circuit language: even circuits push the entire signed-Pascal charge to off-cycle slots; odd circuits retain one doubled seam term.

## 4.3 Full-slot errors give the same boundary law, not an extra modulus

Define the slot error
\[
E_s:=A_s-t\kappa_s.
\tag{4.5}
\]
By (3.8), `P_s^2|E_s`. For the four slots occupied by one component `Z_i`, the exact quotient collapse and residue law give
\[
\sum_h\sigma_h A_{s_h(Z_i)}
=\sum_h\sigma_h(n_h-r_h)=X-X=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\sum_h\sigma_hE_{s_h(Z_i)}=-tD_i.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Alternating (4.6) around an even cycle cancels the shared `E_s` exactly and leaves the off-cycle boundary errors. After substituting `E_s=A_s-t\kappa_s`, this reduces to the same boundary charge (4.2). Therefore the full-slot square powers do not, by themselves, manufacture a new global circuit modulus; this agrees with the previous generalized-CRT no-go from the shared-slot formulation.

## 4.4 Why the desired distinguished-modulus divisibility does not follow

Write `D_i=Z_i q_i`. Equation (4.2) becomes
\[
B_C=\sum_i\epsilon_iZ_iq_i,
\tag{4.7}
\]
where `B_C` is the explicit off-cycle boundary charge. For the distinguished component `Z_0`, reducing modulo `Z_0` gives
\[
B_C\equiv \sum_{i\ne0}\epsilon_iZ_iq_i\pmod{Z_0}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
The other moduli are powers of distinct primes and are coprime to `Z_0`; the component-local facts `Z_i|D_i` provide no reason for the right side of (4.8) to vanish modulo `Z_0`. Abstractly this implication is impossible from local divisibility alone: for pairwise-coprime moduli one may take `D_0=0`, `D_1=Z_1`, and all other `D_i=0`, and the alternating sum need not be divisible by `Z_0`.

So the hoped-for B12 statement
\[
Z_0\mid B_C
\]
is **not** a consequence of circuit cancellation plus the existing local divisibilities. Any proof of such a charge must use additional special structure of the signed-Pascal values / actual residues, not merely the graphic cycle and coprime moduli.

**B12 outcome:** positive exact conservation law, but negative for the intended direct charge. The shared terms can be eliminated completely on even circuits, yet the remaining divisibility is still local to different prime powers.

# 5. B20 — transfer formulation: the first genuine nondeterminism is one modular curve per component

The boundary identity suggests viewing a fundamental circuit as a transfer problem. This makes the missing theorem precise.

Fix a component `Z` on a path through the circuit. Suppose the path enters in row `j` with residue `x`, exits in a distinct row `k` with residue `y`, and the two off-path rows are `u,v` with residues `a,b`. The exact residue law is
\[
\sigma_jx+\sigma_ky+\sigma_ua+\sigma_vb=X.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Because every `sigma` is `+1` or `-1`, fixing `(x,y,a)` determines `b` uniquely:
\[
b=\sigma_v\bigl(X-\sigma_jx-\sigma_ky-\sigma_ua\bigr).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Legality only asks that this integer lie in its row range.

The signed-Pascal condition is then the single congruence
\[
\sigma_j\kappa_{k_j,x}
+\sigma_k\kappa_{k_k,y}
+\sigma_u\kappa_{k_u,a}
+\sigma_v\kappa_{k_v,b}
\equiv0\pmod Z.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Thus, after the incoming residue `x` is fixed, the local transfer does **not** determine the outgoing residue. Before (5.3) it has two free discrete variables `(y,a)`; imposing (5.3) leaves a one-equation correspondence in those two variables. In the worst case it can therefore carry a one-dimensional family of legal transitions rather than bounded branching.

There is a useful hypergeometric recurrence
\[
\frac{\kappa_{k,r+1}}{\kappa_{k,r}}
=-\frac{k-1-r}{r+1},
\tag{5.4}
\]
whenever both sides are legal. Since every clean prime base exceeds `4m+8`, all denominators in (5.4) are units modulo the base prime. However this does **not** turn (5.3) into a fixed-degree equation: the relevant residue displacement can be `Theta(m)`, so clearing the recurrence over a long move introduces `Theta(m)` factors / degree. The hypothesis `p>4m+8` alone therefore does not supply a constant root bound for the transfer correspondence.

The full-slot error version is equally explicit. If `s^-` and `s^+` are the incoming/outgoing shared slots and `b_1,b_2` the off-path slots, (4.6) gives
\[
\boxed{
\sigma(s^+)E_{s^+}
=-\sigma(s^-)E_{s^-}
-\sigma(b_1)E_{b_1}
-\sigma(b_2)E_{b_2}
-tD(Z).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
So propagation is deterministic only after the two boundary errors and local defect multiple are known. Cofactor-lightness controls the factorization of the boundary slots but not the Archimedean size of `kappa`, and therefore does not currently collapse these free boundary terms.

**B20 outcome:** the circuit algorithm fails at a precisely identified step. The accepted-forest path does not create an unbounded combinatorial search tree for mysterious reasons; it creates one modular one-dimensional transfer correspondence at each component. A source-valid bounded-branching theorem must control (5.3), or a global argument must cancel / charge the accumulated boundary variables.

# 6. B3 — stronger theorem: every distinguished closer is half-scale, hence there are linearly many independent circuits

The previous run left open a “dust” scenario: the required `delta_cyc L` rejected weight might be distributed over many small cycle-closing components. The following theorem does not eliminate dust, but it proves something that was not previously extracted: **every** distinguished rejected component is at most half-scale, so the constant rejected weight forces a linear number of explicit independent circuits.

### Theorem 6.1 — half-scale closing bound
Let `Z in R` be any rejected distinguished component in the cofactor-light descending-weight construction. Then
\[
\boxed{
\log Z\le \frac{d_0}{2}m+O(\log m).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

**Proof.** The fundamental cycle `C_Z` contains `Z` and a path in the previously accepted forest. At either of the two cycle slots incident to `Z`, say `s=(h,r)`, the next component vertex on the accepted path is some distinct accepted component `Y`. Because components were processed in nonincreasing logarithmic weight and `Y` was already accepted when `Z` was rejected,
\[
Y\ge Z
\]
as positive integers. The components are prime powers with distinct prime bases, hence `gcd(Y,Z)=1`. Both occupy the same row-residue slot, so both divide
\[
A_s=n_h-r.
\]
Therefore
\[
ZY\mid A_s,
\qquad
Z^2\le ZY\le A_s.
\]
Using the uniform plastic scale (3.1),
\[
2\log Z\le \log A_s=d_0m+O(\log m),
\]
which proves (6.1). ∎

### Corollary 6.2 — a linear independent circuit family
The number of rejected distinguished components obeys
\[
\boxed{
|R|\ge (2\delta_{\rm cyc}-o(1))m
=(0.0823095090092626\ldots-o(1))m.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Hence there are at least this many linearly independent cofactor-light fundamental incidence cycles.

**Proof.** By (3.12),
\[
\sum_{Z\in R}\log Z\ge\delta_{\rm cyc}L-o(L)

\]
Theorem 6.1 bounds every summand above by `(d_0/2)m+O(log m)`. Divide. The cycles are independent by the unique-rejected-component argument from the previous run. ∎

This converts the constant weighted obstruction into a genuine linear circuit multiplicity theorem. It does **not** assert that a positive fraction of the distinguished components have weight `Omega(m)`; many can still be much smaller than half-scale.

### Corollary 6.3 — the distinguished mass is spread over linearly many endpoint slots

\[
\log P_s\le\log A_s=d_0m+O(\log m).

Therefore the rejected family uses at least

\boxed{(\delta_{\rm cyc}-o(1))m}
\tag{6.3}

distinct charged endpoint slots.

\boxed{

The proof is the same capacity division: each component is half-scale by Theorem 6.1, while one row-slot can carry at most `d_0m+O(log m)` total component weight.
