## 2. Preserved arithmetic setup

Put

\[
L:=\log t,
\qquad
M:=\max\{m:2\le m\le n/2,\ \binom nm=t\},

and define the common `M`-rough core

\[
W:=W_M(t):=\prod_{\substack{p\mid t\\p>M}}p^{v_p(t)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For each rough component

\[
Z_p:=p^{v_p(t)}\mid W
\]

and each represented left-half row `(n_i,m_i)`, because `p>M\ge m_i`, the whole prime-power component lies in exactly one numerator factor. Thus there is a unique residue

\[
0\le r_{i,p}<m_i,
\qquad
Z_p\mid n_i-r_{i,p}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Order consecutive represented rows by

\[
m_i<m_{i+1},\qquad n_i>n_{i+1},
\]

and write

\[
h_i:=m_{i+1}-m_i,
\qquad
D_i:=n_i-n_{i+1}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The previous reports prove the following facts that are used here and are not reproved:

1. In the surviving deep edge `n_{i+1}\ge2m_{i+1}^2`, one has `D_i\ge m_{i+1}`.
2. A rough component that stays in an old slot at the later row satisfies

3. If `A_i` is the logarithmic mass of the old rough product for transition `i`, the old-slot saturation excess is

## 3. B5 — a sharper one-step transport bound for nonzero saturation excess

The preceding run compared the difference law with the completely uniform law on `2m-1` values. For quantitative optimization, the correct lower bound on the difference second moment comes from **filling the smallest absolute differences at the allowed atom cap**.

### Lemma 3.1 — capped integer law has a sharp asymptotic second-moment floor

Let `X_m` be an integer-valued random variable. Suppose

\[
\sup_d \Pr(X_m=d)
\le
\frac{1+\beta+o(1)}{2m-1},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `beta>=0` is fixed. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E X_m^2
\ge
\left(\frac{1}{3(1+\beta)^2}-o(1)\right)m^2.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Among probability laws on the integers with atom bound `q`, the second moment about zero is minimized by putting mass `q` on the available integers in increasing order of absolute value (`0,1,-1,2,-2,...`) until the unit mass is exhausted, with at most one partially filled final atom. This is the elementary rearrangement principle: moving any positive mass from a larger `|d|` to an unfilled smaller `|d|` decreases the second moment without violating the atom cap.

Here

\[
q=\frac{1+\beta+o(1)}{2m-1},
\]

so the number of essentially fully occupied atoms is

\[
q^{-1}=\frac{2m}{1+\beta}+o(m),
\]

and the filled radius is

\[
R=\frac{m}{1+\beta}+o(m).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E X_m^2
&\ge
q\left(2\sum_{j=1}^{R+O(1)}j^2\right)-o(m^2)\\
&=
\frac{1+\beta+o(1)}{2m}
\left(\frac{2}{3}R^3+O(R^2)\right)-o(m^2)\\
&=
\left(\frac{1}{3(1+\beta)^2}-o(1)\right)m^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

`QED`

### Lemma 3.2 — quantitative anti-diagonal energy

Consider one close deep-edge transition `(n,m)->(n',m+h)` and restrict to any rough product `G` of logarithmic mass `(1-o(1))L` whose components stay in old slots. Normalize each component by its logarithmic mass. Write its two residues as `R,S` and put

\[
A:=R+S-(m-1).
\]

Suppose the transition saturation excess is `sigma=O(1)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E A^2}{m^2}
\le
f(\sigma)+o(1),
\qquad
f(x):=\frac13\left(1-\frac1{(1+x)^2}\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Center the residues at `c=(m-1)/2`:

\[
U:=R-c,\qquad V:=S-c.
\]

For each residue slot, the product of the rough components occupying that slot divides one numerator factor. Since

\[
\log G=(1-o(1))L,
\qquad
m\log n=L+o(L),
\]

the normalized residue marginals satisfy

\[
\sup_r\Pr(R=r)\le\frac{1+o(1)}m,
\qquad
\sup_s\Pr(S=s)\le\frac{1+o(1)}m.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence rearrangement at the opposite end gives

\[
\mathbb E U^2,\ \mathbb E V^2
\le
\left(\frac1{12}+o(1)\right)m^2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For a difference class `d=R-S`, all corresponding pairwise-coprime rough components divide `D-d`, so from the definition of `sigma`, after restricting from the old product to `G=(1-o(1))` of its logarithmic mass,

\[
\sup_d\Pr(R-S=d)
\le
\frac{1+\sigma+o(1)}{2m-1}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Lemma 3.1 therefore yields

\[
\mathbb E(U-V)^2
=
\mathbb E(R-S)^2
\ge
\left(\frac1{3(1+\sigma)^2}-o(1)\right)m^2.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Using

\[
(U+V)^2+(U-V)^2=2U^2+2V^2
\]

and `A=U+V`, equations (3.5)-(3.7) give (3.3). `QED`

This improves the previous `O(sigma)` anti-diagonal bound to an explicit function valid for every fixed bounded saturation excess.

## 4. B12 — the two-step defect has an independent arithmetic energy floor

Take three consecutive close deep-edge represented rows

\[
(n_0,m_0),\quad(n_1,m_1),\quad(n_2,m_2),
\qquad
m_0<m_1<m_2,
\tag{4.1}
\]

with

\[
h_j:=m_{j+1}-m_j=o(m_0),
\qquad
m_j\sim m_0=:m,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and assume the surviving extremal conditions

\[
m\log m=o(L),
\qquad
\log W=(1-o(1))L.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `G` be the product of rough components that remain in old slots across **both** transitions. Each transition loses only `o(L)` logarithmic mass to its new slots, so

\[
\log G=(1-o(1))L.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Normalize the components of `G` by logarithmic mass. For each such component define

\[
A_{0,p}:=r_{0,p}+r_{1,p}-(m_0-1),
\]

\[
A_{1,p}:=r_{1,p}+r_{2,p}-(m_1-1),
\]

and the **two-step transport defect**

\[
\Delta_p:=A_{0,p}-A_{1,p}
=r_{0,p}-r_{2,p}+h_0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The upper energy bound follows from Lemma 3.2:

\[
\frac{\mathbb E\Delta_p^2}{m^2}
\le
2f(\sigma_0)+2f(\sigma_1)+o(1).
\tag{4.6}
\]

The key new observation is that `Delta_p` also has a **lower** energy bound from exact divisibility.

### Lemma 4.1 — two-step defect atom cap

For every integer `d`, the logarithmic weight of components in `G` satisfying `Delta_p=d` is at most

\[
\frac{1+o(1)}m.
\tag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

Put

\[
Q:=n_0-n_2+h_0.
\]

Since `Z_p` divides both `n_0-r_{0,p}` and `n_2-r_{2,p}`,

\[
Z_p\mid
(n_0-r_{0,p})-(n_2-r_{2,p})
=Q-\Delta_p.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The components `Z_p` are pairwise coprime across distinct rough primes. Hence the product of all components with `Delta_p=d` divides the single positive integer `Q-d`. (Deep-edge transition separation gives `Q>|d|` for all allowable defects.) Therefore that class has logarithmic mass at most

\[
\log(Q+O(m))\le\log(n_0+O(m)).
\]

But the binomial equation in the deep edge gives

\[
\log n_0=\frac L{m_0}+O(\log m_0),
\]

and `m_0\log m_0=o(L)`, while `\log G=(1-o(1))L`. Dividing by `\log G` proves (4.7). `QED`

### Corollary 4.2 — two-step defect energy floor

A probability law on the integers with atom cap `(1+o(1))/m` has minimum second moment by filling the `m+o(m)` integers nearest zero. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E\Delta_p^2}{m^2}
\ge
\frac1{12}-o(1).
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Combining (4.6) and (4.9) gives the central quantitative inequality of this run:

\[
\boxed{
f(\sigma_0)+f(\sigma_1)
\ge
\frac1{24}-o(1).
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

This is stronger than merely saying that two consecutive saturation excesses cannot both tend to zero.

## 5. B5 — optimize the saturation parameter

The function

\[
f(x)=\frac13\left(1-(1+x)^{-2}\right)
\]

is increasing and concave on `[0,\infty)`. If

\[
S:=\sigma_0+\sigma_1,
\]

then concavity gives

\[
f(\sigma_0)+f(\sigma_1)
\le
2f(S/2).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Together with (4.10),

\[
2f(S/2)\ge\frac1{24}-o(1).
\]

Ignoring the vanishing term and solving the equality exactly,

\[
\frac23\left(1-(1+S/2)^{-2}\right)=\frac1{24},
\]

so

\[
(1+S/2)^{-2}=\frac{15}{16},
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_0+\sigma_1
\ge
\frac8{\sqrt{15}}-2-o(1).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Numerically,

\[
\frac8{\sqrt{15}}-2=0.0655911179\ldots.
\]

This is an explicit quantitative version of the previous no-double-saturation theorem.

## 6. B25 — sum the same two-step obstruction along a long chain

Consider `K` consecutive close deep-edge transitions satisfying the hypotheses above uniformly, with saturation excesses

\[
\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_{K-1}.
\]

Applying (5.2) to every adjacent pair gives

\[
\sigma_i+\sigma_{i+1}
\ge
\frac8{\sqrt{15}}-2-o(1)
\qquad(0\le i\le K-2).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Summing (6.1),

\[
\sigma_0+2\sum_{i=1}^{K-2}\sigma_i+\sigma_{K-1}
\ge
(K-1)\left(\frac8{\sqrt{15}}-2-o(1)\right).
\]

Since every `sigma_i>=0`, the left side is at most `2 sum_i sigma_i`. Hence, for `K->infinity`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1K\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}\sigma_i
\ge
\frac4{\sqrt{15}}-1-o(1).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the long-chain non-saturation requested by the previous report is now quantitative and linear in the number of close transitions.

### Corollary 6.1 — average gap exponent beats one half

From the definition of `sigma_i`, `A_i=(1-o(1))L`, and `m_i->infinity`,

\[
\frac{m_i\log(D_i+m_i-1)}L
=
\frac{1+\sigma_i}{2}+o(1).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Averaging (6.3) and using (6.2),

\[
\boxed{
\frac1K\sum_{i=0}^{K-1}
\frac{m_i\log(D_i+m_i-1)}L
\ge
\frac2{\sqrt{15}}-o(1).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The pairwise factor-two barrier corresponds to coefficient `1/2`; the multi-transition arithmetic raises the average coefficient to

\[
\frac2{\sqrt{15}}=0.5163977794\ldots.
\]

Equivalently, in this averaged sense the effective old-slot exponent drops from `2` to

\[
\boxed{\frac{\sqrt{15}}2=1.9364916731\ldots<2.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is the first explicit quantitative reduction below two produced by the multi-transition route.

## 7. Why the frozen source exponent still does not move

Equation (6.4) is real progress, but it does **not** imply a fixed `rho_SM<1` in the source theorem.

Write locally

\[
x:=\frac Lm.
\]

In the surviving deep edge of this argument,

\[
m\log m=o(L),
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
x\gg\log m.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The real inverse-binomial row has

\[
\log n=x+O(\log m),
\tag{7.2}
\]

while the new averaged arithmetic information is of the form

\[
\log D\gtrsim c x,
\qquad
c=\frac2{\sqrt{15}}<1
\tag{7.3}
\]

on average over close transitions.

A pure gap-packing argument can therefore bound the number of such gaps only by roughly

\[
\frac{n}{e^{cx}}
\lesssim
m\,e^{(1-c)x+O(\log m)}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Because `1-c` is a fixed positive constant and `x\gg\log m`, the right side of (7.4) is much larger than the trivial number `m` of available lower indices. Thus a **fixed** improvement from `1/2` to `c<1` does not even beat the trivial row-count bound in the ultraflat edge, let alone certify `N(t)\ll L^rho` with fixed `rho<1`.

This precisely identifies what a continuation of this route would need: the average coefficient in (6.4) must approach **one**, not merely exceed one half by a constant. Equivalently, the average saturation excess would have to satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\frac1K\sum_i\sigma_i=1-o(1)
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

on a sufficiently long extremizing chain, or one needs a genuinely different global invariant that does not pay for transitions by Archimedean gap packing.

The current B25/B12/B5 machinery proves only the fixed lower bound `4/sqrt(15)-1`, far short of (7.5). This is the exact failure; no metric movement is claimed.
