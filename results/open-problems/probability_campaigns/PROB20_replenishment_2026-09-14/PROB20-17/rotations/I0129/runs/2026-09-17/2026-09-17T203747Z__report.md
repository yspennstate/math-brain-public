## 4. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: the full prime-power profile

The previous run bounded the largest *prime* factor in terms of `N(t)`. The same argument has a stronger invariant that uses every `p`-adic valuation at once.

### Proposition 4.1 — prime-power flatness barrier

For every integer `t>1`,

\[
\boxed{
Q(t)\le \frac{\log t}{\log 2}\,t^{2/N(t)}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently, if `Q(t)>=t^delta` for a fixed `delta>0`, then

\[
N(t)\le \frac{2}{\delta}+o(1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, every source-level refutation sequence `t_j` with `N(t_j)->infinity` must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log Q(t_j)}{\log t_j}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

So a counterexample sequence must be not only subpower-smooth; **every individual prime-power component** of `t_j` must carry `o(log t_j)` logarithmic mass.

#### Proof

Let `R(t)` be the number of left-half representations `t=binom(n,m)` with `1<=m<=n/2`. Symmetry gives `R(t)>=N(t)/2`. For fixed `m`, `binom(n,m)` is strictly increasing in `n`, hence all left-half representations have distinct lower indices. Let `M` be the largest such lower index, and write

\[
t=\binom nM,\qquad n\ge2M.
\]

Then

\[
M\ge R(t)\ge \frac{N(t)}2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

As in the prior report,

\[
t=\prod_{j=0}^{M-1}\frac{n-j}{M-j}
\ge\left(\frac nM\right)^M,
\]

so

\[
n\le M t^{1/M}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Also `t>=binom(2M,M)>=2^M`, hence

\[
M\le\frac{L}{\log2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Now fix **any** prime `p|t`, and put `a=v_p(t)`. Legendre's formula gives

\[
a=
\sum_{j\ge1}
\left(
\left\lfloor\frac n{p^j}\right\rfloor
-\left\lfloor\frac M{p^j}\right\rfloor
-\left\lfloor\frac{n-M}{p^j}\right\rfloor
\right).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Each summand is either `0` or `1`, because it is the carry indicator for adding the two real ratios whose numerators sum to `n`; and every summand vanishes when `p^j>n`. Therefore

\[
a\le\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor,
\qquad
p^a\le n.
\tag{4.8}
\]

This holds for every prime divisor of `t`, so `Q(t)<=n`. Combining (4.4)–(4.6),

\[
Q(t)\le n\le M t^{1/M}
\le \frac{L}{\log2}\,t^{2/N(t)},
\]

proving (4.1). Taking logarithms gives

\[
\log Q(t)\le \log(L/\log2)+\frac{2L}{N(t)}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Equations (4.2) and (4.3) follow immediately. `QED`

This strictly strengthens the previous report's bound using `P(t)`, since `Q(t)>=P(t)`. I found no source for this exact packaging in the bounded search; **novelty is unverified and not claimed**.

### Proposition 4.2 — one shared prime-power modulus synchronizes every representation

Let `y=P(t)` be the largest prime factor of `t`, let `a=v_y(t)`, and put `Y=y^a`. For every left-half representation

\[
t=\binom nm,
\]

we have `m<y` by Sylvester–Schur. Moreover

\[
\boxed{0\le n\bmod Y<m}
\tag{4.10}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{n\bmod (yY)\ge m.}
\tag{4.11}
\]

#### Proof

Since `m<y`, for every `r>=1` we have `m<y^r`. The `r`th Legendre summand for `v_y(binomial(n,m))` is therefore

\[
\left\lfloor\frac n{y^r}\right\rfloor
-
\left\lfloor\frac{n-m}{y^r}\right\rfloor,
\]

which equals `1` exactly when `n mod y^r<m`. If the condition holds for `r+1`, then the residue modulo `y^(r+1)` is already `<m<y<=y^r`, so the same residue occurs modulo `y^r`; hence the set of contributing `r` is an initial segment. Its cardinality is exactly `a`, so the contributing exponents are `1,...,a` and the next one does not contribute. This is exactly (4.10)–(4.11). `QED`

Thus all row indices belonging to the same repeated coefficient are constrained by the **same modulus `Y`** and lie just above multiples of `Y`, with representation-dependent error `<m`.

Combining (4.10) with the MRSTT edge approximation from the prior report,

\[
n=(m!t)^{1/m}+\frac{m-1}{2}+O(mt^{-1/m}),
\]

gives a synchronized near-lattice target:

\[
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(
(m!t)^{1/m}+\frac{m-1}{2},
Y\mathbb Z+\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
\right)
\ll m t^{-1/m}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

The important point is that `Y` is common to **all** representations of the same `t`. This is substantially stronger structure than treating each near-integer condition separately.

## 5. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: large-prime mass and the 1978 theorem

The closest classical theorem is not a generic smooth-number theorem but Ecklund–Eggleton–Erdős–Selfridge (1978). For `n>=2m`, write

\[
\binom nm=u_m v_m,
\]

where the prime factors of `u_m` are `<m` and those of `v_m` are `>=m`. Apart from their 12 explicitly determined exceptional pairs, they prove

\[
u_m<v_m.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a Singmaster representation this implies

\[
v_m>\sqrt t.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let

\[
r_m(t)=\#\{p\mid t:p\ge m\}.
\]

Proposition 4.1's pointwise argument actually showed `p^{v_p(t)}<=n` for **every** prime `p|t`. Hence

\[
v_m=\prod_{\substack{p\mid t\\p\ge m}}p^{v_p(t)}\le n^{r_m(t)}.
\]

Using (5.2) and `n<=m t^{1/m}` therefore gives the complete inequality

\[
\boxed{
r_m(t)>
\frac{L}{2(L/m+\log m)}
=
\frac{m}{2(1+m\log m/L)}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In the unresolved MRSTT edge

\[
m\le \frac{L}{(\log L)^{5/4}},
\]

we have `m log m/L=o(1)`, so every nonexceptional edge representation with `m->infinity` forces

\[
\boxed{r_m(t)\ge(1/2-o(1))m.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is a useful structural fact: a deep edge representation requires linearly many **distinct** prime divisors of the common `t` at or above its lower index.

It also explains the ceiling of a prime-support-only strategy. If `M` is the largest unresolved edge index, then (5.4) gives `omega(t)>= (1/2-o(1))M`, while the number of left-half edge representations is at most `M`. Hence this route gives only `N(t)=O(omega(t))`; the standard primorial/PNT bound `omega(t)=O(L/log L)` recovers an old logarithmic-scale saving but is weaker than Kane's `L` divided by three powers of `log log L` up to a `log log log` factor. It cannot move `rho_SM` below 1 without additional information about how the same prime set serves **many** distinct lower indices.

### Why the 2026 Erdős-684 machinery does not directly port

The new 2026 Erdős-684 papers study how the small-prime part of `binom(n,k)` changes as `k` varies for a fixed row `n`, with worst-case and density-one threshold results. Singmaster fixes `t` and varies **both** `n` and `m`. The `p`-adic carry vector of `binom(n,m)` is not monotone in `m`, and the shared-value constraint couples different rows. Thus the 684 results supply useful prime-factor technology but do not by themselves bound the number of representations of one fixed `t`.

The clean port that *does* survive is (5.3): combine their classical predecessor's large-prime-mass theorem with the conserved prime-power profile. To beat Kane one now needs to exploit the simultaneous use of the same support/valuation vector by many `(n,m)`, not only the support size for one representation.

## 6. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER: a flatness dichotomy

Proposition 4.1 suggests parameterizing the remaining problem by the largest prime-power component.

Fix `0<beta<1`. There are two branches.

### Branch A: a moderately large prime-power component

If

\[
\log Q(t)\ge L^{1-\beta},
\]

then (4.9) yields

\[
N(t)
\le
\frac{2L}{L^{1-\beta}-\log(L/\log2)}
=(2+o(1))L^\beta.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus on this branch the frozen exponent improves all the way to `beta`.

### Branch B: prime-power-flat integers

If instead

\[
\log Q(t)<L^{1-\beta},
\tag{6.2}
\]

then, writing `omega(t)` for the number of distinct prime factors,

\[
t=\prod_{p\mid t}p^{v_p(t)}\le Q(t)^{\omega(t)},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\omega(t)>L^\beta.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore for every fixed `beta in (0,1)`, either

\[
N(t)\le(2+o(1))(\log t)^\beta,
\]

or `t` lies in a very specific exceptional class: more than `(log t)^beta` distinct primes, with **every prime-power component** below `exp((log t)^(1-beta))`.

For example at `beta=1/2`, either

\[
N(t)\le(2+o(1))\sqrt{\log t},
\]

or `t` has more than `sqrt(log t)` distinct prime factors and no prime-power component reaches `exp(sqrt(log t))`.

This is a genuine power-saving conditional split, but it is **not** a global power-saving theorem because Branch B is not excluded. The maximal-order bound for `omega(t)` is still compatible with (6.3) for every fixed `beta<1`. Hence `rho_SM` remains 1.

The hard case has therefore become more rigid than merely “smooth `t`”: any hypothetical high-multiplicity sequence must be simultaneously

1. subpower-smooth;
2. prime-power-flat (`Q(t)=t^{o(1)}`);
3. supported on an increasing number of distinct primes;
4. synchronized modulo the common top prime power `Y=P(t)^{v_{P(t)}(t)}` through (4.10)–(4.12).

## 7. Failures / what did not move the metric

1. **Prime-support mass alone stops at exponent 1.** The 1978 `u<v` theorem plus the prime-power cap gives linear support consumption (5.4), but maximal `omega(t)` is still of order `L/log L`; this cannot improve Kane's exponent.
2. **Smooth-number short-interval results have the wrong direction.** Recent work proves existence/density of smooth numbers in short intervals under various regimes; Singmaster would need to show that a *whole run* of numerator integers with one exact shared factorial quotient cannot occur for too many distinct lower indices. Existence theorems do not provide that exclusion.
3. **Erdős-684 is a neighboring, not identical, quantifier pattern.** It varies `k` in one row. We need simultaneous representations across different rows with a fixed binomial value.
4. **The 2026 Lean/Zenodo claim is not usable as a resolution.** Its posted Lean source defines the desired proposition but does not prove it or prove the staged reductions.
