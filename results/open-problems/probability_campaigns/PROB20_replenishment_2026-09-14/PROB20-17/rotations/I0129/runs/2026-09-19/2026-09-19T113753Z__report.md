# 4. B1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: finite-étale root algebra and an exact saturation theorem

The previous algebraic closure was
\[
\Phi_k(x,Y)=\frac{F_k(x-F_k(x)Y)}{F_k(x)}.
\]
Because the roots of `F_k` are exactly `0,1,...,k-1`, over `Q(x)` one has
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_k(x,Y)=
\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}
\left(1-\frac{F_k(x)}{x-r}Y\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
At a common-binomial point `n` with `F_k(n)=t`, the `k` rational roots are therefore
\[
y_r=\frac{n-r}{t},\qquad 0\le r<k.
\tag{4.2}
\]
This makes all basic étale invariants explicit.

### Theorem 4.1 — trace, norm and discriminant are exactly saturated

For the root set (4.2),
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}y_r
=
\frac{kn-k(k-1)/2}{t},
\tag{4.3}
\]
\[
\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}y_r
=
\frac{k!}{t^{k-1}},
\tag{4.4}
\]
and
\[
\prod_{0\le r<u<k}(y_r-y_u)^2
=
\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(j!)^2}{t^{k(k-1)}}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
If `p>k` and `p^f||t` is a clean component for this row, then exactly one root `y_{r_p}` has `v_p(y_{r_p})=0`, every other root has valuation `-f`, and every root difference has valuation `-f`. Consequently (4.4) and (4.5) attain exactly the p-adic valuations forced by this one-integral-root pattern; neither the norm nor the discriminant contains any extra clean-prime surplus.

**Proof.** Equation (4.3) is the sum of `(n-r)/t`. For (4.4),
\[
\prod_r(n-r)=k!F_k(n)=k!t.
\]
For (4.5),
\[
y_r-y_u=\frac{u-r}{t},
\]
and the Vandermonde product on `0,...,k-1` satisfies
\[
\prod_{r<u}(u-r)^2=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(j!)^2.
\]
For clean `p>k`, the preserved localization gives a unique `r_p` with `v_p(n-r_p)=f`; for `u!=r_p`, the difference `r_p-u` is a nonzero integer of absolute value `<p`, so `v_p(n-u)=0`. Thus the selected root has valuation `f-f=0`, the other `k-1` roots have valuation `-f`, and every difference `(u-r)/t` has valuation `-f`. Summing these valuations gives `(1-k)f` for the norm and `-k(k-1)f` for the discriminant, exactly matching (4.4)-(4.5) because `p>k` makes every factorial there a p-unit. QED.

This proves a useful **route no-go**: a product-formula argument using only trace/norm/discriminant of the individual row root algebra cannot create the missing positive fraction of rough mass. Those invariants merely repackage the already-known unique-factor localization.

### Theorem 4.2 — the naive four-row resultant vanishes for combinatorial reasons

Up to a nonzero power of `t` and nonzero leading constants, the resulting product over root choices contains the factors
\[
X-(r_0-r_1-r_2+r_4).
\tag{4.6}
\]
For fixed bounded integer `X` and all sufficiently large `m`, at least one tuple in the allowed root ranges satisfies
\[
r_0-r_1-r_2+r_4=X.
\]
Hence the unconditioned four-row resultant is identically zero on the plastic four-row shape. It cannot distinguish the clean p-adic selected roots from irrelevant root tuples.

**Proof.** Substituting the rational roots gives

\frac{X-(r_0-r_1-r_2+r_4)}{t}.

The multivariate resultant/product over root choices therefore has the stated factors. If `X>=0`, choose for instance `r_0=X` and the other three labels zero once `m>|X|`; if `X<0`, choose `r_1=-X` and the others zero. Thus one factor vanishes. QED.

# 5. B10 — DERANDOMIZE: nested root sets force a low-degree cross-row transfer

The key observation omitted in previous runs is that the row polynomials are not unrelated:
\[
\boxed{
F_{k+1}(x)=F_k(x)\frac{x-k}{k+1}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
The root set of `F_k` is literally nested inside that of `F_{k+1}`.

At every old root `0<=r<k`, put `x=r+z`. The local reciprocal germs satisfy the exact identity
\[
\psi_{k+1,r}(z)
=
\frac{z}{F_{k+1}(r+z)}
=
\frac{k+1}{r-k+z}\,\psi_{k,r}(z).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Equivalently, in the product of the `k` old-root completions, the canonical root selectors agree exactly:
\[
\Theta_{k+1}=\Theta_k,
\]
so for the infinite formal reciprocal branches
\[
\boxed{
(x-k)\mathcal J_{k+1}(x)=(k+1)\mathcal J_k(x).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
The finite Hermite interpolants inherit a strong truncation of (5.3).

### Theorem 5.1 — adjacent-row transfer

For every `k>=2` and `s>=1` there is a unique polynomial `H_{k,s}\in Q[x]` with
\[
\boxed{
(x-k)J_{k+1,s}(x)-(k+1)J_{k,s}(x)
=F_k(x)^s H_{k,s}(x),
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\deg H_{k,s}\le s.}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Moreover
\[
\boxed{(k!)^{s-1}H_{k,s}(x)\in\mathbf Z[x],}
\tag{5.6}
\]
and there is an absolute constant `C` such that
\[
\log H_{\rm coeff}((k!)^{s-1}H_{k,s})
\le Csk\log(k+s+2).
\tag{5.7}
\]

**Proof.** At an old root `r<k`, both `J` polynomials match their germs through order `s-1`, while (5.2) is exact. Hence the left side of (5.4) vanishes to order at least `s` at each of the `k` distinct roots. It is therefore divisible in `Q[x]` by
\[
P_k(x)^s:=\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}(x-r)^s=(k!)^sF_k(x)^s.
\]
The degree of the left side is at most `s(k+1)`, while `deg P_k^s=sk`, so the quotient by `F_k^s` has degree at most `s`, proving (5.4)-(5.5).

By the preceding uniform denominator theorem, `(k!)^{2s-1}` clears the coefficients of both `J_{k+1,s}` and `J_{k,s}`. Therefore
\[
D^*:=(k!)^{2s-1}\big((x-k)J_{k+1,s}-(k+1)J_{k,s}\big)\in\mathbf Z[x].
\]
Since `P_k^s` is monic and divides `D^*` in `Q[x]`, Gauss's lemma gives `D^*=P_k^sG` with `G\in Z[x]`. Comparing with (5.4),
\[
H_{k,s}=\frac{G}{(k!)^{s-1}},
\]
which is (5.6).

For (5.7), evaluate on the circle `|x|=2k`. The uniform coefficient bound for the cleared `J`'s gives `|D^*(x)|<=exp(C_1sk log(k+s+2))(2k)^{s(k+1)}`. On the same circle, every `|x-r|>=k`, so `|P_k(x)|^s>=k^{ks}`. Therefore the quotient `G=D^*/P_k^s`, of degree at most `s`, has sup norm `exp(O(sk log(k+s+2)))` on `|x|=2k`; Cauchy's coefficient estimate gives the same exponential bound for its coefficients. QED.

This is the first cross-row relation in the jet route whose **defect degree is independent of the row size `k`**.

### Corollary 5.2 — exact first-jet transfer

For `s=1`,
\[
\boxed{
(x-k)I_{k+1}(x)-(k+1)I_k(x)
=(k+1)\binom{2k}{k}\left(x-\frac{3k}{2}\right)F_k(x).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

**Proof.** Theorem 5.1 says the quotient is linear, `H_{k,1}=alpha x+beta`. The leading coefficient of `I_k` is
\[
\frac{A_k}{k!},\qquad A_k=k^2\binom{2k-2}{k-1},
\]
so comparison of top-degree coefficients gives
\[
\alpha=rac{A_{k+1}}{k+1}=(k+1)\binom{2k}{k}.
\]
At `x=k`, `F_k(k)=1` and
\[
I_k(k)
=k^2\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\frac{\binom{k-1}{r}^2}{k-r}
=k\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{r}\binom{k}{r}
=\frac{k}{2}\binom{2k}{k}
\]
by Vandermonde. Thus
\[
H_{k,1}(k)=-(k+1)I_k(k)
=-\frac{k(k+1)}2\binom{2k}{k},
\]
which with the known slope yields (5.8). QED.

The digit polynomials also inherit a symmetry. Since
\[
F_k(k-1-x)=(-1)^kF_k(x),
\]
uniqueness of the Hermite interpolant gives
\[
\boxed{
J_{k,s}(k-1-x)=(-1)^{k+1}J_{k,s}(x).
}
\tag{5.9}
\]
Using `J_{k,s+1}-J_{k,s}=F_k^sR_{k,s}` therefore yields
\[
\boxed{
R_{k,s}(k-1-x)=(-1)^{k+1-ks}R_{k,s}(x).
}
\tag{5.10}
\]
This halves the naive digit degrees of freedom, although by itself it does not remove the `Theta(m^3 log m)` determinant-height barrier.

## 6. Fixed-shift iteration: all four rows compress to one base row plus degree `O(s)` corrections

For `h>=0` define
\[
D_h(x):=(x-m)_{\underline h}=\prod_{j=0}^{h-1}(x-m-j),
\qquad
C_h:=(m+1)^{\overline h}=\prod_{j=0}^{h-1}(m+j+1),
\tag{6.1}
\]
with `D_0=C_0=1`.

### Theorem 6.1 — iterated nested-row compression

For every fixed `h>=0` and every `s>=1`, there is a polynomial `G_{m,h,s}(x)` such that
\[
\boxed{
D_h(x)J_{m+h,s}(x)
=C_hJ_{m,s}(x)+F_m(x)^sG_{m,h,s}(x),
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
with
\[
\boxed{\deg G_{m,h,s}\le hs+h-1}
\tag{6.3}
\]
(for `h=0`, take `G=0`). For the only shifts used by the plastic quartet, `h in {0,1,2,4}`, the correction degree is at most `4s+3`, completely independent of `m`.

An explicit formula is
\[
G_{m,h,s}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}
\left(\prod_{\ell=j+1}^{h-1}(m+\ell+1)\right)
C_j^{-s}D_j(x)^{s+1}H_{m+j,s}(x).
\tag{6.4}
\]
After multiplication by a power of `(m+4)!` depending linearly on `s`, its coefficients are integral and have logarithmic height `O_h(sm log(m+s+2))`.

**Proof.** Write `d_j=x-m-j`, `c_j=m+j+1`. The adjacent identity (5.4) is
\[
d_jJ_{m+j+1,s}=c_jJ_{m+j,s}+F_{m+j}^sH_{m+j,s}.
\]
Iterating gives
\[
D_hJ_{m+h,s}=C_hJ_{m,s}
+\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}D_j
\left(\prod_{\ell=j+1}^{h-1}c_\ell\right)
F_{m+j}^sH_{m+j,s}.
\]
But
\[
F_{m+j}(x)=F_m(x)\frac{D_j(x)}{C_j},
\]
which yields (6.2) and (6.4). The `j`th summand of `G` has degree at most
\[
j(s+1)+s,
\]
maximized at `j=h-1`, giving `hs+h-1`. Denominator and height bounds follow from Theorem 5.1 and the fact that `h<=4` is fixed. QED.

Now evaluate at the actual top parameter `n_h`, where `F_{m+h}(n_h)=t`. Since
\[
F_m(n_h)=t\frac{C_h}{D_h(n_h)},
\tag{6.5}
\]
(6.2) becomes
\[
\boxed{
D_h(n_h)^{s+1}J_{m+h,s}(n_h)
=C_hD_h(n_h)^sJ_{m,s}(n_h)
+t^sC_h^sG_{m,h,s}(n_h).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Using the exact base-row retraction (3.6) and (6.5) gives a still cleaner form.

### Theorem 6.2 — common-row retraction formula

For each `h in {0,1,2,4}`,
\[
\boxed{
J_{m+h,s}(n_h)
=
\frac{n_h-T_{m,s+1}(n_h)}{t}
+
\frac{t^sC_h^s}{D_h(n_h)^{s+1}}G_{m,h,s}(n_h).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
For `h=0` the second term is zero.

**Proof.** From (3.6),
\[
J_{m,s}(n_h)=\frac{n_h-T_{m,s+1}(n_h)}{F_m(n_h)}
=\frac{D_h(n_h)}{tC_h}(n_h-T_{m,s+1}(n_h)).
\]
Substitute into (6.6) and divide by `D_h(n_h)^{s+1}`. QED.

Summing with the plastic signs yields the run's main compression identity.

### Theorem 6.3 — exact four-row common-base decomposition

Let
\[
S_s=
J_{m,s}(n_0)-J_{m+1,s}(n_1)-J_{m+2,s}(n_2)+J_{m+4,s}(n_4).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
S_s
=
\frac{X-\sum_{h\in\{0,1,2,4\}}\sigma_hT_{m,s+1}(n_h)}{t}
+t^s\sum_{h\in\{1,2,4\}}
\sigma_h\frac{C_h^sG_{m,h,s}(n_h)}{D_h(n_h)^{s+1}}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]
Every cross-row correction polynomial in the second sum has degree at most `4s+3`; **all dependence on the large row parameter `m` has been moved into explicit coefficients and the single base-row retraction `T_{m,s+1}`.**

This is a qualitatively stronger reduction than the preceding generic `4m+7`-dimensional digit-space statement. It supplies the requested structured cross-row relation without taking a generic determinant.

What is still missing is a sharp enough arithmetic/Archimedean estimate on the right side of (6.8). Crude coefficient height remains large enough to saturate the known clean-core divisibility. No source-valid exponent improvement follows in this run.

**Proof.** Multiply the root relation by `t` and use

## 8. Why the new reduction is not yet a source theorem
