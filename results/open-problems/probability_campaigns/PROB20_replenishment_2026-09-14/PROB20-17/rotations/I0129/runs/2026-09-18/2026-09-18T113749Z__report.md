## 2. The exact blocker — one displayed formula

Let

\[
L:=\log t,
\qquad
\mathcal M_t:=\left\{m:\ 2\le m\le M,\ \exists n\ge2m\text{ with }\binom nm=t\right\},
\]

where `M=max mathcal M_t`, and let `f_t(m)=n` denote the smooth inverse-binomial row curve on the left edge. The surviving exponent-one obstruction is already reduced to the ultraflat regime

\[
M\log M=o(L),\qquad \log W_M(t)=(1-o(1))L,
\]

with

\[
W_M(t)=\prod_{\substack{p\mid t\\p>M}}p^{v_p(t)}.
\]

The collective rough-core theorem says that a zero-sum integer row form of height `A=||c||_1` entering its natural residue band forces almost all rough logarithmic mass to satisfy the corresponding exact quotient relation, provided `A M log(AM)=o(L)`.

The current blocker is therefore the following **relation-or-sparsity** implication: for some fixed `beta<1` and some support bound that does not itself consume the `L/M` entropy scale, prove

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal M_t|>(\log t)^\beta
\Longrightarrow
\begin{array}{l}
\exists\ 0\ne c=(c_1,\ldots,c_s)\in\mathbb Z^s,\quad
m_{i_1}<\cdots<m_{i_s}\in\mathcal M_t,\\[1mm]
\sum_{j=1}^s c_j=0,\quad
A:=\sum_j|c_j|,\quad
A M\log(AM)=o(L),\\[1mm]
\displaystyle
\left|\sum_{j=1}^s c_j f_t(m_{i_j})\right|
\le {A\over2}(M-1).
\end{array}
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Alternatively, prove directly that the failure of every such low-complexity relation forces `|mathcal M_t|=O((log t)^beta)` for some fixed `beta<1`.

Equation (2.1) is a **blocking target**, not a theorem. It isolates the exact missing bridge between the real inverse-binomial geometry and the already-effective simultaneous rough-modulus machinery.

## 8. Nearest solved literature problem and the missing ingredient

The nearest solved source-specific analogue is **Lemma 2.2 of Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Shao–Tao–Teräväinen (2022)**, their “small non-zero derivative implies few integer values” interpolation lemma.

Their setup is a smooth real function on an interval. If the normalized `k`th derivative is nonzero but smaller than the reciprocal Vandermonde scale of the interval, then `k+1` integer inputs cannot all map to integers. The proof uses Lagrange interpolation: integer sample values make the leading coefficient of the interpolating polynomial either zero or at least one reciprocal Vandermonde unit; Rolle's theorem identifies that coefficient with a derivative value, yielding a contradiction.

What that solved argument has which the present edge problem lacks:

1. **Derivative-versus-denominator separation.** Their interior scale makes the derivative small enough relative to the interpolation denominator. In the small-`m` edge, the inverse-binomial derivatives are enormous and the relevant normalized comparison changes character.
2. **A controlled interval geometry.** The interpolation denominator can be bounded in terms of one interval length. Here arbitrary represented nodes create irregular Vandermonde factors, and clearing them is exactly the denominator-growth obstruction already seen in Kane-style higher interpolation.
3. **A regime where the analytic smallness wins.** MRSTT explicitly identify small `m/log t` as the main obstruction to improving total multiplicity. Our ultraflat regime is the extreme form of that obstruction.

The support-three theorem from the previous run succeeded by reversing the usual MRSTT perspective: the second derivative is so **large** that the primitive three-node band becomes impossible. SC-4 asks whether the same large-derivative exclusion survives one derivative higher before sign/cancellation phenomena appear.

A secondary solved analogue remains fixed-degree integer-value interpolation / determinant-method arguments. They work because the number of nodes/degree is fixed while the denominator can be controlled. Singmaster lacks the uniformity needed when support or degree must grow.
