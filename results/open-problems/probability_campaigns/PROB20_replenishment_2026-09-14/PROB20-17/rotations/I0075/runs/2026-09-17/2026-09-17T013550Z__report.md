## 5. A necessary open-packing lemma

The finite search is substantially reduced by the following elementary necessary condition.

**Lemma.** In a source-valid edge-disjoint center-star replacement network, the lower-left corners of selected squares have pairwise lattice `L1` distance at least `3`, including between periodic translates.

**Proof.** Edge-disjointness already forbids `L1` distance `1`.  Suppose two selected squares have lower-left corners at `L1` distance `2`.

Up to translation/reflection there are two geometries.

1. The offset is `(2,0)` or `(0,2)`.  The unit square between them is unselected and has two opposite boundary edges deleted, one by each selected neighbor.  For either diagonal pair of opposite corners of that middle square, the two length-two lattice `L` routes each use one of the deleted edges.
2. The offset is `(1,1)` (the other diagonal signs are reflections).  Take the unit square with lower-left corner `(1,0)` when the selected squares are based at `(0,0)` and `(1,1)`.  Its left and top edges are deleted.  For its bottom-right and top-left corners, both length-two lattice `L` routes are destroyed.

In either case the chosen city pair is at Euclidean distance `sqrt(2)`, so the source stretch threshold permits route length at most `2`.  A grid-only route of length `2` would have to be one of the two destroyed `L` routes.  Any route using a selected-square center passes between two city corners of that selected square at cost at least `sqrt(2)`; because the chosen endpoints are not opposite corners of any selected square, such a route also requires at least one additional positive city-to-city segment, whose minimum cost in this network is `1`.  Thus every star-using alternative has length at least `1+sqrt(2)>2`.  Hence the source stretch condition fails.

For a periodic pattern, a toroidal pair at cyclic `L1` distance below `3` has periodic lifts with the corresponding actual lattice separation, so the same argument applies.  This proves the lemma.  `□`

Thus every source-valid candidate in this topology is an **open packing** on the square torus: selected cells have toroidal `L1` separation at least `3`.

For period `n=1`, edge-disjointness already fails for any positive-density pattern.  For period `n=2`, every selected square has a periodic translate at lattice distance `2`, so the lemma rules it out.  The exact enumeration therefore begins at `n=3`.
