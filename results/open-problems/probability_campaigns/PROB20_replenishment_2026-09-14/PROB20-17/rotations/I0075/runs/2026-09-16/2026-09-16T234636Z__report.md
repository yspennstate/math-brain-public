## 4. B11 + B25: a sharp four-terminal local length certificate

The previous report proved only that a **one-hub** replacement of a unit square cannot be shorter than the center star while preserving the four adjacent-corner source bounds. The source's Proposition 8.2 proof suggests the correct finite dual statement.

Let the unit-square corners be

\[
A=(0,0),\quad B=(1,0),\quad C=(1,1),\quad D=(0,1).
\]

Consider any rectifiable line-segment network `Gamma` containing these four points. Suppose it contains routes

\[
P_{AB},\ P_{DC},\ P_{AD},\ P_{BC}
\]

with

\[
\ell(P_{AB}),\ell(P_{DC}),\ell(P_{AD}),\ell(P_{BC})\le\sqrt2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{len}(\Gamma)\ge 2\sqrt2.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is sharp, attained by the center star.

### Proof

First, the two horizontal routes cannot have a common point other than the square center. If `z` lies on both `P_AB` and `P_DC`, then by (4.1)

\[
2\sqrt2
\ge \ell(P_{AB})+\ell(P_{DC})
\ge |A-z|+|z-B|+|D-z|+|z-C|.
\]

Regrouping the four terms along opposite corners gives

\[
|A-z|+|z-C|\ge|A-C|=\sqrt2,
\]

and

\[
|B-z|+|z-D|\ge|B-D|=\sqrt2.
\]

Thus equality holds everywhere. Equality in both diagonal triangle inequalities forces `z` to lie on both diagonals `AC` and `BD`, hence `z=(1/2,1/2)`. Therefore `P_AB` and `P_DC` share no positive-length segment. The identical argument shows that `P_AD` and `P_BC` share no positive-length segment.

Replace the four routes by simple subroutes if needed. Along a rectifiable path from `A` to `B`, total variation of the x-coordinate is at least one, so

\[
\int_{P_{AB}} |dx|\ge1,
\qquad
\int_{P_{DC}} |dx|\ge1.
\]

Similarly,

\[
\int_{P_{AD}} |dy|\ge1,
\qquad
\int_{P_{BC}} |dy|\ge1.
\]

Hence the sum of the four projection integrals is at least `4`.

Now inspect a road segment of `Gamma` making angle `theta` with the x-axis. Because the two horizontal routes overlap only on a length-zero set, that segment is used by at most one horizontal route almost everywhere; similarly it is used by at most one vertical route. Its contribution to the four projection integrals is therefore at most

\[
(|\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|)\,ds\le\sqrt2\,ds.
\]

Integrating over the network gives

\[
4\le \sqrt2\,\operatorname{len}(\Gamma),
\]

which is (4.2). QED.

### What this does and does not prove

This removes the artificial one-hub restriction: **no self-contained four-terminal gadget** replacing the square boundary can beat total length `2sqrt(2)` while giving all four side pairs routes of length at most `sqrt(2)`.

It does **not** imply that arbitrary global square-grid networks have length at least `2`. Global routes for nearby city pairs may leave the square and share infrastructure across many cells. Indeed Aldous–Lando's Proposition 8.2 uses the same projection philosophy globally and obtains only normalized length `sqrt(2)` from the nearest-neighbor condition. So this is an obstruction to a local-gadget shortening route, not a proof of the original conjecture, and its core mechanism is source-authored prior art.

## 5. B29: exact periodic quotient for the one-residue congruence-star family

The period-eight counterexample belongs to a natural two-parameter family. For integers

\[
q\ge2,\qquad 1\le k\le q-1,
\]

define `N(q,k)` by selecting unit squares whose lower-left corners satisfy

\[
a+kb\equiv0\pmod q,
\tag{5.1}
\]

then replacing each selected square boundary by its center star exactly as above.

Because `k` is nonzero modulo `q`, horizontally neighboring squares change the residue by `1` and vertically neighboring squares by `k`; hence no two selected squares share an edge. Exactly one residue class out of `q` is selected, so the square density is `1/q`. Therefore the normalized length is exactly

\[
\boxed{
L(q)=2+\frac{2\sqrt2-4}{q}
    =2-\frac{4-2\sqrt2}{q}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `4-2sqrt(2)>0`, `L(q)` is strictly increasing with `q`. Thus any member with `q<8` would improve the current witness if it satisfied the full source stretch condition.

### Exact classification through q=8

The new exact checker enumerates every pair `(q,k)` with

\[
2\le q\le8,\qquad 1\le k<q,
\]

and all source translation phases. For each pattern it searches exact city-to-city shortest routes in `Z[sqrt(2)]` for displacements of squared Euclidean norm at most `25`.

For a returned distance

\[
A+B\sqrt2,
\]

the source comparison

\[
A+B\sqrt2\le\sqrt2\sqrt{dx^2+dy^2}
\]

is decided by integer arithmetic. With `A,B>=0`, writing

\[
C=2(dx^2+dy^2)-A^2-2B^2,
\]

the inequality is equivalent to

\[
C\ge0,
\qquad
C^2\ge 8A^2B^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The Dijkstra search uses a geometric box extending ten city units from each source and accepts a violating route only when its exact cost is strictly below `9`. Any route that leaves this box must first reach a point at Euclidean distance at least `10` from the source, so its length is at least `10`; hence such an outside excursion cannot improve a certified `<9` route. The finite-box shortest path used in each violation is therefore also the infinite-network shortest path.

The executed classification gives:

1. **Every one of the 21 patterns with `2 <= q < 8` has an exact source-level stretch violation.**
2. For `q=8`, the patterns `k=1,2,4,6,7` also have exact violations.
3. The only `q=8` patterns surviving these local tests are
   \[
   (q,k)=(8,3),(8,5).
   \]
4. `N(8,3)` is exactly the already globally certified counterexample. `N(8,5)` is its reflection under `b -> -b`, because `5 congruent -3 (mod 8)`, and therefore has the same full global stretch and length.

Representative exact failures include

\[
(q,k)=(7,1):\quad d=(-3,-3),\quad r=5+\sqrt2>6=\sqrt2\,|d|,
\]

and

\[
(q,k)=(8,1):\quad d=(-4,-3),\quad r=6+\sqrt2>5\sqrt2=\sqrt2\,|d|.
\]

The artifact records an explicit exact failure for every rejected `(q,k)`.

### Restricted-family theorem

It follows immediately that any **source-valid** member of the family `N(q,k)` must have

\[
q\ge8.
\]

Since (5.2) is increasing in `q` and `N(8,3)` is source-valid,

\[
\boxed{
\min\{L(N(q,k)):N(q,k)\text{ is source-valid},\ 1\le k<q\}
=\frac{6+\sqrt2}{4}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the existing period-eight witness is **exactly optimal inside the full one-residue non-edge-sharing congruence-star family**.

This is a restricted-family result only. It does not show global optimality among arbitrary star-placement patterns, arbitrary periodic networks, or arbitrary Steiner networks.
