## 3. Mathematical idea before code: turn the global proof into a descent algorithm

The previous proof handled large displacements using `(8,0)` / `(8,8)` macros, a residual box, and an exact finite core out to radius `130`. The new idea is to choose the macro **according to the direction of the displacement**.

For each certified macro vector `v` with route-cost upper bound `c`, normalize it to the vector `v/c`. If the convex hull of these normalized vectors contains a disk of radius strictly greater than `1/sqrt(2)`, then every displacement direction has some macro whose forward projection is efficient enough to pay for that macro while preserving the target `sqrt(2)` stretch bound.

For the period-eight network, only eight macros are needed:

- four axial vectors `(+/-8,0),(0,+/-8)` with cost at most
  `A=6+2sqrt(2)`;
- four diagonal vectors `(+/-16,+/-16)` with cost at most
  `D=18+7sqrt(2)`.

Their normalized convex hull has inradius **greater than `3/4`**, comfortably above `1/sqrt(2)`. This gives a strict Euclidean-norm descent for every displacement of norm at least `52`. The entire infinite all-pairs statement therefore follows by induction from a finite exact radius-52 core.

This mechanism was written down before the new checker was built.

## 4. The network and its length

For completeness, the witness network `N_8` is restated.

Cities are all points of `Z^2`. Start with every horizontal and vertical unit edge of the square lattice. Select the unit squares whose lower-left corner `(a,b)` satisfies

`a+3b == 0 (mod 8)`.

For each selected square, delete its four boundary unit edges, add the square center as a Steiner point, and join the center to all four corners.

Side-neighboring squares alter `a+3b` by `+/-1` or `+/-3`, so no two selected squares share an edge. The homomorphism `(a,b) -> a+3b mod 8` is onto, hence selected squares have density exactly `1/8` per unit area.

Each replacement removes length `4` and adds four spokes of length `sqrt(2)/2`, total `2sqrt(2)`. Therefore

\[
L(N_8)=2+\frac{2\sqrt2-4}{8}
      =\boxed{\frac{6+\sqrt2}{4}}<2.
\]

Connectivity is preserved because every deleted adjacent-city edge can be traversed through the corresponding star with length `sqrt(2)`.

It remains to prove the **full all-pairs** stretch bound.

## 6. B14: normalized macro octagon has inradius > 3/4

By the octagonal symmetry every edge has the same distance from the origin. Hence the convex hull of the eight normalized macro vectors contains the Euclidean disk of radius `h`, and in particular the disk of radius `3/4`.

## 7. B13: the macro-descent inequality

Take any nonzero displacement `d` with

\[
r:=\|d\|_2\ge52,
\qquad e=d/r.
\]

Choose a macro `(v,c)` from the octagon so that

\[
d\cdot v\ge\frac34 rc.
\]

I claim

\[
\boxed{c+s\|d-v\|_2\le sr.}
\tag{1}
\]

First `sr-c>0` at `r>=52` for both macro costs. At the threshold this reduces to

\[
52s-A=50s-6>0,
\qquad
52s-D=45s-18>0.
\]

Thus (1) may be squared after moving `c` to the right. Since `s^2=2`, (1) is equivalent to

\[
4d\cdot v
\ge
2src+2\|v\|_2^2-c^2.
\tag{2}
\]

The projection bound gives

\[
4d\cdot v\ge3rc.
\]

So it suffices to prove

\[
(3-2s)rc\ge2\|v\|_2^2-c^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Because `3-2s>0`, it is enough to check (3) at `r=52` for each macro type.

For an axial macro, `|v|^2=64`, `c=A`, and

\[
2\|v\|^2-A^2=84-24s.
\]

The threshold margin is

\[
(3-2s)52A-(84-24s)=436-288s>0,
\]

because

\[
436^2=190096>165888=2\,288^2.
\]

For a diagonal macro, `|v|^2=512`, `c=D`, and

\[
2\|v\|^2-D^2=602-252s.
\]

The threshold margin is

\[
(3-2s)52D-(602-252s)=750-528s>0,
\]

because

\[
750^2=562500>557568=2\,528^2.
\]

This proves (1) for every `r>=52`.

Because `c>0`, (1) also yields

\[
s\|d-v\|_2\le sr-c<sr,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{\|d-v\|_2<\|d\|_2.}
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (4) is the strict monovariant needed for induction.

## 8. B4: exact radius-52 base and complete induction

### Exact base

For each of the eight phase representatives `(rho,0)`, the new exact checker constructs and validates a route for every nonzero integer displacement `(dx,dy)` satisfying

\[
dx^2+dy^2<52^2.
\]

There are exactly `8,484` such displacements per phase, hence

\[
\boxed{8\times8484=67,872}
\]

source-phase/displacement cases.

Every route label has the form

\[
P+Q\sqrt2,
\qquad P,Q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]

The source bound

\[
P+Q\sqrt2\le\sqrt2\sqrt{dx^2+dy^2}
\]

is decided exactly. Writing `R2=dx^2+dy^2`, after one squaring it is enough to have

\[
C:=2R2-(P^2+2Q^2)\ge0
\]

and then

\[
C^2\ge8P^2Q^2.
\]

The checker uses only integer arithmetic for these decisions.

The executed output reports:

- `8` source phases;
- `8,484` bounded nonzero displacements per phase;
- `67,872` exact finite-core stretch cases;
- `64` phase-uniform macro cases;
- zero stretch failures;
- final line `ALL_ASSERTIONS_PASSED`.

### Induction for all displacements

We prove, simultaneously for every source phase and every integer displacement `d`, that

\[
r_{N_8}(z,z+d)\le s\|d\|_2.
\tag{5}
\]

The claim is trivial for `d=0` and is exactly certified for every `0<|d|<52`.

Now assume `|d|>=52` and that (5) holds for every strictly smaller Euclidean displacement norm. Choose the macro `(v,c)` from Sections 6–7. The phase-uniform macro lemma gives a route from `z` to `z+v` of length at most `c`. By (4), `|d-v|<|d|`, so the induction hypothesis gives a route from `z+v` to `z+d` of length at most

\[
s\|d-v\|_2.
\]

Concatenating and applying (1),

\[
r_{N_8}(z,z+d)
\le c+s\|d-v\|_2
\le s\|d\|_2.
\]

This proves (5) for every pair of square-grid cities.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{S(N_8)\le\sqrt2.}
\]

Together with the exact length calculation,

\[
\boxed{
S(N_8)\le\sqrt2,
\qquad
L(N_8)=\frac{6+\sqrt2}{4}<2.
}
\]

So `N_8` remains a source-valid counterexample to Aldous–Lando Conjecture 8.1.
