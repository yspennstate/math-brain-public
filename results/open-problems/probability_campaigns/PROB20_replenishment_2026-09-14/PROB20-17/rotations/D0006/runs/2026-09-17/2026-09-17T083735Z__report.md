## 3. (a) Exact blocker

For an integrable `F` on `R^d` with mean `mu`, let `Pi_n(F)` denote the compact/weakly compact family of `n`-couplings with all marginals `F` in the bounded-support cases used by the current frontier, and let

`D(x_1,...,x_n) = x_1 + ... + x_n - n mu`.

For a unit direction `u`, define the directional feasibility face

`K_u(F) = { pi in Pi_n(F) : u . D = 0 pi-a.s. }`.

A scalar complete mix of `u#F` can be lifted through regular conditional laws of `F` given `u.X`, so the source hypothesis gives nonemptiness of each directional face. Full vector complete mixability requires a coupling lying in every directional face at once.

The exact obstruction is therefore the following quantifier/gluing step:

\[
\boxed{
\forall u\in S^{d-1},\ K_u(F)\neq\varnothing
\quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
\bigcap_{u\in S^{d-1}} K_u(F)\neq\varnothing .
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is a diagnostic reformulation of the already-recovered projective-consistency wall, not a new theorem claim. In `d=2`, finding one common point in `K_u cap K_v` for two independent directions already gives a vector complete mix; the hypothesis supplies only the two singleton nonemptiness statements separately.

## 4. B24 — find the extremal object: what would an extremizer have to look like?

There are two natural extremal objects, one on each side of (3.1).

**Primal extremizer.** If the implication is true, the desired object is a coupling in the total intersection of the faces `K_u`. All positive subclass proofs found so far create such a coupling by imposing extra structure that synchronizes all directions at once:

- finite/countable support: one generic projection is injective on the support and the `n`-fold sumset, so one scalar mix already lifts to the vector one;
- axisymmetric sphere laws in the automatic regimes: the axial coupling plus the polygon closure condition synchronizes horizontal directions;
- centered elliptic shells for `n=3`: the directional cap inequalities force a single `120-degree` rotational symmetry, which itself constructs the common vector coupling.

The common feature is a **rigidity principle converting separate directional feasibility into one geometric object**. No such rigidity has been found for a generic absolutely continuous law.

**Dual extremizer.** If the implication is false, multi-marginal separation gives a vector dual obstruction. The earlier B11 analysis showed why merely summing ridge-function witnesses cannot work: every directional hypothesis kills the ridge cone. ## 5. B25 — count the same thing twice: why averaging directional mixes does not glue them

A tempting double-counting route is to average the separate directional complete mixes. For each unit `u`, choose some `pi_u in K_u(F)`. Let `sigma` be rotation-invariant measure on the sphere and form the averaged coupling

`bar pi = integral pi_u d sigma(u)`.

Every marginal of `bar pi` is still `F`. What fails is the constant-sum condition. For a fixed `u`, the chosen plan kills only the `u` component of the defect:

`E_{pi_u}[(u.D)^2] = 0`.

The vector energy of the averaged plan is instead

\[
E_{\bar\pi}\|D\|^2
=
\int_{S^{d-1}} E_{\pi_u}\|D\|^2\,d\sigma(u).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The spherical identity

`||D||^2 = d * integral (v.D)^2 d sigma(v)`

turns (5.1) into a double integral over pairs `(u,v)`. The hypothesis kills the diagonal terms `v=u` for their own plans, but that diagonal has spherical product measure zero and gives no control on the off-diagonal defects. Thus the most obvious “count directions twice and average” argument has no coercive content.

This is the same obstruction seen in the earlier matrix formulation: a value function can vanish on every rank-one direction while remaining positive at the identity because the minimizer changes with the direction.

A successful B25 argument would need an **exchange relation between minimizers in different directions**—for example, an inequality showing that making the defect zero in direction `u` cannot increase all orthogonal defects independently. No recovered mixability theorem provides such a relation.

**B25 outcome:** directional averaging fails for a precise reason: the zero-cost plan depends on the direction, and the diagonal information disappears under the required double count. No metric movement.

## 6. B29 — compactify: the missing finite-intersection principle

The coupling space supplies the natural compactification. Each `K_u(F)` is a closed convex feasibility face (equivalently, the zero set of the nonnegative linear functional `pi -> int (u.D)^2 d pi` when second moments are available). In bounded-support cases, `Pi_n(F)` is weakly compact.

Compactness would solve (3.1) immediately if the family `{K_u(F)}` had the **finite intersection property**:

\[
K_{u_1}(F)\cap\cdots\cap K_{u_m}(F)\neq\varnothing
\quad\text{for every finite }\{u_1,\ldots,u_m\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

But the source hypothesis gives only the `m=1` cases. There is no generic Helly theorem that upgrades nonempty individual closed convex subsets of an infinite-dimensional compact convex set to a common intersection. Even in `d=2`, (6.1) for two independent directions is already the desired vector mixability statement.

This explains why Cramér–Wold-style intuition is insufficient. Cramér–Wold reconstructs one probability measure from a family of **compatible projections of that same measure**. Here the data are existential: every direction may choose a different coupling. The missing hypothesis is precisely compatibility of those projected couplings.

The first exact `Q0` PL certificate can also be read through this lens. It establishes a finite-dimensional consistency statement for one test-function space, but no theorem says that these finite consistencies form a nested projective family whose inverse limit is a true coupling. D0005's retired handoff identified the same missing stabilization on the dual side: refinement can keep introducing new break lines near asymmetric boundary fibers.

**B29 outcome:** compactness is available, but it only converts a finite-intersection theorem into a solution; the source hypothesis does not currently provide that theorem. No metric movement.

## 10. (e) Nearest solved literature mechanisms and what D0006 lacks

The nearest general projective analogy is the classical **Cramér–Wold principle**: a probability law on `R^d` is determined by all one-dimensional projections. Its crucial premise, however, is that those projections are projections of **one common law**. Open Problem 6 supplies only existence of a possibly different coupling for each projected marginal. Cramér–Wold therefore identifies the missing ingredient rather than providing it: projective compatibility.

The nearest modern framework is multi-marginal optimal transport. It gives compactness and dual separation for one fixed vector marginal problem. The recovered 2024 Koike–Lin–Wang work connects joint mixability to multi-marginal optimal transport/negative dependence, but the recovered scope does not furnish a theorem that glues individually feasible directional optimal plans into one common plan.

Thus the piece absent from all nearest solved machinery is the same: **a consistency theorem for a family of existentially chosen projected complete-mix couplings**.
