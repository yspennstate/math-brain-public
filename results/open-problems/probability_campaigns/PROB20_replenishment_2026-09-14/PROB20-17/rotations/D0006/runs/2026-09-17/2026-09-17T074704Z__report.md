## 7. Additional B11 consequence — an elliptic-shell synchronization theorem

The failed quadrilateral search suggested looking for a class where a **single scalar necessary inequality, averaged over all directions, forces the missing projective consistency**. On a planar circle, the geometry is rigid enough for this to work.

### Theorem (centered elliptic-shell subclass)

Let `F` be a probability law supported on the boundary of a nondegenerate ellipse in `R^2`, and assume that the geometric center of that ellipse equals the mean `mu` of `F`.

For `n=3`, the following are equivalent:

1. every one-dimensional projection of `F` is `3`-CM;
2. after translating by `mu` and applying an invertible linear map carrying the ellipse to the unit circle, the resulting angular law is invariant under rotation by `2*pi/3`;
3. `F` is vector `3`-CM.

In particular, **Open Problem 6 has a positive answer on this entire non-atomic, potentially highly nonuniform elliptic-shell class.**

This is a proper subclass theorem and therefore does not move `C_proj`.

### Proof

Affine transformations preserve vector complete mixability and preserve scalar complete mixability of every projected law up to translation/scaling. Thus it is enough to work on the unit circle with mean zero.

Write

\[
U=(\cos\Theta,\sin\Theta)
\]

and let `rho` be the law of `Theta` on the circle.

Fix a unit direction

\[
u_\phi=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)
\]

and define

\[
Y_\phi=u_\phi\cdot U=\cos(\Theta-\phi).
\]

By the hypothesis, `Y_phi` is scalar `3`-CM. Its mean is zero and it lies in `[-1,1]`. Let

\[
(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)
\]

be a complete mix of this scalar law, so

\[
Y_1+Y_2+Y_3=0
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

At most one of the three variables can exceed `1/2`: if two did, then since the third is at least `-1`, their sum would be strictly positive. Therefore

\[
3\,\mathbb P(Y_\phi>1/2)
=
\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^3\mathbf 1_{\{Y_i>1/2\}}
\le1,
\]

and hence

\[
\mathbb P(Y_\phi>1/2)\le\frac13.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The event `Y_phi>1/2` is exactly the open angular arc

\[
A_\phi=(\phi-\pi/3,\phi+\pi/3),
\]

so (7.1) says

\[
\rho(A_\phi)\le\frac13
\quad\text{for every }\phi.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Now average (7.2) over `phi`. For each fixed angle `theta`, the set of centers `phi` for which `theta in A_phi` has angular length `2*pi/3`. Fubini therefore gives

\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\rho(A_\phi)\,d\phi
=
\frac13.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since the integrand is everywhere at most `1/3`, equations (7.2)-(7.3) force

\[
\rho(A_\phi)=\frac13
\quad\text{for almost every }\phi.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Let `K` be the indicator of the arc `(-pi/3,pi/3)` on the circle. Equation (7.4) says that the circular convolution `rho*K` is constant almost everywhere. For every nonzero integer `k`,

\[
\widehat K(k)
=
\frac{\sin(k\pi/3)}{\pi k}.
\]

Thus `\widehat K(k)` is nonzero whenever `3` does not divide `k`. Fourier transforming (7.4) gives

\[
\widehat\rho(k)\widehat K(k)=0,
\]

and therefore

\[
\widehat\rho(k)=0
\qquad(3\nmid k).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Equation (7.5) is exactly invariance of `rho` under rotation by `2*pi/3`: rotating a finite measure by that angle multiplies its `k`th Fourier coefficient by `e^{-2*pi*i*k/3}`, which changes nothing for multiples of three and acts on coefficients already zero otherwise. Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients for finite measures on the circle gives the claimed invariance.

Let `R` be rotation through `2*pi/3`. If `U~rho`, then by invariance

\[
U,\ RU,\ R^2U
\]

all have the same marginal law, while

\[
U+RU+R^2U=0.
\]

This is a vector `3`-complete mix. Mapping back by the original affine transformation gives three marginals equal to `F` and constant vector sum `3mu`.

The reverse implication, vector `3`-CM implies every projection is scalar `3`-CM, is exactly the elementary projection direction already recorded in the source. This completes the equivalence.

### A candidate family killed exactly

A natural asymmetric perturbation of the uniform angular law is

\[
d\rho_\varepsilon(\theta)
=
\frac{1+\varepsilon\cos 2\theta}{2\pi}\,d\theta,
\qquad |\varepsilon|\le1.
\]

For the cap above,

\[
\rho_\varepsilon(A_\phi)
=
\frac13+
\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt3}{4\pi}\cos 2\phi.
\]

If `epsilon != 0`, some direction has cap mass strictly larger than `1/3`, contradicting the necessary scalar `3`-CM inequality (7.1). Thus this tempting non-threefold shell perturbation fails the source hypothesis in an explicit direction; it cannot be a counterexample.

This theorem is analytic; no finite checker is needed. A bounded fresh search did not locate this exact projection-to-threefold-invariance statement, but no novelty claim is made from that search miss.
