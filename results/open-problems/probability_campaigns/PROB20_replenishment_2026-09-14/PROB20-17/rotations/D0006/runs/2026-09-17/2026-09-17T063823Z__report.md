## 3. (a) Exact statement that blocks

Let `P_V` denote projection to an essential proper subspace `V`, and let `Pi_n(G)` be the set of couplings of `n` copies of a distribution `G`. For integrable `F` with mean `mu`, the exact unresolved implication is

\[
\boxed{
\left[
\forall V\ \exists\pi_V\in\Pi_n(P_VF):
\ \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i=nP_V\mu\quad\pi_V\text{-a.s.}
\right]
\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}
\left[
\exists\pi\in\Pi_n(F):
\ \sum_{i=1}^n X_i=n\mu\quad\pi\text{-a.s.}
\right].
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The left side permits `pi_V` to depend arbitrarily on `V`. The right side demands one `pi`. Every successful route so far either solves a special class where one can explicitly synchronize the choices, or shows that a particular finite/parametric attempt cannot produce a separating example. No route gives a projective selection theorem or a source-valid inconsistency witness.

A useful equivalent transport formulation for the right side is: define

\[
H_F:=\{(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in(\mathbb R^d)^n:\ x_1+\cdots+x_n=n\mu\}.
\]

The conclusion is exactly the existence of a probability measure on `H_F` whose `n` coordinate marginals all equal `F`. Projection-wise complete mixability says that after applying `P_V` to this marginal problem, a feasible projected measure exists for every `V`; it does **not** say these projected feasible measures are the projections of a single measure on `H_F`.

This compatibility gap, not scalar mixability itself, is the blocker.

## 4. (b) Route ledger and precise reason each route failed to resolve the source

The current D0006 handoff must preserve the actual failed routes rather than restart them.

### Route 1 — finite or countable support counterexample

**Result:** eliminated.

For countable support `A`, one can choose a linear functional injective on both `A` and the countable `n`-fold sumset `nA`. A scalar complete mix in that one generic direction then lifts uniquely to a vector complete mix. Thus no finite or countably supported law can be the all-projections-yes / vector-no counterexample sought here.

**Why it fails as a full solution:** the separating direction relies on countability. A non-atomic law has uncountably many points and sum values, so one scalar coordinate is no longer an injective code for the entire vector problem.

### Route 2 — finite transportation/Farkas search

**Result:** useful only for atomic approximants.

For finite support the vector complete-mix problem is an exact rational transportation feasibility problem. But because of Route 1, any exact finite-support vector infeasibility certificate automatically forces failure of a generic projected scalar problem as well.

**Why it fails:** finite support cannot realize the source counterexample. Discretization can only help if one proves a limiting incompatibility survives while **all** continuum projections retain complete mixability; no such compatible approximation theorem is known.

### Route 3 — swap the quantifiers directly

**Result:** identifies the obstruction but supplies no selector.

`forall V exists pi_V` is genuinely weaker in form than `exists pi forall V`. Cramer--Wold determines a vector **law** from a consistent family of one-dimensional laws, but here the unknown object is a coupling on `(R^d)^n`, and the source assumptions do not specify a consistent family of projected coupling laws.

**Why it fails:** there is no recovered compactness/selection principle forcing independently chosen feasible projected couplings to agree on overlaps.

### Route 4 — PSD/minimax defect functional

For a coupling `pi`, let `D=sum_i X_i-n mu`; vector CM asks for `D=0` a.s. Projection-wise CM suggests directional costs can vanish when optimized separately. The previous report encoded this in a value function `Phi_F(Q)` for PSD quadratic forms.

**Why it fails:** zero optimized cost on every rank-one matrix does not force zero optimized cost at `I_d` for a general concave value function. An abstract PSD convex-set model demonstrates the logical failure. Any proof must exploit special geometry of realizable coupling-defect matrices.

### Route 5 — multi-marginal dual / ridge-cone generation

Vector CM admits a multi-marginal Kantorovich dual. Projection-wise CM kills dual obstructions assembled from admissible one-dimensional ridge potentials.

**Why it fails:** no theorem is known that every positive vector dual separator lies in the closed cone generated by such ridge potentials. A counterexample would amount to a genuinely non-ridge vector separator; a proof would amount to a ridge-generation theorem. Neither has been obtained.

### Route 6 — atomic approximation

Vector complete mixes are stable under compatible weak limits, and an existing vector complete mix can be approximated by finite complete mixes.

**Why it fails:** the direction needed here is the opposite. Projection-wise scalar CM provides, at best, separate approximation/coupling data for each direction. Nothing ensures these data arise from one common sequence of vector laws or vector couplings.

### Route 7 — symmetric/simplex/orbit constructions

Symmetry can synchronize projections because one explicit vector coupling is available. The D-intake simplex construction shows this for simplexes in the source-adjacent convex-body problem; centrally symmetric/rotation-rich examples are similarly non-separating.

**Why it fails:** these examples solve themselves by global symmetry. Generic bodies/laws have no such action, so the method assumes away the compatibility problem.

### Route 8 — axisymmetric sphere reduction

For an axisymmetric law on `S^2`, vector `n`-CM reduces to a scalar constant-sum coupling whose horizontal radii close to a polygon. This generated several exact automatic-lifting regimes and a sharp `n=3` semialgebraic support condition.

**Why it fails:** the tractable candidate families examined so far either lift explicitly or violate a scalar projection condition before the vector obstruction can be activated. Moreover, in `d=3`, proving all **line** projections are `n`-CM does not by itself verify the source's hypothesis for every essential proper subspace, which includes planes.

### Route 9 — zonal linear/quadratic perturbations of the sphere

The linear tilt was explicitly vector `3`-CM. The quadrupole obstruction lost scalar projection CM before the vector second-moment obstruction became available. A two-parameter zonal quadratic family has rigorously tractable all-line `3`-CM, but the low-order vector obstruction remains nonnegative on the certified parameter region.

**Why it fails:** no pointwise multi-marginal support obstruction has been proved for that family, and line-only verification would still be insufficient in `d=3`.

### Route 10 — planar convex body / D0005 transfer

This is the strongest remaining source-faithful counterexample route. In `d=2`, every proper nonzero subspace is a line. The source itself notes that projections of `Unif(C)` for planar convex `C` have concave densities; the published one-dimensional theorem then makes those projections `3`-CM. Thus deciding vector `3`-CM for one planar convex body directly decides whether that body is a D0006 counterexample.

Neighbor D0005 has already reduced the generic planar convex-body case to a continuous zero-sum-triple fractional-matching/fractional-cover problem. Triangles, parallelograms, and trapezoid-like special architectures are not the unresolved core; a generic quadrilateral is.

**Why it fails so far:** the exact rational piecewise-linear dual hierarchy is a semidecision procedure for **refutation**. ## 5. (c) What a full proof or refutation must contain that no route currently contains

A **positive source theorem** needs a synchronization principle. In at least one source-valid formulation it must take independently guaranteed projected feasibility and manufacture a single measure on

\[
H_F=\{x_1+\cdots+x_n=n\mu\}
\]

with all marginals equal to `F`. It is not enough to show that every linear statistic can be made constant under a direction-dependent coupling. Concretely, a successful proof would need one of:

1. a projective-consistency/selection theorem for the family of feasible projected coupling sets;
2. a dual theorem proving every vector obstruction is generated by one-dimensional/ridge obstructions;
3. a compatible approximation theorem that builds one sequence of finite vector problems from the continuum projected hypotheses.

No existing route contains any of these three ingredients.

A **negative source result** needs one genuinely non-atomic `F` for which every essential proper-subspace projection is source-valid `n`-CM, together with a rigorous vector infeasibility certificate. In the planar `n=3` convex-body route, the projection half comes for free from the published concave-density theorem; the missing object is therefore an explicit convex body plus a rigorous continuous dual separator/fractional cover showing that no zero-sum triple coupling with uniform marginals exists.

The important practical conclusion is that another finite-support search, another direction sample, or another low-order moment calculation cannot close the source. They test the wrong layer of the obstruction.

## 7. (e) Nearest solved problem in the literature, and the ingredient it lacks

The nearest published theorem is the one the source itself invokes: Puccetti--Wang--Wang (2012), *Advances in Complete Mixability*, Theorem 4.2, proving complete mixability of one-dimensional distributions with concave densities for `n>=3`.

For planar `Unif(Q)`, that theorem solves **every individual line projection**. In other words, the nearest literature already solves the full scalar family that appears on the left side of (3.1) in the `d=2,n=3` convex-body setting.

What it lacks is exactly the missing D0006 ingredient: its construction/proof may choose a different scalar complete mix for each projected density. Thus the gap between the nearest solved theorem and D0006 is not a sharper scalar inequality; it is a **compatibility theorem across directions**.

That work reinforces that vector couplings can be highly nonunique, but its law is designed to have the desired flexibility and it does not solve the fixed-`F` projective-consistency implication.

## 8. B18 — say it in another language

**Language chosen:** multi-marginal feasibility / marginal compatibility.

The source question is not best viewed as “does enough one-dimensional information determine the answer?” Cramer--Wold already says one-dimensional laws determine a vector law. The correct object is the **set of couplings**. For each subspace `V`, the hypothesis says a projected feasible set

\[
\mathcal C_V
=
\left\{\pi_V\in\Pi_n(P_VF):\sum_iY_i=nP_V\mu\right\}
\]

is nonempty. The desired conclusion is nonemptiness of the vector feasible set

\[
\mathcal C
=
\left\{\pi\in\Pi_n(F):\sum_iX_i=n\mu\right\}.
\]

The missing theorem would need the family `(C_V)_V` to satisfy a consistency/Helly/inverse-limit property strong enough that nonemptiness of all projected sets forces nonemptiness of `C`.

**Outcome:** this translation sharply identifies the target theorem but gives no reason such an inverse-limit principle should hold. The coupling choices need not agree on intersections of subspaces, and the source hypotheses do not prescribe compatible transition maps between the feasible sets. B18 therefore diagnoses the obstacle rather than moving `C_proj`.
