## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The initial counterexample plan was finite support: encode vector `n`-CM as an exact transportation polytope, ask every scalar projection to be `n`-CM, and seek a Farkas certificate showing that no common vector complete mix exists.

That route collapses for a structural reason that is stronger than a failed search. A **single generic scalar projection** of a countable support remembers both every support point and every possible `n`-fold vector sum. Therefore any scalar complete mix in that generic direction lifts uniquely to a vector complete mix.

This completely eliminates finite support, and in fact all countable support, from the counterexample search.

## 4. B2 — attack the negation first: no countably supported counterexample exists

For a set `A subset R^d`, write its `n`-fold Minkowski sum as

\[
nA:=\{a_1+\cdots+a_n:a_1,\ldots,a_n\in A\}.
\]

For `u in R^d`, write

\[
\ell_u(x):=\langle u,x\rangle.
\]

### Theorem 1 — sum-separating projection lift

Let `F` be a probability distribution on `R^d`, let `A` be a Borel set with `F(A)=1`, and fix `n>=2`. Suppose there exists `u` such that

1. `ell_u` is injective on `A`, and
2. `ell_u` is injective on `nA`.

If the scalar projection `(ell_u)_# F` is `n`-completely mixable on `R`, then `F` is `n`-completely mixable on `R^d`.

### Proof

Let

\[
G=(\ell_u)_\#F.
\]

By `n`-complete mixability of `G`, there is a scalar coupling

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n),\qquad Y_i\sim G,
\]

and a constant `c in R` such that

\[
Y_1+\cdots+Y_n=c\qquad\text{a.s.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because `ell_u` is injective on the Borel carrier `A`, its inverse on `ell_u(A)` is measurable. For countable `A` this is immediate; in the stated Borel form it follows from the Lusin--Souslin theorem for an injective Borel map between standard Borel spaces. Denote the inverse by

\[
g:\ell_u(A)\to A.
\]

Since every `Y_i` has law `G`, we have `Y_i in ell_u(A)` almost surely. Define

\[
X_i:=g(Y_i),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]

Then `X_i~F` for every `i`, because `g` is the almost-sure inverse of `ell_u` under `F`.

Set

\[
T:=X_1+\cdots+X_n.
\]

Almost surely `T in nA`, and by (4.1),

\[
\ell_u(T)
 =\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_u(X_i)
 =\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i
 =c.
\tag{4.2}
\]

But `ell_u` is injective on `nA`. Hence there is **at most one** point `t in nA` satisfying `ell_u(t)=c`. Since `T` takes values in `nA` and satisfies (4.2) almost surely, `T` is almost surely equal to one fixed vector `t`.

Therefore `(X_1,...,X_n)` is a vector complete mix with every marginal equal to `F`. Thus `F` is `n`-CM on `R^d`. QED.

### Corollary 2 — countable-support projection theorem

Let `F` be supported on a countable set `A subset R^d`. If every one-dimensional projection of `F` is `n`-CM, then `F` is `n`-CM.

### Proof

The sets `A`, `nA`, and therefore

\[
D:=\big((A-A)\setminus\{0\}\big)
\cup
\big((nA-nA)\setminus\{0\}\big)
\]

are countable. A direction `u` fails injectivity on `A` or on `nA` only if

\[
\langle u,v\rangle=0
\]

for some `v in D`. Thus all bad `u` lie in the countable union

\[
\bigcup_{v\in D}v^\perp
\]

of proper hyperplanes in `R^d`. This union has Lebesgue measure zero and cannot equal all of `R^d`. Choose `u` outside it. Then `ell_u` is injective on both `A` and `nA`. By hypothesis this scalar projection is `n`-CM, so Theorem 1 applies. QED.

### Consequences for the counterexample search

This is not a sampled-direction statement. It proves exactly that:

\[
\boxed{\text{there is no finite-support or countably-supported counterexample to the line-projection implication.}}
\]

It also uses no moment assumption: the scalar complete-mix center `c` is supplied by the scalar coupling itself.

Therefore the initially assigned B2 counterexample search cannot legitimately continue inside finite supports. Any source-level counterexample must evade every sum-separating scalar projection; in particular, it must have genuinely uncountable geometry (or exploit an interpretation of `essential subspace` different from the line-projection hypothesis fixed by this task).

**B2 outcome:** the intended finite/polyhedral counterexample class is ruled out by a complete theorem rather than by an unsuccessful numerical search.

## 5. B8 — exact finite coupling core: the scalar and vector LPs coincide in a generic direction

The preceding theorem has a finite-dimensional transportation interpretation that makes the obstruction especially transparent.

Let

\[
A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\subset\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
F=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\delta_{a_j}.
\]

An `n`-fold coupling is specified by nonnegative variables

\[
q_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}\ge0,
\]

with marginal constraints

\[
\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_n:\ i_r=j}q_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}=p_j
\qquad
(r=1,\ldots,n;\ j=1,\ldots,m).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because a finite-support law has a finite mean

\[
\mu=\sum_j p_j a_j,
\]

any vector complete mix must have vector center `n mu`. Thus the vector `n`-CM feasibility LP is (5.1) with support restricted to tuples satisfying

\[
a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_n}=n\mu.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Choose a generic `u` separating every pair of distinct elements of `nA`. Then

\[
\ell_u(a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_n})
 =\ell_u(n\mu)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_n}=n\mu.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Hence the scalar complete-mix LP for the projected law has **exactly the same admissible tuple support** as the vector LP. A Farkas certificate making the vector LP infeasible would therefore make this generic scalar projection infeasible as well.

So the finite-support B8 program does not merely fail experimentally: after generic projection, the scalar and vector feasibility problems are the same polytope.

No checker was written for this point, because the finite claim has been reduced to the exact symbolic equivalence (5.3); manufacturing an enumeration would add no mathematical information.

**B8 outcome:** finite-support exact transportation cannot produce a D0006 counterexample; future discretization must be used as an approximation/duality tool for a genuinely non-atomic candidate, not as the candidate itself.

## 6. B27 — swap the quantifiers carefully: why bare minimax still does not solve the continuous case

The countable-support theorem might tempt the invalid inference

\[
\forall u\ \exists\pi_u
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists\pi\ \forall u.
\]

For continuous laws there is no generic scalar projection that uniquely reconstructs all support points and all `n`-fold sums. The remaining quantifier issue can be written cleanly as a multi-marginal quadratic-transport problem.

Assume for this subsection that `F` is bounded (finite second moment is enough for the formulas), with mean `mu`. Let `Pi(F)` be the convex set of all couplings of `n` copies of `F`. For `pi in Pi(F)` write

\[
D=X_1+\cdots+X_n-n\mu,
\]

and define the positive-semidefinite defect matrix

\[
M(\pi):=\mathbb E_\pi[DD^\top]\succeq0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For `Q succeq 0`, define

\[
\Phi_F(Q):=
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(F)}
\mathbb E_\pi[D^\top QD]
=
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(F)}\operatorname{tr}(QM(\pi)).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then:

- scalar `n`-CM of the projection in direction `u` is equivalent to
  \[
  \Phi_F(uu^\top)=0;
  \]
- vector `n`-CM is equivalent to
  \[
  \Phi_F(I_d)=0.
  \]

Thus the unrestricted one-dimensional-projection question asks whether zero optimal cost on **every rank-one PSD ray** forces zero optimal cost at an interior positive-definite matrix.

The map `Phi_F` is concave and positively homogeneous in `Q`, being an infimum of linear functionals. But concavity alone is not enough. The following abstract PSD model shows exactly why a generic minimax argument fails.

Let

\[
\mathcal K=\{M\succeq0:\operatorname{tr}M=1\}.
\]

For every nonzero `u` in dimension `d>=2`, choose a unit `v perpendicular u` and `M=vv^top`. Then

\[
\min_{M\in\mathcal K}u^\top Mu=0.
\]

Yet

\[
0\notin\mathcal K,
\qquad
\min_{M\in\mathcal K}\operatorname{tr}(I_dM)=1.
\]

Equivalently, the associated concave value function is

\[
Q\mapsto\min_{M\in\mathcal K}\operatorname{tr}(QM)=\lambda_{\min}(Q),
\]

which vanishes on every rank-one `Q=uu^top` when `d>=2` but is positive at `I_d`.

This abstract `K` is **not** asserted to be realizable as `\{M(\pi):\pi\in\Pi(F)\}` for any probability law. Its role is narrower and exact: it disproves the proof schema that would use only convexity/compactness/PSD structure to exchange the quantifiers. A full positive solution must exploit additional structure special to coupling defect matrices; a counterexample would amount to realizing an analogous boundary-versus-interior separation inside an actual coupling class.

**B27 outcome:** the historical projective-consistency wall is now split sharply into two regimes. Atomic/countable support is positive by a generic lifting direction. The genuinely continuous case is equivalent, at second-moment level, to a simultaneous zero-cost problem in which rank-one costs can all vanish separately without abstract convexity forcing a common zero-cost plan.
