# 3. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

This method was used only to identify the same obstruction in three equivalent-looking languages; I am not claiming an equivalence theorem beyond the exact reformulations already present in the previous TH18 reports.

## 3.1 Convex-feasibility / alternating-projection language

The dual clipping iteration is alternating projection between an affine Fourier-constraint space and the nonnegative orthant. Once the negative support stabilizes to a face `S`, the residual evolution is governed by the compression

\[
P_S P_D P_S.
\]

Classical alternating-projection theory says that linear convergence is controlled by an angle / transversality / error-bound quantity. In TH18 the missing quantity is therefore a lower bound on the angle between the random Fourier affine space and the **active orthant face actually generated by the same random Fourier mask**.

The difficulty is not ordinary convergence: finite-dimensional alternating projection converges. The source objective charges the accumulated `l1` correction, so a qualitative convergence theorem is useless. What is needed is a source-uniform enough quantitative angle to make the path length cost `O(sqrt n)` or at least better than `sqrt(n log log n)`.

## 3.2 Positive-cone Fourier language

The primary paper's argument replaces the positive feasible cone by a full random nullspace and therefore needs a worst-case sparse Fourier theorem. The preceding TH18 reports instead isolate positive vectors / negative parts generated by the LP dual. In this language the missing statement is not a new RIP theorem for **all** kernel vectors. It is a density/spread theorem for a very special positive sequence `r_k(D)` correlated with the same Fourier mask `D`.

This language makes clear why the known sparse sign-changing kernel obstructions do not end the route, but also why fixed-vector concentration cannot simply be substituted for a mask-dependent generated vector.

## 3.3 Projected-simplex / convex-depth language

The previous report rewrote the frequency primal as directional depth of a coordinate-block projection of the regular Fourier simplex, with exact polar parameter `M_D`. In that language the source target asks for a sharp typical directional depth, while the upper-exponent target asks only for the correct order up to constants.

Here the same blocker becomes: random half-block Fourier projection must not create an anomalously shallow facet in the distinguished direction. Standard random-projection results are strongest for Haar/Gaussian projections; the source projection is a fixed Fourier system with a Bernoulli block mask and substantial arithmetic dependence. Importing a rotationally invariant theorem would change the model.

### B18 outcome

The translation does **not** produce a new theorem. It does identify one common missing object: a quantitative **generated-face transversality / positive-cone depth** estimate. Any future route that cannot say how it controls that object is probably only renaming the same failure.

# 5. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT

No extremal theorem is asserted here. The point is to identify the object that a proof must defeat.

For a stabilized active negative support `S`, the late-stage alternating-projection behavior is governed by the compressed random Fourier projector

\[
P_S P_D P_S.
\]

The dangerous configuration is therefore not an arbitrary sparse vector in `ker(F_D)`; it is an **active orthant face reached by the nonlinear trajectory** for which this compression is nearly singular in the wrong direction, equivalently for which the affine Fourier space is nearly tangent to the face. Such a face permits slow repair and a large accumulated `l1` objective cost.

On the convex-depth side the same danger is a shallow supporting facet of the random block-projected Fourier simplex in the distinguished direction. On the leave-one-frequency-out side it is a residual whose support/amplitudes respond too strongly to the same frequency bit being toggled.

A successful proof must therefore contain at least one ingredient absent from all routes so far:

1. a quantitative theorem that **trajectory-generated** active faces have a nonvanishing angle from the random Fourier affine space;
2. a leave-one-frequency-out stability estimate strong enough to decouple `r_k` from one mask bit and then sum the influence errors;
3. a structural description of the positive optimizer/residual showing that concentration patterns capable of making the angle tiny cannot arise from the source dynamics.

Worst-case sparse RIP alone does not provide any of these, and ordinary alternating-projection convergence does not provide the source-scale quantitative constant.

## (e) Nearest solved literature and what it has that TH18 lacks

The nearest solved *geometric mechanism* is the classical theory of alternating projections under transversality / positive angle / error bounds. For two subspaces the rate is governed by their Friedrichs angle; Lewis--Malick (2008, *Alternating Projections on Manifolds*) proves local linear convergence under transversal intersection and relates the rate to an angle/metric-regularity modulus. Related convex-feasibility/error-bound results likewise obtain quantitative convergence when an error bound is available.

What TH18 lacks is precisely a **random source-uniform error bound for the Fourier affine space against the orthant face selected by the same random mask**. The active face changes with the iterate and is statistically dependent on the Fourier subspace. Classical angle theorems tell us what quantity would control convergence once an angle is known; they do not supply that angle in this structured random model, nor do they automatically turn `l2` convergence into the `l1` path-length bound required by the theta dual objective.

The primary random-circulant paper supplies the other nearest solved mechanism: subsampled-DFT RIP gives a worst-case nullspace estimate and thereby the `alpha_LL=1/2` bound. What it lacks, by its own discussion, is exploitation of positivity. The current TH18 route is therefore trying to replace a worst-case RIP statement with an active-positive-face statement, not to re-prove the existing RIP.
