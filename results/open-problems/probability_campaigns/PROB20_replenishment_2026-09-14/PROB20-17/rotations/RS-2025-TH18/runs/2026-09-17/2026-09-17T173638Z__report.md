## 3. Recovered exact static reduction

The previous B3 run proved the exact static identity used here. Let `n` be odd and let

`A = {1,...,(n-1)/2}`

index conjugate nonzero Fourier pairs. Let `D subset A` be the source Bernoulli-selected pair set and `C=A\D`. For each pair `a`, let `Q_a` be the real orthogonal Fourier-pair projector, and set

`P_D = sum_{a in D} Q_a`, `P_C = sum_{a in C} Q_a`, `x_D=P_D e_0`.

The current exact one-sided completion parameter is

`kappa_D = inf_{q in Range(P_C)} max_j (x_D-q)_j`,

with

`theta(bar G_D) = 1+n kappa_D`.

Source complement invariance gives

`E theta(G_n) = 1+n E kappa_D`.

Thus any source-valid estimate

`E kappa_D = O((log log n)^beta/sqrt(n))`, `beta<1/2`,

moves `alpha_LL`, and `E kappa_D=O(1/sqrt(n))` gives `alpha_LL=0` (but still not the sharp leading constant one).

# 4. B4 — PROVE SOMETHING WEAKER, COMPLETELY

The partial-coloring port becomes clearer after an exact reparameterization of `kappa_D` as a finite moment-polytope radius. This is derived completely below from the already-proved B3 identity. I make **no literature-novelty claim** for the reparameterization; it is another exact form of the same LP.

## 4.1 Fourier-pair normalization

For `a in A`, put

`c_a(j)=sqrt(2/n) cos(2 pi a j/n)`,

`s_a(j)=sqrt(2/n) sin(2 pi a j/n)`.

For odd `n`, the vectors `c_a,s_a` are orthonormal across pair indices and

`Q_a e_0 = sqrt(2/n)c_a`,

so entrywise

`v_a(j)=(2/n) cos(2 pi a j/n)`.

Every `x_D` is even under `j -> -j` and has zero mean.

## 4.2 Exact probability-moment equivalence

Fix `t>0`. I claim that

`kappa_D <= t`

is equivalent to existence of a **symmetric probability vector** `p=(p_j)_{j in Z_n}`, `p_j=p_{-j}`, such that for every `a in D`,

`sum_j p_j cos(2 pi a j/n) = -1/(nt)`.

Because `p` is symmetric, all corresponding sine moments vanish automatically.

### Forward direction

Assume `kappa_D<=t`. Then there is `q in Range(P_C)` for which

`z=x_D-q <= t 1`

coordinatewise. Since `x_D` and `q` have no zero Fourier mode,

`sum_j z_j=0`.

Define

`mu=t 1-z >=0`.

Then `sum_j mu_j=nt`. Also

`P_D mu = -P_D z = -x_D`.

Symmetrizing `mu` under `j -> -j` preserves nonnegativity, total mass and every cosine Fourier component, while killing sine components, so we may assume `mu` is symmetric. Set

`p=mu/(nt)`.

Then `p` is a symmetric probability vector and

`Q_a p = -(1/(nt)) Q_a e_0 = -(1/(nt)) sqrt(2/n)c_a`

for each `a in D`. Taking the `c_a` coordinate gives exactly

`sum_j p_j cos(2 pi a j/n)=-1/(nt)`.

### Reverse direction

Conversely, suppose a symmetric probability vector `p` has these cosine moments. Set

`mu=nt p`, `z=t1-mu`.

Then `z<=t1`, `sum_j z_j=0`, and for every `a in D`, symmetry plus the prescribed cosine moment gives

`Q_a p = -(1/(nt))Q_a e_0`,

hence

`Q_a z = -nt Q_a p = Q_a e_0`.

Therefore `P_D z=x_D`. Put

`q=x_D-z`.

It has zero zero-frequency component and zero `D`-frequency component. Since the real Fourier decomposition is the orthogonal sum of the zero mode, the `D` pairs and the `C` pairs, it follows that

`q in Range(P_C)`.

Thus `x_D-q=z<=t1`, proving `kappa_D<=t`.

## 4.3 Radial moment parameter

Define

`delta_D = max { delta>=0 : there exists a symmetric probability p with E_p cos(2 pi a J/n)=-delta for every a in D }`.

The maximum exists by compactness. It is positive: the uniform law on `Z_n\{0}` has every nonzero Fourier cosine moment equal to `-1/(n-1)`. The equivalence above therefore gives the exact identity

`kappa_D = 1/(n delta_D)`.

Combining with complement invariance,

`E theta(G_n) = 1 + E[1/delta_D]`.

So the current upper-exponent problem is equivalently a **radial-depth problem for the moment polytope** generated by

`r_j=(cos(2 pi a j/n))_{a in D}`:

how far, in the special direction `-1_D`, does `conv{r_j}` extend?

The target `kappa_D=O(1/sqrt(n))` is exactly `delta_D=Omega(1/sqrt(n))` at the appropriate expectation/tail strength.

## 4.4 Why the obvious linear perturbation of uniform measure cannot work

This exact form immediately exposes a concrete failed port. Let

`S_D(j)=sum_{a in D} cos(2 pi a j/n)`

and try the first-order candidate

`p_delta(j)=(1/n)(1-2 delta S_D(j))`.

Orthogonality of the cosine system shows, **exactly**, that `p_delta` sums to one and has the desired moments

`E_{p_delta} cos(2 pi bJ/n)=-delta`, `b in D`,

whenever it is nonnegative. But at `j=0`,

`p_delta(0)=(1/n)(1-2 delta |D|)`.

Therefore positivity forces

`delta <= 1/(2|D|)`.

For source-typical `|D|=Theta(n)`, this gives only `delta=O(1/n)`, hence `kappa_D=Omega(1)` — far from the required `1/sqrt(n)` scale. The failure is not an accumulation of small errors; it is the coherent origin spike `S_D(0)=|D|`.

### B4 outcome

The one-sided Fourier extension problem is exactly a positive equal-moment / moment-polytope radial-depth problem. A linearized perturbation of the uniform law is rigorously incapable of reaching the metric-moving scale. Any successful port must use **nonlinear redistribution of mass** that suppresses the coherent origin spike while maintaining all `|D|` equal negative Fourier moments.

This is a complete derivation, but it does **not** improve `alpha_LL`.

## 5.3 A smoother randomized candidate: entropy/Gibbs mass redistribution

The exact moment form suggests a different randomization that at least builds positivity in from the start. Let

`r_j=(cos(2 pi a j/n))_{a in D} in R^{|D|}`

and for `lambda in R^{|D|}` define

`p_lambda(j)= exp(-<lambda,r_j>)/Z(lambda)`,

`Z(lambda)=sum_j exp(-<lambda,r_j>)`.

This is automatically a positive symmetric probability law. Its moment map is

`m(lambda)=E_{p_lambda} r_J = -grad log Z(lambda)`

and

`-D m(lambda)=Cov_{p_lambda}(r_J)`.

At `lambda=0`, Fourier orthogonality gives

`m(0)=0`,

`Cov_{p_0}(r_J)=(1/2) I_D`.

Therefore infinitesimally the desired target `m=-delta 1_D` points toward

`lambda approx 2 delta 1_D`.

At the desired `delta=c/sqrt(n)`, the Euclidean size of this first-order parameter is only `Theta(1)` because `|D|=Theta(n)`. Unlike the linear probability perturbation in B4, the Gibbs law cannot become negative and exponentially suppresses the coherent point `j=0` in the intended positive-`lambda` direction.

This does **not** prove feasibility. To continue the moment map to the desired target, one needs quantitative nondegeneracy of the covariance matrix along the relevant path. A sufficient source-specific statement would be a lower bound

`lambda_min Cov_{p_lambda}(r_J) >= c0 >0`

through the part of parameter space traversed by the optimizer at `delta=Theta(n^{-1/2})`.

A uniform bound over a full constant-radius Euclidean ball is too much to expect: an adversarial parameter can Gibbs-concentrate on a small set and collapse covariance. What is needed is a **direction/path-specific transversality estimate** for the entropy optimizer. That is another formulation of the same geometric obstruction, but it has two advantages: positivity is automatic, and the barrier failure at `j=0` from the linearized candidate is handled nonlinearly rather than by an ad hoc clip.

# 6. B10 — DERANDOMIZE: deterministic convex program and the exact remaining certificate

The randomized ideas above should not become a proof unless their output can be converted into a deterministic source-valid completion. In the moment formulation, that conversion is clean.

## 6.1 Entropy dual is deterministic

For fixed `D` and target `delta`, consider the maximum-entropy feasibility problem

maximize `-sum_j p_j log p_j`

subject to

- `p_j>=0`, `sum_j p_j=1`;
- `p_j=p_{-j}` (or equivalently impose zero sine moments);
- `sum_j p_j cos(2 pi a j/n)=-delta` for every `a in D`.

Whenever the target lies in the relative interior of the moment polytope, its optimizer has the Gibbs form above. The dual is the finite-dimensional convex problem

minimize over `lambda`

`log Z(lambda) - delta <lambda,1_D>`

(up to the harmless normalization convention for `Z`). Its first-order condition is precisely

`E_{p_lambda} r_J=-delta 1_D`.

Thus, if the B9 transversality argument could show that this dual has an interior optimizer for `delta=c/sqrt(n)` on a source-sufficient event, the resulting `p_lambda` would give a deterministic `D`-dependent theta certificate through Section 4's exact construction. No simulation or random witness would remain in the final proof.

## 6.2 A conditional covariance criterion

Let

`F(lambda)=log((1/n) sum_j exp(-<lambda,r_j>))`.

Then

`grad F(lambda)=-E_{p_lambda}r_J`,

`Hess F(lambda)=Cov_{p_lambda}(r_J) >=0`.

Suppose for a realized source mask `D` one can find a convex region containing zero and the minimizing path for

`F(lambda)-delta <lambda,1_D>`

such that throughout that region

`Hess F(lambda) >= c0 I`.

Strong convexity would then force a unique stationary point and, once the region has radius larger than a constant multiple of `delta sqrt(|D|)/c0`, place the minimizer in its interior. Since `delta sqrt(|D|)=Theta(1)` at `delta=Theta(n^{-1/2})`, a **constant-scale pathwise covariance lower bound** would be sufficient for `kappa_D=O(n^{-1/2})` on that mask.

This is a conditional criterion, not a proved covariance bound. The reason I state it is to isolate exactly what a derandomized port must certify. Proving only `Cov=(1/2)I` at the uniform law is not enough, and proving a global bound over every constant-radius parameter is unnecessarily strong and generally false due to possible Gibbs concentration.
