# 10. B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: complement symmetry is destroyed by repair cost

The theorem above succeeds at `k=0`, so the clipping algorithm is **not** falsified at its first generated residual. The next question is why the same proof cannot simply be repeated.

The reason is exact and already appears after one repair.

Let

\[
r=(-h)_+,
\qquad
r^c=(-h^c)_+,
\qquad

For mask `D`,

\[

\tag{10.1}

For the complementary nonzero mask `D^c_*`,

\[
I-P_{D^c_*}=P_0+P_D,

\[
h_1(D^c_*)
=2u-h+(P_0+P_D)r^c.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Therefore the failure of the initial reflection symmetry after one repair is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\delta_1
:=h_1(D^c_*)-(2u-h_1(D))
=(P_0+P_D)r^c+(I-P_D)r.
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

Using `I-P_D=P_0+P_{D^c_*}` gives the more revealing decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\delta_1
=P_0(r+r^c)+P_Dr^c+P_{D^c_*}r.
}
\tag{10.4}
\]

Its zero-frequency part is

\[
\boxed{
P_0\delta_1
=P_0(r+r^c)
=\frac{\|r\|_1+\|r^c\|_1}{n}\mathbf1.
}
\tag{10.5}
\]

Thus whenever either complement member requires a nonzero repair, the exact start symmetry `h^c=2u-h` **cannot** persist to the next iterate. The loss is not an accidental technical defect: its zero mode is precisely driven by the accumulated repair mass that the dual objective charges.

After subtracting this zero mode, a second obstruction remains:

\[
(I-P_0)\delta_1
=P_Dr^c+P_{D^c_*}r.
\tag{10.6}
\]

These are cross-frequency leakage terms. The first-step proof works because the two starts differ only by the tiny coordinatewise shift `2/n`; after repair, the paired states acquire both an objective/zero-mode defect and these cross-frequency defects. No bound established so far shows that either defect stays small along the trajectory.

**B20 outcome:** the first-step strategy succeeds, but its naive iteration fails for a precise structural reason. Any next proof must either control the complement defect `(10.3)` recursively, recenter/deform the algorithm to restore a usable symmetry, or abandon complement pairing after the first step.
