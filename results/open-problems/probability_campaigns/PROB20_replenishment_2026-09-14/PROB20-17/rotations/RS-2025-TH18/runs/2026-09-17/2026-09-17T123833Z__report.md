# 3. B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: the random mask is an orthogonal circulant involution

Define

\[
\boxed{B_D:=I-2P_D.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because `P_D` is an orthogonal projection,

\[
B_D^*=B_D,
\qquad
B_D^2=I.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus `B_D` is a real symmetric orthogonal circulant involution: in the Fourier basis its zero eigenvalue mode has sign `+1`, and every nonzero conjugate pair receives sign `-1` if the pair is selected into `D` and `+1` otherwise. Moreover

\[
P_D=\frac{I-B_D}{2},
\qquad
P_A=\frac{I+B_D}{2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### 3.1 Exact identification of the random start

Let `e_0` be the time-domain coordinate vector at zero. Since `F^*e_0=1` and `(1/n)F1=e_0`,

\[
P_De_0=\frac1nF1_D.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_De_0
&=e_0-\frac2nF1_D\\
&=\frac1nF(1-2\,1_D)\\
&=h_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence the previous dual start is exactly

\[
\boxed{h_0=B_De_0.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This turns the initial random Fourier series into one column of a random orthogonal circulant reflection.

### 3.2 Exact complement symmetry

Let `D^c_*` denote the complement of `D` among the nonzero frequencies, with conjugate pairs kept together. Then

\[
P_{D^c_*}=I-P_0-P_D.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{B_{D^c_*}=2P_0-B_D.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Writing

\[
u:=P_0e_0=\frac1n\mathbf 1,
\]

we get

\[
\boxed{h_0(D^c_*)=2u-h_0(D).}
\tag{3.6}
\]

At the source density `p=1/2`, `D` and `D^c_*` have the same law. Therefore the centered random start satisfies the exact distributional symmetry

\[
\boxed{h_0-u\ \stackrel d=\ -(h_0-u).}
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is the time-domain version of source complement symmetry at the starting point.

### 3.3 Bernoulli-density interpolation

For later use, replace the source bits temporarily by iid `Ber(p)` bits. Since the pair projections sum to `I-P_0`,

\[
\mathbb E_pP_D=p(I-P_0),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_pB_D=(1-2p)I+2pP_0.}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Applying this to `e_0`,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_p h_0=(1-2p)e_0+2pu.}
\tag{3.9}
\]

At `p=1/2`, the mean is exactly the uniform vector `u`.

For any deterministic vector `r`, (3.3) also gives

\[
\|P_Dr\|_2^2
=\frac12\bigl(\|r\|_2^2-\langle r,B_Dr\rangle\bigr).
\tag{3.10}
\]

At `p=1/2`, averaging a **fixed** `r` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|P_Dr\|_2^2
=\frac12\left(\|r\|_2^2-\|P_0r\|_2^2\right).
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

If `r>=0`, then `||P_0r||_2^2=||r||_1^2/n`.

### B14 outcome and failure

But `r_k` is generated by that same `B_D`, so substituting `r_k(D)` into (3.11) is invalid. Complementation also does **not** conjugate the nonlinear trajectory to itself: (3.6) is centered at `u`, whereas the negative-part map in (2.4) thresholds at zero. # 4. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: TH18 is a random block-coordinate projection of a regular simplex

The source frequency primal in Table I of the primary paper is

\[
\vartheta(G_D)
=
\max\left\{ny_0:
 y\ge0,\ \|y\|_1=1,\ y_j=y_{n-j},\
 \langle y,f_k\rangle=0\ (k\in D)
\right\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The evenness constraint may be dropped without changing the optimum if one keeps the symmetric constraint set `D`: any feasible probability vector can be reflected and averaged with its reflection, preserving `y_0`, positivity, total mass, and all Fourier-zero constraints. Thus it is equivalent to optimize over all probability vectors `mu` on `Z_n` satisfying

\[
\widehat\mu(k)=0
\qquad(k\in D).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let

\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n},
\qquad
v_j=(\zeta^{kj})_{k\in D},
\qquad j\in\mathbb Z_n,
\tag{4.3}
\]

viewed in the real Hilbert space cut out by conjugate symmetry on the coordinates `k,-k`. Then (4.2) is exactly

\[
\sum_j\mu_jv_j=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Define the directional radial depth

\[
\rho_D
:=
\sup\left\{t\ge0:
-tv_0\in\operatorname{conv}\{v_j:j\ne0\}
\right\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

If `a=mu_0`, normalize the remaining mass to a point

\[
c=\frac1{1-a}\sum_{j\ne0}\mu_jv_j
\in\operatorname{conv}\{v_j:j\ne0\}.
\]

Equation (4.4) becomes

\[
c=-\frac{a}{1-a}v_0.
\]

Conversely every such convex representation gives a feasible probability vector. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\max\mu_0=\frac{\rho_D}{1+\rho_D}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(G_D)
=n\frac{\rho_D}{1+\rho_D}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Put

\[
M_D:=\rho_D^{-1}.
\]

Then the exact polar form is

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(G_D)=\frac{n}{1+M_D},
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
M_D=\max\left\{
-\langle q,v_0\rangle:
\langle q,v_j\rangle\le1\ \forall j\ne0
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

The same statement can be written as a one-sided bandlimited peak problem. For

\[
f_j:=-\langle q,v_j\rangle,
\]

we have `sum_j f_j=0`, the Fourier support of `f` is contained in `D`, and the inequalities in (4.9) become `f_j>=-1` for `j!=0`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
M_D=\max\left\{
f_0:
 f\in\mathbb R^n,\ \sum_jf_j=0,\
 \operatorname{supp}\widehat f\subseteq D,\
 f_j\ge-1\ (j\ne0)
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

This is the positive/one-sided structure that a generic kernel `l_1/l_2` inequality discards.

### 4.1 Why it is literally a projected regular simplex

Define the full nonzero-frequency columns

\[
w_j=(\zeta^{kj})_{k=1}^{n-1}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Their Hermitian Gram matrix is real and satisfies

\[
\langle w_j,w_\ell\rangle
=
\begin{cases}
n-1,&j=\ell,\\
-1,&j\ne\ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Also `sum_j w_j=0`. Thus the `n` vectors `w_j`, in their real span of dimension `n-1`, are the vertices of a regular simplex centered at zero. The vector `v_j` in (4.3) is exactly its orthogonal coordinate-block projection onto the conjugate frequency pairs selected by `D`.

For the source ensemble, each of the `(n-1)/2` two-dimensional real Fourier blocks is retained independently with probability `1/2`. Hence TH18 is exactly a **random half-block-coordinate projection of a regular simplex**, followed by asking for the radial depth opposite one distinguished projected vertex.

This places the source next to random projected-simplex / random-cone geometry. There is substantial literature on Haar-random projections of regular simplices, Gaussian polytopes, Donoho--Tanner/Cover--Efron cones, and Hoffman/error-bound constants. But the available results I located do not identify the directional radial depth for this **Fourier-fixed, random coordinate-block** projection. A Haar-rotated simplex projects to a Gaussian-polytope model; the source does not Haar-rotate the simplex. Replacing the Fourier block projection by a Gaussian or Haar projection would change the source model.

### 4.2 Exact complement reciprocity in the polar variable

Let `D^c_*` be the nonzero-frequency complement. Since every circulant graph is vertex-transitive,

\[
\vartheta(G_D)\vartheta(G_{D^c_*})=n.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Substituting (4.8) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
(1+M_D)(1+M_{D^c_*})=n.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

At source density `p=1/2`, `D` and `D^c_*` are identically distributed. Therefore

\[
Z_D:=\log\frac{1+M_D}{\sqrt n}

has the exact reciprocal symmetry

\[
\boxed{Z_D\stackrel d=-Z_D.}
\tag{4.15}
\]

This is the projected-simplex/polar version of the theta reciprocity already used for the source lower bound.

# 5. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE: exact leave-one-frequency-out identity

The Bernoulli-density interpolation from Section 3 can be used to isolate **exactly** how dependence between the current residual and the Fourier mask spoils the independent-vector contraction.

Let the pair bits be iid `Ber(p)`. For a deterministic vector `r`, orthogonality of the pair projections gives

\[
\|P_Dr\|_2^2
=\sum_a\xi_a\|Q_ar\|_2^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Averaging yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_p\|P_Dr\|_2^2
=p\left(\|r\|_2^2-\|P_0r\|_2^2\right).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `r>=0`, this is

\[
\mathbb E_p\|P_Dr\|_2^2
=p\left(\|r\|_2^2-\frac{\|r\|_1^2}{n}\right).
\tag{5.3}
\]

At the source point `p=1/2` this is already a factor-`1/2` mean-square contraction for any fixed nonnegative vector.

The residual `r_k=r_k(\xi)` is adaptive. For a general mask-dependent vector `r(\xi)`, define

\[
f_a(\xi):=\|Q_ar(\xi)\|_2^2
\tag{5.4}
\]

and, holding every bit except `a` fixed,

\[
\Delta_af_a(\xi_{-a})
:=f_a(1,\xi_{-a})-f_a(0,\xi_{-a}).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Conditioning only on `xi_{-a}` gives the elementary exact identity

\[
\mathbb E_p[\xi_af_a]
=p\,\mathbb E_pf_a
+p(1-p)\,\mathbb E_p\Delta_af_a.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Summing over all pair blocks and using

\[
\sum_af_a(\xi)
=\|(I-P_0)r(\xi)\|_2^2
\]

pointwise gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p\|P_Dr\|_2^2
={}&p\,\mathbb E_p\left(\|r\|_2^2-\|P_0r\|_2^2\right)\\
&+p(1-p)\sum_a\mathbb E_p\Delta_a\|Q_ar\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

For a nonnegative adaptive vector define the diagonal leave-one-frequency influence

\[
\mathcal I_p(r)
:=\sum_a\mathbb E_p\Delta_a\|Q_ar\|_2^2.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_p\|P_Dr\|_2^2
=p\,\mathbb E_p\left(\|r\|_2^2-\frac{\|r\|_1^2}{n}\right)
+p(1-p)\mathcal I_p(r).
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

At the source density,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|P_Dr\|_2^2
=\frac12\mathbb E\|r\|_2^2
-\frac1{2n}\mathbb E\|r\|_1^2
+\frac14\mathcal I_{1/2}(r).
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Thus the entire discrepancy from the independent-mask factor-`1/2` law is concentrated in one explicit diagonal influence term.

## 5.1 Annealed influence criterion for deleting the log-log exponent

Apply (5.10) to the actual generated residual `r_k`. The previous report gives

\[
\|r_{k+1}\|_2^2\le\|P_Dr_k\|_2^2.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Suppose there is an `epsilon>0`, independent of `n` and `k`, such that every generated residual obeys

\[

\le
(2-4\epsilon)\mathbb E\|r_k\|_2^2
+\frac2n\mathbb E\|r_k\|_1^2.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

Then (5.10)--(5.11) imply

\[
\mathbb E\|r_{k+1}\|_2^2
\le(1-\epsilon)\mathbb E\|r_k\|_2^2.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Since `||r_0||_2<=||h_0||_2=1` instancewise,

\[
\mathbb E\|r_k\|_1
\le
\sqrt{n\,\mathbb E\|r_k\|_2^2}
\le
\sqrt n\,(1-\epsilon)^{k/2}.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Tonelli and the exact objective increments (2.5) then give

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\vartheta(\bar G_n)
\le
2+\frac{\sqrt n}{1-\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}.
}
\tag{5.15}
\]

By complement invariance this is an `O(sqrt n)` bound for the source expectation, hence would certify

\[
\alpha_{LL}=0.

More generally, if the contraction deficit in (5.13) is

\[
\epsilon_n\ge c(\log\log n)^{-\beta},

then the same geometric summation gives

\[
\mathbb E\vartheta(G_n)
=O\!\left(\sqrt n(\log\log n)^\beta\right).
\tag{5.16}
\]

Thus any source-valid proof with `beta<1/2` moves the frozen metric.

This criterion is genuinely different from the previous report's **pointwise spread** criterion. The prior route asks every generated `r_k` to remain spatially spread. ## 5.2 Why simply differentiating in `p` does not finish the job

Russo's formula for a scalar mask functional `Phi(xi)` involves

\[
\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb E_p\Phi
=\sum_a\mathbb E_p\Delta_a\Phi.
\tag{5.17}
\]

But the influence term in (5.8) is only the **diagonal** part

\[
\sum_a\mathbb E_p\Delta_a f_a.

If one differentiates the total nonzero-frequency energy

\[

\[
\sum_{a,b}\mathbb E_p\Delta_bf_a,

which also contains all cross-influences `a!=b`. I found no sign or cancellation theorem that isolates the diagonal from those cross terms. Therefore the `p` interpolation has succeeded in identifying the exact dependence correction but has **not** yet bounded it.
