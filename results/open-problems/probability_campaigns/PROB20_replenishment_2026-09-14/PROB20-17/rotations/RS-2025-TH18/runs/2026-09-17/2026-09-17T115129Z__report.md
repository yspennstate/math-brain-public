## 2. Exact setup for the corrected dual

Let `D` be the symmetric set of nonzero source edge offsets and put

\[
A:=\{0\}\cup D^c.
\]

Use the paper's nonunitary DFT `F`, so `F^*F=nI`, and define orthogonal time-domain Fourier projections

\[
P_D:=\frac1nF\,\mathrm{diag}(1_D)F^*,
\qquad
P_A:=I-P_D.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `theta(bar G)`, the frequency dual is

\[
\vartheta(\bar G)=
\min_t\left\{1+nt_0:\ Ft\ge0,\ t_k=-\frac1n\ (k\in D),\ t_k=t_{n-k}\right\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The real-even symmetry is preserved automatically below because `D` is reflection invariant.

Let

\[
b_0=1,\qquad
b_k=\begin{cases}-1,&k\in D,\\ +1,&k\in A\setminus\{0\},\end{cases}
\]

and start from

\[
t^{(0)}:=\frac bn,
\qquad
h_0:=Ft^{(0)}=\frac1nFb.
\tag{2.3}
\]

This already satisfies every affine frequency constraint in (2.2); only nonnegativity of `h_0` may fail. Parseval gives the exact normalization

\[
\|h_0\|_2^2
=\frac1{n^2}\|Fb\|_2^2
=\frac1n\|b\|_2^2=1.
\tag{2.4}
\]

For any current `h_k`, define

\[
r_k:=(-h_k)_+\ge0,
\qquad
c_k:=(h_k)_+=h_k+r_k,
\tag{2.5}
\]

and perform the clipping correction from the previous report:

\[
\boxed{h_{k+1}:=h_k+P_A r_k.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Because the correction lies in the free-frequency direction `Ran(P_A)`, all constrained coefficients `t_k=-1/n`, `k in D`, remain fixed.

# 3. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: clipping is exactly alternating orthogonal projection

The previous report gave (2.6) as a direct correction scheme. The new observation is that it is not an ad hoc iteration: it is **exactly the classical alternating-projection algorithm** between one affine Fourier space and the nonnegative orthant.

Define

\[
\mathcal H:=\{Ft:\ t_k=-1/n\ (k\in D),\ t_k=t_{n-k}\},
\qquad
\mathcal C:=\mathbb R_+^n
\tag{3.1}
\]

inside the real-even subspace. Since `h_k in H`, the orthogonal projection of any vector `z` onto `H` is

\[
P_{\mathcal H}(z)=h_k+P_A(z-h_k).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The orthogonal projection onto `C` is simply coordinatewise positive part:

\[
P_{\mathcal C}(h_k)=c_k=h_k+r_k.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Combining (3.2) and (3.3),

\[
P_{\mathcal H}P_{\mathcal C}(h_k)
=h_k+P_A r_k=h_{k+1}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{h_{k+1}=P_{\mathcal H}P_{\mathcal C}h_k.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently, on the affine space `H`, this is projected gradient descent with unit step for the convex piecewise-quadratic feasibility objective

\[
\Phi(h):=\frac12\operatorname{dist}(h,\mathcal C)^2
=\frac12\|(-h)_+\|_2^2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Indeed `grad Phi(h_k)=h_k-P_C h_k=-r_k`, and projecting the gradient into the tangent direction `Ran(P_A)` gives exactly (2.6).

This imports a large body of alternating-projection / Hoffman-error-bound intuition, but it does **not** by itself solve the asymptotic problem. Fixed-dimensional convergence of alternating projections says nothing about whether the relevant angle/error-bound constant stays bounded away from zero as `n -> infinity`.

### Exact objective accounting

The dual objective in (2.2) can be written in the time domain as

\[
1+nt_0=1+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}h_j,
\tag{3.6}
\]

because `t_0=n^{-1}\sum_j h_j`. Since the zero Fourier mode lies in `A`, `P_A 1=1`, and therefore

\[
\sum_j(P_A r_k)_j=\sum_j(r_k)_j=\|r_k\|_1.
\]

Thus each clipping step raises the dual objective by **exactly** the negative mass repaired:

\[
\boxed{J_{k+1}-J_k=\|r_k\|_1,\qquad J_0=2.}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Consequently, any proof of

\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\|r_k\|_1=O(\sqrt n)
\tag{3.8}
\]

would immediately give a source-valid expectation upper bound `E theta(G)=O(sqrt n)` after complement invariance, hence `alpha_LL=0`.

### Fixed-face linearization

There is also an exact local linear description. Suppose that from one step to the next the negative support is the same set `S` and no sign changes occur outside it. Because `r_k` is supported on `S` and `h_k^+=0` on `S`, (2.6) gives on `S`

\[
r_{k+1}=P_S P_D P_S r_k.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus once an active face stabilizes, the tail rate is governed by the Fourier concentration operator `P_S P_D P_S`. A strict bound

\[
\lambda_{\max}(P_S P_D P_S)\le\rho<1
\tag{3.10}
\]

would give geometric `l_2` decay on that face. The hard point is not the linear algebra after the face is known; it is proving a source-uniform bound for the **dependent active faces actually generated by the random mask**.

### B19 outcome

This method produces a new exact category change for the previous dual idea: TH18's dual-repair route is a random polyhedral alternating-projection / projected-gradient problem with an exact path-cost objective. The relevant missing theorem is now a quantitative random error bound or angle bound **along this generated trajectory**, not generic convergence of the algorithm.

# 4. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED / DECREASES: exact Fejer identity

The alternating-projection formulation yields an exact energy identity stronger than the elementary residual inequality from the previous run.

Let `z` be any feasible dual image,

\[
z\in\mathcal H\cap\mathcal C.
\]

Because `c_k=P_C h_k`, we have `h_k=c_k-r_k`, with `c_k,r_k>=0` and disjoint coordinate supports. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|h_k-z\|_2^2
&=\|c_k-z-r_k\|_2^2\\
&=\|c_k-z\|_2^2+\|r_k\|_2^2+2\langle z,r_k\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Next, `h_{k+1}=P_H c_k`, while `z in H`. Orthogonal projection gives the Pythagorean identity

\[
\|c_k-z\|_2^2
=\|h_{k+1}-z\|_2^2+\|c_k-h_{k+1}\|_2^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

But by (2.6),

\[
c_k-h_{k+1}
=h_k+r_k-h_k-P_A r_k=P_D r_k.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (4.1)--(4.3) yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\|h_k-z\|_2^2-\|h_{k+1}-z\|_2^2
=
\|r_k\|_2^2+\|P_Dr_k\|_2^2+2\langle z,r_k\rangle.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Therefore the iterates are Fejer monotone with respect to the feasible set, and in particular

\[
\sum_k\|r_k\|_2^2\le\|h_0-z\|_2^2,
\qquad
\sum_k\|P_Dr_k\|_2^2\le\|h_0-z\|_2^2.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Also,

\[
2\sum_k\langle z,r_k\rangle\le\|h_0-z\|_2^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is real progress in understanding the route, but it is **not yet the needed path-length bound**. Square summability of `||r_k||_2` does not imply summability of `||r_k||_1`; an arbitrary sequence can have square-summable step sizes and infinite total length.

There is a second exact invariant that is more directly tied to the source exponent. From

\[
h_{k+1}=c_k-P_Dr_k
\]

with `c_k>=0`,

\[
r_{k+1}\le(P_Dr_k)_+
\]

coordinatewise, so

\[
\|r_{k+1}\|_2^2
\le\|P_Dr_k\|_2^2
=\|r_k\|_2^2-\|P_A r_k\|_2^2.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The zero-frequency projection `P_0` is contained in `P_A`. Since `r_k>=0`,

\[
\|P_A r_k\|_2^2
\ge\|P_0r_k\|_2^2
=\frac{\|r_k\|_1^2}{n}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|r_{k+1}\|_2^2
\le
\|r_k\|_2^2-rac{\|r_k\|_1^2}{n}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This inequality uses only positivity plus the fact that the zero Fourier frequency is free. All the other free frequencies can only improve the contraction.

Define the dimensionless residual-spread statistic

\[
\eta_k:=
\frac{\|r_k\|_1^2}{n\|r_k\|_2^2}
\in[1/n,1]
\qquad(r_k\ne0).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|r_{k+1}\|_2\le\sqrt{1-\eta_k}\,\|r_k\|_2.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

This is the cleanest scalar diagnostic found so far for the dual algorithm.

# 5. B28 — REPLACE "FOR ALL" WITH "FOR TYPICAL": a generated-residual criterion that would move `alpha_LL`

The primary paper explicitly explains why a global `l_1/l_2` delocalization estimate on the whole Fourier kernel cannot hold: sparse sign-changing kernel vectors exist. That obstruction does **not** directly address the sequence here. Every `r_k` is nonnegative and is generated by one very specific nonlinear alternating-projection orbit from the random sign start `h_0`.

Equation (4.11) converts that distinction into an exact sufficient criterion.

## Theorem — generated-residual spread criterion

Assume for a given source instance that the clipping orbit satisfies, for every nonzero residual,

\[
\eta_k
=\frac{\|r_k\|_1^2}{n\|r_k\|_2^2}
\ge\eta>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(\bar G)
\le
2+\frac{\sqrt n}{1-\sqrt{1-\eta}}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

By (4.11), with `q=sqrt(1-eta)<1`,

\[
\|r_k\|_2\le q^k\|r_0\|_2.
\]

By Parseval (2.4), `||r_0||_2<=||h_0||_2=1`. Also `||r_k||_1<=sqrt(n)||r_k||_2`. Therefore

\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\|r_k\|_1
\le\sqrt n\sum_{k\ge0}q^k
=\frac{\sqrt n}{1-q}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The increments `P_A r_k` are absolutely summable in `l_2`, so `h_k` converges. Since `||r_k||_2 ->0`, the limit is nonnegative; since every correction lies in `Ran(P_A)`, the constrained frequency values remain `-1/n` on `D`. Hence the limit is dual feasible. Using the exact objective path formula (3.7) gives (5.2). QED.

This theorem has an immediate polylogarithmic form. If on a high-probability event the actual generated orbit obeys

\[
\eta_k\ge c(\log\log n)^{-\beta}
\qquad\text{for all }k,
\tag{5.4}
\]

then, using `1-sqrt(1-x)>=x/2`,

\[
\vartheta(\bar G)
\le 2+C\sqrt n(\log\log n)^\beta
\tag{5.5}
\]

on that event, with the exceptional event handled separately by the paper's crude `O(sqrt(n log n))` / trivial bounds if its probability is sufficiently small. Thus a source-valid probabilistic proof of (5.4) with any

\[
\boxed{\beta<1/2}
\]

would **strictly move the frozen metric**, while a constant lower bound `eta_k>=c>0` would give `alpha_LL=0`.

This is weaker and more source-adapted than controlling every vector in a random Fourier kernel. It asks only that the **negative parts produced by the algorithm never become too concentrated relative to their `l_2` mass**.

A convenient stronger but still trajectory-only condition is an anti-spike estimate

\[
\sqrt n\,\|r_k\|_\infty\le C\|r_k\|_2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Indeed `||r||_2^2<=||r||_infty||r||_1` then gives `eta_k>=C^{-2}`. The point is again that (5.6) is required only for generated residuals, not for arbitrary kernel vectors.

### Why this does not already follow from the published RIP argument

The paper's uniform RIP input controls all sufficiently sparse vectors and leads to a worst-case kernel estimate after discarding positivity. The generated residuals start dense, and their active sets are strongly dependent on the same random Fourier mask that defines `P_D`. One cannot condition on a support as though it were independent and apply a standard partial-Fourier RIP theorem. Conversely, sparse sign-changing kernel vectors do not refute (5.1), because `r_k>=0` and is not an adversarially chosen kernel vector.

The fixed-face formula (3.9) shows exactly what a successful typical-set argument would need to control: a dependent sequence of Fourier concentration operators on the active sets selected by the orbit. This is the place where leave-one-out, random-cone, or state-evolution ideas could enter.

### B28 outcome

No source-valid proof of (5.4) was obtained in this run, so the metric does not move. But the target has been reduced from a vague "positivity may help" statement to one explicit scalar quantity `eta_k` whose lower bound translates quantitatively and immediately into a new `alpha_LL`.

## 6. What failed, precisely

1. **The preceding dual was labeled on the wrong graph.** The source Table I puts frequency-dual constraints for `theta(G)` on nonedges. This run repairs the route by treating the edge-constrained clipping scheme as a dual construction for `theta(bar G)` and using only the ensemble's complement invariance to return to the source expectation. No per-instance shortcut is retained.

2. **Generic alternating-projection convergence is too weak.** Classical theory supplies convergence in each fixed finite-dimensional instance, often with linear rates under transversality/Hoffman-type conditions. The source asymptotic needs a quantitative rate whose constant is controlled uniformly as `n` grows. That is exactly the missing random-geometric estimate.

3. **The Fejer identity is not enough by itself.** Equation (4.4) gives `sum ||r_k||_2^2<infinity`, but the dual objective is the `l_1` path length `sum ||r_k||_1`. Square-summability alone does not bound that path length.

4. **Worst-case sparse-RIP is not the right quantifier.** The primary paper already records sparse vectors in the random Fourier kernel and explains why the desired global `l_1/l_2` estimate fails without positivity. Re-running that argument cannot remove the logarithmic loss. What remains plausible is a bound only for the nonnegative, dynamically generated residuals.

5. **A uniform positive-cone angle would be stronger than needed.** One could ask for `||P_Dr||_2<=rho||r||_2` for every nonnegative `r` and some absolute `rho<1`. I did not prove this and do not assume it. Near-critical random-cone geometry makes such a global statement suspect; the report therefore keeps the quantifier on the actual residual orbit.
