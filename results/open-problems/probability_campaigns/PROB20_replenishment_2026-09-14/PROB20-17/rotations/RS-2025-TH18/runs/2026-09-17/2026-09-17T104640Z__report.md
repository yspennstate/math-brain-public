## 1. Exact source, model, and frozen metric

The outside source is Afonso S. Bandeira, Daniil Dmitriev, Kevin Lucca, Petar Nizić-Nikolac and Almut Rödder, *Randomstrasse101: Open Problems of 2025*, arXiv:2603.29571v1, Entry 9, Conjecture 18. The source asks, for its dense random circulant graph ensemble,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\,\vartheta(G_n)=(1+o(1))\sqrt n.}
\]

The primary partial-result paper is Afonso S. Bandeira, Jarosław Błasiok, Daniil Dmitriev, Ulysse Faure, Anastasia Kireeva and Dmitriy Kunisky, *The Lovász number of random circulant graphs*, arXiv:2502.16227v1, published at SampTA 2025, DOI `10.1109/SampTA64769.2025.11133544`.

I recovered its exact source model before doing new mathematics. For odd `n`, put `m=(n-1)/2`, sample

\[
x\in\{0,1\}^m
\]

uniformly, use the symmetric first adjacency row

\[
(0,\;x,\;\overleftarrow{x}),
\]

and circulate it over `Z_n`. Thus the graph is determined by `m` independent bits but every bit controls an entire translation orbit of edges. This is the model used below; no Erdős–Rényi, Paley, or alternate circulant ensemble is substituted.

The primary theorem gives

\[
\sqrt n\le \mathbb E\vartheta(G_n)
\le C\sqrt{n\log\log n}
\]

for an absolute finite `C`. Therefore the frozen successor metric starts at

\[
\boxed{\alpha_{LL}=1/2.}
\]

It moves only if this track proves a source-valid bound

\[
\mathbb E\vartheta(G_n)
\le C\sqrt n(\log\log n)^\alpha
\]

with a smaller certified exponent. The present run does **not** prove such an improvement, so `alpha_LL` remains `1/2`. Removing the log-log factor would still not prove the source leading constant `1`.

# 3. B2 — attack the negation first: refutation is equivalent to persistent reciprocal spread

A circulant graph is vertex-transitive, so the standard Lovász identity gives

\[
\vartheta(G)\vartheta(\overline G)=n.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The dense random-circulant ensemble is invariant under complementing all of the independent generator bits. Hence

\[
\vartheta(G_n)\ \stackrel d=\ \vartheta(\overline G_n)
=\frac{n}{\vartheta(G_n)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Set

\[
A_n:=\frac{\vartheta(G_n)}{\sqrt n}>0.
\]

Then

\[
A_n\stackrel d=A_n^{-1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The familiar lower bound `E A_n >= 1` follows immediately, but the exact algebra gives a sharper target:

\[
\begin{aligned}
2(\mathbb E A_n-1)
&=\mathbb E\left(A_n+A_n^{-1}-2\right)\\
&=\mathbb E\frac{(A_n-1)^2}{A_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently, in the original normalization,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\vartheta(G_n)-\sqrt n
=\frac12\,\mathbb E
\frac{(\vartheta(G_n)-\sqrt n)^2}{\vartheta(G_n)}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is only an algebraic refinement of the complement-symmetry lower-bound mechanism, and I do **not** claim novelty for that mechanism.

It does, however, make a source-valid refutation target exact. If

\[
X_n:=\log A_n,
\]

then `X_n d= -X_n` and

\[
\mathbb E A_n=\mathbb E\cosh X_n.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Consequently:

- Conjecture 18 is equivalent to

\[
\mathbb E\frac{(A_n-1)^2}{A_n}=o(1).
\tag{3.7}
\]

- A refutation with asymptotic leading constant strictly larger than `1` would follow from **persistent multiplicative dispersion**. For example, if along some subsequence there are fixed `delta,eta>0` with

\[
\mathbb P(|X_n|\ge\delta)\ge\eta,
\]

then

\[
\mathbb E A_n\ge
1+\eta(\cosh\delta-1)>1.
\tag{3.8}
\]

- Conversely, the source conjecture forces `X_n -> 0` in probability, because `cosh X_n-1` is nonnegative and its expectation tends to zero.

### B2 outcome

This route did **not** produce a refutation. It shows why finite anomalous graphs and even a finite-n strict Jensen gap are irrelevant: one needs a nonvanishing amount of reciprocal spread of `theta/sqrt(n)` as `n -> infinity`. I found no source-valid mechanism in the current proof/literature that forces such spread. The exact identity (3.5) is retained as a diagnostic for future lower-bound attacks, not as movement of `alpha_LL`.

# 4. B11 — dualize: an exact iterative clipping program with an `O(sqrt n)` first correction

The most promising route this run came from using the paper's **frequency-domain dual** instead of trying to strengthen the full-kernel RIP statement that the authors already explain cannot be true.

Let `D` be the symmetric set of nonzero generator frequencies corresponding to edges from `0`, and let

\[
A:=\{0\}\cup D^c
\]

be the free frequency set. With the paper's nonunitary DFT `F`, its frequency dual is

\[
\vartheta(G)=
\min_t\{1+nt_0:\ Ft\ge0,\ t_k=-1/n\ (k\in D),\ t_k=t_{n-k}\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the sign vector used by the paper,

\[
b_0=1,\qquad
b_k=
\begin{cases}
-1,&k\in D,\\
+1,&k\notin D,
\end{cases}
\]

and let

\[
g:=Fb.
\]

The starting dual vector

\[
t^{(0)}:=b/n
\]

already has the required values `-1/n` on every constrained edge frequency and is symmetric. Its defect is only positivity:

\[
h_0:=Ft^{(0)}=g/n
\]

need not be nonnegative.

Let `P_A` and `P_D` denote the orthogonal Fourier projections in the **time** domain onto frequencies `A` and `D`, respectively:

\[
P_A=\frac1nF\,\mathrm{diag}(1_A)F^*,
\qquad
P_D=I-P_A.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For any current time-domain dual image `h_k`, define its negative residual

\[
r_k:=(-h_k)_+\ge0
\]

coordinatewise, and update

\[
\boxed{
h_{k+1}:=h_k+P_A r_k.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because the correction uses only free frequencies, every constrained coefficient `t_j=-1/n`, `j in D`, remains untouched. Since `D` and `A` are reflection invariant, the real/even symmetry is preserved as well.

The update has two exact identities which make it potentially useful.

First,

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{k+1}
&=h_k+r_k-P_Dr_k\\
&=(h_k)_+-P_Dr_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
r_{k+1}\le (P_Dr_k)_+}
\tag{4.5}
\]

coordinatewise. Moreover `0 notin D`, so `P_Dr_k` has mean zero and hence

\[
\|(P_Dr_k)_+\|_1
=\frac12\|P_Dr_k\|_1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Second, the **dual objective increment is exactly the negative mass repaired at that step**. Indeed the frequency-zero coefficient of the correction is

\[
\delta t_{k,0}=\frac1n\sum_j(r_k)_j,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
(1+nt^{(k+1)}_0)-(1+nt^{(k)}_0)=\|r_k\|_1.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The starting objective is `2`. Thus after `K` corrections,

\[
1+nt^{(K)}_0
=2+\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\|r_j\|_1.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The striking point is that the **first repair already has the desired scale**. For each coordinate,

\[
g_j
=1+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{(n-1)/2}
\varepsilon_\ell\cos(2\pi j\ell/n),
\]

with independent Rademacher signs `epsilon_l`. Hence `E g_j^2=O(n)`, uniformly in `j`, and

\[
\mathbb E(-g_j)_+
\le \mathbb E|g_j|
\le (\mathbb E g_j^2)^{1/2}
=O(\sqrt n).
\]

Since `r_0=(-g/n)_+`, summing `n` coordinates gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\|r_0\|_1=O(\sqrt n).}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This leads to a clean sufficient theorem.

## Dual-clipping criterion

If there is an absolute `rho<1` such that for the actual random residual sequence generated by (4.3),

\[
\mathbb E\|r_{k+1}\|_1
\le \rho\,\mathbb E\|r_k\|_1
\qquad\text{for every }k\ge0,
\tag{4.10}
\]

then

\[
\mathbb E\vartheta(G_n)=O(\sqrt n),
\]

and therefore the frozen exponent moves all the way to `alpha_LL=0`.

### Proof of the criterion

By (4.9) and (4.10),

\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\mathbb E\|r_k\|_1
\le \frac{C\sqrt n}{1-\rho}<\infty.
\]

Tonelli then gives summability almost surely. The Fourier coefficient of each correction is bounded in absolute value by `||r_k||_1/n`, so the dual coefficients converge; similarly, `P_A r_k` converges absolutely coordinatewise. Because `||r_k||_1 ->0`, the limiting time-domain image is nonnegative. The constrained edge-frequency values never moved. Hence the limit is dual feasible, and (4.8) gives

\[
\vartheta(G_n)
\le2+\sum_{k\ge0}\|r_k\|_1.
\]

Taking expectation proves the claim. QED.

By (4.5)-(4.6), it is enough to obtain a strict average contraction for the very special positive residuals `r_k` under the random half-Fourier projection `P_D`. This is **not** the false global claim that every vector in the kernel has `l_2 <= C l_1/sqrt(n)`. The sparse sign-changing kernel vectors identified in the primary paper do not by themselves rule out (4.10).

### B11 outcome

The first correction costs only `O(sqrt n)` in expectation, so **all remaining logarithmic loss is concentrated in whether later residuals can be shown to contract**.

# 5. B30 — one scale at a time: where the existing proof pays `sqrt(log log n)` and a weaker positivity-aware substitute

The primary proof's logarithmic loss can be isolated very cleanly.

Its random objective `g` has subgaussian coordinates of scale `sqrt(n)`. If one clips at

\[
\tau\asymp\sqrt{n\log\log n},
\]

then the exceptional coordinate set has size at most roughly `n/log^C n` with sufficiently high probability. That sparsity is small enough for the **uniform** subsampled-Fourier RIP estimate used in the paper. The exceptional part then contributes `O(sqrt n)`. But the bounded part is estimated only by

\[
\langle y,g_{\mathrm{small}}\rangle
\le \|y\|_1\|g_{\mathrm{small}}\|_\infty
\le \tau,
\]

and this is exactly where `sqrt(log log n)` enters.

So the current logarithm is not an accumulation of many genuinely costly scales. It is the price of choosing **one threshold high enough that a worst-case uniform sparse-vector theorem becomes applicable**.

That suggests replacing the uniform theorem by a statement only about the actual positive feasible measures and the actual random level sets of `g`.

Let `K_D` be the frequency-primal feasible set

\[
K_D:=\{y\ge0:\ \|y\|_1=1,\ y\text{ even},\ \widehat y(k)=0\ \forall k\in D\},
\]

and for `t>=0` define the actual superlevel set

\[
I_t(g):=\{j:g_j>t\}.
\]

For a fixed `g`, layer cake gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\vartheta(G)
&=\sup_{y\in K_D}\langle y,g\rangle\\
&\le \sup_{y\in K_D}\langle y,g_+\rangle\\
&\le \int_0^\infty
\sup_{y\in K_D}y(I_t(g))\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore the following **scale-local mass profile** would remove the logarithmic loss:

> There are absolute `C,c0>0` and a high-probability event `H_n`, with `P(H_n^c)<=n^{-2}`, such that for every `t>=c0 sqrt(n)`,
> \[
> \sup_{y\in K_D}y(I_t(g))
> \le C\sqrt{\frac{|I_t(g)|}{n}}.
> \tag{5.2}
> \]

Indeed the low levels contribute at most `c0 sqrt(n)` because every `y` has mass one. On `H_n`, (5.1)-(5.2) and Jensen give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\vartheta(G)1_{H_n}]
&\le c_0\sqrt n
+C\int_{c_0\sqrt n}^\infty
\mathbb E\sqrt{|I_t(g)|/n}\,dt\\
&\le c_0\sqrt n
+C\int_{c_0\sqrt n}^\infty
\sqrt{\mathbb E|I_t(g)|/n}\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Each `g_j` is a bounded Rademacher sum at variance scale `n`, so Hoeffding gives

\[
\mathbb P(g_j>t)
\le C_1e^{-c_1(t-1)_+^2/n}.
\]

Correlations among the coordinates are irrelevant for the first moment of the level-set size:

\[
\mathbb E|I_t(g)|
=\sum_j\mathbb P(g_j>t)
\le C_1n e^{-c_1(t-1)_+^2/n}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Substituting (5.4) into (5.3) gives a convergent Gaussian integral of order `sqrt(n)`. On `H_n^c`, the trivial bound `theta(G)<=n` costs only `O(1/n)`. Hence (5.2) implies

\[
\mathbb E\vartheta(G)=O(\sqrt n),
\]

again yielding `alpha_LL=0`.

The point of (5.2) is its scope. It does **not** demand `l_2/l_1` delocalization for every sign-changing vector in the Fourier kernel. It asks only for positive probability measures in `K_D`, and only against the particular Fourier superlevel sets created by the same random instance. The primary paper's sparse-kernel obstruction therefore does not immediately kill this route.

### B30 outcome

The source loss is now localized to one missing phenomenon:

\[
\boxed{\text{positivity-aware control of feasible mass on the actual high Fourier level sets.}}
\]

The existing uniform RIP estimate is too expensive because it must cover **all** sparse supports. A next proof should try to exploit that `I_t(g)` is not an adversarial support and that `y` is nonnegative. This is the same structural advantage seen from the dual-clipping route: both ask for typical positive objects rather than arbitrary kernel vectors.

# 6. Failed routes and what is genuinely missing

1. **Pure complement/refutation route:** complement symmetry gives the exact reciprocal-spread identity (3.5), but no current argument forces persistent spread. A finite strict gap or a single anomalous graph is not asymptotic evidence.

2. **Strengthen the paper's whole-kernel `l_1/l_2` estimate:** the primary paper explicitly explains why this is false at constant scale; sparse sign-changing kernel vectors exist. Repeating that route would be wasted work.

3. **One-shot clipping at a constant multiple of `sqrt(n)`:** it leaves a linear-size exceptional set, outside the range of the available uniform sparse-DFT RIP theorem. Raising the threshold until the set becomes polylogarithmically sparse recreates exactly the existing `sqrt(log log n)` loss.

4. **Dual clipping without a contraction theorem:** (4.3) repairs the negative coordinates but the free-frequency projection can create new negative mass elsewhere. The first repair has the correct `O(sqrt n)` expected cost; the unresolved point is summability of all later repairs.

The two new sufficient conditions, (4.10) and (5.2), are intentionally not promoted to source theorems. They identify the precise missing estimates.
