## 2. Main theorem — the inherited `63/64` threshold is not sharp even at the elementary Peierls level

The old E0011 block lemma used the standard dual-barrier representation for failure of an oriented good crossing. It bounded the number of length-`m` closed barriers by `4^m` and, under 1-dependence, selected every third closed block to obtain probability at most `epsilon^{ceil(m/3)}`. This gave the sufficient condition `4 epsilon^(1/3)<1`, i.e. `epsilon<1/64`.

The factor `4^m` pays for immediate backtracking and loops that a minimal barrier never needs.

### Lemma 2.1 — self-avoiding barrier count

In the same dual-barrier representation used by the inherited E0011 Lemma 5.2, every closed blocking barrier contains a self-avoiding closed blocking barrier. From a fixed anchoring neighborhood, the number of self-avoiding barriers of length `m>=1` is at most

`4 * 3^(m-1)`.

#### Proof

Take any finite closed blocking walk. If a vertex is visited twice, delete the loop segment between its first and last visit. Because the deleted segment begins and ends at the same dual vertex, the resulting walk still connects the same two sides of the blocking geometry and uses only vertices of the original closed walk. Iterating removes all repetitions and produces a self-avoiding closed barrier.

The dual barrier graph used in the inherited proof has degree at most four. There are at most four choices for the first step. At every later step, immediate return to the preceding vertex is forbidden by self-avoidance, leaving at most three choices; further previously visited vertices only reduce this number. Hence the count is at most `4*3^(m-1)`. QED.

### Lemma 2.2 — improved 1-dependent Peierls threshold

Let the translated good-block variables be the same 1-dependent oriented site field as in the inherited E0011 comparison, and suppose every site is good with probability at least `1-epsilon`. If

`epsilon < 1/27`,

then good blocks percolate with positive probability; from a fully seeded bottom level they have positive asymptotic block density in the same sense as the inherited comparison.

#### Proof

The geometric fact used in the old proof remains unchanged: along a dual closed barrier, every third closed block is at dependency distance greater than one from the others, so those selected blocks are mutually independent under 1-dependence. Therefore a specified length-`m` closed barrier has probability at most

`epsilon^{ceil(m/3)}`.

By Lemma 2.1, the probability of a closed self-avoiding barrier of length `m` from a fixed anchor is at most

`4*3^(m-1) epsilon^{ceil(m/3)}`.

Group lengths by residues modulo three. For `k>=0`, the three relevant bounds are, up to omission of the finitely many zero/negative starting indices,

- `m=3k`: `(4/3)(27 epsilon)^k`;
- `m=3k+1`: `4 epsilon (27 epsilon)^k`;
- `m=3k+2`: `12 epsilon (27 epsilon)^k`.

Thus the tail over barriers of length at least `M` is bounded by a constant multiple of a geometric tail with ratio `27 epsilon`. When `epsilon<1/27`, the probability of arbitrarily long blocking barriers is zero. The same planar dual-barrier implication used in the inherited E0011 lemma therefore yields an infinite oriented good path with positive probability, and the same translation argument gives positive block density from a fully seeded lower level. QED.

### Consequence 2.3 — source closure only needs good probability `>26/27`

The deterministic two-child implication, finite-range construction, continuity in `p`, and coexistence extraction are unchanged. Replacing only the Peierls estimate shows that a source-valid finite 1-dependent good-block field with

`P(good) > 26/27`

already suffices for the same source closure route.

This is a rigorous strengthening of the old `>63/64` sufficient threshold. It is not claimed optimal.

## 3. Stronger regenerative comparison

For a source-valid regenerative attempt `T`, let `a=a(T)` be SUCCESS probability, `f=f(T)` FATAL probability, and let every other outcome RETRY to the same admissible seed class.

Define the auxiliary metric

`M_reg26 := sup_T max(0, a(T)-26 f(T))`.

### Theorem 3.1 — positive `M_reg26` is sufficient for the finite block route

If one source-valid regenerative attempt has

`delta_26 := a-26f > 0`,

then a finite number of RETRYs can be truncated to produce a finite-range good-block event with probability strictly larger than `26/27`.

A sufficient truncation is

`L >= ceil(delta_26^(-1) log(54/delta_26))`.

More precisely the good probability is at least

`26/27 + delta_26/54`.

#### Proof

Exactly as in the coordinator's robust finite-retry argument, if `V_n` is the worst-case chance of SUCCESS before FATAL within at most `n` attempts, then

`V_n >= a + (1-a-f)V_(n-1)`.

Hence

`V_L >= [a/(a+f)] [1-(1-a-f)^L]`.

Put `q=a/(a+f)` and `r=1-a-f`. The improved margin gives the exact identity

`q - 26/27 = (a-26f)/(27(a+f)) = delta_26/(27(a+f))`.

Since `a+f<=1`,

`q >= 26/27 + delta_26/27`.

Also `delta_26<=a<=a+f`, so

`r <= 1-delta_26`.

The displayed choice of `L` gives

`r^L <= exp(-delta_26 L) <= delta_26/54`.

Therefore

`V_L >= q-r^L >= 26/27 + delta_26/54`.

Finite horizon and finite range follow exactly as in the coordinator compiler because there are only `L` finite attempts. QED.
