## 3. Main deterministic theorem: arbitrary-stage monotone completion

### Theorem 3.1 — every stage word completes optimally to the translated clean class

From **every** stage vector `s in {0,1,2}^N`, there is a legal designated source word of exactly

`D(s):=sum_j (2-s_j)`

marks that reaches

`(2,2,...,2)`,

i.e. the exact clean phase ladder translated outward by one lattice layer.

Moreover the word can be chosen so that:

1. first, every stage-0 cell is hit once, in arbitrary order;
2. during this first subphase the bad-boundary count `B(s)` is nonincreasing;
3. at the end of this subphase no stage 0 remains, hence `B=0` and the two active levels contain a winding / crossing vacancy separator;
4. second, every remaining stage-1 cell is hit once, in arbitrary order;
5. throughout the second subphase `B=0` remains true.

The word is source-count minimal among all designated words reaching the exact all-2 translated state.

### Proof

A designated first hit on a stage-0 cell changes only `0->1`. Such a move cannot create a `0|2` boundary. If either neighbor is stage 2, the corresponding bad boundary is removed; otherwise the local boundary was not bad before and is not bad after. Hence first-hitting stage-0 cells can only decrease `B`.

After all stage-0 cells have been hit, every coordinate lies in `{1,2}`. Therefore no `0|2` adjacency exists and `B=0`; by the inherited ladder theorem the active two-level vacancy separator exists.

Now hit every remaining stage-1 cell once. With no stage-0 cells left, a `1->2` move cannot create a `0|2` adjacency, so `B` remains zero. The terminal vector is all 2, which by the exact double-hit phase theorem is the clean phase ladder translated outward by one layer.

The number of used marks is one for each missing stage increment, namely `D(s)`. Every designated mark increases exactly one coordinate by one, so any designated route from `s` to all 2 needs at least `D(s)` marks. Hence the construction is source-count minimal. QED.

### Interpretation

A premature second hit does not create a new kind of repair work. It merely creates a temporary mismatch between a cell that is already translated and a neighboring cell that has not yet received its **first** useful hit. Giving that zero cell its ordinary first productive hit repairs the cut and simultaneously advances the translation. Repair cost is therefore included in the useful-work budget rather than added on top of it.

## 4. Exact local repair cost for a current `0|2` cut set

Define the zero endpoints of the current bad boundaries by

`R(s):={j: s_j=0 and (s_{j-1}=2 or s_{j+1}=2)}`.

### Lemma 4.1 — exact monotone repair cost

If existing stage-2 cells are not demoted and one is allowed only designated monotone increments, then the minimum number of marks needed to remove **all currently existing** `0|2` bad boundaries, without making any other stage change, is exactly

`|R(s)|`.

A minimizing word is: first-hit each cell in `R(s)` once, in arbitrary order.

### Proof

Every bad boundary has a stage-0 endpoint and a stage-2 endpoint. Under monotone designated dynamics the stage-2 endpoint cannot move backward. Therefore the bad boundary cannot disappear unless its stage-0 endpoint is incremented to stage 1. Thus every distinct zero cell in `R(s)` must be hit at least once.

Conversely one first hit on such a zero cell changes it to stage 1 and removes every bad boundary incident to that zero, possibly two at once. A `0->1` move creates no new bad boundary. After every member of `R(s)` has been hit, no originally bad boundary remains and no new one was created. QED.

This is sharper than assigning one separate service job to every bad edge: one useful first hit can remove two cuts when a zero cell lies between two stage-2 cells.

## 5. Generator calculus: the designated defect potential has no positive drift

Let

`N_a(s)=#{j:s_j=a}`

and let `E_01(s)` be the number of adjacent unordered `0|1` cell boundaries on the cycle. Recall `B(s)=E_02(s)`.

Let `L_des` be the generator of the designated stage chain alone. Every active `0` or `1` coordinate jumps at rate `lambda`.

### Lemma 5.1 — exact drift identities

For every stage vector,

`L_des B(s) = lambda [ E_01(s) - B(s) ]`,                 (5.1)

`L_des N_0(s) = -lambda N_0(s)`,                          (5.2)

and, for the remaining-work potential

`D(s)=2N_0(s)+N_1(s)`,

`L_des D(s) = -lambda [N_0(s)+N_1(s)]`.                   (5.3)

### Proof

For `B`: if a stage-0 cell `j` receives its first hit, each stage-2 neighbor removes one bad `0|2` boundary, so

`Delta_j B = -n_2(j;s)`.

If a stage-1 cell receives its second hit, each stage-0 neighbor creates one bad `0|2` boundary, so

`Delta_j B = +n_0(j;s)`.

Summing the first contribution over all stage-0 cells counts each `0|2` boundary exactly once, giving `-lambda B`. Summing the second contribution over all stage-1 cells counts each `0|1` boundary exactly once, giving `+lambda E_01`. This proves (5.1).

Equation (5.2) is immediate because every stage-0 cell leaves stage 0 at rate `lambda` and no transition creates a new stage-0 cell.

Every designated jump, whether `0->1` or `1->2`, lowers `D` by exactly one. The number of currently active designated clocks is `N_0+N_1`, proving (5.3). QED.

### Theorem 5.2 — a supermartingale defect potential

Define

`V(s):=B(s)+2N_0(s)`.

Then

`boxed{ L_des V(s) <= -lambda B(s) <= 0 }.                (5.4)

Hence `V(s_t)` is a nonnegative supermartingale under the designated ladder dynamics.

### Proof

By (5.1)--(5.2),

`L_des V = lambda [E_01-B-2N_0]`.

Every zero cell has at most two neighbors, so `E_01<=2N_0`. Therefore

`L_des V <= -lambda B`.

QED.

This gives a precise analytic meaning to the deterministic repair theorem. Premature second hits can create `B`, but the coupled quantity `B+2N_0` has no positive drift. The source of a bad boundary is always unfinished first-hit work next to it; there is no autonomous designated-defect population.

### Corollary 5.3 — exponential decay of expected remaining work

Since

`N_0+N_1 >= D/2`,

(5.3) gives

`L_des D <= -(lambda/2) D`.

Therefore

`E_s D(s_t) <= D(s) exp(-lambda t/2)`.                    (5.5)

In particular,

`P_s(s_t != all-2) <= D(s) exp(-lambda t/2)`.             (5.6)

This is a coarse but uniform completion bound for the designated subsystem.

## 6. Exact designated restoration and completion probabilities

The capped Poisson representation gives sharper finite-time formulae.

Let `x=lambda t`.

### Separator restoration bound

A stage-0 cell leaves stage 0 at its first ring, an independent `Exp(lambda)` time. If the initial stage vector contains `z=N_0(s)` zeros, then the time by which **all initial zeros** have received a first hit has distribution

`P(sigma_0 <= t) = (1-e^{-lambda t})^z`.                  (6.1)

Once all zeros are gone, `B=0` forever under designated dynamics. Hence

`P_s(B(s_t)>0) <= 1-(1-e^{-lambda t})^z`
`                <= z e^{-lambda t}`.                    (6.2)

The actual separator may be restored earlier, because only zeros adjacent to stage-2 cells need immediate service; (6.2) is a convenient uniform bound.

### Exact full-translation probability

A current stage-0 cell needs at least two further rings by time `t`, a current stage-1 cell needs at least one, and a stage-2 cell needs none. Independence of the disjoint designated clocks therefore gives

`boxed{`
`P_s(s_t=all-2)`
` = [1-e^{-x}(1+x)]^{N_0(s)}`
`   [1-e^{-x}]^{N_1(s)}.`
`}`                                                            (6.3)

This is an exact designated-core formula, not a prescribed-order cylinder lower bound.

It also shows explicitly that arbitrary interleavings are harmless for terminal translation: the terminal event depends only on how many rings each designated edge has accumulated, not on their global order.
