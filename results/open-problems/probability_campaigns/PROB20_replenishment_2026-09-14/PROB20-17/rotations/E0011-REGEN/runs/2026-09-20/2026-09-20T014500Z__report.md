## 2. Exact source dynamics used

At `p=1/2` the source normalization gives `q=1`. I use only the following source events.

- A CO arrival at a vacant site occurs at rate `p=1/2`. If an adjacent O exists, instantaneous reaction removes the incoming CO and one adjacent O, leaving both sites vacant. If no adjacent O exists, the CO survives.
- An O2 arrival on a nearest-neighbour vacant pair occurs at rate `q/4=1/4`. Each deposited O instantaneously reacts with an adjacent CO if one exists; with a unique adjacent CO that reaction is deterministic. If no adjacent CO exists, that O survives.

A stable configuration has no adjacent CO--O pair. All constructions below use finitely many legal source events and never alter the chemistry or add stirring.

## 3. Main new lemma: a two-column vacancy strip can erase arbitrary stable data ahead of it

### Lemma 3.1 — vacancy bulldozer

Fix integers `m,n>=2`. Let

`R={1,...,m} x {1,...,n}`.

Assume the two columns

`C_-1={-1} x {1,...,n}`, `C_0={0} x {1,...,n}`

are entirely vacant. The configuration on `R` and everywhere else may be an **arbitrary stable source configuration**.

Then there is a finite adaptive sequence of legal source-model events, supported in `R`, the two vacant columns and their nearest-neighbour halo, that makes every site of `R` vacant while keeping a two-column vacant workspace behind the advancing front. The construction uses at most `4mn` source arrivals.

In particular the statement is uniform in the arbitrary exterior state; it is not a special all-CO or all-O calculation.

### Proof

Sweep the target one column at a time. Suppose columns strictly to the left of column `i` have already been cleared. Thus columns `i-2` and `i-1` are vacant (for `i=1`, these are the assumed columns `C_-1,C_0`). We clear column `i` site by site.

Take `y=(i,j)` and put `x=(i-1,j)`. The site `x` is vacant. Away from the two row endpoints choose a vertical neighbour `z=(i-1,j+1)` (or `j-1`) in the already-cleared column. Then the other three neighbours of `x` are vacant except possibly `y`.

**Case 1: `y` is already vacant.** Do nothing.

**Case 2: `y` contains O.** Trigger a CO arrival at `x`. Since `y` is the only possible adjacent O, the incoming CO reacts with `y`. Both `x` and `y` are vacant afterward. One source arrival clears `y` and preserves the workspace.

**Case 3: `y` contains CO.** Trigger O2 on the vacant edge `xz`. At `x`, the site `y` is the unique possible adjacent CO, so the O placed at `x` reacts with `y`; therefore `x` and `y` are vacant afterward. The O placed at `z` either reacts with the only possibly occupied site immediately to its right (which is harmless and may clear that site early), or survives at `z`. In the latter case trigger a CO arrival at `x`; `z` is then the unique adjacent O and is removed, restoring `x,z` to vacancies. Thus at most two source arrivals clear an interior-row CO site.

Only the top and bottom row endpoints require one extra observation, because `x` has one neighbour `u` just outside the height-`n` strip whose state is arbitrary.

If endpoint `y` is O, repeated CO arrivals at `x` remove adjacent O's one at a time. The only possible O neighbours of `x` are `y` and `u`. If the first reaction removes `u`, then `u` becomes vacant and the second CO arrival necessarily removes `y`. Hence at most two arrivals clear endpoint O.

If endpoint `y` is CO, trigger O2 on `xz`. The O at `x` can see CO only at `y` and possibly `u`; whichever one it removes becomes vacant. If it removes `u` first, a second O2 attempt removes `y`, because then `y` is the unique adjacent CO at `x`. After either O2 attempt, if the O at `z` survives, repeated CO arrivals at `x` remove at most the two possible adjacent O's (`z` and possibly `u`) and restore the workspace. A crude bound of four source arrivals suffices for an endpoint site.

Thus every target site can be cleared using at most four source arrivals, while the already-cleared columns remain vacant. After column `i` is cleared it becomes part of the two-column workspace for column `i+1`. Induction over `i=1,...,m` proves the result with at most `4mn` arrivals. QED.

### Lemma 4.1 — builder

From an all-vacant copy of `B`, the exact template `S` can be constructed by a finite legal source word of 36 arrivals.

### Proof

Tile the `4 x 4` O core by eight nearest-neighbour dimers and perform O2 adsorption on each dimer. Because the entire box is vacant and no CO has yet been installed, every deposited O survives. This uses eight O2 arrivals.

The CO circuit is the boundary of an `8 x 8` rectangle and has perimeter 28. Deposit CO once at each of those 28 vacant sites. None is adjacent to the O core, so every CO survives. All unmentioned sites remain vacant, in particular all four launch-strip columns. Total: `8+28=36` arrivals. QED.

## 5. Main theorem: explicit source-valid two-child self-replication under arbitrary exterior data

The sequence can be chosen adaptively from the observed finite state and uses at most

### Proof

The left side is the mirror image. Parent columns `0,1` are vacant and form a two-column workspace for a leftward sweep. Clear `B_-` in at most 384 arrivals, then build the exact translated template in 36 arrivals.

The two constructions occupy disjoint target boxes. The parent is not required to survive after both children have been produced. Total source-arrival count is at most 840. ## 8. A sharp warning from the same construction: sequential one-site bulldozing is not the margin proof

The deterministic bulldozer should not be mistaken for a high-probability mechanism. The exact one-dimensional front race explains why.

In an ideal all-CO corridor, if the active vacancy frontier has one vacant neighbour behind it, then at `p=1/2`:

- O2 on the front/back vacant edge has rate `1/4` and advances the vacancy frontier by one site by reacting with the front CO;
- CO at the frontier vacancy has rate `1/2` and retreats the vacancy frontier by one site.

After ignoring waiting times, the embedded front walk jumps `+1` with probability `1/3` and `-1` with probability `2/3`. If there are `d` reserve vacancy sites behind the front and one needs to advance `m` sites, the restricted-front gambler's-ruin probability is exactly

`P(hit +m before -d) = (2^d-1)/(2^(m+d)-1)`.

For fixed `m`, widening the reserve `d -> infinity` only raises this to `2^(-m)`. Therefore **buffer width alone cannot turn a sequential one-site eraser into the desired high-margin regeneration mechanism**.

This is a route diagnosis, not an upper bound on the full two-dimensional catalyst process: lateral O2 events and two-dimensional interface roughening can help. It tells us exactly what the next attack must exploit.
