The previous report bounded the conditional fatal probability by `d_j/s`, which throws away the large fresh-safe rate `r_j`. Keeping that denominator yields a factor-three improvement.

## 3. Main new theorem: `14/m`, not `42/m`

### Theorem 3.1 — sharp geometric-series fatal bound

Let `F_m^(infty)` be the probability that MICRO-FATAL occurs before MICRO-SUCCESS in the ideal interface trial with no timeout. Then for every `m>=2`,

`F_m^(infty) <= 14 m/(m-1)^2`.

Hence

`F_m^(infty) <= 14/m + O(m^-2)`.

### Proof

Let `R_j` be the event that defect count `j` is reached before stopping. At count `j`, an additional fresh-safe transition has conditional probability

`r_j/(s+r_j+d_j)`

which is at most

`r_j/(s+r_j)`.

Since `r_j` decreases in `j`,

`r_j/(s+r_j) <= r_0/(s+r_0)`

`= (m/2)/((m-1)/4+m/2)`

`= 2m/(3m-1) =: rho_m`.

Therefore

`P(R_j) <= rho_m^j`.                                                (3.1)

Now condition on reaching defect count `j>=1`. The actual fatal-next probability is

`d_j/(s+r_j+d_j)`.

The function `x -> x/(s+r_j+x)` is increasing, so using `d_j<=7j/4`,

`P(next relevant event is fatal | R_j)`

`<= (7j/4)/(s+r_j+7j/4)`

`= 7j/(3m-1+5j)`

`<= 7j/(3m-1)`.                                                     (3.2)

A fatal stop occurs at a unique defect count. Summing (3.1)--(3.2) and extending the finite sum to infinity,

`F_m^(infty)`

`<= [7/(3m-1)] sum_{j>=1} j rho_m^j`

`= [7/(3m-1)] rho_m/(1-rho_m)^2`.

Because

`rho_m=2m/(3m-1)`

and

`1-rho_m=(m-1)/(3m-1)`,

the right side is exactly

`14m/(m-1)^2`.

QED.

### Why this is materially stronger

The old estimate used

`P(fatal next | R_j) <= d_j/s <= 7j/(m-1)`,

whose geometric summation gives the constant `42`. The new proof retains both SUCCESS and fresh-safe clocks in the denominator. No new stochastic independence is assumed.

## 5. Exact post-birth target under the new coefficient-2 comparison

The next statement is deliberately conditional: it isolates the exact property a post-birth compiler must have. It does **not** assume that property has already been built.

### Corollary 5.1 — safe-retry compiler threshold

Suppose that after the protected plaquette birth there is a source-valid compiler `C` with the following two properties.

1. Conditional on entering the compiler, it produces the required forward regenerative child packet with probability at least `c`.
2. Every non-success compiler outcome is repaired back into a valid RETRY member of the same recurrent seed class and causes no additional FATAL mass.

Then the composite attempt satisfies

`a >= c A_src`,

`f <= F_src`,

and hence

`a-2f >= c A_src - 2F_src`.

Consequently the coefficient-2 P02-B comparison closes the local margin whenever

`c > 2F_src/A_src`.

With the explicit bounds above, it is enough that

`c > 0.006891365`.

Thus, **under exact safe retry**, the post-birth geometry no longer needs a high-probability compiler. A conditional success probability below seven-tenths of one percent would already be enough at `m=4096`.

For comparison, the historical coefficient `63` would require a much larger post-birth ratio. This is why the new reserved-slot comparison changes the P02-A design problem qualitatively.

## 6. New obstruction theorem: fixed-local serial depth `>=2` is asymptotically too weak

The preceding corollary might suggest using the already-proved deterministic bulldozer/rebuild word after a plaquette birth. The next lemma shows why that is still the wrong architecture when the long interface remains live.

### Lemma 6.1 — serial local compiler race bound

After a plaquette birth, suppose a proposed compiler needs `ell>=1` ordered indispensable local source marks before it can succeed. Assume that, until `K` additional fresh interface defects have accumulated,

- the total rate of the **next required compiler mark** is at most a constant `Lambda` independent of `m`;
- the fresh-defect rate is at least `(m-K)/2`.

Then the probability of completing all `ell` required marks before the `(K+1)`-st fresh defect is at most

`binom(K+ell,ell) [2 Lambda/(m-K)]^ell`.                              (6.1)

### Proof

Ignore every event except the next required compiler mark and a fresh-defect mark. Ignoring other events can only increase the compiler's chance to finish before accumulating defects.

At every stage before the `(K+1)`-st defect, the conditional probability that the next event among these two classes is the required compiler mark is at most

`q_m = Lambda/(Lambda+(m-K)/2)`

`<= 2 Lambda/(m-K)`.

To complete `ell` ordered compiler marks before more than `K` fresh defects, the reduced event word contains `ell` compiler symbols and at most `K` defect symbols. There are at most `binom(K+ell,ell)` placements of the compiler symbols among the first `K+ell` relevant events. Each such word has probability at most `q_m^ell` after dropping all defect factors, which are at most one. A union bound gives (6.1). QED.

### Corollary 6.2 — the deterministic fixed-word rebuild is the wrong scaling

For fixed `K`, `Lambda`, and every `ell>=2`, (6.1) is `O(m^-2)`.

By Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 5.1, the coefficient-2 safe-retry compiler requirement is only `Theta(m^-1)` in the long-interface regime: with zero leakage and timeout, the sufficient threshold is asymptotically

`c_need(m) ~ 28/m`.

Therefore **any post-birth architecture that needs two or more fixed-local ordered indispensable marks while permitting only a fixed defect budget is asymptotically below the required scale**. This does not rule out all multi-mark compilers. It rules out the serial fixed-local class. To beat it one must use at least one of:

- a one-mark topological completion;
- a defect budget `K` that grows with `m`, together with a proof that its added fatal risk remains subcritical;
- a compiler whose useful transition rate itself grows with `m` through many parallel lanes;
- a different recurrent seed class in which post-birth states are already child seeds.

This is exactly why merely appending the old finite bulldozer word to the new microtrial does not exploit the progress made by the interface estimate.

## 7. A concrete one-mark phenomenon already present

There is one useful one-mark transformation hidden in the existing geometry.

Immediately after a clean plaquette birth on base edge `{b_i,b_{i+1}}`, the two lower CO sites have become vacancies while the old base edge is still vacant and the two upper sites are still O if those columns had no prior upper defect. If an O2 mark then occurs on the old base edge before a competing disturbance, the two newly deposited O atoms have no adjacent CO in the local phase-sandwich geometry and survive. The old base edge becomes an O pair. The newly created lower vacancy edge now has

- O immediately above, and
- the untouched CO row immediately below.

So **one O2 mark converts the plaquette birth into a translated two-column phase interface**.

This is not yet a full two-child compiler: it transports one interface rather than branching one interface into two. But it shows that the serial-depth barrier is not vacuous. The correct next geometry should try to make one analogous mark complete **two** same-class children, or arrange many such one-mark lanes in parallel.
