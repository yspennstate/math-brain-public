## 3. Full-source cut depth for a vacant strip

Let

`S_{L,w}={1,...,L} x {1,...,w}`

be a rectangular strip of lattice sites. Initially every site of `S_{L,w}` is vacant. No clocks are suppressed. Call the strip **cut** at time `t` if its vacant nearest-neighbor subgraph has no left-to-right crossing.

The following bound is pathwise and uses the original source process only.

### Lemma 3.1 — an occupied creator event covers at most two strip sites

At a stable time, assign every occupied strip site to the most recent primitive source occurrence that changed that site from vacant to occupied.

One primitive CO label can be the creator of at most one occupied strip site. One primitive O2 edge label can be the creator of at most its two adsorption endpoints. Instantaneous reactions never create occupied particles on new sites; they only remove unlike particles.

Hence one primitive source label can account for at most two sites of any occupied cutset.

### Lemma 3.2 — cut of a width-`w` vacant strip needs at least `ceil(w/2)` distinct creator labels

If the strip is cut at a stable time, planar site duality on the square lattice gives an occupied `*`-connected top-to-bottom crossing of the strip, where `*` adjacency is nearest-neighbor or diagonal adjacency. Such a crossing contains at least `w` distinct occupied sites, one in each horizontal row. By Lemma 3.1, those sites require at least

`k_w := ceil(w/2)`

distinct primitive creator labels.

Moreover those creator labels form a connected set in a fixed finite-range **label graph**: join two primitive labels whenever their adsorption supports contain sites at `l_infinity` distance at most one. Consecutive sites along the occupied `*` crossing have creator labels equal or adjacent in this graph, so the set of distinct creators is connected.

In particular, for `w=5`, every cut contains a connected triple of distinct primitive source labels.

### Theorem 3.3 — width-five full-source cut bound

There is a finite constant `C_bus`, depending only on the fixed local source-label geometry and on the compact interval `I_delta`, such that for every `L>=1` and `0<tau<=1`,

`boxed{ P_p(S_{L,5} is cut by time tau) <= C_bus L tau^3 }`          (3.1)

uniformly for `p in I_delta`.

#### Proof

Consider all primitive labels whose adsorption support intersects `S_{L,5}`. Their number is at most `A L` for a lattice constant `A`. In the finite-range label graph from Lemma 3.2, the maximum degree is bounded by a lattice constant `Delta`, independent of `L`.

If a cut occurs by time `tau`, Lemma 3.2 supplies a connected triple of distinct creator labels. The number of connected ordered triples is at most `A L (Delta+1)^2`; this crude count is sufficient.

Let `r^*` be a uniform upper bound, on `I_delta`, for the raw rate of one primitive label. For any specified three distinct labels, independence of their raw Harris clocks gives

`P(each label rings at least once by tau)`
` <= (r^* tau)^3`.

Every dynamically legal creator occurrence is in particular a raw ring, so the same event upper-bounds the creator triple. A union bound over the connected triples proves (3.1), with for example

`C_bus=A(Delta+1)^2 (r^*)^3`.

No favorable reaction choice, thinning of the source process, or independence between nearby occupations is assumed. QED.

### General width statement

The same proof gives, for each fixed `w`,

`P_p(S_{L,w} is cut by tau) <= C_w L tau^{ceil(w/2)}`.                (3.2)

The constants are finite for fixed `w`. Width five is the first width for which the exponent exceeds the two-ring productive depth. Thus width five is **minimal for this particular order-separation mechanism**; this is not a claim that every conceivable width-four construction is impossible.

## 4. Turn/corner version of the bypass bound

The same estimate survives the finitely many annular turns needed in a rectangular coarse circuit.

Take a width-five L-shaped vacant corridor formed by two width-five strips meeting in a `5 x 5` vacant square. If there is no vacant connection from the incoming port of one leg to the outgoing port of the other, the planar matching-lattice separation theorem gives an occupied `*`-connected cut through one of the two width-five necks or through the fixed `5 x 5` bend box. In every case at least five occupied sites are needed to separate the two width-five ports; hence at least three creator labels occur. `boxed{ P_p(bypass loses its required vacancy connection by tau)`
`        <= C_ann L tau^3 }`                                           (4.1)

for another finite uniform constant `C_ann`.

This is the finite turn/corner insulation statement missing from the straight periodic ladder: the **bypass** itself needs no special rare corner chemistry. It is a geometric reserve whose full-source destruction remains third order.

## 5. Parallel phase bank behind the bypass

Now place `m` clean two-column phase cells along a straight active bank of length `Theta(m)`. Separate their influence guards by a fixed distance larger than the guard diameter. The guards are then pairwise disjoint in Harris space. Put the bank next to, but outside the support of, a width-five vacant bypass corridor of total length at most `L_0 m` for a fixed packing constant `L_0`.

The bypass is exactly the deeper untouched vacancy reserve contemplated in the `T165000Z` report. It need not be chemically inert; ordinary source clocks act on it. Theorem 3.3 controls the probability that those clocks destroy its crossing.

Let

`tau_m = c/sqrt(m)`                                                     (5.1)

for a fixed `c>0`. For the `j`th phase cell let `E_j` be the exact protected two-hit event of Section 2. The `E_j` are independent because their guards are disjoint, and each satisfies (2.1). Therefore

`P(no exact translated cell in the bank by tau_m)`
` <= exp[-m r_2(tau_m)]`.                                               (5.2)

For all sufficiently large `m`,

`exp(-Gamma_* c/sqrt(m)) >= 1/2`,

so

`m r_2(tau_m)`
` >= lambda_*^2 c^2/4`.                                                 (5.3)

Consequently

`boxed{ P(no translated phase cell) <= exp(-lambda_*^2 c^2/4) }`       (5.4)

for all sufficiently large `m`, uniformly on `I_delta`.

At the same time, Theorem 3.3 gives

`P(bypass cut by tau_m)`
` <= C_bus L_0 m (c/sqrt(m))^3`
` = C_bus L_0 c^3 / sqrt(m) ->0`.                                      (5.5)

This is the central scale separation of the run: **two-hit useful events accumulate at order one while every full-source five-wide bypass cut is third order and vanishes.**

## 6. Two forward banks and an arbitrary fixed comparison coefficient

Use two macroscopically separated active banks, each containing `m` guard-disjoint candidate cells, behind one protected annular width-five bypass system (or two disjoint bypasses; only constants change). Let `G_m` be the event that

1. bank 1 contains at least one exact translated phase cell by `tau_m`;
2. bank 2 contains at least one exact translated phase cell by `tau_m`;
3. the required width-five bypass connections survive through `tau_m`.

After increasing the annular length constant in (4.1), a union bound gives, uniformly on `I_delta`,

`boxed{ P_p(G_m^c)`
` <= 2 exp(-lambda_*^2 c^2/4) + C c^3/sqrt(m) }`                       (6.1)

for a finite constant `C` and all sufficiently large `m`.

### Theorem 6.1 — arbitrarily reliable source-valid port/bypass tile

For every `epsilon>0`, there exist finite `c` and finite `m` such that

`inf_{p in I_delta} P_p(G_m) >= 1-epsilon`.                             (6.2)

#### Proof

First choose `c` so large that

`2 exp(-lambda_*^2 c^2/4) < epsilon/2`.

Then choose finite `m` so large that the large-`m` condition used in (5.3) holds and

`C c^3/sqrt(m) < epsilon/2`.

Substitute into (6.1). QED.

This is stronger, probabilistically, than requiring one protected microchannel to have a numerically large success probability: a two-hit channel may be arbitrarily rare at a fixed short time, but a finite parallel bank plus width-five reserve makes the joint useful-progress / insulation tile as reliable as desired **in the full source process**.

### Corollary 6.2 — one-shot port margin beats every finite coefficient

On this port-level trial, pessimistically declare

- SUCCESS = `G_m`;
- FATAL = `G_m^c`;
- RETRY = none.

Then `a_port=1-f_port` and, for every fixed finite `kappa`,

`a_port-kappa f_port`
` = 1-(kappa+1) f_port`.                                                (6.3)

Choose `epsilon<1/(kappa+1)` in Theorem 6.1. Then

`boxed{ a_port-kappa f_port >0 }`.                                     (6.4)

In particular this gives positive full-source **port/bypass** margins for both `kappa=2` and `kappa=63`.

The qualifier `port/bypass` is essential. ## 8. Why the scale `tau_m=c/sqrt(m)` is the right one

The exponent balance is transparent.

For a useful local word of source depth two,

`r_2(tau) = Theta(tau^2)`.

With `m` independent candidates, `m r_2(tau)` is order one precisely when `tau=Theta(m^{-1/2})`.

For a width-`w` initially vacant bypass, the creator-depth theorem gives

`P(cut) = O(m tau^{ceil(w/2)})`.

At `tau=c m^{-1/2}`, this becomes

`O(m^{1-ceil(w/2)/2})`.

It tends to zero iff

`ceil(w/2)>2`,

i.e. iff `w>=5`.

Thus the width-five reserve is not an arbitrary safety factor. It is the first fixed width that creates a strict source-depth gap against exact two-hit phase translation.
