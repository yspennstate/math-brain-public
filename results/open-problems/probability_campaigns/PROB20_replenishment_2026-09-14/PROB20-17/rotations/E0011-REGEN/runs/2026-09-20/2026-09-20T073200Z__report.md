## 2. Exact source dynamics used

- A CO source adsorption at a vacant site has rate `1/2`. If an adjacent O is present, the incoming CO and one adjacent O annihilate immediately.

`Q=N_CO-N_O`

## 3. New local theorem: the two-mark zipper move

The post-birth obstruction in the last P02-A report was that a vacancy plaquette was not yet the same recurrent long-interface class. The following local move gives an exact same-class phase deformation instead.

Consider three consecutive vacancies on a straight horizontal phase boundary,

`v_0=(0,0), v_1=(1,0), v_2=(2,0)`.

Assume locally that

- the three sites immediately on the interior side, `(i,1)`, are O;
- the three sites immediately on the exterior side,

  `w_i=(i,-1)`, `i=0,1,2`,

  are CO;
- the boundary continues through `v_0,v_2` and the displayed neighborhood is otherwise the canonical stable phase sandwich.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact zipper

One O2 source arrival on `{v_0,v_1}` and one on `{v_1,v_2}`, in either order, transform the old vacancy path

`v_0 -- v_1 -- v_2`

into the outward detour

`v_0 -- w_0 -- w_1 -- w_2 -- v_2`,

with `v_1` becoming O. Thus the completed two-mark move sends the local phase-separated vacancy-interface class to itself.

### Proof

Suppose `{v_0,v_1}` fires first. Each deposited O has a unique adjacent exterior CO, respectively `w_0,w_1`. Those two CO particles annihilate with the deposited O atoms. Hence `v_0,v_1,w_0,w_1` are vacant after the first mark.

Now fire `{v_1,v_2}`. At `v_2`, the exterior site `w_2` is still CO, so the deposited O annihilates with it; both `v_2,w_2` are vacant afterward. At the shared center `v_1`, the exterior neighbor `w_1` is already vacant. The interior neighbor is O and the two tangential neighbors are vacancies, so there is no adjacent CO. Hence the newly deposited O at `v_1` survives.

The final vacant sites along the interface are exactly

`v_0,w_0,w_1,w_2,v_2`,

while `v_1` has joined the interior O phase. The exterior immediately beyond the new detour was untouched and remains CO. The reverse firing order is the reflected argument. QED.

### Topological bookkeeping

At completion, the old local path had three vacancy vertices and two vacancy edges; the new path has five vacancy vertices and four vacancy edges. Hence

`Delta V=+2`, `Delta E_00=+2`.

For a simple connected vacancy circuit the cycle rank

`beta_1=E_00-V+1`

is therefore unchanged. In particular a completed zipper on an exposed straight segment of a simple circuit again gives a simple circuit. The intermediate state after the first O2 mark may temporarily contain an extra vacancy plaquette; the statement is about the completed two-mark source move, not a false claim that `E_00=V` holds at every intermediate instant.

The charge change is also exact: three exterior CO are removed and one old boundary vacancy becomes O, so `Delta Q=-4`, exactly the charge of two O2 source adsorptions.

### Interpretation

This replaces the old fixed-shape viewpoint by a more useful recurrent class: simple rectilinear vacancy circuits with O on the inside and CO on the outside. A zipper is an **ear-addition move** inside that class. A failed attempt need not return to the identical polygon in order to be a legitimate RETRY candidate; it is enough to return to the same protected phase-interface class. The remaining work is to quantify that stochastic recurrence.

## 4. Main new theorem: exact one-layer rectangular annulus expansion

The local zipper can be shared across neighboring vertices. For a rectangular circuit, all old boundary edges can be used once and the whole phase boundary moves outward by one lattice layer.

Fix integers `a,b>=2` and set

`R=[0,a] x [0,b] intersect Z^2`.

Let `C=partial R` be its lattice boundary. The number of boundary vertices and boundary edges is

`P=2a+2b`.

Define the canonical protected phase seed `S(R)` by

1. every site of `C` is vacant;
2. every strict interior site of `R` is O;
3. every site in a width-two exterior collar is CO.

The width-two collar is only to ensure that after moving the boundary outward by one layer, the new boundary still has a one-layer CO exterior protection. This finite pattern is stable.

Let

`R^+=[-1,a+1] x [-1,b+1] intersect Z^2`.

Its boundary has `P+8` sites.

### Theorem 4.1 — edge sweep plus four corners

From `S(R)` there is a legal exact source sequence of

`P+4` O2 adsorptions and no CO adsorptions whose terminal configuration on the protected region is exactly `S(R^+)`:

- every site of `partial R^+` is vacant;
- every site in the strict interior of `R^+` is O;
- the next exterior collar remains CO.

The `P` old boundary-edge O2 marks can be fired in **arbitrary order**. Four additional corner marks then finish the four new diagonal corners.

### Proof — Step 1: sweep every old boundary edge once

Fire one O2 adsorption on every edge of the vacancy cycle `C`.

Consider a noncorner boundary vertex `v`. It has exactly one exterior CO neighbor and one interior O neighbor. Before either incident boundary edge has fired at `v`, it is vacant. On the **first** incident edge mark, the O deposited at `v` has the unique exterior CO available; they annihilate, so `v` remains vacant and that exterior site becomes vacant. On the **second** incident boundary-edge mark, the exterior CO has already been removed. The interior neighbor is O and the two tangential neighbors are boundary sites, never CO. Hence the second deposited O at `v` survives. Thus after both incident boundary edges have fired, `v` is O.

This also proves legality in arbitrary edge order. A noncorner vertex is converted from vacancy to O only on its second incident edge mark, and at that moment both incident boundary edges have already fired. Therefore no unfired boundary edge ever loses a vacant endpoint because of a previous second hit.

Now consider one of the four old corners `c`. It has two exterior axial CO neighbors and its other two neighbors lie on the vacant boundary. The first incident boundary-edge mark removes one exterior CO and leaves `c` vacant. The second incident edge mark removes the remaining exterior CO and again leaves `c` vacant. Hence after the complete boundary sweep all four old corners remain vacant.

Counting what has happened after the `P` edge marks:

- all `P-4` noncorner old boundary sites have become O;
- the four old corners are still vacant;
- every exterior axial site adjacent to the old boundary is vacant.

There are `(P-4)+8=P+4` such newly cleared exterior sites. The only four sites of `partial R^+` still occupied by CO are the four **diagonal new corners**.

### Step 2: finish one new diagonal corner with one O2 mark

Take the old top-right corner `c=(a,b)`. Its two outward axial neighbors

`u=(a,b+1)`, `r=(a+1,b)`

are vacant after Step 1, while the new diagonal corner

`d=(a+1,b+1)`

is still CO.

Fire one O2 mark on the vacant edge `{c,u}`. At `c`, both old side neighbors are now O and both outward axial neighbors are vacant, so there is no adjacent CO; the O deposited at `c` survives. At `u`, require the deposited O to annihilate with the adjacent diagonal CO at `d` (if the farther exterior collar supplies another CO choice, choosing `d` is a legal positive-probability reaction outcome). Therefore `u` remains vacant and `d` becomes vacant.

This converts the old corner `c` into the interior O phase and creates the missing new diagonal vacancy. The identical operation at the other three corners is spatially separate and completes `partial R^+`.

After the four corner operations, every old boundary site is O, so all of `R`, which is exactly the strict interior of `R^+`, is O. Every site of `partial R^+` is vacant, and the next exterior collar was never touched. The terminal pattern is exactly `S(R^+)`. Total source arrivals: `P+4`, all O2. QED.

## 5. Stronger result: `P+4` is source-count optimal

The preceding compiler is not just explicit. Its number of source arrivals is forced by the source stoichiometry.

### Theorem 5.1 — optimality by charge

Among **all** finite exact source trajectories that transform the canonical protected seed `S(R)` into the canonical one-layer expansion `S(R^+)`, the total number of successful source adsorptions is at least

`P+4`.

The construction in Theorem 4.1 attains this lower bound. Hence the exact source-arrival complexity of one-layer canonical rectangular expansion is

`L_rect(P)=P+4`.                                                       (5.1)

### Proof

Compare initial and terminal configurations in the finite protected box. The expanded boundary contains `P+8` sites. Every one of them was initially exterior CO and is finally vacant, so

`Delta N_CO=-(P+8)`.

Every one of the `P` old boundary vacancies is finally in the strict interior of `R^+` and is O, so

`Delta N_O=+P`.

No other site changes its final species. Therefore

`Delta Q=Delta(N_CO-N_O)=-(P+8)-P=-2P-8`.                             (5.2)

For an arbitrary source trajectory let `A_CO,A_O2` be the numbers of successful CO and O2 source adsorptions. The exact pathwise charge identity (2.1) gives

`A_CO-2A_O2=-2P-8`.

Thus

`A_O2=P+4+A_CO/2`,

and the total number of successful source arrivals is

`L=A_CO+A_O2=P+4+(3/2)A_CO >= P+4`.                                  (5.3)

Theorem 4.1 uses `A_CO=0,A_O2=P+4`, so equality is attained. QED.

### Lemma 6.1 — short-window no-go for full expansion in a fixed-width Harris tube

Suppose a protected interface compiler for a perimeter-`P` seed uses a fixed-width Harris tube containing at most `C P` source clocks in total rate, where `C` is independent of `P`. If completion requires the exact canonical expansion `S(R)->S(R^+)`, then for

`t_P=theta/sqrt(P)`

the completion probability is at most

`P(Poisson(C P t_P) >= P+4)`

and hence, by the standard Poisson Chernoff bound,

`P(completion by t_P)`

`<= [ e C theta sqrt(P)/(P+4) ]^(P+4)`

`= exp[-(1/2+o(1)) P log P]`.                                         (6.1)

### Proof

Theorem 5.1 says completion needs at least `P+4` successful source arrivals. The number of successful arrivals is bounded by the number of Harris clock rings in the active tube. The latter is stochastically bounded by a Poisson random variable of mean `C P t_P=C theta sqrt(P)`. Apply `P(Pois(mu)>=k) <= (e mu/k)^k` for `k=P+4`. QED.
