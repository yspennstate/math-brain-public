## 2. Exact sharpness of the segment anchor-loss exponent

Write the initially vacant path as

`v_0-v_1-...-v_m`.

The complementary report proved that if no adjacent pair of segment sites is vacant, at least `ceil(m/3)` primitive source occurrences intersecting the segment must have occurred. That lower bound is in fact sharp as a source depth.

### Theorem 2.1 — exact anchor-destruction depth

For every `m>=1` there is a stable exterior and a legal source word of exactly

`boxed{k_m=ceil(m/3)}`

source occurrences after which the original `P_m` contains no adjacent vacant pair.

Consequently the source-depth of complete anchor loss, under the arbitrary-stable-exterior quantifier used by the segment construction, is **exactly** `ceil(m/3)`.

### Proof

Take an exterior containing no CO adjacent to the segment; for example all off-segment sites in a sufficiently large guard may be O wherever stability permits, with the segment itself vacant. We shall use only disjoint internal O2 edges, so every deposited O survives.

It is enough to cover all `m` path edges by disjoint adjacent occupied pairs. Use the following explicit domino sets.

- If `m=3q`, fire O2 on

  `{v_{3j+1},v_{3j+2}}`, `j=0,...,q-1`.

  The only vacant segment vertices left are `v_0,v_3,...,v_{3q}`, no two adjacent.

- If `m=3q+1`, fire O2 on

  `{v_{3j},v_{3j+1}}`, `j=0,...,q`.

  The remaining vacancies are among `v_2,v_5,...`, again pairwise nonadjacent.

- If `m=3q+2`, fire O2 on

  `{v_{3j+1},v_{3j+2}}`, `j=0,...,q`.

  The remaining vacancies are `v_0,v_3,...,v_{3q}`, pairwise nonadjacent.

The selected edges are pairwise vertex-disjoint. Hence each is still a legal vacant edge when its turn comes. Since there is no adjacent CO in the chosen exterior, both deposited O atoms survive on every selected pair. The number of marks is respectively `q`, `q+1`, `q+1`, i.e. `ceil(m/3)`.

The complementary lower bound shows that fewer marks cannot destroy every adjacent vacancy anchor. QED.

### Cylinder consequence

Fix a compact source interval `I_delta subset (0,1)`. Every designated O2 edge above has rate at least

`lambda_* := inf_{p in I_delta} (1-p)/2 >0`.

In a fixed finite guard with total rate at most `Gamma_*`, requiring exactly the `k_m` designated O2 marks in a prescribed order and no other guarded mark gives, for small `t`, a fatal anchor-loss cylinder of probability at least

`exp(-Gamma_* t) (lambda_* t)^{k_m}/k_m!`.

Thus the `t^{ceil(m/3)}` fatal order is not merely an upper-bound artifact. It is source-realizable.

## 3. A vacancy-mass lower bound for two disjoint child segments

The local `d=1` compiler produces strongly overlapping translated windows. Historical block SUCCESS, however, needs genuinely separated forward children so that their protected supports can be assigned to distinct coarse descendants. There is a simple source-depth obstruction to making those children from one arbitrary-exterior vacancy segment in very small time.

### Lemma 3.1 — one primitive source occurrence clears at most two pre-existing target CO particles

Fix any set `W` of sites that initially contain CO. During one primitive source occurrence, at most two particles of that initial CO population can be removed.

### Proof

A CO source occurrence deposits one CO and cannot directly remove an already-present CO. An O2 source occurrence deposits two O atoms. Each deposited O can annihilate at most one neighboring CO before that O itself disappears. Instantaneous unlike-neighbor reaction removes particles; it does not create additional reactive O particles. Hence at most two initially designated CO particles are removed by one primitive occurrence. QED.

### Theorem 3.2 — disjoint two-child depth lower bound

Start from one exact vacant `P_m`, so the parent support has `m+1` sites. Require SUCCESS to contain two **site-disjoint** translated exact copies of `P_m` at the terminal time. Under the arbitrary-stable-exterior quantifier there is a stable test exterior for which every such SUCCESS history uses at least

`boxed{s_m = ceil((m+1)/2)}`

primitive source occurrences.

If both child supports are additionally disjoint from the parent support, the lower bound improves to `m+1` occurrences.

### Proof

Two disjoint child copies contain `2(m+1)` sites. Their union can intersect the `(m+1)`-site parent support in at most `m+1` sites. Therefore at least `m+1` child sites lie outside the initially vacant parent.

Choose a stable test exterior in which every possible prescribed forward child site outside the parent is initially CO; a vacancy guard can be inserted where needed between any separately prescribed phase collar and the target region. At terminal SUCCESS all of the at least `m+1` outside target sites must be vacant, so their initial CO particles must have been removed. Lemma 3.1 says one primitive source occurrence removes at most two of them. Hence at least `ceil((m+1)/2)` source occurrences are necessary.

If both children are disjoint from the parent, all `2(m+1)` child sites start outside the parent and may be taken CO. Clearing them needs at least `m+1` source occurrences. QED.

The lower bound is independent of the particular endpoint-extension script. It applies to **any** exact source strategy that starts from only the all-vacant segment and is required to work under the same arbitrary-exterior quantifier.

## 4. Parallel vacancy segments do not repair the small-time depth inversion

It is tempting to place many independent segment tracks in parallel and hope that redundancy raises fatal depth faster than productive depth. For same-class two-child duplication, the vacancy-count scaling prevents that.

Let a packet consist of `R` pairwise disjoint exact `P_m` segments, with total vacant support size

`N=R(m+1)`.

Suppose full SUCCESS requires two site-disjoint translated child packets of the same type, each containing `R` exact `P_m` segments.

### Theorem 4.1 — packet depth barrier

For every `m>=2` and `R>=1`:

1. there is an arbitrary stable exterior in which complete loss of every adjacent vacancy anchor in every parent segment is source-realizable in

   `k_{m,R}=R ceil(m/3)`

   primitive occurrences;

2. there is an arbitrary stable exterior in which any two-disjoint-child packet SUCCESS requires at least

   `s_{m,R}=ceil(R(m+1)/2)`

   primitive occurrences;

3. the productive depth is strictly larger:

   `boxed{s_{m,R} > k_{m,R}}`.

Hence increasing the number of parallel straight vacancy segments does **not** reverse the small-time source-depth ordering for same-class duplication.

### Proof

For (1), separate the `R` parent segments by fixed guard gaps and apply Theorem 2.1 independently on each segment. The poisoning words use disjoint internal edges, for a total of `R ceil(m/3)` O2 marks.

For (2), the two child packets together contain `2N` sites while the parent packet contains only `N`. Therefore at least `N=R(m+1)` child sites lie outside the parent support. In the all-CO test exterior on those sites, Lemma 3.1 forces at least `ceil(N/2)` primitive occurrences.

For (3), use

`ceil(m/3) <= (m+2)/3`.

For `m>=2`,

`(m+1)/2 - (m+2)/3 = (m-1)/6 >0`.

The ceiling cases preserve strict inequality; directly, `ceil(R(m+1)/2) > R ceil(m/3)` for `m>=2`. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — no uniform small-time promotion of the `d=1` segment ratio

Let `a_*(t)` denote a success probability lower bound required uniformly over the arbitrary stable exteriors of the segment class, and let `f^*(t)` denote a corresponding worst-exterior fatal probability. For the two-child packet architecture above,

`a_*(t)=O(t^{s_{m,R}})`,

while a source-valid poisoning cylinder gives

`f^*(t)=Omega(t^{k_{m,R}})`.

Therefore

`a_*(t)/f^*(t) -> 0`

as `t downarrow 0`.

This does **not** refute the segment seed as a component of a longer construction. It proves something narrower and operationally important: the new P02-B `d=1` order separation cannot be converted into the historical separated-two-child `M_reg` certificate merely by taking `m` larger, adding finitely many parallel identical vacancy segments, and shrinking the trial window. A stronger phase collar / mass-neutral recurrent object is genuinely needed.

That closes one tempting promotion route rather than spending additional runs optimizing its constants.

## 6. Three-state ladder calculus for an extensive phase boundary

Take an even straight phase segment of `2N` columns and partition it into matched two-column cells

`C_j={2j,2j+1}`, `j=0,...,N-1`.

For each cell use the same designated boundary O2 edge. Ignore non-designated marks for the deterministic statement and let

`s_j in {0,1,2}`

be the number of completed designated hits on cell `j`, capped at two.

The exact local states are:

- `s_j=0` (**old level**): the two `v` sites are vacant and the two `w` sites are CO;
- `s_j=1` (**bridge / EAR**): both the two `v` sites and the two `w` sites are vacant;
- `s_j=2` (**new level**): the two `v` sites are O and the two `w` sites are vacant.

Because neighboring cells can only present vacancy or O on their `v` sites and vacancy or CO on their `w` sites, the forced first-hit and second-hit local reactions remain valid for every stage vector `s`. Thus these cell maps commute in the required source-valid sense.

### Theorem 6.1 — exact vacancy-crossing criterion

Consider the two-row ladder formed by the old boundary row `v` and the new boundary row `w` across the `N` matched cells. Its vacant subgraph has a left-to-right crossing if and only if the stage word contains **no adjacent `0,2` or `2,0` pair**.

Equivalently,

`boxed{|s_{j+1}-s_j| <= 1 for every adjacent pair}`

is exactly the crossing condition.

For the periodic two-row ladder (the local graph model of a two-layer annular circuit), the same cyclic condition is equivalent to the existence of a vacancy circuit winding once around the annulus.

### Proof

A stage-0 cell has a vacant horizontal edge only on the old `v` row. A stage-2 cell has a vacant horizontal edge only on the new `w` row. A stage-1 cell has both horizontal vacancy edges and vertical vacancy rungs joining them.

If a stage-0 cell is adjacent directly to a stage-2 cell, then across their common boundary the old-row connection is blocked by the stage-2 O site and the new-row connection is blocked by the stage-0 CO site. No vacancy edge crosses that cell boundary, so a left-right vacancy crossing is impossible.

Conversely, assume no `0` is adjacent to a `2`. Traverse the cells from left to right. Stay on the old row through every stage-0 region. Whenever the word passes from old-level cells toward stage-2 cells, it must pass through at least one stage-1 cell; use a vertical rung there to switch to the new row. Similarly, every return from stage 2 toward stage 0 passes through stage 1 and permits a switch back. This constructs a complete left-right vacancy path.

On a periodic ladder the same construction closes after one turn around the cycle; every change between old-level and new-level arcs occurs through a stage-1 rung. Conversely a cyclic `0|2` or `2|0` adjacency cuts both rows at that boundary, so no winding vacancy circuit exists. QED.

### Interpretation

The EAR state is not merely an intermediate chemical state. It is the **exact topological switch** that lets a separating vacancy circuit move between the old and new radial levels.

This is the finite local switching rule sought by the logarithmic-redundancy coordinator report: at the graph level, one bridge cell is enough to switch a vacancy separator between two neighboring phase levels, and the only forbidden local interface is a direct `0|2` jump with no bridge.

## 7. New staged-sweep theorem: optimal translation with a separator at every prefix

The arbitrary-interleaving perfect-matching lift proved earlier guarantees the correct terminal strip, but arbitrary interleavings can contain a temporary `0|2` adjacency. The following schedule removes that topological weakness without adding a single source mark.

### Theorem 7.1 — two-phase separator-preserving lift

Translate the `2N`-column clean phase strip by the following designated schedule:

**Phase I.** Give every matched cell its **first** boundary O2 hit, in arbitrary order.

**Phase II.** After Phase I is complete, give every matched cell its **second** boundary O2 hit, in arbitrary order.

Then:

1. the schedule uses exactly `2N` source occurrences, which is the already-proved source-count minimum for the exact full-strip translation;
2. after every stable prefix of Phase I, the entire old boundary row is vacant, hence is a full separating vacancy path;
3. at the end of Phase I, both old and new boundary rows are completely vacant;
4. after every stable prefix of Phase II, the entire new boundary row is vacant, hence is a full separating vacancy path;
5. at completion the exact clean phase strip is translated outward by one lattice layer.

Thus **none of the designated productive marks need ever be charged to topology loss**: there exists a source-count-optimal order in which a separating vacancy path is present at every intermediate stable state.

### Proof

During Phase I each cell is stage 0 or 1. In both states its old-row `v` sites are vacant, so the whole old boundary row remains vacant regardless of the order of first hits. When all first hits are complete every cell is stage 1, so both rows are vacant.

During Phase II each cell is stage 1 or 2. In both states its new-row `w` sites are vacant, so the whole new boundary row remains vacant regardless of the order of second hits. When all second hits are complete every cell is stage 2, which is exactly the translated clean strip.

The schedule uses one first hit and one second hit on each of `N` cells, hence `2N` source marks. The preceding charge calculation for the full strip proves that fewer than `2N` marks cannot realize the exact translation. QED.

### Annular corollary

On a periodic matched ladder, the same two-phase schedule preserves a winding vacancy circuit at every prefix: the old circuit survives all of Phase I, both circuits are present at the handoff, and the new circuit survives all of Phase II.

Therefore, once fixed corner gadgets are included, a rectangular annular implementation should charge FATAL only to **non-designated interference that creates a genuine `0|2` cut or damages the deeper protected belt**, not to the useful first/second zipper marks themselves.

The corner bookkeeping is not silently declared solved here; the proved statement is the exact periodic-ladder / straight-strip invariant.
