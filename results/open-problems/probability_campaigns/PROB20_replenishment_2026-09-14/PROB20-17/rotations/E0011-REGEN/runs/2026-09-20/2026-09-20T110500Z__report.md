## 2. Canonical rectangle and the straight-side sweep set

Let

`R=[0,a] x [0,b] intersect Z^2`,

with `a,b>=4`. Let `C=partial R` be its lattice boundary and

`P=2a+2b`

its number of boundary edges (and boundary vertices). Start from the canonical phase seed `S(R)` used in the preceding annular theorem:

1. every site of `C` is vacant;
2. every strict interior site of `R` is O;
3. a width-two exterior collar is CO.

On each of the four straight sides, reserve the two boundary edges incident to the two corners. Let `E^o` be the set of all remaining boundary edges. Then

`N := |E^o| = P-8`.

Because `a,b>=4`, the edges of `E^o` form four disjoint path components, one along the interior of each side, each containing at least two edges. Every component has two degree-one edges in the adjacency graph of `E^o`; all other edges have degree two. Hence there are exactly eight degree-one sweep edges and `N-8` degree-two sweep edges.

The corner reservation is not cosmetic. It ensures that local outward ears made on different sides cannot meet while the straight-side stage is in progress.

## 3. Main deterministic theorem: every partial first-hit sweep is already in the recurrent circuit class

For a subset `F subset E^o`, perform exactly one O2 adsorption on every edge of `F`, in an arbitrary order, and perform no edge of `E^o` more than once. Ignore for the moment all other source clocks; this is a deterministic source path statement.

### Theorem 3.1 — monotone partial annular sweep

After any such partial first-hit sweep, the configuration contains an exact simple rectilinear vacancy circuit `C_F` which separates O on its interior side from CO on its exterior side. Moreover:

- `C_emptyset=C`;
- adding one previously unused edge of `E^o` never destroys the existence of such a phase-separated vacancy circuit;
- if all edges of `E^o` have fired once, the four straight sides have been moved outward by one lattice layer except for fixed-size corner neighborhoods;
- the eight reserved corner-adjacent old edges remain available for the finite corner-completion stage of the preceding `P+4` expansion theorem.

Thus **all `N=P-8` useful straight-side marks commute at the level of the recurrent seed class**. A partial sweep is progress inside the class, not a dirty state that must first be reset.

### Proof

Consider one straight side. Write its old boundary vertices consecutively as

`v_0,v_1,...,v_m`,

and let `w_i` be the unique exterior nearest neighbor of `v_i` perpendicular to the side. Before the sweep, every `v_i` is vacant and every `w_i` is CO.

Take one maximal consecutive run of fired sweep edges

`{v_i,v_{i+1}}, {v_{i+1},v_{i+2}}, ..., {v_{j-1},v_j}`.

On the first incident fired edge at a boundary vertex `v_k`, the deposited O has the exterior CO at `w_k` available. It annihilates with that CO, so `v_k` remains vacant and `w_k` becomes vacant. If `v_k` is an **interior vertex of the fired run**, its second incident fired edge later deposits O at `v_k` again. By then `w_k` is already vacant. Its inward neighbor is O and its tangential boundary neighbors are vacant or O, never CO. Therefore the second deposited O survives and `v_k` becomes O.

At each endpoint `v_i,v_j` of the maximal fired run, exactly one incident sweep edge has fired. Hence that endpoint remains vacant. Every outward site

`w_i,w_{i+1},...,w_j`

has been cleared to vacancy. Consequently the old vacant side segment from `v_i` to `v_j` can be replaced in the separating circuit by the outward detour

`v_i -- w_i -- w_{i+1} -- ... -- w_j -- v_j`.

The old vertices strictly between `v_i` and `v_j` have become O and therefore correctly lie on the interior side of the new detour. The sites one step beyond the `w`-row were untouched and remain CO on the exterior side.

Do this independently for every maximal fired run on the side. Distinct runs are separated by at least one unused old boundary edge, so their detours are disjoint. Do the same on all four sides. Because the two corner-adjacent edges were reserved, the outward detours from distinct sides remain separated by untouched corner neighborhoods and cannot intersect.

Splicing all these detours into the portions of the old rectangle boundary that remain vacant produces a simple rectilinear vacancy circuit `C_F`. Its interior consists of the old O region plus the one-cell ears swept outward; the old boundary vertices swallowed by those ears are exactly the vertices that received two incident O2 hits and became O. Its exterior remains CO except at the new vacancy circuit itself. This proves the phase-separated circuit claim.

The argument depends only on which edges have fired once, not on their order. QED.

### Why this is a genuine regeneration improvement

The `073200Z` theorem said that a complete canonical expansion costs `P+4` source events. Theorem 3.1 now shows that the `P-8` extensive straight-side part of that work has no serial prerequisite graph: **every permutation of first hits is legal, and every prefix stays in the same flexible phase-circuit class**.

## 4. Exact duplicate-hazard representation for the designated-edge skeleton

Now run the following auxiliary process on the same initial seed. Retain only the O2 clocks on edges of `E^o`, each of rate `r=1/4`, and suppress every other source clock. Stop if a sweep edge rings for a second time while it is still enabled. Call such a second ring a **harmful duplicate**.

This is deliberately an ideal designated-edge skeleton, not yet a source-valid full trial. Its purpose is to quantify the ordering issue exactly.

For every `e in E^o`, let

`T_e`

be the time of its first ring. Until its first ring, `e` stays enabled: a neighboring sweep edge can hit a shared old boundary vertex at most once before `e` itself fires, and a first hit leaves that vertex vacant. Hence the random variables `{T_e}` are independent `Exp(r)`.

After `e` fires for the first time, it stays enabled only while none of its sweep-neighbors has fired. As soon as a neighboring edge fires, the shared old boundary vertex receives its second incident O2 hit and becomes O, permanently disabling `e` in the designated skeleton.

For an interior sweep edge (two sweep-neighbors), put

`M_e = min(T_{e^-},T_{e^+})`.

For one of the eight endpoint sweep edges (one sweep-neighbor), let `M_e` be the first-ring time of that sole sweep-neighbor. Let `E_e` be the second interarrival time of the Poisson clock of `e` after `T_e`; the `E_e` are independent `Exp(r)` and independent of all first-ring times.

Then a harmful duplicate occurs at `e` exactly when

`E_e < (M_e-T_e)_+`.

Therefore, conditional on the first-ring times,

`P(no harmful duplicate before all first hits | {T_e})`

`= exp[-r sum_{e in E^o} (M_e-T_e)_+]`.                         (4.1)

This is an exact formula for the designated-edge skeleton.

## 5. Explicit exponential lower bound

Scale the first-ring times by `r`: write `X_e=rT_e`. Then the `X_e` are iid `Exp(1)`, and the exponent in (4.1) becomes

`S = sum_e (Y_e-X_e)_+`,

where `Y_e` is the minimum of the neighboring first-hit variables (or the sole neighbor at a component endpoint).

For a degree-two sweep edge, `Y_e` is `Exp(2)` and independent of `X_e`. Hence

`E[(Y_e-X_e)_+]`

`= integral_0^infinity P(Y_e>X_e+t) dt`

`= integral_0^infinity e^{-2t} dt * integral_0^infinity e^{-3x} dx`

`= 1/6`.                                                        (5.1)

For one of the eight degree-one endpoint edges, `Y_e` and `X_e` are iid `Exp(1)`, so similarly

`E[(Y_e-X_e)_+] = 1/2`.                                        (5.2)

There are `N-8` degree-two edges and eight degree-one edges. Thus

`E S = (N-8)/6 + 8/2 = N/6 + 8/3`.                             (5.3)

Since `x -> exp(-x)` is convex, Jensen's inequality applied to (4.1) gives the rigorous lower bound

`P(no harmful duplicate before all N first hits)`

`= E[e^{-S}]`

`>= exp[-E S]`

`= exp[-N/6 - 8/3]`

`= exp[-P/6 - 4/3]`.                                           (5.4)

### Theorem 5.1 — exponential straight-side completion skeleton

For every canonical rectangular phase seed with `a,b>=4`, the designated-edge skeleton completes all `P-8` straight-side first hits while remaining throughout the exact phase-separated vacancy-circuit class with probability at least

`boxed{ exp(-P/6-4/3) }`.                                      (5.5)

In particular the extensive annular work pays an **exponential** lower bound, not a factorial or `exp[-Theta(P log P)]` ordering penalty.

The bound is conservative: Jensen discards correlations among neighboring exposure gaps. No independence between the summands of `S` was assumed.

## 6. Completion time is logarithmic in the perimeter in the same skeleton

## 8. Parallel clean lift on the exact SOS front

The same mechanism has an even simpler SOS formulation that may be useful for the next repair/service argument.

Start from an infinite flat SOS front `h(y)=k`. Choose `n` consecutive rows. For each chosen row, designate the horizontal O2 clock on its two-vacancy gap.

### Lemma 8.1 — arbitrary-order one-layer bank lift

If each of the `n` designated row clocks fires exactly once, in any order and with no other source marks, then every intermediate state is an exact clean SOS state with row heights in `{k,k+1}`, and the final state has all chosen rows at height `k+1`.

### Proof

After any subset of rows has fired, every height is `k` or `k+1`; neighboring heights differ by at most one. Any row still at height `k` is a local minimum because both neighboring heights are at least `k`. The one-mark SOS theorem therefore applies to its designated horizontal O2 clock and raises it exactly to `k+1`, preserving the clean SOS family. Induct on the number of fired rows. QED.

In the designated-clock skeleton the `n` first-ring times are iid `Exp(1/4)`, so the exact completion law is

`P(tau_n <= t)=(1-e^{-t/4})^n`,

`E tau_n = 4 H_n`.

Thus both the rectangular annulus and the SOS interface exhibit the same structural fact: **macroscopic clean forward work is parallel and order-free once it is expressed in the correct recurrent class.**
