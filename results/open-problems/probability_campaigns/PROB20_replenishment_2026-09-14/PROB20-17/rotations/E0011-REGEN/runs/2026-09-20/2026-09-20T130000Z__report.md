## 2. Clean phase-column notation

On a straight phase boundary write, for integer column index `i`,

- `u_i=(i,1)` for the interior site,
- `v_i=(i,0)` for the vacancy-boundary site,
- `w_i=(i,-1)` for the exterior site.

A clean phase column has

`A_i := (u_i,v_i,w_i) = (O,0,CO)`.

Tangential `v`-neighbors are vacant, the next interior layer is O, and the exterior layer is CO. Define `C_i` to be a CO adsorption at `v_i`. It has the unique unlike neighbor `u_i`, hence instantaneously removes that O and leaves

`C_i: (O,0,CO) -> (0,0,CO)`.

The previous report used the radial service `R_i=O2{u_i,v_i}` to map this defect to `(O,0,0)`. The observation below adds an exact RETRY route and then extends it to arbitrary contiguous defect runs.

## 3. Exact one-column RETRY in two service marks

### Lemma 3.1 — `C_i R_i B_i` is the identity on a clean phase column

After `C_i`, fire

`R_i := O2{u_i,v_i}`.

The new O at `u_i` has no neighboring CO and survives. The new O at `v_i` has the unique neighboring CO `w_i`, reacts with it, and disappears. Therefore

`C_i R_i: (O,0,CO) -> (O,0,0)`.

Now fire

`B_i := CO(w_i)`.

At this point `w_i` has no neighboring O: `v_i` is vacant and its other neighbors lie in the exterior CO/vacancy phase. Hence this CO survives and

`boxed{ C_i R_i B_i (A_i) = A_i. }`                         (3.1)

Throughout the word the original boundary site `v_i` is vacant except for the instantaneous O deposited by `R_i`, which immediately reacts with `w_i`; in the stable post-reaction states the boundary vacancy is present.

### Lemma 3.2 — the two-mark continuation is source-count optimal

Let `Q=N_CO-N_O`. A CO source mark changes `Q` by `+1`, an O2 source mark changes `Q` by `-2`, and instantaneous reactions preserve `Q`.

After `C_i`, the accumulated source charge is `+1`. A continuation returning to the exact initial state must therefore contribute charge `-1`. No single source mark has charge `-1`, so at least two continuation marks are necessary. `R_i B_i` contributes `-2+1=-1` and attains this lower bound.

Thus the exact local RETRY is not hiding a gratuitously long reset word.

## 4. A stronger paired reset: two CO defects need only one service mark

The preceding one-column reset is not optimal once neighboring defects are allowed to cooperate.

### Lemma 4.1 — paired interior O2 reset

Suppose two adjacent clean columns `i,i+1` both receive their first boundary CO hits `C_i,C_{i+1}`. Then

`u_i=u_{i+1}=0`,
`v_i=v_{i+1}=0`,
`w_i=w_{i+1}=CO`.

Fire one O2 adsorption on the **interior** vacant edge

`H_i := O2{u_i,u_{i+1}}`.

Both new O atoms survive: their outward neighbors `v_i,v_{i+1}` are vacant, their deeper/tangential interior neighbors are O or the co-deposited O, and there is no neighboring CO. Hence the two original clean columns are restored exactly:

`boxed{ C_i C_{i+1} H_i (A_i,A_{i+1}) = (A_i,A_{i+1}). }`   (4.1)

This reset preserves the old vacancy boundary `v_i-v_{i+1}` in every stable state of the word.

### Lemma 4.2 — the one-mark paired continuation is charge-optimal

The two CO defects contribute source charge `+2`. Exact return needs continuation charge `-2`, which one O2 mark supplies. Therefore no continuation with fewer than one source mark exists, and `H_i` is optimal.

This is strictly shorter than servicing the two columns separately.

## 5. Charge-optimal reset of an arbitrary contiguous first-CO defect run

Take a contiguous run of `ell>=1` clean phase columns, and apply the first-CO defect `C_i` once in every column of the run, with no other source marks yet.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact run reset and optimal source length

The exact clean phase state can be restored with

`L_reset(ell) = ell/2`                 if `ell` is even,

`L_reset(ell) = (ell+3)/2`             if `ell` is odd,                 (5.1)

additional source marks. These lengths are globally source-count optimal among all exact source-valid continuations.

#### Construction

If `ell` is even, pair neighboring defect columns and fire the disjoint interior service clocks `H` on those `ell/2` pairs. Lemma 4.1 resets every pair.

If `ell` is odd, pair `ell-1` columns and reset them by `(ell-1)/2` interior `H` marks. On the remaining endpoint column use the exact two-mark single-column reset `R B` from Lemma 3.1. Total continuation length is

`(ell-1)/2 + 2 = (ell+3)/2`.

All pair resets act on disjoint interior edges and commute. The original straight vacancy boundary remains present throughout all paired resets and throughout the stable states of the leftover single-column reset.

#### Optimality

After the `ell` first-CO defects, source charge has increased by `ell`. Let a returning continuation use `x` additional CO marks and `y` additional O2 marks. Exact return forces

`x - 2y = -ell`,

so `x=2y-ell>=0` and the continuation length is

`x+y = 3y-ell`.

Thus `y>=ceil(ell/2)`. If `ell` is even, the minimum is `y=ell/2,x=0`, giving length `ell/2`. If `ell` is odd, the minimum is `y=(ell+1)/2,x=1`, giving length `(ell+3)/2`. The constructions above attain these lower bounds. QED.

### Parallel-time consequence in the designated-service skeleton

For an even run, once the `ell` CO defects have occurred, the `ell/2` disjoint interior `H` clocks are simultaneously enabled. At `p=1/2` each has rate `1/4`. If all non-designated clocks are suppressed only for this skeleton calculation, the completion time is the maximum of `ell/2` iid `Exp(1/4)` variables, so

`P(tau_reset <= t) = (1-e^{-t/4})^(ell/2)`,

`E tau_reset = 4 H_{ell/2} = 4 log ell + O(1)`.                    (5.2)

Therefore an extensive run of first-CO defects is not intrinsically a serial `Theta(ell)` repair workload. Its **source count** is linear but its designated parallel service time is logarithmic. The actual full process still contains competing clocks; (5.2) is not by itself a source-valid probability bound.

## 6. Terminal EAR port geometry

The coordinator correction requires a local reserved useful clock that is terminal for the local outgoing-port trial. The natural object is the exact EAR produced by a direct or serviced zipper.

Use four consecutive columns `-1,0,1,2` and one additional exterior layer `z_i=(i,-2)`. In the guarded EAR state `E` take

- `u_{-1},u_0,u_1,u_2 = O`,
- `v_{-1},v_0,v_1,v_2 = 0`,
- `w_0,w_1 = 0`, while `w_{-1},w_2 = CO`,
- `z_0,z_1 = CO`,

with the obvious clean O interior and CO exterior continuation in the finite guard.

The old boundary edge `{v_0,v_1}` and the new outward edge `{w_0,w_1}` are both vacant.

Define the reserved terminal useful event

`F := O2{v_0,v_1}`.

Because `w_0,w_1` are already vacant, the two deposited O atoms at `v_0,v_1` see no neighboring CO and survive. The resulting local state has

`v_0=v_1=O`, `w_0=w_1=0`, `z_0=z_1=CO`,

which is an exact clean phase packet shifted outward by one layer. Thus `F` is a genuinely **terminal local SUCCESS** in the sense required by the corrected P02-B retry theorem. At `p=1/2` its rate is

`s=1/4`.                                                       (6.1)

## 7. Complete first-order core-interference repair catalogue around the terminal EAR

### Theorem 7.1 — every first-order nonterminal core mark has an exact adaptive reset of depth at most two

Starting from the guarded EAR state `E`, let exactly one non-`F` core label above ring, and perform the forced instantaneous reaction(s). Then, after observing the resulting stable state, there is an adaptive legal source word of length at most **two** that returns the entire guarded local pattern to the exact EAR state `E`.

No favorable reaction tie is assumed. When a deposited O has more than one exterior CO reaction partner, the repair simply observes which partner was actually removed and refills that site.

#### Case 1: `CO(v_i)`, `i=0,1`

The CO removes the unique interior O `u_i`, leaving `u_i=v_i=0`. Fire `O2{u_i,v_i}`. Both new O atoms survive because `w_i` is vacant. Then fire `CO(w_i)`: it reacts with the O at `v_i`, removing both and restoring `u_i=O,v_i=w_i=0`. This is the exact two-mark reset

`CO(v_i) ; O2{u_i,v_i} ; CO(w_i)`.

#### Case 2: `CO(w_i)`

The CO survives because every neighbor of `w_i` is vacancy or exterior CO. Fire the side zipper on `{v_{-1},v_0}` for `i=0` or `{v_1,v_2}` for `i=1`. Its O at `v_i` annihilates the offending `CO(w_i)`, while the side endpoint clears the corresponding side exterior CO. Then refill that cleared side exterior site by one CO mark. The exact original EAR guard is restored in two additional marks.

#### Case 3: side O2 on `{v_{-1},v_0}` or `{v_1,v_2}`

The side endpoint O clears its side exterior CO. The core endpoint O survives at `v_i` because `w_i` is vacant. Fire `CO(w_i)`, which annihilates that surviving `v_i` oxygen, and then refill the side exterior site by CO. Exact EAR is restored in two marks.

#### Case 4: radial O2 on `{v_i,w_i}`

The O at `v_i` survives. The O at `w_i` has one or more exterior CO neighbors and therefore annihilates exactly one of them; call the actually removed site `x`. Fire `CO(w_i)`, which annihilates the O at `v_i`, and then fire `CO(x)`. At the refill time `x` has no neighboring O, so the CO survives. This adaptively restores the exact EAR guard in two marks regardless of the reaction tie outcome.

#### Case 5: outer O2 on `{w_0,w_1}`

Each new O has exterior CO neighbors and annihilates exactly one of them. The two removed exterior sites are distinct on the square lattice. The core remains vacant. Refill those two actually removed sites by two CO marks. This restores the exact EAR guard.

These cases exhaust every enabled nonterminal primitive source label whose support intersects `K`. QED.

### Corollary 7.2 — uniform positive finite repair cylinder

Fix a finite guard containing the catalogue and all sites whose state is read by its adaptive repair choices. Let `Gamma<infinity` bound the total primitive source rate in that guard at `p=1/2`.

Use a repair window of length one, split into two half-slots. For a two-mark reset, require the first desired mark in the first half-slot and the second desired mark in the second half-slot, with no other guarded mark during the window. Every desired mark has rate at least `1/4`. Therefore, uniformly over all nine first-order defect states,

`boxed{ r_EAR >= e^{-Gamma}/64 > 0. }`                          (7.1)

One-mark repairs, if one chooses the monotone-side-progress active class rather than exact guard restoration, only improve this bound.

More generally, on any compact interval `I subset (0,1)`, let

`lambda_* = inf_{p in I} min(p,(1-p)/2) > 0`

and `Gamma_*` bound the guard rate. The same construction gives the uniform interval bound

`r_EAR(I) >= (lambda_*/2)^2 e^{-Gamma_*} > 0`.                  (7.2)

Thus the first-order repair geometry itself is not an anchor-only phenomenon.
