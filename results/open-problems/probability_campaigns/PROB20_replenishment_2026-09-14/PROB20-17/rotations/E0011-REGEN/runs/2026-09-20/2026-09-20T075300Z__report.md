## 2. Exact source clocks at the anchor

Work at `p=1/2`, hence `q=1`.

- Each vacant site has a CO source clock of rate `1/2`.
- Each nearest-neighbor vacant edge has an O2 source clock of rate `1/4`.
- A CO source occurrence can make at most one previously vacant adsorption site occupied.
- An O2 source occurrence can make at most its two adsorption endpoints occupied.
- Instantaneous reactions only delete occupied particles; they do not create a new occupied site away from an adsorption endpoint.

Therefore one source occurrence can turn at most **two previously vacant sites into occupied sites**. This elementary two-site creation bound is the key radial-depth input below.

## 3. The redundant phase belt

Let

`R=[0,a] x [0,b] intersect Z^2`,

with `a,b` large. Write

`R^{+j}=[-j,a+j] x [-j,b+j] intersect Z^2`.

Define the five-layer belt

`A_5 := R^{+5} \ R`.

The canonical redundant phase seed has:

- every site of `R` occupied by O;
- every site of `A_5` vacant;
- a finite exterior CO collar outside `R^{+5}`;
- a further finite causal buffer; beyond that buffer the stable exterior is arbitrary.

Let `L` be the lattice perimeter of the outer rectangle `R^{+5}`. The belt contains

`|A_5| = 5L-80 <= 5L`

sites.

The outermost vacant boundary has long straight pieces. On any straight piece choose three consecutive boundary vacancies

`v_0-v_1-v_2`

and their three exterior axial CO neighbors

`w_0,w_1,w_2`.

Exactly as in the previous report, an O2 source mark on `{v_0,v_1}`` clears `w_0,w_1` and leaves `v_0,v_1,w_0,w_1` vacant. A subsequent O2 mark on `{v_1,v_2}` clears `w_2`, leaves the outward detour

`v_0-w_0-w_1-w_2-v_2`

vacant, and turns `v_1` into O. The inner neighbor of `v_1` may be vacant rather than O; that only strengthens the conclusion that the second deposited O at `v_1` has no adjacent CO and survives. Thus the two-mark zipper remains exact on the outer edge of a thick vacancy belt.

## 4. Topological depth lemma: five vacant layers force three source occurrences

The first theorem is deterministic.

### Lemma 4.1 — three-occurrence barrier

Start from the canonical five-layer belt. Suppose at some time the annulus no longer contains a nearest-neighbor vacant circuit separating `R` from the exterior of `R^{+5}`. Then, before that time, there have been at least **three source occurrences** whose adsorption endpoint sets form a connected cluster in the square-lattice matching (`*`) geometry.

More generally, for a `w`-layer initially vacant belt, destruction of all nearest-neighbor vacant separating circuits requires a connected cluster of at least

`k_w := ceil(w/2)`

source occurrences.

### Proof

Use the elementary matching-lattice form of planar site duality: if an annulus contains no nearest-neighbor vacant circuit around its inner boundary, then the occupied sites contain a `*`-connected radial crossing from the inner side to the outer side. A `*`-step changes the layer index by at most one. Hence any such crossing of a `w`-layer belt contains at least `w` sites that were initially vacant.

At the time of the crossing, assign to each occupied crossing site its most recent source occurrence that changed that site from vacant to occupied. A CO occurrence can be assigned to at most one site and an O2 occurrence to at most two, so at least `ceil(w/2)` distinct source occurrences are needed.

Consecutive occupied sites of the `*`-crossing are at `l_infinity` distance at most one. Therefore the adsorption endpoint sets of the assigned source occurrences form a connected family in a finite-range matching graph. This proves the general statement. For `w=5`, `ceil(5/2)=3`. QED.

### Why width five is the first useful redundant width

A completed zipper needs two prescribed source marks. A four-layer belt can in principle lose its separator after only two source occurrences, so both mechanisms live at the same small-time order. A five-layer belt needs at least three source occurrences. Thus `w=5` is the first radial width at which two-mark progress and topology loss separate by one full order in time.

This is the central structural observation of the run.

## 5. Quantitative topology-loss bound

The source Harris system has finite range. Let `F_5(t)` be the event that the five-layer belt loses every vacant separating circuit at some time in `[0,t]`, before accounting for arbitrary-exterior causal leakage.

### Lemma 5.1 — cubic small-time bound

There is a universal finite constant `C_top` such that, for all sufficiently small `t`,

`P(F_5(t)) <= C_top L t^3 exp(C_top t)`.                                (5.1)

Consequently, for

`t_L = theta / sqrt(L)`,                                                (5.2)

with fixed `theta>0`,

`P(F_5(t_L)) = O_theta(L^(-1/2))`.                                     (5.3)

### Proof

By Lemma 4.1, `F_5(t)` implies a connected cluster of at least three source occurrences whose adsorption endpoints touch the belt. Root such a cluster at one of the at most `5L` belt sites. A source occurrence has one or two adjacent adsorption endpoints, and two occurrence labels can be adjacent in the matching graph only if their endpoint sets are within a fixed lattice distance. Hence the graph of possible source-clock labels adjacent to a fixed label has a universal bounded degree `D`.

For any fixed connected triple of clock labels, the probability that the necessary three rings occur by time `t` is `O(t^3)`; the individual source rates are at most `1/2`. The number of connected triples rooted at one belt site is bounded by a constant depending only on `D`, not on `L`. Summing over the `O(L)` possible roots gives (5.1). The exponential factor is a harmless Poisson-tail envelope that also covers repeated rings of the same local clock label.

Substituting (5.2) yields

`L t_L^3 = theta^3 / sqrt(L)`,

which proves (5.3). QED.

### General width form

The same argument and Lemma 4.1 give, for fixed `w`,

`P(F_w(t)) <= C_w L t^(ceil(w/2)) exp(C_w t)`.                          (5.4)

At the `1/sqrt(L)` zipper scale this is

`O(L^(1-ceil(w/2)/2))`.                                                 (5.5)

Thus the gain at width five is not a numerical accident; it is the first strict exponent gain.

## 6. Linear many independent zipper cells

Now obtain a lower bound for useful progress on the same time scale.

Choose zipper cells on the straight parts of the outer boundary, separated far enough that the finite Harris clock sets capable of changing their local zipper neighborhoods are disjoint. Since the required guard radius is fixed, there is a universal `c_pack>0` such that at least

`N_L >= c_pack L`

such cells can be selected for all large `L`.

For one selected cell define a **clean zipper event** `Z` over `[0,t]` by:

1. the first designated O2 edge rings exactly once during `[0,t/2]` and not during `(t/2,t]`;
2. the second designated O2 edge rings exactly once during `(t/2,t]` and not during `[0,t/2]`;
3. no other source clock whose finite reaction support can change the six-site zipper neighborhood rings during `[0,t]`.

There is a universal finite guard rate `Gamma`. A crude direct neighborhood count gives `Gamma<=225`, but its precise value is irrelevant. Since each designated O2 clock has rate `1/4`,

`P(Z) >= (t^2/64) exp(-Gamma t)`.                                      (6.1)

The clean zipper events of the selected cells are independent because their guarded Harris clock sets are disjoint.

### Lemma 6.1 — one-bank progress stays positive

Let `B` be any bank containing at least `c L` separated zipper cells for a fixed `c>0`. Then

`P(at least one clean zipper in B by t)`

`>= 1-exp[- c L t^2 exp(-Gamma t)/64 ]`.                              (6.2)

At `t=t_L=theta/sqrt(L)`,

`liminf_{L->infinity} P(bank progress)`

`>= 1-exp(-c theta^2/64) > 0`.                                        (6.3)

### Proof

Apply independence and `(1-x)^N<=exp(-Nx)` to (6.1). QED.

## 7. Two-port progress before topology loss

Split the selected cells into two macroscopically separated banks, for example one on an upper forward arc and one on a lower forward arc, each containing at least `c_2 L` guarded cells. Their guard-clock sets are pairwise disjoint.

Let `G_2(t)` be the event that by time `t` there is at least one clean completed zipper in **each** bank.

From Lemma 6.1,

`P(G_2(t_L))`

`>= [1-exp(-c_2 theta^2 exp(-Gamma theta/sqrt(L))/64)]^2`.              (7.1)

Hence

`liminf_{L->infinity} P(G_2(t_L))`

`>= s_*(theta) := [1-exp(-c_2 theta^2/64)]^2 > 0`.                     (7.2)

This is a genuine two-direction local growth event, not a single plaquette statistic.

Define `A_2(t)` to be the event `G_2(t)` occurs and the vacancy belt retains a separating circuit through time `t`. Then

`P(A_2(t_L)) >= P(G_2(t_L)) - P(F_5(t_L))`.                            (7.3)

By (5.3) and (7.2),

`liminf_{L->infinity} P(A_2(t_L)) >= s_*(theta)>0`,                    (7.4)

while

`P(F_5(t_L)) -> 0`.                                                     (7.5)

Therefore the progress/topology-loss ratio tends to infinity.

## 8. Source-valid arbitrary exterior costs lower order

The inherited causal-insulation lemma gives, for a read region of `O(L)` sites and a protected buffer requiring `N` causal source steps to enter the belt,

`eta_L <= C L sum_{n>=N} (38 t_L)^n/n!`                               (8.1)

at `p=1/2`.

Choose a **fixed** finite buffer with `N=6` (for example any buffer distance large enough under the previously proved event-support diameter-three bound). Then

`eta_L = O_theta(L * L^(-3)) = O_theta(L^(-2))`.                       (8.2)

Thus arbitrary stable exterior information is asymptotically negligible compared with the already vanishing `O(L^-1/2)` topology-loss probability. No growing or infinite protective wall is required.

Let

`f_L := P(F_5(t_L)) + eta_L`

and let `a_L` be the exact-source probability of two-bank clean zipper progress before either topology loss or causal leakage. Then

`a_L >= P(G_2(t_L)) - f_L`.                                            (8.3)

Combining the previous bounds gives the main theorem.

### Theorem 8.1 — arbitrary fixed coefficient local margin

For every fixed `kappa<infinity` and every fixed `theta>0`, there is `L_0(theta,kappa)` such that for all `L>=L_0`,

`a_L - kappa f_L > 0`.                                                  (8.4)

In particular, for the historical coefficient,

`a_L - 63 f_L > 0`                                                     (8.5)

for all sufficiently large `L`.

### Proof

From (8.3),

`a_L-kappa f_L >= P(G_2(t_L))-(kappa+1)f_L`.

The first term has strictly positive liminf `s_*(theta)` by (7.2), while `f_L=O(L^-1/2)` by (5.3) and (8.2). Hence the right-hand side is positive for all sufficiently large `L`. QED.

### Interpretation

This proves that **annular boundary insulation is no longer the ratio bottleneck**, even under the original `63:1` comparison coefficient. A five-layer vacancy belt makes genuine topology destruction a third-order local event, while same-class zipper progress is second-order and can be amplified on `Theta(L)` disjoint boundary cells.

The theorem is stronger than merely checking one favorable exact word: it is an arbitrary-size family with an asymptotic separation of mechanisms.

## 10. What failed / what this rules out

The previous report showed that a full one-layer canonical annulus expansion costs exactly `P+4` source marks, so searching for a constant-mark exact duplicator was the wrong scale.

A possible response was to make the entire annulus rigid and demand that essentially no defects occur. That is also the wrong scale: `Theta(L)` harmless local activity occurs naturally over useful windows. The present theorem shows that rigidity is unnecessary. Five radial vacancy layers absorb sparse local damage while retaining an exponentially large family of possible local zipper moves; true topology loss needs a connected three-occurrence witness and is parametrically rarer.

Thus the next run should not spend effort lowering a one-shell hazard constant or searching for a rarer exact no-defect word.
