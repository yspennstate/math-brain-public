## 2. A fixed straight vacancy-segment seed class

Fix a lattice unit vector `e` and an integer `m>=5`. For a lattice point `a`, define

`P_m(a,e) := {a, a+e, ..., a+m e}`.                         (2.1)

Call the configuration on this set an **`m`-segment seed** when every one of its `m+1` sites is vacant. The stable chemistry outside the segment is arbitrary unless a finite Harris guard is explicitly imposed for a probability estimate.

This is deliberately a small fixed seed, not a growing annulus. It has three properties needed by the regeneration program:

1. it contains many adjacent vacancy pairs, so the arbitrary-exterior extension lemma can be applied at either endpoint;
2. it can produce separated exact translated copies of itself in a number of source arrivals depending only on a fixed chosen separation, not on any large block size;
3. one primitive interference cannot destroy all of its local vacancy anchors, and in fact can be repaired exactly for `m>=5`.

The third point is proved in Section 4.

## 3. Main geometric theorem: symmetric same-class two-child compilation at arbitrary fixed separation

### Lemma 3.1 — one-sided extension by `d` sites

Let `P_m(a,e)` be vacant and let `d>=1`. Uniformly over arbitrary stable exterior chemistry, there is an adaptive legal source word of length at most

`2d`                                                             (3.1)

whose terminal state has every site of

`{a, a+e, ..., a+(m+d)e}`                                      (3.2)

vacant.

#### Proof

Apply the inherited vacancy-edge extension lemma at the right endpoint. Initially the last two sites `a+(m-1)e,a+m e` are adjacent vacancies. Clear `a+(m+1)e` in at most two source arrivals while preserving the old segment. The last two sites of the enlarged segment are now vacant, so repeat. After `d` iterations, all sites in (3.2) are vacant and at most `2d` source arrivals were used. QED.

The reflected statement holds at the left endpoint.

### Theorem 3.2 — symmetric sliding-window two-child compiler

Fix integers `m>=1` and `d>=1`. From the exact seed `P_m(a,e)`, uniformly over arbitrary stable exterior chemistry, there is an adaptive legal source word of length at most

`boxed{4d}`                                                     (3.3)

whose terminal state contains the two exact same-class translated children

`P_m(a-d e,e)` and `P_m(a+d e,e)`.                              (3.4)

If `d>=m+1`, the two child seed supports are disjoint. More generally, `d` can be chosen once and for all large enough that any prescribed fixed finite Harris guards around the two children are disjoint.

#### Proof

First use Lemma 3.1 at the right endpoint to clear

`a+(m+1)e,...,a+(m+d)e`

in at most `2d` arrivals. The original segment is preserved throughout. Now use the reflected endpoint extension at the left endpoint to clear

`a-e,...,a-d e`

in at most another `2d` arrivals, preserving every vacancy already created on the right.

At termination the entire straight segment

`{a-d e, a-(d-1)e, ..., a+(m+d)e}`                             (3.5)

is vacant. Its left `m+1`-site window is `P_m(a-d e,e)`. Its right translated `m+1`-site window beginning at `a+d e` is `P_m(a+d e,e)`. Hence both exact children in (3.4) are present. QED.

### Corollary 3.3 — the old macroscopic stitching problem is not intrinsic

The deterministic two-child obligation does **not** require rebuilding an entire translated annulus or phase strip. One fixed vacancy segment can create two exact translated members of its own seed class at any chosen finite separation using a bounded number of legal source arrivals. Taking `d` larger than the child-guard diameter makes the two outgoing child supports source-locally separable.

This is substantially stronger than the earlier four-mark result for duplicating a single vacancy edge: the recurrent object may now be taken to have any fixed length `m`, while the two outgoing copies may be placed at an arbitrarily chosen fixed separation. Thus the seed class can carry enough redundancy for RETRY while still admitting a finite same-class two-child compiler.

### Proposition 3.4 — the two-arrival cost of one worst-exterior extension step is sharp

The inherited upper bound of two arrivals for adding one prescribed next site cannot be improved uniformly over all stable exteriors.

Take adjacent vacancies `x,y`, let the prescribed next site `z` adjacent to `y` contain CO, and take every neighbor of `x` other than `y` vacant and every neighbor of `y` other than `x,z` vacant. A single state-changing source mark that can remove the CO at `z` must use O2 on the vacant edge `{x,y}` and have the O deposited at `y` react with `z`. But the simultaneously deposited O at `x` then has no adjacent CO and survives, so the three sites are not all vacant after that one mark. A CO mark cannot remove the CO already at `z`, and an O2 mark not using the endpoint pair does not simultaneously preserve the required three-site path. Hence some stable exteriors genuinely require two source arrivals.

The theorem above is therefore built from a locally sharp arbitrary-exterior primitive rather than from a long nonessential word.

## 4. New exact RETRY theorem: every first primitive interference is repairable in at most four arrivals

A recurrent seed is useful only if common interruptions need not be declared FATAL. For the segment class there is a simple structural classification which avoids a large occupation-string catalogue.

### Lemma 4.1 — one primitive source occurrence occupies at most two adjacent segment sites

Start from an exact vacant segment `P_m(a,e)` in an arbitrary stable exterior and expose one state-changing primitive source occurrence whose adsorption support intersects the segment. After all instantaneous reactions caused by that source occurrence have finished, the subset of sites of `P_m(a,e)` that are no longer vacant is either

- empty,
- one site, or
- two adjacent sites.

#### Proof

A CO source occurrence has one adsorption site. If it survives, at most that one segment site becomes occupied; if it reacts with O, the adsorption site is vacant after the instantaneous annihilation. No reaction creates a second occupied segment site.

An O2 source occurrence has two adjacent adsorption endpoints. Only those endpoints can receive new O particles. Each deposited O either survives at its endpoint or reacts and disappears, so at most the two adjacent endpoints can become occupied segment sites. Instantaneous reactions only remove particles and cannot create an additional occupied segment site elsewhere on the segment. QED.

### Lemma 4.2 — an intact neighboring vacancy edge always survives when `m>=5`

Let `m>=5`, so the segment contains at least six vertices. Remove from its path graph either one vertex or two adjacent vertices. At least one connected component of the remaining path contains two adjacent vertices.

#### Proof

For one removed vertex, the two remaining side lengths sum to `m`, so one side contains at least `ceil(m/2)>=3` vertices. For two adjacent removed vertices, the two remaining side lengths sum to `m-1>=4`, so one side contains at least two vertices. QED.

### Theorem 4.3 — complete first-order exact segment reset

Let `m>=5`. Starting from an exact `m`-segment seed in arbitrary stable exterior chemistry, expose any one state-changing primitive source occurrence whose adsorption support intersects the segment. After observing the resulting stable state, there is an adaptive legal source word of length at most

`boxed{4}`                                                      (4.1)

that restores **every site of the original `P_m(a,e)` to vacancy**.

Thus no first primitive core interference needs to be classified as FATAL for the segment seed class.

#### Proof

By Lemma 4.1, zero, one or two adjacent segment sites are occupied. If zero, no reset is needed.

Otherwise, by Lemma 4.2 there is an intact adjacent vacant pair in the segment on one side of the damaged block. Starting from that pair, apply the arbitrary-exterior vacancy-edge extension lemma along the segment toward the damage.

- If one segment site is occupied, one invocation clears it in at most two source arrivals.
- If two adjacent segment sites are occupied, invoke the extension lemma twice, clearing the nearer damaged site and then the farther one, in at most four arrivals total.

Each invocation preserves all previously carved vacancies, including the untouched remainder of the original segment. Hence the exact original all-vacant segment is restored. QED.

### Consequence for the SUCCESS/FATAL/RETRY partition

The theorem does **not** yet prove a positive `a-63f` because several interfering source occurrences may accumulate before the adaptive reset is completed. What it proves is sharper than a reachability statement: the first-order state space has an exact same-class RETRY compiler of uniformly bounded depth. Any future finite-core trial should therefore reserve FATAL for genuinely higher-order loss of all usable vacancy anchors, not for a single local mark.

This removes the first-order macroscopic-state mismatch that was still present after the EAR/translation catalogue.

## 5. Uniform positive cylinders for the segment compiler

The deterministic words above can be embedded in a source-valid finite Harris cylinder with a positive parameter-uniform probability.

Fix a compact interval

`I_delta=[1/2-delta,1/2+delta] subset (0,1)`.

Let

`r_* := inf_{p in I_delta} min(p,(1-p)/2) >0`.              (5.1)

Fix `m,d` and a finite guard containing the entire symmetric compiler. Let `Gamma_*<infinity` bound the total primitive source rate in this guard over `I_delta`. Set

`L:=4d`.

Divide a fresh compiler interval of length `tau` into `L` equal subintervals. At each step require the next adaptive source mark supplied by Theorem 3.2 in its assigned subinterval, suppress all other guarded marks, and, when a reaction partner must be selected, require the specified partner. Since the square lattice has degree four, every required partner has conditional probability at least `1/4`.

Choosing `tau` so that `r_* tau/(4L)<=1`, the probability of the complete protected compiler is bounded below uniformly on `I_delta` by the conservative constant

`boxed{ c_child >= exp(-Gamma_* tau) [r_* tau/(4L)]^L >0. }`   (5.2)

If the adaptive word finishes in fewer than `L` marks, the unused subintervals are simply required to contain no guarded mark; because the base in (5.2) is at most one, the `L`-power remains a valid lower bound.

An identical argument gives a positive uniform protected cylinder for the at-most-four-mark reset of Theorem 4.3. The numerical constants are intentionally not optimized; the important point is that the depth is fixed once `m,d` are fixed.

`L_m = 2(m-1)`                                               (6.1)

As in Section 5, enlarge the guard once and use a fresh Harris time slab. There is a constant

`sigma_m(delta,tau)>0`                                      (6.2)

such that, conditional on an exact translated phase packet, the fresh-slab probability of producing the prescribed exact `m`-segment and suppressing all other guarded marks is at least `sigma_m`, uniformly over `p in I_delta` and over arbitrary stable chemistry outside the fixed guard.

`c_* sigma_m >0`                                            (6.3)
