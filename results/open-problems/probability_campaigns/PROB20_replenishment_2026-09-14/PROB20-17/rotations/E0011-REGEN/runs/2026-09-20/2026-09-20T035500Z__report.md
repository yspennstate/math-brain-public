## 3. New exact structural theorem: a vacancy edge is the minimal universal carving nucleus

The preceding run used a `2 x 2` vacancy square as the regenerative geometric nucleus. That is not minimal.

### Lemma 3.1 — one-site extension from a vacant edge in at most two source arrivals

Let `x,y` be adjacent vacant lattice sites and let `z` be a nearest neighbor of `y`, with `z != x`. The rest of the stable source configuration is arbitrary. There is an adaptive legal source sequence of length at most two after which `x,y,z` are all vacant. Existing vacancies outside these three sites need not be changed.

#### Proof

There are three cases.

1. `z` is already vacant: do nothing.

2. `z` contains O. Trigger a CO arrival at the vacant site `y`. If there are several adjacent O atoms, choose the reaction with the O at `z`. The incoming CO and that O annihilate immediately, so both `y` and `z` are vacant afterward; `x` was untouched. This costs one source arrival.

3. `z` contains CO. Trigger an O2 arrival on the vacant edge `{x,y}`. Require the O deposited at `y` to react with the CO at `z` (if several adjacent CO atoms are available, choose `z`). Then `y` and `z` are vacant afterward. If the O deposited at `x` also reacts with a CO, then `x` is vacant as well and the job is done in one arrival. If that O survives at `x`, trigger a CO arrival at `y` and require it to react with the O at `x`; then `x` and `y` are again vacant while `z` remains vacant. This costs two arrivals.

Every requested mark is legal in the exact source process. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — arbitrary self-avoiding vacancy-path carving

Starting from one vacant nearest-neighbor edge, any prescribed self-avoiding lattice path with `n>=2` vertices whose first two vertices are that edge can be made entirely vacant using at most

\[
2(n-2)
\]

legal source arrivals, uniformly over arbitrary stable exterior chemistry.

#### Proof

Apply Lemma 3.1 successively to the last two vacant vertices of the already-carved path and the next prescribed vertex. The lemma preserves all earlier vacancies. QED.

### Corollary 3.3 — one vacancy edge gives two disjoint translated vacancy-edge children in at most four arrivals

From the horizontal vacant edge `{(0,0),(1,0)}`, clear `(2,0)` by Lemma 3.1 and then clear `(-1,0)` using the original edge in the reflected direction. In at most four arrivals the four consecutive sites

\[
(-1,0),(0,0),(1,0),(2,0)
\]

are vacant. Hence the two disjoint edges

\[
\{(-1,0),(0,0)\},
\qquad
\{(1,0),(2,0)\}
\]

are exact translated vacancy-edge children.

This improves the preceding `2 x 2` theorem's worst-case adjacent two-child event depth from 16 to 4.

### Corollary 3.4 — an edge reaches a `2 x 2` vacancy square in at most four arrivals

Starting from `{(0,0),(1,0)}`, use Lemma 3.1 to clear `(0,1)` and then `(1,1)`. Hence a vacancy edge reaches a `2 x 2` all-vacant square in at most four legal arrivals. Combining with the preceding report's stationary-law argument, positive stationary density of vacancy edges already implies positive stationary one-site densities of both CO and O.

### Proposition 3.5 — minimality among vacancy-only arbitrary-exterior seeds

No one-site vacancy seed can have the preceding arbitrary-exterior universal reachability property.

#### Proof

Take the stable exterior configuration in which every site except the chosen vacancy is CO. There is no vacant nearest-neighbor pair, so no O2 adsorption is enabled. The only state-changing source event is a CO arrival at the unique vacancy, which occurs at positive rate and fills it. The system then reaches the all-CO absorbing state. Thus from one vacancy there is no positive-probability route, uniformly over arbitrary stable exteriors, to create a second vacancy. QED.

So an adjacent vacant pair is the smallest vacancy-only universal source nucleus.

## 4. New probabilistic idea: a long phase-separated interface makes the dangerous second-hit probability `O(1/m)`

The geometric lemma above does **not** by itself solve the regeneration probability problem: a prescribed carving word can still be rare. The next theorem attacks that probability obstruction directly.

Consider the stable reference interface

\[
\eta^*(i,j)=
\begin{cases}
2,&j\ge1,\\
0,&j=0,\\
1,&j\le-1.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus a horizontal vacancy line separates an O half-plane above from a CO half-plane below. In a finite protected trial we only use a long central window of this interface; a finite buffer and the already-proved causal-insulation lemma transfer the calculation to arbitrary exterior data.

Fix `m` consecutive base vacancies

\[
b_i=(i,0),\qquad 1\le i\le m,
\]

with `u_i=(i,1)` initially O and `d_i=(i,-1)` initially CO.

Define a stopped ideal-core microtrial. Until stopping, the only nonterminal moves we permit are **fresh upper-consumption moves**:

- if `u_i` is still O, a CO arrival at `b_i` reacts uniquely upward with `u_i`; `b_i` remains vacant and `u_i` becomes vacant. Call index `i` a defect.

Stop with **MICRO-SUCCESS** at the first O2 mark on an internal base edge `{b_i,b_{i+1}}`. Stop with **MICRO-FATAL** at the first one of:

- a CO arrival at a base site `b_i` whose upper O has already been consumed;
- any state-changing source mark using one of the newly vacant defect sites `u_i`;
- the fixed time horizon, if no prior stop occurred.

All other boundary effects are handled by the finite protected buffer and charged through the inherited causal-insulation error.

### Why MICRO-SUCCESS is a real geometric birth

At a MICRO-SUCCESS edge `{b_i,b_{i+1}}`, both deposited O atoms have their respective lower sites `d_i,d_{i+1}` as unique adjacent CO partners: horizontal neighbors are vacant and upper neighbors are O or vacancy defects. Hence both O atoms react downward. The four sites

\[
\{b_i,b_{i+1},d_i,d_{i+1}\}
\]

are then vacant. Therefore every MICRO-SUCCESS creates an exact `2 x 2` vacancy plaquette while retaining the two original base vacancies. In particular it creates multiple vacancy-edge nuclei of the type in Section 3.

The key point is that before stopping, fresh CO marks **do not damage the base vacancy interface**; only a second hit or a mark involving a defect site can do so.

## 5. Exact hazard bound

Let `j` be the number of distinct fresh upper-consumption defects already created. Before stopping, the transient configuration is completely described for this calculation by the defect set: the `m` base sites remain vacant, exactly `j` corresponding upper sites are vacant, every other upper site is O, and all lower sites remain CO.

At such a state:

### SUCCESS rate

Every internal base edge remains vacant, so the MICRO-SUCCESS rate is the constant

\[
s_m=\frac{m-1}{4}.
\]

### Fresh safe rate

There are `m-j` base sites whose upper O is untouched. Their CO clocks have total rate

\[
r_j=\frac{m-j}{2}.
\]

Each such event increases `j` by one and remains transient.

### Fatal rate

There are two conservative contributions.

1. A repeated CO mark at one of the `j` used base sites has total rate `j/2`. Such a base site has no adjacent O left in the reference transient state, so the incoming CO survives and fills a base vacancy.

2. For each defect vacancy `u_i`, a CO mark there has rate `1/2`. Also `u_i` has at most three vacant nearest neighbors before stopping: its base site and, at worst, two horizontally adjacent defect sites. Hence O2 marks on vacant edges incident to defect sites have total rate at most `3j/4` (this overcounts shared defect-defect edges, which is harmless for an upper bound).

Thus the total fatal rate is bounded by

\[
d_j\le \frac{j}{2}+\frac{j}{2}+\frac{3j}{4}=\frac{7j}{4}.
\]

No independence approximation has been used; these are source-clock rates in the stopped chain.

### Theorem 5.1 — eventual local fatal probability is `O(1/m)`

Let `f_m^{(\infty)}` be the probability that MICRO-FATAL occurs before MICRO-SUCCESS when the time horizon is removed in the ideal protected core. Then

\[
\boxed{
 f_m^{(\infty)}
 \le
 \frac{14m(3m-1)}{(m-1)^3}.
}
\]

In particular `f_m^{(\infty)}=42/m+O(m^{-2})`.

#### Proof

At defect count `j`, the probability that the next relevant transition is another fresh safe CO mark is at most

\[
\frac{r_j}{s_m+r_j}
\le
\frac{m/2}{(m-1)/4+m/2}
=
\rho_m:=\frac{2m}{3m-1}.
\]

Fatal transitions only decrease the probability of reaching larger defect counts, so

\[
P(\text{reach defect count }j)\le \rho_m^j.
\]

Conditional on reaching `j`, the probability that the next relevant transition is fatal is at most

\[
\frac{d_j}{s_m}
\le
\frac{7j}{m-1}.
\]

Therefore, extending the finite sum to an infinite geometric series,

\[
 f_m^{(\infty)}
 \le
 \frac{7}{m-1}
 \sum_{j\ge1} j\rho_m^j
 =
 \frac{7}{m-1}
 \frac{\rho_m}{(1-\rho_m)^2}.
\]

Since

\[
1-\rho_m=\frac{m-1}{3m-1},
\]

this equals

\[
\frac{14m(3m-1)}{(m-1)^3}.
\]

QED.

This is the first quantitative result on this route where the bad-event probability tends to zero **without** asking for an increasingly long prescribed source word.
