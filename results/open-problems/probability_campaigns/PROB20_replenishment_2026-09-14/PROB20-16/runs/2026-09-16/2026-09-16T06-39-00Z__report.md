## 2. A rank-one line lemma for complete DPP entropy

Let `F=F*` and `P=vv*` have rank at most one. On any interval of real `r` for which
\[
0\preceq F+rP\preceq I,
\]
the complete DPP law `P_{F+rP}` is affine in `r`.

Indeed, for every inclusion set `S`, the rank-one determinant lemma / determinant multilinearity gives
\[
\det(F_S+rP_S)=A_S+rB_S.
\]
Every exact atom probability is an inclusion-exclusion linear combination of these inclusion probabilities, hence is also affine in `r`. Shannon entropy is concave as a function of a probability vector, so
\[
\boxed{r\longmapsto H(P_{F+rP})\text{ is concave on its full feasible interval}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]
This statement is valid for complex `v` and at spectral/atom boundaries by finite-dimensional entropy continuity. No eigenvalue entropy is involved.

A useful centered consequence is the following. Fix `0<a<1`, a Hermitian `B`, and `P=vv*`. Whenever both kernels below are feasible, define
\[
\Psi_{a,B}(s)
=aH(P_{B-(1-a)sP})+(1-a)H(P_{B+asP}),\qquad s\ge0.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\Psi_{a,B}(s)\text{ is nonincreasing in }s.}
\tag{2.3}
\]
For strict data let `f(r)=H(P_{B+rP})`. Concavity makes `f'` nonincreasing, and
\[
\Psi'_{a,B}(s)
=a(1-a)\{f'(as)-f'(-(1-a)s)\}\le0,
\tag{2.4}
\]
because `as >= -(1-a)s`. The boundary statement follows from one-sided secant slopes and continuity. Notice that (2.3) uses the **measured complete subset entropy** along a rank-one covariance line, not von Neumann entropy or a spectral surrogate.

## 3. General collinear-leaf lifting lemma

Let the finite measured ground set be `{p} union Y`, with `|Y|=m`. Let two Hermitian positive contractions have block form
\[
K_j=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&w_j^*\\w_j&B_j\end{pmatrix},\qquad j=0,1,
\tag{3.1}
\]
with the same distinguished marginal `0<a<1`. Assume their coupling columns are complex-collinear:
\[
\boxed{w_j=z_jv\quad(j=0,1)}
\tag{3.2}
\]
for one fixed `v in C^m` and complex scalars `z_0,z_1`. Zero coupling at either endpoint is allowed. Put
\[
P=vv^*,\qquad
s_j=\frac{|z_j|^2}{a(1-a)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
The exact DPP conditional kernels on `Y` are
\[
F_{1,j}=B_j-(1-a)s_jP
\quad (p\text{ occupied}),
\tag{3.4}
\]
\[
F_{0,j}=B_j+as_jP
\quad (p\text{ absent}).
\tag{3.5}
\]
They are genuine positive contractions because they are conditional DPP kernels.

### Theorem 3.1 (conditional chord lifting)
Assume that for each `epsilon in {0,1}` the lower-dimensional entropy is concave on the specific chord
\[
\bar F_{\epsilon,t}=(1-t)F_{\epsilon,0}+tF_{\epsilon,1},\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Then the original chord `K_t=(1-t)K_0+tK_1` satisfies Lyons's complete-subset entropy inequality:
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{K_t})\ge(1-t)H(P_{K_0})+tH(P_{K_1})
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
for every `0<=t<=1`.

### Proof
Write
\[
z_t=(1-t)z_0+tz_1,
\qquad B_t=(1-t)B_0+tB_1,
\]
\[
s_t=\frac{|z_t|^2}{a(1-a)},
\qquad
\bar s_t=(1-t)s_0+ts_1.
\tag{3.8}
\]
The scalar parallelogram identity gives the exact defect
\[
\boxed{
\bar s_t-s_t
=\frac{t(1-t)|z_1-z_0|^2}{a(1-a)}\ge0.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Conditioning `K_t` on the distinguished bit gives
\[
F_{1,t}^{\rm act}=B_t-(1-a)s_tP,
\qquad
F_{0,t}^{\rm act}=B_t+as_tP.
\tag{3.10}
\]
On the other hand, direct interpolation of the endpoint conditional kernels gives
\[
\bar F_{1,t}=B_t-(1-a)\bar s_tP,
\qquad
\bar F_{0,t}=B_t+a\bar s_tP.
\tag{3.11}
\]
Both pairs are feasible. The actual pair comes from the genuine kernel `K_t`; the barred pair consists of convex combinations of the endpoint conditional contractions. For every `s` between `s_t` and `bar s_t`, both kernels in (2.2) remain feasible by convexity.

The entropy chain rule and (2.3) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(P_{K_t})-h(a)
&=aH(P_{F_{1,t}^{\rm act}})+(1-a)H(P_{F_{0,t}^{\rm act}})\\
&=\Psi_{a,B_t}(s_t)\\
&\ge\Psi_{a,B_t}(\bar s_t)\\
&=aH(P_{\bar F_{1,t}})+(1-a)H(P_{\bar F_{0,t}}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.12}
\]
Now apply the assumed concavity of the two lower-dimensional chords (3.6):
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(P_{K_t})-h(a)
&\ge(1-t)\{aH(P_{F_{1,0}})+(1-a)H(P_{F_{0,0}})\}\\
&\quad+t\{aH(P_{F_{1,1}})+(1-a)H(P_{F_{0,1}})\}\\
&=(1-t)\{H(P_{K_0})-h(a)\}+t\{H(P_{K_1})-h(a)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.13}
\]
Adding `h(a)` proves (3.7). `square`

The proof keeps the exact conditioning accelerations implicitly through the actual conditional kernels; no unproved sign is assigned to a moving Schur complement. The only nonlinear interpolation defect is the explicit positive scalar (3.9), paid by the monotonicity (2.3).

## 4. New global three-site theorem: complex triangles with a collinear coupling row

For three sites the conditional ground set `Y` has size two, and C055 supplies entropy concavity for **every** Hermitian two-site chord. Thus Theorem 3.1 immediately yields the following closed-domain result.

### Theorem 4.1
Let
\[
Q_j=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&w_j^*\\w_j&B_j\end{pmatrix},\qquad
0\preceq Q_j\preceq I_3,\quad j=0,1,
\tag{4.1}
\]
where `B_j` are arbitrary Hermitian `2x2` blocks and the distinguished diagonal is common:
\[
(Q_0)_{11}=(Q_1)_{11}=a.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Assume only that
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}[w_0\;w_1]\le1.}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Then for every `0<=t<=1`,
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{(1-t)Q_0+tQ_1})
\ge(1-t)H(P_{Q_0})+tH(P_{Q_1}).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
The two entries of `w_j` may both be nonzero, so `Q_j` may be a genuine triangle rather than a path. The common complex amplitude multiplying the fixed coupling shape may change both magnitude and phase between endpoints. The remaining `2x2` block `B_j`, including its complex off-diagonal entry, may vary arbitrarily subject only to the positive-contraction constraints.

By permuting coordinates, the theorem holds with any distinguished vertex.

### Boundary cases
If `0<a<1`, Theorem 3.1 and closed two-site concavity already allow spectral-boundary conditional kernels. Equivalently, one may regularize both endpoints by
\[
Q_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-\varepsilon)Q_j
+\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(a,1/2,1/2),
\tag{4.5}
\]
which preserves the common diagonal and collinearity and makes the kernels strict, then let `epsilon downarrow 0`.

If `a=0`, positivity forces `w_j=0`; if `a=1`, positivity of `I-Q_j` forces `w_j=0`. The distinguished bit is deterministic and the assertion reduces exactly to closed two-site entropy concavity for `B_0,B_1`. Thus (4.4) holds on the complete closed domain.

### Strict extension beyond the preceding path theorem
The preceding task-local theorem required one component of `w_j` to vanish at both endpoints because the support was the path `1-2-3`. Theorem 4.1 allows, for example,
\[
w_0=z_0(1,i/2)^T,\qquad w_1=z_1(1,i/2)^T
\]
with both components nonzero, while the `23` entry inside `B_j` can independently be nonzero. Hence all three measured edges can be present throughout the chord.

## 5. Higher-dimensional consequence from any certified lower-dimensional chord class

Theorem 3.1 is dimension-free as a **lifting principle**: it does not require the full `(m)`-site Lyons conjecture, only concavity on the two specific conditional chords (3.6).

A concrete corollary uses the already archived diagonal-endpoint theorem. Suppose the setup (3.1)-(3.5) holds in arbitrary finite dimension and, for each `epsilon=0,1`, the affine line through `F_{epsilon,0}` and `F_{epsilon,1}` contains a feasible diagonal contraction in the original measured coordinates. Then the diagonal-endpoint theorem proves entropy concavity on each conditional line, so Theorem 3.1 proves Lyons concavity on the original higher-dimensional chord.

Thus a common-marginal rank-one leaf can lift any lower-dimensional certified chord class, not just the universal two-site theorem. This is a reusable reduction rather than a claim that arbitrary lower-dimensional chords are solved.

## 6. What was checked and what was not

The proof above is analytic. A deterministic numerical diagnostic was run separately on 3,000 independently generated strict complex three-site endpoint pairs satisfying (4.2)-(4.3). It checked the exact conditioning entropy chain rule, the strength defect (3.9), the monotonic comparison in (3.12), the auxiliary two-site chord Jensen step, and the final full three-site Jensen gap. The smallest sampled full entropy gap was positive (about `9.49e-06`), and the maximum absolute chain-rule residual was about `8.88e-16`. These samples are corroborative only and are not used to infer universality.

No attempt was made to infer the unrestricted conjecture from this family. In particular, if `w_0,w_1` span a two-dimensional complex subspace, the outer-product interpolation defect is matrix-valued rather than a single fixed rank-one strength coordinate, and the monotonicity argument above no longer applies directly. That is the sharp obstruction exposed by this proof method, not evidence of a counterexample.

## 8. Next attack

The proof identifies a precise next obstruction. For noncollinear coupling columns,
\[
(1-t)w_0w_0^*+tw_1w_1^*-w_tw_t^*
=t(1-t)(w_1-w_0)(w_1-w_0)^*
\tag{8.1}
\]
is still rank one and positive semidefinite, but the baseline conditional displacement `w_tw_t^*` need not lie in the same rank-one direction as the defect. The scalar derivative ordering behind (2.4) therefore becomes a **two-rank-one parallelogram inequality** for the lower-dimensional entropy.

The next high-value target is to prove, or refute with an exact two-site certificate, the required two-direction inequality for the pair of conditional two-site kernels. Because the lower-dimensional space is only `2x2`, this is a finite scalar/matrix problem. A positive result would remove the collinearity assumption and settle the entire common-one-marginal three-site chord class, including arbitrary complex triangles. A negative result would identify a concrete failed proof mechanism and could be optimized for a genuine three-site Hessian/Jensen counterexample; it would not by itself refute Lyons.
