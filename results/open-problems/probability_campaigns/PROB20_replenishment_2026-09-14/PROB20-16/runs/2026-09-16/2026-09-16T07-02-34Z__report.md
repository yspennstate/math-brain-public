## 1. Recalled lifting lemma from the newest checkpoint

Let two kernels on \(1+m\) measured sites have the same distinguished marginal \(0<a<1\):
\[
K_j=\begin{pmatrix}a&w_j^*\\ w_j&B_j\end{pmatrix},\qquad j=0,1.
\]
Assume the coupling columns are complex-collinear,
\[
w_j=z_jv,
\]
with fixed \(v\in\mathbb C^m\). Put
\[
P=vv^*,\qquad s_j=\frac{|z_j|^2}{a(1-a)}.
\]
The two conditional kernels after revealing the distinguished site are
\[
F_{1,j}=B_j-(1-a)s_jP,
\qquad
F_{0,j}=B_j+a s_jP.
\tag{1}
\]
The previous checkpoint proved:

> If Shannon entropy is concave on both lower-dimensional affine chords \((F_{1,0},F_{1,1})\) and \((F_{0,0},F_{0,1})\), then it is concave on the original chord \((K_0,K_1)\).

The essential point is
\[
\bar s_t-s_t
=\frac{t(1-t)|z_1-z_0|^2}{a(1-a)}\ge0,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(s_t=|(1-t)z_0+tz_1|^2/[a(1-a)]\) and \(\bar s_t=(1-t)s_0+ts_1\). Along the fixed rank-one direction \(P\), the centered conditional entropy
\[
\Psi_{a,B}(s)
=aH(P_{B-(1-a)sP})+(1-a)H(P_{B+asP})
\]
is nonincreasing in \(s\), because each lower DPP atom is affine on a rank-one kernel line and Shannon entropy is concave on that affine probability line. This pays exactly the nonlinear defect (2); no conditional acceleration is discarded.

## 2. New four-site theorem

Take \(m=3\), so \(K_0,K_1\) are four-site kernels. Let
\[
\Delta B=B_1-B_0,
\qquad
\Delta s=s_1-s_0,
\]
and define the two actual three-site conditional chord directions
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_1=\Delta B-(1-a)\Delta s\,P,
\qquad
\Delta_0=\Delta B+a\Delta s\,P.
}
\tag{3}
\]

### Theorem A — semidefinite/singular conditional lift
Assume \(0\preceq K_j\preceq I_4\), the distinguished diagonal \(a\) is common, and \(w_j=z_jv\). If for each \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\) the Hermitian matrix \(\Delta_\epsilon\) is either

- semidefinite (positive or negative, of any rank), or
- singular,

then
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{(1-t)K_0+tK_1})
\ge(1-t)H(P_{K_0})+tH(P_{K_1})
\quad(0\le t\le1).
}
\tag{4}
\]

#### Proof
Each \(F_{\epsilon,j}\) in (1) is a three-site positive contraction, by ordinary DPP conditioning/Schur complementation.

If \(\Delta_\epsilon\) is semidefinite, the two conditional endpoints are Loewner comparable, so X015 proves entropy concavity on that conditional chord.

If \(\Delta_\epsilon\) is singular but not semidefinite, then in dimension three it has rank exactly two and inertia \((1,1,1_0)\). Agent 019 proves entropy concavity for every such rank-two indefinite chord (indeed through dimension five).

Thus each of the two lower conditional chords is certified. The recovered collinear-leaf lifting lemma then gives (4). Nothing in this proof assumes real kernels or fixed phases. ∎

In dimension three, the only conditional direction excluded by the displayed criterion is a **full-rank indefinite** matrix. Thus Theorem A converts a large lower-dimensional union of independently proved chord classes into an explicit four-site class.

### Quantitative semidefinite version
If both \(\Delta_1\) and \(\Delta_0\) are semidefinite, X015's quantitative three-site gap survives the lift. Writing
\[
\Gamma_4(t)=H(P_{K_t})-(1-t)H(P_{K_0})-tH(P_{K_1}),
\qquad K_t=(1-t)K_0+tK_1,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_4(t)
\ge
\frac67 t(1-t)
\left[
 a\sum_{i=1}^3(\Delta_1)_{ii}^2
 +(1-a)\sum_{i=1}^3(\Delta_0)_{ii}^2
\right].
}
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed the scalar-strength payment from (2) is nonnegative, while the two lower conditional Jensen gaps are weighted by \(a\) and \(1-a\).

### Boundary endpoints
For \(0<a<1\), conditional kernels (1) exist as closed positive contractions even when an endpoint is spectrally singular. X015 and Agent 019 both include their relevant boundary chords by regularization, and the rank-one-strength monotonicity in the lifting proof extends by continuity of the finite atom law and of \(-p\log p\) at \(p=0\). Hence (4) is a closed-domain theorem. If \(a\in\{0,1\}\), positivity forces the coupling column to vanish and the distinguished site is deterministic; the problem reduces directly to the three-site conditional chord.

## 4. Exact four-site complex example

The theorem is nonvacuous for phase-changing, non-comparable four-site endpoints.
Take
\[
a=\frac12,
\qquad
v=\frac1{10}(1,i,1+i)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
z_0=1,\quad z_1=i.
\tag{6}
\]
Then \(|z_0|=|z_1|\), hence \(s_0=s_1=4\) and \(\Delta s=0\).

Let
\[
B_0=\begin{pmatrix}
\frac9{20}&\frac1{100}+\frac{i}{200}&-\frac1{125}+\frac{i}{250}\\
\frac1{100}-\frac{i}{200}&\frac9{20}&\frac1{150}-\frac{i}{120}\\
-\frac1{125}-\frac{i}{250}&\frac1{150}+\frac{i}{120}&\frac9{20}
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
r=(1,1,i)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
R=\frac1{200}rr^*\succeq0,
\qquad
B_1=B_0+R.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
K_j=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&(z_jv)^*\\
z_jv&B_j
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(\Delta s=0\), both conditional differences are exactly
\[
\Delta_1=\Delta_0=R\succeq0.
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore Theorem A and the quantitative bound (5) apply.

For completeness, strict physical feasibility is certified without floating-point eigenvalues. Here
\[
2P=2vv^*,
\qquad
F_{1,j}=B_j-2P,
\qquad
F_{0,j}=B_j+2P.
\]
Using \(|x+iy|\le|x|+|y|\), strict diagonal dominance gives the following exact lower margins for the Hermitian matrices \(F_{1,0},F_{1,1},I-F_{0,0},I-F_{0,1}\), row by row:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
F_{1,0}&343/1000&53/150&949/3000\\
F_{1,1}&87/250&209/600&241/750\\
I-F_{0,0}&477/1000&7/15&1331/3000\\
I-F_{0,1}&231/500&277/600&329/750
\end{array}
\tag{11}
\]
All are positive. Schur complementation then gives \(0<K_j<I_4\).

The endpoint difference is not semidefinite; numerically its nonzero eigenvalues are approximately
\[
-0.28043909,\quad 0.00999375,\quad 0.28544534,
\]
with one exact zero mode up to numerical roundoff. Thus the original four-site direction is rank three and indefinite even though both three-site conditional differences are rank-one PSD.

From (5), because every diagonal entry of \(R\) is \(1/200\),
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_4(t)\ge\frac9{140000}t(1-t).
}
\tag{12}
\]
At the midpoint this is \(9/560000\). A direct finite-law diagnostic gives an actual midpoint gap approximately
\[
0.00369089755667,
\]
well above the theorem's certified lower bound. This decimal is diagnostic only; (12) is the proof.

The coupling phase changes by \(\pi/2\): \(z_0=1\), \(z_1=i\). The scalar-strength defect in the lifting proof is explicitly
\[
\bar s_t-s_t=8t(1-t),
\tag{13}
\]
so the proof is using a genuinely nonlinear conditional correction rather than pretending that conditioning commutes with affine kernel interpolation.

## 5. General noncollinear obstruction identity

I also pushed directly on the next missing step: removing the collinearity hypothesis. The following finite-chord identity is exact and isolates the required compensation.

For a common marginal \(a\), but arbitrary coupling columns \(w_0,w_1\), set
\[
c=a(1-a),\quad
U_j=\frac{w_jw_j^*}{c},\quad
w_t=(1-t)w_0+tw_1,
\]
\[
U_t=\frac{w_tw_t^*}{c},\qquad
\bar U_t=(1-t)U_0+tU_1,\qquad d=w_1-w_0.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\bar U_t-U_t=\frac{t(1-t)}c dd^*\succeq0.
}
\tag{14}
\]
For any lower-dimensional entropy function define
\[
\Phi_a(B,U)=aH(B-(1-a)U)+(1-a)H(B+aU).
\tag{15}
\]
Whenever the two lower endpoint chords are in a concavity class, joint concavity of the two terms gives
\[
J_{\rm aff}(t)=
\Phi_a(B_t,\bar U_t)
-(1-t)\Phi_a(B_0,U_0)-t\Phi_a(B_1,U_1)\ge0.
\tag{16}
\]
Parametrize
\[
U_{t,s}=U_t+s(\bar U_t-U_t),\qquad0\le s\le1,
\]
and let
\[
G_{t,s}=\nabla H(B_t-(1-a)U_{t,s})
-\nabla H(B_t+aU_{t,s}).
\tag{17}
\]
Direct differentiation of (15) gives
\[
\frac{d}{ds}\Phi_a(B_t,U_{t,s})
=-t(1-t)d^*G_{t,s}d.
\]
Therefore the **exact original Jensen gap** is
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma(t)=J_{\rm aff}(t)
+t(1-t)\int_0^1d^*G_{t,s}d\,ds.
}
\tag{18}
\]
This is the finite-chord analogue of the local conditional-gradient remainder in C057.

Equation (18) explains precisely why the collinear argument works and why the naive matrix generalization fails. In the collinear case all \(U_{t,s}\) lie on one fixed rank-one ray; one-dimensional rank-one entropy concavity makes the second term nonnegative. For genuinely noncollinear couplings, no universal Loewner sign of \(G_{t,s}\) is available. C057 already contains an exact rational strict three-site example with a negative eigenvalue of its conditional gradient secant and a positive collapsed remainder, so I do **not** claim that obstruction as new here. The new useful object is the finite-gap identity (18): a future proof must show that the affine conditional Jensen payment (16) dominates the possible negative integral, rather than dropping it.
