## 2. New theorem

Consider two three-site Hermitian positive contractions supported on the same path `1-2-3`,
\[
Q_j=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&x_j&0\\
\bar x_j&b_j&y_j\\
0&\bar y_j&c_j
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\preceq Q_j\preceq I_3,\qquad j=0,1,
\tag{2.1}
\]
with a **common leaf diagonal**
\[
(Q_0)_{11}=(Q_1)_{11}=a.
\tag{2.2}
\]
The two path edges may have arbitrary complex phases and may change phase independently; `b_j,c_j` are unrestricted subject only to positivity/contraction.

### Theorem 2.1 (fixed leaf marginal, full complex path chord)
For every `0<=t<=1`, with `Q_t=(1-t)Q_0+tQ_1`,
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{Q_t})\ge (1-t)H(P_{Q_0})+tH(P_{Q_1}).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
The result includes spectral-boundary endpoints. By permuting sites, the same statement holds when either leaf marginal is fixed.

Combined with the previous checkpoint's fixed-middle-marginal theorem, this yields the cumulative corollary:

### Corollary 2.2
For any two three-site positive contractions supported on the same path, if **any one of the three diagonal entries agrees at the two endpoints**, Lyons's complete-subset entropy inequality holds along the entire affine chord, with arbitrary complex path-edge phases and boundary endpoints.

The proof of the new leaf theorem is short but uses a useful composition principle that does not require the middle marginal to be fixed.

## 3. Exact leaf-conditioning normal form

First assume strictness and `0<a<1`. Write a generic path kernel as
\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&x&0\\
\bar x&b&y\\
0&\bar y&c
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<K<I_3.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Condition on the occupation of leaf `1`. The exact determinantal Schur-complement kernels on sites `{2,3}` are
\[
F_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
b-|x|^2/a&y\\
\bar y&c
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
b+|x|^2/(1-a)&y\\
\bar y&c
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
The entropy chain rule is therefore
\[
\boxed{H_3(K)=h(a)+aH_2(F_1)+(1-a)H_2(F_0),}
\tag{3.3}
\]
where `h` is binary entropy and `H_2` is the complete two-site DPP entropy.

Introduce the normalized leaf-edge strength
\[
\boxed{u=\frac{|x|^2}{a(1-a)}\ge0.}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Then
\[
F_1=M(b-(1-a)u,c,y),\qquad
F_0=M(b+au,c,y),
\tag{3.5}
\]
where
\[
M(d,c,y)=\begin{pmatrix}d&y\\\bar y&c\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For fixed `a`, define
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_a(b,c,y,u)
:=aH_2(M(b-(1-a)u,c,y))
 +(1-a)H_2(M(b+au,c,y)).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
Thus `H_3(K)=h(a)+Phi_a(b,c,y,u)`.

Let `D_a` be the set of `(b,c,y,u)` for which both matrices in (3.5) are positive contractions. Since both matrices depend **affinely** on `(b,c,Re y,Im y,u)` and the positive-contraction set is convex, `D_a` is convex.

## 4. Two structural facts about `Phi_a`

### Lemma 4.1 — joint concavity
`Phi_a` is jointly concave on `D_a` in the real variables
\[
(b,c,\Re y,\Im y,u).
\]

**Proof.** C055 proves that `H_2` is concave on the complete two-site Hermitian positive-contraction set, including arbitrary complex off-diagonal directions. Each map in (3.5) is affine in the displayed variables. Therefore each composition in (3.6) is concave; their positive weighted sum is concave. `square`

### Lemma 4.2 — monotonicity in the normalized leaf-edge strength
For fixed `(b,c,y)`, `Phi_a(b,c,y,u)` is nonincreasing in `u` on every physical interval in `D_a`.

**Proof.** On the strict domain define
\[
f(d):=H_2(M(d,c,y)).
\]
By two-site entropy concavity, `f` is concave, so `f'` is nonincreasing. Put
\[
d_1=b-(1-a)u,\qquad d_0=b+au,
\]
so `d_0-d_1=u>=0`. Differentiating (3.6),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_u\Phi_a
&=-a(1-a)f'(d_1)+a(1-a)f'(d_0)\\
&=a(1-a)\{f'(d_0)-f'(d_1)\}\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Boundary monotonicity follows by continuity from strict kernels. `square`

No spectral entropy or basis rotation is used. `H_2` is the measured two-site subset entropy in the original coordinates.

## 5. Affine complex chords: convex edge strength plus a decreasing concave outer function

Let
\[
K_t=(1-t)Q_0+tQ_1.
\]
Because the common first diagonal is `a`, the quantities `b_t,c_t,y_t` are affine in `t`, while
\[
x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1.
\]
Define
\[
u_j=\frac{|x_j|^2}{a(1-a)},\qquad
u_t=\frac{|x_t|^2}{a(1-a)},
\]
and the affine endpoint interpolation
\[
\bar u_t=(1-t)u_0+tu_1.
\]
The parallelogram identity gives the exact strength defect
\[
\boxed{
\bar u_t-u_t
=\frac{t(1-t)|x_1-x_0|^2}{a(1-a)}\ge0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The endpoint tuples belong to `D_a`; by convexity of `D_a`,
\[
(b_t,c_t,y_t,\bar u_t)\in D_a.
\]
The actual tuple `(b_t,c_t,y_t,u_t)` also belongs to `D_a` because it is generated by the genuine contraction `K_t`. Convexity of `D_a` therefore keeps the whole `u`-segment between them physical.

Apply Lemma 4.2 first and Lemma 4.1 second:
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_3(K_t)-h(a)
&=\Phi_a(b_t,c_t,y_t,u_t)\\
&\ge \Phi_a(b_t,c_t,y_t,\bar u_t)\\
&\ge (1-t)\Phi_a(b_0,c_0,y_0,u_0)
+t\Phi_a(b_1,c_1,y_1,u_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Adding the common constant `h(a)` proves (2.3) for strict kernels.

This is the key mechanism: the only nonlinear quantity produced by conditioning is `|x|^2`; along an affine complex edge it lies **below** the affine interpolation of its endpoint strengths, and the conditional entropy functional is decreasing in precisely that strength coordinate.

## 6. Boundary limits

Assume first `0<a<1` but allow either endpoint to lie on the spectral boundary. Set
\[
D_a=\operatorname{diag}(a,1/2,1/2)
\]
and regularize both endpoints by
\[
Q_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-\varepsilon)Q_j+\varepsilon D_a,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1.
\tag{6.1}
\]
This preserves the path support and the exact common leaf marginal `a`; because `D_a` and `I-D_a` are positive definite, every `Q_j^(epsilon)` is a strict contraction. Apply the strict theorem and let `epsilon downarrow0`. Every finite DPP atom is polynomial in the kernel entries, and `-p log p` is continuous at `p=0`, so the entropy inequality passes to the limit.

If `a=0`, positivity of `Q_j` forces the incident edge `x_j=0`. If `a=1`, positivity of `I-Q_j` forces `x_j=0`. In either case site `1` is deterministic and the claim reduces exactly to the already-proved two-site entropy concavity of the `{2,3}` kernel. This completes the closed-domain proof.

## 7. Local consequence and the sharpened remaining path obstruction

At every strict complex path base, Theorem 2.1 implies nonpositive entropy curvature for every **path-tangent** Hermitian direction with `A_11=0`; by symmetry the same holds with `A_33=0`. The preceding checkpoint already supplies the corresponding fixed-center statement for `A_22=0` (in fact, it proved the larger ambient hyperplane there).

Thus, on the seven-real-dimensional tangent space of complex path kernels (`A_13=0`), a positive-curvature witness, if one exists, must change **all three diagonal marginals simultaneously**.

There is an exact finite-dimensional reduction. Split a path-tangent direction into its four real edge coordinates `e` and three real diagonal coordinates `d`. At a connected strict path write the Hessian quadratic form as
\[
Q(e,d)=e^TBe+2e^TCd+d^TDd.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The edge block satisfies `B<0` by the previous checkpoint's strict `A_22=0` theorem. Eliminating the edge coordinates gives the diagonal Schur matrix
\[
\boxed{S=D-C^TB^{-1}C,}
\tag{7.2}
\]
with
\[
\sup_e Q(e,d)=d^TSd.
\]
The three fixed-marginal chord theorems imply that every two-by-two principal submatrix of `S` is negative semidefinite: set one component of `d` to zero and optimize over `e`. Hence all one-by-one signs and all order-two principal-minor signs required for `S<=0` are already settled. For a real symmetric `3x3` matrix with those signs, the **only remaining principal-minor condition** is
\[
\boxed{\det S\le0.}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Therefore unrestricted local path concavity is reduced to one scalar determinant inequality. A failure of (7.3) would directly yield an optimized path-tangent Hessian counterexample; a proof of (7.3) would settle the full local path Hessian.

This reduction is not a proof that (7.3) has the desired sign.

This is **false globally**: near a two-site spectral boundary the Hessian in `(a,b,s)` can have a positive eigenvalue. The new three fixed-marginal theorems already certify its three order-two principal submatrices. The remaining task is therefore to derive `det S` as an exact combination of posterior success/failure interaction terms and the two-site Fisher forms.
