## 2. Setup: a strict three-site path and its middle-site conditionals

Start with a real strict connected path
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
a&x&0\\
x&b&y\\
0&y&c
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<K<I_3,\qquad xy\ne0.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Condition on the middle occupation `X_2`. Reordering the middle coordinate first, write
\[
B=\operatorname{diag}(a,c),\qquad z=\binom{x}{y}.
\]
The two conditional endpoint kernels are the exact Schur complements
\[
F_1=B-\frac{zz^{\mathsf T}}b,
\qquad
F_0=B+\frac{zz^{\mathsf T}}{1-b}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Both are strict contractions. Define the C057 gradient secant
\[
\boxed{G:=\nabla H_2(F_1)-\nabla H_2(F_0),}
\tag{2.3}
\]
where `H_2` is the measured two-site DPP Shannon entropy in the original endpoint coordinates.

The main new lemma is
\[
\boxed{G\succ0.}
\tag{2.4}
\]
This is special to the path geometry. C057 explicitly shows that a corresponding secant need not have a universal Loewner sign for arbitrary conditioning pairs.

## 3. Posterior normal form

Since `K_13=0`, the endpoint occupations are independent marginally:
\[
P(X_1=i,X_3=j)=a^i(1-a)^{1-i}c^j(1-c)^{1-j}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Put
\[
u=\frac{x^2}{a(1-a)},\qquad v=\frac{y^2}{c(1-c)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Connectedness gives `u,v>0`. Let
\[
r_{ij}=P(X_2=1\mid X_1=i,X_3=j).
\]
Directly from the DPP inclusion minors (or Bayes applied to the eight atom determinants),
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
r_{00}&=b+au+cv,\\
r_{10}&=b-(1-a)u+cv=r_{00}-u,\\
r_{01}&=b+au-(1-c)v=r_{00}-v,\\
r_{11}&=b-(1-a)u-(1-c)v=r_{00}-u-v.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Every endpoint atom has positive marginal mass and every full three-site atom is positive, so
\[
0<r_{ij}<1\quad\text{for all }i,j.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Two strict margins that will drive the proof are therefore
\[
\boxed{(1-a)u+(1-c)v<b}
\tag{3.5}
\]
from `r_11>0`, and
\[
\boxed{au+cv<1-b}
\tag{3.6}
\]
from `r_00<1`.

There are two positive posterior interaction parameters. For the success table (`X_2=1`) set
\[
\lambda_+=\log\frac{r_{10}r_{01}}{r_{00}r_{11}}.
\tag{3.7}
\]
The exact identity
\[
r_{10}r_{01}-r_{00}r_{11}=uv>0
\tag{3.8}
\]
shows `lambda_+>0`. For the failure table set
\[
\lambda_-=\log\frac{(1-r_{10})(1-r_{01})}
{(1-r_{00})(1-r_{11})}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
Again
\[
(1-r_{10})(1-r_{01})-(1-r_{00})(1-r_{11})=uv>0,
\tag{3.10}
\]
so `lambda_->0`.

## 4. Exact decomposition of the gradient secant

For a strict two-site real kernel
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}U&Z\\Z&V\end{pmatrix}
\]
let its atom probabilities be `p_00,p_10,p_01,p_11`, and define
\[
c_1=\log\frac{p_{00}}{p_{10}},\qquad
c_3=\log\frac{p_{00}}{p_{01}},\qquad
\lambda=\log\frac{p_{10}p_{01}}{p_{00}p_{11}}\ge0.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Freezing `-log p` and using
`E f=c_0+c_1U+c_3V+lambda det(F)` gives the exact measured-entropy gradient
\[
\boxed{
\nabla H_2(F)=
\begin{pmatrix}
c_1+\lambda V&-\lambda Z\\
-\lambda Z&c_3+\lambda U
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the success posterior define
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_0&=\log(r_{00}/r_{10}),&s_1&=\log(r_{01}/r_{11}),\\
t_0&=\log(r_{00}/r_{01}),&t_1&=\log(r_{10}/r_{11}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]
All four are positive and
\[
s_1-s_0=t_1-t_0=\lambda_+.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Set
\[
S_1=(1-c)s_0+cs_1=s_0+c\lambda_+>c\lambda_+,
\tag{4.5}
\]
\[
S_3=(1-a)t_0+at_1=t_0+a\lambda_+>a\lambda_+.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For the failure posterior define
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0&=\log\frac{1-r_{10}}{1-r_{00}},&
f_1&=\log\frac{1-r_{11}}{1-r_{01}},\\
g_0&=\log\frac{1-r_{01}}{1-r_{00}},&
g_1&=\log\frac{1-r_{11}}{1-r_{10}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]
Again all are positive, while
\[
f_0-f_1=g_0-g_1=\lambda_-.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Set
\[
B_1=(1-c)f_0+cf_1=f_1+(1-c)\lambda_->(1-c)\lambda_-,
\tag{4.9}
\]
\[
B_3=(1-a)g_0+ag_1=g_1+(1-a)\lambda_->(1-a)\lambda_-.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Substituting the conditional atom probabilities into (4.2) and subtracting gives the exact split
\[
\boxed{G=G_++G_-}
\tag{4.11}
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
G_+=
\begin{pmatrix}
S_1-\lambda_+y^2/b&\lambda_+xy/b\\
\lambda_+xy/b&S_3-\lambda_+x^2/b
\end{pmatrix}}
\tag{4.12}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
G_-=
\begin{pmatrix}
B_1-\lambda_-y^2/(1-b)&\lambda_-xy/(1-b)\\
\lambda_-xy/(1-b)&B_3-\lambda_-x^2/(1-b)
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{4.13}
\]
The two matrices correspond respectively to the success and failure posterior log-odds contributions. No entropy or acceleration term is omitted.

## 5. New lemma: both posterior pieces are positive definite

### 5.1 Success piece

Use `x^2=a(1-a)u`, `y^2=c(1-c)v`. From (3.5), individually `(1-c)v<b` and `(1-a)u<b`. Equations (4.5)-(4.6) therefore give
\[
S_1>c\lambda_+>\frac{\lambda_+y^2}{b},
\qquad
S_3>a\lambda_+>\frac{\lambda_+x^2}{b}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Thus the diagonal entries of `G_+` are positive.

Its determinant has an exact cancellation:
\[
\det G_+
=S_1S_3-\frac{\lambda_+}{b}(S_1x^2+S_3y^2).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Now
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\lambda_+}{b}
\left(\frac{x^2}{S_3}+\frac{y^2}{S_1}\right)
&<\frac1b\bigl((1-a)u+(1-c)v\bigr)\\
&<1
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]
by (4.5)-(4.6) and (3.5). Multiplying (5.3) by `S_1S_3` proves
\[
\boxed{G_+\succ0.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### 5.2 Failure piece

From (3.6), individually `cv<1-b` and `au<1-b`. Equations (4.9)-(4.10) give
\[
B_1>(1-c)\lambda_->\frac{\lambda_-y^2}{1-b},
\qquad
B_3>(1-a)\lambda_->\frac{\lambda_-x^2}{1-b}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Again the diagonal entries are positive, and
\[
\det G_-
=B_1B_3-\frac{\lambda_-}{1-b}(B_1x^2+B_3y^2).
\tag{5.6}
\]
Furthermore
\[
\frac{\lambda_-}{1-b}
\left(\frac{x^2}{B_3}+\frac{y^2}{B_1}\right)
<\frac{au+cv}{1-b}<1.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{G_-\succ0.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Combining (4.11), (5.4), and (5.8) proves the promised special secant theorem:

### Theorem 5.1 (diagonal-straddling rank-one gradient secant)
For every connected strict real three-site path (2.1),
\[
\boxed{\nabla H_2(F_1)-\nabla H_2(F_0)\succ0.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

The proof uses only strict path feasibility and exact two-by-two posterior identities. It does not assume the full three-site conjecture.

## 6. Eight-dimensional ambient Hessian theorem

Let `A=A*` be an arbitrary **complex Hermitian** direction with
\[
\boxed{A_{22}=0.}
\tag{6.1}
\]
After putting the middle site first, write
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&w^*\\w&C\end{pmatrix},
\tag{6.2}
\]
where `w in C^2` and `C=C*` is an arbitrary two-by-two Hermitian matrix.

C057's exact conditioning identity, with pivot velocity `alpha=0`, is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D^2H_3(K)[A,A]
={}&bD^2H_2(F_1)[E_1,E_1]
 +(1-b)D^2H_2(F_0)[E_0,E_0]\\
&-2w^*Gw,
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6.3}
\]
where
\[
E_1=C-\frac{wz^{\mathsf T}+z w^*}{b},
\qquad
E_0=C+\frac{wz^{\mathsf T}+z w^*}{1-b}.
\tag{6.4}
\]
This identity retains both conditional Schur accelerations exactly.

C055 gives nonpositive two-site Hessians for every complex Hermitian `E_i`. Theorem 5.1 gives `G>0`. Therefore (6.3) is strictly negative whenever `w != 0`. If `w=0` but `A != 0`, then `C != 0` and `E_1=E_0=C`. Both conditional kernels have nonzero endpoint coherence (`-(xy)/b` and `xy/(1-b)`), so C055's coercive bound makes at least (indeed both) conditional Hessian terms strictly negative. Thus:

### Theorem 6.1
At every connected strict real three-site path base,
\[
\boxed{A\ne0,\ A_{22}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D^2H(P_K)[A,A]<0}
\tag{6.5}
\]
for **every complex Hermitian direction** `A`.

This is an eight-real-dimensional negative-definite hyperplane inside the full nine-real-dimensional Hermitian space. It strictly contains the purely imaginary sector proved in the previous run.

### Complex path bases
Any complex three-site path with `K_13=0` is diagonal-unitary gauge equivalent to a real path, because its support graph is a tree. Principal determinants, all DPP atoms, and entropy are invariant under a fixed diagonal unitary. The tangent map `A -> UAU*` preserves `A_22=0`. Hence Theorem 6.1 immediately extends to every connected strict **complex** path base:
\[
\boxed{K_{13}=0,\ A_{22}=0,\ A\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H''_K(A,A)<0.}
\tag{6.6}
\]
No common phase convention between different bases is required.

At a strict path where one edge vanishes, the nonpositive version follows by continuity from connected paths (all atom probabilities remain positive in the strict contraction domain). Thus every strict complex path base has
\[
D^2H(P_K)[A,A]\le0\quad\text{for all }A=A^*\text{ with }A_{22}=0.
\tag{6.7}
\]

## 7. New global chord family, including complex kernels and boundary limits

Let `Q_0,Q_1` be arbitrary three-site Hermitian positive contractions satisfying
\[
(Q_0)_{13}=(Q_1)_{13}=0,
\qquad
(Q_0)_{22}=(Q_1)_{22}=b.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The two path edges may be complex and may have different phases at the two endpoints. Put
\[
Q_t=(1-t)Q_0+tQ_1.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Every `Q_t` is again a path kernel with the same middle diagonal `b`, and the constant affine velocity
\[
A=Q_1-Q_0
\]
satisfies `A_22=0`.

If the endpoints are strict, convexity of the strict positive-contraction domain makes the whole open chord strict. Equation (6.7) gives
\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{Q_t})\le0.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{Q_t})\ge(1-t)H(P_{Q_0})+tH(P_{Q_1})
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
for all `0<=t<=1`.

For closed-domain endpoints with `0<b<1`, choose
\[
D_b=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,b,1/2)
\]
and regularize both endpoints by
\[
Q_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-\varepsilon)Q_j+\varepsilon D_b.
\tag{7.5}
\]
They remain in the same path/fixed-middle-diagonal affine class and become strict for `epsilon>0`. Apply (7.4) and let `epsilon downarrow 0`. Every finite DPP atom is a polynomial in the kernel entries and `-p log p` is continuous at `p=0`, so the Shannon entropy converges. If `b=0` or `b=1`, positive semidefiniteness of `Q_j` and `I-Q_j` forces both incident edges to vanish; with `Q_13=0` the law is diagonal and the result reduces to binary entropy concavity. Thus (7.4) holds for all closed positive-contraction endpoints satisfying (7.1).

### Theorem 7.1 (fixed-center-marginal path chord)
For every pair of three-site Hermitian positive contractions whose support is contained in the path `1-2-3` and whose middle diagonal entries agree, Lyons's Shannon-entropy inequality holds along their entire affine chord, including arbitrary complex path-edge phases and spectral-boundary endpoints.

The same statement holds after permuting the sites.

This is a genuine global chord theorem, not merely a local Hessian statement.

## 8. Exact remaining obstruction at a path base

The full path Hessian is not yet proved nonpositive. Theorem 6.1 makes its restriction to the codimension-one real hyperplane
\[
\mathcal V=\{A=A^*:A_{22}=0\}
\]
negative definite at every connected strict path. Therefore, in the block decomposition `Herm(3)=V direct-sum R E_22`, the full Hessian has at most one potentially nonnegative eigenvalue. Equivalently, if `B` is the negative-definite Hessian matrix on `V`, `ell(A)=D^2H[E_22,A]`, and `d=D^2H[E_22,E_22]`, then full nonpositivity is equivalent to the **single scalar Schur condition**
\[
\boxed{d-\ell^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}\ell\le0.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The normal direction itself is already strictly negative. Along `K+tE_22`, every atom determinant is affine in `t` because only one diagonal matrix entry varies. Hence
\[
D^2H(P_K)[E_{22},E_{22}]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S[E_{22}])^2}{p_S}<0.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Thus a path counterexample, if one exists, cannot be a pure middle-diagonal direction; it would have to arise from cross-coupling in the scalar Schur complement (8.1).

This reduces the unresolved connected strict complex-path Hessian problem from nine real variables to one scalar after an explicitly identified negative eight-dimensional block.

## 11. Failed/avoided route and next attack

A generic use of C057's sufficient induction theorem would require a global Loewner sign and a quantitative `eta` condition for arbitrary conditioning pairs. C057 itself records that the secant sign is not universal, so importing `G>=0` without exploiting path structure would be invalid. The successful route instead uses the four exact posterior probabilities (3.3): the two strict Schur-feasibility margins (3.5)-(3.6) separately pay for the success and failure secant determinants.

The highest-value next attack is now sharply one-dimensional: derive the cross functional `ell` in (8.1) in the same posterior coordinates and prove
\[
-\ell^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}\ell\le -d.
\]
Because `B` now has a constructive C057 decomposition into two two-site Fisher/Hessian forms plus the positive matrix `G`, there is a realistic route to a posterior conditional-variance proof of this final scalar. If that scalar fails, the exact optimizer yields a certified real-symmetric path counterexample direction; failure of an intermediate sufficient estimate will not be mislabeled as a refutation.
