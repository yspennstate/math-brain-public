# 3. New analytic lemma: gradient sign above the half kernel in a rank-one direction

Let
\[
C_+(z)=\frac12I_2+2zz^*,
\qquad z\in\mathbb C^2,\qquad \|z\|^2<\frac14.
\tag{3.1}
\]

## Lemma 3.1

\[
\boxed{\nabla H_2(C_+(z))\preceq0.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

By complementation,
\[
\boxed{\nabla H_2\!\left(\frac12I_2-2zz^*\right)\succeq0.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

Diagonal coordinate phases preserve the measured determinantal law and conjugate the gradient covariantly, so gauge `z_1,z_2` to be nonnegative real. Define
\[
\alpha=4z_1^2,\qquad
\beta=4z_2^2,\qquad
t=\alpha+\beta<1,\qquad
d=\alpha-\beta.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then
\[
C_+=
\begin{pmatrix}
(1+\alpha)/2&\sqrt{\alpha\beta}/2\\
\sqrt{\alpha\beta}/2&(1+\beta)/2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Its complete two-site DPP atom vector is exceptionally simple:
\[
p_{00}=\frac{1-t}4,\qquad
p_{10}=\frac{1+d}4,\qquad
p_{01}=\frac{1-d}4,\qquad
p_{11}=\frac{1+t}4.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Use worker 036's exact two-site gradient formula
\[
\nabla H_2(C)=
\begin{pmatrix}
(1-y)\log(p_{00}/p_{10})+y\log(p_{01}/p_{11})&-Lr\\
-Lr&(1-x)\log(p_{00}/p_{01})+x\log(p_{10}/p_{11})
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{3.7}
\]
where `r=C_12` after gauge and
\[
L=\log\frac{p_{10}p_{01}}{p_{00}p_{11}}.
\]

Set
\[
U=\log\frac{1+t}{1-t},\qquad
V=\log\frac{1+d}{1-d},\qquad
L=\log\frac{1-d^2}{1-t^2}\ge0.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Substitution of (3.6) into (3.7) gives
\[
\boxed{
-2\nabla H_2(C_+)=
N:=
\begin{pmatrix}
U+V-\beta L&\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\,L\\
\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\,L&U-V-\alpha L
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

It remains to show `N` is positive semidefinite.

Its trace is
\[
\operatorname{tr}N=2U-tL.
\tag{3.10}
\]
Its determinant expands with an exact cancellation of the `alpha beta L^2` terms:
\[
\boxed{
\det N
=
U^2-V^2-L(tU+dV).
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Now put
\[
A=\operatorname{artanh}t,\qquad
B=\operatorname{artanh}|d|,
\qquad 0\le B\le A,
\tag{3.12}
\]
and
\[
\Delta=\log\cosh A-\log\cosh B.
\]
Then
\[
U=2A,\qquad |V|=2B,\qquad
L=2\Delta,\qquad
t=\tanh A,\qquad |d|=\tanh B.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Since
\[
\Delta=\int_B^A\tanh s\,ds
\le(A-B)\tanh A,
\tag{3.14}
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{\det N}4
=
A^2-B^2-\Delta\,[A\tanh A+B\tanh B]
\]
\[
\ge
(A-B)
\left[
A+B-\tanh A(A\tanh A+B\tanh B)
\right]
\]
\[
=
\boxed{
(A-B)
\left[
A\,\operatorname{sech}^2A
+B(1-\tanh A\tanh B)
\right]\ge0.
}
\tag{3.15}
\]

Also `Delta <= A-B <= A` and `t<1`, so from (3.10)
\[
\operatorname{tr}N
=
4A-2t\Delta
\ge4A-2A
=2A\ge0.
\tag{3.16}
\]
For a real symmetric `2x2` matrix, nonnegative determinant and nonnegative trace imply nonnegative eigenvalues. Hence `N >= 0`, proving (3.2).

Finally measured DPP entropy is invariant under complementation:
`H_2(I-C)=H_2(C)`. Differentiating gives
\[
\nabla H_2(I-C)=-\nabla H_2(C),
\]
which proves (3.3). `square`

The symbolic checker archived with this run verifies the atom identities, the matrix determinant cancellation (3.11), the exact algebra behind the lower bound (3.15), and
`d/dx log(cosh x)=tanh x`. The inequality step (3.14) is the elementary monotonicity of `tanh`, written on the page rather than delegated to numerics.

# 4. Main restricted theorem: all center-fixed Hermitian directions at every balanced three-site star

Consider the strict balanced star
\[
K(z)=
\frac12I_3+
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\overline z_1&\overline z_2\\
z_1&0&0\\
z_2&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2<\frac14.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The exact strict-contraction condition follows from the centered eigenvalues
`0, plus/minus ||z||`.

C073 already proves the entropy-Hessian sign at such balanced stars for every star-supported Hermitian direction plus the diagonal sector, but explicitly leaves **leaf-to-leaf directions** outside its theorem. The statement below adds those missing leaf-to-leaf perturbations whenever the center diagonal is held fixed.

## Theorem 4.1 — center-fixed balanced-star Hessian sign

For every complex Hermitian direction
\[
D=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\overline w_1&\overline w_2\\
w_1&a&\overline u\\
w_2&u&b
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{4.2}
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{D^2H_3(K(z))[D,D]\le0.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus the leaf-to-leaf entry `u` may vary arbitrarily and jointly with both incident center edges and both leaf diagonals. No reality assumption is imposed.

### Proof

Condition on the center site, i.e. use worker 036's identity (2.1) with the leaves as the two-site block. Here
\[
c=a_{\rm weight}=\frac12,\qquad
A=\frac12I_2,\qquad B=z.
\]
Therefore
\[
C_0=\frac12I_2+2zz^*,\qquad
C_1=\frac12I_2-2zz^*=I_2-C_0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Because the direction has center diagonal zero, it has exactly the form required in worker 036's fixed-`c` identity. Write its arbitrary leaf block as `E=E^*` and its arbitrary center-to-leaf column as `V`. Then
\[
D^2H_3(K)[D,D]
=
\frac12D^2H_2(C_0)[E+2W,E+2W]
+\frac12D^2H_2(C_1)[E-2W,E-2W]
+2V^*GV,
\tag{4.5}
\]
where
\[
W=Vz^*+zV^*,
\qquad
G=\nabla H_2(C_0)-\nabla H_2(C_1).
\]

The complete two-site theorem gives
\[
D^2H_2(C_j)[X,X]\le0
\quad(j=0,1)
\tag{4.6}
\]
for every complex Hermitian `X`.

By complementation and Lemma 3.1,
\[
\nabla H_2(C_1)
=
-\nabla H_2(C_0),
\]
hence
\[
G=2\nabla H_2(C_0)\preceq0.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
2V^*GV\le0.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Every term on the right of (4.5) is nonpositive, proving (4.3). `square`
