## 2. New theorem: all purely imaginary Hermitian directions at every strict real three-site path

Let
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
a&x&0\\
x&b&y\\
0&y&c
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<K<I_3,
\tag{2.1}
\]
with real \(a,b,c,x,y\). Assume first that the path is connected, so \(xy\ne0\). (Disconnected cases reduce to one- or two-site blocks and are discussed below.)

Let \(S\) be an arbitrary real skew-symmetric \(3\times3\) matrix. Orient the triangle as \(1\to2\to3\to1\) and write
\[
\alpha=S_{12},\qquad \beta=S_{23},\qquad \gamma=S_{31}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Thus \(A=iS\) is an arbitrary purely imaginary Hermitian direction, including an arbitrary missing-chord entry \(A_{13}\).

### Theorem 2.1
For every nonzero real skew-symmetric \(S\),
\[
\boxed{D^2H(P_K)[iS,iS]<0.}
\tag{2.3}
\]
More precisely, define the two conditional log-odds interactions for each pair \(ij\), with complementary site \(k\), by
\[
\lambda_{ij}^{(s)}
:=\log\frac{p(X_i=0,X_j=1,X_k=s)\,p(X_i=1,X_j=0,X_k=s)}
{p(X_i=0,X_j=0,X_k=s)\,p(X_i=1,X_j=1,X_k=s)},
\quad s\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Every \(\lambda_{ij}^{(s)}\ge0\) by the two-site conditional DPP determinant identity. Put
\[
W_{12}=(1-c)\lambda_{12}^{(0)}+c\lambda_{12}^{(1)},
\tag{2.5}
\]
\[
W_{23}=(1-a)\lambda_{23}^{(0)}+a\lambda_{23}^{(1)},
\tag{2.6}
\]
\[
W_{13}=(1-b)\lambda_{13}^{(0)}+b\lambda_{13}^{(1)}.
\tag{2.7}
\]
Let \(\tau\) be the degree-three Möbius coefficient of the frozen function \(-\log p_X\):
\[
\tau=\log\frac{p_{000}p_{110}p_{101}p_{011}}
{p_{100}p_{010}p_{001}p_{111}}.
\tag{2.8}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D^2H(P_K)[iS,iS]
={}&-2W_{12}\alpha^2-2W_{23}\beta^2-2W_{13}\gamma^2\\
&-4\tau\gamma(y\alpha+x\beta),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.9}
\]
where the sign of the mixed term corresponds to the orientation (2.2); reversing the cycle reverses the relevant oriented variables and leaves the quadratic form unchanged.

Moreover
\[
\boxed{
W_{13}>\tau^2\left(\frac{y^2}{W_{12}}+\frac{x^2}{W_{23}}\right)
}
\tag{2.10}
\]
for every connected strict path. Hence completing squares in (2.9) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2H(P_K)[iS,iS]
={}&-2W_{12}\left(\alpha+\frac{\tau y}{W_{12}}\gamma\right)^2
-2W_{23}\left(\beta+\frac{\tau x}{W_{23}}\gamma\right)^2\\
&-2\gamma^2\left[
W_{13}-\tau^2\left(\frac{y^2}{W_{12}}+\frac{x^2}{W_{23}}\right)
\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.11}
\]
which proves (2.3).

## 3. Proof of the exact interaction relation

The key elementary identity is that the same three-body coefficient is the change in every conditional pair interaction:
\[
\boxed{\lambda_{ij}^{(1)}-\lambda_{ij}^{(0)}=\tau.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
For example, for pair \(12\) and complementary site \(3\), direct subtraction of (2.4) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{12}^{(1)}-\lambda_{12}^{(0)}
&=\log\frac{p_{011}p_{101}}{p_{001}p_{111}}
 +\log\frac{p_{000}p_{110}}{p_{010}p_{100}}\\
&=\log\frac{p_{000}p_{110}p_{101}p_{011}}
{p_{100}p_{010}p_{001}p_{111}}=\tau.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]
The other two pairs are identical after relabeling.

Equations (2.5)--(2.7) are exactly X002's weights
\(W_{ij}=\sum_Tq_{-[i,j]}(T)\Lambda_{ij}(T)\): on a single complementary coordinate the marginal weights are simply \(1-K_{kk}\) and \(K_{kk}\).

## 4. Derivation of the three-variable imaginary Hessian

X002 proves at every strict real-symmetric base, for \(A=iS\),
\[
D^2H(P_K)[iS,iS]
=-2\sum_e a_e^2W_e
+4\sum_{C\text{ simple cycle}}(-1)^{|C|-1}R_C(S,K)\Psi_{V(C)}(K).
\tag{4.1}
\]
At the path base (2.1), the union support of \(K\) and \(S\) can contain only one cycle, the triangle \(123\). With the orientation (2.2), its cycle polynomial is
\[
R_{123}=\gamma(y\alpha+x\beta),
\tag{4.2}
\]
because the base chord \(K_{31}=0\). The full-set alternating log coefficient in X002 is
\[
\Psi_{123}=-\tau.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Substitution of (4.2)--(4.3) into (4.1) gives (2.9). Notice that the score/Fisher term vanishes for a purely imaginary perturbation at a real base because every principal determinant is even in the perturbation parameter to first order. The curvature is nevertheless nontrivial because of the second-order triangle circulation.

This is exactly the ambient term not covered by merely saying that the two path edges are bridges: an imaginary velocity on the absent \(13\) chord creates a triangle at second order and couples to the two existing bridge angular velocities.

## 5. Proof of the decisive Schur bound (2.10)

All \(\lambda_{ij}^{(s)}\) are nonnegative. There are three cases.

### Case 1: \(\tau>0\)
By (3.1),
\[
\lambda_{ij}^{(1)}=\lambda_{ij}^{(0)}+\tau.
\]
Therefore
\[
W_{12}\ge c\tau,\qquad
W_{23}\ge a\tau,\qquad
W_{13}\ge b\tau.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Strict positivity of \(K\) and Schur complementation of the \(\{1,3\}\) diagonal block give
\[
\boxed{b>\frac{x^2}{a}+\frac{y^2}{c}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau^2\left(\frac{y^2}{W_{12}}+\frac{x^2}{W_{23}}\right)
&\le \tau\left(\frac{y^2}{c}+\frac{x^2}{a}\right)\\
&<b\tau\le W_{13}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]
This proves (2.10).

### Case 2: \(\tau<0\)
Put \(\sigma=-\tau>0\). Equation (3.1) gives
\[
\lambda_{ij}^{(0)}=\lambda_{ij}^{(1)}+\sigma,
\]
and hence
\[
W_{12}\ge(1-c)\sigma,\qquad
W_{23}\ge(1-a)\sigma,\qquad
W_{13}\ge(1-b)\sigma.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Apply the same Schur complement to the strict positive matrix \(I-K\):
\[
\boxed{1-b>\frac{x^2}{1-a}+\frac{y^2}{1-c}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau^2\left(\frac{y^2}{W_{12}}+\frac{x^2}{W_{23}}\right)
&\le \sigma\left(\frac{y^2}{1-c}+\frac{x^2}{1-a}\right)\\
&<(1-b)\sigma\le W_{13}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]
Again (2.10) follows.

### Case 3: \(\tau=0\)
Then the right-hand side of (2.10) is zero. For a connected path, \(W_{13}>0\): conditioning the endpoints \(1,3\) on the middle bit produces an induced nonzero conditional edge proportional to \(xy/b\) or \(xy/(1-b)\). Likewise \(W_{12},W_{23}>0\) because the corresponding direct edges are nonzero. Thus (2.11) is strictly negative for every nonzero \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\).

This completes the proof of Theorem 2.1.

## 6. Exact block reduction of the full Hessian at a real path base

At any real-symmetric kernel, complex conjugation preserves every DPP atom and hence preserves \(H\). Write an arbitrary Hermitian direction as
\[
A=R+iS,
\quad R=R^{\mathsf T}\text{ real},
\quad S^{\mathsf T}=-S\text{ real}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Conjugation fixes \(R\) and sends \(iS\mapsto-iS\). Polarizing the Hessian therefore gives
\[
\boxed{D^2H(P_K)[R,iS]=0.}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Hence
\[
D^2H(P_K)[A,A]
=D^2H(P_K)[R,R]+D^2H(P_K)[iS,iS].
\tag{6.3}
\]
Together with Theorem 2.1:

### Corollary 6.1
At every connected strict real three-site path base, the full nine-real-dimensional entropy Hessian is nonpositive **if and only if** its restriction to the six-dimensional real-symmetric sector is nonpositive. Any positive-curvature witness, if one exists at such a base, can be chosen real symmetric.

This removes the entire three-dimensional imaginary sector, including missing-chord motion, from the unresolved real-path problem.

This reduction is stronger than X002's forest corollary for the present three-site path because that corollary controls angular directions on the existing bridge support but not an arbitrary imaginary perturbation of the absent chord.

## 7. Disconnected and boundary remarks

If \(x=0\) or \(y=0\), the base decomposes into a singleton plus a two-site block or is fully diagonal. The corresponding already-proved two-site/full-diagonal results give nonpositive imaginary curvature. The strict connected theorem above is the new case where the missing chord couples both existing edges through the triangle term.

No arbitrary boundary Hessian is inferred: Hessians can be singular when atom probabilities vanish. Valid closed-domain consequences must be obtained by approximating a specific controlled family by strict contractions and using continuity of the finite atom probabilities and \(-p\log p\). This run does not claim a global boundary theorem from the local Hessian statement.

## 8. Checks actually performed

The proof above is analytic. Floating-point work was used only as a diagnostic and formula cross-check, never as the theorem certificate.

A direct independent atom-Hessian implementation used
\[
p_S=(-1)^{|S^c|}\det(K-I_{S^c}),
\]
\[
Dp_S[A]=p_S\operatorname{tr}(Q_S^{-1}A),
\]
\[
D^2p_S[A,B]=p_S\left[
\operatorname{tr}(Q_S^{-1}A)\operatorname{tr}(Q_S^{-1}B)
-\operatorname{tr}(Q_S^{-1}AQ_S^{-1}B)
\right].
\tag{8.1}
\]
On 5,000 randomly generated strict real path bases it checked
\(\lambda_{ij}^{(1)}-\lambda_{ij}^{(0)}=\tau\) for all three pairs, with maximum floating residual \(8.88\times10^{-16}\). The minimum observed value of the completed-square bracket in (2.10) was positive (about \(6.90\times10^{-11}\)); this is only a stress diagnostic near degeneracy.

On 1,000 additional strict random path bases, the full \(3\times3\) imaginary-sector Hessian assembled independently from (8.1) agreed with (2.9) to maximum absolute entry error about \(8.51\times10^{-13}\).

Broader current-run exploratory random Hessian searches in dimensions 3--6 did not produce a positive eigenvalue; those searches are not a counterexample certificate and do not enter any proof.

## 9. Failed/limited routes and why they are not claims

1. **Bridge elimination alone is insufficient for the ambient imaginary sector.** The absent \(13\) edge is not a bridge edge of the base support, and an imaginary \(13\) velocity creates the triangle term (4.2). The new proof controls that term using the common conditional-interaction increment \(\tau\) and the two Schur complements (5.2), (5.5).

2. **Facewise real-current squares remain insufficient.** X002 already gives an exact example where conditioned imaginary circulation is invisible to all face real currents while contributing dangerous positive face acceleration. The present proof works only after coupling all three triangle terms globally.

3. **No full real-path theorem is claimed.** The remaining six-dimensional real-symmetric sector has not been signed here. X022 also records arbitrary nonbalanced three-site path curvature as unresolved. Numerics are encouraging but are not a substitute for a proof.

4. **No inference from local Hessian sign to arbitrary distant endpoints.** Theorem 2.1 is a pointwise Hessian statement at real path bases. It is not a proof that an arbitrary complex affine segment leaving that locus has concave entropy.

## 11. Next attack

The highest-value next step is now sharply reduced: **prove or refute the real-symmetric Hessian on a strict three-site path**.

A promising exact route is conditioning on the middle site. The two conditional endpoint kernels are
\[
F_1=\operatorname{diag}(a,c)-\frac1b\binom{x}{y}(x\ y),
\qquad
F_0=\operatorname{diag}(a,c)+\frac1{1-b}\binom{x}{y}(x\ y),
\tag{11.1}
\]
and their mixture is the diagonal endpoint marginal \(\operatorname{diag}(a,c)\). The C057 exact site-conditioning identity then expresses the remaining real Hessian as two exact two-site Hessians plus one gradient-secant term, with no discarded acceleration. The key unresolved matrix inequality is to exploit the special fact that (11.1) straddles a **diagonal** kernel along a rank-one line. If that special secant can be controlled sharply enough, Theorem 2.1 plus Hessian orthogonality would give the full ambient Hessian theorem for every real three-site path base.

A failure of that secant inequality should instead be converted into an exact real-symmetric Hessian witness using old-097's scalar Schur criterion, rather than interpreted as a counterexample by itself.
