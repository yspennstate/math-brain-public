## 5. Exact interval method

No floating-point logarithm is used for the theorem-critical determinant signs.

For a positive rational \(r\), write
\[
r=2^m s,\qquad 1\le s<2,
\]
and
\[
z=\frac{s-1}{s+1},\qquad 0\le z<\frac13.
\]
Then
\[
\log s
=
2\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1}+R_N,
\]
with the exact rational remainder bound
\[
0\le R_N
\le
\frac{2z^{2N+3}}{(2N+3)(1-z^2)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The same expansion at \(z=1/3\) encloses \(\log2\). The checker uses \(N=30\), with every interval endpoint represented as a Python `Fraction`. The maximum width among the four logarithm intervals is \(<10^{-25}\).

All subsequent complex matrix products, traces, determinants, covariance entries, and determinant enclosures use exact rational interval arithmetic. The checker establishes, without floating-point sign decisions,
\[
\det C_4<0,\qquad
\det C_5<0,
\]
\[
\det(C_4+\delta\Sigma_4)>10^7,
\qquad
\det(C_5+\delta\Sigma_5)<-10^9.
\tag{5.2}
\]
