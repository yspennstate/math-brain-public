## 1. New theorem: a full physical chord in one complex cycle quadrature

Let the ground set have three sites. After a fixed diagonal unitary gauge, consider
\[
K(y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
 a&r&u+i y\\
 r&b&s\\
 u-i y&s&c
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,b,c,r,s,u,y\in\mathbb R.
\tag{1.1}
\]
Assume \(K(y)\) is a Hermitian positive contraction for every \(y\) in an interval \(I\subset\mathbb R\). The entries \(a,b,c,r,s,u\) are fixed and only the imaginary quadrature \(y\) moves.

### Theorem 1.1 — cycle-quadrature chord concavity
The complete determinantal subset entropy
\[
\mathcal H(y):=H(P_{K(y)})
\]
is concave on the whole physical interval \(I\). Consequently for any \(y_0,y_1\in I\) and \(0\le t\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{K((1-t)y_0+t y_1)})
\ge
(1-t)H(P_{K(y_0)})+tH(P_{K(y_1)}).
}
\tag{1.2}
\]
This includes strict kernels, complex kernels with a genuine triangle phase, and spectral-boundary endpoints by continuity.

By permuting the three sites, the same theorem holds for any chosen edge whose orthogonal complex quadrature moves while the other matrix entries remain fixed.

This is a global chord theorem, not merely a pointwise Hessian statement and not a small-coupling result.

## 2. Exact atom law is affine in \(z=y^2\)

For \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\{0,1\}^3\), write
\[
p_x(y)=P_{K(y)}(X=x).
\]
For a finite DPP,
\[
p_x(y)=(-1)^{3-|x|}\det\bigl(K(y)-I_{x^c}\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Put \(z=y^2\). In the determinant of a generic shifted matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
 d_1&r&u+i y\\
 r&d_2&s\\
 u-i y&s&d_3
\end{pmatrix},
\]
the dependence on \(y\) is exactly
\[
-d_2y^2,
\tag{2.2}
\]
because
\[
\det= d_1d_2d_3-d_1s^2-d_2(u^2+y^2)-d_3r^2+2rsu.
\tag{2.3}
\]
There is no linear term in \(y\); the triangle term uses only the fixed real quadrature \(u\).

Let
\[
\pi_1=b,\qquad \pi_0=1-b.
\tag{2.4}
\]
A direct parity check in (2.1) gives the exact coefficient
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\partial p_{x_1x_2x_3}}{\partial z}
=-\pi_{x_2}(-1)^{x_1+x_3}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Thus every one of the eight exact atom probabilities is affine in \(z\).

When \(0<b<1\), define the conditional four-atom laws on sites \(\{1,3\}\)
\[
q^{(j)}_{ab}(z)
=\frac{p_{a j b}(z)}{\pi_j},
\qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2.6}
\]
In atom order \(00,10,01,11\), (2.5) becomes the universal derivative
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dz}
\bigl(q^{(j)}_{00},q^{(j)}_{10},q^{(j)}_{01},q^{(j)}_{11}\bigr)
=(-1,+1,+1,-1),
}
\tag{2.7}
\]
independent of \(j\).

## 3. Conditional two-site interactions are nonnegative

Conditioning a DPP on the state of site 2 gives another DPP on sites \(\{1,3\}\). For the strict case the two conditional kernels are
\[
F_1(y)=
\begin{pmatrix}a&u+i y\\u-i y&c\end{pmatrix}
-\frac1b\binom r s(r\;s),
\tag{3.1}
\]
and
\[
F_0(y)=
\begin{pmatrix}a&u+i y\\u-i y&c\end{pmatrix}
+\frac1{1-b}\binom r s(r\;s).
\tag{3.2}
\]
Their off-diagonal entries are
\[
(F_1)_{13}=u-\frac{rs}{b}+iy,
\qquad
(F_0)_{13}=u+\frac{rs}{1-b}+iy.
\tag{3.3}
\]
For any strict two-site DPP atom law \((q_{00},q_{10},q_{01},q_{11})\) with kernel off-diagonal \(\xi\),
\[
q_{10}q_{01}-q_{00}q_{11}=|\xi|^2\ge0.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Hence its log interaction
\[
\lambda
:=\log\frac{q_{10}q_{01}}{q_{00}q_{11}}
\tag{3.5}
\]
is nonnegative.

Define for the two conditionals
\[
\lambda_j(z)
=\log\frac{q^{(j)}_{10}(z)q^{(j)}_{01}(z)}
{q^{(j)}_{00}(z)q^{(j)}_{11}(z)}\ge0.
\tag{3.6}
\]
No external stochastic-order theorem is needed for this sign; (3.4) proves it directly.

## 4. Exact first and second derivatives in the squared quadrature

By the entropy chain rule with respect to the middle occupation,
\[
H(P_{K(y)})
=h(b)
+bH(q^{(1)}(z))
+(1-b)H(q^{(0)}(z)),
\qquad z=y^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Define the right side as \(F(z)\). Since each conditional atom in (2.7) is affine in \(z\), direct differentiation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F'(z)
&=-b\lambda_1(z)-(1-b)\lambda_0(z),\\
\boxed{F'(z)&\le0.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Thus entropy is nonincreasing in the squared orthogonal cycle quadrature.

Differentiating once more and using (2.7),
\[
\boxed{
F''(z)
=-b\sum_{a,c\in\{0,1\}}\frac1{q^{(1)}_{ac}(z)}
-(1-b)\sum_{a,c\in\{0,1\}}\frac1{q^{(0)}_{ac}(z)}<0
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
throughout the strict physical region. So \(F\) is strictly concave in \(z\), in addition to being nonincreasing.

The actual affine kernel coordinate is \(y\), not \(z\). Since \(z=y^2\),
\[
\mathcal H''(y)
=2F'(y^2)+4y^2F''(y^2).
\tag{4.4}
\]
Combining (4.2) and (4.3),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H''(y)
=-2\Bigl[b\lambda_1(y^2)+(1-b)\lambda_0(y^2)\Bigr]
-4y^2\Biggl[
 b\sum_{a,c}\frac1{q^{(1)}_{ac}(y^2)}
 +(1-b)\sum_{a,c}\frac1{q^{(0)}_{ac}(y^2)}
\Biggr]
\le0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
This proves Theorem 1.1 in the strict case.

Formula (4.5) is stronger than the qualitative chord theorem: it gives an exact curvature decomposition into two nonnegative conditional log-interaction terms and a positive Fisher-type reciprocal-atom term.

At \(y=0\), equality in the first term requires both conditional coherences in (3.3) to vanish. If both \(r s\) and \(u\) do not simultaneously vanish, at least one conditional interaction is positive and the curvature is already strictly negative at \(y=0\). When \(rs=u=0\), the curvature can vanish at the origin, but (4.3) still gives the expected quartic entropy loss away from the origin.

## 5. Gauge-invariant formulation

Diagonal unitary conjugation preserves every principal determinant, hence preserves the complete DPP law and its Shannon entropy. Suppose two three-site positive contractions \(Q_0,Q_1\) agree in all entries except one off-diagonal pair, say \((1,3),(3,1)\). Assume a single diagonal gauge makes their common \(12\) and \(23\) entries real and makes the common real quadrature of the varying \(13\) entry the same at both endpoints. Equivalently, when the common product \(q_{12}q_{23}\neq0\), the edge displacement \(\delta=q^{(1)}_{13}-q^{(0)}_{13}\) satisfies the gauge-invariant quadrature condition
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\delta\,\overline{q_{12}q_{23}}\right)=0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Then the whole affine chord is of the form (1.1), so Lyons's inequality holds at every \(t\), not only at the midpoint.

If one of the adjacent common edges vanishes, the canonical-gauge statement of Theorem 1.1 is the safe formulation; the residual gauge can be chosen directly.

The same statement holds after any permutation of the sites.

This family is genuinely complex: \(r,s,u\) may all be nonzero and \(y\neq0\), so the triangle has nontrivial gauge-invariant cycle phase.

## 6. Spectral-boundary passage

The proof above used strict atoms only to write logarithms and reciprocals. Let a closed physical chord \(K(y)\) have spectral-boundary endpoints or intermediate points. For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), define
\[
K_\varepsilon(y)
=(1-2\varepsilon)K(y)+\varepsilon I_3.
\tag{6.1}
\]
This is a strict positive contraction and retains exactly the same structural form: the two real adjacent edges, the real quadrature \(u\), and the imaginary quadrature \(y\) are merely scaled by the common factor \(1-2\varepsilon\). Therefore Theorem 1.1 applies to every regularized chord.

For a finite DPP every exact atom is a polynomial in the kernel entries, and \(-p\log p\) extends continuously to \(p=0\). Sending \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives (1.2) on the closed physical chord.

No stationary entropy-rate statement is inferred from this finite theorem beyond limits that are independently justified.

## 9. Next attack

The useful structural lesson is that the orthogonal cycle quadrature is unusually tractable because the complete law is affine in its square and the derivative is exactly the conditional two-site interaction. The next attack should use this as one axis in the general complex edge plane: write a moving edge in canonical gauge as \(q_{13}(t)=u(t)+iy(t)\), separate the already-solved \(y\)-quadrature curvature (4.5), and isolate the remaining mixed \(u\)-\(y\) and radial \(u\) terms. If those mixed terms can be paid by the reciprocal-atom term in (4.5), this would enlarge the theorem from a one-real-dimensional quadrature chord to a two-real-dimensional moving complex edge with the other five kernel parameters fixed. A failure should be tested against the full exact entropy Hessian before being called a counterexample.
