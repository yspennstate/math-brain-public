## 1. Exact noncollinear decomposition from the recovered checkpoint

\[
K_j=\begin{pmatrix}a&w_j^*\\ w_j&B_j\end{pmatrix},\qquad j=0,1,
\tag{1.1}

\[
c=a(1-a),\qquad U_j=\frac{w_jw_j^*}{c},\qquad d=w_1-w_0,
\tag{1.2}

\[
w_t=(1-t)w_0+tw_1,

\quad \bar U_t=(1-t)U_0+tU_1.
\tag{1.3}

Then exactly
\[
\boxed{\bar U_t-U_t=\frac{t(1-t)}cdd^*.}
\tag{1.4}

\Phi_a(B,U)=aH_2(B-(1-a)U)+(1-a)H_2(B+aU).
\tag{1.5}

The exact conditional kernels at an original three-site kernel are the two entries in (1.5). Let
\[
B_t=(1-t)B_0+tB_1,
\qquad
U_{t,s}=U_t+s(\bar U_t-U_t),\quad0\le s\le1,
\tag{1.6}
\]
and
\[
F_{1,t,s}=B_t-(1-a)U_{t,s},
\qquad
F_{0,t,s}=B_t+aU_{t,s},
\tag{1.7}

\[
G_{t,s}=\nabla H_2(F_{1,t,s})-\nabla H_2(F_{0,t,s}).
\tag{1.8}

\boxed{
\Gamma(t)=J_{\rm aff}(t)

\tag{1.9}

J_{\rm aff}(t)

\tag{1.10}

The first term is precisely the weighted sum of the two affine two-site conditional Jensen gaps. The second term is the entire nonlinear price of noncollinearity. No Schur-complement acceleration is dropped.

## 2. New lemma: the adverse part of a two-site PSD secant is an adjugate

The useful extension of X071 is the following.

### Lemma 2.1 — PSD secant decomposition
Let \(F\) and \(F+D\) be strict two-site contractions and assume \(D\succeq0\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\nabla H_2(F)-\nabla H_2(F+D)=P_D-\bar\lambda_D\operatorname{adj}D,
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
where \(P_D\succeq0\) and
\[
\bar\lambda_D=\int_0^1\lambda(F+rD)\,dr\ge0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof
Integrate (0.2) along \(F+rD\):
\[
\nabla H_2(F)-\nabla H_2(F+D)
=
\int_0^1\left[\Phi_{F+rD}(D)-\lambda(F+rD)\operatorname{adj}D\right]dr.
\]
Set
\[
P_D=\int_0^1\Phi_{F+rD}(D)dr.
\]
Because \(D\succeq0\), each summand \(Y_xDY_x\) in the completely positive Fisher map is positive semidefinite, hence \(P_D\succeq0\). For a two-by-two matrix the adjugate is linear, so it stays fixed along this integration. This is (2.1). ∎

The point is not that the whole secant is positive; X071 already contains exact negative-eigenvalue examples. The point is that its entire possibly adverse part is the explicit scalar \(-\bar\lambda_D\operatorname{adj}D\).

## 3. Exact wedge identity for the noncollinear defect

Apply Lemma 2.1 to (1.8). The conditional separation is
\[
F_{0,t,s}-F_{1,t,s}=U_{t,s}\succeq0.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Hence
\[
G_{t,s}=P_{t,s}-\bar\lambda_{t,s}\operatorname{adj}U_{t,s},
\qquad P_{t,s}\succeq0.
\tag{3.2}
\]
For vectors \(u,v\in\mathbb C^2\), write \(u\wedge v=u_1v_2-u_2v_1\). The elementary two-by-two identity
\[
v^*\operatorname{adj}(uu^*)v=|u\wedge v|^2
\tag{3.3}
\]
continues to complex vectors.

By (1.4),
\[
U_{t,s}=\frac1c\left[w_tw_t^*+s\,t(1-t)dd^*\right].
\tag{3.4}
\]
The adjugate is linear in two dimensions, and
\[
d^*\operatorname{adj}(dd^*)d=0.
\]
Moreover
\[
w_t\wedge d
=((1-t)w_0+tw_1)\wedge(w_1-w_0)=w_0\wedge w_1.
\]
Therefore the entire transverse adjugate cost collapses to the **constant**
\[
\boxed{
d^*\operatorname{adj}U_{t,s}d
=
\frac{|w_0\wedge w_1|^2}{c},
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
independent of both \(t\) and \(s\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{
d^*G_{t,s}d
\ge
-\bar\lambda_{t,s}\frac{|w_0\wedge w_1|^2}{c}.}
\tag{3.6}
\]
This identifies the exact geometric noncollinearity invariant. It vanishes precisely in the previously solved collinear case.

## 4. Quantitative three-site wedge-compensation theorem

Define the two affine conditional endpoint pairs
\[
F_{1,j}=B_j-(1-a)U_j,
\qquad
F_{0,j}=B_j+aU_j,
\tag{4.1}
\]
and their directions
\[
\Delta_1=F_{1,1}-F_{1,0},
\qquad
\Delta_0=F_{0,1}-F_{0,0}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Write
\[
F_{\epsilon,r}^{\rm aff}=(1-r)F_{\epsilon,0}+rF_{\epsilon,1}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Assume there are constants \(\kappa_1,\kappa_0\ge0\) such that
\[
|(F_{\epsilon,r}^{\rm aff})_{12}|\ge\kappa_\epsilon
\quad\text{for every }0\le r\le1.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Suppose also that a finite number \(\Lambda\) bounds the conditional interaction along every vertical two-site segment used by (3.2):
\[
\lambda(F_{1,t,s}+rU_{t,s})\le\Lambda
\quad(0\le t,s,r\le1).
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — wedge compensation
Under (1.1)–(1.8) and (4.4)–(4.5),
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma(t)
\ge
t(1-t)
\left[
2a\kappa_1^2\|\Delta_1\|_F^2
+2(1-a)\kappa_0^2\|\Delta_0\|_F^2
-\frac{\Lambda}{a(1-a)}|w_0\wedge w_1|^2
\right].
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
In particular, the whole chord satisfies Lyons whenever the bracket is nonnegative.

#### Proof
By C055 (0.1), along either affine two-site conditional chord,
\[
-\frac{d^2}{dr^2}H_2(F_{\epsilon,r}^{\rm aff})
\ge4\kappa_\epsilon^2\|\Delta_\epsilon\|_F^2.
\]
The Green kernel for a scalar Jensen gap has integral \(t(1-t)/2\). Hence
\[
J_{\rm aff}(t)
\ge2t(1-t)
\left[
a\kappa_1^2\|\Delta_1\|_F^2
+(1-a)\kappa_0^2\|\Delta_0\|_F^2\right].
\tag{4.7}
\]
On the other hand (3.6) and (4.5) give
\[
\int_0^1d^*G_{t,s}d\,ds
\ge-\Lambda\frac{|w_0\wedge w_1|^2}{c}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Insert (4.7)–(4.8) into the exact identity (1.9), with \(c=a(1-a)\). ∎

### A simple spectral-margin corollary
Assume all kernels in the conditional rectangle satisfy, for some \(0<\delta\le1/2\),
\[
\delta I_2\preceq F_{\epsilon,t,s}\preceq(1-\delta)I_2,
\qquad \epsilon=0,1,\quad0\le t,s\le1.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Every intermediate point \(F_{1,t,s}+rU_{t,s}\) is then also in this spectral strip. Its four exact two-site DPP atoms obey
\[
p_{11}=\det F\ge\delta^2,
\quad p_{00}=\det(I-F)\ge\delta^2,
\]
\[
p_{10}=F_{11}(1-F_{22})+|F_{12}|^2\ge\delta^2,
\quad
p_{01}\ge\delta^2.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Thus
\[
0\le\lambda(F)
=\log\frac{p_{10}p_{01}}{p_{00}p_{11}}
\le4\log(1/\delta).
\tag{4.11}
\]
So Theorem 4.1 applies with
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_\delta=4\log(1/\delta).}
\tag{4.12}
\]
This gives a fully explicit open noncollinear region around every common-marginal chord for which the lower conditional coercivity beats the wedge cost.
