# 4. Main theorem

## Theorem 4.1 — Global concavity on every fixed-nullspace rank-two face

Let `U:C^2 -> C^3` be an isometry, and for a Hermitian `2x2` contraction `A` define

\[
K(A)=UAU^\ast.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{A\longmapsto H(P_{K(A)})\ \text{is concave on}\ 0\preceq A\preceq I_2.}
\]

Equivalently, if two three-site positive contractions have range contained in the same fixed two-dimensional subspace (or, equivalently, share a common one-dimensional nullspace), then for every `0<=t<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
H(P_{U((1-t)A_0+tA_1)U^\ast})
\ge
(1-t)H(P_{UA_0U^\ast})+tH(P_{UA_1U^\ast}).
}
\]

The statement is valid for complex `U`, complex Hermitian directions, and spectral/atom boundary limits.

### Proof

It suffices first to prove strict Hessian negativity on the generic interior:
`0<A<I_2` and the null vector of `U^*` has all three coordinates nonzero. The closed statement then follows by approximation.

Let `n` be a unit vector spanning `ker U^*`, and put

\[
q_i=|n_i|^2>0,\qquad q_1+q_2+q_3=1.
\]

Let

\[
f_i=U^\ast e_i\in\mathbb C^2.
\]

Because `UU^*=I-nn^*`,

\[
\|f_i\|^2=1-q_i,
\]

and the two-dimensional Pluecker/Naimark-complement identity gives

\[
|\det(f_i,f_j)|^2=q_k,\qquad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let

\[
d=\det A,\qquad e=\det(I-A),\qquad C=A-dI_2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

If the eigenvalues of `A` are `alpha,beta in (0,1)`, then the eigenvalues of `C` are

\[
\gamma_1=\alpha(1-\beta),\qquad
\gamma_2=\beta(1-\alpha),
\]

so `C>0` and

\[
\det C=ed.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.1. Exact boundary atom law

Since `K=UAU^*` has rank two, `p_{123}=0`. The remaining atoms are

\[
\boxed{
p_\varnothing=e,\qquad
p_{ij}=dq_k,\qquad
p_i=f_i^\ast C f_i.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For the pair atoms this follows from the Cauchy-Binet/Pluecker identity (4.1). For the singleton atoms it is inclusion-exclusion, equivalently the compression identity in (4.4).

For distinct `i,j` and complementary `k`, the `2x2` Gram determinant identity applied to `C^{1/2}f_i,C^{1/2}f_j` gives

\[
p_ip_j-|f_i^\ast C f_j|^2
=(\det C)|\det(f_i,f_j)|^2
=edq_k.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\lambda_k:=
\log\frac{p_ip_j}{edq_k}\ge0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Define

\[
\mathcal L:=\sum_{k=1}^3q_k\lambda_k\ge0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

### 4.2. Exact Hessian reduction on the face

Let `D=D^*` be a Hermitian `2x2` affine direction and put

\[
\delta=\det D,\qquad
a=D(\det A)[D]=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A)D).
\]

Along `A_t=A+tD`, a `2x2` determinant has second derivative `2 det D`. Hence, from (4.4),

\[
p_\varnothing''=2\delta,\qquad
p_{ij}''=2\delta q_k,\qquad
p_i''=-2\delta(1-q_i).
\tag{4.8}
\]

The entropy Hessian identity on the seven positive atoms is

\[
H''(0)
=
-\sum_S\frac{(p_S')^2}{p_S}
-\sum_S p_S''\log p_S.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Write

\[
\mathrm{FI}:=\sum_S\frac{(p_S')^2}{p_S}\ge0.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Using (4.8), `sum q_i=1`, and (4.6), the frozen-log term groups exactly as

\[
\sum_Sp_S''\log p_S
=
2\delta\sum_{k=1}^3q_k
\log\frac{p_ip_j}{edq_k}
=
2\delta\mathcal L.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{-H''(0)=\mathrm{FI}-2\delta\mathcal L.}
\tag{4.12}
\]

If `delta<=0`, then (4.7) immediately yields `-H''>=FI>=0`. The only difficult case is `delta>0`.

### 4.3. B11: cardinality Fisher information pays every positive-determinant direction

Assume `delta>0`. A Hermitian `2x2` matrix with positive determinant is definite. Replacing `D` by `-D` leaves the quadratic Hessian unchanged, so assume `D>0`.

Diagonalize `A`:

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\0&\beta\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}x&z\\\bar z&y\end{pmatrix},
\]

with `x,y>0` and

\[
\delta=xy-|z|^2\le xy.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Coarse-grain the seven-atom law by cardinality `N=|X|`. Since `K` has rank two,

\[
P(N=0)=e=(1-\alpha)(1-\beta),
\]
\[
P(N=1)=m:=\alpha+\beta-2\alpha\beta,
\]
\[
P(N=2)=d=\alpha\beta.
\tag{4.14}
\]

The Fisher information decreases under deterministic coarse-graining (equivalently, Cauchy-Schwarz within each cardinality class), hence

\[
\mathrm{FI}\ge\mathrm{FI}_N.
\tag{4.15}
\]

The first derivatives of the three cardinality probabilities depend only on `x,y`. Direct expansion gives

\[
\mathrm{FI}_N=A_0x^2+2B_0xy+C_0y^2,
\tag{4.16}
\]

where

\[
B_0=\frac1m,
\tag{4.17}
\]

\[
A_0=
\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2+\alpha(1-\alpha)}
{\alpha(1-\alpha)m},
\qquad
C_0=
\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2+\beta(1-\beta)}
{\beta(1-\beta)m}.
\tag{4.18}
\]

Because `delta<=xy` and AM-GM,

\[
\frac{\mathrm{FI}_N}{2\delta}
\ge
\frac{\mathrm{FI}_N}{2xy}
\ge
\sqrt{A_0C_0}+B_0.
\tag{4.19}
\]

Define

\[
r=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}},
\qquad
s=\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}},
\qquad
\zeta=\frac rs+\frac sr\ge2.
\tag{4.20}
\]

The exact algebraic identity

\[
\boxed{
A_0C_0-(2+\zeta-B_0)^2
=
\frac{(r+s)^2(rs-1)^2}{r^2s^2}\ge0
}
\tag{4.21}
\]

implies

\[
\boxed{\sqrt{A_0C_0}+B_0\ge2+\zeta.}
\tag{4.22}
\]

Indeed, if `2+zeta-B_0>=0`, (4.21) permits taking square roots; if it is negative, then already `B_0>2+zeta`.

Combining (4.15), (4.19), and (4.22),

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{FI}\ge2\delta(2+\zeta).
}
\tag{4.23}
\]

### 4.4. B24: exact extremal bound for the logarithmic coefficient

Now bound `mathcal L`.

The eigenvalues of `C` are

\[
\gamma_1=\alpha(1-\beta),\qquad
\gamma_2=\beta(1-\alpha).
\tag{4.24}
\]

By (4.3),

\[
\gamma_1+\gamma_2=m,\qquad
\gamma_1\gamma_2=ed.
\tag{4.25}
\]

Define the singleton probability vector

\[
y_i=\frac{p_i}{m},
\qquad \sum_i y_i=1,
\tag{4.26}
\]

and the probability vector

\[
R_i=\frac{1-q_i}{2},
\qquad \sum_iR_i=1.
\tag{4.27}
\]

Expanding (4.7) and using (4.26)-(4.27) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L
=
\log\frac{m^2}{ed}
+H(q)-2H(R)-2D_{\rm KL}(R\|y).
}
\tag{4.28}
\]

Since relative entropy is nonnegative,

\[
\mathcal L
\le
\log\frac{m^2}{ed}+H(q)-2H(R).
\tag{4.29}
\]

It remains to maximize the purely three-point entropy expression. Define

\[
F(q):=\sum_i\big[(1-q_i)\log(1-q_i)-q_i\log q_i\big].
\tag{4.30}
\]

Because `H(q)-2H(R)=F(q)-2\log2`, it suffices to maximize `F` on the simplex.

On a boundary edge, say `q_3=0` and `q_2=1-q_1`, direct cancellation gives `F=0`.

At an interior stationary point under `sum q_i=1`, the Lagrange multiplier equation implies that all `q_i(1-q_i)` are equal. For a fixed `c`, the equation `x(1-x)=c` has only the complementary roots `x` and `1-x`. Three positive numbers summing to one cannot contain both complementary roots unless one reaches the boundary; hence the only interior stationary point is

\[
q_1=q_2=q_3=\frac13.
\]

At this point

\[
F=\log\frac43>0,
\]

while every boundary edge has value zero. Therefore it is the global maximum, and

\[
\boxed{
H(q)-2H(R)\le-\log3.
}
\tag{4.31}
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal L
\le
\log\frac{m^2}{3ed}.
\tag{4.32}
\]

Let

\[
\kappa=\frac{\gamma_1}{\gamma_2}=\left(\frac rs\right)^2.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{m^2}{ed}
=
\frac{(\gamma_1+\gamma_2)^2}{\gamma_1\gamma_2}
=
\left(\sqrt\kappa+\frac1{\sqrt\kappa}\right)^2
=
\zeta^2.
\tag{4.33}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L\le2\log\zeta-\log3.}
\tag{4.34}
\]

### 4.5. Completion of the difficult case

For `zeta>=2`,

\[
g(\zeta):=2+\zeta-2\log\zeta+\log3
\]

satisfies

\[
g'(\zeta)=1-\frac2\zeta\ge0
\]

and

\[
g(2)=4-\log\frac43>0.
\]

Therefore

\[
2+\zeta>2\log\zeta-\log3\ge\mathcal L.
\tag{4.35}
\]

Using (4.23),

\[
\mathrm{FI}
\ge2\delta(2+\zeta)
>
2\delta\mathcal L.
\]

By (4.12),

\[
-H''(0)>0
\]

for every nonzero direction with `delta>0`.

### 4.6. Strictness in the remaining generic directions

If `delta<0`, (4.12) gives

\[
-H''=\mathrm{FI}+2|\delta|\mathcal L.
\]

At least one `lambda_k` is positive: otherwise the three nonzero frame vectors `C^{1/2}f_i` would be pairwise orthogonal in a two-dimensional space. Hence `mathcal L>0`, and `-H''>0`.

If `delta=0`, then `-H''=FI`. If `FI=0`, every positive atom has zero first derivative. The pair-atom derivatives force

\[
D(\det A)[D]=0,
\]

and the empty-atom derivative then forces `tr D=0`. For Hermitian `2x2` `D`, `det D=0` and `tr D=0` imply `D=0`. Thus every nonzero generic tangent direction has `FI>0`.

Hence the Hessian is negative definite throughout the generic interior of the fixed-nullspace face.

### 4.7. Complex kernels and boundary limits

The proof used only Hermitian algebra, complex Gram determinants, and Shannon entropy of exact atoms; no real-kernel restriction was imposed.

For a nongeneric isometry `U` with some `q_i=0`, approximate it by generic isometries `U_\varepsilon`. For endpoint matrices `A_j` that may have eigenvalues 0 or 1, use

\[
A_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)A_j+\varepsilon I_2.
\]

The midpoint relation is preserved. The strict generic theorem applies to
`U_\varepsilon A_j^{(\varepsilon)}U_\varepsilon^*`.
Every finite DPP atom is polynomial in the kernel entries, and `-p log p` is continuous at `p=0`; hence Shannon entropy is continuous on the finite positive-contraction body. Letting `epsilon downarrow0` proves the closed midpoint inequality.

This completes Theorem 4.1. `\square`
