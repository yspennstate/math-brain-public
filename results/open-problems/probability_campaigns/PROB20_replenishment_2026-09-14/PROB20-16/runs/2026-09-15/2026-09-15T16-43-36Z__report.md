# 2. Family: a moving exchangeable core with coherent satellites

Let \(U\) be a measured core of size \(n\ge2\). Fix a flat unit vector
\[
u\in\mathbb C^n,
\qquad |u_i|=n^{-1/2},
\qquad P=uu^*.
\]
The standard choice is \(u=n^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)^T\); diagonal phase gauges give every flat vector.

For real parameters \(a,c\), define the exchangeable core kernel
\[
A(a,c)=a(I_U-P)+cP.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Its eigenvalues are \(a\) with multiplicity \(n-1\) and \(c\) with multiplicity one.

Let there be \(m\) mutually disjoint satellite blocks
\[
O_1,\ldots,O_m.
\]
For each \(j\), fix an arbitrary Hermitian positive contraction \(C_j\) on \(O_j\) and a vector
\[
v_j\in\mathbb C^{O_j}.
\]
No diagonality, exchangeability, or rank restriction is imposed inside \(C_j\).

For complex amplitudes \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\), define the full measured kernel
\[
K(a,c,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
A(a,c) & z_1uv_1^* & \cdots & z_muv_m^*\\
\bar z_1v_1u^* & C_1 &&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
\bar z_mv_mu^*&0&&C_m
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Thus every satellite couples to the same collective flat mode of the core, but the satellite vectors, block sizes, internal coherences, and complex amplitudes are arbitrary.

The total core-to-satellite cross matrix has rank at most one on the core side, but the affine direction of the whole kernel need not have small rank: varying \(a\) changes the entire \((n-1)\)-dimensional transverse eigenspace, varying \(c\) changes the uniform mode, and the satellite amplitudes may all move independently.

Put
\[
s_j=|z_j|^2.
\tag{2.3}
\]

# 3. Exact physical domain is convex and downward closed

Initially assume
\[
0<C_j<I
\]
for every satellite block. Set
\[
\alpha_j=v_j^*C_j^{-1}v_j\ge0,
\qquad
\beta_j=v_j^*(I-C_j)^{-1}v_j\ge0.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Since the fixed satellite principal block is \(\operatorname{diag}(C_1,\ldots,C_m)\), the Schur complement gives
\[
K(a,c,z)\ge0
\iff
\begin{cases}
a\ge0,\\ c-\sum_j\alpha_js_j\ge0.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Likewise the Schur complement of the satellite block of \(I-K\) gives
\[
I-K(a,c,z)\ge0
\iff
\begin{cases}1-a\ge0,\\1-c-\sum_j\beta_js_j\ge0.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Therefore the exact squared-strength domain is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D=
\left\{
(a,c,s):
0\le a\le1,\ s_j\ge0,
\ c\ge\sum_j\alpha_js_j,
\ 1-c\ge\sum_j\beta_js_j
\right\}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
It is convex. At fixed \(a,c\), it is coordinatewise downward closed in \(s\).

Multiplying all coordinates in a satellite block \(O_j\) by one phase removes the phase of \(z_j\) while commuting with \(C_j\). Hence every principal determinant, every exact atom probability, and the full Shannon entropy depend on \(z_j\) only through \(s_j=|z_j|^2\).

# 4. Exact conditional exchangeable core

For an exact atom \(y_j\subseteq O_j\), define
\[
Q_{j,y_j}=C_j-P_{O_j\setminus y_j},
\qquad
q_j(y_j)=(-1)^{|O_j\setminus y_j|}\det Q_{j,y_j}>0,
\tag{4.1}
\]
and
\[
d_{j,y_j}=v_j^*Q_{j,y_j}^{-1}v_j\in\mathbb R.
\tag{4.2}
\]
The number in (4.2) need not have a fixed sign.

The satellite principal marginal is the block-diagonal DPP
\[
P_{C_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{C_m},
\]
so the exact satellite atoms are mutually independent and have fixed weight
\[
q(y)=\prod_jq_j(y_j).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Condition on the complete satellite configuration
\[
y=(y_1,\ldots,y_m).
\]
A Schur complement in the exact-atom determinant gives the conditional core kernel
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_y(a,c,s)
&=A(a,c)-\sum_js_j\,u v_j^*Q_{j,y_j}^{-1}v_ju^*\\
&=a(I-P)+\left(c-\sum_js_jd_{j,y_j}\right)P.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Thus **every conditional core remains exchangeable**. The conditional parameters are affine functions of \((a,c,s)\).

The conditional matrix is automatically a positive contraction whenever the full kernel is a positive contraction. This follows either from ordinary included/excluded-site conditioning for DPPs or directly from the two Schur complements defining the conditional atom. No sign assumption on \(d_{j,y_j}\) is required.

Let
\[
H_n^{\rm ex}(a,c)
:=H(P_{a(I-P)+cP}).
\tag{4.5}
\]
C075 proves that this function is concave on the full closed square \([0,1]^2\), in fact as the restriction of a nonpositive full Hermitian entropy Hessian at every interior exchangeable kernel.

The Shannon chain rule therefore gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
F(a,c,s)
:=H(P_{K(a,c,z)})
=
\sum_{j=1}^m H(P_{C_j})
+
\sum_y q(y)
H_n^{\rm ex}\!\left(
a,
 c-\sum_js_jd_{j,y_j}
\right).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Every term on the right is a concave function composed with an affine map. The weights are fixed and nonnegative. Hence
\[
\boxed{F\text{ is jointly concave in }(a,c,s)\text{ on }\mathcal D.}
\tag{4.7}
\]
This is the key lifting step.

---

# 5. Coordinatewise strength monotonicity

Fix \(a,c\), all strengths except \(s_j\), and a feasible value of \(s_j\). Let
\[
s^{\setminus j}
\]
be obtained by setting only \(s_j=0\).

At zero \(j\)-th strength the satellite block \(O_j\) is completely decoupled from every other measured coordinate. Its own marginal remains \(P_{C_j}\), while the principal marginal on the complement \(E\setminus O_j\) is unchanged because principal DPP marginals depend only on the corresponding principal kernel. Therefore
\[
F(a,c,s^{\setminus j})-F(a,c,s)
=I(Y_j;X,Y_{-j})\ge0,
\tag{5.1}
\]
where \(X\) is the measured core occupation vector and \(Y_j\) the full occupation vector of block \(O_j\).

Since the physical domain is downward closed and \(F\) is concave in \(s_j\), zero strength is the maximum of the entire one-dimensional feasible slice. Thus
\[
\boxed{
0\le s'\le s\text{ coordinatewise}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F(a,c,s')\ge F(a,c,s).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
No monotonicity of the individual conditional scalar \(d_{j,y_j}\) is needed.

# 6. Main theorem: global affine-kernel concavity

### Theorem 6.1 — exchangeable core with coherent satellites

Fix the satellite kernels \(C_j\), satellite vectors \(v_j\), and flat core vector \(u\). On the complete closed physical domain of kernels (2.2), the map
\[
\boxed{
(a,c,z_1,\ldots,z_m)
\longmapsto
H(P_{K(a,c,z)})
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
is jointly concave when each complex amplitude is viewed as two real variables.

Equivalently, take any two feasible endpoints
\[
(a_0,c_0,z^0),\qquad(a_1,c_1,z^1),
\]
and put
\[
a_t=(1-t)a_0+ta_1,
\qquad
c_t=(1-t)c_0+tc_1,
\qquad
z_t=(1-t)z^0+tz^1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{K(a_t,c_t,z_t)})
\ge
(1-t)H(P_{K(a_0,c_0,z^0)})
+tH(P_{K(a_1,c_1,z^1)}).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof
Write
\[
s^r_j=|z_j^r|^2,
\qquad
\bar s_j=(1-t)s^0_j+ts^1_j,
\qquad
s^t_j=|z^t_j|^2.
\]
The elementary complex parallelogram identity gives
\[
\boxed{
\bar s_j-s^t_j
=t(1-t)|z^1_j-z^0_j|^2\ge0.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Because \(\mathcal D\) is convex,
\[
(a_t,c_t,\bar s)\in\mathcal D.
\]
Because it is downward closed,
\[
(a_t,c_t,s^t)\in\mathcal D.
\]
Then (5.2) and (4.7) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(P_{K(a_t,c_t,z_t)})
&=F(a_t,c_t,s^t)\\
&\ge F(a_t,c_t,\bar s)\\
&\ge (1-t)F(a_0,c_0,s^0)+tF(a_1,c_1,s^1).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is exactly (6.3). \(\square\)

The theorem permits all of the following simultaneously:

- arbitrary core size \(n\);
- full-rank moving exchangeable core \(a(I-P)+cP\);
- arbitrary changes of both \(a\) and \(c\);
- any number of coherent satellite blocks of arbitrary sizes;
- arbitrary fixed internal satellite coherences;
- arbitrary independent complex amplitude slopes, signs, and phases;
- high-rank full-kernel affine directions.

It is global on the whole physical convex domain, not a weak-coupling or local theorem.

# 7. Exact finite Jensen-gap decomposition

The proof above signs the Jensen gap but can be sharpened into an exact sum of nonnegative quantities.

For \(0<t<1\), define
\[
\delta_j=t(1-t)|z_j^1-z_j^0|^2,
\qquad
\lambda_j=
\begin{cases}
\delta_j/\bar s_j,&\bar s_j>0,\\
0,&\bar s_j=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Thus \(0\le\lambda_j\le1\) and
\[
s_j^t=(1-\lambda_j)\bar s_j.
\]

Let \(L\) denote the auxiliary DPP at
\[
(a_t,c_t,\bar s).
\]
Independently form a random subset \(R\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) by including block \(j\) with probability \(\lambda_j\). Put
\[
s_j^R=\bar s_j\mathbf 1_{\{j\notin R\}},
\qquad
L^R=K(a_t,c_t,\sqrt{s^R}).
\tag{7.2}
\]
Every \(L^R\) is feasible by downward closure.

For a fixed satellite atom \(y\), define the endpoint, auxiliary, removed-block, and target conditional core parameters
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_y^r&=c_r-\sum_js_j^r d_{j,y_j},\\
\bar c_y&=c_t-\sum_j\bar s_jd_{j,y_j},\\
c_y^R&=c_t-\sum_js_j^Rd_{j,y_j},\\
c_y^t&=c_t-\sum_js_j^td_{j,y_j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Because the removal indicators are independent,
\[
\mathbb E_R c_y^R=c_y^t.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Define two conditional exchangeable-core Jensen gaps
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\rm end}
&=\sum_yq(y)
\Big[
H_n^{\rm ex}(a_t,\bar c_y)
-(1-t)H_n^{\rm ex}(a_0,c_y^0)
-tH_n^{\rm ex}(a_1,c_y^1)
\Big],\\
J_{\rm remove}
&=\sum_yq(y)
\Big[
H_n^{\rm ex}(a_t,c_y^t)
-\mathbb E_RH_n^{\rm ex}(a_t,c_y^R)
\Big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.5}
\]
Both are nonnegative by C075's exchangeable-core concavity.

The satellite blocks are mutually independent under \(P_L\), because their principal kernel is block diagonal. Removing all cross couplings for blocks in \(R\) leaves their original product marginal independent of the unchanged marginal on the complementary coordinates. Hence
\[
H(P_{L^R})-H(P_L)
=I_L(Y_R;X,Y_{R^c}).
\tag{7.6}
\]
Using the chain-rule representation (4.6), equations (7.4)–(7.6) give the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_t
=J_{\rm end}+J_{\rm remove}
+\mathbb E_R I_L(Y_R;X,Y_{R^c}),
}
\tag{7.7}
\]
where
\[
\Gamma_t=H(P_{K(a_t,c_t,z_t)})
-(1-t)H(P_{K(a_0,c_0,z^0)})
-tH(P_{K(a_1,c_1,z^1)}).
\]
Thus the finite chord inequality is not only a consequence of differential curvature; it is decomposed into two conditional exchangeable-core Jensen gaps plus a classical information-loss term.

# 8. Additive information lower bound and strictness

Because the satellite vectors \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\) are mutually independent under the auxiliary law \(L\), the entropy chain rule gives, for every fixed removal set \(R\),
\[
I_L(Y_R;X,Y_{R^c})
\ge\sum_{j\in R}I_L(Y_j;X).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Taking expectation over the independent removal set,
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_t
\ge
\sum_j\lambda_j I_L(Y_j;X).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

For a core coordinate \(i\in U\) and satellite coordinate \(k\in O_j\), determinantal covariance under \(L\) is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_L(X_i,(Y_j)_k)
=-\bar s_j|u_i|^2|v_{j,k}|^2
=-\frac{\bar s_j}{n}|v_{j,k}|^2.
\tag{8.3}
\]
A binary joint law of covariance \(r\) is at total-variation distance \(2|r|\) from the product of its marginals. Pinsker's inequality in natural logarithms gives
\[
I(X_i;(Y_j)_k)\ge8r^2.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Data processing \(I(Y_j;X)\ge I((Y_j)_k;X_i)\), followed by (8.2), yields
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_t
\ge
\frac{8t(1-t)}{n^2}
\sum_{j=1}^m
|z_j^1-z_j^0|^2\,
\bar s_j\,
\max_{k\in O_j}|v_{j,k}|^4.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]
A zero vector \(v_j\) contributes zero.

Consequently, whenever \(0<t<1\) and some nonzero satellite vector has
\[
z_j^0\ne z_j^1,
\]
we have \(ar s_j>0\) and the right side of (8.5) is strictly positive. Therefore the full Jensen inequality is strict, including when the core endpoint parameters \(a,c\) are unchanged.

Strictness caused solely by changing the core parameters is governed separately by C075: at strict exchangeable cores with \(a\ne c\), its full Hermitian Hessian is negative definite. The present theorem does not need a global equality classification when all amplitudes are fixed.

# 9. Boundary kernels and complex Hermitian scope

The derivation above assumed strict satellite blocks only to write \(C_j^{-1}\), \((I-C_j)^{-1}\), and exact-atom inverses without one-sided notation. The entropy inequality extends to every closed physical kernel of the form (2.2).

Given a closed-family kernel, regularize the entire measured kernel by
\[
K^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)K+\varepsilon I,
\qquad0<\varepsilon<1/2.
\tag{9.1}
\]
This preserves the family:
\[
a^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)a+\varepsilon,
\qquad
c^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)c+\varepsilon,
\]
\[
C_j^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)C_j+\varepsilon I,
\qquad
v_j^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)v_j,
\]
with the same amplitudes \(z_j\). Every regularized block is strict. Apply Theorem 6.1 and pass to the limit.

Every exact DPP atom probability is a polynomial in the real and imaginary matrix entries, and \(-p\log p\) is continuous at \(p=0\). Hence the full finite Shannon entropy is continuous on the closed positive-contraction set. This proves the result for complex Hermitian boundary kernels without assigning logarithms to impossible atoms.

# 10. Relation to earlier results and genuinely new scope

The proof is a conditional-lifting composition of established ingredients, but the resulting theorem has a scope not found in the bounded repository search.

- **C075** supplies arbitrary-dimensional exchangeable-core concavity but has no satellite amplitudes.
- **C059** supplies independent complex amplitudes around a freely moving two-dimensional core. It already closes the previously proposed four-site rank-two target and is therefore now removed from this task's open ledger.
- **C062** supplies independent coherent satellite blocks around a freely moving measured pair; its core dimension is two.
- The earlier task-local **rank-one bridge theorem** allows arbitrary coherent core/exterior sizes and mixed complex amplitudes, but the internal core and satellite kernels are fixed along the segment.
- The theorem here allows the exchangeable core to move globally in both \(a\) and \(c\) for arbitrary core size \(n\), simultaneously with arbitrary independent satellite amplitude changes.

The full affine direction can have rank growing with \(n\): an \(a\)-slope acts on an \((n-1)\)-dimensional eigenspace. Thus this is not merely a repackaged low-rank direction theorem.

# 12. Failed/revised route and next attack

### Revised route

The previous task-local next attack — global rank-two squared-strength concavity for two independent leaves over a two-coordinate coherent reservoir — is **retired** as a research target because C059 already proves the stronger free-core theorem. The earlier exploratory search remains valid numerical work, but it is no longer evidence toward an open repository scope.

### New next attack

The lifting proof reveals the exact obstruction to replacing the exchangeable core by a genuinely moving three-dimensional core.

For independent leaves or coherent satellites, conditioning produces an affine family
\[
M_y(\theta,s)=A(\theta)-\sum_js_jD_{j,y_j}.
\]
The outer entropy inherits joint concavity whenever the conditional core kernels remain in a convex domain on which core DPP entropy is known to be concave. The repository now contains several full-dimensional three-site concavity regions (weak-edge cone, strong-interaction certified regions, normalized compact charts, and boundary tubes), but they are not automatically invariant under the signed conditional shifts \(D_{j,y_j}\).

The next concrete target is therefore a **three-site invariant-core lifting theorem**:

1. select a convex three-site concavity region with an explicit Hessian gap, preferably the weak-edge cone or a certified compact chart;
2. derive finite matrix inequalities on fixed satellite coupling shapes guaranteeing every conditional shift stays inside that region for the whole physical parameter domain;
3. apply the general conditional chain rule to obtain a global higher-dimensional mixed-amplitude family with a genuinely nonexchangeable moving three-site core;
4. if global invariance is too strong, derive an explicit maximal strength polytope from the region inequalities, rather than merely a small norm ball.

A successful result would go beyond both C059's two-dimensional core and the present exchangeable-core theorem while reusing already verified three-site curvature certificates noncircularly.
