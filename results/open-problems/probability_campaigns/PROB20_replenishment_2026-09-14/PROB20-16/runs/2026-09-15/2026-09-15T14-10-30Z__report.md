# 2. Rank-one bridge set-up recalled

Partition the measured coordinates as
\[
E=V_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_m\sqcup O.
\]
Let
\[
A=\bigoplus_{r=1}^mA_r
\]
be block diagonal on the active blocks and let \(C\) be arbitrary Hermitian on the exterior. Assume the total active-to-exterior bridge has rank at most one:
\[
B=wv^*,
\qquad
w=(w_1,\ldots,w_m),
\qquad
w_r\in\mathbb C^{V_r},
\quad v\in\mathbb C^O.
\]
Suppose
\[
K_*=
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^*&C\end{pmatrix}
\]
is a positive contraction.

For \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\in\overline{\mathbb D}^m\), define
\[
Z(z)=\bigoplus_{r=1}^m z_rI_{V_r},
\qquad
K(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&Z(z)B\\
B^*Z(z)^*&C
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The recovered theorem proves:

1. every \(K(z)\) is a positive contraction;
2. the DPP law depends only on \(x_r=|z_r|^2\);
3. its exact atom vector is affine jointly in \(x\):
   \[
   p(x)=p^{(0)}+\sum_{r=1}^m x_rd_r;
   \tag{2.2}
   \]
4. therefore
   \[
   \mathcal H(x):=H(P_{K(z)})
   \]
   is jointly concave and coordinatewise nonincreasing;
5. consequently every complex affine amplitude path \(z_r(t)=\alpha_r+\beta_rt\) staying in the polydisk gives an actual affine-kernel path with concave determinantal entropy, without a common-sign radial restriction.

For strict kernels, (2.2) gives the exact squared-amplitude Hessian
\[
-D^2\mathcal H(x)[u,u]
=\sum_Y\frac{(\sum_ru_rd_{r,Y})^2}{p_Y(x)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The new work begins from (2.3).

# 3. New theorem: a dimension-free strong-concavity modulus in squared amplitudes

Assume every active bridge component \(w_r\) is nonzero and \(v\ne0\). Choose one exterior coordinate \(j\in O\) with \(v_j\ne0\). For each \(r\), choose \(i_r\in V_r\) with \(w_{i_r}\ne0\), and define
\[
\gamma_r:=|w_{i_r}|^2|v_j|^2>0,
\qquad
\lambda:=\frac1m\min_r\gamma_r^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — uniform strong concavity

At every strict point of the rank-one-bridge polydisk and every \(u\in\mathbb R^m\),
\[
\boxed{
-D^2\mathcal H(x)[u,u]\ge
\max_r u_r^2\gamma_r^2
\ge\lambda\|u\|_2^2.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus \(\mathcal H\) is uniformly strongly concave in the squared-amplitude coordinates, with an explicit modulus depending only on the chosen nonzero bridge entries, not on the current amplitudes or the remaining coherent blocks.

### Proof

Let
\[
q_Y:=\sum_ru_rd_{r,Y}
\]
be the atom tangent associated with the squared-amplitude direction \(u\). Fix \(r\). Consider the bounded statistic
\[
F_r(Y)=\mathbf 1_{\{i_r,j\}\subseteq Y}.
\]
Its expectation under \(P_{K(x)}\) is the two-point inclusion probability
\[
\mathbb EF_r
=\det K(x)_{\{i_r,j\}}
=A_{i_ri_r}C_{jj}-x_r|w_{i_r}|^2|v_j|^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]
No other \(x_s\) appears in this two-coordinate determinant. Hence
\[
\sum_Yq_YF_r(Y)
=\frac d{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\mathbb E_{x+su}F_r
=-u_r\gamma_r.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Cauchy-Schwarz in \(L^2(P_{K(x)})\) gives
\[
(u_r\gamma_r)^2
=\left(\sum_Yq_YF_r(Y)\right)^2
\le
\left(\sum_Y\frac{q_Y^2}{p_Y(x)}\right)
\left(\sum_Yp_Y(x)F_r(Y)^2\right).
\tag{3.5}
\]
Since \(F_r^2=F_r\) and its expectation is at most one,
\[
\sum_Y\frac{q_Y^2}{p_Y(x)}\ge u_r^2\gamma_r^2.
\]
Using (2.3) and maximizing over \(r\) gives the first bound in (3.2). Finally
\[
\max_r u_r^2\gamma_r^2
\ge\frac1m\sum_ru_r^2\min_s\gamma_s^2
=\lambda\|u\|_2^2.
\]
This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

### Consequence

The affine map \(x\mapsto p(x)\) is injective whenever every \(w_r\ne0\) and \(v\ne0\): the \(m\) pair-inclusion statistics (3.3) recover the \(m\) coordinates separately. Theorem 3.1 is a quantitative strengthening of that injectivity and of ordinary strict concavity.

# 4. New theorem: explicit radial derivative bound from pair mutual information

For fixed \(x_{-r}\), the point \(x_r=0\) disconnects the entire measured block \(V_r\) from its complement. Hence
\[
\mathcal H(0,x_{-r})-\mathcal H(x_r,x_{-r})
=I(X\cap V_r;X\cap(E\setminus V_r)).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Data processing under projection to the pair \((i_r,j)\) gives
\[
I(X\cap V_r;X\cap(E\setminus V_r))
\ge I(X_{i_r};X_j).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For any DPP,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i_r},X_j)
=-|K_{i_rj}|^2
=-x_r\gamma_r.
\tag{4.3}
\]
A binary joint law differs from the product of its marginals by the checkerboard vector
\[
(c,-c,-c,c),
\qquad c=\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i_r},X_j),
\]
so its total-variation distance from the product law is \(2|c|\). Pinsker's inequality in natural logarithms gives
\[
I(X_{i_r};X_j)
\ge2\operatorname{TV}^2
=8x_r^2\gamma_r^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H(0,x_{-r})-\mathcal H(x_r,x_{-r})
\ge8x_r^2\gamma_r^2.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because the one-variable slice is concave, its derivative at \(x_r>0\) lies below the secant from zero:
\[
\partial_{x_r}\mathcal H(x)
\le
\frac{\mathcal H(x)-\mathcal H(0,x_{-r})}{x_r}.
\]
Combining with (4.5) yields the explicit derivative estimate
\[
\boxed{
-\partial_{x_r}\mathcal H(x)
\ge8x_r\gamma_r^2.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
The right side vanishes at the decoupled face, as expected: entropy loss begins quadratically in \(x_r\), equivalently quartically in the raw bridge amplitude \(|z_r|\).

# 5. Quantitative curvature for arbitrary complex affine mixed-sign paths

Let
\[
z_r(t)=\alpha_r+\beta_rt,
\qquad
x_r(t)=|z_r(t)|^2.
\]
Then
\[
x_r'(t)=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{z_r(t)}\beta_r),
\qquad
x_r''(t)=2|\beta_r|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The chain rule is
\[
H''(t)
=x'(t)^T\nabla^2\mathcal H(x(t))x'(t)
+\sum_r\partial_{x_r}\mathcal H(x(t))x_r''(t).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Using Theorems 3.1 and (4.6),
\[
\boxed{
-H''(t)
\ge
\lambda\|x'(t)\|_2^2
+16\sum_r|\beta_r|^2x_r(t)\gamma_r^2.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
This is an explicit nonnegative decomposition valid for arbitrary complex affine amplitude motions, including opposite radial derivatives and phase rotation.

### Strictness

If the affine path is nonconstant, then (5.3) is strictly positive at every time except possibly a time at which **all moving amplitudes vanish simultaneously**. Such a common zero can occur at at most one parameter value unless the path is constant in those coordinates. Thus every nonconstant path in this family has a strict Jensen gap between distinct endpoints, even when its pointwise second derivative can vanish at one common-zero instant.

### Uniform margin criterion

If there exists an active coordinate \(r\) with
\[
\beta_r\ne0,
\qquad
|z_r(t)|\ge\rho>0
\quad(0\le t\le1),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
-H''(t)\ge16|\beta_r|^2\rho^2\gamma_r^2=:\kappa>0
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
uniformly on the whole segment. This replaces the earlier qualitative compactness argument with an explicit curvature margin.

# 6. Full-dimensional open stability tube

Take a strict rank-one-bridge segment satisfying the uniform condition (5.4). Let its endpoint pair be \((K_0,K_1)\), its direction \(D=K_1-K_0\), and let
\[
\mu:=\min_{0\le t\le1}
\min\{\lambda_{\min}(K(t)),\lambda_{\min}(I-K(t))\}>0.
\]
The atom probabilities and the Shannon entropy Hessian are analytic on the strict positive-contraction domain. Hence
\[
(K,D)\longmapsto D^2H_K[D,D]
\]
is continuous.

The explicit bound (5.4) gives
\[
D^2H_{K(t)}[D,D]\le-\kappa
\]
uniformly in \(t\). Uniform continuity on the compact segment therefore supplies \(\varepsilon>0\) such that any arbitrary Hermitian endpoint perturbations \(E_0,E_1\) with
\[
\max(\|E_0\|_{\rm op},\|E_1\|_{\rm op})<\varepsilon
\]
produce a strict perturbed segment
\[
\widetilde K(t)=(1-t)(K_0+E_0)+t(K_1+E_1)
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{\widetilde K(t)})<0
\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
The perturbed endpoints need not retain rank-one bridge structure, block diagonality, fixed diagonal blocks, or any replacement-channel representation. Thus each quantitatively nondegenerate structured segment seeds a full-dimensional open set of genuinely higher-rank endpoint pairs satisfying Lyons concavity.

No explicit universal \(\varepsilon\) is claimed in this run; (5.4) supplies the numerator for such an estimate, but a useful denominator requires a chosen local third-derivative/Hessian-Lipschitz bound.

# 8. Exploratory rank-two search and failure to close it

The next natural extension is a bridge of rank two. As preparation, an exploratory numerical search considered two selected singleton blocks, arbitrary coherent exteriors of sizes one through four, and arbitrary complex two-row couplings. The entropy was parameterized by the two squared row amplitudes. Exact corner interpolation gives a bilinear atom model
\[
p(x,y)=p_{00}+xa+yb+xyc.
\]
Across 64,000 randomly sampled strict cases and interior points, no positive eigenvalue of the squared-amplitude entropy Hessian was found above numerical tolerance; the largest observed eigenvalue was approximately \(-1.7\times10^{-11}\), occurring near a nearly decoupled direction.

This is **not** a theorem or certificate. A direct conditional reduction shows why the proof is harder: after conditioning on the exterior, the two selected bits have a DPP kernel
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha-a x&-\sqrt{xy}\,c\\
-\sqrt{xy}\,\overline c&\beta-b y
\end{pmatrix},
\]
so its four atom probabilities are bilinear rather than affine. For arbitrary binary tables of the same bilinear form, joint entropy concavity can fail; therefore a proof must use additional determinantal compatibility across exterior fibers, not only pointwise two-bit Shannon concavity.

This closes the naive affine-atom route and identifies the precise rank-two obstruction: control the mixed atom-acceleration vector \(c\) by the Fisher term after summing the actual determinantal exterior fibers.

No universal rank-two claim is made.

# 11. Next concrete attack

The next target is the minimal genuinely rank-two bridge:

- two active singleton blocks;
- arbitrary coherent exterior;
- arbitrary two-row complex coupling matrix.

For its bilinear atom representation
\[
p(x,y)=p_{00}+xa+yb+xyc,
\]
the exact entropy Hessian is
\[
H_{xx}=-\sum\frac{(a+yc)^2}{p},
\qquad
H_{yy}=-\sum\frac{(b+xc)^2}{p},
\]
\[
H_{xy}=-\sum\frac{(a+yc)(b+xc)}p-\sum c\log p.
\]
The open obligation is to show
\[
H_{xx}H_{yy}-H_{xy}^2\ge0
\]
using the determinantal structure of the coefficient vectors across the exterior fibers, or to produce an exact counterexample. The numerical search suggests looking first for a Fisher/conditional-logarithmic-mean Gram inequality rather than a counterexample.

A secondary quantitative goal is to combine (5.4) with an explicit third-derivative bound on a strict spectral slab, producing a computable operator-norm radius for the open stability tube.
