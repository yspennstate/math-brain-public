# 2. General setting

Let
\[
E=L\sqcup O,
\qquad L=\{1,\ldots,m\},
\]
with independent measured leaves
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_m),
\qquad 0<d_i<1,
\]
and an arbitrary strict coherent reservoir
\[
0<C<I.
\]
Let
\[
B\in\mathbb C^{m\times |O|}
\]
be arbitrary. For amplitudes \(z_i\in\mathbb C\), put
\[
K(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
D&ZB\\
B^*Z^*&C
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Z=\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\ldots,z_m),
\qquad
x_i=|z_i|^2.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Diagonal phase gauges show the complete DPP law depends only on \(x=(x_i)\).

By the preceding report, every exact atom probability has a multi-affine expansion
\[
\boxed{
p_\omega(x)=\sum_{J\subseteq L}c_{J,\omega}x_J,
\qquad x_J:=\prod_{j\in J}x_j.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
At \(x=0\), all atom probabilities are strictly positive. Put
\[
p_*:=\min_\omega c_{\varnothing,\omega}>0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For every active leaf \(i\), define the exact decoupled Fisher curvature
\[
\lambda_i
:=
\sum_\omega\frac{c_{\{i\},\omega}^2}{c_{\varnothing,\omega}}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
The prior Hessian theorem gives
\[
\nabla^2\mathcal H(0)
=-\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m),
\qquad
\mathcal H(x):=H(P_{K(\sqrt x)}).
\tag{2.5}
\]
If every retained attachment row is nonzero, then every \(\lambda_i>0\). Set
\[
\lambda_*:=\min_i\lambda_i>0.
\tag{2.6}
\]

# 3. A finite coefficient certificate

Fix any trial radius \(r_0>0\) for which the matrix family is physically feasible. Define
\[
\Delta_0(r_0)
:=
\max_\omega
\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq L}
|c_{J,\omega}|r_0^{|J|}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Assume
\[
\boxed{\Delta_0(r_0)\le p_*/2.}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Then every atom on the cube \([0,r_0]^m\) satisfies
\[
p_\omega(x)\ge \mu:=p_*/2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For \(s=1,2,3\), define the coefficient derivative bounds
\[
M_s(r_0)
:=
\max_{\substack{\omega,\ I\subseteq L\\ |I|=s}}
\sum_{J\supseteq I}
|c_{J,\omega}|r_0^{|J|-s},
\tag{3.4}
\]
with \(M_s=0\) if \(s>m\). Multi-affinity immediately gives, throughout the cube,
\[
|\partial_Ip_\omega(x)|\le M_{|I|}(r_0)
\qquad (|I|\le3).
\tag{3.5}
\]
Let \(N=2^{|E|}\) be the number of atoms.

### Lemma 3.1 — uniform third derivative bound

For every indices \(a,b,k\), throughout \([0,r_0]^m\),
\[
\boxed{
|\partial_{abk}^3\mathcal H(x)|\le T(r_0),
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
where the fully explicit rational-algebraic bound
\[
\boxed{
T(r_0)
:=N\left[
\frac{M_3}{\mu}
+
\frac{3M_2M_1}{\mu}
+
\frac{M_1^3}{\mu^2}
\right]
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
is valid.

### Proof
For a finite positive atom law,
\[
H_{ab}
=-\sum_\omega p_{ab}\log p
-\sum_\omega\frac{p_ap_b}{p}.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Differentiating once more gives
\[
H_{abk}
=-\sum p_{abk}\log p
-\sum\frac{p_{ab}p_k+p_{ak}p_b+p_{bk}p_a}{p}
+\sum\frac{p_ap_bp_k}{p^2}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
Because \(0<p\le1\) and \(p\ge\mu\),
\[
|\log p|\le-\log\mu\le \mu^{-1}.
\tag{3.10}
\]
Apply (3.5), bound each denominator below by \(\mu\), and sum over the \(N\) atoms. This gives (3.7). \(\square\)

# 4. Effective joint-concavity radius

For \(x\in[0,r]^m\subseteq[0,r_0]^m\), integrate the third derivative bound along the segment \(t\mapsto tx\):
\[
|H_{ab}(x)-H_{ab}(0)|
\le
\sum_{k=1}^m |x_k|\sup_t|H_{abk}(tx)|
\le mrT(r_0).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Hence every row sum of the Hessian perturbation is at most
\[
m^2rT(r_0),
\]
and therefore
\[
\|\nabla^2\mathcal H(x)-\nabla^2\mathcal H(0)\|_{\rm op}
\le m^2rT(r_0).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Combining with (2.5), Weyl's inequality gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2\mathcal H(x))
\le
-\lambda_*+m^2rT(r_0).
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — explicit weak-coupling certificate

Under (3.2), define
\[
\boxed{
r_{\rm cert}
:=
\min\left\{
r_0,
\frac{\lambda_*}{2m^2T(r_0)}
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Then on the full cube
\[
0\le x_i\le r_{\rm cert}
\]
we have the quantitative Hessian bound
\[
\boxed{
\nabla^2\mathcal H(x)
\preceq
-\frac{\lambda_*}{2}I.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Therefore \(\mathcal H\) is jointly strongly concave there.

Since the preceding report proves coordinatewise monotonicity globally, every complex affine amplitude path
\[
z_i(t)=\alpha_i+\beta_it
\]
whose squared amplitudes stay in this certified cube satisfies Lyons concavity, with no common-sign restriction on the radial motions and no rank restriction on \(B\).

This turns the previous qualitative existence theorem into a finite reproducible certificate.

# 5. A simple physical-feasibility radius

The certificate needs a physically feasible trial cube. This too can be bounded explicitly without diagonalizing every intermediate kernel.

Let
\[
\sigma
:=
\min\left\{
\min_i d_i,
\min_i(1-d_i),
\lambda_{\min}(C),
\lambda_{\min}(I-C)
\right\}>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]
At \(x=0\), both \(K_0=D\oplus C\) and \(I-K_0\) are bounded below by \(\sigma I\). The off-diagonal perturbation has operator norm
\[
\left\|
\begin{pmatrix}0&ZB\\B^*Z^*&0\end{pmatrix}
\right\|_{\rm op}
=\|ZB\|_{\rm op}
\le\sqrt r\,\|B\|_{\rm op}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus every squared-strength cube with
\[
\boxed{
r\le r_{\rm phys}:=rac{\sigma^2}{4\|B\|_{\rm op}^2}}
\tag{5.3}
\]
is strict, with both \(K\) and \(I-K\) bounded below by \(\sigma I/2\). One may replace \(\|B\|_{\rm op}\) by the larger Frobenius norm to obtain a purely entrywise computable conservative bound.

Hence a fully automatic certificate can choose any
\[
r_0\le r_{\rm phys}
\]
and shrink it until (3.2) holds; existence follows because \(\Delta_0(r_0)\to0\) as \(r_0\downarrow0\).

# 6. Exact rational four-site rank-two example

Take two leaves and a two-site coherent reservoir:
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(2/5,3/5),
\qquad
C=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/10\\
1/10&1/2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/5&1/8\\
1/7&-1/6
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
The attachment matrix is genuinely rank two:
\[
\det B=-43/840\ne0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

At the decoupled face, the smallest exact atom is
\[
p_*=24/625.
\tag{6.3}
\]
The two exact squared-strength curvatures are
\[
\lambda_1=127625/3833856\approx0.0332889393863515,
\]
\[
\lambda_2=64380625/2912547456\approx0.0221045754524523.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Thus
\[
\lambda_*=64380625/2912547456.
\]

For the conservative trial radius
\[
r_0=1/4,
\tag{6.5}
\]
the exact coefficient bounds are
\[
\Delta_0(r_0)\approx0.00798531391723356
< p_*/2=0.0192,
\]
\[
M_1\approx0.02034261621315193,
\qquad
M_2\approx0.002620464852607710,
\qquad M_3=0.
\tag{6.6}
\]
The physical cube is also certified: using \(\|B\|_{\rm op}\le\|B\|_F\),
\[
\|B\|_F^2=73249/705600,
\qquad
r_{\rm phys}\ge28224/73249\approx0.38531584>1/4.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Equation (3.7) gives
\[
T(r_0)=13323094003/24700642848
\approx0.498642031603405.
\tag{6.8}
\]
Therefore the fully explicit certificate is
\[
\boxed{
r_{\rm cert}
=
\frac{93034432512000}{16789602147896279}
\approx0.00554119339413037.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]
On the complete square
\[
0\le x_1,x_2\le r_{\rm cert},
\]
we have the rigorous quantitative bound
\[
\boxed{
\nabla^2\mathcal H(x)
\preceq
-\frac{64380625}{2\cdot2912547456}I
\approx-0.0110522877262262\,I.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

A dense numerical grid over the certified square found the actual largest Hessian eigenvalue to be approximately
\[
-0.0220991729293787,
\]
so the certificate is conservative by roughly a factor of two in curvature. The proof of (6.10), however, is the exact coefficient estimate above, not the grid.

# 9. Failed route and next attack

The minimal genuinely new global obstruction remains two independent leaves over a two-site coherent reservoir with full-rank \(2\times2\) attachment matrix. In squared strengths the exact atom law is bilinear:
\[
p(x,y)=p_{00}+xa+yb+xyc.
\]
The Hessian is
\[
H_{xx}=-\sum\frac{(a+yc)^2}{p},
\qquad
H_{yy}=-\sum\frac{(b+xc)^2}{p},
\]
\[
H_{xy}
=-\sum\frac{(a+yc)(b+xc)}p
-\sum c\log p.
\tag{9.1}
\]
The effective-radius theorem controls a neighborhood of \((0,0)\), but not the whole physical rectangle.

For this four-site case, conditional-on-reservoir calculations give the rigid identity
\[
q_Y\det(BQ_Y^{-1}B^*)
=(-1)^{|O\setminus Y|}|\det B|^2.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Thus the bilinear acceleration coefficients have common absolute magnitude and checkerboard parity across the four reservoir fibers. The next concrete attack is to insert (9.2) into the logarithmic acceleration term in (9.1), rewrite it as a four-fiber discrete mixed derivative of the conditional two-leaf log odds ratio, and seek a logarithmic-mean/Fisher domination. If that inequality fails, this is also the smallest setting suitable for an interval-certified counterexample.

A second quantitative attack is to replace the coarse global third-derivative bound (3.7) by atomwise weighted bounds, which should enlarge \(r_{\rm cert}\) substantially while remaining exact.
