No universal proof or counterexample is claimed. No historical-priority claim is made. Self-review is not independent verification.

# 2. Set-up: independent measured leaves over an arbitrary coherent reservoir

Let the measured set split as
\[
E=L\sqcup O,
\qquad L=\{1,\ldots,m\}.
\]
Fix
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_m),
\qquad 0<d_i<1,
\]
and an arbitrary strict coherent reservoir
\[
0<C<I
\]
on \(O\). Let
\[
B\in\mathbb C^{m\times |O|}
\]
be arbitrary; its rows may be linearly independent and its rank is unrestricted.

For complex amplitudes \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\), define
\[
K(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
D&ZB\\
B^*Z^*&C
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Z=\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\ldots,z_m).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Put
\[
x_i=|z_i|^2.
\]
A diagonal phase gauge on the leaf coordinates removes the phases of every \(z_i\) without changing any principal determinant. Thus the full DPP law depends on the amplitudes only through \(x=(x_i)\); write
\[
\mathcal H(x):=H(P_{K(z)}).
\]

Because \(D\oplus C\) is strict, there exists a nonzero cube \([0,\varepsilon_{\rm phys}]^m\) on which every \(K(\sqrt x)\) is a strict positive contraction. No rank restriction on \(B\) is needed for this local feasibility.

# 3. Exact multi-affinity of the atom law

### Proposition 3.1
Every inclusion probability and every exact atom probability of \(P_{K(z)}\) is a multi-affine polynomial in the squared amplitudes \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\).

### Proof
Fix \(R\subseteq L\) and \(T\subseteq O\). For strict \(C\), Schur complementing \(C_T\) gives
\[
\det K_{R\cup T}
=\det C_T\,\det\!\left(D_R-S_RM_TS_R\right),
\qquad
M_T=B_{R,T}C_T^{-1}B_{R,T}^*,
\tag{3.1}
\]
where
\[
S_R=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{x_i}:i\in R).
\]
Since \(D_R\) is diagonal, the principal-minor expansion gives
\[
\det(D_R-S_RM_TS_R)
=
\sum_{J\subseteq R}
(-1)^{|J|}
\det(M_T[J])
\left(\prod_{i\in J}x_i\right)
\left(\prod_{i\in R\setminus J}d_i\right).
\tag{3.2}
\]
Every variable appears with exponent at most one. Exact atom probabilities are Boolean Möbius transforms of inclusion probabilities, so they are multi-affine as well. \(\square\)

Thus for every exact configuration \(\omega\),
\[
p_\omega(x)=
\sum_{J\subseteq L}c_{J,\omega}\prod_{i\in J}x_i.
\tag{3.3}
\]
In particular, every one-variable slice is affine in that strength.

# 4. Global coordinatewise entropy monotonicity

Fix all strengths except \(x_i\). At \(x_i=0\), leaf \(i\) is completely decoupled from the rest of the measured coordinates. Its Bernoulli marginal \(\operatorname{Bern}(d_i)\) and the marginal on \(E\setminus\{i\}\) do not depend on \(x_i\). Hence Shannon subadditivity gives
\[
\mathcal H(0,x_{-i})\ge \mathcal H(x_i,x_{-i}).
\tag{4.1}
\]
By Proposition 3.1 the full atom vector is affine in \(x_i\), so Shannon concavity makes the slice \(x_i\mapsto\mathcal H(x_i,x_{-i})\) concave throughout every feasible interval. A concave function whose maximum is at the left endpoint is nonincreasing. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H\text{ is coordinatewise nonincreasing on its whole physical strength domain.}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
This part is global and does not require weak coupling.

# 5. Reservoir rank-one Fisher coefficients

Let
\[
q_y=P_C(Y=y)>0
\]
be the exact reservoir atom law. Write the \(i\)-th attachment row of \(B\) as \(b_i^*\), with column vector
\[
u_i:=b_i\in\mathbb C^{|O|}.
\]
For the rank-one reservoir perturbation \(C+t u_i u_i^*\), every exact atom probability is affine in \(t\). Define its atom derivative
\[
a_{i,y}:=
\frac d{dt}\Big|_{t=0}P_{C+t u_i u_i^*}(Y=y).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Define the finite measured Fisher coefficient
\[
J_i:=\sum_y\frac{a_{i,y}^2}{q_y}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
If \(u_i\ne0\), then \(J_i>0\): otherwise every atom derivative would vanish, hence every inclusion-probability derivative would vanish, contradicting
\[
\frac d{dt}\Big|_{0}P_{C+t u_i u_i^*}(j\in Y)=|(u_i)_j|^2>0
\]
for a nonzero component.

A useful elementary lower bound follows from Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the centered singleton statistic:
\[
\boxed{
J_i\ge
\frac{|(u_i)_j|^4}{C_{jj}(1-C_{jj})}
\qquad\text{for every }j\text{ with }(u_i)_j\ne0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

# 6. Exact diagonalization of the squared-strength entropy Hessian at the decoupled face

At \(x=0\), the full law factors:
\[
P_0
=
\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^m\operatorname{Bern}(d_i)\right)
\otimes P_C.
\tag{6.1}
\]
For a leaf bit \(s_i\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
R_i(1)=\frac1{d_i},
\qquad
R_i(0)=-\frac1{1-d_i}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E R_i=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E R_i^2=\frac1{d_i(1-d_i)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Conditioning on the value of leaf \(i\), its attached reservoir kernel is exactly
\[
C-x_iR_i(s_i)u_i u_i^*.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Therefore, differentiating the joint atom law at the decoupled face gives the factorization
\[
\partial_{x_i}p_0(s,y)
=-\mu(s)R_i(s_i)a_{i,y},
\tag{6.5}
\]
where \(\mu\) is the product leaf law.

### Theorem 6.1 — exact decoupled Hessian

At \(x=0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\partial^2\mathcal H}{\partial x_i\partial x_j}(0)
=
-\delta_{ij}\,
\frac{J_i}{d_i(1-d_i)}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

### Proof
For a finite atom law,
\[
H_{ij}
=-\sum_\omega\frac{p_i(\omega)p_j(\omega)}{p(\omega)}
-\sum_\omega p_{ij}(\omega)\log p(\omega).
\tag{6.7}
\]
Pure second atom derivatives vanish because the atom law is affine in every individual \(x_i\).

For \(i=j\), inserting (6.5) into the Fisher term and using (6.3) gives
\[
-H_{ii}(0)
=\frac1{d_i(1-d_i)}\sum_y\frac{a_{i,y}^2}{q_y}
=\frac{J_i}{d_i(1-d_i)}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

For \(i\ne j\), the Fisher cross term vanishes because the leaf factors are independent and centered:
\[
\sum_\omega\frac{p_i p_j}{p_0}
\propto (\mathbb ER_i)(\mathbb ER_j)=0.
\tag{6.9}
\]
The atom-acceleration term also vanishes. Indeed the full joint leaf marginal remains the fixed product law for all \(x\), while the full reservoir marginal remains \(P_C\). Hence for
\[
e_{ij}:=\partial_{x_i x_j}p\big|_{0}
\]
we have
\[
\sum_y e_{ij}(s,y)=0\quad\text{for each leaf configuration }s,
\qquad
\sum_s e_{ij}(s,y)=0\quad\text{for each reservoir atom }y.
\tag{6.10}
\]
But
\[
\log p_0(s,y)=\log\mu(s)+\log q_y,
\]
so (6.10) implies
\[
\sum_{s,y}e_{ij}(s,y)\log p_0(s,y)=0.
\tag{6.11}
\]
Thus \(H_{ij}(0)=0\). \(\square\)

If every attachment row is nonzero, (6.6) is negative definite.

# 7. New coherent-reservoir quartic entropy law

Because the atom law is polynomial and every decoupled atom is positive, \(\mathcal H(x)\) is analytic near \(x=0\). Coordinatewise monotonicity gives \(\nabla\mathcal H(0)=0\), and Theorem 6.1 gives its complete quadratic term. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H(x)
=
H(P_D)+H(P_C)
-rac12\sum_{i=1}^m
\frac{J_i}{d_i(1-d_i)}x_i^2
+O(\|x\|^3).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Equivalently, in the raw complex attachment amplitudes,
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{K(z)})
=
H(P_D)+H(P_C)
-rac12\sum_{i=1}^m
\frac{J_i}{d_i(1-d_i)}|z_i|^4
+O(\|z\|^6).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

There are **no mixed quartic terms**, even when the reservoir is fully coherent and the attachment rows have arbitrary mutual angles and arbitrary rank. The first interaction between distinct attachment rows appears only at degree six or higher in the raw amplitudes.

For a one-coordinate reservoir \(C=(c)\),
\[
J_i=\frac{|u_i|^4}{c(1-c)},
\]
and (7.2) reduces to the previously known diagonal-kernel quartic coefficient. Thus (7.2) is a coherent-reservoir extension of that local law.

# 8. New theorem: a nonzero weak-coupling mixed-sign domain for arbitrary-rank attachments

Assume every nontrivial attachment row \(u_i\ne0\). Define
\[
\lambda_*:=
\min_i\frac{J_i}{d_i(1-d_i)}>0.
\tag{8.1}
\]
By continuity of the strength Hessian and Theorem 6.1, there exists
\[
\varepsilon_{\rm conc}>0
\]
such that throughout the cube
\[
0\le x_i\le\varepsilon_{\rm conc}
\]
we have strict feasibility and
\[
\boxed{
\nabla_x^2\mathcal H(x)\preceq-\frac{\lambda_*}{2}I.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal H\) is jointly strongly concave in **all** squared row strengths on a nonzero cube, with no rank restriction on \(B\).

Now let
\[
z_i(t)=\alpha_i+\beta_it
\]
be arbitrary complex affine functions whose squared moduli remain in that cube. Since \(|z_i(t)|^2\) is convex in \(t\), joint concavity of \(\mathcal H\) together with the global coordinatewise monotonicity (4.2) gives
\[
\boxed{
t\longmapsto H(P_{K(z(t))})\text{ is concave.}
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
The radial derivatives may have opposite signs; phases may rotate; the attachment matrix \(B\) may have any rank up to \(\min(m,|O|)\).

This theorem supplies the mixed-sign arbitrary-orientation extension that is missing from C021 in a genuine open neighborhood of the decoupled face. It is not implied by C059/C062, which rely on a measured two-dimensional core, nor by X058, which assumes a common reservoir direction.

### Strict finite chords
If a changed amplitude is attached through a nonzero row, a nonconstant complex affine chord has a strict entropy Jensen gap inside the weak cube. If the endpoint squared strengths differ, strong concavity in \(x\) gives strictness directly. If the endpoint moduli agree but the phases differ, strict convexity of squared modulus along the complex chord makes the midpoint strength strictly smaller, and coordinatewise entropy monotonicity is strict away from zero because the leaf has nonzero DPP covariance with some reservoir coordinate.

# 9. What this does and does not settle

### Newly proved here

- arbitrary reservoir dimension and arbitrary reservoir coherence;
- arbitrary-rank complex attachment matrix \(B\);
- exact multi-affine atom law in squared leaf strengths;
- global separate concavity and monotonicity in each strength;
- exact diagonal strength Hessian at the fully decoupled face;
- additive coherent-reservoir quartic entropy loss with no mixed quartic terms;
- a nonzero joint-concavity cube in all strengths;
- arbitrary mixed-sign/phase affine amplitude concavity inside that cube.

### Not proved

- global joint concavity in the strengths for arbitrary-rank \(B\);
- a universal or dimension-free explicit value of \(\varepsilon_{\rm conc}\);
- arbitrary motion of the reservoir kernel \(C\);
- the unrestricted Lyons conjecture.

The prior rank-one bridge theorem remains stronger globally when the entire attachment matrix has rank one. The present theorem is stronger in allowed attachment rank but local in strength.

# 10. Rank-two frontier after this run

The earlier exploratory search considered two independent leaves over a coherent exterior with a rank-two attachment matrix. The exact atom law then has the bilinear form
\[
p(x,y)=p_{00}+xa+yb+xyc.
\tag{10.1}
\]
The entropy Hessian is
\[
H_{xx}=-\sum\frac{(a+yc)^2}{p},
\qquad
H_{yy}=-\sum\frac{(b+xc)^2}{p},
\tag{10.2}
\]
\[
H_{xy}=
-\sum\frac{(a+yc)(b+xc)}p
-\sum c\log p.
\tag{10.3}
\]
The new theorem proves that this Hessian is negative definite in a neighborhood of \((x,y)=(0,0)\), for every strict coherent exterior and every nonzero pair of attachment rows. The unresolved obligation is whether
\[
H_{xx}H_{yy}-H_{xy}^2\ge0
\]
continues throughout the entire physical strength domain.

A previous floating-point search tested 64,000 strict samples for exterior sizes one through four and found no positive Hessian direction, but this remains evidence only. The next analytic target is to control the mixed atom-acceleration term in (10.3), exploiting the determinantal constraints relating \(c\) to the first-order atom tangents.

A particularly promising exact subcase is a two-coordinate exterior. For a fixed exterior atom \(Y\), if
\[
M_Y=BQ_Y^{-1}B^*,
\]
then
\[
q_Y\det M_Y
=\pm|\det B|^2,
\]
with the sign determined only by the exterior exact-atom parity. Thus the bilinear acceleration coefficient has constant magnitude across exterior fibers. This parity rigidity may allow the logarithmic acceleration in (10.3) to be paired against Fisher surplus by a four-bit Walsh/logarithmic-mean inequality.
