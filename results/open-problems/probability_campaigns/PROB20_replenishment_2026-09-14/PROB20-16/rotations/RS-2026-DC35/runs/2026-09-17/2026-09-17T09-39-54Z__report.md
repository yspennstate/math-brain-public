## 5. Proof of Theorem 4.1

### 5.1 The coordinate envelope and mixed-cell curvatures

The least separately concave majorant remains `(2.9)`. For completeness, if `u` is any separately concave majorant of the five data, then

\[
w=u-st
\]

is separately concave, nonnegative at the four corners, and

\[
w(a,b)\ge\delta.
\]

Concavity on the line `t=b` gives

\[
w(s,b)\ge \delta T_a(s),
\]

and then concavity on every vertical line gives

\[
w(s,t)\ge\delta T_a(s)T_b(t).
\]

Thus

\[
u\ge U_\delta.
\tag{5.1}
\]

On each of the four coordinate rectangles, `U_delta` is bilinear. Along the third-direction line `t=r-alpha s`, its second derivative is

\[
\frac{d^2}{ds^2}U_\delta(s,r-\alpha s)
=
\begin{cases}
-2\alpha\left(1+\dfrac{\delta}{ab}\right),&SW,\\[2mm]
2\alpha q_N,&NW,\\[1mm]
2\alpha q_S,&SE,\\[1mm]
-2\alpha\left(1+\dfrac{\delta}{(1-a)(1-b)}\right),&NE.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence `NW` and `SE` are exactly the two convex defects in the regime `(4.10)`.

### 5.2 Exact local chord formula

On a defective interval `[s_0,s_1]`, the restriction has constant positive second derivative `2 alpha q`, hence quadratic coefficient `alpha q`. Its endpoint chord exceeds the quadratic by exactly

\[
\alpha q(s-s_0)(s_1-s).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equations `(4.8)` and `(4.9)` are therefore not guessed corrections: they are exactly the endpoint chords on the maximal `NW` and `SE` portions of every third-direction line.

The only possible failure is global: two individually correct defect chords might require replacement by a longer chord across the intervening concave piece, or their assembly might destroy horizontal/vertical concavity.

The remainder of the proof excludes both mechanisms.

### 5.3 No long chord across the two defects

Write

\[

\tag{5.4}

the value of `r` on the line through the spike.

For `r<r_*`, the two defects, when both are present, are separated by a southwest concave segment. For `r>r_*`, they are separated by a northeast concave segment. At `r=r_*`, the two defective cells meet at the spike.

Use increasing `s` as the orientation along a line. Concavity means that the line slope must be nonincreasing.

#### The central line `r=r_*`

There are three endpoint-order possibilities.

If `r_*<=1`, the northwest chord starts on `s=0` and the southeast chord exits through `t=0`. Their slope gap is

\[

\tag{5.5}

If `1<r_*<alpha`, both outer endpoints are on `t=1` and `t=0` respectively, and

\[

\tag{5.6}

If `r_*>=alpha`, the northwest chord starts on `t=1` and the southeast chord exits through `s=1`, giving

(1-a)(1-b)

>0.
\tag{5.7}

Thus no central long chord is needed in any compactified breakpoint order.

### 5.5 Minimality

Let `M` be any `D_2`-concave majorant of the five height data.

Because coordinate directions are in `D_2`, `M` is separately concave. By `(5.1)`,

\[
M\ge U_\delta.
\]

Restrict to any line parallel to `d=(1,-alpha)`. Since `M` is concave there and majorizes `U_delta`, it majorizes the linewise least concave majorant of `U_delta`. By `(5.16)`, that majorant is `V`.

Thus

\[
M\ge V.
\tag{5.31}
\]

Since `V` itself is `D_2`-concave and interpolates/majorizes the five height data, it is the least `D_2`-concave majorant.

### 5.6 The lower boundary remains `h`

The upward spike cannot raise the greatest `D_2`-convex minorant.

The old `h` is `D_2`-convex and satisfies

\[
h(a,b)\le ab<ab+\delta,
\]

so it remains an admissible lower minorant of all five data.

Conversely, every `D_2`-convex minorant admissible for `K_5` is in particular admissible for the old four corners, whose exact greatest minorant is `h`.

Therefore the lower boundary remains exactly `(2.5)`.

### 5.7 Outer inclusion by separators

Since `V` is `D_2`-concave,

\[

\]

is `D`-convex and is nonpositive on the five data. It forces

\[

on the functional hull.

Since `h` is `D_2`-convex,

\[

\]

is `D`-convex and forces

\[
z\ge h(s,t).
\]

The coordinate-box separators give `0<=s,t<=1`. Hence

\[
\operatorname{conv}_D(K_5)
\subseteq
\{0\le s,t\le1,\ h\le z\le V\}.
\tag{5.32}

### 5.8 Reverse inclusion by finite lamination

The reverse inclusion is constructive.

First obtain the four cross-grid nodes

\[
(a,0,0),\quad
(a,1,a),\quad
(0,b,0),\quad
(1,b,b)
\tag{5.33}
\]

by coordinate-direction laminations from the four corners.

Together with the spike and the original corners these are the nine lifted nodes over

\[
\{0,a,1\}\times\{0,b,1\}.
\]

On each of the four coordinate rectangles, `U_delta` is bilinear. Hence every graph point

\[
(s,t,U_\delta(s,t))
\]

is obtained from the corresponding four grid nodes by two coordinate-direction laminations.

On `NW` and `SE`, equation `(5.3)` shows that

\[
(s,t,V(s,t))
\]

is a convex combination, along the allowed third-direction plane, of the two graph-`U_delta` points at the endpoints of the appropriate defect interval. Thus every graph-`V` point is obtained after one additional third-direction lamination. On `SW` and `NE`, `V=U_delta`.

The graph of `h` already lies in a finite lamination stage of the old four-corner hull by the pinned exact `K_4` theorem.

Finally, the vertical direction belongs to every vertical plane in `D=D_2 x R`, so for each fixed `(s,t)` the entire segment

\[
\{(s,t,z):h(s,t)\le z\le V(s,t)\}
\]

is obtained by one vertical lamination between the lower and upper graph points.

Therefore

\[
\{0\le s,t\le1,\ h\le z\le V\}
\subseteq
K_5^{(\infty,D)}
\subseteq
\operatorname{conv}_D(K_5).
\tag{5.34}
\]

Together with `(5.32)`, this proves `(4.11)`.

### 5.9 Semialgebraicity

The functions `ell_N,u_N,ell_S,u_S` are finite maxima/minima of affine functions. On each of the finitely many regions determined by

\[
s=a,\quad t=b,\quad r=1,\quad r=r_*,\quad r=\alpha,
