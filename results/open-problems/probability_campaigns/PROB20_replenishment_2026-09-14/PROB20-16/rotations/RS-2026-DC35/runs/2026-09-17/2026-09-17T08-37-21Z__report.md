## 2. Exact blocking statement

The source-complete blocker is not a single inequality. It is the missing equivalence between geometric data of the projective direction set and finite real-algebraic definability of an infinite family of Jensen constraints:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Characterize all cones }D\subset\mathbb R^d\text{ such that }
\forall K\subset\mathbb R^d\text{ finite},\quad
\operatorname{conv}_D(K)\text{ is semialgebraic}.}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

After the reductions already proved in the preceding reports, the operative finite-dimensional obstruction can be written as follows. For ordered support `P=(p_1,...,p_N)` and `x`, define

\[
\Lambda_D(P,x)
=
\left\{\lambda\in\Delta_{N-1}:
 f(x)\le\sum_i\lambda_i f(p_i)
 \text{ for every real-valued }D\text{-convex }f\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then source hull membership is equivalent to nonemptiness of this set. The unresolved issue is therefore to understand when the projection

\[
\boxed{
\{x:\Lambda_D(P,x)\neq\varnothing\}}
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

is semialgebraic for every finite `P`, **without assuming in advance a finite parametrization of all `D`-convex functions or all Jensen measures**.

The previous reports show why merely replacing `(BLOCK)` by semialgebraicity of the raw cone, finite Carathéodory number, or finite lamination depth cannot be justified as a necessary-and-sufficient criterion.

## 4. What a source-complete proof must contain that no current route contains

A complete answer to Problem 35 needs substantially more than a catalog of positive cone families. At minimum it must supply all of the following.

1. **An invariant input datum.** The condition must depend only on the effective closed projective direction set after span reduction, not on a raw cone presentation.
2. **A finite-definability mechanism for the positive side.** For every finite support, the infinite family of `D`-convex Jensen inequalities must reduce to finitely many semialgebraic conditions, or to a definable construction closed under projection.
3. **A necessary obstruction for the negative side.** Whenever the condition fails, one must construct a finite source-valid `K` whose functional hull is provably nonsemialgebraic, not merely a lamination hull with infinitely many stages.
4. **Control of hidden Jensen variables.** The proof must distinguish semialgebraicity of the projected hull from semialgebraicity of the full Jensen-weight relation; the common-linear-factor examples show that the hidden relation can become geometrically visible after lifting.
5. **A termination/nontermination invariant.** Existing positive proofs have a finite closure mechanism. A negative theorem needs a rigorously nonperiodic/noncoincident mechanism that forces infinitely much algebraic boundary information for one fixed finite input.

No route so far contains items 2 and 3 in a single criterion.

## 6. Nearest solved problems and the missing ingredient

### Franěk–Matoušek (2009): finite planar direction sets

`Computing D-convex hulls in the plane`, Computational Geometry 42(1), 81–89, provides an algorithm for the functional `D`-convex hull of a finite planar point set for arbitrary finite direction set `D`. The proposer page cites this as a positive semialgebraic/computable regime.

**What it has:** planar order, finite directional incidence geometry, and a terminating computation of the projected functional hull.

**What the present blocker lacks:** an extracted finite description of all Jensen weights `lambda` over a moving query point `x`. Membership alone existentially hides those weights.

### Matoušek–Plecháč (1998): separate convexity

`On Functional Separately Convex Hulls`, Discrete & Computational Geometry 19, 105–130, gives fixed-dimensional algorithms and structural results for basis/separate convexity.

**What it has:** coordinate-product structure and a finite combinatorial closure procedure.

**What the present blocker lacks:** a replacement finite structure when extra directions interact and hidden barycentric variables are retained.

### Aumann–Hart (1986): bi-convexity and bi-martingales

Aumann and Hart relate biconvex hulls, separation and coordinatewise martingale splittings.

**What it has:** a probabilistic splitting/Jensen language very close to the `Lambda_D` viewpoint.

**What the present blocker lacks:** a real-algebraic finiteness theorem for the full finite-support parameter space of admissible splitting laws. Martingale representation is conceptual, not by itself a semialgebraic certificate.

### Meroni–Raiţă (2025/2026): triangular `2 x 2` rank-one hulls

Their theorem proves semialgebraicity using the special algebraic geometry of the triangular rank-one cone and explicit low-degree structure.

**What it has:** a source-specific finite algebraic closure mechanism.

**What the present blocker lacks:** an invariant criterion telling exactly which general projective direction sets admit an analogous finite mechanism.

These are the closest solved analogues found in the bounded search. The common missing object is a **finite parametric description of Jensen/splitting weights**, not merely a finite description of the projected hull.

## 9. B27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS

The previous reports introduced the stronger property:

> for every finite support `P`, the full relation `J_D(P)` is semialgebraic.

Call this *Jensen-tameness* for shorthand. The minimal-fiber encoding showed why this stronger property is natural: it is exactly what is needed for stability when arbitrary common linear factors are adjoined.

Swapping the source quantifier from

\[
\forall P,\quad \{x:\exists\lambda\ (x,\lambda)\in J_D(P)\}\text{ semialgebraic}
\]

to

\[
\forall P,\quad J_D(P)\text{ semialgebraic}
\]

therefore produces a more rigid target with better closure behavior under the cylindrical constructions already present in the source geometry.

**Outcome:** this does not solve Problem 35 because the stronger property has not been shown equivalent to ordinary source positivity for a fixed cone `D`; projection may erase a nonsemialgebraic hidden fiber. The quantifier swap is a plausible intermediate theorem, not a substitute for the source classification.
