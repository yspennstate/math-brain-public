## 2. Starting notation: the hidden Jensen body already carries barycentric equations

Let `V` have dimension `r`, let `D_0 subset V` span `V`, and let

\[
P=(p_1,\ldots,p_N)
\]

be a finite ordered support whose affine span is all of `V`. Recall

\[
\Lambda_{D_0}(P,x)=\left\{\lambda\in\Delta_{N-1}:
 f(x)\le\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i f(p_i)
 \text{ for every real-valued }D_0\text{-convex }f\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Every affine function and its negative are `D_0`-convex. Therefore every Jensen weight satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_i\lambda_i=1,\qquad
x=\sum_i\lambda_i p_i.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The first equality is already part of `lambda in Delta`, while the second follows by applying the Jensen inequality to every linear functional and its negative.

Define the affine-coordinate map

\[
A:\mathbb R^N\to \mathbb R\oplus V,
\qquad
A\lambda=\left(\sum_i\lambda_i,\sum_i\lambda_i p_i\right).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since `P` affinely spans `V`,

\[
\operatorname{rank}A=r+1,
\qquad
H:=\ker A,
\qquad
\dim H=N-r-1.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus `N-r-1` is the exact number of barycentric degrees of freedom that the functional Jensen condition can still cut down after the ordinary affine constraints are imposed.

## 3. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: minimal-fiber encoding theorem

The previous cylindrical theorem showed that semialgebraicity of the full Jensen relation is sufficient for semialgebraicity of every cylindrical lift. The converse looked unavailable because a scalar lift can collapse many different Jensen weights. The correct fix is to add exactly enough fiber coordinates to remember all hidden barycentric degrees of freedom.

### Theorem 3.1 (minimal-fiber encoding of the full Jensen relation)

Let `V`, `D_0`, `P` and `H` be as above, and put

\[
k=N-r-1=\dim H.
\]

Let `W` be a real vector space of dimension `k`. Choose a linear map

\[
Q:\mathbb R^N\to W
\]

whose restriction

\[
Q|_H:H\to W
\]

is an isomorphism. For the standard basis `e_i` of `R^N`, put

\[
q_i=Qe_i,
\qquad
\widetilde P=\{(p_i,q_i):1\le i\le N\}\subset V\times W,
\]

and set

\[
D=D_0\times W.
\]

Then the map

\[
\Theta:\mathcal J_{D_0}(P)\to V\times W,
\qquad
\Theta(x,\lambda)=(x,Q\lambda),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal J_{D_0}(P)=\{(x,\lambda):\lambda\in\Lambda_{D_0}(P,x)\},
\]

is an affine bijection from `mathcal J_{D_0}(P)` onto the single functional hull

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{conv}_{D_0\times W}(\widetilde P).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Its inverse is affine. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_{D_0}(P)\text{ is semialgebraic}
\iff
\operatorname{conv}_{D_0\times W}(\widetilde P)
\text{ is semialgebraic}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The fiber dimension `k=N-r-1` is minimal for a linear encoding that is injective on every barycentric fiber, because those fibers have translation space `H` of dimension `k` whenever their relative interior is nonempty.

### Proof

Existence of `Q` is elementary: choose a linear complement `C` with

\[
\mathbb R^N=H\oplus C,
\]

choose any isomorphism `H -> W`, and extend it linearly to all of `R^N`, for example by setting it to zero on `C`.

Consider the square linear map

\[
M:\mathbb R^N\to (\mathbb R\oplus V)\oplus W,
\qquad
M\lambda=(A\lambda,Q\lambda).
\tag{3.4}
\]

The source and target both have dimension

\[
N=(r+1)+k.
\]

If `M lambda=0`, then `A lambda=0`, hence `lambda in H`; also `Q lambda=0`. Since `Q|_H` is injective, `lambda=0`. Therefore `M` is injective, hence an isomorphism.

Now let `(x,lambda) in mathcal J_{D_0}(P)`. By (2.2),

\[
A\lambda=(1,x).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Apply the cylindrical Jensen formula (1.1) with fiber labels `q_i=Qe_i`. It gives

\[
\operatorname{conv}_{D_0\times W}(\widetilde P)
=
\{(x,Q\lambda):(x,\lambda)\in\mathcal J_{D_0}(P)\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus `Theta` is surjective onto the lifted hull.

It is injective as well. Indeed, if `(x,Qlambda)` is known, equations (3.5) and `Qlambda=q` give

\[
M\lambda=(1,x,q).
\]

Since `M` is invertible,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda=M^{-1}(1,x,q).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is affine in `(x,q)`. Therefore `Theta` and its inverse are affine, proving (3.2) and the affine equivalence. Semialgebraicity is invariant under affine bijections, proving (3.3).

For the minimality statement, fixing `(1,x)` makes the full affine solution set of `A lambda=(1,x)` a translate of `H`. A linear fiber statistic landing in a vector space of dimension smaller than `dim H` cannot be injective on `H`; hence it cannot distinguish all barycentric weights on a fiber with nonempty relative interior. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 (Jensen-tameness equals stability under all full linear factors)

Call a spanning cone `D_0 subset V` **Jensen-tame** when `mathcal J_{D_0}(P)` is semialgebraic for every finite support `P`.

Then

\[
\boxed{
D_0\text{ is Jensen-tame}
\iff
D_0\times W\text{ has semialgebraic finite functional hulls for every finite-dimensional }W.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Proof: the forward implication is the previous cylindrical theorem plus Tarski–Seidenberg. For the reverse implication, take any finite support `P`, choose its minimal fiber `W` and labels `q_i` as in Theorem 3.1, and use the affine equivalence (3.3). QED.

This is a useful classification reformulation: **ordinary hull semialgebraicity of `D_0` is weaker than the parametric property required to guarantee semialgebraicity after adjoining arbitrary common linear directions.** The three-planes-through-a-line geometry is precisely the first one-dimensional instance of this distinction.

## 4. B26 — HUNT THE SPORADIC CASE: four planar points are exactly the scalar hidden-variable case

Take now `V=R^2` and an affinely spanning four-point support

\[
P=(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4).
\]

Then `r=2`, `N=4`, and

\[
\dim H=1.
\]

Let

\[
c=(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)\ne0
\]

span `H`, so

\[
\sum_i c_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i c_i p_i=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let `h=(h_1,...,h_4)` be scalar heights and form the lifted points

\[
\widetilde p_i=(p_i,h_i)\in\mathbb R^3.
\]

The cylindrical cone `D_0 x R` is exactly a union of planes through the vertical common line whenever `D_0` is a union of planar lines.

There is a sharp dichotomy.

### Proposition 4.1 (four-point height dichotomy)

1. If
   \[
   h\cdot c=0,
   \tag{4.2}
   \]
   then there is an affine function `phi:R^2->R` such that `h_i=phi(p_i)` for all `i`, and
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{conv}_{D_0\times\mathbb R}(\widetilde P)
   =\{(x,\phi(x)):x\in\operatorname{conv}_{D_0}(P)\}.
   }
   \tag{4.3}
   \]

2. If
   \[
   h\cdot c\ne0,
   \tag{4.4}
   \]
   then the scalar map `Q_h(lambda)=h dot lambda` is an isomorphism on `H`, and Theorem 3.1 gives an affine bijection
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal J_{D_0}(P)
   \cong
   \operatorname{conv}_{D_0\times\mathbb R}(\widetilde P).
   }
   \tag{4.5}
   \]

Moreover, (4.4) is equivalent to affine independence of the four lifted points in `R^3`. Hence for a fixed planar support `P`, **all noncoplanar height choices produce affinely equivalent functional hulls**, while all coplanar height choices collapse to the graph of an affine function over the planar base hull.

### Proof

The row space of `A` consists exactly of the vectors of sampled affine functions

\[
(\phi(p_1),\ldots,\phi(p_4)).
\]

Since `H=ker A` is one-dimensional, its annihilator is the row space of `A`. Thus `h dot c=0` iff `h` is sampled from an affine function `phi`, proving the first structural assertion. If `lambda in Lambda_{D_0}(P,x)`, then its barycenter is `x`, so

\[
\sum_i\lambda_i h_i
=\sum_i\lambda_i\phi(p_i)
=\phi\left(\sum_i\lambda_i p_i\right)
=\phi(x).
\]

The cylindrical formula (1.1) now yields (4.3).

If `h dot c !=0`, then `Q_h` is nonzero on the one-dimensional space `H`, hence an isomorphism `H -> R`. Theorem 3.1 gives (4.5).

Finally, the four lifted points are affinely dependent exactly when there is a nonzero coefficient vector `a` satisfying

\[
\sum_i a_i=0,\quad
\sum_i a_i p_i=0,\quad
\sum_i a_i h_i=0.
\]

The first two conditions force `a` to be a scalar multiple of `c`; thus affine dependence is equivalent to `h dot c=0`. QED.

### Consequence for the source-suspected three-plane cone

Let `D_0` be three distinct planar directions. The previous run proved that all ordered triples of such directions are projectively/linearly equivalent, so the corresponding three-plane pencils in `R^3` carry no continuous cone modulus.

For an affinely spanning four-point base support, Proposition 4.1 now removes the height modulus as well:

- coplanar lifts reduce to a planar functional hull, which is semialgebraic for finite planar `D_0` by Franěk–Matoušek;
- every noncoplanar lift is just an affine coordinate realization of the **same planar Jensen relation** `mathcal J_{D_0}(P)`.

Therefore a four-point wild example for three planes through a line exists **if and only if** there is an affinely spanning planar four-point support whose three-direction Jensen relation is nonsemialgebraic. This does not assert that such a support exists. It turns the source's three-dimensional four-point search into a precise planar parametric problem.

For the canonical square

\[
p_1=(0,0),\ p_2=(1,0),\ p_3=(0,1),\ p_4=(1,1),
\]

one can take

\[
c=(1,-1,-1,1),
\]

and the height vector `h=(0,0,0,1)`. Writing `x=(s,t)` and `q=lambda_4`, every barycentric weight has the exact form

\[
\boxed{
(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,\lambda_4)
=(1-s-t+q,\ s-q,\ t-q,\ q).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The exact arithmetic in (4.6), together with a five-point `k=2` example, is archived in the self-check artifact. It is only a finite linear-algebra self-check and is **not** evidence that the Jensen relation itself is semialgebraic.

## 5. B22 — READ THE FAILURES: bounded Carathéodory certificates cannot characterize the positive cones

A tempting answer to Problem 35 would be that semialgebraic hulls are exactly those directional geometries with a finite Carathéodory number or some uniform bounded-support certificate. Published directional-convexity failures rule this out even on the **positive** side.

Matoušek's 2001 paper *On directional convexity* proves that for **separate convexity in `R^3`** — i.e. `D` the orthonormal basis — there are arbitrarily large finite sets `A` such that

\[
\operatorname{conv}_D(A)\ne A
\]

but every proper subset `B subsetneq A` satisfies

\[
\operatorname{conv}_D(B)=B.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The same cone belongs to the classic semialgebraic/computable positive regime of Matoušek–Plecháč. Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{“finite Carathéodory number” is not a necessary condition for Problem-35 positivity.}
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

More generally, any proposed classification that requires every hull point/nontrivial hull phenomenon to have a uniformly bounded small subset witness is too strong: a cone can have semialgebraic finite hulls while its minimal combinatorial dependencies have unbounded cardinality.

This is important for the new Jensen reformulation. The desired finite description cannot simply be “enumerate all supports up to a universal size.” If Problem 35 has a clean positive criterion, it has to permit *unbounded combinatorial support complexity while retaining definability* — for example through recursive elimination, algebraic envelopes, or another finite symbolic description whose size may grow with `|K|`.

The newer computational literature reinforces that distinction. Franěk–Matoušek give a polynomial-time planar hull-membership construction for finite direction sets, but the statement verified in this run is a hull algorithm, not a theorem that the **entire parameterized Jensen relation** `mathcal J_D(P)` has a finite semialgebraic formula. Basu–Amini Khorasgani–Maji–Nguyen obtain finite semialgebraic descriptions for certain lamination-hull systems by reducing them to linear inequalities over convex sets; that is a different object and cannot simply be substituted for the functional Jensen relation. None of these facts closes (3.8).

## 6. What the three methods say together

The preceding two RS runs and this one now isolate a substantially smaller conceptual target.

1. Raw presentations of `D` do not matter; the closed projective direction set does.
2. Common linear factors can be removed exactly, but doing so exposes the full Jensen-weight relation.
3. The Jensen relation is not merely an auxiliary infinite-dimensional object: **Theorem 3.1 realizes it exactly as one ordinary functional directional-convex hull with the minimal number of added fiber coordinates.**
4. For four affinely spanning planar points, one scalar fiber coordinate is enough. Hence the source's concrete three-plane/common-line four-point question is literally the semialgebraicity question for a planar four-point Jensen relation.
5. A characterization cannot be based on finite Carathéodory number or uniformly bounded support witnesses, because separate convexity in `R^3` is semialgebraically tame while having arbitrarily large minimal dependencies.

This suggests a more plausible dichotomy than any condition tried so far:

> **Definability criterion candidate.** The right invariant may be whether the finite evaluation/Jensen cones of `D` admit finite semialgebraic elimination uniformly for each finite support, not whether their combinatorial support complexity is uniformly bounded.

This is deliberately labeled a candidate organizing principle, not a theorem or answer to Problem 35.
