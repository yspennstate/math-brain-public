## 3. B30 — LOCALIZE: exact span/fiber reduction

The first reduction removes a large amount of irrelevant ambient geometry.

### Theorem 3.1 (affine-coset decomposition)

Let `V = span(D) subset R^d`. For finite `K subset R^d` and every affine coset `A=a+V`, write `K_A=K cap A`. Then

1. if `K_A` is empty, `conv_D(K) cap A` is empty;
2. if `K_A` is nonempty, then, after identifying `A` with `V`,

`conv_D^{R^d}(K) cap A = conv_D^A(K_A)`.

Consequently `conv_D(K)` is the finite union of the intrinsic hulls in those `V`-cosets that contain points of `K`.

### Proof

Every line with direction in `D` stays inside one affine `V`-coset.

If `x in conv_D^A(K_A)` and `f:R^d->R` is globally `D`-convex, then `f|_A` is intrinsically `D`-convex. Hence

`f(x) <= max_{k in K_A} f(k) <= max_{k in K} f(k)`,

so `x in conv_D^{R^d}(K)`.

Conversely, suppose `K_A` is nonempty and `x notin conv_D^A(K_A)`. By the definition of the intrinsic hull there is a real-valued intrinsically `D`-convex `g:A->R` such that

`g(x) > M := max_{k in K_A} g(k)`.

Define `F:R^d->R` by `F=g` on `A` and `F=M` on every other `V`-coset. Every `D`-line lies entirely in one coset, so each restriction of `F` to a `D`-line is either a restriction of `g` or a constant. Thus `F` is globally `D`-convex. Every point of `K` outside `A` has value `M`, every point of `K_A` has value at most `M`, while `F(x)>M`. Therefore `x notin conv_D^{R^d}(K)`.

If `K_A` is empty, take `F=1` on `A` and `F=0` on every other coset. It is again `D`-convex and excludes every `x in A` from the hull. This proves both claims. QED.

### Classification consequence

The source property

`P(D): conv_D(K) is semialgebraic for every finite K`

is unchanged after replacing the ambient space by `span(D)`. Hence Problem 35 can be classified first for **spanning cones**. Rank-zero and rank-one cases can be removed completely below.

## 4. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE, part I: projective closure is the true direction datum

For a spanning cone `D subset V`, define its closed projective direction set

`Omega_D = closure{ [v] : v in D\{0} } subset RP(V)`.

### Theorem 4.1 (projective-closure invariance)

For a spanning cone `D`, the class of real-valued `D`-convex functions, and therefore every finite-set functional hull, depends only on `Omega_D`. In particular

`conv_D(K) = conv_{Omega_D}(K)`

in the evident direction-set sense.

### Proof

Scaling a direction by a nonzero scalar and changing its sign do not change the underlying affine lines, so only projective directions matter.

It remains to show closure. Choose a basis `b_1,...,b_r` from `D`, possible because `D` spans `V`. In the associated linear coordinates, every finite-valued `D`-convex function is convex in each coordinate separately. Such a separately convex finite function is continuous; a self-contained local argument is as follows.

On a cube `[-2,2]^r`, iterated one-dimensional convex interpolation bounds the function above by a finite number `M` determined by the corner values. For each coordinate `i`, the slice with coordinate `i` equal to zero is separately convex in `r-1` variables, so by induction it is continuous and has a finite minimum `m_i` on `[-1,1]^(r-1)`. For any one-dimensional coordinate section `g` and `|t|<=1`, convexity, the bound `g(+-2)<=M`, and `g(0)>=m_i` give the uniform lower bound

`g(t) >= (3 m_i - M)/2`.

A convex function uniformly bounded above and below on `[-1,1]` has uniformly bounded secant slopes on the smaller interval `[-1/2,1/2]`. Telescoping one coordinate at a time therefore makes the original function locally Lipschitz. The induction begins with ordinary finite convex functions on an interval. Thus the function is continuous on `V`.

Now let projective directions `[y_n]` from `D` converge to `[y]`; choose unit representatives (changing signs if needed) so `y_n -> y`. Convexity of `t -> f(x+t y_n)` is the Jensen inequality

`f(x+((1-s)t_0+s t_1)y_n) <= (1-s) f(x+t_0 y_n)+s f(x+t_1 y_n)`.

Continuity permits `n->infinity`, yielding the same inequality along `y`. Thus every `D`-convex function is convex in all directions of `Omega_D`; the reverse inclusion is immediate. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 (linear/projective invariance)

For every invertible linear map `T`,

`conv_{T D}(T K) = T conv_D(K)`.

Hence the classification is naturally a classification of compact closed spanning subsets of real projective space, modulo projective changes induced by `GL(V)`.

### B2 obstruction to a naive characterization

A tempting necessary condition would be that the raw cone `D` itself be semialgebraic. That is false in the strongest elementary way.

For `r>=2`, let

`D_Q = {t q : t>=0, q in Q^r\{0}}`.

Its projective directions are dense in `RP^(r-1)`, so Theorem 4.1 implies that `D_Q`-convexity is ordinary convexity and

`conv_{D_Q}(K)=conv(K)`

for every finite `K`; all such hulls are polytopes. Yet `D_Q` is not semialgebraic: it is dense in `R^r` and has empty interior (it is a countable union of one-dimensional rays), whereas a dense semialgebraic subset of `R^r` has full semialgebraic dimension and therefore nonempty interior.

This is **not** a counterexample to Problem 35. It refutes only the invented auxiliary guess “semialgebraicity of every finite `D`-hull forces the raw cone `D` to be semialgebraic.” Any source-level characterization must at least be invariant under projective closure.

## 5. B30 — exact two-point and one-dimensional classification

Projective closure also decides all two-point hulls exactly.

### Theorem 5.1 (two-point dichotomy)

Work after the span reduction, so `D` spans `V`, and let `Omega=Omega_D`. For distinct `a,b in V`, put `w=b-a`. Then

`conv_D({a,b}) = [a,b]` if `[w] in Omega`,

and

`conv_D({a,b}) = {a,b}` if `[w] notin Omega`.

### Proof

Affine functions are `D`-convex, so the functional hull is always contained in the ordinary segment `[a,b]`.

If `[w] in Omega`, Theorem 4.1 says every `D`-convex function is convex along the line in direction `w`. Hence every point of `[a,b]` satisfies the defining maximum inequality, proving equality.

Suppose `[w] notin Omega` and normalize `w`. Let

`S={u u^T : ||u||=1, [u] in Omega}`

inside the symmetric matrices and let

`C=cone(conv S)`.

The set `C` is closed: every member of `conv S` is positive semidefinite with trace one, so along a convergent sequence `t_n Q_n` the trace determines the radial parameters `t_n`, while `conv S` is compact. Moreover `w w^T notin C`. Indeed trace forces any representation of `w w^T` in `conv S` to have total radial coefficient one, and for every `z perpendicular to w`,

`0 = z^T w w^T z = sum_j alpha_j (z·u_j)^2`

forces every contributing `u_j` to be parallel to `w`, contradicting `[w] notin Omega`.

By finite-dimensional separation of the point `w w^T` from the closed convex cone `C`, there is a symmetric matrix `A` with

`u^T A u >= 0` for every `[u] in Omega`, while `w^T A w < 0`.

For an interior point `z=a+theta w`, `0<theta<1`, define

`q_z(v)=(v-z)^T A (v-z)`.

Along every allowed direction `y`, the second derivative is `2 y^T A y >=0`, so `q_z` is `D`-convex. But

`q_z(z)=0`,

`q_z(a)=theta^2 w^T A w<0`,

`q_z(b)=(1-theta)^2 w^T A w<0`.

Thus `z` is excluded from the functional hull. QED.

### Corollary 5.2 (all collinear finite sets)

If a finite set `K` lies on an affine line of projective direction `[w]`, then

- if `[w] in Omega`, `conv_D(K)` is the ordinary interval between the two extreme points of `K`;
- if `[w] notin Omega`, `conv_D(K)=K`.

For the second clause, the same quadratic separator centered at any `x in conv(K)\K` makes all values at `K` strictly negative and the value at `x` zero.

Combining this with Theorem 3.1 completely settles source semialgebraicity for `dim span(D)<=1`: **every finite-set functional hull is a finite union of intervals and points.** This is a proper subclass result only; `E_characterization` remains zero.

## 6. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE, part II: finite-dimensional Jensen-measure duality

The source definition quantifies over an infinite cone of functions. For finite `K`, however, membership has an exact finite-dimensional dual form.

Let `K={k_1,...,k_N}` and fix `x`. Define

`E_x = { (f(k_1)-f(x),...,f(k_N)-f(x)) : f is real-valued D-convex } subset R^N`.

This is a convex cone.

### Theorem 6.1 (Jensen-weight criterion)

The following are equivalent:

1. `x in conv_D(K)`;
2. there exists a probability vector `lambda in Delta_(N-1)` such that

`f(x) <= sum_i lambda_i f(k_i)`

for **every** real-valued `D`-convex function `f`.

### Proof

Condition 1 says exactly that `E_x` does not meet the open negative orthant `O=(-infinity,0)^N`: if all coordinates of one element of `E_x` were negative, the corresponding function would have `f(x)>max_K f`.

The convex cone `E_x` and open convex cone `O` are disjoint. Finite-dimensional separation gives a nonzero vector `lambda` with

`lambda·e >= 0` for every `e in E_x`,

`lambda·o <= 0` for every `o in O`.

The second inequality and the unbounded coordinate rays of `O` force every `lambda_i>=0`. Since `lambda` is nonzero, normalize `sum_i lambda_i=1`. The first inequality becomes

`sum_i lambda_i (f(k_i)-f(x)) >=0`

for every `D`-convex `f`, which is condition 2. The converse is immediate because a weighted average of the values on `K` never exceeds their maximum. QED.

Define the compact closed convex **Jensen-weight set**

`Lambda_D(K,x)={lambda in Delta_(N-1): sum_i lambda_i(f(k_i)-f(x))>=0 for every D-convex f}`.

Then

`x in conv_D(K) iff Lambda_D(K,x) is nonempty`.

### Why this reframing matters for Problem 35

If, for a fixed cone class, the graph

`{(x,lambda): lambda in Lambda_D(K,x)}`

admits a semialgebraic description for every finite `K`, Tarski–Seidenberg immediately makes `conv_D(K)` semialgebraic by projection. Thus one sufficient architecture for a positive characterization is:

> the infinite system of `D`-convex Jensen inequalities on the finite simplex has a finite semialgebraic elimination/certificate theorem.

The 2026 Basu–Amini Khorasgani–Maji–Nguyen paper is highly relevant methodologically because it supplies finite symbolic elimination for recursively generated convex-set systems and applies it to certain **lamination hulls**. It does not directly identify the functional Jensen sets `Lambda_D(K,x)`, and its own application is stated for lamination hulls. Treating it as a solution to Problem 35 would conflate the two source hulls.

I am not claiming novelty for the abstract finite-dimensional separation/Jensen formulation; it is basic convex duality and should be treated as structural organization unless a deeper prior-art check proves otherwise.

## 7. B2 — a rigorous nonsemialgebraicity certificate if an infinite boundary cascade is ever realized

C29 and Meroni–Raiţă both isolate noncoincidence of candidate support planes as a central missing step. The following elementary real-algebraic lemma makes the final implication precise.

### Lemma 7.1 (infinitely many planar boundary patches obstruct semialgebraicity)

Let `S subset R^3` be semialgebraic. Then `partial S` cannot contain relatively open two-dimensional patches lying in infinitely many pairwise distinct affine planes.

### Proof

The boundary `partial S` is semialgebraic and has dimension at most two. A standard real-algebraic fact says that a semialgebraic subset of dimension at most two in `R^3` has proper Zariski closure, so some nonzero polynomial `P` vanishes on `partial S`.

If `partial S` contains a relatively open two-dimensional patch in an affine plane `Pi`, the restriction of `P` to `Pi` is a two-variable polynomial that vanishes on a nonempty open set. Hence it vanishes identically on `Pi`, so the affine linear defining polynomial of `Pi` divides `P`. Infinitely many pairwise distinct affine planes would therefore give infinitely many pairwise nonassociate linear factors of the fixed nonzero polynomial `P`, impossible. QED.

### Lemma 7.2 (generic noncoincidence reduces one C29/Meroni failure mode)

Suppose an exact compatible-grid or alternating-concavification construction depends on a finite real parameter vector `h`, and the normalized affine-plane coefficient of its `n`th candidate patch is an algebraic map `p_n(h)`. If for every `n!=m` the maps `p_n` and `p_m` are not identically the same projective plane, then the coincidence set

`{h : p_n(h)=p_m(h) as projective planes}`

is a proper algebraic subset of parameter space. Therefore outside a countable union of proper algebraic sets — in particular, for a Baire-generic and full-measure choice whenever the parameter domain has interior — all candidate planes are pairwise distinct simultaneously.

This observation removes **accidental coincidence** once structural distinctness of the plane maps is proved. It does **not** prove Conjecture 34 or Problem 35, because two much harder obligations remain: derive an infinite sequence of structurally distinct plane maps and prove that infinitely many of those candidates are actually realized as relatively open boundary patches of the **same functional `D`-convex hull**. If those obligations were proved, Lemma 7.1 would give the final nonsemialgebraicity step.

This route is deliberately distinct from C29's exhausted finite spike calculations: it says exactly what has to be proved before another patch computation has source-level value.
