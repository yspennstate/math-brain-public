## 1. Source target and exact convention

The live source page was re-read on 2026-09-17:

`https://randomstrasse101.math.ethz.ch/posts/clique-low-degree/`

It defines, for distributions `P,Q`, the degree-`D` low-degree advantage by

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
=
\max_{\deg f\le D}
\frac{\mathbb E_P f-\mathbb E_Q f}
{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_Q f}}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The source then asks for conditions on sequences `(P_n,Q_n)` under which bounded advantage at

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\Omega(1)}
\tag{1.2}
\]

rigorously implies that polynomials of a specified nontrivial degree cannot distinguish the two distributions. This is a framework/conditions problem, not the two later unplanted-clique boxes on the same page.

Hsieh–Kane–Kothari–Li–Mohanty–Tiegel (2026) identify `(1.1)` with the centered `L^2(Q)` norm of the degree-`D` likelihood-ratio projection: if `L=dP/dQ`, then

\[
(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D})^2
=\operatorname{Var}_Q(L^{\le D}).
\tag{1.3}
\]

This is the convention used below.

## 3. Dated literature/current-status check

### 3.1 Kireeva / Hopkins baseline

The Kireeva page is still live on 2026-09-17 and still displays the framework question as open. It records Hopkins' informal conjectural picture: uniform product null, relabeling-invariant planted law, bounded polylogarithmic-degree LDLR, and independent noise should imply algorithmic hardness. The source already notes that broad versions had been refuted before June 2026.

### 3.2 Buhai–Hsieh–Jain–Kothari (2025)

R.-D. Buhai, J.-T. Hsieh, A. Jain, P. Kothari, *The Quasi-Polynomial Low-Degree Conjecture is False*, arXiv:2505.17360. For each fixed hyperedge arity `k>=2` and fixed positive noise level, they construct a permutation-invariant planted distribution over a uniform Boolean product null with vanishing very-high-degree advantage, while noisy distinguishing is still possible in quasi-polynomial time via noisy polynomial interpolation/list decoding. Their real-matrix construction additionally shows a spectral failure mode for a non-product null.

### 3.3 Hsieh–Kane–Kothari–Li–Mohanty–Tiegel (2026)

J.-T. Hsieh et al., *Rigorous Implications of the Low-Degree Heuristic*, arXiv:2601.05850 / STOC 2026. They prove genuine positive implications in three structured settings. In particular, full coordinate-permutation symmetry on Boolean vectors plus low-degree indistinguishability yields statistical indistinguishability after noise; in Gaussian vector and matrix settings they obtain lower bounds against specified symmetric-polynomial/subgraph-statistic classes. These are important positive templates but not a general conditions theorem for graph/hypergraph relabeling symmetry.

### 3.4 Mao (2026): mandatory post-source stress test

Songtao Mao, *The Polynomial-Time Low-Degree Conjecture is False*, arXiv:2607.20318v1, submitted 2026-07-22. For each fixed `r>=3`, Mao constructs a vertex-relabeling-invariant distribution `P_n` on simple graphs with `Q_n=G(n,1/2)` and

\[
D_n=\Theta((\log n)^{r-1})
\]

such that **every marginal on at most `D_n` edges is exactly uniform**, hence

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)=0,
\tag{3.1}
\]

while a deterministic polynomial-time rank test still strongly distinguishes after fixed positive independent edge resampling. Thus the package

`uniform product null + vertex-relabeling symmetry + exact polylog-wise moment matching + independent noise`

is not sufficient.

The construction uses Reed–Muller evaluation vectors with no short linear dependencies and a random alternating bilinear form. This is the global algebraic/rank mechanism every candidate condition now has to exclude for a substantive reason.

### 3.5 Later positive graph result: Chen–Li (2026)

Xuan Chen and Shuangping Li, *Rigorous Low-Degree Implications for Planted Subgraph Detection: Noise and Treewidth*, arXiv:2608.06279v1, submitted 2026-08-06. For sparse planted-subgraph detection they prove a positive implication after noise under a treewidth/decomposability condition in the supercritical regime, and without that condition at/below criticality when `D_n=omega(log n)`. Their proof breaks large Fourier supports into low-degree pieces with small interfaces and lets noise pay the reassembly cost. This postdates Kireeva's June page and is a direct source-relevant positive structural example.

### 3.6 Additional failure mode: Jia–Vijayaraghavan (2026)

He Jia and Aravindan Vijayaraghavan, *Low-Degree Method Fails to Predict Robust Subspace Recovery*, COLT 2026 / arXiv:2603.02594. They give a natural continuous problem that is polynomial-time solvable by anti-concentration methods although low-degree predicts hardness up to large degree. This matters because a general condition should not merely say “exclude algebraic codes”; anti-concentration can also support algorithms invisible to low moments.

## 5. B21 — ask who else has this shape: the common high-degree-tail problem

The positive and negative papers suggest a common abstraction.

1. **Low degree is already controlled** by `(1.1)`.
2. **Noise damps degree**, but only helps if the total high-degree likelihood-ratio energy does not grow fast enough to overcome that damping.
3. Positive structural results such as full coordinate symmetry (Hsieh et al.) or low-interface/treewidth decomposability (Chen–Li) are mechanisms for controlling this high-degree energy from much less information.
4. Mao/Buhai/Jia exhibit mechanisms by which useful algorithmic structure survives while all low-degree information remains blind.

This points to a minimal general theorem: separate the universally easy spectral/noise step from the genuinely hard model-specific step that bootstraps control of the high-degree tail.

## 6. A general spectral entropy-budget theorem

The following theorem is complete as stated. It is deliberately presented as an **elementary sufficient-condition theorem / framework lemma**, not as a claim of publication novelty and not as a source-complete characterization.

### Theorem 6.1 (degree-contractive noise + Rényi-2 budget)

For each `n`, let `(Omega_n,Q_n)` be a probability space with an orthogonal decomposition

\[
L^2(Q_n)=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}\mathcal H_{n,j},
\qquad \mathcal H_{n,0}=\operatorname{span}\{1\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

such that polynomials of degree at most `D` are contained in (and, for the source convention, identified with)

\[
\bigoplus_{0\le j\le D}\mathcal H_{n,j}.
\]

Let `T_{n,rho}` be a `Q_n`-stationary, self-adjoint Markov operator such that every `H_{n,j}` is invariant and

\[
\|T_{n,\rho}h\|_{L^2(Q_n)}
\le \rho^j\|h\|_{L^2(Q_n)}
\quad\text{for all }h\in\mathcal H_{n,j},
\tag{6.2}
\]

for some fixed `rho in (0,1)`. This includes ordinary independent Boolean resampling (in the centered Fourier basis, `rho=1-epsilon`) and the Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup.

Let `P_n << Q_n` with likelihood ratio `L_n=dP_n/dQ_n`, and let `P_n^{(rho)}` denote the law obtained by applying the Markov kernel `T_{n,rho}` to a sample from `P_n`. Then for every integer `D>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_n^{(\rho)}\|Q_n)
\le
\big(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P_n,Q_n)\big)^2
+
\rho^{2(D+1)}\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently, if for a sequence `D_n -> infinity`

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\le A<\infty
\tag{6.4}
\]

and

\[
\sup_n\rho^{2(D_n+1)}\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)\le B<\infty,
\tag{6.5}
\]

then **no sequence of tests, even computationally unbounded tests, strongly distinguishes** `P_n^{(rho)}` from `Q_n`.

A particularly clean sufficient condition, independent of the chosen fixed positive noise level, is

\[
D_2(P_n\|Q_n)
:=\log\big(1+\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)\big)
=o(D_n).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Under `(6.4)` and `(6.6)`, for every fixed `rho in (0,1)` the noised total variation stays bounded away from `1`. If in addition

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\to0,
\tag{6.7}
\]

then

\[
\|P_n^{(\rho)}-Q_n\|_{TV}\to0.
\tag{6.8}
\]

More generally, for one fixed `rho`, the weaker rate condition

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{D_2(P_n\|Q_n)}{D_n}
<2\log(1/\rho)
\tag{6.9}
\]

is enough to make the high-degree term in `(6.3)` vanish.

### Proof

Write the orthogonal chaos expansion

\[
L_n=1+\sum_{j\ge1}L_{n,j},
\qquad L_{n,j}\in\mathcal H_{n,j}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Because `T_{n,rho}` is self-adjoint and preserves `Q_n`, the noised law has likelihood ratio `T_{n,rho}L_n`. Indeed, for every bounded test function `f`,

\[
\mathbb E_{P_n^{(\rho)}}f
=
\mathbb E_{P_n}[T_{n,\rho}f]
=
\langle T_{n,\rho}f,L_n\rangle_{Q_n}
=
\langle f,T_{n,\rho}L_n\rangle_{Q_n}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Therefore

\[
\chi^2(P_n^{(\rho)}\|Q_n)
=
\|T_{n,\rho}(L_n-1)\|_2^2.
\tag{6.12}
\]

The invariant orthogonal grading and `(6.2)` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|T_{n,\rho}(L_n-1)\|_2^2
&=
\sum_{j\ge1}\|T_{n,\rho}L_{n,j}\|_2^2\\
&\le
\sum_{1\le j\le D}\rho^{2j}\|L_{n,j}\|_2^2
+
\sum_{j>D}\rho^{2j}\|L_{n,j}\|_2^2\\
&\le
\sum_{1\le j\le D}\|L_{n,j}\|_2^2
+
\rho^{2(D+1)}\sum_{j>D}\|L_{n,j}\|_2^2\\
&\le
\big(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P_n,Q_n)\big)^2
+
\rho^{2(D+1)}\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n),
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.13}
\]

where `(1.3)` identifies the first sum with the squared source advantage. This proves `(6.3)`.

It remains to convert a bounded chi-square divergence into failure of strong distinction. For any `P<<Q` with likelihood ratio `L`,

\[
1-\|P-Q\|_{TV}
=
\mathbb E_Q[\min\{L,1\}]
\ge
\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\frac{L}{1+L}\right].
\tag{6.14}
\]

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[
1=(\mathbb E_Q L)^2
\le
\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\frac{L}{1+L}\right]
\mathbb E_Q[L(1+L)].
\tag{6.15}
\]

Since `E_Q L=1` and `E_Q L^2=1+chi^2(P||Q)`, `(6.15)` yields

\[
\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\frac{L}{1+L}\right]
\ge
\frac{1}{2+\chi^2(P\|Q)}.
\tag{6.16}
\]

Thus a uniform bound `chi^2<=K` implies

\[
\|P-Q\|_{TV}
\le1-\frac1{2+K}<1,
\tag{6.17}
\]

so the optimal testing error cannot vanish. This proves the strong-indistinguishability conclusion under `(6.4)`–`(6.5)`.

If `(6.6)` holds, then

\[
\rho^{2D_n}\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)
\le
\exp\!\left(
-2D_n\log(1/\rho)+o(D_n)
\right)
\to0,
\tag{6.18}
\]

which proves the fixed-noise statement. Condition `(6.9)` gives the same conclusion with a positive linear exponent gap. Finally, under `(6.7)`, `(6.3)` implies `chi^2(P_n^{(rho)}||Q_n)->0`, and

\[
\|P_n^{(\rho)}-Q_n\|_{TV}
\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P_n^{(\rho)}\|Q_n)}
\to0.
\tag{6.19}
\]

This completes the proof. `square`

## 7. B27 — swap the quantifiers: what the theorem actually says

The source statement is intentionally vague about the degree/class of distinguishers. The theorem above fixes the quantifiers cleanly:

> **For every fixed noise correlation `rho in (0,1)`**, for every sequence of planted/null pairs admitting the chaos/noise structure `(6.1)`–`(6.2)`, if there exists `D_n >= (log n)^{1+c}` for some fixed `c>0` such that the source low-degree advantage is uniformly bounded and the Rényi-2 growth satisfies `(6.9)`, then no test of any computational complexity strongly distinguishes the `rho`-noised planted law from the null. If the Rényi-2 growth is `o(D_n)`, this implication holds for **every fixed positive noise rate**.

This is stronger in its conclusion than “no polynomial of a certain degree”: it rules out all tests. The price is an additional global information-size condition. It is not equivalent by definition to the conclusion: `D_2(P||Q)` is measured before noise and `(6.9)` is a direct quantitative property of the original pair. Nevertheless, it is strong enough that I do **not** count it as a source-complete characterization without further evidence that it captures a broad intended graph/hypergraph class.

The quantifier order also clarifies an important distinction. Requiring `(6.9)` for one fixed `rho` permits a positive linear Rényi-2 rate below `2 log(1/rho)`; requiring one hypothesis that works for all fixed positive noise rates naturally leads to the cleaner sublinear condition `(6.6)`.

## 8. Exact stress test against Mao

The theorem gives a quantitative reason Mao's example escapes.

Mao has

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)=0
\tag{8.1}
\]

and, at the fixed positive resampling rate where the rank test strongly distinguishes,

\[
\|P_n^{(\rho)}-Q_n\|_{TV}\to1.
\tag{8.2}
\]

By `(6.16)`, `(8.2)` forces

\[
\chi^2(P_n^{(\rho)}\|Q_n)\to\infty.
\tag{8.3}
\]

But `(6.3)` and `(8.1)` give

\[
\chi^2(P_n^{(\rho)}\|Q_n)
\le
\rho^{2(D_n+1)}\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Hence necessarily

\[
\boxed{
\rho^{2(D_n+1)}\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)\to\infty.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Equivalently, Mao's construction must carry at least the critical linear-in-`D_n` Rényi-2 rate needed to overcome the noise:

\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{D_2(P_n\|Q_n)}{D_n}
\ge2\log(1/\rho).
\tag{8.6}
\]

Thus the new condition excludes Mao for a concrete structural reason: **the hidden high-degree likelihood-ratio mass is exponentially large enough in the polylogarithmic degree scale to survive fixed noise.** It does not exclude Mao merely by naming rank tests or by assuming hardness.

The same diagnostic applies to any zero-low-degree counterexample with strong noisy distinguishability, including other code/list-decoding constructions: if low-degree mass vanishes but strong noisy distinction survives, the noise-weighted high-degree `L^2` mass must diverge.

## 9. What this contributes, and what it does not

### 9.1 Genuine mathematical contribution of the run

The run produces a complete general implication `(6.3)` and a clean non-tautological sufficient condition `(6.6)` / `(6.9)` that is stated directly in the source low-degree language. It also turns Mao from a qualitative counterexample into a quantitative entropy-rate obstruction `(8.5)`–`(8.6)`.

Conceptually, it separates Kireeva's framework problem into two modules:

1. **Universal spectral module:** noise converts any control of low-degree advantage plus a high-degree energy budget into rigorous indistinguishability. This module is Theorem 6.1.
2. **Structural bootstrap module:** find natural hypotheses—symmetry, treewidth, factorization, invariance, hypercontractivity, pseudorandomness, or something else—that force the needed high-degree budget from low-degree information alone.

The second module is where Hsieh et al. and Chen–Li obtain their nontrivial positive results, and where Mao/Buhai/Jia show broad statements can fail.

It also looks like an elementary spectral estimate that may well be folklore. No claim is made that it is the sought optimal/natural boundary.
