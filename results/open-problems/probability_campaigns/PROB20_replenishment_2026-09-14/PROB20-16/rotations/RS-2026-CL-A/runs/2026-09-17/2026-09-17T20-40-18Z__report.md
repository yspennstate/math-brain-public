## 3. Model and exact likelihood-ratio identities

The preceding report used a homogeneous product null and introduced an unnecessary worst-support factor `kappa`. We now work in a strictly more general heterogeneous product model and eliminate that factor.

Let `E` be a finite coordinate set and

\[
Q=\bigotimes_{e\in E}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_e),
\qquad 0<p_e<1,
\qquad
\lambda_e:=\frac{1-p_e}{p_e}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `nu` be a law on random supports `A subseteq E`. The planted law `P=P_{nu,Q}` is generated by drawing `A~nu`, forcing `X_e=1` for `e in A`, and sampling every `e notin A` independently from its `Bernoulli(p_e)` null coordinate. Put

\[
q_e:=\Pr_{A\sim\nu}(e\in A).
\tag{3.2}
\]

For the standard orthonormal Boolean coordinate

\[
\psi_e(x_e)=\frac{x_e-p_e}{\sqrt{p_e(1-p_e)}},
\qquad
\psi_S=\prod_{e\in S}\psi_e,
\tag{3.3}
\]

write `L=dP/dQ`.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact Fourier coefficient

For every `S subseteq E`,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat L(S)
=
\left(\prod_{e\in S}\sqrt{\lambda_e}\right)
\Pr_{A\sim\nu}(S\subseteq A).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

**Proof.** Conditional on a planted support `A`, the one-coordinate likelihood ratio for `e in A` is

\[
\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{x_e=1\}}}{p_e}
=1+\sqrt{\lambda_e}\,\psi_e(x_e).
\]

Thus

\[
L_A(x)=\prod_{e\in A}
\left(1+\sqrt{\lambda_e}\,\psi_e(x_e)\right).
\]

Averaging over `A` and taking the coefficient of `psi_S` gives (3.4). `□`

In particular, the exact degree-one likelihood-ratio energy is

\[
\boxed{
b_1=\sum_{e\in E}\lambda_e q_e^2.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

For product Boolean `Q`, every function has a multilinear representative without increasing degree. Hence the source's variational low-degree advantage satisfies

\[
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\right)^2
=
\sum_{1\le |S|\le D}\widehat L(S)^2,
\qquad D\ge1,
\tag{3.6}
\]

and therefore

\[
b_1\le \left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\right)^2.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Let `T` independently resample coordinate `e` from its null marginal with probability `1-rho_e`, so

\[
T\psi_S=\left(\prod_{e\in S}\rho_e\right)\psi_S,
\qquad 0\le\rho_e\le1.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Let `A,A'` be independent draws from `nu` and set

\[
J_e:=\mathbf 1_{\{e\in A\cap A'\}}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Parseval and (3.4) give the exact two-copy identity

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(TP\|Q)
=
\mathbb E\prod_{e\in E}
\left(1+\rho_e^2\lambda_e J_e\right).
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

Indeed, expanding the right side sums one monomial for every `S subseteq A intersect A'`, and averaging recovers precisely the squared Fourier coefficient of `S` multiplied by its noise eigenvalue squared.

## 4. B3 — stronger theorem: negative association removes `kappa` completely

The preceding report bounded (3.10) after conditioning on one support `A`, which introduced

\[
C=\sup_A\sum_{e\in A}q_e
\]

and the ratio `kappa=C/sum q_e^2`. That loses the rarity of extreme supports. The correct move is to keep both copies and use negative association on their intersection.

### Lemma 4.1 — intersections of two independent NA support vectors are NA

Suppose the indicator vector

\[
X=(\mathbf 1_{\{e\in A\}})_{e\in E}
\]

is negatively associated, and let `X'` be an independent copy. Then

\[
J=(X_eX'_e)_{e\in E}
\]

is negatively associated.

**Proof.** Let `I,K subseteq E` be disjoint and let `f,g` be bounded coordinatewise increasing functions on the corresponding `J` blocks. Condition on `X=x`. Negative association of `X'` gives

\[
\mathbb E_{X'}
\big[f(x_I X'_I)g(x_K X'_K)\big]
\le
F(x_I)G(x_K),
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
F(x_I):=\mathbb E_{X'}f(x_I X'_I),
\qquad
G(x_K):=\mathbb E_{X'}g(x_K X'_K).
\]

Because `f,g` are increasing and all coordinates are `0/1`, `F` and `G` are increasing functions of disjoint blocks of `X`. Negative association of `X` therefore gives

\[
\mathbb E F(X_I)G(X_K)
\le
\mathbb E F(X_I)\,\mathbb E G(X_K).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The two factors on the right are exactly `E f(J_I)` and `E g(J_K)`. This proves negative association of `J`. `□`

### Theorem 4.2 — matched-independent Rényi-2 domination

Assume the random support law `nu` is negatively associated. Then for every coordinatewise product-resampling operator `T` as above,

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(TP\|Q)
\le
\prod_{e\in E}
\left(1+\rho_e^2\lambda_e q_e^2\right).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently, with `D_2` denoting order-two Rényi divergence,

\[
\boxed{
D_2(TP\|Q)
\le
\sum_{e\in E}
\log\left(1+\rho_e^2\lambda_e q_e^2\right)
\le
\sum_{e\in E}\rho_e^2\lambda_e q_e^2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Writing

\[
\rho_*:=\max_{e\in E}\rho_e,
\]

we obtain for every `D>=1`

\[
\boxed{
D_2(TP\|Q)
\le
\rho_*^2 b_1
\le
\rho_*^2
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\right)^2.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(TP\|Q)
\le
\exp\!\left(
\rho_*^2
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\right)^2
\right)-1.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

**Proof.** By Lemma 4.1, the intersection indicators `J_e` are negatively associated. For

\[
a_e:=\rho_e^2\lambda_e\ge0,
\]

each function `h_e(J_e)=1+a_eJ_e` is nonnegative and increasing. Iterating the defining NA inequality over singleton coordinate blocks yields

\[
\mathbb E\prod_e(1+a_eJ_e)
\le
\prod_e\mathbb E(1+a_eJ_e).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `A,A'` are independent,

\[
\Pr(J_e=1)=q_e^2.
\]

Combining (4.7) with the exact identity (3.10) proves (4.3). Taking logarithms and using `log(1+x)<=x` proves (4.4). Finally

\[
\sum_e\rho_e^2\lambda_eq_e^2
\le
\rho_*^2\sum_e\lambda_eq_e^2
=
\rho_*^2 b_1,
\]

and (3.7) gives (4.5); exponentiating gives (4.6). `□`

### Corollary 4.3 — source-style implication

Let `(P_n,Q_n)` be any sequence of the above forced-support models with negatively associated support laws, allowing arbitrary nonexchangeable supports and heterogeneous product Bernoulli nulls. Let `D_n>=1`; in particular one may take the source scale

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\Omega(1)}.
\]

If

\[
\sup_n \operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\le A<\infty,
\]

then even with no noise (`rho_*=1`),

\[
\sup_n D_2(P_n\|Q_n)\le A^2,
\qquad
\sup_n\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)\le e^{A^2}-1.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus strong distinguishing is impossible for **every measurable test**, a conclusion stronger than exclusion of any specified polynomial-degree class.

For completeness, if `L=dP/dQ`,

\[
1-\|P-Q\|_{TV}
=\mathbb E_Q\min(L,1)
\ge
\mathbb E_Q\frac{L}{1+L}
=
\mathbb E_P\frac1{1+L}
\ge
\frac1{1+\mathbb E_P L}
=
\frac1{2+\chi^2(P\|Q)}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Therefore under (4.8),

\[
\boxed{
\|P_n-Q_n\|_{TV}
\le
1-\frac1{1+e^{A^2}}
<1
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

uniformly in `n`. If the low-degree advantage tends to zero, then (4.6) makes `chi^2 ->0`, and `TV <= (1/2)sqrt(chi^2)` gives `TV->0`.

This theorem is broader than the previous `kappa` statement in two independent senses: it removes the worst-support parameter entirely, and it permits heterogeneous null marginals/noise levels.

## 6. B20 — why the old `kappa` condition fails: rare extremal supports are irrelevant

The previous report defined, in the homogeneous case,

\[
S_2=\sum_e q_e^2,
\qquad
C=\sup_{A\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)}\sum_{e\in A}q_e,
\qquad
\kappa=C/S_2.
\]

That quantity can diverge merely because an astronomically rare support is large.

Take `E=[N]` and let the support indicators be **independent** Bernoulli variables with

\[
q_e=1/N.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is stronger than negative association. Yet every subset, including the full set `E`, has positive probability. Therefore

\[
S_2=N\frac1{N^2}=\frac1N,
\qquad
C=N\frac1N=1,
\qquad
\boxed{\kappa=N\to\infty.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

So the old bounded-`kappa` hypothesis fails maximally.

Nevertheless, because the support coordinates are independent, (5.1) is exact. In the homogeneous case `p_e=p`, `lambda=(1-p)/p`, and `rho_e=rho`,

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
=
\left(1+\frac{\rho^2\lambda}{N^2}\right)^N,
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

hence

\[
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
\le
\exp(\rho^2\lambda/N)-1
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

At degree one,

\[
\boxed{
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le1}(P,Q_p)\right)^2
=b_1=\lambda/N\to0.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus `kappa` was not a structural necessity at all: it was an artifact of conditioning on one support and then taking a worst-support supremum. The rare full support drove `C`, even though it contributes negligibly to the true two-copy moment.

The strengthened theorem answers the more ambitious failure question inside this model class as well. **There is no negatively associated forced-support family with bounded low-degree advantage but a noise-surviving high-degree chi-square signal:** (4.5) rules it out. A Mao-type phenomenon must therefore leave this structural class — for example by failing a monotone forced-support representation, failing negative association, or encoding its signal through a qualitatively different global algebraic mechanism.

**B20 outcome:** successful. It supplies an exact failure family for the old condition and identifies precisely why the proof route, rather than the phenomenon, was defective.
