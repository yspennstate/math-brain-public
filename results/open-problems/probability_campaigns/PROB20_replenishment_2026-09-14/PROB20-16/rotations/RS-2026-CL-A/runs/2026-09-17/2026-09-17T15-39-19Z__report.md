## 3. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE: the spectral pressure

Define the finite spectral partition polynomial

\[
\boxed{
F(q):=\sum_{j=0}^{M} b_j q^j,
\qquad b_0:=1,
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `M` is the maximal polynomial degree in the finite observation space. For product resampling noise with exact degree multiplier `rho^j`, (1.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
F(\rho^2)-1=\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For every `q>0`, define the tilted degree law

\[
\boxed{
\nu_q(j):=\frac{b_jq^j}{F(q)}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let `J` denote a random variable with law `nu_q`. Writing `Theta=q d/dq`, direct differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{
\Theta\log F(q)=\mathbb E_{\nu_q}J,
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Theta^2\log F(q)=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_q}(J)\ge0.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Proof of (3.4)–(3.5)

Since

\[
qF'(q)=\sum_j j b_jq^j,
\]

division by `F(q)` gives (3.4). Applying `Theta` once more,

\[
\Theta\left(\frac{\sum_j j b_jq^j}{F(q)}\right)
=
\frac{\sum_j j^2b_jq^j}{F(q)}
-
\left(\frac{\sum_j j b_jq^j}{F(q)}\right)^2,
\]
which is exactly (3.5).

Thus the spectral-cliff issue can be read as a pressure problem. `Theta log F` is the mean spectral degree seen at noise parameter `q`, and `Theta^2 log F` is its variance. A sudden migration of mass from low degrees to a recoverable high-degree band must manifest as rapid pressure growth somewhere between `q=0` and the observation scale.

This differential language by itself is only a reformulation. The next theorem identifies a concrete structural condition under which the pressure cannot develop such a cliff.

## 4. B5 — a real-rooted spectral certificate

### Theorem 4.1 — real nonpositive spectral zeros rule out hidden high-degree recovery

Assume the nonnegative-coefficient spectral polynomial `F` from (3.1) has only real nonpositive zeros. Then there are numbers `alpha_1,...,alpha_m>=0` such that

\[
\boxed{
F(q)=\prod_{r=1}^{m}(1+\alpha_r q).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
F(q)\le \exp(qb_1)
\quad\text{for every }q\ge0,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

and if `Adv^{<=D}(P,Q)<=A` for any `D>=1`, then

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q)
\le e^{\rho^2A^2}-1.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular, the same conclusion at `rho=1` gives the **unnoised** bound

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P\|Q)\le e^{A^2}-1.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence no measurable test — and therefore no polynomial statistic of any degree — can strongly distinguish `P` from `Q`. More explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
\|T_\rho P-Q\|_{TV}
\le
1-\frac{1}{1+e^{\rho^2A^2}}
<1.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

If the low-degree advantage tends to zero, then the right side of (4.3) tends to zero and therefore total variation tends to zero.

### Proof

Because `F(0)=1`, all coefficients are nonnegative, and every zero is real and nonpositive, the factorization has the form (4.1). The linear coefficient is

\[
b_1=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\alpha_r.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For `q>=0`, `log(1+x)<=x` gives

\[
\log F(q)
=
\sum_r\log(1+\alpha_rq)
\le
q\sum_r\alpha_r
=qb_1.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Exponentiating proves (4.2). Since `D>=1`,

\[
b_1\le\sum_{j=1}^{D}b_j
=\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
\le A^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Combining (3.2), (4.2), and (4.8) proves (4.3); taking `rho=1` proves (4.4).

For any density `M=d\mu/dQ`,

\[
1-\|\mu-Q\|_{TV}
=\mathbb E_Q\min(1,M)
\ge
\mathbb E_Q\frac{M}{1+M}.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz in Engel form gives

\[
\mathbb E_Q\frac{M}{1+M}
\ge
\frac{(\mathbb E_QM)^2}{\mathbb E_Q[M(1+M)]}
=
\frac{1}{2+\chi^2(\mu\|Q)}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Using (4.3),

\[
2+\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q)
\le1+e^{\rho^2A^2},
\]
which yields (4.5).

### Corollary 4.2 — the tilted spectrum is Poisson-binomial

Under the same real-rooted hypothesis,

\[
\frac{F(qs)}{F(q)}
=
\prod_{r=1}^{m}
\frac{1+\alpha_rqs}{1+\alpha_rq}
=
\prod_{r=1}^{m}\bigl((1-p_r(q))+p_r(q)s\bigr),
\tag{4.10}
\]
where

\[
p_r(q)=\frac{q\alpha_r}{1+q\alpha_r}.
\]

Therefore `nu_q` is the law of a sum of independent Bernoulli variables. In particular,

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_q}(J)
=\sum_r p_r(q)(1-p_r(q))
\le
\sum_rp_r(q)
=\mathbb E_{\nu_q}J.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Combining with (3.4)–(3.5), the pressure obeys the differential no-cliff inequality

\[
\boxed{
\Theta^2\log F(q)\le \Theta\log F(q).
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

This gives an explicit mechanism: real-rootedness converts the entire high-degree spectrum into a Bernoulli-sum tilt whose fluctuations cannot grow faster than its mean.

### Stress test against Mao

If `b_1=0` under (4.1), then every `alpha_r=0`, hence `F\equiv1` and all higher `b_j` vanish. Mao’s construction has a long exact low-degree void followed by a recoverable high-degree signal. Therefore its spectral polynomial cannot satisfy this real-nonpositive-zero certificate. This excludes the counterexample for a structural spectral-shape reason, not by naming rank or Reed–Muller codes in the hypothesis.

### Concrete covered forced-support subclass

In the forced-support model of (1.2), suppose latent support coordinates are included independently with probabilities `theta_i`. Then

\[
\Pr(S\subseteq A)=\prod_{i\in S}\theta_i,
\]
so

\[
b_j=\lambda^j e_j(\theta_1^2,\ldots,\theta_N^2)
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
F(q)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1+\lambda\theta_i^2q).
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Thus Theorem 4.1 applies exactly. This is a concrete distributional mechanism yielding the real-rooted certificate; it is not merely a condition stated on the desired testing error.

I do **not** claim that arbitrary symmetric planted-subgraph overlap polynomials are real-rooted. Establishing or refuting real-rootedness in broad dependent-support classes is a separate problem and would require its own evidence.

## 5. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: exact extension-flow identity

The previous report expressed the ratio `b_{j+1}/b_j` through a size-biased overlap law. The same quantity admits a more local subset-lattice interpretation that exposes a conserved mass flow.

Continue with the forced-support model. For every `S subseteq [N]`, define

\[
\boxed{
w(S):=\Pr(S\subseteq A)^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

For each level `j`, let

\[
a_j:=\sum_{|S|=j}w(S).
\tag{5.2}
\]

By the two-copy identity,

\[
a_j=\mathbb E\binom Zj,
\qquad
b_j=\lambda^j a_j.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Whenever `w(S)>0`, define the local extension load

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E(S)
:=
\sum_{i\notin S}
\frac{w(S\cup\{i\})}{w(S)}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and let the level-`j` size-biased law be

\[
\pi_j(S):=\frac{w(S)}{a_j},
\qquad |S|=j.
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — extension mass is conserved between adjacent levels

For every `j` with `a_j>0`,

\[
\boxed{
a_j\,\mathbb E_{\pi_j}\mathcal E(S)=(j+1)a_{j+1}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_j}
=
\frac{\lambda}{j+1}
\mathbb E_{\pi_j}\mathcal E(S).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

### Proof

Multiply (5.4) by `w(S)` and sum over all `j`-sets:

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_j\mathbb E_{\pi_j}\mathcal E(S)
&=
\sum_{|S|=j}w(S)\mathcal E(S)\\
&=
\sum_{|S|=j}\sum_{i\notin S}w(S\cup\{i\}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Every `(j+1)`-set `T` appears exactly `j+1` times on the right, once for each deletion `T\setminus\{i\}`. Hence the right side equals

\[
(j+1)\sum_{|T|=j+1}w(T)=(j+1)a_{j+1},
\]
proving (5.6). Multiplying the ratio `a_{j+1}/a_j` by `lambda` proves (5.7).

Because `Pr(S\cup\{i\}\subseteq A)<=Pr(S\subseteq A)`, each summand in (5.4) is at most one. Thus

\[
0\le\mathcal E(S)\le N-j.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Equation (5.6) is the conserved quantity this run was looking for: **the total weighted extension flow out of level `j` is exactly `(j+1)` times the total mass at level `j+1`.** A high-degree spectral cliff must therefore be paid for by anomalously large extension flow under the level-`j` planted-support bias.

This identity is equivalent algebraically to the previous overlap ratio formula, but its local form is better suited to separator/interface arguments because it asks how many one-coordinate extensions remain compatible with a typical partially shared planted support.

## 6. B28 — REPLACE FOR ALL WITH FOR TYPICAL: exceptional supports may be harmless

The previous no-cliff theorem required a pointwise-in-degree bound on `b_{j+1}/b_j`. The local extension identity allows a first weakening inside each degree.

Fix a threshold `u_j>=0` and define the good set

\[
G_j:=\{S:|S|=j,\ \mathcal E(S)\le u_j\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
\varepsilon_j:=\pi_j(G_j^c)
\tag{6.2}
\]

be the **weighted**, not counting, fraction of bad level-`j` states. By (5.9),

\[
\mathbb E_{\pi_j}\mathcal E(S)
\le
u_j+\varepsilon_j(N-j),
\tag{6.3}
\]

where the first term is `u_j` (the symbol `u_j`, not Greek nu). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_j}
\le
\frac{\lambda}{j+1}
\left[u_j+\varepsilon_j(N-j)\right].
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This proves a genuine “typical support” version of the interface condition: a small exceptional family is permitted, provided its **planted-support mass** is small enough. One does not need to control every partial support `S`.

The inequality also makes clear which notion of typicality is relevant. Uniform counting measure on level `j` is generally wrong; the correct measure is `pi_j`, proportional to the squared containment probability `Pr(S subseteq A)^2`, because that is the measure which controls the likelihood-ratio energy.

## 7. B28 — averaged-degree no-cliff theorem

There is a second, independent way to replace “for all” by a typical/average condition: allow some degree transitions themselves to be expansive, provided the cumulative geometric mean contracts.

Fix `q=rho^2 in (0,1)` and suppose `b_D>0`. Define

\[
\boxed{
r_j:=q\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_j}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then for every `m>=1`, telescoping gives the exact identity

\[
q^{D+m}b_{D+m}
=
q^Db_D
\prod_{\ell=D}^{D+m-1}r_\ell.
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — negative average logarithmic growth suffices

Assume there exist constants `C>=1` and `eta>0` such that for every `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{\ell=D}^{D+m-1}r_\ell
\le Ce^{-\eta m}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{\ell=D}^{D+m-1}\log r_\ell
\le\log C-\eta m
\tag{7.4}
\]

whenever all ratios are positive. Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j>D}q^jb_j
\le
q^Db_D\frac{C}{e^\eta-1}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

If moreover the source low-degree advantage through `D` is at most `A`, then `b_D<=A^2` and

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q)
\le
A^2+A^2q^D\frac{C}{e^\eta-1}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

For fixed `rho<1`, `D->infinity`, bounded `A,C,1/eta` therefore imply a uniform total-variation gap from perfect distinguishing. If `A->0`, total variation tends to zero.

### Proof

Sum (7.2) over `m>=1` and use (7.3):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j>D}q^jb_j
&=q^Db_D\sum_{m\ge1}
\prod_{\ell=D}^{D+m-1}r_\ell\\
&\le q^Db_D\,C\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-\eta m}\\
&=q^Db_D\frac{C}{e^\eta-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (7.5). The low-degree contribution to noisy `chi^2` is at most `A^2`, and `b_D<=A^2`, proving (7.6).

### Why this is strictly weaker than pointwise contraction

The preceding run required `r_j<=1-delta` for every `j>=D`. Theorem 7.1 permits individual `r_j>1`, even repeatedly, as long as the prefix products contract at a uniform exponential rate. Thus isolated or compensated spectral spikes are allowed. What is prohibited is a sustained positive log-growth rate capable of rebuilding macroscopic high-degree energy faster than noise damps it.

If `b_D=0` but a later `b_j>0`, the ratio chain is undefined at the onset. That is not a technical nuisance: it is exactly the “spectral cliff” phenomenon exemplified by Mao. The averaged-ratio hypothesis is intentionally a no-cliff condition and must fail at such a zero-to-positive transition.

## 8. Combining B12 and B28: a typical-interface criterion

From (5.7), with `q=rho^2`,

\[
r_j
=
\frac{q\lambda}{j+1}
\mathbb E_{\pi_j}\mathcal E(S).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Using (6.3), it is enough to set

\[
\widetilde r_j
:=
\frac{q\lambda}{j+1}
\left[u_j+\varepsilon_j(N-j)\right]
\tag{8.2}
\]

and verify the averaged prefix-product condition

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{\ell=D}^{D+m-1}\widetilde r_\ell
\le Ce^{-\eta m}
\quad\text{for every }m\ge1.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Then Theorem 7.1 follows automatically.

This is the strongest structural output of B12+B28 in this run:

> It is not necessary that every partial planted support have a small interface, nor that every adjacent spectral ratio contract. It suffices that under the *likelihood-ratio-relevant size-biased support law*, most partial supports have controlled extension load, and that the resulting degree-by-degree extension factors have negative cumulative logarithmic growth after noise.

That is closer in spirit to a usable separator/interface framework than the previous worst-case ratio condition. It also identifies a concrete place where a Chen–Li-style decomposition could enter: their small-interface argument may be viewed as one method for proving a bound on the weighted extension flow. I have **not** proved that their theorem is a special case of (8.3), and I do not claim such containment.

## 9. Relation to the mandatory counterexamples

### 9.1 Mao

Mao has exact zero low-degree advantage through a long cutoff yet a fixed-noise polynomial-time distinguisher. Therefore:

- The real-rooted spectral certificate of Theorem 4.1 must fail unless all higher spectral energy is zero.
- Any positive-anchor averaged ratio condition such as (7.3) must fail at the first zero-to-positive onset.
- In a support-style representation, a typical-interface criterion capable of proving (8.3) cannot hold across that onset.

This is a substantive exclusion mechanism: the counterexample is ruled out because it hides recoverable energy behind a spectral cliff, not because the hypotheses say “not a rank construction.”

### 9.2 Buhai et al.

The same qualitative diagnosis applies to the quasi-polynomial counterexample: very long low-degree pseudorandomness coexists with global list-decoding structure. The present criteria would require either real-rooted spectral regularity or negative average log-growth of the noisy spectral ratios; a global signal that reappears after a long low-degree void necessarily violates such a no-cliff condition.

### 9.3 Chen–Li

Chen–Li supply a positive graph-side mechanism based on tree decomposition and small interfaces. The extension-flow identity (5.6) gives a plausible common language: high-degree likelihood energy equals weighted mass transported through compatible one-step extensions. But this report does **not** prove an equivalence or strict generalization. Establishing a shape-by-shape inequality from their interface decomposition to (8.3) remains open work.
