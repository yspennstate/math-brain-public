## 4. B18 — say it in another language: a finite-block/Fock filtered-coercivity theorem

The correct first abstraction is not a graph-specific Ising computation but an orthogonal block decomposition.

### Theorem 4.1 (finite-block product nulls have linear source-degree coercivity)

Let `q` be a full-support probability law on `{-1,+1}^r`, where `r` is fixed. For `0<=s<=r`, let

\[
V_s^{(0)}
=
\{\text{ordinary algebraic polynomials of degree }\le s\}
\subset L^2(q),
\]

and define the orthogonal layers

\[
U_0=\operatorname{span}\{1\},
\qquad
U_s=V_s^{(0)}\cap(V_{s-1}^{(0)})^\perp
\quad(1\le s\le r).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let `A_0>=0` be any self-adjoint reversible Markov generator on `L^2(q)` with `A_0 1=0`, spectral gap

\[
g
:=
\inf_{0\ne f\perp1}
\frac{\langle f,A_0f\rangle_q}{\|f\|_{2,q}^2}
>0,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and operator norm `Lambda=||A_0||`.

For `m>=1`, put

\[
Q_m=q^{\otimes m},
\qquad
A_m=\sum_{k=1}^m A_0^{(k)},
\tag{4.3}
\]

on `rm` binary coordinates. Let `P_D^{(m)}` denote the `L^2(Q_m)` orthogonal projection onto **ordinary source algebraic degree** at most `D`, and let `lambda_D^{(m)}` be `(3.2)` for `(Q_m,A_m)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_D^{(m)}
\ge
 g\left\lceil\frac{D+1}{r}\right\rceil.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This needs no local degree preservation and no commutator assumption.

### Proof

Because the local layers `(U_s)_{s=0}^r` are mutually orthogonal and span `L^2(q)`, the product space has the exact orthogonal decomposition

\[
L^2(Q_m)
=
\bigoplus_{\mathbf s\in\{0,\ldots,r\}^m}
U_{s_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{s_m}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

A global squarefree monomial factors block-by-block, and its total ordinary algebraic degree is the sum of its block degrees. Decomposing each block polynomial into `(U_s)` therefore gives

\[
V_D^{(m)}
=
\bigoplus_{s_1+\cdots+s_m\le D}
U_{s_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{s_m}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the source filtration, not the spectrum of `A_m`.

Now group `(4.5)` by the **active block set**

\[
I(\mathbf s)=\{k:s_k>0\}.
\]

For a fixed `I`, let

\[
\mathcal H_I
=
\bigotimes_{k\in I}U_0^\perp
\otimes
\bigotimes_{k\notin I}U_0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `A_0 1=0` and `A_0` is self-adjoint, both `U_0` and `U_0^\perp` are invariant. Hence every `H_I` is invariant under `A_m`. On `H_I`, each active block contributes at least the local gap `g`, so tensorization gives

\[
\langle f,A_m f\rangle
\ge g|I|\|f\|_2^2,
\qquad f\in\mathcal H_I.
\tag{4.8}
\]

If `|I|<=floor(D/r)`, then every tensor in `H_I` has total source degree at most `r|I|<=D`; hence

\[
\mathcal H_I\subseteq V_D^{(m)}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Therefore a vector in `(V_D^{(m)})^perp` has no component in any active-set sector with `|I|<=floor(D/r)`. Orthogonality of the active-set decomposition and `(4.8)` yield

\[
\langle h,A_mh\rangle
\ge
 g\left(\left\lfloor\frac Dr\right\rfloor+1\right)\|h\|_2^2
=
 g\left\lceil\frac{D+1}{r}\right\rceil\|h\|_2^2.
\]

This proves `(4.4)`. `square`

### Meaning

This is a useful broadening of the exact 1D Ising calculation. Independent finite nonproduct Gibbs blocks automatically have `lambda_D=Omega(D)` in the **source algebraic filtration**, even when the local generator mixes algebraic degrees. The problem is therefore not filtered coercivity alone. The unresolved issue is the commutator `epsilon_D`, which B24 now isolates sharply.

## 5. B24 — find the extremal object: Hamming-slice amplification of any local degree mixing

The extremal obstruction is coherent tensor amplification of a single nonzero local cross-degree matrix element.

### Theorem 5.1 (any fixed local degree mixing produces macroscopic commutator leakage)

Keep the setup of Theorem 4.1. Suppose there exist local layers `U_a,U_b` with

\[
0\le a<b\le r
\]

and

\[
c
:=
\|P_{U_b}A_0P_{U_a}\|_{2\to2}
>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the `m`-block tensor product choose unit vectors `u in U_a`, `v in U_b` with

\[
\langle v,A_0u\rangle=c
\tag{5.2}
\]

after changing the sign of `v` if necessary. Put

\[
r_m=\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
D_m=am+(b-a)r_m.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then the source-degree commutator satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{D_m}^{(m)}
\ge
c\sqrt{(r_m+1)(m-r_m)}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Moreover, since `lambda_{D_m}^{(m)}<=m Lambda`,

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{m\to\infty}
\frac{\varepsilon_{D_m}^{(m)}}{\lambda_{D_m}^{(m)}}
\ge
\frac{c}{2\Lambda}
>0.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus `epsilon_D=o(lambda_D)` is impossible for tensor powers of **any** fixed block whose reversible generator has one nonzero cross-degree matrix element.

### Proof

Inside `L^2(Q_m)` consider the `2^m`-dimensional tensor subspace

\[
\mathcal W=(\operatorname{span}\{u,v\})^{\otimes m}.
\]

For `0<=k<=m`, let `W_k` be the span of tensor words having exactly `k` copies of `v` and `m-k` copies of `u`. Since `u in U_a` and `v in U_b`, every vector of `W_k` lies in the exact orthogonal source-degree layer

\[
am+(b-a)k.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence by `(5.3)`,

\[
W_{r_m}\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}P_{D_m}^{(m)},
\qquad
W_{r_m+1}\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}(I-P_{D_m}^{(m)}).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Compress `A_m=sum_l A_0^{(l)}` from `W_{r_m}` to `W_{r_m+1}`. To increase the number of `v` factors by exactly one, a single local term must act on one of the `u` positions and contribute its `v` component. By `(5.2)` that amplitude is exactly `c`. Therefore, in the natural bases indexed by `r_m`- and `(r_m+1)`-subsets of `[m]`,

\[
P_{W_{r_m+1}}A_m|_{W_{r_m}}
=c\,\mathcal U_{r_m},
\tag{5.8}
\]

where `U_r` is the inclusion matrix between adjacent Hamming slices:

\[
(\mathcal U_r)_{T,S}=1_{\{S\subset T\}},
\qquad |S|=r,\ |T|=r+1.
\]

This bipartite incidence graph is `(m-r,r+1)`-biregular. Its all-ones left and right vectors exhibit the singular value

\[
\sqrt{(r+1)(m-r)}.
\]

Conversely the Schur bound (or the row/column-sum bound for the nonnegative incidence matrix) gives no larger singular value. Thus

\[
\|\mathcal U_r\|
=
\sqrt{(r+1)(m-r)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

For an orthogonal projection `P` and self-adjoint `A`, the commutator has off-diagonal block form and

\[
\|[A,P]\|
=
\|(I-P)AP\|.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Combining `(5.7)`–`(5.10)` proves `(5.4)`.

For `(5.5)`, choose any nonzero vector in `W_{r_m+1}`. Its Rayleigh quotient under `A_m` is at most `||A_m||<=m Lambda`, so the infimum defining the high-degree coercivity obeys

\[
\lambda_{D_m}^{(m)}\le m\Lambda.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Since

\[
\frac1m\sqrt{(r_m+1)(m-r_m)}\to\frac12,
\]

`(5.4)` and `(5.11)` yield `(5.5)`. `square`

### Consequence for the proposed high-temperature route

The theorem changes the target. A broad weak-dependence theorem cannot reasonably aim for

\[
\varepsilon_D=o(\lambda_D)
\]

unless the local model has exact or asymptotically exact source-degree preservation. What may still be true and useful is a **small relative commutator** estimate

\[
\varepsilon_D\le \rho\lambda_D,
\qquad \rho<1/2,
\tag{5.12}
\]

with `rho` controlled by interaction strength. The preceding abstract semigroup theorem was already designed to tolerate a nonzero commutator, so `(5.12)` is the more natural next target.

## 6. B2 — attack the negation first: an exact pairwise high-temperature Ising block

Theorem 5.1 becomes a direct counterexample to the hoped-for implication “small Dobrushin dependence => relative commutator tends to zero” once one supplies a genuine pairwise high-temperature block with `c>0`.

### 6.1 Exact three-spin block

On the three-vertex path `1-2-3`, define

\[
q_{h,J}(x_1,x_2,x_3)
\propto
\exp\{h x_2+Jx_1x_2+Jx_2x_3\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is an ordinary pairwise Ising model with maximum graph degree two. Let `A_0=sum_{i=1}^3(I-E_i)` be its heat-bath generator.

For the center spin,

\[
E_2[x_2\mid x_1,x_3]
=
\tanh(h+Jx_1+Jx_3).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Choose the exact parameters

\[
e^{2h}=2,
\qquad
e^{2J}=\frac43.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Then

\[
\tanh(h+2J)=\frac{23}{41},
\qquad
\tanh h=\frac13,
\qquad
\tanh(h-2J)=\frac1{17}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The coefficient of `x_1x_3` in the squarefree Walsh expansion of `(6.2)` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
d
&=\frac14\left[
\tanh(h+2J)-2\tanh h+\tanh(h-2J)
\right]\\
&=\boxed{-\frac{49}{4182}}\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Since `A_0x_2=x_2-E_2x_2`, `(6.5)` shows that `A_0` sends an ordinary degree-one polynomial to a polynomial with a genuine degree-two component.

Because the Gibbs law has full support, the squarefree monomials are linearly independent as functions, so `x_1x_3 notin V_1^{(0)}`. Also `A_0` annihilates constants. Writing `U_1=V_1^{(0)}\cap1^perp` and `U_2=V_2^{(0)}\cap(V_1^{(0)})^perp`, we therefore have

\[
\boxed{P_{U_2}A_0P_{U_1}\ne0.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus the constant `c` in Theorem 5.1 is strictly positive.

### 6.2 Yet the Dobrushin constant is small

For an Ising edge of coupling `J`, the single-neighbor total-variation influence is bounded by `tanh|J|`. Here

\[
\tanh J
=
\frac{(4/3)-1}{(4/3)+1}
=
\frac17.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The center has two neighbors and the endpoints one, so the Dobrushin row-sum constant obeys

\[
\boxed{\alpha_{\rm Dob}\le\frac27<1.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

This is safely in the uniqueness/high-temperature regime.

More strongly, fix any `h>0` and take `0<J<h/2`. Since `tanh` is strictly concave on `(0,infinity)`,

\[
\frac14\{	anh(h+2J)-2\tanh h+\tanh(h-2J)\}<0,
\tag{6.9}
\]

so the cross-degree element stays nonzero for every `J>0`, while

\[
\alpha_{\rm Dob}\le2\tanh J\to0
\quad(J\downarrow0).
\tag{6.10}
\]

Therefore arbitrarily weak but fixed pairwise interaction can produce a fixed nonzero local degree-mixing matrix element.

### 6.3 Tensorization gives a non-vanishing relative commutator

Take `m` independent copies of `(6.1)`. This is still a pairwise Ising model, now on a disjoint union of three-vertex paths, with maximum degree two and the same Dobrushin constant. Theorem 4.1 gives

\[
\lambda_D^{(m)}
\ge
 g\left\lceil\frac{D+1}{3}\right\rceil
=
\Omega(D),
\tag{6.11}
\]

where `g>0` is the fixed three-spin block gap. But Theorem 5.1 with `a=1,b=2` gives cutoffs

\[
D_m=m+r_m\sim\frac32m
\tag{6.12}
\]

for which

\[
\liminf_m
\frac{\varepsilon_{D_m}^{(m)}}{\lambda_{D_m}^{(m)}}
>0.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Thus even a very high-temperature bounded-degree **pairwise** Ising family can have linear filtered coercivity and yet fail `epsilon_D=o(lambda_D)`.

### 6.4 Padding places the obstruction exactly at Kireeva's superlogarithmic cutoff

The preceding tensor family has `D_m=Theta(m)`. To make the same obstruction occur at the source scale, fix any `delta>0` and append independent unbiased spins, which introduce no extra interactions and do not alter the active block commutator. Choose the total number of coordinates

\[
N_m
=
\left\lceil
\exp\left(D_m^{1/(1+\delta)}\right)
\right\rceil.
\tag{6.14}
\]

For all sufficiently large `m`, `N_m>3m` and

\[
\left\lfloor(\log N_m)^{1+\delta}\right\rfloor
=D_m
\tag{6.15}
\]

(up to an immaterial eventual `O(1)` adjustment if one chooses a neighboring integer). The Dobrushin row sum is unchanged, because the appended sites are independent.

The lower bound `(5.4)` still acts entirely inside the first `3m` coordinates. The high-degree coercivity denominator also remains `O(m)`: a unit vector from `W_{r_m+1}` is constant on every padded coordinate, so its Rayleigh quotient receives no dummy-site contribution and is at most `m Lambda`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_m
\frac{\varepsilon_{D_m}}{\lambda_{D_m}}
>0
\quad\text{at}\quad
D_m=(\log N_m)^{1+\delta}+O(1).
}
\tag{6.16}
\]

This directly refutes the proposed structural implication

\[
\text{bounded degree + arbitrarily small fixed Dobrushin constant}
\Longrightarrow
\varepsilon_{D_n}=o(\lambda_{D_n})
\]

at Kireeva's source scale.

It does **not** refute the more plausible quantitative statement `(5.12)` with a small interaction-dependent constant `rho`, and it does not itself produce a Mao-type efficiently distinguishable alternative. It is a boundary result about which physical-space hypotheses can power the source-degree semigroup theorem.
