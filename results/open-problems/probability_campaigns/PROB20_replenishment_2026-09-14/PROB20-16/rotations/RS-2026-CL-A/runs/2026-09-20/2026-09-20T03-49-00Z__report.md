## 3. A variance-sensitive sandwiching defect

Fix a probability law `Q` on a finite state space, a polynomial filtration `P_{<=D}` containing constants, and a Markov operator `T` on functions such that `Q` is stationary:

`E_Q[Tf]=E_Q[f]`.

Write

`A_D(P,Q)=Adv_Q^{<=D}(P,Q)`.

For a function `u`, define the degree-`D` **L2 sandwiching defect**

`Sand_D(u;Q)`

`:= inf ||a||_{L2(Q)}`

over pairs `g,a in P_{<=D}` satisfying pointwise

`a>=0` and `|u-g|<=a`.

Equivalently, `g-a <= u <= g+a` are degree-`D` polynomial lower/upper sandwiches whose half-width has small `L2(Q)` norm. The use of `L2`, rather than only the classical `L1` sandwiching slack, is forced by the fact that bounded LDA controls expectation errors in units of standard deviation.

## 4. Main theorem — bounded source LDA transfers through a low-degree sandwich

### Theorem 4.1 (variance-sensitive sandwiching transfer)

Let `P,Q,T,D` be as above and set `A=A_D(P,Q)<infinity`. Then for every `f in L2(Q)`,

`|E_{PT} f - E_Q f|`

`<= A sqrt(Var_Q f) + (2+2A) Sand_D(Tf;Q)`.

Here `PT` denotes the law obtained by applying the Markov kernel dual to `T` to a sample from `P`.

Consequently, for any test class `F`, if

`eta_D(F,T;Q)`

`:= sup_{f in F, Var_Q f>0} Sand_D(Tf;Q)/sqrt(Var_Q f)`,

then

`sup_{f in F, Var_Q f>0}`

` |E_{PT}f-E_Qf|/sqrt(Var_Q f)`

`<= A + (2+2A) eta_D(F,T;Q)`.

Thus bounded source LDA through degree `D` implies bounded post-noise distinguishing advantage for **any much richer test class** whose noised members have uniformly bounded degree-`D` sandwiching defect. No chi-square, Renyi, smoothed-L2, covariance-pressure, raw-Gram, or adapted-frame condition on the planted likelihood is required.

### Proof

Fix `f` and let `sigma>Sand_D(Tf;Q)`. Choose `g,a in P_{<=D}` with

`a>=0`, `|Tf-g|<=a`, and `||a||_{2,Q}<=sigma`.

Let `m=E_Q f=E_Q Tf` by stationarity. Because a stationary Markov operator is an `L2(Q)` contraction,

`||Tf-m||_{2,Q} <= sqrt(Var_Q f)`.

Also

`||g-m||_{2,Q}`

`<= ||Tf-m||_{2,Q}+||g-Tf||_{2,Q}`

`<= sqrt(Var_Q f)+sigma`.

Hence

`|E_P g-E_Q g|`

`<= A sqrt(Var_Q g)`

`<= A( sqrt(Var_Q f)+sigma )`.          (4.1)

Put `r=Tf-g`, so `|r|<=a`. Then

`|E_P r-E_Q r| <= E_P a+E_Q a`.         (4.2)

Since `a` is itself degree at most `D`, bounded LDA gives

`E_P a <= E_Q a + A sqrt(Var_Q a)`.

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

`E_Q a <= ||a||_2 <= sigma`,

`sqrt(Var_Q a)<=||a||_2<=sigma`.

Therefore (4.2) is at most

`(2+A)sigma`.                             (4.3)

Finally,

`E_{PT}f-E_Qf = E_P[Tf]-E_Q[Tf]`

`=(E_Pg-E_Qg)+(E_Pr-E_Qr)`.

Combining (4.1) and (4.3) gives

`|E_{PT}f-E_Qf|`

`<= A sqrt(Var_Q f)+(2+2A)sigma`.

Let `sigma downarrow Sand_D(Tf;Q)`. This proves the first claim. Divide by `sqrt(Var_Qf)` and take the supremum over `F` for the second. `square`

## 5. Strong bounded tests: no rare planted-tail assumption at all

The preceding theorem gives a particularly clean strong-distinguishing corollary.

### Corollary 5.1

Assume `sup_n A_{D_n}(P_n,Q_n)<=C<infinity`. Let `T_n` preserve `Q_n`, and let `f_n:Omega_n->[0,1]` satisfy

`q_n:=E_{Q_n} f_n ->0`.

If

`Sand_{D_n}(T_nf_n;Q_n)->0`,

then

`E_{P_nT_n} f_n ->0`.

Hence `f_n` cannot strongly distinguish `P_nT_n` from `Q_n`.

### Proof

Because `0<=f_n<=1`,

`Var_{Q_n}(f_n)<=E_{Q_n}f_n=q_n`.

Theorem 4.1 yields

`|E_{P_nT_n}f_n-E_{Q_n}f_n|`

`<= C sqrt(q_n)+(2+2C)Sand_{D_n}(T_nf_n;Q_n)->0`.

Since `E_{Q_n}f_n=q_n->0`, the planted acceptance probability also tends to zero. `square`

This conclusion is strictly different from the previous likelihood-tail route: the planted law may have arbitrarily large raw or smoothed chi-square, arbitrary fixed-order Renyi divergence, and arbitrary high-degree spectral mass. If the *particular candidate distinguisher class* admits the stated sandwiches after noise, bounded source LDA still rules it out.

## 6. Classical sandwiching as the exact `A=0` endpoint

Suppose `A_D(P,Q)=0`, i.e. `P` and `Q` agree on all degree-`D` polynomial expectations. If `p_-<=Tf<=p_+` pointwise with `p_-,p_+ in P_{<=D}`, then directly

`E_{PT}f-E_Qf`

`<= E_P p_+ - E_Q p_-`

`= E_Q(p_+-p_-)`.

The analogous lower inequality gives

`|E_{PT}f-E_Qf| <= E_Q(p_+-p_-)`.

This is precisely the classical bounded-independence / sandwiching-polynomial paradigm. Theorem 4.1 is the variance-sensitive extension needed when source LDA is only **bounded**, not exactly zero: an `L1` gap by itself is no longer enough, because each sandwich polynomial's expectation can move by `A` times its standard deviation.

This explains why the source's bounded-LDA quantifier naturally asks for a second-moment notion of sandwich quality.

## 7. Mao yields a quantitative *test-side* obstruction

Mao has `Q_n=G(n,1/2)` and exact source LDA zero through `D_n=Theta((log n)^(r-1))`. Let `T_n` be any fixed positive edge-resampling operator, and let `f_n in {0,1}` be the acceptance indicator of the deterministic rank test oriented so that

`E_{Q_n}f_n ->0`,

`E_{P_nT_n}f_n ->1`.

Applying Theorem 4.1 with `A=0` gives

`1-o(1)`

`<= 2 Sand_{D_n}(T_nf_n;Q_n)`.

Therefore

`Sand_{D_n}(T_nf_n;Q_n) >= 1/2-o(1)`.      (7.1)

This is a decisive source-valid obstruction: the noised Mao rank test **cannot** admit degree-`D_n` polynomial upper/lower sandwiches with vanishing `L2(Q_n)` half-width. Mao is excluded from the new sufficient framework for a mathematically substantive reason on the *test side*, without invoking any likelihood-ratio tail condition.

Equation (7.1) is complementary to the previous distribution-side Mao obstruction (persistent smoothed high-degree likelihood energy). Together they show two dual failure certificates for the same counterexample:

- distribution side: high-degree likelihood energy survives noise;
- test side: the successful noised rank test has a macroscopic low-degree sandwiching defect.

## 8. A concrete class-level corollary for polynomial distinguishers

Let `F_{n,L_n}` be any class of degree-`<=L_n` polynomial tests (or any other test class) normalized in `L2(Q_n)`. Define

`eta_n = sup_{f in F_{n,L_n}, Var_Q f=1} Sand_{D_n}(T_nf;Q_n)`.

If

`sup_n Adv_{Q_n}^{<=D_n}(P_n,Q_n)<=C`

and `sup_n eta_n<infinity`, then every noised test in `F_{n,L_n}` has uniformly bounded normalized distinguishing advantage. If `eta_n->0`, its post-noise advantage is at most `C+o(1)`.

Thus the following is a fully rigorous sufficient-condition template aimed directly at the Randomstrasse box:

> **bounded source LDA through `D_n` + variance-controlled degree-`D_n` sandwichability of the specified noised degree-`L_n` test class => bounded degree-`L_n` distinguishing advantage.**

This may permit `L_n` to be much larger than `D_n`; the burden is shifted to proving a sandwiching theorem for that structured test class. The result is not tautological: sandwiching is a null/noise/test-class approximation property that can be established without knowing `P_n`, exactly as in pseudorandomness.

## 11. What failed / what this theorem does not solve

- This does **not** solve the unrestricted source question. For the class of all bounded tests, Mao proves that the required sandwiching defect cannot vanish at polylogarithmic source degree.
- It does not yet give the best `L_n` for broad natural polynomial classes. That is now a concrete approximation-theory problem.
- For Gaussian unbounded domains, global polynomial sandwiches are difficult because nonconstant polynomials are unbounded. A useful implementation likely needs nonnegative polynomial tail guards plus accurate bulk approximants.
- It does not by itself recover Hsieh et al.'s strongest symmetric Boolean TV theorem; that theorem exploits symmetry-specific truncation and Krawtchouk structure.
- It removes the **planted high-degree-tail** assumption only when one is willing to specify a target test class. Full statistical TV against all tests still needs distribution-side regularity or symmetry strong enough to supply universal test approximation.

These are limitations of scope, not gaps in Theorem 4.1.
