# 3. B11 — DUALIZE: the canonical support-pure basis exists for every full-support binary null

The predecessor correctly abandoned raw centered monomial conditioning. The next question is whether an adapted source-degree decomposition even has to be assumed. For a finite binary full-support law, it does not: there is a canonical triangular construction.

Let

\[
Q(x)>0,\qquad x\in\{-1,+1\}^n.
\]

For `S subseteq [n]`, define

\[
\mathcal V_S:=L^2(\sigma(X_i:i\in S))
\]

and

\[
\mathcal W_S:=\sum_{T\subsetneq S}\mathcal V_T.
\]

Because the marginal on `X_S` has full support, the ordinary characters

\[
\chi_T(x)=\prod_{i\in T}x_i,\qquad T\subseteq S,
\]

form an algebraic basis of `V_S`. Consequently

\[
\dim\mathcal V_S=2^{|S|},\qquad
\mathcal W_S=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_T:T\subsetneq S\},\qquad
\dim\mathcal W_S=2^{|S|}-1.
\]

Define the **local support-pure space**

\[
\mathcal H_S:=\mathcal V_S\cap\mathcal W_S^{\perp_Q}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then `dim H_S=1`. Choose a unit vector `psi_S` spanning it. Since `psi_S` does not lie in `W_S`, its Walsh expansion inside `V_S` has a nonzero top coefficient:

\[
\psi_S=c_S\chi_S+\sum_{T\subsetneq S}c_{S,T}\chi_T,
\qquad c_S\neq0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Ordering subsets by cardinality, `(3.2)` is triangular with nonzero diagonal. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_{\le D}
=
\operatorname{span}\{1\}\oplus
\operatorname{span}\{\psi_S:1\le |S|\le D\}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

for every `D<=n`, where `P_{<=D}` is exactly the source polynomial space.

This is a useful correction to the wording of the predecessor. For finite binary full-support nulls, **existence of a support-pure source-degree basis is automatic**. The hard issue is quantitative stability of the resulting nonorthogonal frame, not existence of a direct-sum representation.

The construction is closely related to hierarchical/dependent Hoeffding decompositions and is not claimed as new functional-analysis machinery.

## 3.1 Exact adapted dual formula

Let

\[
h=\frac{dP}{dQ}-1,
\]

and index the nonconstant pure vectors with `1<=|S|<=D`. Define

\[
G_D^{\mathrm{ad}}(S,T)=\langle\psi_S,\psi_T\rangle_Q,
\qquad
b_S=\langle h,\psi_S\rangle_Q.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Triangular independence implies `G_D^ad` is positive definite. A polynomial

\[
f=a_0+\sum_{1\le|S|\le D}a_S\psi_S
\]

has

\[
\operatorname{Var}_Q(f)=a^\top G_D^{\mathrm{ad}}a,
\qquad
\mathbb E_Pf-\mathbb E_Qf=b^\top a.
\]

Hence exactly

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
=b^\top(G_D^{\mathrm{ad}})^{-1}b.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This equality is coordinate-independent even though `(3.5)` is written in adapted coordinates.

The key B11 point is that one need **not** control the worst eigenvalue of the frame if the likelihood functional avoids the bad directions.

### Weighted adapted-frame theorem

Let `R_D` be any positive-definite comparison matrix satisfying

\[
G_D^{\mathrm{ad}}\succeq R_D.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then matrix monotonicity gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
\le b^\top R_D^{-1}b.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

In particular, if positive weights `w_S` satisfy

\[
G_D^{\mathrm{ad}}\succeq\operatorname{diag}(w_S),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
\le
\sum_{1\le|S|\le D}\frac{|\langle h,\psi_S\rangle_Q|^2}{w_S}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Proof: `(3.5)` plus inversion reverses Loewner order. Equivalently, Cauchy–Schwarz in the weighted coefficient norm proves `(3.8)` directly.

This is elementary linear algebra, not a novelty claim. Its research value is structural: the old raw-Gram route asked for a uniform lower bound in **every** coefficient direction. The correct dual problem permits a local comparison `R_D` matched to the actual current/cluster decay of `b_S`. A bad frame direction is harmless if the planted/null likelihood functional has proportionally negligible mass in that direction.

The exact variational form

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
=
\sup_a\left\{2b^\top a-a^\top G_D^{\mathrm{ad}}a\right\}
\tag{3.9}
\]

is likely the right entry point for current/polymer estimates: control the signed functional against the same quadratic form rather than invert a huge coordinate matrix.

## 5.1 Exact degree-two Gram spectrum for a spin-flip/exchangeable law

Let `Q` be a full-support, exchangeable, globally spin-flip-invariant law on `{-1,+1}^n`, `n>=4`. Put, for distinct indices,

\[
r=\mathbb E_Q[X_1X_2],
\qquad
q=\mathbb E_Q[X_1X_2X_3X_4].
\]

By spin-flip symmetry, single-spin means and all odd moments vanish. Therefore the local support-pure vector for a pair is simply

\[
\psi_{ij}
=
\frac{X_iX_j-r}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Indeed it is orthogonal to `1,X_i,X_j`, which span the lower-support functions on `{i,j}`.

For the pair-indexed Gram matrix, write

\[
a=1-r^2,
\qquad b=r-r^2,
\qquad c=q-r^2.
\]

Before normalization, the entry is `a` on the diagonal, `b` when two pairs share one vertex, and `c` when they are disjoint. This is the Johnson association scheme `J(n,2)`. Its three exact eigenvalues are

\[
\lambda_0
=
a+2(n-2)b+{n-2\choose2}c,
\tag{5.2}
\]

with multiplicity `1`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_1
=1+(n-4)r-(n-3)q,
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

with multiplicity `n-1`, and

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_2
=1-2r+q,
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

with multiplicity `n(n-3)/2`. The normalized adapted Gram eigenvalues are these quantities divided by `a=1-r^2`.

No checker is needed: `(5.2)`–`(5.4)` follow from the three standard eigenvalues of the line graph of `K_n` and its disjointness complement.

## 5.2 High-temperature Curie-Weiss calibration

Consider

\[
Q_{n,\beta}(x)
\propto
\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{2n}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\Big)^2\right),
\qquad 0<\beta<1.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is a useful stress model because its Dobrushin row sum is

\[
(n-1)\tanh(\beta/n)<\beta<1.
\]

Let

\[
\alpha=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}.
\]

The classical high-temperature Curie-Weiss central limit theorem with moment convergence gives

\[
r=\frac{\alpha}{n}+O(n^{-2}),
\qquad
q=\frac{3\alpha^2}{n^2}+O(n^{-3}).
\tag{5.6}
\]

For completeness, the first identity follows from

\[
r=\frac{\mathbb E S_n^2-n}{n(n-1)},
\]

and the second from the exact expansion

\[
\mathbb E S_n^4
=n+3n(n-1)+n(n-1)(6n-8)r
+n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)q,
\]

together with `S_n/sqrt(n)` converging with fourth moments to `N(0,(1-beta)^(-1))`.

Substituting `(5.6)` into `(5.2)`–`(5.4)` yields

\[
\frac{\lambda_2}{a}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{\lambda_1}{a}\to1+\alpha=\frac1{1-\beta},
\qquad
\frac{\lambda_0}{a}\to(1+\alpha)^2=\frac1{(1-\beta)^2}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Thus the entire degree-two support-pure frame stays uniformly well-conditioned for every fixed `beta<1`.

There is an even stronger special-case comparison. Let `U_n` be the uniform product law. The normalizing factor in `(5.5)` obeys

\[
Z_{n,\beta}
=\mathbb E_{U_n}e^{\beta S_n^2/(2n)}
\le (1-\beta)^{-1/2}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

To see this, write the quadratic exponential as a Gaussian moment and use `cosh u <= e^{u^2/2}`. Therefore

\[
\frac{dQ_{n,\beta}}{dU_n}
\ge \sqrt{1-\beta}
\]

pointwise, and consequently

\[
\boxed{
\|f\|_{L^2(Q_{n,\beta})}^2
\ge
\sqrt{1-\beta}\,\|f\|_{L^2(U_n)}^2
\quad\text{for every }f.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

So this mean-field Dobrushin model has excellent signed geometry at every degree. It is a calibration example, not a broad Gibbs theorem.
