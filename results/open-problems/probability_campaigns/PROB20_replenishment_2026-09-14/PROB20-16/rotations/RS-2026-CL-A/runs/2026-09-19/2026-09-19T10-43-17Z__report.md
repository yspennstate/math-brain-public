# 3. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: a paired Lyapunov invariant for degree/noise alignment

The preceding B23 obstruction showed that good mixing in some unrelated spectral basis does not control source algebraic degree. The missing invariant is not ordinary entropy or a log-Sobolev constant. It is a **paired source-degree/noise Lyapunov structure**.

Let

\[
L^2(Q)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}\mathcal H_k,
\qquad
\Pi_k:L^2(Q)\to\mathcal H_k,
\]

be the orthogonal ordinary source-degree decomposition and define the source-number operator

\[
N=\sum_{k\ge0} k\Pi_k.
\]

Let `A=A^*>=0` be the generator of a `Q`-reversible contraction semigroup

\[
S_t=e^{-tA}.
\]

For `s>0`, define the symmetrically tilted form

\[
C_s(A,N)
:=
\frac12\left(e^{-sN}Ae^{sN}+e^{sN}Ae^{-sN}\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The two conditions are:

\[
\boxed{A\succeq \kappa N}
\tag{3.2}
\]

for some fixed `kappa>0`, and

\[
\boxed{C_s(A,N)\succeq0}
\tag{3.3}
\]

for some fixed `s>0`.

Condition (3.2) says source degree has a genuine energy cost. Condition (3.3) says the semigroup remains accretive after an exponential reweighting by source degree. Together they rule out the B23 basis-rotation pathology.

## Theorem 3.1 — tilted alignment implies full high-source-degree damping

If (3.2) and (3.3) hold, then for every integer `K>=1` and every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|S_t\Pi_{\ge K}\|_{2\to2}
\le
\exp\left\{-sK\left(1-e^{-\kappa t/s}\right)\right\}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Define the fixed-time alignment rate

\[
\alpha_t(s,\kappa)
:=
s\left(1-e^{-\kappa t/s}\right)>0.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then (3.4) is simply `||S_t Pi_{>=K}|| <= e^{-alpha_t K}`.

### Proof

Take `f in Ran(Pi_{>=K})` with `||f||_2=1` and set

\[
u_t=S_tf.
\]

First define the exponentially weighted norm

\[
v(t):=\|e^{-sN}u_t\|_2^2.
\]

Put `w_t=e^{-sN}u_t`. Since `u_t'=-Au_t`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
v'(t)
&=-2\,\Re\langle w_t,e^{-sN}Ae^{sN}w_t\rangle\\
&=-2\langle w_t,C_s(A,N)w_t\rangle\\
&\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus `v(t)` is the desired monotone quantity. Because the initial vector has source degree at least `K`,

\[
v(t)\le v(0)\le e^{-2sK}=:\varepsilon.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Now put

\[
y(t):=\|u_t\|_2^2.
\]

Ordinary coercivity gives

\[
y'(t)
=-2\langle u_t,Au_t\rangle
\le
-2\kappa\langle u_t,Nu_t\rangle.
\tag{3.8}
\]

When `y(t)>0`, define the source-degree probability distribution

\[
p_k(t):=\frac{\|\Pi_k u_t\|_2^2}{y(t)}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_k p_k e^{-2sk}
=
\frac{v(t)}{y(t)}
\le
\frac{\varepsilon}{y(t)}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Because `x -> e^{-2sx}` is convex, Jensen gives

\[
e^{-2s\sum_k kp_k}
\le
\sum_kp_ke^{-2sk}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Hence whenever `y(t)>=\varepsilon`,

\[
\sum_k kp_k
\ge
\frac1{2s}\log\frac{y(t)}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\langle u_t,Nu_t\rangle
\ge
\frac{y(t)}{2s}\log\frac{y(t)}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Substituting into (3.8),

\[
y'(t)
\le
-\frac\kappa s y(t)\log\frac{y(t)}\varepsilon
\qquad (y(t)\ge\varepsilon).
\tag{3.13}
\]

Compare with the scalar ODE

\[
Y'=-\frac\kappa sY\log(Y/\varepsilon),
\qquad Y(0)=1.
\]

Its exact solution is

\[
Y(t)
=
\varepsilon\exp\left\{\log(1/\varepsilon)e^{-\kappa t/s}\right\}
=
\exp\left\{-2sK(1-e^{-\kappa t/s})\right\}.
\tag{3.14}
\]

For finite `t`, this comparison trajectory remains above `epsilon`; if `y` ever drops below `epsilon`, the desired bound is already stronger. Therefore

\[
y(t)\le Y(t).
\]

Taking square roots proves (3.4). □

# 4. B20 — FIND WHY THE UPGRADE FAILS: ordinary coercivity alone is insufficient

It is tempting to replace the paired hypotheses by the simpler inequality `A>=kappa N`. That is false as a route to exponential high-degree damping.

For each `K>=1`, take the two-dimensional source-number operator

\[
N_K=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\0&K
\end{pmatrix}
\]

and the positive self-adjoint generator

\[
A_K=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-\sqrt K\\
-\sqrt K&2K
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
A_K-N_K
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-\sqrt K\\
-\sqrt K&K
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1\\-\sqrt K\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&-\sqrt K\end{pmatrix}
\succeq0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the strongest simple normalization `A_K>=N_K` holds exactly.

However the smaller eigenvalue is

\[
\lambda_-(K)
=
\frac{2K+1-\sqrt{4K^2+1}}2
\le\frac12.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `v_-` be a normalized eigenvector. From

\[
(1-\lambda_-)x-\sqrt K\,y=0
\]

we obtain

\[
|\langle v_-,e_2\rangle|^2
=
\frac{(1-\lambda_-)^2}{K+(1-\lambda_-)^2}
\ge
\frac1{4(K+1)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore the pure source-degree-`K` vector `e_2` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA_K}e_2\|_2
\ge
\frac{e^{-t/2}}{2\sqrt{K+1}}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The decay in source degree is only polynomial. Ordinary degree-energy coercivity by itself does not prevent a high-degree vector from having a polynomial-size projection onto a slow eigenmode.

The tilted form diagnoses the exact failure. Since `N_K` has levels `0,K`,

\[
C_s(A_K,N_K)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-\sqrt K\cosh(sK)\\
-\sqrt K\cosh(sK)&2K
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\det C_s
=K\left(2-\cosh^2(sK)\right),
\tag{4.7}
\]

which is negative for all sufficiently large `K` at every fixed `s>0`. Thus this family violates exactly the exponential tilted-accretivity hypothesis needed by Theorem 3.1.

## Theorem 6.1 — same-degree bounded-LDA transfer

Since `S_{n,t}` is an `L^2` contraction,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_nS_{n,t},Q_n)
&=\|\Pi_{\le D_n}S_{n,t}h_n\|_2\\
&\le
\|S_{n,t}\Pi_{\le D_n}h_n\|_2
+
\|S_{n,t}\Pi_{>D_n}h_n\|_2\\
&\le
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)
+
\exp\left\{\frac{\Pi_{2,n}}2-\alpha_t(D_n+1)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Therefore, if

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)<\infty
\]

and

\[
\frac{\Pi_{2,n}}2-\alpha_t(D_n+1)\to-\infty,
\tag{6.5}

then the noisy low-degree advantage remains bounded through the **same degree `D_n`**.

\[
\boxed{\Pi_{2,n}=o(D_n)}
\tag{6.6}
