# 3. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: arbitrary parity families on a connected Ising tree

The previous run handled one global parity on a one-dimensional Ising chain. The stronger statement below allows an arbitrary linearly independent family of parity constraints and any finite tree null.

## 3.1 Tree Ising null and exact character geometry

Let `T=(V,E)` be a finite tree, `|V|=n`, rooted at `o`. For clarity first take a homogeneous ferromagnetic zero-field Ising law

\[
Q_{T,\rho}(g)\propto
\prod_{\{u,v\}\in E}(1+\rho g_ug_v),
\qquad 0\le \rho<1,
\tag{3.1}
\]

which is equivalent to coupling `J=atanh(rho)`. Generate it by choosing `G_o` uniformly in `{+-1}` and, independently over oriented edges `e=(p(v),v)`, drawing bond variables

\[
Z_e=G_{p(v)}G_v,
\qquad
\mathbb E Z_e=\rho.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The `Z_e` are independent.

For a binary vertex vector `c in F_2^V`, write

\[
\chi_c(G)=\prod_{v:c_v=1}G_v.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For each oriented edge `e`, let `T_e` be the descendant component cut off by deleting `e`. Put

\[
J_T(c)=\{e\in E: |\operatorname{supp}(c)\cap T_e|\equiv1\pmod2\}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If `|c|` is even, `J_T(c)` is the unique `T`-join whose odd boundary is `supp(c)`, and

\[
\chi_c=\prod_{e\in J_T(c)} Z_e.
\tag{3.5}
\]

If `|c|` is odd there is one uncancelled root spin `G_o`, so its expectation is zero. Thus exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mu_T(c):=\mathbb E_Q\chi_c=
\begin{cases}
\rho^{\tau_T(c)},& |c|\text{ even},\\
0,& |c|\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\tau_T(c)=|J_T(c)|.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

On a path, if `supp(c)={i_1<...<i_{2m}}`, this reduces to

\[
\tau_T(c)=\sum_{a=1}^m(i_{2a}-i_{2a-1}),
\tag{3.7}
\]

the bridge length used implicitly in the preceding one-parity calculation.

## 3.2 Conditioning on many parity constraints

Let rows `r_1,...,r_M in F_2^V` be linearly independent and let

\[
C=\operatorname{span}_{F_2}\{r_1,...,r_M\}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Condition on all parity constraints

\[
\chi_{r_j}(G)=1,\qquad 1\le j\le M.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Let `P=Q(. | (3.9))`. Since

\[
\mathbf1_{(3.9)}=2^{-M}\sum_{c\in C}\chi_c,
\tag{3.10}
\]

and `rho>=0` makes every nonzero expectation in (3.6) nonnegative,

\[
Z_C:=\sum_{c\in C}\mu_T(c)=2^M Q((3.9))\ge1.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Therefore the exact likelihood ratio is

\[
\boxed{
L=\frac{dP}{dQ}=\frac1{Z_C}\sum_{c\in C}\chi_c.
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

This is the many-constraint replacement for the previous single-parity formula.

## 3.3 Tree Markov innovations form a degree-preserving orthonormal basis

For the root let `phi_o=G_o`. For each `v!=o`, define

\[
\phi_v=\frac{G_v-\rho G_{p(v)}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Conditional on the parent spin,

\[
\mathbb E[\phi_v\mid G_{p(v)}]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\phi_v^2\mid G_{p(v)}]=1.
\tag{3.14}
\]

For `S subset V`, put `Phi_S=prod_{v in S}phi_v`. Prune leaves recursively in `Phi_S Phi_{S'}`. At the first leaf occurring in exactly one of `S,S'`, (3.14) makes the expectation zero; a leaf occurring in both contributes conditional second moment one and can be removed. Induction gives

\[
\boxed{\langle\Phi_S,\Phi_{S'}\rangle_Q=\mathbf1_{S=S'}.}
\tag{3.15}
\]

Each `Phi_S` is an ordinary multilinear polynomial of degree at most `|S|`. There are exactly `sum_{k<=D} binom(n,k)` such basis vectors with `|S|<=D`, the same as the dimension of the ordinary degree-`<=D` polynomial space. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{span}\{\Phi_S:|S|\le D\}
=
\{\text{ordinary multilinear polynomials of degree }\le D\}.
}
\tag{3.16}
\]

So the innovation basis is not an alternate notion of degree: it is an orthonormal basis of the source's ordinary degree filtration.

Let

\[
C_\rho=\frac{1+\rho}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
\tag{3.17}
\]

Expanding a product of `k` innovations into ordinary characters gives

\[
\Phi_S=\sum_{|T'|\le k}a_{S,T'}\chi_{T'},
\qquad
\sum_{T'}|a_{S,T'}|\le C_\rho^k.
\tag{3.18}
\]

## 3.4 Robust tree-join distance and the collective code bound

For `c in F_2^V`, define the `k`-toggle robust tree-join length

\[
\delta_k^T(c)
=
\min_{|T'|\le k}
\begin{cases}
\tau_T(c\oplus T'),& |c\oplus T'|\text{ even},\\
+\infty,& |c\oplus T'|\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.19}
\]

and the collective erasure-spectrum sum

\[
\boxed{
B_k(C;\rho,T)
=
\sum_{c\in C\setminus\{0\}}
\rho^{\delta_k^T(c)},
}
\tag{3.20}
\]

with `rho^(+infinity)=0`.

For `|S|=k`, (3.6), (3.18), and the definition of `delta_k` imply

\[
|\langle\chi_c,\Phi_S\rangle_Q|
\le
C_\rho^k\rho^{\delta_k^T(c)}.
\tag{3.21}
\]

Since every nonconstant `Phi_S` has mean zero, (3.12) gives

\[
|\langle L-1,\Phi_S\rangle_Q|
\le
\frac{C_\rho^k}{Z_C}B_k(C;\rho,T)
\le C_\rho^k B_k(C;\rho,T).
\tag{3.22}
\]

Summing squares in the orthonormal basis yields the central theorem of this run.

### Theorem 3.1 — collective many-parity low-degree bound on an Ising tree

Under (3.1)–(3.12), for every `D<=n`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
\le
\sum_{k=1}^D
{n\choose k}C_\rho^{2k}
B_k(C;\rho,T)^2.
}
\tag{3.23}
\]

In particular, if the right side is `O(1)` along a sequence, source low-degree advantage is bounded; if it tends to zero, source low-degree advantage vanishes.

This is an explicit collective condition on the **whole row-span**, not a union of one-row conclusions.

## 3.5 A simpler robust-distance corollary

Suppose that for every nonzero `c in C_n`, toggling at most `D_n` vertices leaves tree-join length at least `L_n`, i.e.

\[
\delta_{D_n}^{T_n}(c)\ge L_n.
\tag{3.24}
\]

Then for every `k<=D_n`,

\[
B_k\le(2^{M_n}-1)\rho^{L_n}.
\tag{3.25}
\]

Using `sum_{k<=D}{n choose k}C_\rho^{2k} <= D (e n C_\rho^2/D)^D` for `D<=n/2`,

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)
\le
\sqrt{D_n}
\left(\frac{e n C_\rho^2}{D_n}\right)^{D_n/2}
2^{M_n}\rho^{L_n}.
\tag{3.26}
\]

Thus a clean sufficient asymptotic condition is

\[
M_n\log2+rac{D_n}{2}\log\frac{e n C_\rho^2}{D_n}
-L_n|\log\rho|\to-\infty.
\tag{3.27}
\]

For the preceding single full parity on a path, `M_n=1` and `L_n>=n/2-D_n/2`, recovering the earlier exponentially small low-degree bound. But (3.23)–(3.27) now permit **many** parity generators and arbitrary tree geometry.

## 3.6 Why this is useful and where it stops

The correct geometric quantity is not Hamming distance alone. A high-Hamming-weight parity can have a small tree-join if its marked vertices pair across short edges. The relevant condition is a **robust tree-join spectrum**: few codewords should remain cheap after an adversary toggles at most `D_n` vertices.

The theorem extends immediately to positive heterogeneous edge correlations `rho_e=tanh J_e` by replacing `rho^{tau_T(c)}` with `prod_{e in J_T(c)}rho_e` and replacing (3.19) by the minimum weighted energy `sum_{e in J_T(c xor T')} -log rho_e`. The homogeneous statement is retained above for readability.

For sufficiently weak bounded-degree tree interactions, the previously proved aligned heat-bath semigroup theorem supplies the noise-side high-degree damping. What remains nontrivial for a full noisy conclusion with many parity constraints is to certify the **interior discounted likelihood spectrum** without reverting to global chi-square pressure. B28 provides the exact criterion in the Haar/group-code case; extending it to the tree-Ising conditioning model is a next obligation.

# 4. B28 — REPLACE “FOR ALL” WITH “FOR TYPICAL”: spectral escape instead of hard minimum distance

Hard minimum distance is sufficient but unnecessarily rigid. In the exact product finite-group/subgroup setting from the preceding run, define

\[
A_{n,k}
=
\sum_{\operatorname{wt}(\pi)=k}
 d_\pi\dim V_\pi^{H_n}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For binary codes this is simply the number of likelihood-spectrum codewords of weight `k`.

The exact identities are

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)^2
=
\sum_{1\le k\le D_n}A_{n,k},
\tag{4.2}
\]

and under independent coordinate Haar refresh at time `t`,

\[
\chi^2(P_nS_t\|Q_n)
=
\sum_{k\ge1}A_{n,k}e^{-2tk}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

These identities yield a criterion strictly broader than `d_min>D_n`.

### Theorem 4.1 — bounded low-degree mass + escape of exceptional spectrum + discounted tail

Fix `t>0`. Suppose `D_n->infinity` and:

1. **bounded source mass**
   \[
   \sup_n\sum_{1\le k\le D_n}A_{n,k}<\infty;
   \tag{4.4}
   \]
2. **escape of the exceptional low spectrum:** for every fixed `K`,
   \[
   \sum_{1\le k\le K}A_{n,k}\to0;
   \tag{4.5}
   \]
3. **discounted high tail:**
   \[
   \sum_{k>D_n}A_{n,k}e^{-2tk}\to0.
   \tag{4.6}
   \]

Then the source low-degree advantage through `D_n` is uniformly bounded and

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_nS_t\|Q_n)\to0,
\qquad
\|P_nS_t-Q_n\|_{TV}\to0.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

### Proof

Let the bound in (4.4) be `C`. For any fixed `K<D_n`, split (4.3):

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}A_{n,k}e^{-2tk}
=
\sum_{k\le K}+
\sum_{K<k\le D_n}+
\sum_{k>D_n}.
\]

The first term tends to zero by (4.5). The middle term is at most

\[
e^{-2t(K+1)}\sum_{k\le D_n}A_{n,k}
\le C e^{-2t(K+1)}.
\]

The last tends to zero by (4.6). Take `limsup_n`, then `K->infinity`. This proves chi-square convergence; total variation follows from `TV<=sqrt(chi2)/2`. Equation (4.2) gives bounded source LDA. QED.

## 4.2 Strictly broader than hard distance

Hard distance `d_min>D_n` sets the entire sum in (4.4) to zero. Theorem 4.1 allows a bounded number of low-support irreducibles/codewords **inside** the source low-degree window as long as their support drifts to infinity.

For example, a binary likelihood code with exactly one nonzero spectrum vector of weight

\[
k_n=\lfloor\sqrt{D_n}\rfloor
\]

has source low-degree advantage exactly `1`, so hard distance fails maximally at the source cutoff, but after any fixed coordinate-refresh time

\[
\chi^2(P_nS_t\|Q_n)=e^{-2t k_n}\to0.
\]

Thus a bounded source-visible exceptional spectrum can be harmless under noise. The structural issue is whether the low spectrum **escapes to infinity**, not whether it is literally empty.

## 4.3 A spectral-envelope version

A convenient sufficient form of (4.6) is an exponential envelope. If for `k>D_n`

\[
A_{n,k}\le C_n e^{\sigma k}
\tag{4.8}
\]

with `2t>sigma` and

\[
\log C_n-(2t-\sigma)D_n\to-\infty,
\tag{4.9}
\]

then the tail in (4.6) is geometric and vanishes. This converts a coding-theory weight-spectrum estimate directly into the source's noisy implication.

This theorem is still a structured subclass: exact diagonal noise and exact representation-support Fourier decomposition are doing real work. It does not characterize arbitrary `(P_n,Q_n)`.

# 5. B17 — LOOK FOR YOUR NUMBERS SOMEWHERE ELSE: expander/Tanner/LDPC spectra as ready-made certificates

The quantities `A_{n,k}` and `W_C(z)=1+sum_k A_{n,k}z^k` are standard coding-theory objects. This lets classical coding results certify the inference conditions without pretending those coding bounds are new.

## 5.1 Expander codes: a crude but explicit transfer

Sipser–Spielman expander codes give explicit asymptotically good binary linear-code families: for suitable fixed parameters, code dimension and minimum distance are both linear in block length.

Use such a linear code `C_n` as the row-span of parity constraints in the product-Rademacher likelihood construction. Let

\[
M_n=\dim C_n,
\qquad d_n=d_{\min}(C_n).
\]

Then for every `0<z<1`, simply because every nonzero codeword has weight at least `d_n`,

\[
\boxed{
W_{C_n}(z)-1
\le (2^{M_n}-1)z^{d_n}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

For coordinate-refresh noise at time `t`, `z=e^{-2t}`, so

\[
\chi^2(P_nS_t\|Q_n)
\le
\exp\{M_n\log2-2t d_n\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
M_n\log2-2t d_n\to-\infty
\Longrightarrow
TV(P_nS_t,Q_n)\to0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `M_n~Rn` and `d_n~delta n`, the crude distance-only threshold is `2t delta>R log2`. If `M_n=o(d_n)`, every fixed `t>0` eventually satisfies (5.3). For `D_n=polylog(n)`, any linear-distance family has zero source LDA through `D_n`.

This is not a new expander-code theorem. It is the inference consequence of inserting classical distance data into the exact likelihood-spectrum identity.

## 5.2 LDPC/Tanner spectral shapes give a sharper radius than minimum distance alone

Suppose classical coding analysis supplies a uniform exponential spectrum bound

\[
A_{n,\lfloor \omega n\rfloor}
\le
\exp\{n(g(\omega)+o(1))\},
\qquad \omega\in[\delta,1].
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then Laplace's principle applied to the weight enumerator gives

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
\log\bigl(W_{C_n}(e^{-2t})-1\bigr)
\le
\sup_{\omega\in[\delta,1]}
\{g(\omega)-2t\omega\}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus a negative right-hand side certifies noisy chi-square and TV convergence. Unlike (5.2), this uses the **whole weight spectrum**, not only rate and minimum distance.

Rathi (regular LDPC ensembles), Yang et al. (regular LDPC codes including the small-weight regime), and Flanagan et al. (D-GLDPC/Tanner spectral shape) provide precisely this kind of coding-side spectral information for broad ensembles. Their formulas and concentration statements are classical coding results; the only new step here is the source-facing translation (5.5).

A caution is essential: linear-weight spectral-shape asymptotics alone do not automatically control the polylogarithmic `k=O(D_n)` window. Theorem 4.1 separates that issue cleanly. One needs either genuine linear minimum distance, a small-weight theorem such as those developed in the regular-LDPC literature, or a direct bound on the sublinear spectrum. This prevents a false claim that an asymptotic rate function by itself settles the source-scale low-degree window.

Wentworth-Nice's nonabelian MacWilliams/Greene framework supplies dual transforms for group-code enumerators closely related to the representation-support enumerator from B14. Therefore any future coding-theory upper bound on the low representation-support spectrum or on `W_H^rep(z)` at an interior radius plugs directly into Theorem 4.1.

\[

or its analytic/semigroup analogue, not on global `chi2` at radius one. Theorem 4.1 shows that even bounded exceptional source-visible spectrum is harmless if it escapes to higher support and the discounted tail is controlled.

# 7. Failure modes and what did not work

### B3 limitation

The tree theorem is exact because edge bonds are independent. On a general graph, loop constraints destroy that factorization and the simple `T`-join character expectation becomes a high-temperature even-subgraph sum. Extending (3.23) without an exponential monomial-count loss requires a genuine cluster/current estimate, not another application of the tree proof.

### B28 limitation

A linear-weight large-deviation spectrum does not control the source's polylogarithmic window. A code can have a good macroscopic spectral shape and still contain a sparse exceptional family of `o(n)`-weight codewords. The theorem therefore keeps the low-window escape condition separate.

### B17 limitation

Expander/LDPC theory gives powerful sufficient code families, but these are still subgroup/coset structured planted models under highly aligned coordinate noise. They are evidence that the structural conditions are nonempty and broad, not a source-wide answer.
