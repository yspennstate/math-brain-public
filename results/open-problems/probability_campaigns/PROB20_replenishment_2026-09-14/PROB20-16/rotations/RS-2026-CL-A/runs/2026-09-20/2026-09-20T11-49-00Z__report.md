## 3. Setup: latent-product planted laws

Let

`Q=U_N=Unif({-1,+1}^N)`

and let `T_rho`, `rho in (0,1)`, be standard independent product noise, so a conditional coordinate bias `b_i` is changed to `rho b_i`.

Assume `P` has a latent-product representation: there is a latent variable `Z` and a measurable vector

`b(Z)=(b_1(Z),...,b_N(Z)) in [-1,1]^N`

such that, conditional on `Z`, the coordinates `X_i` are independent and

`E[X_i | Z]=b_i(Z)`.                                           (3.1)

Define the latent energy

`E_lat = E_Z ||b(Z)||_2^2`,                                    (3.2)

and the off-diagonal latent second-moment tensor

`M_ij = E_Z[b_i(Z)b_j(Z)]`,  `i<j`,                            (3.3)

with

`||M||_off = (sum_{i<j} M_ij^2)^(1/2)`.                        (3.4)

The structural condition is the **latent-energy observability bound**

`E_lat <= C_obs ||M||_off`                                     (3.5)

for some finite `C_obs`. If `||M||_off=0`, (3.5) is interpreted literally and forces `E_lat=0`.

This condition says that the diagonal latent energy is visible, up to a fixed coercivity constant, in the pair-correlation tensor that degree-2 polynomials can see. It is automatic with constant `C_obs` in the homogeneous one-factor/de Finetti subclass treated below.

## 4. Exact degree-2 information supplied by LDA

Let

`A = Adv_U^{<=D}(P,U)`,  with `D>=2`.                           (4.1)

Under `U`, the nonconstant Walsh characters are orthonormal. Writing

`mu_S = E_P chi_S`,

one has exactly

`A^2 = sum_{1<=|S|<=D} mu_S^2`.                                (4.2)

Indeed, for every centered degree-`D` polynomial

`f=sum_{1<=|S|<=D} a_S chi_S`,

its source mean shift is `sum a_S mu_S` and its null variance is `sum a_S^2`; the supremum over coefficients is the Euclidean norm of the moment vector.

For `i<j`, conditional independence in (3.1) gives

`mu_{ij}=E_P[X_i X_j]=E_Z[b_i b_j]=M_ij`.                       (4.3)

Therefore

`boxed{ ||M||_off <= A. }`                                     (4.4)

Combining (3.5) and (4.4),

`boxed{ E_lat <= C_obs A. }`                                   (4.5)

This is the only place where low-degree information enters the all-test theorem.

## 5. One-bit Hellinger inequality

For `u in [-1,1]`, let `R_u` be the Rademacher law with mean `u`. Its Hellinger affinity with the uniform bit is

`a(u)=Aff(R_u,U_1)
     =(sqrt(1+u)+sqrt(1-u))/2`.                                (5.1)

A direct calculation gives

`a(u)^2=(1+sqrt(1-u^2))/2`.                                    (5.2)

### Lemma 5.1

For all `u in [-1,1]`,

`boxed{ a(u) >= 2^{-u^2/2}. }`                                 (5.3)

### Proof

Put `t=u^2` and `s=sqrt(1-t)`. Inequality (5.3), after squaring and taking logs, is equivalent to

`h(s):=log((1+s)/2)+(1-s^2)log 2 >=0`,  `s in [0,1]`.          (5.4)

Now

`h(0)=h(1)=0`,

and

`h''(s)= -1/(1+s)^2 - 2 log 2 <0`.                             (5.5)

Thus `h` is concave on `[0,1]`, hence lies above the chord joining its zero endpoints. So `h(s)>=0`. QED.

## 6. New all-test transfer theorem

### Theorem 6.1 (latent-product all-test transfer)

Assume (3.1) and the observability bound (3.5). If `D>=2` and

`Adv_U^{<=D}(P,U) <= A`,                                       (6.1)

then for every fixed `rho in (0,1)`,

`boxed{
 Aff(T_rho P,U) >= 2^{-(rho^2/2) C_obs A}
}`                                                               (6.2)

and therefore

`boxed{
 TV(T_rho P,U)
 <= sqrt(1-2^{-rho^2 C_obs A}).
}`                                                               (6.3)

In particular, if along a sequence `A_n=O(1)` and `C_obs,n=O(1)`, then the total variation distance is bounded uniformly away from one. Hence **no tests whatsoever** — polynomial, exponential-time, or information-theoretic — can strongly distinguish `T_rho P_n` from `U_n`. If `C_obs,n A_n ->0`, then

`TV(T_rho P_n,U_n)->0`.                                        (6.4)

### Proof

Conditional on `Z=z`, product noise sends the product law with bias vector `b(z)` to the product law with bias vector `rho b(z)`. By tensorization of Hellinger affinity and Lemma 5.1,

`Aff(T_rho P(.|Z=z),U)
 = prod_i a(rho b_i(z))
 >= 2^{-(rho^2/2)||b(z)||_2^2}.`                               (6.5)

Hellinger affinity is concave in its first argument. Explicitly, if `p_z` is the conditional noisy density, then pointwise concavity of the square root gives

`sum_x sqrt(U(x) E_Z p_z(x))
 >= E_Z sum_x sqrt(U(x)p_z(x))`.                               (6.6)

Hence

`Aff(T_rho P,U)
 >= E_Z 2^{-(rho^2/2)||b(Z)||_2^2}.`                           (6.7)

The map `x -> 2^{-(rho^2/2)x}` is convex, so Jensen yields

`Aff(T_rho P,U)
 >= 2^{-(rho^2/2) E_lat}.`                                     (6.8)

Now use (4.5) to obtain (6.2). Finally the standard Hellinger/TV inequality

`TV(R,S) <= sqrt(1-Aff(R,S)^2)`                                (6.9)

gives (6.3). QED.

## 7. Homogeneous de Finetti corollary: degree two alone is enough

A particularly transparent subclass is

`P = integral R_theta^{tensor N} dnu(theta)`,                   (7.1)

where `theta in [-1,1]` is a single latent bias shared by all coordinates. This is the homogeneous one-factor/de Finetti product-mixture model.

Here

`b_i(theta)=theta`,

so, writing `m_2=E theta^2`,

`E_lat = N m_2`,

`||M||_off = sqrt(binomial(N,2)) m_2`.                         (7.2)

Therefore one may take

`C_obs = N/sqrt(binomial(N,2))
       = sqrt(2N/(N-1)).`                                      (7.3)

### Corollary 7.1

If `D>=2` and `Adv_U^{<=D}(P,U)<=A`, then

`boxed{
 TV(T_rho P,U)
 <= sqrt(1-2^{-rho^2 A sqrt(2N/(N-1))}).
}`                                                               (7.4)

Thus any uniformly bounded low-degree advantage already excludes strong noisy distinction for **all** tests in this class. No polylogarithmic degree is needed; degree two suffices.

If the pairwise moments match the uniform null exactly, then `m_2=0`, hence `theta=0` almost surely and `P=U` exactly. In this subclass, exact degree-2 matching does not merely imply computational hardness; it collapses the planted law to the null.

## 10. A general coercivity interpretation

Let

`Sigma_b = E[b(Z)b(Z)^T]`.                                     (10.1)

Then `E_lat=tr(Sigma_b)`, while degree-2 Boolean polynomials observe only the off-diagonal entries of `Sigma_b` because `X_i^2=1` makes the diagonal quadratic coordinates trivial. Condition (3.5) is precisely

`boxed{
 tr(Sigma_b)
 <= C_obs ||(Sigma_b)_off||_{F,unordered}.
}`                                                               (10.2)

Thus the theorem is a tensor coercivity statement: low-degree success follows when the latent diagonal energy cannot hide in a kernel of the observable quadratic tensor map.

This viewpoint is useful beyond the one-factor model and directly connects to the current frame/correlation program. The obstruction is no longer an inverse Gram issue; it is the existence of a latent-energy direction annihilated by the low-degree observation map.

## 11. Gaussian-process / tensor handoff lemma

The same Hellinger mechanism has an exact Gaussian analogue useful for P08/P09.

Let `Q=gamma_m=N(0,I_m)`. Suppose conditional on a latent variable `Z`,

`X | Z ~ N(b(Z),I_m)`.                                        (11.1)

Under Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise of correlation `rho`,

`T_rho X | Z ~ N(rho b(Z),I_m)`.                               (11.2)

For a fixed mean shift `v`,

`Aff(N(v,I_m),N(0,I_m)) = exp(-||v||_2^2/8)`.                 (11.3)

Exactly the same mixture-concavity and Jensen argument gives

`boxed{
 Aff(T_rho P,gamma_m)
 >= exp[-(rho^2/8) E||b(Z)||_2^2],
}`                                                               (11.4)

and hence

`boxed{
 TV(T_rho P,gamma_m)
 <= sqrt(1-exp[-(rho^2/4) E||b(Z)||_2^2]).
}`                                                               (11.5)

Therefore any Gaussian-process/tensor model in which a low-degree covariance or contraction estimate controls the latent mean energy immediately obtains an all-test noisy indistinguishability bound. The lemma itself is source-independent and is shared only as a reusable tool; RS-2026-CL-A ownership remains unchanged.
