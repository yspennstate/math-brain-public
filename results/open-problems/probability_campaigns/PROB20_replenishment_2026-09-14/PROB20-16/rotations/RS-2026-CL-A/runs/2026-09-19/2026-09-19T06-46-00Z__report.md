# 3. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: posterior-random-interface replica theorem

The preceding run required a deterministic interface `I_n`. This run removes that restriction. The natural random interface law is not the raw latent prior; it is the **one-replica posterior induced by the partition function weights**.

## 3.1 Exact posterior pair identity

Let latent states be `z`. Let `a_z>=0` be fixed coefficients. Suppose positive one-replica conditional partition functions `Z_1(z)>0` and nonnegative two-replica conditional partition functions `Z_2(z,z')` satisfy

\[
Z_1=\sum_z a_z Z_1(z),
\qquad
Z_2=\sum_{z,z'}a_z a_{z'} Z_2(z,z').
\tag{3.1}
\]

Define the one-replica posterior

\[
\nu(z):=\frac{a_z Z_1(z)}{Z_1}
\tag{3.2}
\]

and the conditional replica ratio

\[
R(z,z'):=\frac{Z_2(z,z')}{Z_1(z)Z_1(z')}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then, exactly,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Z_2}{Z_1^2}
=
\mathbb E_{(z,z')\sim\nu\otimes\nu}R(z,z').
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Proof: substitute (3.2) into the right side:

\[
\sum_{z,z'}\frac{a_zZ_1(z)}{Z_1}\frac{a_{z'}Z_1(z')}{Z_1}
\frac{Z_2(z,z')}{Z_1(z)Z_1(z')}
=
\frac{Z_2}{Z_1^2}.
\]

When `Pi_2=log Z_2-2log Z_1` is the residual order-2 Rényi pressure from the preceding reports,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_2
=
\log\mathbb E_{\nu\otimes\nu}R(z,z').
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the right averaging law for random interfaces.

## 3.2 Random-interface theorem

Assume every latent pair `(z,z')` has an interface `I(z,z')` and that a conditional replica-linear cluster expansion gives

\[
\log R(z,z')
\le
c\,|I(z,z')|+M(z,z')
\tag{3.6}
\]

for some fixed `c>0`. Then by (3.5),

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_2
\le
\log\mathbb E_{\nu\otimes\nu}
\exp\{c|I|+M\}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

In particular, if the approximate bulk replica mismatch is uniformly bounded by `M(z,z')<=N_n rho_n`, then

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_2
\le
N_n\rho_n
+
\log\mathbb E_{\nu\otimes\nu}e^{c|I|}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus deterministic `|I|=o(D_n)` can be replaced by a posterior exponential-moment condition.

### Tail form permitting rare large interfaces

Suppose there are `a_n>=0`, `C_0<infty` and `gamma>c` such that under `nu_n otimes nu_n`, for every integer `m>=0`,

\[
\mathbb P\{|I_n|\ge a_n+m\}
\le C_0e^{-\gamma m}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Let `Y=(|I_n|-a_n)_+`. For integer-valued `Y`, summation by parts gives

\[
\mathbb E e^{cY}
=
1+(e^c-1)\sum_{m\ge0}e^{cm}\mathbb P\{Y\ge m+1\}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Using (3.9),

\[
\mathbb E e^{cY}
\le
1+
\frac{C_0(e^c-1)}{e^\gamma-e^c}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathbb E e^{c|I_n|}
\le
c a_n+
\log\left(1+\frac{C_0(e^c-1)}{e^\gamma-e^c}\right).
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

Therefore

\[
a_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
N_n\rho_n=o(D_n)
\tag{3.13}
\]

imply

\[
\boxed{\Pi_{2,n}=o(D_n)}
\tag{3.14}
\]

even though interfaces of arbitrarily large size may occur with nonzero probability.

This is strictly stronger than the preceding deterministic-interface theorem.

## 3.3 Feed into the sharp degree-loss theorem

# 4. B30 — ONE SCALE AT A TIME: an explicit factor-table polymer certificate

The preceding theorem still assumed an abstract convergent cluster expansion. This section derives one directly from bounded local factor tables under an explicit small-activity condition.

## 4.1 Product-reference factorization and exact polymer gas

Let the elementary variables `(X_v)_{v in V}` be independent under

\[
\mu_0=\bigotimes_{v\in V}\mu_v.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let `A` be bulk factors and `B_I` interface/defect factors. Every factor depends on at most `r` variables. Write bulk normalized factors

\[
1+u_a(X_{S_a}),
\qquad a\in A,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and replica-dependent marked factors

\[
1+v_b^{(q)}(X_{S_b}),
\qquad b\in B_I,
\quad q\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For `q=0`, put `v_b^(0)=0`. Define

\[
\widetilde Z_q
:=
\mathbb E_{\mu_0}
\left[
\prod_{a\in A}(1+u_a)
\prod_{b\in B_I}(1+v_b^{(q)})
\right],
\tag{4.4}
\]

and residual ratios `Z_q=widetilde Z_q/widetilde Z_0`. Then

\[
\Pi_2
=
\log\widetilde Z_2
-2\log\widetilde Z_1
+\log\widetilde Z_0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Construct the factor-intersection graph: two factors are adjacent when their variable supports intersect. Let its maximum degree be `Delta_F`.

Expand every product in (4.4). Any selected factor set decomposes uniquely into connected components in the factor-intersection graph. Different connected components have disjoint variable supports, so the product reference (4.1) makes their expectations factorize. Therefore (4.4) is exactly a hard-core polymer gas whose polymers are nonempty connected factor subsets `gamma`, with activity

\[
\boxed{
z_q(\gamma)
=
\mathbb E_{\mu_0}
\prod_{c\in\gamma}h_c^{(q)},
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

where `h_a^(q)=u_a` for bulk factors and `h_b^(q)=v_b^(q)` for marked factors. Compatibility means no factor in one polymer overlaps a factor in the other.

This is an exact construction from local factor tables; no cluster expansion is assumed yet.

## 4.2 A crude explicit KP condition from table sup norms

Put

\[
\eta_0:=\sup_{a\in A}\|u_a\|_\infty,
\qquad
\eta_q:=\sup_{b\in B_I}\|v_b^{(q)}\|_\infty,
\qquad
\eta:=\max(\eta_0,\eta_1,\eta_2).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then for every polymer,

\[
|z_q(\gamma)|\le\eta^{|\gamma|}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

A graph of maximum degree `Delta_F` has at most `(e Delta_F)^(m-1)` connected vertex sets of size `m` containing a fixed factor. Hence, with exponential polymer weight `e^{|gamma|}`,

\[
R
:=
\sup_c\sum_{\gamma\ni c}|z_q(\gamma)|e^{|\gamma|}
\le
\frac{\eta e}{1-e^2\Delta_F\eta}
\tag{4.9}
\]

whenever `e^2 Delta_F eta<1`.

A polymer `gamma` can be incompatible only with polymers rooted at one of at most `(Delta_F+1)|gamma|` factors in the closed factor-neighborhood of `gamma`. Therefore the standard Kotecký–Preiss criterion with size function `a(gamma)=|gamma|` follows from

\[
(\Delta_F+1)R\le1.
\tag{4.10}
\]

A simple explicit sufficient table-smallness condition is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\eta
\le
\frac{1}{e(\Delta_F+1)+e^2\Delta_F}.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

The constant is intentionally crude; the point is that it is finite, local and directly computable from bounded-arity factor tables.

Under (4.11), the logarithms `log widetilde Z_q` have absolutely convergent polymer-cluster expansions.

## 4.3 Exact bulk cancellation and an explicit interface constant

Every cluster in `log widetilde Z_q` containing only pure bulk polymers is independent of `q`. Its coefficient in (4.5) is therefore

\[
1-2+1=0.
\tag{4.12}
\]

So every surviving term in `Pi_2` contains at least one marked interface factor. This is factor-table-level replica linearity: it is automatic from the local representation, not a separate global hypothesis.

For a marked root `b`, every connected polymer containing `b` has one factor bounded by `eta_q` and all remaining factors bounded by `eta`. Therefore

\[
R_q^{(I)}
:=
\sup_{b\in B_I}
\sum_{\gamma\ni b}|z_q(\gamma)|e^{|\gamma|}
\le
\boxed{
\frac{\eta_q e}{1-e^2\Delta_F\eta}.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

The standard rooted KP cluster bound says the absolute sum of connected log-cluster weights containing a chosen polymer is bounded by its activity times the exponential size weight. Summing over marked roots (overcounting is harmless) gives

\[
|\log\widetilde Z_q-\log\widetilde Z_0|
\le
|B_I|R_q^{(I)}.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Since `Pi_2>=0`, (4.5) yields the explicit factor-table pressure theorem

\[
\boxed{
0\le\Pi_2
\le
|B_I|\,
\frac{e(\eta_2+2\eta_1)}{1-e^2\Delta_F\eta}.
}
\tag{4.15}
\]

Thus a bounded-arity local model satisfying the finite table condition (4.11) has source-scale pressure whenever

\[
|B_{I,n}|=o(D_n).
\tag{4.16}
\]

For bounded factor degree, `|B_I|` and physical interface size differ only by a fixed multiplicative constant.

## 4.4 First two interface scales are explicit finite table calculations

The first marked polymer is the single local defect factor `b`:

\[
z_q(\{b\})=\mathbb E_{\mu_0}v_b^{(q)}.
\tag{4.17}
\]

Its replica-connected contribution is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_1(b)
=
\mathbb E v_b^{(2)}
-2\mathbb E v_b^{(1)}.
}
\tag{4.18}
\]

At the next scale, let `a` be one bulk factor overlapping `b`. Introducing formal amplitudes and differentiating `log E[(1+t u_a)(1+s v_b^(q))]` at `(0,0)` gives the exact mixed connected coefficient

\[
\kappa_q(a,b)
=
\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_0}(u_a,v_b^{(q)}).
\tag{4.19}
\]

Therefore the second-scale replica mismatch is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_2(a,b)
=
\operatorname{Cov}(u_a,v_b^{(2)})
-2\operatorname{Cov}(u_a,v_b^{(1)}).
}
\tag{4.20}
\]

Similarly, two adjacent marked factors contribute their local connected covariance. Because every support has bounded size, (4.18)–(4.20) are computable by enumerating at most `2^{O(r)}` assignments in the binary case.

This is the requested one-scale-at-a-time certification: local replica decoupling can be tested at the first nonzero scales directly from the factor tables, while (4.11)–(4.15) provide the all-order KP tail.

## 4.5 Weak-Ising specialization

For a weak Ising base relative to the uniform product reference,

\[
\frac{e^{\alpha J_e x_ix_j}}{\cosh(\alpha J_e)}
=
1+\tanh(\alpha J_e)x_ix_j.
\tag{4.21}
\]

Hence the bulk table norm is exactly

\[
\eta_0=\max_e|\tanh(\alpha J_e)|.
\tag{4.22}
\]

Any bounded-arity latent/defect factor that, after the local neutralizing latent sum, produces tables `1+v_b^(q)` with computable `eta_1,eta_2` can be inserted into (4.11) and (4.15). Thus the pressure half can now be certified from finite local tables plus the factor-intersection degree, without assuming an abstract polymer theorem.

The displayed constants are conservative and are **not** claimed optimal.
