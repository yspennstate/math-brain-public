## 4. B11 — dualize: every positive factorization produces a compound-Poisson majorant

Let

\[
F(z)=\prod_{\ell=1}^m G_\ell(z),
\qquad
G_\ell(z)=1+g_\ell(z)
=1+\sum_{r=1}^M c_{\ell,r}z^r,
\qquad c_{\ell,r}\ge0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Any positive factorization can be put in this constant-one form because `F(0)=1` and the positive factor constants multiply to one.

Define its **primitive activity profile**

\[
C_r:=\sum_{\ell=1}^m c_{\ell,r},
\qquad
C(z):=\sum_{r=1}^M C_r z^r.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The terminology here is descriptive, not a novelty claim.

### Lemma 4.1 — coefficientwise compound-Poisson majorant

For every such factorization,

\[
\boxed{
F(z)\preceq \exp(C(z))
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

coefficientwise, and

\[
\boxed{C_r\le b_r\quad\text{for every }r.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

Because `g_\ell` has nonnegative coefficients,

\[
1+g_\ell(z)
\preceq
\exp(g_\ell(z))
=
1+g_\ell(z)+\frac{g_\ell(z)^2}{2!}+\cdots.
\]

Multiplication preserves coefficientwise order for nonnegative-coefficient power series, hence

\[
F(z)=\prod_\ell(1+g_\ell(z))
\preceq
\prod_\ell e^{g_\ell(z)}
=e^{\sum_\ell g_\ell(z)}
=e^{C(z)}.
\]

For (4.4), inspect the coefficient of `z^r` in `F`. Selecting the `z^r` coefficient from exactly one factor and constant terms from every other factor contributes exactly `C_r`; all other contributions are nonnegative. Thus `b_r\ge C_r`. `□`

After normalization at `z=1`, `exp(C(z)-C(1))` is a compound-Poisson probability generating function with jump-size `r` intensity `C_r`. Thus a positive factorization gives a **compound-Poisson majorant certificate** for the spectral-energy distribution. Compound Poisson itself is classical; the useful point is that the certificate separates low primitive activity from noise-paid high primitive activity.

## 5. Robust approximate-width theorem: high primitives may exist

For a cutoff `D`, define the high primitive activity of the witnessing factorization

\[
H_D:=\sum_{r>D} C_r.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Unlike exact positive-factorization width, `H_D` allows arbitrary positive atoms/factors above degree `D`; it records only their total primitive activity.

### Theorem 5.1 — robust positive-primitive tail bound

Under the setup above, for every `0<=q<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P\|Q)
\le
\exp\left[
q\,\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
+
q^{D+1}H_D
\right].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

Using `1+x<=e^x` factor by factor,

\[
\log F(q)
\le
\sum_{r=1}^M C_r q^r.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the low part, because `q^r<=q` for `r>=1`, (4.4) gives

\[
\sum_{r\le D} C_rq^r
\le
q\sum_{r\le D}C_r
\le
q\sum_{r\le D}b_r
=
q\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For the high part, `q^r<=q^{D+1}` for `r>D`, hence

\[
\sum_{r>D}C_rq^r
\le
q^{D+1}H_D.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Combining (3.5), (5.3), (5.4), and (5.5) proves (5.2). `□`

### Corollary 5.2 — source-scale robust implication

Let

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\delta_n},
\qquad
\inf_n\delta_n>0,
\tag{5.6}
\]

or any equivalent source-scale `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`. Consider any sequence of finite graded pairs `(P_n,Q_n)` with scalar degree noise. Suppose

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\le A<\infty,
\tag{5.7}
\]

and suppose there are positive factorizations of `F_n` whose high primitive activities obey

\[
\boxed{
\log^+ H_{n,D_n}=o(D_n).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Then for every fixed `q in (0,1)`,

\[
q^{D_n+1}H_{n,D_n}\to0,
\tag{5.9}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P_n\|Q_n)
\le e^{qA^2}-1<\infty.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Hence no measurable test — and a fortiori no polynomial test of any degree — strongly distinguishes the noised planted sequence from the null sequence.

The proof of (5.9) is immediate after taking logarithms:

\[
\log(q^{D_n+1}H_{n,D_n})
=(D_n+1)\log q+o(D_n)\to-\infty.
\]

If, in addition, the low-degree advantage tends to zero, then the right-hand exponent in (5.2) tends to zero and total variation tends to zero.

For an explicit uniform TV gap, if the exponent in (5.2) is at most `M`, then `E_Q L_q^2<=e^M`. Paley–Zygmund at threshold `1/3` gives

\[
Q(L_q\ge1/3)\ge \frac{4}{9e^M},
\]

hence

\[
1-\|T_{\sqrt q}P-Q\|_{TV}
=E_Q\min(L_q,1)
\ge\frac{4}{27e^M}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

This robust theorem is strictly weaker than exact width `<=D`: high-degree positive primitives are permitted, even in large number, provided their total activity grows subexponentially on the `D` scale.

### A simple polynomial-activity sufficient condition

If a factorization has at most `n^c` nonconstant factors and every factor satisfies

\[
G_\ell(1)-1\le n^c,
\]

then

\[
H_{D_n}
\le
\sum_\ell(G_\ell(1)-1)
\le n^{2c}.
\tag{5.12}
\]

Since `D_n` is superlogarithmic, (5.8) follows. Thus **polynomially many polynomial-activity positive components are automatically harmless after any fixed degree noise once source-scale LDA is bounded.**

The exact positive-width theorem is recovered by `H_D=0`.

## 6. B30 — one scale at a time: banded primitive activity

A single number `H_D` still discards where the high primitive activity sits. Noise discounts degree `100D` vastly more than degree `D+1`, so the natural refinement is bandwise.

For `k>=1`, define

\[
H_k^{(D)}
:=
\sum_{kD<r\le(k+1)D}C_r.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{r>D}C_rq^r
\le
\sum_{k\ge1}q^{kD+1}H_k^{(D)}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Hence the exact same proof gives:

### Theorem 6.1 — degree-banded robust bound

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P\|Q)
\le
\exp\left[
q\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
+
\sum_{k\ge1}q^{kD+1}H_k^{(D)}
\right].
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This permits even exponentially growing primitive activity if the growth rate per degree is below the noise rate. For example, if for constants `A_0<infinity` and `alpha>=0`,

\[
H_k^{(D)}\le A_0 e^{\alpha kD},
\tag{6.4}
\]

and

\[
q e^\alpha<1,
\tag{6.5}
\]

then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\ge1}q^{kD+1}H_k^{(D)}
&\le
A_0q\sum_{k\ge1}(qe^\alpha)^{kD}\\
&=
A_0q\frac{(qe^\alpha)^D}{1-(qe^\alpha)^D},
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.6}
\]

which tends to zero whenever `D->infinity`.

This is the strongest theorem from this route so far: the relevant boundary is not literal factor degree, but **primitive exponential growth rate versus noise decay rate**.

## 7. A natural breadth instantiation outside Hsieh and Chen–Li: heterogeneous product blocks

The robust theorem has a clean nonexchangeable model class that is neither Hsieh's permutation-invariant setting nor Chen–Li's planted-subgraph/treewidth setting.

Let

\[
Q_n=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m_n}Q_{n,i},
\qquad
P_n=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m_n}P_{n,i},
\tag{7.1}
\]

where the blocks may all have different distributions and different dimensions. Give the global space the additive product grading. Let the block likelihood-ratio energy polynomials be

\[
F_{n,i}(z)
=1+\sum_{r\ge1}b_{n,i,r}z^r.
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Lemma 7.1 — exact tensor-product spectral factorization

\[
\boxed{
F_n(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{m_n}F_{n,i}(z).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

#### Proof

Write the orthogonal block expansion

\[
L_{n,i}=1+\sum_{r\ge1}h_{n,i,r},
\qquad
\|h_{n,i,r}\|_2^2=b_{n,i,r}.
\]

The global likelihood ratio is `L_n=prod_i L_{n,i}`. Distinct tuples of block degrees are orthogonal under the product null, and the squared norm of a tensor product is the product of the squared norms. Summing all tuples with total degree `r` gives exactly the coefficient of `z^r` in `prod_i F_{n,i}(z)`. `□`

Use the block polynomials as the positive factors. Their total primitive activity is

\[
\sum_i(F_{n,i}(1)-1)
=
\sum_i\chi^2(P_{n,i}\|Q_{n,i}).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore, if for some constant `c`,

\[
m_n\le n^c,
\qquad
\max_i\chi^2(P_{n,i}\|Q_{n,i})\le n^c,
\tag{7.5}
\]

then `H_{n,D}\le n^{2c}` for every `D`, and bounded source-scale low-degree advantage implies the fixed-noise full-testing TV gap via Corollary 5.2.

This class can be highly nonexchangeable: each block may have its own null, planted law, dimension, and local degree spectrum. The tensor-product identity itself is elementary and no novelty is claimed for it. Its role is to demonstrate that the robust certificate applies to a broad model family not requiring permutation symmetry, a planted graph, or a tree decomposition.

## 8. B2 — attack the negation: parity blocks show the threshold is essentially sharp

Before treating (5.8) as the right robust condition, I tried to violate it with an exact realizable likelihood ratio.

Fix an integer

\[
N=D+1.
\]

On a Boolean block of `N` unbiased signs under the uniform null, let

\[
\chi(x)=\prod_{i=1}^N x_i,
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{blk}}(x)=1+\varepsilon\chi(x),
\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is a valid density because it is nonnegative and has null mean one. Its block energy polynomial is

\[
G(z)=1+a z^N,
\qquad a:=\varepsilon^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Take `m` independent copies. Then

\[
F(z)=(1+a z^N)^m,
\tag{8.3}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}=0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

For the displayed atomization, the high primitive activity is exactly

\[
H_D=ma.
\tag{8.5}
\]

After scalar degree noise,

\[
1+\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P\|Q)
=(1+a q^N)^m.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Thus if

\[
ma q^N\to\tau\in[0,\infty),
\]

then

\[
\chi^2\to e^\tau-1.
\tag{8.7}
\]

If instead

\[
ma q^N\to\infty,
\tag{8.8}
\]

then the noised laws are in fact strongly distinguishable.

### Lemma 8.1 — divergence of `ma q^N` forces TV -> 1

Under noise the block density is

\[
1+\theta\chi,
\qquad
\theta=\varepsilon q^{N/2}.
\]

Its Hellinger affinity with the uniform block null is

\[
A(\theta)
=\frac{\sqrt{1+\theta}+\sqrt{1-\theta}}{2}
=1-\frac{\theta^2}{8}+O(\theta^4).
\tag{8.9}
\]

For `m` independent blocks, affinity tensorizes:

\[
A_m=A(\theta)^m.
\tag{8.10}
\]

When `N->infinity`, `\theta->0`. Under (8.8),

\[
m\theta^2=ma q^N\to\infty,
\]

so

\[
\log A_m
=-\frac18ma q^N(1+o(1))\to-\infty.
\]

Hence `A_m->0`. Since

\[
1-\|P-Q\|_{TV}
=\int\min(dP,dQ)
\le\int\sqrt{dP\,dQ}=A_m,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{\|T_{\sqrt q}P-Q\|_{TV}\to1.}
\tag{8.11}
\]

Thus the noise-scaled quantity

\[
q^{D+1}H_D
\tag{8.12}
\]

is not merely a proof artifact. In an exact likelihood-ratio family, boundedness versus divergence of that quantity marks the correct transition up to constants. Any theorem that allowed `q^{D+1}H_D->infinity` without additional structure would be false.

This also sharpens the earlier failed-parity observation: at Kireeva's superlogarithmic `D`, obtaining (8.8) with a polynomial number of blocks is impossible. One needs roughly `q^{-D}` blocks, which is superpolynomial. Mao achieves strong distinction with polynomial ambient size through a much more efficient code/rank reassembly.

## 9. A stronger structural necessity for Mao than `w_+(F)>D`

The previous report proved only that Mao's exact zero-LDA window forces positive-factorization width greater than `D_n`. The robust theorem gives a quantitatively stronger statement.

Fix the scalar `q in (0,1)` corresponding to a fixed positive edge-resampling rate in Mao's construction. Mao has

\[
b_{n,1}=\cdots=b_{n,D_n}=0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Take **any** positive factorization of the corresponding spectral-energy polynomial and let `H_{n,D_n}` be its high primitive activity. Theorem 5.1 reduces to

\[
F_n(q)
\le
\exp(q^{D_n+1}H_{n,D_n}).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Mao's noised planted law is strongly distinguishable from the null, so its TV distance tends to one. A uniformly bounded chi-square divergence would give a fixed positive overlap by (5.11), contradicting TV tending to one. Therefore

\[
\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P_n\|Q_n)=F_n(q)-1\to\infty.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Combining (9.2) and (9.3) forces

\[
\boxed{
q^{D_n+1}H_{n,D_n}\to\infty
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

for **every** positive factorization of `F_n`.

Equivalently,

\[
H_{n,D_n}\gg q^{-D_n}.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Because `D_n=Theta((log n)^{r-1})` with `r>=3`, `q^{-D_n}` is superpolynomial in `n`. Thus Mao's counterexample is not merely hiding one primitive above the low-degree cutoff: **every positive-factor representation of its spectral energy must carry superpolynomial high-degree primitive activity at the fixed-noise scale.**

This is a substantially more informative structural exclusion than “the algorithm is a rank test.” It identifies the exact resource a counterexample must possess in the positive-factorization language.
