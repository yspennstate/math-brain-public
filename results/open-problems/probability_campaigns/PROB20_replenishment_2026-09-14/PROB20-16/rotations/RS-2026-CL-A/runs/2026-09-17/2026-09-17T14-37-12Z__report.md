## 4. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER I: exact two-copy invariant kernel identity

Keep the previous abstract finite graded setup:

\[
L^2(Q)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{M}\mathcal H_j,
\]

with a finite symmetry group `G`, invariant subspaces `H_j^G`, and a `Q`-self-adjoint Markov operator `T` commuting with the group and preserving grades.

Choose an orthonormal basis

\[
\Psi_j(x)
=(\psi_{j,1}(x),\ldots,\psi_{j,d_j}(x))^\top
\]

of `H_j^G`. Let `T_j` be the matrix of `T` restricted to `H_j^G` in this basis. Define the two-copy noise kernel

\[
\boxed{
K_j^T(x,y)
:=
\Psi_j(x)^\top T_j^2\Psi_j(y).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

If `P<<Q` is `G`-invariant and `L=dP/dQ`, write `L_j=Pi_j(L-1)`. Then the degree-`j` coefficient vector is

\[
c_j=\mathbb E_P\Psi_j(X).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|TL_j\|_2^2
&=c_j^\top T_j^2c_j\\
&=\left(\mathbb E_P\Psi_j(X)\right)^\top
T_j^2
\left(\mathbb E_P\Psi_j(Y)\right)\\
&=\mathbb E_{X,Y\stackrel{iid}{\sim}P}
K_j^T(X,Y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Summing orthogonal grades gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(TP\|Q)
=
\sum_{j\ge1}
\mathbb E_{P\otimes P}K_j^T(X,Y).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

When independent product noise acts as the scalar `rho^j` on exact degree `j`, this becomes

\[
K_j^T(x,y)=\rho^{2j}K_j^G(x,y),
\qquad
K_j^G(x,y)=\sum_a\psi_{j,a}(x)\psi_{j,a}(y).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The previous leverage function is exactly the diagonal

\[
\kappa_j^G(x)=K_j^G(x,x).
\]

Thus the previous report's Jensen step was precisely the replacement of an exact two-copy kernel average by a one-copy diagonal upper bound. Equation (4.4) restores the cancellations lost there.

This is standard Hilbert-space/reproducing-kernel algebra in spirit, so the result is presented as a useful exact reformulation, not a novelty claim.

## 5. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER II: a general no-spectral-cliff theorem

Now specialize to a product-null grading in which independent resampling noise has exact eigenvalue `rho^j` on degree `j`, `0<rho<1`. No symmetry is needed for this theorem.

Let

\[
b_j:=\|\Pi_j(L-1)\|_2^2\ge0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then exactly

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\bigr)^2
=
\sum_{j=1}^{D}b_j
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q)
=
\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^{2j}b_j.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — bounded consecutive growth excludes a hidden noisy high-degree cliff

Assume

\[
\sum_{j\le D}b_j\le A^2
\tag{5.4}
\]

and for every `j>=D`,

\[
\boxed{b_{j+1}\le Rb_j}
\tag{5.5}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{\rho^2R\le1-\eta}
\qquad\text{for some }\eta>0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q)
\le
A^2
+
A^2\rho^{2D}
\frac{\rho^2R}{1-\rho^2R}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

In particular, if `D=D_n->infinity`, `A=O(1)`, and `eta` is uniformly positive, the high-degree contribution is `o(1)` and the noisy laws retain a uniform total-variation gap from perfect distinguishability.

#### Proof

From (5.4), `b_D<=A^2`. Iterating (5.5),

\[
b_{D+m}\le A^2R^m,
\qquad m\ge1.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j>D}\rho^{2j}b_j
&\le
A^2\rho^{2D}
\sum_{m\ge1}(\rho^2R)^m\\
&=
A^2\rho^{2D}
\frac{\rho^2R}{1-\rho^2R}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.9}
\]

The low-degree part is at most `A^2` because `rho^{2j}<=1`. This proves (5.7).

For any density `M=d\mu/dQ`,

\[
1-\|\mu-Q\|_{TV}
=\mathbb E_Q\min(1,M)
\ge
\mathbb E_Q\frac{M}{1+M}.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz in Engel form gives

\[
\mathbb E_Q\frac{M}{1+M}
\ge
\frac{(\mathbb E_QM)^2}{\mathbb E_Q[M(1+M)]}
=
\frac{1}{2+\chi^2(\mu\|Q)}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Thus a uniform bound in (5.7) precludes strong distinguishing. If the low-degree advantage tends to zero as well, then (5.7) gives `chi^2->0` and hence total variation tends to zero.

### Variable-ratio version

The uniform `R` is only a clean sufficient condition. If

\[
b_{D+m}
\le
b_D\prod_{\ell=D}^{D+m-1}R_\ell,
\]

then it is enough that

\[
\sum_{m\ge1}
\rho^{2m}
\prod_{\ell=D}^{D+m-1}R_\ell
\]

be uniformly bounded. This form is useful when local extension complexity changes with the degree.

### Why this stress-tests Mao and Buhai correctly

Mao has exact zero low-degree energy through a long cutoff but remains strongly distinguishable after fixed noise. Therefore some later high-degree energy must be nonzero. A finite consecutive-ratio chain starting from `b_D=0` would force every later `b_j` to remain zero. Thus Mao must have a **spectral cliff**: the finite-growth property (5.5) fails at the first nonzero high degree.

The same diagnosis applies qualitatively to the list-decoding counterexamples of Buhai et al.: their computational signal is not a gradual local continuation of the low-degree spectrum; it reappears through a global algebraic structure after a long low-degree-pseudorandom regime.

This gives a mathematically substantive exclusion condition rather than a rule saying “ban rank tests” or “ban codes.”

## 6. B10 — DERANDOMIZE: exact overlap theorem for forced-support planted models

The ratio condition becomes substantially more concrete in an important planted family.

Let

\[
Q_p=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)^{\otimes N},
\qquad 0<p<1,
\]

and define normalized centered coordinates

\[
\phi_i(x)=\frac{x_i-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}.
\]

For `S subseteq [N]`, put `phi_S=prod_{i in S}phi_i`. This is the standard orthonormal Fourier basis.

Let `A subseteq [N]` be a latent random support. Conditional on `A`, define the planted law by forcing every coordinate in `A` to equal `1`, while coordinates outside `A` remain independent `Bernoulli(p)`. Let `P` be the mixture over `A`.

Set

\[
\lambda:=\frac{1-p}{p}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a fixed support `A`, the likelihood ratio against `Q_p` is

\[
L_A(x)
=
\prod_{i\in A}\frac{x_i}{p}
=
\prod_{i\in A}
\left(1+\sqrt\lambda\,\phi_i(x)\right).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore, after averaging over the latent support,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat L(S)
=
\lambda^{|S|/2}\Pr(S\subseteq A).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Let `A'` be an independent copy of `A` and define the two-copy overlap

\[
Z:=|A\cap A'|.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Then for every `j>=1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_j
&=
\sum_{|S|=j}\widehat L(S)^2\\
&=
\lambda^j
\sum_{|S|=j}
\Pr(S\subseteq A)^2\\
&=
\lambda^j
\mathbb E
\sum_{|S|=j}\mathbf 1_{\{S\subseteq A\cap A'\}}\\
&=
\boxed{
\lambda^j\mathbb E\binom Zj.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q_p)\bigr)^2
=
\mathbb E
\sum_{j=1}^{D}
\lambda^j\binom Zj.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

If independent resampling noise retains each centered coordinate with factor `rho`, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
&=
\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^{2j}\lambda^j
\mathbb E\binom Zj\\
&=
\boxed{
\mathbb E(1+\rho^2\lambda)^Z-1.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is an exact identity, not an upper bound.

### A directly checkable overlap-tail condition

Since all terms are nonnegative,

\[
\sum_{j>D}
(\rho^2\lambda)^j
\mathbb E\binom Zj
\le
\mathbb E\!\left[
(1+\rho^2\lambda)^Z\mathbf 1_{\{Z>D\}}
\right].
\tag{6.8}
\]

Therefore bounded low-degree advantage plus

\[
\boxed{
\sup_n
\mathbb E\!\left[
(1+\rho^2\lambda_n)^{Z_n}
\mathbf 1_{\{Z_n>D_n\}}
\right]
<\infty
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

is an explicit overlap condition implying a uniform noisy total-variation gap. Unlike a condition directly on `chi^2(TP||Q)`, (6.9) is expressed in terms of the latent planted-support overlap law.

### Local extension form of the spectral ratio

The identity

\[
\binom z{j+1}
=
\binom zj\frac{z-j}{j+1}
\tag{6.10}
\]

gives, whenever `b_j>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_j}
=
\frac{\lambda}{j+1}
\frac{\mathbb E[\binom Zj(Z-j)]}
{\mathbb E\binom Zj}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Define the `j`-overlap size-biased law by weighting the original overlap law proportionally to `binom(Z,j)`. If `E_j` denotes expectation under that law, then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_j}
=
\frac{\lambda}{j+1}
\mathbb E_j[Z-j].
}
\tag{6.12}
\]

Thus the no-spectral-cliff hypothesis has a clean local meaning:

> after conditioning two independent planted supports to share a typical `j`-subset, the expected number of *additional* common planted coordinates cannot grow faster than linearly with `j`, at a rate below the inverse noise threshold.

In particular,

\[
\mathbb E_j[Z-j]
\le
\frac{j+1}{\lambda}R
\quad\text{for all }j\ge D,
\qquad
\rho^2R<1,
\tag{6.13}
\]

implies Theorem 5.1.

This is the most concrete new structural condition obtained in this run. It is model-intrinsic, two-copy, and excludes code/rank counterexamples because their recoverable signal must emerge through a forbidden spectral cliff rather than gradual local overlap extension.

## 7. B10 — deterministic orbit certificate

The overlap law itself can be derandomized exactly in symmetric orbit-planted models.

Suppose a finite permutation group `G` acts on `[N]`, a deterministic support `A_0` is fixed, and the planted support is a uniformly random orbit translate

\[
A=gA_0,
\qquad g\sim\operatorname{Unif}(G).
\]

For two independent translates, the relative element `h=g^{-1}g'` is uniform on `G`, and

\[
Z=|A_0\cap hA_0|.
\]

Define the deterministic overlap enumerator

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_{A_0}(u)
:=
\frac1{|G|}
\sum_{h\in G}
u^{|A_0\cap hA_0|}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Here the displayed symbol should be read with `u` in place of the typographical `nu`; explicitly,

\[
\mathcal O_{A_0}(u)
=
\frac1{|G|}
\sum_{h\in G}u^{|A_0\cap hA_0|}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then

\[
\frac{\mathcal O_{A_0}^{(j)}(1)}{j!}
=
\mathbb E\binom Zj,
\tag{7.3}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\bigl(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q_p)\bigr)^2
=
\sum_{j=1}^{D}
\frac{\lambda^j}{j!}
\mathcal O_{A_0}^{(j)}(1),
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
=
\mathcal O_{A_0}(1+\rho^2\lambda)-1.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus the high-degree problem is exactly the control of the Taylor tail of a **deterministic finite overlap polynomial** away from `u=1`. No Monte Carlo or planted-law likelihood-ratio enumeration is required once the orbit intersections are known.

This is a genuine derandomized certificate format, although the overlap-polynomial method itself belongs to established second-moment/Franz–Parisi technology.

## 8. Graph specialization and exact bridge to Chen–Li

Take coordinates to be the edges of the complete graph `K_n`. Let `A_0=E(\Gamma)` for a fixed planted graph `Gamma`, and let `G=S_n` act by vertex relabeling. Then

\[
Z(\pi)
=|E(\Gamma)\cap E(\pi\Gamma)|
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_\Gamma(u)
=
\frac1{n!}
\sum_{\pi\in S_n}u^{Z(\pi)}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

For an abstract graph `F` with `j` edges and no isolated vertices, let `N_\Gamma(F)` denote the number of edge-subgraphs of `Gamma` isomorphic to `F`. The number of labeled copies of `F` in `K_n` is

\[
|\mathcal O_F|
=
\frac{(n)_{v(F)}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(F)|}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For any fixed labeled copy `S` of shape `F`,

\[
\Pr(S\subseteq \pi E(\Gamma))
=
\frac{N_\Gamma(F)}{|\mathcal O_F|}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Grouping (6.5) by graph isomorphism type gives the exact aggregate degree energy

\[
\boxed{
 b_j
=
\lambda^j
\sum_{F:\,e(F)=j}
N_\Gamma(F)^2
\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(F)|}{(n)_{v(F)}}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
 b_j
=
\frac{\lambda^j}{j!}
\mathcal O_\Gamma^{(j)}(1).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

For the sparse null `p=c/n`, the coefficient

\[
\lambda^j
N_\Gamma(F)^2
\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(F)|}{(n)_{v(F)}}
\]

is exactly the natural weighted subgraph-count quantity appearing in Chen–Li's Fourier decomposition, before the extra noise attenuation. This makes the relationship precise:

- Chen–Li control the high-degree Fourier contribution **shape by shape**, using tree decompositions and small interfaces;
- the present ratio criterion controls the **aggregate consecutive-degree mass** `b_j`;
- the overlap polynomial packages all those masses as Taylor coefficients of one deterministic orbit enumerator.

This bridge is useful because it translates an abstract low-degree framework condition into the same combinatorial quantities already used by the current strongest positive graph theorem.
