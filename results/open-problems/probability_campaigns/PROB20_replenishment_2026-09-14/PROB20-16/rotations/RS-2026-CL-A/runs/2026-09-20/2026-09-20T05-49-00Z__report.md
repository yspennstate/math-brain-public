## 3. Boolean setup

Work first on

`Omega_N={-1,+1}^N`, `Q=U_N`.

For `S subseteq [N]`, let

`chi_S(x)=prod_{i in S} x_i`.

Every real `f` has Walsh expansion

`f=sum_S fhat(S) chi_S`,

with Parseval

`Var_U(f)=sum_{S nonempty} fhat(S)^2`.

Let `T_rho`, `0<rho<1`, independently retain each coordinate with probability `rho` and otherwise redraw it uniformly. Then

`T_rho chi_S = rho^{|S|} chi_S`.

For a planted law `P`, write

`beta_S=E_P chi_S`.

Because `chi_S` is `+/-1`,

`|beta_S|<=1`.                                                (3.1)

Moreover the degree-`D` source LDA is exactly

`Adv_U^{<=D}(P,U)^2`

`= sum_{1<=|S|<=D} beta_S^2`.                                (3.2)

Indeed the nonconstant Walsh characters of degree at most `D` are an orthonormal basis for the centered degree-`D` polynomial space.

## 4. Main theorem — robust noise-weighted Fourier-algebra transfer

For a test `f`, define its high-degree noised Fourier-algebra tail

`Tau_{D,rho}(f)`

`:= sum_{|S|>D} rho^{|S|} |fhat(S)|`.                        (4.1)

### Theorem 4.1 (Boolean robust spectral-tail transfer)

If

`Adv_U^{<=D}(P,U)<=A`,

then for every `f in L2(U)` whose displayed Fourier-algebra tail is finite,

`|E_{T_rho P} f-E_U f|`

`<= A [sum_{1<=|S|<=D} rho^{2|S|} fhat(S)^2]^(1/2)`

`   + Tau_{D,rho}(f)`                                      (4.2)

and hence

`boxed{|E_{T_rho P} f-E_U f| <= A sqrt(Var_U f)+Tau_{D,rho}(f).}`   (4.3)

If

`||f-E_Uf||_A := sum_{S nonempty}|fhat(S)|`

is finite, then the coarser but convenient estimate is

`boxed{|E_{T_rho P} f-E_U f|`

`<= A sqrt(Var_U f)+rho^(D+1)||f-E_Uf||_A.}`                (4.4)

#### Proof

Self-adjointness of product resampling with respect to `U`, or direct conditioning, gives

`E_{T_rho P}f-E_Uf`

`= E_P(T_rho f)-E_Uf`

`= sum_{S nonempty} rho^{|S|} fhat(S) beta_S`.              (4.5)

For `1<=|S|<=D`, Cauchy--Schwarz and (3.2) give

`|sum_{1<=|S|<=D} rho^{|S|} fhat(S) beta_S|`

`<= [sum_{1<=|S|<=D} beta_S^2]^(1/2)`

`   [sum_{1<=|S|<=D} rho^{2|S|}fhat(S)^2]^(1/2)`

`<= A [sum_{1<=|S|<=D} rho^{2|S|}fhat(S)^2]^(1/2)`.        (4.6)

For the remaining degrees, the pointwise character bound (3.1) gives

`|sum_{|S|>D} rho^{|S|}fhat(S)beta_S|`

`<= sum_{|S|>D}rho^{|S|}|fhat(S)|`

`=Tau_{D,rho}(f)`.                                         (4.7)

Equations (4.2)--(4.3) follow. Since `rho^{|S|}<=rho^{D+1}` on the tail, (4.4) follows as well. QED.

### What assumption was removed?

No permutation invariance of `P` is used. No global or smoothed `L2`, chi-square, Renyi, adapted-frame, covariance-pressure, or high-degree likelihood regularity of `P` is used. Above degree `D`, the proof uses only the universal character bound `|E_P chi_S|<=1` and a structural condition on the test.

This is the finite-product counterpart of the previous distribution-side theory, but it is not equivalent to it: a planted law may have arbitrarily bad high-degree likelihood energy and the theorem still applies to every test with small `Tau_{D,rho}`.

## 5. Arbitrary-degree sparse polynomials

Let

`M(f)=#{S nonempty : fhat(S) != 0}`.

Cauchy--Schwarz gives

`||f-E_Uf||_A <= sqrt(M(f)) sqrt(Var_U f)`.                 (5.1)

Therefore Theorem 4.1 immediately yields:

### Corollary 5.1 (arbitrary-degree sparse-polynomial closure)

For every nonconstant polynomial `f`, with **no upper bound on its maximum degree**,

`boxed{ |E_{T_rho P}f-E_Uf| / sqrt(Var_U f)`

`<= A + rho^(D+1) sqrt(M(f)). }`                           (5.2)

Consequently, if `D_n -> infinity`, `A_n=O(1)`, `rho in (0,1)` is fixed, and

`log M(f_n)=o(D_n)`,                                       (5.3)

then the noised null-normalized distinguishing advantage of `f_n` remains bounded by `A_n+o(1)`.

In the source scale

`D_n=(log n)^(1+c)`, `c>0`,

this includes every polynomial with `M(f_n)=n^{O(1)}` monomials, regardless of the degrees of those monomials. Such a polynomial is directly evaluable from its sparse representation in polynomial time when the ambient number of coordinates is polynomial in `n`.

Thus bounded degree-`D_n` LDA rigorously propagates, after any fixed positive resampling noise, to an explicit class of **arbitrary-degree polynomial-time sparse polynomials**. This is a genuine degree-extension class rather than a restatement that degree at most `D` is controlled.

### Bounded-test consequence

If additionally `0<=f<=1` and `q=E_Uf`, then

`sqrt(Var_U f)<=sqrt(q)`,

so an `M`-sparse bounded test satisfies

`E_{T_rho P}f`

`<= q + [A+rho^(D+1)sqrt(M)] sqrt(q)`.                      (5.4)

Hence if `q_n->0` and `log M_n=o(D_n)`, it cannot strongly distinguish (`E_{T_rho P}f_n` cannot tend to one).

## 6. Algorithmic corollary — decision trees

The Fourier-algebra condition has a simple algorithmic certificate. A leaf of a deterministic Boolean decision tree corresponds to an indicator of the form

`prod_{j in J}(1+sigma_j x_j)/2`,

whose Fourier `A`-norm is exactly one. If a tree has `L` leaves and leaf outputs in `[-1,1]`, its output is the sum of `L` such disjoint leaf indicators times coefficients of modulus at most one. Therefore

`||f||_A <= L`.                                            (6.1)

The same bound holds for the acceptance function of a randomized decision tree whose every realized tree has at most `L` leaves, by convexity of Fourier `A`-norm.

### Corollary 6.1

For any such test,

`|E_{T_rho P}f-E_Uf|`

`<= A sqrt(Var_U f)+rho^(D+1)L`.                           (6.2)

In particular, if `D_n=(log n)^(1+c)`, `A_n=O(1)`, and `L_n=n^{O(1)}`, then the explicit noise term is

`rho^(D_n+1)L_n = exp(-Omega((log n)^(1+c)))`,              (6.3)

up to a polynomial factor, and therefore tends to zero superpolynomially.

This does not claim to cover all polynomial-time algorithms. It covers a concrete representation class, and Mao below proves that his polynomial-time rank test necessarily lies far outside it at the relevant spectral scale.

## 7. Exact finite-uniform-alphabet extension

The source formulation allows a fixed finite alphabet `Omega`, not only bits. Let `|Omega|=q` and identify the alphabet with the cyclic group `Z_q` purely as a Fourier coordinate system. Let

`Q=Unif(Omega^N)`.

For `alpha in Z_q^N`, define the unit-modulus character

`chi_alpha(x)=exp(2 pi i <alpha,x>/q)`

and support degree

`deg(alpha)=#{j:alpha_j != 0}`.

These characters are orthonormal in complex `L2(Q)`, satisfy `|chi_alpha|=1`, and coordinate resampling with retention `rho` gives

`T_rho chi_alpha = rho^{deg(alpha)} chi_alpha`.            (7.1)

If the source low-degree space contains all characters of support degree at most `D` (equivalently the standard tensor/Efron--Stein degree-`D` space), then the identical proof gives

`|E_{T_rho P}f-E_Qf|`

`<= A sqrt(Var_Q f)`

` + sum_{deg(alpha)>D}rho^{deg(alpha)}|fhat(alpha)|.        (7.2)

For a fixed alphabet, tensor/support degree and ordinary polynomial degree under a fixed one-hot (or interpolating) coordinate encoding differ only by an alphabet-dependent constant factor. Thus (7.2) is a source-aligned finite-alphabet theorem, with that encoding factor stated rather than hidden.

This extension again requires no permutation invariance of `P`.

### Fourier-support lower bound

If `M_n` is the number of nonconstant Fourier coefficients of `f_n`, then Parseval and Cauchy give

`||f_n-E_Uf_n||_A`

`<= sqrt(M_n) sqrt(q_n(1-q_n))`,

where `q_n=E_U f_n`. Hence

`boxed{M_n >= (1-o(1)) rho^(-2(D_n+1)) / [q_n(1-q_n)].}`   (8.5)

In particular `M_n=exp(Omega(D_n))`; because `q_n->0`, (8.5) is actually stronger than the bare exponential statement.

From (6.1), every exact deterministic decision tree for the acceptance function must have

`boxed{L_n >= (1-o(1)) rho^(-(D_n+1)).}`                   (8.6)

The same lower bound applies to a randomized bounded-leaf representation through its acceptance function.

For Mao's

There is no contradiction with Mao's polynomial-time Gaussian-elimination/rank computation: polynomial-time arithmetic computation need not have a polynomial-size decision-tree leaf representation or small Fourier spectral norm. That separation is precisely the obstruction exposed here.

## 9. Robust obstruction — even uniform approximation must remain spectrally large

The previous lower bound is not an artifact of exact representation.

Let `g_n` be any real function with

`||g_n-f_n||_infinity <= eta`,                             (9.1)

for a fixed `0<=eta<1/2`. Then

`|(E_{T_rho P_n}-E_U)(g_n-f_n)| <= 2 eta`.                (9.2)

Since Mao has `A_n=0`, applying (4.4) to `g_n` gives

`1-o(1)-2eta`

`<= rho^(D_n+1)||g_n-E_U g_n||_A`.                        (9.3)

Thus

`boxed{||g_n-E_Ug_n||_A`

`>= (1-2eta-o(1))rho^(-(D_n+1)).}`                         (9.4)

So the noised rank acceptance function has **approximate Fourier spectral norm exponential in `D_n`** for every fixed uniform error below `1/2`.

If `g_n` has `M_n(eta)` nonconstant Fourier coefficients, then

`sqrt(Var_U g_n) <= sqrt(Var_U f_n)+2eta <= 1/2+2eta`,

and therefore

`M_n(eta) >= c_eta rho^(-2(D_n+1))`                        (9.5)

for a constant `c_eta>0` (when `eta<1/2` is fixed away from the endpoint; one may retain the explicit ratio from (9.4) for all fixed `eta<1/2`).

Likewise, any bounded decision-tree acceptance function that uniformly `eta`-approximates Mao's test requires exponentially many leaves.

This is stronger than merely saying “Mao has a high-degree likelihood tail.” It locates an unavoidable property of the **successful distinguisher itself**.

A safe reusable classical ingredient is the following scalar Ornstein--Uhlenbeck chaos-tail estimate. If `U_rho` is Gaussian OU, `Pi_{<=D}` the Hermite-degree projector, `q>=2`, and

`rho sqrt(q-1)<1`,

`||(I-Pi_{<=D})U_rho f||_q`

`<= [rho sqrt(q-1)]^(D+1) ||f-Ef||_2`.                    (12.1)

Proof: put `eta=1/sqrt(q-1)` and write `U_rho=U_eta U_{rho/eta}`. Hypercontractivity gives `||U_eta h||_q<=||h||_2`; on Hermite degrees above `D`, `U_{rho/eta}` contracts `L2` by at most `(rho/eta)^(D+1)`.
