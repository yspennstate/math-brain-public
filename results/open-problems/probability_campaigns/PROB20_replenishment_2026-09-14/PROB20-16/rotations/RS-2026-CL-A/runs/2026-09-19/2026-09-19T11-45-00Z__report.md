# 3. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE: optimize the admissible source-degree tilt

The previous run obtained a full-tail bound from a paired source-degree/noise alignment condition. This run asks the next natural question: once local tables certify a *family* of admissible tilts, which tilt gives the strongest fixed-time damping?

Let

\[
L^2(Q)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}\mathcal H_k,
\qquad
N=\sum_{k\ge0}k\Pi_k,
\]

and let `A=A^*>=0` generate the reversible Markov semigroup `S_t=e^{-tA}`. Assume

\[
A\succeq \kappa N
\tag{3.1}
\]

for fixed `kappa>0`. For `s>0` define

\[
C_s(A,N)=\frac12\left(e^{-sN}Ae^{sN}+e^{sN}Ae^{-sN}\right).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The preceding theorem required only `C_s>=0`. In the local Kato/Toda certificates one often obtains the stronger form

\[
C_s(A,N)\succeq \delta_s N,
\qquad \delta_s\ge0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The extra margin gives a finite-time improvement and also makes the optimization problem explicit.

## Theorem 3.1 — margin-refined tilted damping

Assume (3.1) and (3.3), and assume the degree-zero subspace consists of constants so the Markov semigroup preserves its orthogonal complement. Then for every integer `K>=1`, every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|S_t\Pi_{\ge K}\|_{2\to2}
\le
\exp\{-K\alpha_t(s)-\delta_s\beta_t(s)\},
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

where

\[
\alpha_t(s)
:=s\left(1-e^{-\kappa t/s}\right),
\tag{3.5}
\]

and

\[
\beta_t(s)
:=t-\frac{s}{\kappa}\left(1-e^{-\kappa t/s}\right)
=t-\frac{\alpha_t(s)}\kappa\ge0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus the earlier theorem is the `delta_s=0` case.

### Proof

Take `f in Ran(Pi_{>=K})`, `||f||_2=1`, and set `u_t=S_tf`. Since `K>=1`, `f` is mean zero; reversibility/Markov preservation keeps `u_t` mean zero.

Define

\[
v(t)=\|e^{-sN}u_t\|_2^2.
\]

Writing `w_t=e^{-sN}u_t`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
v'(t)
&=-2\,\Re\langle w_t,e^{-sN}Ae^{sN}w_t\rangle\\
&=-2\langle w_t,C_s(A,N)w_t\rangle\\
&\le -2\delta_s\langle w_t,Nw_t\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

On the mean-zero subspace `N>=I`, hence

\[
v'(t)\le-2\delta_s v(t).
\]

Since `f` has degree at least `K`,

\[
v(0)\le e^{-2sK},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{v(t)\le e^{-2sK-2\delta_s t}.}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Now set `y(t)=||u_t||_2^2`. From (3.1),

\[
y'(t)
=-2\langle u_t,Au_t\rangle
\le-2\kappa\langle u_t,Nu_t\rangle.
\tag{3.8}
\]

For `y(t)>0`, let

\[
p_k(t)=\frac{\|\Pi_k u_t\|_2^2}{y(t)}.
\]

Jensen applied to the convex function `x -> e^{-2sx}` gives

\[
e^{-2s\sum_k k p_k}
\le
\sum_k p_ke^{-2sk}
=
\frac{v(t)}{y(t)}.
\]

Whenever `y(t)>=e^{-2sK-2delta_s t}` this implies

\[
\sum_k k p_k
\ge
\frac1{2s}
\left[
\log y(t)+2sK+2\delta_s t
\right].
\tag{3.9}
\]

Let

\[
z(t)=\log y(t)+2sK+2\delta_s t.
\]

Combining (3.8) and (3.9), while `z>=0`,

\[
z'(t)
\le
-\frac\kappa s z(t)+2\delta_s.
\]

The comparison equation with `z(0)=2sK` has solution

\[
z_*(t)
=2sK e^{-\kappa t/s}
+\frac{2\delta_s s}{\kappa}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa t/s}\right).
\]

If `z` ever becomes negative, then `y` is already below the comparison envelope. Therefore globally

\[
\log y(t)
\le
-2sK\left(1-e^{-\kappa t/s}\right)
-2\delta_s\left[
t-\frac{s}{\kappa}(1-e^{-\kappa t/s})
\right].
\]

Taking square roots proves (3.4). □

## 3.2 Exact optimization of the leading degree rate

Put `x=kappa t/s`. Differentiating (3.5),

\[
\alpha_t'(s)
=1-(1+x)e^{-x}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

For `x>0`, `e^x>1+x`, so

\[
\boxed{\alpha_t'(s)>0.}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Thus the coefficient of the large source degree `K` is strictly improved by increasing the admissible tilt.

Let

\[
s_*:=\sup\{s>0:C_s(A,N)\succeq0\}
\]

or, in a local-table certificate, the supremum of the verified interval where its error majorant remains below the coercivity threshold. Then the best asymptotic degree coefficient available from this argument is

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_t^*
=
\sup_{0<s<s_*}\alpha_t(s)
=
\begin{cases}
 s_*(1-e^{-\kappa t/s_*}),&s_*<\infty,\\
 \kappa t,&s_*=\infty.
\end{cases}}
\tag{3.12}
\]

For finite `K`, the margin term can favor an interior tilt. The exact certificate objective is

\[
\Gamma_{t,K}(s)
=K\alpha_t(s)+\delta_s\beta_t(s).
\tag{3.13}
\]

If `delta_s` is differentiable then, since `beta_t'= -alpha_t'/kappa`,

\[
\Gamma_{t,K}'(s)
=\left(K-\frac{\delta_s}{\kappa}\right)\alpha_t'(s)
+\delta_s'\beta_t(s).
\tag{3.14}
\]

This is a one-dimensional optimization problem over the tilt already returned by the local-table/Kato recursion. No `2^n` global diagonalization is required.

## 3.3 Consequence for the existing weak-Ising certificate

The previous weak-Ising work provides a recursively computable local error envelope `E_s(|alpha|)` with a certificate of the form

\[
C_s(A_\alpha,N_\alpha)\succeq [1-E_s(|\alpha|)]N_\alpha
\]

on its verified interval, together with ordinary coercivity `A_\alpha>=delta_0 N_\alpha`. Therefore one may take

\[
\delta_s=1-E_s(|\alpha|),
\qquad
s_*(\alpha)=\sup\{s:E_s(|\alpha|)<1\},
\]

and optimize (3.13) directly from local factor tables. Constants remain deliberately nonsharp; the point is that the best fixed-time degree damping is now an explicit scalar optimization rather than an arbitrary chosen tilt.

# 4. B11 — DUALIZE: discounted degree pressure replaces global chi-square pressure

The previous report used the global collision pressure

\[
\Pi_2=\log(1+\chi^2(P\|Q))
\]

to bound the high-degree likelihood-ratio tail. This is convenient but stronger than the semigroup argument actually needs.

Let

\[
h=\frac{dP}{dQ}-1,
\qquad
h_k=\Pi_k h,
\qquad
a_k=\|h_k\|_2.
\]

The source advantage is exactly

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
=\left(\sum_{k=1}^D a_k^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 4.1 First failure: raw tail pressure is not a real weakening

A naive replacement would be to assume only a bound on

\[
\|\Pi_{>D}h\|_2^2=\sum_{k>D}a_k^2.
\]

But orthogonality gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P\|Q)
=
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
+\|\Pi_{>D}h\|_2^2.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence under the source premise that the degree-`D` advantage is bounded, asking for

\[
\log(1+\|\Pi_{>D}h\|_2^2)=o(D)
\]

is equivalent at exponential scale to the old global condition `Pi_2=o(D)`. That route does **not** broaden the theorem.

The correct move is to charge high source degrees less because the aligned noise damps them more.

## 4.2 Discounted degree pressure

For `r>=0`, define the discounted high-degree energy

\[
M_{r,D}(h)^2
:=
\sum_{k>D}e^{-2rk}a_k^2
\tag{4.3}
\]

and its logarithmic pressure

\[
\Theta_{r,D}(P\|Q)
:=
\log\bigl(1+M_{r,D}(h)^2\bigr).
\tag{4.4}
\]

At `r=0` this is essentially the old unweighted tail pressure. For every `r>0` it can be exponentially smaller when the likelihood ratio has huge mass only at very high source degrees.

Assume the aligned semigroup has the levelwise consequence

\[
\|S_t\Pi_k\|_{2\to2}
\le e^{-\alpha_t k},
\qquad k\ge1,
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `alpha_t>0`; Theorem 3.1 provides this with any certified tilt, and the optimized choice uses `alpha_t^*` from (3.12).

## Theorem 4.1 — discounted-pressure same-degree transfer

For every `0<=r<alpha_t`,

\[
\boxed{
\|S_t\Pi_{>D}h\|_2
\le
\frac{
 e^{-(\alpha_t-r)(D+1)}
}{
\sqrt{1-e^{-2(\alpha_t-r)}}
}
M_{r,D}(h).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(PS_t,Q)
\le
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
+
C_{t,r}
\exp\left\{
\frac12\Theta_{r,D}(P\|Q)
-(\alpha_t-r)(D+1)
\right\},
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

where

\[
C_{t,r}=(1-e^{-2(\alpha_t-r)})^{-1/2}.
\]

Also

\[
\boxed{
\|PS_t-Q\|_{\rm TV}
\le
\frac12\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
+
\frac{C_{t,r}}2
\exp\left\{
\frac12\Theta_{r,D}(P\|Q)
-(\alpha_t-r)(D+1)
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

### Proof

By (4.5) and the triangle inequality,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|S_t\Pi_{>D}h\|_2
&\le\sum_{k>D}\|S_th_k\|_2\\
&\le\sum_{k>D}e^{-\alpha_t k}a_k\\
&=\sum_{k>D}e^{-(\alpha_t-r)k}\bigl(e^{-rk}a_k\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\|S_t\Pi_{>D}h\|_2
\le
\left(\sum_{k>D}e^{-2(\alpha_t-r)k}\right)^{1/2}
M_{r,D}(h),
\]

and summing the geometric series proves (4.6).

For the low-degree source component, semigroup contraction gives

\[
\|S_t\Pi_{\le D}h\|_2
\le
\|\Pi_{\le D}h\|_2
=
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q).
\]

Projection at the output can only decrease norm, so (4.7) follows. Finally

\[
\|PS_t-Q\|_{\rm TV}
=\frac12\|S_th\|_1
\le\frac12\|S_th\|_2,
\]

and the same low/high split gives (4.8). □

## Corollary 4.2 — source-scale criterion

Let `D_n=(log n)^{1+eta}` and fix a noise time `t>0`. Suppose a uniform aligned certificate gives `alpha_t>0`. If for some fixed

\[
0<r<\alpha_t
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{\Theta_{r,D_n}(P_n\|Q_n)=o(D_n),}
\tag{4.9}
\]

then bounded source LDA through `D_n` implies bounded noisy LDA through the **same** `D_n`. If source LDA tends to zero, then the noisy laws converge in total variation.

For a theorem valid at every fixed positive noise time, a sufficient profile condition is that (4.9) hold for every fixed `r>0` (or for a sequence of `r` below the corresponding optimized alignment rate).

This is strictly weaker than `Pi_2=o(D_n)`: total unweighted chi-square may be enormous because of very-high-degree spectral mass while the discounted pressure remains source-scale.

## 4.3 Strictness example

Take the uniform Boolean null `Q_n` and a point mass `delta_x`. Let

\[
P_n=(1-\varepsilon_n)Q_n+\varepsilon_n\delta_x.
\]

In the Walsh basis every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of `d delta_x/dQ_n-1` has magnitude `1`, so

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)^2
=
\varepsilon_n^2\sum_{k=1}^{D_n}{n\choose k},
\tag{4.10}
\]

while

\[
\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)
=
\varepsilon_n^2(2^n-1).
\tag{4.11}
\]

The discounted energy is

\[
M_{r,D_n}^2
\le
\varepsilon_n^2(1+e^{-2r})^n.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Choose a fixed `c` with

\[
\frac12\log(1+e^{-2r})<c<\frac12\log2
\]

and set `epsilon_n=e^{-cn}`. Then for polylogarithmic `D_n`, source LDA vanishes, the **global** collision pressure is `Theta(n)` by (4.11), but the discounted energy in (4.12) decays exponentially. Thus the new discounted-pressure theorem can apply where the old global `Pi_2=o(D_n)` hypothesis provably fails.

This example is intentionally only a strictness witness, not a claim of a new hard statistical problem: `P_n` is already TV-close to `Q_n` because the contamination mass vanishes.

## 4.4 Mao is excluded substantively by discounted pressure

Mao's construction uses native independent edge resampling over a product Boolean null, so source degree and noise energy are perfectly aligned. It also has exactly zero LDA through `D_n=Theta((log n)^{r-1})`, yet retains nonvanishing distinguishing power after every fixed positive noise level.

Therefore Theorem 4.1 gives a forced spectral obstruction: for every `0<r<alpha_t`, Mao's likelihood ratios cannot satisfy (4.9). More quantitatively, if the noised TV distance stays at least `c_0>0`, then from (4.8),

\[
\Theta_{r,D_n}(P_n\|Q_n)
\ge
2(\alpha_t-r)(D_n+1)-O_{t,r,c_0}(1).
\tag{4.13}
\]

Thus the framework excludes Mao through a mathematically substantive fact: the counterexample must hide at least exponential-in-`D_n` *discounted* high-degree likelihood-ratio mass. No reference to the rank algorithm is needed in the hypothesis.

# 5. B22 — READ THE FAILURES, NOT THE SUCCESSES: what Hsieh and Chen–Li actually replace global pressure with

The literature comparison now points to a cleaner abstraction than the old scalar chi-square pressure.

## 5.1 Hsieh et al.: symmetry quotient plus tail regularization

Their Boolean theorem uses a feature unavailable in generic tensor/graph models: `S_n` symmetry reduces an invariant distribution to its one-dimensional Hamming-weight law. In the corresponding orthogonal Krawtchouk basis, native product noise attenuates degree, while conditioning/truncation to the typical Hamming-weight window makes the remaining full second moment manageable. They then show that the low-degree information is stable enough under this regularization and use the noise to eliminate the high-degree part.

The key lesson for CL-A is **not** “assume global chi-square is small.” It is:

1. quotient by a sufficient symmetry statistic;
2. regularize/truncate rare states until the high-degree spectral mass is controllable;
3. prove the low-degree coefficients survive that regularization;
4. use degree-aligned noise to damp the regularized high tail.

This is exactly the shape of a discounted-degree-pressure certificate. Their proof obtains the needed weighted tail from symmetry and truncation rather than assuming it as an input condition.

## 5.2 Chen–Li: positive Fourier weights plus low-interface reassembly

For planted subgraphs, Chen–Li do not have the one-dimensional quotient. Their load-bearing replacement is combinatorial. The squared Fourier norm expands into nonnegative weights indexed by subgraphs. Their isomorphism-triple correspondence makes those weights factor under restriction to pieces, up to a factor determined by the number of interface vertices. A treewidth decomposition cuts every large support into pieces that lie within the low-degree regime, and the native edge-noise factor decays exponentially in support size. Their condition

\[
\operatorname{tw}(\Gamma_n)=o(D_n/\log n)
\]

makes the reassembly cost subdominant to this noise decay.

Again, the proof controls a **noise-weighted high-degree sum** from low-degree pieces; it does not require an a priori small unweighted full chi-square divergence of the original planted law.

## 5.3 Common abstraction

The two papers use different model-specific certificates for the same analytic object:

\[
\boxed{
\text{noise-discounted high-source-degree likelihood-ratio energy}.
}
\]

Hsieh certifies it by symmetry quotient + truncation. Chen–Li certify it by nonnegative Fourier weights + low-interface factorization/reassembly. Theorem 4.1 shows that, once a nonproduct Gibbs noise has source/noise alignment, a direct discounted-degree-pressure estimate is enough to obtain the same-degree polynomial implication and, with vanishing LDLR, TV convergence.

This gives a more faithful source-directed framework than the previous global-pressure theorem:

- **alignment condition:** a checkable pair `(A,N)` supplies `alpha_t>0`;
- **discounted-tail condition:** for some `r<alpha_t`, `Theta_{r,D_n}=o(D_n)`;
- **source premise:** bounded (or vanishing) LDA through `D_n`.

Then bounded source LDA yields bounded noisy LDA through the same `D_n`; vanishing source LDA yields noisy TV convergence.

The framework remains only **sufficient**. It is not claimed necessary-and-sufficient, and the discounted pressure condition still needs broad model-level certificates rather than being a characterization by itself.
