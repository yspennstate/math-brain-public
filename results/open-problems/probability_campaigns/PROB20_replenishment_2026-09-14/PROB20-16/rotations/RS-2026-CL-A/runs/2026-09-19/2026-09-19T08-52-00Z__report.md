# 3. B11 — DUALIZE: soft collision as entropy versus energy, and a dependent-support theorem

## 3.1 Abstract soft-collision kernel

Let `mu` be a law on subsets of `[n]`. Write `S_i=1_{i in S}` and let `S,T` be independent draws from `mu`. Suppose a model-specific two-component overlap obeys

\[
K(S,T)\le \exp\{S^\top H T\},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `H=(H_ij)` is a nonnegative matrix. In the high-temperature Ising theorem from the preceding run one may take

\[
H=\beta^2(I-C)^{-1},
\tag{3.2}
\]

with `C_ij=tanh|K_ij|` and Dobrushin row sum below one.

Define the latent soft-collision pressure

\[
\Psi_\mu(H)
:=
\log \mathbb E_{S,T\sim\mu}
\exp\{S^\top H T\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then automatically

\[
\Pi_2(P\|Q)=\log(1+\chi^2(P\|Q))\le \Psi_\mu(H).
\tag{3.4}
\]

## 3.2 Exact Gibbs variational dual

Let `mu_2=mu otimes mu`. The elementary Gibbs variational formula gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
=
\sup_{\nu\ll\mu_2}
\left\{
\mathbb E_\nu[S^\top H T]
-
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu\|\mu_2)
\right\}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

**Proof.** Put `Y=S^THT` and `Z=E_{mu_2}e^Y`. Define the exponentially tilted law

\[
\frac{d\nu_*}{d\mu_2}=\frac{e^Y}{Z}.
\]

For arbitrary `nu<<mu_2`, nonnegativity of `D_KL(nu||nu_*)` gives

\[
0\le D_{KL}(\nu\|\nu_*)
=D_{KL}(\nu\|\mu_2)-\mathbb E_\nu Y+\log Z,
\]

hence the objective in (3.5) is at most `log Z`; equality holds at `nu_*`. □

This is the exact entropy-versus-soft-collision tradeoff. In particular, for every event `E` of positive `mu_2` probability, choosing `nu=mu_2(.|E)` yields

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\ge
\inf_{(S,T)\in E}S^\top H T
+
\log \mu_2(E).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

For any atom `(S,T)`,

\[
\Psi_\mu(H)
\ge
S^\top H T+\log\mu(S)+\log\mu(T).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Equation (3.6) is the soft-kernel version of the previous heavy-tail obstruction: a rare collision can be harmless only if its negative log-probability competes with its collision energy.

## 3.3 Negatively-associated latent supports

A useful sufficient structural condition is **negative association**. Assume the coordinate family `(S_i)` is negatively associated: for disjoint coordinate sets and bounded coordinatewise increasing nonnegative functions `f,g`,

\[
\mathbb E[f g]\le \mathbb E f\,\mathbb E g.
\tag{3.8}
\]

This includes, under standard hypotheses, fixed-size sampling without replacement and many strongly-Rayleigh/determinantal support laws; those facts are prior art and are not part of the present theorem.

Let

\[
p_i:=\mathbb P_\mu(i\in S).
\tag{3.9}
\]

For each column `j`, put

\[
W_j:=\sum_i H_{ij}
\tag{3.10}
\]

and define

\[
\phi(w):=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{e^w-1}{w},&w>0,\\
1,&w=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
b_i:=\sum_j p_jH_{ij}\phi(W_j).
\tag{3.11}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — negatively-associated soft-collision pressure

If `H>=0` and `mu` is negatively associated, then

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\le
\sum_i p_i\bigl(e^{b_i}-1\bigr).
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

**Proof.** Condition first on `S` and write

\[
a_j(S):=\sum_i H_{ij}S_i.
\]

Because `0<=a_j<=W_j`, all functions `T_j -> exp(a_j T_j)` are nonnegative increasing functions of disjoint single coordinates. Repeated negative association gives

\[
\mathbb E_Te^{S^THT}
\le
\prod_j\mathbb E e^{a_jT_j}
=
\prod_j(1-p_j+p_je^{a_j}).
\tag{3.13}
\]

Using `1+x<=e^x`,

\[
\mathbb E_Te^{S^THT}
\le
\exp\left\{\sum_jp_j(e^{a_j}-1)\right\}.
\tag{3.14}
\]

Convexity of `x -> e^x` on `[0,W_j]` gives the chord bound

\[
e^{a_j}-1\le \phi(W_j)a_j.
\tag{3.15}
\]

Substituting `a_j=sum_iH_ijS_i`,

\[
\sum_jp_j(e^{a_j}-1)
\le
\sum_i b_iS_i.
\tag{3.16}
\]

All `b_i` are nonnegative. Apply negative association to `S` once more:

\[
\mathbb E_Se^{\sum_ib_iS_i}
\le
\prod_i(1-p_i+p_ie^{b_i})
\le
\exp\left\{\sum_ip_i(e^{b_i}-1)\right\}.
\tag{3.17}
\]

Taking logs proves (3.12). □

This is stronger than a first-moment estimate: it controls the exponential collision moment by a deterministic transform of the one-site marginals and `H`.

## 3.4 A uniform scalar corollary

Assume both row and column sums of `H` are at most `W`, and let

\[
\rho:=\max_i p_i,
\qquad
A:=e^W-1.
\tag{3.18}
\]

Since `phi` is increasing,

\[
b_i
\le
\frac{A}{W}(Hp)_i
\le
A\rho.
\tag{3.19}
\]

Also `e^x-1<=xe^x` for `x>=0`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_\mu(H)
&\le e^{A\rho}\sum_ip_ib_i\\
&\le \frac{A}{W}e^{A\rho}\,p^THp.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.20}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\le
\frac{e^W-1}{W}
\,e^{\rho(e^W-1)}
\,p^THp.
}
\tag{3.21}
\]

For uniform marginals `p_i=p`,

\[
p^THp\le np^2W,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\le
np^2(e^W-1)
\exp\{p(e^W-1)\}.
}
\tag{3.22}
\]

For the Ising kernel `H=beta^2(I-C)^-1`, one may use

\[
W\le \frac{\beta^2}{1-q}.
\tag{3.23}
\]

This recovers the preceding iid Bernoulli scale but now for every negatively-associated latent support law with the same marginals.

Suppose the null sequence also has the previously established fixed tilted transport gate at some `s_0>0`. If a latent-mixture model has an overlap bound (3.1), a negatively-associated support law, and the right side of (3.12) or (3.21) is `o(D_n)`, then the scalar pressure is `o(D_n)`. With

# 4. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: exact product-null maximizer and minimum in the sparse regime

The heavy-tail construction from the previous run suggested that the two-point law `{empty,V}` might not merely be a counterexample but the exact **worst latent dependence structure** at fixed one-site marginals. At the product null this can be solved exactly.

Fix `c>0` and put

\[
a:=e^c-1>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For two support sets `S,T subseteq[n]`, consider the exact product-null collision kernel

\[
K_c(S,T)=e^{c|S\cap T|}
=\prod_{i=1}^n\left(1+a1_{i\in S}1_{i\in T}\right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For a support law `mu`, define inclusion probabilities

\[
q_A:=\mathbb P_\mu(A\subseteq S),
\qquad A\subseteq[n].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Expanding the product in (4.2) and using independence of `S,T` gives the exact positive-feature identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{S,T}K_c(S,T)
=
\sum_{A\subseteq[n]}a^{|A|}q_A^2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is also an explicit Gram-feature decomposition of the latent kernel.

## 4.1 Exact maximum at fixed uniform marginal

Assume

\[
\mathbb P(i\in S)=p
\quad\text{for every }i.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For every nonempty `A`, choosing any `i in A` gives

\[
q_A\le \mathbb P(i\in S)=p.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Because all coefficients in (4.4) are nonnegative,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E K_c(S,T)
&\le
1+p^2\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}a^{|A|}\\
&=
1+p^2\left((1+a)^n-1\right)\\
&=
\boxed{1+p^2(e^{cn}-1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The upper bound is attained by

\[
S=
\begin{cases}
[n],&\text{with probability }p,\\
\varnothing,&\text{with probability }1-p.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.8}
\]

For `0<p<1`, equality in (4.7) forces `q_A=p` for every nonempty `A`. In particular `P(i,j in S)=p` for every pair, so

\[
P(i\in S,j\notin S)=P(i\in S)-P(i,j\in S)=0,
\]

and symmetrically all coordinate indicators coincide almost surely. Thus (4.8) is the unique law up to null events.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact extremal latent law

Among all support laws with common one-site marginal `p`, the product-null collision pressure is maximized exactly by the empty/full two-point law, with

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_{\max}(n,p,c)
=
\log\left[1+p^2(e^{cn}-1)\right].
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

So the previous expectation-only counterexample was not an artificial choice: it is the exact worst dependence pattern allowed by the marginals.

## 4.2 Exact minimum when `np<=1`

Keeping only `|A|=0,1` terms in (4.4),

\[
\mathbb E K_c(S,T)
\ge
1+a\sum_i p_i^2.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Under (4.5),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E K_c(S,T)
\ge
1+a n p^2.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

If `np<=1`, equality is attainable by the empty/singleton law

\[
P(S=\{i\})=p\quad(i=1,\dots,n),
\qquad
P(S=\varnothing)=1-np.
\tag{4.12}
\]

No two coordinates ever co-occur, so every `q_A` with `|A|>=2` vanishes. Therefore (4.11) is the exact minimum in this sparse-marginal range.

This already refutes the idea that iid Bernoulli support is the pressure minimizer. For example, at `n=2`, `p=1/2`, and `a=2`, the singleton law gives

\[
\mathbb E K=2,
\]

whereas iid Bernoulli gives

\[
\mathbb E K=(1+ap^2)^2=(3/2)^2=9/4.
\tag{4.13}
\]

The new exact-arithmetic self-check verifies (4.4), the upper extremizer, the sparse lower extremizer, and this iid non-minimality example.

At fixed marginal sparsity `p`, collision pressure ranges from approximately a quadratic scale to a possible volume scale depending entirely on latent dependence:

# 5. B30 — ONE SCALE / ONE DIRECTION AT A TIME: exact two-color cancellation and a polymer-to-soft-kernel bridge

The clean answer is to color the two replicas and use connected-cluster cancellation.

## 5.1 Four partition functions and exact bichromatic cancellation

Start from a product reference measure `mu_0` on physical spins/variables. Let bulk factors be `1+u_a`, indexed by a finite factor set `A`. A red latent support `S` activates red mark factors `1+r_i`; a blue support `T` activates blue mark factors `1+b_j`. Write the resulting partition function as

\[
Z(S,T)
=
\mathbb E_{\mu_0}
\left[
\prod_{a\in A}(1+u_a)
\prod_{i\in S}(1+r_i)
\prod_{j\in T}(1+b_j)
\right].
\tag{5.1}
\]

The fixed-pair normalized overlap free energy has the inclusion-exclusion form

\[
\Delta(S,T)
=
\log Z(S,T)+\log Z(\varnothing,\varnothing)
-
\log Z(S,\varnothing)-\log Z(\varnothing,T).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Assume the four log partition functions admit one common absolutely convergent linked-cluster expansion, with each connected cluster remembering whether it contains red marks, blue marks, both, or neither. Then coefficient-by-coefficient in (5.2):

- a cluster with no colored mark contributes `+1+1-1-1=0`;
- a cluster with red but no blue contributes `+1-1=0`;
- a cluster with blue but no red contributes `+1-1=0`;
- a cluster containing at least one red and one blue mark survives.

Hence, **exactly**,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(S,T)
=
\sum_{\Gamma:\,r(\Gamma)\ge1,\,b(\Gamma)\ge1}
\Phi(\Gamma),
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

where `r(Gamma)` and `b(Gamma)` are the numbers of red and blue marks in `Gamma`. This is the two-color version of the previous replica-linear cancellation theorem, but now the surviving objects are explicitly the clusters that connect the two latent replicas.

## 5.2 Turn bichromatic clusters into a pair kernel

For every ordered site pair `(i,j)`, define

\[
H^{\mathrm{TC}}_{ij}
:=
\sum_{\substack{\Gamma:\ i\text{ is a red mark in }\Gamma,\\
 j\text{ is a blue mark in }\Gamma}}
\frac{|\Phi(\Gamma)|}{r(\Gamma)b(\Gamma)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Every bichromatic cluster with `r` red and `b` blue marks is counted exactly `rb` times on the right after summing over its colored pairs. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|\Delta(S,T)|
\le
\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{j\in T}H^{\mathrm{TC}}_{ij}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This step is exact and does not use a tree approximation. Consequently, if the linked-cluster expansion gives uniform row and column sums

\[
\sup_i\sum_jH^{\mathrm{TC}}_{ij}\le W_{\mathrm{TC}},
\qquad
\sup_j\sum_iH^{\mathrm{TC}}_{ij}\le W_{\mathrm{TC}},
\tag{5.6}
\]

then B11 applies verbatim with `H=H^TC`.

This is the key bridge:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local factor tables}
\to
\text{two-color connected clusters}
\to
H^{\mathrm{TC}}
\to
\text{latent collision pressure}
\to
\text{degree loss}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

\tag{5.8}
\]

For a fixed-amplitude field mark,

The local calculations from the previous run showed:

This is the local origin of a summable soft-collision kernel in a high-temperature regime.

## 5.4 A conservative all-order tree-majorant certificate
