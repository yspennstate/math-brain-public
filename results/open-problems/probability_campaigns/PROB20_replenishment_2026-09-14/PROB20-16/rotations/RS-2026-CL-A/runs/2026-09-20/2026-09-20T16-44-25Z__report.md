The broad box asks:

> **Open Problem 35.** What are the conditions on the distributions `P_n` and `Q_n` such that if the low-degree advantage remains bounded for `D=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}` as `n -> infinity`, it rigorously implies that no polynomials of a certain degree can distinguish between `P_n` and `Q_n`?

Mandatory later boundary: Songtao Mao, **“The Polynomial-Time Low-Degree Conjecture is False,”** arXiv:2607.20318, submitted 22 July 2026:

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20318>

For every fixed integer `r>=3`, Mao constructs a permutation-invariant graph law against `G(n,1/2)` whose marginals on every set of at most `D_n=Theta((log n)^{r-1})` edges are exactly uniform, hence whose low-degree advantage is exactly zero through `D_n`, while after every fixed positive edge-resampling rate a deterministic polynomial-time rank test strongly distinguishes. Therefore symmetry + product null + low-degree matching + fixed noise are not sufficient in general. Every theorem below is a restricted sufficient theorem, not a proof of the unrestricted conjecture.

## 2. Inherited theorem and the real bottleneck

The 15:40 report treated an arbitrary fixed bounded symmetric row-centered graphon kernel `W` on a probability space, with conditionally independent edges. It proved that bounded source-scale LDA plus fixed retention noise `rho in (0,1)` implies bounded noisy LDA through every degree `L_n` satisfying

`L_n^3/n -> 0`.

The apparent `n^(1/3)` barrier came from bounding the number of positive-excess motifs of edge count `e` and excess `r` by

`C^r e^(3r-1)`

and then forcing the geometric sum in `r` to converge uniformly for every `e<=L_n`.

That order of summation is unnecessarily lossy. The missing factorials are already present in the labeled embedding problem: one must divide by permutations of the branch vertices and, crucially, by permutations of the suppressed paths. Once those factors are retained, the correct effective shape factor is

`C^r e^(3r-1) / r^(2r-1)`,

and the fixed-noise factor `beta^e`, with `beta<1`, allows the edge count to be summed **before** the excess. This removes the cubic cutoff entirely and yields a positive **linear-degree** window.

## 3. Model and inherited analytic input

Let `(S,mu)` be a probability space and let

`W:S x S -> R`

be a fixed bounded symmetric measurable kernel satisfying

`int W(x,y)dmu(y)=0`

for `mu`-a.e. `x`. Let `T_W` be its self-adjoint Hilbert-Schmidt integral operator, and define

`Lambda=||T_W||_op>0`,   `M=||W||_infty`,

`B_W=max{1,M/Lambda,(M/Lambda)^2}`.

For each `n`, sample latent variables `sigma_1,...,sigma_n iid mu`; conditional on them, sample independent edge signs `X_ij in {-1,+1}` with

`E[X_ij | sigma]=theta_n W(sigma_i,sigma_j)`,

assuming `|theta_n|M<=1`. Let `P_n` be this planted law and `Q_n` the uniform product law on edge signs.

For every edge set `F`, with Walsh character `chi_F`,

`E_{P_n} chi_F = theta_n^{|F|} t(F,W)`

where

`t(F,W)=E_sigma prod_{uv in F}W(sigma_u,sigma_v)`.

After retention noise `T_rho`,

`Adv_{<=L}(T_rho P_n,Q_n)^2`

`= sum_{1<=|F|<=L} rho^(2|F|) theta_n^(2|F|) t(F,W)^2`.       (3.1)

The previous report proved two facts used unchanged here.

**Cycle pressure.** If source LDA is uniformly bounded through degrees `D_n` containing even `ell_n -> infinity` with `ell_n^2/n ->0`, then

`limsup_n n theta_n^2 Lambda^2 <=1`.                         (3.2)

Hence for any fixed `rho<1` we may choose `beta_*` with

`rho^2<beta_*<1`

such that eventually

`beta_n:=n rho^2 theta_n^2 Lambda^2 <= beta_*`.              (3.3)

**Excess contraction.** If `F` is connected, minimum degree at least two, not a cycle, with `e` edges and excess

`r=e-v>=1`,

then suppressing its degree-two chains gives

`|t(F,W)| <= B_W^(3r) Lambda^e`.                             (3.4)

The current run changes only the combinatorics and summation order.

## 4. New factorial-aware embedded-kernel count

Fix a connected simple graph `F` with minimum degree at least two, not a cycle. Let

- `e=|E(F)|`;
- `v=|V(F)|`;
- `r=e-v>=1`;
- `s` be the number of branch vertices, i.e. vertices of degree at least three;
- `m` be the number of edges in the degree-two-suppressed kernel.

Then

`m=s+r`,   `s<=2r`,   `m<=3r`,                              (4.1)

and the number of degree-two vertices is

`q=v-s=e-m`.

For fixed `s`, count such graphs on a fixed `v`-element labeled vertex set as follows.

1. Choose the `s` branch vertices: `binom(v,s)` choices.
2. Let `K=s(s+1)/2` be the number of unordered branch-endpoint types when loops are allowed. Allowing loops and invalid parallel/direct configurations only overcounts.
3. Temporarily order the `m` suppressed paths. Choose their endpoint types in at most `K^m` ways.
4. Choose a weak composition `q=q_1+...+q_m`: `binom(q+m-1,m-1)=binom(e-1,m-1)` choices.
5. Order the `q` degree-two labels and cut the order according to the composition: `q!` choices.
6. Divide by `m!`: in every valid simple graph the `m` maximal suppressed paths are distinct path objects, and permuting those path objects among the temporary slots gives at least `m!` representations of the same graph. Reversing a path only increases multiplicity, so division by `m!` is safe for an upper bound.

Therefore the number of such graphs on a fixed labeled `v`-set is at most

`sum_s binom(v,s) q! binom(e-1,m-1) K^m/m!`.                (4.2)

Now choose the `v` active vertices from `[n]`. Since

`binom(n,v) binom(v,s) q! = (n)_v/s! <= n^v/s!`,

the number of embedded graphs normalized by `n^v` is bounded by

`S_{e,r}:=sum_{1<=s<=2r, s+r<=e}`

`          binom(e-1,s+r-1) K(s)^(s+r) / [s!(s+r)!]`.       (4.3)

This is the missing factorial gain.

### Lemma 4.1 (factorial-aware excess count)

There is an absolute constant `C_1` such that for every `e>=1`, `r>=1`,

`boxed{S_{e,r} <= C_1^r e^(3r-1) r^(1-2r)}.`               (4.4)

#### Proof

Fix `s`, set `m=s+r`, and use `K(s)<=s^2`. Since `m>=2`,

`binom(e-1,m-1) <= e^(m-1)/(m-1)!`.

Using `k! >= (k/e_E)^k` and `m-1>=m/2`, the corresponding summand in (4.3) is at most

`C^(s+2m) e^(m-1) s^(2m-s) / m^(2m-1)`

for an absolute `C`. Because `s<=2r` and `m=s+r<=3r`, the exponential constant is `C_0^r`. Also

`s^(2m-s)/m^(2m-1)`

`=m^(1-s)(s/m)^(2m-s) <= m^(1-s) <= r^(1-s)`.

Thus the summand is at most

`C_0^r e^(r+s-1) r^(1-s)`.

Since `s<=2r` and `e>=m>=r`,

`e^(r+s-1) r^(1-s)`

`<= e^(3r-1) r^(1-2r)`

because the ratio of the right side to the left side is

`(e/r)^(2r-s)>=1`.

There are at most `2r` values of `s`, and `2r<=4^r`; absorb this into the constant. QED.

A quick exact arithmetic check for small `r` confirms the normalized ratio

`S_{e,r} r^(2r-1)/e^(3r-1)`

grows only exponentially in `r`, consistent with (4.4); the proof above is the arbitrary-size argument.

## 5. Summing edge count first: the cubic barrier disappears

For fixed `(e,r)`, combine (3.4), the embedding count above, and

`n^(e-r)(rho^2 theta_n^2 Lambda^2)^e=beta_n^e n^(-r)`

to obtain the total connected positive-excess contribution

`A_{e,r}`

`<= beta_*^e n^(-r) B_W^(6r) S_{e,r}`

`<= beta_*^e e^(3r-1) r^(1-2r)`

`   * [C_1 B_W^6/n]^r`.                                    (5.1)

Now fix `r` and sum over **all** edge counts, not merely `e<=L`. Because `beta_*<1`, if `a=-log beta_*>0`, then for every integer `p>=1`,

`sum_{e>=1} beta_*^e e^p <= C_a^(p+1) p!`                 (5.2)

for a finite constant `C_a` depending only on `beta_*` (compare the sum with the Gamma integral for `exp(-a x)x^p`). Taking `p=3r-1` and using `(3r-1)! <= (3r)^(3r)`, there is a finite constant

`K=K(W,rho)`

such that the entire excess-`r` connected contribution satisfies

`boxed{A_r <= r (K r/n)^r}.`                               (5.3)

This is the decisive new estimate.

The earlier `L^3/n` condition came from requiring the bracket in (5.1) to be small uniformly at the largest permitted `e`. Equation (5.3) shows that fixed product noise already geometrically suppresses large `e`; after the factorials are retained, only `r/n` remains.

## 6. Main theorem: a positive linear-degree window

### Theorem 6.1 (bounded-graphon linear-degree amplification; PROVED PARTIAL)

Assume the bounded row-centered graphon model of Section 3 and bounded source LDA through degrees `D_n` containing even `ell_n -> infinity` with `ell_n^2/n ->0`.

Fix any retention parameter `rho in (0,1)`.

Then there exist constants

`c=c(W,rho)>0`,   `C=C(W,rho)<infinity`

such that, for all sufficiently large `n`,

`boxed{Adv_{<= floor(c n)}(T_rho P_n,Q_n) <= C.}`           (6.1)

More generally, for every sequence `L_n=o(n)`, the entire positive-excess connected contribution to the squared low-degree advantage through degree `L_n` tends to zero.

#### Proof

By (3.2)–(3.3), choose `beta_* in (rho^2,1)` so that `beta_n<=beta_*` eventually.

**Cycle components.** Let

`H_W=||W||_2/Lambda`.

Since `T_W` is Hilbert-Schmidt,

`|Tr(T_W^e)| <= ||W||_2^2 Lambda^(e-2)=H_W^2 Lambda^e`.

The number of simple `e`-cycles is at most `n^e/(2e)`, so their total connected activity is at most

`(H_W^4/2) sum_{e>=3} beta_*^e/e < infinity`.              (6.2)

**Positive-excess components.** By (5.3), if only graphs with total degree at most `L_n` are retained, then `r<=L_n` and

`sum_{1<=r<=L_n} A_r`

`<= sum_{1<=r<=L_n} r (K r/n)^r`.                           (6.3)

If `L_n=o(n)`, put `eta_n=K L_n/n ->0`. Then

`sum_{r<=L_n} r (K r/n)^r <= sum_{r>=1} r eta_n^r ->0`.    (6.4)

For the linear window, choose any fixed

`0<c<1/(2K)`.

Then for `L_n<=cn`,

`sum_{r<=L_n} r (K r/n)^r <= sum_{r>=1} r (Kc)^r < infinity`. (6.5)

Thus the total activity of all connected nonzero motifs of degree at most `L_n` is uniformly bounded.

Finally, row-centering kills every connected motif with a degree-one vertex. Every surviving connected component is therefore either a cycle or a positive-excess component. For a general edge set `F`, the moment and its squared noisy weight factor across vertex-disjoint connected components. Dropping both the total-degree constraint and the disjointness constraint gives the standard polymer bound

`sum_{1<=|F|<=L_n} w(F)`

`<= prod_{connected C, |E(C)|<=L_n}(1+w(C))-1`

`<= exp(sum_C w(C))-1`.                                    (6.6)

The exponent is uniformly bounded by (6.2) and (6.5). By the exact Walsh identity (3.1), this proves (6.1). QED.

## 7. What materially changed

The old theorem required

`L_n^3/n ->0`,

so it protected degrees only up to `n^(1/3-o(1))`.

The new theorem protects **every degree up to a fixed positive linear fraction of `n`**, with the fraction depending on the fixed kernel and the fixed noise level:

`boxed{L_n <= c(W,rho)n.}`

In particular it contains every `n^(1-delta)` degree for fixed `delta>0`, and much more.

The structural reason is not a stronger pointwise contraction of one motif. It is a corrected global counting principle: branch-vertex and path-slot factorials must be kept until after embeddings are counted, and fixed noise should be summed over edge count before one sums over excess.

Classical fixed-excess graph enumeration also proceeds through cubic/minimum-degree-three kernels and subdivisions; I therefore do **not** claim the combinatorial kernel philosophy itself is novel. Novelty of this exact low-degree/noise application is UNVERIFIED.

## 8. Mao boundary and exact scope

This theorem still does **not** solve Kireeva's unrestricted Open Problem 35.

It applies to the explicit sufficient class:

- Boolean graph observations;
- a fixed bounded symmetric row-centered graphon kernel;
- iid latent vertices;
- conditionally independent edges given the latent vertices.

Mao's Reed–Muller/rank construction is not of this form. Indeed, every nonzero self-adjoint graphon kernel has a positive even cycle moment, e.g.

`t(C_4,W)=Tr(T_W^4)>0`,

whereas Mao exactly matches all edge marginals through a growing polylogarithmic degree. His dependence therefore escapes before the diagram expansion used here applies.

The new result nevertheless pushes the source implication much farther inside a natural `S_n`-invariant class: polylogarithmic bounded source LDA plus any fixed positive product noise forces bounded noisy LDA not merely at another polylogarithmic or small power degree, but throughout a **linear** degree window.

## 9. Failed route that taught something

The planned attack was to search for a Schatten or branch-cancellation gain beating the apparent cubic `n^(1/3)` barrier. That attack was unnecessary at this stage. The barrier was an artifact of two bookkeeping losses:

1. counting suppressed kernels independently of their labeled embeddings, which discarded `s!` and `m!` factors;
2. demanding a uniform bound at the largest edge count before using the geometric `beta_*^e` decay supplied by noise.

After correcting both points, absolute values alone reach linear degree. Any future Schatten/cancellation argument should therefore target the **remaining linear-scale constant or a non-graphon dependence class**, not merely attempt to beat `1/3`.

## 10. Reusable handoff to P08/P09

The transferable combinatorial principle is:

> In a diagram expansion where degree-two propagation chains contract by a factor `beta^length` with `beta<1`, and a connected excess-`r` diagram reduces to `O(r)` branch objects, count embedded diagrams before forgetting path permutations. If the resulting fixed-`(e,r)` factor has the form `C^r e^(3r-1)/r^(2r-1)`, summing `e` first converts it to `r(C' r/N)^r`.

For Gaussian/Wick or tensor diagrams this suggests that an apparent `L^3/N` cutoff may likewise be a counting-order artifact. However, the Boolean bounded-kernel contraction (3.4) does not automatically transfer: P08/P09 still need their own branch-amplitude or Schatten estimate. The shared artifact records only the generic counting/summation lemma and preserves RS-2026-CL-A ownership.

## 11. Exact remaining gap and next attack

The principal gap is no longer the polynomial degree range inside bounded graphons. It is the **conditional-independence / iid-vertex factorization itself**.

A materially different next attack should therefore move toward Mao rather than optimize `c(W,rho)`:

1. formulate a diagram/excess expansion for finite-rank matrix computations, low-rank message-passing iterates, or bounded-width branching programs in which dependence need not factor edgewise;
2. identify the analogue of excess `r` measuring the number of genuinely global algebraic constraints;
3. prove that fixed noise makes the corresponding propagation factor subcritical, or exhibit exactly why Mao's rank test has unbounded effective excess/width.

A secondary graphon question is whether the linear constant `c(W,rho)` can be made universal under a natural normalization such as `||W||_infty/||T_W||_op=O(1)`. That is useful, but less important than leaving the graphon class.
