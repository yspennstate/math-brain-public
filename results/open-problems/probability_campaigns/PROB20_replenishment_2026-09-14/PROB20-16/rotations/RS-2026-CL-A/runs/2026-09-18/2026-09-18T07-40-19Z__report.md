## 4. B30 — one scale/direction at a time: exact filtered coercivity for the 1D zero-field Ising cycle

### 4.1 Model and source polynomial filtration

Fix `n>=4` and `beta in R`. On `Omega_n={-1,+1}^n` with periodic indices, let

\[
Q_{n,\beta}(x)
=
\frac1{Z_{n,\beta}}
\exp\left(\beta\sum_{i=1}^n x_ix_{i+1}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a genuinely nonproduct Gibbs null whenever `beta != 0`.

Let `E_i` denote conditional expectation over spin `i` under `Q_{n,beta}` given all other spins, and let the continuous-time single-site heat-bath generator be

\[
A=\sum_{i=1}^n(I-E_i).
\tag{4.2}
\]

`A` is nonnegative and self-adjoint in `L^2(Q_{n,beta})`; `S_t=e^{-tA}` is the reversible heat-bath semigroup.

For `S subseteq [n]`, write

\[
\chi_S(x)=\prod_{i\in S}x_i.
\]

Let

\[
V_j=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_S:|S|\le j\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because `Q_{n,beta}` has full support, the Walsh monomials remain linearly independent as functions, and `(V_j)` is exactly the usual algebraic polynomial-degree filtration appearing in the source definition of LDA. No Glauber spectral degree is substituted.

### 4.2 Exact conditional mean

Set

\[
\gamma:=\tanh(2\beta).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The Gibbs conditional at site `i` gives

\[
E_i[x_i]
=
\tanh\bigl(\beta(x_{i-1}+x_{i+1})\bigr)
=
\frac\gamma2(x_{i-1}+x_{i+1}).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The second equality is exact because the neighbor sum takes only the values `-2,0,2`.

### 4.3 Exact degree preservation and zero commutator

For `i notin S`, `(I-E_i)chi_S=0`. For `i in S`, using `(4.5)`,

\[
E_i\chi_S
=
\frac\gamma2\chi_{S\setminus\{i\}}(x_{i-1}+x_{i+1}).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Each term on the right has degree either `|S|` or `|S|-2`:

- if the corresponding neighbor is not in `S`, multiplication replaces `i` by that neighbor and keeps degree `|S|`;
- if the neighbor is already in `S`, `x_j^2=1` cancels that neighbor and the degree drops by two.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{A V_j\subseteq V_j\quad\text{for every }j.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `A` is self-adjoint, `V_j^perp` is invariant as well. Consequently the `L^2(Q)` orthogonal projection `P_D` onto source degree `<=D` commutes exactly with `A`:

\[
\boxed{[A,P_D]=0,\qquad \varepsilon_D=0.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is precisely the source-degree-preserving property that was missing in the preceding abstract theorem.

### 4.4 The leading-degree quotient is an exclusion graph

Consider `V_j/V_{j-1}` with basis classes `[chi_S]`, `|S|=j`. Terms in `(4.6)` that drop degree disappear in the quotient. The quotient operator is

\[
\overline A_j[\chi_S]
=
 j[\chi_S]
-
\frac\gamma2
\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\ i-1\notin S}}
[\chi_{S-\{i\}\cup\{i-1\}}]
-
\frac\gamma2
\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\ i+1\notin S}}
[\chi_{S-\{i\}\cup\{i+1\}}].
\tag{4.9}
\]

Let `B_j` be the adjacency matrix of the `j`-particle simple-exclusion configuration graph on the cycle: a state is a `j`-subset and an edge moves one occupied site one lattice step into an empty site. Then

\[
\overline A_j=jI-\frac\gamma2 B_j.
\tag{4.10}
\]

`B_j` is a real symmetric adjacency matrix. Every `j`-subset has at most `2j` legal directed one-step moves, hence graph degree at most `2j`; therefore

\[
\|B_j\|_{2\to2}\le2j.
\tag{4.11}
\]

It follows that every eigenvalue `xi` of the quotient satisfies

\[
\xi\ge j-\frac{|\gamma|}{2}\|B_j\|
\ge j(1-|\gamma|).
\tag{4.12}
\]

No orthonormality of the monomial coordinate basis is needed for this spectral conclusion: `(4.10)` is the coordinate matrix of the quotient linear map, so its eigenvalues are basis-independent; the coordinate matrix itself is symmetric and obeys `(4.11)`.

### 4.5 From quotient spectra to `L^2(Q)` high-degree coercivity

Define the orthogonal degree layer

\[
U_j:=V_j\cap V_{j-1}^\perp.
\tag{4.13}
\]

By self-adjointness of `A` and invariance of both `V_j` and `V_{j-1}`, each `U_j` is invariant. The induced operator on `U_j` is naturally isomorphic to the quotient operator on `V_j/V_{j-1}`, hence has the same eigenvalues. Therefore

\[
\langle u,Au\rangle
\ge j(1-|\gamma|)\|u\|_2^2,
\qquad u\in U_j.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Since

\[
V_D^\perp=\bigoplus_{j>D}U_j,
\]

orthogonally, we obtain the exact source-degree filtered coercivity theorem

\[
\boxed{
\langle h,Ah\rangle
\ge
(D+1)c_\beta\|h\|_2^2,
\qquad h\in V_D^\perp,
\qquad
c_\beta:=1-|\tanh(2\beta)|>0.
}
\tag{4.15}
\]

Thus in the abstract theorem of the previous run one may take

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_D\ge c_\beta(D+1),\qquad \varepsilon_D=0.
}
\tag{4.16}
\]

for every fixed finite `beta`.

### 4.6 Direct semigroup/LDA consequence

Let `P` be any alternative absolutely continuous with respect to `Q=Q_{n,beta}`, let `L=dP/dQ`, and put `f=L-1`. Exact commutation gives the orthogonal decomposition

\[
S_tf=S_tP_Df+S_t(I-P_D)f.
\]

The low-degree sector contracts in `L^2`, while `(4.15)` gives exponential damping on the high sector. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|S_t(L-1)\|_2^2
\le
\|P_D(L-1)\|_2^2
+
 e^{-2c_\beta(D+1)t}\|(I-P_D)(L-1)\|_2^2.
}
\tag{4.17}
\]

Using `(3.1)` and `||L-1||_2^2=chi^2(P||Q)`, this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P S_t\|Q)
\le
\bigl(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\bigr)^2
+
 e^{-2c_\beta(D+1)t}\chi^2(P\|Q).
}
\tag{4.18}
\]

Here `PS_t` denotes the distribution after the reversible heat-bath channel; self-adjointness means its density with respect to `Q` is `S_tL`.

### 4.7 Source-scale corollary

Take a sequence of cycles with fixed finite `beta`,

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\Omega(1)}.
\]

If

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_{n,\beta})<\infty
\tag{4.19}
\]

and, for example,

\[
\log^+\chi^2(P_n\|Q_{n,\beta})=o(D_n),
\tag{4.20}
\]

then for every fixed `t>0`, `(4.18)` gives a uniformly bounded full noisy chi-square divergence. More generally, `(4.20)` can be replaced by the exact requirement that

\[
e^{-2c_\beta(D_n+1)t}\chi^2(P_n\|Q_{n,\beta})
\]

remain uniformly bounded.

A bounded chi-square second moment prevents strong testing separation by **any measurable test**, hence is stronger than the source's requested polynomial-class indistinguishability. For instance, if `E_Q L_t^2<=C`, Paley–Zygmund applied to the nonnegative density `L_t` gives

so the overlap is at least `1/(8C)` and

\|PS_t-Q\|_{TV}\le1-\frac1{8C}.
\tag{4.21}

This is a genuine nonproduct natural model satisfying the preceding abstract source-degree theorem. It remains **PARTIAL** because it is a structured Gibbs class, not a source-complete characterization.

## 5. B23 — do what the proposer did not expect: can very small Dobrushin dependence hide a huge source-degree commutator?

The aim here was counterexample-first: try to show that ordinary weak dependence is essentially orthogonal to source-degree preservation by constructing a Gibbs null with arbitrarily small Dobrushin constant but cutoff-scale commutator leakage.

The natural high-order parity Gibbs family creates the largest possible **degree jump** in one heat-bath update. The attempt does not produce cutoff-scale leakage; the reason it fails is informative.

### 5.1 Exact parity Gibbs family

For `m>=3` and `|t|<1`, let

\[
Q_{m,t}(x)
=2^{-m}\bigl(1+t\chi_{[m]}(x)\bigr)
=
\frac{\exp(J\chi_{[m]}(x))}{2^m\cosh J},
\qquad t=\tanh J.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For site `i`,

\[
E[x_i\mid x_{-i}]
=t\chi_{[m]\setminus\{i\}}(x).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Flipping any other spin reverses this conditional mean. Thus the Dobrushin influence coefficient from `j` to `i` is exactly `|t|`, and the row sum is

\[
\boxed{\alpha_{Dob}=(m-1)|t|.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Choosing

\[
t=\frac\alpha{m-1},\qquad 0<\alpha<1,
\tag{5.4}
\]

makes the model uniformly Dobrushin-unique with arbitrarily prescribed small row-sum constant `alpha`.

### 5.2 Exact source-degree jump

Let `A=sum_i(I-E_i)` again. If `S subseteq[m]` has `s=|S|`, `(5.2)` gives

\[
\boxed{
A\chi_S=s\chi_S-st\chi_{S^c}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus one heat-bath generator application can jump from source degree `s` to source degree `m-s`.

Assume

\[
s\le D<m-s.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Under `Q_{m,t}` the Walsh inner products satisfy

\[
\langle\chi_A,\chi_B\rangle_Q
=0
\]

unless `A=B` or `A=B^c`, with complementary inner product `t`. Since `S^c` lies above the cutoff while `S` lies below it, the orthogonal projection of `chi_{S^c}` onto `V_D` is exactly

\[
P_D\chi_{S^c}=t\chi_S.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore the high-degree leakage caused by `(5.5)` is

\[
(I-P_D)A\chi_S
=-st\bigl(\chi_{S^c}-t\chi_S\bigr),
\]

whose norm is

\[
\boxed{
\|(I-P_D)A\chi_S\|_2
=s|t|\sqrt{1-t^2}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Hence

\[
\varepsilon_D=\|[A,P_D]\|_{2\to2}
\ge s|t|\sqrt{1-t^2}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

If `m` is chosen on the order of `3D`, `s=D`, and `t=alpha/(m-1)`, then the degree jump is from approximately `D` to approximately `2D`, but the lower bound `(5.9)` is only

\[
\varepsilon_D\gtrsim \alpha/3,
\tag{5.10}
\]

not `Omega(D)`.

### 5.3 Why the intended Mao-like construction fails

The Dobrushin constraint itself forces the coefficient of the `m`-body parity interaction to shrink like `1/m`. Multiplying by `s=Theta(m)` produces only `O(alpha)` leakage amplitude. Thus this family demonstrates that **small Dobrushin dependence does not prevent very long jumps in algebraic degree**, but it does not make those jumps cutoff-scale in operator norm.

The same exact two-dimensional calculation shows that the high-degree orthogonalized vector

\[
h=\chi_{S^c}-t\chi_S
\]

has

\[
\frac{\langle h,Ah\rangle}{\|h\|_2^2}
=(m-s)+st^2.
\tag{5.11}
\]

For `m about 3D`, this is of order `D`, so the high-degree piece is strongly damped rather than Mao-like.

**Outcome of B23:** the attempted counterexample **fails**. It does, however, isolate a sharp distinction that any general theorem must respect:

> Ordinary Dobrushin smallness can coexist with arbitrarily large *degree bandwidth* of the generator, but in this exact high-order family it forces the *amplitude* of that leakage to stay `O(alpha)` while high-degree coercivity stays linear.

This supports, rather than refutes, the possibility of a broader theorem based on the dimensionless ratio `epsilon_D/lambda_D`, but does not prove such a theorem for general high-temperature Gibbs systems.

## 6. B19 — make it an algorithm: a deterministic factor-specification certificate

The previous run's exact typical-Hellinger theorem for product nulls is broad but still involves the noised density `g_q=T_{sqrt q}L`, which may appear to require global computation. Here is a simple local certificate computable directly from a bounded-arity factor specification of the raw likelihood ratio.

### 6.1 Product binary null and raw local log oscillations

Let

\[
Q=\operatorname{Unif}(\{-1,+1\}^m),
\qquad L=\frac{dP}{dQ}>0,
\qquad \rho=\sqrt q\in[0,1).
\]

Let

\[
g_q=T_\rho L.
\]

For each coordinate define the raw likelihood-ratio log oscillation

\[
R_i
:=
\sup_{x_{-i}}
\left|
\log L(+1,x_{-i})-\log L(-1,x_{-i})
\right|.
\tag{6.1}
\]

We will bound the noised fiber oscillation without computing `g_q` globally.

### 6.2 Exact binary projective contraction

First apply noise to all coordinates except `i`. For a fixed output boundary `y_{-i}`, define

\[
U_+=T_{\rho,-i}L(+1,y_{-i}),
\qquad
U_-=T_{\rho,-i}L(-1,y_{-i}).
\]

Because the same positive kernel averages numerator and denominator and the pointwise ratio `L(+1,x_-i)/L(-1,x_-i)` lies in `[e^{-R_i},e^{R_i}]`, one has

\[
e^{-R_i}\le \frac{U_+}{U_-}\le e^{R_i}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Now apply the binary noise in coordinate `i`. With

\[
a=\frac{1+\rho}{2},\qquad b=\frac{1-\rho}{2},
\]

we have

\[
g_q(+1,y_{-i})=aU_++bU_-,
\qquad
g_q(-1,y_{-i})=bU_++aU_-.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If `r=U_+/U_->0`, then direct algebra gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\tanh\left(
\frac12\log\frac{ar+b}{br+a}
\right)
=
\rho\tanh\left(\frac12\log r\right).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Taking absolute values and the worst boundary, the noised coordinate log oscillation `eta_i(q)` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\tanh^2\bigl(\eta_i(q)/2\bigr)
\le
q\tanh^2(R_i/2).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Equation `(6.4)` is the two-state instance of the classical Hilbert-projective/Birkhoff contraction phenomenon; it is not claimed as new.

### 6.3 Pressure certificate without the full spectrum

The previous run proved

\[
\mu(q)
\le
\sum_i
E_{\nu_{q,-i}}\tanh^2(\eta_i/2),
\tag{6.6}
\]

where `nu_q proportional g_q^2 Q` is the energy tilt. The deterministic bound `(6.5)` therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)
\le
q\sum_{i=1}^m\tanh^2(R_i/2).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Combining `(6.7)` with the preserved pressure lemma gives the explicit source-style condition

\[
q\sum_i\tanh^2(R_i/2)
\le\theta(D+1),\qquad\theta<1,
\tag{6.8}
\]

under which bounded source LDA through `D` implies a uniform full noisy chi-square/TV gap.

This certificate is conservative because it uses worst raw fibers rather than the exact energy-tilted typical fibers, but it is directly computable from a local model description.

### 6.4 Bounded-arity factor graph algorithm

Suppose the likelihood ratio is given in factorized log form

\[
L(x)=Z^{-1}\exp\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal F}\phi_a(x_a)\right),
\tag{6.9}
\]

where each factor depends on a bounded-size coordinate set `a`. Define

\[
\omega_{a,i}
:=
\sup_{x_{a\setminus\{i\}}}
\left|
\phi_a(+1,x_{a\setminus\{i\}})
-
\phi_a(-1,x_{a\setminus\{i\}})
\right|.
\tag{6.10}
\]

The normalizing constant cancels from coordinate log differences, and the triangle inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
R_i\le\sum_{a\ni i}\omega_{a,i}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Therefore the fully local deterministic quantity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_q
:=
q\sum_{i=1}^m
\tanh^2\left(
\frac12\sum_{a\ni i}\omega_{a,i}
\right)
}
\tag{6.12}
\]
