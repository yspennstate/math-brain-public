# 3. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM

The previous run left a concrete obligation: turn the prior-typical/reference-pressure conditions into something computable from bounded-arity high-temperature factor specifications. There is one clean route when the null has a Gaussian concentration bound in the local-oscillation norm.

## 3.1 Abstract Gaussian-concentration assumption

Let `Q` be a finite spin law. For a real observable `H`, write the one-coordinate oscillation

`delta_i(H)=sup{|H(x)-H(x')|: x_{-i}=x'_{-i}}`

and

`V(H)=sum_i delta_i(H)^2`.

Assume the Gaussian concentration bound

\[
\log E_Q e^{H-E_QH}\le C_{\rm GCB} V(H)
\tag{3.1}
\]

for every real `H`. In the Dobrushin uniqueness regime such a bound is classical, with an explicit finite constant depending on the Dobrushin contraction coefficient (under standard conventions it is of order `(1-c)^{-2}`; I do not freeze a convention-dependent numerical factor here).

Let a latent component be an exponential local tilt of `Q`:

\[
L_z=\frac{dP_z}{dQ}=\frac{e^{F_z}}{E_Qe^{F_z}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `T_t` be any reversible `Q`-stationary Markov semigroup. Put `g_{z,t}=T_tL_z`.

## 3.2 Component collision bound

Let `Lambda(F)=log E_Qe^F`. By (3.1),

`Lambda(2F_z) <= 2E_QF_z + 4 C_GCB V(F_z)`.

Jensen gives `Lambda(F_z)>=E_QF_z`, hence

\[
\Lambda(2F_z)-2\Lambda(F_z)\le4C_{\rm GCB}V(F_z).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|L_z\|_{L^2(Q)}\le e^{2C_{\rm GCB}V(F_z)}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Since a reversible Markov semigroup is an `L^2(Q)` contraction,

\[
\|g_{z,t}\|_2\le\|L_z\|_2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For a latent mixture `P=sum_z pi_zP_z`, `L=sum_z pi_zL_z`. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\chi^2(T_tP\|Q)
 &=\|T_tL\|_2^2\\
 &\le\left(\sum_z\pi_z\|T_tL_z\|_2\right)^2\\
 &\le\left(E_{Z\sim\pi}e^{2C_{\rm GCB}V(F_Z)}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Consequently a directly checkable one-reference sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{
\log E_{Z\sim\pi_n}\exp\{2C_{{\rm GCB},n}V(F_{n,Z})\}=o(D_n).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

It implies zero `D_n`-scale collision pressure uniformly for every later noise time `t>=0`.

## 3.3 Direct prior-row version, feeding B28

The same calculation can be applied to `F_z+F_{z'}`. Since

`K_t(z,z')=<T_tL_z,T_tL_z'> <= ||L_z||_2 ||L_z'||_2`,

we have

\[
K_t(z,z')\le
\exp\{2C_{\rm GCB}(V(F_z)+V(F_{z'}))\}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus the prior row load from B28 obeys

\[
r_t(z)\le e^{2C_{\rm GCB}V(F_z)}
E_\pi e^{2C_{\rm GCB}V(F_Z)},
\tag{3.9}
\]

and the diagonal obeys

\[
K_t(z,z)\le e^{4C_{\rm GCB}V(F_z)}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Therefore a typical-oscillation set can be inserted directly into the previous B28 theorem: control (3.9) on typical latents and control the prior-weighted tail of (3.10) on exceptional latents.

## 3.4 Local factor-table certificate

Suppose

\[
F_z(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_z}\phi_{a,z}(x_{\partial a}).
\tag{3.11}
\]

For each factor and incident variable define the exact table oscillation

`omega_{a,i}(z)=sup_{x,x':x_{-i}=x'_{-i}} |phi_{a,z}(x)-phi_{a,z}(x')|`.

Then

\[
\delta_i(F_z)\le\sum_{a\ni i}\omega_{a,i}(z),
\qquad
V(F_z)\le\sum_i\left(\sum_{a\ni i}\omega_{a,i}(z)\right)^2.
\tag{3.12}
\]

This quantity is computable in time linear in the explicit factor incidences once the bounded-size factor tables are given; no partition function and no global Fourier expansion are needed.

If every active planted factor has arity at most `r`, each variable lies in at most `Delta_p` active planted factors, every local oscillation is at most `b`, and `m_z=|A_z|`, then with `s_i=# {a in A_z:i in partial a}`,

`sum_i s_i <= r m_z`, `max_i s_i<=Delta_p`, and hence

\[
\boxed{
V(F_z)\le r\Delta_p b^2m_z.
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

Therefore

\[
\log E_\pi\exp\{2C_{\rm GCB}r\Delta_pb^2m_Z\}=o(D_n)
\tag{3.14}
\]

is an explicit model-description-size certificate for zero reference pressure. In particular, a uniform `m_z=o(D_n)` bound suffices when the displayed constants stay bounded.

# 4. B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS

The B19 certificate controls individual tilted components before mixing. The exact obstruction is latent cancellation.

## 4.1 Product-null cancellation family

Let `Q_n` be uniform on `{+1,-1}^n`. Let the latent state `z` also be uniform on `{+1,-1}^n`. Fix `h != 0` and define

\[
L_z(x)=\frac{\exp(h\sum_{i=1}^nz_ix_i)}{(\cosh h)^n}
=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+\tanh(h)z_ix_i).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then averaging over the latent signs gives, pointwise for every `x`,

\[
E_z L_z(x)=\prod_i E_{z_i}(1+\tanh(h)z_ix_i)=1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

So the mixture is **exactly the null**:

\[
P_n=Q_n,
\qquad
\chi^2(TP_n\|Q_n)=0
\tag{4.3}
\]

for every `Q_n`-stationary noise operator `T`.

But each component is exponentially far in `L^2`:

\[
1+\chi^2(P_z\|Q_n)
=E_Q L_z^2
=(1+\tanh^2 h)^n.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Moreover `F_z=h sum_i z_ix_i` has

\[
V(F_z)=4h^2n.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus every componentwise oscillation/Gaussian-concentration certificate pays `exp(Theta(n))`, even though the actual mixture collision mass is identically one.

## 4.2 Why the B28 row load is the right object here

Let `g_z=TL_z` and `g=E_zg_z=T1=1`. Then the exact prior row load is

\[
r(z)=E_{z'}\langle g_z,g_{z'}\rangle
=\langle g_z,1\rangle=1
\tag{4.6}
\]

for **every** latent `z`. So B28 is exact with `R=1` and no exceptional set.

This identifies the precise failure of a naive local-factor algorithm:

> Bounding each planted component separately destroys exponentially strong cancellation coming from latent symmetry. For extensive planted tilts, a broad source-valid condition must control prior-averaged overlap loads (or an equivalent symmetry/interface quantity), not merely component `L^2` norms or local oscillation energy.

This is not a pathology caused by long-range dependence: it occurs under the product null, the strongest possible mixing regime. Therefore no strengthening of the null's Dobrushin constant alone repairs the loss.

# 5. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT

The previous transport-pressure theorem used an `ell_1` layer envelope and a one-way maximal-downward comparison. Self-adjointness allows a stronger weighted-energy calculation. The resulting characteristic flow is Riccati/Möbius rather than affine.

## 5.1 Reversible degree-block hypotheses

Let

`H=direct_sum_{k>=1} H_k`

be the mean-zero ordinary source-degree decomposition for one finite problem size, with projections `Pi_k`. Let `A=A^*>=0` be the reversible generator and `A_jk=Pi_j A Pi_k`. Put

`c_jk=||A_jk||=c_kj`.

Assume uniformly:

1. **diagonal degree coercivity**
   \[
   \langle u,A_{kk}u\rangle\ge\kappa k\|u\|^2;
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
2. **symmetric off-diagonal row budget**
   \[
   \sum_{j\ne k}c_{jk}\le\beta k;
   \tag{5.2}
   \]
3. **degree bandwidth** `c_jk=0` when `|j-k|>w`;
4. `0<=beta<kappa`.

For `f_t=e^{-tA}f`, define the weighted squared layer energy

\[
M(t,z)=\sum_{k\ge1}z^k\|\Pi_kf_t\|^2,
\qquad 0<z\le1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

## 5.2 Differential inequality

Differentiate. The diagonal blocks contribute at most

\[
-2\kappa\sum_k k z^k\|\Pi_kf_t\|^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For a pair `j<k`, self-adjointness combines the two directed cross terms. Writing `x_j=||Pi_j f_t||`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
&-2\Big(z^j\langle f_j,A_{jk}f_k\rangle
+z^k\langle f_k,A_{kj}f_j\rangle\Big)\\
&\qquad\le 2(z^j+z^k)c_{jk}x_jx_k\\
&\qquad=4c_{jk}\cosh\!\left(\frac{|j-k|}{2}|\log z|\right)
(z^{j/2}x_j)(z^{k/2}x_k)\\
&\qquad\le2c_{jk}\cosh\!\left(\frac{|j-k|}{2}|\log z|\right)
\big(z^jx_j^2+z^kx_k^2\big).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Using (5.2), bandwidth `w`, and `z partial_z M=sum_k k z^k x_k^2`, we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t M(t,z)
\le
-2\left[\kappa-\beta\cosh\!\left(\frac w2|\log z|\right)\right]
 z\,\partial_zM(t,z).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the reversible `ell_2` pressure evolution that the prior one-way comparison was missing.

## 5.3 Solve the characteristic exactly

Set

\[
\Delta=\sqrt{\kappa^2-\beta^2}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

For `beta>0` and `w>0`, define

\[
r=\frac{\beta}{\kappa+\Delta}
=\frac{\kappa-\Delta}{\beta}\in(0,1),
\qquad
a_t=e^{-w\Delta t},
\tag{5.8}
\]

and the **reversible Möbius radius**

\[
\boxed{
R_t^{\rm rev}
=
\left(\frac{r+a_t}{1+r a_t}\right)^{1/w}.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

For `beta=0` (and for the degenerate `w=0` case, where there are no off-diagonal jumps) use

\[
R_t^{\rm rev}=e^{-\kappa t}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

To verify (5.9), let `y=z^{w/2}`. The characteristic equation associated with (5.6) is

\[
\dot z=2z\left[\kappa-\frac\beta2(z^{w/2}+z^{-w/2})\right],
\tag{5.11}
\]

so

\[
\dot y=w\left[\kappa y-\frac\beta2(1+y^2)\right]
=-\frac{w\beta}{2}(y-r)(y-r^{-1}).
\tag{5.12}
\]

The cross-ratio

\[
\frac{y-r}{r^{-1}-y}
\tag{5.13}
\]

grows by the factor `e^{w Delta t}`. The unique initial point that reaches `y=1` at time `t` is

\[
y_0=\frac{r+a_t}{1+r a_t}.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Since `z_0=y_0^{2/w}`, its square-root is precisely (5.9).

Along the characteristic, (5.6) says `M` is nonincreasing. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}f\|_2^2
=M(t,1)
\le M(0,(R_t^{\rm rev})^2)
=\sum_{k\ge1}(R_t^{\rm rev})^{2k}\|\Pi_kf\|_2^2.
}
\tag{5.15}
\]

This is the main new transport theorem of the run.

### Comparison with the previous relaxed radius

The earlier `ell_1` path envelope had long-time floor

`(beta/kappa)^(1/w)`.

The reversible `ell_2` envelope has long-time floor

\[
\left(\frac{\beta}{\kappa+\sqrt{\kappa^2-\beta^2}}\right)^{1/w},
\tag{5.16}
\]

which is strictly smaller for `0<beta<kappa` and is asymptotic to `(beta/(2kappa))^(1/w)` at weak coupling. I do **not** claim global sharpness over all reversible generators: equality in the proof would require simultaneous alignment of every active block direction and spending the transition budget at the extremal bandwidth. The theorem is a rigorous upper envelope under the stated block hypotheses.

## 5.4 Stronger source-valid transport-pressure theorem

Return to a sequence `(P_n,Q_n)` with likelihood ratio `L_n` and `f_n=L_n-1`, ordinary source-degree layers, and source cutoff

`D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`.

Let

\[
A_n^{\le D_n}=\|P_{\le D_n}f_n\|_2
\tag{5.17}
\]

be the source low-degree advantage, and for fixed `v in (0,1)` define

\[
\mathcal F_n(v)=\sum_{k\ge1}v^{2k}\|\Pi_kf_n\|_2^2.
\tag{5.18}
\]

Suppose the reversible actual-noise generators obey (5.1)–(5.2) and the bandwidth bound uniformly in `n`. Fix a noise time `t>0`. If there exists a fixed

\[
R_t^{\rm rev}<v<1
\tag{5.19}
\]

such that

\[
\log^+(1+\mathcal F_n(v))=o(D_n),
\tag{5.20}
\]

then by (5.15), splitting the initial weighted energy at `D_n`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2(P_nS_t\|Q_n)
&=\|e^{-tA_n}f_n\|_2^2\\
&\le\sum_{k\le D_n}(R_t^{\rm rev})^{2k}\|\Pi_kf_n\|_2^2
+\sum_{k>D_n}(R_t^{\rm rev})^{2k}\|\Pi_kf_n\|_2^2\\
&\le (A_n^{\le D_n})^2
+\left(\frac{R_t^{\rm rev}}v\right)^{2(D_n+1)}\mathcal F_n(v).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.21}
\]

The second term is `o(1)` by (5.20). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_nS_t\|Q_n)
\le (A_n^{\le D_n})^2+o(1).
}
\tag{5.22}
\]

Thus bounded source LDA rules out every measurable **strong** distinguisher after the fixed noise, not only a polynomial subclass. If the source LDA tends to zero, then noisy `chi^2 -> 0` and hence total variation tends to zero.

Compared with the previous B3 result, the self-adjoint weighted-energy argument removes the triangle/geometric-series prefactor and replaces the relaxed transport radius by the reversible Möbius radius. The conclusion remains a **source-valid sufficient framework, not a source-wide characterization**.

# 6. How B19 and B24 combine — and why B20 matters

There are now two distinct model-level routes to the reference-pressure hypothesis required by (5.20).

1. **Local oscillation / high-temperature route.** If the unnoised planted mixture itself satisfies the B19 bound

   `log(1+chi2(P_n||Q_n))=o(D_n)`, then automatically `F_n(v)<=chi2(P_n||Q_n)` for every `v<1`, and B24 applies whenever the reversible degree transport hypotheses hold. This yields a completely local sufficient class when planted factor support is `o(D_n)` or has the exponential-moment bound (3.14).

2. **Prior-typical overlap route.** B20 proves that B19 can be exponentially too pessimistic for extensive latent tilts, even under a product null. In such cases the correct object is the B28 prior row load or a symmetry/interface computation that keeps latent cancellation. A generic high-temperature extension therefore needs a **direct row-load/overlap theorem**, not merely stronger concentration of each component.

This distinction is important for the source question: high-temperature concentration can certify a real subclass, but it cannot by itself be promoted to the sought general condition.
