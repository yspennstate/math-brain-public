# 3. B6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE: exact motion of the source-degree flag

The previous report's local theorem says that in zero-field pairwise Ising, nonlinear single-site degree creation is uniformly `O(alpha^3)`. A tempting route is to identify the `Q_alpha` source layers with product Walsh chaos by a globally well-conditioned moving basis and lift the local estimate that way. This run shows that this global-basis route is structurally wrong: the source-degree flag itself can move at operator norm `Theta(sqrt(n))` at first order, even though the **commutator of degree with the generator** begins only cubically.

## 3.1 Fixed-Hilbert-space representation

Let `mu=Q_0` be a fixed reference probability measure on a finite state space, and let

\[
W_\alpha=\frac{dQ_\alpha}{d\mu}>0.
\]

Multiplication by `W_alpha^{1/2}` defines a unitary

\[
U_\alpha:L^2(Q_\alpha)\to L^2(\mu),
\qquad
U_\alpha f=W_\alpha^{1/2}f.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `V_{<=k}` be the **fixed ordinary algebraic filtration** (the same functions, but viewed in `L^2(Q_alpha)`). Its image in the fixed Hilbert space is

\[
S_k(\alpha)=U_\alpha V_{\le k}\subset L^2(\mu).
\]

Let `P_k(alpha)` be the `L^2(mu)`-orthogonal projection onto `S_k(alpha)`, and put

\[
\Pi_k(\alpha)=P_k(\alpha)-P_{k-1}(\alpha).
\]

Write

\[
B_\alpha
:=U_\alpha' U_\alpha^{-1}
=\frac12 M_{\partial_\alpha\log W_\alpha}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

`B_alpha` is self-adjoint because it is multiplication by a real function.

## 3.2 Exact projection-flow identity

### Proposition 3.1 — moving algebraic projection

For every `k`,

\[
\boxed{
P_k'
=(I-P_k)B_\alpha P_k
+P_k B_\alpha(I-P_k).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

Every differentiable vector `v_alpha=U_alpha v` with `v in V_{<=k}` lies in `S_k(alpha)` and satisfies

\[
v_\alpha'=B_\alpha v_\alpha.
\]

Therefore the normal component of the velocity of the moving subspace is `(I-P_k)B_alpha P_k`. Differentiating `P_k^2=P_k` gives

\[
P_kP_k'P_k=0,
\qquad
(I-P_k)P_k'(I-P_k)=0,
\]

so `P_k'` has only the two off-diagonal blocks. Since `P_k'` is self-adjoint, the opposite block is the adjoint `P_kB_alpha(I-P_k)`. This proves (3.3). `square`

This is a standard moving-subspace/Kato-type mechanism; only its use here to diagnose source-degree transport is specific to this track.

## 3.3 Exact derivative of the source-number operator

In the fixed Hilbert representation define the source-number operator

\[
\mathcal N_\alpha
=\sum_{j=0}^n j\Pi_j(\alpha).
\]

The nested filtration gives the exact identity

\[
\mathcal N_\alpha
=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(I-P_m(\alpha)).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Take `j!=k`. If `j<k`, then in `-sum_m P_m'` only the cuts `m=j,...,k-1` separate layer `j` from layer `k`; each contributes `-Pi_j B Pi_k`. The same argument with the adjoint block handles `j>k`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_j\mathcal N_\alpha'\Pi_k
=-|j-k|\,\Pi_jB_\alpha\Pi_k,
\qquad j\ne k,
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

and the diagonal blocks of `N_alpha'` vanish.

Equation (3.5) is the exact infinitesimal cost of transporting the **whole degree flag** under a change of measure.

## 3.4 Extensive first-order flag motion in pairwise Ising

Now specialize to the zero-field pairwise Ising deformation from the preceding runs,

\[
Q_\alpha(x)
\propto
\exp\!\left(\alpha H(x)\right),
\qquad
H(x)=\sum_{e=\{u,v\}}J_e x_ux_v,
\]

with `mu=Q_0` uniform. Since `E_mu H=0`,

\[
B_0=\frac12M_H.
\]

At the product point, `Pi_0` is the constant layer and `Pi_2` is homogeneous Walsh degree two. Hence

\[
\Pi_2B_0\Pi_0(1)=\frac12 H,
\]

and from (3.5),

\[
\Pi_2\mathcal N_0'\Pi_0(1)=-H.
\]

Orthogonality of distinct edge characters gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\Pi_2\mathcal N_0'\Pi_0\|_{2\to2}
=\|H\|_2
=\left(\sum_eJ_e^2\right)^{1/2}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

For bounded-degree graphs with `Theta(n)` nonzero couplings of constant size, (3.6) is `Theta(sqrt(n))`.

# 4. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: tilted Dirichlet coercivity

The preceding obstruction suggests replacing the moving basis by a quadratic-form quantity that already combines `A` and `N` and is invariant under how individual basis vectors inside a source layer are chosen.

Let

\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}\mathcal H_k,
\qquad
\mathcal N=\sum_{k\ge0}k\Pi_k,
\]

and let `A=A^*>=0` be a reversible generator with `A1=0`. For `s>=0`, define the symmetrized exponentially tilted generator

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_s(A,\mathcal N)
:=\frac12\left(e^{-s\mathcal N}Ae^{s\mathcal N}
+e^{s\mathcal N}Ae^{-s\mathcal N}\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Its block matrix is particularly transparent:

\[
\Pi_j\mathcal C_s\Pi_k
=\cosh(s(j-k))\,\Pi_jA\Pi_k.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define the tilted coercivity

\[
\boxed{
\delta_s^*(A,\mathcal N)
:=
\inf_{f\perp1,\,f\ne0}
\frac{\langle f,\mathcal C_s f\rangle}
{\langle f,\mathcal N f\rangle}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.1 Exact degree-distance gate

### Theorem 4.1 — tilted Dirichlet transport

If `delta_s^*>0`, then for every integers `K>D>=0` and every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\Pi_{\le D}e^{-tA}\Pi_{\ge K}\|_{2\to2}
\le
\exp\{-s(K-D)-\delta_s^*t\}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Take `h=Pi_{>=K}h`, with `K>=1`, and define

\[
y(t)=e^{-s\mathcal N}e^{-tA}h.
\]

Then

\[
y'(t)=-e^{-s\mathcal N}Ae^{s\mathcal N}y(t).
\]

Taking the real part of the energy identity,

\[
\frac d{dt}\|y(t)\|_2^2
=-2\langle y(t),\mathcal C_s y(t)\rangle.
\]

The mean-zero sector is invariant. Since `N>=I` there and `C_s>=delta_s^* N` as a quadratic form,

\[
\frac d{dt}\|y(t)\|_2^2
\le -2\delta_s^*\|y(t)\|_2^2.
\]

Thus

\[
\|y(t)\|_2
\le e^{-\delta_s^*t}\|e^{-s\mathcal N}h\|_2
\le e^{-\delta_s^*t-sK}\|h\|_2.
\]

Finally,

\[
\|\Pi_{\le D}e^{-tA}h\|_2
=\|\Pi_{\le D}e^{s\mathcal N}y(t)\|_2
\le e^{sD}\|y(t)\|_2,
\]

which is (4.4). `square`

## 4.2 Relation to the earlier block/commutator criteria

Theorem 4.1 is not a competing new semigroup trick; it packages the previous transport arguments in their invariant quadratic-form form.

If

\[
\Pi_kA\Pi_k\succeq \kappa k\Pi_k
\]

and the exponentially weighted off-diagonal form is bounded by `B_s N`, then (4.2) gives

\[
\delta_s^*\ge\kappa-B_s.
\]

Under the older finite-band row-budget hypothesis this recovers the previous `kappa-beta cosh(sw)` gate. Under the source-number commutator formulation, the same off-diagonal information is encoded by

\[
\Pi_j[\mathcal N,A]\Pi_k=(j-k)\Pi_jA\Pi_k.
\]

The important conceptual change is that one need not control the motion or condition number of the source layers themselves. It is enough to prove **tilted coercivity of the actual reversible generator against the actual source-number operator**.

This is the natural local-specification target after B6: prove `delta_s^*>0` from local Gibbs/factor data, rather than proving a false uniform bound on `N_alpha'`.

# 5. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: spectral independence is not the missing degree geometry

The literature check above makes a tempting conjecture: perhaps spectral independence or approximate tensorization supplies the missing B12 tilted coercivity automatically. A simple high-order parity Gibbs family shows that, without an explicit locality/arity control, this cannot be true.

## 5.1 Exact parity-interaction stress family

For an integer `r>=3`, let

\[
Q_{r,\alpha}(x)
=2^{-r}\left(1+t\chi_{[r]}(x)\right),
\qquad
t=\tanh\alpha,
\tag{5.1}
\]

which is the Gibbs law proportional to `exp(alpha x_1\cdots x_r)`.

Every proper marginal is uniform. After pinning variables, the remaining model is again a parity interaction of the same magnitude; when at least three variables remain, all pair marginals are still uniform, and when exactly two remain the only nonzero pair influence has magnitude `|t|`. Thus the usual influence/spectral-independence parameter remains bounded by a constant depending only on `|t|`, uniformly in `r` (up to the convention-dependent diagonal normalization).

Yet the heat-bath generator has a degree jump growing with `r`.

Let

\[
x=x_1,
\qquad
y=x_2\cdots x_r.
\]

Then

\[
\langle x,y\rangle_{Q_{r,\alpha}}=t.
\]

For `r>2`, the degree-one source vector is `x`. The normalized source vector in degree `r-1` paired with it is

\[
v=\frac{y-tx}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Only the update of site `1` acts nontrivially on `x`, and

\[
A x=x-ty.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\langle v,Ax\rangle
=-t\sqrt{1-t^2}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The source-degree gap is `r-2`. Consequently any exponentially weighted block budget of the type needed in B12/B11 contains at least

\[
\boxed{
\cosh(s(r-2))\,|t|\sqrt{1-t^2},
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

which diverges exponentially in `r` for every fixed `s>0` and fixed nonzero `t`, even though the pairwise influence/spectral-independence parameter stays `O(1)`.

### Consequence

**Spectral independence, Poincare inequalities, or approximate tensorization by themselves do not encode the source-degree gap profile.** A source-valid local Gibbs theorem needs at least a locality/arity or conditional-degree-tail input in addition to influence/mixing control.

This does not weaken the bounded-arity program. In fact, it cleanly separates the roles:

1. spectral independence / Dobrushin / approximate tensorization can provide dimension-free variance and Glauber coercivity;
2. bounded local arity and the exact conditional-mean transform can provide exponentially weighted degree-jump control;
3. B12 asks that these two ingredients combine into positive tilted Dirichlet coercivity.

The dependent-Hoeffding literature also reinforces the distinction: under dependence one still has useful decompositions, but the natural projectors are generally oblique. That is qualitatively compatible with B6's finding that the full orthogonal degree flag may move extensively, even while a generator-adapted commutator remains small.
