## 3.1 A connected Dobrushin-unique test family

Let `n=2m` and let `Q_m` be the homogeneous zero-field Ising law on the open path:

\[
Q_m(x)\propto
\exp\left(J\sum_{i=1}^{2m-1}X_iX_{i+1}\right),
\qquad
\rho:=\tanh J\in(0,1/2).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is connected, pairwise, bounded degree, and uniformly weak. The one-site Dobrushin influence of a neighbor is `rho`, so every interior row sum is at most `2rho<1`.

Introduce bond variables

\[
B_i=X_iX_{i+1},\qquad i=1,\dots,2m-1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The map

\[
(X_1,B_1,\dots,B_{2m-1})\longleftrightarrow(X_1,\dots,X_{2m})
\]

is bijective, and `(3.2)` factorizes in these coordinates. Hence `X_1` is uniform and the bonds are independent Rademacher variables with

\[
\mathbb E B_i=\rho.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Select the odd bonds

\[
Y_j:=B_{2j-1}=X_{2j-1}X_{2j},\qquad j=1,\dots,m.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then `Y_1,...,Y_m` are iid with mean `rho`, although the original visible Ising graph is connected.

Put

\[
\xi_j=Y_j-\rho,
\qquad
\sigma^2:=\mathbb E\xi_j^2=1-\rho^2.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For `S subset [m]`, write

\[
\chi_S^Y=\prod_{j\in S}Y_j,
\qquad
m_S^Y=\chi_S^Y-\rho^{|S|}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Because the pairs `(2j-1,2j)` are disjoint, `chi_S^Y` is an ordinary visible-spin monomial of source degree exactly `2|S|`. Thus every `m_S^Y` is literally a member of the predecessor's raw centered-source-monomial family.

## 3.2 The stable adapted chaos

For `1<=j<=m`, define the independent-bond chaos sums

\[
\Psi_j
:=
\sum_{\substack{T\subset[m]\\|T|=j}}
\prod_{i\in T}\xi_i.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The summands are mutually orthogonal across distinct subsets, so

\[
\boxed{
\|\Psi_j\|_{L^2(Q_m)}^2
=
{m\choose j}(1-\rho^2)^j.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Now aggregate the raw centered monomials of a fixed bond order:

\[
M_k:=
\sum_{\substack{S\subset[m]\\|S|=k}}m_S^Y.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Expanding `Y_i=rho+xi_i` gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
M_k
=
\sum_{j=1}^k
\rho^{k-j}{m-j\choose k-j}\Psi_j.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Indeed, for fixed `T` of size `j`, there are exactly `{m-j choose k-j}` supersets `S` of size `k` containing it.

The triangular transform has the exact inverse

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_r
=
\sum_{k=1}^r
(-\rho)^{r-k}{m-k\choose r-k}M_k.
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

To verify `(3.12)`, the coefficient of `Psi_j` on its right side is

\[
\rho^{r-j}
\sum_{k=j}^r
(-1)^{r-k}
{m-k\choose r-k}{m-j\choose k-j}.
\]

Using

\[
{m-k\choose r-k}{m-j\choose k-j}
=
{m-j\choose r-j}{r-j\choose k-j},
\]

the sum is `(1-1)^(r-j)`, hence zero unless `j=r`, when it equals one.

This triangular bond-chaos order is the B12 invariant that the raw Gram approach missed.

# 4. B6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE: the raw Gibbs Riesz bound fails immediately away from product

\tag{4.1}

Therefore the Euclidean coefficient norm is

\[
\|a\|_{\ell^2}^2

\tag{4.2}

In particular, retaining only `k=1`,

\[

\tag{4.3}

On the other hand, `(3.12)` and `(3.9)` give

\|\Psi_r\|_2^2

\tag{4.4}

The raw monomials in this principal family all have visible source degree at most `2r`. By Rayleigh's principle and principal-submatrix interlacing, the full centered source-monomial Gram matrix through degree `2r` obeys

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\min}(G_{2r})
&\le
\frac{{m\choose r}(1-\rho^2)^r}
{m\rho^{2r-2}{m-1\choose r-1}^2}\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{(1-\rho^2)^r}
{r\rho^{2r-2}{m-1\choose r-1}}.

\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}

This is the promised obstruction.

## 4.2 Polylogarithmic degree is much worse

For `r<=m/2`,

\[
{m-1\choose r-1}
\ge
\left(\frac{m-1}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Hence for fixed `rho in (0,1/2)`,

\[
\log\lambda_{\min}(G_{2r})
\le
-(r-1)\log\frac{m-1}{r-1}+O_\rho(r).
\tag{4.8}

\boxed{

\tag{4.9}

This is parametrically smaller than `exp(-C D_n)` for every fixed `C`.
