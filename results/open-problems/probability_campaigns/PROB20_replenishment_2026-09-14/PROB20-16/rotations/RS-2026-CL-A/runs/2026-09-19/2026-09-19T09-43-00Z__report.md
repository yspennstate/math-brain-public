# 3. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: a directly checkable Laplace-domination collision theorem

The preceding run obtained the desired quadratic collision pressure for negatively associated latent support laws. Negative association is useful but unnecessarily specific. The proof only needs an exponential-moment domination that can be checked directly from a latent specification.

## 3.1 Abstract Laplace-domination hypothesis

Let `mu` be a law on `{0,1}^n`, write `S=(S_i)`, and let `r_i>=0`. Assume that for every vector `h=(h_i)` with `h_i>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\log \mathbb E_\mu e^{\langle h,S\rangle}
\le
\sum_{i=1}^n r_i\,\psi_i(h_i),
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where each `psi_i:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity)` is increasing and convex with `psi_i(0)=0`.

This is a directly stated one-replica Laplace certificate. It includes the product/sub-Poisson form `psi_i(h)=e^h-1`, but the theorem below does not require that specific function.

Let `S,T` be independent draws from `mu`, and let `H=(H_ij)` be nonnegative. Define

\[
\Psi_\mu(H):=
\log \mathbb E e^{S^\top H T}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For each column put

\[
W_j:=\sum_i H_{ij}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For `W_j>0`, define

\[
\kappa_j:=\frac{\psi_j(W_j)}{W_j},
\tag{3.4}
\]

and set the term involving column `j` to zero when `W_j=0`. Finally define

\[
b_i:=\sum_j r_j\kappa_j H_{ij}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — two-stage Laplace collision bound

Under (3.1),

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\le
\sum_i r_i\psi_i(b_i).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

**Proof.** Condition on `S` and write

\[
a_j(S):=\sum_iH_{ij}S_i.
\]

Because `0<=a_j(S)<=W_j`, applying (3.1) to `T` gives

\[
\log \mathbb E_T e^{S^THT}
\le
\sum_j r_j\psi_j(a_j(S)).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Convexity and `psi_j(0)=0` imply the chord bound

\[
\psi_j(a_j)
\le
\frac{a_j}{W_j}\psi_j(W_j)
=
\kappa_j a_j
\qquad (0\le a_j\le W_j).
\tag{3.8}
\]

Hence

\[
\log \mathbb E_T e^{S^THT}
\le
\sum_i b_iS_i.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Exponentiating and averaging over `S`, then applying (3.1) a second time, yields

\[
\mathbb E_{S,T}e^{S^THT}
\le
\mathbb E_Se^{\sum_i b_iS_i}
\le
\exp\left\{\sum_i r_i\psi_i(b_i)\right\},
\]

which proves (3.6). □

The result is intentionally stronger than the prior B11 theorem in scope: no negative-association statement appears in the hypothesis. Everything is reduced to a one-replica Laplace certificate.

## 3.2 Sub-Poisson specialization

Take

\[
\psi_i(h)=e^h-1,
\qquad r_i=\bar p_i.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Then

\[
\kappa_j=
\frac{e^{W_j}-1}{W_j},
\qquad
b_i=\sum_j\bar p_jH_{ij}\frac{e^{W_j}-1}{W_j},
\tag{3.11}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\le
\sum_i\bar p_i(e^{b_i}-1).
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

This has the same deterministic form as the negative-association theorem, but it now applies to any latent law satisfying the sub-Poisson Laplace domination

\[
\log \mathbb E e^{\langle h,S\rangle}
\le
\sum_i\bar p_i(e^{h_i}-1).
\tag{3.13}
\]

If row and column sums of `H` are at most `W`, let `rho=max_i \bar p_i` and `A=e^W-1`. Then exactly as in the previous scalar reduction,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\le
\frac{A}{W}e^{A\rho}\,\bar p^TH\bar p.
}
\tag{3.14}
\]

and for a common upper intensity `\bar p_i=\bar p`,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\mu(H)
\le
n\bar p^2 A e^{A\bar p}.
}
\tag{3.15}
\]

Thus the quadratic replica-collision scale follows from a one-replica sub-Poisson property, not from independence or negative association per se.

## 3.3 A finite local sufficient condition: sequential conditional occupancy caps

The Laplace hypothesis is directly certifiable from conditional probabilities. Fix an ordering of the latent coordinates and assume

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(S_i=1\mid S_1,\dots,S_{i-1})
\le \bar p_i
\quad\text{a.s. for every }i.
}
\tag{3.16}
\]

For `h_i>=0`, conditional expectation gives

\[
\mathbb E[e^{h_iS_i}\mid S_1,\dots,S_{i-1}]
\le
1+\bar p_i(e^{h_i}-1).
\]

Iterating over coordinates,

\[
\mathbb E e^{\langle h,S\rangle}
\le
\prod_i[1+\bar p_i(e^{h_i}-1)]
\le
\exp\left\{\sum_i\bar p_i(e^{h_i}-1)\right\}.
\tag{3.17}
\]

Therefore (3.16) implies (3.13).

A stronger but often easier local check is

\[
\mathbb P(S_i=1\mid S_{-i}=s_{-i})\le\bar p_i
\quad\text{for every full boundary condition }s_{-i}.
\tag{3.18}
\]

By the tower property, averaging (3.18) over unobserved future coordinates gives the prefix bound (3.16) in any fixed ordering.

This admits sparse **dependent latent Gibbs supports**. For example, for a pairwise binary latent Gibbs law

\[
\mu(s)\propto
\exp\left\{
\sum_i\theta_is_i+
\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}s_is_j
\right\},
\tag{3.19}
\]

the full conditional log-odds at site `i` equal

\[
\theta_i+\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}s_j.
\]

Hence the explicit bound

\[
B_i:=\theta_i+\sum_{j:J_{ij}>0}J_{ij},
\qquad
\bar p_i:=\frac{e^{B_i}}{1+e^{B_i}}
\tag{3.20}
\]

verifies (3.18). Bounded-arity factor laws have the analogous certificate obtained by adding the maximal positive contribution of every incident factor. The bound may be crude, but it is local, finite, and directly checkable.

This is the main B3 source-generalization advance: the latent class now includes sparse attractive or mixed-sign dependent support laws whenever their worst-case occupancy remains sub-Poisson at the source scale. Negative association is sufficient but no longer necessary.

# 4. B23 — DO WHAT THE PROPOSER DID NOT EXPECT: log-Sobolev smoothing still does not force full TV

The previous framework proves a polynomial-indistinguishability implication. A tempting strengthening is: perhaps collision pressure plus source-degree transport plus a strong log-Sobolev/hypercontractive inequality forces **full noisy statistical indistinguishability**. Hsieh et al. obtain such a result in an important native product-noise setting, so it is worth testing whether the extra analytic smoothing property is the missing generic condition.

It is not.

## 4.1 Exact basis-misalignment counterexample

Let

\[
Q_n=\operatorname{Unif}(\{\pm1\}^n).
\]

Fix a set `R_n subset [n]` with

\[
m_n:=|R_n|>D_n.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Complete its incidence vector over `F_2` to a basis of `F_2^n`. Equivalently, choose an invertible binary matrix whose first row is the indicator of `R_n`. Define a measure-preserving Boolean linear change of variables

\[
y_1=\chi_{R_n}(x)=\prod_{i\in R_n}x_i,
\]

with `y_2,...,y_n` the remaining basis characters.

Now let `S_t` be ordinary independent coordinate-refresh noise **in the `y` coordinates** at rate one. Conjugating product noise by the invertible Boolean linear map gives a `Q_n`-reversible Markov semigroup on the original `x` cube. It has the same tensor-product log-Sobolev and hypercontractive constants as ordinary product noise, because it is unitarily conjugate to that chain.

Every `x`-Fourier character is also a `y`-Fourier character (the invertible linear map merely permutes the character basis). Therefore each `x` character is an eigenfunction of `S_t`. In particular, for ordinary source-degree projections in the `x` coordinates,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{\le d}S_t\Pi_{\ge k}=0
\qquad(k>d).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

So the semigroup has the strongest possible **off-diagonal no-downward-leakage** property.

Define the planted likelihood ratio

\[
L_n(x)=1+a\chi_{R_n}(x),
\qquad 0<a\le1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

It is nonnegative and has `E_Q L_n=1`. Since its nonconstant Fourier mass lies entirely at source degree `m_n>D_n`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)=0.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Its full collision pressure is constant:

\[
\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)=a^2,
\qquad
\Pi_{2,n}=\log(1+a^2)=O(1)=o(D_n).
\tag{4.5}
\]

But `chi_{R_n}=y_1` is a **degree-one eigenfunction in the noise coordinates**, so

\[
S_t\chi_{R_n}=e^{-t}\chi_{R_n}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The noised likelihood ratio is

\[
L_{n,t}=1+a e^{-t}\chi_{R_n},
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|P_nS_t-Q_n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\frac12\mathbb E_Q|a e^{-t}\chi_{R_n}|
=\frac{a e^{-t}}2.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

For every fixed `t>0`, this stays bounded away from zero.

### Consequence

Even the conjunction of

1. zero source LDA through `D_n`,
2. scalar collision pressure `Pi_2=o(D_n)`,
3. perfect off-diagonal source-degree transport (4.2), and
4. a dimension-free product log-Sobolev / hypercontractive inequality

does **not** imply full noisy total-variation indistinguishability.

This does not contradict Hsieh et al.: their Boolean statistical-indistinguishability theorem uses the **native coordinate resampling noise** together with strong permutation structure. The counterexample deliberately misaligns the noise energy basis with the source algebraic-degree basis.

The exact missing issue is not generic smoothing strength; it is **degree-energy alignment**.

## 4.2 A sufficient full-tail damping condition

The counterexample suggests the correct strengthening. Suppose, in addition to `Q`-reversibility and `L^2` contraction, that for some `kappa_t>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|S_t\Pi_{>D}\|_{2\to2}
\le e^{-\kappa_t(D+1)}
\quad\text{for every relevant }D.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is stronger than merely suppressing downward leakage: all high source-degree mass must actually decay at a rate proportional to source degree.

Let `f=dP/dQ-1`, and write

\[
f=f_{\le D}+f_{>D}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
2\|PS_t-Q\|_{TV}
&\le \|S_tf\|_2\\
&\le \|S_tf_{\le D}\|_2+\|S_tf_{>D}\|_2\\
&\le \|f_{\le D}\|_2+e^{-\kappa_t(D+1)}\|f\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Under the standard LDA convention,

\[
\|f_{\le D}\|_2=\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q),
\]

and if `Pi_2=log(1+chi^2(P||Q))`, then

\[
\|f\|_2=\sqrt{\chi^2(P\|Q)}\le e^{\Pi_2/2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|PS_t-Q\|_{TV}
\le
\frac12\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
+
\frac12\exp\{\Pi_2/2-\kappa_t(D+1)\}.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Therefore, if source LDA **vanishes** through `D_n`, `Pi_{2,n}=o(D_n)`, and (4.8) holds with a fixed positive `kappa_t`, then

\[
\|P_nS_t-Q_n\|_{TV}\to0.
\tag{4.11}
\]

This cleanly separates two tiers of the framework:

- bounded source LDA plus pressure plus a degree-transport gate yields the source's polynomial indistinguishability conclusion below a controlled degree loss;
- vanishing source LDA plus pressure plus **full source-degree damping** upgrades to total-variation statistical indistinguishability.

A generic log-Sobolev inequality does not imply the second condition because it is basis-blind. Native product resampling does satisfy exact degree-resolved damping. For nonproduct weak-Gibbs heat-bath dynamics, proving an analogue of (4.8) is a separate obligation from the earlier Kato/Toda tilted **leakage** estimates.

# 5. B29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY: an exchangeable large-deviation collision variational principle

The previous exact extremal theorem shows that at fixed one-site marginal, latent dependence can move collision pressure from the benign quadratic scale to volume scale. The natural asymptotic description is therefore not one-site marginals but the large-deviation cost of macroscopic latent support.

## 5.1 Exchangeable support law and size LDP

Let `mu_n` be exchangeable on subsets `S subset [n]`, and let

\[
K_n:=|S|,
\qquad
X_n:=K_n/n.
\]

Assume `X_n` satisfies a full large-deviation principle at speed `n` with good rate function `I:[0,1]->[0,infinity]`.

Conditional on `K_n=k`, exchangeability makes `S` uniform over all `k`-subsets. Let `S,T` be independent latent replicas and define

\[
x=|S|/n,
\qquad
y=|T|/n,
\qquad z=|S\cap T|/n.
\]

Conditioned on sizes `k,m`, the intersection size `ell` is hypergeometric:

\[
\mathbb P(|S\cap T|=\ell\mid |S|=k,|T|=m)
=
\frac{\binom{k}{\ell}\binom{n-k}{m-\ell}}
{\binom{n}{m}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
h(u):=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)
\]

with the usual continuous boundary convention. Stirling's formula gives the conditional rate

\[
\boxed{
J(z;x,y)
=
h(y)
-xh(z/x)
-(1-x)h((y-z)/(1-x)),
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

on

\[
\max(0,x+y-1)\le z\le\min(x,y),
\tag{5.3}
\]

and `J=+infinity` outside the feasible domain.

The joint triple `(x,y,z)` therefore has rate

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I(x,y,z)=I(x)+I(y)+J(z;x,y).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The statement follows by combining the independent size LDPs with the exact hypergeometric conditional law; boundary cases are obtained by continuity.

## 5.2 Replica collision free energy

For the product-null collision kernel

\[
K_c(S,T)=e^{c|S\cap T|},
\qquad c>0,
\]

the reward `cz` is bounded and continuous on the compact feasible domain. Varadhan's lemma gives

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
\log\mathbb E e^{c|S\cap T|}
=
\sup_{x,y,z}
\left\{
cz-I(x)-I(y)-J(z;x,y)
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Call the right-hand side `Lambda_I(c)`.

This is the macroscopic entropy-versus-collision criterion anticipated by the B11 Gibbs dual, now made explicit for exchangeable latent supports.

## 5.3 Calibrations

### iid Bernoulli support

If `S_i iid Bernoulli(p)`, then

\[
I(x)=D(x\|p).
\]

Independently, the exact finite-`n` formula is

\[
\mathbb E e^{c|S\cap T|}
=[1+p^2(e^c-1)]^n,
\]

so (5.5) reduces to

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_I(c)=\log[1+p^2(e^c-1)].
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This recovers the benign quadratic collision scale for small `p`.

### Fixed-size support

If `|S|=pn` deterministically (up to rounding), then `I(p)=0` and `I(x)=+infinity` for `x\ne p`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_I(c)
=
\sup_z\{cz-J(z;p,p)\}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This isolates the hypergeometric/negative-dependence collision cost directly.

### Empty/full heavy tail with constant mass

If `S=[n]` with fixed probability `p in (0,1)` and is empty otherwise, then both `x=0` and `x=1` have zero exponential rate. The point `x=y=z=1` gives

\[
\Lambda_I(c)=c,
\tag{5.8}
\]

matching the volume-order pressure in the exact B24 maximizer.

### Exponentially rare macroscopic support

If

\[
\mathbb P(S=[n])\asymp e^{-a n},
\qquad
\mathbb P(S=\varnothing)=1-e^{-a n}+o(e^{-an}),
\]

then `I(0)=0`, `I(1)=a`, and all other sizes have infinite rate in the simplest two-point model. Formula (5.5) yields

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_I(c)=(c-2a)_+.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This exactly matches the earlier heavy-tail calibration `epsilon_n^2=e^{-gamma n}` after setting `gamma=2a`.

## 5.4 Near-necessary macroscopic condition at polylogarithmic source scale

For the source scale

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\eta}=o(n),
\]

if

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_I(c)>0,
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

then the collision pressure is `Theta(n)` and cannot satisfy the source-framework pressure condition `Pi_{2,n}=o(D_n)`.

Therefore any source-scale theorem that permits exchangeable rare macroscopic supports must at minimum lie on the **zero collision-free-energy boundary**

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_I(c)=0.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

This condition is only macroscopic: `Lambda_I(c)=0` says the pressure is `o(n)`, not necessarily `o(D_n)`. Moderate-deviation or finite-size information is still required to reach the polylogarithmic source scale. That distinction is essential and is recorded rather than silently claiming necessity/sufficiency.

B29 therefore unifies three formerly separate phenomena:

- iid/sub-Poisson sparse supports: collision energy is paid quadratically and can be `o(D_n)`;
- fixed-size/negatively dependent supports: hypergeometric entropy suppresses collision;
- empty/full or rare macroscopic supports: pressure is controlled by the explicit competition `collision energy - two latent rate costs`.

The large-deviation machinery is classical; this report does not claim the variational formula as new statistical mechanics/probability theory.
