# 3. B28 — REPLACE “FOR ALL” WITH “FOR TYPICAL”

The previous run showed that the crude certificate

`log(1+chi2) <= log lambda_max(K)-H_2(pi)`

can be exponentially loose if the Perron mode is concentrated on rare latent states. The correct replacement is to control the overlap load on **prior-typical** latent states and pay an explicit diagonal tail penalty for the exceptional set.

## 3.1 Setup

Let

`P = sum_z pi_z P_z`

be a finite latent mixture, let `Q` be the null, and let `T` be any positivity-preserving `Q`-stationary Markov operator (for this lemma reversibility is not needed). Put

`L_z=dP_z/dQ`, `g_z=T L_z`, `g=sum_z pi_z g_z`.

Define the nonnegative Gram kernel

`K(z,z')=<g_z,g_z'>_Q`,

its prior row load

`r(z)=sum_z' pi_z' K(z,z')=<g_z,g>_Q`,

and

`S=pi^T K pi=||g||_2^2=1+chi2(TP||Q)`.

Let `G` be a good latent set, `B=G^c`, and

`delta=pi(B)`,

`J_B=sum_{z in B} pi_z K(z,z)`.

Suppose the typical rows obey

`r(z)<=R` for every `z in G`.

## 3.2 Typical-overlap truncation theorem

Then

\[
\boxed{
S\le \left(\sqrt R+\sqrt{\delta J_B}\right)^2.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

**Proof.** Split the exact quadratic form by its first latent coordinate:

`S=sum_{z in G} pi_z r(z)+sum_{z in B}pi_z r(z)`.

The good part is at most `R`. For the bad part, Cauchy–Schwarz in observation space gives

`r(z)=<g_z,g> <= ||g_z||_2 ||g||_2 = sqrt(K(z,z) S)`.

Therefore

`sum_{z in B}pi_z r(z) <= sqrt(S) sum_{z in B}pi_z sqrt(K(z,z))`.

A second Cauchy–Schwarz inequality over the latent index gives

`sum_{z in B}pi_z sqrt(K(z,z)) <= sqrt(delta J_B)`.

Hence

`S <= R + sqrt(delta J_B) sqrt(S)`.

Writing `x=sqrt(S)` and `a=sqrt(delta J_B)` gives `x^2<=R+ax`, so

`x <= (a+sqrt(a^2+4R))/2 <= a+sqrt(R)`.

Squaring proves (3.1). `QED`

## 3.3 D-scale pressure consequence

For a sequence with source cutoff `D_n`, if at one fixed reference noise level one can choose good sets `G_n` such that

\[
\log^+R_n=o(D_n),\qquad
\log^+(\delta_n J_{B_n})=o(D_n),
\tag{3.2}
\]

then

\[
\log(1+\chi_n^2)=o(D_n).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the one-reference pressure condition can be certified without bounding `lambda_max(K)` or every row.

This exactly repairs the rare-latent stress family from the preceding run. There the exceptional state has prior mass `delta=1/M`; its diagonal overlap is of order `M^2`, so `J_B` is of order `M`, but the product `delta J_B` remains order one. The good-state row load is order one. Formula (3.1) therefore sees the true bounded mixture chi-square even though the Perron-radius surrogate grows like `M` after the Rényi correction.

### Interpretation

The natural regularity is not “the Perron vector is delocalized” as an abstract spectral demand. A weaker and more model-facing condition is:

*most prior mass has controlled average overlap with the planted mixture, and the rare set carries only subexponential prior-weighted diagonal collision mass.*

This is a real weakening of max-row/Perron control and is robust to isolated high-overlap latent spikes.

# 4. B6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE

The goal was to start from an exactly transitive/product model where collision pressure is tractable and ask what quantitative perturbation size preserves zero reference pressure.

## 4.1 Kernel/prior deformation lemma

Let `(pi_0,K_0)` be a baseline latent model and `(pi,K)` a perturbed model on the same finite latent set. Suppose

\[
\pi_z\le e^{\xi}\pi_{0,z}\quad\text{for all }z,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
K(z,z')\le e^{\eta}K_0(z,z')\quad\text{for all }z,z'.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\pi^T K\pi\le e^{\eta+2\xi}\pi_0^T K_0\pi_0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is immediate by termwise comparison of the nonnegative double sum. Hence if the baseline has zero `D_n`-scale reference pressure and

`eta_n+2 xi_n=o(D_n)`, then the perturbed model also has zero reference pressure.

A useful observation-level sufficient condition is the following. Suppose the perturbed null and baseline null satisfy

`dQ/dQ_0 <= e^a`,

and at the chosen reference noise level the nonnegative noised likelihoods satisfy pointwise

`g_z <= e^b g_{0,z}` for every latent `z`.

Then

`K(z,z')<=e^{a+2b}K_0(z,z')`,

so (4.3) applies with `eta=a+2b`.

## 4.2 What this says — and what it does not say — about high-temperature Gibbs perturbations

This deformation result is exact but also exposes a limitation of the naive route. A fixed-strength bounded-degree Gibbs perturbation of a product law can have a **global** Radon–Nikodym oscillation of order `exp(Theta(n))`, because the Hamiltonian perturbation is extensive. At Kireeva’s source scale `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`, a global Holley–Stroock-style density-ratio comparison can therefore cost far more than `exp(o(D_n))` and is useless for reference pressure.

So B6 gives a genuine boundary:

*global measure comparison is too crude; a successful high-temperature extension must compare the specific noisy overlap kernel locally, or exploit strong spatial mixing/interface structure so that the log-distortion of the relevant overlaps is only `o(D_n)`.*

This aligns with Chen–Li’s successful mechanism: they do not globally compare the entire planted law to a product perturbation; they decompose large supports through small interfaces and let noise absorb the reassembly cost.

I do **not** claim here that generic Dobrushin uniqueness automatically gives `eta=o(D_n)`. Establishing such an overlap-local comparison for bounded-degree Gibbs/factor models remains open on this track.

# 5. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER

The source only asks for a rigorous polynomial-indistinguishability implication. The stronger target below rules out **every measurable strong distinguisher after a fixed reversible noise**, under explicit algebraic-pressure and source-degree transport hypotheses.

## 5.1 Source-algebraic setup

For each `n`, let `Q_n` be a finite null law and let

`L_n=dP_n/dQ_n`, `f_n=L_n-1`.

Let

`L^2_0(Q_n)=direct_sum_{j>=1} H_{n,j}`

be the ordinary algebraic source-degree decomposition, with orthogonal projections `Pi_{n,j}` and

`P_{n,<=D}=sum_{j<=D}Pi_{n,j}`.

Then the source low-degree advantage is

\[
A_n:=\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)
=\|P_{n,\le D_n}f_n\|_2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `v in (0,1)` define the **source-algebraic reference pressure norm**

\[
\mathcal F_n(v)
:=\sum_{j\ge1}v^{2j}\|\Pi_{n,j}f_n\|_2^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For product Boolean/Gaussian nulls this is exactly the noisy chi-square at the corresponding scalar degree-damping radius. For general nonproduct nulls it is a source-degree weighted pressure, not a claim that the degree-damping operator is itself a Markov noise.

Let `S_{n,t}=e^{-tA_n}` be a reversible `Q_n`-stationary Markov semigroup. Assume the degree blocks of `A_n` satisfy the already-proved signed-path hypotheses with constants independent of `n`:

1. diagonal coercivity at degree `k`: `a_{n,k}>=kappa k`;
2. total off-diagonal transition budget from degree `k`: `sum_{j!=k}c_{n,jk}<=beta k`;
3. downward bandwidth at most `w`;
4. `0<=beta<kappa`.

For fixed `t>0` define

\[
p=\beta/\kappa,
\qquad
R_t=\left[p+(1-p)e^{-w\kappa t}\right]^{1/w},
\tag{5.3}
\]

with the previously established `w=0` interpretation `R_t=e^{-\kappa t}`.

The earlier signed-path theorem gives, for every `D`,

\[
\|S_{n,t}f_n\|_2
\le A_n+\sum_{k>D}R_t^k\|\Pi_{n,k}f_n\|_2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

## 5.2 Unified transport-pressure theorem

Assume

`D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`,

`sup_n A_n <= A < infinity`,

and there is a fixed `v` satisfying

\[
R_t<v<1
\tag{5.5}
\]

such that

\[
\log^+(1+\mathcal F_n(v))=o(D_n).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|S_{n,t}f_n\|_2\le A_n+o(1).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_nS_{n,t}\|Q_n)\le (A+o(1))^2.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

In particular no sequence of **measurable** tests can strongly distinguish `P_nS_{n,t}` from `Q_n`. If `A_n->0`, then

\[
\chi^2(P_nS_{n,t}\|Q_n)\to0,
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(P_nS_{n,t},Q_n)\to0.
\tag{5.9}
\]

### Proof

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the high-degree term in (5.4):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k>D_n}R_t^k\|\Pi_{n,k}f_n\|_2
&=\sum_{k>D_n}(R_t/v)^k\,v^k\|\Pi_{n,k}f_n\|_2\\
&\le
\left(\sum_{k>D_n}(R_t/v)^{2k}\right)^{1/2}
\mathcal F_n(v)^{1/2}\\
&=\frac{(R_t/v)^{D_n+1}}{\sqrt{1-(R_t/v)^2}}\,\mathcal F_n(v)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Because `R_t/v<1` is fixed and `log^+(1+F_n(v))=o(D_n)`, the right side is `o(1)`. This proves (5.7) and (5.8).

If measurable tests strongly distinguished, an event-level coarse graining would have type-I error tending to zero and power tending to one; the binary coarse-grained chi-square would then diverge. This contradicts the uniform bound (5.8). If `A_n->0`, (5.7) gives chi-square convergence to zero, and `TV<=sqrt(chi2)/2` gives (5.9). `QED`

The hypotheses are not equivalent to the desired conclusion:

* bounded source LDA is exactly Kireeva’s input quantity;
