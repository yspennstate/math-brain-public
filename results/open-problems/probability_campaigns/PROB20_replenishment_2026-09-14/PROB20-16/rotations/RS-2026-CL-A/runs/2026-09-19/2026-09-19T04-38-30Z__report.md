## 3.1 Exact posterior reduction

Let `Q` be any null measure on a finite observation space. Let positive baseline component weights `w_z^(0)(x)`, positive constants `c_z`, and perturbations `U_z(x)` satisfy

\[
w_z(x)=w_z^{(0)}(x)e^{U_z(x)}.
\tag{3.1}

\[
\boxed{

}
\quad\text{for every }x,
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `C_0>0` is independent of `x`. Define the baseline posterior

\[
\boxed{
q_x(z):=\frac{c_z w_z^{(0)}(x)}{C_0}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then the aggregate unnormalized likelihood is

\[
W(x)=\sum_z c_z w_z(x)

\tag{3.4}

Under the same normalizer-balanced prior from the previous report, the mixture likelihood is `L=W/E_Q W`. Therefore only

\[
F(x):=\log \mathbb E_{q_x}e^{U_z(x)}
\tag{3.5}

Let

\[
m(x):=\mathbb E_{q_x}U_z(x),
\qquad
R(x):=\log\mathbb E_{q_x}e^{U_z(x)-m(x)}.
\tag{3.6}

Then exactly

\[
F(x)=m(x)+R(x),
\tag{3.7}

and Jensen gives `R(x)>=0`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{osc}F
\le
\operatorname{osc}m+\sup_x R(x).
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Since the previous aggregate argument gives

\[
\log(1+\chi^2(P\|Q))\le\operatorname{osc}F,
\tag{3.9}

we obtain the first posterior-typical theorem.

## 3.2 A checkable exponential-tail corollary

\[

Suppose there exist `a_n>=0`, fixed `C<infty`, and fixed `gamma>1` such that uniformly in observations `x`,

\boxed{

\le C e^{-\gamma r}
\qquad(r\ge0).

\tag{3.13}

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{q_x}e^{Y_x}
&\le e^{a_n}

\left(1+C+\frac{C}{\gamma-1}\right)\\

\left(1+\frac{C\gamma}{\gamma-1}\right).

\tag{3.14}

Therefore

\[
\boxed{

\[

\operatorname{osc}m=o(D_n),

## 4.2 Gibbs variational formula inside each observation

\tag{4.7}

Then directly

\[
\boxed{
\max_r\{\ell_r(x)-h_r(x)\}

\tag{4.8}
