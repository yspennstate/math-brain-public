# 3. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE IN IT

The previous report left a concrete transport obligation: for an overlapping high-temperature Gibbs null, can one show the off-degree budget `beta` is smaller than the diagonal coercivity `kappa`? The first derivative at the product point is decisive. In the zero-field pairwise Ising model, the dangerous cross-degree term cancels **exactly to first order** once degree is interpreted in Kireeva's ordinary algebraic filtration with the correct `Q_alpha` orthogonal layers.

## 3.1 Setup

Fix a finite graph `G=(V,E)` and symmetric couplings `J_{ij}=J_{ji}`, zero off edges and zero diagonal. For `alpha` near zero define

\[
Q_\alpha(x)=Z_\alpha^{-1}
\exp\!\left(\alpha H(x)\right),
\qquad
H(x)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}J_{ij}x_ix_j,
\qquad x\in\{-1,+1\}^V.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `A_alpha` be the positive continuous-time single-site heat-bath generator

\[
A_\alpha=\sum_{i\in V}(I-E_i^\alpha),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `E_i^alpha` is conditional expectation over spin `i` given all other spins. `A_alpha` is self-adjoint and nonnegative in `L^2(Q_alpha)`.

Let `V_{<=k}` be the **ordinary algebraic polynomial space** of degree at most `k`, independent of `alpha`, and let

\[
U_{\alpha,k}=V_{\le k}\cap V_{\le k-1}^{\perp_{Q_\alpha}}
\tag{3.3}
\]

be the corresponding orthogonal source-degree layer. Write `Pi_{alpha,k}` for its orthogonal projector. At `alpha=0`, `Q_0` is uniform, the Walsh monomials

\[
\chi_S(x)=\prod_{i\in S}x_i
\]

are orthonormal, `U_{0,k}=span{chi_S:|S|=k}`, and

\[
A_0\chi_S=|S|\chi_S.
\tag{3.4}
\]

## 3.2 First-order orthogonalization of an ordinary monomial

Fix `S` with `|S|=k`. For every `T` with `|T|<=k-1`,

\[
\left.\frac d{d\alpha}\langle\chi_S,\chi_T\rangle_{Q_\alpha}\right|_{\alpha=0}
=
E_0[\chi_{S\triangle T}H].
\tag{3.5}
\]

The right side is nonzero exactly when `S triangle T` is an edge. Because `|T|<=k-1`, this forces that edge `e` to be contained in `S` and `T=S\setminus e`. Hence an analytic vector in `U_{alpha,k}` with leading term `chi_S` has the expansion

\[
\boxed{
u_S(\alpha)
=
\chi_S-
\alpha\sum_{e\in E:e\subseteq S}J_e\chi_{S\setminus e}
+O_G(\alpha^2).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The error is in any fixed finite-dimensional norm; all norms are equivalent for fixed `G`.

This is the key point: the source-degree layer itself rotates at first order. Looking only at the fixed product Walsh basis creates an apparent degree-drop term that is not a genuine first-order cross-*source-degree* coupling.

## 3.3 First-order action of the heat-bath generator

For `i in S`, the Ising conditional mean is

\[
m_i^\alpha(x_{-i})
=E_{Q_\alpha}[x_i\mid x_{-i}]
=
\tanh\!\left(\alpha\sum_jJ_{ij}x_j\right)
=
\alpha\sum_jJ_{ij}x_j+O_G(\alpha^3).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_\alpha\chi_S
&=
\sum_{i\in S}\left(\chi_S-\chi_{S\setminus\{i\}}m_i^\alpha\right)\\
&=
k\chi_S
-
\alpha\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\j\notin S}}J_{ij}
\chi_{S\setminus\{i\}\cup\{j\}}
-
2\alpha\sum_{e\subseteq S}J_e\chi_{S\setminus e}
+O_G(\alpha^3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]

The internal edge term is doubled because each internal edge can be updated from either endpoint.

Applying `A_alpha` to (3.6), the explicit correction is already multiplied by `alpha`, so to first order we may use `A_0 chi_{S\setminus e}=(k-2)chi_{S\setminus e}`. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_\alpha\nu_S(\alpha)
&=
k\chi_S
-
\alpha\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\j\notin S}}J_{ij}
\chi_{S-i+j}\\
&\qquad
-
\alpha k\sum_{e\subseteq S}J_e\chi_{S\setminus e}
+O_G(\alpha^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Using (3.6) again, and replacing each same-degree `chi_{S-i+j}` by `nu_{S-i+j}(alpha)` at an additional `O_G(alpha^2)` cost,

\[
\boxed{
A_\alpha\nu_S(\alpha)
=
k\nu_S(\alpha)
-
\alpha\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\j\notin S}}J_{ij}
\nu_{S-i+j}(\alpha)
+O_G(\alpha^2).
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

The entire order-`alpha` term stays **inside source degree `k`**.

## 3.4 Infinitesimal source-degree cancellation theorem

Equation (3.10) gives, for every fixed finite graph and every `j!=k`,

\[
\boxed{
\left.\frac d{d\alpha}
\left(\Pi_{\alpha,j}A_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha,k}\right)
\right|_{\alpha=0}
=0.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

So zero-field pairwise Ising heat-bath dynamics has **no first-order cross-source-degree mixing at the product point**.

The first-order restriction inside degree `k` is the weighted exclusion-hopping operator

\[
(T_k f)(S)=
\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\j\notin S}}J_{ij}f(S-i+j).
\tag{3.12}
\]

If

\[
J_{\rm row}=\max_i\sum_j|J_{ij}|,
\tag{3.13}
\]

then the absolute row sum of `T_k` is at most `k J_row`, hence

\[
\|T_k\|_{2\to2}\le kJ_{\rm row}.
\tag{3.14}
\]

Consequently the degree-`k` diagonal coercivity satisfies, for fixed finite `G`,

\[
\frac1k\lambda_{\min}
\left(\Pi_{\alpha,k}A_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha,k}\right)
\ge
1-|\alpha|J_{\rm row}+O_G(\alpha^2).
\tag{3.15}
\]

Define the finite-volume normalized cross-degree row budget

\[
\beta_G(\alpha)=
\sup_{k\ge1}\frac1k
\sum_{j\ne k}
\|\Pi_{\alpha,j}A_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha,k}\|.
\tag{3.16}
\]

Finite-dimensional analyticity and (3.11) give

\[
\boxed{
\beta_G(\alpha)=O_G(\alpha^2),
\qquad
\kappa_G(\alpha)
\ge1-|\alpha|J_{\rm row}+O_G(\alpha^2).
}
\tag{3.17}
\]

Thus every fixed finite graph has a nontrivial high-temperature interval around the product point in which `beta_G(alpha)<kappa_G(alpha)`.

### Why this is real movement but not yet the broad Gibbs theorem

The crucial missing uniformity is explicit in the notation: the second-order constant in `O_G(alpha^2)` may grow with the graph. The next task is to prove, under bounded degree / small interaction, a **uniform** estimate such as

\[
\beta_n(\alpha)\le C(\Delta,J_*)\alpha^2,
\qquad
\kappa_n(\alpha)\ge1-C'(\Delta,J_*)|\alpha|,
\tag{3.18}
\]

with constants independent of `n`. A connected high-temperature/cluster or Gram expansion is the natural route. I do not claim (3.18) in this run.

This theorem also retrospectively explains the exact three-spin computation from earlier runs: the pairwise zero-field/biased-block source-degree leakage begins quadratically in the weak interaction parameter rather than linearly after the source-degree layer rotation is accounted for.

# 4. B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING

The B20 cancellation example from the previous run shows that componentwise pressure can be exponentially misleading. Transitive latent symmetry gives an exact replacement: compute the overlap **by orbit shell**, then quantify how much weak symmetry breaking is allowed.

## 4.1 Exact association-shell pressure theorem

Let a finite group `G` act transitively on a finite latent set `Z`, `|Z|=M`, and let the baseline prior be uniform. Let the diagonal action of `G` on `Z x Z` have orbitals (relations) `R_r`. For every fixed `z`, let

\[
v_r=|\{z':(z,z')\in R_r\}|,
\tag{4.1}
\]

which is independent of `z` by transitivity.

Suppose a nonnegative overlap kernel is invariant and takes the value `kappa_r` on `R_r`:

\[
K_0(z,z')=\kappa_r\quad\text{if }(z,z')\in R_r.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then every prior row load is equal, and the exact collision mass is

\[
\boxed{
S_0
=
\frac1M\sum_rv_r\kappa_r.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is elementary orbit counting, and in the Schurian case it is standard association-scheme structure. The inference use is that an exponentially large latent space is reduced to a one-dimensional or low-dimensional shell sum.

## 4.2 Weak symmetry breaking

Now allow a perturbed prior and overlap kernel. Assume

\[
\pi(z)\le \frac{e^\xi}{M}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and, whenever `(z,z') in R_r`,

\[
K(z,z')\le e^{\eta_r}\kappa_r.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then every prior row load satisfies

\[
\boxed{
r(z)
=
\sum_{z'}\pi(z')K(z,z')
\le
\frac{e^\xi}{M}
\sum_rv_r e^{\eta_r}\kappa_r.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently a direct model-level sufficient condition for zero `D_n`-scale row-load pressure is

\[
\boxed{
\xi_n+
\log\!\left[
\frac1{M_n}
\sum_rv_{n,r}e^{\eta_{n,r}}\kappa_{n,r}
\right]
=o(D_n).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is stronger than a global `sup eta_r=o(D)` requirement because expensive distortion is allowed on shells carrying negligible baseline overlap mass.

## 4.3 Hamming shell: the exact B20 cancellation becomes a binomial identity

Take `Z={-1,+1}^m`, with the Hamming association scheme. Relation `d` is Hamming distance `d_H(z,z')` and

\[
v_d={m\choose d}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

For the exact product-null family from B20, after product noise with one-coordinate Fourier multiplier `rho`, put

\[
a=\rho^2\tanh^2h\in[0,1).
\tag{4.9}
\]

The overlap between two latent signs at Hamming distance `d` is

\[
\kappa_d=(1+a)^{m-d}(1-a)^d.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Therefore (4.3) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_0
&=2^{-m}\sum_{d=0}^m{m\choose d}
(1+a)^{m-d}(1-a)^d\\
&=2^{-m}\bigl[(1+a)+(1-a)\bigr]^m\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.11}
\]

So the earlier “latent cancellation” is exactly an association-scheme shell identity. It is not mysterious cancellation that a local theorem must somehow guess; it is a computable orbit pressure.

## 4.4 Johnson shell: a directly relevant planted-subset template

Take `Z={S subset [n]:|S|=k}` with the natural symmetric-group action. Parameterize the Johnson relations by

\[
d=k-|S\cap S'|.
\tag{4.12}
\]

The valency is

\[
\boxed{
v_d={k\choose d}{n-k\choose d}.}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Hence every permutation-invariant two-copy overlap kernel depending only on intersection size reduces exactly to

\[
\boxed{
S_0
={1\over {n\choose k}}
\sum_{d=0}^k
{k\choose d}{n-k\choose d}\kappa_d.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

This is a concrete, polynomial-description shell pressure for planted-subset models. Weak nontransitivity can be handled by (4.6) if its shellwise overlap distortion is controlled.

# 5. B22 — READ THE FAILURES, NOT THE SUCCESSES

The previous B6 calculation showed why global Gibbs density-ratio comparison fails: a fixed-strength perturbation can cost `exp(Theta(n))`, hopeless against a cutoff `D_n=polylog(n)`. The literature's useful mechanism is not global comparison but **connected-cluster locality**. The following abstract lemma isolates exactly the interface hypothesis needed.

## 5.1 Abstract convergent-cluster interface lemma

Suppose two finite-volume specifications `theta` and `theta_0` admit absolutely convergent connected-cluster expansions

\[
\log Z_\theta=\sum_C\Phi_\theta(C),
\qquad
\log Z_{\theta_0}=\sum_C\Phi_{\theta_0}(C).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Assume they coincide away from a primitive interface set `I`, so every cluster disjoint from `I` has the same contribution:

\[
\Phi_\theta(C)=\Phi_{\theta_0}(C)
\quad\text{if }\operatorname{supp}(C)\cap I=\varnothing.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Assume also the rooted absolute cluster sums are uniformly bounded:

\[
\sup_a\sum_{C:a\in\operatorname{supp}(C)}|\Phi_\theta(C)|\le C_{\rm ce},
\qquad
\sup_a\sum_{C:a\in\operatorname{supp}(C)}|\Phi_{\theta_0}(C)|\le C_{\rm ce}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then only clusters meeting `I` survive in the difference, and a union bound over a root in `I` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\log{Z_\theta\over Z_{\theta_0}}\right|
&\le
\sum_{C:C\cap I\ne\varnothing}
(|\Phi_\theta(C)|+|\Phi_{\theta_0}(C)|)\\
&\le
\boxed{2C_{\rm ce}|I|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is a standard cluster-expansion principle written in the exact form needed here; the mechanism is classical and is **not** claimed novel.

## 5.2 From interface ratios to overlap-shell pressure

Suppose a two-copy overlap kernel is represented by a product/ratio of at most `m` partition functions, with `m=O(1)`, and relative to a solvable symmetric baseline all modified specifications differ only on an interface `I(z,z')`. Applying (5.4) termwise yields

\[
\boxed{
K(z,z')
\le
K_0(z,z')
\exp\{2mC_{\rm ce}|I(z,z')|\}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

If the baseline latent action has shell `r` with valency `v_r`, overlap `kappa_r`, and interface size bounded by `I_r`, B14 gives

\[
\boxed{
r(z)
\le
{e^\xi\over M}
\sum_rv_r\kappa_r
\exp\{2mC_{\rm ce}I_r\}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence the explicit source-scale condition

\[
\boxed{
\xi_n+
\log\!\left[
{1\over M_n}
\sum_rv_{n,r}\kappa_{n,r}
\exp\{cI_{n,r}\}
\right]
=o(D_n),
\qquad c=2mC_{\rm ce},
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

forces zero `D_n`-scale prior-row reference pressure.

The form is important: large interfaces are allowed provided the baseline shell weight/overlap suppresses them strongly enough. A worst-case interface bound is unnecessary.

## 5.3 The precise failure modes

This run's literature-first question was: why do standard high-temperature comparison tools not already prove what we need? The answer splits into three distinct failures.

**Failure 1: global Holley–Stroock comparison pays volume.** If two Hamiltonians differ by fixed-strength local interactions across `Theta(n)` factors, the oscillation of the full Hamiltonian difference is `Theta(n)`. A global density-ratio comparison therefore pays `exp(Theta(n))`, vastly larger than `exp(o(D_n))` when `D_n=polylog(n)`.

**Failure 2: strong spatial mixing alone is local-marginal control, not a free-energy cancellation theorem.** SSM says distant boundary changes have rapidly decaying influence on a local observable. If the *bulk* overlap Hamiltonian itself differs at `Theta(n)` sites, summing local effects may still cost `Theta(n)`. SSM is useful only after one identifies a representation in which the two-copy overlap models agree through the bulk and differ near a small interface, or after a symmetry cancellation reduces the bulk contribution.

**Failure 3: cluster expansion only buys interface size when bulk terms really cancel.** Equation (5.4) is cheap because cluster weights away from `I` agree exactly. For an extensive latent tilt without such agreement, `I` is volume-sized and the bound reverts to the useless global scale.

Thus the missing structural hypothesis for the broad Gibbs route is now explicit:

> **Two-copy bulk cancellation/local agreement + a uniform rooted cluster bound + subexponential interface-shell pressure.**

This is materially sharper than the earlier vague instruction “use high temperature.” High temperature supplies convergence/locality; it does not by itself supply the cancellation needed at Kireeva's polylogarithmic source scale.
