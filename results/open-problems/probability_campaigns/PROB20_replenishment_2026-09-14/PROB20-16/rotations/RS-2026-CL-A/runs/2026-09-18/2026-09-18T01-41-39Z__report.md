## 4. B5 — add a parameter and differentiate: the pressure/barycenter criterion

Fix `q in (0,1]`. Normalize the noised spectral energy into a probability law on degrees:

\[
\pi_q(j):=\frac{b_jq^j}{F(q)},
\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,M.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let `J_q` denote a random variable with law `pi_q`, and define its mean

\[
\mu(q):=\mathbb E_{\pi_q}J_q
=\frac{qF'(q)}{F(q)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently, introduce the **noise pressure**

\[
\Psi(t):=\log F(e^{-t}),
\qquad t\ge0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then direct differentiation gives the exact identities

\[
\boxed{
-\Psi'(t)=\mu(e^{-t}),
\qquad
\Psi''(t)=\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{e^{-t}}}(J).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus `mu(q)` is the pressure elasticity / tilted spectral barycenter, and the pressure curvature is exactly the tilted spectral variance.

### Theorem 4.1 — factorization-free pressure-barycenter implication

For every finite graded pair `(P,Q)`, every integer `D>=0`, every `q in (0,1]`, and every `theta in [0,1)`, if

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)\le \theta(D+1),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P\|Q)
\le
\frac{1+q\,\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2}{1-\theta}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently, if a sequence has source-scale `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`, bounded source low-degree advantage

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\le A,
\]

and for some fixed `q>0` and `theta<1`

\[
\sup_n\frac{\mu_n(q)}{D_n+1}\le\theta,
\tag{4.7}
\]

then the noised sequence has uniformly bounded chi-square divergence and therefore a uniform total-variation gap from one against **every measurable test**, a fortiori against every polynomial-degree class.

#### Proof

Write

\[
L_D(q):=\sum_{j=0}^{D}b_jq^j,
\qquad
T_D(q):=\sum_{j>D}b_jq^j,
\qquad
F(q)=L_D(q)+T_D(q).
\]

Because every `j>D` satisfies `j>=D+1`,

\[
(D+1)T_D(q)
\le
\sum_{j>D}j b_jq^j
\le
\sum_{j\ge0}j b_jq^j
=\mu(q)F(q).
\]

Under (4.5), `T_D(q)<=theta F(q)`. Hence

\[
(1-\theta)F(q)\le L_D(q).
\]

For `j>=1` and `0<q<=1`, `q^j<=q`; using (3.1),

\[
L_D(q)
=1+\sum_{j=1}^{D}b_jq^j
\le
1+q\sum_{j=1}^{D}b_j
=1+q\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2.
\]

Combining the two inequalities and (3.2) proves (4.6). `□`

### Explicit testing gap

If the right-hand side of (4.6) is at most `M`, then for the noised likelihood ratio `L_q`, `E_Q L_q=1` and `E_Q L_q^2<=M`. Paley–Zygmund at threshold `1/2` gives

\[
Q(L_q\ge1/2)\ge\frac{1}{4M}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\int\min(dT_{\sqrt q}P,dQ)
=E_Q\min(L_q,1)
\ge\frac1{8M},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|T_{\sqrt q}P-Q\|_{\rm TV}
\le
1-\frac{1-\theta}{8(1+q\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2)}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

If both the low-degree advantage and `mu(q)/(D+1)` tend to zero, then (4.6) gives `chi^2->0`, hence total variation tends to zero.

### Curvature form

Because `mu(e^{-t})->0` as `t->infinity`, (4.4) also gives

\[
\boxed{
\mu(e^{-t})
=\int_t^{\infty}\Psi''(s)\,ds.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

So a sufficient factorization-free source condition is an integrated pressure-curvature budget strictly below the cutoff:

\[
\int_{t_0}^{\infty}\Psi_n''(s)\,ds
\le\theta(D_n+1),
\qquad \theta<1.
\tag{4.10}
\]

The identity itself is elementary/noise-spectral folklore; the useful point for this track is that the source implication can be stated without a factorization, positivity representation, leverage envelope, or model-specific graph decomposition.

## 6. B14 — find the group acting: exact orbit-overlap formula for forced supports

The second route asks whether a natural symmetry smaller than full coordinate permutation can make the pressure condition checkable.

Let `E` be a finite coordinate set, let the null be product Bernoulli

\[
Q_p=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)^{\otimes E},
\qquad
\lambda:=\frac{1-p}{p},
\]

and let a random support `A` be chosen from some law `nu`; conditional on `A`, force every coordinate in `A` to equal one and draw all remaining coordinates from the null.

With the standardized product basis, the likelihood-ratio Fourier coefficient is

\[
\widehat L(S)
=\lambda^{|S|/2}\Pr(S\subseteq A).
\tag{6.1}
\]

For independent `A,A'~nu`, let

\[
Z:=|A\cap A'|.
\]

Then the exact degree energies are

\[
\boxed{
b_j
=\lambda^j\mathbb E\binom Zj.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\sum_{|S|=j}\Pr(S\subseteq A)^2
=\mathbb E\sum_{|S|=j}\mathbf 1_{\{S\subseteq A\cap A'\}}
=\mathbb E\binom Zj.
\]

If `nu` is a uniform group orbit of a fixed support, the right side reduces the spectral problem to orbit-intersection counting. This is an exact general bridge; it does not require full coordinate-permutation symmetry.

## 7. B14 breadth test — planted k-partite perfect hypermatching

Fix an integer `k>=3`. The ambient coordinates are the `n^k` cells of `[n]^k`. Let the null be

\[
Q_{n,p_n}=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_n)^{\otimes[n]^k}.
\]

Under the planted law, choose independent uniform permutations

\[
\pi_2,\ldots,\pi_k\in S_n
\]

and force to one the `n` coordinates

\[
A_{\pi}
:=\{(i,\pi_2(i),\ldots,\pi_k(i)):i\in[n]\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently, this is the orbit of the diagonal perfect `k`-partite hypermatching under the product-coordinate action. The law is highly symmetric under the natural product group, but **not** invariant under all permutations of the `n^k` Boolean coordinates. Hence Hsieh et al.'s full-coordinate-permutation Boolean theorem does not directly subsume this example. It is also a `k`-uniform hypergraph model rather than Chen–Li's planted ordinary graph setting.

Let

\[
\lambda_n:=\frac{1-p_n}{p_n}.
\]

### Lemma 7.1 — exact common-hyperedge factorial moments

For two independent planted hypermatchings `A,A'`, let `Z_n=|A\cap A'|`. Then for every `0<=j<=n`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\binom{Z_n}{j}
=\frac{1}{j!(n)_j^{k-2}},
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

where `(n)_j=n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)`.

#### Proof

A specified `j`-set of first-coordinate labels is common to both hypermatchings exactly when each of the `k-1` independent relative permutations fixes all `j` labels. A uniform permutation fixes a specified `j`-set pointwise with probability

\[
\frac{(n-j)!}{n!}=\frac1{(n)_j}.
\]

The `k-1` relative permutations are independent, so the probability is `(n)_j^{-(k-1)}`. Summing over the `binom(n,j)=(n)_j/j!` possible label sets gives (7.2). `□`

Combining (6.2) and (7.2) yields the exact spectral polynomial

\[
\boxed{
F_n(z)
=\sum_{j=0}^{n}
\frac{(\lambda_n z)^j}
{j!(n)_j^{k-2}}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
b_{n,1}=\frac{\lambda_n}{n^{k-2}}.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus any bounded low-degree advantage through any `D>=1` already forces the natural signal parameter

\[
c_n:=\frac{\lambda_n}{n^{k-2}}
\]

to remain bounded.

### Theorem 7.2 — exact asymptotic noised chi-square for the hypermatching orbit

Fix `k>=3`, let `c_n=lambda_n/n^{k-2}`, and suppose `c_n->c<infinity`. Then for every fixed `q in [0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
F_n(q)\longrightarrow e^{qc},
\qquad
\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P_n\|Q_n)\longrightarrow e^{qc}-1.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

More generally, if `sup_n c_n<=C`, then `sup_n F_n(q)<infinity` for every fixed `q in [0,1]`; in fact the sequence is uniformly bounded even at `q=1`.

#### Proof

Set `m:=k-2>=1`. By (7.3),

\[
u_{n,j}:=
\frac{(q\lambda_n)^j}{j!(n)_j^m}
=
\frac{(qc_n)^j}{j!}
\left(\frac{n^j}{(n)_j}\right)^m.
\tag{7.6}
\]

For each fixed `j`, `n^j/(n)_j->1`, so `u_{n,j}->(qc)^j/j!`.

For `j<=n/2`, `(n)_j>=(n/2)^j`, hence

\[
0\le u_{n,j}
\le
\frac{(qC2^m)^j}{j!}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

This is a summable Poisson envelope.

It remains to control `j>n/2`. The consecutive ratio is

\[
R_{n,j}:=\frac{u_{n,j+1}}{u_{n,j}}
=
\frac{q c_n n^m}{(j+1)(n-j)^m}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

For `j>=ceil(n/2)`, the ratios are nondecreasing because

\[
\frac{R_{n,j+1}}{R_{n,j}}
=
\frac{j+1}{j+2}
\left(\frac{n-j}{n-j-1}\right)^m
\ge1
\]

for all sufficiently large `n` (and directly for the integer range where the expression is defined), using `m>=1` and `j>=n/2`. Hence the positive sequence `u_{n,j}` is log-convex on the upper half, so its maximum there occurs at an endpoint.

At `j_0=ceil(n/2)`, (7.7) gives

\[
u_{n,j_0}
\le
\frac{(qC2^m)^{j_0}}{j_0!}
\to0
\]

superexponentially. At the other endpoint,

\[
u_{n,n}
=\frac{(q c_n n^m)^n}{(n!)^{m+1}}
\le
\left(\frac{qC e^{m+1}}{n}\right)^n
\to0,
\tag{7.9}
\]

where `n!>=(n/e)^n` was used. Therefore

\[
\sum_{j>n/2}u_{n,j}
\le
n\max\{u_{n,j_0},u_{n,n}\}
\to0.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Dominated convergence on the lower half using (7.7), plus (7.10), yields

\[
F_n(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}u_{n,j}
\to
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(qc)^j}{j!}
=e^{qc}.
\]

The same bounds, without assuming convergence of `c_n`, give uniform boundedness when `c_n<=C`. `□`

## 9. Failed routes and limits exposed in this run

### 9.1 Pressure curvature alone is not yet a recognizable distributional hypothesis

But integrating this differential inequality from `q=0` does not by itself control the mean at a fixed `q` when the first nonzero spectral degree is strictly below `R`; the boundary condition at zero is too weak. This route did not replace the primitive-activity or barycenter conditions.

### 9.3 The hypermatching model is breadth, not the unrestricted answer

The exact orbit calculation uses a positive forced-support representation and a very structured product-group orbit.
