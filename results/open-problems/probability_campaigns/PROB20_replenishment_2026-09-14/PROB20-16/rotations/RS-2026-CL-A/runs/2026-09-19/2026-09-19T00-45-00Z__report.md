# 3. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE: freeze the moving source flag exactly

The preceding report found that the source-degree flag itself can move at norm `Theta(sqrt(n))`, even though the actual generator's cross-source-degree leakage in zero-field pairwise Ising begins only at cubic order. Bounding the flag motion separately is therefore the wrong object. The correct move is to choose a gauge in which the source flag does not move at all.

## 3.1 Simultaneous Kato gauge for the whole source-degree resolution

After any smooth unitary identification of the varying spaces `L^2(Q_alpha)` with one fixed Hilbert space, let

\[
I=\sum_{k=0}^n \Pi_k(\alpha)
\]

be the orthogonal resolution into the ordinary algebraic source layers. Define

\[
K_\alpha:=\sum_{k=0}^n \Pi_k'(\alpha)\Pi_k(\alpha).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because differentiating `Pi_k^2=Pi_k` gives

\[
\Pi_k'\Pi_k+\Pi_k\Pi_k'=\Pi_k',
\]

summing over `k` yields

\[
K_\alpha+K_\alpha^*=0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus `K_alpha` is skew-adjoint. For each `j`, orthogonality of the resolution gives

\[
[K_\alpha,\Pi_j(\alpha)]=\Pi_j'(\alpha).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed `K Pi_j=Pi_j'Pi_j`, while differentiating `Pi_j Pi_k=0` for `k!=j` gives

\[
\Pi_jK=-\Pi_j'(I-\Pi_j).
\]

Let `V_alpha` solve

\[
V_\alpha'=-V_\alpha K_\alpha,\qquad V_0=I.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then `V_alpha` is unitary and

\[
\frac d{d\alpha}\left(V_\alpha\Pi_j(\alpha)V_\alpha^*\right)=0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{V_\alpha\Pi_j(\alpha)V_\alpha^*=\Pi_j(0)\quad\text{for every }j.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular, for the source-number operator

\[
N_\alpha=\sum_j j\Pi_j(\alpha),
\]

we get the exact frozen gauge

\[
\boxed{V_\alpha N_\alpha V_\alpha^*=N_0.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the right response to the prior `Theta(sqrt(n))` flag-motion obstruction: one does not try to bound `N_alpha'`; one parallel-transports the whole orthogonal resolution.

## 3.2 Generator in the frozen-source gauge

Put

\widehat A_\alpha:=V_\alpha A_\alpha V_\alpha^*.
\tag{3.7}

\[
\Pi_j(0)\widehat A_\alpha\Pi_k(0)
=V_\alpha\Pi_j(\alpha)A_\alpha\Pi_k(\alpha)V_\alpha^*.
\tag{3.8}
\]

So all previously proved source-block statements transfer without loss to the fixed decomposition.

Differentiating (3.7) gives

At the product point of the zero-field pairwise Ising deformation, `A_0=N_0`. Equation (3.3) implies

\[
N_0'=[K_0,N_0]

\[
\boxed{\widehat A_0'=A_0'-N_0'.}

The previous report identified this exactly as the weighted exclusion-hopping operator on each fixed degree layer. Thus the cancellation of the extensive flag motion is not merely a first-variation coincidence: it is the derivative of an exact gauge in which the source number is constant.

# 4. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: a rooted local-Hamiltonian coercivity theorem

The frozen-source gauge turns the transport problem into the stability of the product number Hamiltonian under a connected quasi-local perturbation.

## 4.1 Product number Hamiltonian

At `alpha=0`, let

\[
N_0=\sum_{i=1}^n D_i,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `D_i` is the one-coordinate number projection in the product Walsh decomposition. For a finite coordinate set `X`, let

\[
N_X:=\sum_{i\in X}D_i
\]

and let `P_{X,0}` be projection onto functions constant in the coordinates of `X`. On the local product space,

\[
I-P_{X,0}\preceq N_X,
\tag{4.2}
\]

because every nonconstant local Walsh character has degree at least one.

## 4.2 Rooted-cluster coercivity theorem

Assume the Kato-gauged tilted form admits an absolutely convergent self-adjoint expansion

\[
\widehat C_s(\alpha)
=N_0+\sum_{X}B_X,
\tag{4.3}
\]

where every `B_X` is supported on `X` and annihilates the local constant vector. Define the rooted perturbation norm

\[
\varepsilon_s
:=\sup_i\sum_{X\ni i}\|B_X\|.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — rooted local-Hamiltonian domination

If `epsilon_s<1`, then

\[
\boxed{
(1-\varepsilon_s)N_0
\preceq \widehat C_s(\alpha)
\preceq(1+\varepsilon_s)N_0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular the tilted degree coercivity constant satisfies

\[
\boxed{\delta_s^*\ge1-\varepsilon_s>0.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Because `B_X` is self-adjoint, supported on `X`, and kills the local constant,

\[
|B_X|\preceq \|B_X\|(I-P_{X,0})
\preceq \|B_X\|N_X.
\]

Therefore for every `f`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\sum_X\langle f,B_Xf\rangle\right|
&\le\sum_X\|B_X\|\langle f,N_Xf\rangle\\
&=\sum_i\left(\sum_{X\ni i}\|B_X\|\right)
\langle f,D_if\rangle\\
&\le\varepsilon_s\langle f,N_0f\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is exactly (4.5). `square`

Combining (4.6) with the prior tilted-Dirichlet gate yields, for `K>D`,

\[
\|\Pi_{\le D}e^{-tA_\alpha}\Pi_{\ge K}\|_{2\to2}
\le
\exp\{-s(K-D)-(1-\varepsilon_s)t\}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

## 4.3 What this language clarifies

The theorem has the shape of a frustration-free/local-Hamiltonian stability statement, but the reference operator is the **ordinary algebraic degree number operator**, not a physical ground-state Hamiltonian. Classical detectability-lemma, Knabe-type local-gap, and frustration-free stability methods therefore provide useful analogies but do not directly supply (4.3).

# 5. B30 — ONE SCALE AT A TIME: first connected scales and the exact gluing obstruction

Introduce independent edge parameters `theta_e` around the product point rather than the single scalar `alpha`, and apply the factorizing Kato gauge from Section 3. This lets us localize the relative tilted form by interaction cluster size.

## 5.1 Scale one: one edge is exactly local and degree-preserving

For one pair interaction `e={i,j}`, the first derivative in the frozen gauge is the edge exchange operator on the local degree-one sector:

\[
B_e^{(1)}x_i=-J_e x_j,
\qquad
B_e^{(1)}x_j=-J_e x_i,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and it vanishes on the local constant and local degree-two character. Hence

\[
\boxed{\|B_e^{(1)}\|=|J_e|.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The first-scale rooted norm is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon^{(1)}
\le |\alpha|\max_i\sum_{e\ni i}|J_e|
=|\alpha|J_{\rm row}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This recovers the prior first-variation Schur bound in the local-Hamiltonian language.

## 5.2 Scale two: disconnected pairs cancel exactly

Take two distinct edges `e,f` with disjoint vertex supports and turn on only `theta_e,theta_f`. At the product point the model factors into the `e` block, the `f` block, and singletons. Choose the componentwise Kato gauge. The transformed source number is additive and the transformed generator/tilted form is an exact sum of the two block contributions. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\partial_{\theta_e}\partial_{\theta_f}
\widehat C_s\big|_{\theta=0}=0
\qquad(e\cap f=\varnothing).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the second Taylor coefficient contains **only connected two-edge wedges** (and repeated-edge derivatives; for a single zero-field pair block the second derivative vanishes because the exact local coupling is odd in `tanh(theta_e J_e)`).

The previous cubic leakage theorem says every source-off-diagonal block is zero through order two. Hence all scale-two wedge terms are degree-preserving in the frozen source decomposition and the exponential tilt does not enlarge them.

Let `c_2` denote the maximum operator norm of the normalized mixed second derivative over the finitely many three-vertex zero-field pairwise wedge types with `|J_e|,|J_f|<=1`. This is a finite local constant, computable from an `8 x 8` local problem. On graphs of maximum degree `Delta` and `|J_e|<=J_*`, the scale-two rooted contribution obeys the dimension-free bound

\[
\boxed{

\le c_2\,\Delta^2J_*^2\,\alpha^2.

\tag{5.5}

No numerical value of `c_2` is claimed here; no finite checker is therefore needed in this run.

\[
