# 3. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: discounted pressure is an analytic degree-generating function

The preceding run introduced, for `h=dP/dQ-1`, source-degree components

## Definition 3.1 — source-degree energy generating function

For `0<=z<=1`, define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_h(z)
:=\sum_{k\ge1} a_k^2 z^k.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal G_h(1)=\chi^2(P\|Q),
\tag{3.2}
\]

whereas for `z=e^{-2r}`,

\[
\mathcal G_h(e^{-2r})
=\sum_{k\ge1} e^{-2rk}a_k^2
=\|e^{-rN}h\|_2^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the previous discounted tail is simply the tail of `G_h` at an interior point of the unit interval:

\[
M_{r,D}^2
=\mathcal G_h(e^{-2r})
-\sum_{k=1}^D e^{-2rk}a_k^2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If the source low-degree advantage through `D` is bounded by `C`, then

\[
0\le \sum_{k\le D}e^{-2rk}a_k^2\le C^2,
\]

so on the source exponential scale the full interior generating-function pressure

\[
\Phi_r(P\|Q)
:=\log\bigl(1+\mathcal G_h(e^{-2r})\bigr)
\tag{3.5}
\]

and the tail pressure `Theta_{r,D}` differ by at most an additive `O_C(1)`. This is the right analytic object to certify locally.

### Native diagonal-noise interpretation

If the native noise is exactly source-degree diagonal, `T_r=e^{-rN}`, and is a Markov operator preserving `Q`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_h(e^{-2r})
=\|T_r h\|_2^2
=\chi^2(P T_r\|Q).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

So discounted degree pressure is literally ordinary collision pressure **after an auxiliary amount of source-aligned noise**. This gives a simple conceptual interpretation of why it can be much smaller than the original `Pi_2` while still being exactly the quantity relevant to later fixed noise.

For a general nonproduct Gibbs source, `e^{-rN}` need not itself be a positivity-preserving Markov kernel. Equation (3.3) is still an exact Hilbert-space identity, while (3.6) is asserted only in the degree-diagonal Markov cases where it is valid.

## Theorem 3.2 — analytic pressure budget for same-degree transfer

Assume the fixed-noise semigroup satisfies the degreewise aligned bound

\[
\|S_t\Pi_k\|_{2\to2}\le e^{-\alpha_t k}
\tag{3.7}

\[
\boxed{
\|S_t\Pi_{>D}h\|_2
\le
\frac{\exp[-(\alpha_t-r)(D+1)]}
{\sqrt{1-e^{-2(\alpha_t-r)}}}
\,\mathcal G_h(e^{-2r})^{1/2}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

### Proof

Write `h_k=Pi_k h`. By the triangle inequality and (3.7),

\[
\|S_t\Pi_{>D}h\|_2
\le
\sum_{k>D} e^{-\alpha_t k}\|h_k\|_2.
\]

Factor `e^{-\alpha_t k}=e^{-(\alpha_t-r)k}e^{-rk}` and apply Cauchy–Schwarz:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|S_t\Pi_{>D}h\|_2
&\le
\left(\sum_{k>D}e^{-2(\alpha_t-r)k}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{k>D}e^{-2rk}a_k^2\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le
\frac{e^{-(\alpha_t-r)(D+1)}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2(\alpha_t-r)}}}
\,\mathcal G_h(e^{-2r})^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is (3.8). □
