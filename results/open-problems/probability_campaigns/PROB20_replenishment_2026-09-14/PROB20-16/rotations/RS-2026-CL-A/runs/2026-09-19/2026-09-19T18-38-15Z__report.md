# 3. B3 — prove something stronger: a bounded-overlap defect-Dobrushin interpolation theorem

The predecessor's cactus theorem was exact but required pairwise edge-disjoint fundamental cycles. The useful feature was not cactus topology itself: it was that the logarithmic derivative under chord fugacity saw only the defect. The next step is to keep that principle when cycles overlap.

## 3.1 Exact cycle-variable model for arbitrary fundamental-cycle overlap

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite connected zero-field ferromagnetic Ising graph in high-temperature variables

\[
t_f=\tanh J_f\in(0,1),\qquad
w(F)=\prod_{f\in F}t_f.
\]

Fix a spanning tree `T` and let `H=E\setminus T` be the chord set. For each chord `e in H`, let `C_e` be its fundamental cycle. The cycles may overlap arbitrarily.

For an even vertex set `A`, let `J=J_T(A) subseteq T` be the unique tree `T`-join with `partial J=A`. For `x in {0,1}^H`, write

\[
X(x):=\triangle_{e:x_e=1}C_e.
\]

Every current with boundary `A` is uniquely `J triangle X(x)`, and every even current is uniquely `X(x)`.

Introduce a common chord fugacity `lambda in [0,1]` by multiplying each selected fundamental-cycle/chord coordinate by `lambda`. Define

\[
Z_A(\lambda)=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^H}
\lambda^{|x|}w(J\triangle X(x)),
\]

\[
Z_0(\lambda)=\sum_x\lambda^{|x|}w(X(x)),
\qquad
\mu_\lambda(A)=\frac{Z_A(\lambda)}{Z_0(\lambda)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

At `lambda=0`, only `x=0` remains, hence

\[
\mu_0(A)=w(J).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `pi_A^lambda` and `pi_0^lambda` be the normalized probability laws on cycle-selection vectors induced by the two partition functions.

Differentiating `(3.1)` gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\lambda\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log\mu_\lambda(A)
=
\sum_{e\in H}
\left(
\pi_A^\lambda[x_e=1]
-
\pi_0^\lambda[x_e=1]
\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the problem is a comparison of two finite Gibbs measures on **cycle variables**. Their factor graph is the fundamental-cycle intersection graph.

## 3.2 Direct perturbations are supported only on cycles touching the defect

For `sigma in {A,0}`, fix the other cycle variables and write the single-site conditional odds, with the explicit fugacity removed, as

\[
r_e^\sigma(x_{-e})
:=
\frac{w(J_\sigma\triangle X(x^{e=1}))}
     {w(J_\sigma\triangle X(x^{e=0}))},
\qquad
J_A=J,\quad J_0=\varnothing.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then

\[
\pi_\sigma^\lambda(x_e=1\mid x_{-e})
=
\frac{\lambda r_e^\sigma(x_{-e})}
     {1+\lambda r_e^\sigma(x_{-e})}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

If `C_e cap J=empty`, then the two configurations in `(3.4)` differ on `C_e` only, and the defect `J` is identically zero there. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
r_e^A(x_{-e})=r_e^0(x_{-e})
\quad\text{whenever }C_e\cap J=\varnothing.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the overlapping-cycle analogue of exact cactus cancellation: the **direct specification perturbation** is supported only on the root set

\[
R(J):=\{e\in H:C_e\cap J\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Remote cycles can still be affected indirectly through overlapping cycles, but that propagation is now exactly the object controlled by a comparison theorem.

## 3.3 A directly checkable local-table condition

For two positive numbers `r,r'`, the map

\[
p_\lambda(r)=\frac{\lambda r}{1+\lambda r}
\]

satisfies

\[
|p_\lambda(r)-p_\lambda(r')|
\le \lambda |r-r'|.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Define a direct-defect constant

\[
B:=
\sup_{e\in R(J)}
\sup_{x_{-e}}
|r_e^A(x_{-e})-r_e^0(x_{-e})|.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Then the local specification discrepancy obeys

\[
b_e(\lambda):=
\sup_{x_{-e}}
\left\|
\gamma_{e,A}^\lambda(\cdot\mid x_{-e})
-
\gamma_{e,0}^\lambda(\cdot\mid x_{-e})
\right\|_{TV}
\le
\lambda B\,1_{\{e\in R(J)\}}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Let `C(lambda)` be a nonnegative Dobrushin interdependence matrix dominating the single-cycle conditional influences of **both** specifications, and suppose a uniform column-sum form of the Dobrushin criterion holds:

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}
\max_j\sum_i C_{ij}(\lambda)
\le \kappa<1.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

This is a finite local-table hypothesis. For a cycle-intersection graph of maximum degree `Delta_C`, it is enough to verify local pair influences `c_{ij}` with `sum_i c_{ij}<1`; a cruder sufficient condition is `Delta_C c_*<1` when every adjacent-cycle influence is at most `c_*`.

Finally suppose each tree edge belongs to at most `R` fundamental cycles. Then

\[
|R(J)|\le R|J|.
\tag{3.12}
\]

## 3.4 Defect-Dobrushin theorem

The classical Dobrushin comparison theorem gives, for the marginal occupancy difference,

\[
\left|
\pi_A^\lambda[x_i=1]-\pi_0^\lambda[x_i=1]
\right|
\le
\sum_j D_{ij}(\lambda)b_j(\lambda),
\qquad
D=(I-C)^{-1}.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Under `(3.11)`, every column of `D` has sum at most `(1-kappa)^{-1}`. Summing `(3.13)` over `i`, using `(3.10)` and `(3.12)`, yields

\[
\sum_i
\left|
\pi_A^\lambda[x_i=1]-\pi_0^\lambda[x_i=1]
\right|
\le
\frac{\lambda B}{1-\kappa}|R(J)|
\le
\frac{\lambda BR}{1-\kappa}|J|.
\tag{3.14}
\]

Insert `(3.14)` into the exact derivative `(3.3)`:

\[
\left|
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log\mu_\lambda(A)
\right|
\le
\frac{BR}{1-\kappa}|J|.
\tag{3.15}
\]

Integrating from `0` to `1` and using `(3.2)` gives the all-size theorem

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\log\frac{\mu_G(A)}{w(J_T(A))}
\right|
\le
\frac{BR}{1-\kappa}|J_T(A)|.
}
\tag{3.16}
\]

In particular, if all tree-edge high-temperature weights are at most `t_*<1`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mu_G(A)
\le
\left[
 t_*\exp\!\left(\frac{BR}{1-\kappa}\right)
\right]^{|J_T(A)|}.
}
\tag{3.17}
\]

Whenever

\[
\boxed{
|\log t_*|>\frac{BR}{1-\kappa},
}
\tag{3.18}
\]

parity/current correlations decay exponentially in tree-current distance uniformly in system size **even when the cyclomatic rank is `Theta(n)`**.

### What this did and did not solve

This is the requested B3 strengthening of the cactus defect interpolation: edge-disjointness is replaced by a local comparison hypothesis on a bounded-overlap cycle-variable factor graph. The theorem is non-tautological: `B`, `R`, and `kappa` are finite local-table/cycle-incidence quantities.

However, it is still only a **correlation/current theorem**. # 4. B27 — swap the quantifiers: exact characterization of “every fixed positive noise level”

The predecessor had sufficient discounted-pressure conditions at a **single** noise radius. The source's Hopkins-style statement quantifies over every fixed positive noise level. For an exact degree-diagonal noise basis this quantifier has a clean necessary-and-sufficient answer.

## 4.1 Abstract nonnegative degree profile

Let

\[
A_{n,k}\ge0,
\qquad k=1,2,\dots,
\]

have finite support for each `n`. In the likelihood-ratio setting, `A_{n,k}` is the squared `L_2(Q_n)` mass of source degree `k` (or representation-support degree `k`). Define the Laplace/noise profile

\[
L_n(s):=\sum_{k\ge1} A_{n,k}e^{-sk},
\qquad s>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For product refresh with amplitude attenuation `e^{-tk}`, the noised chi-square is `L_n(2t)`.

Define fixed-prefix mass

\[
F_n(K):=\sum_{1\le k\le K}A_{n,k},
\tag{4.2}
\]

and the positive per-degree spectral entropy

\[
R_n:=
\sup_{k:A_{n,k}>0}
\frac{[\log A_{n,k}]_+}{k},
\tag{4.3}
\]

with `R_n=0` if all masses vanish.

## 4.2 Vanishing all-noise theorem

**Theorem.** The following are equivalent:

1. for **every fixed `s>0`**, `L_n(s)->0`;
2. both
   - for every fixed `K`, `F_n(K)->0`, and
   - `R_n->0`.

### Necessity

Fix `s>0`. Since

\[
F_n(K)
\le
 e^{sK}L_n(s),
\]

condition 1 implies `F_n(K)->0` for every fixed `K`.

Also, for every `k`,

\[
A_{n,k}e^{-sk}\le L_n(s).
\]

For large `n`, `L_n(s)<1`, so whenever `A_{n,k}>1`,

\[
\frac{\log A_{n,k}}{k}<s.
\]

Hence `limsup R_n<=s`. Since this holds for every `s>0`, `R_n->0`.

### Sufficiency

Fix `s>0`. For large `n`, `R_n<=s/2`, hence for every `k`

\[
A_{n,k}\le e^{sk/2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Choose a fixed `K` so large that

\[
\sum_{k>K}e^{-sk/2}<\varepsilon.
\]

Then

\[
L_n(s)
\le
F_n(K)
+
\sum_{k>K}e^{-sk}A_{n,k}
\le
F_n(K)+\varepsilon.
\]

The first term tends to zero, and then `epsilon` is arbitrary. Thus `L_n(s)->0`. `square`

This theorem is elementary but important: **every positive exponential noise discount kills the spectrum iff fixed-degree mass escapes and the multiplicity at degree `k` is only `exp(o(k))`.** No global chi-square bound is required.

## 4.3 Bounded all-noise theorem — the tier matching bounded source LDA

The source hypothesis is bounded, not necessarily vanishing, LDA. Define the tail exponential type

\[
R^{>K}
:=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{k>K}
\frac{[\log A_{n,k}]_+}{k}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then the following are equivalent:

1. for every fixed `s>0`, `limsup_n L_n(s)<infinity`;
2. for every fixed `K`, `limsup_n F_n(K)<infinity`, and

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}R^{>K}=0.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Necessity

If `L_n(s)<=C_s` eventually, then

\[
F_n(K)\le e^{sK}C_s.
\]

Moreover

\[
A_{n,k}\le C_se^{sk},
\]

so for `k>K`,

\[
\frac{[\log A_{n,k}]_+}{k}
\le
s+\frac{\log_+ C_s}{K}.
\]

Take `limsup_n`, then `K->infinity`, then `s downarrow0`, obtaining `(4.6)`.

### Sufficiency

Fix `s>0`. Choose `K` so large that `R^{>K}<s/2`. For all sufficiently large `n`, every `k>K` then has `A_{n,k}<=e^{sk/2}`. The tail of `L_n(s)` is bounded by the geometric series `sum_{k>K}e^{-sk/2}`, while the fixed prefix is bounded by hypothesis. Thus `limsup_n L_n(s)<infinity`. `square`

This gives a clean all-positive-noise formulation of the source architecture in exact degree-diagonal settings:

- bounded source-window mass plus **zero asymptotic tail exponential type** implies bounded noised total `L_2` advantage at every fixed positive noise level;
- if fixed-degree mass additionally escapes, the noised chi-square and hence TV go to zero at every fixed positive noise level.

## 4.4 Quantitative Mao obstruction

Mao's construction has zero source low-degree advantage through a diverging `D_n`, so in the exact product-degree profile

\[
A_{n,k}=0\qquad(k\le D_n).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Suppose at some fixed product-noise parameter `s>0` the noised TV distance stays at least `c_s>0`. Since

\[
TV\le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2},
\]

we have

\[
L_n(s)=\chi^2(P_nS_s,Q_n)\ge4c_s^2
\]

along a subsequence.

If instead `R_n<=s-delta` eventually for some `delta>0`, then `(4.7)` gives

\[
L_n(s)
\le
\sum_{k>D_n}e^{-sk}e^{(s-\delta)k}
=
\frac{e^{-\delta(D_n+1)}}{1-e^{-\delta}}
\to0,
\]

a contradiction. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}R_n\ge s
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

for every semigroup noise exponent `s` at which Mao remains strongly distinguishable.

Thus Mao is not merely “a rank-test exception”: under the native degree-diagonal refresh, it must contain **exponentially many units of likelihood energy per unit source degree at some escaping degrees**. This is the exact structural obstruction demanded by the source-facing framework.

# 5. B25 — count the same thing twice: exact calibrated primal/dual all-noise criterion

The previous Tanner report correctly found that minimum odd boundary is insufficient. The right comparison is against the ambient binomial baseline. B27 lets us state this as an exact primal/dual equivalence for binary subgroup alternatives.

## 5.1 Primal side

Let `C_n<=F_2^{N_n}` be a binary spectral code, with weight counts

\[
A_{n,k}=\#\{c\in C_n:|c|=k\}.
\]

For the subgroup/coset alternative over the uniform Boolean null,

\[
1+\chi^2(P_nS_t\|Q_n)
=
W_{C_n}(e^{-2t})
=
\sum_kA_{n,k}e^{-2tk}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore B27 says that noisy chi-square tends to zero for **every fixed `t>0`** iff

\[
\#\{c:1\le |c|\le K\}\to0
\quad\text{for every fixed }K,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{k:A_{n,k}>0}
\frac{[\log A_{n,k}]_+}{k}
\to0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Because the counts are integers, `(5.2)` means there are eventually no nonzero codewords in each fixed weight window. Hard linear minimum distance is sufficient but not necessary: the minimum weight may diverge arbitrarily slowly as long as the multiplicity profile has subexponential type.

## 5.2 Dual side: exact ambient calibration

Let `D_n=C_n^perp`, let `W` be uniform on `D_n`, and let `U` be uniform on the ambient cube `F_2^{N_n}`. Set

\[
z\in(0,1),
\qquad
q=\frac{1-z}{1+z}\in(0,1).
\tag{5.4}
\]

MacWilliams gives

\[
W_{C_n}(z)
=(1+z)^{N_n}\,\mathbb E q^{|W|}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

But

\[
\mathbb E q^{|U|}
=\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\right)^{N_n}
=(1+z)^{-N_n}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence the exact calibrated identity is

\[
\boxed{
W_{C_n}(z)
=
\frac{\mathbb E q^{|W|}}
     {\mathbb E q^{|U|}}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Thus the all-positive-noise condition has the exact dual formulation

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E q^{|W_n|}}
     {\mathbb E q^{|U_n|}}
\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{for every fixed }q\in(0,1).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is not a minimum-boundary condition. It says that the **entire lower-tail Laplace transform of the dual row-space weight distribution must match the ambient binomial transform at every interior point**.

## 5.3 Tilted-binomial density-ratio identity

Write

\[
p_W(j)=\Pr\{|W|=j\},
\qquad
p_U(j)=2^{-N}{N\choose j},
\]

and define the weight-shell density ratio

\[
r_n(j):=\frac{p_W(j)}{p_U(j)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Tilt the ambient binomial by `q^j`. Its normalized law is exactly

\[
\nu_z=\operatorname{Bin}\left(N,\frac{q}{1+q}\right)
=
\operatorname{Bin}\left(N,\frac{1-z}{2}\right).
\tag{5.10}
\]

Substituting `(5.9)` into `(5.7)` gives the second exact count:

\[
\boxed{
W_C(z)=\mathbb E_{J\sim\nu_z} r_n(J).
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

This is the calibrated object missing from a minimum-odd-boundary analysis. The relevant dual weights are not sampled uniformly in `j`; they are sampled from the **noise-dependent tilted binomial window** centered at

\[
\frac{1-z}{2}N.
\]

## 5.4 Local-CLT / large-deviation certificate

Equation `(5.11)` immediately gives a practical two-part criterion. Let `I_n(z)` be any typical window with

\[
\nu_z(I_n(z))\to1.
\]

If

\[
\sup_{j\in I_n(z)}|r_n(j)-1|\to0
\tag{5.12}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_z}
\left[r_n(J)1_{\{J\notin I_n(z)\}}\right]
\to0,
\tag{5.13}
\]

then

\[
W_C(z)\to1.
\tag{5.14}
\]

More generally, for the source-scale interior-pressure theorem it is enough that

\[
\sup_{j\in I_n(z)}\log_+r_n(j)=o(D_n)
\tag{5.15}
\]

and

\[
\log_+
\mathbb E_{\nu_z}
\left[r_n(J)1_{\{J\notin I_n(z)\}}\right]
=o(D_n),
\tag{5.16}
\]

because `(5.11)` then gives

\[
\log W_C(z)=o(D_n).
\tag{5.17}
\]

This converts the vague “control Tanner near dependencies” instruction into a calibrated finite-size target:
