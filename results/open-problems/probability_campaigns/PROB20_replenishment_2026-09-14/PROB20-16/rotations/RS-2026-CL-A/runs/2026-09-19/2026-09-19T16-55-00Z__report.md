## 3.1 High-temperature character formula on a general graph

Let `G=(V,E)` be finite, connected, zero-field ferromagnetic Ising, written in high-temperature form

\[
Q_G(g)\propto \prod_{e=\{u,v\}\in E}(1+\rho_e g_ug_v),
\qquad 0\le \rho_e<1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For an edge set `F`, let `partial F` be its odd-degree vertex boundary and

\[
w(F)=\prod_{e\in F}\rho_e.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Expanding the product and summing spins gives the classical exact current identity

\[
\boxed{
\mu_G(A):=\mathbb E_Q\chi_A
=
\frac{\sum_{F:\partial F=A}w(F)}{\sum_{Z:\partial Z=\varnothing}w(Z)}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

for even `|A|`, while odd `|A|` gives zero. If the cyclomatic number is

\[
b=|E|-|V|+1,
\tag{3.4}
\]

then the even-subgraph cycle space has exactly `2^b` elements. Fix any particular `F_A` with `partial F_A=A`; all solutions are the affine cycle-space coset `F_A triangle Z`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mu_G(A)=
\frac{\sum_{Z\in\mathcal Z(G)}w(F_A\triangle Z)}
     {\sum_{Z\in\mathcal Z(G)}w(Z)}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the exact loop correction to the tree T-join formula.

## 3.2 Exactly one cycle

Let `G=T+e_*` be unicyclic and let `C_*` be its unique cycle of length `ell`. In the homogeneous case `rho_e=rho`, let `J_T(A)` be the unique tree T-join for even `A`. There are exactly two edge sets with boundary `A`: `J_T(A)` and `J_T(A) triangle C_*`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu_G(A)=
\frac{\rho^{|J_T(A)|}+\rho^{|J_T(A)\triangle C_*|}}
     {1+\rho^{\ell}}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

If `a=|J_T(A)\cap C_*|` and `b_0=|J_T(A)\setminus C_*|`, then

\[
\mu_G(A)=
\rho^{b_0}
\frac{\rho^a+\rho^{\ell-a}}{1+\rho^\ell}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus the first loop does not destroy exponential character decay; it replaces the tree length by the shorter of the two routes around the cycle, with an explicit second-route correction. In particular, if

\[
\tau_G(A)=\min_{F:\partial F=A}|F|,
\tag{3.8}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{0\le\mu_G(A)\le 2\rho^{\tau_G(A)}.}
\tag{3.9}
\]

This is the precise first loop term requested by B30.

## 3.3 Bounded cyclomatic number

For homogeneous `0<rho<1`, (3.5) immediately gives

\[
\boxed{
0\le\mu_G(A)\le 2^b\rho^{\tau_G(A)}
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

because the numerator has `2^b` terms, every term has at least `tau_G(A)` edges, and the denominator is at least the empty even subgraph contribution `1`.

The heterogeneous version is equally clean. Let

\[
E(F)=\sum_{e\in F}-\log\rho_e,
\qquad
E_G(A)=\min_{\partial F=A}E(F).
\tag{3.11}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mu_G(A)\le 2^b e^{-E_G(A)}.
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

So bounded cyclomatic number produces only a finite multiplicative loop penalty in the parity-correlation geometry.

## 3.4 From monomial moments back to source LDA

Let `T` be a spanning tree of `G`, and suppose all edge correlations are at most `rho_*<1`. Relative to the tree Ising law `Q_T`,

\[
R:=\frac{dQ_G}{dQ_T}
=
\frac{\prod_{e\in E\setminus T}(1+\rho_e\chi_e)}
     {\mathbb E_{Q_T}\prod_{e\in E\setminus T}(1+\rho_e\chi_e)}.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Since there are exactly `b` chords,

\[
\boxed{
R\ge m_b:=\left(\frac{1-\rho_*}{1+\rho_*}\right)^b.
}
\tag{3.14}
\]

Therefore any degree-`<=D` polynomial satisfies

\[
\|f\|_{L_2(Q_T)}\le m_b^{-1/2}\|f\|_{L_2(Q_G)}.
\tag{3.15}
\]

Use the tree innovation basis from the predecessor on `T`. Each degree-`k` basis vector expands into ordinary characters with total absolute coefficient at most

\[
C_*^k,
\qquad
C_*:=\frac{1+\rho_*}{\sqrt{1-\rho_*^2}}.
\tag{3.16}
\]

Now let `C` be the parity row-span used to condition `Q_G`, and define the loopy robust distance

\[
\delta_k^G(c)=
\min_{|U|\le k}\tau_G(c\triangle U),
\tag{3.17}
\]

with odd boundaries interpreted as `+infinity`, and

\[
B_k^G(C;\rho)=\sum_{c\in C\setminus\{0\}}\rho^{\delta_k^G(c)}.
\tag{3.18}

For a monomial `chi_U`, the exact conditioned moment identity is

\[
\langle L-1,\chi_U\rangle_{Q_G}
=
\frac1{Z_C}\sum_{c\ne0}\mu_G(c\triangle U)
-
\frac{Z_C-1}{Z_C}\mu_G(U).
\tag{3.19}

Using `Z_C>=1` and (3.10),

\[
|\langle L-1,\chi_U\rangle_{Q_G}|
\le
2^b\left(B_k^G+B_0^G\right)
\tag{3.20}

for `|U|<=k`. Combining (3.15), (3.16), and Cauchy-Schwarz in the tree innovation coordinates yields the bounded-cyclomatic low-degree theorem

\[
\boxed{

# 4. B7 — source-scale limit: a uniform polylog small-weight bound for random regular LDPC

The predecessor correctly identified a gap: a weight-spectrum theorem proved for fixed `l` or linear `l=omega n` cannot simply be asserted uniformly at `l=(log n)^{1+eta}`. Here that gap is handled directly for the standard regular configuration-model ensemble.

## 4.1 Configuration-model setup

Take a binary `(c,d)`-regular Tanner configuration model with fixed integers `c,d>=3`. There are `n` variable nodes, each of degree `c`, and

\[
m=\frac{cn}{d}
\tag{4.1}
\]

check nodes, each of degree `d`. Pair the `cn` variable sockets uniformly with the `cn` check sockets. Let `A_l(H)` be the number of codewords of Hamming weight `l` in `ker H`.

Fix a support `S` of `l` variable nodes. It has exactly `cl` active variable sockets. Their matched check sockets form a uniform `cl`-subset of the `cn` check sockets.

If `S` is a codeword, every occupied check contains an even positive number of active sockets, hence the number `s` of occupied checks obeys

\[
s\le \frac{cl}{2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore, for `cl<=cn/2`,

\[
\Pr(S\text{ is a codeword})
\le
\sum_{s\le cl/2}
\frac{{m\choose s}{ds\choose cl}}{{cn\choose cl}}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Both numerator factors are increasing over the relevant range, so

\[
\Pr(S\text{ codeword})
\le
\frac{cl}{2}
\frac{{m\choose cl/2}{dcl/2\choose cl}}{{cn\choose cl}},
\tag{4.4}
\]

with the obvious floor/zero modification when parity prevents `cl/2` from being integral.

Use

\[
{N\choose K}\le\left(\frac{eN}{K}\right)^K,
\qquad
{N\choose K}\ge\left(\frac NK\right)^K.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then

\[
{m\choose cl/2}
\le
\left(\frac{2en}{dl}\right)^{cl/2},
\tag{4.6}
\]

\[
{dcl/2\choose cl}\le\left(\frac{ed}{2}\right)^{cl},
\qquad
{cn\choose cl}^{-1}\le\left(\frac ln\right)^{cl}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Multiplying by `{n\choose l}<= (en/l)^l` yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E A_l(H)
\le
\frac{cl}{2}
\left[
K_{c,d}
\left(\frac ln\right)^{c/2-1}
\right]^l,
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

where one explicit safe constant is

\[
K_{c,d}=e^{1+3c/2}\left(\frac d2\right)^{c/2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

No fixed-`l` asymptotic has been extrapolated: (4.8) is uniform in the whole displayed range.

## 4.2 The polylogarithmic window

Let

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\eta}=o(n).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Set `a=c/2-1>0`. For all `l<=D_n`,

\[
K_{c,d}(l/n)^a
\le
x_n:=K_{c,d}(D_n/n)^a\to0.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{l=1}^{D_n}\mathbb E A_l(H)
\le
\frac{cD_n}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{D_n}x_n^l
\le
\frac{cD_n}{2}\frac{x_n}{1-x_n}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.12}
\]

By Markov's inequality,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{\exists\,0\ne x\in\ker H:\ |x|\le D_n\}\to0.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Thus a random fixed-degree regular LDPC configuration has **no nonzero codeword anywhere in the entire source-scale polylog window with high probability**. Conditioning on simplicity changes this by only the standard constant-factor contiguity for fixed degrees, so the same high-probability conclusion holds for the corresponding simple random regular Tanner ensemble.

This is exactly the source-scale uniform statement that the predecessor could not legitimately obtain from a linear-weight spectral shape alone.

Equation (4.8) is not presented as a new coding theorem. Its value here is that it is a **one-page uniform bridge from coding theory to Kireeva's polylogarithmic source cutoff** with no hidden interchange of `n->infinity` and `l->infinity`.

# 5. B11 — dualize: MacWilliams as a local Tanner odd-boundary partition

The previous code-weight-enumerator language can be dualized exactly. The useful point is not merely the classical MacWilliams formula; it is the local Tanner interpretation of its dual partition function.

## 5.1 Exact MacWilliams/Poisson identity

Let `C<=F_2^n` have dimension `M` and dual dimension `m=n-M`. Write

\[
W_C(z)=\sum_{k=0}^n A_k z^k.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `0<=z<1`, define

\[
q=\frac{1-z}{1+z}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

MacWilliams gives

\[
\boxed{
W_C(z)=2^{-m}(1+z)^nW_{C^\perp}(q).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Let `H` be any `m_0 x n` parity-check matrix with row space `C^perp`, and let

\[
r_H=\dim\ker(H^T)=m_0-m.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Every dual codeword has exactly `2^{r_H}` check-selection preimages. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
W_{C^\perp}(q)
=2^{-r_H}\sum_{y\in F_2^{m_0}} q^{|H^Ty|}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Combining,

\[
\boxed{
W_C(z)
=2^{-m-r_H}(1+z)^n
\sum_{y\in F_2^{m_0}}q^{|H^Ty|}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Here `|H^Ty|` is exactly the number of variable nodes incident to an **odd** number of selected checks. Thus the noisy/interior likelihood pressure is a sparse local Tanner partition function whose energy is the odd boundary of the selected-check set.

## 5.2 One interior point already bounds the source low-weight window

Because `0<z<1`, every codeword of weight `k<=D` contributes at least `z^D` to `W_C(z)-1`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{1\le k\le D}A_k
\le
z^{-D}\bigl(W_C(z)-1\bigr).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

For product-group/subgroup models where `A_k` is exactly likelihood spectral mass, (5.7) says that a sufficiently small **single interior partition value** controls the entire source low-degree window. Under coordinate Haar refresh at time `t`, the same object at `z=e^{-2t}` is exactly the noisy chi-square divergence.

This gives a direct dual route from a local Tanner partition estimate to both sides of the source implication.

## 5.3 Two distinct local expansion mechanisms

There are two natural deterministic Tanner conditions, and they control different pieces.

### Variable-side small-set expansion controls primal low weights

Suppose every variable node has degree `c`. If a nonzero codeword has support `S`, every neighboring check has even positive degree into `S`, hence at least two. Therefore

\[
c|S|\ge2|N(S)|.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
|N(S)|>\frac c2|S|
\quad\text{for every }1\le|S|\le D
\Longrightarrow
A_1=\cdots=A_D=0.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This is the classical expander-code mechanism, now stated exactly at the source cutoff rather than asymptotically at linear distance.

### Check-side odd-boundary expansion controls the dual pressure

For selected check set `Y`, let

\[
\partial_{\rm odd}Y=\operatorname{supp}(H^T1_Y).
\tag{5.10}
\]

If for all check sets of size `j<=J`

\[
|\partial_{\rm odd}Y|\ge\lambda j,
\tag{5.11}
\]

then their contribution to (5.6) is at most

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{J}{m_0\choose j}q^{\lambda j}.
\tag{5.12}
\]

The remaining `j>J` contribution can be supplied by a coding spectral-shape/large-deviation estimate. Thus a **two-scale dual certificate** is possible: exact local odd-boundary expansion for the small check-selection window plus a classical bulk spectrum for the large window.

The important negative lesson is also precise: ordinary variable-side Tanner expansion and check-side odd-boundary expansion are not the same statement. MacWilliams duality does not magically turn one into the other with positive coefficients at each low weight; coefficient recovery uses oscillatory Krawtchouk transforms. A source-valid local criterion therefore needs to track which side of the Tanner graph controls which quantity.
