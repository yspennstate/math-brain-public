## 3. B3 — stronger theorem: a fixed relative commutator is allowed without exponential blowup

The previous semigroup estimate contained a factor `exp(O(epsilon_D^2 t/lambda_D))`; with `epsilon_D=rho lambda_D` and fixed `rho`, that becomes exponential in `lambda_D t` and is useless at the source cutoff. The right move is to spectrally gate the actual generator instead of Gronwalling the low/high norms.

### Theorem 3.1 (relative-commutator spectral gate)

Let `A>=0` be self-adjoint on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. Let `P` be any orthogonal projection and `H=I-P`. Write

\[
C=HAH|_{\operatorname{Ran}H},
\qquad
B=HAP,
\]

and assume

\[
C\ge \lambda I_H,
\qquad
\|B\|\le\varepsilon.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Set `rho=epsilon/lambda`. For any `0<tau<1`, any `t>=0`, and any vector `f`,

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}f\|
\le
\|Pf\|
+
\left(
\frac{\rho}{1-\tau}
+e^{-\tau\lambda t}
\right)\|f\|.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

No hypothesis `rho->0` is needed for the theorem itself.

#### Proof

Let

\[
E=\mathbf 1_{[0,\tau\lambda]}(A)
\]

be the spectral projection of the full generator onto energies below `tau lambda`. Put `A_E=A|_{Ran E}`, `X=HE:Ran E -> Ran H`, and `Y=PE`.

Because `E` commutes with `A`,

\[
HAE=HEA_E=XA_E.
\]

On the other hand, decomposing the input of `A` into `P+H` gives

\[
HAE=HAP\,PE+HAH\,HE=BY+CX.
\]

Therefore `X` solves the Sylvester equation

\[
CX-XA_E=-BY.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since `C>=lambda I` and `0<=A_E<=tau lambda I`, the two spectra are separated by `(1-tau)lambda`. The standard integral solution is

\[
X
=-\int_0^\infty e^{-sC}BYe^{sA_E}\,ds,
\]

hence

\[
\|HE\|=\|X\|
\le
\int_0^\infty e^{-(1-\tau)\lambda s}\,ds\;\varepsilon
=
\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\tau)\lambda}
=
\frac\rho{1-\tau}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

By adjointness, `||EH||=||HE||`. Split the semigroup spectrally:

\[
e^{-tA}f=e^{-tA}Ef+e^{-tA}(I-E)f.
\]

The second term has norm at most `e^{-tau lambda t}||f||`. For the first,

\[
\|Ef\|
\le
\|EPf\|+\|EHf\|
\le
\|Pf\|+\frac\rho{1-\tau}\|f\|.
\]

Combining proves `(3.2)`. `square`

### Corollary 3.2 (source-degree form)

Take `P=P_D`, the `L^2(Q)` projection onto the source's ordinary algebraic degree-`<=D` mean-zero space, and `f=L-1`. Then `||Pf||=Adv^{<=D}(P,Q)` and `||f||=sqrt(chi2(P||Q))`. If `lambda_D` is high-source-degree coercivity and `epsilon_D=||[A,P_D]||=||H_DAP_D||`, then for every `tau in (0,1)`,

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{\chi^2(P S_t\|Q)}
\le
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
+
\left(
\frac{\epsilon_D}{(1-\tau)\lambda_D}
+e^{-\tau\lambda_Dt}
\right)
\sqrt{\chi^2(P\|Q)}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the correct fixed-relative-commutator hypothesis is not `epsilon_D=o(lambda_D)` by itself. What matters is the **leakage-damping product**

\[
\left(
\frac{\epsilon_D}{\lambda_D}+e^{-\Theta(\lambda_Dt)}
\right)\sqrt{\chi^2(P\|Q)}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For example `tau=1/2` gives the clean bound `Adv_D + (2 rho_D + e^{-lambda_D t/2}) sqrt(chi2)`.

### Sharpness in order: the `rho M` floor cannot be removed

Take the positive semidefinite two-dimensional block matrix

\[
A_\rho
=\lambda
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho^2&\rho\\
\rho&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Then `HAH=lambda`, `||HAP||=rho lambda`, and the kernel is spanned by `(1,-rho)`. Starting with purely high vector `f=(0,1)`, as `t->infinity`, `e^{-tA_rho}f` converges to the kernel projection of `f`, whose norm is

\[
\frac\rho{\sqrt{1+\rho^2}}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Therefore a general theorem cannot replace the `O(rho)||f||` leakage floor by `o(rho)||f||`. The order in `(3.2)` is structurally sharp.

**Outcome B3:** success. The fixed-relative-commutator route survives, but only together with a quantitative bound on `rho_D sqrt(chi2)`. This replaces the previous false hope that `rho_D` alone should vanish under generic high-temperature assumptions.

## 4. B5 — perturb the interaction: upward algebraic-degree mixing begins quadratically in the exact Ising witness

Return to the previous run's exact three-spin path block

\[
q_{h,J}(x)
\propto
\exp(hx_2+Jx_1x_2+Jx_2x_3).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The center heat-bath conditional mean is

\[
m_{h,J}(x_1,x_3)=\tanh(h+Jx_1+Jx_3).
\]

Its degree-two coefficient is exactly

\[
d(h,J)
=
\frac14\left[
\tanh(h+2J)-2\tanh h+\tanh(h-2J)
\right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

Treat `J` as the deformation parameter. The central second-difference expansion gives

\[
d(h,J)
=J^2\tanh''(h)+O(J^4)
=-2\tanh h\,\operatorname{sech}^2 h\,J^2+O(J^4).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The Dobrushin row-sum parameter for the center is

\[
\alpha(J)=2\tanh|J|=2|J|+O(|J|^3),
\]

so for fixed `h != 0`,

\[
\boxed{
d(h,J)
=-\frac12\tanh h\,\operatorname{sech}^2h\;\alpha(J)^2
+O(\alpha(J)^4).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is not merely a coefficient identity; it identifies the first source-degree leakage order. Let `V_1` be the local ordinary algebraic degree-`<=1` space in `L^2(q_{h,J})`, `P_1` its orthogonal projection, and `H_1=I-P_1`. Since

\[
A_0x_2=x_2-m_{h,J}(x_1,x_3),
\]

all pieces except `d(h,J)x_1x_3` lie in `V_1`, hence

\[
H_1A_0(x_2-E x_2)
=-d(h,J)H_1(x_1x_3).
\tag{4.5}
\]

At `J=0`, both `||x_2-E x_2||` and `||H_1(x_1x_3)||` are strictly positive. By continuity, the local `U_1 -> U_2` block norm therefore obeys

\[
\boxed{c_{12}(h,J)=\Theta_h(J^2)=\Theta_h(\alpha(J)^2)}
\tag{4.6}
\]

for fixed nonzero `h` and sufficiently small `J`.

For `m` independent copies of this block, the previous Hamming-slice amplification theorem supplies a lower bound `epsilon_D/lambda_D >= c_h alpha^2` at middle source-degree cutoffs. Conversely, because the block is finite and the only upward filtration failure is analytic and starts at order `J^2`, the norm of the local upward block is `O_h(J^2)`; summing the `m` block generators gives `epsilon_D=O_h(mJ^2)`, while the finite-block Fock theorem gives `lambda_D=Omega_h(m)` at those cutoffs. Hence on this exact family

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\epsilon_D}{\lambda_D}=\Theta_h(\alpha^2)
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

at the amplified middle cutoffs.

**Outcome B5:** success. The exact witness shows that a universal weak-coupling theorem cannot generally improve the relative commutator below quadratic order in interaction strength, while also making `rho=O(alpha^2)` a plausible sharp target for bounded-block perturbative classes. This does **not** yet prove such a bound for arbitrary overlapping bounded-degree pairwise Gibbs models.
