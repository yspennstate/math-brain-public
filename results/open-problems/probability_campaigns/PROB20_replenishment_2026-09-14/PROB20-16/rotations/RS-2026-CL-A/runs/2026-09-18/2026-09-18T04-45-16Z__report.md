## 4. B13 — exact factorial-size-bias drift and a monotone reinforcement budget

For `A_j>0`, define the `j`-th factorial-size-biased overlap by

\[
\Pr(Z^{[j]}=z)
=\frac{(z)_j\Pr(Z=z)}{A_j}
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the residual overlap

\[
R_j:=Z^{[j]}-j.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The preceding report noted only its mean. The whole one-step evolution is explicit.

### Lemma 4.1 — factorial-size-bias chain

Whenever `A_{j+1}>0`,

\[
\boxed{
R_{j+1}\ \stackrel d=\ R_j^{\mathrm s}-1,
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

where `R_j^s` denotes ordinary size biasing of `R_j`. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\bar h_j=\mathbb E R_j,
\qquad
\bar h_{j+1}-\bar h_j
=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(R_j)}{\mathbb E R_j}-1.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

For `z>=j+1`,

\[
\Pr(Z^{[j+1]}=z)
=\frac{(z)_{j+1}P(Z=z)}{A_{j+1}}
=\frac{(z-j)(z)_jP(Z=z)}{A_j\bar h_j}.
\]

Under `Z^{[j]}`, the factor `z-j` is exactly `R_j`; hence the right side is the size-biased law of `R_j`, followed by the shift from `z-j` to `z-(j+1)`. This proves (4.3). Also

\[
\mathbb E R_j
=\frac{\mathbb E (Z)_j(Z-j)}{A_j}
=\frac{A_{j+1}}{A_j}=\bar h_j.
\]

Finally,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar h_{j+1}
&=\mathbb E R_{j+1}
=\frac{\mathbb E R_j^2}{\mathbb E R_j}-1\\
&=\mathbb E R_j+rac{\operatorname{Var}(R_j)}{\mathbb E R_j}-1,
\end{aligned}
\]

which gives (4.4). `□`

This identity isolates the exact reinforcement mechanism: the average extension rises precisely when the factorial-size-biased residual is overdispersed relative to Poisson, is flat at Poisson dispersion, and falls when it is underdispersed.

### Definition 4.2 — cumulative positive reinforcement budget

Define

\[
\delta_j^+
:=\left(
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(R_j)}{\mathbb E R_j}-1
\right)_+,
\qquad
\Delta_j:=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\delta_i^+.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The quantity `Delta_j` is monotone nondecreasing by construction. Telescoping (4.4) gives the nontrivial envelope

\[
\boxed{
\bar h_j\le \mathbb EZ+\Delta_j.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore a physical-space dispersion budget implies the preceding affine theorem: if for all relevant `j`,

\[
\lambda\Delta_j\le\beta j,
\tag{4.7}
\]

then

\[
\lambda\bar h_j
\le \underbrace{\lambda\mathbb EZ}_{b_1}+\beta j.
\tag{4.8}
\]

For every fixed `q` with `q beta<1`, the preceding negative-binomial majorant yields

\[
\boxed{
F(q)\le(1-\beta q)^{-b_1/\beta},
\qquad
\mu(q)\le\frac{b_1q}{1-\beta q}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Here `b_1<=Adv^{<=D}(P,Q)^2`. Thus at the source cutoff, bounded LDA plus a uniform subcritical bound on the **cumulative positive Fano excess of the factorial-size-biased residuals** implies the same full noised-testing gap as the preceding orbit theorem.

## 5. B10 — derandomize the orbit condition to a finite quotient certificate

The preceding extension conditions were expressed as expectations over a random group translate. They can be reduced to finite orbit-incidence data without computing the full likelihood-ratio spectrum.

Let

\[
H:=\operatorname{Stab}_G(A_0)
\]

be the setwise stabilizer of the template. For each `j`, let `C_j` be the collection of `H`-orbits of `j`-subsets of `A_0`. For a class `C in C_j`, choose a representative `S_C` and define

\[
n_C:=|C|,
\qquad
a_C:=\Pr(S_C\subseteq A).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Both are representative-independent. If `C' in C_{j+1}`, define the extension incidence

\[
e_{C,C'}
:=\#\{x\in A_0\setminus S_C:
S_C\cup\{x\}\in C'\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Again this is well-defined because `H` preserves `A_0` and acts transitively within `C`.

### Proposition 5.1 — deterministic orbit-quotient birth certificate

The level mass and conditional extension are exactly

\[
\boxed{
m_j=\sum_{C\in C_j}n_Ca_C,}
\tag{5.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
h_C
=\sum_{C'\in C_{j+1}}
e_{C,C'}\frac{a_{C'}}{a_C},}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\bar h_j
=\frac{
\sum_{C\in C_j}\sum_{C'\in C_{j+1}}
 n_Ce_{C,C'}a_{C'}
}{
\sum_{C\in C_j}n_Ca_C
}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

In particular the exact spectral birth ratio

\[
\boxed{
r_j:=\frac{(j+1)b_{j+1}}{b_j}=\lambda\bar h_j}
\tag{5.6}
\]

is a deterministic quotient quantity.

#### Proof

Equation (5.3) just groups the exact sum `m_j=sum_{|S|=j,S subset A0} a(S)` into `H`-orbits. For a representative `S_C`,

\[
P(x\in A\mid S_C\subseteq A)
=\frac{a(S_C\cup\{x\})}{a_C}.
\]

Grouping `x` by the orbit class of `S_C union {x}` gives (5.4). Multiplying by `n_Ca_C`, summing over `C`, and dividing by (5.3) gives (5.5). Equation (5.6) is the preserved exact recurrence. `□`

The cylinder numbers `a_C` themselves can be computed by the preceding full-group orbit/stabilizer identity. Thus a model with a manageable stabilizer quotient admits a finite exact certificate consisting only of orbit sizes, extension incidences, and cylinder counts. Orbit–stabilizer and association-scheme quotienting are classical; the point here is operational: it certifies the relevant low-degree-to-noise birth sequence without enumerating group elements or Fourier coefficients one by one.

### Corollary 5.2 — deterministic majorizing birth sequence

Suppose a finite calculation or symbolic orbit argument gives numbers `u_j` satisfying

\[
r_j\le u_j.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Set

\[
\widehat b_0=1,
\qquad
\widehat b_{j+1}=\frac{u_j}{j+1}\widehat b_j.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Then induction on `j` gives

\[
\boxed{b_j\le\widehat b_j\quad\text{for every }j.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Hence

\[
F(q)\le\sum_j\widehat b_jq^j.
\tag{5.10}
\]

This is the promised derandomization: a deterministic majorizing birth sequence can replace full probabilistic overlap analysis. It will matter below because pointwise subcriticality of `u_j` is not necessary.

## 6. B26 — sporadic supercritical levels and the exact cumulative barrier

The preceding affine theorem imposed one subcritical envelope at every degree. This run tests whether isolated violations are actually fatal.

Assume the positive-overlap sequence has support through degree `M` and no internal zero before `M`, as holds for the orbit-overlap factorial moments. Put

\[

\qquad
c_j(q):=\frac{t_{j+1}(q)}{t_j(q)}
=\frac{q r_j}{j+1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a cutoff `D<M`, iteration gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j>D}b_jq^j
=b_Dq^D\,\mathcal B_D(q),
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_D(q)
:=\sum_{k=1}^{M-D}
\prod_{i=D}^{D+k-1}c_i(q).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If only deterministic majorants `u_i>=r_i` are known, define

\[
\widehat c_i(q):=\frac{qu_i}{i+1},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal B}_D(q)
:=\sum_{k=1}^{M-D}
\prod_{i=D}^{D+k-1}\widehat c_i(q).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{j>D}b_jq^j
\le b_Dq^D\widehat{\mathcal B}_D(q).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Since `b_D<=Adv^{<=D}(P,Q)^2` and `q^j<=q` for `1<=j<=D`,

\[
\boxed{
F(q)
\le 1+q\,Adv^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
+Adv^{\le D}(P,Q)^2 q^D
\widehat{\mathcal B}_D(q).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus the genuinely relevant quantity is not whether **every** local continuation ratio is below one. Sporadic `c_j(q)>1` are harmless whenever the cumulative product barrier satisfies

\[
q^D\widehat{\mathcal B}_D(q)=O(1)
\tag{6.7}
\]

(and are asymptotically negligible if this tends to zero). I do **not** present (6.7) as a source-complete abstract answer: without the orbit quotient it is close to restating a spectral tail. Its value is as a deterministic certificate generated from physical orbit data by Section 5.

## 7. Exact sporadic stress: planted perfect k-partite hypermatchings

The 2026-09-18T01-41-39Z report already derived the exact spectrum for a planted perfect `k`-partite hypermatching (`k>=3`), so I do not redo the fixed-point count. Preserve

\[
\boxed{
b_j
=\frac{\lambda_n^j}{j!(n)_j^{k-2}},
\qquad 0\le j\le n.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Write

\[
\lambda_n=c_n n^{k-2},
\qquad 0<c_n\le C.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then the exact birth and local noised continuation ratios are

\[
\boxed{
r_j=\frac{\lambda_n}{(n-j)^{k-2}},}
\tag{7.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
c_j(q)
=\frac{q\lambda_n}{(j+1)(n-j)^{k-2}}.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### 7.1 Local supercriticality really occurs

At the terminal level,

\[
c_{n-1}(q)
=\frac{q\lambda_n}{n}
=q c_n n^{k-3}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Therefore:

- for every `k>=4` and every fixed `c_n -> c>0`, `c_{n-1}(q)->infinity`;
- for `k=3`, choosing any bounded `c>1/q` gives `c_{n-1}(q)->qc>1`.

So even a very regular model with bounded degree-one energy can contain genuinely supercritical high-degree continuation ratios. Uniform pointwise subcriticality is **not necessary**.

### 7.2 Nevertheless the whole high-degree tail is superexponentially small

For `1<=j<=n`,

\[
(n)_j=j!\binom nj
\ge j!\left(\frac nj\right)^j.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Using also `j! >= (j/e)^j`, (7.1)–(7.2) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_jq^j
&=\frac{(q\lambda_n)^j}{j!(n)_j^{k-2}}\\
&\le
\frac{(qC)^j n^{j(k-2)}}
{(j!)^{k-1}(n/j)^{j(k-2)}}\\
&\le
\boxed{
\left(\frac{qC e^{k-1}}{j}\right)^j.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Put `K=qCe^{k-1}`. If `D+1>2K`, then for every `j>D`,

\[
\left(\frac Kj\right)^j
\le\left(\frac K{D+1}\right)^j,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j>D}b_jq^j
\le
\frac{(K/(D+1))^{D+1}}
{1-K/(D+1)}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Thus the enormous terminal birth ratio in (7.5) is harmless: the spectral term arriving at that degree has already been driven to a superexponentially tiny scale by the preceding product of continuation ratios. The true boundary is cumulative/pathwise, not pointwise.
