## 3.1 The `r`-body parity Gibbs block

Fix `r>=3` and `|t|<1`, and put

\[
Q_{r,t}(x)=2^{-r}\bigl(1+t\chi_{[r]}(x)\bigr),
\qquad x\in\{\pm1\}^r.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently `t=tanh(alpha)` for a single parity factor `exp(alpha chi_[r])`. Let

\[
A=\sum_{i=1}^r(I-E_i)
\tag{3.2}
\]

be the single-site heat-bath generator. Every proper marginal of `Q_{r,t}` is uniform.

Take

\[
x=x_1,
\qquad
y=x_2\cdots x_r.
\]

Then `E_Q[xy]=t`. The degree-one source vector is exactly `x`. The vector

\[
v=\frac{y-tx}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}
\tag{3.3}
\]

lies in the source layer of ordinary algebraic degree `r-1`: for every monomial of degree at most `r-2`, the only possible lower-degree correlation of `y` is with `x`, and (3.3) subtracts it.

Write `q=1-t^2`. For site 1,

\[
E_1x=ty,
\]

while all other site updates leave `x` fixed. Therefore

\[
Ax=x-ty=q\,x-t\sqrt q\,v.
\tag{3.4}
\]

For `y`, site 1 leaves it fixed, while for each `i>=2`,

\[
E_i y=t x.
\]

Hence

\[
Ay=(r-1)(y-tx).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Using `y=tx+sqrt(q)v`, equations (3.4)-(3.5) give

\[
Av=-t\sqrt q\,x+(r-1+t^2)v.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus on the exact two-dimensional source-degree subspace `span{x,v}`,

\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
q & -t\sqrt q\\
-t\sqrt q & r-1+t^2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
N=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\0&r-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

## 3.2 Exact tilted obstruction

For the tilted form

\[
C_s(A,N)=\frac12(e^{-sN}Ae^{sN}+e^{sN}Ae^{-sN}),
\]

the off-diagonal entry in (3.7) is multiplied by

\[
c_s=\cosh(s(r-2)).
\]

Therefore the restriction is

\[
C_s|_{\{x,v\}}=
\begin{pmatrix}
q & -t\sqrt q\,c_s\\
-t\sqrt q\,c_s & r-1+t^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Normalize by `N^{-1/2}`. The generalized two-dimensional tilted-coercivity eigenvalue is

\[
\lambda_-^{(r,t)}(s)
=\frac12\left[
q+1+\frac{t^2}{r-1}
-\sqrt{\left(q-1-\frac{t^2}{r-1}\right)^2
+\frac{4t^2q}{r-1}c_s^2}
\right].
\tag{3.9}
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\frac{q}{r-1}
\left(r-1-t^2\sinh^2(s(r-2))\right).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Hence a **necessary** condition for global positive tilted coercivity is

\[
\boxed{
|t|\sinh(s(r-2))<\sqrt{r-1}.

\tag{3.11}

\[
L^2(Q_a)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{|B_a|}\mathcal H_{a,k}

N_a=\sum_k k\Pi_{a,k}.

\[
\boxed{N=\sum_{a=1}^mN_a.}

\[
e^{-sN}A_a e^{sN}

\[
\boxed{

# 5. B25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE: cancellation in the relative tilted form

## 5.1 A general co-deformation identity

Let `alpha -> (A_alpha,N_alpha)` be differentiable finite-dimensional self-adjoint families on a fixed Hilbert space and assume

\[
A_0=N_0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Put

\[
R_\alpha=A_\alpha-N_\alpha.
\]

Because `N_alpha` commutes with every function of itself,

\[
C_s(A_\alpha,N_\alpha)-N_\alpha
=
\frac12\left(
 e^{-sN_\alpha}R_\alpha e^{sN_\alpha}
+e^{sN_\alpha}R_\alpha e^{-sN_\alpha}
\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `R_0=0`, differentiating (5.2) at zero kills every derivative of the conjugating exponentials:

\[
\left.\frac d{d\alpha}\right|_0(C_s-N)
=
\frac12\left(
 e^{-sN_0}R_0'e^{sN_0}
+e^{sN_0}R_0'e^{-sN_0}
\right).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Now suppose the first derivative of the degree commutator vanishes,

\[
\left.\frac d{d\alpha}\right|_0[N_\alpha,A_\alpha]=0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since `A_0=N_0`,

\[
0=[N_0,A_0'-N_0']=[N_0,R_0'].
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus `R_0'` is source-degree preserving, and both conjugations in (5.3) disappear. We obtain the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\left.\frac d{d\alpha}\right|_0
\bigl(C_s(A_\alpha,N_\alpha)-N_\alpha\bigr)
=A_0'-N_0',
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

and the right-hand side commutes with `N_0`. In particular, the first variation of the **relative tilted coercivity** is independent of `s`.

Equation (5.6) counts the same cancellation in two languages:

- projection flow says `N_0'` can be extensive;
- degree leakage says `[N,A]'_0=0`;
- the relative form `C_s-N` subtracts exactly the extensive co-motion and keeps only the degree-preserving dynamical perturbation.

## 5.2 Zero-field pairwise Ising: uniform first-order relative form bound

For

\[
Q_\alpha(x)\propto
\exp\!\left(\alpha\sum_{\{i,j\}}J_{ij}x_ix_j\right)
\]

with heat-bath generator `A_alpha`, the prior B5 computation in the moving source basis gave, for `|S|=k`,

\[
A_\alpha u_S(\alpha)
=
k u_S(\alpha)
-\alpha\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\j\notin S}}
J_{ij}\,u_{S-i+j}(\alpha)
+O(\alpha^2).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore `R_0'=A_0'-N_0'` restricted to the product degree-`k` layer is the weighted exclusion-hopping operator

\[
(T_k a)(S)
=-\sum_{\substack{i\in S\\j\notin S}}
J_{ij}\,a(S-i+j).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Let

\[
J_{\rm row}=\max_i\sum_j|J_{ij}|.
\]

The absolute row sum in (5.8) is at most

\[
\sum_{i\in S}\sum_j|J_{ij}|
\le kJ_{\rm row}.
\]

Because the operator is symmetric, Schur's bound gives

\[
\|T_k\|_{2\to2}\le kJ_{\rm row}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Summing over homogeneous source layers,

\[
\boxed{
-J_{\rm row}N_0
\preceq
\left.\frac d{d\alpha}\right|_0(C_s-N)
\preceq
J_{\rm row}N_0,
\qquad\text{for every }s\ge0.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

This is dimension-free even though `||N_0'||=Theta(sqrt n)` may diverge.

### Consequence and limitation

For each fixed finite graph, (5.10) implies the infinitesimal tilted-coercivity slope is at worst `-J_row`, uniformly in `s`; the global source-flag motion is not the first-order obstruction. What is still missing for a source-wide high-temperature theorem is an **n-uniform finite-alpha remainder bound** for the relative form, e.g.

\[
C_s(A_\alpha,N_\alpha)
\succeq
\bigl(1-J_{\rm row}|\alpha|-C_s\alpha^2\bigr)N_\alpha
\tag{5.11}
