## 4. B12 — what is conserved: exact group-orbit overlap and stabilizer formulas

Consider a finite coordinate set `E` with a finite group `G` acting on it. Fix a support template `A_0 subseteq E`, draw `g` uniformly from `G`, and let

\[
A=gA_0.
\]

Against the homogeneous product-Bernoulli null

\[
Q_p=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)^{\otimes E},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{1-p}{p},
\]

construct `P` by forcing all coordinates in `A` to one and sampling the others according to the null. Put

\[
a(S):=\Pr(S\subseteq A).
\]

The standard orthonormal product basis gives

\[
\widehat L(S)=\lambda^{|S|/2}a(S),
\]

and therefore

\[
b_j=\lambda^j\sum_{|S|=j}a(S)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 — orbit/stabilizer cylinder identity

Let `O` be a `G`-orbit of `j`-subsets of `E`, and define

\[
N_O(A_0):=\#\{T\in O:T\subseteq A_0\}.
\]

For every `S in O`,

\[
\boxed{
a(S)=\frac{N_O(A_0)}{|O|}
      =\frac{N_O(A_0)|\operatorname{Stab}_G(S)|}{|G|}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

Count group elements `g` for which `S subseteq gA_0`. Equivalently, for some `T subseteq A_0` lying in the orbit `O`, we have `gT=S`. For each such `T`, the set of group elements sending `T` to `S` is a coset of `Stab_G(S)` and has cardinality `|Stab_G(S)|`. There are exactly `N_O(A_0)` admissible `T`. Dividing by `|G|` and using `|O|=|G|/|Stab_G(S)|` proves (4.2). `□`

Consequently, if

\[
m_j:=\sum_{|S|=j}a(S)^2,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
m_j
=\sum_{O\in G\backslash\binom Ej}\frac{N_O(A_0)^2}{|O|}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

There is also the exact two-copy interpretation

\[
\boxed{
m_j=\mathbb E\binom{|A\cap A'|}{j},
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

for independent orbit supports `A,A'`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
b_j=\lambda^j\sum_O\frac{N_O(A_0)^2}{|O|}
      =\lambda^j\mathbb E\binom{|A\cap A'|}{j}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This makes the full likelihood-ratio spectrum an orbit/stabilizer object, rather than an unspecified Fourier tail.

A second useful identity fixes one copy at the template:

\[
m_j=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq A_0\\|S|=j}}a(S),
\tag{4.6}
\]

because the overlap law of `(A,A')` equals that of `(A_0,A)` under the transitive orbit distribution.

## 5. B12/B3 — a model-checkable conditional-extension theorem

For `S subseteq A_0`, define the **conditional orbit-extension load**

\[
h(S)
:=\sum_{x\in A_0\setminus S}
\Pr(x\in A\mid S\subseteq A)
=\sum_{x\in A_0\setminus S}\frac{a(S\cup\{x\})}{a(S)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every term in (5.1) is explicitly computable from orbit/stabilizer data by (4.2). It has the direct interpretation

\[
h(S)=\mathbb E[|A_0\cap A|-|S|\mid S\subseteq A].
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus this is an ordinary conditional-overlap property of the planted orbit, not a full-spectrum assumption.

### Lemma 5.1 — exact extension recurrence

For every `0<=j<|A_0|`,

\[
\boxed{
(j+1)m_{j+1}
=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq A_0\\|S|=j}}a(S)h(S).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

Each `(j+1)`-subset `T subseteq A_0` is counted exactly `j+1` times as a pair `(S,x)` with `S subset T`, `|S|=j`, and `x in T\setminus S`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{|S|=j}a(S)h(S)
&=\sum_{|S|=j}\sum_{x\in A_0\setminus S}a(S\cup\{x\})\\
&=(j+1)\sum_{|T|=j+1}a(T)
=(j+1)m_{j+1},
\end{aligned}
\]

using (4.6). `□`

### Theorem 5.2 — affine orbit-extension control implies the pressure criterion

Assume there are nonnegative numbers `alpha,beta` such that for every `S subseteq A_0`, with `j=|S|`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda h(S)\le \alpha+\beta j.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
(j+1)b_{j+1}\le(\alpha+\beta j)b_j
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

for every `j`, and for every `q` with `q beta<1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)\le\frac{q\alpha}{1-q\beta}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

#### Proof

Multiply (5.3) by `lambda^(j+1)` and use (5.4):

\[
\begin{aligned}
(j+1)b_{j+1}
&=\lambda^{j+1}\sum_{|S|=j}a(S)h(S)\\
&\le(\alpha+\beta j)\lambda^j\sum_{|S|=j}a(S)
=(\alpha+\beta j)b_j.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now multiply by `q^(j+1)` and sum over `j`:

\[
qF'(q)
\le q\alpha F(q)+q\beta\,qF'(q).
\]

If `q beta<1`, division by `F(q)(1-q beta)` gives (5.6). `□`

### Corollary 5.3 — source-scale consequence

Let `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}` and suppose

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\le A.

If an orbit representation as above satisfies, for some fixed `q_0 in (0,1]`,

\quad\text{for some }\delta>0,
\qquad
\alpha_n=o(D_n),
\tag{5.7}
\]

then uniformly for `0<q<=q_0`,

\[
\frac{\mu_n(q)}{D_n+1}\to0.
\]

Hence (3.4) yields

\[
\sup_n\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P_n\|Q_n)<\infty
\]

for every fixed `q<=q_0` (after discarding finitely many `n`), and therefore a uniform total-variation gap from one against **every measurable test**. This is stronger than excluding a named polynomial-degree class.

## 7. B3 — stronger arbitrary-likelihood criterion from Poisson-relative log-concavity

The orbit theorem is still a positive-support representation. We now return to the arbitrary signed/cancellative graded likelihood-ratio setting of Section 3.

Assume the nonnegative energy sequence has no internal zeros and satisfies

\[
\boxed{
j b_j^2\ge(j+1)b_{j-1}b_{j+1}
\qquad(j\ge1).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently, the sequence

\[
c_j:=j!b_j
\]

is log-concave. This is the classical Poisson-relative factorial log-concavity inequality; depending on convention it appears under strong/ultra log-concavity terminology. The coefficient-shape property itself is prior art.

### Theorem 7.1 — factorial log-concavity collapses the entire tail to degree one

Under (7.1), for every `j>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
(j+1)b_{j+1}\le b_1b_j.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Consequently, for every `0<=q<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
F(q)\le e^{qb_1},
\qquad
\mu(q)\le qb_1.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

In particular, for every `D>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(P\|Q)=F(1)
\le e^{b_1}
\le \exp\!\big(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2\big).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus any sequence `(P_n,Q_n)` whose normalized degree-energy law obeys (7.1) and whose source low-degree advantage is uniformly bounded is not strongly distinguishable even **before** noise, by any measurable test.

#### Proof

Log-concavity of `c_j` with no internal zeros implies that successive ratios are nonincreasing:

\[
\frac{c_{j+1}}{c_j}
\le\frac{c_j}{c_{j-1}}
\le\cdots\le\frac{c_1}{c_0}=b_1.
\]

Since `c_{j+1}/c_j=(j+1)b_{j+1}/b_j`, this proves (7.2). Iterating gives

\[
b_j\le\frac{b_1^j}{j!},
\]

hence `F(q)<=exp(qb_1)`. Summing (7.2) after multiplication by `q^(j+1)` also gives

\[
qF'(q)\le qb_1F(q),
\]

which is the barycenter estimate in (7.3). Finally `b_1<=sum_{j<=D}b_j=Adv^{<=D}^2`, proving (7.4). `□`

### Strongly-Rayleigh spectral-sample corollary

On a finite Boolean product space, normalize the squared Fourier weights into the spectral-sample measure

\[
\nu_{\rm spec}(S)=\frac{\widehat L(S)^2}{\|L\|_2^2}.
\]

If this subset measure is strongly Rayleigh, its rank-generating polynomial is real-rooted and its rank sequence has the classical ultra-log-concavity properties. In particular the stronger Poisson-relative inequality (7.1) follows, so Theorem 7.1 applies. This corollary is a bridge to established stable-polynomial/negative-dependence theory; neither strong Rayleigh nor its rank-sequence consequences are claimed new.

### Why this is useful but not source closure

Theorem 7.1 is genuinely source-general in the sense that it permits arbitrary signed/cancellative likelihood ratios and is not tied to a planted graph, a Gaussian model, or a positive support mixture. But (7.1) is still a sufficient spectral-shape condition, not a necessary-and-sufficient characterization of all natural statistical models. It therefore remains `PARTIAL` for Kireeva's broad request.

## 8. B22 — read the failure: Mao is a high Rayleigh-quotient / influence construction

\qquad

\tag{8.1}

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)
=\frac{\langle T_\rho L,N T_\rho L\rangle}

\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus the pressure barycenter is exactly the normalized Fourier total-influence/number-operator Rayleigh quotient of the noised likelihood ratio.

\boxed{
