## 3. Exact source convention and low-degree identity

Let `(Omega,Q)` be a finite probability space with full support, `P<<Q`, and `L=dP/dQ`. Let

\[
L^2(Q)=\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal H_M,
\qquad \mathcal H_0=\mathrm{span}\{1\},
\]

be the exact polynomial-degree grading. Write `Pi_{<=D}` for projection onto degrees at most `D`. Under the source convention,

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
=
\max_{\deg f\le D}
\frac{\mathbb E_P f-\mathbb E_Q f}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_Q f}}.
\]

The standard Hilbert-space duality gives

\[
\boxed{
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\right)^2
=\|\Pi_{\le D}(L-1)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the quantity that must remain bounded at `D=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`.

For product-resampling noise with retention `rho in (0,1)`, exact degree `j` is multiplied by `rho^j`. Therefore if the squared Fourier/chaos coefficients are aggregated into nonnegative atoms `beta(a)>=0` of degree `d(a)>=1`, then

\[
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}\right)^2
=\sum_{d(a)\le D}\beta(a),
\qquad
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q)
=\sum_a \rho^{2d(a)}\beta(a).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The previous stuck report identified the absence of a *structural reason* for the second sum to be controlled by the first. The next theorem supplies exactly such a reason.

## 4. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: two-copy witness systems and interface factorization

The useful language is neither a diagonal leverage envelope nor a raw sequence of spectral coefficients. It is a **positive two-copy witness expansion with local reassembly**.

### 4.1 Definition: low-interface factorization witness

Fix a cutoff `D` and a scale `ell` with `1<=ell<=D`. Let `A` be the atom set in (3.2). A **low-interface factorization witness at scale `(ell,D)`** consists of the following data for every atom `a` with `d(a)>D`:

- an integer `m(a)>=2`;
- an ordered tuple
  \[
  \delta(a)=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)
  \]
  with
  \[
  \ell\le d(a_i)\le D,
  \qquad
  d(a)=\sum_{i=1}^m d(a_i);
  \tag{4.1}
  \]
- a factorization-cost number `kappa>=0` such that
  \[
  \beta(a)
  \le
  e^{\kappa(m-1)}\prod_{i=1}^m\beta(a_i);
  \tag{4.2}
  \]
- a reassembly-multiplicity number `mu>=0` such that for every fixed ordered tuple `(a_1,...,a_m)`,
  \[
  \#\{a:\delta(a)=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\}
  \le
  e^{\mu(m-1)}.
  \tag{4.3}
  \]

The **reassembly entropy per interface** is

\[
\Xi:=\kappa+\mu.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The point is that (4.2)–(4.3) are local decomposition/reconstruction conditions. They do not say that the high-degree tail is small. They say that every high-degree two-copy witness is assembled from low-degree witnesses, with a controlled price per gluing operation.

In Chen–Li's planted-subgraph proof, the correct atoms are not merely abstract graph isomorphism types. They are the underlying *isomorphism triples* that give a positive two-copy count. Restricting one triple to a low-interface edge partition makes the piece data essentially injective, while shared vertices contribute the reassembly-label cost. Their displayed product estimate has the form

\[
\beta(F)\le n^{\operatorname{ov}(F;T_1,\ldots,T_m)}\prod_i\beta(T_i),
\]

and treewidth gives

\[
\operatorname{ov}(F;T_1,\ldots,T_m)
\le a_n(m-1)
\]

with `a_n=tw(Gamma_n)+1`. Thus the graph theorem's interface budget is precisely an instance of `Xi=O(a_n log n)` after the positive witness refinement. This is the conceptual port selected by the previous Section-I report.

## 5. B30 — LOCALIZE AND GLUE: abstract interface bootstrap theorem

### Theorem 5.1 — two-copy low-interface bootstrap

Assume (3.2) and a low-interface factorization witness (4.1)–(4.3). Put

\[
A_D:=\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q),
\qquad
S_{\rho,\ell,D}
:=
\sum_{\ell\le d(a)\le D}
\rho^{2d(a)}\beta(a).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
S_{\rho,\ell,D}
\le
\rho^{2\ell}A_D^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

If

\[
q:=e^{\Xi}S_{\rho,\ell,D}<1,
\tag{5.3}
\]

then the complete high-degree noisy tail satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d(a)>D}\rho^{2d(a)}\beta(a)
\le
S_{\rho,\ell,D}\frac{q}{1-q}
\le
\rho^{2\ell}A_D^2
\frac{e^{\Xi}\rho^{2\ell}A_D^2}
{1-e^{\Xi}\rho^{2\ell}A_D^2}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q)
\le
A_D^2
+
S_{\rho,\ell,D}\frac{q}{1-q}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

In particular, for a sequence of problems, if

\[
\sup_n A_{D_n}<\infty,
\qquad
\ell_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\Xi_n=o(\ell_n),
\tag{5.6}
\]

then for every fixed `rho in (0,1)`, `q_n->0` and `chi^2(T_rho P_n||Q_n)=O(1)`. Hence strong distinguishing after noise is impossible. If additionally `A_{D_n}->0`, then `chi^2(T_rho P_n||Q_n)->0`, so total variation tends to zero.

### Proof

Equation (5.2) is immediate from nonnegativity and the source low-degree identity:

\[
S_{\rho,\ell,D}
\le
\rho^{2\ell}
\sum_{d(a)\le D}\beta(a)
=
\rho^{2\ell}A_D^2.
\]

For `m>=2`, sum the noisy weights of high atoms having `m(a)=m`. Degree additivity and (4.2) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a:m(a)=m}\rho^{2d(a)}\beta(a)
&\le
 e^{\kappa(m-1)}
 \sum_{a:m(a)=m}
 \prod_{i=1}^m
 \rho^{2d(a_i)}\beta(a_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Group atoms `a` by the ordered tuple `delta(a)`. By (4.3), every tuple has at most `e^{mu(m-1)}` preimages. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{a:m(a)=m}\rho^{2d(a)}\beta(a)
&\le
 e^{(\kappa+\mu)(m-1)}
 \sum_{(a_1,\ldots,a_m)}
 \prod_{i=1}^m
 \rho^{2d(a_i)}\beta(a_i)\\
&\le
 e^{\Xi(m-1)}
 \left(
 \sum_{\ell\le d(b)\le D}
 \rho^{2d(b)}\beta(b)
 \right)^m\\
&=
 e^{\Xi(m-1)}S_{\rho,\ell,D}^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Summing over `m>=2`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{d(a)>D}\rho^{2d(a)}\beta(a)
&\le
\sum_{m\ge2}e^{\Xi(m-1)}S_{\rho,\ell,D}^m\\
&=
S_{\rho,\ell,D}
\sum_{m\ge2}
\left(e^{\Xi}S_{\rho,\ell,D}\right)^{m-1}\\
&=
S_{\rho,\ell,D}\frac{q}{1-q},
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves (5.4). Adding the degree-at-most-`D` noisy contribution, which is at most `A_D^2`, gives (5.5).

Under (5.6),

\[
\log q_n
\le
\Xi_n+2\ell_n\log\rho+O(1)
=
-2|\log\rho|\ell_n+o(\ell_n)+O(1)
\to-\infty,
\]

so `q_n->0`. This proves the asymptotic statements. `□`

### Testing consequence

The earlier track proof established the elementary overlap bound

\[
1-\|R-Q\|_{TV}
\ge
\frac{1}{2+\chi^2(R\|Q)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Therefore a uniform finite right-hand side in (5.5) yields a uniform total-variation gap from one, ruling out **strong distinguishing by any measurable test**, which is stronger than ruling out only the specified polynomial class.

### Why this is not the old leverage-tail assumption in disguise

The previous leverage criterion directly assumed control of an expectation of a high-grade profile. The present hypothesis instead supplies a *local recursive witness*: high objects must decompose into low objects and the only loss is a controlled interface/reassembly entropy. Once that local statement is proved, (5.4) derives the complete tail. This is exactly the distinction demanded by the previous stuck report's blocker (2.1).

## 6. B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY ON PURPOSE: matching versus Mao

The same vertex-relabeling symmetry can hide radically different reassembly structures. Two examples make the distinction explicit.

### 6.1 Dense planted matching laboratory

Let `Q=G(n,p)` for fixed `p in (0,1)`, let `lambda=(1-p)/p`, choose a uniformly random matching `M` of size `m<=n/2`, and force the edges of `M` present. This is a genuine graph `k=2` symmetry class, but its overlap geometry is tree-like/disjoint rather than code-like.

Use the orthonormal edge coordinate

\[
r_e(X)=\frac{X_e-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}.
\]

For a labeled set `S` of `j` edges, the Fourier coefficient is zero unless `S` is a matching. If `S` is a `j`-matching, then

\[
\mathbb P(S\subseteq M)
=
\frac{M(n-2j,m-j)}{M(n,m)}
=
\frac{2^j(m)_j}{(n)_{2j}},
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

\[
M(n,j)=\frac{(n)_{2j}}{2^j j!}
\]

is the number of `j`-matchings on `[n]`. Hence the exact degree-`j` likelihood-ratio energy is

\[
\boxed{
 b_j
=
\lambda^j
\frac{2^j(m)_j^2}{j!(n)_{2j}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The consecutive ratio is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_j}
=
\lambda
\frac{2(m-j)^2}
{(j+1)(n-2j)(n-2j-1)}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is the opposite of a Mao-style spectral cliff: once `m<=alpha n` for any fixed `alpha<1/2`, there is a constant `C=C(alpha,p)` such that, for all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
\frac{b_{j+1}}{b_j}\le \frac{C}{j+1}
\qquad(1\le j<m).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Indeed `n-2j>=n-2m>= (1-2alpha)n` and `n-2j-1>=((1-2alpha)/2)n` for large `n`, while `(m-j)^2<=alpha^2 n^2`. Thus one may take for example

\[
C=\frac{4\lambda\alpha^2}{(1-2\alpha)^2}
\]

for large `n`. Iterating,

\[
b_j\le b_1\frac{C^{j-1}}{j!},
\]

so even before noise the full chi-square is uniformly bounded:

\[
\chi^2(P\|Q)
=\sum_{j\ge1}b_j
\le
b_1\frac{e^C-1}{C}
=O_{\alpha,p}(1).
\tag{6.5}
\]

This laboratory is not claimed as a source solution; it is an explicit check that graph vertex symmetry alone does not determine the outcome. A low-interface/disjoint plant has smooth factorial spectral decay.

### 6.2 Mao necessarily violates low-interface factorization

Mao's construction has

\[
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)=0
\]

through `D_n=Theta((log n)^(r-1))`, while fixed-noise strong distinguishing remains possible. In any nonnegative expansion (3.2), zero low-degree advantage forces

\[
\beta(a)=0
\qquad\text{for every }d(a)\le D_n.
\]

If the low-interface factorization (4.2) held for every surviving high atom, then every high atom would satisfy

\[
\beta(a)
\le e^{\kappa(m-1)}\prod_i 0=0,
\]

forcing all high coefficients to vanish as well. That contradicts the existence of a nontrivial noised likelihood-ratio signal. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Mao's counterexample cannot admit a complete low-interface factorization through its zero low-degree sector.}
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is the structural exclusion requested by the previous stuck report. It does not mention rank, Reed–Muller codes, or decoding in the hypothesis. The failure is precisely *indecomposable high-degree reassembly*: a global algebraic signal exists that cannot be reconstructed as a controlled product of low-degree positive witnesses.

The same logic applies to any exact-moment-matching counterexample with a surviving noisy signal.

## 7. Relation to Chen–Li and what is genuinely abstracted

Chen–Li already prove the planted-subgraph theorem; this run does **not** claim their result. The new organizational point is the following abstract separation:

1. **Analytic module:** once a positive two-copy expansion and a low-interface factorization witness exist, Theorem 5.1 is a generic geometric-series bootstrap from bounded low degree to bounded full noisy chi-square.
2. **Combinatorial module:** prove (4.2)–(4.3) from the data-generating mechanism. Chen–Li do this with isomorphism triples plus tree decompositions. Other models could in principle use factor graphs, tensor networks, dependency hypergraphs, separator theorems, or latent-support overlap decompositions.
3. **Obstruction module:** Mao shows that not every symmetric/noisy model admits such a decomposition; exact low-degree moment matching plus a surviving global signal is an immediate certificate of failure.

This is materially different from the earlier leverage-tail criterion: it says what *kind of local structure* would imply the required tail control and why the code/rank obstruction lies outside that class.

I am not yet claiming that every Hsieh positive regime can be represented in exactly this witness form, nor that Theorem 5.1 is absent from all prior literature under another name. Those are audit/prior-art tasks.
