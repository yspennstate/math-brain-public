## 3. B11 DUALIZE — replace the global leakage multiplier by an exact localized leakage functional

The previous report proved the uniform spectral-gate bound

\[
\|e^{-tA}f\|
\le \|Pf\|+
\left(\frac{\epsilon}{(1-\tau)\lambda}+e^{-\tau\lambda t}\right)\|f\|,
\]

for `C=HAH>=lambda`, `B=HAP`, `H=I-P`. Its `epsilon*||f||/lambda` term is sharp as a uniform operator bound, but it can be grossly pessimistic for a particular likelihood ratio if the high-energy mass lies in sectors that do not couple back through `B*`.

### Theorem 3.1 (initial-vector localized low-energy leakage)

Let `A>=0` be self-adjoint on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. Let `P` be an orthogonal projection, `H=I-P`,

\[
C=HAH|_{\operatorname{Ran}H},\qquad B=HAP,
\]

and assume

\[
C\ge \lambda I_H,\qquad \lambda>0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Fix `0<tau<1`. For `h in Ran(H)`, define the localized leakage functional

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{\tau}(h)
:=
\int_0^\infty
 e^{\tau\lambda s}
 \|B^*e^{-sC}h\|\,ds.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then for every `p in Ran(P)`, every `h in Ran(H)`, and every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}(p+h)\|
\le
\|p\|+\Lambda_{\tau}(h)
+e^{-\tau\lambda t}\|p+h\|.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\Lambda_{\tau}(h)
\le
\frac{\|B\|}{(1-\tau)\lambda}\|h\|,
\tag{3.4}
\]

so `(3.3)` strictly refines the previous uniform spectral-gate theorem whenever the actual high vector weakly couples to the low sector.

If `h` lies in the `C`-spectral subspace `[Lambda,infinity)` with `Lambda>tau lambda`, then the sharper elementary bound

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{\tau}(h)
\le
\frac{\|B\|}{\Lambda-\tau\lambda}\|h\|
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

holds. In particular `Lambda_tau(h)=0` whenever `B^*e^{-sC}h=0` for all `s>=0`.

#### Proof

Let

\[
E=\mathbf 1_{[0,\tau\lambda]}(A)
\]

be the full-generator low-energy spectral projection, and let `A_E=A|_{Ran E}`. Put

\[
X=HE:\operatorname{Ran}E\to\operatorname{Ran}H,
\qquad
Y=PE.
\]

Because `E` commutes with `A`,

\[
HAE=HEA_E=XA_E.
\]

On the other hand,

\[
HAE=HAP\,PE+HAH\,HE=BY+CX.
\]

Therefore

\[
CX-XA_E=-BY.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Since `C>=lambda` and `0<=A_E<=tau lambda`, the Sylvester spectra are separated by `(1-tau)lambda`. The standard integral solution is

\[
X=-\int_0^\infty e^{-sC}BYe^{sA_E}\,ds.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Taking adjoints and applying to a particular high vector `h` gives

\[
EHh=X^*h
=-\int_0^\infty e^{sA_E}Y^*B^*e^{-sC}h\,ds.
\]

Hence, using `||e^{sA_E}||<=e^{tau lambda s}` and `||Y||<=1`,

\[
\|EHh\|
\le
\int_0^\infty e^{\tau\lambda s}
\|B^*e^{-sC}h\|\,ds
=\Lambda_\tau(h).
\tag{3.8}
\]

Now spectrally split the full semigroup:

\[
e^{-tA}(p+h)
=e^{-tA}E(p+h)+e^{-tA}(I-E)(p+h).
\]

The first term has norm at most

\[
\|E(p+h)\|
\le \|Ep\|+\|Eh\|
\le \|p\|+\Lambda_\tau(h),
\]

while the second is at most `e^{-tau lambda t}||p+h||`. This proves `(3.3)`.

For `(3.4)`, use `||e^{-sC}||<=e^{-lambda s}`. For `(3.5)`, on the assumed spectral support `||e^{-sC}h||<=e^{-Lambda s}||h||`. `square`

### Source-degree corollary

Take `P=P_D`, the `L^2(Q)` orthogonal projection onto the source's ordinary algebraic degree-`<=D` mean-zero subspace, and `f=L-1`. Then `||P_Df||=Adv^{<=D}(P,Q)`. Let `H_D=I-P_D`, `C_D=H_DAH_D`, `B_D=H_DAP_D`, and define

\[
\Lambda_{\tau,D}(f)
=
\int_0^\infty e^{\tau\lambda_Ds}
\|P_DAH_D e^{-sC_D}H_Df\|\,ds.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{\chi^2(P S_t\|Q)}
\le
Adv^{\le D}(P,Q)
+\Lambda_{\tau,D}(L-1)
+e^{-\tau\lambda_Dt}\sqrt{\chi^2(P\|Q)}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

The new term is not a global norm of the entire likelihood ratio; it asks only how the **actual high-degree likelihood-ratio vector**, after internal high-sector damping, reaches the low source-degree sector through the generator coupling.

**Outcome B11:** success. The `rho_D sqrt(chi2)` floor is unavoidable uniformly over all vectors, but it is not intrinsic to a particular statistical family. The correct localized object is a resolvent/Sylvester-weighted source-high-sector coupling such as `(3.9)`.

## 4. B30 — a signed/cancellative degree-band path barrier

The remaining `e^{-tau lambda_D t}||f||` in `(3.10)` is still globally pessimistic when the actual likelihood-ratio mass starts far above the first high layer. To keep the source's algebraic degree and avoid positivity assumptions, decompose by orthogonal algebraic-degree layers and compare **norms** rather than coefficients.

Let

\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{j=0}^J \mathcal H_j,
\qquad \Pi_j:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H_j
\]

be any finite orthogonal grading (for the source application, `H_j=V_j\ominus V_{j-1}` for the ordinary polynomial filtration). Write

\[
A_{jk}=\Pi_jA\Pi_k,
\qquad
a_j=\inf_{0\ne v\in\mathcal H_j}
\frac{\langle v,A_{jj}v\rangle}{\|v\|^2},
\qquad
c_{jk}=\|A_{jk}\|\quad(j\ne k).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the Metzler matrix `G` by

\[
G_{jj}=-a_j,
\qquad
G_{jk}=c_{jk}\ (j\ne k).
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (signed degree-band comparison)

For `u(t)=e^{-tA}f`, put

\[
x_j(t)=\|\Pi_j u(t)\|,
\qquad
x_j(0)=\|\Pi_jf\|.
\]

Then componentwise

\[
\boxed{x(t)\le e^{tG}x(0).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}f\|_2
\le
\|e^{tG}x(0)\|_{\ell_2}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The same comparison separately controls the source-low output `(j<=D)` and source-high output `(j>D)`.

#### Proof

Whenever `x_j(t)>0`, differentiate its norm:

\[
\frac{d}{dt}x_j(t)
=
-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\langle \Pi_ju,A\,u\rangle}{x_j(t)}.
\]

Split the input into degree bands. The diagonal term satisfies

\[
-\frac{\langle \Pi_ju,A_{jj}\Pi_ju\rangle}{x_j}
\le -a_jx_j,
\]

while every off-diagonal term is at most `c_{jk}x_k` by Cauchy–Schwarz. Therefore

\[
D^+x_j(t)
\le -a_jx_j(t)+\sum_{k\ne j}c_{jk}x_k(t)
=(Gx(t))_j.
\tag{4.5}
\]

At `x_j=0` the same inequality holds for the upper Dini derivative by the triangle inequality / standard norm regularization. Since `G` is Metzler, the positive-cone comparison theorem gives `(4.3)`. Orthogonality of the bands gives `(4.4)`. `square`

### Exact path expansion

Writing `s_0=0`, every entry of the nonnegative matrix `e^{tG}` has the Dyson path representation

\[
(e^{tG})_{jk}
=
\sum_{m\ge0}
\sum_{j_0=k,\ldots,j_m=j}
\int_{0<s_1<\cdots<s_m<t}
 e^{-a_{j_m}(t-s_m)}
\prod_{\ell=1}^m
\left[
 c_{j_\ell j_{\ell-1}}
 e^{-a_{j_{\ell-1}}(s_\ell-s_{\ell-1})}
\right]
 ds_1\cdots ds_m,
\tag{4.6}
\]

with the `m=0` term interpreted as `delta_{jk}e^{-a_kt}`.

Thus the noised norm is controlled by an explicit **sum over source-degree paths**, with every degree change paid by the corresponding commutator block and every residence time paid by the relevant diagonal coercivity.

If the generator has degree bandwidth `w`, so `c_{jk}=0` for `|j-k|>w`, then an initial band `k>D` cannot reach any source-low band `j<=D` in fewer than

\[
r(k)=\left\lceil\frac{k-D}{w}\right\rceil
\tag{4.7}
\]

off-diagonal steps. If in addition

\[
\kappa=\max_k\sum_{j\ne k}c_{jk}<\infty,
\]

then dropping all diagonal damping gives the coarse but fully explicit bound

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\le D}(e^{tG})_{jk}
\le
\sum_{m\ge r(k)}\frac{(\kappa t)^m}{m!}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The exact `(4.6)` is usually much sharper because it retains degree-dependent damping `a_j` and the actual allowed path graph.

**Outcome B30:** success. This is the signed/cancellative analogue of the earlier positive birth-path barrier: positivity of likelihood-ratio coefficients is not needed, only operator block norms. A source-valid sufficient condition can therefore be stated in terms of a model-checkable degree-transition graph and coercivity profile rather than total chi-square.

## 5. B2 — attack the negation: small relative commutator + enormous chi-square can still be harmless

The previous uniform gate suggested a budget involving `rho_D*sqrt(chi2(P||Q))`. Is the full second moment genuinely necessary, or merely an artifact of taking an operator norm before looking at where the likelihood-ratio mass lives? The following exact family shows it is an artifact.

### Construction

Fix once and for all the three-spin full-support pairwise Ising block `q_0` from the preceding runs for which the heat-bath generator `A_0` has a nonzero ordinary source-degree `U_1 -> U_2` block. Let

\[
c=\|P_{U_2}A_0P_{U_1}\|>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For an integer cutoff `d->infinity`, introduce:

1. the fixed three-spin gadget with null `q_0`;
2. `d-1` independent uniform carrier bits `Y`;
3. `M_d` independent uniform parity blocks `Z^{(b)}` of size

\[
R_d=d+1,
\]

where

\[
M_d=\left\lfloor\frac{e^{\sqrt d}}{d+1}\right\rfloor.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The full null is the product

\[
Q_d=q_0\otimes U_{d-1}\otimes
\bigotimes_{b=1}^{M_d}U_{R_d}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Let the reversible noise generator be

\[
A_d=A_0+N_Y+\sum_{b=1}^{M_d}N_{Z^{(b)}},
\tag{5.4}
\]

where each `N` is the ordinary product heat-bath number operator on its uniform coordinates. The source degree is ordinary total algebraic degree in all coordinates. Let `P_d` project onto mean-zero degree `<=d`.

Fix `0<eta<1` and define the alternative likelihood ratio

\[
\boxed{
L_d
=
\prod_{b=1}^{M_d}
(1+\eta\,\chi_b),
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

where `chi_b` is the full parity of the `R_d=d+1` bits in block `b`. This is strictly positive and normalized because each parity has uniform mean zero.

### Exact low-degree advantage

Expanding `(5.5)`, every nonconstant term is a product of at least one full parity block and therefore has ordinary algebraic degree at least `R_d=d+1`. The remaining gadget and carrier coordinates are absent from `L_d`. Hence

\[
\boxed{P_d(L_d-1)=0,\qquad Adv^{\le d}(P_d,Q_d)=0.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

### The generator really has a small but nonzero source-degree commutator

All uniform heat-bath pieces preserve algebraic degree exactly, so only the fixed gadget can contribute to `[A_d,P_d]`. Therefore

\[
\epsilon_d:=\|[A_d,P_d]\|
\le 2\|A_0\|.
\tag{5.7}
\]

It is also bounded below uniformly away from zero. Let `u_1 in U_1`, `u_2 in U_2` be normalized vectors witnessing `(5.1)`, and multiply each by the full carrier parity `chi_Y`, of degree `d-1`. Then `u_1chi_Y` has total degree `d` while `u_2chi_Y` has degree `d+1`, so

\[
\epsilon_d\ge c.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For high-sector coercivity, decompose the fixed gadget into its four local orthogonal algebraic layers `U_a`, `0<=a<=3`, and the uniform coordinates by exact degree `r`. Any vector orthogonal to total degree `<=d` must have `a+r>d`; since `a<=3`, necessarily `r>=d-2`. The uniform number operator alone therefore yields

\[
\boxed{\lambda_d\ge d-2.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Conversely the test vector `u_2chi_Y` has Rayleigh quotient at most `d-1+||A_0||`, so

\[
\lambda_d\le d-1+\|A_0\|.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Combining `(5.7)`–`(5.10)`,

\[
\boxed{
\rho_d:=\epsilon_d/\lambda_d=\Theta(1/d)\to0.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

### Pre-noise chi-square is enormous

Independence of the parity blocks gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(P_d\|Q_d)
=(1+\eta^2)^{M_d}.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

Thus `sqrt(chi2)` grows like `exp(Theta(M_d))`, while `rho_d` decays only polynomially. In particular

\[
\rho_d\sqrt{\chi^2(P_d\|Q_d)}\to\infty
\tag{5.13}
\]

astronomically fast. The previous global gate is therefore useless on this family.

### Nevertheless fixed noise makes the models statistically indistinguishable

The likelihood ratio is independent of the Ising gadget and carrier. Each block parity is an exact eigenfunction of its uniform heat-bath number operator with eigenvalue `R_d=d+1`. Hence for every fixed `t>0`,

\[
S_tL_d
=
\prod_{b=1}^{M_d}
\left(1+\eta e^{-(d+1)t}\chi_b\right),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(P_dS_t\|Q_d)
=
\left(1+\eta^2e^{-2(d+1)t}\right)^{M_d}.
}
\tag{5.14}
\]

Because

\[
M_de^{-2(d+1)t}
\le
\frac{\exp(\sqrt d-2td-2t)}{d+1}
\longrightarrow0,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_dS_t\|Q_d)\to0,
\qquad
\|P_dS_t-Q_d\|_{TV}\to0.
}
\tag{5.15}
\]

This is not merely an off-source toy scale. The ambient coordinate count is

\[
N_d=3+(d-1)+M_d(d+1)=d+2+M_d(d+1),
\]

so

\[
\log N_d=\sqrt d+o(1),
\qquad
\boxed{d=(1+o(1))(\log N_d)^2.}
\tag{5.16}
\]

Thus the cutoff lies exactly in Kireeva's required `D=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}` regime.

### Why the localized criteria see the truth

Let `f_d=L_d-1`. The entire span generated by the parity-block products appearing in `f_d` is invariant under `A_d`, lies in the source-high sector, and never couples to the Ising gadget/carrier transition that creates the nonzero global commutator. Therefore

\[
B_d^*e^{-sC_d}H_df_d=0
\quad\text{for every }s\ge0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\Lambda_{\tau,d}(f_d)=0.}
\tag{5.17}
\]

Likewise, in the band-path matrix `G`, the bands occupied by `f_d` have no paths into the low source-degree bands through the only non-degree-preserving gadget edge. The exact path barrier therefore retains the true strong damping instead of multiplying the unrelated global commutator by the whole second moment.

**Outcome B2:** decisive success against the negation. The full `sqrt(chi2)` multiplier is a uniform-proof artifact, not a necessary statistical obstruction.
