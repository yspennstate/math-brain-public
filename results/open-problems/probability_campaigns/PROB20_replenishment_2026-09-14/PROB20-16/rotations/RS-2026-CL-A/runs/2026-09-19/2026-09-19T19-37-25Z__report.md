# 3. B18 — say it in another language: generator-energy spectral type

The predecessor's B27 theorem was exact when noise was diagonal in source degree. The obvious concern is that a natural nonproduct Gibbs heat-bath generator is not diagonal in ordinary source degree. The correct language is therefore not source-degree coefficients but the **spectral measure of the reversible generator itself**.

## 3.1 Reversible semigroup setup

Let `Q_n` be a reference law and let

\[
\mathsf A_n\ge0
\]

be a self-adjoint Markov generator on `L^2(Q_n)`, with reversible semigroup

\[
\mathsf S_{n,t}=e^{-t\mathsf A_n}.
\]

Let

\[
h_n=\frac{dP_n}{dQ_n}-1\in L^2_0(Q_n).
\]

Write `E_n(B)` for the spectral projector of `A_n` on a Borel set `B subset [0,infinity)`. Define the positive spectral measure

\[
\nu_n(B):=\|E_n(B)h_n\|_{L^2(Q_n)}^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then the spectral theorem gives the exact noised collision norm

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathsf S_{n,t}h_n\|_2^2
=
\int_{[0,\infty)} e^{-2t\lambda}\,d\nu_n(\lambda).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

No commutation with source degree is assumed. The generator may mix every ordinary polynomial degree.

## 3.2 Unit energy shells and spectral exponential type

For integers `m>=0`, define shell masses

\[
a_{n,m}:=\nu_n([m,m+1)),
\tag{3.3}
\]

and the energy-shell exponential type

\[
\boxed{
R_n^{\mathsf A}
:=
\sup_{m:a_{n,m}>0}
\frac{[\log a_{n,m}]_+}{m+1}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Also define the fixed-energy prefix

\[
F_n^{\mathsf A}(K):=\nu_n([0,K]).
\tag{3.5}
\]

The shell width `1` is arbitrary: any fixed positive width yields an equivalent criterion after changing constants.

## 3.3 Exact all-positive-noise theorem for reversible generators

**Theorem 3.1.** The following are equivalent:

1. for every fixed `t>0`,
   \[
   \|\mathsf S_{n,t}h_n\|_2^2\to0;
   \tag{3.6}
   \]
2. for every fixed `K<infinity`,
   \[
   F_n^{\mathsf A}(K)\to0,
   \tag{3.7}
   \]
   and
   \[
   R_n^{\mathsf A}\to0.
   \tag{3.8}
   \]

Thus every positive amount of reversible noise kills the likelihood ratio exactly when **low generator-energy mass escapes to infinity** and **spectral multiplicity per unit energy is subexponential**.

### Proof: (2) implies (1)

Fix `t>0` and put `s=2t`. From `(3.2)`,

\[
\|S_{n,t}h_n\|_2^2
\le
F_n^{\mathsf A}(K)
+
\sum_{m\ge K} a_{n,m}e^{-sm}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`, `(3.8)` gives `R_n^A<=s/2`. Hence for every shell,

\[
a_{n,m}\le e^{(s/2)(m+1)}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{m\ge K}a_{n,m}e^{-sm}
\le
 e^{s/2}\sum_{m\ge K}e^{-sm/2}
=
 e^{s/2}\frac{e^{-sK/2}}{1-e^{-s/2}}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Choose `K` so that `(3.11)` is arbitrarily small, then use `(3.7)` on the finite prefix. This proves `(3.6)`.

### Proof: (1) implies (2)

For every fixed `K` and fixed `t>0`, `(3.2)` gives

\[
\|S_{n,t}h_n\|_2^2
\ge
 e^{-2tK}\nu_n([0,K]),
\tag{3.12}
\]

so `(3.6)` forces `(3.7)`.

Suppose `(3.8)` fails. Then for some `epsilon>0`, along a subsequence there exist shell indices `m_n` with

\[
a_{n,m_n}\ge e^{\epsilon(m_n+1)}.
\tag{3.13}
\]

If `m_n` stays bounded, `(3.7)` fails. Hence, after a further subsequence, `m_n->infinity`. Take fixed `t=epsilon/8`, so `2t=epsilon/4`. Then the contribution of that one shell to `(3.2)` is at least

\[
a_{n,m_n}e^{-2t(m_n+1)}
\ge
\exp\left(\frac{3\epsilon}{4}(m_n+1)\right),
\tag{3.14}
\]

contradicting `(3.6)`. Therefore `R_n^A->0`.

This proves the theorem. `square`

## 3.4 Bounded tier

The same shell argument gives the bounded analogue.

Define the tail type after energy cutoff `K` by

\[
R_n^{\mathsf A}(K)
=
\sup_{m\ge K:a_{n,m}>0}
\frac{[\log a_{n,m}]_+}{m+1}.
\tag{3.15}
\]

Then

\[
\sup_n F_n^{\mathsf A}(K)<\infty
\quad\text{for every fixed }K,
\tag{3.16}
\]

and

\[
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}R_n^{\mathsf A}(K)=0
\tag{3.17}
\]

are equivalent to

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|S_{n,t}h_n\|_2^2<\infty
\quad\text{for every fixed }t>0.
\tag{3.18}
\]

The proof is the same prefix/tail argument, while failure of `(3.17)` produces a shell making one fixed Laplace transform diverge.

This is the natural reversible-semigroup analogue of the source's **bounded** LDA tier: bounded low-energy mass plus vanishing tail exponential type gives bounded noisy collision advantage, while vanishing low-energy mass gives TV convergence.

## 4.1 Exact Gram formula for source LDA

Let

\[
\mathcal I_D=\{S\subseteq[n]:1\le |S|\le D\},
\]

and define centered source monomials

\[
m_S(x)=\chi_S(x)-\mathbb E_Q\chi_S(x).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let `G_D` be their Gram matrix,

\[
(G_D)_{S,T}=\langle m_S,m_T\rangle_Q,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and let the moment-difference vector be

\[
b_S=\langle h,m_S\rangle_Q
=
\mathbb E_P\chi_S-\mathbb E_Q\chi_S.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If the centered monomials are linearly independent, the exact projection formula is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
=b^\top G_D^{-1}b.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

In general replace the inverse by the Moore–Penrose inverse on the span. Therefore, with `gamma_D` the smallest positive eigenvalue of `G_D`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
\le
\gamma_D^{-1/2}\|b\|_2.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equation `(4.4)` is the exact static bottleneck. Correlation decay controls entries of `b`; it does not by itself control `G_D^{-1}`.

## 4.2 Parity-code conditioning under a correlated null

For a binary parity-code planting on a general null `Q`, with row-span code `C`, likelihood

\[
L=Z_C^{-1}\sum_{c\in C}\chi_c,
\qquad
Z_C=\sum_{c\in C}\mu(c),
\quad
\mu(A)=\mathbb E_Q\chi_A,
\tag{4.6}
\]

we have exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_S
&=\mathbb E_P\chi_S-\mathbb E_Q\chi_S\\
&=\frac1{Z_C}
\sum_{c\in C\setminus\{0\}}
\left[
\mu(c\triangle S)-\mu(c)\mu(S)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus a current-decay theorem controls the **numerator vector** `b`. For example, if a robust-current condition gives

\[
|\mu(c\triangle S)|+|\mu(c)\mu(S)|
\le 2e^{-aL_D}
\tag{4.8}
\]

for every `0!=c in C` and `|S|<=D`, then

\[
|b_S|\le 2|C|e^{-aL_D}/Z_C.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Since

\[
|\mathcal I_D|\le
\sum_{k=1}^D{n\choose k}
\le D\left(\frac{en}{D}\right)^D,
\tag{4.10}
\]

we get the conditional theorem

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)
\le
\frac{2|C|}{Z_C}
\gamma_D^{-1/2}
\sqrt D\left(\frac{en}{D}\right)^{D/2}
 e^{-aL_D}.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

Therefore, if one can prove a **restricted Riesz lower bound**

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_D\ge e^{-cD}
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

uniformly in `n`, then the current-distance mechanism closes whenever

\[
aL_D
-
\log|C|
-
\frac D2\log\frac{en}{D}
-
\frac{cD}{2}
\to+\infty.
\tag{4.13}
\]

This is a precise, non-tautological loopy-Gibbs source theorem conditional on one static norm-comparison input.

## 4.3 Which obstruction is actually blocking?

Three candidates were separated.

### (i) Current decay itself — **not the remaining obstruction**

B3 already gives a summable defect/current theorem under a directly checkable Dobrushin cycle-variable condition. Equation `(4.7)` converts that into entrywise bounds on `b`.

### (ii) Semigroup/source-number commutators — **important for noisy transfer, but not the static LDA blockage**

The earlier tilted-alignment theorem controls off-diagonal semigroup transport and now, via `(3.20)`, controls angles between source degree and low generator energy. But source LDA is defined before noise is applied. Even perfect semigroup alignment does not invert `G_D`.

### (iii) Restricted Gram conditioning / degree-preserving orthogonalization — **the actual blockage**

On a tree, the exact innovations

\[
\phi_v=(G_v-\rho G_{p(v)})/\sqrt{1-\rho^2}
\]

produce an orthonormal basis whose source degree is exactly preserved, which is why the tree theorem closed cleanly.

On a loopy pairwise Gibbs graph, sequential conditional expectations generally depend on an induced marginal over many earlier variables. Exact Gram–Schmidt therefore need not remain local or degree-preserving. A local one-site conditional mean is a function of finitely many neighbors, but a **sequential** conditional mean after eliminating future variables can acquire long-range effective interactions. Thus the naive “orthogonalize locally” algorithm does not prove `(4.12)`.

The weakest missing theorem is now explicit:

> **Restricted Gibbs Riesz problem.** Under a quantitative weak-dependence condition (Dobrushin uniqueness, spectral independence, or a stronger complete-analyticity hypothesis), prove or refute a bound of the form `lambda_min(G_D)>=exp(-O(D))` for the ordinary source monomial Gram matrix through `D=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`, uniformly in ambient dimension.

This is substantially narrower than Kireeva's source problem and is the simplest unresolved special case exposed by B20.

## 4.4 Failed shortcuts

Three shortcuts fail for precise reasons.

1. **Global density comparison.** `E_Q f^2 >= (min_x Q(x)/U(x)) E_U f^2` is true but the density ratio can be `exp(-Theta(n))`, useless at polylog degree.
2. **Entrywise correlation decay plus Gershgorin.** There are about `n^D` source monomials. Even exponentially decaying local correlations need not make the Gram matrix diagonally dominant after summing over this entropy.
3. **Ordinary one-site finite-energy bounds.** They control functions depending on a fixed set of `D` coordinates, but a general degree-`D` polynomial can involve all `n` coordinates through a sum of many monomials.

So the next useful proof must control the **whole degree-D polynomial subspace**, not one support at a time.

# 5. B10 — derandomize: a vanishing-noise diagonal and a dimension/distance criterion

The predecessor's B27 theorem characterized convergence under every fixed positive discount. There is an even simpler equivalent quantifier form that is useful for derandomization.

## 5.1 Vanishing-noise diagonal lemma

Let `L_n(s)` be any nonnegative function decreasing in `s>0`; in the spectral setting,

\[
L_n(s)=\int e^{-s\lambda}\,d\nu_n(\lambda).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
L_n(s)\to0\text{ for every fixed }s>0
\iff
\exists s_n\downarrow0\text{ such that }L_n(s_n)\to0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

The reverse direction is immediate: for fixed `s>0`, eventually `s>s_n`, hence monotonicity gives

\[
0\le L_n(s)\le L_n(s_n)\to0.
\]

For the forward direction, for each integer `j>=1` choose `N_j` so large that

\[
n\ge N_j\implies L_n(1/j)\le1/j.
\]

Choose `N_j` increasing. Let `j(n)` be the largest `j` with `N_j<=n` and set `s_n=1/j(n)`. Then `j(n)->infinity`, so `s_n->0`, and `L_n(s_n)<=s_n->0`. `square`

Thus “survives **every** fixed positive noise” can be attacked by finding a single noise level that is itself allowed to vanish with `n`.

## 5.2 Deterministic binary-code consequence

For a binary spectral code `C_n` of dimension `M_n` and minimum distance `d_n`, coordinate-refresh discount `s` gives

\[
L_n(s)=W_{C_n}(e^{-s})-1
\le (2^{M_n}-1)e^{-sd_n}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Suppose

\[
M_n=o(d_n).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Set, for example,

\[
s_n=\sqrt{\frac{M_n\log2+1}{d_n}}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then `s_n->0`, while

\[
M_n\log2-s_nd_n
=
M_n\log2-
\sqrt{d_n(M_n\log2+1)}
\to-\infty.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Hence `(5.2)` and `(5.1)` imply

\[
\boxed{
W_{C_n}(e^{-s})-1\to0
\quad\text{for every fixed }s>0.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

If also

\[
d_n>D_n,
\tag{5.7}
\]

then source LDA through `D_n` is exactly zero. Therefore:

**Theorem 5.1.** Any deterministic binary code sequence satisfying

\[
\boxed{d_n>D_n\quad\text{and}\quad M_n=o(d_n)}
\tag{5.8}
\]

gives an exact restricted Kireeva-style implication: zero source LDA through `D_n`, and TV convergence after **every fixed positive coordinate-refresh noise level**.

This strictly generalizes the predecessor's concrete choice `M_n=Theta(D_n)`, `d_n=Theta(n)`.

## 5.3 What happened to the planned dual-profile derandomization?
