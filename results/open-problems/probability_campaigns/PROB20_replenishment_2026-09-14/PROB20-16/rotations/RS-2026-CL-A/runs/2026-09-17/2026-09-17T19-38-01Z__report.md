## 4. Random-support planted models: exact low-degree and two-copy formulas

Let `E` be a finite coordinate set. Fix `p in (0,1)` and let

\[
Q_p=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)^{\otimes E}.
\]

Let `nu` be a probability law on subsets `A subseteq E`. Define the planted law `P=P_{nu,p}` by:

1. sample `A~nu`;
2. force `X_e=1` for every `e in A`;
3. for `e notin A`, sample `X_e~Bernoulli(p)` independently.

Write

\[
\lambda:=\frac{1-p}{p},
\qquad
q_e:=\Pr_{A\sim\nu}(e\in A).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the standard product orthonormal basis

\[
\psi_e(x)=\frac{x_e-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}},
\qquad
\psi_S=\prod_{e\in S}\psi_e,
\]

let `L=dP/dQ_p`.

### Lemma 4.1 — exact Fourier coefficient

For every `S subseteq E`,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat L(S)
:=\mathbb E_P\psi_S(X)
=\lambda^{|S|/2}\Pr_{A\sim\nu}(S\subseteq A).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** Conditional on `A`, if some `e in S` is not planted, then the factor `psi_e(X_e)` is centered under its independent `Bernoulli(p)` draw, so the product expectation is zero. If `S subseteq A`, every coordinate in `S` is forced to one and

\[
\psi_e(1)=\frac{1-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}=\sqrt\lambda.
\]

Multiplying the factors and averaging over `A` gives (4.2). `□`

Consequently, if

\[
b_j:=\sum_{|S|=j}\widehat L(S)^2,
\]

then for independent `A,A'~nu` and

\[
Z:=|A\cap A'|,
\]

we have the exact factorial-moment identity

\[
\boxed{
b_j=\lambda^j\,\mathbb E\binom Zj.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\mathbb E\binom Zj
=
\sum_{|S|=j}\Pr(S\subseteq A)^2.
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
b_1=\lambda\sum_{e\in E}q_e^2.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Under the source convention, for any cutoff `D>=1`,

\[
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q_p)\right)^2
=\sum_{j=1}^D b_j,
\tag{4.5}
\]

so `b_1 <= (Adv^{<=D})^2`.

Let `T_rho` be independent product resampling noise which retains the centered degree-one coordinate with eigenvalue `rho in [0,1]`. Since `T_rho psi_S=rho^{|S|}psi_S`, Parseval and (4.3) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
&=1+\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^{2j}b_j\\
&=\sum_{j\ge0}(\rho^2\lambda)^j\mathbb E\binom Zj\\
&=\boxed{\mathbb E(1+\rho^2\lambda)^Z.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This identity is exact for every finite ground set and every support law `nu`.

## 5. B26 + B9 — a negative-association bridge from degree one to the entire spectrum

The key sporadic structure suggested by random spanning trees is **negative association** rather than a polymer decomposition.

Assume `nu` is negatively associated: for increasing functions depending on disjoint coordinate sets, their expectations multiply submultiplicatively in the standard negative-association sense. Define

\[
S_2(\nu):=\sum_{e\in E}q_e^2,
\qquad
C(\nu):=\sup_{A\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)}\sum_{e\in A}q_e.
\tag{5.1}
\]

When `S_2>0`, define the **self-overlap leverage ratio**

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(\nu):=\frac{C(\nu)}{S_2(\nu)}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This quantity is determined entirely by the planted-support law; it does not contain the testing conclusion.

### Theorem 5.1 — negative-association support-mixture theorem

For the planted-support model above, assume `nu` is negatively associated. Then for every `rho in [0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
\le
\exp\!\left(\rho^2\lambda C(\nu)\right).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `S_2>0`, then for every `D>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
\le
\exp\!\left(
\rho^2\kappa(\nu)
\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q_p)\right)^2
\right)-1.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence for a sequence of such models, if

\[
\sup_n\kappa(\nu_n)<\infty
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)<\infty
\tag{5.5}
\]

for any `D_n>=1` — in particular for the source scale `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}` — then even at `rho=1` the full chi-square divergence is uniformly bounded. Therefore no measurable test, and a fortiori no polynomial of any degree, strongly distinguishes `P_n` from `Q_n`. If the low-degree advantage tends to zero and `kappa` stays bounded, then total variation tends to zero.

#### Proof

Let `A,A'` be independent draws from `nu` and condition on `A`. For `t>=0`,

\[
(1+t)^{|A\cap A'|}
=\prod_{e\in A}(1+t\mathbf 1_{\{e\in A'\}}).
\]

Each factor is a nonnegative increasing function of one coordinate of `A'`, and the coordinates in the product are distinct. Negative association, iterated over the singleton coordinate blocks, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[(1+t)^{|A\cap A'|}\mid A]
&\le \prod_{e\in A}(1+tq_e)\\
&\le \exp\!\left(t\sum_{e\in A}q_e\right)\\
&\le e^{tC(\nu)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Averaging over `A` and substituting `t=rho^2 lambda` into the exact identity (4.6) proves (5.3).

Next, by (4.4),

\[
\lambda C(\nu)
=\kappa(\nu)\lambda S_2(\nu)
=\kappa(\nu)b_1.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Since `D>=1`, (4.5) gives

\[
b_1\le\left(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}\right)^2.
\]

Combining with (5.3) proves (5.4).

For the testing statement, use the elementary overlap bound already proved on this track:

\[
1-\|R-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\ge \frac{1}{2+\chi^2(R\|Q)}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Thus if the right side of (5.4) is at most `e^{K A^2}-1`,

\[
\boxed{
\|T_\rho P-Q_p\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le 1-\frac{1}{1+e^{\rho^2KA^2}}<1.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

If `Adv^{<=D}->0` and `kappa` is bounded, (5.4) gives `chi^2->0`, and the standard inequality `TV <= (1/2)sqrt(chi^2)` gives `TV->0`. `□`

### Corollary 5.2 — homogeneous uniform-marginal negative association

Suppose every support set has exactly `m` elements and every coordinate has the same marginal

\[
q_e=\frac{m}{N},\qquad N=|E|.
\]

Then for every support point `A`,

\[
\sum_{e\in A}q_e=\frac{m^2}{N}
=\sum_{e\in E}q_e^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\kappa(\nu)=1.}
\tag{5.10}
\]

For every negatively associated law with this homogeneous fixed-size property,

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(T_\rho P\|Q_p)
\le
\exp\!\left(
\rho^2(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D})^2
\right)-1.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

This is a broad structural subclass, not a single planted graph.

## 6. Concrete stress test: a random spanning tree plant

Take `E=E(K_n)` and let `nu_n` be the uniform spanning-tree law on `K_n`. Classical results give much more structure than we need:

- Feder–Mihail (1992) proved negative association for uniform spanning trees (more generally, balanced matroid bases).
- The Burton–Pemantle transfer-current theorem realizes spanning-tree edges as a determinantal process.
- Borcea–Brändén–Liggett (2009) place uniform spanning-tree measures in the strongly Rayleigh class, which implies strong negative-dependence properties.

For `K_n`, every spanning tree has

\[
m=n-1
\]

edges and edge symmetry gives

\[
q_e=\frac{n-1}{\binom n2}=\frac2n.
\]

Hence `kappa(nu_n)=1` by Corollary 5.2. Also

\[
S_2(\nu_n)
=\binom n2\frac4{n^2}
=\frac{2(n-1)}n,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
b_1=\frac{2(n-1)}n\lambda.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

For any fixed background density `p in (0,1)`, Theorem 5.1 gives the uniform full-information bound

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_n\|Q_{p,n})
\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{2(n-1)}n\lambda\right)-1
<e^{2\lambda}-1.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus this random support contains `n-1` planted edges — growing support size — but the **two-copy overlap load stays order one** because negative dependence disperses the support across `Theta(n^2)` coordinates. The conclusion is not offered as a novel spanning-tree theorem; the negative-dependence machinery is classical. Its role is to demonstrate a framework mechanism genuinely different from Chen–Li treewidth decomposition and from dense-SBM primitive pressure.

There is an equivalent determinantal proof: conditional on one tree `T`, if `K_T` is the principal transfer-current matrix on the edges of `T`, then

\[
\mathbb E[(1+s)^{|T\cap T'|}\mid T]=\det(I+sK_T),
\]

and `det(I+sK_T)<=exp(s tr K_T)`. On `K_n`, `tr K_T=2(n-1)/n`. This matches (6.2). I do not count this as an independent theorem; it is a check that the negative-association derivation lands on the classical determinantal structure.
