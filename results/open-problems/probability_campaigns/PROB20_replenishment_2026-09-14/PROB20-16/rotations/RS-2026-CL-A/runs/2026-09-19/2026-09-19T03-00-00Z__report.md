# 3. B4 — PROVE SOMETHING WEAKER, COMPLETELY

## 3.1 Concrete nonproduct class

Let `G_n=(V_n,E_n)` be finite bounded-degree graphs, `|V_n|=n`, equipped with a transitive automorphism group `Gamma_n`. Let the edge couplings `J_{ij}` be invariant under the action and satisfy

\[
J_{\rm row}:=\sup_n\max_i\sum_j |J_{ij}|<\infty.
\]

Fix a sufficiently weak zero-field pairwise Ising interaction `alpha` and let

\[
Q_n(x)=\frac1{Z_n(0)}
\exp\!\left(\alpha\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_n}J_{ij}x_ix_j\right),
\qquad x\in\{-1,1\}^{V_n}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

“ sufficiently weak ” is now model-checkable rather than black-box: use the preceding run's local-table / block-Toda certificate. Namely choose one `s>0` for which the recursively computed rooted tilted norm satisfies

\[
\mathcal E_s(|\alpha|)<1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The preceding rooted local-Hamiltonian theorem then gives a uniform degree-distance estimate for the `Q_n`-reversible heat-bath semigroup `S_{n,t}=e^{-tA_n}`:

\[
\boxed{
\|\Pi_{\le d}S_{n,t}\Pi_{\ge k}\|_{2\to2}
\le e^{-s(k-d)-\delta_s t},
\qquad \delta_s:=1-\mathcal E_s(|\alpha|)>0.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Here `Pi_j` are the ordinary algebraic source-degree layers in `L^2(Q_n)`. This is the transport input proved in earlier runs; it is not re-proved here.

Now choose a template vector `a_n in [-1,1]^{V_n}` and let `Z_n=Gamma_n/H_n` be its orbit. Write `a_{n,z}=g_z a_n`. For a scalar amplitude `lambda_n`, define the planted component

\[
\frac{dP_{n,z}}{dQ_n}(x)
=L_{n,z}(x)
:=\frac{\exp(F_{n,z}(x))}
{\mathbb E_{Q_n}e^{F_{n,z}}},
\qquad
F_{n,z}(x)=\lambda_n\sum_i a_{n,z,i}x_i.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Finally let `P_n` be the uniform orbit mixture

\[
P_n=\frac1{M_n}\sum_{z\in Z_n}P_{n,z},
\qquad M_n=|Z_n|.
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is genuinely nonproduct whenever `alpha != 0`: the null has overlapping Ising interactions and the planted components are extensive Gibbs tilts. The template may have support much larger than `D_n`; nothing below assumes component likelihoods are low-degree.

## 3.2 Exact doubled partition-function overlap

Let

\[
Z_n(h):=\sum_x
\exp\!\left(\alpha\sum_eJ_ex_{e_1}x_{e_2}+\sum_i h_ix_i\right).
\]

For `h_z=lambda_n a_{n,z}`, the component overlap is exactly

\[
\boxed{
K_n(z,z')
:=\langle L_{n,z},L_{n,z'}\rangle_{Q_n}
=
\frac{Z_n(h_z+h_{z'})Z_n(0)}{Z_n(h_z)Z_n(h_{z'})}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus the reference-pressure side is a concrete two-insertion partition-function ratio on the **same interacting Ising class** as the transport certificate.

## 3.3 Dobrushin/Gaussian-concentration pressure bound

In the Dobrushin uniqueness regime, finite-volume Ising laws satisfy a Gaussian concentration bound with a volume-independent constant. Use the normalized form

\[
\log\mathbb E_{Q_n}e^{H-\mathbb E H}
\le C_{\rm GCB}\sum_i\delta_i(H)^2,
\tag{3.7}
\]

where `C_GCB` is independent of `n` and `delta_i` is coordinate oscillation. For `F_{n,z}`,

\[
\delta_i(F_{n,z})=2|\lambda_n a_{n,z,i}|.
\]

Applying (3.7) to `F_z+F_{z'}` and Jensen to each denominator of (3.6) gives

\[
\boxed{
\log K_n(z,z')
\le
4C_{\rm GCB}\lambda_n^2
\|a_{n,z}+a_{n,z'}\|_2^2.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

This is a direct, finite-volume, nonasymptotic overlap certificate. It can be replaced by a convergent cluster expansion when sharper shell/interface dependence is needed, but no such machinery is necessary for this restricted theorem.

# 4. B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: exact orbit-shell pressure

Because `Q_n`, the heat-bath dynamics, and the template orbit are `Gamma_n`-equivariant, the kernel (3.6) is invariant under the diagonal action. Let `R_{n,r}` be the orbitals of `Z_n x Z_n`, with valencies `v_{n,r}`, and choose one representative `g_{n,r}`. Set

\[
q_{n,r}:=\|a_n+g_{n,r}a_n\|_2^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the uniform prior, the latent row load is constant and the exact collision identity is

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)
=
\frac1{M_n}\sum_r v_{n,r}\,\kappa_{n,r},
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `kappa_{n,r}=K_n(z,z')` on shell `r`. Combining with (3.8) gives the explicit shell pressure

\[
\boxed{
\log(1+\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n))
\le
\Xi_n
:=
\log\!\left[
\frac1{M_n}
\sum_r v_{n,r}
\exp\{4C_{\rm GCB}\lambda_n^2q_{n,r}\}
\right].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is strictly better than a worst-component bound whenever the orbit has cancellation or small typical overlap. A simple sufficient condition is

\[
\lambda_n^2\|a_n\|_2^2=o(D_n),
\tag{4.4}
\]

because `q_{n,r}<=4||a_n||_2^2`, but (4.3) is the actual model-level certificate.

## 4.1 Complete restricted source theorem

Let

\[
f_n:=\frac{dP_n}{dQ_n}-1,
\qquad
A_n(D):=\|\Pi_{\le D}f_n\|_2
=
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P_n,Q_n)
\tag{4.5}
\]

with Kireeva's normalized low-degree convention.

Fix any `theta in (0,1)` and define

\[
d_n:=\lfloor(1-\theta)D_n\rfloor.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Since `S_{n,t}` is self-adjoint and `Q_n`-stationary, the noised likelihood ratio is `1+S_{n,t}f_n`. Decompose at source degree `D_n`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Pi_{\le d_n}S_{n,t}f_n\|_2
&\le
\|\Pi_{\le d_n}S_{n,t}\Pi_{\le D_n}f_n\|_2\\
&\quad+
\|\Pi_{\le d_n}S_{n,t}\Pi_{\ge D_n+1}f_n\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The first term is at most `A_n(D_n)` by contraction. By (3.3), the second is at most

\[
e^{-s(D_n+1-d_n)-\delta_s t}\|f_n\|_2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

From (4.3),

\[
\|f_n\|_2
\le\sqrt{1+\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)}
\le e^{\Xi_n/2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Therefore, whenever

\[
\boxed{\Xi_n=o(D_n),}
\tag{4.10}
\]

we obtain for every fixed `t>0` and every fixed `theta in (0,1)`

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le d_n}(P_nS_{n,t},Q_n)
\le
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)+o(1).
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

More generally, for any degree loss `g_n=D_n-d_n`, it suffices that

\[
s g_n-\Xi_n/2\longrightarrow+\infty.
\tag{4.12}
\]

### Restricted theorem, source-valid quantifiers

Take `D_n=(log n)^{1+eta}` for any fixed `eta>0`. Assume:

1. the bounded-degree zero-field Ising null satisfies the explicit local-table/Toda transport certificate (3.2) for some fixed `s>0`;
2. the orbit-tilt shell pressure (4.3) obeys `Xi_n=o(D_n)`;
3. the source low-degree advantage `Adv^{<=D_n}(P_n,Q_n)` remains bounded.

Then for **every fixed noise time `t>0` and every fixed `theta in (0,1)`**, the noised pair `(P_nS_{n,t},Q_n)` has bounded low-degree advantage through degree `(1-theta)D_n`. In particular no sequence of degree-`(1-theta)D_n` polynomials has diverging normalized distinguishing advantage after that noise. If the source advantage tends to zero, then the noised advantage through degree `(1-theta)D_n` tends to zero.

This is a complete theorem on one genuinely nonproduct class and directly answers the source question on that class. It is still **PARTIAL** because it is not a characterization and because the noise is the natural `Q_n`-reversible heat-bath noise rather than the uniform-product resampling of Hopkins' original Boolean conjecture. At `alpha=0`, the heat-bath noise reduces to ordinary independent coordinate resampling.

## 4.2 Nontriviality: the component likelihood may have degree far above `D_n`

The theorem is not merely saying “the planted likelihood itself has degree below `D_n`.” For example, let the template support have size

\[
m_n=D_n^2,
\]

and choose

\[
\lambda_n=D_n^{-3/4}.
\]

Then a component `L_{n,z}` can have ordinary algebraic degree as large as `m_n=D_n^2`, while

\[
\lambda_n^2m_n=D_n^{1/2}=o(D_n).
\]

The simple pressure criterion (4.4) holds, but the source cutoff sees only a small fraction of the component's possible algebraic degrees. Thus the low-degree premise and the noisy transfer step are genuinely doing work.

De Roeck–Schütz (arXiv:1512.07612) already provides volume-uniform exponentially local dressing/spectral-flow technology for weak perturbations of noninteracting spins, including non-self-adjoint perturbations and discussion of Glauber-type generators. What is not directly supplied there is the **ordinary algebraic source-degree filtration** used by low-degree inference, the shell pressure (4.3), or the implication (4.11) from source LDLR control to a noised polynomial degree class. Whether their machinery technically subsumes the transport lemma after a careful identification is still unresolved and must be reconciled before any publication claim.

## 5.2 What Dobrushin/cluster expansion supplies — and what it does not

Dobrushin uniqueness classically implies a volume-uniform Gaussian concentration bound, which is exactly what produced (3.8). Kotecký–Preiss and modern algorithmic cluster expansions classically give convergent logarithmic partition-function expansions and polynomial-time truncations for bounded-degree polymer systems under suitable decay conditions.

The failure lesson is important: **high temperature alone does not force `Xi_n=o(D_n)`**. If a constant-amplitude planted field affects `Theta(n)` sites and the orbit does not provide cancellation, then (3.8) gives `Xi_n=Theta(n)`, hopeless at a polylogarithmic source cutoff. To go beyond the weak-amplitude regime, one needs genuine two-copy bulk cancellation or an interface theorem whose cost is `o(D_n)`. This is precisely the obstruction already visible in Chen–Li's treewidth/interface proof and in this track's earlier B22/B28 analysis.

Thus the current restricted theorem closes the weak-amplitude orbit-tilt class, but it does **not** solve the harder constant-amplitude large-plant regime by calling it “high temperature.”
