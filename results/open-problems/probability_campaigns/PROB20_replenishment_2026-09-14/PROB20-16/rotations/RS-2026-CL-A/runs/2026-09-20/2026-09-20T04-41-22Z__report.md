## 3. Setup

Let `Omega` be a finite state space and let `Q` have full support. Let `V_D` be the finite-dimensional real function space represented by source polynomials of degree at most `D`, reduced modulo equality on `Omega`; assume constants lie in `V_D`.

For a distribution `P`, define

`Adv_{V_D}(P,Q)`

`:= sup_{f in V_D, Var_Q(f)>0} (E_P f-E_Q f)/sqrt(Var_Q f)`.

Because `-f` is also in `V_D`, this is automatically the absolute operator norm of the expectation-difference functional on the centered space.

Write

`C_A(Q,V_D) = { P probability on Omega : Adv_{V_D}(P,Q) <= A }`.

For a test `u:Omega->R`, define the robust upper and lower low-degree envelopes

`Maj_{D,A}^Q(u)`

`:= inf_{g in V_D, g>=u pointwise} [ E_Q g + A sqrt(Var_Q g) ]`,

and

`Min_{D,A}^Q(u)`

`:= sup_{g in V_D, g<=u pointwise} [ E_Q g - A sqrt(Var_Q g) ]`.

These are null/test-side quantities. They know `Q`, the source polynomial filtration, the radius `A`, and the test `u`, but not the planted distribution.

## 4. Main theorem — exact robust moment-majorant duality

### Theorem 4.1

For every finite full-support `Q`, every finite-dimensional `V_D` containing constants, every `A>=0`, and every real function `u` on `Omega`,

`sup_{P in C_A(Q,V_D)} E_P u = Maj_{D,A}^Q(u)`,             (4.1)

and

`inf_{P in C_A(Q,V_D)} E_P u = Min_{D,A}^Q(u)`.             (4.2)

Equivalently,

`sup_{P in C_A} (E_Pu-E_Qu)`

`= Maj_{D,A}^Q(u)-E_Qu`,                                    (4.3)

with the analogous exact lower-tail formula.

Thus a degree-`D` majorant penalized by `A` times its `Q` standard deviation is not just a sufficient certificate: it is the **sharp universal certificate obtainable from the single assumption `Adv^{<=D}<=A`**.

### Proof

Let `H_D={f in V_D:E_Q f=0}` and choose a `Q`-orthonormal basis `phi_1,...,phi_m` of `H_D`. Put

`Phi(x)=(phi_1(x),...,phi_m(x)) in R^m`.

For any probability law `P`, set

`m(P)=E_P Phi`.

Every centered `f in H_D` has the form `f=a dot Phi` and

`||f||_{L2(Q)}=||a||_2`.

Therefore

`Adv_{V_D}(P,Q)`

`= sup_{a!=0} a dot m(P)/||a||_2`

`= ||m(P)||_2`.                                              (4.4)

Consequently the feasible set is exactly the Euclidean moment ball

`||E_P Phi||_2 <= A`.                                       (4.5)

Now use the support-function identity for the Euclidean ball:

`1_{||z||<=A}^{convex-indicator}`

`= sup_{y in R^m} [ y dot z - A||y||_2 ]`,

where the left side is `0` on the ball and `+infinity` outside. Hence

`sup_{P in C_A} E_Pu`

`= sup_{P probability} inf_{y in R^m}`

`   [ E_P(u-y dot Phi) + A||y||_2 ]`.                        (4.6)

The probability simplex on finite `Omega` is compact convex; the displayed payoff is affine in `P` and convex continuous in `y`. Finite-dimensional minimax/strong convex duality therefore gives

`= inf_{y in R^m}`

`   [ A||y||_2 + sup_{P probability} E_P(u-y dot Phi) ]`

`= inf_{y in R^m}`

`   [ A||y||_2 + max_{x in Omega}(u(x)-y dot Phi(x)) ]`.     (4.7)

For a fixed `y`, define

`c_y=max_x(u(x)-y dot Phi(x))`

and

`g_y(x)=c_y+y dot Phi(x)`.

Then `g_y in V_D`, `g_y>=u` pointwise,

`E_Q g_y=c_y`

because `E_Q Phi=0`, and

`sqrt(Var_Q g_y)=||y||_2`

because the `phi_i` are orthonormal. Thus the right side of (4.7) is at least the infimum defining `Maj_{D,A}^Q(u)`.

Conversely, every `g in V_D` has a unique representation `g=c+y dot Phi`; if `g>=u`, then necessarily

`c>=max_x(u(x)-y dot Phi(x))`.

Replacing `c` by this maximum weakly lowers the objective while retaining majorization. Hence the right side of (4.7) is exactly `Maj_{D,A}^Q(u)`. This proves (4.1).

Apply (4.1) to `-u` and negate to obtain (4.2). `square`

## 5. The previous sandwiching theorem is a corollary, with the source of its constants exposed

Suppose `g,a in V_D`, `a>=0`, and `|u-g|<=a`. Then

`h_+=g+a >= u`,

`h_-=g-a <= u`.

If `||a||_2<=sigma`, then

`E_Q h_+ <= E_Qu+2sigma`,

and

`sqrt(Var_Q h_+) <= sqrt(Var_Q u)+2sigma`.

Therefore Theorem 4.1 gives

`sup_{P in C_A}(E_Pu-E_Qu)`

`<= A sqrt(Var_Q u)+(2+2A)sigma`.

Taking the infimum over such `(g,a)` recovers the 03:49 bound. The exact dual shows why the factor arose: a symmetric two-sided half-width was converted into a one-sided majorant. For one-sided testing, direct majorants can be strictly sharper.

## 6. Exact symmetry reduction — preserve the source's `S_n` hypothesis rather than discarding it

Let a finite group `G` act on `Omega`. Assume `Q` is `G`-invariant and `V_D` is stable under the induced action on functions.

### Lemma 6.1 (LDA lives in the invariant subspace for invariant pairs)

If both `P` and `Q` are `G`-invariant, then

`Adv_{V_D}(P,Q)=Adv_{V_D^G}(P,Q)`,                           (6.1)

where `V_D^G` denotes invariant degree-`D` functions.

### Proof

For any `f in V_D`, let

`bar f = |G|^{-1} sum_{g in G} f(g^{-1} x)`.

Then `bar f in V_D^G`. Invariance of `P,Q` gives

`E_P bar f-E_Q bar f = E_P f-E_Q f`,

while conditional Jensen / orthogonal projection in `L2(Q)` gives

`Var_Q(bar f)<=Var_Q(f)`.

Thus every source ratio is matched or improved by an invariant polynomial. `square`

### Theorem 6.2 (symmetry-preserving robust dual)

If additionally `u` is `G`-invariant, then

`sup_{P in C_A(Q,V_D), P G-invariant} E_Pu`

`= inf_{g in V_D^G, g>=u}`

`    [E_Qg+A sqrt(Var_Qg)]`.                                 (6.2)

### Proof

Given any feasible `P`, average it over `G`. The averaged law `bar P` is invariant, preserves `E_Pu`, and has low-degree moment vector equal to the orthogonal group-average of the original moment vector, so its norm — hence its LDA — cannot increase. Therefore the unrestricted supremum in Theorem 4.1 equals the supremum over invariant feasible laws.

On the dual side, average any majorant `g>=u` over the group. Since `u` is invariant, `bar g>=u`; `E_Q bar g=E_Qg`; `bar g in V_D^G`; and `Var_Q(bar g)<=Var_Q(g)`. Thus the infimum may also be restricted to invariant majorants. `square`

This is directly aligned with the Hopkins/Kireeva symmetry hypothesis rather than being a generic theorem that throws the symmetry away.

## 7. Noise corollary — exact condition for what bounded LDA alone can rule out

Let `T` be a `Q`-stationary Markov operator and assume it commutes with the `G` action. Let `f` be a bounded invariant test and put `u=Tf`. Then `u` is invariant and

`E_{PT}f=E_P(Tf)`.

Theorem 6.2 gives the exact identity

`sup_{P G-invariant, Adv^{<=D}(P,Q)<=A} E_{PT}f`

`= inf_{g in V_D^G, g>=Tf}`

`   [E_Qg+A sqrt(Var_Qg)]`.                                  (7.1)

Therefore, for a class `F_n` of invariant bounded tests, a uniform low-degree-majorant theorem for the **noised tests** is not merely sufficient but necessary and sufficient for a conclusion that is required to hold uniformly over *all* invariant planted laws inside the bounded-LDA ball.

This does **not** solve the full source question for a fixed natural pair `(P_n,Q_n)`: a pair can possess additional distribution-side structure not encoded by its degree-`D` moment vector. What (7.1) proves is the exact boundary of what bounded LDA + symmetry, by themselves, can ever force against a specified test.

## 8. Concrete graph consequence — reduce the source moment geometry to subgraph-count coordinates

In the Boolean graph setting, take `Omega={0,1}^{binom(n,2)}`, `G=S_n`, `Q=G(n,1/2)`, and `V_D` the multilinear polynomials of edge degree at most `D`.

Every invariant multilinear monomial orbit is indexed by the isomorphism type of a simple graph with at most `D` edges and no relevant isolated vertices. Hence `V_D^{S_n}` is spanned by degree-`<=D` subgraph-count/orbit-sum polynomials.

The number of such graph types is at most

`sum_{e<=D} (2e)^{2e} = exp(O(D log D))`.                    (8.1)

Indeed a graph with `e` edges and no isolated vertices has at most `2e` vertices; label its vertices by a subset of `[2e]` and encode its `e` unordered edges by at most `(2e)^{2e}` ordered endpoint strings. This crude bound is sufficient.

Thus under the source's `S_n` symmetry, the exact robust moment-majorant dual is an `exp(O(D log D))`-dimensional moment problem rather than an `n^{Theta(D)}` raw monomial problem. Pointwise majorization remains difficult, but the *moment geometry* itself is compressed to graph-shape coordinates.

This identifies a concrete next mathematical object: construct or rule out low-mean invariant subgraph-count polynomial majorants for the noised higher-degree test class.

## 9. Mao becomes a sharper invariant approximate-majorization lower bound

`E_{Q_n}f_n ->0`,

`E_{P_n T_epsilon}f_n ->1`.

For **every** invariant degree-`D_n` polynomial `g_n` satisfying

`g_n >= T_epsilon f_n`

`E_{Q_n}g_n = E_{P_n}g_n`

`>= E_{P_n}[T_epsilon f_n]`

`=1-o(1)`.                                                   (9.1)

`inf_{g in V_{D_n}^{S_n}, g>=T_epsilon f_n} E_{Q_n}g`
