# 3. B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: the support-pure vector is an exact inverse-density Walsh contrast

The predecessor proved that for every finite full-support binary null there is a unique one-dimensional support-pure space

\[
\mathcal H_S
=
\mathcal V_S\cap
\left(\sum_{T\subsetneq S}\mathcal V_T\right)^{\perp_Q},
\]

but left its generator abstract. On a finite cube the local group action identifies it exactly.

Fix a nonempty coordinate set `S` with `d=|S|`. Let `Q_S` denote the marginal of `Q` on `{-1,+1}^S`, let `U_S` be uniform on that cube, and define

\[
r_S(x_S)
:=
\frac{dU_S}{dQ_S}(x_S)
=
\frac{2^{-d}}{Q_S(x_S)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Full support makes this finite. Put

\[
c_S
:=
\|r_S\|_{L^2(Q_S)}
=
\left(\mathbb E_{Q_S}r_S^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let

\[
\chi_S(x_S)=\prod_{i\in S}x_i
\]

be the top character of the local Boolean group. Define

\[
\boxed{
\psi_S(x_S)
=
\frac{\chi_S(x_S)r_S(x_S)}{c_S}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — exact canonical support-pure contrast

The function `(3.3)` is the unit generator, up to sign, of the predecessor's canonical support-pure space `H_S`.

### Proof

Let `T subsetneq S` and let `f` be any function depending only on `X_T`. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle\psi_S,f\rangle_Q
&=
\frac1{c_S}
\mathbb E_{Q_S}[\chi_S r_S f]\\
&=
\frac1{c_S}
\mathbb E_{U_S}[\chi_S f].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Because `T` is a proper subset, choose `i in S\T`. Under `U_S`, the coordinate `X_i` is an independent mean-zero Rademacher and appears once in `chi_S`; hence

\[
\mathbb E_{U_S}[\chi_S f]=0.
\]

Therefore `psi_S` is orthogonal under `Q` to every proper-support subspace and lies in `H_S`. Moreover

\[
\|\psi_S\|_{L^2(Q)}^2
=
\frac{\mathbb E_{Q_S}r_S^2}{c_S^2}=1.
\]

The predecessor proved `dim H_S=1`, completing the identification. `square`

The normalization has two exact interpretations:

\[
\boxed{
c_S^2
=1+\chi^2(U_S\|Q_S)
=4^{-d}\sum_{x_S}\frac1{Q_S(x_S)}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Indeed `E_Q r_S=1`, so `chi^2(U_S||Q_S)=E_Q(r_S-1)^2=E_Qr_S^2-1`.

There is also an exact triangular coefficient identity. Expanding `psi_S` in the **uniform** Walsh basis on `S`, its top coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\psi_S(S)
=
\mathbb E_{U_S}[\psi_S\chi_S]
=c_S.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

To see this,

\[
\mathbb E_{U_S}[\psi_S\chi_S]
=
\frac1{c_S}\mathbb E_{U_S}r_S
=
\frac{c_S^2}{c_S}=c_S,
\]

where `E_U r_S=E_Q r_S^2=c_S^2`. Since `psi_S` depends only on `S`, it has no Walsh degree above `d`; equation `(3.6)` gives its unique top term.

This result explains the geometry from the local symmetry side. The dependent support-pure contrast is the top irreducible character of the local uniform cube, multiplied by the Radon–Nikodym correction that transports `Q_S` back to Haar/uniform measure. This is close in spirit to inverse-likelihood weighted generalized ANOVA constructions in current prior art; the source-directed use below is the research point, not a claim that inverse-density orthogonalization itself is new.

## 4.1 The source-degree quotient shell is the better invariant

The predecessor correctly showed that one should not demand a lower bound for a bad raw spanning family. This run sharpens the replacement further: even the **full adapted-frame** minimum eigenvalue may be more than is needed. Source degree is naturally filtered, so the stable object is the quotient

\[
\mathcal P_{\le d}/\mathcal P_{\le d-1}.
\]

Assume for this subsection that `Q` is a full-support law on the entire Boolean cube and that, pointwise,

\[
\frac{dQ}{dU}\ge m>0,
\tag{4.4}
\]

where `U` is the uniform product law.

For coefficients `a_S` with `|S|=d`, set

\[
F_a=\sum_{|S|=d}a_S\psi_S.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact quotient-shell lower bound

Under `(4.4)`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}_{L^2(Q)}
(F_a,\mathcal P_{\le d-1})^2
\ge
m\sum_{|S|=d}a_S^2c_S^2
\ge
m\sum_{|S|=d}a_S^2.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof

For every `g in P_{<=d-1}`, pointwise domination gives

\[
\|F_a-g\|_{L^2(Q)}^2
\ge
m\|F_a-g\|_{L^2(U)}^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

By `(3.6)`, the degree-`d` uniform-Walsh part of `F_a` is exactly

\[
\sum_{|S|=d}a_Sc_S\chi_S.
\]

No polynomial `g` of degree at most `d-1` can change those coefficients. Orthogonality of the uniform Walsh basis therefore gives

\[
\|F_a-g\|_{L^2(U)}^2
\ge
\sum_{|S|=d}a_S^2c_S^2.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Take the infimum over `g`; finally `c_S^2=1+chi^2(U_S||Q_S)>=1`. `square`

In matrix terms, `(4.5)` says the Schur complement obtained after quotienting out all lower source degrees is bounded below by `m diag(c_S^2)`, hence by `mI`. This bound preserves the actual filtration and avoids asking for global stability across all cross-degree coordinate directions.

That is a material correction to the remaining obligation. A source-valid theorem may only need:

## 4.2 All-degree Curie–Weiss benchmark

The predecessor diagonalized the degree-two adapted frame in high-temperature Curie–Weiss and found healthy signed eigenvalues. The quotient theorem above extends the benchmark to **every source degree** with no representation-theory calculation.

Let `U_n` be uniform on `{-1,+1}^n` and for `0<=beta<1` define

\[
\frac{dQ_{n,\beta}}{dU_n}(x)
=
\frac{\exp\{\beta S_n(x)^2/(2n)\}}
{Z_{n,\beta}},
\qquad S_n(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The Gaussian identity and `cosh u <= exp(u^2/2)` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_{n,\beta}
&=
\mathbb E_U e^{\beta S_n^2/(2n)}\\
&=
\mathbb E_{Z\sim N(0,1)}
\left[\cosh\left(\sqrt{\beta/n}\,Z\right)^n\right]\\
&\le
\mathbb E_Z e^{\beta Z^2/2}
=(1-\beta)^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Since the numerator in `(4.8)` is at least one,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dQ_{n,\beta}}{dU_n}
\ge\sqrt{1-\beta}
\quad\text{pointwise, uniformly in }n.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Therefore Theorem 4.1 gives, for every `d<=n`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}_{Q_{n,\beta}}
(F_a,\mathcal P_{\le d-1})^2
\ge
\sqrt{1-\beta}\sum_{|S|=d}a_S^2.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

This is a dimension-free all-degree source-shell bound throughout the whole high-temperature region `beta<1`.

There is also a useful upper norm comparison on the entire degree-`d` polynomial space. Let `f` have uniform-Walsh degree at most `d`. Choose conjugate exponents `p,q>1` with `q beta<1`. Hölder, `(4.9)`, and cube hypercontractivity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f\|_{L^2(Q_{n,\beta})}^2
&\le
\mathbb E_U[f^2e^{\beta S_n^2/(2n)}]\\
&\le
\|f\|_{L^{2p}(U)}^2
\left(\mathbb E_Ue^{q\beta S_n^2/(2n)}\right)^{1/q}\\
&\le
(2p-1)^d
(1-q\beta)^{-1/(2q)}
\|f\|_{L^2(U)}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.12}
\]

For `beta>0`, take

\[
q=\frac{1+\beta}{2\beta},
\qquad
p=\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|f\|_{Q_{n,\beta}}^2
\le
\left(\frac{1+3\beta}{1-\beta}\right)^d
\left(\frac{2}{1-\beta}\right)^{\beta/(1+\beta)}
\|f\|_U^2.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Together with `(4.10)`, high-temperature Curie–Weiss has an ambient-dimension-free degree-`d` norm comparison with only `exp(O_beta(d))` loss. Hypercontractivity and the Curie–Weiss Gaussian transform are classical; this is a benchmark for the source-directed geometry, not a novelty claim.

# 5. B2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST: repeated bounded weak-dependence gadgets cannot break the adapted frame superexponentially in degree

The most important unresolved question after the predecessor was whether Dobrushin uniqueness itself might still permit catastrophic **adapted** frame conditioning. This run first tried the natural counterexample mechanism: repeat a fixed weakly dependent gadget many times. It fails for an exact structural reason.

Partition the coordinates into disjoint blocks `B_1,...,B_M` and suppose

\[
Q=\bigotimes_{j=1}^M Q_j,
\tag{5.1}
\]

with every `Q_j` full support. For a global support `S`, write `S_j=S cap B_j`. Define the local canonical support-pure contrasts using Theorem 3.1, with the convention

\[
\psi^{(j)}_\varnothing=1,
\qquad c^{(j)}_\varnothing=1.
\]

Because both the local marginal `Q_S` and the local uniform reference factor over blocks,

\[
r_S=\prod_j r^{(j)}_{S_j},
\qquad
c_S=\prod_j c^{(j)}_{S_j}.
\]

Hence Theorem 3.1 gives the exact tensorization

\[
\boxed{
\psi_S
=
\prod_{j=1}^M\psi^{(j)}_{S_j}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let

\[
J(S)=\{j:S_j\neq\varnothing\}
\]

be the occupied block set. If `J(S) != J(T)`, choose a block occupied by exactly one support. The nonempty local pure contrast has mean zero, while the other factor is one, so

\[
\boxed{
\langle\psi_S,\psi_T\rangle_Q=0
\qquad\text{whenever }J(S)\neq J(T).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the global adapted Gram matrix is block diagonal by occupied block set.

For each block `j`, let `G_j^+` be the Gram matrix of **all nonempty local support-pure vectors** in that block, and suppose

\[
\lambda_{\min}(G_j^+)\ge\gamma,
\qquad 0<\gamma\le1,
\tag{5.4}
\]

uniformly in `j`.

### Theorem 5.1 — bounded-gadget tensor obstruction

For the global canonical adapted frame restricted to supports `1<=|S|<=D`,

\[
\boxed{
G_{\le D}^{\rm ad}\succeq\gamma^D I.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Proof

Fix an occupied block set `J`. If all nonempty local supports in the blocks of `J` were retained, the Gram matrix would be the tensor product

\[
\bigotimes_{j\in J}G_j^+,
\]

whose minimum eigenvalue is at least `gamma^{|J|}`. Restricting to global supports of total cardinality at most `D` takes a principal submatrix; by eigenvalue interlacing its minimum eigenvalue can only increase. Every occupied block contributes at least one coordinate, so `|J|<=D`. Since `gamma<=1`,

\[
\gamma^{|J|}\ge\gamma^D.
\]

Different `J` blocks are orthogonal by `(5.3)`, so the same lower bound holds globally. `square`

A uniform local `gamma` is automatic for bounded gadgets away from zero atom probabilities. More precisely, if

\[
|B_j|\le b,
\qquad
Q_j(x)\ge p_*>0
\]

for every atom, then the finite-dimensional local adapted Gram matrix depends continuously on a compact set of probability vectors, is positive definite at every point by triangular independence, and therefore has

\[
\lambda_{\min}(G_j^+)\ge\gamma(b,p_*)>0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

### Consequence of the failed counterexample search

Repeating a bounded dependent gadget — including a bounded Dobrushin-unique gadget — can produce at worst an `exp(-O(D))` adapted-frame loss. It **cannot** refute the desired source-scale adapted stability by ambient multiplicity alone.

Therefore, if Dobrushin uniqueness is false as a sufficient geometric hypothesis, a counterexample must use genuinely growing connected overlap/geometry: long-range accumulation through a connected interaction graph, a growing local state/interaction structure, or some other mechanism not reducible to tensor repetition of a bounded piece.

This is the required B2 outcome: the attempted negation did not produce a counterexample, but it rigorously eliminates a broad and tempting counterexample architecture.

The second natural negation test, high-temperature Curie–Weiss, also fails as a counterexample for the stronger reason `(4.10)`–`(4.13)`: the full degree-filtered norm is uniformly comparable to the product-cube norm with `exp(O_beta(D))` cost.
