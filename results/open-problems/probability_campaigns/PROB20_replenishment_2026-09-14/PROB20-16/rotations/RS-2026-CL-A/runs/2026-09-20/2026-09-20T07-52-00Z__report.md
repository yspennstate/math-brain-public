## 3. Read-k setup

Let `U_N` be uniform on `{-1,+1}^N`. Let

`f(x) = OR_{j=1}^m A_j(x_{B_j})`,                            (3.1)

where each `A_j` is a conjunction of literals on the coordinates in `B_j`, with width

`w_j = |B_j|`, `p_j=E_U A_j=2^{-w_j}`.                       (3.2)

The family is **read-k** if every coordinate belongs to at most `k` sets `B_j`:

`max_i #{j:i in B_j} <= k`.                                  (3.3)

Write

`q = E_U f`, `L_q = -log(1-q)`.                              (3.4)

No disjointness of the `B_j` is assumed.

## 4. A reusable read-k moment lemma

The needed dependence estimate is a direct consequence of Finner/Shearer-type inequalities and is consistent with the read-k concentration theory of Gavinsky--Lovett--Saks--Srinivasan.

### Lemma 4.1 (read-k complement and variance bounds)

Let `Y_1,...,Y_m` be Boolean functions of independent base coordinates, and assume each base coordinate influences at most `k` of the `Y_j`. Put

`p_j=E Y_j`, `lambda=sum_j p_j`, `G=sum_j Y_j`, and `Q_OR=P(max_j Y_j=1)`.

Then

`boxed{lambda <= k[-log(1-Q_OR)]}`                            (4.1)

and

`boxed{Var(G) <= k lambda.}`                                  (4.2)

#### Proof of (4.1)

Finner's inequality with fractional weights `1/k` gives

`E product_j (1-Y_j)`

`<= product_j (E(1-Y_j))^(1/k)`

`= product_j (1-p_j)^(1/k)`

`<= exp(-lambda/k)`.                                          (4.3)

The left side equals `1-Q_OR`. Taking `-log` gives (4.1).

#### Proof of (4.2)

For `t>=0`, the same inequality applied to `exp(k t Y_j)` gives

`E exp(t G)`

`<= product_j [E exp(k t Y_j)]^(1/k)`

`= product_j [1+p_j(e^(kt)-1)]^(1/k)`

`<= exp((lambda/k)(e^(kt)-1)).`                               (4.4)

Both sides of (4.4) equal `1` at `t=0` and have first derivative `lambda` there. If the second derivative of the left side at zero exceeded that of the right side, (4.4) would fail for all sufficiently small positive `t`. Hence

`E G^2 <= lambda^2+k lambda`,                                 (4.5)

which is equivalent to `Var(G)<=k lambda`. QED.

This lemma is not claimed as new; the read-k concentration mechanism is prior art. Its role here is to robustify the low-degree/noise implication.

## 5. Main theorem — read-k DNF transfer

### Theorem 5.1 (robust read-k DNF transfer)

Assume

`Adv_U^{<=D}(P,U) <= A`.                                     (5.1)

Fix an integer `W<=D`. Then

`boxed{
E_{T_rho P} f
<= k L_q + A k sqrt(L_q)
 + m[2^{-(W+1)} + A 2^{-(W+1)/2} + r_rho^{W+1}],
}`                                                            (5.2)

where

`r_rho=(1+rho)/2 < 1`.                                       (5.3)

Consequently, if along a sequence

`A_n=O(1)`, `k_n=O(1)`, `q_n->0`, and

`D_n/log(m_n+2) -> infinity`,                                 (5.4)

then after every fixed positive product-noise rate,

`boxed{E_{T_rho P_n} f_n -> 0.}`                             (5.5)

Thus no polynomial-size constant-read DNF whose null acceptance tends to zero can strongly distinguish the noised planted law from uniform when `D_n=(log n)^(1+Omega(1))`.

The theorem uses neither permutation invariance nor any planted high-degree likelihood regularity.

### Proof

Split terms at width `W`:

`S={j:w_j<=W}`, `L={j:w_j>W}`,                               (5.6)

and let `f_S,f_L` be the ORs over the two sets. Since

`f <= f_S+f_L`,                                               (5.7)

we bound the two pieces separately.

### Step 1: narrow terms

Let

`g_S=sum_{j in S} A_j`, `lambda_S=sum_{j in S}p_j`,           (5.8)

and `q_S=E_U f_S<=q`. The subfamily remains read-k. Lemma 4.1 gives

`lambda_S <= k[-log(1-q_S)] <= kL_q`,                         (5.9)

and

`Var_U(g_S) <= k lambda_S <= k^2 L_q`.                        (5.10)

Because `f_S<=g_S` and `T_rho` is positivity preserving,

`T_rho f_S <= T_rho g_S`.                                    (5.11)

The polynomial `T_rho g_S` has degree at most `W<=D`. Product noise is an `L2(U)` contraction, so

`Var_U(T_rho g_S) <= Var_U(g_S)`.                             (5.12)

Applying the source LDA bound to `T_rho g_S`,

`E_{T_rho P}f_S`

`=E_P(T_rho f_S)`

`<=E_P(T_rho g_S)`

`<=E_U g_S + A sqrt(Var_U(T_rho g_S))`

`<=lambda_S + A sqrt(k lambda_S)`

`<= kL_q + A k sqrt(L_q)`.                                   (5.13)

### Step 2: wide terms — avoid the overlap obstruction entirely

By a union bound and positivity of the noise operator,

`E_{T_rho P}f_L <= sum_{j in L} E_{T_rho P}A_j`.             (5.14)

Apply the inherited Fourier-tail theorem (2.1) separately to a width-`w` conjunction. Its Fourier coefficients all have absolute value `2^{-w}`. Therefore

`Tau_{D,rho}(A_j)`

`=2^{-w_j} sum_{s>D} binom(w_j,s)rho^s`

`<=2^{-w_j}(1+rho)^{w_j}`

`=r_rho^{w_j}`.                                              (5.15)

Also

`E_U A_j=2^{-w_j}`, `Var_U(A_j)<=2^{-w_j}`.                  (5.16)

Hence

`E_{T_rho P}A_j`

`<=2^{-w_j}+A2^{-w_j/2}+r_rho^{w_j}`.                        (5.17)

Since every `w_j>W`, summing (5.17) yields

`E_{T_rho P}f_L`

`<=m[2^{-(W+1)}+A2^{-(W+1)/2}+r_rho^{W+1}]`.                (5.18)

Adding (5.13) and (5.18) proves (5.2).

### Step 3: asymptotic choice of W

Let

`c_rho=min{(log 2)/2, -log r_rho}>0`.                         (5.19)

Choose

`W_n=ceil(2 log(m_n+2)/c_rho)`.                              (5.20)

Then

`m_n[2^{-(W_n+1)}+A_n2^{-(W_n+1)/2}+r_rho^{W_n+1}] ->0`,    (5.21)

while `D_n/log(m_n+2)->infinity` implies `W_n<=D_n` eventually. With `A_n,k_n=O(1)` and `q_n->0`, the first two terms in (5.2) also vanish. This proves (5.5). QED.

## 6. A quantitative form allowing growing read number

The proof itself gives more than the constant-read corollary. It is enough that

`k_n L_{q_n} + A_n k_n sqrt(L_{q_n}) ->0`                    (6.1)

and `D_n/log(m_n+2)->infinity`.

For bounded `A_n` and small `q_n`, (6.1) is essentially

`k_n sqrt(q_n) ->0`.                                         (6.2)

Thus some slowly growing read numbers are allowed when the null false-positive probability is correspondingly small.

## 7. Fixed finite-alphabet extension

Let the null be uniform on `Omega^N`, `|Omega|=s>=2`, and let a cylinder term fix all `w_j` coordinates in its block to prescribed symbols. Then

`p_j=s^{-w_j}`.                                              (7.1)

The read-k Lemma 4.1 is unchanged because it uses only independence of the base coordinates.

`r_{s,rho}=(1+(s-1)rho)/s<1`.                                (7.3)

`s^{-(W+1)} + A s^{-(W+1)/2} + r_{s,rho}^{W+1}`.             (7.4)

## 8. Quantitative Mao obstruction

Mao has `A_n=0` through `D_n`. Let `f_n` be his successful noised rank-test acceptance indicator, oriented so

`q_n=E_U f_n ->0`, `E_{T_rho P_n} f_n ->1`.                 (8.1)

Suppose `f_n` admitted a read-`k_n` DNF representation with `m_n` terms and `log m_n=o(D_n)`. Taking `W=D_n` in (5.2) gives

`1-o(1)`

`<= k_n[-log(1-q_n)]`

` + m_n[2^{-(D_n+1)}+r_rho^{D_n+1}]`.                       (8.2)

The second line is `o(1)`. Therefore

`boxed{k_n[-log(1-q_n)] >= 1-o(1).}`                         (8.3)

Since `q_n->0`,

`boxed{k_n q_n >= 1-o(1)}`                                  (8.4)

up to the standard `-log(1-q_n)=q_n(1+o(1))` equivalence.

Hence Mao's successful rank test cannot be represented by any subexponential-in-`D_n` constant-read DNF. More sharply, any such representation must have read number at least inverse-order in its null acceptance probability. This is a structural obstruction beyond the earlier statement that its Fourier `A`-norm or read-once size is large.
