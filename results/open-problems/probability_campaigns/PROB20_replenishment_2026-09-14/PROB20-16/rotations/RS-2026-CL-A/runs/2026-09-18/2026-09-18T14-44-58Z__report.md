## 3.2 New reversible exponential degree-distance gate

Let

\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m}\mathcal H_j
\]

be the orthogonal algebraic-degree decomposition, with number operator `N|_{H_j}=j`. Let `A=A^*>=0` be a reversible generator, with constants in `H_0` and `A H_0=0`. Write

\[
A_{jk}=\Pi_jA\Pi_k,\qquad c_{jk}=\|A_{jk}\|_{2\to2}.
\]

Assume three **model-level block conditions**:

1. diagonal degree coercivity
   \[
   A_{kk}\succeq \kappa k I\quad(k\ge1);
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
2. symmetric transition budget
   \[
   \sum_{j\ne k}c_{jk}\le \beta k\quad(k\ge1);
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
3. finite degree bandwidth
   \[
   A_{jk}=0\qquad\text{if }|j-k|>w.
   \tag{3.3}
   \]

Self-adjointness gives `c_{jk}=c_{kj}`. Suppose `beta<kappa`.

### Theorem 3.1 — reversible degree-distance gate

For every `s>=0` satisfying

\[
\delta_s:=\kappa-\beta\cosh(sw)>0,
\tag{3.4}
\]

every `K>D>=0`, and every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\bigl\|\Pi_{\le D}e^{-tA}\Pi_{\ge K}\bigr\|_{2\to2;\,\mathcal H_0^\perp}
\le
\exp\{-s(K-D)-\delta_s t\}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus a reversible finite-band generator cannot move high source-degree mass downward across a gap `K-D` without paying an exponential degree-distance cost. The self-adjoint cross-degree price is `cosh(sw)`, not the one-sided `e^{sw}` price that appears in nonreversible weighted transport.

### Proof

Let

\[
W_s=e^{sN},\qquad B_s=W_s^{-1}AW_s.
\]

Then

\[
W_s^{-1}e^{-tA}W_s=e^{-tB_s}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For `g=sum_j g_j` with `g_0=0`, self-adjointness yields

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle g,B_sg\rangle
=
\sum_k\langle g_k,A_{kk}g_k\rangle
+2\sum_{j<k}\cosh(s(k-j))
\operatorname{Re}\langle g_j,A_{jk}g_k\rangle.
\tag{3.7}
\]

By (3.1), the diagonal term is at least

\[
\kappa\sum_{k\ge1}k\|g_k\|^2.
\tag{3.8}
\]

For each off-diagonal pair, bandwidth and Cauchy-Schwarz give

\[
2\cosh(s(k-j))
\operatorname{Re}\langle g_j,A_{jk}g_k\rangle
\ge
-2\cosh(sw)c_{jk}\|g_j\|\|g_k\|
\]

and hence

\[
\ge
-\cosh(sw)c_{jk}(\|g_j\|^2+\|g_k\|^2).
\tag{3.9}
\]

Summing unordered pairs and using (3.2),

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle g,B_sg\rangle
\ge
[\kappa-\beta\cosh(sw)]
\sum_{k\ge1}k\|g_k\|^2.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Because `k>=1` on `H_0^perp`, this is at least

\[
\delta_s\|g\|^2.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Thus the numerical range of `B_s` on the mean-zero sector lies in `Re z>=delta_s`, and the standard dissipativity estimate gives

\[
\|e^{-tB_s}\|_{\mathcal H_0^\perp\to\mathcal H_0^\perp}
\le e^{-\delta_s t}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Finally, from (3.6),

\[
\Pi_{\le D}e^{-tA}\Pi_{\ge K}
=
\Pi_{\le D}W_s e^{-tB_s}W_s^{-1}\Pi_{\ge K}.
\]

The two diagonal weight factors obey

\[
\|\Pi_{\le D}W_s\|\le e^{sD},
\qquad
\|W_s^{-1}\Pi_{\ge K}\|\le e^{-sK}.
\]

Combining with (3.12) proves (3.5). `QED`

### Interpretation and limitations

- If `beta=0`, the degree filtration is invariant and the bound correctly allows arbitrarily large `s`, forcing exact zero leakage across a nonzero degree gap.
- If `beta>0`, the admissible exponential weight is bounded by
  \[
  s<\frac1w\operatorname{arcosh}(\kappa/\beta).
  \]
- Optimizing `s` for a fixed degree gap and time yields a Combes–Thomas/Davies-type tradeoff between distance and time.
- This theorem controls **propagation into lower source degree**. It does not by itself bound the entire noisy norm of an arbitrary likelihood ratio; it is meant to combine with the pressure/tail conditions already developed.

# 5. B30 — DO ONE SCALE, ONE DIRECTION AT A TIME

The previous run showed only that Mao's collision pressure is enormous somewhere above `D_n`. This move localizes that pressure to a precise degree scale.

## 5.1 Setup

Let `C<=F_2^E` be a binary linear code of dimension `k`, and let `G` be any coordinate-permutation group used to symmetrize its uniform law. For each `G`-orbit `O` in `F_2^E`, set

\[
N_O=|C^\perp\cap O|.
\]

The exact collision spectrum is

\[
S_G(q)=\sum_O q^{\mathrm{wt}(O)}\frac{N_O^2}{|O|}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
A_j=|C^\perp\cap\{y:\mathrm{wt}(y)=j\}|
\]

and

\[
W_{C^\perp}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^E A_jq^j.
\]

For fixed `q in (0,1)`, define

\[
\theta=\frac{q}{1+q}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

## 5.2 Central-band orbit-collision theorem

Define

\[
\delta_{E,k}
=
\sqrt{\frac{(k+2)\log2}{2E}}
\tag{5.3}
\]

and the degree band

\[
\mathcal B_{E,k}(q)
=
\{j:\ |j-\theta E|\le \delta_{E,k}E\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Theorem 5.1

For every binary linear code `C`, every coordinate-permutation group `G`, and every fixed `q in (0,1)`, the contribution to (5.1) from orbits whose weight lies in `B_{E,k}(q)` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{O:\,\mathrm{wt}(O)\in\mathcal B_{E,k}(q)}
q^{\mathrm{wt}(O)}\frac{N_O^2}{|O|}
\ge
4^{-(k+1)}(1+q)^E.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the MacWilliams collision pressure can be localized to a binomial central band of width `O(sqrt(kE))`, with no transitivity assumption.

### Proof

The classical MacWilliams identity gives

\[
W_{C^\perp}(q)
=
2^{-k}(1+q)^E
W_C\!\left(\frac{1-q}{1+q}\right)
\ge
2^{-k}(1+q)^E,
\tag{5.6}
\]

because the zero codeword contributes `1` to `W_C` and `(1-q)/(1+q)>=0`.

Trivially

\[
A_j\le \binom Ej.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore the weighted dual-code mass outside the band is at most

\[
\sum_{|j-\theta E|>\delta E}A_jq^j
\le
\sum_{|j-\theta E|>\delta E}\binom Ej q^j.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Since

\[
\binom Ej q^j
=(1+q)^E\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(E,\theta)=j\},
\]

Hoeffding's inequality yields

\[
\sum_{|j-\theta E|>\delta E}\binom Ej q^j
\le
2(1+q)^E e^{-2\delta^2E}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

With `delta=delta_{E,k}` from (5.3),

\[
2e^{-2\delta^2E}=2^{-k-1}.
\]

Combining with (5.6), the weighted dual-code mass inside the central band obeys

\[
W_{\mathcal B}(q)
:=
\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}A_jq^j
\ge
2^{-k-1}(1+q)^E.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Now sum by `G`-orbits and apply weighted Cauchy-Schwarz on the band:

\[
W_{\mathcal B}(q)^2
=
\left(\sum_{O:\,\mathrm{wt}(O)\in\mathcal B}
q^{\mathrm{wt}(O)}N_O\right)^2
\]

\[
\le
\left(\sum_{O:\,\mathrm{wt}(O)\in\mathcal B}
q^{\mathrm{wt}(O)}\frac{N_O^2}{|O|}\right)
\left(\sum_{O:\,\mathrm{wt}(O)\in\mathcal B}
q^{\mathrm{wt}(O)}|O|\right).
\tag{5.11}
\]

The second factor is at most the full Boolean-cube partition function `(1+q)^E`. Therefore (5.10)-(5.11) give

\[
S_{G,\mathcal B}(q)
\ge
\frac{2^{-2k-2}(1+q)^{2E}}{(1+q)^E}
=
4^{-(k+1)}(1+q)^E.
\]

This proves (5.5). `QED`
