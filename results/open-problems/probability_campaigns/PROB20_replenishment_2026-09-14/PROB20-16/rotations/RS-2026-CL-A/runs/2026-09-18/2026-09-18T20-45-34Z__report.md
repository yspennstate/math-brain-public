# 3. B7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: the first genuine Ising source-degree leakage is cubic

The preceding run proved that the first derivative of every cross-source-degree block vanishes at the product point. The stronger fact is that **the quadratic term vanishes as well**. The first possible leakage is cubic and is generated locally by three-neighbor stars.

## 3.1 Algebraic-filtration lemma

Retain the zero-field pairwise Ising setup on a finite graph `G=(V,E)`:

\[
Q_\alpha(x)\propto
\exp\!\left(\alpha\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}J_{ij}x_ix_j\right),
\qquad
A_\alpha=\sum_i(I-E_i^\alpha).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `V_{<=k}` be the ordinary algebraic polynomial space of degree at most `k`, independent of `alpha`, and let

\[
U_{\alpha,k}=V_{\le k}\cap V_{\le k-1}^{\perp_{Q_\alpha}},
\qquad \Pi_{\alpha,k}:L^2(Q_\alpha)\to U_{\alpha,k}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Take `f in V_{<=k}`. Relative to site `i`, write uniquely

\[
f(x)=a_i(x_{-i})+x_i b_i(x_{-i}),
\qquad
\deg a_i\le k,\quad \deg b_i\le k-1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The conditional mean is

\[
m_i^\alpha(x_{-i})
=E_{Q_\alpha}[x_i\mid x_{-i}]
=\tanh(\alpha h_i),
\qquad
h_i=\sum_jJ_{ij}x_j.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence

\[
E_i^\alpha f=a_i+b_i m_i^\alpha.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Taylor expansion at `alpha=0` gives

\[
m_i^\alpha
=\alpha h_i-\frac{\alpha^3}{3}h_i^3+O_G(\alpha^5),
\tag{3.6}
\]

with **no quadratic term**. Multiplication of `b_i` by the linear function `h_i` stays in `V_{<=k}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
A_\alpha V_{\le k}
\subseteq V_{\le k}+\alpha^3 V_{\le k+2}+O_G(\alpha^5)
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

in finite-volume analytic expansion. In particular, the quotient map from `V_{<=k}` to `V/V_{<=k}` has zero coefficients through order two.

For `j>k`, `U_{\alpha,j}` is orthogonal to `V_{<=j-1}`, hence to `V_{<=k}`. Because `\Pi_{\alpha,k}` has range inside `V_{<=k}`, (3.7) yields

\[
\Pi_{\alpha,j}A_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha,k}=O_G(\alpha^3)
\qquad (j>k).
\tag{3.8}
\]

For `j<k`, self-adjointness of `A_alpha` in `L^2(Q_alpha)` gives the same conclusion by adjunction. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{\alpha,j}A_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha,k}=O_G(\alpha^3)
\quad\text{for every }j\ne k.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

This strictly improves the preceding finite-volume statement `O_G(alpha^2)`.

## 3.2 Exact class: maximum degree at most two

If `deg_G(i)<=2` for every vertex, then `m_i^alpha` is an odd Boolean function of at most two neighboring spins. Every such function has Fourier/algebraic degree one: for two variables, global oddness eliminates the constant and `x_1x_2` terms. Hence `m_i^alpha` is **exactly linear** in the neighboring spins for every `alpha`, not only perturbatively.

Equation (3.5) then gives

\[
A_\alpha V_{\le k}\subseteq V_{\le k}
\quad\text{for every }k\text{ and every finite }\alpha.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Since `A_alpha` is self-adjoint, invariance of the nested filtration implies invariance of each orthogonal difference `U_{alpha,k}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{\alpha,j}A_\alpha\Pi_{\alpha,k}=0
\quad(j\ne k)
\quad\text{whenever }\Delta(G)\le2.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Thus paths and cycles are an exact nonproduct class with **zero source-degree leakage**, regardless of interaction strength. The finite-volume high-temperature issue begins only when a site can see at least three neighbors.

## 3.3 The explicit cubic three-star coefficient

Let `chi_S=prod_{i in S}x_i`, `|S|=k`, at the product point. Since

\[
\left.\frac{d^3}{d\alpha^3}\tanh(\alpha h_i)\right|_{\alpha=0}
=-2h_i^3,
\tag{3.12}
\]

we get

\[
A_0^{(3)}\chi_S
=2\sum_{i\in S}\chi_{S\setminus\{i\}}h_i^3.
\tag{3.13}
\]

The degree-`k+2` part comes exactly from three **distinct** neighbors outside `S`. The coefficient of a distinct triple in `h_i^3` is `6J_{ij}J_{i\ell}J_{im}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
P^{(0)}_{k+2}A_0^{(3)}\chi_S
=12\sum_{i\in S}
\sum_{\{j,\ell,m\}\subseteq N(i)\setminus S}
J_{ij}J_{i\ell}J_{im}
\chi_{S\setminus\{i\}\cup\{j,\ell,m\}}.
}
\tag{3.14}
\]

Equivalently, the coefficient of `alpha^3` in the upward leakage is the same sum with factor `2` instead of `12`.

This identifies the local weak-limit object controlling the first nonzero leakage: **radius-one three-neighbor stars**. Vertices of degree at most two contribute exactly zero. On graphs of maximum degree `Delta` with `|J_e|<=J_*`, elementary row/column counting of such stars gives an `n`-independent bound of order

\[
O(\Delta^3J_*^3)
\tag{3.15}
\]

for the normalized leading cubic off-degree coefficient. The important qualification is that this is an **infinitesimal coefficient bound**. It does not yet justify interchanging `sup_G` with a finite-`alpha` remainder, and I do not claim the uniform estimate `beta_G(alpha)<=C|alpha|^3` for a fixed nonzero interval in this run.

# 4. B11 — DUALIZE: one source-number commutator controls all degree transport

The previous transport theorem used a finite bandwidth `w` and a scalar row budget. A more intrinsic object is the commutator of the generator with the source-degree number operator.

## 4.1 Source-number operator and exact block identity

For a fixed reversible null and its orthogonal source-degree layers, define

\[
\mathcal N=\sum_{k\ge0}k\Pi_k.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `j ne k`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_j[\mathcal N,A]\Pi_k
=(j-k)\Pi_jA\Pi_k.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus all off-degree blocks are encoded exactly by one commutator.

For `s>=0`, define the weighted commutator budget

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak B_s(A,\mathcal N)
=
\sup_{k\ge1}\frac1k
\sum_{j\ne k}
\cosh(s|j-k|)
\frac{\|\Pi_j[\mathcal N,A]\Pi_k\|}{|j-k|}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

By (4.2), this is exactly

\[
\mathfrak B_s
=
\sup_{k\ge1}\frac1k
\sum_{j\ne k}\cosh(s|j-k|)\|\Pi_jA\Pi_k\|.
\tag{4.4}
\]

If the old assumptions `sum_{j ne k}||Pi_j A Pi_k||<=beta k` and `|j-k|<=w` hold, then immediately

\[
\mathfrak B_s\le\beta\cosh(sw),
\tag{4.5}
\]

so this formulation strictly contains the previous finite-band certificate.

## 4.2 Dirichlet lower bound under exponential conjugation

Assume diagonal source-degree coercivity

\[
\Pi_kA\Pi_k\succeq \kappa k\Pi_k
\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Let `W_s=e^{-s\mathcal N}` and `A_s=W_sAW_s^{-1}`. For `g=sum_k g_k`, self-adjointness of `A` pairs the `(j,k)` and `(k,j)` terms. Taking real parts,

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle g,A_sg\rangle
=\sum_k\langle g_k,A_{kk}g_k\rangle
+2\sum_{j<k}\cosh(s(k-j))
\operatorname{Re}\langle g_j,A_{jk}g_k\rangle.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Using `2ab<=a^2+b^2`,

\[
2|\langle g_j,A_{jk}g_k\rangle|
\le
\|A_{jk}\|\bigl(\|g_j\|^2+\|g_k\|^2\bigr).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Summing the resulting row charges and applying (4.4) gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}\langle g,A_sg\rangle
\ge
(\kappa-\mathfrak B_s)
\langle g,\mathcal N g\rangle.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

On the mean-zero sector `mathcal N>=I`. Therefore, whenever

\[
\delta_s:=\kappa-\mathfrak B_s>0,
\tag{4.10}
\]

`A_s` is accretive with lower bound `delta_s`, and

\[
\|e^{-tA_s}\|_{L^2_0\to L^2_0}\le e^{-\delta_st}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Undoing the conjugation gives the degree-distance gate

\[
\boxed{
\|\Pi_{\le D}e^{-tA}\Pi_{\ge K}\|_{2\to2}
\le
\exp\{-s(K-D)-\delta_st\}
\qquad(K>D).
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

This is the earlier reversible gate with the artificial bandwidth parameter removed. The actual degree-gap profile enters through `mathfrak B_s`.

## 4.3 Why this is the right dual object for B7

For the Ising family, let `mathcal N_alpha` be the number operator built from the `Q_alpha` source-degree layers. B7 shows that

\[
[\mathcal N_\alpha,A_\alpha]
=O_G(\alpha^3)
\tag{4.13}
\]

at the product point, with the leading local terms generated by three-neighbor stars. Therefore the transport problem can be reduced to a single high-temperature task:

> Prove an `n`-uniform connected expansion for the weighted commutator norm `mathfrak B_s(A_alpha,mathcal N_alpha)` and show it is `O(|alpha|^3)` on a fixed high-temperature interval.

That is more compact and more local than bounding every cross-degree block separately. Commutator conjugation and Combes–Thomas/Davies ideas are classical; the source-degree inference use is not claimed novel.

# 5. B28 — REPLACE “FOR ALL” WITH “FOR TYPICAL”: overlap-weighted interface tails

The previous B14+B22 theorem controlled an exact shell sum with a deterministic interface cost. The next relaxation is to permit rare shells with very large interfaces, provided they are rare under the **overlap-weighted shell law**, not merely under the raw latent prior.

## 5.1 Overlap-weighted shell distribution

In the transitive baseline notation, let

\[
w_r=\frac{v_r\kappa_r}{M},
\qquad
S_0=\sum_rw_r,
\qquad
\mu(r)=\frac{w_r}{S_0}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus `mu` is the baseline two-copy overlap mass normalized to a probability distribution on shells. Suppose weak symmetry breaking plus the cluster-interface lemma gives

\[
K(z,z')\le e^\xi K_0(z,z')e^{cI_r}
\quad\text{on shell }r.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then every good-row load obeys

\[
\boxed{
R\le e^\xi S_0\,E_\mu[e^{cI}].
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This exposes the correct typicality measure: a shell with tiny latent probability can still matter if its baseline overlap is huge, and conversely a combinatorially large shell can be harmless if `kappa_r` suppresses it.

## 5.2 Exponential-tail criterion

Assume there is a cutoff `a_n=o(D_n)`, constants `C_0<infty` and `gamma>c`, such that for all integers `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu_n\{I_n\ge a_n+m\}
\le C_0e^{-\gamma m}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Let `Y=(I-a_n)_+`. The tail-sum identity for a nonnegative integer-valued random variable gives

\[
E e^{cY}
=1+(e^c-1)\sum_{m\ge1}e^{c(m-1)}P(Y\ge m).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Using (5.4),

\[
E e^{cY}
\le
1+
C_0\frac{(e^c-1)e^{-\gamma}}{1-e^{c-\gamma}}
=:C(c,\gamma,C_0)<\infty.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Since `e^{cI}<=e^{ca_n}e^{cY}`,

\[
\boxed{
E_{\mu_n}e^{cI_n}
\le C(c,\gamma,C_0)e^{ca_n}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
\log^+S_{0,n}=o(D_n),
\qquad
\xi_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
a_n=o(D_n),
\tag{5.8}
\]

then `log^+ R_n=o(D_n)`. Rare interfaces of order `D_n` or larger are allowed; only their overlap-weighted tail must beat the cluster cost exponent `c`.

A convenient corollary is that if `I_n` under `mu_n` is stochastically dominated by a Poisson/binomial-type variable with mean `m_n=o(D_n)` and a fixed exponential moment at `c`, then the interface contribution has log moment `O(m_n)=o(D_n)`.

## 5.3 Nontransitive exceptional latents

Now combine this with the earlier exact typical-overlap truncation theorem. Let `G_n` be a good latent set on which the shell-tail criterion gives row load at most `R_n`. Let

\[
\delta_n=\pi_n(G_n^c),
\qquad
J_{B,n}=\sum_{z\notin G_n}\pi_n(z)K_n(z,z).
\tag{5.9}
\]

The prior theorem gives

\[
1+\chi_n^2
\le
\left(\sqrt{R_n}+\sqrt{\delta_nJ_{B,n}}\right)^2.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log^+R_n=o(D_n)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log^+(\delta_nJ_{B,n})=o(D_n)
\Longrightarrow
\frac1{D_n}\log(1+\chi_n^2)\to0.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

This directly handles the previous rare-latent Perron stress family: a very large diagonal overlap on a vanishing exceptional prior set is harmless exactly when its **prior-weighted** diagonal mass remains subexponential.
