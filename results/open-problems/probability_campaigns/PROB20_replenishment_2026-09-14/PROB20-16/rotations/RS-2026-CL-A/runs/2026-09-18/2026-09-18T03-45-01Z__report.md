## 4. B28 — replace worst-fragment extension by the exact typical extension

For `S subseteq A_0`, retain the conditional extension load

\[
h(S)=\sum_{x\in A_0\setminus S}
\Pr(x\in A\mid S\subseteq A).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The preceding run assumed an inequality on **every** `S`. That is stronger than the exact recurrence needs.

### Definition 4.1 — level-averaged extension load

Whenever `m_j>0`, define

\[
\boxed{
\bar h_j
:=
\frac{
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq A_0\\|S|=j}}a(S)h(S)
}{m_j}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the extension load of a `j`-fragment drawn with probability proportional to its cylinder probability `a(S)`. Rare fragments with huge reinforcement contribute only in proportion to their actual conditional mass.

The exact extension recurrence from the preceding report immediately becomes

\[
\boxed{
(j+1)m_{j+1}=m_j\bar h_j
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
(j+1)b_{j+1}=\lambda\bar h_j b_j.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is an identity, not an inequality.

### Theorem 4.2 — exact `q`-tilted typical-extension identity

Define a probability law on template fragments by

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_q(S)
=
\frac{(\lambda q)^{|S|}a(S)}{F(q)},
\qquad S\subseteq A_0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)=q\lambda\,\mathbb E_{\Pi_q}h(S).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

#### Proof

Normalization follows from

\[
\sum_{S\subseteq A_0}(\lambda q)^{|S|}a(S)
=\sum_j(\lambda q)^jm_j
=F(q).
\]

Using (4.3),

\[
\begin{aligned}
q\lambda F(q)\mathbb E_{\Pi_q}h(S)
&=q\lambda\sum_j(\lambda q)^j
  \sum_{|S|=j}a(S)h(S)\\
&=q\lambda\sum_j(\lambda q)^j(j+1)m_{j+1}\\
&=qF'(q).
\end{aligned}
\]

Divide by `F(q)`. `□`

Thus the pressure barycenter is the **typical conditional extension under the noised spectral Gibbs law**. A tiny collection of pathological fragments is harmless unless it receives nonnegligible `q`-tilted mass.

### Corollary 4.3 — overlap-tilt representation

Let

\[
t=\lambda q,
\qquad
s=\frac{t}{1+t}.
\]

Tilt the ordinary overlap `Z=|A_0 cap A|` by

\[
\frac{d\mathbb P_q}{d\mathbb P}(Z)
=\frac{(1+t)^Z}{F(q)}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)=s\,\mathbb E_q Z.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Moreover, `Pi_q` can be generated in physical space by first drawing `A` under the overlap tilt (4.7), then independently retaining each element of `A_0 cap A` with probability `s`. Indeed the factors `(1+t)^Z s^{|S|}(1-s)^{Z-|S|}` collapse to `t^{|S|}`.

Equation (4.8) is still an identity, so by itself it is not advertised as a solution condition. Its value is that it exposes the exact physical-space quantity that a model-specific argument must control.

## 5. B18 — factorial size bias: the extension recurrence in a standard count-distribution language

Because

\[
m_j=\frac{\mathbb E (Z)_j}{j!},
\]

(4.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
\bar h_j
=
\frac{\mathbb E (Z)_{j+1}}{\mathbb E (Z)_j}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define the `j`-th factorial-size-biased overlap by

\[
\Pr(Z^{[j]}=z)
=
\frac{(z)_j\Pr(Z=z)}{\mathbb E(Z)_j}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\bar h_j
=\mathbb E[Z^{[j]}-j].
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

So the group-orbit extension condition can be stated without referring to individual fragments at all: it is a bound on the **residual overlap after `j`-fold factorial size biasing**.

This is close in spirit to Stein/size-bias characterizations of Poisson and negative-binomial laws, but it is important not to conflate it with the classical Panjer recursion. Panjer's `(a,b,0)` relation is a recursion for the pmf `p_k`; (5.1) is a recursion for successive factorial moments. The bounded literature check did not locate an exact theorem identifying (5.1), in the present likelihood-ratio/noise setting, with Kireeva's low-degree implication.

## 6. B6 — affine average extension and the sharp negative-binomial envelope

The all-fragment condition of the preceding run can now be weakened to the level-average condition

\[
\boxed{
\lambda\bar h_j\le\alpha+\beta j
\qquad(j\ge0).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is strictly weaker than requiring `lambda h(S)<=alpha+beta|S|` for every `S`.

### Theorem 6.1 — coefficientwise negative-binomial majorant

Under (6.1),

\[
\boxed{
(j+1)b_{j+1}\le(\alpha+\beta j)b_j.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

If `beta=0`, then for every `q>=0`,

\[
\boxed{F(q)\le e^{\alpha q}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If `beta>0` and `q beta<1`, then

\[
\boxed{
F(q)\le(1-\beta q)^{-\alpha/\beta},
\qquad
\mu(q)\le\frac{\alpha q}{1-\beta q}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (6.2) is immediate from the exact identity (4.4). Iteration gives

\[
b_j
\le
\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(\alpha+\beta i)}{j!}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For `beta=0`, (6.5) gives `b_j<=alpha^j/j!`, hence (6.3). For `beta>0`, put `r=alpha/beta`; then

\[
b_j\le\frac{\beta^j(r)^{\overline j}}{j!},
\]

and the standard binomial-series identity gives

\[
F(q)\le\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{(r)^{\overline j}}{j!}(\beta q)^j
=(1-\beta q)^{-r}.
\]

For the barycenter bound, multiply (6.2) by `q^{j+1}` and sum:

\[
qF'(q)
\le q\alpha F(q)+q\beta\,qF'(q).
\]

If `q beta<1`, rearrangement gives the second inequality in (6.4). `□`

### Corollary 6.2 — source-scale implication under the weaker average condition

Let

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\Omega(1)},
\qquad
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\le A.
\]

Suppose the orbit-overlap laws satisfy (6.1) with parameters `alpha_n,beta_n` and, for some fixed `q_0>0` and `delta>0`,

\[
\alpha_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
q_0\beta_n\le1-\delta.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Then uniformly for fixed `0<q<=q_0`,

\[
\frac{\mu_n(q)}{D_n+1}
\le
\frac{q_0\alpha_n}{\delta(D_n+1)}
\to0.
\]

The preserved pressure theorem therefore yields

\[
\sup_n\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P_n\|Q_n)<\infty
\]

(after finitely many initial `n`), and hence a uniform TV gap from one against **every measurable test**. This is stronger than excluding one polynomial-degree class, but it still covers a structured orbit family and is therefore `PARTIAL` for the full source question.

## 7. Sharpness: the affine extremal is exactly negative binomial

The coefficient majorant in Theorem 6.1 is not an arbitrary proof convenience. Equality in (6.2) produces

\[
b_j^*
=
\frac{\beta^j(\alpha/\beta)^{\overline j}}{j!},
\qquad
F^*(q)=(1-\beta q)^{-\alpha/\beta}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is exactly the factorial-moment generating function generated by a negative-binomial overlap.

Let

\[
Z\sim\operatorname{NB}(r,p),
\qquad
r=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},
\qquad
p=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\beta},
\]

using the convention that `Z` is the number of failures before `r` successes. Its PGF is

\[
G_Z(s)
=\left(\frac{p}{1-(1-p)s}\right)^r.
\]

Since `(1-p)/p=beta/lambda`,

\[
\mathbb E(1+\lambda q)^Z
=(1-\beta q)^{-r},
\tag{7.2}
\]

and its factorial moments obey

\[
\frac{\mathbb E(Z)_{j+1}}{\mathbb E(Z)_j}
=(r+j)\frac{1-p}{p}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\lambda\bar h_j
=\alpha+\beta j
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

with equality at every level.

Therefore the surface

\[
\boxed{q\beta=1}
\tag{7.4}
\]

is the genuine analytic critical boundary of the affine factorial-size-bias class. As `q beta` approaches one from below, the extremal pressure diverges. At or above one, the truncated coefficient extremals

\[
F_M(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{M}
\frac{(\alpha/\beta)^{\overline j}}{j!}(\beta q)^j
\]

obey the same affine recurrence up to their terminal coefficient and are unbounded as `M->infinity` (`q beta=1` gives polynomially divergent partial sums; `q beta>1` gives exponential divergence). Thus no uniform bound can follow from the affine moment-ratio hypothesis alone beyond the critical surface. I do **not** assert that every truncated extremal polynomial is itself a realizable likelihood-ratio spectrum; the sharpness statement here is for the coefficient/moment inequality, while the full negative-binomial law provides the exact infinite-support probabilistic extremal.

This is the main B6 outcome: the denominator `1-q beta` in the preceding orbit theorem is not merely algebraic slack. It is the natural negative-binomial/branching criticality parameter.

## 8. B28 stress test — rare bad fragments really can be averaged away

To verify that level-averaging is genuinely weaker than worst-fragment control, consider a product group orbit with two independent components.

- A “good” component chooses one coordinate uniformly from `N_0` possibilities.
- A “rare cluster” component chooses one block uniformly from `N` blocks, each block containing `M` coordinates, and plants the whole selected block.

Take the template to be one good coordinate plus all `M` coordinates of block 1. Under the product action,

\[
Z=X+MB,
\qquad
X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/N_0),
\qquad
B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/N),
\]

independently. Conditioning on a single template coordinate from the rare cluster forces `B=1`, so for such a fragment

\[
h(S)\ge M-1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus the worst-fragment extension load diverges with `M`.

However, with `t=lambda q`, the exact spectral pressure is

\[
F(q)
=\left(1+\frac{t}{N_0}\right)
\left[1+\frac{(1+t)^M-1}{N}\right],
\tag{8.2}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)
=
\frac{t/N_0}{1+t/N_0}
+
\frac{tM(1+t)^{M-1}/N}
{1+((1+t)^M-1)/N}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For every fixed `q_0`, if `N` grows faster than `(1+lambda q_0)^M M`, the second term is uniformly negligible for `q<=q_0`, even though (8.1) is large. This confirms exactly what B28 was meant to test: rare high-reinforcement fragments need not control the noised likelihood ratio.

This example is a structural strictness test, not a claim of a new natural inference model and not a source-level separation theorem. At Kireeva's superlogarithmic cutoff, some choices of `M,N` may also make the old worst-case condition sufficient; the point here is the exact logical weakening and the correct tilted weighting.
