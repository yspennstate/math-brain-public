## 3. B24 FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT — closed-form extremal barrier for a banded signed degree process

\[

and define

\[
a_j=\inf_{0\ne v\in\mathcal H_j}
\frac{\langle v,A_{jj}v\rangle}{\|v\|^2},
\qquad
c_{jk}=\|A_{jk}\|\quad(j\ne k).
\]

For `u(t)=e^{-tA}f`, `x_j(t)=||Pi_j u(t)||`, the previous theorem gives

\[
x'(t)\le Gx(t),
\qquad
G_{jj}=-a_j,\quad G_{jk}=c_{jk}\ (j\ne k),
\tag{3.1}

componentwise in the Dini-derivative sense.

The question in this run is: under a small amount of **model-level information** about damping and allowed degree jumps, can the entire signed path expansion be collapsed to a closed form?

### Theorem 3.1 (bandwidth/transition-budget barrier)

Assume constants `kappa>0`, `beta>=0`, and an integer `w>=1` such that for every degree `k>=1`:

1. linear diagonal damping:

\[
a_k\ge \kappa k;
\tag{3.2}
\]

2. column transition budget:

\[
\sum_{j\ne k}c_{jk}\le \beta k;
\tag{3.3}
\]

3. no single downward jump exceeds `w`:

\[
c_{jk}=0\qquad\text{if }j<k-w.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Assume `beta<kappa` and put

\[
p=\frac{\beta}{\kappa},
\qquad
\boxed{
R_t=
\left[p+(1-p)e^{-w\kappa t}\right]^{1/w}
<1
\quad(t>0).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then for every vector `h` supported only in degrees `k>D`,

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}h\|_2
\le
\sum_{k>D}R_t^k\|\Pi_kh\|_2.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Consequently, for arbitrary `f`,

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}f\|_2
\le
\|P_Df\|_2
+
\sum_{k>D}R_t^k\|\Pi_kf\|_2.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

If there are no off-diagonal transitions (`w=0`), the same conclusion holds with `R_t=e^{-\kappa t}`.

#### Proof

By the signed band comparison it is enough to control the positive system `(3.1)`. For `0<z<=1`, define its weighted moment

\[
W(t,z)=\sum_{j=0}^J x_j(t)z^j.
\]

Using `(3.2)`–`(3.4)`, for every source degree `k>=1`, every target degree carrying `c_{jk}` obeys `j>=k-w`; since `0<z<=1`,

\[
\sum_{j\ne k}c_{jk}z^j
\le
z^{k-w}\sum_{j\ne k}c_{jk}
\le
\beta k z^{k-w}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\partial_tW(t,z)
\le
\left(\beta z^{1-w}-\kappa z\right)\partial_zW(t,z).
\tag{3.8}
\]

Run the characteristic backward from terminal point `z(t)=1`. Along

\[
z'(s)=\kappa z(s)-\beta z(s)^{1-w}
\tag{3.9}
\]

we have `dW(s,z(s))/ds<=0`. Setting `y=z^w` gives the linear equation

\[
y'=w\kappa y-w\beta,
\]

and the terminal condition `y(t)=1` gives

\[
y(0)=p+(1-p)e^{-w\kappa t}=R_t^w.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_jx_j(t)=W(t,1)
\le
W(0,R_t)=\sum_kR_t^kx_k(0).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Since `||u(t)||_2<=(sum_j||Pi_j u(t)||)`, `(3.6)` follows for high-supported `h`. For general `f=P_Df+H_Df`, contractivity of `e^{-tA}` gives

\[
\|e^{-tA}P_Df\|_2\le\|P_Df\|_2,
\]

and `(3.6)` applies to `H_Df`, yielding `(3.7)`. `square`

### Why the pure-down chain is extremal for this relaxed information

For any fixed `z<1`, the weighted contribution `z^j` is maximized by spending the entire allowed transition budget at the **smallest permitted target degree**, namely `j=k-w`. Thus the scalar transport equation `(3.8)` is attained by the relaxed one-direction chain with damping `kappa k` and downward rate `beta k` in jumps of size `w`. This identifies the extremal object for the weighted-moment relaxation. It need not itself be realizable as a self-adjoint generator, so no self-adjoint sharpness claim is made.

Put

b_k=\|\Pi_kf\|_2^2,
\qquad

\le

{\sqrt{1-(R_t/v)^2}}

\tag{3.11}

Therefore

\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}f\|_2
\le

## 4. B19 MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM — a polynomial-description-size certificate from bounded blocks and bounded treewidth

The previous theorem is only useful if `kappa`, `beta`, `w`, and the intermediate-radius energy can be computed from an ordinary model description. Here is one exact class where they can.

### Theorem 4.1 (independent bounded blocks give explicit band constants)

Let

\[
Q_m=\bigotimes_{a=1}^m q_a,
\]

where each block `q_a` has full support on at most `r` binary variables, with `r` fixed. Let `A_a>=0` be a self-adjoint reversible local generator on `L^2(q_a)`, with `A_a 1=0`, and let

\[
A=\sum_{a=1}^m A_a.
\]

For block `a`, let

\[
V_{a,d}=\{\text{ordinary algebraic polynomials of degree }\le d\},
\qquad
U_{a,d}=V_{a,d}\ominus V_{a,d-1},
\]

with projections `P_{a,d}`. Define the finite local constants

\[
\kappa
=
\min_{a,\,d\ge1}
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(P_{a,d}A_aP_{a,d}|_{U_{a,d}})}{d},
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\beta_0
=
\max_a
\left\|
A_a-\sum_dP_{a,d}A_aP_{a,d}
\right\|,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
w=
\max\{|b-d|:
P_{a,b}A_aP_{a,d}\ne0\text{ for some }a\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because `A_a1=0` and `A_a` is self-adjoint, constants do not couple to nonconstants; hence `w<=r-1` whenever the off-diagonal part is nonzero.

For the global ordinary algebraic degree decomposition, the comparison constants satisfy

\[
\boxed{a_k\ge\kappa k,}
\tag{4.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\ne k}c_{jk}
\le 2w\beta_0\,k,
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and `c_{jk}=0` for `|j-k|>w`. Therefore Theorem 3.1 applies with

\[
\boxed{\beta=2w\beta_0}
\tag{4.6}
\]

whenever `2w beta_0<kappa`.

#### Proof

Since the block measures are independent, the global orthogonal algebraic grading is the tensor grading by local layers `U_{a,d_a}`, with total degree `sum_a d_a`. On a tensor sector of total degree `k`, the degree-preserving compression of `A` is the sum of the local same-degree compressions. By `(4.1)`, its quadratic form is at least

\[
\sum_a\kappa d_a=\kappa k,
\]

which proves `(4.4)`.

Write each local generator as its local-degree diagonal part plus `R_a`. A block with `d_a=0` is constant, and `R_a` annihilates/cannot enter that block because self-adjointness and `A_a1=0` give

\[
P_{a,0}A_aP_{a,d}=P_{a,d}A_aP_{a,0}=0
\qquad(d\ge1).
\]

Thus on a global degree-`k` sector only the **active** blocks with `d_a>=1` can contribute to a degree-changing transition. There are at most `k` active blocks. For any fixed total-degree change `delta`, triangle inequality gives the corresponding block norm at most `k beta_0`. There are at most `2w` nonzero values of `delta` with `0<|delta|<=w`. Summing these target-degree norms gives `(4.5)`. `square`

All quantities in `(4.1)`–`(4.3)` come from constant-size local matrices when `r` is fixed. They can therefore be computed in time polynomial in the explicit block description size.

### Theorem 4.2 (bounded-treewidth alternative gives the needed energy pressure without Fourier enumeration)

The remaining quantity in `(3.13)` is a **single evaluation** of the degree-energy polynomial, not its coefficient list. Suppose the positive likelihood ratio relative to `Q_m` is represented by a bounded-arity factor graph

L(x)=Z^{-1}\prod_{e\in\mathcal F}\psi_e(x_e),
\tag{4.7}
\]

whose block-factor graph has treewidth at most a fixed `tau`. Choose on each block an orthonormal algebraic basis

\[
\{\phi_{a,s}:s\in\mathcal S_a\},
\qquad d(s)=\text{local algebraic degree},

with the constant basis element having degree zero. Define the local doubled kernel

\[
K_{a,z}(x_a,x_a')
=
\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_a}
 z^{d(s)}\phi_{a,s}(x_a)\phi_{a,s}(x_a').
\tag{4.8}
\]

Then exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{X,X'\sim Q_m}
\left[
L(X)L(X')\prod_{a=1}^mK_{a,z}(X_a,X_a')
\right]
=1+F_{L-1}(z).
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Indeed, expanding the kernels and using independence/orthonormality gives

\[
\sum_{\alpha}\widehat L_\alpha^2 z^{\deg\alpha},

whose constant term is `1` and whose nonconstant part is `F_{L-1}(z)`.

The numerator in `(4.9)` and the normalizing factor `Z` are finite partition-function/tensor contractions on the doubled bounded-treewidth factor graph. For fixed block alphabet, arity and treewidth, standard junction-tree/tensor-network dynamic programming computes `(4.9)` at a specified `z` in polynomial time in `m`. ### Theorem 5.1 (exact Fourier energy of a symmetrized linear code)

Let the observation space be `F_2^E` with uniform null `Q`. Let `C<=F_2^E` be a binary linear subspace and let `P_C` be uniform on `C`. For `y in F_2^E`, write

\[
\chi_y(x)=(-1)^{\langle x,y\rangle}.
\]

Then the likelihood ratio is exactly

\[
\boxed{
L_C(x)=\sum_{y\in C^\perp}\chi_y(x).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Now let a finite coordinate-permutation group `G` act on `F_2^E`, and let `P` be the uniform mixture of `gP_C`, `g in G`. For a `G`-orbit `O` in the dual coordinate space, define

\[
N_O=|C^\perp\cap O|.
\]

Then every `y in O` has Fourier coefficient

\[
\boxed{\widehat L(y)=\frac{N_O}{|O|}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently the degree-`j` Fourier energy is

\[
\boxed{
b_j
=
\sum_{\substack{O\\\mathrm{wt}(O)=j}}
\frac{N_O^2}{|O|}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If

\[
A_j=|\{y\in C^\perp:\mathrm{wt}(y)=j\}|
=\sum_{\mathrm{wt}(O)=j}N_O
\tag{5.4}
\]

is the ordinary dual weight enumerator coefficient, then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A_j^2}{\binom{|E|}{j}}
\le b_j\le A_j.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

The standard character-orthogonality identity gives

\[
\sum_{y\in C^\perp}\chi_y(x)
=|C^\perp|\mathbf 1_{\{x\in C\}}
=\frac{2^{|E|}}{|C|}\mathbf 1_{\{x\in C\}},
\]

which is exactly the density of the uniform law on `C` relative to uniform `Q`, proving `(5.1)`.

Averaging over `g in G`, the coefficient at `y` is the probability that `g^{-1}y` lies in `C^perp`. Conditional on the orbit of `y`, `g^{-1}y` is uniform on that orbit, giving `(5.2)`. Summing coefficient squares over weight-`j` characters gives `(5.3)`.

For `(5.5)`, `N_O<=|O|` gives `N_O^2/|O|<=N_O`; summing gives the upper bound. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_O\frac{N_O^2}{|O|}
\ge
\frac{(\sum_ON_O)^2}{\sum_O|O|}
=
\frac{A_j^2}{\binom{|E|}{j}},
\]

because the coordinate-permutation orbits of weight `j` partition all `j`-subsets. `square`

### Noise and dual distance

Independent coordinate resampling at rate `epsilon` multiplies a weight-`j` Fourier character by

\[
\rho^j,\qquad \rho=1-\epsilon.
\]

Thus with `q=rho^2`,

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(T_\epsilon P\|Q)
=
\sum_{j\ge0}q^j b_j
=
\sum_{j\ge0}q^j
\sum_{\mathrm{wt}(O)=j}\frac{N_O^2}{|O|}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Moreover, the nonconstant low-degree energy through `D` vanishes exactly when

\[
C^\perp\setminus\{0\}
\quad\text{has no word of weight }\le D,
\tag{5.7}
\]

i.e. when the dual minimum distance exceeds `D`. This is the classical coding-theoretic orthogonal-array / Delsarte relation in the present likelihood-ratio language, not a novelty claim.

A simple sufficient condition follows immediately from `(5.5)`:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j>D}A_jq^j=O(1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\chi^2(T_\epsilon P\|Q)=O(1).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

If the left side tends to zero and the nonconstant spectrum begins above `D`, then the noised chi-square tends to zero. Again, weight-enumerator bounds are classical; only their role as one explicit obstruction/certificate for Kireeva's framework is being recorded here.
