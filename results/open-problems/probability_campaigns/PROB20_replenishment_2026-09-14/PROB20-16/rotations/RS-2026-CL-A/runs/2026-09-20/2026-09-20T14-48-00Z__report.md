## 3. Model and exact Fourier formula

Fix a finite state space `S`, `q=|S|`, and a full-support law `mu`; write

`mu_* = min_{s in S} mu(s) > 0`.

Let `W:S x S -> R` be symmetric and row-centered:

`sum_t mu(t) W(s,t)=0` for every `s`.

Let `T_W` be the self-adjoint operator on `L2(mu)`

`(T_W f)(s)=sum_t mu(t) W(s,t)f(t)`,

and put

`Lambda=||T_W||_op>0`.

For each `n`, sample latent labels `sigma_1,...,sigma_n iid mu`. Conditional on the labels, sample independent edge signs `X_ij in {-1,+1}` with

`E[X_ij | sigma] = theta_n W(sigma_i,sigma_j)`.

Assume `|theta_n| max|W|<=1` for all sufficiently large `n`. Let `P_n` be this planted law and let `Q_n` be the uniform law on all edge signs. The model is `S_n`-invariant.

Let `T_rho`, `0<rho<1`, be independent resampling noise with Walsh retention eigenvalue `rho`. Thus every degree-`e` Walsh coefficient is multiplied by `rho^e`.

For an edge set `F` write

`chi_F(X)=prod_{e in F} X_e`

and

`t(F,W)=E_{sigma} prod_{uv in F} W(sigma_u,sigma_v)`.

Conditional edge independence gives the exact moment identity

`E_{P_n} chi_F = theta_n^{|F|} t(F,W)`.

Therefore orthogonality of the Walsh basis under `Q_n` gives, for every `L`,

`Adv_{<=L}(T_rho P_n,Q_n)^2`

`= sum_{1<=|F|<=L} rho^{2|F|} theta_n^{2|F|} t(F,W)^2`.      (3.1)

This formula is the starting point for the new theorem.

## 4. Cycle pressure recovered from source-scale LDA

For a simple cycle `C_l`,

`t(C_l,W)=Tr(T_W^l)`.

For even `l`, all eigenvalue powers are nonnegative, hence

`Tr(T_W^l) >= Lambda^l`.

There are `(n)_l/(2l)` unoriented simple `l`-cycles, so bounded LDA through degree `D_n` implies, for every even `l<=D_n`,

`A_{n,D_n}^2 >= [(n)_l/(2l)] (theta_n^2 Lambda^2)^l`.      (4.1)

If `sup_n A_{n,D_n}<infinity` and there exists an even `l_n<=D_n` such that

`l_n -> infinity`,     `l_n^2/n -> 0`,

then taking `l_n`-th roots in (4.1) yields

`limsup_n n theta_n^2 Lambda^2 <= 1`.                    (4.2)

Kireeva’s source scale `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}` permits such a choice of `l_n` automatically.

After fixed noise define

`beta_n = n rho^2 theta_n^2 Lambda^2`.

By (4.2),

`limsup beta_n <= rho^2 < 1`.                             (4.3)

Thus there is a fixed `beta_*<1` with `beta_n<=beta_*` for all sufficiently large `n`.

## 5. New structural lemma: excess controls every non-cycle motif

The key point is that for a finite-state kernel, long degree-two chains cost only the operator norm. The actual combinatorial complexity is concentrated at branch vertices.

### Lemma 5.1 (subdivision/excess contraction)

Let `F` be a connected simple graph with `e` edges and `v` nonisolated vertices.

1. If `F` has a degree-one vertex then `t(F,W)=0`.
2. If every vertex has degree two, then `F` is a cycle and

   `|t(F,W)| <= q Lambda^e`.
3. Otherwise suppose `F` has minimum degree at least two and define its excess

   `r=e-v>=1`.

   Then

   `|t(F,W)| <= Lambda^e mu_*^{-3r}`.                    (5.1)

#### Proof

A degree-one vertex occurs in exactly one kernel factor; integrating its label uses row-centering of `W` and gives zero.

For a cycle, `t(C_e,W)=Tr(T_W^e)`, and the trace is bounded by `q ||T_W||_op^e=q Lambda^e`.

Now assume `F` has minimum degree two and is not a cycle. Suppress every maximal path whose internal vertices have degree two. The resulting connected multigraph `H` may have loops and parallel edges, but every vertex of `H` has degree at least three. If `v_0,m_0` are its vertex and edge counts, suppressing one degree-two vertex removes one vertex and one edge, so excess is preserved:

`m_0-v_0 = e-v = r`.

The degree bound gives

`2m_0 >= 3v_0`,

hence

`v_0<=2r`,     `m_0<=3r`.                                (5.2)

Let a kernel edge `a` of `H` correspond to a path of length `ell_a` in `F`; the path lengths sum to `e`.

Conjugate `T_W` to the ordinary Euclidean matrix

`A = diag(sqrt(mu)) W diag(sqrt(mu))`.

Then `||A||_op=Lambda`. If `W^{(ell)}` denotes the integral kernel of `T_W^ell`,

`W^{(ell)}(s,t) = [A^ell]_{s,t}/sqrt(mu(s)mu(t))`,

so

`|W^{(ell)}(s,t)| <= Lambda^ell/mu_*`.                    (5.3)

Integrating out every suppressed degree-two path rewrites `t(F,W)` as a sum over labels on the `v_0` branch vertices of a product of the `m_0` path kernels. Using (5.3) and the fact that the branch-label probability weights sum to one,

`|t(F,W)| <= Lambda^{sum_a ell_a} mu_*^{-m_0}`

`             <= Lambda^e mu_*^{-3r}`

by (5.2). QED.

This is the new technical bridge. It requires neither the global overlap-entropy condition nor a special Potts/flow identity.

## 6. Counting connected motifs by excess

Fix edge count `e` and excess `r>=1`. The suppression kernel from Lemma 5.1 has at most `2r` vertices and `3r` edges.

A deliberately crude but uniform encoding is enough:

- choose `v_0<=2r`;
- encode the ordered endpoint pair of each of at most `3r` kernel edges: at most `(2r)^{6r}` possibilities;
- choose positive subdivision lengths summing to `e`: at most `e^{3r}` possibilities.

Summing over `v_0` gives at most

`2r (2r)^{6r} e^{3r}`

kernel/subdivision descriptions. Since `r<=e`, this is bounded by

`(128 e^{10})^r`.                                        (6.1)

Every resulting unlabeled shape with `v=e-r` vertices has at most `n^v` injective embeddings into `[n]`. Combining (5.1), (6.1), and the definition of `beta_n`, the total noisy squared-moment weight of all connected non-cycle shapes with fixed `(e,r)` is at most

`beta_n^e [C_mu e^{10}/n]^r`,                            (6.2)

where one may take

`C_mu=128 mu_*^{-6}`.

## 7. Main theorem: fixed noise amplifies the protected polynomial degree

### Theorem 7.1 (finite-state polynomial-degree amplification; PROVED PARTIAL)

Assume the finite-state row-centered graphon model of Section 3. Suppose source-scale LDA is uniformly bounded through degrees `D_n` containing even `l_n->infinity` with `l_n^2/n->0`.

Fix any retention parameter `0<rho<1`. Let `L_n` be any sequence satisfying

`L_n^{10}/n -> 0`.                                        (7.1)

Then

`sup_n Adv_{<=L_n}(T_rho P_n,Q_n) < infinity`             (7.2)

(after discarding finitely many initial `n`).

More explicitly, choose any fixed `beta_*` with

`rho^2 < beta_* < 1`.

For all sufficiently large `n`,

`limsup Adv_{<=L_n}(T_rho P_n,Q_n)^2`

`<= exp(C_cycle(beta_*))-1`,                              (7.3)

where

`C_cycle(beta_*)`

`= (q^2/2) sum_{e>=3} beta_*^e/e`

`= (q^2/2)[-log(1-beta_*)-beta_*-beta_*^2/2]`.            (7.4)

The right-hand side is finite.

#### Proof

By Section 4, eventually `beta_n<=beta_*<1`.

**Cycle components.** There are `(n)_e/(2e)<=n^e/(2e)` labeled simple cycles of length `e`, and Lemma 5.1 gives `|t(C_e,W)|<=q Lambda^e`. Hence their total connected weight is at most

`(q^2/2) sum_{e=3}^{L_n} beta_n^e/e <= C_cycle(beta_*)`.  (7.5)

**Positive-excess connected components.** By (6.2), their total weight is at most

`sum_{e<=L_n} beta_*^e sum_{r>=1}[C_mu e^{10}/n]^r`.

Condition (7.1) implies `C_mu L_n^{10}/n=o(1)`, so the inner sum is at most `2C_mu e^{10}/n` for all large `n`. Therefore

`S_excess`

`<= (2C_mu/n) sum_{e>=1} beta_*^e e^{10}`

`= O_{mu,beta_*}(1/n)=o(1)`.                              (7.6)

**Disconnected graphs.** The squared moment weight factors over connected components because the latent labels on disjoint vertex sets are independent. Every nonzero graph is a vertex-disjoint collection of connected components with minimum degree at least two. For nonnegative polymer weights, dropping the vertex-disjointness constraint gives the standard exponential bound

`1 + Adv_{<=L_n}^2`

`<= exp(S_connected)`

`<= exp(C_cycle(beta_*)+o(1))`.                           (7.7)

Equations (7.3)–(7.4) follow. QED.

## 8. What “no polynomial distinguishes” means here

The exact conclusion is the same `L2(Q_n)` low-degree notion used by the source:

for every polynomial `f_n` of degree at most `L_n`,

`|E_{T_rho P_n}f_n-E_{Q_n}f_n|`

`<= C sqrt(Var_{Q_n} f_n)`                                (8.1)

with `C` independent of `n`.

In particular, if `0<=f_n<=1` is itself a degree-`L_n` polynomial test and its null acceptance probability tends to zero, then

`E_{T_rho P_n} f_n`

`<= E_Q f_n + C sqrt(E_Q f_n) -> 0`.

So bounded polynomial tests of this degree cannot strongly separate the models.

The theorem does **not** claim that thresholding an arbitrary degree-`L_n` real polynomial remains a degree-`L_n` polynomial, nor does it convert the conclusion into an unrestricted runtime lower bound.

## 9. Potts obstruction revisited: local polynomial hardness versus global distinguishability

The 13:52 report exhibited a `q`-state Potts kernel (`q>=11`) and parameters below the local cycle threshold for which:

- polylogarithmic LDA is bounded;
- after sufficiently weak fixed noise, an exhaustive maximization over `q^n` latent assignments strongly distinguishes;
- the global replica overlap has a macroscopically separated diagonal state, so full chi-square contiguity can fail.

Theorem 7.1 now shows, for the **same kind of finite-state model**, that after every fixed positive noise level all polynomial statistics of degree `n^delta`, `delta<1/10`, still have uniformly bounded normalized advantage whenever the source-scale LDA is bounded.

So the Potts phenomenon is not a failure of polynomial-degree transfer. It is a separation between:

1. **local diagram pressure**, probed by sublinear-degree polynomials and controlled by cycles plus excess suppression; and
2. **global overlap pressure**, exploited by an exhaustive search over macroscopically coherent latent assignments.

This is the degree-sensitive implication missing from the prior checkpoint.

## 10. Mao boundary under the new theorem

Mao is not contradicted. His construction is not a fixed finite-state iid-vertex graphon with conditionally independent edges of the above form; it uses Reed–Muller evaluation vectors and a random alternating bilinear form, producing genuinely higher-order global algebraic dependence.

`t(C_4,W)=Tr(T_W^4)>0`.

So a Mao-type law with every marginal through degree at least four exactly uniform cannot lie in this class unless the planted law is actually the uniform null.

The theorem therefore supplies a transparent extra structural condition that excludes Mao while proving a source-style higher-degree consequence. It remains only a **restricted sufficient-condition theorem**, not a repair of the unrestricted low-degree conjecture.

## 12. Reusable tensor/correlation lemma for P08/P09

The proof exposes a basis-independent diagram principle worth sharing:

> If degree-two chains in a diagram contract through an operator of norm `Lambda`, then suppressing all degree-two vertices leaves only `O(r)` branch objects, where `r=e-v` is diagram excess. For a finite-state kernel, the exact bound is `|t(F,W)|<=Lambda^e mu_*^{-3r}`. Consequently the cost of a long diagram is governed by its branch excess, not its total chain length.

For Gaussian/Wick or tensor-chaos expansions, an analogous statement holds whenever each maximal degree-two chain corresponds to an operator contraction of norm at most `Lambda` and the remaining branch tensors have a uniform contraction norm. Then a connected diagram with excess `r` has at most `2r` branch vertices and `3r` branch edges, so all non-chain losses can be charged to `O(r)` objects.

This may help P08/P09 separate long harmless propagation chains from the genuinely dimension-losing branch structure. A separate handoff records the precise finite-state lemma and the conditional Hilbert/tensor version; it does not transfer RS-2026-CL-A source ownership.

## 13. Exact remaining gap and next attack

The present exponent `1/10` is a **proof exponent**, coming from a deliberately crude kernel/subdivision count `(128 e^{10})^r`. It is not claimed sharp. The natural next mathematical attacks are:

1. sharpen the excess enumeration and contraction norm to enlarge `L_n` well beyond `n^{1/10}`;
