## 3. Preserved arbitrary graded pressure setup

Let `Q` be a reference law with an orthogonal degree decomposition and let `L=dP/dQ`. Write

\[
L-1=\sum_{j\ge1}L_j,
\qquad
b_j:=\|L_j\|_{L^2(Q)}^2,
\qquad
F(q):=1+\sum_{j\ge1}b_jq^j.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For scalar degree noise with eigenvalue `q^{j/2}` on degree `j`, the noised likelihood ratio is `g_q=T_{\sqrt q}L` and

\[
\boxed{
F(q)=\|g_q\|_2^2
=1+\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P\|Q).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Also

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2
=\sum_{j=1}^Db_j.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The noised spectral barycenter is

\[
\mu(q)
:=\frac{\sum_j j b_jq^j}{F(q)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The earlier pressure lemma says that if

\[
\mu(q)\le\theta(D+1),\qquad 0\le\theta<1,
\tag{3.5}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
F(q)\le
\frac{1+q\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2}{1-\theta}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

For completeness, the only step used later is the decomposition

\[
\sum_{j>D}b_jq^j
\le\frac{\mu(q)}{D+1}F(q)\le\theta F(q),
\]

while the low part is at most `1+q Adv^{<=D}^2`.

A uniform bound `F(q)<=C` yields a uniform testing gap. Indeed, under `Q`, `g_q>=0`, `E_Q g_q=1`, and `E_Q g_q^2=F(q)`. Paley–Zygmund gives

\[
Q(g_q\ge1/2)\ge\frac{1}{4F(q)}.
\]

Therefore the overlap satisfies

\[
1-\|T_{\sqrt q}P-Q\|_{TV}
=E_Q\min(g_q,1)
\ge\frac1{8F(q)},
\tag{3.7}
\]

so bounded `F(q)` rules out strong distinction by every measurable test.

## 4. B1 — change category: from spectral degree to a physical Dirichlet form

The missing piece after the preceding positive-overlap work was a signed/cancellative **physical-space** condition. On product nulls, the noised spectral barycenter is exactly a Dirichlet-form Rayleigh quotient of the positive noised likelihood ratio, even when the original likelihood ratio has arbitrary signed Fourier/Hoeffding components.

Let

\[
Q=\bigotimes_{i=1}^m Q_i
\]

be a finite product law (the same formulas hold on standard product spaces under the usual `L^2` assumptions). Let `E_i` denote conditional expectation in coordinate `i` with all other coordinates frozen. The ANOVA/Hoeffding degree operator is

\[
N:=\sum_{i=1}^m(I-E_i).
\tag{4.1}
\]

On a Hoeffding component indexed by `S subseteq [m]`, `N` acts by multiplication by `|S|`. If `rho=sqrt(q)` and `g_q=T_rho L`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)=
\frac{\langle g_q,Ng_q\rangle_Q}{\|g_q\|_2^2}
=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^m E_Q\operatorname{Var}_{Q_i}(g_q\mid X_{-i})}{E_Qg_q^2}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the signed/cancellative bridge sought in the preceding run: it does not require a positive-support mixture, cylinder probabilities, or a factorization of the spectral polynomial.

### Definition 4.1 — local relative Dirichlet loads

For each coordinate define

\[
\kappa_i(q):=
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{-i}}
\frac{
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_i}(g_q(x_{-i},X_i))
}{
E_{Q_i}[g_q(x_{-i},X_i)^2]
},
\tag{4.3}
\]

with the ratio defined as zero on a fiber whose denominator is zero. Since conditional variance is at most the conditional second moment, `0<=kappa_i<=1`.

Integrating (4.3) and using (4.2) gives the immediate but useful physical-space estimate

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)\le K(q):=\sum_{i=1}^m\kappa_i(q).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Theorem 4.2 — local-Dirichlet low-degree implication

Fix a degree cutoff `D>=1` and a noise level `q in (0,1]`. If

\[
K(q)\le\theta(D+1)
\qquad\text{for some }\theta<1,
\tag{4.5}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
1+\chi^2(T_{\sqrt q}P\|Q)
\le
\frac{1+q\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)^2}{1-\theta}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently, if along a sequence `(P_n,Q_n)` with product nulls and source cutoff `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}` we have

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)<\infty,
\qquad
\sup_n\frac{K_n(q)}{D_n+1}\le\theta<1
\tag{4.7}
\]

for a fixed `q>0`, then the noised laws have a uniform TV gap from one:

\[
\boxed{
\|T_{\sqrt q}P_n-Q_n\|_{TV}
\le
1-
\frac{1-\theta}{8(1+qA^2)}
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

whenever the LDA is bounded by `A`. In particular **no measurable test**, and hence no polynomial of any degree, strongly distinguishes.

#### Proof

Equation (4.4) turns (4.5) into the pressure hypothesis (3.5); then (3.6) gives (4.6), and (3.7) gives (4.8). `□`

The criterion is not asserted to be necessary. Its value is that `K(q)` is built from one-coordinate conditional fluctuations of the **noised likelihood ratio in physical space**, rather than from the entire Fourier spectrum.

## 5. A still more concrete sufficient condition: conditional log-oscillation

The local loads in (4.3) can themselves be bounded by multiplicative oscillation. Suppose a positive random variable `Y` satisfies

\[
0<m\le Y\le M<\infty.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}{E Y^2}
\le
\left(\frac{M-m}{M+m}\right)^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

A direct proof avoids importing any heavy machinery. From `(Y-m)(M-Y)>=0`,

\[
EY^2\le(M+m)EY-Mm.
\]

Writing `s=EY`, the right side divided by `s^2` is at most `(M+m)^2/(4Mm)` (its maximum over `s in [m,M]` occurs at the harmonic mean `2Mm/(M+m)`). Thus

\[
\frac{s^2}{EY^2}\ge\frac{4Mm}{(M+m)^2},
\]

which is equivalent to (5.1). This is a Kantorovich-type bound and is classical in substance.

For each coordinate define the conditional logarithmic oscillation of the noised likelihood ratio

\[
\eta_i(q):=
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{-i}}
\left[
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_i}\log g_q(x)
-
\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x_i}\log g_q(x)
\right],
\tag{5.2}
\]

with `eta_i=+infty` if a relevant fiber has a zero and a positive value. On each positive fiber, `M/m<=e^{eta_i}`. Therefore (5.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_i(q)\le\tanh^2\!\left(\frac{\eta_i(q)}2\right),
\qquad
\mu(q)\le
\sum_i\tanh^2\!\left(\frac{\eta_i(q)}2\right).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Corollary 5.1 — local multiplicative-smoothness criterion

If

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}
\tanh^2\!\left(\frac{\eta_{n,i}(q)}2\right)
\le\theta(D_n+1),
\qquad\theta<1,
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and the source LDA through `D_n` is uniformly bounded, then (4.8) holds.

For small oscillations this asks essentially for an aggregate squared local log-sensitivity

\[
\sum_i\eta_{n,i}(q)^2=O(D_n)
\]

with a strict enough constant. The condition is deliberately phrased after noise: noise can regularize an originally singular likelihood ratio, and the source itself asks about noise-tolerant implications.

## 6. B20 — where Mao necessarily breaks the signed physical-space condition

against the uniform product graph null, yet after every fixed positive edge-resampling rate there is strong polynomial-time distinction. Let the corresponding fixed noise parameter be `q in (0,1)`. Strong distinction implies

\[
F_n(q)\to\infty.
\tag{6.2}

Using (6.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_n(q)
&=\frac{\sum_{j>D_n}j b_{n,j}q^j}{F_n(q)}\\
&\ge(D_n+1)\frac{F_n(q)-1}{F_n(q)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\mu_n(q)}{D_n+1}\ge1.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Combining with (4.4) gives the new physical-space diagnosis

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_n
\frac{\sum_i\kappa_{n,i}(q)}{D_n+1}
\ge1.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

If all conditional log oscillations are finite, (5.3) further forces

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_n
\frac{\sum_i\tanh^2(\eta_{n,i}(q)/2)}{D_n+1}
\ge1
}
\tag{6.5}

for any attempted coordinatewise oscillation certificate strong enough to dominate the actual conditional variances. In words: Mao does not merely hide signal in a rank computation. ## 7. B25 — count the same orbit overlap twice: planted Hamiltonian cycles

To stress the deterministic-orbit machinery on a genuinely multi-stratum orbit, take a fixed undirected Hamiltonian cycle `A_0=C_n` in `K_n` and let `A` be a uniformly random Hamiltonian cycle. Plant all edges of `A` over a fixed-density product Bernoulli graph null. The acting group is `S_n`, but `j`-edge fragments of the template split into many stabilizer types according to the number of path runs; this is not a one-stratum hypergeometric example.

Put

\[
Z:=|A_0\cap A|,
\qquad
m_j:=E\binom Zj.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For `1<=j<=n-3`, every `j`-edge subset `S subset A_0` is a disjoint union of `c=c(S)` nonempty paths. Contracting those paths leaves `n-j` supervertices. There are `2^c` choices of path orientations and `(n-j-1)!/2` undirected Hamiltonian cycles on the contracted labels, out of `(n-1)!/2` total. Therefore the cylinder probability is exactly

\[
\boxed{
a(S)=Pr(S\subseteq A)
=\frac{2^{c(S)}}{(n-1)_j}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is the stabilizer/incidence-side count.

Now count the same factorial moment by run type. The number of `j`-edge subsets of the cyclic template having exactly `c` runs is

\[
\boxed{
N_{n,j,c}
=\frac nc
\binom{j-1}{c-1}
\binom{n-j-1}{c-1}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Indeed, after marking a starting run, write the `j` chosen edges as a composition into `c` positive run lengths and the `n-j` omitted edges as a composition into `c` positive gaps. There are `n` choices of the marked starting edge, and each unmarked subset is counted once for each of its `c` runs.

Summing (7.2) over all `j`-subsets gives the exact two-copy factorial moment

\[
\boxed{
E\binom Zj
=
\frac{1}{(n-1)_j}
\sum_{c=1}^{\min(j,n-j)}
\frac nc
\binom{j-1}{c-1}
\binom{n-j-1}{c-1}2^c,
\quad 1\le j\le n-3.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Equation (7.4) is simultaneously the stabilizer/run-incidence count and the two-copy overlap factorial moment. Thus the exact spectral birth ratio is the finite deterministic quotient

\[
\boxed{
r_j
=\frac{(j+1)b_{j+1}}{b_j}
=\lambda(j+1)\frac{m_{j+1}}{m_j},
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

with each `m_j` given by the finite run sum (7.4). No Fourier likelihood enumeration is required.

### 7.1 A direct cumulative tail barrier from geometry

The finite formula immediately gives a useful majorant. Since `c(S)<=j`,

\[
m_j
\le
\frac{2^j\binom nj}{(n-1)_j}
=
\frac{n}{n-j}\frac{2^j}{j!}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Hence for `j<=n/2`,

\[
\boxed{
m_j\le\frac{2^{j+1}}{j!},
\qquad
b_jq^j\le 2\frac{(2\lambda q)^j}{j!}.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Therefore for any `D<n/2`,

\[
\sum_{j=D+1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}b_jq^j
\le
2e^{2\lambda q}
\frac{(2\lambda q)^{D+1}}{(D+1)!}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

For the terminal half use instead `c(S)<=n-j`, since every path run requires a missing-edge gap. Thus

\[
m_j\le
\frac{2^{n-j}\binom nj}{(n-1)_j}
=
\frac{n}{n-j}\frac{2^{n-j}}{j!},
\qquad j>n/2.
\tag{7.9}
\]

For fixed `a=lambda q`, with `M=max(1,a)`, summing the crude bound gives

\[
\sum_{j>n/2}b_jq^j
\le
\frac{n^2\,2^{n/2}M^n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor!}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Thus every source-superlog cutoff `D_n=o(n)` has a factorially small middle tail and a superexponentially small terminal tail in this dense fixed-`p` model. This is a genuinely multi-stratum geometric certificate: fragments are classified by run count, yet the entire noised tail is controlled by a two-count argument.

The limiting Poisson(2) overlap and related Hamiltonian-circuit intersection asymptotics are classical. I do not claim (7.4)–(7.10) as publication novelty; they serve as an exact stress test showing that orbit/stabilizer geometry can certify the pressure barrier without a one-stratum symmetry assumption.
