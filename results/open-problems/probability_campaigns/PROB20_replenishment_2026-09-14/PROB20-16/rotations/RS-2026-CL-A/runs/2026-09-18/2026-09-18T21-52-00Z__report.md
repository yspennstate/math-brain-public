# 3. B25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE: finite-alpha local star tails

The previous report identified the first nonzero zero-field pairwise-Ising cross-degree coefficient at order `alpha^3`, supported on three-neighbor stars. The missing concern was whether that was merely infinitesimal. At the level of each **local conditional expectation**, it is not: there is an exact finite-`alpha`, system-size-uniform cubic tail bound.

## 3.1 Setup

Let

\[
Q_\alpha(x)\propto
\exp\!\left(\alpha\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}J_{ij}x_ix_j\right),
\qquad x\in\{\pm1\}^V,
\tag{3.1}
\]

with zero external field. For a site `i`, write

\[
h_i(x_{N(i)})=\sum_{j\in N(i)}J_{ij}x_j,
\qquad
m_i^\alpha(x_{N(i)})=E_{Q_\alpha}[x_i\mid x_{-i}]
=\tanh(\alpha h_i).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `d_i=|N(i)|`, `H_i=sum_{j in N(i)}|J_ij|`, and expand `m_i^alpha` in the ordinary Walsh basis on the **local** neighbor cube with uniform normalization:

\[
m_i^\alpha(x_{N(i)})
=\sum_{T\subseteq N(i)}\widehat m_i^\alpha(T)\chi_T(x).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Global spin-flip symmetry makes `m_i^alpha` odd, so only odd `|T|` occur. The degree-one part cannot raise ordinary algebraic degree in the site heat-bath map. Define the local nonlinear tail

\[
r_i^\alpha
=\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq N(i)\\|T|\ge3}}
\widehat m_i^\alpha(T)\chi_T.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Because `alpha h_i` has degree one,

\[
r_i^\alpha=P_{\ge3}\bigl(\tanh(\alpha h_i)-\alpha h_i\bigr).
\tag{3.5}
\]

## 3.2 First count: a uniform real-variable remainder bound

For real `u`,

\[
|u-\tanh u|
=\left|\int_0^u \tanh^2 t\,dt\right|
\le \frac{|u|^3}{3},
\tag{3.6}
\]

since `|tanh t|<=|t|`. Therefore

\[
\|\tanh(\alpha h_i)-\alpha h_i\|_\infty
\le \frac{(|\alpha|H_i)^3}{3}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

For a function `g` on a `d`-bit cube, normalized Parseval plus Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_T|\widehat g(T)|
\le 2^{d/2}\|g\|_2
\le 2^{d/2}\|g\|_\infty.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Combining (3.5)–(3.8),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|T|\ge3}|\widehat m_i^\alpha(T)|
\le
\frac{2^{d_i/2}}{3}(|\alpha|H_i)^3.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

For the exponentially weighted degree-gap tail relevant to the source-number commutator, define

\[
\tau_{i,s}(\alpha)
=\sum_{|T|\ge3}e^{s(|T|-1)}|\widehat m_i^\alpha(T)|.
\tag{3.10}
\]

If `d_i<=Delta`, then

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{i,s}(\alpha)
\le
\frac{2^{\Delta/2}e^{s(\Delta-1)}}{3}
(|\alpha|H_i)^3.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Thus for bounded degree and bounded row coupling `H_i<=J_row`, the **local nonlinear conditional-mean tail is uniformly `O(|alpha|^3)` for every finite system**, with an explicit constant and no asymptotic remainder.

This already extends the preceding infinitesimal three-star coefficient. It does **not** by itself imply the global source-layer commutator bound `mathfrak B_s=O(alpha^3)`, because the source layers are orthogonalized in `L^2(Q_alpha)` rather than in the local uniform cube. That lifting issue is isolated below instead of hidden.

## 3.3 Second count: exact iterated discrete differences

There is an independent coefficientwise derivation that exposes every higher connected star size.

For a one-variable function `g`, the central difference in coordinate `j` satisfies

\[
\frac{g(u+a)-g(u-a)}2
=\frac a2\int_{-1}^1 g'(u+at)\,dt,
\tag{3.12}
\]

so its absolute value is at most `|a| sup|g'|`. Iterating this identity over `T` and then averaging the untouched Rademacher variables gives

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat m_i^\alpha(T)|
\le
|\alpha|^{|T|}
\left(\prod_{j\in T}|J_{ij}|\right)
M_{|T|},
\qquad
M_q:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb R}|\tanh^{(q)}u|<\infty.
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{i,s}(\alpha)
\le
\sum_{\substack{3\le q\le d_i\\q\text{ odd}}}
 e^{s(q-1)}M_q|\alpha|^q
e_q\bigl((|J_{ij}|)_{j\in N(i)}\bigr),
}
\tag{3.14}
\]

where `e_q` is the elementary symmetric polynomial. Since

\[
e_q((|J_{ij}|))\le \frac{H_i^q}{q!},
\tag{3.15}
\]

for every fixed `Delta`, `s`, `J_row`, and `alpha_0`, (3.14) gives the explicit uniform finite-alpha form

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{i,s}(\alpha)
\le C_{\mathrm{loc}}(s,\Delta,J_{\mathrm{row}},\alpha_0)|\alpha|^3
\qquad (|\alpha|\le\alpha_0),
}
\tag{3.16}
\]

with, for example,

\[
C_{\mathrm{loc}}
=\sum_{\substack{3\le q\le\Delta\\q\text{ odd}}}
 e^{s(q-1)}M_q\alpha_0^{q-3}\frac{J_{\mathrm{row}}^q}{q!}.
\tag{3.17}
\]

The leading `q=3` term matches the previous derivative calculation because `tanh'''(0)=-2`:

\[
\widehat m_i^\alpha(\{j,\ell,m\})
=-2\alpha^3J_{ij}J_{i\ell}J_{im}+O(\alpha^5).
\tag{3.18}
\]

Inserted into `(I-E_i)chi_S`, the minus sign converts this to the factor `+2` in the coefficient of `alpha^3`, equivalently the factor `12` in the third derivative recorded in the previous report. Thus the Taylor/star calculation and the exact finite-difference calculation count the same leakage mechanism consistently.

# 4. B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY ON PURPOSE: what exactly causes cubic suppression?

The zero-field pairwise result is special. Breaking either of two structural ingredients lowers the perturbative order of cross-degree leakage.

## 4.1 General conditional-logit form

Let

\[
Q_\alpha(x)\propto
\exp\!\left(\sum_i b_ix_i+\alpha H(x)\right),
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `H` is a multilinear interaction polynomial. For site `i`, define the conditional interaction field

\[
h_i(x_{-i})
=\frac{H(x_i=1,x_{-i})-H(x_i=-1,x_{-i})}{2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
m_i^\alpha(x_{-i})
=E[x_i\mid x_{-i}]
=\tanh(b_i+\alpha h_i).
\tag{4.3}
\]

For `f=a_i+x_i c_i` with `deg c_i<=k-1`, the only possible degree increase in `E_i^alpha f` comes from the algebraic degree of `m_i^alpha`.

This gives a clean classification.

## 4.2 Higher-arity interactions: generic linear leakage

Suppose `b_i=0`, but `H` contains an interaction `e` of arity `r>=3` containing `i`. Then `h_i` contains

\[
J_e\chi_{e\setminus\{i\}}
\tag{4.4}
\]

of degree `r-1`. Since

\[
\tanh(\alpha h_i)=\alpha h_i+O(\alpha^3),
\tag{4.5}
\]

cross-degree leakage already appears at **first order**. At the product point, for a Walsh monomial `chi_S` with `i in S` and `(e\setminus{i})` disjoint from `S\setminus{i}`, the top-degree term is

\[
\boxed{
P^{(0)}_{k+r-2}A_0'\chi_S
=-\sum_{\substack{i\in S,\ e\ni i\\|e|=r}}
J_e\chi_{(S\setminus\{i\})\cup(e\setminus\{i\})},
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

with the displayed disjointness condition selecting the top-degree part. Thus **pairwise interaction is essential** to the absence of linear leakage: when `r=2`, the first-order conditional field is degree one and preserves the source filtration; when `r>=3`, it generically does not.

## 4.3 Fixed external field plus pairwise interactions: generic quadratic leakage

Now keep interactions pairwise so that

\[
h_i=\sum_jJ_{ij}x_j
\tag{4.7}
\]

has degree one, but take a nonzero base field `b_i`. Taylor expansion gives

\[
\tanh(b_i+\alpha h_i)
=	anh b_i
+\alpha\operatorname{sech}^2b_i\,h_i
-\alpha^2\tanh b_i\operatorname{sech}^2b_i\,h_i^2
+O(\alpha^3).
\tag{4.8}
\]

The constant and linear terms do not increase degree. The quadratic term generically does. For `i in S`, its distinct-neighbor-pair contribution to the second generator derivative is

\[
\boxed{
P^{(0)}_{k+1}A_0''\chi_S
=
4\sum_{i\in S}
\tanh b_i\,\operatorname{sech}^2b_i
\sum_{\{j,\ell\}\subseteq N(i)\setminus S}
J_{ij}J_{i\ell}
\chi_{(S\setminus\{i\})\cup\{j,\ell\}}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Hence a fixed nonzero external field lowers the generic first cross-degree order from cubic to **quadratic** whenever a site sees two relevant neighbors.

If an external field is itself scaled by `alpha` rather than present at the base point, the expansion is again `tanh(alpha h_i^{tot})`, hence odd in `alpha`; there is no quadratic term. Its cubic term can nevertheless contain degree-two pieces through `3b_i(\sum Jx)^2`, so cubic leakage may then include degree jump `+1`. This distinction matters when stating the structural hypothesis.

## 4.4 Minimal structural explanation

The cubic suppression in the preceding zero-field pairwise model comes from two independent facts:

1. **Pairwise conditional-field linearity:** the first perturbative conditional logit `h_i` has algebraic degree at most one. This prevents `O(alpha)` degree raising.
2. **Centering at zero conditional field:** `tanh` is odd at the expansion point, so the quadratic Taylor coefficient vanishes. This prevents `O(alpha^2)` degree raising.

Breaking (1) with a genuine `r>=3` interaction gives generic **linear** leakage. Keeping (1) but breaking (2) with a fixed external field gives generic **quadratic** leakage. Keeping both gives the **cubic** three-star mechanism from B7/B25; maximum degree at most two gives exact zero leakage for all interaction strengths.
