# 3. B5 — add a parameter and differentiate: exact chord-fugacity localization on cactus Ising graphs

The predecessor showed that a graph with cyclomatic number `b` admits the crude parity bound

\[
\mu_G(A)=\mathbb E_Q\chi_A\le 2^b\rho^{\tau_G(A)}.
\]

That is useless for `b=Theta(n)`. The key question is whether all cycles really contribute to the defect correlation. On a cactus they do not: cycles untouched by the defect current cancel **exactly**.

## 3.1 Setup

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite connected zero-field ferromagnetic Ising graph in high-temperature variables

\[
t_f=\tanh J_f\in(0,1),\qquad
w(F)=\prod_{f\in F}t_f.
\]

Choose a spanning tree `T`. Let `H=E\setminus T` be the chord set. For each chord `e in H`, let `C_e` be its fundamental cycle. Assume throughout this section that the fundamental cycles are pairwise **edge-disjoint**. This is the natural cactus/edge-disjoint-cycle-basis class; the number of chords may be `Theta(|V|)`.

For an even vertex set `A`, let `J=J_T(A)` be the unique tree `T`-join with `partial J=A`.

Every edge set with boundary `A` is uniquely

\[
J\triangle\Big(\triangle_{e\in S}C_e\Big),
\qquad S\subseteq H,
\tag{3.1}
\]

because the fundamental cycles form a cycle-space basis. Edge-disjointness makes the weight ratio factorize over selected cycles.

Define

\[
c_e:=w(C_e),
\qquad
r_e(A):=\frac{w(J\triangle C_e)}{w(J)}
=
\frac{\prod_{f\in C_e\setminus J}t_f}
     {\prod_{f\in C_e\cap J}t_f}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 3.2 Exact cactus factorization theorem

The high-temperature numerator with boundary `A` is

\[
Z_A
:=
\sum_{F:\partial F=A}w(F)
=
 w(J)\prod_{e\in H}(1+r_e(A)),
\tag{3.3}
\]

while the even-subgraph partition is

\[
Z_0
:=
\sum_{Z:\partial Z=\varnothing}w(Z)
=
\prod_{e\in H}(1+c_e).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu_G(A)
=
\mathbb E_Q\chi_A
=
 w(J)
 \prod_{e\in H}
 \frac{1+r_e(A)}{1+c_e}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is exact for every finite cactus graph, with no small-coupling approximation.

### Proof

The cycle-space parameterization `(3.1)` is bijective. If `S` is selected, the cycles `C_e` are edge-disjoint, so toggling one cycle changes a disjoint edge block and

\[
\frac{w(J\triangle(\triangle_{e\in S}C_e))}{w(J)}
=
\prod_{e\in S}r_e(A).
\]

Summing over `S subseteq H` gives `(3.3)`. The same argument with `J=emptyset` gives `(3.4)`. Their ratio is `(3.5)`. `square`

## 3.3 Exact cancellation of cycles not touched by the defect current

If

\[
J\cap C_e=\varnothing,
\]

then

\[
r_e(A)=w(C_e)=c_e,
\]

so the corresponding factor in `(3.5)` equals **exactly one**:

\[
\frac{1+r_e(A)}{1+c_e}=1.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus an extensive number of remote cycles contributes zero correction. The relevant entropy is the number of cycles **touched by the defect current**, not the total cyclomatic number.

This is already a strict improvement over the predecessor's `2^b`: one may have `b=Theta(n)` and nevertheless a parity correlation controlled by only `O(tau_G(A))` local cycle choices.

## 3.4 Chord fugacity and exact defect susceptibility

Now multiply every chord weight by a common fugacity `lambda in [0,1]`, leaving the spanning-tree edge weights fixed. Because each fundamental cycle contains exactly one chord and `J subseteq T`,

\[
c_e(\lambda)=\lambda c_e,
\qquad
r_e(A;\lambda)=\lambda r_e(A).
\]

Equation `(3.5)` becomes

\[
\log\mu_\lambda(A)
=
\log w(J)
+
\sum_e
\left[
\log(1+\lambda r_e(A))
-
\log(1+\lambda c_e)
\right].
\tag{3.7}
\]

Differentiating gives the exact susceptibility identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\log\mu_\lambda(A)
=
\sum_{e:\,J\cap C_e\ne\varnothing}
\left[
\frac{r_e(A)}{1+\lambda r_e(A)}
-
\frac{c_e}{1+\lambda c_e}
\right].
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Every untouched cycle cancels not only at `lambda=1` but **pointwise along the entire interpolation**. This is the exact B5 object that a general loopy proof should try to dominate by Dobrushin/KP locality: a defect-localized logarithmic derivative, rather than the total free-energy derivative of all chords.

## 3.5 Homogeneous extensive-cycle theorem

Assume now `t_f=t in (0,1)`. For a touched cycle `C_e` of length `ell_e`, put

\[
a_e=|J\cap C_e|,
\qquad
m_e=\min\{a_e,\ell_e-a_e\}.
\]

The contribution of the `J`-edges on that cycle, combined with its exact loop factor, is

\[
t^{a_e}\frac{1+t^{\ell_e-2a_e}}{1+t^{\ell_e}}
=
\frac{t^{a_e}+t^{\ell_e-a_e}}{1+t^{\ell_e}}
\le
2t^{m_e}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Let `J_out` be the edges of `J` outside all fundamental cycles. Since the cycles are edge-disjoint, minimization over the affine cycle space separates cycle by cycle, so

\[
\tau_G(A)
=
|J_{out}|+
\sum_e m_e.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Let `s(A)` be the number of touched cycles. Every touched cycle has `m_e>=1`, hence

\[
s(A)\le\sum_e m_e\le\tau_G(A).
\]

Combining `(3.5)`, `(3.9)`, and `(3.10)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mu_G(A)
\le
2^{s(A)}t^{\tau_G(A)}
\le
(2t)^{\tau_G(A)}.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Consequently, whenever

\[
\boxed{t<\frac12,}
\tag{3.12}
\]

parity/current correlations decay exponentially in the **true minimum current length** uniformly in the total number of cycles, even when the cyclomatic number is extensive.

The constant `1/2` is only the simplest entropy-versus-energy consequence of the factor `2` per touched cycle; it is not claimed sharp. The exact product `(3.5)` is the stronger statement and can be much better when alternate routes are long or heterogeneous.

## 4.1 Exact product-group/subgroup certificate

Let

\[
Q_N=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb F_2^N),
\]

and let `C_N <= F_2^N` be a binary linear code of dimension `M_N`. Define

\[
P_N=\operatorname{Unif}(C_N^\perp)
\]

(or any coset; coset phases do not alter squared Fourier masses).

The exact Fourier identity is

\[
\boxed{
L_N(x):=\frac{dP_N}{dQ_N}(x)
=
\sum_{c\in C_N}\chi_c(x).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore, writing `d_N=d_min(C_N)`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P_N,Q_N)^2
=
\#\{c\in C_N\setminus\{0\}:|c|\le D\}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
d_N>D
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P_N,Q_N)=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Under independent coordinate refresh whose character multiplier is `e^{-t|c|}`, Parseval gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_NS_t\|Q_N)
=
W_{C_N}(e^{-2t})-1
=
\sum_{c\ne0}e^{-2t|c|}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The crude dimension-distance certificate is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_NS_t\|Q_N)
\le
(2^{M_N}-1)e^{-2td_N},
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|P_NS_t-Q_N\|_{TV}
\le
\frac12\sqrt{(2^{M_N}-1)e^{-2td_N}}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

A certificate verifier can consequently accept:

- an exact rank/dimension `M_N`;
- target source cutoff `D_N`;
- a **certified** distance lower bound `d_N^{lb}` (for example from a proved Tanner-expansion certificate or a code-family theorem);
- noise level `t`;

and return source-window zero-LDA if `d_N^{lb}>D_N`, together with the rigorous noisy bounds `(4.5)`–`(4.6)` and the threshold

\[
t_{cert}
=
\frac{M_N\log2}{2d_N^{lb}}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is a verifier, not a universal minimum-distance solver. Jia et al.'s 2026 hardness theorem makes that distinction essential even for regular LDPC Tanner graphs.

Take any explicit asymptotically good binary linear code family `E_N` with

\qquad
d(E_N)\ge\delta N
\tag{4.8}
\]

for fixed `R,delta>0`; classical expander/Tanner constructions supply such families.

Let

\[
D_N=(\log N)^{1+\eta},
\qquad
M_N=\lceil D_N\rceil,

and choose **any** `M_N`-dimensional subcode

\[
C_N\le E_N.
\]

Because taking a subcode cannot decrease minimum distance,

\[
d(C_N)\ge\delta N.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Set `P_N=Unif(C_N^perp)` and `Q_N=Unif(F_2^N)`. For all sufficiently large `N`,

\[
d(C_N)>D_N,

so by `(4.3)`

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_N}(P_N,Q_N)=0.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

For every fixed `t>0`, `(4.5)` gives

\[
\log\bigl(1+\chi^2(P_NS_t\|Q_N)\bigr)
\le
M_N\log2-2t\delta N+o(1)
\longrightarrow-\infty
\]

at the exponent level, hence

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_NS_t\|Q_N)\to0,
\qquad
\|P_NS_t-Q_N\|_{TV}\to0
\quad\text{for every fixed }t>0.

This is a complete source-valid **noisy implication** on a concrete, generally non-permutation-invariant structural class. It is not Hsieh's permutation-symmetric theorem in disguise. # 5. B22 — read the failures: near dependencies, not just dependencies, control Tanner interior pressure

The predecessor's MacWilliams identity gave a promising local dual partition. Error-floor/trapping-set work shows what can go wrong: exact low-weight codewords are not the only dangerous sparse objects; many small **near** dependencies can dominate a weighted partition.

## 5.1 Exact dual partition and a near-dependency lower obstruction

Let `C<=F_2^N` have dimension `M`, dual dimension `m=N-M`, and parity-check matrix `H` with `m_0` rows and row-dependency dimension

\[
r_H=m_0-m.
\]

For `0<z<1`, put

\[
q=\frac{1-z}{1+z}\in(0,1).
\]

The exact predecessor identity is

\[
\boxed{
W_C(z)
=
2^{-m-r_H}(1+z)^N
\sum_{y\in\mathbb F_2^{m_0}}
q^{b_H(y)},
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where

\[
b_H(y):=|H^Ty|
\]

is the odd-boundary size of the selected check set.

Define the near-dependency counting function

\[
\mathcal N_H(B)
:=
\#\{y\in\mathbb F_2^{m_0}: b_H(y)\le B\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because `q<1`, every such term contributes at least `q^B`; hence

\[
\boxed{
\log W_C(z)
\ge
N\log(1+z)
-(m+r_H)\log2
+\log\mathcal N_H(B)
+B\log q.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is a rigorous **lower obstruction**. If the right side is of order `cD_N` at an admissible interior radius, then the desired source-scale interior-pressure condition cannot hold. Exact row dependencies (`B=0`) are only the first case; exponentially or subexponentially many small-boundary selected-check sets can be just as dangerous.

## 5.2 The crucial baseline correction

A tempting but false heuristic is: “large odd boundary for every nonzero selected check set makes `W_C(z)` small.” The exact formula shows why that is insufficient. Let `W` be uniform on the row space `C^perp`. Then

\[
W_C(z)
=
(1+z)^N\,
\mathbb E_{W\sim\operatorname{Unif}(C^\perp)}q^{|W|}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

If `U` is uniform on all of `F_2^N`,

\[
\mathbb E q^{|U|}
=
\left(\frac{1+q}{2}\right)^N
=(1+z)^{-N}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
W_C(z)-1
=
(1+z)^N
\left[
\mathbb E_{W\in C^\perp}q^{|W|}
-
\mathbb E_{U\in\mathbb F_2^N}q^{|U|}
\right].
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The target is thus not merely to push every nonzero boundary away from zero. The **full boundary-weight distribution** of the dual row space has to be close enough to the ambient binomial reference at the exponential test `q^{|w|}`. This is the correct calibration missing from the coarse check-side expansion heuristic.

Equivalently, define the dual defect-pressure excess

\[
\mathfrak D_H(q)
:=
\log\mathbb E_{W\in C^\perp}q^{|W|}
-
N\log\frac{1+q}{2}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\log W_C(z)=\mathfrak D_H(q).}
\tag{5.8}
\]

So the exact interior spectral pressure is itself a **relative defect MGF** against the ambient Bernoulli baseline.

This gives a much more precise deterministic Tanner target:

- variable-side expansion controls the primal source window;
- the dual side needs a near-dependency/odd-boundary **large-deviation profile relative to the binomial baseline**, not only a minimum-boundary certificate.

## 5.3 What LDPC failures teach the source framework

The trapping/absorbing-set literature studies small Tanner subgraphs with a small number of unsatisfied checks; they are the error-floor analogue of small `b_H(y)` configurations. The precise decoding objects differ from `(5.2)`, so I do not identify them theorem-for-theorem. The methodological lesson is exact: a good linear-scale distance or asymptotic spectral shape can coexist with rare sparse local structures that dominate a low-temperature/weighted observable.

That is exactly why the predecessor's random-regular polylog minimum-distance theorem does not, on its own, certify `(5.8)`. A complete deterministic B19 certificate needs two scales:

1. a source-window primal certificate (distance or bounded low-weight mass);
2. a dual **defect-spectrum** certificate controlling the count of selected-check sets at each odd-boundary size, calibrated against `(5.5)`.

The 2026 regular-LDPC minimum-distance hardness result further says that a universal exact algorithm for the first item is already unrealistic; theorem-backed structural certificates are the right level of generality.

## 5.4 What Ising/current failures teach the same framework

The cactus theorem in Section 3 is the exact positive analogue. Its cycle entropy is local: each **touched** cycle offers at most two routes, while untouched cycle entropy cancels against the denominator. On a general loopy graph, overlapping cycles can form large interacting loop clusters; the chord-fugacity derivative then needs a uniform defect-susceptibility bound, not a bound on the total number of cycles.

High-temperature Dobrushin/KP/complete-analyticity regimes are natural sufficient domains for such a bound. Near criticality, susceptibilities/local correlations can lose uniform summability, so a source-wide theorem cannot simply assume “bounded degree” or “Gibbs” and expect the interpolation to remain local.

The coding and current pictures therefore share the same structural competition:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local defect entropy}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\text{noise/current energy penalty}.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This does not yet constitute a source-wide characterization, but it is a more precise candidate architecture than global chi-square pressure or generic high temperature alone.
