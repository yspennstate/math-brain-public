## 4. B11 — DUALIZE: the exact conditional-Hellinger representation

Define the **energy-tilted law**

\[
\nu_q(dx):=\frac{g_q(x)^2}{F(q)}Q(dx).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a coordinate `i` and frozen boundary `x_{-i}`, set

\[
m_i(x_{-i})=E_{Q_i}g_q(x_{-i},X_i)^2,
\qquad
u_{q,i}^{x_{-i}}(dx_i)
=\frac{g_q(x_{-i},x_i)^2}{m_i(x_{-i})}Q_i(dx_i).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The `(-i)` marginal of `nu_q` is

\[
\nu_{q,-i}(dx_{-i})
=\frac{m_i(x_{-i})}{F(q)}Q_{-i}(dx_{-i}).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because `g_q>=0`, the Hellinger affinity of the conditional energy tilt with the null fiber is

\[
\mathsf A_i(x_{-i})
:=\int\sqrt{\frac{d\nu_{q,i}^{x_{-i}}}{dQ_i}}\,dQ_i
=\frac{E_{Q_i}g_q}{\sqrt{E_{Q_i}g_q^2}}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
1-\mathsf A_i(x_{-i})^2
=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{Q_i}(g_q\mid x_{-i})}{E_{Q_i}g_q^2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Multiplying by (4.3), integrating and summing gives the exact dual identity

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)
=\sum_{i=1}^m
E_{\nu_{q,-i}}
\left[1-\mathsf A_i(X_{-i})^2\right].
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

So the pressure quantity is exactly the total **energy-tilted conditional Hellinger defect from the product null fibers**. This is not presented as a new probability identity in isolation; its use here is to translate the source's spectral obstruction into a conditional physical-space quantity.

### 4.1 Why ordinary Dobrushin/approximate tensorization is not by itself the missing theorem

Dobrushin and approximate-tensorization hypotheses control how the tilted conditional law changes when the boundary changes. Equation (4.6) instead also measures how far each conditional law is from the reference null fiber `Q_i`. These are logically different pieces: interaction control does not by itself control an external field relative to `Q_i`.

This is the precise reason a direct appeal to classical approximate tensorization does not close Kireeva's question. Those results provide global spectral-gap/log-Sobolev information under weak dependence, but Kireeva's bridge needs a **degree-resolved** or null-relative quantity such as (4.6).

### 4.2 A field-plus-interaction certificate in Dobrushin language

Write

\[
f(x)=\log g_q(x).
\]

Fix a reference boundary `x^*`. Define the anchored one-site field oscillation

\[
a_i:=\sup_{u,v}
|f(u,x^*_{-i})-f(v,x^*_{-i})|,
\tag{4.7}
\]

and the cross-coordinate contrast oscillation

\[
d_{ij}:=
\sup_{\substack{x_{-i},y_{-i}\text{ differ only at }j}}
\sup_{u,v}
\left|
[f(u,x_{-i})-f(v,x_{-i})]
-[f(u,y_{-i})-f(v,y_{-i})]
\right|.
\tag{4.8}
\]

For any boundary, telescope from `x^*_{-i}` one coordinate at a time. For each fixed pair `u,v`, every boundary-coordinate change contributes at most `d_{ij}`. Taking the supremum over `u,v` yields

\[
\eta_i(x_{-i})
:=\operatorname{osc}_{x_i} f(x_i,x_{-i})
\le a_i+\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

The standard bounded-positive-variable inequality says that if `Y` has log-oscillation at most `eta`, then

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}{EY^2}
\le \tanh^2(\eta/2).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Applying this fiberwise and using (4.6) gives the explicit field-plus-interaction certificate

\[
\boxed{
\mu(q)
\le
\sum_i
\tanh^2\!\left(
\frac{a_i+\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}}2
\right).
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

The matrix `(d_ij)` is a log-oscillation analogue of a Dobrushin interdependence matrix. For the energy tilt `nu_q proportional g_q^2 Q`, changing boundary coordinate `j` changes the `i`-conditional log density by a function whose oscillation is at most `2d_ij`; standard projective/oscillation comparison therefore gives a Dobrushin-TV coefficient bounded at the same scale (up to the usual `tanh` conversion). Equation (4.11) shows the extra object absent from dependence-only theory: the anchored field `a_i` measuring displacement from the null fiber itself.

Hence, if

\[
\sum_i
\tanh^2\!\left(
\frac{a_i+\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}}2
\right)
\le \theta(D+1),\qquad\theta<1,
\tag{4.12}
\]

then (3.1) holds. This is a model-checkable sufficient condition in the same physical vocabulary as Dobrushin high-temperature estimates, but it explicitly includes both field and interaction control.

### 5.2 Exact strictness family: bad fibers can have diverging oscillation and vanishing total load

Take two product coordinates `(B,X)` with

\[
B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\delta_M),\qquad
X\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{-1,+1\},\qquad
\delta_M=e^{-4M}.
\]

Define an unnormalized positive density

\[
\widetilde g_M(B,X)=
\begin{cases}
1,&B=0,\\
e^{MX},&B=1,
\end{cases}
\]

and normalize `g_M=widetilde g_M/E_Q widetilde g_M`. Scaling cancels from every relative local load.

For the `X` fiber, the bad boundary `B=1` has log oscillation `2M`, so the worst-fiber oscillation diverges and the worst relative conditional variance tends to `1/2`. Nevertheless the global `X`-Dirichlet contribution is

\[
N_X=\delta_M\operatorname{Var}_{X}(e^{MX})
=e^{-4M}\sinh^2 M
=O(e^{-2M}).
\tag{5.6}
\]

For the `B` fiber,

\[
N_B=
\frac{\delta_M(1-\delta_M)}2
\left[(e^M-1)^2+(e^{-M}-1)^2\right]
=O(e^{-2M}).
\tag{5.7}
\]

The denominator is

\[
E_Q\widetilde g_M^2
=1-\delta_M+\delta_M\cosh(2M)
=1+O(e^{-2M}).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mu_M=(N_X+N_B)/E\widetilde g_M^2=O(e^{-2M})\to0,

\tag{5.9}
\]

while `sup eta_X=2M->infinity`. This proves strictness of the typical criterion over the preceding worst-fiber certificate without any numerical checker: the estimates are closed-form identities.

## 6. B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: a nonproduct-null theorem that keeps source polynomial degree

A danger in moving to a Gibbs/nonproduct null is silently redefining “degree” to mean the spectrum of the Glauber generator. That would no longer answer the source question. Here the polynomial subspace is kept fixed and the mismatch is measured explicitly by a commutator.

Let `Q` now be an arbitrary finite null law. Let `V_D` be the **source polynomial subspace** of mean-zero polynomials of algebraic degree at most `D`, completed in `L^2(Q)`, and let

\[
P_D:L^2_0(Q)\to V_D
\]

be the orthogonal projection. Then, exactly as in the usual LDLR variational identity,

\[
\boxed{
\|P_D(L-1)\|_2=\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let `A>=0` be a self-adjoint reversible Markov generator with `A1=0`, and let

\[
S_t=e^{-tA}
\]

be the noise semigroup. Set `H_D=I-P_D` on the mean-zero space. Assume two explicit alignment conditions:

1. **high-degree coercivity**
\[
\langle h,Ah\rangle\ge\lambda_D\|h\|_2^2
\qquad\forall h\in\operatorname{Ran}H_D;
\tag{6.2}
\]

2. **small degree leakage**
\[
\varepsilon_D:=\|[A,P_D]\|_{2\to2}<\infty.
\tag{6.3}
\]

When `Q` is product and `A` is the usual number/heat-bath generator, `epsilon_D=0` and `lambda_D=D+1`; (6.2)-(6.3) therefore measure exactly how a nonproduct Glauber dynamics departs from the source polynomial grading.

### Theorem 6.1 — commutator/coercivity low-degree implication

Let `h=L-1`,

\[
A_D:=\|P_Dh\|_2=\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q),
\qquad
M:=\|h\|_2=\sqrt{\chi^2(P\|Q)}.
\]

Assume `epsilon_D<=lambda_D/2`. Then for every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|S_t h\|_2
\le
\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_D}{\lambda_D}\right)
\exp\!\left(\frac{2\varepsilon_D^2t}{\lambda_D}\right)
\left(A_D+\frac{2\varepsilon_D}{\lambda_D}M\right)
+e^{-\lambda_D t}M.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently, if along a source sequence

\[
\sup_n A_{D_n}<\infty
\tag{6.5}
\]

and for a fixed noise time `t>0`

\[
\left[
 e^{-\lambda_{D_n}t}
 +\frac{\varepsilon_{D_n}}{\lambda_{D_n}}
  \exp\!\left(\frac{2\varepsilon_{D_n}^2t}{\lambda_{D_n}}\right)
\right]
\sqrt{\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)}
=O(1),
\tag{6.6}
\]

with `epsilon_D/lambda_D` uniformly bounded below `1/2`, then

\[
\sup_n\chi^2(S_tP_n\|Q_n)<\infty.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Hence the noised laws have a uniform TV gap from one against **every measurable test**.

#### Proof

Write

\[
u(t)=S_th,
\qquad
a(t)=\|P_Du(t)\|_2,
\qquad
b(t)=\|H_Du(t)\|_2.
\]

The semigroup is an `L^2` contraction, so `||u(t)||<=M`.

Because `A>=0`, the diagonal low block cannot increase `a`; only the cross block can. Using

\[
P_DAH_D=-P_D[A,P_D]H_D
\]

and the upper Dini derivative if a norm vanishes,

\[
a'(t)\le\varepsilon_D b(t).
\tag{6.8}
\]

For the high block, (6.2) and

\[
H_DAP_D=[A,P_D]P_D
\]

give

\[
b'(t)
\le-\lambda_D b(t)+\varepsilon_D a(t).
\tag{6.9}
\]

Set

\[
w(t)=a(t)+\frac{2\varepsilon_D}{\lambda_D}b(t).
\]

Using (6.8)-(6.9),

\[
\begin{aligned}
w'(t)
&\le \varepsilon_D b
+\frac{2\varepsilon_D}{\lambda_D}
 (-\lambda_D b+\varepsilon_D a)\\
&=-\varepsilon_D b
+\frac{2\varepsilon_D^2}{\lambda_D}a\\
&\le\frac{2\varepsilon_D^2}{\lambda_D}w(t).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
w(t)\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{2\varepsilon_D^2t}{\lambda_D}\right)
\left(a(0)+\frac{2\varepsilon_D}{\lambda_D}b(0)\right).
\tag{6.10}
\]

Also, solving (6.9),

\[
b(t)
\le e^{-\lambda_Dt}b(0)
+\varepsilon_D\int_0^t e^{-\lambda_D(t-s)}a(s)\,ds
\le e^{-\lambda_Dt}M
+\frac{\varepsilon_D}{\lambda_D}\sup_{s\le t}w(s).
\tag{6.11}
\]

Since `a(t)<=w(t)`, combine (6.10)-(6.11):

\[
\|u(t)\|_2\le a(t)+b(t)
\le
\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon_D}{\lambda_D}\right)
\sup_{s\le t}w(s)
+e^{-\lambda_Dt}M,
\]

which is exactly (6.4), using `a(0)=A_D` and `b(0)<=M`. Squaring gives (6.7). `□`

### 6.2 Source-scale interpretation

A transparent sufficient regime is

\[
\lambda_{D_n}\ge cD_n,
\qquad
\log^+\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)=o(D_n),
\qquad
\frac{\varepsilon_{D_n}}{D_n}
\sqrt{\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)}=O(1),
\tag{6.12}
\]

with `epsilon_D^2/D_D=O(1)` at fixed `t`. Since `D_n=(log n)^{1+Omega(1)}`, the high-degree semigroup damping can absorb subexponential second-moment growth while the commutator explicitly prices the leakage of source-high-degree signal back into the source-low-degree polynomial space.

This is a genuine nonproduct extension because the low-degree space remains the source's algebraic polynomial space. The theorem does **not** identify “degree” with the Glauber spectrum.

### 6.3 What Dobrushin/approximate tensorization does and does not give

Classical Dobrushin/spectral-independence/approximate-tensorization theory can give a dimension-free global variance inequality or a global Glauber spectral gap. That alone is not (6.2): Kireeva's superlog cutoff needs damping that grows with polynomial degree, or another mechanism controlling the high-degree reassembly. Thus the new theorem isolates two extra quantities a high-temperature argument would still have to prove:

- degree-filtered coercivity `lambda_D`, ideally `Omega(D)`;

This is the exact obstruction to silently porting a product-space proof to a nonproduct null.
