## 4. B4 — the weakest standard condition is NUOD, and it is exact for universal chi-square domination

The preceding report assumed full negative association. That is stronger than what (3.1) actually needs.

### Definition 4.1 — Bernoulli NUOD / upper-cylinder condition

A random support `A subseteq E` is **upper-cylinder negatively dependent** if

\[
\boxed{
 a_S=\Pr(S\subseteq A)
 \le
 \prod_{e\in S}q_e
 \quad\text{for every }S\subseteq E.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a `{0,1}^E` indicator vector this is exactly negative upper orthant dependence: arbitrary threshold events reduce to requiring a specified subset of coordinates to equal one. It is strictly weaker than negative association in general.

### Theorem 4.2 — exact NUOD boundary for the forced-support channel

Fix a finite support law `nu` on subsets of `E`, with marginals `q_e`. The following are equivalent.

**(A)** `nu` is NUOD, i.e. (4.1) holds for every `S`.

**(B)** For every vector of nonnegative numbers `(t_e)_{e in E}`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{S\subseteq E}t_Sa_S^2
\le
\prod_{e\in E}(1+t_eq_e^2).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

**(C)** For every heterogeneous product-Bernoulli null `Q` with all `0<p_e<1` and every coordinatewise null-resampling operator `T`, the associated forced-support planted law is Rényi-2 / chi-square dominated by the marginal-matched independent support law:

\[
\boxed{
D_2(TP\|Q)\le D_2(T\widetilde P\|Q).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

`(A) => (B)`. All coefficients `t_S` are nonnegative. Squaring (4.1) gives

\[
a_S^2\le\prod_{e\in S}q_e^2.
\]

Therefore, coefficient by coefficient,

\[
\sum_St_Sa_S^2
\le
\sum_St_S\prod_{e\in S}q_e^2
=
\prod_e(1+t_eq_e^2).
\]

This is (4.2).

`(B) => (A)`. Suppose (4.1) fails for some nonempty `S_0`, so

\[
a_{S_0}^2>\prod_{e\in S_0}q_e^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Set `t_e=t` for `e in S_0` and `t_e=0` outside `S_0`. Subtract the right side of (4.2) from the left side. The resulting polynomial in `t` has degree `|S_0|`, and its leading coefficient is exactly

\[
a_{S_0}^2-\prod_{e\in S_0}q_e^2>0.
\]

Hence the difference is positive for all sufficiently large `t`, contradicting (B). Thus every upper-cylinder inequality holds.

`(B) <=> (C)`. Equations (3.1)–(3.2) turn (4.2) exactly into chi-square domination; `D_2=\log(1+\chi^2)` is increasing. Conversely, every vector `t_e>=0` is realizable by a permitted product-null/noise pair: for `t_e>0` take no noise (`rho_e=1`) and `p_e=1/(1+t_e)`, so `lambda_e=t_e`; for `t_e=0` take `rho_e=0`. Therefore universal channel domination is equivalent to (4.2). `□`

This is stronger conceptually than the preceding NA statement: **full negative association is not the structural boundary. NUOD is exactly the weakest standard upper-cylinder condition that characterizes universal matched-independent Rényi-2 domination for this channel.**

### Corollary 4.3 — source-style consequence under NUOD

For every NUOD forced-support model and every degree cutoff `D>=1`, (3.2) and Theorem 4.2 give

\[
1+\chi^2(TP\|Q)
\le
\prod_e(1+\rho_e^2\lambda_eq_e^2)
\le
\exp\!\left(\sum_e\rho_e^2\lambda_eq_e^2\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The exact degree-one likelihood-ratio energy is

\[
b_1=\sum_e\lambda_eq_e^2,
\]

so, with `rho_*:=max_e rho_e`,

\[
\boxed{
D_2(TP\|Q)
\le
\rho_*^2 b_1
\le
\rho_*^2\bigl(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D}(P,Q)\bigr)^2.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

In particular, take a sequence with the source cutoff

\[
D_n=(\log n)^{1+\Omega(1)}
\]

and suppose

\[
\sup_n\operatorname{Adv}^{\le D_n}(P_n,Q_n)\le A<\infty.
\]

For `T=Id`,

\[
\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)\le e^{A^2}-1.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus no measurable test, hence no polynomial of any degree, can strongly distinguish. Indeed, for `L=dP/dQ`,

\[
1-\|P-Q\|_{TV}
=
\mathbb E_Q\min(1,L)
\ge
\mathbb E_Q\frac{L}{1+L}.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz applied to

\[
L=\sqrt{\frac{L}{1+L}}\sqrt{L(1+L)}
\]

gives

\[
1
\le
\left(\mathbb E_Q\frac{L}{1+L}\right)
\mathbb E_Q[L(1+L)]
=
\left(\mathbb E_Q\frac{L}{1+L}\right)(2+\chi^2),
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\|P_n-Q_n\|_{TV}
\le
1-\frac{1}{2+\chi^2(P_n\|Q_n)}
\le
1-\frac{1}{1+e^{A^2}}<1.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

If the low-degree advantage tends to zero, (4.7) gives `chi^2 -> 0`, hence total variation tends to zero.

### Corollary 4.4 — direct Fourier condition beyond forced-support representations

The algebra also identifies a representation-free sufficient condition on a product-Bernoulli pair. Let `L=dP/dQ` and write its orthonormal Boolean Fourier coefficients as `Lhat(S)`. If

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat L(S)|^2
\le
\prod_{e\in S}|\widehat L(\{e\})|^2
\quad\text{for every nonempty }S,
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

then Parseval under coordinatewise resampling gives

\[
1+\chi^2(TP\|Q)
\le
\prod_e\left(1+\rho_e^2|\widehat L(\{e\})|^2\right),
\]

and therefore the same bound (4.6) follows with

\[
b_1=\sum_e|\widehat L(\{e\})|^2.
\]

For forced-support models, NUOD is exactly the condition that implies (4.9) through the explicit coefficient formula. I do **not** claim (4.9) is necessary for the source problem or novel; it is an elementary direct sufficient condition exposed by the NUOD calculation.

## 6. B15 — break symmetry: a genuinely nonexchangeable strongly-Rayleigh spanning-tree family

The previous examples were highly symmetric. Here the support law is strongly Rayleigh but deliberately not invariant under all vertex relabelings.

Let `K_n` have positive vertex weights `w_1,...,w_n`, write

\[
W:=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i,
\]

and draw a spanning tree `T` with probability

\[
\Pr(T)
\propto
\prod_{\{i,j\}\in T} w_iw_j
=
\prod_{i=1}^n w_i^{\deg_T(i)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is a weighted spanning-tree / determinantal measure and is strongly Rayleigh; in particular it is negatively associated and hence NUOD. Plant the random edge support `A=E(T)` by forcing those edges into a product null `G(n,p)` with fixed `0<p<1`.

### Lemma 6.1 — exact partition function

\[
\boxed{
Z_n(w)
:=
\sum_T\prod_{i=1}^n w_i^{\deg_T(i)}
=
\left(\prod_{i=1}^n w_i\right)W^{n-2}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

**Proof.** Under the Prüfer correspondence, vertex `i` appears exactly `deg_T(i)-1` times in the length-`n-2` Prüfer word. Hence

\[
\prod_iw_i^{\deg_T(i)}
=
\left(\prod_iw_i\right)
\prod_{r=1}^{n-2}w_{a_r},
\]

where `(a_1,...,a_{n-2})` is the Prüfer word. Summing independently over every word gives `(prod_i w_i)W^{n-2}`. `□`

### Lemma 6.2 — exact nonuniform edge marginals

For every `i!=j`,

\[
\boxed{
q_{ij}:=\Pr(\{i,j\}\in T)=\frac{w_i+w_j}{W}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

**Proof.** Give edge `{i,j}` conductance `w_iw_j`. By deletion–contraction, the total weight of trees containing `{i,j}` is `w_iw_j` times the spanning-tree partition function after contracting `i,j`. The contracted vertex has weight `w_i+w_j`, the total vertex weight remains `W`, and Lemma 6.1 on `n-1` vertices gives

\[
w_iw_j(w_i+w_j)
\left(\prod_{k\ne i,j}w_k\right)W^{n-3}.
\]

Divide by `Z_n(w)=w_iw_j(prod_{k\ne i,j}w_k)W^{n-2}` to obtain (6.3). `□`

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_2
&:=\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}^2\\
&=\frac{1}{W^2}\sum_{i<j}(w_i+w_j)^2\\
&=\boxed{1+(n-2)\frac{\sum_iw_i^2}{W^2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

For bounded positive weights with `W=Theta(n)`, this is `O(1)`. With `lambda=(1-p)/p`, the degree-one source advantage is exactly

\[
\bigl(\operatorname{Adv}^{\le1}(P,Q)\bigr)^2
=\lambda S_2=O(1),
\tag{6.5}
\]

and NUOD plus Theorem 4.2 yields the **full** bound

\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P\|Q)
\le
\exp(\lambda S_2)-1=O(1).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Hence all low-degree advantages at the source cutoff are bounded and no measurable test strongly distinguishes.

For a concrete asymmetric sequence, take even `n`, set

\[
w_i=1\quad(i\le n/2),
\qquad
w_i=2\quad(i>n/2).
\]

Then

\[
W=\frac{3n}{2},
\qquad
\sum_iw_i^2=\frac{5n}{2},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
S_2
=
1+\frac{10(n-2)}{9n}
=
\frac{19}{9}-\frac{20}{9n}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

The three edge types have distinct inclusion probabilities

\[
q_{11}=\frac{4}{3n},
\qquad
q_{12}=\frac{2}{n},
\qquad
q_{22}=\frac{8}{3n}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus the support law is genuinely nonexchangeable under the full symmetric group, while the low-degree-to-full-testing implication still follows from negative dependence. This demonstrates that the protected mechanism is not merely Hsieh-style global permutation symmetry.

This is **not** claimed as a new spanning-tree detection theorem. Weighted spanning trees and their strong-Rayleigh property are classical, and planted spanning-tree detection has current dedicated literature. The role of (6.1)–(6.8) is to demonstrate breadth of the structural condition.
