# 3. B11 — dualize pressure into a Piola transport plus a zero-order remainder

The key obstacle in the previous report was that the source noise
\[
B_bu:=\mathbb P[(b\cdot\nabla)u]
\tag{3.1}
\]
is not literally the Lie derivative of an incompressible vector field when `b` varies in space.

There is a source-valid way to separate the genuinely first-order geometric part from the Hodge correction.

For a smooth vector field `b` and a divergence-free smooth vector field `u`, define the **Piola transport generator**
\[
\mathcal G_bu
:=
(b\cdot\nabla)u-(u\cdot\nabla)b+(\nabla\cdot b)u.
\tag{3.2}
\]

A direct index computation gives
\[
\nabla\cdot(\mathcal G_bu)=0
\qquad\text{whenever}\qquad
\nabla\cdot u=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_i(\mathcal G_bu)_i
={}&
(\partial_i b_j)(\partial_j u_i)
-(\partial_i u_j)(\partial_j b_i)\\
&-u_j\partial_j(\partial_i b_i)
+u_i\partial_i(\partial_j b_j),
\end{aligned}
\]
after the terms containing `div u` vanish. The first line cancels after interchanging `i,j` in the second term, and the second line cancels by relabeling the dummy index.

Consequently `P G_bu=G_bu`, and the source noise has the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P[(b\cdot\nabla)u]
=
\mathcal G_bu+\mathcal R_bu,
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_bu
=
\mathbb P\!\left[(u\cdot\nabla)b-(\nabla\cdot b)u\right].
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the decisive simplification: **all first derivatives of `u` are in the geometric Piola generator; the remainder is zero order in `u`.**

On `T^3`, for every `1<q<infinity`,
\[
\|\mathcal R_bu\|_{L^q}
\le
C_q\bigl(\|\nabla b\|_\infty+\|\nabla\cdot b\|_\infty\bigr)
\|u\|_{L^q},
\tag{3.6}
\]
because the Leray projector is bounded on `L^q`. For an `ell^2` family the same pointwise multiplication structure gives the corresponding vector-valued bound under the source's smooth `ell^2` coefficient control. The `R^3` low-frequency/infinity bookkeeping should be kept tied to the source's additional infinity assumptions; this report does not silently extend (3.6) beyond the hypotheses needed there.

Equation (3.2) is exactly the infinitesimal generator, up to orientation/sign, of the Piola action of a diffeomorphism on vector fields. If `Phi_s` is the flow of `b`, then
\[
(\Phi_s)_*^{\rm Piola}u(x)
=
\frac{D\Phi_s(y)}{\det D\Phi_s(y)}u(y),
\qquad y=\Phi_s^{-1}(x),
\tag{3.7}
\]
and differentiation at `s=0` gives `-G_bu`. Thus, with the inverse orientation, a stochastic Piola flow is the natural transform that cancels the **whole first-order part** in (3.4) while preserving ordinary divergence.

For divergence-free `b`, (3.5) reduces to
\[
\mathcal R_bu=\mathbb P[(u\cdot\nabla)b],
\tag{3.8}
\]
recovering the previous report's Lie/Hodge decomposition.

For the single shear
\[
b(x)=\sigma a(x_2)e_1,
\tag{3.9}
\]
one obtains the especially simple zero-order remainder
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_bu
=
\sigma\,\mathbb P[a'(x_2)u_2e_1].
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

### Outcome of B11

The dangerous stochastic derivative is not an irreducible `L^6` stochastic-convolution singularity: it is the generator of a divergence-preserving Piola flow plus a zero-order stochastic coefficient. The endpoint difficulty moves into the **random transformed Stokes operator**, while the residual stochastic forcing is lower order.

# 4. B12 — the conserved geometry of the single shear

Keep the simplest unresolved source-valid case from the first stuck report:
\[
O=\mathbb T^3,\qquad
g=0,\qquad
b(x)=\sigma a(x_2)e_1,
\quad a\in C^\infty(\mathbb T)\text{ nonconstant},
\tag{4.1}
\]
with
\[
\sigma^2\|a\|_\infty^2<2\nu.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The characteristic shear is explicit:
\[
\Phi_t(y)
=
\bigl(y_1+\sigma W_t a(y_2),y_2,y_3\bigr)
\tag{4.3}
\]
(up to the harmless orientation sign chosen to cancel the noise).

Set
\[
q_t(y_2)=\sigma W_t a'(y_2).
\tag{4.4}
\]
Then
\[
F_t=D\Phi_t
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&q_t&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
F_t^{-1}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-q_t&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The first invariant is exact:
\[
\boxed{\det F_t=1.}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Thus the shear is volume preserving and the Piola transform has no Jacobian growth.

The scalar pullback metric is
\[
A_t=F_t^{-1}F_t^{-T}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+q_t^2&-q_t&0\\
-q_t&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Again,
\[
\boxed{\det A_t=1.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The two nontrivial eigenvalues are reciprocal: their polynomial is
\[
\lambda^2-(2+q_t^2)\lambda+1=0.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Therefore every stopped finite-time interval on which `|W_t|` is bounded has an explicit finite ellipticity constant. There is no hidden determinant collapse.

The transformed scalar principal operator also has a useful exact square form:
\[
\boxed{
\nabla\cdot(A_t\nabla f)
=
\partial_1^2 f+
(\partial_2-q_t\partial_1)^2f+
\partial_3^2 f.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]
The derivative of `q_t` is included in the square when it is expanded. This identity was checked symbolically after the mechanism was written down.

### Outcome of B12

The single-shear transformed geometry is not arbitrary rough geometry. It is an explicit determinant-one, rank-one nilpotent deformation with reciprocal principal eigenvalues. The obstruction is therefore not loss of ellipticity; it is endpoint stability of the incompressible Hodge/Stokes machinery under this time-dependent deformation.

# 5. B8 — discretize the random geometry on parabolic tents

The remaining question is whether the moving generalized Stokes geometry is small enough, scale by scale, for an endpoint coefficient-freezing argument.

Fix a Brownian sample path on a finite interval. For every
\[
0<\alpha<\frac12
\]
there is a finite random constant `C_{alpha,omega}` such that
\[
|W_t-W_s|\le C_{\alpha,\omega}|t-s|^\alpha.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Consider a parabolic cylinder of spatial radius `r` and temporal length `r^2`. For the shear coefficient (4.4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
|q_t(x_2)-q_s(y_2)|
\le{}&
|\sigma|\,\|a'\|_\infty |W_t-W_s|\\
&+
|\sigma|\,|W_s|\,\|a''\|_\infty |x_2-y_2|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence, on a fixed finite time horizon,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{osc}_{Q_r}q
\le C_\omega(r^{2\alpha}+r)
\le C_\omega r^\gamma,
\qquad
\gamma:=\min\{2\alpha,1\}>0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since `A(q)` in (4.7), `F(q)` and `F(q)^{-1}` are smooth functions of `q` on every bounded range,
\[
\operatorname{osc}_{Q_r}A
+
\operatorname{osc}_{Q_r}F
+
\operatorname{osc}_{Q_r}F^{-1}
\le C_\omega r^\gamma.
\tag{5.4}
\]

At dyadic radii `r_j=2^{-j}`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\ge0}
\operatorname{osc}_{Q_{r_j}}A
<\infty
\quad\text{pathwise}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the shear-generated coefficients have a **pathwise parabolic Dini modulus**, much stronger than an arbitrary measurable-in-time coefficient field. This is exactly the kind of scale summability that a freezing argument can spend.

There is also a useful endpoint scaling test. Suppose, only for the purpose of exposing exponents, that a fixed-reference endpoint estimate supplied
\[
\|\nabla v(s)\|_{6}\lesssim M s^{-3/4}
\tag{5.6}
\]
for data of formal smoothness `-1/2`, and suppose a coefficient perturbation contributes a factor `s^\alpha`. The fixed-heat Duhamel term would have the scale
\[
t^{1/4}
\int_0^t
(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{\alpha-3/4}\,ds
=
B\!\left(\alpha+\frac14,\frac12\right)t^\alpha.
\tag{5.7}
\]

For every `alpha>0`, this tends to zero as `t downarrow0`. Therefore the Brownian near-identity geometry is **subcritical by a positive time power** in this model, provided the missing endpoint gradient/maximal-regularity estimate is available. Equation (5.7) is a conditional scaling calculation, not a proof of the stochastic Navier–Stokes endpoint.

### Outcome of B8

The coefficient-freezing errors do not look scale-critical or divergent for the shear. Their dyadic oscillations are summable. This makes an immediate endpoint obstruction from the random geometry less plausible and turns the remaining task into a sharply stated operator-stability problem.
