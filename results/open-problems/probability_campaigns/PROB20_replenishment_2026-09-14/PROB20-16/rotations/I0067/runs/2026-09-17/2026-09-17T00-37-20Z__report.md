## 3. B20 — exact diagnosis of why the `q<6` route stops

The first useful question is whether `q<6` is forced by the Navier–Stokes product itself or by the maximal-regularity implementation.

Set

\[
d=3,\qquad \delta=-\frac12,\qquad q=6.
\]

The scale used in the proof of Theorem 8.26 is

\[
X_0=H^{-1+\delta,q}=H^{-3/2,6},\qquad
X_1=H^{1+\delta,q}=H^{1/2,6}.
\]

For the Navier–Stokes nonlinearity
\[
F(u)=\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u),
\]
the source’s Lemma 8.29 introduces
\[
\lambda=\frac{dq}{d-\delta q},\qquad
\theta=\frac dq-\frac d{2\lambda},\qquad
\beta=\frac12\left(1-\frac\delta2+\frac d{2q}\right).
\]
At the endpoint,
\[
\boxed{\lambda=3,\qquad \theta=0,\qquad \beta=\frac34.}
\]

The strict source assumption `q<d/(-delta)` is used there to obtain `theta>0`. But for the **pure transport-noise case `g=0`**, the `F` estimate itself survives at equality. Indeed, strong Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev/Sobolev embedding gives

\[
L^3(\mathbb R^3)\hookrightarrow H^{-1/2,6}(\mathbb R^3),
\]
and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|F(v)-F(w)\|_{H^{-3/2,6}}
&\lesssim
\|v\otimes v-w\otimes w\|_{H^{-1/2,6}}\\
&\lesssim
\|v\otimes v-w\otimes w\|_{L^3}\\
&\lesssim
(\|v\|_{L^6}+\|w\|_{L^6})\|v-w\|_{L^6}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since `X_beta=H^{0,6}=L^6`, this is exactly the endpoint local-Lipschitz estimate needed for `F`.

So the quadratic Navier–Stokes product is **not** the fundamental obstruction at `q=6` in the pure transport case.

The critical trace relation is more revealing. Theorem 8.26 uses

\[
\kappa_c=-1+\frac p2\left(2+\delta-\frac dq\right).
\]
At `(d,delta,q)=(3,-1/2,6)`,
\[
\boxed{\kappa_c=\frac p2-1.}
\]

For `p>2`, this is exactly the excluded upper endpoint of the time-weight range
\[
\kappa\in[0,p/2-1).
\]
The only remaining critical choice in that framework is therefore
\[
\boxed{p=2,\qquad \kappa=0.}
\]

But the transport-noise stochastic maximal-regularity input used by Agresti–Veraar does not cover `p=2,q=6`; its endpoint `p=2` case is the Hilbert case `q=2`. The 2026 Lorist–van Neerven–Veraar theorem sharpens the structural warning: the generic stochastic-convolution geometry `(S_2)` is Hilbertian, whereas the spatial `L^6`/Bessel scale is not Hilbert.

## 5. B4 — complete endpoint theorem for nonzero constant transport noise

### Theorem 5.1 (constant transport reaches smoothness `-1/2` on `R^3`)

Let `nu>0`, and let `(b_n)_{n>=1}` be a nonzero sequence of constant vectors in `R^3` such that

\[
Q:=\sum_{n\ge1}b_nb_n^\top
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad
Q\preceq 2\nu_0I
\quad\text{for some }\nu_0<\nu.
\]

Take `g_n=0` in the source equation. Consider the projected Itô system

\[
du=
[\nu\Delta u-\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)]dt
+\sum_{n\ge1}(b_n\cdot\nabla)u\,dW_t^n,
\qquad \operatorname{div}u=0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Set
\[
D:=\nu I-\frac12Q\succ0,
\qquad
L_D:=\sum_{i,j=1}^3D_{ij}\partial_i\partial_j,
\qquad
S_D(t):=e^{tL_D}.
\]

Let `X_D` be the completion of divergence-free Schwartz vector fields under

\[
\|f\|_{X_D}:=
\sup_{t>0}t^{1/4}\|S_D(t)f\|_{L^6(\mathbb R^3)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then:

1. `X_D` is the little homogeneous critical Besov space
   \[
   X_D\simeq \dot b^{-1/2}_{6,\infty,\sigma}(\mathbb R^3),
   \]
   with equivalent norm; in particular its smoothness is exactly `-1/2` and its Sobolev/scaling index is `-1`.

2. For every `u_0 in X_D` there exists `T>0` and an adapted mild solution of (5.1) such that, after undoing the Brownian translation defined below, the solution belongs to the critical Kato class
   \[
   \sup_{0<t<T}t^{1/4}\|v(t)\|_6<\infty,\qquad
   \lim_{t\downarrow0}t^{1/4}\|v(t)\|_6=0,
   \]
   and
   \[
   u\in C([0,T];X_D)\quad\text{a.s.}
   \]

3. The solution is pathwise unique in this critical Kato class and depends locally continuously on the initial datum.

Thus a genuine nonzero smooth transport-noise subclass reaches the source endpoint `-1/2`. This theorem is **PARTIAL for Problem 10** because the source asks about general smooth nonzero transport coefficients, not only constants.

### Proof

#### Step 1. The Brownian translation is well defined and leaves a uniformly parabolic deterministic operator.

The stochastic parabolicity bound implies
\[
\operatorname{tr}Q=\sum_n|b_n|^2<\infty.
\]
Hence
\[
X_t:=\sum_{n\ge1}b_nW_t^n
\]
is a well-defined continuous `R^3`-valued Brownian motion with covariance matrix `tQ`.

Moreover,
\[
\xi^\top D\xi
=
\nu|\xi|^2-\frac12\sum_n(b_n\cdot\xi)^2
\ge(\nu-\nu_0)|\xi|^2,
\]
so `D` is strictly positive definite.

#### Step 2. Exact Itô–Wentzell cancellation.

For a smooth solution define
\[
v(t,x):=u(t,x-X_t).
\]

Because every `b_n` is constant, the transport operators
\[
B_n:=b_n\cdot\nabla
\]
commute with translations, with one another, with the Helmholtz projection, and with constant-coefficient derivatives.

Applying Itô–Wentzell, the stochastic terms `B_nu dW^n` cancel the differential of the shift. The shift’s quadratic variation contributes
\[
+\frac12\sum_nB_n^2v\,dt,
\]
while the Itô–Wentzell cross variation between the stochastic coefficient and the shift contributes
\[
-\sum_nB_n^2v\,dt.
\]
The net transport correction is therefore
\[
-\frac12\sum_nB_n^2v.
\]

Translations commute with the quadratic nonlinearity and with `P`. Consequently `v` solves the deterministic anisotropic Navier–Stokes equation

\[
\partial_tv
=
L_Dv-\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes v),
\qquad
v(0)=u_0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Conversely, if `v` solves (5.3), then
\[
u(t,x):=v(t,x+X_t)
\tag{5.4}
\]
satisfies (5.1). The rough-data statement follows by smooth approximation after the deterministic construction below.

#### Step 3. `X_D` is a critical smoothness `-1/2` space.

Uniform ellipticity gives, on a Littlewood–Paley annulus `|xi|~2^j`,
\[
\|S_D(t)\Delta_jf\|_6
\lesssim e^{-c t2^{2j}}\|\Delta_jf\|_6.
\]
The standard heat characterization therefore identifies (5.2), up to constants depending only on ellipticity, with
\[
\sup_j2^{-j/2}\|\Delta_jf\|_6,
\]
and the completion of Schwartz fields imposes the corresponding `c_0` (“little Besov”) condition.

For the Navier–Stokes scaling
\[
f_\lambda(x)=\lambda f(\lambda x),
\]
one has
\[
S_D(t)f_\lambda(x)
=
\lambda [S_D(\lambda^2t)f](\lambda x),
\]
hence
\[
t^{1/4}\|S_D(t)f_\lambda\|_6
=
(\lambda^2t)^{1/4}\|S_D(\lambda^2t)f\|_6.
\]
Thus the norm is scaling invariant. Since `q=6`, its Besov smoothness is
\[
3/6-1=-1/2.
\]

#### Step 4. The critical bilinear estimate.

For `T>0`, define
\[
\|v\|_{E_T}:=\sup_{0<t<T}t^{1/4}\|v(t)\|_6.
\]

The anisotropic heat kernel has the same Gaussian scaling as the ordinary heat kernel. Since the Helmholtz projection is bounded on `L^3` and commutes with `S_D`,

\[
\|S_D(r)\mathbb P\operatorname{div}F\|_6
\le C r^{-3/4}\|F\|_3.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let
\[
\mathcal B(v,w)(t)
:=
-\int_0^tS_D(t-s)\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes w)(s)\,ds.
\]
Then Hölder and (5.5) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
t^{1/4}\|\mathcal B(v,w)(t)\|_6
&\le
C t^{1/4}
\int_0^t(t-s)^{-3/4}
\|v(s)\|_6\|w(s)\|_6\,ds\\
&\le
C\|v\|_{E_T}\|w\|_{E_T}
t^{1/4}
\int_0^t(t-s)^{-3/4}s^{-1/2}\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
The beta integral scales as `t^{-1/4}`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal B(v,w)\|_{E_T}
\le C_B\|v\|_{E_T}\|w\|_{E_T}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

#### Step 5. The linear critical norm becomes small on short intervals.

Put
\[
\eta_T(u_0)
:=
\sup_{0<t<T}t^{1/4}\|S_D(t)u_0\|_6.
\]

If `u_0` is Schwartz, `eta_T(u_0)->0` by `L^6` continuity and the explicit factor `t^{1/4}`. Since `X_D` is the completion of Schwartz data and
\[
\eta_T(f)\le\|f\|_{X_D},
\]
the same conclusion holds for every `u_0 in X_D`:
\[
\eta_T(u_0)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(T\downarrow0).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Choose `T` so that
\[
4C_B\eta_T(u_0)<1.
\]
The map
\[
\Phi(v)=S_D(\cdot)u_0+\mathcal B(v,v)
\]
is then a contraction on the closed `E_T` ball of radius `2 eta_T(u_0)`. It has a unique fixed point `v`, which is the mild solution of (5.3).

#### Step 6. Critical trace continuity.

For `0<t<T`, let
\[
M_t:=\sup_{0<s<t}s^{1/4}\|v(s)\|_6.
\]
The fixed-point estimate on `(0,t)` gives
\[
M_t\le\eta_t(u_0)+C_BM_t^2.
\]
Since the original contraction ball makes `C_BM_t<1/2`, it follows that
\[
M_t\le2\eta_t(u_0)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5.8}
\]

To control the nonlinear term in the critical trace norm, use the semigroup once more:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathcal B(v,v)(t)\|_{X_D}
&=
\sup_{\tau>0}
\tau^{1/4}\|S_D(\tau)\mathcal B(v,v)(t)\|_6\\
&\le
C M_t^2
\sup_{\tau>0}
\tau^{1/4}
\int_0^t(\tau+t-s)^{-3/4}s^{-1/2}\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
After the substitutions `tau=at`, `s=rt`, the supremum is finite and independent of `t`. Hence
\[
\|\mathcal B(v,v)(t)\|_{X_D}\lesssim M_t^2\to0.
\]
The semigroup is strongly continuous on the little Besov completion, so
\[
v(t)\to u_0\quad\text{in }X_D.
\]
The same estimates at positive times give `v in C([0,T];X_D)`.

#### Step 7. Uniqueness and continuous dependence.

If `v,w` are two critical-Kato mild solutions with the same initial datum and with the vanishing short-time norm in (5.8), then on a sufficiently short initial interval
\[
\|v-w\|_{E_t}
\le
C_B(\|v\|_{E_t}+\|w\|_{E_t})\|v-w\|_{E_t}
\]
forces equality. At a positive time both solutions are in `L^6`; the same mild estimate restarted from that time gives ordinary local uniqueness, and finitely many restarts cover every compact subinterval of `[0,T)`. The same contraction estimate with different initial data gives local Lipschitz/continuous dependence.

#### Step 8. Return to the stochastic equation.

For smooth approximating data `u_0^m`, the deterministic solutions `v^m` are smooth for positive time, and Itô–Wentzell rigorously yields stochastic solutions
\[
u^m(t,x)=v^m(t,x+X_t).
\]
Translations are isometries of `L^6` and `X_D`, and are strongly continuous on the little Besov completion. The deterministic contraction estimates imply convergence of `v^m`; therefore `u^m` converges to
\[
u(t,x)=v(t,x+X_t).
\]
Passing to the mild equation gives (5.1) for the limiting rough datum. Adaptedness follows because `X_t` is adapted, and pathwise uniqueness follows by applying the inverse translation to any solution in this class.

This proves Theorem 5.1. `□`

### What the theorem does and does not say

The theorem is a genuine source-valid endpoint result:
- transport noise is nonzero;
- the coefficients are smooth;
- stochastic parabolicity holds;
- the `R^3` infinity condition is satisfied with `epsilon=0`;
- the critical spatial smoothness is exactly `-1/2`.

But it adds the substantial restriction that every `b_n` is constant and takes `g=0`. Therefore it **does not answer** Problem 10 for arbitrary smooth source coefficients and does not determine the optimal threshold. It is labeled **PARTIAL**, and `E_endpoint` remains `0`.

The conceptual consequence is still useful: **nonzero transport noise by itself does not force a `>-1/2` threshold.** At least for constant transport, the noise is exactly removable at the price of changing the viscosity tensor.
