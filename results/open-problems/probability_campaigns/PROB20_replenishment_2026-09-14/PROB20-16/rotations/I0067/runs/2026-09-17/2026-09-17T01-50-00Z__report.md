# 3. B19 — turn the constant-transport route into a Koch–Tataru algorithm

The previous run proved an exact `-1/2` theorem for nonzero constant transport by conjugating to deterministic anisotropic Navier–Stokes and using the `L^6` Kato class. The deterministic literature says the correct endpoint mechanism is the Koch–Tataru tent space, so the first question is whether the same conjugation reaches order `-1`.

It does.

## Theorem 3.1 — constant nonzero transport reaches the order `-1` critical endpoint on `R^3`

Let `nu>0`, let `(b_n)_{n>=1}` be a nonzero sequence of **constant** vectors in `R^3`, and suppose

\[
Q:=\sum_{n\ge1} b_nb_n^\top\preceq 2\nu_0 I
\qquad\text{for some }\nu_0<\nu.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Take `g_n=0` in (1.1). Put

\[
D:=\nu I-\frac12Q\succ0,\qquad
L_D:=\sum_{i,j=1}^3D_{ij}\partial_i\partial_j,\qquad
S_D(t):=e^{tL_D}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define the anisotropic Koch–Tataru path norm

\[
\|v\|_{\mathcal E_D(T)}
:=
\sup_{0<t<T}\sqrt t\,\|v(t)\|_\infty
+
\sup_{\substack{x_0\in\mathbb R^3\\0<R^2<T}}
\left(
\frac1{R^3}\int_0^{R^2}\int_{B(x_0,R)}
|v(t,x)|^2\,dx\,dt
\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let `BMO_D^{-1}` be the adapted initial-data space whose heat extension under `S_D` has finite infinite-horizon norm (equivalently the standard Koch–Tataru `BMO^{-1}` space, with constants depending on the ellipticity of `D`), and let `VMO_D^{-1}` be the closure of divergence-free Schwartz data in its local norm.

Then:

1. For every divergence-free `u_0 in VMO_D^{-1}` there is `T>0` and a unique adapted local solution of the source equation in the **moving Koch–Tataru class**
   \[
   u(t,x)=v(t,x+X_t),\qquad v\in\mathcal E_D(T),
   \tag{3.4}
   \]
   where
   \[
   X_t:=\sum_{n\ge1}b_nW_t^n.
   \tag{3.5}
   \]
2. The solution map is locally continuous in the corresponding critical data/solution norms.
3. If `||u_0||_{BMO_D^{-1}}` is sufficiently small, the solution is global in the moving Koch–Tataru class.
4. `BMO_D^{-1}` has Navier–Stokes scaling order `-1`: under
   \[
   u_0(x)\mapsto \mu\,u_0(\mu x)
   \tag{3.6}
   \]
   its norm is invariant. Therefore this is a genuine critical space of smoothness/order `-1`, strictly beyond the source’s `-1/2` threshold, for a nonzero source-valid constant-transport subclass.

This theorem remains **PARTIAL** for Problem 10 because arbitrary smooth `x`-dependent `b_n` and the source’s possible `g` terms are not treated.

### Proof

### Step 1. The Brownian translation exists and the corrected diffusion is elliptic.

Since `Q` is positive semidefinite and `Q<=2 nu_0 I` in three dimensions,

\[
\sum_n|b_n|^2=\operatorname{tr}Q\le6\nu_0<\infty.
\]

Thus (3.5) converges as an `R^3` Brownian martingale with covariance `tQ`. Moreover,

\[
D\succeq(\nu-\nu_0)I>0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

### Step 2. The deterministic conjugate is anisotropic Navier–Stokes.

Consider

\[
\partial_t v
=
L_Dv-\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes v),
\qquad
\operatorname{div}v=0,
\qquad
v(0)=u_0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

For smooth `v`, set `u(t,x)=v(t,x+X_t)`. Ordinary Itô applied to this random translation gives

\[
du
=
\left[
L_Dv+\frac12Q:D^2v
-\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes v)
\right](t,x+X_t)\,dt
+
\sum_n(b_n\cdot\nabla)v(t,x+X_t)\,dW_t^n.
\]

Since `D+Q/2=nu I`, and translations commute with `Delta`, `div`, and the Helmholtz projection,

\[
du=
[\nu\Delta u-\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)]dt
+\sum_n(b_n\cdot\nabla)u\,dW_t^n.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For constant `b_n`, `(b_n·nabla)u` is divergence-free whenever `u` is, so the projected and unprojected transport terms coincide.

Conversely, applying the Itô–Wentzell formula to `v(t,x)=u(t,x-X_t)` cancels the stochastic transport and recovers (3.8). Smooth-data equivalence extends to the critical mild class by approximation. Hence the stochastic and deterministic problems are bijective in the moving class (3.4).

### Step 3. The anisotropic linear semigroup has the exact Koch–Tataru structure.

The heat kernel of `S_D` is explicit:

\[
G_D(t,x)
=
\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{3/2}\sqrt{\det D}}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^\top D^{-1}x}{4t}\right).
\tag{3.10}
\]

The Leray projection and `S_D` commute, because their Fourier multipliers are respectively

\[
P(\xi)=I-\frac{\xi\xi^\top}{|\xi|^2},
\qquad
e^{-t\,\xi^\top D\xi}I.
\]

Uniform ellipticity and Fourier scaling give the anisotropic Oseen bounds, for `|beta|=1`,

\[
\left|
t^{|\beta|/2}\partial^\beta k^D_t(x)
\right|
\le
C_D t^{-3/2}
\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\sqrt t}\right)^{-3-|\beta|},
\tag{3.11}
\]

where `k^D_t` is the kernel of `S_D(t)P`. The same Gaussian/off-diagonal bounds imply tent-space maximal regularity for `L_D`.

Auscher–Frey’s proof of their Theorem 2.4 for the Koch–Tataru bilinear operator uses precisely: (i) the Oseen derivative bound of the form (3.11), (ii) tent-space maximal regularity for the second-order generator, (iii) the commutation of the Leray projector with the semigroup, and (iv) the corresponding scale-invariant `L^2` multiplier/off-diagonal bounds. All four hold here. Replacing `Delta` by `L_D` line-by-line therefore yields

\[
\left\|
\int_0^t
S_D(t-s)\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes w)(s)\,ds
\right\|_{\mathcal E_D(T)}
\le
C_D
\|v\|_{\mathcal E_D(T)}
\|w\|_{\mathcal E_D(T)}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

No stochastic maximal `L^2(L^6)` estimate is used.

### Step 4. Critical fixed point.

Let

\[
\mathcal B_D(v,w)(t)
:=
-\int_0^t S_D(t-s)\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes w)(s)\,ds.
\]

Equation (3.8) is

\[
v=S_D(\cdot)u_0+\mathcal B_D(v,v).
\tag{3.13}
\]

By (3.12), Picard contraction works whenever the free evolution is sufficiently small in `E_D(T)`. For small `BMO_D^{-1}` data this holds with `T=infinity`, yielding a global small solution. For `u_0 in VMO_D^{-1}`, the local adapted norm of `S_D(t)u_0` tends to zero as `T downarrow0` by the defining closure/vanishing property, so some `T>0` gives the contraction. The same bilinear estimate gives uniqueness and continuous dependence.

The standard heat-semigroup characterization of `BMO^{-1}` is unchanged, up to equivalent constants, when the isotropic Gaussian is replaced by the uniformly elliptic constant Gaussian (3.10); equivalently one may use an invertible linear change of variables. Hence `BMO_D^{-1}` is the usual critical order-`-1` class up to norm equivalence.

### Step 5. Return to the source SPDE.

Equation (3.9) gives existence. The inverse Itô–Wentzell map gives uniqueness in the moving critical class. At `t=0`, `X_0=0`, so the stochastic initial datum is exactly `u_0`. The stochastic solution is adapted because `X_t` is adapted and `v` is deterministic for deterministic `u_0` (or conditionally determined by the initial sigma-field for random `F_0`-measurable data).

This proves Theorem 3.1. `square`

### Important solution-space qualification

A time-dependent Brownian translation does not literally preserve the fixed-center parabolic Carleson part of the usual Eulerian Koch–Tataru path norm. The theorem therefore states uniqueness in the **moving-frame** class (3.4), where the pullback `v(t,x)=u(t,x-X_t)` lies in `E_D(T)`. The *initial-data* space is the ordinary order-`-1` critical space up to an equivalent constant-elliptic norm. I do not silently identify the moving solution norm with the fixed Eulerian tent norm.

**B19 outcome:** the previous `-1/2` constant-transport theorem improves all the way to critical order `-1`, using a tent-space fixed point rather than vertical stochastic maximal regularity. This is a substantive restricted theorem but does not move `E_endpoint`.

# 4. B14 — classify the exact symmetry behind the reduction

The natural temptation is to hope that a clever nonconstant coordinate change removes general smooth transport noise while leaving the Navier–Stokes algebra unchanged. The next proposition shows why the constant case is genuinely special.

## Proposition 4.1 — exact componentwise Euclidean conjugation forces translations

Let `(phi_s)` be a `C^2` one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms of `R^3`, with `phi_0=id`. Assume that for every smooth scalar `f`,

\[
\Delta(f\circ\phi_s)=(\Delta f)\circ\phi_s,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and for every smooth divergence-free vector field `u`, the **componentwise pullback** `u\circ\phi_s` is divergence-free. Then

\[
\boxed{\phi_s(x)=x+a_s.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, the infinitesimal transport generator is a constant vector field.

### Proof

Take `f(y)=y_j`. Equation (4.1) gives

\[
\Delta\phi_s^j=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Take `f(y)=y_j^2`. By the chain rule and (4.3),

\[
2|\nabla\phi_s^j|^2
=
\Delta[(\phi_s^j)^2]
=
2,
\]

so every row of `D phi_s` has norm one. Taking `f(y)=y_jy_k`, `j!=k`, similarly gives

\[
\nabla\phi_s^j\cdot\nabla\phi_s^k=0.
\]

Thus

\[
D\phi_s(x)D\phi_s(x)^\top=I
\tag{4.4}
\]

at every point. Hence `phi_s` is a local Euclidean isometry; on connected `R^3` it is rigid:

\[
\phi_s(x)=R_sx+a_s,\qquad R_s\in O(3).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Now test the divergence condition on affine divergence-free fields `u(x)=Ax` with `tr A=0`. Then

\[
0=\operatorname{div}(u\circ\phi_s)
=\operatorname{tr}(A R_s)
\qquad\text{for every }A\text{ with }\operatorname{tr}A=0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The Frobenius orthogonal complement of the trace-zero matrices is the one-dimensional span of `I`, so `R_s=c_sI`. Orthogonality gives `c_s in {+1,-1}`. Continuity in `s` and `R_0=I` force `R_s=I`. This proves (4.2). `square`

The same identity-component conclusion holds on the flat torus.

This proposition is deliberately narrow: it classifies transformations that preserve the scalar Laplacian and divergence **under componentwise composition without changing coefficients**. It does not say general stochastic flows are useless. In fact, Agresti–Sauerbrey–Veraar (2025) transform smooth-transport SPDEs into random PDEs with nontrivial transformed diffusion and prove useful flow estimates; their paper explicitly identifies constant noise as the random-shift case and treats certain commuting vector fields. For Navier–Stokes, however, a nonconstant flow necessarily leaves the exact translation regime and introduces random geometry / Helmholtz / vector-pushforward issues.

**B14 outcome:** exact removal with the original Euclidean Navier–Stokes operators is maximal at constant transport. The arbitrary-coefficient problem needs a genuinely variable-coefficient critical theory, not a hidden extension of the translation trick.

# 5. B2 — attack the endpoint with a nonconstant high-frequency sideband

To test whether spatial variation in `b` causes an immediate *linear* supercritical instability, consider on `T^3` the single smooth nonconstant divergence-free transport field

\[
b(x)=\sigma\cos(x_2)e_1,
\qquad
0<\sigma^2<2\nu,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and linearize the source equation at zero:

\[
du=\nu\Delta u\,dt+\mathbb P[(b\cdot\nabla)u]\,dW_t.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Take the divergence-free mode

\[
u_{0,N}(x)=A\,e_3e^{iNx_1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since

\[
(b\cdot\nabla)u_{0,N}
=
\frac{i\sigma AN}{2}e_3
\left(
e^{i(Nx_1+x_2)}
+
e^{i(Nx_1-x_2)}
\right),
\tag{5.4}
\]

the first stochastic Picard correction has only the two sidebands

\[
k_\pm=(N,\pm1,0).
\]

Because `e_3` is perpendicular to both `k_+` and `k_-`, the Helmholtz projection acts as the identity on these sidebands.

For either sideband, Itô isometry gives the exact variance at time `t`:

\[
V_N(t)
=
\frac{\sigma^2A^2N^2}{4}
\int_0^t
e^{-2\nu(N^2+1)(t-s)}
e^{-2\nu N^2s}\,ds
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
V_N(t)
=
\frac{\sigma^2A^2N^2}{8\nu}
e^{-2\nu(N^2+1)t}
\left(e^{2\nu t}-1\right).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

At the critical parabolic time `t=N^{-2}` and unit input amplitude,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{N\to\infty}V_N(N^{-2})
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{4}e^{-2\nu}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

If the input is normalized with amplitude `A=N`, as appropriate for an order-`-1` Fourier scaling, then after dividing the output variance by the output frequency squared `N^2+1`,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\frac{V_N(N^{-2})}{N^2+1}
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{4}e^{-2\nu}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

So this explicit nonconstant transport does **not** create a first-iterate supercritical amplification: the sideband is critical, with a finite nonzero scaling limit. This does not prove well-posedness; it rules out one obvious obstruction mechanism.
