## 4. The all-distance-4 two-P3 class

Consider the corrected canonical class with parity split \(6+6\). Inside each parity class, exactly three active centers form a length-two path whose two close edges both have folded distance 4; the two endpoints have folded distance 6. The other three centers of that parity are isolated in the close graph, so every remaining same-parity distance is 6. Every cross-parity distance is 5.

For this class the independent third-Bonferroni coarse upper bounds on the two parity halves are
\[
(U_0,U_1)=(1026,1026).
\]

### 4.1 All 24 variable \((4,5,5)\) triples must be high

Take a distance-4 close edge in the even parity class and an odd center at distance 5 from both endpoints. Exact quotient enumeration shows that the corresponding triple intersection on the two parity halves is either
\[
(1,3)
\]
or
\[
(0,0).
\]
For an odd close edge and an even third center the high split is the swapped value \((3,1)\), again with low value \((0,0)\).

Let \(L_E\) be the number of low triples based on even close edges and \(L_O\) the number based on odd close edges. A cover would require both half-union upper bounds to remain at least 1024, so
\[
1026-L_E-3L_O\ge1024,
\]
\[
1026-3L_E-L_O\ge1024.
\]
The only nonnegative integer solution is
\[
L_E=L_O=0.
\]
Therefore **all 24 variable triples must be in the high orbit**.

### 4.2 Normalize one parity's path

By translation and coordinate permutation, normalize the even length-two path to centers
\[
0,\qquad A=\{0,1,2,3\},\qquad B=\{0,4,5,6\}.
\]
Then
\[
\delta(0,A)=\delta(0,B)=4,
\qquad
\delta(A,B)=6.
\]
This is the unique orbit of a distance-4/distance-4 path with endpoint distance 6: the two 4-set edge differences must intersect in exactly one coordinate.

An odd center must be at folded distance 5 from all three normalized even centers and must be in the high \((4,5,5)\) triple orbit for both close edges. Exact enumeration leaves precisely
\[
\boxed{135}
\]
possible odd centers.

### 4.3 Exhaust all possible odd six-center classes

Within those 135 candidates, the odd class must itself have exactly the same internal close graph: one length-two path with both close edges at distance 4, endpoint distance 6, and three additional vertices at distance 6 from every odd center except for those two path edges.

The exact search enumerates:

- 120,420 possible centered path triples inside the 135-candidate pool;
- 436,320 complete odd six-center configurations after adjoining the three required distance-6 isolates.

No heuristic pruning or floating-point computation is used.

### 4.4 No odd configuration can be completed by the three even isolates

Before seeing the odd class, there are exactly 90 possible even isolate centers at distance 6 from each of \(0,A,B\).

For each of the 436,320 exact odd six-center configurations, filter those 90 candidates by all remaining requirements:

1. folded distance 5 from every odd center;
2. high triple orbit for each of the two odd distance-4 close edges.

Across the entire exhaustive search, the largest resulting even-isolate candidate pool has size only
\[
\boxed{4}.
\]
The required three even isolates would have to form a distance-6 triangle in that pool. No such triangle occurs for any of the 436,320 odd configurations.

Therefore the all-distance-4 two-P3 canonical class is impossible.

This is a finite exact nonexistence certificate for that K=4 class.
