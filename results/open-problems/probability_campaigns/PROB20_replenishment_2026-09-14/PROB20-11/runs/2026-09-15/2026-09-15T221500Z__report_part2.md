## 2. Exact monotone deficit/excess criterion

Fix one parity half `H` of the folded cube. For a complete 12-center configuration with the prescribed pair distances, let

- `U_H` be the coarse third-Bonferroni value obtained by replacing every triple intersection by the exact maximum over the corresponding folded-distance/parity orbit;
- `d_H` be the sum of the deficits `max triple intersection - actual triple intersection`;
- `E_H` be the third-Bonferroni higher-multiplicity excess.

If `m(x)` is the number of radius-3 balls containing a quotient point `x`, then pointwise
\[
m-\binom m2+\binom m3=1+\binom{m-1}{3}\qquad(m\ge1).
\]
Therefore, on a parity half,
\[
B_3(H)=|\cup_i B_i\cap H|+E_H,
\qquad
E_H=\sum_{x:m(x)>0}\binom{m(x)-1}{3}\ge0.
\]
Also
\[
B_3(H)=U_H-d_H.
\]
If the balls cover the full parity half, whose size is 1024, then necessarily
\[
\boxed{d_H+E_H=U_H-1024.}
\]
Both `d_H` and `E_H` are monotone nondecreasing as centers are added. Hence for every partial center assignment of a genuine cover,
\[
d_H^{\rm partial}+E_H^{\rm partial}\le U_H-1024.
\]
This gives an exact finite pruning condition. No heuristic score, floating point tolerance, solver timeout, or failed optimization run enters the proof.

The computation maintains the two parity-half deficits exactly. It also maintains point multiplicities on the 2048 quotient points; when a new ball is added at a point of old multiplicity `m`, the increase in `E_H` is
\[
\binom{m}{3}-\binom{m-1}{3}=\binom{m-1}{2}.
\]
Thus every pruning decision is integer-exact.
