## 2. Cumulative certified bracket

### Upper bound \(M_{12}\le6\)

For a fixed row \(r\) and uniformly random signing \(\varepsilon\), \(r\cdot\varepsilon\) is a sum of twelve independent Rademachers.  Therefore
\[
\Pr(|r\cdot\varepsilon|\ge8)
=\frac{2\left[\binom{12}{0}+\binom{12}{1}+\binom{12}{2}\right]}{2^{12}}
=\frac{158}{4096}.
\]
For twelve rows,
\[
12\cdot158=1896<4096.
\]
Thus some signing satisfies \(|r_i\cdot\varepsilon|\le6\) for every row, proving \(M_{12}\le6\).

## 5. All three triangle patterns are impossible

### 5.1 Triangle 222: elementary low-triple contradiction

Normalize the three distance-2 edge differences to the three 2-subsets of three coordinates:
\[
\{1,2\},\qquad \{1,3\},\qquad \{2,3\}.
\]
For an opposite-parity center to be in the high \((2,5,5)\) orbit for a distance-2 edge, its canonical weight-5 representative must meet that 2-set in exactly one coordinate.

Writing the three membership bits as \(x_1,x_2,x_3\in\{0,1\}\), being high for all three edges would require
\[
x_1+x_2=x_1+x_3=x_2+x_3=1,
\]
which has no \(0/1\) solution.  Thus every one of the seven opposite-parity centers contributes at least one low \((2,5,5)\) triple.

The Bonferroni slack for triangle 222 allows at most three such lows (each loses \((4,6)\) after orientation), while seven are forced.  Contradiction.

### 5.2 Triangle 224: a pure incidence contradiction

Normalize the two distance-2 edge differences so that the high conditions force every opposite-parity center, represented as a weight-5 block, to choose:

- exactly one point from a 2-set \(A\);
- exactly one point from a disjoint 2-set \(D\);
- exactly three points from the remaining 8-set \(R\).

The seven opposite-parity centers are mutually non-close, so their weight-5 representatives must be pairwise at Hamming distance 6, equivalently every two blocks intersect in exactly two points.  Hence the total pair intersection count must equal
\[
2\binom72=42.
\]
But the two 2-column groups each carry seven incidences, and convexity gives at least
\[
\binom42+\binom32=9
\]
per 2-group?  Equivalently, summing \(\binom{m_j}{2}\) over each 2-group gives the sharp minimum 15 across the seven rows; the two groups contribute at least \(15+15\).  The 8-column group carries 21 incidences and contributes at least 18.  Therefore the total pair intersection count is at least
\[
15+15+18=48>42,
\]
a contradiction.

### 5.3 Triangle 244: exact clique certificate

Here the Bonferroni slack forces every one of the 21 variable triples to be high.  Normalize the distance-2 edge difference as \(A=\{0,1\}\) and a distance-4 difference as \(B=\{0,2,3,4\}\).  The high conditions leave exactly 168 weight-5 candidates for each opposite-parity center.

Two such centers must be at folded distance 6, which for these weight-5 representatives means intersection size exactly 2.  Exact clique enumeration on the 168-vertex compatibility graph gives clique number
\[
\omega=4<7.
\]
So seven opposite-parity centers cannot exist.

## 10. New pair-overlap corollary

Let \(a\) and \(12-a\) be the two center parity counts.  If every pair were at the far parity-compatible distance, the pair-overlap sum would be
\[
S_2^{\rm far}
=40\left[\binom a2+\binom{12-a}{2}\right]+20a(12-a)
=2640-20a(12-a)
\ge1920,
\]
with equality only at the balanced \(6+6\) split.

A close same-parity pair raises its intersection contribution by at least
\[
J_4-J_6=62-40=22,
\]
and a close opposite-parity pair raises it by at least
\[
J_3-J_5=62-20=42.
\]
Since a cover needs at least four close pairs,
\[
\boxed{S_2\ge1920+4\cdot22=2008.}
\]
If the parity split is not \(6+6\), the baseline itself is at least 1940, hence \(S_2\ge2028\).

For an actual cover let \(f(x)\ge1\) be the number of balls covering quotient point \(x\).  Since
\[
\sum_x f(x)=12\cdot299=3588,
\qquad
\sum_x(f(x)-1)=3588-2048=1540,
\]
and
\[
S_2=\sum_x\binom{f(x)}2,
\]
we obtain
\[
\sum_x\binom{f(x)-1}{2}
=S_2-1540
\ge\boxed{468}.
\]
Thus any hypothetical cover must concentrate at least 468 units of second-order excess multiplicity.  The previous lower bound from the two-distance/Delsarte stage was 380, so the close-pair theorem strengthens the required overlap concentration substantially.
