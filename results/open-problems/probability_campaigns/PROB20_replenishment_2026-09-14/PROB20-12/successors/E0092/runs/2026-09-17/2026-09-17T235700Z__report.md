## 2. B3 — prove a stronger block-scale theorem

The old memo chose specifically `b(n)=ceil(log n log log n)`. The source does not care about construction time, and the proof mechanism is more transparent in the following stronger form.

### Theorem A (general deterministic block-scale family)

Let `(b_n)` be any deterministic integer sequence satisfying

1. `b_n -> infinity`,
2. `b_n / log n -> infinity`,
3. `b_n^2 / n -> 0`.

For every block dimension `b`, let `G_b` be the lexicographically first `b x 2b` binary Toeplitz matrix satisfying the exact shell-collision inequalities in Section 3 below. For all sufficiently large `n`, partition the `n` coordinates into blocks of sizes `b_n` and `b_n+1`, and in each block place the `2b_j` columns of `G_{b_j}`, padded by zero outside that block. Let `S_n` be the resulting ordered list of exactly `2n` vectors in `F_2^n`. Patch the finitely many remaining `n` by any fixed deterministic spanning aperiodic `2n`-list.

Then `S_n` is independent of epsilon and has the following property: for every fixed `epsilon>0`, for all sufficiently large `n`, the walk which at each step adds a uniformly chosen element of `S_n` is ergodic and, for every integer

`k > (1+epsilon) n t_*`,

its total-variation distance from uniform on `F_2^n` is less than `epsilon`.

Thus Theorem A is exactly the admitted mathematical target, and it is stronger than the old memo in allowing any block scale satisfying the three displayed asymptotic conditions.

The rest of Sections 2–5 proves Theorem A.

### 2.1 Projection to the cube and shell collision

Let `M` be a `d x N` binary generator matrix and let `Z_K` be nearest-neighbor walk on `F_2^N` from zero, obtained by flipping one uniformly chosen coordinate each step. The generated walk is pathwise

`X_K = M Z_K`.

Conditional on `W_K := wt(Z_K)=w`, permutation symmetry makes `Z_K` uniform on the Hamming sphere `S_w`. For independent `z,z'` uniform on `S_w`, define

`Q_w(M) := 2^d P(Mz=Mz') - 1`.

If `f_w` is the law of `Mz`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

`TV(f_w,U_d) <= (1/2) sqrt(Q_w(M))`.                                        (2.1)

This exact finite inequality is the part already certified in `U_E0092.lean`. By convexity, for every weight window `I`,

`TV(L(X_K),U_d) <= P(W_K notin I) + (1/2) max_{w in I} sqrt(Q_w(M)).`        (2.2)

No ergodicity assumption is used in (2.1) or (2.2).

### 2.2 Good Toeplitz blocks exist simultaneously on every shell

Fix block dimension `b`, put `N=2b`, and for a seed `s in F_2^{3b-1}` define the Toeplitz matrix

`(M_s)_{r,c} = s_{c-r+b-1}`.

For every nonzero `y in F_2^{2b}`, the map `s -> M_s y` is onto `F_2^b`: if `c_0` is the least coordinate with `y_{c_0}=1`, the seed coordinates indexed by `c_0-r+b-1` form a unitriangular `b x b` minor. Therefore, for uniform seed,

`P_s(M_s y=0)=2^{-b}`.                                                     (2.3)

For a fixed shell `w`, (2.3) gives

`E_s Q_w(M_s) = (2^b-1)/C(2b,w)`.                                         (2.4)

Set

`eta_{b,w} := 2(2b+1) 2^b / C(2b,w)`.

Since `Q_w>=0`, Markov and (2.4) imply

`P_s(Q_w > eta_{b,w}) < 1/[2(2b+1)]`.

There are `2b+1` shells. A union bound is strictly below `1/2`. Hence more than half of all Toeplitz seeds satisfy

`Q_w(M_s) <= eta_{b,w}` for every `w=0,...,2b`.                             (2.5)

Call such a matrix good.

### 2.3 One good block mixes exponentially just above Wilson's threshold

Fix `epsilon in (0,1]` and set

`theta = epsilon/2`,

`alpha_theta = [1-(1-2alpha)^(1+theta)]/2`,

`delta = (alpha_theta-alpha)/2 > 0`.                                       (2.6)

For the `2b`-cube weight process after `K` steps,

`E W_K = b[1-(1-1/b)^K]`.                                                  (2.7)

Total variation contracts under additional convolution, so to bound every

`K >= (1+theta)b t_*`

it suffices to bound `K_0=ceil((1+theta)b t_*)`. Since `alpha<1/4` (because `H_2(1/4)>1/2`) we have `t_*<log 2`; hence `K_0<=2b` for all large `b`.

Using `(1-1/b)^K <= exp(-K/b)` and the definition of `t_*`,

`E W_{K_0} >= 2b alpha_theta`, while always `E W_{K_0} <= b`.

Let

`w_0 = ceil(2b(alpha+delta))`.

Then, for large `b`, both distances from the mean to the two excluded tails are at least `b delta`: indeed

`E W_{K_0}-w_0 >= 2b delta-1 >= b delta`,

and

`(2b-w_0)-E W_{K_0} >= 2b(1/2-alpha-delta)-1 >= 2b delta-1 >= b delta`,

because `2delta=alpha_theta-alpha <= 1/2-alpha`.

Changing one of the `K_0` chosen cube coordinates changes the final weight by at most two. McDiarmid therefore gives each tail probability at most

`exp[-(b delta)^2/(2K_0)] <= exp(-b delta^2/4)`,                            (2.8)

using `K_0<=2b`. Thus the total discarded weight probability is at most `2 exp(-b delta^2/4)`.

It remains to control shell collisions in the central window. Let

`m = (alpha+1/2)/2 < 1/2`,

`c_0 = H_2'(m) = log_2((1-m)/m) > 0`.                                     (2.9)

Because `alpha+delta=(alpha+alpha_theta)/2 <= m` and `H_2'` decreases on `(0,1/2)`, the mean value theorem gives the fully analytic bound

`H_2(alpha+delta)-H_2(alpha) >= c_0 delta`.                                (2.10)

Since `H_2(alpha)=1/2`, every `w` in `[w_0,2b-w_0]` has

`C(2b,w) >= 2^{2b H_2(w/(2b))}/(2b+1)
          >= 2^{2b(1/2+c_0 delta)}/(2b+1)`.                                (2.11)

For a good block, (2.5) and (2.11) yield

`Q_w <= 2(2b+1)^2 2^{-2c_0 delta b}`.                                      (2.12)

Combining (2.2), (2.8), and (2.12), for every sufficiently large `b` and every

`K >= (1+theta)b t_*`,

`d_b(K) := TV(L(M_s Z_K),U_b)
 <= 2 exp(-b delta^2/4) + (2b+1) 2^{-c_0 delta b}`.                         (2.13)

This removes the old memo's numerical entropy-chord constant `2.4`; only positivity of the explicit analytic constant `c_0` is needed.

### 2.4 Product assembly preserves Wilson's constant

For large `n`, put `b=b_n`, `L=floor(n/b)`, and `r=n-Lb`. Since `b^2/n ->0`, eventually `L>=b>r`. Therefore `n` can be partitioned into exactly `L` blocks: `r` blocks of size `b+1` and `L-r` blocks of size `b`.

The total number of generator slots is exactly

`sum_j 2b_j = 2n`.

Run the global walk for an integer `k>(1+epsilon)n t_*`. Let `K_j` be the number of its first `k` generator choices that land in block `j`. Marginally

`K_j ~ Bin(k,b_j/n)`

with mean

`mu_j > (1+epsilon)t_* b_j`.

Put the local threshold at `(1+epsilon/2)t_*b_j`. The relative lower-tail gap is at least

`gamma = 1-(1+epsilon/2)/(1+epsilon) = epsilon/[2(1+epsilon)] >= epsilon/4`  (2.14)

for `epsilon<=1`. Chernoff therefore gives

`P(K_j < (1+epsilon/2)t_*b_j)
 <= exp(-epsilon^2 t_* b_j/32)`.                                           (2.15)

Conditional on the vector `(K_j)`, the endpoint laws of the blocks are independent: after the block labels are fixed, the selected generator indices within each block are independent and uniform. The uniform law on `F_2^n` is the product of the block uniforms. Hence the standard product-TV inequality and (2.13) give

`d_n(k) <= sum_j [
    exp(-epsilon^2 t_* b_j/32)
  + 2 exp(-delta^2 b_j/4)
  + (2b_j+1) 2^{-c_0 delta b_j}
]`.                                                                         (2.16)

There are at most `n/b` blocks. For every fixed `epsilon>0`, all exponential rates in (2.16) are fixed positive constants. Since `b_n/log n -> infinity`,

`(n/b_n) exp(-c b_n) ->0`

and likewise

`(n/b_n)(2b_n+3) exp(-c b_n) ->0`

for every fixed `c>0`. Therefore the right-hand side of (2.16) tends to zero. In particular, for every fixed `epsilon in (0,1]`, for all sufficiently large `n`, it is less than `epsilon`, uniformly for every integer `k>(1+epsilon)n t_*`.

For `epsilon>1`, the desired inequality is automatic from `TV<=1<epsilon` once eventual ergodicity has been established.

### 2.5 Ergodicity follows from the same estimate; it is not an extra assumption

Take one fixed value, say `epsilon=1/2`. Equation (2.16) gives, for all sufficiently large `n`, a finite time at which the global walk is at TV distance strictly below `1/2` from uniform.

If the generators did not span `F_2^n`, every time law would be supported on a proper subgroup of size at most `2^{n-1}`, forcing TV distance at least `1/2`.

If the walk were irreducible but periodic, its period would be two: a two-step return always exists by choosing the same nonzero generator twice, and periodicity means every relation among generators has even length. Word-length parity then defines a nonzero linear functional `ell:F_2^n -> F_2` with `ell(V_i)=1` for every generator. At each fixed time the walk is therefore supported on one affine half-space `ell(x)=k mod 2`, again forcing TV distance at least `1/2`.

Both alternatives contradict the bound. Hence the constructed global walk is irreducible and aperiodic for all sufficiently large `n`.

This completes the mathematical proof of Theorem A.

## 3. B19 — make the family a literal deterministic finite algorithm

The previous section used “the lexicographically first good Toeplitz block.” Here is a complete exact definition, with no floating-point tests and no epsilon hidden in the object.

### 3.1 Integer block scale

Define

`a_n = ceil(log_2(n+2))`,

`c_n = ceil(log_2(a_n+2))`,

`b_n = max(2,a_n c_n)`.

This sequence is computed entirely from integers (equivalently, each ceiling of a binary logarithm is a bit length), and it satisfies

`b_n -> infinity`, `b_n/log n -> infinity`, and `b_n^2/n ->0`.

### 3.2 Exact good-block search

For each block dimension `b`, enumerate the `2^{3b-1}` seeds in lexicographic binary order. For a candidate seed and each `w=0,...,2b`, compute the exact integer fibre counts

`c_{w,x} = #{z in F_2^{2b}: wt(z)=w, M_s z=x}`.

Since

`Q_w = 2^b [sum_x c_{w,x}^2]/C(2b,w)^2 -1`,

the good-block condition `Q_w<=eta_{b,w}` is exactly

`2^b sum_x c_{w,x}^2 - C(2b,w)^2
 <= 2(2b+1)2^b C(2b,w)`.                                                   (3.1)

All quantities in (3.1) are integers. Accept the first seed satisfying (3.1) simultaneously for all `w`. Section 2.2 proves that more than half the seeds pass, so the search always terminates.

### 3.3 Exact global family

Given `n`, compute `b=b_n`, `L=floor(n/b)`, and `r=n-Lb`.

- If `L>=b` (hence `r<b<=L`), partition the coordinates into `r` blocks of size `b+1` and `L-r` blocks of size `b`. For each size use the lexicographically first block from Section 3.2, and embed its columns by zero-padding outside that coordinate block.
- Otherwise use the deterministic fallback list `(e_1,...,e_n,0,...,0)` with exactly `n` zero vectors appended, giving exactly `2n` generators. This fallback is spanning and aperiodic and affects only finitely many `n` relevant to the asymptotic theorem.

This is one total deterministic algorithm `n -> S_n`. There is no epsilon input and no probabilistic choice after the existence proof. Its running time is finite; with the displayed block scale it is `n^{O(log log n)}` up to polynomial/logarithmic factors, but the source question does not impose a complexity bound.

Thus the construction is “explicit” in the admitted source-matching sense: every vector is determined by a finite, exact, deterministic computation. A demand for a closed coordinate formula would be an additional requirement not present in Diaconis's wording.

## 4. B22 — read the nearby successes and failures rather than assuming they close the question

The closest modern vocabulary is code smoothing. The map `M:F_2^{2b}->F_2^b` pushes spherical noise toward uniformity, and `ker M` is the dependency code. This explains both the strength and the limitation of prior coding-theory approaches.

1. Debris-Alazard--Ducas--Resch--Tillich study smoothing codes/lattices and distinguish average/random-code behavior from worst-case guarantees. The failed route, therefore, is not the mathematical construction but an unnecessarily strengthened interpretation of “explicit”: requiring a uniform coordinate formula forces a harder coding-theory problem that Diaconis did not state. ## 5. Adversarial self-checks of the new analytic proof

This section records the places most likely to hide a gap.

### 5.1 Does the central entropy bound cover the whole window?

Yes. The window is symmetric around `1/2`, entropy is symmetric and increasing on `[0,1/2]`, so its minimum is at relative weight approximately `alpha+delta`. Because `alpha+delta <= (alpha+1/2)/2=m`, monotonicity of `H_2'` gives (2.10) without any numerical slope estimate.

### 5.2 Is the cube-weight concentration being applied at a time larger than the number of cube coordinates?

No. The contraction step reduces to `K_0=ceil((1+theta)b t_*)`, with `theta<=1/2` for `epsilon<=1`. Since `t_*<log 2`, `K_0<1.5(log 2)b+1<2b` for large `b`. This is exactly what is needed in (2.8).

### 5.3 Can conditioning only on block counts destroy independence?

No. Each global step may be sampled by first choosing the block label with probability `b_j/n` and then, conditionally on that label, choosing one of that block's `2b_j` generators uniformly. Given all label counts, the within-block generator draws remain independent across blocks. The endpoints are therefore a product conditional on the count vector.

### 5.4 Does the partition into `b` and `b+1` blocks always exist?

Eventually yes. Writing `n=Lb+r`, `0<=r<b`, needs only `r<=L`. Since `b^2/n->0`, eventually `L=floor(n/b)>=b>r`.

### 5.5 Did the proof assume ergodicity in order to prove ergodicity?

No. Projection, collision, entropy, McDiarmid, Chernoff, and product-TV bounds are valid for any generator multiset. They yield a time with TV `<1/2`; only afterward is the subgroup/period obstruction used to deduce ergodicity.

### 5.6 Is the family chosen before epsilon?

Yes. Sections 3.1–3.3 contain no epsilon. Epsilon enters only the proof through fixed positive constants `theta`, `delta`, and the exponential rates. Since `b_n/log n ->infinity`, the same preselected family works eventually for each fixed epsilon.

No counterexample to Theorem A was found in this run.
