# 2. B11 — DUALIZE

The useful dualization this run is a **blocking reduction from a binary 3-block factor to an ordinary 2-block factor**, followed by Matus's published two-block-factor inequality.

Assume for the moment that the degree-two process has a 3-block representation

\[
X_i=h(Y_i,Y_{i+1},Y_{i+2}),
\]

where the `Y_i` are i.i.d.

## 2.1 Pair blocking: four observed sites become a four-state two-block-factor problem

Define i.i.d. latent pairs

\[
W_i=(Y_{2i},Y_{2i+1})
\]

and observed pairs

\[
Z_i=(X_{2i},X_{2i+1})\in\{0,1\}^2.
\]

Then

\[
Z_i=F(W_i,W_{i+1}),
\]

with

\[
F\big((u_0,u_1),(v_0,v_1)\big)
 =\big(h(u_0,u_1,v_0),\;h(u_1,v_0,v_1)\big).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus **every binary 3-block factor yields a four-state 2-block factor after pairing consecutive outputs**. This is exact and loses no information from the four-site law: the law of `(Z_0,Z_1)` is precisely the four-site cylinder table of `(X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3)`.

This puts the requested four-site obstruction squarely inside Matus's two-block-factor language. However, Matus's explicit published inequality is tailored to **five distinct output states**. Simply adjoining a fifth state of probability zero makes its right-hand side collapse, and identifying two of its labels is not justified by the proof's disjoint-case decomposition. Therefore that theorem does **not** immediately produce a nontrivial inequality involving only the four-state pair alphabet.

There is also a structural reason the first naive Cauchy split stalls. For `u,v` latent middle labels define

\[
L_{ab}(u,v)
 =\Pr\big(h(Y_1,Y_2,u)=a,\ h(Y_2,u,v)=b\mid u,v\big)
\]

and

\[
R_{cd}(u,v)
 =\Pr\big(h(u,v,Y_5)=c,\ h(v,Y_5,Y_6)=d\mid u,v\big).
\]

Then the four-site cylinder is

\[
p_{abcd}=\mathbb E\big[L_{ab}(Y_3,Y_4)R_{cd}(Y_3,Y_4)\big].
\tag{2.2}
\]

A direct Cauchy--Schwarz inequality introduces `E[L_{ab}^2]` and `E[R_{cd}^2]`. Those are off-path six-vertex orbit probabilities and are not determined by the four-site table. So plain root-pair Cauchy does not eliminate to an observable four-site inequality; one really needs either a new four-state Matus-style elimination or additional off-path orbit variables.

## 2.2 Triple blocking gives a concrete published inequality on six observed sites

There is nevertheless an exact certificate template available immediately. Define

\[
\widetilde W_i=(Y_{3i},Y_{3i+1},Y_{3i+2}),\qquad
\widetilde Z_i=(X_{3i},X_{3i+1},X_{3i+2})\in\{0,1\}^3.
\]

Again `\widetilde Z_i` is a 2-block factor of the i.i.d. sequence `(\widetilde W_i)`. Hence, for **every** surjective map

\[
\phi:\{0,1\}^3\longrightarrow\{1,2,3,4,5\},
\]

the five-state process

\[
V_i=\phi(\widetilde Z_i)
\]

is a two-block factor. Matus's theorem therefore applies. With

\[
[s]=\Pr(V_0=s),\qquad [st]=\Pr(V_0=s,V_1=t),
\]

one must have

\[
\boxed{
[13]+\sqrt{[24]}+\sqrt{[32]}+[41]+[51]+[52]+[55]
\ge \frac14[5]^2[11][12][23].
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Every quantity in (2.3) is a sum of exact **three-site or six-site cylinder probabilities of `X`**. For a degree-two determinantal process those cylinder probabilities are exact finite Toeplitz-determinant/inclusion--exclusion expressions in the Fourier coefficients. Consequently (2.3), over all five-state coarsenings `phi`, is a genuine finite source-valid obstruction family.

This is not a newly claimed theorem: (2.3) is Matus's published inequality, and the blocking/coarsening step is elementary. The advance is that it gives C0035 a concrete analytic dual certificate family without guessing a finite latent alphabet for `h`.

## Proposition — universal degree-two three-run lower bound

Let

\[
f(t)=p+a e^{2\pi i t}+\bar a e^{-2\pi i t}
+b e^{4\pi i t}+\bar b e^{-4\pi i t},
\qquad 0\le f\le1,
\]

and put `A=|a|^2`, `B=|b|^2`, `v=p(1-p)`. Then for the stationary determinantal process `P^f`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(X_0=X_1=X_2)\ge\frac1{16}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

From the previous run, the covariance spectral polynomial is

\[
S(\theta)=v-2A\cos\theta-2B\cos(2\theta)\ge0.
\]

At `theta=0`,

\[
A+B\le\frac v2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now define the centered bounded trigonometric polynomial

\[
g(t):=2f(t)-1.
\]

Since `0<=f<=1`, we have `|g(t)|<=1` for every `t`. Shifting by one half period changes the sign of the first harmonic and leaves the second harmonic fixed, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
g(t)-g(t+1/2)
&=4a e^{2\pi i t}+4\bar a e^{-2\pi i t}\\
&=8\operatorname{Re}(a e^{2\pi i t}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The left side has absolute value at most `2`. Choosing `t` so that `a e^{2\pi i t}` is real and nonnegative gives

\[
8|a|\le2,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
A\le\frac1{16}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The exact identity from the previous run is

\[
R_3:=\Pr(X_0=X_1=X_2)
=1-3v-2A-B.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Using (4.2) and (4.4),

\[
2A+B=A+(A+B)
\le\frac1{16}+\frac v2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
R_3
\ge 1-3v-\frac1{16}-\frac v2
=\frac{15}{16}-\frac72v.
\]

Finally `v=p(1-p)<=1/4`, so

\[
R_3\ge\frac{15}{16}-\frac78=\frac1{16}.
\]

This proves (4.1). `\square`

At `p=1/2` the same proof says

\[
2A+B\le\frac3{16},
\qquad
R_3=\frac14-2A-B\ge\frac1{16}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The previous report's Alon--Feldheim necessary lower bound for a binary 3-block factor was only `R_3>2^{-45}`. Since

\[
\frac1{16}=2^{-4}\gg2^{-45},
\]

**no degree-two determinantal process can refute Question 9.5 through the three-site constant-run inequality.** The exact extremal value of `R_3` may be larger than `1/16`, but that optimization is no longer useful for this obstruction branch.
