# 2. B2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

I started at `m=2`. Write

\[
f(t)=p+a e^{2\pi i t}+\bar a e^{-2\pi i t}
       +b e^{4\pi i t}+\bar b e^{-4\pi i t},
\qquad 0\le f\le 1,
\]

and set

\[
A:=|a|^2,\qquad B:=|b|^2,\qquad v:=p(1-p).
\]

Let `(X_j)` have law `P^f`. For a stationary determinantal process,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_r)=-|\widehat f(r)|^2
\]

for `r != 0`. Hence for degree two,

\[
\gamma_0=v,\qquad \gamma_1=-A,\qquad \gamma_2=-B,\qquad
\gamma_r=0\quad(|r|>2).
\]

Because this is a covariance sequence, its spectral polynomial

\[
S(\theta)=v-2A\cos\theta-2B\cos(2\theta)
\]

is nonnegative. Evaluating at `theta=0` gives the exact necessary inequality

\[
\boxed{v\ge 2(A+B).}\tag{2.1}
\]

This simple inequality already kills the most obvious low-window counterexample attack.

## 2.1 Exact two-site constant-run probability

By the determinantal definition,

\[
\Pr(X_0=X_1=1)=p^2-A.
\]

The complement process is determinantal with symbol `1-f`, so

\[
\Pr(X_0=X_1=0)=(1-p)^2-A.
\]

Therefore

\[
R_2(f):=\Pr(X_0=X_1)
 =p^2+(1-p)^2-2A
 =1-2v-2A.
\]

Using (2.1), `2A <= v-2B`, hence

\[
R_2(f)\ge 1-3v+2B\ge 1-3/4=\boxed{1/4}.\tag{2.2}
\]

This is a complete exact calculation, not a numerical observation.

Alon--Feldheim's universal run theorem, specialized to binary `k=3` block factors and run length `ell=2`, gives only a very small universal lower bound. Their displayed construction can take `M=2^{ell^2}=16`, yielding

\[
\Pr(Z_1=Z_2)>M^{-(k+\ell-1)}=16^{-4}=2^{-16}.
\]

Thus **no degree-two determinantal counterexample can possibly be detected by that two-site constant-run inequality**: every candidate has `R_2 >= 1/4`, far inside the necessary block-factor region.

This is useful negative information: the first obstruction must use more structure than the adjacent equal/not-equal statistic.

## 2.2 Three-site constant-run identity: first live run obstruction

For three consecutive ones the Toeplitz determinant is

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_3(f)
&=\det\begin{pmatrix}
 p&\bar a&\bar b\\
 a&p&\bar a\\
 b&a&p
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=p^3-p(2A+B)+2\Re(a^2\bar b).
\end{aligned}
\]

For `1-f`, the off-diagonal Fourier coefficients are `-a,-b`, so

\[
D_3(1-f)
=(1-p)^3-(1-p)(2A+B)-2\Re(a^2\bar b).
\]

The phase-sensitive cubic term cancels in the probability of a constant binary triple. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
R_3(f):=\Pr(X_0=X_1=X_2)
=1-3v-2A-B.
}\tag{2.3}
\]

Equation (2.1) gives only

\[
2A+B\le 2(A+B)\le v,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{R_3(f)\ge 1-4v=(2p-1)^2.}\tag{2.4}
\]

The bound becomes zero at `p=1/2`, so unlike the two-site calculation it does **not** settle the run obstruction.

For a binary 3-block factor, the explicit Alon--Feldheim construction at `ell=3` has `M=2^{3^2}=512`, hence requires

\[
\Pr(Z_1=Z_2=Z_3)>512^{-5}=2^{-45}.\tag{2.5}
\]

This leaves a sharply finite, source-valid refutation route:

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\substack{0\le f\le1\\ \deg f=2}}
\big(D_3(f)+D_3(1-f)\big)
\stackrel{?}{<}2^{-45}.
}\tag{2.6}
\]

If an exact degree-two symbol satisfies the strict inequality in (2.6), Question 9.5 is refuted immediately. I do **not** claim such a symbol exists. Conversely, proving a strong lower bound for (2.6) only eliminates this one obstruction and would not prove block-factor realizability.

I did not create a numerical checker for (2.6) in this run. That would reverse the owner's research/validation ratio before there is a theorem-level finite claim to certify.

# 3. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

The useful language is not a generic hidden Markov model. It is Matus's **jointly exchangeable, dissociated array** language, generalized from edges to ordered triples.

## 3.1 Forward 3-array encoding

Suppose a binary process is a 3-block factor:

\[
X_i=h(Y_i,Y_{i+1},Y_{i+2}),
\]

where the `Y_i` are i.i.d. nonatomic labels. For every ordered triple of distinct indices define

\[
\zeta_{ijk}:=h(Y_i,Y_j,Y_k).
\]

Then:

1. `(\zeta_{ijk})` is jointly exchangeable under simultaneous finite permutations of the vertex labels;
2. subarrays supported on disjoint vertex sets are independent (dissociation);
3. the tight superdiagonal is exactly the original process:
   \[
   X_i=\zeta_{i,i+1,i+2}.
   \]

Thus every 3-block factor has a **3-uniform directed hyperarray extension** satisfying exchangeability plus dissociation.

This is not being claimed as new. Matus proves the analogous `m=1` statement and explicitly states that the `m>1` generalization is straightforward. The point here is to use the known characterization as the correct obstruction engine for C0035.

## 3.2 Why this is stronger than the naive factor graph

A direct hidden-variable formula for an `n`-cylinder is

\[
\Pr(X_1=x_1,\ldots,X_n=x_n)
=\int \prod_{i=1}^n
  \mathbf 1\{h(y_i,y_{i+1},y_{i+2})=x_i\}
  \prod_{j=1}^{n+2}d\mu(y_j).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equation (3.1) by itself is hard to eliminate because `h` and `mu` are infinite-dimensional. The exchangeable-array formulation adds **counterfactual off-path variables** such as `zeta_124`, `zeta_153`, etc. Joint exchangeability forces probabilities of isomorphic overlap diagrams to agree, while dissociation factors diagrams on disjoint vertex sets. Matus's 1996 proof for 2-block factors obtains nontrivial superdiagonal inequalities exactly by introducing these off-path variables and then eliminating them with Cauchy--Schwarz and dissociation.

That is the structural mechanism the old C0035 intake did not use.

## 3.3 Finite exact inflation hierarchy for `m=2`

For a candidate degree-two symbol `f`, fix `N`. Consider all ordered triples of distinct vertices in `[N]`. Introduce probabilities for finite colored directed 3-uniform hypergraphs:

\[
q(H)=\Pr(\zeta_e=c_e\text{ for every colored ordered triple }e\in E(H)).
\]

Any 3-block-factor representation must admit such numbers obeying:

* **positivity:** `q(H)>=0`;
* **normalization / marginalization:** summing over the color of an added hyperedge returns the smaller-diagram probability;
* **joint exchangeability:** `q(H)=q(pi H)` for every vertex permutation `pi`;
* **dissociation:** if `H_1,H_2` have disjoint vertex sets,
  \[
  q(H_1\sqcup H_2)=q(H_1)q(H_2);
  \tag{3.2}
  \]
* **tight-path data:** whenever the colored edges are
  `(1,2,3),(2,3,4),...`, `q(H)` must equal the corresponding exact determinantal cylinder probability of `P^f`. Those cylinder probabilities are finite sums of Toeplitz minors by inclusion--exclusion, hence algebraic in `p,a,b,bar a,bar b`.

This produces a **finite polynomial feasibility problem** at each `N`. It is not an LP because dissociation contributes products such as (3.2). A Positivstellensatz/SOS or hand-derived Cauchy--Schwarz certificate of infeasibility at any finite `N` would be an exact source-valid counterexample.

Conversely, Matus's characterization indicates why this hierarchy is not arbitrary: a projectively consistent jointly exchangeable dissociated hyperarray extension is precisely the right object behind a block-factor representation. I am treating the general characterization as prior-art machinery, not claiming it as a theorem newly proved here.

# 4. B30 — ONE SCALE AT A TIME

The goal was to find the first finite scale at which a block-factor obstruction can contain information not already encoded by ordinary 2-dependence.

## 4.1 Why the obvious Finner/dependency-hypergraph inequalities are a dead end

For a 3-block factor, each event depending only on `X_i` is a function of the latent triple

\[
\{Y_i,Y_{i+1},Y_{i+2}\}.
\]

One might apply Finner/Hölder inequalities to the interval hypergraph of these latent triples. This does not create the missing obstruction.

The incidence matrix of consecutive length-three intervals on a line has the consecutive-ones property and is totally unimodular. Hence the fractional-matching polytope

\[
\left\{(w_i): w_i\ge0,\ 
\sum_{i:j\in\{i,i+1,i+2\}}w_i\le1\ \forall j\right\}
\]

has integral extreme points. Therefore the strongest inequalities obtained by this bare fractional-matching/Finner mechanism reduce to choosing mutually vertex-disjoint output windows. Those are precisely outputs separated by at least three sites, whose independence is already part of **2-dependence**.

So a successful proof/refutation cannot come merely from the sliding-window dependency hypergraph. It must use the fact that **the same measurable function `h` is reused at every window**, which is exactly what the exchangeable-array counterfactuals retain.

## 4.2 First scale at which dissociation itself becomes visible

For the ordered-triple array, two coordinates can have disjoint vertex supports only once there are at least six vertices. Thus:

* `N<=5`: the defining **dissociation** relation cannot yet compare two nonempty triple-supported subarrays on disjoint vertex sets;
* `N=6`: for the first time one has, for example,
  \[
  \zeta_{123}\perp\!\!\!\perp\zeta_{456},
  \]
  together with many exchangeable off-path triples sharing one or two vertices with the tight path.

The tight path on six vertices is

\[
(123),(234),(345),(456),
\]

so its observable part is exactly a **four-site cylinder** `(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)`.

The direct independence of the endpoints `X_1` and `X_4` is already implied by 2-dependence; the new information comes from combining that disjointness with **off-path** exchangeable triples and then eliminating them, in the same spirit as Matus's array inequalities.

Therefore the first finite inflation scale worth attacking is:

\[
\boxed{N=6\text{ vertices / four observed consecutive outputs}.}\tag{4.1}
\]

I am **not** claiming that no exchangeability-only inequality can exist at `N=4` or `N=5`. The precise proved statement is narrower and sufficient for research planning: `N=6` is the first scale where the characterization's dissociation ingredient can enter at all. Hence brute-force work below that scale cannot test the full exchangeable+dissociated condition.

## 4.3 Exact next finite problem

For `m=2`, the next useful object is not an unconstrained SAT encoding of a measurable map. It is the orbit-reduced `N=6` hyperarray feasibility problem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does the four-site DPP cylinder law extend to a positive,
 jointly exchangeable, dissociated binary ordered-3-array law on six vertices?}
}\tag{4.2}
\]

The variables should be indexed by isomorphism classes of small colored directed 3-uniform hypergraphs, not by all `2^{120}` assignments of the 120 ordered triples. The constraints are marginalization, exchangeability, positivity, and the dissociation products. A dual Cauchy--Schwarz/SOS certificate is preferable to a large floating-point feasibility result because only an exact certificate can support a source-level refutation.
