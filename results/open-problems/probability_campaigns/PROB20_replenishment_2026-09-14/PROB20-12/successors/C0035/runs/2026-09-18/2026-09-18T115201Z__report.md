## 2. B29 — ADD THE BOUNDARY: the 3-block-factor class is closed

This run first resolves the compactness question that had been left implicit.

### 2.1 Source-backed characterization to be used

Matúš (1996), *On two-block-factor sequences and one-dependence*, proves for `m=1` that a sequence is a two-block factor iff it is the superdiagonal of a jointly exchangeable, dissociated array. In the introduction he explicitly says that the same characterization of `(m+1)`-block factors by jointly exchangeable, dissociated arrays has a **straightforward generalization for `m>1`** and restricts the printed proof to `m=1` only for simplicity.

For `m=2`, use arrays indexed by ordered triples of distinct positive integers,

\[
\zeta=(\zeta_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k\ \mathrm{distinct}},\qquad \zeta_{i,j,k}\in S,
\]

and the tight superdiagonal map

\[
\lambda_3(\zeta):=(\zeta_{i,i+1,i+2})_{i\ge1}.
\]

A law on such arrays is **jointly exchangeable** if simultaneous finite permutations of the vertex labels leave it invariant, and **dissociated** if the subarrays induced by disjoint vertex sets are independent.

For completeness, the reverse direction of the characterization can also be seen from the standard higher-order Hoover/Aldous representation: a dissociated jointly exchangeable triple array admits a representation using independent latent variables indexed by nonempty subsets of `{i,j,k}`. Along the tight triples `(i,i+1,i+2)`, assign each subset-latent variable to the iid block variable whose index is the minimum vertex of that subset. Then each tight output depends only on three consecutive iid block variables. This is exactly the higher-order analogue of the packing step written explicitly by Matúš for pairs.

### 2.2 Closure theorem

**Proposition 2.1 (Matúš compactness argument extended to `m=2`).**  
For a finite output alphabet `S` (in particular `S={0,1}`), the set `BF_3(S)` of laws of stationary 3-block factors of iid processes is compact, hence closed, in the weak/product topology on `S^Z`.

**Proof.** Let

\[
I_3:=\{(i,j,k)\in\mathbb N^3:i,j,k\text{ pairwise distinct}\}.
\]

Because `S` is finite, the product space `S^{I_3}` is compact metrizable. Therefore the space `Prob(S^{I_3})` of probability laws on triple arrays is compact in the weak topology.

Let `JED_3(S)` be the subset of jointly exchangeable and dissociated array laws.

1. **Joint exchangeability is closed.** For each finite permutation `pi` of `N` and each finite cylinder event `C`, exchangeability is the equality
   \[
   P(C)=P(\pi C).
   \]
   Cylinder probabilities are continuous under weak convergence on this finite-alphabet product space. Hence each equality defines a closed set; intersecting over the countable family of finite permutations/cylinders remains closed.

2. **Dissociation is closed.** For finite disjoint vertex sets `A,B` and cylinder events `C_A,C_B` measurable with respect to triple coordinates wholly contained in `A` and `B`, dissociation is
   \[
   P(C_A\cap C_B)=P(C_A)P(C_B).
   \tag{2.1}
   \]
   Again every term is a continuous cylinder probability, so (2.1) is closed. Intersecting all such finite constraints shows that dissociation is closed.

Thus `JED_3(S)` is a closed subset of compact `Prob(S^{I_3})`, hence compact.

Now define

\[
\lambda_3:P\mapsto \mathcal L_P(\zeta_{i,i+1,i+2};i\ge1).
\]

Every finite cylinder of the output sequence depends on finitely many array coordinates, so `lambda_3` is continuous. By the Matúš higher-order characterization,

\[
BF_3(S)=\lambda_3(JED_3(S)).
\]

The continuous image of a compact space is compact. Since the target law space on the finite alphabet is Hausdorff, `BF_3(S)` is closed. ∎

### 2.3 Finite-cylinder separation consequence

If a binary stationary law `mu` is **not** a 3-block factor, closedness implies that there is a weak neighborhood of `mu` disjoint from `BF_3({0,1})`. Because finite-alphabet weak topology is generated by cylinder coordinates, there exist a finite window length `L` and `delta>0` such that every 3-block factor law differs from `mu` by at least `delta` in at least one cylinder probability supported in that window.

So a non-3-block-factor law always has a **finite observable separator in principle**. The previous difficulty was not existence of finite separation; it was finding a usable explicit separator.

### B29 outcome

**Substantive structural progress, but not a source decision.** The binary 3-block-factor class is closed, by the same compactness mechanism Matúš prints for two-block factors together with his explicit statement that the array characterization generalizes to `m>1`. This should be treated as a written-out prior-art-level extension, not as a novelty claim.

## 3. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: a complete finite-N hierarchy

Closedness can be sharpened into a complete finite extension hierarchy for the fixed law `mu=P^{f_*}`.

### 3.1 Finite extension spaces

For `N>=3`, let

\[
I_{3,N}:=\{(i,j,k)\in[N]^3:i,j,k\text{ pairwise distinct}\}.
\]

Let `K_N(mu)` be the set of probability laws `q_N` on binary colorings

\[
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{I_{3,N}}
\]

satisfying all of the following exact conditions.

**(E) Finite joint exchangeability.** For every `pi in S_N`, the law is invariant under the induced action on ordered triples.

**(D) Finite dissociation.** For every pair of disjoint vertex subsets `A,B subset [N]`, the sigma-fields generated by triple coordinates contained entirely in `A` and entirely in `B` are independent. Since the state space is finite, it is enough to impose, for every pair of atoms `alpha,beta`,

\[
q_N[\alpha\wedge\beta]=q_N[\alpha]q_N[\beta].
\tag{3.1}
\]

**(T) Exact tight-path marginal.** The vector

\[
(\zeta_{1,2,3},\zeta_{2,3,4},\ldots,\zeta_{N-2,N-1,N})
\]

has exactly the same law as `(X_1,...,X_{N-2})` under `mu`.

For `mu=P^{f_*}`, every right-hand side in (T) is an exact rational number: all all-one probabilities are Toeplitz determinants with entries in `{0,1/8,1/2}`, and inclusion-exclusion gives every binary cylinder.

### 3.2 Completeness theorem

**Proposition 3.1 (complete finite hierarchy).**  
For a binary stationary process law `mu`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu\in BF_3(\{0,1\})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
K_N(\mu)\ne\varnothing\ \text{for every }N\ge3.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

**Proof.**

`=>`: if `mu` is a 3-block factor, take the jointly exchangeable, dissociated infinite triple-array extension supplied by the higher-order Matúš characterization and restrict it to vertices `[N]`. The restriction satisfies (E), (D), and (T), so `K_N(mu)` is nonempty for every `N`.

`<=`: assume `K_N(mu)` is nonempty for every `N`. Choose one `q_N in K_N(mu)` for each `N`; they need **not** be mutually consistent.

For any fixed `k`, the restrictions `q_N|_{I_{3,k}}`, `N>=k`, live in a finite-dimensional probability simplex, hence in a compact space. By successive subsequence extraction and a diagonal argument, there is a sequence `N_j -> infinity` for which, for every fixed `k`, the restrictions converge to a law `q^{(k)}` on `{0,1}^{I_{3,k}}`.

These limiting laws are automatically projectively consistent. Indeed, if `ell<k`, then for every `j` large enough,

\[
(q_{N_j}|_{I_{3,k}})|_{I_{3,\ell}}=q_{N_j}|_{I_{3,\ell}},
\]

and marginalization is continuous, so the same identity holds for the limits. Kolmogorov extension therefore yields an infinite triple-array law `q` with finite restrictions `q^{(k)}`.

All constraints pass to the limit:

- finite permutation invariance is a system of linear equalities in cylinder probabilities;
- dissociation is the polynomial identity (3.1), hence is preserved by convergence;
- for each finite path length, condition (T) is eventually present at every level and therefore survives exactly in the limit.

Consequently `q` is a jointly exchangeable, dissociated infinite triple-array law whose tight superdiagonal has law `mu`. The higher-order Matúš characterization now gives `mu in BF_3({0,1})`. ∎

### 3.3 Exact finite algebraic feasibility

Write one variable `x_omega` for each binary coloring `omega` of `I_{3,N}`. Then `K_N(P^{f_*})` is specified by a finite system consisting of

- `x_omega >= 0`, `sum x_omega=1`;
- linear exchangeability equalities;
- linear tight-path marginal equalities with rational right-hand sides;
- quadratic dissociation equalities of the form (3.1).

Thus each level is a **finite semialgebraic feasibility problem over Q**. It is decidable in principle by real quantifier elimination. State-permutation / vertex-permutation orbit reduction can quotient many `x_omega` variables before elimination, but no finite latent alphabet is assumed.

Most importantly, (3.2) makes the hierarchy complete: if `P^{f_*}` is not a 3-block factor, then there exists some finite `N` with `K_N(P^{f_*})=empty`. A finite-level infeasibility certificate is therefore guaranteed to exist in principle in the negative case.

### B19 outcome

**The previous “finite inflation may not glue” concern is resolved at the logical level.** Independent feasibility at every finite `N` already forces a global extension by diagonal compactness; cross-`N` consistency variables do not have to be imposed in advance. What remains is computational/mathematical: find the first infeasible level, or construct a global extension/factor.

## 4. B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS in practice

The hierarchy survives the main adversarial checks.

### 4.1 Can every finite `N` be feasible with no global extension?

**No**, not under the exact `K_N` definition above. The diagonal compactness proof in Proposition 3.1 converts arbitrary feasible choices at all levels into a projectively consistent limiting family. The compactness argument is exactly what prevents a “locally feasible at every size but globally impossible” pathology.

### 4.2 Does dissociation survive the limit?

**Yes.** On a finite alphabet, independence of induced subarrays on disjoint finite vertex sets is equivalent to finitely many identities

\[
P(\alpha,\beta)-P(\alpha)P(\beta)=0,
\]

which are continuous polynomial equations in the finite-cylinder coordinates.

### 4.3 Is the block-factor class closed?

**Yes for the finite alphabet needed here**, by Proposition 2.1. This is the exact boundary/compactification fact needed to justify finite separation.

### 4.4 Why this still does not decide C0035

The logical hierarchy is exact but enormous. The raw number of ordered triple coordinates is

\[
|I_{3,N}|=N(N-1)(N-2),
\]

so a naive primal distribution has `2^{N(N-1)(N-2)}` atom variables before symmetry reduction. The constraints are polynomial rather than linear because exact dissociation multiplies marginals. Orbit reduction is necessary but does not by itself guarantee tractability.

The previous low-order inequalities were hand-derived projections of this hierarchy. They passed `f_*` with large slack. The new result says that, if `f_*` is a counterexample, **some finite exact level must fail**, but this run does not identify that level or produce its dual certificate.
