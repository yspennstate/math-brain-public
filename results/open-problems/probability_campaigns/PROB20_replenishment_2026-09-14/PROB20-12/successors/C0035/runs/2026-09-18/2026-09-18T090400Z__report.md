# 2. B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING

\pi_s:=\Pr(Z_0=s),\qquad p_{st}:=\Pr(Z_0=s,Z_1=t).

## 2.1 Correction to the preceding report

The preceding report said that Matúš's five-state inequality does not directly yield a nontrivial four-state inequality by adjoining a zero-probability state. That statement was too strong. It is true only for the bad choice in which the absent state is assigned Matúš label `5`, because then the right-hand side collapses. Since Matúš's theorem may be applied after arbitrary relabeling of the output alphabet, assign the **absent fifth state to label `4`** instead.

Matúš's published inequality is

\[
[13]+\sqrt{[24]}+\sqrt{[32]}+[41]+[51]+[52]+[55]
\ge \frac14[5]^2[11][12][23].
\tag{2.1}
\]

Choose any ordering `(a,b,c,d)` of the four actual states and map

\[
a\mapsto1,\qquad b\mapsto2,\qquad c\mapsto3,\qquad d\mapsto5,
\]

while label `4` is unused. Then `[24]=[41]=0`, whereas `[5]=\pi_d` is generally nonzero. Therefore every four-state 2-block factor obeys the **direct four-state Matúš corollary**

\[
\boxed{
p_{ac}+\sqrt{p_{cb}}+p_{da}+p_{db}+p_{dd}
\ge \frac14\pi_d^2p_{aa}p_{ab}p_{bc}
\quad\text{for every permutation }(a,b,c,d)\text{ of }S.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

No new inequality theorem is being attributed to this lane: (2.2) is an immediate relabeling/zero-state specialization of Matúš's published inequality. The advance is the correction that **it is already an observable four-site obstruction family for C0035**. Since `Z_0,Z_1` are precisely the four bits `X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3`, every term in (2.2) is an exact four-site cylinder or two-site marginal of the original determinantal process.

The `S_4` action produces 24 labeled inequalities. Complementing the binary process or reversing the line can identify some coefficient families, but I did not assume that `h` itself is symmetric in its arguments.

# 4. B6 — DEFORM TO A SOLVABLE POINT

Use the genuinely solved sublattice point

\[
f_0(t)=\frac12+\frac14\cos(4\pi t)
\]

and turn on the first harmonic:

\[
f_s(t)=\frac12+2s\cos(2\pi t)+\frac14\cos(4\pi t),
\qquad |s|\le\frac18.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The triangle bound gives `0<=f_s<=1` throughout this interval, so it is a source-valid degree-two deformation. For `s=0`, (3.1) shows

\[
\min_{u,v}p_{uv}=225/4096,
\qquad \pi_u=1/4.
\]

For every permutation in (2.2), at the base point

\[
\sqrt{p_{cb}}\ge\frac{15}{64}.
\]

Meanwhile a crude upper bound on the right side is

\[
\frac14\pi_d^2p_{aa}p_{ab}p_{bc}
\le \frac1{64}\left(\frac{289}{4096}\right)^3
<\frac1{64}.
\]

Thus every direct four-state Matúš inequality has strict slack exceeding

\[
\frac{15}{64}-\frac1{64}=\frac7{32}
\tag{4.2}
\]

before even adding the other nonnegative left-side terms. Four-site determinantal cylinders are polynomial/continuous functions of the Fourier coefficients, and the square-root terms are continuous here because all pair probabilities at the base point are strictly positive. Therefore there exists a `delta>0` such that **all 24 direct Matúš inequalities remain strict for `|s|<delta`**.

This is a rigorous local-blindness statement for this certificate family. It is *not* a proof that 3-block-factor realizability itself is open under the deformation. I found no justification for such openness and do not claim it. A counterexample could in principle lie arbitrarily close to the solved sublattice point while evading every inequality (2.2); detecting it would require a stronger off-path/higher-order condition.
