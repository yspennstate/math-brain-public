2. The text then states that many earlier theorems generalize to the `m`-dependent HSR, and explicitly lists **Theorems 3.5, 3.6, 4.1, 4.3, and 4.5**.
3. **Theorem 4.4 is not in that list.** Theorem 4.4 is precisely the one-dependent result saying, in the finite-dimensional real HSR setting, that an invariant simplicial cone with the appropriate distinguished vector gives a **two-block-factor** representation.
4. **Theorem 6.6:** for a generalized `m`-dependent HSR, if `dim H=1` the process is iid; if `dim H=2`, the proof forces the representation into the one-dependent/rank-one situation and the process is a **two-block factor**. It is not an `m=2` three-block criterion.

This is the key correction to the hoped-for recovery route. ### 2.3 Consequence for C0035

The existence of a generalized HSR is **not** a new restriction on a degree-two determinantal law. De Valk's Theorem 6.2 makes HSR existence universal for finite-state 2-dependent processes, while the HSR axioms imply the corresponding dependence property. Therefore any inequality deduced solely from the bare generalized HSR axioms can encode, at best, consequences of 2-dependence; it cannot by itself distinguish 3-block factors from arbitrary 2-dependent processes.

This explains why the previous strategy kept encountering hidden variables rather than a clean observable separator. The missing object is not “an HSR”; it is **extra positive/factorization structure inside an HSR** analogous to the one-dependent cone theorem, or equivalently the higher-order exchangeable/dissociated extension supplied by the block-factor map.

## 3. B11 — DUALIZE: what the first genuinely stronger condition must contain

Let an HSR use operators `A_s` indexed by symbols and put

\[
S:=\sum_s A_s.
\]

For a binary process, after summing over unspecified intermediate symbols, a finite cylinder at ordered sites

\[
e_1<e_2<\cdots<e_r
\]

is represented by an operator word of the form

\[
\Pr(X_{e_1}=s_1,\ldots,X_{e_r}=s_r)
=
\left\langle
A_{s_1}S^{e_2-e_1-1}A_{s_2}\cdots
S^{e_r-e_{r-1}-1}A_{s_r}y,
 x
\right\rangle,
\tag{3.1}
\]

with the orientation convention chosen consistently with de Valk's HSR. For `m=2`, `S^2` is rank one, so sufficiently large gaps factor exactly as required by 2-dependence.

Equation (3.1) is useful because it retains the arbitrary-gap cylinders Broman says become indispensable beyond degree one. But it also makes the obstacle transparent:

- the observable law supplies the scalar word moments (3.1);
- bare HSR existence supplies an operator realization for **every** finite-state 2-dependent law;
- a 3-block factor would require an additional common positive/map-reuse factorization linking all these words simultaneously.

Thus the first C0035-relevant “positivity condition” cannot simply be `S^2` rank one, positivity of ordinary probabilities, or HSR existence. Those are already present for arbitrary 2-dependent processes. One needs one of the following extra structures:

1. an actual `m=2` analogue of de Valk Theorem 4.4, e.g. a positive invariant tensor/cone factorization proven sufficient (or necessary) for a three-block representation; or
2. Matúš's higher-order formulation: an ordered three-index jointly exchangeable, dissociated extension whose tight superdiagonal is the observed sequence.

Matúš's 1996 paper explicitly says the exchangeable/dissociated characterization has a straightforward generalization for `m>1`. That gives the right exact **latent** object. The dual obstruction is elimination: tight-path cylinder probabilities do not determine the off-path moments of the three-index array. The previous `N=6` inflation attempt was precisely a finite truncation of this hidden extension problem.

### 3.1 Why no new observable inequality is claimed here

It would be invalid to treat arbitrary positivity of an HSR Gram matrix as a new 3-block-factor inequality: because all finite-state 2-dependent processes have an HSR, such a condition need not contain any block-factor information beyond dependence. It would likewise be invalid to assume the higher-order Matúš array is determined by the tight path. The off-path coordinates are the variables one is trying to prove exist.

Therefore this run does **not** manufacture an SOS/PSD inequality from hidden HSR quantities. The exact dual/elimination obstruction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{observable operator-word moments}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{a common positive three-input factorization}
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

without an additional cone/tensor/extension theorem.

### B11 outcome

**No source-valid separating inequality yet.** The failure is now theorem-level rather than computational: generalized HSR is universal for 2-dependent laws, while the extra positive structure characterizing 3-block factors has not been supplied by the recovered de Valk theorem list. ## 4. B18 — translate the operator words back to determinantal cylinders

For a determinantal process with symbol `f`, for every finite set

\[
E=\{e_1<\cdots<e_r\}\subset\mathbb Z,
\]

Broman's determinantal cylinder formula is

\[
\Pr^f(X_{e_1}=\cdots=X_{e_r}=1)
=
\det\!\big[\widehat f(e_j-e_i)\big]_{i,j=1}^r.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Binary inclusion-exclusion then determines every finite cylinder. Thus arbitrary-gap observable data are not missing: they are available exactly as Toeplitz minors.

For the fixed mixed source-valid degree-two test symbol

\[
f_*(t)=\frac12+\frac14\cos(2\pi t)+\frac14\cos(4\pi t),
\tag{4.2}
\]

we have

\[
\widehat f_*(0)=\frac12,
\qquad
\widehat f_*(\pm1)=\widehat f_*(\pm2)=\frac18,
\qquad
\widehat f_*(d)=0\quad(|d|>2).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (3.1) and (4.1), any HSR for this process must reproduce, for every gap pattern,

\[
\det\!\big[\widehat f_*(e_j-e_i)\big]
=
\left\langle
A_1S^{e_2-e_1-1}A_1\cdots
S^{e_r-e_{r-1}-1}A_1y,
 x
\right\rangle.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Concrete examples illustrate that this really keeps the nonconsecutive information:

\[
\Pr(X_0=X_2=1)
=
\det\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/8\\1/8&1/2\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{15}{64},
\tag{4.5}
\]

corresponding to an `A_1 S A_1` word, while

\[
\Pr(X_0=X_1=X_3=1)
=
\det\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/8&0\\
1/8&1/2&1/8\\
0&1/8&1/2
\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{7}{64},
\tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
\Pr(X_0=X_1=X_2=1)
=
\det\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/8&1/8\\
1/8&1/2&1/8\\
1/8&1/8&1/2
\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{27}{256}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

These exact fractions are elementary evaluations of (4.1), not a new certificate and therefore no checker was created merely to archive them.

### 4.1 The bridge that is still missing

Equations (4.1)--(4.7) show that the determinantal side provides an unusually explicit full finite-dimensional law. But this does **not** solve the block-factor problem, because de Valk's generalized HSR can also encode arbitrary finite-state 2-dependent laws that are not known to be three-block factors. The missing step is to prove that these particular Toeplitz-minor word moments admit the additional common positive factorization corresponding to a single measurable map

\[
h(Y_i,Y_{i+1},Y_{i+2}),
\]

or else prove that no such factorization exists.

Matúš translates that same missing factorization into existence of a higher-order exchangeable/dissociated array extension. That viewpoint is exact, but the off-path array moments remain free hidden variables. The previous finite inflation and Matúš inequalities are projections of this extension problem; none has yet eliminated enough hidden variables to decide (4.2).

**No construction and no counterexample this run.** The exact determinantal-to-operator dictionary is now explicit, including nonconsecutive gaps, but the factorization/extension variable is still hidden. This is a precise obstruction, not a numerical failure.
