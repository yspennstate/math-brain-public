## 2. Source and literature check — 2026-09-18

### 2.1 Current primary source

The Lyons--Steif author PDF still identifies itself as **Version of 1 June 2025** and in its open-questions section asks verbatim whether, for a trigonometric polynomial `f:T->[0,1]` of degree `m`, `P^f` is an `(m+1)`-block factor of iid; it notes only Broman's `m=1` result. The sentence after the question says that sufficiently explicit such block factors would yield explicit iid factors for general determinantal processes, but this is motivation rather than an extra condition in the yes/no question.

A fresh bounded search dated 2026-09-18 did not locate a later paper claiming the full all-degree result. This is bounded negative evidence only, not a novelty certificate or proof of worldwide openness.

### 2.2 Broman 2005: what the solved neighbor actually uses

Erik Broman, *One-dependent trigonometric determinantal processes are two-block-factors*, Ann. Probab. 33 (2005), 601--609, DOI `10.1214/009117904000000595`, proves exactly the `m=1` case.

The point relevant to this stuck diagnosis is not merely that his construction is explicit. His proof exploits a structural collapse special to one-dependence: the binary stationary one-dependent law is determined by the probabilities of **consecutive all-one runs**, and the tridiagonal Toeplitz determinants satisfy a scalar second-order recurrence. The local two-input factor is built to reproduce that recurrence.

Broman's final paragraph explicitly diagnoses the obstruction to higher degree. For degree `m`, it is no longer enough to match consecutive runs; one must also match

\[
\mathbf P^f[\eta(e_1)=\cdots=\eta(e_k)=1]
\]

for arbitrary integer sites `e_i`, not necessarily consecutive. Broman says these new cylinder events add complexity and make the generalization nontrivial. This is exactly the failure that the present C0035 route has encountered: low-order run statistics are too lossy.

### 2.3 Matúš 1996: exact structural representation, but hidden off-path variables

František Matúš, *On two-block-factor sequences and one-dependence*, Proc. AMS 124 (1996), 1237--1242, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9939-96-03094-8`, identifies two-block factors with superdiagonals of jointly exchangeable, dissociated arrays. Importantly, his introduction states that the analogous characterization of `(m+1)`-block factors by higher-order partially/jointly exchangeable dissociated arrays has a **straightforward generalization for `m>1`**, and he writes out only `m=1` for simplicity.

Thus there is no conceptual mystery about what a degree-two 3-block factor must look like at the latent-array level: it must admit the corresponding ordered three-index exchangeable/dissociated extension. The difficulty is **elimination**. Observable tight-superdiagonal cylinders expose only a tiny part of that array. The earlier C0035 inflation route introduces off-path moments; current low-order Cauchy/Matúš inequalities do not eliminate them strongly enough to separate the determinantal law.

### 2.4 de Valk 1993: the nearest operator technology, and the exact evidence gap

Vincent de Valk, *Hilbert Space Representations of m-Dependent Processes*, Ann. Probab. 21 (1993), 1550--1570, DOI `10.1214/aop/1176989130`, gives Hilbert-space/operator representations for one-dependent processes and states that **all `m`-dependent processes admit a generalized Hilbert-space representation**. For the one-dependent representation, de Valk proves a sufficient two-block-factor criterion: if there is a closed convex invariant cone spanned by finitely many linearly independent vectors, the corresponding process is a two-block factor. The abstract explicitly says the Hilbert-space concept is generalized to `m`-dependent processes and that several theorems generalize.

This run did **not** recover a theorem-by-theorem full-text specialization of de Valk's general `m` construction. Therefore I do **not** assert that the one-dependent invariant-cone criterion carries over verbatim as a necessary-and-sufficient degree-two 3-block criterion. That exact point is the literature/technical item the chosen recovery move must resolve before using the Hilbert representation as a theorem engine.

Rüschendorf--de Valk, *On regression representations of stochastic processes*, Stochastic Process. Appl. 46 (1993), 183--198, gives a constructive check for **monotone** `(m+1)`-block factor representations of certain `m`-dependent sequences. The monotonicity restriction is not present in Lyons's question, so this cannot simply be substituted for the required arbitrary factor.

### 2.5 Nearby warning examples

Aaronson--Gilat--Keane (1992) show that stationary one-dependent Markov chains with at most four states are two-block factors, while a five-state example need not be. Burton--Goulet--Meester (1993) construct one-dependent processes that are not any finite block factor. These results reinforce that finite dependence by itself cannot decide C0035; extra determinantal structure must be used.

## 5. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE

The degree-two C0035 problem sits at the intersection of four established languages.

### 5.1 Sliding-window/block-factor language

A 3-block factor reuses one measurable map on overlapping triples. Generic independence inequalities see the overlap graph but do not fully encode map reuse. Broman's degree-one construction works because one-dependence collapses the binary law to a one-parameter family of consecutive-run recurrences. Degree two does not.

### 5.2 Exchangeable hyperarray language

Matúš's `m>1` remark points to ordered three-index jointly exchangeable, dissociated arrays. Here map reuse becomes exchangeability, and finite-range independence becomes dissociation. This is the cleanest language for **necessary and sufficient latent consistency**, but the price is unobserved off-path coordinates.

The missing transferable ingredient is an **elimination theorem**: a way to turn positivity/exchangeability/dissociation of the ordered 3-array into finitely checkable inequalities solely in tight-superdiagonal cylinder probabilities, or else a constructive representation from those probabilities.

### 5.3 Hilbert/operator language

De Valk encodes dependence through vectors and bounded operators. In the one-dependent case, finite simplicial invariant-cone geometry is sufficient for a two-block factor. The abstract states that the Hilbert representation extends to general `m`.

This language may be better than raw hyperarray inflation because hidden-variable elimination can become positivity of an operator/tensor Gram object. But the exact `m=2` theorem, its tensor/order structure, and the direction of its cone criterion must be recovered from the full paper before using it. The current evidence does **not** justify inventing a tensor PSD condition and calling it de Valk's.

### 5.4 Monotone regression language

Rüschendorf--de Valk give a constructive method for **monotone** `(m+1)`-block factors. This is adjacent technology, not a solution, because Lyons allows arbitrary measurable factor maps and there is no established reason a determinantal representation can be chosen monotone.

### 5.5 Finite-dependence counterexample literature

Aaronson--Gilat--Keane and Burton--Goulet--Meester show that finite dependence alone does not imply a finite block factor. Hence any successful affirmative proof for C0035 must use determinantal/Toeplitz structure in an essential way; any purported argument using only `m`-dependence is structurally incapable of proving the source statement.

### B21 diagnosis

The transferable missing ingredient is not another generic dependence inequality. It is a bridge

\[
\text{Toeplitz determinant cylinders}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{degree-two Hilbert / ordered-3-array positivity}
\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{constructive 3-block map},\\
\text{or observable separating inequality}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every obstruction so far fails item 3 or item 4. Every construction so far is restricted to `m=1` or special sublattice symbols.
