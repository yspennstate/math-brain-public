## 3. B3 — a general exact-total-mass decorated-GW theorem

### 3.1 Model and hypotheses

Let `T` be an ordered critical one-type Galton–Watson tree. At each skeleton vertex `u`, independently sample a decoration record

\[
\mathcal D_u=
\bigl(\xi_u,V_u,(L_{u,j})_{1\le j\le\xi_u},\mathcal B_u\bigr),
\]

where:

- `xi_u` is the number of skeleton children;
- `V_u>=1` is the number of original vertices represented by the block;
- `L_{u,j}>=1` is the original graph distance through the block from its root attachment to child attachment `j`;
- `B_u` is the finite internal metric-measure block, of height `H_u` from its root attachment.

Assume

\[
E\xi=1,\qquad 0<\sigma^2:=\operatorname{Var}(\xi)<\infty,
\qquad \mu:=EV\in(0,\infty),
\]

and for some `theta>0`,

\[
E\exp\!\left\{\theta\left(\xi+V+H+\sum_{j=1}^{\xi}L_j\right)\right\}<\infty.
\]

Write

\[
X=\xi-1,
\qquad
\lambda:=E\sum_{j=1}^{\xi}L_j.
\]

Because `E xi=1`, `lambda` is also the mean edge length under the ordinary child-size-biased spine law.

Let

\[
N=\sum_{u\in T}V_u
\]

be the total original/decorated mass. Assume the lattice generated by `(X,V)` has rank two and work along attainable `n`; the rank-one covariance case is simpler because the endpoint equations determine `K=|T|` up to a fixed arithmetic relation and reduces to ordinary one-dimensional size conditioning.

Glue the blocks along the skeleton to obtain a finite metric tree-like space `T^dec`, with graph distance `d_dec` and uniform probability measure on its `N` original vertices.

### Theorem 3.1 — exact decorated-mass Brownian CRT limit

Under the hypotheses above, along attainable `n`,

\[
\boxed{
\left(
T^{\rm dec},
\frac{\sigma\sqrt\mu}{2\lambda\sqrt n}\,d_{\rm dec},
\operatorname{unif}_{N}
\right)
\ \Big|\ \{N=n\}
\Longrightarrow
(\mathcal T_{\rm CRT},d_{\rm CRT},\mu_{\rm CRT})
}
\]

in rooted Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov topology.

The proof occupies the rest of this section.

### 3.2 Exact bivariate cyclic identity

Let `K=|T|`. For iid records `(X_i,V_i)`, the classical cyclic lemma applied to the first coordinate gives, exactly,

\[
\Pr(K=k,N=n)
=
\frac1k
\Pr\!\left(
\sum_{i=1}^kX_i=-1,
\sum_{i=1}^kV_i=n
\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The mass sum is invariant under cyclic shifts, so no additional factor appears. This is the identity proved in the previous run.

Since `(X,V)` has an exponential moment and nonsingular covariance, the two-dimensional lattice local CLT applied uniformly for

\[
k=\frac n\mu+O(\sqrt n)
\]

gives a Gaussian profile in `k`. Summing that profile yields

\[
\Pr(N=n)=\Theta(n^{-3/2})
\tag{3.2}
\]

on the attainable lattice and

\[
K=\frac n\mu+O_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)
\quad\text{under }N=n.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Outside any window `|k-n/mu|<=A sqrt(n)`, Cramér bounds plus (3.1) make the conditional probability tend to zero as `A to infinity`.

### 3.3 Condition on `K=k` first: the skeleton excursion

Fix a central sequence

\[
k=k_n=\frac n\mu+O(\sqrt n).
\]

Before the cyclic shift, the iid pair sequence is conditioned on

\[
\sum_{i=1}^kX_i=-1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^kV_i=n.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The multivariate conditional Donsker theorem / bridge local CLT gives

\[
\left(
\frac1{\sqrt k}\sum_{i\le kt}X_i,
\frac1{\sqrt k}\sum_{i\le kt}(V_i-\mu)
\right)_{0\le t\le1}
\Longrightarrow
(B_X(t),B_V(t)),
\tag{3.5}
\]

where `(B_X,B_V)` is the centered two-dimensional Brownian bridge with the covariance matrix of `(X,V)`. In particular

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(B_X(s),B_X(t))
=\sigma^2(\min\{s,t\}-st).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus fixing the `V` endpoint does **not** reduce the first-coordinate bridge variance; the limiting first coordinate is the same variance-`sigma^2` Brownian bridge that appears under ordinary GW size conditioning.

Because `X>=-1` and its total is `-1`, exactly one cyclic shift is the Łukasiewicz excursion. The discrete Vervaat transform is continuous at the Brownian bridge limit almost surely, so the shifted first coordinate converges to `sigma` times normalized Brownian excursion.

The exponential moment implies that, under (3.4),

\[
\max_{i\le k}\xi_i=o_{\Pr}(\sqrt k),
\tag{3.7}
\]

by a union bound divided by the `Theta(k^{-1})` two-dimensional endpoint probability. Hence the standard finite-variance equivalence between Łukasiewicz, height, and contour encodings applies uniformly on the central `k` window. Therefore

\[
\left(T,\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt k}d_{\rm skel},\operatorname{unif}_{K}\right)
\Big|\{K=k,N=n\}
\Longrightarrow \mathrm{CRT}
\tag{3.8}
\]

uniformly for `k=n/mu+O(sqrt n)`. Mixing over `k` with (3.3) changes `sqrt(k)` to `sqrt(n/mu)`, so the skeleton metric scale under `N=n` is

\[
\frac{\sigma\sqrt\mu}{2\sqrt n}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

### 3.4 Uniform weighted-edge concentration: the missing estimate

For a skeleton edge chosen under the usual many-to-one law, let `L^*` denote its original decorated length. Its law is the child-size-biased mark law

\[
E\phi(L^*)
=
E\sum_{j=1}^{\xi}\phi(L_j),
\]

because `E xi=1`. Hence

\[
EL^*=\lambda,
\]

and `L^*` has an exponential moment.

For a skeleton vertex `v`, let

\[
W(v)=\sum_{e\in[\rho,v]}L_e
\]

be decorated root distance ignoring only internal displacement at the terminal block. The many-to-one identity for a critical one-type GW tree gives, for each generation `h`,

\[
E\#\Bigl\{v:|v|=h,\ |W(v)-\lambda h|>\varepsilon\sqrt n\Bigr\}
=
\Pr\!\left(
\left|\sum_{r=1}^{h}(L_r^*-\lambda)\right|>\varepsilon\sqrt n
\right).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Fix `A<infinity`. By Bernstein/Cramér bounds for the iid exponential-moment increments, uniformly for `h<=A sqrt(n)`,

\[
\Pr\!\left(
\left|\sum_{r=1}^{h}(L_r^*-\lambda)\right|>\varepsilon\sqrt n
\right)
\le C_A e^{-c_{A,\varepsilon}\sqrt n}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Summing (3.10) over `h<=A sqrt(n)` and using Markov's inequality gives the **unconditioned** estimate

\[
\Pr\!\left(
\exists v:\ |v|\le A\sqrt n,
\ |W(v)-\lambda|v||>\varepsilon\sqrt n
\right)
\le C_A\sqrt n\,e^{-c_{A,\varepsilon}\sqrt n}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Now condition on `N=n`. Dividing by (3.2),

\[
\Pr\!\left(
\exists v:\ |v|\le A\sqrt n,
\ |W(v)-\lambda|v||>\varepsilon\sqrt n
\mid N=n
\right)
\le O(n^2)e^{-c_{A,\varepsilon}\sqrt n}\to0.
\tag{3.13}
\]

By (3.8)–(3.9), skeleton height divided by `sqrt n` is tight under `N=n`. First take `n to infinity` in (3.13), then `A to infinity`, obtaining

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{v\in T}
|W(v)-\lambda d_{\rm skel}(\rho,v)|
=o_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)
\quad\text{given }N=n.
}
\tag{3.14}
\]

This is exactly the weighted-edge estimate left open in the previous H0011 report. The key point is that it does **not** require a bespoke concentration theorem under the doubly conditioned law; exponential unconditioned concentration survives division by the polynomial local conditioning probability.

The same argument applied to pairs through their lowest common ancestor gives uniform distortion of all pairwise distances:

\[
\sup_{u,v\in T}
\left|
 d_{\rm dec}^{\rm root}(u,v)
-\lambda d_{\rm skel}(u,v)
\right|
=o_{\Pr}(\sqrt n),
\tag{3.15}
\]

where `d_dec^root` is distance between block roots.

### 3.5 Internal block diameter is negligible

Fix central `k`. Conditional on the two endpoint sums (3.4), exchangeability and the exponential moment give

\[
\Pr\left(\max_{i\le k}H_i>\varepsilon\sqrt n\mid (3.4)\right)
\le
\frac{k\,E[1_{\{H>\varepsilon\sqrt n\}};\,\text{remaining endpoint can be met}]}{\Pr((3.4))}.
\]

The numerator is at most `k C e^{-c sqrt(n)}` and the two-dimensional local-CLT denominator is `Theta(k^{-1})`; hence the bound is `O(k^2)e^{-c sqrt(n)} ->0`. Mixing over the central `k` window and using (3.3) yields

\[
\boxed{\max_{u\in T}H_u=o_{\Pr}(\sqrt n)\mid N=n.}
\tag{3.16}
\]

Thus collapsing every internal block onto its root attachment changes Hausdorff distance by `o_P(sqrt n)`.

### 3.6 The original uniform mass measure has the CRT limit

Under the bridge conditioning (3.4), the second coordinate of (3.5) implies uniformly on the central `k` window

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le1}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{i\le\lfloor kt\rfloor}V_i-t
\right|
\xrightarrow{\Pr}0.
\tag{3.17}
\]

Let

\[
A_n(t)=\frac1n\sum_{i\le\lfloor kt\rfloor}V_i.
\]

Then `||A_n-id||_infinity ->0`; the generalized inverse `A_n^{-1}` also converges uniformly to the identity. The skeleton height/contour coding in (3.8) converges in `C[0,1]` to a continuous Brownian excursion. Therefore composing the coding map with the time change `A_n^{-1}` has the same limit. This means that weighting preorder vertex `i` by `V_i/n`, rather than by `1/k`, leaves the limiting CRT mass measure unchanged.

Equivalently, after collapsing each block to its root, the pushforward of uniform measure on all original vertices is

\[
\sum_{u\in T}\frac{V_u}{n}\delta_u,
\]

and its Prokhorov distance from the skeleton empirical measure tends to zero in the common CRT embedding induced by the coding convergence.

Finally, before collapse each of the `V_u` original vertices lies within `H_u` of block root `u`. By (3.16), transporting every such atom to its block root costs `o_P(sqrt n)` in original metric, hence `o_P(1)` after the theorem's scaling. So the **actual uniform original-vertex measure**, not only block weights, converges to CRT mass.

### 3.7 Finish the theorem

By (3.15), after multiplying decorated distance by

\[
\frac{\sigma\sqrt\mu}{2\lambda\sqrt n},
\]

the block-root metric differs by `o_P(1)` from the skeleton metric scaled as in (3.9). Equation (3.16) removes internal block Hausdorff error. Section 3.6 removes the measure error. Hence the Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov distance between the rescaled decorated tree and its rescaled skeleton representation tends to zero in probability under `N=n`. Combining with (3.8) proves Theorem 3.1. ∎

### 3.8 Exceptional initial block / a finite regeneration forest

H0011 does not necessarily start with the regeneration-root law `eta`. Starting from a deterministic or source root type produces one exponentially light initial block and a random number `R` of first-regeneration endpoints; conditional on `R`, their types are iid `eta`, so beyond that block there are `R` iid copies of Theorem 3.1's critical decorated GW tree.

The critical total-mass law from (3.2) is locally subexponential: for each fixed `r`, if `p_n=P(N=n)~c n^{-3/2}` on its lattice, then

\[
(p^{*r})_n\sim r p_n.
\tag{3.18}
\]

This follows by splitting the convolution into terms with one summand at least `n-M` and a central region; regular variation gives the edge terms `r p_n P(N\le M)^{r-1}`, while the central region is `o(p_n)`, then `M to infinity`. If `R` has an exponential moment, truncate `R` first and use exponential domination to extend (3.18) to the random finite forest.

Therefore, conditioned on total mass `n`, with probability tending to one exactly one regeneration component has mass `n-o(n)` while the other components and the initial block have total mass `o(n)` and height `o(sqrt n)`. The dominant component has the same CRT limit from Theorem 3.1; attaching finitely many vanishing components at a vanishing initial block does not alter rooted GHP convergence.

This completes the decorated-regeneration transfer at the probabilistic level, modulo only the source-specialization and normalization notation check recorded below.

**B3 outcome:** the previously missing weighted-edge tightness estimate is proved as part of a general exact-total-decorated-mass GHP theorem. No finite-type discretisation is required.

## 5. B5 — vary the Nummelin split and find what survives

Hold the common minorizing law `nu_0` fixed and vary the admissible split probability `delta` in `(0,delta_0]`. Then `Z=int h^{-1}dnu_0` is fixed and (4.1) gives

\[
\mu_\delta=\frac1{\delta Z},
\qquad
\lambda_\delta=\frac1\delta,
\qquad
\sigma_{\xi,\delta}^2
=\frac Z\delta\Sigma_{\rm FR}^2,
\qquad
\Sigma_{\rm FR}^2:=\pi(v).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\delta\mu_\delta=Z^{-1},
\qquad
\delta\lambda_\delta=1,
\qquad
\delta\sigma_{\xi,\delta}^2=Z\Sigma_{\rm FR}^2,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and, most importantly,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sigma_{\xi,\delta}^2\mu_\delta}{\lambda_\delta^2}
=\Sigma_{\rm FR}^2
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

for every admissible split. Differentiating logarithmically with respect to `delta` gives

\[
\frac{d\log\mu_\delta}{d\log\delta}
=
\frac{d\log\lambda_\delta}{d\log\delta}
=
\frac{d\log\sigma_{\xi,\delta}^2}{d\log\delta}
=-1,
\]

so the three split-dependent factors cancel in the exact combination used by Theorem 3.1.

## 7. Failed/corrected routes this run

1. **Prove weighted-edge concentration directly under the conditioned tree.** Unnecessary. The unconditioned many-to-one probability is exponentially small; dividing by `P(N=n)=Theta(n^{-3/2})` preserves convergence to zero.
2. **Let the extra mass endpoint change the Brownian excursion variance.** It does not. The first coordinate of the full vector Brownian bridge retains covariance `sigma^2(min(s,t)-st)` when the entire endpoint vector is pinned; correlation changes the joint bridge, not that marginal covariance.
