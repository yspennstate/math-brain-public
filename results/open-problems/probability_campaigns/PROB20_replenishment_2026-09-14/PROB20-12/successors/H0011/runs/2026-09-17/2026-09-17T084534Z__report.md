## 3. B18 — change language: from continuous type to an iid decorated one-type skeleton

### 3.1 Notation inherited from the previous run

Let `M` be the critical mean offspring kernel, normalized so that

\[
Mh=h,
\]

for a strictly positive Perron function `h`. Normalize the left Perron probability measure `pi` by

\[
\pi M=\pi,\qquad \pi(1)=1,\qquad \pi(h)=1.
\]

Then the invariant law of the Doob `h`-spine is

\[
\varpi_*(dx)=h(x)\,\pi(dx).
\]

The previous run proved, for the exact bounded-mean fixed-variance Gaussian OU kernel of source Theorem 5.1, that `h` is bounded above and below and that the spine kernel admits a global minorization

\[
P(a,dx)\ge \delta\,\nu_0(dx),\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Define

\[
C(a,dx):=\delta\frac{h(a)}{h(x)}\nu_0(dx),\qquad B:=M-C.
\]

Let

\[
Z:=\int h(x)^{-1}\nu_0(dx),\qquad
\eta(dx):=Z^{-1}h(x)^{-1}\nu_0(dx).
\]

Then

\[
C(a,dx)=\delta Z h(a)\eta(dx),
\]

and the previous exact renewal calculation gave

\[
F:=\sum_{r\ge0}B^rC,
\qquad
F(a,dx)=Z h(a)\eta(dx).
\]

### 3.2 Distribution-level splitting is available in the source model

The source does not posit an arbitrary point-process branching law. In its sensitive regime, a parent's offspring count is a Cox/Binomial thinning of the forward degree, and conditional child strengths are drawn from the OU strength kernel. In particular, conditional on the parent state and the count/potential-child environment, distinct child-strength draws are independent with a type-determined law.

Because `C<=M` as kernels, each actual child of type `x` born to a parent of type `a` may be independently marked with the Radon–Nikodym probability corresponding to `dC(a,.)/dM(a,.)`. Conditional on a child being marked, its type law is

\[
\frac{C(a,dx)}{C(a,E)}=\eta(dx),
\]

which is independent of `a`.

A **regeneration block** rooted at a marked vertex contains that root and all descendants reached through unmarked edges, stopping immediately before each next marked vertex. The marked vertices at the boundary are the children of the root in the regeneration skeleton. Every original vertex belongs to exactly one such block, apart from the harmless initial pre-regeneration block when the original root type is deterministic rather than `eta`.

### Proposition 3.1 — regeneration vertices form a genuine one-type GW tree

Start a regeneration vertex with type `A~eta`. Let `xi` be the number of first marked descendants of its block. Then there is a probability law `q` on nonnegative integers such that:

1. `xi~q` for every regeneration vertex;
2. conditional on `xi=k`, the `k` endpoint types are iid `eta` and independent of the future descendant blocks;
3. the complete decorations of distinct regeneration blocks are iid;
4. the regeneration skeleton is therefore an ordinary one-type Galton–Watson tree with offspring law `q`.

**Proof.** Explore the unmarked descendants of one regeneration root breadth-first, revealing all original offspring variables and all split marks. The source branching property makes offspring families of distinct original vertices independent. At any boundary birth that is marked, the endpoint type has conditional law `eta`, independent of the parent type, because the marked-child kernel factorizes as `delta Z h(a) eta(dx)`. Distinct child draws and split coins are independent conditional on the explored parent data, hence the terminal endpoint types are iid `eta` conditional on the number of terminals. Once a terminal marked child is reached, its unexplored descendants are independent of the explored block and have the same law as a fresh process rooted at its `eta`-distributed type. Iterating at each terminal gives iid regeneration-block decorations. This is precisely the branching property of a one-type GW tree. ∎

The initial source root need not have type `eta`; it contributes one exceptional initial block. Its size and height have the same uniform exponential tail proved in the previous run, so it is asymptotically negligible under `sqrt(n)` metric scaling. All subsequent blocks are iid.

### 3.3 Criticality and exponential moments

The expected skeleton offspring from a type `a` is `F1(a)=Zh(a)`. Therefore under `eta`,

\[
\mathbb E\xi
=Z\eta(h)
=1.
\]

So the one-type skeleton is critical.

The previous block-size exponential estimate extends to the number of boundary marks because every marked boundary child is an original child of an unmarked block vertex and the source assumes an exponential moment for offspring count. Consequently, for some positive `theta`,

\[
\mathbb E e^{\theta(\xi+V+H)}<\infty,
\]

where `V` is block vertex count and `H` block height. In particular the skeleton offspring variance is finite.

**B18 outcome:** the regeneration reduction is no longer only rank-one at the mean level. Under the concrete Cox/iid-child structure of H0011, it yields an iid decorated one-type critical GW skeleton.

## 5. B25 — count the same thing twice: exact bivariate Otter–Dwass

For each iid regeneration block let

\[
X:=\xi-1,\qquad V:=\text{number of original vertices in the block}.
\]

Let `K` be the number of regeneration-skeleton vertices and let

\[
N:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}V_i
\]

be the total number of original vertices represented by the iid skeleton blocks. The exceptional initial block can be convolved in afterward and does not change the asymptotic mechanism.

### Proposition 5.1 — exact bivariate cyclic identity

For every attainable pair `(k,n)`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(K=k,N=n)
=
\frac1k\,
\mathbb P\!\left(
\sum_{i=1}^k X_i=-1,
\ \sum_{i=1}^kV_i=n
\right).
}
\]

**Proof.** Take `k` iid records `(X_i,V_i)`. The event `sum X_i=-1` is exactly the Łukasiewicz endpoint condition for a plane tree with `k` vertices. The classical cyclic lemma says exactly one of the `k` cyclic shifts of an increment sequence with increments at least `-1` and total `-1` has all proper partial sums nonnegative and therefore encodes a rooted plane tree. The extra condition `sum V_i=n` is invariant under cyclic shifts. Hence exactly one out of the `k` equally likely cyclic shifts satisfies the tree condition while preserving the mass sum, yielding the factor `1/k`. ∎

This identity is the exact iid replacement for applying one-dimensional Otter–Dwass to a stationary average of type-dependent offspring counts.

### 5.2 Consequence of the two-dimensional local CLT

Write

\[
\mu:=\mathbb EV,
\qquad
\sigma_\xi^2:=\operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\operatorname{Var}(X),
\qquad
c:=\operatorname{Cov}(X,V),
\]

and, in the nondegenerate covariance case,

\[
\tau^2:=\operatorname{Var}(V)-\frac{c^2}{\sigma_\xi^2}>0.
\]

The record `(X,V)` has an exponential moment. Therefore the two-dimensional lattice local CLT applied to Proposition 5.1 gives, uniformly for

\[
k=\frac n\mu+O(\sqrt n),
\]

a Gaussian profile in `k`. On the relevant attainable lattice,

\[
\frac{K-n/\mu}{\sqrt n}\ \Big|\ \{N=n\}
\Longrightarrow
N\!\left(0,\frac{\tau^2}{\mu^3}\right)
\]

in the nondegenerate case. In particular,

\[
K=\frac n\mu+O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt n)
\quad\text{under }N=n.
\]

If the covariance is singular, then `V-mu` is affine in `X` almost surely; because `sum X=-1` is fixed on a tree, conditioning on `N=n` fixes `K` up to an `O(1)` arithmetic relation, which is even simpler for scaling purposes.

For a span-one nondegenerate joint lattice, summing the local Gaussian profile over `k` gives the explicit check

\[
\mathbb P(N=n)
\sim
\frac{\sqrt\mu}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_\xi}\,n^{-3/2}.
\]

For a nontrivial lattice span the usual span factor and restriction to attainable `n` must be inserted. This report does not suppress that arithmetic qualification.

In a genuinely type-dependent branching process, stationary averaging by itself does not make successive preorder offspring counts iid. Therefore that sentence is not, on its face, a complete justification of the iid hypothesis required by the classical formula.

## 6. An exact split-independent variance identity

This is the strongest algebraic result of the run.

Define the local ordered-pair `h`-factorial moment

\[
v(a):=
\mathbb E_a\!\left[
\sum_{i\ne j}h(A_i)h(A_j)
\right],
\]

so that

\[
\Sigma_{\rm FR}^2=\pi(v).
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — mean block mass

Because `pi M=pi`,

\[
\pi(I-B)=\pi C.
\]

Using `pi(h)=1` and `C(a,dx)=delta Z h(a)eta(dx)`,

\[
\pi C=\delta Z\eta.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\eta(I-B)^{-1}=\frac{\pi}{\delta Z}.
}
\]

Taking the test function `1` and using `pi(1)=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu=\mathbb EV=\frac1{\delta Z}.
}
\]

### Lemma 6.2 — mean original graph length of a skeleton edge

Let `L_1,...,L_xi` be the original graph lengths from one regeneration vertex to its first marked descendants. Then

\[
\lambda:=\mathbb E\sum_{j=1}^{\xi}L_j
=
\eta\sum_{r\ge0}(r+1)B^rC1.
\]

Now

\[
C1=\delta Z h,
\qquad
Bh=(1-\delta)h,
\qquad
\eta(h)=1/Z.
\]

Therefore

\[
\lambda
=
\sum_{r\ge0}(r+1)\delta(1-\delta)^r
=
\boxed{\frac1\delta}.
\]

Since `E xi=1`, this is also the mean edge length seen under the usual child-size-biased spine of the one-type skeleton.

### Lemma 6.3 — exact skeleton offspring variance

Conditional on `xi`, the terminal marked types are iid `eta`. Since `eta(h)=1/Z`,

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\sum_{j\ne k}h(A_j)h(A_k)
\right]
=
\frac{\mathbb E[\xi(\xi-1)]}{Z^2}
=
\frac{\sigma_\xi^2}{Z^2},
\]

because the skeleton is critical, `E xi=1`, so `E[xi(xi-1)]=Var(xi)`.

Compute the same ordered-pair quantity by the first original vertex at which the two terminal lineages split. For a child of type `x`, the expected total `h`-mass of first marked descendants is

\[
(Fh)(x)=h(x),
\]

since

\[
F(a,dy)=\frac{h(a)}{h(y)}\nu_0(dy).
\]

Thus at any unmarked block vertex of type `a`, the expected contribution from two distinct original child branches is exactly `v(a)`. Summing over all unmarked vertices in the block gives

\[
\mathbb E_\eta\!\left[
\sum_{j\ne k}h(A_j)h(A_k)
\right]
=
\eta(I-B)^{-1}v
=
\frac{\pi(v)}{\delta Z}.
\]

Equating the two expressions yields

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_\xi^2
=
\frac{Z}{\delta}\,\pi(v)
=
\frac{Z}{\delta}\,\Sigma_{\rm FR}^2.
}
\]

This identity is exact. It is not a numerical fit and it does not depend on choosing a particular rational approximation of the type space.

### Corollary 6.4 — the arbitrary Nummelin split cancels from the CRT metric constant

For a critical one-type GW tree with `k` vertices and offspring variance `sigma_xi^2`, the Aldous metric normalization is

\[
\frac{\sigma_\xi}{2\sqrt{k}}.
\]

Under original total mass `n`, Proposition 5.1 gives `k~n/mu`, while one skeleton edge represents asymptotically `lambda` original graph edges. The resulting candidate normalization on original graph distance is therefore

\[
\Sigma_{\rm reg}
:=
\frac{\sigma_\xi\sqrt\mu}{2\lambda}.
\]

Substitute Lemmas 6.1–6.3:

\[
\Sigma_{\rm reg}^2
=
\frac{1}{4}
\left(\frac{Z}{\delta}\pi(v)\right)
\left(\frac1{\delta Z}\right)
\delta^2
=
\boxed{\frac{\pi(v)}4}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_{\rm reg}=\frac{\Sigma_{\rm FR}}2.
}
\]

The right-hand side is intrinsic to the original continuous-type process and is independent of the arbitrary minorization `delta,nu_0`. This cancellation is the key consistency check that the regeneration route is capturing the correct geometry rather than an artifact of the split.

For a one-type critical GW process, `h=1`, `pi=1`, and `pi(v)=E[xi(xi-1)]=Var(xi)`, so the formula reduces to the classical `sigma/2` scaling constant.

This also clarifies what must be compared with the source symbol `Sigma_c`: the intrinsic pair-variance `pi(v)` is the natural continuous-type analogue. The current v1 source does not give a displayed formula for `Sigma_c`, so I do not assert that the authors' `Sigma_c` has already been shown equal to this quantity; the exact identification must be written explicitly in a closure proof.

## 7. B27 — swap the quantifiers: condition on skeleton size first

The bivariate cyclic identity suggests conditioning in the order

\[
K=k\quad\text{and then}\quad N=n,
\]

rather than trying to analyze the original tree directly under `N=n`.

### 7.1 The conditioned record sequence is a two-dimensional bridge

Given `(K,N)=(k,n)`, the preorder iid records `(X_i,V_i)` are conditioned on

\[
\sum_{i=1}^kX_i=-1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^kV_i=n,
\]

and then cyclically shifted to the unique Łukasiewicz excursion.

For `k=n/mu+O(sqrt(n))`, the multivariate local CLT and bridge invariance principle give a two-dimensional Brownian bridge for the centered partial sums. Crucially, conditioning the second coordinate endpoint does **not** change the first-coordinate bridge covariance: for a vector Brownian bridge with covariance matrix `Sigma`,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(B_X(s),B_X(t))
=
\sigma_\xi^2(\min\{s,t\}-st).
\]

The `X` endpoint is `-1/sqrt(k)->0`, so the first coordinate has the same limiting bridge variance `sigma_xi^2` as in ordinary size-conditioned critical GW. The cyclic/Vervaat transform therefore points to the same Brownian excursion and hence the same CRT skeleton limit, uniformly over the central `sqrt(n)` window of `k` selected by `N=n`.

This is more than a heuristic about `K`: it identifies why the extra additive-mass conditioning does not change the Brownian universality class of the skeleton.

### 7.2 Weighted mass measure

The same bivariate bridge gives, uniformly on the central `k` window,

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le1}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{i\le kt}V_i-t
\right|
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

Thus replacing uniform mass on skeleton vertices by the original block-mass weights does not change the limiting CRT probability measure.

### 7.3 Internal block geometry is negligible

The previous run proved a uniform exponential tail for block size and height. Proposition 5.1 plus the local CLT gives `P(N=n)` of order `n^{-3/2}` on the attainable lattice. Therefore, choosing `C` sufficiently large,

\[
\mathbb P\big(\max_{1\le i\le K}H_i>C\log n\mid N=n\big)\to0.
\]

Hence the maximum metric distortion caused *inside* one block is

\[
O_{\mathbb P}(\log n)=o_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt n).
\]

### 7.4 What remains for a full GHP proof

What is still not written at theorem-level completeness is the **uniform weighted-edge law of large numbers under the doubly conditioned tree**:

\[
\sup_{v\text{ in skeleton}}
\left|
D_{\rm original}(\rho,v)-\lambda\,d_{\rm skel}(\rho,v)
\right|
=o_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt n)
\quad\text{under }N=n.
\]

The decorations have exponential moments, so a Bernstein/spinal argument should prove this. Archer–Croydon (2025) prove a GHP theorem for general decorated two-type critical GW trees under exponential-moment assumptions, providing a close published template. Their conditioning is not the present exact total decorated mass, however, so I am not substituting their theorem for this missing uniform conditional estimate.

Once this weighted-edge estimate is supplied, Sections 5–6 identify the normalization automatically as `Sigma_FR/2`, block diameters vanish, and the block-mass measure converges to CRT mass. That would produce the exact conditioned GHP theorem for the regeneration-rooted process; the exceptional initial block is exponentially light and then disappears as well.

**B27 outcome:** exact-size conditioning no longer looks like an uncontrolled continuous-type problem. It is a two-dimensional iid bridge plus one uniform weighted-edge concentration estimate for exponentially light decorations.
