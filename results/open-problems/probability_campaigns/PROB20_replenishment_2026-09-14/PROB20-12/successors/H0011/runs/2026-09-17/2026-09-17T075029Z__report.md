## 2. Mathematical idea before code: force an atom by Nummelin splitting

The source's OU strength inheritance is unusually favorable. Because its child Gaussian has **fixed positive variance and a mean confined to a bounded interval**, all parent types share a nonzero common part. After the Perron `h`-transform this becomes a global Doeblin minorization. Split off that common part as a regeneration mark.

This changes the geometry of the missing tightness problem:

1. the spine has geometric regeneration times uniformly over the starting strength;
2. the genealogical pieces between regeneration marks have uniform exponential size tails under the source's offspring exponential moment;
3. the first-regeneration mean kernel is exactly rank one and critical after averaging against the regeneration-type law;
4. the remaining difficulty is no longer “types can escape to infinity”; it is a **conditioned decorated critical-GW problem**: transfer the original conditioning on total vertices through exponentially light regeneration blocks and identify the exact metric scaling constant.

No numerical checker is appropriate for this analytic argument. The proof is written out below.

## 3. B2 — attack the negation: can strength escape destroy tightness?

The most obvious counterexample mechanism is an ancestral line whose continuous type drifts farther and farther into the tails, making discretisation nonuniform and producing atypically tall conditioned clusters. Under the source's concrete OU kernel, this mechanism fails because the spine satisfies a global Doeblin condition.

### Lemma 3.1 — global upper and lower bounds for the Perron eigenfunction

Assume the concrete OU kernel used in the proof of source Theorem 5.1: for each parent strength `a`, `Gamma(a,.)` is a Gaussian law with fixed variance `s^2>0` and mean `m(a)` ranging in a bounded interval, and `p(x) >= p_min > 0`. At criticality let `Kh=h`, with positive Perron eigenfunction `h in L^2(varpi)`.

Then there are constants

\[
0<h_-\le h(a)\le h_+<\infty\qquad\text{for every }a.
\]

**Proof.** Write `q_a=dGamma(a,.)/dvarpi` in the source's `L^2(varpi)` realization. The bounded-mean fixed-variance Gaussian family used in the Hilbert-Schmidt proof has a uniform `L^2(varpi)` bound

\[
C_2:=\sup_a\|q_a\|_{L^2(\varpi)}<\infty.
\]

Therefore, using `p<=1` and `Kh=h`,

\[
h(a)
=\bar\nu\int p(x)h(x)q_a(x)\,\varpi(dx)
\le \bar\nu C_2\|h\|_{L^2(\varpi)}=:h_+.
\]

For the lower bound choose any compact interval `I` with

\[
J:=\int_I p(x)h(x)\,\varpi(dx)>0.
\]

Such an interval exists because `h>0` a.e. and `p>=p_min`. Since the family of Gaussian means is bounded and the variance is fixed positive, the kernel density has a strictly positive common lower bound on a fixed compact target interval:

\[
c_I:=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R,\;x\in I}q_a(x)>0
\]

(in the equivalent Lebesgue-density formulation, divide by the positive Gaussian reference density on `I`). Hence

\[
h(a)\ge \bar\nu c_I J=:h_->0.
\]

This is uniform in `a`. ∎

**Scope caveat.** The conclusion uses the concrete bounded-mean/fixed-variance OU fact invoked by the source in its own Theorem 5.1 proof. It is not being claimed for an arbitrary “Gaussian-type, W2-Lipschitz” kernel absent that stronger concrete structure.

### Lemma 3.2 — the raw OU strength kernel has a parent-independent minorization

After recentering, suppose the possible Gaussian means lie in `[-M,M]`. Let

\[
g_u(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}s}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-u)^2}{2s^2}\right),\qquad |u|\le M,
\]

and define

\[
g_*(x):=\inf_{|u|\le M}g_u(x),\qquad
\varepsilon_\Gamma:=\int_{\mathbb R}g_*(x)\,dx>0,
\]

\[
\nu_0(dx):=\varepsilon_\Gamma^{-1}g_*(x)\,dx.
\]

Then for every parent strength `a`,

\[
\Gamma(a,dx)\ge \varepsilon_\Gamma\,\nu_0(dx).
\]

For a symmetric mean interval one can compute `epsilon_Gamma=2 Phi(-M/s)>0`; the exact value is not needed.

### Proposition 3.3 — global Doeblin minorization for the spine

At criticality, the source spine kernel is

\[
P(a,dx)=\frac{h(x)}{h(a)}\,\bar\nu p(x)\Gamma(a,dx).
\]

Combining Lemmas 3.1–3.2 gives

\[
P(a,dx)
\ge
\frac{\bar\nu p_{\min}h_-}{h_+}\,
\varepsilon_\Gamma\nu_0(dx).
\]

Choose

\[
0<\delta\le
\min\!\left\{\frac12,
\frac12\frac{\bar\nu p_{\min}h_-}{h_+}\varepsilon_\Gamma\right\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{P(a,\cdot)\ge \delta\nu_0(\cdot)\quad\text{for every }a.}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
P(a,\cdot)=\delta\nu_0+(1-\delta)R_a
\]

for a Markov residual kernel `R_a`. Under the standard Nummelin construction, the first regeneration time `tau` satisfies

\[
\sup_a\mathbb P_a(\tau>k)\le(1-\delta)^k.
\]

Thus a persistent “escaping strength” lineage cannot be the missing obstruction: independently of how large the current strength is, every spine step has a fixed positive chance to forget it completely.

## 4. B21 — port the useful part of infinitely-many-types theory without pretending the type space is countable

The correct replacement for “return to an exact type” is an **artificial regeneration mark**.

Let `M(a,dx)=bar nu p(x) Gamma(a,dx)` denote the critical mean offspring kernel, so

\[
P(a,dx)=\frac{h(x)}{h(a)}M(a,dx).
\]

From `P>=delta nu_0`, define the nonnegative subkernel

\[
C(a,dx):=\delta\frac{h(a)}{h(x)}\nu_0(dx),
\qquad
B:=M-C.
\]

Because `C<=M`, this can be realized at the level of the source's offspring point process by independently marking an actual child with the Radon–Nikodym thinning probability `dC/dM` whenever that child exists. The mean kernels of marked and unmarked children are respectively `C` and `B`.

### Lemma 4.1 — exact eigenvalue split

Since `nu_0` is a probability measure,

\[
(Ch)(a)=\delta h(a),
\qquad
(Bh)(a)=(1-\delta)h(a).
\]

Thus the unmarked mean operator is strictly subcritical in the Perron direction.

### Proposition 4.2 — first-regeneration renewal kernel is exactly rank one

Let

\[
F:=\sum_{k\ge0}B^kC,
\]

where `F(a,dx)` is the mean measure of the first marked descendants reached after any number of unmarked generations. Then

\[
(B^kC)(a,dx)
=
\delta(1-\delta)^k\frac{h(a)}{h(x)}\nu_0(dx),
\]

so summing the geometric series yields

\[
\boxed{F(a,dx)=\frac{h(a)}{h(x)}\nu_0(dx).}
\]

Define

\[
Z:=\int h(x)^{-1}\nu_0(dx)<\infty,
\qquad
\eta(dx):=Z^{-1}h(x)^{-1}\nu_0(dx).
\]

Then

\[
F(a,dx)=Z h(a)\eta(dx).
\]

In particular, if a regeneration vertex has type distributed as `eta`, the expected number of its first-regeneration descendants is exactly

\[
\int\eta(da)\int F(a,dx)
=Z\int h(a)\eta(da)=1.
\]

So the renewal skeleton is **critical and rank one at the mean level**.

The source's Cox/i.i.d.-child construction makes the next distribution-level step natural: after the childwise thinning, a marked endpoint has type law `eta` independent of its parent, so regeneration vertices should form an ordinary one-type critical GW skeleton with an i.i.d. type decoration. I am not using that sentence as a closure step yet. A full source-level proof must write the offspring point-process disintegration and verify that the number of first-regeneration descendants and their endpoint types have exactly the required i.i.d. branching factorisation. The rank-one mean identity above is already exact and does not depend on that remaining bookkeeping.

**B21 outcome:** the old direct de Raphélis citation fails on type-space scope, but its regenerative philosophy can be recreated inside the exact OU model. Continuous type is replaced by an artificial atom rather than by pretending `R` is countable.

## 5. B30 — localize the missing tightness one regeneration block at a time

The key estimate is stronger than a second moment: regeneration blocks have a uniform exponential size tail.

A **block** consists of one vertex and all descendants reachable through unmarked edges before the first marked edges. For a block rooted at type `a`, define the Perron mass

\[
T_a:=\sum_{v\text{ in the block}}h(A_v).
\]

Let

\[
Y_a:=\sum_{u\text{ unmarked child of the root}}h(A_u).
\]

By Lemma 4.1,

\[
\mathbb E_aY_a=(Bh)(a)=(1-\delta)h(a).
\]

The source hypothesis gives a uniform exponential moment for offspring count. Since `h<=h_+`, `Y_a` also has a uniform exponential moment. Therefore there exist `theta_0>0` and `C<infinity` such that, uniformly in `a` and `0<=theta<=theta_0`,

\[
\log\mathbb E_a e^{\theta Y_a}
\le \theta(1-\delta)h(a)+C\theta^2.
\]

Using `h(a)>=h_-`, choose `theta>0` small enough that

\[
C\theta\le \frac{\delta}{2}h_-.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_a e^{\theta Y_a}
\le e^{\theta(1-\delta/2)h(a)}
\quad\text{uniformly in }a.
}
\]

Set `s=theta delta/2`. For the block truncated at generation `r`, induction on `r` gives

\[
\mathbb E_a e^{sT_a^{(r)}}\le e^{\theta h(a)}.
\]

Indeed, conditional on the unmarked first generation and using the branching property,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_a e^{sT_a^{(r+1)}}
&\le e^{s h(a)}
\mathbb E_a\exp\!\left(\theta\sum_{u\text{ unmarked}}h(A_u)\right)\\
&\le e^{s h(a)}e^{\theta(1-\delta/2)h(a)}
=e^{\theta h(a)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Monotone convergence gives

\[
\boxed{
\sup_a\mathbb E_a e^{sT_a}\le e^{\theta h_+}<\infty.
}
\]

Finally `h>=h_-`, so block vertex count `V_a` satisfies

\[
V_a\le T_a/h_-,
\]

and hence for `lambda=s h_->0`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_a\mathbb E_a e^{\lambda V_a}<\infty.
}
\]

The block height is bounded by its size, so it has a uniform exponential tail as well.

### What this does and does not prove

This is precisely the kind of estimate that a mesh-discretisation proof was missing, but it is obtained without a mesh. It says all continuous-type excursions between regeneration marks are exponentially light in both size and height. Thus macroscopic `sqrt(n)` geometry must come from the critical regeneration skeleton, not from a single pathological type excursion.

It is **not yet** the conditioned GHP theorem. Conditioning the original tree on exactly `n` vertices conditions a random sum of regeneration-block masses, not the skeleton simply on its number of vertices. The remaining work is a weighted-conditioning invariance principle.

**B30 outcome:** the tightness problem is localized to a one-type critical skeleton with exponentially light decorations; within each block, uniform size/height tightness is proved.

## 6. The new reduced obstruction

The source's original missing statement was “uniform GHP tightness across continuous-type approximations”. After the regeneration argument, a sharper obligation is:

> **Weighted conditioned-skeleton problem.** Prove that the critical first-regeneration skeleton, with its i.i.d./Markovian regeneration-block vertex masses and graph-length decorations having uniform exponential moments, conditioned on total decorated mass `n`, has the Brownian CRT as its GHP limit after `n^{-1/2}` rescaling; then identify the deterministic scaling constant with the source's exact `Sigma_c`.

Three things must be completed before `E_CRT_GHP` may move to 1:

1. **Distribution-level skeleton law.** Write the exact Cox offspring point-process thinning and prove that regeneration vertices form a genuine one-type critical GW skeleton (not merely rank-one at the mean level), including finite nonzero offspring variance and the joint law of each block's mass/metric decoration.
2. **Conditioning transfer.** Prove a bivariate local limit/cyclic-lemma statement for `(skeleton offspring sum, total block mass)` that transfers conditioning on original total mass `n` to the critical skeleton, uniformly over the relevant skeleton sizes `k = n/E[V] + O(sqrt(n))`.
3. **GHP and constant identification.** Show exponentially small block diameters disappear after `sqrt(n)` scaling, compare uniform measure on original vertices with the weighted skeleton empirical measure, and compute the resulting distance factor. The factor must equal the source's `Sigma_c`; an unspecified positive constant is not enough.

The first two items are the real gap. Once they are established, the block exponential tail makes the metric-measure replacement plausible: the maximum block diameter among `O(n)` blocks is `O_P(log n)=o_P(sqrt n)`.
