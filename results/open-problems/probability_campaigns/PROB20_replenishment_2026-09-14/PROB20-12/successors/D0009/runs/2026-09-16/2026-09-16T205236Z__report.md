## 2. Exact source hypotheses recovered

Wang’s Section 2.2 lists the sufficient results that motivate Open Problems 8–9. In particular, class **(b)** is:

> `F` has a monotone density on its essential support `[a,b]`, with finite mean `mu`, and is `n`-CM iff
> 
> `a + (b-a)/n <= mu <= b - (b-a)/n`.

This is the class explicitly cited “for instance” in Open Problem 9 and is the class used by the recovered D0009-A001 obstruction. I therefore use it as the main nontrivial benchmark for `n>=3`. Nothing in this run claims that solving only class (b) would automatically settle every other sufficient family in the source.

The original Open Problem 9 is exactly: if `F` satisfies one of the sufficient conditions and hence is `n`-CM, can one simulate a sample from an `n`-complete mix with margins `F`?

The source itself explains why this is not already constructive: for the combinatorial/discretization results (including monotone densities), the dependence structure is unclear.

The 2011 Bin Wang–Ruodu Wang monotone-density paper is even more explicit. Corollary 2.9 gives the monotone-density mean condition; immediately afterward the authors state that they **did not construct** random variables `X_1,...,X_n ~ P` with constant sum, although existence is known. Its continuous proof first builds discrete centered approximants, proves each discrete approximant completely mixable, then invokes preservation under weak limits. That final compactness/weak-limit step does not itself specify a sampler for the limiting coupling.

### Theorem 1 — exact universal sampler for `n=2`

Let `F` be a distribution on `R` that is `2`-completely mixable with center `mu`. Then the reflection map

\[
R(x)=2\mu-x
\]

preserves `F`. Consequently the following is an exact sampler:

1. sample one exact marginal draw `X~F` (equivalently, take `U~Unif(0,1)` and `X=F^{-1}(U)` under a quantile interface);
2. set
   \[
   X_1=X,\qquad X_2=2\mu-X.
   \]

Then `X_1~F`, `X_2~F`, and `X_1+X_2=2 mu` identically.

#### Proof

Because `F` is `2`-CM with center `mu`, by definition there exist `Y_1,Y_2~F` with

\[
Y_1+Y_2=2\mu\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

Thus `Y_2=R(Y_1)` almost surely. Since both `Y_1` and `Y_2` have law `F`,

\[
R_{\#}F=F.
\]

Now sample `X~F`. Then `R(X)~F`, so the returned pair has both required marginals, and its sum is exactly `2 mu`. No approximation, rejection, limiting argument, or unknown coupling measure is used. QED.

This mechanism is classical rather than a novelty claim: Wang’s survey notes that `M_2` is fully characterized, and the 2011 monotone-density paper explicitly notes that in its normalized `n=2` case only the uniform law satisfies the relevant decreasing-density hypothesis. The value of this theorem here is to **measure the previously unmeasured frozen metric honestly** and to make the random primitive/procedure explicit.

For the monotone-density benchmark (b), the same conclusion can be seen directly: at `n=2` the mean condition forces the mean to be the midpoint of `[a,b]`; a monotone density with midpoint mean must be constant a.e., hence uniform. But the reflection proof above is strictly broader and avoids relying on this collapse.

No `n>=3` metric value is claimed.

## 4. B19 — make the sampler explicit and identify every random primitive

The exact `n=2` algorithm above uses exactly one random primitive: a marginal draw from `F` (or one uniform random real plus one quantile evaluation). The second coordinate is deterministic. This fully certifies `n_exact>=2`.

For `n>=3`, the source’s existence proofs do not yield a comparable list of random primitives. The recovered 2011 construction has two levels:

1. **finite discrete level:** a decreasing centered mass function is recursively decomposed into “simply mixable” integer mass functions; this is constructive in finite arithmetic once the discrete masses are specified;
2. **continuous level:** centered discrete distributions `P_N` converging weakly to `P` are built, each is shown `n`-CM, and closure of complete mixability under weak convergence is invoked.

The first level can in principle be sampled. The second level contains the exact-simulation gap: it gives no coherent random choice across resolutions `N` whose almost-sure limit can be sampled as a real vector.

This is more precise than merely saying “the proof uses discretization.” The missing datum is a **compatible refinement of the finite coupling measures**, not a proof that each finite approximation has some coupling.

## 5. B11 — the mixing-measure representation is algorithmically equivalent to the original sampling problem

Let

\[
H_\mu=\{a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in\mathbb R^n:\;a_1+\cdots+a_n=n\mu\}
\]

and let

\[
F_a=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{a_i}
\]

be the empirical `n`-point uniform law attached to `a`.

Wang’s Theorem 2 says that `F` is `n`-CM with center `mu` iff there is a probability measure `h` on `H_mu` satisfying

\[
F=\int_{H_\mu}F_a\,dh(a).
\tag{1}
\]

The prior attempt correctly identified `h` as the apparent sampling object. The useful refinement is that this is not merely a sufficient route; it is **sampling-equivalent** to Open Problem 9 up to a finite random permutation.

### Proposition 2 — exact `h`-sampling iff exact complete-mix sampling

Assume one can sample exactly `A~h` for an `h` satisfying (1). Independently sample a uniformly random permutation `Pi` of `{1,...,n}` and return

\[
X=(A_{\Pi(1)},\ldots,A_{\Pi(n)}).
\]

Then each `X_j~F` and `sum_j X_j=n mu` a.s.

Conversely, from any exact complete-mix sampler `X` with margins `F`, the law `h=L(X)` is supported on `H_mu` and satisfies (1) after the same uniform-coordinate averaging (or one may first symmetrize `X` by an independent uniform permutation).

#### Proof

The sum is permutation-invariant, so `sum_j X_j=n mu` immediately. For any Borel set `B` and any fixed output coordinate `j`, conditioning on `A=a` gives

\[
\Pr(X_j\in B\mid A=a)
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1_{\{a_i\in B\}}
=F_a(B).
\]

Integrating against `h` and using (1) yields `Pr(X_j in B)=F(B)`. This proves the forward direction.

For the converse, if `X` is an exact complete mix, then `X in H_mu` a.s. Let `h=L(X)`. If `J` is uniform on `{1,...,n}` and independent of `X`, then `X_J~F` because all coordinates have marginal `F`, while conditionally on `X=a`, the law of `X_J` is exactly `F_a`. Therefore `F=int F_a dh`. QED.

### Why Theorem 2 does not itself solve Open Problem 9

The reverse implication in Wang’s proof constructs `h` from the law of an **already existing complete mix** `X`: in the source, `h` is chosen from `P(X in da)`. Thus using Theorem 2 as “first sample `h`” is circular unless one supplies an independent constructive description of `h`.

This is now an exact obstruction statement:

\[
\boxed{\text{for exact simulation, constructing/sampling }h\text{ is equivalent in difficulty to sampling the complete mix itself.}}
\]

The representation remains extremely useful because it tells us what a successful constructive proof must output, but it is not a free reduction in complexity.

## 6. B10 — an exact refinement-lifting criterion that would turn the discrete proof into a sampler

The 2011 proof does contain genuinely constructive finite decompositions. The natural derandomization question is therefore: what additional compatibility would let those finite couplings converge **algorithmically**, rather than only existentially?

The following lemma gives a sharp sufficient bridge.

### Lemma 3 — summable refinement kernels give an exact sampler

Fix `n` and `mu`. For each `k>=0`, suppose:

- `h_k` is a finitely supported probability measure on `H_mu` that can be sampled exactly;
- its empirical barycenter is
  \[
  F_k=\int F_a\,dh_k(a);
  \]
- `F_k => F` weakly;
- there is an exactly samplable transition kernel `K_k(a,da')` such that if `A_k~h_k` and `A_{k+1}|A_k~K_k(A_k,.)`, then `A_{k+1}~h_{k+1}`;
- for deterministic numbers `epsilon_k>=0` with `sum_k epsilon_k<infinity`,
  \[
  \|A_{k+1}-A_k\|_\infty\le \epsilon_k\quad\text{a.s.}
  \tag{2}
  \]

Then recursively sampling `A_0,A_1,...` and taking the coordinatewise limit

\[
A=\lim_{k\to\infty}A_k
\]

produces an exact mixing vector on `H_mu`. An independent uniform random permutation of `A` is an exact `n`-complete mix with every marginal `F`.

Moreover, the deterministic tail bound

\[
\|A-A_k\|_\infty\le\sum_{j\ge k}\epsilon_j
\tag{3}
\]

provides a Cauchy-name exact-real implementation: any requested output precision is reached after finitely many refinements once the tail sum is below that precision.

#### Proof

By (2) and summability, `(A_k)` is almost surely Cauchy in `R^n`; let its limit be `A`. Every `A_k` lies in the closed hyperplane `H_mu`, hence so does `A`.

For every bounded continuous test function `phi:R->R`, almost-sure convergence of each coordinate and bounded convergence give

\[
\mathbb E\left[\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(A_i)\right]
=
\lim_{k\to\infty}
\mathbb E\left[\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi((A_k)_i)\right].
\]

The expectation on the right is `int phi dF_k` by definition of the empirical barycenter. Since `F_k=>F`, the limit is `int phi dF`. Therefore a uniformly random coordinate of `A` has law `F`. Uniformly permuting the coordinates makes every output coordinate have that law, while the sum remains exactly `n mu`. The tail estimate (3) is the triangle inequality. QED.

### What the 2011 monotone-density proof supplies, and what it does not

For a decreasing density, the proof constructs discrete centered laws from rounded variables plus a small centering correction, and shows each discrete law is `n`-CM via a recursive mass decomposition. It then invokes weak closure to obtain the continuous result.

It does **not** provide:

- a choice of finite mixing laws `h_k` that is compatible across levels;
- transition kernels `K_k` between successive decomposition measures;
- a summable almost-sure displacement bound such as (2).

The discrete recursive decomposition depends on current support endpoints and boundary slack. Refining the grid can alter those choices discontinuously, so simply “run the finite proof at resolution `2^k`” does not produce nested coupling vectors. This is the exact failure of the naive derandomization attempt in this run.

Lemma 3 therefore isolates a concrete next obligation: **construct a nested/refinable version of the finite decomposition**. That would solve the simulation problem for the class covered by the approximation, without ever needing a closed-form limiting density `h`.

## 7. A second circular route checked: the 2011 copula construction

The same 2011 paper later constructs a family of variance-minimizing copulas by splitting quantile space into a tail region with an explicit line-segment coupling and a central region whose variables are required to have a constant transformed sum. But the central variables are obtained by invoking Corollary 2.9 — exactly the monotone-density complete-mix existence theorem.

At the completely mixable point, the paper identifies the cutoff `c_n=0`. Then the explicit tail part disappears and the construction is entirely the unresolved central complete mix. Thus this section does **not** secretly contain an exact sampler for Open Problem 9; at the point relevant to D0009 it calls the same existence theorem whose coupling is missing.

## 10. Failures / non-results preserved

1. **“Just sample Wang’s `h`.”** Circular unless `h` is constructed independently; the source’s proof obtains `h` from the desired complete mix itself.
2. **“Use finer and finer discrete complete mixes.”** Existence of exact samplers at every resolution is not enough. Without a compatible coupling across resolutions, there is no exact limiting random vector that the algorithm can refine.
3. **“Use the 2011 variance-minimizing copula formula.”** At the complete-mix cutoff `c_n=0`, the explicit tail mechanism vanishes and the construction invokes the unknown central complete mix.
4. **Numerical rearrangement / MDP.** Useful for detection/approximation, not an exact constant-sum sampler with exact margins.
