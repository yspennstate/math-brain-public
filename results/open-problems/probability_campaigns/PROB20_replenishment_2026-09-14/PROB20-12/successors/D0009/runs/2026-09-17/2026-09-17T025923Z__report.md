## 3. Mathematical idea before code: boundary-line atoms

Normalize the decreasing `n=3` lower-mean boundary to support `[0,1]` and mean `1/3`. In the fixed-mean extreme reduction recovered earlier, this corresponds to

\[
m=\frac23,\qquad 0<a<\frac23,
\]

and the two-step extreme

\[
F_a
=
\frac{1/3}{1-a}\,U[0,a]
+
\frac{2/3-a}{1-a}\,U[0,1].
\tag{3.1}
\]

Its density is

\[
f_a(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1/3+a}{a},&0<x<a,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{2/3-a}{1-a},&a<x<1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Every exact complete mix must have sum one.

The constructive primitive is the following elementary line atom.

**Boundary-line lemma.** Let

\[
I_i=[c_i-w_i/2,c_i+w_i/2],\qquad w_i>0,
\]

and suppose

\[
c_1+c_2+c_3=S,
\qquad
w_3=w_1+w_2
\]

after relabeling so that `w_3` is the unique largest width. If `U~U[-1/2,1/2]`, define

\[
Y_1=c_1+w_1U,\qquad
Y_2=c_2+w_2U,\qquad
Y_3=c_3-w_3U.
\]

Then `Y_i~U[I_i]` exactly and

\[
Y_1+Y_2+Y_3
=c_1+c_2+c_3+(w_1+w_2-w_3)U
=S
\]

identically. If an independent uniform random permutation is then applied to the coordinates, the three final marginals are identical and equal to

\[
\frac13\left(U[I_1]+U[I_2]+U[I_3]\right),
\tag{3.3}
\]

while the sum remains `S`.

Thus a positive mixture of such atoms whose exchangeabilized marginal is `F_a` is already an explicit exact sampler: first sample the branch, then one uniform scalar `U`, then one uniform coordinate permutation.

## 6. B12 — what the interval-flow library conserves

Two conservation laws are intrinsic to every exchangeabilized boundary-line atom with sum one. If its grid-cell density vector is `d_0,...,d_11`, then exactly

\[
\frac1{12}\sum_{k=0}^{11}d_k=1
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{11}
 d_k\frac{2k+1}{2\cdot12^2}
=\frac13.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Equation (6.1) is mass conservation. Equation (6.2) is mean conservation: before exchangeabilization the average coordinate center is `(c_1+c_2+c_3)/3=1/3`. The exact checker verifies these identities for all 511 quotient atoms, not only the sparse certificates.

There is also a useful **grid-parity invariant**. Write the unique widest integer-grid width as

\[
w_3=w_1+w_2.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i(l_i+r_i)
=2(l_1+l_2+l_3)+w_1+w_2+w_3
=2(l_1+l_2+l_3+w_1+w_2),
\]

which is even. For a general target branch sum `S`, the grid equation is

\[
\sum_i(l_i+r_i)=2DS.
\]

Hence a denominator-`D` boundary-line library can be empty solely because `2DS` has the wrong parity; doubling the grid can remove that obstruction. This is a warning against mistaking finite-grid infeasibility for a mathematical obstruction to complete mixability. At the present boundary `S=1`, the parity condition is automatically satisfied for every `D`.

**B12 outcome:** mass and mean are the basic affine conserved quantities; parity is a discretization invariant. None supplies a source-level obstruction. The fact that every denominator-12 boundary breakpoint is feasible despite the earlier occupancy obstructions is further evidence that those old integer constraints belonged to the architecture, not to complete mixability.
