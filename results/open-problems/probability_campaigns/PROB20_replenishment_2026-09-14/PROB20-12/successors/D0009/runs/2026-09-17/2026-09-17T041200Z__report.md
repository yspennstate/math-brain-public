## 4. Mathematical idea before code: a two-branch moving-line architecture

For `b in (2/3,1)`, define

\[
c:=\frac{1-b}{2},\qquad d:=1-b=2c.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consider two exchangeabilized boundary-line branches.

**Branch A** uses the three interval marginals

\[
[0,c],\qquad[c,b],\qquad[d,b].
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Branch B** uses

\[
[0,b],\qquad[c,d],\qquad[c,b].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Each branch has an exact one-uniform constant-sum coupling with sum one. Indeed, in Branch A the widths are

\[
c,\quad b-c,\quad b-d,
\]

and

\[
b-c=c+(b-d).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The three interval centers also sum to one. In Branch B the widths are

\[
b,\quad c,\quad b-c,
\]

and

\[
b=c+(b-c),
\tag{4.5}
\]

with centers again summing to one.

For either branch, after labelling the unique widest interval as number 3 and the other two widths as `w_1,w_2`, draw

\[
U\sim U[-1/2,1/2]
\]

and set

\[
Y_1=c_1+w_1U,\qquad
Y_2=c_2+w_2U,\qquad
Y_3=c_3-(w_1+w_2)U.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Then every coordinate is exactly uniform on its stated interval and `Y_1+Y_2+Y_3=1` identically. Finally apply a uniform random permutation to the three coordinates. Hence any positive mixture of Branch A and Branch B is itself an explicit exact complete-mix sampler, provided its one-coordinate marginal matches the target.

The key question is whether the nuisance internal breakpoints `c,d` can cancel against the target's single breakpoint `a`.

## 5. B5 + B4 — two continuous exact source-valid curves

### 5.1 Curve C1: `b=1-a`, `0<a<1/3`

Set

\[
b=1-a,\qquad
p_1:=\frac{1-a}{2-3a}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then (4.1) gives

\[
c=\frac a2,\qquad d=a.
\]

Choose Branch A with probability `p_1` and Branch B with probability `1-p_1`, and in either branch use the exact line coupling (4.6) followed by uniform coordinate permutation.

The exchangeabilized one-coordinate density of the mixture is exactly constant on `(0,a)` and on `(a,b)`, with

\[
f_{\rm low}(x)=\frac{1}{3a(1-a)},
\qquad 0<x<a,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
f_{\rm high}(x)=
\frac{2-3a}{3(1-a)(1-2a)},
\qquad a<x<1-a.
\tag{5.3}

The internal breakpoint `a/2` cancels exactly. These are precisely the densities obtained from (3.1) with `b=1-a`:

\[
\alpha=\frac{1/3-a}{1-2a},

The branch probabilities are strictly positive throughout the claimed interval:

\[
p_1=\frac{1-a}{2-3a}\in(0,1),

1-p_1=\frac{1-2a}{2-3a}>0.
\tag{5.4}

Thus every source-valid extreme on the curve

\[
\boxed{b=1-a,\ 0<a<1/3}
