## 4. New mathematical idea before code — B1: boundary-line atoms

The previous layer language makes the generic obstruction look like a noninteger count of long layers. That obstruction disappears if the primitive object is not a choice of the original two layers, but a **triple of arbitrary subintervals** whose centers and lengths are compatible with a one-dimensional constant-sum line.

### Proposition 4.1 — exact boundary-line atom

Let

\[
I_i=[\ell_i,r_i],\qquad
w_i=r_i-\ell_i>0,\qquad
c_i=\frac{\ell_i+r_i}{2},
\]

and suppose, after relabeling, that

\[
w_3=w_1+w_2,\qquad c_1+c_2+c_3=S.
\tag{4}
\]

Draw `U ~ Unif[-1/2,1/2]` and set

\[
X_1=c_1+w_1U,\qquad
X_2=c_2+w_2U,\qquad
X_3=c_3-w_3U.
\tag{5}
\]

Then `X_i ~ U[I_i]` exactly and

\[
X_1+X_2+X_3=S
\quad\text{almost surely.}
\tag{6}
\]

**Proof.** Each affine map in (5) sends the uniform `U` bijectively to the corresponding interval. The random coefficient in the sum is `w_1+w_2-w_3=0`, while the constant part is the center sum `S`. QED.

Consequently, if a probability law `F` can be represented as

\[
F=\sum_j\lambda_j\frac13
\bigl(U[I_{j1}]+U[I_{j2}]+U[I_{j3}]\bigr),
\tag{7}
\]

where every triple is a boundary-line atom with the same center sum `S`, then there is an exact 3-complete-mix sampler: select `j` with probability `lambda_j`, sample the line atom by (5), and apply an independent uniformly random permutation of the three coordinates. Equation (7) gives each output coordinate marginal `F`, and (6) gives the deterministic sum.

This changes the remaining search from “choose how many original long layers occur” into a positive interval-flow decomposition problem.

## 5. B16 — a generic exact source-valid two-step sampler beyond integer occupancy

Take the source-valid boundary extreme

\[
a=\frac25,\qquad m=\frac23,\qquad b=1.
\tag{8}
\]

Its layer weights are

\[
\alpha=\frac59,\qquad \beta=\frac49,
\qquad 3\beta=\frac43\notin\mathbb Z.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus it is not in the previous exact integer-*layer*-occupancy strata. It is also useful to rewrite a `b=1` extreme by its actual low/high support regions. Let

\[
q=m-a.
\]

A direct density calculation gives

\[
F=(1-q)U[0,a]+qU[a,1].
\tag{10}
\]

Here

\[
q=\frac4{15},\qquad 3q=\frac45\notin\mathbb Z,
\tag{11}
\]

so even a deterministic count of low/high *regions* cannot explain the construction below.

The exact target density is

\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
11/6,&0\le x\le2/5,\\
4/9,&2/5<x\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]

The following seven boundary-line branches give an exact sampler. In each row, select the row with the displayed probability, sample the three interval uniforms by Proposition 4.1, then uniformly permute coordinates:

| weight | interval 1 | interval 2 | interval 3 |
|---:|---|---|---|
| `1/30` | `[0,1/10]` | `[0,1/10]` | `[4/5,1]` |
| `1/4` | `[0,1/5]` | `[0,3/10]` | `[1/2,1]` |
| `1/60` | `[0,1/5]` | `[1/10,1/5]` | `[7/10,4/5]` |
| `2/5` | `[0,3/10]` | `[0,3/10]` | `[2/5,1]` |
| `1/20` | `[1/10,3/10]` | `[1/5,3/10]` | `[2/5,7/10]` |
| `1/20` | `[1/10,3/10]` | `[3/10,2/5]` | `[2/5,1/2]` |
| `1/5` | `[1/5,2/5]` | `[3/10,2/5]` | `[3/10,2/5]` |

The weights sum to one. For every row, the three interval centers sum to `1=3 mu`, and the longest width is exactly the sum of the other two widths, so every row has deterministic sum one by Proposition 4.1.

To verify the marginal, refine `[0,1]` into the ten cells `[k/10,(k+1)/10]`. Summing `weight * (1/3) * 1/(interval length)` over all interval appearances covering each cell gives exactly

\[
\underbrace{\frac{11}{6},\ldots,\frac{11}{6}}_{4\text{ cells}},
\underbrace{\frac49,\ldots,\frac49}_{6\text{ cells}},
\tag{13}
\]

which is precisely (12). Therefore this is an exact 3-complete mix for a source-valid two-step extreme with both `3 beta` and `3q` noninteger.

This is the first construction on this D0009 route that demonstrates concretely that the noninteger occupancy obstruction is an artifact of the original layer architecture, not an obstruction to an exact finite sampler.

### 5.1 Exact rigidity of this particular seven-branch topology

The example does **not** secretly solve a parameter neighborhood by simply sliding its breakpoints. Fix its incidence pattern and write the internal breakpoints as

\[
x_1,x_2,x_3,A,x_5,x_6,x_7
\]

with common branch center sum `S`. Impose, for each of the seven rows, both the corresponding boundary-width equation and the center-sum equation. This is a 14-equation rational linear system in eight unknowns. Exact Gaussian elimination has rank eight and the unique solution

\[
(x_1,x_2,x_3,A,x_5,x_6,x_7,S)
=
\left(\frac1{10},\frac15,\frac3{10},\frac25,\frac12,\frac7{10},\frac45,1\right).
\tag{14}
\]

Thus this exact incidence topology is **sporadic/rigid**. A source-general construction needs a topology change or a richer family of interval flows; simply differentiating this seven-branch pattern cannot cover the two-parameter extreme domain.

## 6. B27 — fix the topology first: two continuous exact arcs

There is nevertheless a simple topology that covers two one-parameter arcs exactly. For `b=1`, keep the region-mixture form (10), where `q=m-a`.

### Arc A: `q=1/3`

Then

\[
F=\frac23U[0,a]+\frac13U[a,1],
\qquad m=a+\frac13.
\tag{15}
\]

Source validity `m>=2/3` and `m<1` give

\[
\frac13\le a<\frac23.
\tag{16}
\]

Take the three *nonidentical* margins

\[
U[0,a],\quad U[0,a],\quad U[a,1].
\tag{17}
\]

Their lengths are `a,a,1-a`; throughout (16) the uniform-interval triangle criterion

\[
2\max(a,a,1-a)\le 1+a
\tag{18}
\]

holds. Their centers sum to

\[
\frac a2+\frac a2+\frac{a+1}{2}
=\frac{3a+1}{2}
=\frac{3m}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]

The constructive proof of Wang–Wang Theorem 3.1 gives an exact joint mix for any three uniform intervals satisfying the length condition: the boundary case is a one-uniform line coupling, and the interior case is decomposed into six explicitly weighted boundary-uniform triples. Apply that exact construction to (17), then uniformly permute the three coordinates. Each final coordinate has exactly the mixture (15), and the sum is deterministic `3m/2`.

### Arc B: `q=2/3`

Likewise

\[
F=\frac13U[0,a]+\frac23U[a,1],
\qquad m=a+\frac23,
\qquad 0<a<\frac13,
\tag{20}
\]

and use the margins

\[
U[0,a],\quad U[a,1],\quad U[a,1].
\tag{21}
\]

Their widths are `a,1-a,1-a`, which satisfy the uniform triangle condition for every `a>0`, and their centers sum exactly `3m/2`. The same constructive uniform-interval sampler plus random permutation gives an exact complete mix for every law on this arc.

These arcs are not merely the old layer-integer strata: generically their layer parameter

\[
\beta=\frac{q}{1-a}
\tag{22}
\]

is not in `{0,1/3,2/3,1}`. Thus they provide continuous new exact source-valid subclasses, though still not the full two-dimensional domain.
