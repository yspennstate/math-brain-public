## 3. (a) The exact statement that blocks

The recovered fixed-mean layer reduction turns the decreasing-density `n=3` source class into a two-parameter extreme kernel. Let

\[
0<a<m<1,\qquad m\ge \frac23,
\]

and define

\[
F_{a}^{(m)}
=
\frac{1-m}{1-a}\,U[0,a]
+
\frac{m-a}{1-a}\,U[0,1].
\]

Its mean is `m/2`, so an exact three-complete mix must have deterministic sum `3m/2`.

The source-matching blocking statement is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{For every }0<a<m<1,\ m\ge\frac23,\
\text{ explicitly sample }(X_1,X_2,X_3)\text{ with }
X_1+X_2+X_3=\frac{3m}{2}\ \text{a.s. and }X_i\sim F_a^{(m)}\ (i=1,2,3).
}
\tag{B}
\]

Equivalently, construct an explicitly samplable probability kernel `Pi_{a,m}` on

\[
\Delta_{a,m}
=
\left\{x\in[0,1]^3:\ x_1+x_2+x_3=\frac{3m}{2}\right\}
\]

whose three coordinate pushforwards all equal `F_a^{(m)}`.

A solution of (B), with parameter dependence explicit enough to sample after drawing the layer-extreme selector, would lift through the already recovered fixed-mean extreme decomposition to every decreasing source-valid density at `n=3`; reflection handles the increasing class. That is the first route presently capable of moving the frozen metric from 2 to 3.

## 4. (b) Every recovered route and the precise reason it did not close (B)

### Route 1 — hyperplane mixing law `h` (`D0009-A001`)

Representation of an existing complete mix as a mixture of finite constant-sum orbit laws is mathematically valid, and if `h` were explicitly samplable then the simulation problem would be solved. The failure is algorithmic circularity: the recovered construction of `h` is obtained from a complete mix that one already assumes exists. Open Problem 9 asks for the sampler itself, so “sample from `h`” is not an algorithm until `h` is constructed independently from `F`.

### Route 2 — source proof by discretization and weak closure

The Wang–Wang monotone-density existence proof establishes source-valid existence through discrete/mass decompositions and limiting arguments. The gap for Open Problem 9 is not existence but **compatible exact transitions**. Finite approximants can be sampled, but the recovered proof does not provide a nested coupling of successive approximants whose almost-sure limit is an exactly samplable complete mix with marginal exactly `F`.

### Route 3 — exact refinement-kernel program (B19/B11/B10)

## 7. B21 — who else has this shape?

The closest abstract formulation is a support-constrained marginal problem: find a measure on the plane

\[
x_1+x_2+x_3=3m/2
\]

with three prescribed identical marginals. Classical marginal-problem duality, including Kellerer's framework, explains why support constraints can be tested by families of dual inequalities. That perspective is useful for impossibility certificates for a *restricted* kernel family, but dual existence by itself does not produce the exact sampler demanded by Open Problem 9.

The closest **constructive** solved mechanism remains joint mixability of uniform intervals. For three uniform interval marginals, the known interval-length condition comes with finite boundary decompositions/line couplings. This is exactly the primitive used successfully in the solved `q=1/3` and `q=2/3` arcs and in the boundary-line atom language.

The source-general two-step problem differs at one critical point: all three *final* marginals must be the same two-step mixture. What is missing is a positive decomposition

\[
F_a^{(m)}
=
\int \frac13\bigl(U[I_1]+U[I_2]+U[I_3]\bigr)\,\Lambda_{a,m}(dI_1\,dI_2\,dI_3)
\]

where every interval triple is jointly mixable at the common center `3m/2` and where `\Lambda_{a,m}` itself is explicitly samplable from `(a,m)` over the entire source-valid parameter domain.

The Rüschendorf–Uckelmann symmetric-unimodal construction is another nearby solved case: symmetry supplies a rigid reflection/orbit mechanism. The decreasing two-step extremes in (B) lack that symmetry, which is why the reflection trick that completely settles `n=2` and symmetric cases does not directly transfer.

**Outcome of B21:** the literature analogy suggests that the missing theorem is not another existence/duality statement. It is a *positive decomposition theorem with explicit branch law* for the two-step family.

## 8. (c) What a successful proof must contain that no recovered route contains

A metric-moving proof at `n=3` must contain all of the following in one construction:

1. **Full two-parameter coverage.** It must work for every `0<a<m<1` with `m>=2/3`, including nonintegral `3q` and `3beta`.
2. **Positive, explicit branch law.** Branch probabilities or a continuous selector law must be explicit and nonnegative throughout each parameter region; compactness/Choquet existence alone is insufficient.
3. **Exact branch coupling.** Conditional on the branch, the three coordinates must be sampled by a finite or exact-real procedure with deterministic sum `3m/2`.
4. **Exact marginal identity.** Integrating the branch law must reproduce the two-step density exactly, not in a mesh limit or in Wasserstein distance.
5. **A samplable parameter atlas.** If the topology changes, the parameter plane must be partitioned into explicitly decidable regions with formulas on each, or replaced by one continuous selector that can be sampled exactly.
6. **Compatibility with the fixed-mean layer selector.** The two-step kernel must depend measurably/algorithmically on `(a,m)` so the already recovered extreme decomposition can be sampled and lifted to a general monotone density.

No recovered route currently supplies items 2 and 5 simultaneously over the full domain.

## 10. (e) Nearest solved problem and the ingredient it has that D0009 lacks

The nearest solved constructive problem is: **jointly mix three prescribed uniform intervals whose lengths satisfy the joint-mixability criterion**. Its proof has a finite geometric decomposition into boundary cases in which a one-parameter line coupling gives the exact marginals and exact sum.

D0009 lacks the *outer positive decomposition*: given one two-step marginal, choose a positive distribution over triples of such uniform intervals so that each coordinate averages back to the same two-step marginal. The inner interval coupling is known; the outer interval-flow law is not.

That distinction is strategically important. Re-proving the inner uniform theorem or adding finer discretizations will not move `n_exact`. The next effort should target the outer flow directly.
