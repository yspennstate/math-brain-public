`R = {ij : i<j, i+j<=6}`,

`U = {ij : i<j, i+j>=8}`.

Explicitly,

```text

M = {16,25,34},
U = {26,35,36,45,46,56}.
```

Thus the 15 coordinate bivectors split as `6+3+6`, and `M` is the anti-diagonal perfect matching.

For a rank-two plane `H_c`, let

`mu_c(ij)=|p_c(ij)|^2`

be its squared Pluecker coordinate / projection-DPP edge probability. The representative of the new 270-orbit found in the preceding run is, after the recorded coordinate relabelling,

`mu_0(M union U)+mu_1(M union U)+mu_2(U) <= 2`.                 (2.1)

Since every rank-two projection DPP has total edge mass one, (2.1) is exactly equivalent to

`mu_0(R)+mu_1(R)+mu_2(R union M) >= 1`,                         (2.2)

and therefore to the particularly clean form

**`mu_2(U) <= mu_0(R)+mu_1(R)`.**                               (2.3)

## 3. NEW exact support lemma

Let `A,B,C` be the three pairwise-disjoint edges of a perfect matching of `{1,...,6}`, so `C` is the complement of `A union B`.

### Lemma 3.1 — a U complement forces an R input

If `C in U`, then at least one of `A,B` lies in `R`.

### Proof

If neither `A` nor `B` lies in `R`, then their endpoint sums are each at least 7. Hence the sum of the four vertices covered by `A union B` is at least 14. The sum of all six vertex labels is

`1+2+3+4+5+6=21`,

so the endpoint sum of the complementary edge `C` is at most `21-14=7`. But `C in U` means its endpoint sum is at least 8. Contradiction. QED.

This tiny additive observation is the main structural reason the new 270-facet has an exterior-algebra attack at all.

## 4. Exterior-algebra formulation

Let `V=C^6` with coordinate orthonormal basis `e_1,...,e_6`. For the orthogonal decomposition

`V=H_0 direct-sum H_1 direct-sum H_2`, `dim H_c=2`,

choose unit decomposable top forms

`alpha in Lambda^2 H_0`, `beta in Lambda^2 H_1`, `gamma in Lambda^2 H_2`.

Write `P_R,P_M,P_U` for the orthogonal coordinate projections in `Lambda^2 V` onto the spans of the edge basis vectors indexed by `R,M,U`. Then

`mu_0(R)=||P_R alpha||^2`,

`mu_1(R)=||P_R beta||^2`,

`mu_2(U)=||P_U gamma||^2`.

Because the three planes are mutually orthogonal and fill `V`, `alpha wedge beta` is a unit decomposable 4-form spanning `Lambda^4(H_0 direct-sum H_1)`. Its Hodge complement spans `Lambda^2 H_2`. Thus, up to a unit scalar (and the harmless conjugation convention in the complex Hodge star),

`gamma = star(alpha wedge beta)`.

Only coordinate absolute values and Hilbert norms are used below, so this convention does not affect any statement.

Lemma 3.1 implies the exact coordinate-support identity

`P_U star( (I-P_R)alpha wedge (I-P_R)beta ) = 0`.              (4.1)

Indeed, a coordinate monomial contributing to a `U` output corresponds to two disjoint input edges whose complement is the output edge, so at least one input must be in `R`.

## 5. NEW theorem: exact determinantal validity on the opposite-Schubert boundary

### Theorem 5.1

For every real or complex orthogonal rank-(2,2,2) decomposition of `C^6`, the new 270-facet (2.3) is valid whenever either

`mu_0(R)=0`

or

`mu_1(R)=0`.

More strongly:

- if `mu_1(R)=0`, then `mu_2(U) <= mu_0(R)`;
- if `mu_0(R)=0`, then `mu_2(U) <= mu_1(R)`.

### Proof

Assume first that `mu_1(R)=0`. Then

`P_R beta=0`.

By the support identity (4.1), all `U`-coordinates of `star(alpha wedge beta)` coming from `(I-P_R)alpha` vanish. Hence

`P_U star(alpha wedge beta)`

`= P_U star( (P_R alpha) wedge beta )`.                         (5.1)

Now use a standard but elementary contraction fact: if `beta` is a unit decomposable 2-form, the map

`L_beta : Lambda^2 V -> Lambda^4 V`, `eta -> eta wedge beta`

has operator norm at most one. To see this, rotate an orthonormal basis so that

`beta=f_5 wedge f_6`.

Wedge multiplication by `beta` kills every component of `eta` containing `f_5` or `f_6` and is an isometry on `Lambda^2 span{f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4}`. Therefore

`||eta wedge beta|| <= ||eta||`.                                (5.2)

Hodge star is an isometry and `P_U` is an orthogonal projection. Applying (5.2) to (5.1) gives

`||P_U gamma||`

`= ||P_U star(alpha wedge beta)||`

`<= ||(P_R alpha) wedge beta||`

`<= ||P_R alpha||`.

Squaring yields

`mu_2(U) <= mu_0(R)`,                                           (5.3)

which is stronger than (2.3) because `mu_1(R)=0`.

The case `mu_0(R)=0` is symmetric in `alpha,beta`. QED.

### Interpretation

The theorem proves the entire new 270-facet on the union of two nontrivial boundary strata of the orthogonal flag manifold: whenever either `H_0` or `H_1` has Pluecker support entirely in `M union U`, the facet is automatically satisfied, with a one-sided stronger estimate.

This is not a proof of the interior case `mu_0(R),mu_1(R)>0`, so no full determinantal-validity claim is made.

## 6. NEW triangular matrix reformulation

The same `R/M/U` split has a useful matrix meaning.

For a rank-two plane with orthonormal basis columns `u,v`, form the skew-symmetric Pluecker matrix

`X = u v^T - v u^T`.

Thus for `i<j`,

`X_ij = p(ij)`

and `|X_ij|^2=mu(ij)`.

Let `J` be the reversal permutation matrix, `J e_j=e_{7-j}`, and define

`Y=XJ`.

For each edge `i<j`, the two matrix entries carrying its Pluecker coefficient are

`Y_{i,7-j}=X_ij`,

`Y_{j,7-i}=-X_ij`.

Both are:

- strictly upper triangular if `i+j<7`;
- diagonal if `i+j=7`;
- strictly lower triangular if `i+j>7`.

Consequently

`||Y_upper||_F^2 = 2 mu(R)`,

`||Y_diag||_F^2  = 2 mu(M)`,

`||Y_lower||_F^2 = 2 mu(U)`.                                   (6.1)

For the three planes write `Y_c=X_c J`. Orthogonality of the planes implies

`X_c X_d^*=0` for `c!=d`,

and each `Y_c` is a rank-two partial isometry with Frobenius norm squared two. In particular the new 270-facet is exactly

**`||Y_0,upper||_F^2 + ||Y_1,upper||_F^2 >= ||Y_2,lower||_F^2`.** (6.2)

This reformulation turns the facet into a triangular-energy inequality for three mutually orthogonal rank-two partial-isometry pieces. It is likely better suited to an operator/flag proof than the original 24-term marginal inequality.

## 7. A stronger local operator inequality that would finish the 270-orbit

Fix a unit decomposable `beta` and let

`W=H_1^perp`.

On `Lambda^2 W` define the isometry

`T_beta(eta)=star(eta wedge beta)`.

For every admissible `alpha in Lambda^2 W`, the desired inequality is

`||P_U T_beta alpha||^2 <= ||P_R alpha||^2 + ||P_R beta||^2`.

Therefore the following operator inequality would be sufficient, and in fact is stronger because it would hold for every 2-form in `Lambda^2 W`, not merely decomposable `alpha`:

`T_beta^* P_U T_beta <= (P_R compressed to Lambda^2 W) + ||P_R beta||^2 I`.  (7.1)

The boundary theorem above is exactly the `||P_R beta||=0` endpoint of this picture. I do **not** have a proof of (7.1) in this checkpoint, so it is recorded as the next sharp proof obligation rather than a theorem.

## 9. NEW signed-Laplace reduction to 36 strict states

Retain the canonical signed full-marginal solution from the earlier exterior-algebra work. For a deterministic colored perfect matching `I`, define

`h(I)=1_{edge_0 in R}+1_{edge_1 in R}+1_{edge_2 in R union M}`. (9.1)

The combinatorial 270-facet says `h(I)>=1`. Exact deterministic classification gives only the values

`h(I)=1` or `2`.

```text

RUR : 8 states
URR : 8 states
RUM : 6 states

```

for a total of 36.

Let `z(I)` be the canonical Laplace/Jacobi signed coupling from the earlier checkpoint. It satisfies

`sum_I z(I)=1`

and has exactly the required one-color marginals, although individual `z(I)` need not be nonnegative. Therefore the determinantal facet slack is

`mu_0(R)+mu_1(R)+mu_2(R union M)-1`

`= sum_I z(I)(h(I)-1)`

`= sum_{I strict} z(I)`.                                        (9.2)

Hence universal determinantal validity of the new 270-facet is **equivalent** to the nonnegativity of one explicit 36-term partial Laplace sum. Equation (9.2) is exact; it does not assume that the canonical signed solution itself is a probability coupling.

## 10. Failed / non-promoted routes this iteration

Several tempting shortcuts do not presently yield a proof and are kept separate from the theorem above.

1. **Pure one-body conservation is too weak.** The facet cannot be deduced merely from total edge mass and aggregate vertex-degree identities; its content is genuinely higher-order.

2. **Universal compound-Birkhoff replacement is too strong / insufficiently targeted.** Earlier task work already warned that realizing all compound-minor marginals with one random permutation is stronger than Lyons's three-block problem. The current 270-facet should not be attacked by assuming that stronger property.

3. **A naive mixed-determinant intermediate inequality is false.** A route that tries to dominate the left side of sparse form (8.3) by a single determinant of `(P_0+P_1)[34]` and then bound the resulting `P_2` correction by only the `15,56` terms fails in generic numerical exploration. It is not used anywhere in the proof ledger.

4. **The full operator inequality (7.1) is unproved.** It is a sharply stated conjectural strengthening motivated by the exact boundary argument, not an established result.

No numerical search is treated as universal proof or independent verification.
