## 2. K6 colored-perfect-matching slack cone

`Omega={sigma:{0,...,5}->{0,1,2}: |sigma^{-1}(c)|=2 for c=0,1,2}`.

There are 90 states. Write `x_sigma in {0,1}^45` for the colored-edge incidence vector: for each color, exactly one of the 15 K6 edge coordinates is 1.

Let `L` be the vector space of affine evaluation vectors on the 90 states,

`L={ (b-a.x_sigma)_(sigma in Omega) : a in R^45, b in R }`.

Exact row reduction gives

`dim L=38`.

Fix the base vertex

`v0=001122`.

A supporting inequality `a.x<=b` through `v0` is represented by its nonnegative slack vector

`s(sigma)=b-a.x_sigma`.

Hence the local normal cone, in slack coordinates, is

`N(v0)={s in L: s>=0, s(v0)=0}`.

Because every pair of K6 states is adjacent, for any `v!=v0` the corresponding normal wall is

`W(v)={s in L: s>=0, s(v0)=s(v)=0}`.                 (2.1)

It has dimension 36.

The stabilizer

`G0=Stab(v0) <= S6 x S3`

has order 48 and has seven orbits on the other 89 states, of sizes

`2,3,8,12,16,24,24`.                                            (2.2)

The earlier exact certificate checked that, on a representative of each orbit, the known incident facet slacks have rank 36. What was not known was whether their **positive cone** fills the wall.

## 3. NEW reduction — exact fixed-wall cone

For a wall representative `v`, define the ordered-edge stabilizer

`G(v)=Stab(v0,v)`.

Let

`W(v)^G={s in W(v): g.s=s for every g in G(v)}`.                 (3.1)

If the `G(v)`-orbits on `Omega` are `O_1,...,O_q`, an invariant slack is determined by its orbit values

`y_j=s|_{O_j}`.

The fixed affine space `L^G` is obtained exactly by Reynolds-averaging the 46 basic affine functions `1` and the 45 colored-edge indicators. If `D` denotes a rational basis matrix for those averaged functions evaluated on the state orbits, then imposing

`s(v0)=s(v)=0`

produces a rational matrix `S_v` whose column space is the fixed wall subspace. Therefore

`W(v)^G = { S_v z : S_v z >= 0 }`.                              (3.2)

This converts the symmetry-fixed part of a 36-dimensional wall into a very small exact orthant section.

If `d=dim W(v)^G`, every extreme ray of (3.2) has at least `d-1` linearly independent active coordinate inequalities. Thus its rays can be enumerated exactly by checking every `(d-1)`-subset of orbit-coordinate rows of `S_v`, solving the resulting rational nullspace, and retaining the one-dimensional nonnegative solutions. No floating-point cone enumeration is needed.

## 5. Proof that no invariant missing facet can occur on these walls

Suppose `r` were an undiscovered extreme ray of the full wall `W(v)` and suppose it were fixed by `G(v)`. By (4.2), (4.3), or (4.4),

`r = sum_j alpha_j * Av_G(f_j)`,                                 (5.1)

with `alpha_j>=0` and each `f_j` a known incident facet ray. Expanding the Reynolds averages gives

`r = sum_(j,g) beta_(j,g) * g.f_j`,                              (5.2)

where every `g.f_j` is itself a known ray of the **full** wall and every coefficient is nonnegative.

But `r` is assumed extreme in the full pointed cone `W(v)`. Therefore every summand with positive coefficient in (5.2) must be collinear with `r`. Consequently `r` is already one of the known facet rays, contrary to it being missing.

Therefore:

**Theorem 5.1.** On each of the wall orbit types `2`, `3`, and `8`, there is no missing facet ray that is invariant under the ordered-edge stabilizer `Stab(v0,v)`.

Equivalently, any genuinely missing K6 facet on one of these wall types must **break the full symmetry of that wall**.

For the orbit-size-8 wall the statement is sharper: every invariant wall slack is a nonnegative combination of averages of coordinate nonnegativity facets.

## 7. Why this advances the seven-wall program

The previous wall result only established

`span_R(known incident rays)=linear wall space`

for all seven wall types. A missing extreme ray could still have lived in the same linear span but outside the known positive cone.

This run rules out that possibility completely on the **maximally symmetric slice** of the first three wall types. Thus any future separating certificate for those walls must be symmetry-breaking. This removes the most obvious source of missing rays and gives a natural hierarchy for the remaining exact attack:

1. first complete the fixed cones for successively smaller subgroups of `G(v)`;
2. if these continue to be generated by known rays, force any missing facet to have increasingly large stabilizer orbit;
3. eventually either cover the full wall or obtain a low-symmetry exact separating ray.

The first three wall types are particularly attractive because their ordered-edge stabilizers have orders 24, 16, and 6, respectively, and their full fixed cones have dimensions only 4, 6, and 7.

The remaining wall-orbit sizes `12,16,24,24` have fixed-wall dimensions `13,12,19,25` under the full ordered-edge stabilizer; brute-force active-set enumeration is no longer the right exact method there. They should be attacked by exact dual certificates, chamber decompositions, or subgroup recursion rather than uncontrolled global double description.
