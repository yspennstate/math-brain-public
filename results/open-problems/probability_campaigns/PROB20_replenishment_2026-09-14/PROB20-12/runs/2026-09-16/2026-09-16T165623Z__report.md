# 3. B11 — exact dual separating inequality in splitting-cost form

This is the principal new mathematical result of the run.

Assume first that \(E\) is finite and set

\[
r_i=\dim H_i,\qquad r=r_1+r_2.
\]

Write

\[
p_1(A)=\mathbf P^{H_1}(A),\quad |A|=r_1,
\]
\[
p_2(B)=\mathbf P^{H_2}(B),\quad |B|=r_2,
\]
\[
p_H(U)=\mathbf P^H(U),\quad |U|=r.
\]

A desired coupling is a nonnegative array \(q(A,B)\) over disjoint \(A,B\) satisfying

\[
\sum_{B:B\cap A=\varnothing}q(A,B)=p_1(A),
\tag{3.1}
\]

\[
\sum_{A:A\cap B=\varnothing}q(A,B)=p_2(B),
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{A\sqcup B=U}q(A,B)=p_H(U).
\tag{3.3}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — optimal-splitting dual characterization

The finite coupling exists if and only if, for every pair of real functions

\[
f:\binom E{r_1}\to\mathbb R,
\qquad
g:\binom E{r_2}\to\mathbb R,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{A\sim p_1}f(A)+\mathbb E_{B\sim p_2}g(B)
\ge
\mathbb E_{U\sim p_H}
\min_{\substack{A\subset U\\ |A|=r_1}}
\{f(A)+g(U\setminus A)\}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

The primal feasibility problem consists of (3.1)–(3.3) and \(q\ge0\). By the finite-dimensional Farkas alternative, infeasibility is equivalent to the existence of real row potentials \(f(A),g(B),h(U)\) such that

\[
f(A)+g(B)+h(A\sqcup B)\ge0
\tag{3.5}
\]

for every disjoint pair, while

\[
\sum_Ap_1(A)f(A)+\sum_Bp_2(B)g(B)+\sum_Up_H(U)h(U)<0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For fixed \(f,g\), condition (3.5) is equivalent, separately for each \(U\), to

\[
h(U)\ge-
\min_{\substack{A\subset U\\|A|=r_1}}
\{f(A)+g(U\setminus A)\}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Because \(p_H(U)\ge0\), the smallest possible left side of (3.6) over admissible \(h\) is obtained by equality in (3.7). Substitution gives exactly the negation of (3.4). Hence absence of a Farkas separator is equivalent to (3.4) for every \(f,g\), which is equivalent to primal feasibility. QED.

This gives a transparent interpretation of every exact finite counterexample certificate: it is a pair of colour-specific set-costs whose expected separate cost is strictly smaller than the expected optimal cost of splitting the union.

The frozen normalization is recovered by adjoining

\[
\max\{|f(A)|,|g(B)|,|h(U)|\}\le1
\]

after a common rescaling. Thus any strict rational/algebraic violation of (3.4) can be converted to a positive normalized Farkas margin without changing sign.

# 4. New search reduction: whole classes of duals can never refute Lyons

The splitting-cost form rules out several tempting certificate families before any computation.

## Proposition 4.1 — one-sided duals never separate

If \(g\equiv0\), then (3.4) always holds. Likewise if \(f\equiv0\).

### Proof

Since \(H_1\subset H\), Lyons’s stochastic domination theorem gives a monotone coupling \((A,U)\) with

\[
A\sim p_1,\qquad U\sim p_H,\qquad A\subset U\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

Therefore for arbitrary \(f\),

\[
\min_{\substack{A'\subset U\\|A'|=r_1}}f(A')\le f(A)
\]

pointwise. Taking expectations gives (3.4) with \(g=0\). The \(f=0\) case follows from \(H_2\subset H\). QED.

So a counterexample cannot be detected by a cost attached to only one colour. It must encode genuinely coupled information about how the two colours split the union.

## Proposition 4.2 — additive two-colour duals never separate

Suppose

\[
f(A)=\sum_{i\in A}a_i,
\qquad
g(B)=\sum_{i\in B}b_i.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then (3.4) always holds.

### Proof

Put \(c_i=a_i-b_i\). For a fixed union \(U\),

\[
\min_{A\subset U,|A|=r_1}\{f(A)+g(U\setminus A)\}
=
\sum_{i\in U}b_i+
\min_{A\subset U,|A|=r_1}\sum_{i\in A}c_i.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The latter minimum is the sum of the \(r_1\) smallest \(c_i\) among \(i\in U\).

For a projection DPP the one-point inclusion probabilities satisfy

\[
\mathbf P(i\in X_j)=(P_{H_j})_{ii},
\]

and since \(P_H=P_{H_1}+P_{H_2}\),

\[
\mathbb E_{p_H}\sum_{i\in U}b_i
=
\mathbb E_{p_1}\sum_{i\in A}b_i+
\mathbb E_{p_2}\sum_{i\in B}b_i.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus (3.4) reduces to

\[
\sum_i c_i(P_{H_1})_{ii}
\ge
\mathbb E_{U\sim p_H}
\min_{A\subset U,|A|=r_1}\sum_{i\in A}c_i.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Use any monotone coupling \(A\subset U\) of \(p_1\) with \(p_H\). Pointwise,

\[
\min_{A'\subset U,|A'|=r_1}\sum_{i\in A'}c_i
\le
\sum_{i\in A}c_i.
\]

Taking expectations gives (4.4). QED.

### Consequence for counterexample search

Any positive exact Farkas certificate must use **both** colours and must be nonlinear beyond one-site additive statistics. This is a real reduction of the dual search space. Pairwise/higher-order set interactions are not optional decoration; they are logically necessary.

For \(r_1=r_2=2\), arbitrary \(f,g\) are edge-cost functions. Then the inner problem in (3.4) is a coloured minimum perfect-matching split of each four-set \(U\). This explains why the earlier K6 facet programme naturally produced matching-type inequalities: in that profile it is not an ad hoc language but the full finite Farkas dual.

# 6. B19 — explicit monotone-selection algorithm and the exact failure mechanism

A natural constructive route is to use stochastic domination first and ask what remains.

Because \(H_1\subset H\), choose any monotone coupling

\[
(X_1,U),\qquad X_1\sim p_1,\ U\sim p_H,\ X_1\subset U.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then set

\[
X_2^{\rm alg}=U\setminus X_1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This automatically has all of the following properties:

1. \(X_1\cap X_2^{\rm alg}=\varnothing\);
2. \(X_1\cup X_2^{\rm alg}=U\sim p_H\);
3. \(|X_2^{\rm alg}|=r_2\) almost surely.

The only missing property is

\[
X_2^{\rm alg}\stackrel{?}{\sim}p_2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Write the monotone coupling as a selection kernel

\[
K(A\mid U)\ge0,
\qquad
A\subset U,\ |A|=r_1,
\qquad
\sum_{A\subset U}K(A\mid U)=1.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Its \(p_1\)-marginal condition is

\[
\sum_{U\supset A}p_H(U)K(A\mid U)=p_1(A).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The union-coupling problem is therefore **exactly** the additional demand that the same kernel can be chosen so that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{U\supset B}p_H(U)K(U\setminus B\mid U)=p_2(B)
\quad\text{for every }|B|=r_2.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus Question 2.8 can be restated as a kernel-selection compatibility problem:

> Can one choose a monotone selection of the \(H_1\)-configuration inside the \(H\)-configuration whose complement has exactly the \(H_2\) determinantal law?

This makes the failure mechanism precise. Ordinary stochastic domination proves existence of kernels satisfying (6.4)–(6.5), but it contains no mechanism enforcing the global family of complement equations (6.6).

## Sequential rank-one version

Choose an orthonormal basis \(g_1,\ldots,g_{r_2}\) for \(H_2\) and define

\[
K_j=H_1\oplus\operatorname{span}(g_1,\ldots,g_j),
\qquad j=0,\ldots,r_2.
\]

A rank-one monotone extension kernel can be used at each inclusion

\[
K_{j-1}\subset K_j.
\]

This constructs an ordered sequence of added sites \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{r_2}\), and hence an added set

\[
Y=\{Y_1,\ldots,Y_{r_2}\}
\]

with the correct endpoint union law. The unresolved issue is exactly whether the intermediate extension kernels can be chosen so that

\[
Y\sim\mathbf P^{H_2}.
\]

The law of \(Y\) is sensitive to the chosen extension kernels and to the basis/order used to decompose \(H_2\). Endpoint stochastic domination alone does not impose the needed higher-order Plücker probabilities of \(H_2\).

This is why the additive dual no-go in Section 4 matters: the defect in the sequential algorithm cannot be detected at the level of one-site costs. It lives in nonlinear correlations among the added sites. The B11 dual and B19 constructive route are therefore two descriptions of the same obstruction.
