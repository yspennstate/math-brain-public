## 1. Primary target and literature status

## 2. Finite full-partition setup

Let `E` be finite, `|E|=n`, and let

`C^E = H_0 direct-sum ... direct-sum H_(k-1)`

be a full orthogonal decomposition, with `r_i=dim H_i` and `sum_i r_i=n`.

Choose an orthonormal basis for each `H_i`, concatenate the basis columns, and obtain a unitary matrix `U`. Let `J_i` be the column block for `H_i`.

For an ordered row partition

`I=(I_0,...,I_(k-1))`,

with `|I_i|=r_i`, define

`p_i(I_i)=det U[I_i,J_i]`.

Let `epsilon(I)` be the sign of the row permutation obtained by listing the elements of `I_0`, then `I_1`, and so on, each internally in increasing order.

The projection-DPP basis probability for color `i` is

`mu_i(A)=|p_i(A)|^2`.

Block Laplace expansion gives

`det U = sum_I epsilon(I) product_i p_i(I_i)`.                 (2.1)

Since `U` is unitary, `|det U|=1`.

## 3. Canonical complex coupling and exact marginals

Define the canonical Laplace weight

`z(I)=conj(det U) epsilon(I) product_i p_i(I_i)`.                (3.1)

Then

`sum_I z(I)=1`.                                                 (3.2)

The previously archived Laplace/Jacobi argument gives, for every color `j` and every `A subset E` with `|A|=r_j`,

`sum_(I: I_j=A) z(I)=|p_j(A)|^2=mu_j(A)`.                       (3.3)

For completeness, the mechanism is as follows. Fix `A`. Expanding the complementary minor `det U[A^c,J_j^c]` by the remaining column blocks produces the sum over all complementary row partitions appearing in (3.3), with the same global row-shuffle signs. Jacobi's complementary-minor identity for the unitary `U` identifies the resulting signed complementary minor with

`det U * conj(det U[A,J_j])`

up to exactly the shuffle sign already present. Multiplying by `conj(det U) det U[A,J_j]` yields `|det U[A,J_j]|^2`.

Thus `z` is always an exact **complex signed solution** of the complete marginal system. The unresolved issue in general is positivity.

## 4. NEW theorem — Laplace l1 saturation is exactly positivity of the canonical solution

Define the scalar

`Lambda(H_0,...,H_(k-1))`

` := sum_I product_i sqrt(mu_i(I_i))`                           (4.1)

where the sum is over all ordered row partitions of the prescribed block sizes.

Equivalently, by (3.1),

`Lambda = sum_I |z(I)|`.                                       (4.2)

### Theorem 4.1 — canonical Laplace saturation criterion

For every finite full orthogonal decomposition,

`Lambda >= 1`.                                                  (4.3)

Moreover, the following are equivalent:

1. `Lambda=1`;
2. every nonzero canonical weight `z(I)` is a nonnegative real number;
3. the formula

   `q(I)=product_i sqrt(mu_i(I_i))`                              (4.4)

   is a joint partition coupling with one-color marginals `mu_i`.

In particular, `Lambda=1` is a phase-free sufficient condition for the desired determinantal partition coupling.

### Proof

By (3.2),

`1 = |sum_I z(I)| <= sum_I |z(I)| = Lambda`,

which proves (4.3).

Equality in the triangle inequality holds if and only if all nonzero summands `z(I)` have one common complex argument. Their sum is the positive real number `1`, so that common argument must be zero. Therefore

`Lambda=1 iff z(I)>=0 for every I`.                             (4.5)

If equality holds, then

`z(I)=|z(I)|=product_i |p_i(I_i)|=product_i sqrt(mu_i(I_i))`,

so (4.4) equals the canonical weight. Equations (3.2)-(3.3) then show that `q` is a probability distribution on ordered partitions and has exactly the required marginals.

Conversely, statement 2 immediately implies `Lambda=sum z=1`. QED.

### Important point

`Lambda` depends only on the separate projection-DPP laws `mu_i`, not on choices of bases or Pluecker phases. Thus a phase-coherence condition hidden in the signed exterior solution is detectable from one scalar built only from the marginal laws.

For the original two-subspace Lyons problem, add the complement

`H_0=(H_1 direct-sum H_2)^perp`.

If the resulting three-color full decomposition has `Lambda=1`, Theorem 4.1 gives a partition coupling `(X_0,X_1,X_2)` and hence a coupling of `X_1,X_2` with union marginal `P_(H_1 direct-sum H_2)`.

## 5. Quantitative defect of the canonical signed solution

Define the **Laplace defect**

`delta_L = Lambda-1 >=0`.                                      (5.1)

This is exactly the excess total variation of the canonical complex solution over the mass of an honest probability measure.

In the real-orthogonal case all `z(I)` are real. Write

`N = sum_(I:z(I)<0) |z(I)|`.

Because

`sum z=1`, `sum |z|=Lambda`,

we obtain the exact identity

`N=(Lambda-1)/2 = delta_L/2`.                                  (5.2)

Thus in the real case the scalar defect measures the total negative mass of the canonical signed coupling exactly. This does not by itself provide a positivity repair, but it gives a basis-independent scalar target for such a repair.

## 6. NEW exact K6 Fourier example with Lambda=1

Let `E=Z/6Z`,

`zeta=exp(pi i/3)`,

and let `F` be the unitary discrete Fourier matrix

`F_(j,k)=6^(-1/2) zeta^(jk)`.

Partition the six frequency columns into

`J_0={0,2}`,

`J_1={1,4}`,

`J_2={3,5}`.                                                    (6.1)

Let `H_i` be the two-plane spanned by the columns in `J_i`. These planes are mutually orthogonal and sum to `C^6`. They are not coordinate-separated: every Fourier basis vector has nonzero amplitude at every physical site.

For a row edge `{a,b}`, put `d=b-a mod 6`. For a frequency pair `{p,q}` with gap `g=q-p mod 6`, direct evaluation of the `2x2` Fourier minor gives

`|det F[{a,b},{p,q}]| = |1-zeta^(d g)|/6`.                     (6.2)

For the two gap-2 blocks `J_0,J_2`, a minor is zero exactly when the row edge is opposite (`|a-b|=3 mod 6`), and every nonzero minor has magnitude

`sqrt(3)/6`.                                                    (6.3)

For the gap-3 block `J_1`, a minor is nonzero exactly when the row difference is odd, and every nonzero minor has magnitude

`1/3`.                                                          (6.4)

Therefore an ordered colored perfect matching contributes a nonzero Laplace term exactly when

- the color-1 edge joins one even and one odd site; and
- neither the color-0 nor color-2 edge is an opposite pair.

There are `9` choices for the color-1 even-odd edge. After fixing it, the remaining four sites admit three uncolored perfect matchings. Exactly one uses an opposite pair and is forbidden; the other two are valid, and each can be ordered between colors 0 and 2 in two ways. Hence there are exactly

`9 * 2 * 2 = 36`                                               (6.5)

nonzero ordered partitions.

Every nonzero absolute Laplace term has magnitude

`(sqrt(3)/6)*(1/3)*(sqrt(3)/6)=1/36`.                           (6.6)

Consequently

`Lambda=36*(1/36)=1`.                                          (6.7)

By Theorem 4.1, the canonical Laplace solution is automatically positive and is simply

`q(I)=1/36`

on those 36 admissible colored perfect matchings and zero on the other 54.

This is an exact, explicit, non-coordinate rank-(2,2,2) coupling on the first hard six-site ground set.

It is distinct from the earlier archived consecutive-frequency Fourier seed `J_i={2i,2i+1}`, whose explicit strictly positive coupling used all 90 atoms. The present example is useful for a different reason: positivity follows directly from saturation of the canonical signed Laplace solution.

## 8. Why this is useful for the full problem

The signed exterior solution was previously only a formal exact solution with possible negative/complex weights. Theorem 4.1 turns its positivity into a single basis-independent scalar equality involving only the marginal DPP laws.

This gives three concrete research directions:

1. **Classify saturation families.** Seek structural conditions on orthogonal subspaces forcing `Lambda=1`; the Fourier example shows the class is not restricted to coordinate-separated decompositions.
2. **Exploit small defect.** In the real case, the exact negative mass is `(Lambda-1)/2`. Combine this with the already archived explicit kernel moves/right inverses to ask whether sufficiently small `delta_L` always admits a nonnegative repair.
3. **Use Lambda as a diagnostic on irreducible cores.** For the K6 `(2,2,2)` flag manifold, determine the minimum/critical loci of `Lambda`; canonical saturation gives exact constructive islands while the facet/Farkas route attacks the whole space.

The equality criterion is sufficient, not necessary: a coupling may exist even when the canonical Laplace solution is not positive. The earlier strictly positive consecutive-frequency Fourier coupling is an example where a different coupling can be used; no claim is made here that its canonical `Lambda` equals one.
