## 3. New theorem: a marked determinantal coupling must be independent

A tempting route to Question 2.8 is to duplicate the ground set and try to realize the desired colored pair `(X1,X2)` as one determinantal process on

`E_tilde = (E x {1}) disjoint-union (E x {2})`,

with the two color restrictions having laws `P^{H1}` and `P^{H2}`. The next theorem rules out this entire route in every nontrivial overlapping-support finite-rank instance.

### Theorem 3.1 — fixed-size block marginals force block diagonalization

Let `Q_tilde` be a positive contraction on `K1 direct-sum K2`, written in block form

```
Q_tilde = [ A  C ]
          [ C* B ].
```

Suppose the determinantal process with kernel `Q_tilde`, restricted to block `Ki`, has almost surely fixed cardinality `ri` for `i=1,2`. Then `A` and `B` are orthogonal projections and `C=0`. Consequently the two block restrictions are independent determinantal processes.

#### Proof

Restriction of a DPP to a coordinate block is a DPP with the compressed kernel. Thus the first block has kernel `A`, with `0 <= A <= I`. For a finite positive-contraction DPP,

`Var |X| = tr(A-A^2)`.

The first-block cardinality is fixed, so this variance is zero. Since `A-A^2` is positive semidefinite, every eigenvalue `lambda` of `A` satisfies `lambda(1-lambda)=0`; hence `A^2=A`. The same argument gives `B^2=B`.

It remains to show `C=0`; in fact the projection property of `A` alone is enough. If `x` lies in `ran(A)`, then

`<(I-Q_tilde)(x,0),(x,0)> = ||x||^2 - <Ax,x> = 0`.

Because `I-Q_tilde` is positive semidefinite, zero quadratic form implies `(I-Q_tilde)(x,0)=0`. Looking at the second block gives `C* x=0`.

If instead `x` lies in `ker(A)`, then

`<Q_tilde(x,0),(x,0)> = <Ax,x> = 0`.

Positivity of `Q_tilde` implies `Q_tilde(x,0)=0`, again giving `C* x=0`.

The orthogonal decomposition `K1 = ran(A) direct-sum ker(A)` therefore gives `C*=0`, hence `C=0`. Thus `Q_tilde=A direct-sum B`, and every principal determinant factors between the two blocks, which is exactly independence. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — no nontrivial determinantal marking solution to Lyons 2.8

Let `E` be finite and let `H1,H2` be orthogonal subspaces of `l2(E)`, with projection DPPs `P^{H1},P^{H2}`. If a positive-contraction determinantal process on `E x {1,2}` has these two color restrictions, then the color restrictions are independent.

If its unmarked union also had law `P^{H1 direct-sum H2}`, Lyons Proposition 10.2 says the coupling would necessarily be disjoint. Under independence,

`P(e in X1 and e in X2) = (P_H1)_{ee} (P_H2)_{ee}`.

Therefore a marked-DPP union coupling can exist only if

`(P_H1)_{ee}(P_H2)_{ee}=0` for every `e in E`.

For a positive semidefinite projection, `(P_Hi)_{ee}=||P_Hi e||^2`; if it vanishes then the whole `e` row/column of `P_Hi` vanishes. Hence the displayed condition means the two projection kernels are supported on disjoint coordinate subsets. In that coordinate-separated case `P_H1 + P_H2` is block diagonal after a coordinate permutation and the independent coupling already has the correct union law.

Thus:

> **Among positive-contraction determinantal processes on the duplicated/marked ground set, the Lyons coupling exists exactly in the coordinate-separated case where independence already works.**

This is a route obstruction, not a counterexample to Lyons's question: a successful coupling, if it exists in a genuinely overlapping instance, must be non-determinantal on the marked ground set.

The argument is noncircular: it uses only the standard restriction property of DPPs, fixed cardinality of projection DPPs, positivity of a contraction, and (for the final union statement) Lyons Proposition 10.2.

## 4. Exact high-degree square-free fibre tail for the K6 `(2,2,2)` configuration

The preceding run used the 90 balanced colorings of six vertices by colors `0,1,2`, two vertices per color. Each state is a labeled perfect matching. Let

`A in Z^(45 x 90)`

be the colored-edge marginal matrix. A degree-`d` target is **square-free in each color block** if, for each color, it prescribes `d` distinct edges of `K6`, each once.

For such a target, order the selected color-0 edges `a_1,...,a_d`. Every fibre monomial is determined by a bijection from the selected color-0 edges to selected color-1 edges such that the paired edges are disjoint and the forced complementary color-2 edges are exactly the prescribed color-2 set, each once. This gives a complete finite exact matching enumeration with no floating-point step.

### 4.1 Degree 11: all two-point fibres are reducible lifts

A square-free degree-11 target omits exactly four of the 15 edges in each color. Up to `S6` there are exactly nine isomorphism classes of four-edge omitted graphs. The exact exhaustion used one representative of each of these nine classes for color 0, then all `C(15,4)=1365` choices for the omitted color-1 graph, and enumerated all rainbow perfect matchings, grouping them by the resulting color-2 edge set.

This is exhaustive up to the simultaneous vertex action: every target triple can be moved so its color-0 omitted set is one of those nine representatives.

Exactly 76 normalized target instances have fibre size two. Canonicalization under the full `S6 x S3` action and interchange of the two monomials yields exactly **five** unsigned binomial orbits, of sizes

`4320, 2160, 2160, 2160, 720`,

for a union of 11,520 degree-11 two-point binomials.

However, none is primitive at degree 11. In the five canonical representatives the two degree-11 monomials have respectively

`4, 1, 5, 5, 2`

common state factors. Cancelling the gcd leaves two-point fibres of degrees

`7, 10, 6, 6, 9`,

respectively. If a cancelled fibre had a third monomial, multiplying it by the common factor would give a third monomial in the original degree-11 fibre, impossible. Hence the cancellation is exact.

Therefore:

> **Every square-free degree-11 two-monomial fibre is merely a common-factor lift of a lower-degree two-monomial fibre. There is no new primitive square-free isolated-fibre generator in degree 11.**

This explains why the previous bounded search failed to produce a certified degree-11 extension of the Markov-degree lower bound despite the existence of some size-two degree-11 fibres.

### 4.2 Degrees 12--15

The same exact matching formulation gives a complete tail check.

- **Degree 12.** A target omits three edges per color. There are exactly five `S6`-orbits of three-edge omitted graphs. Fixing one representative for color 0 and exhaustively enumerating all 455 color-1 omitted sets, with the resulting color-2 sets grouped exactly, produced **no fibre of size two**. Every nonempty normalized target fibre had at least four monomials.
- **Degree 13.** A target omits two edges per color. There are two `S6`-orbits (adjacent or disjoint omitted edges). For each color-0 representative, all `105^2=11025` color-1/color-2 omitted-set pairs were checked exactly. No fibre had size two; every nonempty target had at least three monomials.
- **Degree 14.** All `15^3=3375` target triples were checked exactly. 3285 were empty and the remaining 90 had at least three monomials; none had size two.
- **Degree 15.** There is only one square-free target: every edge appears once in each color. Exact dynamic programming counts **6336** fibre monomials.

Consequently the square-free *two-monomial* mechanism is exhausted above degree 10: degree 11 only gives reducible lifts, and degrees 12--15 give no two-point fibre at all.

Important limitation: this does **not** prove that the full toric ideal has no indispensable generators above degree 10. Non-square-free targets remain, and even fibres containing more than two monomials can have nontrivial lower-degree connectivity structure. The theorem only closes the specific square-free isolated-fibre route.
