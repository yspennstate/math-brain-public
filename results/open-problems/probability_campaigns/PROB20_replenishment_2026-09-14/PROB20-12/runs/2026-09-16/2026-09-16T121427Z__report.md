## 2. Coordinate-free form of the 540 operator target

Use the same `3+3` split as the preceding checkpoint:

`C^6 = A direct-sum B`,

`A=span{e0,e2,e3}`, `B=span{e1,e4,e5}`.

Let

`a=e0 in A`, `b=e1 in B`,

`A'=A cap a^perp`, `B'=B cap b^perp`.

Then the relevant coordinate exterior subspaces can be written without edge notation as

`T = Lambda^2 A direct-sum span{a wedge b}`,                    (2.1)

`R0 = Lambda^2 B`,                                             (2.2)

`R1 = Lambda^2 B direct-sum (A' wedge B')`.                    (2.3)

Let `H0` be an arbitrary two-plane, let `alpha` be a unit simple two-form spanning `Lambda^2 H0`, and put

`K=H0^perp`, `S=Lambda^2 K`.

The map

`J_alpha:S->S`, `J_alpha(beta)=star_6(alpha wedge beta)`        (2.4)

is unitary (up to the standard anti-linear convention over `C`). Put

`r=||P_(Lambda^2 B) alpha||^2`.                                (2.5)

The stronger operator inequality isolated in the previous checkpoint is

`J_alpha^* P_T J_alpha <= P_S P_R1 P_S + r I_S`.               (2.6)

If (2.6) holds, then for every unit `beta in S`, in particular the color-1 Pluecker form `alpha_1`,

`||P_T J_alpha beta||^2 <= ||P_R1 beta||^2+r`,                 (2.7)

which is exactly the sparse 540 inequality.

The main point of this run is that (2.6) can be analyzed explicitly by a cosine-sine normal form.

## 3. NEW cosine-sine normal form for the operator problem

Apply the standard CS decomposition to the four-plane `K` relative to `A direct-sum B`. In the generic case there are orthonormal bases

`(m,a1,a2)` of `A`,

`(n,b1,b2)` of `B`,

and numbers `c1,c2 in [0,1]`, `s_i=sqrt(1-c_i^2)`, such that

`H0 = span{-s1 a1 + c1 b1, -s2 a2 + c2 b2}`,                  (3.1)

`K  = span{m,n,k1,k2}`,                                       (3.2)

where

`k1=c1 a1+s1 b1`, `k2=c2 a2+s2 b2`.                           (3.3)

Degenerate cases are obtained by continuity and by allowing endpoint principal angles.

In this normal form,

`r=c1^2 c2^2`.                                                  (3.4)

The distinguished coordinate vectors have expansions

`a=p0 m+p1 a1+p2 a2`,                                         (3.5)

`b=q0 n+q1 b1+q2 b2`,                                         (3.6)

with `|p0|^2+|p1|^2+|p2|^2=1` and similarly for `q`.

Use the orthonormal basis of `Lambda^2 K`

`E0=m wedge n`,

`E1=m wedge k1`,

`E2=m wedge k2`,

`E3=n wedge k1`,

`E4=n wedge k2`,

`E5=k1 wedge k2`.                                              (3.7)

In this basis `J_alpha` is just the four-dimensional Hodge involution: it pairs `E0` with `E5`, `E1` with `E4`, and `E2` with `E3`, up to harmless signs/phases.

Thus the full operator inequality (2.6) has been reduced to positivity of an explicit `6 x 6` Hermitian matrix whose entries depend only on two principal angles and the two unit vectors `p,q`. This is a much smaller semialgebraic target than the original 15-coordinate exterior operator.

The present run proves positivity on the two codimension-one slices `p=(1,0,0)` and `q=(1,0,0)`.

## 4. NEW theorem: validity when `b=e1` is orthogonal to `H0`

### Theorem 4.1

Assume

`P_H0 b=0`.                                                     (4.1)

Then the operator inequality (2.6) holds over both `R` and `C`. Consequently the complete 540-facet inequality is valid for every orthogonal rank-`(2,2,2)` decomposition satisfying (4.1).

### Proof

Condition (4.1) says `b in K cap B`. In the generic CS form we may therefore choose

`n=b`, hence `q=(1,0,0)`.                                      (4.2)

Write

`x=c1^2`, `y=c2^2`,

`u=|p1|^2`, `v=|p2|^2`, `w=|p0|^2=1-u-v`,                    (4.3)

and

`d=(1-x)(1-y)`.                                                 (4.4)

Let

`G=P_S P_R1 P_S + r I_S - J_alpha^*P_TJ_alpha`.                (4.5)

In the basis (3.7), a direct compression calculation gives:

- the `E0` row and column are zero;
- the `E3` and `E4` diagonal entries are both `d`;
- the remaining coordinates `(E1,E2,E5)` form one `3 x 3` Hermitian block `M_b`.

After a diagonal unitary conjugation removing the phases of `p0,p1,p2`, the diagonal entries of `M_b` are

`A = xy-yv+(1-x)(u+v)`,                                       (4.6)

`B = xy-xu+(1-y)(u+v)`,                                       (4.7)

`C = (1-x+xy)u+(1-y+xy)v`.                                    (4.8)

The off-diagonal magnitudes are

`|M12|=sqrt(xyuv)`,                                            (4.9)

`|M13|=x sqrt(ywv)`,                                           (4.10)

`|M23|=y sqrt(xwu)`,                                           (4.11)

with the fixed sign/phase pattern coming from the Hodge involution. The phase-normalization does not change any principal minor.

The diagonal entries are nonnegative. For example

`A = w(xy)+u(1-x+xy)+v d >=0`,                                (4.12)

`B = w(xy)+u d+v(1-y+xy)>=0`,                                 (4.13)

and `C>=0` is immediate.

Now set

`L = xy+(1-x)u+(1-y)v`,                                       (4.14)

`M = xy-(x+y-1)(u+v)`,                                        (4.15)

`J1=(1-x+xy)u+d v`,                                           (4.16)

`J2=d u+(1-y+xy)v`.                                           (4.17)

Exact determinant expansion gives the three principal `2 x 2` minors

`det M_b[{1,2}] = L M`,                                       (4.18)

`det M_b[{1,3}] = L J1`,                                      (4.19)

`det M_b[{2,3}] = L J2`,                                      (4.20)

and the full determinant

`det M_b = d (u+v) L^2`.                                      (4.21)

Every factor is nonnegative. Only `M` needs a two-case comment. If `x+y<=1`, then (4.15) is obviously nonnegative. If `x+y>=1`, use `u+v<=1` to get

`M >= xy-(x+y-1)=(1-x)(1-y)=d>=0`.                            (4.22)

Hence all principal minors of the Hermitian `3 x 3` block are nonnegative, so `M_b>=0`. Together with the two scalar blocks `d>=0` and the zero block, this proves `G>=0`, which is precisely (2.6). QED.

### Why this is genuinely beyond the earlier boundary theorem

Under `P_H0 e1=0`, the color-0 source cost is

`r=mu_0(45)`,                                                  (4.23)

because `mu_0(14)=mu_0(15)=0`. The value `mu_0(45)` can be strictly positive. Therefore Theorem 4.1 is not a consequence of the preceding theorem that assumed `mu_0(R0)=0`.

## 5. NEW theorem: validity when `a=e0` is orthogonal to `H0`

The other endpoint admits a different but equally explicit factorization.

### Theorem 5.1

Assume

`P_H0 a=0`.                                                     (5.1)

Then (2.6) holds over both `R` and `C`, and hence the complete 540-facet is valid.

### Proof

Now `a in K cap A`, so in the CS normal form choose

`m=a`, hence `p=(1,0,0)`.                                      (5.2)

Retain

`x=c1^2`, `y=c2^2`, `d=(1-x)(1-y)`,                           (5.3)

and write

`u=|q1|^2`, `v=|q2|^2`, `w=|q0|^2=1-u-v`.                    (5.4)

For the same gap operator `G` in (4.5), the `E0` coordinate is again zero, while `E1` and `E2` are scalar blocks equal to `xy=r`. The remaining coordinates `(E3,E4,E5)` form a `3 x 3` Hermitian block `M_a`.

After harmless diagonal phase conjugation, its diagonal entries are

`A2=d+xu+(x+y-1)v`,                                           (5.5)

`B2=d+(x+y-1)u+yv`,                                           (5.6)

`C2=(1-y+xy)u+(1-x+xy)v`.                                    (5.7)

The first two are visibly nonnegative from their barycentric forms

`A2=w d+u(1-y+xy)+v(xy)`,                                    (5.8)

`B2=w d+u(xy)+v(1-x+xy)`.                                    (5.9)

The off-diagonal magnitudes are

`|M12|=sqrt(d u v)`,                                          (5.10)

`|M13|=(1-x)sqrt((1-y)wv)`,                                   (5.11)

`|M23|=(1-y)sqrt((1-x)wu)`.                                   (5.12)

Define

`L2=d+xu+yv`,                                                  (5.13)

`M2=d+(x+y-1)(u+v)`,                                          (5.14)

`K1=(1-y+xy)u+xy v`,                                          (5.15)

`K2=xy u+(1-x+xy)v`.                                          (5.16)

Exact expansion gives

`det M_a[{1,2}] = L2 M2`,                                     (5.17)

`det M_a[{1,3}] = L2 K1`,                                     (5.18)

`det M_a[{2,3}] = L2 K2`,                                     (5.19)

and

`det M_a = xy (u+v) L2^2`.                                    (5.20)

Again every factor is nonnegative. If `x+y>=1`, then `M2>=d>=0`; if `x+y<=1`, then `u+v<=1` gives

`M2>=d+(x+y-1)=xy>=0`.                                        (5.21)

Thus `M_a>=0`, hence `G>=0`, proving (2.6). QED.

## 7. What this says about the full operator conjecture

The CS reduction also reveals a useful shape of the remaining proof obligation. The full generic case depends on only

- two numbers `x,y in [0,1]` (the squared CS cosines), and
- two unit vectors `p,q in C^3`, modulo diagonal phase symmetries.

Thus (2.6), originally an inequality between operators in `Lambda^2 C^6`, is equivalent to positivity of a concrete `6 x 6` Hermitian matrix on this compact low-dimensional parameter space.

The two new exact factorizations show that on the boundary faces `p=(1,0,0)` and `q=(1,0,0)` all principal minors collapse to products of elementary nonnegative factors. This is evidence for an SOS/principal-minor attack in the generic CS variables, but it is not itself a proof of the interior.

A direct attempt during this run to symbolically factor the generic `6 x 6` determinant before exploiting the unit-sphere and CS relations became computationally unwieldy and was stopped. No conclusion is drawn from that failure. The appropriate next computation is to reduce modulo

`|p|^2=|q|^2=1`, `s_i^2+c_i^2=1`

before determinant expansion, or to search for a Gram/SOS factorization of the gap matrix rather than factor its raw determinant.
