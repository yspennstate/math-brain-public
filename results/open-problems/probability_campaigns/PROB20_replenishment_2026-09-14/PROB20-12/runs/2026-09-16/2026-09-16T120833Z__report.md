## 4. NEW exact support-cover lemma

Up to a unit phase and the standard conjugation convention,

`alpha_2 = star_6(alpha_0 wedge alpha_1)`.                       (4.1)

Fix `t in T`. A coordinate monomial contributing to `Delta_2(t)` is indexed by an ordered pair of disjoint edges `(e0,e1)` partitioning the four-vertex complement of `t`.

### Lemma 4.1 — every target monomial is covered

For every `t in T` and every ordered complementary pair `(e0,e1)`, at least one of

`e0 in R0` or `e1 in R1`                                       (4.2)

holds.

### Finite proof

For `t=01`, complement `2345`:

`(23,45), (45,23), (24,35), (35,24), (25,34), (34,25)`.

The first and the four cross-edge assignments are covered by `e1 in R1`; `(45,23)` is covered by `e0 in R0`.

For `t=02`, complement `1345`:

`(13,45), (45,13), (14,35), (35,14), (15,34), (34,15)`.

Every pair is covered. The pairs `(14,35)` and `(15,34)` are covered on both sides.

For `t=03`, complement `1245`:

`(12,45), (45,12), (14,25), (25,14), (15,24), (24,15)`.

Again every pair is covered, with `(14,25)` and `(15,24)` double-covered.

For `t=23`, complement `0145`:

`(01,45), (45,01), (04,15), (15,04), (05,14), (14,05)`.

Again every pair is covered.

There are therefore no uncovered target monomials; exactly four ordered pairs are double-covered.

Equivalently,

`P_T star_6( P_(R0^c) alpha_0 wedge P_(R1^c) alpha_1 ) = 0`.    (4.3)

This identity is support-theoretic and holds over both `R` and `C`.

## 5. NEW rigorous boundary theorem for the 540 orbit

### Theorem 5.1

The sparse 540 inequality (3.6) holds for every real or complex orthogonal triple whenever either source cost vanishes:

- if `mu_0(R0)=0`, then `mu_2(T)<=mu_1(R1)`;
- if `mu_1(R1)=0`, then `mu_2(T)<=mu_0(R0)`.

### Proof

Assume first `mu_0(R0)=0`, so `P_R0 alpha_0=0`. By Lemma 4.1, every surviving target monomial must use a color-1 edge in `R1`; hence

`P_T star_6(alpha_0 wedge alpha_1)`

`=P_T star_6(alpha_0 wedge P_R1 alpha_1)`.                      (5.1)

Exterior multiplication by a unit decomposable two-form has operator norm one: after rotating so that the two-form is `f1 wedge f2`, it kills components containing `f1` or `f2` and is an isometry on `Lambda^2 span{f1,f2}^perp`. Hodge star and coordinate projection are contractions. Thus

`||P_T alpha_2|| <= ||P_R1 alpha_1||`,                          (5.2)

and squaring gives the first assertion.

The second case is identical after reversing the roles: if `P_R1 alpha_1=0`, every surviving target monomial uses `R0` on color 0, so

`||P_T alpha_2||<=||P_R0 alpha_0||`.                            (5.3)

QED.

This proves the full new orbit on two nontrivial coordinate-Schubert boundary strata, but not in the generic interior.

## 6. NEW general `3+3` complementary-subspace theorem

The cleanest new analytic result is independent of the detailed four-edge target.

### Theorem 6.1

Let

`C^6=H0 direct-sum H1 direct-sum H2`, `dim H_i=2`,

and let `C^6=A direct-sum B` be any coordinate orthogonal decomposition with

`dim A=dim B=3`.

Then

**`mu_2(Lambda^2 A) <= mu_0(Lambda^2 B)+mu_1(Lambda^2 B)`.**    (6.1)

Here `mu_i(Lambda^2 A)` means the sum of the three squared Pluecker coordinates of `H_i` on the three coordinate edges inside `A`.

For the present 540 facet, with `A={0,2,3}` and `B={1,4,5}`, this proves

`mu_2(02)+mu_2(03)+mu_2(23)`

`<= mu_0(14)+mu_0(15)+mu_0(45)`

`  +mu_1(14)+mu_1(15)+mu_1(45)`.                               (6.2)

Thus three of the four target coordinates in (3.6) are controlled simultaneously by the two common `B`-triangle source masses.

### Proof

Choose orthonormal frames `Q_i` for `H_i` and let

`X_i=P_B Q_i`,                                                  (6.3)

viewed as a `3 x 2` matrix. Put

`B_i=X_i X_i^*`,                                                (6.4)

so `0<=B_i<=I_B`.

By complement/Jacobi duality for the rank-two projection `P_2`, the event that the two color-2 points lie inside `A` is the event that all three coordinates of `B` lie in the complementary rank-four DPP with kernel `P_0+P_1`. Hence

`mu_2(Lambda^2 A)=det(B_0+B_1)`.                               (6.5)

On the other hand, Cauchy–Binet gives

`c_i:=mu_i(Lambda^2 B)=det(X_i^*X_i)`.                         (6.6)

Expand

`det(B_0+B_1)=det([X_0 X_1][X_0 X_1]^*)`                       (6.7)

by Cauchy–Binet. A nonzero `3 x 3` minor must choose two columns from one color and one from the other. Therefore

`det(B_0+B_1)=S_{001}+S_{011}`,                                (6.8)

where

`S_{001}=sum_(j=1)^2 |det[x_(0,1),x_(0,2),x_(1,j)]|^2`,        (6.9)

and `S_{011}` is the color-swapped expression.

If `rank X_0<2`, then `c_0=S_{001}=0`. Otherwise let `n_0` be a unit Hermitian normal to the two columns of `X_0` in the three-space `B`. Then

`|det[x_(0,1),x_(0,2),x_(1,j)]|^2`

`= c_0 |<n_0,x_(1,j)>|^2`.                                    (6.10)

Summing over `j`,

`S_{001}=c_0 <n_0,B_1 n_0> <= c_0`,                            (6.11)

because `B_1<=I_B`. Similarly

`S_{011}<=c_1`.                                                 (6.12)

Combining (6.8), (6.11), and (6.12) proves (6.1). QED.

The proof is valid over both real and complex scalars.

## 7. NEW exact slack identity for Theorem 6.1

The preceding proof has an exact remainder, and that remainder is precisely the shared capacity needed by the unresolved fourth target edge.

For `i=0,1`, define an anti-linear three-dimensional Hodge-normal vector

`v_i = star_B^H( P_(Lambda^2 B) alpha_i ) in B`,                (7.1)

where `star_B^H` is chosen so that `v_i` is Hermitian-orthogonal to the restricted columns `X_i`. Thus

`||v_i||^2=c_i`.                                                (7.2)

When `c_i>0`, `v_i=sqrt(c_i)n_i` up to a unit phase; when `c_i=0`, set `v_i=0`.

Because `n_0`, embedded in `C^6`, is orthogonal to `H_0`,

`1-<n_0,B_1 n_0>=||P_2 n_0||^2`.                              (7.3)

Likewise

`1-<n_1,B_0 n_1>=||P_2 n_1||^2`.                              (7.4)

Therefore the inequalities in Section 6 refine to the exact identity

**`c_0+c_1-mu_2(Lambda^2 A)`**

**`= ||P_2 v_0||^2 + ||P_2 v_1||^2`.**                         (7.5)

This identity is worth isolating: the slack in the complementary `3+3` determinant inequality is exactly the amount of the two source Hodge-normal vectors captured by the remaining plane `H_2`.

## 9. A stronger operator inequality that would also settle the orbit

There is another exact sufficient target. Fix `alpha=alpha_0` and put

`K=H_0^perp`.

Exterior multiplication followed by six-dimensional Hodge complement defines a unitary map on the four-space exterior sector

`T_alpha : Lambda^2 K -> Lambda^2 K`,

`T_alpha(beta)=star_6(alpha wedge beta)`                         (9.1)

(up to the harmless anti-linear convention in the complex case).

Let

`r=||P_R0 alpha||^2=mu_0(R0)`.                                 (9.2)

The operator inequality

**`T_alpha^* P_T T_alpha <= P_(Lambda^2 K) P_R1 P_(Lambda^2 K) + r I`**   (9.3)

would imply, for every unit `beta in Lambda^2 K`,

`||P_T T_alpha beta||^2 <= ||P_R1 beta||^2+r`.                 (9.4)

Taking `beta=alpha_1` gives exactly (3.6).

The support-cover theorem and Theorem 5.1 prove the endpoint behavior when the appropriate source term vanishes, but a proof of the full operator inequality (9.3) is **not** obtained in this checkpoint.

Exploratory complex-Haar tests performed during this run found no violation of (9.3) and numerically approached equality. Those experiments are heuristic only and are not promoted to a theorem, certificate, or independent verification.

## 10. Failed proof route recorded explicitly

The support cover has four double-covered monomials. It is tempting to split each double-covered term by a fixed scalar weight and construct one ambient linear cover operator from the coordinate source spaces `R0` and `R1` to `T`.

That approach is false in its naive form. Endpoint assignments and symmetric fractional assignments can have operator norm strictly greater than one on complex orthogonal examples. Thus the proof cannot simply be "choose a fixed monomial owner and apply Bessel".

The exact slack identity (7.5) explains why: the available capacity depends on the geometry of the actual source planes through `P_2 v_0` and `P_2 v_1`, so a successful proof should preserve this adaptive shared capacity rather than replace it by a fixed coordinate allocation.
