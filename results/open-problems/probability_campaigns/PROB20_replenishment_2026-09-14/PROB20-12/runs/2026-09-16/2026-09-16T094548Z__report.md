## 3. NEW exact support-cover lemma

Let `alpha_c` be a unit decomposable two-form spanning `wedge^2 H_c`. Then

`mu_c(e)=|<alpha_c,e_e>|^2`,

and, up to a unit scalar/Hodge-conjugation convention,

`alpha_2 = star_6(alpha_0 wedge alpha_1)`.

A coordinate contribution to the target coefficient `Delta_2(t)` for `t in T` comes from an ordered pair `(e0,e1)` of disjoint edges whose union is the four-vertex complement of `t`.

### Lemma 3.1 — every target monomial is covered

For every `t in T` and every such ordered complementary pair `(e0,e1)`, at least one of

`e0 in R0` or `e1 in R1`

holds.

### Exact finite proof

There are only 18 ordered complementary pairs. They are:

For `t=02` (complement `{1,3,4,5}`):

`(13,45), (45,13), (14,35), (35,14), (15,34), (34,15)`.

The first and last are covered by `e1 in R1`; the other four are covered by `e0 in R0`.

For `t=13` (complement `{0,2,4,5}`):

`(02,45), (45,02), (04,25), (25,04), (05,24), (24,05)`.

The first and fourth are covered by `e1 in R1`; the other four are covered by `e0 in R0`.

For `t=23` (complement `{0,1,4,5}`):

`(01,45), (45,01), (04,15), (15,04), (05,14), (14,05)`.

The first is covered by `e1 in R1`; the second, fifth and sixth by `e0 in R0`; `(04,15)` and `(15,04)` are covered by both sides.

Therefore there is no uncovered coordinate monomial. Equivalently,

`P_T star_6( P_(R0^c) alpha_0 wedge P_(R1^c) alpha_1 ) = 0`.    (3.1)

This identity is purely support-theoretic and holds over both `R` and `C`.

## 4. NEW rigorous boundary theorem

### Theorem 4.1

For every real or complex orthogonal decomposition

`C^6=H0 direct-sum H1 direct-sum H2`, `dim Hc=2`,

the new 2,160-facet (2.1) holds whenever either

`mu_1(R1)=0`

or

`mu_0(R0)=0`.

More precisely:

- if `mu_1(R1)=0`, then `mu_2(T) <= mu_0(R0)`;
- if `mu_0(R0)=0`, then `mu_2(T) <= mu_1(R1)`.

### Proof

Assume `mu_1(R1)=0`. Then `P_R1 alpha_1=0`. By the exact support identity (3.1), all target terms arising from `P_(R0^c) alpha_0` disappear, so

`P_T star_6(alpha_0 wedge alpha_1)`

`=P_T star_6((P_R0 alpha_0) wedge alpha_1)`.                    (4.1)

Exterior multiplication by a unit decomposable two-form is a contraction. Indeed, rotate coordinates so that `alpha_1=f_5 wedge f_6`; wedge multiplication kills components containing `f_5` or `f_6` and is an isometry on `wedge^2 span{f_1,...,f_4}`. Hodge star is an isometry and `P_T` is an orthogonal projection. Hence

`||P_T alpha_2||`

`<= ||(P_R0 alpha_0) wedge alpha_1||`

`<= ||P_R0 alpha_0||`.

Squaring gives

`mu_2(T)<=mu_0(R0)`.

The case `mu_0(R0)=0` is symmetric, using the support lemma to retain only the `R1` component of `alpha_1`. QED.

Thus the full new orbit is proved on two nontrivial coordinate-Schubert boundary strata. The generic interior case remains open.

## 5. A sharp operator inequality that would settle the full 2,160 orbit

Fix the unit simple form `alpha_1`, put `K=H1^perp`, and define the unitary four-dimensional Hodge-complement map

`T_alpha : wedge^2 K -> wedge^2 K`,

`T_alpha(eta)=star_6(eta wedge alpha_1)`

(up to the usual harmless complex conjugation convention). Put

`r=||P_R1 alpha_1||^2=mu_1(R1)`.

The following operator inequality would immediately prove (2.1):

**`T_alpha^* P_T T_alpha <= P_K P_R0 P_K + r I_(wedge^2 K)`.**    (5.1)

Equivalently, for every `eta in wedge^2 K`,

`||P_T T_alpha eta||^2 <= ||P_R0 eta||^2 + r ||eta||^2`.        (5.2)

Setting `eta=alpha_0`, which is a unit vector in `wedge^2(H1^perp)`, gives exactly

`mu_2(T)<=mu_0(R0)+mu_1(R1)`.

Theorem 4.1 is the endpoint `r=0` of this proposed operator inequality.

I do **not** have a proof of (5.1) in this checkpoint. It is recorded as a precise next proof obligation. Exploratory Haar-complex tests on approximately 50,000 random rank-two planes found no violation of the PSD inequality; local numerical searches drove its smallest eigenvalue toward zero but did not make it negative. Those computations are evidence only, not proof or independent verification.

## 6. NEW exact graph-chart reduction

There is also a purely `4 x 2` matrix formulation of the generic case.

Split

`C^6 = W direct-sum C`,

where

`W=span{e0,e1,e2,e3}`, `C=span{e4,e5}`.

Work on the open chart where both Pluecker coordinates `Delta_0(45)` and `Delta_1(45)` are nonzero. Then each source plane can be represented as a graph over `C`:

`H_c = span{ e4+p_c, e5+q_c }`,

where `p_c,q_c in C^4`. Write

`P_c=[p_c q_c] in C^(4 x 2)`,

`G_c=I_2+P_c^*P_c`.

A normalized top form is

`alpha_c=a_c (e4+p_c) wedge (e5+q_c)`,

with

`|a_c|^2 = 1/det(G_c)`.                                        (6.1)

Orthogonality `H0 perp H1` is exactly

**`P_0^* P_1 = -I_2`.**                                        (6.2)

Let

`D=P_1-P_0`.

Column operations in the four-form give the exact identity

`(e4+p0) wedge (e5+q0) wedge (e4+p1) wedge (e5+q1)`

`=(e4+p0) wedge (e5+q0) wedge (p1-p0) wedge (q1-q0)`.           (6.3)

Therefore, for target edges entirely inside `W`, the Hodge-complement coordinates of `alpha_2` are controlled by the `2 x 2` row minors of `D`. For

`T={02,13,23}`,

the complementary row pairs in `W` are

`S={13,02,01}`.

Hence

`mu_2(T)=|a_0a_1|^2 sum_(ij in {01,02,13}) |det D[{i,j},:]|^2`. (6.4)

The two source costs are also explicit. Define

`A0 = 1`

`     + |p0_0|^2+|q0_0|^2`

`     + |p0_1|^2+|q0_1|^2`

`     + |q0_2|^2 + |p0_3|^2`,                                 (6.5)

and

`A1 = 1 + |q1_0|^2 + |p1_1|^2`.                               (6.6)

Then

`mu_0(R0)=|a_0|^2 A0`,

`mu_1(R1)=|a_1|^2 A1`.                                         (6.7)

Dividing (2.1) by the positive factor `|a_0a_1|^2`, the generic new-facet problem is therefore **exactly** the constrained matrix inequality

**`sum_(ij in {01,02,13}) |det(P1-P0)[{i,j},:]|^2`

` <= A0 det(G1) + A1 det(G0)`,**                               (6.8)

subject to

`P0^*P1=-I_2`.                                                  (6.9)

One further exact identity following from (6.2) is

`(P1-P0)^*(P1-P0)=G0+G1`.                                      (6.10)

Thus the unresolved analytic content of the 2,160-facet has been reduced, on a dense chart, to a three-selected-minor bound for a `4 x 2` difference matrix under the rigid cross-Gram constraint (6.9). Boundary charts are already partially controlled by Theorem 4.1 and, in principle, can be covered separately or reached by continuity once a uniform proof of (6.8) is found.

## 7. Failed approach: a fixed monomial-cover contraction is false

The support lemma naturally suggests assigning every target monomial to either its `R0` source or its `R1` source and building a fixed stacked cover operator, analogous to the successful proof of the new 270 orbit.

This route does **not** work in its naive form. The only double-covered target monomials are `(04,15)` and `(15,04)` over target `23`, so the family of fixed cover operators has only two splitting parameters. Multiple choices, including both endpoint assignments and symmetric half-splitting, produced operator norms strictly above one on random complex orthogonal pairs (typical discovered excesses were on the order of `10^-3` to `10^-2`, with some larger). Thus the statement

`every fixed support-cover assignment gives a contraction`

is false.

Adaptive splitting can lower the numerical norm, but no exact rule or proof was obtained. This failure is useful: the successful 270-orbit proof depended on an annihilating-sector gain that exactly controlled shared capacity; the 2,160 orbit needs a genuinely different compensation mechanism, likely the compressed operator inequality (5.1) or the graph constraint (6.9), rather than only combinatorial monomial assignment.
