## Original/current source

Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures*, arXiv:1406.2707, Question 2.8, was rechecked. The question asks: for orthogonal closed subspaces

\[
H_1\perp H_2,\qquad H=H_1\oplus H_2\subseteq \ell^2(E),
\]

is there a coupling

\[
X_1\sim \mathbf P^{H_1},\qquad X_2\sim \mathbf P^{H_2}

\[
X_1\cup X_2\sim \mathbf P^H?
\]

The source explicitly distinguishes the issue from stochastic domination and records the full-space case `H = ell^2(E)` as affirmative.

# 4. (a) Exact blocking statement

The previous run eliminated the union potential in the Farkas dual. For finite `E`, `r_i=dim H_i`, and `r=r_1+r_2`, put

\[
p_1(A)=\mathbf P^{H_1}(A),\quad
p_2(B)=\mathbf P^{H_2}(B),\quad
p_H(U)=\mathbf P^H(U).
\]

For real costs

\[
f:\binom E{r_1}\to\mathbb R,\qquad
g:\binom E{r_2}\to\mathbb R,
\]

define the optimal split cost

\[
h_{f,g}(U)=
\min_{\substack{A\subset U\\|A|=r_1}}
\bigl(f(A)+g(U\setminus A)\bigr).
\]

The exact statement that blocks both proof and refutation is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P^{H_1}} f
+
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P^{H_2}} g
-
\mathbb E_{\mathbf P^{H}} h_{f,g}
\ \ge 0
\quad\text{for every }H_1\perp H_2\text{ and every }f,g.
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

A single source-valid pair and a normalized nonlinear `f,g` for which `(BLOCK)` is strictly negative would give a finite counterexample and a positive exact Farkas margin. A universal proof must explain `(BLOCK)` simultaneously for every nonlinear two-colour cost.

The previous run already proved that one-colour costs and all site-additive two-colour costs cannot violate `(BLOCK)`. Therefore the unresolved obstruction is genuinely higher-order and two-colour.

# 5. (b) Routes tried and the precise reason each has not closed the target

This section is a handoff inventory, not a claim that the listed methods are globally impossible.

## 5.1 Stochastic domination / monotone selection

Because `H_1 subset H`, stochastic domination yields a monotone coupling of an `H_1` configuration inside an `H` configuration. If `K(A|U)` is such a selection kernel, it can satisfy

\[
\sum_{U\supset A}p_H(U)K(A|U)=p_1(A).
\]

Setting `B=U\setminus A` gives the right cardinality and disjoint union automatically, but the second marginal demands the independent family

\[
\sum_{U\supset B}p_H(U)K(U\setminus B\mid U)=p_2(B)
\qquad\text{for every }B.
\tag{COMP}
\]

**Failure mechanism:** the monotone-coupling theorem has no condition enforcing `(COMP)`. It solves containment, not the image law under complementation inside a random `U`.

## 5.2 Sequential rank-one enlargement

The historical rank-one/codimension-one increment is solvable, so one can try to add a basis of `H_2` one direction at a time.

**Failure mechanism:** the intermediate monotone choices depend on previous random choices, and having the correct endpoint union law does not force the collection of added sites to have the joint Pluecker probabilities of `P^{H_2}`. The missing constraint is again the global complement-law family `(COMP)`.

## 5.3 Independent sampling

Sampling `X_1` and `X_2` independently has the prescribed individual marginals.

# 6. (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

A successful universal proof needs a genuinely **two-colour global compatibility principle**. Concretely, it must provide at least one of the following kinds of mechanism:

1. a canonical monotone selection kernel `K(A|U)` for which the complement equations `(COMP)` follow from orthogonality, not from a separate LP solve;
2. a universal exterior-algebra/Pluecker inequality implying `(BLOCK)` for every nonlinear pair `f,g` simultaneously;
3. an induction or decomposition that reduces every orthogonal pair to rank-one or `|E|<=5` pieces while preserving the second colour's complete joint law, not merely its one-point marginals;
4. a structural representation of the desired joint law whose nonnegativity is manifest.

Every route currently on file lacks exactly this point. Stochastic domination controls one colour. Block reductions require special geometry. Finite LP solves certify individual instances. K6 facet work controls selected nonlinear costs. Exterior algebra controls selected inequalities or strata. None supplies a universal mechanism enforcing the whole complement distribution.

For a refutation, the missing ingredient is dual: an explicit nonlinear two-colour cost pair whose Pluecker-weighted violation can be made strictly positive at a source-valid orthogonal projection pair and then certified exactly.

# 8. (e) Nearest solved problem in the literature and what it has that this problem lacks

There are two especially informative solved relatives.

## 8.1 Lyons full-space case

If

\[
H_1\oplus H_2=\ell^2(E),
\]

orthogonal-complement duality gives the coupling directly: sample `X_1~P^{H_1}` and take `X_2=E\setminus X_1`; then `X_2~P^{H_1^perp}=P^{H_2}` and the union is deterministically `E`, hence has law `P^{\ell^2(E)}`.

**What it uses that the generic case lacks:** a deterministic complement involution on the whole ground set. When `H=H_1\oplus H_2` is proper, `H_1^perp=H_2\oplus H^perp`, so complementing `X_1` produces too many degrees of freedom and does not isolate the `H_2` law.

## 8.2 Vander Werf's determinantal-hypertree special case

Vander Werf's 2024 hypertree structure theorem gives a nontrivial special affirmative case of Lyons 2003 Question 10.1 using the cone/link decomposition of simplicial complexes and a structurally inductive description of the determinantal hypertree law.

**What it uses that the arbitrary subspace problem lacks:** a distinguished simplicial-cone decomposition with recursive lower-dimensional rooted-forest/link structure. A generic pair of orthogonal subspaces in `ell^2(E)` has no analogue of the distinguished vertex, cone/link split, or inherited combinatorial boundary operator that forces the two pieces to have the right joint law.

This is the closest literature lesson for the current obstruction: successful special cases possess an additional decomposition that makes complement compatibility structural rather than an arbitrary transportation constraint.
