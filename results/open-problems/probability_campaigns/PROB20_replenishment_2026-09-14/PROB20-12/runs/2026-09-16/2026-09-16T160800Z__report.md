## 3. Frozen exact finite coupling LP

Fix a finite ground set `E={1,...,n}` and orthogonal projections `P1,P2` with ranks `r1,r2`, `P1 P2=0`, and `PH=P1+P2`. A projection DPP has fixed cardinality equal to the projection rank. Let

- `A` range over `r1`-subsets,
- `B` range over `r2`-subsets,
- `U` range over `(r1+r2)`-subsets,
- variables `q_{A,B}>=0` range over disjoint pairs `A cap B=empty`.

The exact coupling equations are

```
sum_B q_{A,B} = p1(A),
sum_A q_{A,B} = p2(B),
sum_{A union B=U} q_{A,B} = pH(U),
```

where `pi(S)=det((Pi)_S)`. Write all equations as

`M q = b, q>=0`.

The normalization is unchanged:

\[
\delta(b)=\max\{-b^T y: M^T y\ge0,\ \|y\|_\infty\le1\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

A positive exact value is a Farkas certificate of infeasibility and therefore a finite counterexample to the source question. A floating-point positive value is not accepted.

## 4. New theorem: the metric is exactly an `l1` distance to the marginal cone

Let

\[
C:=\{Mq:q\ge0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^m.
\]

**Theorem.** For every right-hand-side vector `b`,

\[
\boxed{
\delta(b)=\operatorname{dist}_{\ell^1}(b,C)
=\min_{q\ge0}\|b-Mq\|_1.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

Put `z=-y`. Then the dual in (3.1) becomes

\[
\delta(b)=\max\{b^Tz:M^Tz\le0,\ \|z\|_\infty\le1\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

First prove the upper bound. For any `q>=0`, put `c=Mq in C`. For every feasible `z` in (4.2),

\[
z^Tc=q^TM^Tz\le0.
\]

Hence

\[
b^Tz=(b-c)^Tz+c^Tz
\le (b-c)^Tz
\le \|b-c\|_1\|z\|_\infty
\le \|b-c\|_1.
\]

Taking the maximum over feasible `z` and then the infimum over `q>=0` gives

\[
\delta(b)\le\min_{q\ge0}\|b-Mq\|_1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For the reverse inequality, linearize the `l1` norm. Write the primal distance problem as

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{q,u,v}\quad &\mathbf1^T(u+v)\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &b-Mq=u-v,\\
&q\ge0,\quad u\ge0,\quad v\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is feasible, for example with `q=0` and the positive/negative parts of `b`, and its objective is bounded below by zero. Its ordinary LP dual is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_z\quad &b^Tz\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &M^Tz\le0,\\
&-\mathbf1\le z\le\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The coordinate box in (4.5) is exactly `||z||_infty<=1`, so (4.5) is precisely (4.2). Finite-dimensional LP strong duality therefore gives equality between (4.4) and (4.5), proving (4.1). QED.

### Consequences

1. `delta(b)=0` if and only if the exact coupling equations are feasible.
2. `delta(b)>0` is not merely a yes/no separation certificate: it is the **minimum total `l1` marginal repair** needed to move `b` into the nonnegative coupling marginal cone.
3. Since a source-valid `b` consists of three probability vectors, `||b||_1=3`; because `0 in C`,
   \[
   0\le\delta(b)\le3.
   \]
4. The preceding run's Lipschitz lemma follows immediately from the general fact that distance to a fixed closed set is 1-Lipschitz in the ambient norm:
   \[
   |\delta(b)-\delta(b')|\le\|b-b'\|_1.
   \]
5. The primal form gives a second certification route: an exact nonnegative `q` with residual `r=b-Mq` proves the rigorous upper bound `delta(b)<=||r||_1`; an exact dual `z` gives the lower bound `delta(b)>=b^Tz`. Matching bounds certify the exact metric.

This theorem does **not** prove that source-valid determinantal vectors always lie in `C`; it only identifies the frozen search metric exactly.
