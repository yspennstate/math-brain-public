## 5. NEW theorem: one common principal pair

### Theorem 5.1

Assume that the CS data can be chosen so that

`p2=0` and `q2=0`.                                                  (5.1)

Equivalently, the distinguished vectors `a` and `b` each lie in the span of the CS kernel direction and the first common principal pair:

`a in span{m,a1}`, `b in span{n,b1}`.                              (5.2)

Then the strong operator inequality (3.13) holds. Hence the complete 540-facet inequality (3.6) is valid.

This theorem is valid over both `R` and `C`.

### Why this is strictly beyond the previous endpoint theorem

The preceding checkpoint treated `p=(1,0,0)` or `q=(1,0,0)`. Here both

`0<|p1|<1`, `0<|q1|<1`                                          (5.3)

are allowed. In such examples neither `a` nor `b` is forced to lie in `K`, and the color-0 source triangle mass can be positive. Thus Theorem 5.1 is a genuine interior extension along a two-sided principal-pair family, not a rephrasing of either endpoint Schubert boundary.

## 7. NEW exact block split

A direct compression in the ordered basis `(E0,...,E5)` shows that under `p2=q2=0`, `G` has no coupling between

`span{E0,E1,E3}` and `span{E2,E4,E5}`.                            (7.1)

Thus

`G = G_A direct-sum G_B`,                                        (7.2)

where both blocks are 3-by-3 real symmetric.

The proof will use principal minors, but only a small subset is needed because each 3-by-3 block can be handled by a one-step Schur complement.

## 8. Exact proof that `G_A>=0`

Use the coordinate order `(E0,E1,E3)` for `G_A`. Its first diagonal entry is

`(G_A)00=UV`.                                                     (8.1)

The two principal 2-by-2 minors containing that pivot reduce exactly to

`det G_A[{0,1}] = UVXY`,                                         (8.2)

`det G_A[{0,2}] = UV(1-X)(1-Y)=UVd`.                             (8.3)

The full determinant is

`det G_A = UVXY(1-X)(1-Y)=UVXYd`.                               (8.4)

If `UV>0`, take the Schur complement of the first entry. Its two diagonal entries are, by (8.2)--(8.3),

`XY` and `d`,                                                     (8.5)

and its determinant is, by (8.4),

`XYd`.                                                           (8.6)

Hence the 2-by-2 Schur complement is positive semidefinite, so `G_A>=0`. If `UV=0`, the result follows by continuity (or directly from the same minors).

No numerical approximation enters this argument.

## 9. Exact proof that `G_B>=0`

Use the coordinate order `(E2,E4,E5)` for `G_B`. Its first diagonal entry is

`b00 = UVX-UX-UY+U+XY`.                                         (9.1)

It has the manifestly nonnegative form

`b00=(1-U)XY + U[(1-X)(1-Y)+XV]`.                               (9.2)

Let

`M01=det G_B[{0,1}]`, `M02=det G_B[{0,2}]`.                      (9.3)

Both are polynomials of multidegree at most `(2,2,2,2)` in `(X,Y,U,V)`. Rather than hide a large expansion behind a floating-point positivity check, I used an exact Bernstein-basis certificate on the unit 4-cube.

For degree vector `(2,2,2,2)`, write

`B_k(X,Y,U,V)=prod_j binom(2,k_j) t_j^(k_j)(1-t_j)^(2-k_j)`,     (9.4)

with `t=(X,Y,U,V)` and `k_j in {0,1,2}`. Every `B_k>=0` on `[0,1]^4`.

The exact rational Bernstein coefficients of `M01` have the following value multiset:

- `0` with multiplicity 56;
- `1/8` with multiplicity 3;
- `1/4` with multiplicity 9;
- `1/2` with multiplicity 6;
- `3/4` with multiplicity 1;
- `1` with multiplicity 6.

Therefore

`M01>=0` on `[0,1]^4`.                                           (9.5)

The exact rational Bernstein coefficients of `M02` have value multiset:

- `0` with multiplicity 48;
- `1/8` with multiplicity 4;
- `1/4` with multiplicity 10;
- `5/16` with multiplicity 1;
- `3/8` with multiplicity 1;
- `1/2` with multiplicity 11;
- `1` with multiplicity 6.

Therefore

`M02>=0` on `[0,1]^4`.                                           (9.6)

Finally, the full determinant factors completely:

`det G_B`

`=[XY+U(1-X)]`                                                   (9.7)

` *[(1-X)(1-Y)+XV]`                                             (9.8)

` *[U(1-X)(1-Y)+VXY]`.                                         (9.9)

Every factor is nonnegative on the unit cube.

If `b00>0`, the Schur complement of the first pivot is 2-by-2. Its two diagonal entries are `M01/b00` and `M02/b00`, while its determinant is `det(G_B)/b00`; (9.5)--(9.9) therefore show that this Schur complement is positive semidefinite. Hence `G_B>=0`. If `b00=0`, pass to the boundary by continuity.

Combining Sections 8 and 9 gives

`G>=0`,                                                          (9.10)

which proves Theorem 5.1.

## 11. Failure log: false complex negatives from a linear Hodge map

A useful failed route was identified and closed in this run.

If one incorrectly replaces the antiunitary map (4.11) by the linear signed permutation `J0`, the resulting Hermitian-looking matrix can have negative eigenvalues. An exact rational complex example was found, and even an integer decomposable bivector gives a negative quadratic value for that *incorrect* matrix.

This initially looked like a possible violation of the new 540 facet. Re-deriving the complement relation from a unitary frame exposed the missing conjugation. With the correct target matrix (4.12), a bounded fresh random scan found no negative eigenvalue, and the spurious witness no longer has the claimed meaning.

Nothing from the incorrect linearized calculation is used in Theorem 5.1. This failure is retained because it is a high-risk pitfall for any future complex exterior-algebra attack.

## 13. Next attack

The most useful next step is now clear.

The new theorem kills the first genuinely coupled two-sided slice, so the remaining generic difficulty requires both `p2` and `q2` to be nonzero (after labeling principal pairs). There is exactly one gauge-invariant phase cycle in the generic complex CS data. The next attack should therefore:

1. put `p0,p1,p2,q0,q1` in nonnegative real gauge and write `q2=|q2| e^{i phi}`;
2. derive the correct antiunitary gap as a Hermitian trigonometric matrix polynomial in `e^{i phi}`;
3. search for an exact Fejer-Riesz/Gram factorization, or prove that the minimum in `phi` occurs at the real endpoints `phi=0,pi`;
4. in parallel, attack the one-sided slice `p2=0` with `q2` arbitrary. That slice has no genuine phase cycle either and may admit a higher-dimensional exact Bernstein certificate.

A universal proof of the strong operator target would settle the entire 540 orbit at once. A certified failure of the strong target would not by itself refute the facet, because the actual color-1 Pluecker form is decomposable; any counterexample search must preserve that distinction.
