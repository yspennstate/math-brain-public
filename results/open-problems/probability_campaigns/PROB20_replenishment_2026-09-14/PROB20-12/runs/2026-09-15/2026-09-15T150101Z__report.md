## 2. NEW THEOREM — a general one-extra-point multi-fibre cover inequality

This run proves a new exact family of **linear multi-fibre cover inequalities for arbitrary ranks**.

Let `E` be finite and

`C^E = H0 direct-sum H1 direct-sum H2`,

with ranks

`r0=dim H0`, `r1=dim H1`, `r2=dim H2`,

so `r0+r1+r2=|E|`. Assume `r0,r1>=1`.

Let `Xi~P_Hi` denote the three individual projection-DPP marginals. Fix a set

`A subset E`, `|A|=r2+1`,

and put

`B=E\A`, so `|B|=r0+r1-1`.

### Theorem 2.1 — one-extra-point cover inequality

The determinantal marginals always satisfy

`P(X2 subset A) <= P(X0 subset B) + P(X1 subset B)`.             (2.1)

Equivalently, in basis-probability notation,

`sum_(D subset A, |D|=r2) mu_2(D)`

`<= sum_(D subset B, |D|=r0) mu_0(D)`

` + sum_(D subset B, |D|=r1) mu_1(D)`.                           (2.2)

This is exactly a valid deterministic cover inequality for any desired joint partition: if `X2 subset A`, there is exactly one point of `A\X2` left for `X0 union X1`; whichever of `X0,X1` receives that one extra point, the other color lies entirely in `B`.

The content is that the three separate DPP marginals satisfy this necessary **multi-fibre** inequality even before the desired joint coupling is known.

### Proof

Let `P_i` be the projection onto `H_i`, and compress to `B`:

`C_i = P_B P_i P_B`, viewed as an `m x m` matrix with

`m=|B|=r0+r1-1`.

Then

`C_0+C_1+C_2=I_m`,

`C_i >=0`, and `rank C_i <= r_i`.

#### Step 1: identify the left side

Because `X2` has fixed cardinality `r2`, its complement `Y=E\X2` has law

`P_(H2^perp)=P_(H0 direct-sum H1)`.

The event `X2 subset A` is the same as `B subset Y`. Therefore the projection-DPP inclusion formula gives

`P(X2 subset A)=det((P_0+P_1)_B)=det(C_0+C_1)`.                  (2.3)

#### Step 2: identify the right side

For color 0,

`P(X0 subset B)`

is the sum of all `r0 x r0` principal minors of `C_0`, because `X0` has fixed cardinality `r0`. Hence

`P(X0 subset B)=e_(r0)(C_0)`,                                  (2.4)

where `e_k` denotes the `k`th elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues. Likewise

`P(X1 subset B)=e_(r1)(C_1)`.                                  (2.5)

It remains to prove the matrix inequality

`det(C_0+C_1) <= e_(r0)(C_0)+e_(r1)(C_1)`.                     (2.6)

#### Step 3: determinant polynomial with nonnegative coefficients

Consider

`F(t0,t1,t2)=det(t0 C_0+t1 C_1+t2 C_2)`.

Every coefficient of `F` is nonnegative. Indeed, factor

`C_i=V_i V_i*`

with `V_i` an `m x r_i` restricted frame, concatenate the three column blocks, and apply Cauchy-Binet to

`[sqrt(t0)V_0, sqrt(t1)V_1, sqrt(t2)V_2]
 [sqrt(t0)V_0, sqrt(t1)V_1, sqrt(t2)V_2]*`.

Each coefficient is a sum of squared absolute `m x m` minors.

Write

`F(t0,t1,t2)=sum_(a+b+c=m) c_(a,b,c) t0^a t1^b t2^c`,

with every `c_(a,b,c)>=0`.

The rank bounds force

`c_(a,b,c)=0` if `a>r0` or `b>r1`.

Since `m=r0+r1-1`, setting `t2=0` leaves only two possible exponent pairs:

`(a,b)=(r0,r1-1)` or `(r0-1,r1)`.

Thus

`det(C_0+C_1)=c_(r0,r1-1,0)+c_(r0-1,r1,0)`.                    (2.7)

Next set `t0=1`, `t1=t2=s`. Then

`F(1,s,s)=det(C_0+s(I-C_0))`.

Since `rank C_0<=r0`, the coefficient of the lowest potentially nonzero power

`s^(m-r0)=s^(r1-1)`

is exactly `e_(r0)(C_0)`. Comparing with the multivariate expansion gives

`e_(r0)(C_0)=sum_(b+c=r1-1) c_(r0,b,c)`.                        (2.8)

Similarly,

`e_(r1)(C_1)=sum_(a+c=r0-1) c_(a,r1,c)`.                        (2.9)

Subtracting (2.7) from (2.8)+(2.9) leaves only coefficients having `c>=1`, all nonnegative. Therefore (2.6) holds, and (2.1) follows. QED.

### Exact slack formula

The proof gives the explicit nonnegative slack

`P(X0 subset B)+P(X1 subset B)-P(X2 subset A)`

`= sum_(b+c=r1-1, c>=1) c_(r0,b,c)`

`  + sum_(a+c=r0-1, c>=1) c_(a,r1,c)`.                         (2.10)

Thus equality has a concrete mixed-minor degeneracy interpretation.

## 3. NEW COROLLARY — a genuinely multi-fibre facet orbit in the first hard K6 case

Specialize to

`r0=r1=r2=2`, `|E|=6`.

Let `A` be any three vertices and `B=E\A` the complementary three vertices. Then Theorem 2.1 becomes

`sum_(e subset A, |e|=2) mu_2(e)`

`<= sum_(e subset B, |e|=2) mu_0(e)
    + sum_(e subset B, |e|=2) mu_1(e)`.                         (3.1)

For example, with

`A={0,1,2}`, `B={3,4,5}`,

we obtain

`mu_2(01)+mu_2(02)+mu_2(12)`

`<= mu_0(34)+mu_0(35)+mu_0(45)
   +mu_1(34)+mu_1(35)+mu_1(45)`.                               (3.2)

This is a true **three-fibre** inequality: its left side contains all three target edges inside the triangle `A`.

The deterministic cover interpretation is transparent. If color 2 occupies an edge inside `A`, then after removing that edge there remains one vertex of `A` and all three vertices of `B`. In any perfect matching of those four vertices, exactly one of colors 0 and 1 must use an edge lying wholly inside `B`.

## 6. General multi-fibre square-root fallback bound

The exterior-algebra viewpoint also gives a weaker but completely general bound for arbitrary deterministic covers.

Let `K` be any family of target color-2 bases. Let `S0` be a family of color-0 bases and `S1` a family of color-1 bases such that every disjoint pair `(B0,B1)` whose complement belongs to `K` satisfies

`B0 in S0` or `B1 in S1`.

Write the unit Pluecker vectors as `omega_0,omega_1`, and let `Pi_K` project the wedge `omega_0 wedge omega_1` onto coordinates whose complement lies in `K`. Decompose

`omega_0=alpha+alpha_perp`, with `alpha` supported on `S0`,

`omega_1=beta+beta_perp`, with `beta` supported on `S1`.

The cover hypothesis gives

`Pi_K(alpha_perp wedge beta_perp)=0`.

Using the contraction bound for exterior multiplication,

`||u wedge v|| <= ||u|| ||v||`,

one gets

`sqrt(mu_2(K)) <= sqrt(mu_0(S0)) + sqrt(mu_1(S1))`,              (6.1)

and therefore

`mu_2(K) <= 2[mu_0(S0)+mu_1(S1)]`.                              (6.2)

This does **not** replace the desired linear cover inequality with constant 1, but it gives a universal factor-two Hall-type bound and clarifies exactly where multi-fibre interference enters. Theorem 2.1 is a class where determinant structure recovers the sharp constant 1.
