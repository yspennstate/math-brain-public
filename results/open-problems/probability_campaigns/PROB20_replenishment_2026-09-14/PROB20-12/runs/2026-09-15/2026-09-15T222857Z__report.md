## 4. A concrete degree-four orientation-cube trade

`eps=(eps1,eps2,eps3) in {0,1}^3`,

Define

`Q = sum_(|eps| odd) e_(s_eps) - sum_(|eps| even) e_(s_eps)`.     (4.1)

### Proposition 4.1 — `AQ=0`

Summing its indicator against the parity sign `(-1)^(eps1+eps2+eps3)` cancels over that unused bit. This holds for every row, so `AQ=0`.

## 9. General cycle-parity move behind the cube

The orientation cube is not an accidental table relation. There is a general construction for rank-two colored pair partitions.

Take a cycle of distinct colors

`c1-c2-...-ck-c1`, `k>=3`,

and assign its `k` color-pairs `{c_j,c_(j+1)}` to `k` disjoint ground pairs. Orient each pair by one bit. Every cycle color then occurs at exactly two ground vertices. If other colors are present, freeze their assigned pairs identically in all states.

The alternating parity sum over all `2^k` orientations gives a kernel relation with `2^(k-1)` states on each side. Any one colored-edge marginal involving a cycle color depends on at most the two orientation bits incident to that color; for `k>=3`, summing over an unused orientation bit cancels the parity signed contribution.

For `k=3`, this is exactly the `4-vs-4` cube relation above.

This general construction may be useful in higher irreducible rank-two profiles; no claim is made here that these cycle moves generate the higher-rank integer kernels.
