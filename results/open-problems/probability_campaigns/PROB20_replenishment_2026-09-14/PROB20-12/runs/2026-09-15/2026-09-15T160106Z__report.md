## 3. NEW THEOREM — mixed-determinant coupling of DPP cardinalities

The most useful form is matrix-theoretic and does not require projections.

### Theorem 3.1 — count coupling for a finite POVM

Let `Q_0,...,Q_{k-1}` be positive semidefinite `m x m` matrices such that

`Q_0+...+Q_{k-1}=I_m`.

For each `i`, let `Y_i` be a determinantal process on an `m`-point ground set with kernel `Q_i`.

Then there exists a coupling of the **cardinalities**

`N_i = |Y_i|`

such that

`N_0+...+N_{k-1}=m` almost surely.

An explicit joint law is obtained from the coefficients of

`F(t_0,...,t_{k-1}) = det(sum_i t_i Q_i)`.                      (3.1)

Writing

`F(t)=sum_{a_0+...+a_{k-1}=m} c_a product_i t_i^{a_i}`,          (3.2)

define

`P(N_0=a_0,...,N_{k-1}=a_{k-1}) = c_a`.                        (3.3)

### Proof

Factor each positive semidefinite matrix as

`Q_i=V_i V_i^*`.

Concatenate the factors,

`V=[V_0 V_1 ... V_{k-1}]`.

If `D(t)` is the block-diagonal matrix that multiplies the columns in block `V_i` by `t_i`, then

`sum_i t_i Q_i = V D(t) V^*`.

Cauchy-Binet gives

`det(V D(t) V^*)`

`= sum_{S: |S|=m} |det V_S|^2 product_{j in S} d_j`,             (3.4)

where each `d_j` is one of the variables `t_i`. Therefore every coefficient `c_a` in (3.2) is a sum of squared absolute minors and hence

`c_a>=0`.                                                        (3.5)

At `t_0=...=t_{k-1}=1`,

`F(1,...,1)=det(sum_i Q_i)=det I_m=1`,                          (3.6)

so the coefficients form a probability distribution supported on compositions of `m`.

It remains to check the marginal of `N_i`. Set all variables except `t_i=z` equal to one. Then

`F(1,...,z,...,1)`

`=det(zQ_i+sum_{j!=i}Q_j)`

`=det(I-Q_i+zQ_i)`.                                             (3.7)

For a finite DPP of kernel `Q_i`, the right side is exactly the probability generating function of `|Y_i|`. Thus the `i`th marginal of (3.3) is the law of `|Y_i|`. Since every coefficient is homogeneous of total degree `m`, the coupled counts sum to `m` almost surely. QED.

### Remarks

1. This is an exact coupling of cardinalities only, not of the point sets `Y_i`.
2. The theorem works for any finite number of colors and arbitrary positive contractions summing to the identity.
3. The coefficient nonnegativity is stronger than the specific coefficient-cover argument used in the previous threshold report: all deterministic inequalities on the composition vector `(N_i)` may now be transferred at once.

No claim is made that this matrix statement is absent from the mixed-discriminant literature; it is recorded here because it gives a direct structural reduction for PROB20-12.

## 4. Corollary for orthogonal projection DPPs on an arbitrary coordinate cut

Let

`l2(E)=H_0 direct-sum ... direct-sum H_{k-1}`

be a finite orthogonal decomposition, or a countable one with `B subset E` finite. Let `P_i` be the corresponding orthogonal projections and let

`X_i~P^{H_i}`

be the separate projection-DPP marginals.

Fix a finite coordinate set `B`. On `l2(B)` define the compressions

`C_i=P_B P_i P_B`.

Then

`C_0+...+C_{k-1}=I_B`.                                          (4.1)

Restriction of a DPP to `B` is the DPP of the compressed kernel, so

`|X_i intersect B|`

has the same law as the cardinality of a DPP with kernel `C_i`. Theorem 3.1 therefore gives:

### Corollary 4.1 — exact cut-count coupling

For every finite `B subset E`, there exists a coupling of integer variables

`N_i stackrel(d)= |X_i intersect B|`

such that

`sum_i N_i=|B|` almost surely.                                  (4.2)

This is valid even when `E` is countably infinite, because only the finite compression to `B` is used.

For the original two-subspace question, adjoin

`H_0=(H_1 direct-sum H_2)^perp`.

Then every fixed coordinate cut admits the **correct three color-count marginals of a genuine partition**, even though consistency of those count couplings across many different cuts is still open.

## 5. Dual consequence — every one-cut-symmetric Farkas inequality is automatic

Assume now that `E` is finite and the full decomposition has ranks `r_i` summing to `|E|`. Fix `B subset E`.

Let each dual cost depend only on the number of selected coordinates in `B`:

`f_i(A)=g_i(|A intersect B|)`.                                   (5.1)

Using the count coupling from Corollary 4.1,

`sum_i E[f_i(X_i)] = E[sum_i g_i(N_i)]`.                        (5.2)

The joint count vector is supported on exactly the deterministic count profiles compatible with an ordered partition: `sum_i N_i=|B|`, together with the automatic rank/capacity bounds on each `N_i`. Hence

`sum_i E[f_i(X_i)]`

`<= max_{A_0 disjoint-union ... disjoint-union A_{k-1}=E}`

`      sum_i g_i(|A_i intersect B|)`.                            (5.3)

Thus no Farkas separator for Lyons's problem can have all of its color costs depending only on the intersection cardinality with one fixed coordinate subset.

Equivalently, for a fixed bipartition `B | E\B`, the projection of the determinantal marginal vector onto the corresponding count statistics already lies in the exact partition-count polytope.

This strictly contains the earlier threshold-cover inequalities: thresholds are only particular choices of the functions `g_i`.

## 6. Dual-column DPP interpretation of the coefficient coupling

There is a useful geometric interpretation of the coefficients in the projection case.

Choose a unitary matrix `U` whose column blocks `J_i` span the mutually orthogonal subspaces `H_i`. For a finite row set `B`, consider the rank-`|B|` projection on the **column index set**

`K_B = U^* P_B U`.                                               (6.1)

Its projection DPP `S_B` satisfies

`P(S_B=S)=|det U[B,S]|^2`, `|S|=|B|`.                           (6.2)

Grouping columns according to the color blocks `J_i`, Cauchy-Binet shows

`P(|S_B intersect J_i|=a_i for all i)=c_a`,                     (6.3)

where `c_a` is exactly the coefficient in Theorem 3.1 for the compressed matrices `C_i=P_B P_i P_B`.

So the synthetic cut-count coupling is itself the color-count vector of a genuine **dual projection DPP on the column labels**.

This interpretation pinpoints the remaining obstruction. For every individual coordinate cut we have a canonical positive count coupling. The unresolved problem is to refine these coarse color counts into one globally consistent coloring of all coordinates with the required set-valued DPP marginals. Interference re-enters precisely at that refinement step.

## 7. Exact K6 classification of all one-cut-symmetric constraints

Return to the first open rank profile

`r_0=r_1=r_2=2`, `|E|=6`.

For a fixed `B subset E`, write

`N_i=|X_i intersect B|`,

`u_i=P(N_i=2)`,

`v_i=P(N_i=0)`.                                                  (7.1)

Because each `X_i` has exactly two points, the full one-color count distribution is determined by `(u_i,v_i)`.

The count-coupling theorem says that `(u_i,v_i)` lies in the convex hull of the deterministic triples `(N_0,N_1,N_2)` with each `N_i in {0,1,2}` and `sum_i N_i=|B|`. These tiny polytopes can be classified exactly.

### 7.1 Two-point side: `|B|=2`

The only deterministic count states are the six permutations of

`(2,0,0)` and `(1,1,0)`.                                         (7.2)

Let `alpha_i` be the mass of the state having `N_i=2`, and let `beta_i` be the mass of the state having `N_i=0` and the other two counts equal one. Then necessarily

`alpha_i=u_i`,                                                   (7.3)

and

`beta_i=v_i-u_j-u_k`, `{i,j,k}={0,1,2}`.                        (7.4)

The affine identity is

`sum_i v_i - sum_i u_i = 1`.                                    (7.5)

Because the six deterministic count states form a 5-simplex, the complete nontrivial count constraints are exactly

`u_i>=0`,                                                        (7.6)

`v_i-u_j-u_k>=0`.                                                (7.7)

After using (7.5), (7.7) is the previously proved `s=2` threshold facet. If `A=E\B` is the complementary four-set, for example

`v_0 >= u_1+u_2`

is equivalent to

`P(X_1 subset A)+P(X_2 subset A) <= 1+P(X_0=B)`.                (7.8)

Thus the `s=2` orbit is not merely one convenient threshold family: modulo elementary nonnegativity, it is the **complete** set of Farkas constraints visible to any cost invariant under permutations within a fixed `2|4` bipartition.

### 7.2 Three-point side: `|B|=3`

The deterministic count states are

`(1,1,1)`

and the six permutations of

`(2,1,0)`.                                                       (7.9)

Let

`t=sum_i u_i=sum_i v_i`.                                         (7.10)

The `(1,1,1)` state has mass `1-t`. The six other states can be indexed by ordered pairs `(i,j)`, `i!=j`, where color `i` has count two and color `j` has count zero. Let their masses be `q_ij`. Then

`sum_{j!=i} q_ij = u_i`,                                        (7.11)

`sum_{i!=j} q_ij = v_j`.                                        (7.12)

This is a transportation problem on the bipartite graph `K_{3,3}` with its diagonal deleted. Fractional Hall is exact. For a singleton left vertex `i`, the neighbor capacity is `t-v_i`, giving

`u_i+v_i<=t`.                                                    (7.13)

For any two or three left vertices the neighborhood is all three right vertices, so the remaining Hall conditions are automatic. Together with nonnegativity and

`t<=1`,                                                          (7.14)

these are necessary and sufficient.

Equation (7.14) is exactly the aggregate three-vertex blossom inequality

`sum_i P(X_i subset B)<=1`.                                     (7.15)

Equation (7.13) is exactly the `s=1` triangle-cover orbit after using `t=sum_j u_j`:

`v_i <= sum_{j!=i} u_j`.                                        (7.16)

Therefore, modulo elementary nonnegativity, the aggregate blossom plus the `s=1` threshold facets are the **complete** one-cut-symmetric constraints for every `3|3` bipartition.

### 7.3 Other cut sizes

- `|B|=0` or `6`: deterministic identities.
- `|B|=1` or `5`: the count coupling is the elementary one-coordinate partition law and yields no new facet beyond one-point constraints.
- `|B|=4`: equivalent to `|E\B|=2` because `|X_i|=2`; hence it gives the same `s=2` orbit.

So in K6 the entire family of one-cut-symmetric dual inequalities is generated by:

1. elementary nonnegativity/count constraints;
2. aggregate matching blossoms on `3|3` cuts;
3. the `s=1` triangle facets;
4. the `s=2` four-set facets.

Any still-missing K6 facet must therefore distinguish configurations that have the **same color count profile across every single chosen bipartition used by that facet**; it cannot be reduced to a cost depending only on one cut count.

## 8. NEW special-case full coupling from common bipartition exchangeability

The count coupling can be lifted all the way to a set-valued partition when the separate DPP marginals have enough symmetry.

### Theorem 8.1 — common two-block exchangeable case

Let `E` be finite and

`l2(E)=H_0 direct-sum ... direct-sum H_{k-1}`

with ranks `r_i`. Suppose there is a bipartition

`E=B disjoint-union C`

such that, for every `i`, the DPP law `P^{H_i}` is invariant under all coordinate permutations in

`S_B x S_C`.                                                     (8.1)

A sufficient matrix condition is that every projection `P_i` commute with these permutation matrices.

Then there exists a joint random ordered partition

`E=X_0 disjoint-union ... disjoint-union X_{k-1}`

with

`X_i~P^{H_i}` for every `i`.                                    (8.2)

In particular, Lyons's desired union coupling holds for every pair of blocks in this decomposition.

### Proof

By invariance under `S_B x S_C` and fixed cardinality `|X_i|=r_i`, the conditional law of `X_i` given

`a_i=|X_i intersect B|`

is uniform over all `r_i`-subsets having exactly `a_i` points in `B` and `r_i-a_i` points in `C`.

Use Corollary 4.1 to couple the count vector `(a_i)` so that

`sum_i a_i=|B|` almost surely.                                   (8.3)

Since `sum_i r_i=|E|`, automatically

`sum_i (r_i-a_i)=|C|`.                                          (8.4)

Conditional on `(a_i)`, choose uniformly an ordered partition of `B` into pieces `B_i` of sizes `a_i`, and independently choose uniformly an ordered partition of `C` into pieces `C_i` of sizes `r_i-a_i`. Set

`X_i=B_i union C_i`.                                             (8.5)

The sets form a partition of `E`. For each fixed `i` and count `a_i`, the marginal `X_i` is uniform on exactly the orbit of `r_i`-subsets with `a_i` points in `B`, which is the conditional law of `P^{H_i}` by (8.1). Averaging over the correct count marginal gives `X_i~P^{H_i}`. QED.

### Concrete non-rank-one `(2,2,2)` example

Let

`E={1,2,3,4,5,6}`, `B={1,2,3}`, `C={4,5,6}`.

Take

- `H_1={x: supp(x) subset B, sum_{b in B} x_b=0}`, rank two;
- `H_0={x: supp(x) subset C, sum_{c in C} x_c=0}`, rank two;
- `H_2=span{1_B,1_C}`, rank two.

These three spaces are mutually orthogonal, sum to `C^6`, and are invariant under `S_B x S_C`. The theorem gives a full three-color partition coupling even though every block has rank two.

For the pair `(H_1,H_2)` the coupling can be seen explicitly: choose `b` uniformly from `B` and `c` uniformly from `C`, let

`X_1=B\{b}`, `X_2={b,c}`.

Then `X_1` is the rank-two projection DPP on the sum-zero subspace of `B`, `X_2` is the direct sum of the two rank-one uniform DPPs on `B` and `C`, and

`X_1 union X_2 = B union {c}`

has the projection-DPP law of `H_1 direct-sum H_2`.

This is only a structured special case, not a solution of the unrestricted `(2,2,2)` problem.
