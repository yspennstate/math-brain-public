## 4. Adapted basis at the singleton vertex

Let `U=[U0 U1 U2]` be a `6 x 6` unitary with two-column blocks spanning `H0,H1,H2`. Write

`p_i = (P_i)_{dd}`,

so `p_i>=0` and

`p_0+p_1+p_2=1`.                                             (4.1)

Within each two-plane `H_i`, choose an orthonormal basis `(f_i,g_i)` with

`f_i(d)=0`,

`g_i(d)=sqrt(p_i)>=0`.                                       (4.2)

Put

`b_i = g_i - sqrt(p_i) e_d`.

Then `b_i` lies in `e_d^perp` and

`||b_i||^2 = 1-p_i =: w_i`.                                  (4.3)

Orthogonality of the six columns gives three key facts.

First, `f_0,f_1,f_2` are orthonormal.

Second, each `b_i` is orthogonal to every `f_j`.

Third, because `e_d=sum_i sqrt(p_i) g_i`,

`sqrt(p_0)b_0 + sqrt(p_1)b_1 + sqrt(p_2)b_2 = 0`.              (4.4)

The vectors `b_i` span the two-dimensional orthogonal complement of `span(f_0,f_1,f_2)` inside `e_d^perp`, and satisfy the Parseval identity

`sum_i |b_i><b_i| = P_A`,                                    (4.5)

where `A` here denotes that two-dimensional vector subspace (not the edge class of Section 3).

## 5. Project onto the two-vertex block R

Identify the coordinate subspace `l2(R)` with `C^2`, and set

`u_i = P_R f_i`,

`v_i = P_R b_i`.                                              (5.1)

Because the `f_i` together with the two-dimensional `b`-space resolve `e_d^perp`, compression of (4.5) gives

`sum_i u_i u_i^* + sum_i v_i v_i^* = I_2`.                   (5.2)

Thus the `2 x 6` matrix

`W = [u_0,u_1,u_2,v_0,v_1,v_2]`

has orthonormal rows.

For any two columns `x,y` of `W`, define

`q(x,y)=|det[x,y]|^2 >=0`.                                    (5.3)

Cauchy–Binet and the rank-two projection identity give

`sum_{x<y} q(x,y)=1`,                                         (5.4)

and, for every column `x`,

`||x||^2 = sum_{y != x} q(x,y)`.                              (5.5)

Equation (4.4) survives projection:

`sqrt(p_0)v_0 + sqrt(p_1)v_1 + sqrt(p_2)v_2 = 0`.              (5.6)

## 6. Rewrite the facet slack

For a fixed color `i`, the basis `(f_i,g_i)` makes all edge-class probabilities explicit.

Edges containing `d` and a point of `R` have total mass

`mu_i(dR)=p_i ||u_i||^2`.                                     (6.1)

Edges wholly inside `R` have mass

`mu_i(E(R))=q(u_i,v_i)`.                                      (6.2)

The remaining non-`d` mass comes from the two-form `f_i wedge b_i`, whose squared norm is `w_i`. Using the decomposition `T disjoint-union R` inside `e_d^perp`, one obtains the standard rank-two count identities. Substituting those identities into (3.1), and using (4.1) plus the Parseval relation (5.2), gives the exact simplification

`1-L = 3D`,                                                    (6.3)

where `L` is the left side of (3.1) and

`D = w_0 ||u_0||^2 + ||v_0||^2 - q(u_0,v_0)`

`    + p_1 ||u_1||^2 + q(u_2,v_2) - p_1`.                    (6.4)

Therefore proving the residue facet is equivalent to proving

`D>=0`.                                                        (6.5)

This reduction was checked both algebraically and against direct Pluecker-minor evaluation on random complex unitaries.

## 7. Expand D into pair-minor weights

Use (5.4)-(5.5) to replace every column norm and the constant `1` in (6.4) by `q`-weights. After collecting terms and using

`w_0=p_1+p_2`, `w_1=p_0+p_2`,

the exact identity is

`D = G + B_1 + B_2`,                                          (7.1)

with

`G = w_0 q(u_0,u_1)`

`  + p_2 [q(u_0,u_2)+q(u_0,v_0)+q(u_0,v_1)+q(u_0,v_2)]`

`  + q(u_1,v_0)`,                                             (7.2)

and

`B_1 = w_1[q(u_2,v_0)+q(u_2,v_2)] - p_1 q(u_2,v_1)`,          (7.3)

`B_2 = w_1[q(v_0,v_1)+q(v_0,v_2)] - p_1 q(v_1,v_2)`.          (7.4)

Clearly `G>=0`. It remains to prove `B_1,B_2>=0`.

## 8. The first potentially negative bracket is Cauchy

Set

`a=det(u_2,v_0)`, `b=det(u_2,v_1)`, `c=det(u_2,v_2)`.

Taking determinant with `u_2` in (5.6) gives

`sqrt(p_0)a + sqrt(p_1)b + sqrt(p_2)c = 0`.                    (8.1)

Hence

`p_1 |b|^2 = |sqrt(p_0)a + sqrt(p_2)c|^2`

`<= (p_0+p_2)(|a|^2+|c|^2)`

`= w_1 (|a|^2+|c|^2)`.                                       (8.2)

This is exactly `B_1>=0`.

No division by any `p_i` is needed, so boundary cases are included.

## 9. The second bracket is controlled by the same frame relation

Let

`A_01=det(v_0,v_1)`,

`A_02=det(v_0,v_2)`,

`A_12=det(v_1,v_2)`.

Wedging (5.6) with `v_0` and `v_2` gives

`sqrt(p_1) A_01 + sqrt(p_2) A_02 = 0`,                         (9.1)

`sqrt(p_0) A_02 + sqrt(p_1) A_12 = 0`.                         (9.2)

If `p_1>0`,

`p_1 |A_01|^2 = p_2 |A_02|^2`,

`p_1 |A_12|^2 = p_0 |A_02|^2`.                                (9.3)

Therefore

`B_2`

`= w_1(|A_01|^2+|A_02|^2)-p_1|A_12|^2`

`= [w_1(p_1+p_2)/p_1 - p_0]|A_02|^2`

`= (p_2/p_1)|A_02|^2 >=0`.                                   (9.4)

When `p_1=0`, the negative term in (7.4) vanishes and `B_2>=0` directly, so the boundary case follows without a limiting argument.

Combining Sections 7-9 gives `D>=0`, hence by (6.3) the desired residue facet.

## 11. Interpretation

The residue facet looked in the predecessor like it might require a new multiregion determinant-count coupling. The adapted-basis proof shows something more specific:

- the singleton row isolates three scalar ownership probabilities `p_i`;
- after removing that row, the orthogonal decomposition produces three orthonormal `f_i` vectors plus a two-dimensional Parseval frame `b_i`;
- projecting this five-dimensional resolution onto the two-coordinate block `R` yields a `2 x 6` row-isometry;
- the full facet slack is exactly three times a weighted sum of its squared `2 x 2` minors;
- the only two apparently negative groups are forced nonnegative by the single frame relation `sum sqrt(p_i)v_i=0`.

This is a different mechanism from the earlier bridge proof. The bridge orbit used Laplace expansion plus a pre-existing single-fibre cut; the residue orbit is an intrinsic rank-two Parseval-frame sum-of-squares argument.
