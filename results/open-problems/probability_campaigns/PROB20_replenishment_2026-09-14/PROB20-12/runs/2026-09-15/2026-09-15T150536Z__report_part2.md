## 1. NEW STRONGER THEOREM — full threshold-cover hierarchy

Let

`C^E = H0 direct-sum H1 direct-sum H2`

be a finite full orthogonal decomposition with ranks `r0,r1,r2`. Let `Xi~P_Hi` denote the separate projection-DPP marginals.

Choose an integer

`1 <= s <= r0+r1`,

a set

`A subset E`, `|A|=r2+s`,

and put

`B=E\A`, `m=|B|=r0+r1-s`.

Choose any integer `q` with

`0 <= q <= s-1`.

### Theorem 1.1 — threshold-cover inequality

The determinantal marginals satisfy

`P(X2 subset A)`

`<= P(|X0 intersect A| <= q)`

` + P(|X1 intersect A| <= s-q-1)`.                              (1.1)

This is exactly the deterministic threshold cover forced by a hypothetical joint partition.

Indeed, on the event `X2 subset A`, exactly `s` points of `A` remain for `X0 union X1`. If

`k=|X0 intersect A|`,

then

`|X1 intersect A|=s-k`.

Either `k<=q`, or `k>=q+1`, in which case `s-k<=s-q-1`. Hence the indicator inequality corresponding to (1.1) holds for every deterministic colored partition.

The theorem says the three **uncoupled** DPP marginals already obey this whole deterministic cover hierarchy.

## 2. Proof by coefficient coverage in one Gram determinant

Compress the three projections to `B`:

`C_i=P_B P_i P_B`,

so

`C_0+C_1+C_2=I_m`.

Consider the homogeneous determinant polynomial

`F(t0,t1,t2)=det(t0 C_0+t1 C_1+t2 C_2)`

`=sum_(a+b+c=m) c_(a,b,c) t0^a t1^b t2^c`.                    (2.1)

As in the first report, Cauchy-Binet applied to restricted frames shows

`c_(a,b,c)>=0`                                                                  (2.2)

for every triple.

### 2.1 Left side

Complement duality gives

`E\X2 ~ P_(H0 direct-sum H1)`.

Therefore

`P(X2 subset A)=P(B subset E\X2)=det(C_0+C_1)`

`=sum_(a+b=m) c_(a,b,0)`.                                      (2.3)

### 2.2 First threshold probability

Let

`N0=|X0 intersect B|`.

The DPP counting generating function on `B` is

`E[z^N0]=det(I-C_0+z C_0)`.

But

`det(t0 C_0+s(C_1+C_2))=F(t0,s,s)`,

so coefficient comparison in (2.1) yields

`P(N0=a)=sum_(b+c=m-a) c_(a,b,c)`.                              (2.4)

Since `|X0|=r0`,

`|X0 intersect A|<=q`

is equivalent to

`N0>=r0-q`.

Thus the first term on the right side of (1.1) is the sum of **all** nonnegative coefficients `c_(a,b,c)` with

`a>=r0-q`.                                                       (2.5)

### 2.3 Second threshold probability

Similarly,

`P(|X1 intersect A|<=s-q-1)`

is the sum of all coefficients with

`b>=r1-(s-q-1)=r1-s+q+1`.                                      (2.6)

### 2.4 Every left-side coefficient is covered

Take a coefficient `c_(a,b,0)` occurring on the left in (2.3). Then

`a+b=m=r0+r1-s`.

If `a>=r0-q`, it is included in the first right-side probability.

Otherwise, because `a` is integral,

`a<=r0-q-1`,

so

`b=m-a`

`>= r0+r1-s-(r0-q-1)`

`=r1-s+q+1`.

Then it is included in the second right-side probability.

Therefore every nonnegative coefficient contributing to the left side occurs at least once on the right side. Equation (1.1) follows. QED.

## 6. What the hierarchy says conceptually

The determinant polynomial

`F(t0,t1,t2)`

acts as a common nonnegative-coefficient certificate for a family of deterministic Hall-type covers.

For a fixed coordinate split `E=A disjoint-union B`, the exponent `a` records how many color-0 columns are used in a Cauchy-Binet term, and `b` records the analogous color-1 count. The event `X2 subset A` selects exactly the `c=0` layer. The two threshold events on the right split that layer by the elementary integer dichotomy

`a>=r0-q` or `b>=r1-s+q+1`.

The proof works because all determinant coefficients are nonnegative, so there is no cancellation after this combinatorial coefficient cover.

This gives a more systematic analytic mechanism than treating each finite facet separately.
