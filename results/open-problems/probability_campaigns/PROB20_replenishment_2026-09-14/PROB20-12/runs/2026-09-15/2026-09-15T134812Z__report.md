## 1. Cumulative reductions retained

### 1.1 Finite projection case suffices

If the theorem holds for orthogonal projections on every finite coordinate set, then it holds for arbitrary finite positive contractions `Q1,Q2` with `Q1+Q2<=I`: dilate the three-outcome POVM `(I-Q1-Q2,Q1,Q2)` to pairwise orthogonal projections, apply the projection coupling, and restrict back to the original coordinates.

For countable `E`, exhaust by finite coordinate sets, couple the compressed positive contractions, extend by zero, and take a weak subsequential limit in the compact product configuration space. Every fixed cylinder marginal is eventually exactly the corresponding restriction of `P_H1`, `P_H2`, and `P_H`.

Thus the unresolved core is finite.

### 1.2 Full three-color formulation

For finite `E`, adjoin

`H0=(H1 direct-sum H2)^perp`.

The desired coupling is equivalent to a random ordered partition `(X0,X1,X2)` of `E` with `Xi~P_Hi`. Correct union marginal automatically implies disjointness by Lyons 2003 Proposition 10.2.

### 1.3 Prior recovered exact fibre cut

The backfill records an exact single-fibre cover inequality. For a full finite decomposition `H0 direct-sum H1 direct-sum H2`, fix a basis candidate `A2` for color 2, put `C=E\A2`, let `S` be any family of possible color-0 bases inside `C`, and put

`T={C\B : B notin S}`.

Then

`mu_2(A2) <= mu_0(S)+mu_1(T)`.

Its strongest fixed-fibre form is

`mu_2(A2) <= sum_B min(mu_0(B),mu_1(C\B))`.

This remains a proved partial result; it does not by itself settle multi-fibre conflicts.

## 2. NEW EXACT THEOREM — aggregate perfect-matching feasibility in the first hard case

The first genuinely unresolved finite rank profile is

`dim H0=dim H1=dim H2=2`, `|E|=6`.

For each color `i` and each edge `e={u,v}` of `K6`, write

`mu_i(e)=P_Hi(Xi=e)=det(P_i[e])`,

where `P_i` is the rank-two orthogonal projection onto `Hi`.

Define the **aggregate edge vector**

`y_e = mu_0(e)+mu_1(e)+mu_2(e)`.

### Theorem 2.1

`y` lies in the perfect-matching polytope of `K6`. Equivalently, there exists a probability distribution on the 15 uncolored perfect matchings `M` of `K6` whose edge marginals are exactly `y`.

This removes every color-blind obstruction from the `(2,2,2)` case.

### Proof

The perfect-matching polytope of `K6` is described by:

1. `y_e>=0`;
2. degree equations `sum_(e incident v) y_e=1` for every vertex `v`;
3. odd-set inequalities `y(E(S)) <= (|S|-1)/2` for every odd `S subset E`.

For `K6`, the only nontrivial odd-set inequalities are the `|S|=3` inequalities; `|S|=1` is trivial, and `|S|=5` follows from the degree equation of the unique outside vertex.

#### Degree equations

For each rank-two DPP,

`sum_(e incident v) mu_i(e)=P(v in Xi)=(P_i)_(v,v)`.

Since `P0+P1+P2=I`,

`sum_(e incident v) y_e = sum_i (P_i)_(v,v)=1`.

#### Three-vertex odd-set inequality

Fix a three-element vertex set `S`. Let

`B_i = P_S P_i P_S`

be the `3 x 3` compression of `P_i` to `S`. Then:

- `B_i` is positive semidefinite;
- `rank B_i<=2`;
- `B0+B1+B2=I_3`.

Because `Xi` always has exactly two points,

`P(Xi subset S) = sum_(e subset S, |e|=2) mu_i(e) = e_2(B_i)`,

where `e_2` is the second elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues.

Consider

`F(t0,t1,t2)=det(t0 B0+t1 B1+t2 B2)`.

All coefficients of `F` are nonnegative. A direct proof uses Cauchy-Binet: write `B_i=A_i A_i*` with `A_i` a `3 x 2` matrix, concatenate the six columns, and expand the determinant of the resulting Gram matrix. Selecting any three columns contributes a squared `3 x 3` minor multiplied by the monomial recording how many selected columns came from each block. Hence every coefficient is a sum of squared absolute minors.

Since each `B_i` has rank at most two, the pure cubic coefficient `t_i^3` is zero.

Write the remaining coefficients schematically as the six `(2,1,0)` coefficients plus the single `(1,1,1)` coefficient `c_111>=0`.

Fix `i`, set `t_i=1` and the other two variables equal to `s`. Then

`F(1,s,s)=det(B_i+s(I-B_i))`.

If the eigenvalues of `B_i` are `lambda_1,lambda_2,0`, then

`det(B_i+s(I-B_i)) = s [lambda_1+s(1-lambda_1)] [lambda_2+s(1-lambda_2)]`.

Therefore the coefficient of `s` is

`lambda_1 lambda_2=e_2(B_i)`.

On the other hand, in the monomial expansion of `F`, that same linear coefficient is exactly the sum of the two `(2,1,0)` coefficients having exponent two on `t_i`.

Summing over `i=0,1,2` therefore gives

`sum_i e_2(B_i) = (sum of all six (2,1,0) coefficients)`.

But

`F(1,1,1)=det(B0+B1+B2)=1`.

The pure cubes vanish and the only remaining degree-three monomial is `t0 t1 t2`, whose coefficient is nonnegative. Hence

`sum_i e_2(B_i)=1-c_111 <=1`.

Thus

`y(E(S))=sum_i P(Xi subset S)<=1`,

which is exactly the `|S|=3` blossom inequality.

All perfect-matching-polytope constraints now hold, proving the theorem. QED.

### Exact slack formula

The proof yields more than the inequality:

`1-y(E(S)) = c_111(S) >=0`,

where `c_111(S)` is the coefficient of `t0 t1 t2` in

`det(t0 B0+t1 B1+t2 B2)`.

By the Cauchy-Binet description, this slack is a sum of squared `3 x 3` minors formed by selecting one column from each of the three restricted two-column frames. Equality in a triple blossom inequality therefore has a concrete geometric degeneracy criterion.

## 3. NEW CONSEQUENCE — every color-blind dual inequality is automatic

A Farkas dual functional in the `(2,2,2)` case consists of arbitrary real edge costs `f_i(e)` for the three colors. A separator would require

`sum_i sum_e mu_i(e) f_i(e) > max_(colored perfect matchings) sum_i f_i(e_i)`.

Suppose the costs are color-blind:

`f_0=f_1=f_2=g`.

Then the left side is

`sum_e y_e g(e)`.

By Theorem 2.1, `y` is a convex combination of uncolored perfect matchings, so

`sum_e y_e g(e) <= max_(perfect matching M) sum_(e in M) g(e)`.

The right side of the colored dual is exactly the same maximum because coloring does not change a color-blind cost. Therefore no color-blind functional can separate the orthogonal Pluecker marginals.

Adding arbitrary constants depending only on the color is an affine gauge, since every deterministic state uses exactly one edge of each color. Thus any genuine `(2,2,2)` separator must be color-sensitive after quotienting by these gauges.

Combined with the prior pairwise-disjoint-coupling result, this sharply narrows the dual search:

- a separator cannot effectively involve only two colors;
- it cannot be color-blind;
- it cannot be a single-fibre cover inequality already handled by the recovered theorem.

Any obstruction must coordinate several fibres and genuinely distinguish all three colors.

## 4. NEW EXACT EXTENDED FORMULATION — uncolored matching plus local Birkhoff coloring

Theorem 2.1 suggests splitting the problem into an uncolored stage and a coloring stage.

Let `M` range over the 15 perfect matchings of `K6`. The desired colored coupling exists **if and only if** there are nonnegative numbers

`p_M`

and

`z_(M,i,e)` for `e in M`, `i in {0,1,2}`,

such that:

1. `sum_M p_M=1`;
2. for every `M,i`, `sum_(e in M) z_(M,i,e)=p_M`;
3. for every `M,e in M`, `sum_i z_(M,i,e)=p_M`;
4. for every color-edge pair `(i,e)`, `sum_(M containing e) z_(M,i,e)=mu_i(e)`.

### Proof of equivalence

If a colored matching coupling exists, let `p_M` be the probability of the underlying uncolored matching and let `z_(M,i,e)` be the joint probability that the underlying matching is `M` and color `i` is assigned to edge `e`. The four displayed conditions are immediate.

Conversely, suppose the four conditions hold. For any `M` with `p_M>0`, the `3 x 3` matrix

`Z_M(i,e)=z_(M,i,e)/p_M`, `e in M`,

has every row sum and every column sum equal to one. Hence `Z_M` is doubly stochastic. By the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem it is a convex combination of the six permutations assigning the three colors bijectively to the three edges of `M`. Sample `M` with probability `p_M`, then sample a color permutation according to such a decomposition. The resulting colored perfect matching has edge-color marginals exactly `mu_i(e)`.

QED.

### What this achieves

Theorem 2.1 proves that the aggregate marginal `y` always admits at least one `p_M` with

`sum_(M containing e) p_M = y_e`.

The remaining problem is now isolated exactly:

> Can one choose an aggregate perfect-matching decomposition `p_M` that admits a simultaneous local doubly-stochastic lift `z` with the three prescribed color marginals?

The artificial non-realizable marginals preserved in the backfill fail precisely at this coloring-lift stage: their aggregate `y` is a valid mixture of two uncolored perfect matchings, but one of those matchings carries no edge in the support of the required third color.

This two-stage formulation is equivalent to the original six-point transport problem but exposes the missing structure more cleanly than the 90-state formulation.

## 5. Relation to recovered single-fibre cuts

Fix a target color-2 edge `e2`. Conditional on the uncolored matching containing `e2`, the remaining four vertices can be paired in exactly three uncolored ways, or equivalently there are six ordered `(color0 edge,color1 edge)` choices.

The recovered single-fibre cover inequalities are exactly the Hall/vertex-cover inequalities ensuring that mass assigned specifically to the fibre `e2` can be split among those six complementary ordered pairs without exceeding the available color-0/color-1 marginal capacities.

The current theorem shows that all uncolored blossom constraints are also automatic. Therefore the unsolved part in `(2,2,2)` is genuinely a **simultaneous multi-fibre coloring-capacity problem**: single fibres are feasible and the aggregate uncolored matching is feasible, but one must prove that all fibres can be realized at once.

This identifies the exact place where a max-flow/min-cut or total-dual-integrality proof would need to enter.
