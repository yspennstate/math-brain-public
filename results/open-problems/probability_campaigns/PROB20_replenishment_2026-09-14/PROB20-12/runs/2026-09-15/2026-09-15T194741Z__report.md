## 4. Exterior-algebra setup

Let `alpha_i` be a unit decomposable two-form spanning `wedge^2 H_i`. Since the three two-planes are mutually orthogonal and fill `C^6`,

`alpha_0 wedge alpha_1 wedge alpha_2`

is a unit volume form up to phase. Hence Jacobi/Hodge complementarity gives

`|Delta_0(01)|^2 = |(alpha_1 wedge alpha_2)_{2345}|^2`,

`|Delta_0(02)|^2 = |(alpha_1 wedge alpha_2)_{1345}|^2`.        (4.1)

Set

`A = span{e1,e2}`,

`C = span{e3,e4}`,

`d = e5`,

and define the two-dimensional target four-form space

`Y = A wedge e3 wedge e4 wedge e5`.                            (4.2)

Then the left side of (3.1) is exactly

`|| P_Y(alpha_1 wedge alpha_2) ||^2`.                          (4.3)

Define selected source spaces in `wedge^2 C^6`:

`D1 = (A wedge e5) direct-sum wedge^2 span{e3,e4,e5}`,          (4.4)

`D2 = C wedge e5`.                                             (4.5)

Thus an orthonormal coordinate basis of `D1` is

`{15,25,34,35,45}`,

and one of `D2` is

`{35,45}`.

Therefore

`||P_D1 alpha_1||^2`

` = mu_1(15)+mu_1(25)+mu_1(34)+mu_1(35)+mu_1(45)`,             (4.6)

`||P_D2 alpha_2||^2 = mu_2(35)+mu_2(45)`.                     (4.7)

The right side of (3.1) is precisely the squared norm in the orthogonal direct sum `D1 direct-sum D2` of

`z = (P_D1 alpha_1, P_D2 alpha_2)`.                            (4.8)

## 5. Exact two-fibre cover identity

Let `Q1=P_D1` and `Q2=P_D2`. Define a linear operator

`M : D1 direct-sum D2 -> Y`

by

`M(x,y) = P_Y( x wedge alpha_2 + ((I-Q1)alpha_1) wedge y )`.   (5.1)

The crucial identity is

`P_Y(alpha_1 wedge alpha_2) = M(Q1 alpha_1,Q2 alpha_2)`.        (5.2)

### Proof of (5.2)

It is enough to check a decomposable coordinate basis vector of `Y`. Take a unit vector `a in A`; after choosing its orthogonal mate `b in A`, the support of

`a wedge e3 wedge e4 wedge e5`

is the four-dimensional coordinate block `{a,3,4,5}`.

The six possible two-subsets assigned to the `alpha_1` factor are

`a3, a4, a5, 34, 35, 45`.

Four of them,

`a5,34,35,45`,

lie in `D1`. The only two that do not, `a3,a4`, have complementary pairs

`45,35`,

which lie in `D2`.

Thus every monomial contributing to `P_Y(alpha_1 wedge alpha_2)` is captured exactly once by the right side of (5.1): either its `alpha_1` pair lies in `D1`, or its `alpha_1` pair lies outside `D1` and then its `alpha_2` complementary pair lies in `D2`. This proves (5.2) by linearity.

This is the multi-fibre combinatorial cover encoded as one exterior operator.

## 6. Rotational fibre-gluing lemma

The nontrivial point is to prove

`||M||_op <= 1`.                                               (6.1)

That is where the earlier single-fibre theorem enters.

### Lemma 6.1

For every unit vector `eta in Y`,

`||M^* eta||^2 <= 1`.                                         (6.2)

Consequently `||M||_op<=1`.

### Proof

Every unit `eta in Y` can be written, up to phase, as

`eta = a wedge e3 wedge e4 wedge e5`                           (6.3)

for a unit `a in A`. Choose a unit `b in A` orthogonal to `a`. Regard

`{e0,b,a,e3,e4,e5}`

as a new orthonormal coordinate basis of `C^6`. This is only a unitary rotation inside the original two-dimensional block `A`, so the orthogonality hypotheses on `H0,H1,H2` are unchanged.

In this rotated basis, `eta` is the four-row complement of the target color-0 edge

`{e0,b}`.                                                      (6.4)

We now compute the squared row norm of `M` against `eta`.

The `D1` terms capable of wedging to `eta` are

`a5,34,35,45`.

Their complementary `alpha_2` coefficients are respectively

`34,a5,a4,a3`.

The `D2` terms are

`35,45`,

and because the second term in (5.1) uses `(I-Q1)alpha_1`, their complementary `alpha_1` coefficients are

`a4,a3`.

Hence

`||M^* eta||^2`

` = mu_2(34)+mu_2(a5)+mu_2(a4)+mu_2(a3)`

`   +mu_1(a4)+mu_1(a3)`.                                      (6.5)

Here, for example, `mu_2(a4)` means the squared Pluecker coordinate of `H2` on the rotated coordinate pair `{a,e4}`.

Now apply the already-proved arbitrary-rank single-fibre cover theorem to the target color-0 edge `{e0,b}`. Its four-coordinate complement is

`{a,e3,e4,e5}`.

Choose the color-1 side of the fibre cover for the four pairs

`a5,34,35,45`,

and choose the color-2 side for the remaining two pairs

`a3,a4`.

In the Cauchy-Schwarz proof of that theorem, the **second factor** is exactly the quantity in (6.5). The disjoint coupling of colors 1 and 2 proves that factor is at most one. Therefore

`||M^* eta||^2<=1`.

Since this holds for every unit vector `eta in Y`,

`||M^*||_op<=1`, hence `||M||_op<=1`. QED.

### Why the rotation is legitimate

The selected source subspaces are invariant under arbitrary unitary rotations of `A`:

- `A wedge e5` is invariant as a two-dimensional subspace;
- `wedge^2 span{e3,e4,e5}` is fixed;
- `C wedge e5` is fixed;
- `Y=A wedge e3 wedge e4 wedge e5` is invariant.

Thus (5.1) defines one basis-independent operator. Rotating `A` merely chooses a convenient coordinate basis in which a given row functional of that operator becomes a literal single-fibre auxiliary factor.

This is the exact mechanism that removes the factor-two loss of a naive triangle inequality.

## 8. General rotational fibre-gluing principle

The proof isolates a reusable principle.

Suppose a multi-target exterior cover is encoded by a linear operator

`M : D -> Y`

from a selected source-amplitude space `D` to a target-amplitude space `Y`. If:

1. the selected source and target subspaces are invariant under a unitary group acting transitively on the unit sphere of `Y`; and
2. after rotating any unit `eta in Y` to a coordinate target, the row norm `||M^*eta||^2` is exactly an auxiliary factor already bounded by a single-fibre theorem,

then `M` is automatically a contraction, because

`||M|| = ||M^*|| = sup_(||eta||=1) ||M^*eta|| <=1`.            (8.1)

This is stronger than proving coordinate rows separately. It explains why two target fibres can share the same right-side capacity sharply rather than paying it twice.

The present sparse facet is the first task-local use of this rotational gluing mechanism.

## 10. Failed / superseded approaches

- **Summing two single-fibre inequalities** loses the common right-side capacity twice and gives a factor-two-type bound; it cannot prove (3.1).
- A tempting reduction through the `s=1` triangle facet plus the three-term inequality

  `mu_2(34) <= mu_0(12)+mu_1(15)+mu_1(25)`

  is false: random orthogonal triples give strict violations of that auxiliary inequality.
- Treating two target fibres independently is therefore genuinely insufficient. The successful proof must retain them as a vector and prove contraction of the stacked operator.
- A direct attempt to prove the stacked operator contraction by guessing orthogonality of its two coordinate rows is false in a fixed basis; the rows generally have nonzero inner product. The correct argument is to bound **every** row functional after rotating the two-dimensional target block.
