## 2. Configuration and a square-free fibre lemma

Let `S` be the 90 balanced colorings of six ground vertices by colors `0,1,2`, with each color appearing twice. Equivalently, each state is a labeled perfect matching. Let

`A in Z^(45 x 90)`

be the colored-edge marginal matrix: a column has one `1` in each color block of the 15 edges of `K6`.

For a degree-`d` monomial `m = product_s x_s^(u_s)`, its `A`-degree records, separately for each color, the multiset of the `d` selected edges.

The new certificates use targets with the following stronger property:

> **square-free in every color block:** for each of the three colors, the target consists of `d` *distinct* edges, each with multiplicity exactly one.

Write the prescribed color-0 edges as `a_1,...,a_d`, color-1 edges as `b_1,...,b_d`, and color-2 edges as `c_1,...,c_d`.

### Lemma 2.1 — exact fibre as a finite 3-dimensional matching problem

For a square-free target, every monomial in the `A`-fibre is square-free in the state variables as well, and after fixing the order of the `a_i`, the fibre is in bijection with permutations `pi in S_d` satisfying

1. `a_i` and `b_{pi(i)}` are disjoint for every `i`;
2. the complementary edge

   `gamma(i,pi(i)) = E \ (a_i union b_{pi(i)})`

   belongs to `{c_1,...,c_d}` for every `i`;
3. the `d` complements `gamma(i,pi(i))` are all distinct, hence equal the prescribed color-2 set.

The corresponding state at position `i` is the unique balanced coloring whose three color edges are

`(a_i, b_{pi(i)}, gamma(i,pi(i)))`.

#### Proof

A state contributing to the target contributes exactly one edge in each color block. Since every target edge has coefficient one, no monomial in the fibre may repeat a colored edge. Thus every prescribed color-0 edge occurs in exactly one state, so we may index the states by the `a_i`. The color-1 edges are then assigned by a permutation `pi`. A triple of two-edge sets forms a balanced coloring precisely when the first two are disjoint and the third is their two-vertex complement. Finally, the color-2 target requires those complements to use every prescribed `c_j` exactly once. This gives a bijection, not merely a necessary condition. QED.

### Corollary 2.2 — two matching solutions give an indispensable binomial

If the finite matching problem in Lemma 2.1 has exactly two solutions `P,N`, and the corresponding state supports are disjoint, then the degree-`d` binomial

`x^P - x^N`

is indispensable.

Indeed the fibre contains exactly two monomials and they are relatively prime. Any binomial generating expression avoiding this binomial would induce either a third monomial in the same fibre or a common factor through which a lower-degree move acts; both are impossible. Equivalently, the two monomials form an isolated two-vertex fibre graph for every move set omitting this relation.

This criterion is exact and is what is used below. No floating-point linear programming enters the certification.

## 9. Relation to the Lyons coupling problem

This is a discrete/toric result about the 90-state transport polytope for the first hard rank profile. It does **not** refute Lyons's coupling conjecture. It closes only a proposed constructive route of the form:

1. scale a rational signed or positive coupling to integer mass;
2. use a bounded-degree universal local move set to navigate every marginal fibre;
3. repair negativity or connect to a canonical positive coupling.

Any such universal move set for the K6 configuration must now contain moves of degree at least 10.

The facet/Farkas route remains logically independent. A proof that the already discovered determinantal-valid inequalities describe the entire 90-vertex marginal polytope could still settle `(2,2,2)` without a low-degree Markov basis.
