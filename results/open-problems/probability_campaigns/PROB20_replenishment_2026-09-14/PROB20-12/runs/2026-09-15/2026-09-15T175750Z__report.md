## 3. New exact theorem: factorization over coordinate-interaction components

Let `E` be finite or countable, and let `H1,H2` be orthogonal closed subspaces of `l2(E)`. Write `P1,P2` for their orthogonal projections.

Define the **coordinate-interaction graph** `G` on `E` by joining distinct coordinates `e,f` whenever

`(P1)_(e,f) != 0` or `(P2)_(e,f) != 0`.                         (3.1)

Let `(E_a)_a` be the connected components of `G`.

### Theorem 3.1 — coordinate-block closure

For every component `E_a`, both `P1` and `P2` reduce `l2(E_a)`. Put

`H_(i,a) = H_i intersect l2(E_a)`.

Then

`H_i = direct-sum_a H_(i,a)`,                                   (3.2)

and each determinantal law factors as an independent product over components:

`P^{H_i} = tensor-product_a P^{H_(i,a)}`.                        (3.3)

Likewise

`P^{H1 direct-sum H2} = tensor-product_a P^{H_(1,a) direct-sum H_(2,a)}`.  (3.4)

Consequently, if Lyons's coupling exists on every component `E_a`, then it exists globally: take the product of the local couplings and union the componentwise configurations.

#### Proof

By definition of connected components, if `e in E_a` and `f in E_b`, `a != b`, then

`(P_i)_(e,f)=0`, `i=1,2`.                                       (3.5)

Thus each projection matrix is block diagonal relative to

`l2(E)=direct-sum_a l2(E_a)`,                                   (3.6)

which proves the reducing-subspace decomposition (3.2).

For a block-diagonal DPP kernel, every finite inclusion determinant factors over the coordinate blocks. Hence its finite-dimensional cylinder probabilities are exactly those of the independent product of the block DPPs, proving (3.3)-(3.4). For countably many components, the product measure is the ordinary countable product and equality follows from equality on finite cylinders.

If `mu_a` is a local coupling on component `E_a`, take the independent product `tensor-product_a mu_a`. Its first and second marginals are (3.3), while the componentwise union has product law (3.4). QED.

### Corollary 3.2

Only coordinate-connected instances need to be attacked. In particular, if every interaction component is finite of cardinality at most five, then the full coupling exists, because Lyons 2003 already settles every finite ground set of size at most five.

More generally, any collection of locally solved blocks — full-space blocks, historical codimension-one/rank-one blocks, block-exchangeable blocks from the earlier checkpoint, or any future solved finite class — may be assembled independently.

This is a closure theorem rather than the missing irreducible argument, but it removes all reducible instances from the remaining search.

## 5. Rank-10 lemma and the canonical real skeleton solution

### Lemma 5.1 — rank of the duad-syntheme incidence matrix

`rank A = 10`.                                                  (5.1)

#### Proof

First characterize `ker A^T`. Let `h=(h_uv)` be an edge weighting whose sum on every perfect matching is zero.

For any four distinct vertices `a,b,c,d`, with the remaining two vertices `e,f`, compare the perfect matchings

`ab | cd | ef` and `ac | bd | ef`.

Their zero sums give

`h_ab+h_cd = h_ac+h_bd`.                                        (5.2)

The analogous comparison with `ad | bc | ef` gives the full four-point additive relation.

Fix vertices `0,1,2` and set

`beta_0=(h_01+h_02-h_12)/2`,

`beta_j=h_0j-beta_0` for `j != 0`.                              (5.3)

Equation (5.2) then gives, for every `u != v`,

`h_uv = beta_u+beta_v`.                                         (5.4)

The sum of (5.4) over a perfect matching is `sum_v beta_v`, so membership in `ker A^T` is equivalent to

`sum_v beta_v=0`.                                               (5.5)

Thus `ker A^T` has dimension five, and `rank A=15-5=10`. QED.

### Proposition 5.2 — canonical affine solution

Suppose `y` satisfies the perfect-matching degree equations

`sum_(e contains v) y_e = 1` for every vertex `v`.               (5.6)

For a perfect matching `M`, write

`y(M)=sum_(e in M) y_e`.

Define

`p^*_M = (1/4) y(M) - 1/12`.                                    (5.7)

Then

`A p^* = y`, `sum_M p^*_M=1`.                                  (5.8)

#### Proof

Fix an edge `e={u,v}` with four-vertex complement `C`. Exactly three perfect matchings contain `e`. Across those three matchings, the other six selected edges run through every edge internal to `C` exactly once. Therefore

`sum_(M contains e) y(M) = 3y_e + y(E(C))`.                      (5.9)

The degree equations imply `sum_e y_e=3`. Also

`deg_y(u)+deg_y(v)=2=2y_e+y(delta({u,v},C))`,                    (5.10)

so

`y(E(C))=3-y_e-(2-2y_e)=1+y_e`.                                (5.11)

Using (5.7),

`sum_(M contains e) p^*_M`

`= (1/4)(3y_e+1+y_e)-3/12 = y_e`.                              (5.12)

Thus `A p^*=y`. Summing (5.7) over all 15 matchings and using that every edge lies in exactly three perfect matchings gives total mass one. QED.

The canonical solution need not be nonnegative; its value is that every other solution differs from it in an explicitly five-dimensional kernel.

## 6. Six pentads and the outer K6

A **pentad** is a set of five perfect matchings that partitions all 15 edges of `K6`, i.e. a 1-factorization.

Exact enumeration gives exactly six pentads. Call them `P_0,...,P_5`. Every perfect matching belongs to exactly two pentads. Hence each original perfect matching `M` may be identified with the unordered pair

`tau(M)={a,b}` such that `M in P_a intersect P_b`.               (6.1)

There are 15 such pairs, so `tau` is a bijection from the 15 original perfect matchings to the 15 edges of a second, **outer** `K6` on vertex set `{0,...,5}`.

Furthermore, fix an original edge `e`. Each pentad contains exactly one perfect matching containing `e`; because each such matching belongs to two pentads, the three original perfect matchings containing `e` pair all six outer vertices. Thus they form a perfect matching `F_e` of the outer `K6`.

So, after the identification `tau`, the original incidence relation (4.2) becomes:

> original edges `e` correspond to perfect matchings `F_e` of the outer K6, and original skeletons `M` correspond to outer edges `tau(M)`.

This is the classical six-point outer-automorphism geometry, but no group-theoretic fact is needed below beyond the explicit incidence just proved/enumerated.

## 7. New exact five-potential parametrization of every aggregate decomposition

View `p_M` as an outer-edge weighting `p_ab` via (6.1).

### Theorem 7.1 — all real solutions of `A p=y`

Every real solution of `A p=y` is uniquely of the form

`p_ab = p^*_(ab) + alpha_a+alpha_b`,                             (7.1)

where

`sum_(a=0)^5 alpha_a = 0`.                                      (7.2)

Equivalently, the aggregate decomposition fibre is a five-dimensional affine space, and its nonnegative part is exactly

`alpha_a+alpha_b >= -p^*_(ab)` for all `0<=a<b<=5`,              (7.3)

with (7.2).

#### Proof

Let `delta=p-p^*`. Then `A delta=0`. Under the outer identification this says:

`sum_(ab in F) delta_ab = 0`                                    (7.4)

for every perfect matching `F` of the outer `K6`.

Apply the same four-point comparison as in Lemma 5.1 to the outer graph. Every edge weighting satisfying (7.4) has the form

`delta_ab=alpha_a+alpha_b`,                                     (7.5)

and summing over any perfect matching forces `sum_a alpha_a=0`. Conversely (7.5)-(7.2) clearly has zero sum on every outer perfect matching. Uniqueness follows because the five-dimensional space `{alpha:sum alpha=0}` maps injectively to the five-dimensional kernel of `A`. QED.

### Pentad basis version

If `1_{P_a}` denotes the 15-vector of skeleton membership in pentad `P_a`, then

`A 1_{P_a} = 1_D` for every `a`.                                (7.6)

Therefore the five differences

`1_{P_a}-1_{P_0}`, `a=1,...,5`,                                 (7.7)

lie in `ker A`. The exact artifact verifies they are independent (one displayed `5 x 5` minor has determinant `2`), so they give another concrete basis of the same kernel.

## 8. Blossom inequalities reappear as the exact obstruction to choosing nonnegative potentials

The system (7.2)-(7.3) is a vertex-potential feasibility problem on the outer `K6`. Farkas duality gives a clean explanation for why ordinary perfect-matching blossoms are exactly the aggregate obstruction.

A nonnegative multiplier of the inequalities (7.3) annihilates all zero-sum potentials precisely when its outer vertex degrees are all equal. After normalization, such multipliers form the **fractional perfect-matching polytope** of the outer `K6`.

Its extreme points are:

1. incidence vectors of ordinary perfect matchings; and
2. one-half the incidence vector of two disjoint triangles.

This is the standard half-integrality description of the fractional perfect-matching polytope, specialized to six vertices.

For an outer perfect matching `F_e` corresponding to an original edge `e`,

`sum_(ab in F_e) p^*_(ab)=y_e`.                                 (8.1)

Thus those Farkas inequalities are just coordinate nonnegativity of `y`.

For a pair of complementary outer triangles, the same expression is, under the outer incidence correspondence, one-half of an original three-vertex blossom slack:

`(1/2) sum_(ab in E(T) union E(T^c)) p^*_(ab)`

`= (1/2)(1-y(E(S)))`                                            (8.2)

for the associated original `3|3` cut `S|S^c`. There are ten complementary triangle pairs and ten complementary original `3|3` cuts. The exact artifact checks the correspondence for all ten; one representative is

`T={0,1,2}` (outer)  <->  `S={0,2,4}` (original).               (8.3)

Consequently, for a degree-one aggregate vector `y`, a nonnegative skeleton decomposition exists iff

`y_e>=0` for every edge and `y(E(S))<=1` for every 3-set `S`.     (8.4)

This is Edmonds's K6 perfect-matching characterization seen directly through the five-potential fibre. In the determinantal problem, the earlier mixed-determinant argument proves exactly these blossom inequalities, so (7.3) is guaranteed to have a solution `alpha`.

The gain here is not a new proof that an uncolored skeleton exists; it is an explicit description of **all** such skeleton decompositions by only five potentials.

## 9. New exact potential-Birkhoff formulation of the full K6 colored problem

The earlier exact extended formulation used 15 skeleton masses `p_M` and local scaled `3 x 3` doubly-stochastic matrices. Theorem 7.1 eliminates the 15 skeleton variables and their 10 independent aggregate equations.

### Theorem 9.1 — five-potential colored lift

A K6 colored marginal vector `x=(x_(i,e))` is in the true 90-vertex colored-perfect-matching polytope iff there exist:

- potentials `alpha_0,...,alpha_5` with `sum alpha_a=0`;
- for every outer edge `ab` / original perfect matching `M_ab`, nonnegative numbers `z_(ab,i,e)` for `i in {0,1,2}` and `e in M_ab`;

such that, writing

`p_ab = (1/4) sum_(e in M_ab) y_e - 1/12 + alpha_a+alpha_b`,      (9.1)

we have

`p_ab >= 0`,                                                     (9.2)

`sum_(e in M_ab) z_(ab,i,e) = p_ab` for every `ab,i`,            (9.3)

`sum_i z_(ab,i,e) = p_ab` for every incidence `e in M_ab`,       (9.4)

and

`sum_(ab: e in M_ab) z_(ab,i,e) = x_(i,e)` for every `i,e`.      (9.5)

#### Proof

Given a genuine colored coupling, let `p_M` be its uncolored skeleton marginal and `z_(M,i,e)` the mass for which skeleton `M` is chosen and edge `e in M` receives color `i`. Conditional on `M`, the normalized `3 x 3` matrix `z/p_M` is doubly stochastic, giving (9.3)-(9.4), while (9.5) is exactly the target color-edge marginal. Theorem 7.1 writes its skeleton marginal uniquely as (9.1).

Conversely, (9.3)-(9.4) say that, for every skeleton with positive mass, `z/p` is a doubly-stochastic `3 x 3` matrix. By Birkhoff-von Neumann it is a convex combination of the six color permutations of the three edges. Mixing those local permutations with weights `p_ab` gives a probability distribution on the 90 colored states. Equation (9.5) gives exactly `x`. QED.

Thus the first hard Lyons case is now equivalent to a five-global-potential plus local-Birkhoff feasibility problem. The aggregate part is completely solved by (7.2)-(8.4); what remains is the genuinely colored compatibility in (9.3)-(9.5).

This formulation appears better suited to exact Farkas elimination than the raw 90-state system: the only global skeleton freedom is the five-dimensional potential vector `alpha`.
