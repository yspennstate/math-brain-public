## B2 — attack the negation first

The source's sharp construction attaches copies of a nearly `r`-regular balloon `L_r` to the leaves of an `r`-ary tree. For even `k`, no `k`-regular subgraph can use a bridge (degree-sum parity on either side of a bridge), so every tree vertex is necessarily omitted. A natural refutation attempt is therefore to look for a modified bridge/balloon family where each bridge block itself forces many additional omissions, enough to make the loss asymptotically larger than `n/r^2`.

The first useful conclusion of this attack is that the *source balloon itself cannot create such an extra loss* for any fixed even `k` once `r>2k`. This follows from the spanning criterion proved under B11 below.

For the source balloon `L_r`, all vertices have degree `r` except its attachment vertex, which has degree `r-1`, so

\[
D_r(L_r)=\sum_v(r-d(v))=1.
\]

For `r>2k`, `1 <= r-2k`. The graph is bridgeless for the relevant range (indeed it is obtained from `K_{r+2}` by deleting only two incident edges and a matching; with minimum degree `r-1` and `r>2k>=8`, a bridge cut would force more vertices than `r+2`). Hence the B11 lemma gives a spanning `k`-factor in every copy of `L_r`.

Consequently, for fixed even `k` and odd `r>2k`, the O–West/Sivashankar construction `G_t` has

\[
f_k(G_t)=|V(G_t)|-t,
\]

not merely the inequality `f_k(G_t) <= |V(G_t)|-t`: no bridge can be used, so the `t` tree vertices are impossible to cover, while the spanning `k`-factors in all balloon copies cover every other vertex. Thus the source lower construction realizes its loss *exactly within that family* for all fixed even `k` and sufficiently large odd `r`.

This does not prove global extremality. It tells the counterexample search what it must beat: a bridge-based refutation now needs a bridgeless component whose total degree deficiency is already on the order of `r` and whose maximum `k`-regular subgraph nevertheless omits substantially more than a constant multiple of `D_r(B)/r` vertices. Low-deficiency balloon modifications are ruled out by B11.

No source-level counterexample was found in this run.

## B11 — dualize through the regular-factor theorem

The key local object in the source's sharp `k=2` proof is a connected bridgeless component left after deleting all bridges. For general even `k`, the right first question is when such a component already has a *spanning* `k`-factor.

### Theorem (low-deficiency bridgeless components have spanning even factors)

Let `k` and `r` be integers with `k` even and `r>2k`. Let `B` be a finite connected bridgeless loopless graph with `Delta(B) <= r`, and define

\[
D_r(B):=\sum_{v\in V(B)}(r-d_B(v)).
\]

If

\[
\boxed{D_r(B)\le r-2k,}
\]

then `B` has a spanning `k`-regular subgraph.

### Proof

Set

\[
s(v):=r-d_B(v)\ge0,
\qquad s(X):=\sum_{v\in X}s(v),
\qquad D:=D_r(B).
\]

We use the Belck–Tutte regular-factor theorem. For disjoint sets `S,T subseteq V(B)`, let `R=V(B)\(S union T)` and let `q(S,T)` be the number of components `C` of `B[R]` for which

\[
k|C|+e_B(C,T)
\]

is odd. The theorem says that `B` has a spanning `k`-factor if and only if, for every disjoint `S,T`,

\[
\Phi(S,T):=
 k|S|+\sum_{v\in T}d_{B-S}(v)-k|T|-q(S,T)\ge0.
\tag{1}
\]

Because `k` is even, a component counted by `q(S,T)` is exactly a component `C` with `e(C,T)` odd. In particular, every counted component sends at least one edge to `T`.

Write `a=|S|`, `b=|T|`. We have

\[
\sum_{v\in T}d_{B-S}(v)
=rb-s(T)-e(S,T).
\tag{2}
\]

We split into two cases.

#### Case 1: `a >= b`

Every component counted by `q(S,T)` has at least one edge to `T`, so

\[
q(S,T)\le e(R,T).
\]

By summing degrees over `T`,

\[
e(R,T)=rb-s(T)-e(S,T)-2e(T).
\tag{3}
\]

Substituting (2) and (3) into (1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(S,T)
&=k(a-b)+rb-s(T)-e(S,T)-q(S,T)\\
&\ge k(a-b)+2e(T)\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

#### Case 2: `b>a`

Consider a component `C` counted by `q(S,T)`. Since `e(C,T)` is a positive odd integer, either `e(C,T)>=3`, or `e(C,T)=1`. In the latter case, because `B` is bridgeless and `C` has a nonempty cut, `|partial C|>=2`, so `e(C,S)>=1`. Therefore in every case

\[
2\le e(C,T)+e(C,S).
\]

Summing over all counted components gives

\[
2q(S,T)\le e(R,T)+e(R,S).
\tag{4}
\]

Degree sums over `S` and `T` yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
e(R,T)+e(R,S)
&=r(a+b)-s(S)-s(T)\\
&\qquad -2e(S)-2e(T)-2e(S,T)\\
&\le r(a+b)-s(S)-s(T)-2e(S,T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Using (4) and (5) in (1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(S,T)
&\ge k(a-b)+rb-s(T)-e(S,T)\\
&\qquad -\frac12\bigl(r(a+b)-s(S)-s(T)-2e(S,T)\bigr)\\
&=\left(\frac r2-k\right)(b-a)+\frac{s(S)-s(T)}2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now `b-a>=1`, `s(S)>=0`, and `s(T)<=D`. Hence

\[
\Phi(S,T)
\ge \frac{r-2k-D}{2}\ge0.
\]

Thus (1) holds for every pair of disjoint sets `S,T`. The regular-factor theorem gives a spanning `k`-factor in `B`. ∎

### What the theorem does and does not give

This is a genuine extension of the *spanning/no-spanning threshold* part of the source's local `k=2` picture to every fixed even `k`: a bridgeless component must accumulate roughly `r` units of degree deficiency before a spanning `k`-factor can fail.

It is not yet a nearly-spanning estimate. The source's `k=2` Lemma 4.2 is stronger because it gives the quantitative loss

\[
|V(B)|-f_2(B)\le \left\lfloor D_r(B)/r\right\rfloor.
\]

For `k>=4`, the analogous quantitative statement is exactly what remains missing.

The proof also explains why the elegant loop trick for `k=2` does not immediately port: one added loop supplies the full required degree two and therefore represents an omitted vertex atomically. For `k>=4`, a naive collection of loops can supply only part of the required degree and does not force the all-or-nothing degree set `{0,k}`. The correct global problem is a general-factor problem with allowed degree set `{0,k}`, not an ordinary linear `k`-factor problem.

## B25 — double-count the bridge tree and expose the exact `r^2` bottleneck

Let `G` be a connected simple `r`-regular graph with `r` odd and `r>2k`. Delete all bridges. Let the resulting connected components be `B_1,...,B_m`, and contract them to the vertices of the bridge tree `T`. Let `c=|E(T)|` be the number of bridges and write

\[
d_i:=d_T(i).
\]

Because `G` is `r`-regular, deleting the incident bridges creates exactly `d_i` units of deficiency in `B_i`, so

\[
\boxed{D_r(B_i)=d_i.}
\tag{6}
\]

Call a component **good** if `d_i<=r-2k`, and **bad** otherwise. By the B11 theorem, every good component has a spanning `k`-factor. Thus all possible local loss is confined to

\[
I:=\{i:d_i\ge r-2k+1\}.
\]

If `I` is nonempty, then, exactly as in the source's bridge-tree count,

\[
\sum_{i\in I}d_i
\le c+e(T[I])
\le c+|I|-1.
\tag{7}
\]

On the other hand, every `i in I` has `d_i>=r-2k+1`, so

\[
(r-2k+1)|I|
\le c+|I|-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{|I|\le \frac{c-1}{r-2k}.}
\tag{8}
\]

For an `r`-regular simple graph, the O–West bridge bound used by the source is

\[
c-1\le
\frac{(r-1)(n-2(r+2))}{r^2-3}
<\frac{(r-1)n}{r^2-3}.
\tag{9}
\]

Combining (8) and (9), the **number of bridge components on which a spanning `k`-factor can fail is already `O_k(n/r^2)`**.

That is the desired `r^2` leverage, but only at the level of the number of bad components. A bad component can contain many vertices, so leaving each bad component wholesale does not prove the conjecture.

### Exact local repair lemma that would close Conjecture 5.1

The preceding calculation reduces the source conjecture to the following quantitative local statement.

For each fixed even `k`, prove that there are constants `C_k,r_0(k)` such that whenever `r>=r_0(k)` and `B` is a finite connected bridgeless loopless graph with `Delta(B)<=r`,

\[
\boxed{
|V(B)|-f_k(B)
\le C_k\frac{D_r(B)}{r}.
}
\tag{LOCAL-REPAIR}
\]

(The B11 theorem already supplies the stronger value zero when `D_r(B)<=r-2k`.)

Here is the complete implication. Apply `(LOCAL-REPAIR)` only to the bad blocks and spanning factors to the good blocks. By (7) and (8),

\[
\begin{aligned}
|V(G)|-f_k(G)
&\le \frac{C_k}{r}\sum_{i\in I}d_i\\
&\le \frac{C_k}{r}(c+|I|-1)\\
&\le \frac{C_k}{r}(c-1)
\left(1+\frac1{r-2k}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Using (9),

\[
\frac{|V(G)|-f_k(G)}{|V(G)|}
\le
C_k\frac{r-1}{r(r^2-3)}
\left(1+\frac1{r-2k}\right)
=O_k(r^{-2}).
\]

Applying this to each connected component of a general `r`-regular graph gives the source upper bound `delta_k(r)=O_k(r^{-2})`. Combined with Sivashankar's existing `Omega_k(r^{-2})` construction, it proves Conjecture 5.1.

Thus the first run has reduced the full conjecture to a source-native local deficiency problem, with no logarithmic or square-root loss in the bridge-tree step.

## Failure map from the three methods

- **B2 / negation:** the obvious way to worsen the source's bridge construction by modifying low-deficiency leaf balloons fails: deficiency at most `r-2k` forces a spanning even `k`-factor. - **B11 / duality:** ordinary `k`-factor duality completely handles the spanning threshold, but not vertex omission. The missing all-or-nothing degree condition `{0,k}` is nonlinear from the perspective of the standard `f`-factor polytope.
- **B25 / double count:** the bridge tree already supplies the conjectured `r^2` scale for the *number* of problematic blocks. The unresolved quantity is the number of vertices that must be sacrificed inside one high-deficiency bridgeless block.
