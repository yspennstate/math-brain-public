## 5. B6 — deform to a clean one-cycle exact-cactus model

Recovery 2 selected the ONE-CYCLE EXACT-CACTUS case as the cleanest place to test the surviving varying-sign obstruction. This run deforms one step further to a self-contained **single-gap** model that is strong enough to admit adversarial row orientations but simple enough to solve completely.

### 5.1 Clean single-gap model

Let `C` be one cycle in the equality-level 2-edge-cut cactus. Replace each cactus position by a nonempty atom

\[
A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{n-1}
\]

in cyclic order. Assume the positive exact-zero-slack rows under consideration satisfy all of the following:

1. every atom is monochromatic in the row: it lies wholly in `T`, wholly in `S`, or wholly in `U`;
2. exactly one cycle atom is a `U` atom (the **gap**);
3. after deleting the gap, the remaining cycle atoms alternate `T,S,T,S,...` around the resulting path;
4. vertices outside these cycle atoms, if present, have zero signed coefficient in every row of this subfamily.

This is strictly narrower than the full ONE-CYCLE EXACT-CACTUS case. In particular, a general exact cactus row may have several `U` intervals/components or may not respect this monochromatic atom model.

Write

\[
w_i:=\sum_{v\in A_i}(r-d_B(v))\ge0,
\qquad D_C:=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}w_i.
\]

Because we are in FIRST-SCALE,

\[
D_C\le D_r(B)\le2(r-2k)<2r.
\tag{2}
\]

For an exact nontrivial zero-slack row of the previously proved feasible LP point `x_v=(r-d_B(v))/r`, we have `d-q=0` and `d_B(T)-d_B(S)=0`. Since `h=1`, equality in the signed LP row gives

\[
\boxed{w(T)-w(S)=s(T)-s(S)=r.}
\tag{3}
\]

Let `P` be the set of positive (`T`) cycle atoms, `N` the negative (`S`) atoms, and `z` the unique zero atom. Since `P,N,{z}` partition the cycle atoms,

\[
w(P)-w(N)=r,
\qquad
w(P)+w(N)=D_C-w_z.
\]

Therefore

\[
w(P)=\frac{D_C-w_z+r}{2},
\qquad
w(N)=\frac{D_C-w_z-r}{2}\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]

The second inequality implies `w_z<=D_C-r<r`, and hence

\[
\boxed{w(P)>D_C/2.}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus every positive side in the clean model carries **strictly more than half of the entire cycle deficiency mass**.

## 6. The clean one-cycle theorem

### Theorem 6.1 (single-gap exact-cactus repair)

In the clean single-gap one-cycle model above, all positive signed rows can be satisfied by an omitted vertex set `X` with

\[
|X|\le
\begin{cases}
1,& n\text{ odd},\\
2,& n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]

Equivalently, the integral signed repair number of this entire row family is at most 1 on an odd cycle and at most 2 on an even cycle.

### Proof: even cactus cycle

Let `n=2m`. Globally 2-color the cycle atoms by parity, say `E` and `O`.

Delete the unique gap of any row. The remaining path has odd length `2m-1`, and its nonzero signs alternate. Consequently the row's positive atom set is either

- all atoms of one parity class, or
- all atoms of one parity class except the gap (when the gap belongs to that parity).

In every case the positive atom set is contained entirely in one of the two fixed parity classes `E,O`.

By (5), the positive side of every row has weight `>D_C/2`. Therefore there cannot exist one row whose positive side lies in `E` and another whose positive side lies in `O`, since `E` and `O` are disjoint and their total weight is exactly `D_C`. Hence there is one common parity class `Q` such that every positive side is either `Q` or `Q\{z}` for that row's unique gap `z`.

If `|Q|=1`, positivity forces the sole atom of `Q` to be positive in every row; choose any one original vertex from it.

If `|Q|>=2`, choose two distinct atoms `q_1,q_2 in Q` and one original vertex from each. For any row:

- neither selected vertex can lie in `S`, because all negative atoms lie in the opposite parity class;
- at most one of `q_1,q_2` can be the row's unique gap;
- therefore at least one selected vertex lies in `T`.

Thus

\[
|X\cap T|-|X\cap S|\ge1
\]

for every positive row, with `|X|=2`. This proves the even case. ∎

### Proof: odd cactus cycle

Let `n=2m+1`. Relabel the atom positions by the cyclic **step-two order**. In this order, deleting one original-cycle atom and alternating signs on the remaining even path has the following exact form:

- the positive atom set `P` is a cyclic interval of exactly `m` consecutive step-two positions;
- the negative atom set is the complementary `m`-interval;
- the unique zero atom is one of the two boundary positions between them.

By (5), every such positive interval has weight `>D_C/2`. Hence any two positive intervals intersect: two disjoint positive intervals would have total weight `>D_C`.

We claim that **all** positive intervals have a common atom. Suppose not. Fix one positive interval `P_1`. Since every other positive interval has length `m<(2m+1)/2`, its intersection with `P_1` is a connected interval in the linear order on `P_1`: two such short circular arcs cannot have two separated intersection components because their total lengths are `2m<2m+1`.

The family `{P_i∩P_1}` has empty total intersection by assumption. Intervals on a line have Helly number 2, so there are `P_2,P_3` for which

\[
P_1\cap P_2\cap P_3=\varnothing.
\tag{6}
\]

All three pairs still intersect, because every pair of positive intervals carries more than half the cycle deficiency mass.

Let `tau_i` be the signed atom vector of row `i`: `+1` on `P_i`, `-1` on its negative interval, and `0` at its unique gap. We now prove the coordinatewise inequality

\[
\boxed{\tau_1+\tau_2+\tau_3\le1.}
\tag{7}
\]

By (6), no atom is positive in all three rows. If an atom is positive in at most one row, its coefficient sum is automatically at most 1. The only dangerous case is an atom `x` positive in exactly two rows: to violate (7), `x` would have to be the zero atom of the third row.

Assume, for contradiction, that `x` is the zero boundary atom of row 3. Cut the step-two cycle at `x` so that `P_3` is the interval immediately to one side of the cut. Any length-`m` circular interval that contains the boundary point `x` and also intersects `P_3` meets `P_3` in a boundary prefix (or, after reversing the cut orientation, a boundary suffix). Such intersections are nested. Since `P_1` and `P_2` both contain `x` and both intersect `P_3`, their intersections with `P_3` therefore share an atom. That atom lies in `P_1∩P_2∩P_3`, contradicting (6).

So an atom positive in two rows is negative, not zero, in the third row, and its coefficient sum is `1+1-1=1`. This proves (7).

Now apply (3) to the three exact rows and sum:

\[
3r
=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\tau_i\cdot w
=(\tau_1+\tau_2+\tau_3)\cdot w
\le\sum_jw_j
=D_C
<2r,
\]

where the last inequality is (2). Contradiction. Hence all positive intervals have a common atom `p`.

Choose any original vertex from `A_p`. It lies in `T` for every row and in `S` for none, so the signed constraint (1) holds for every row with `|X|=1`. This proves the odd case. ∎

## 7. B15 — break symmetry / counterexample-first outcome

The theorem eliminates the most natural adversarial amplification on one exact cactus cycle: rotating a single gap and alternating the `T/S` orientation cannot make the repair number grow.

Without the FIRST-SCALE mass equation (3) and budget `D_C<2r`, an abstract alternating cycle can certainly support many conflicting orientations. The source-valid exact-zero-slack constraints kill that amplification in two different ways:

- **even cycle:** all positive rows are forced into one global parity class, after which two atoms pierce every row;
- **odd cycle:** the positive arcs all have more than half the deficiency mass, and the only possible three-way circular-arc obstruction contradicts `D_C<2r` after summing the exact signed equations.

Therefore any surviving one-cycle counterexample must use structure absent from the clean theorem, most plausibly **multiple zero/U intervals in a row**, split/non-monochromatic cactus bags, or later the `d-q>0` near-tight excess. No graph-realizable counterexample to FIRST-SCALE or to the source conjecture was produced.

## 8. B21 — what was successfully ported from cactus matching, and what was not

The useful part of the cactus port is not a perfect-matching theorem. It is the reduction of exact 2-edge-cut obstructions to a one-dimensional cycle state space on which row orientations can be compared globally.

In the clean single-gap model, the replacement for “the matching crosses this cut once” is the exact signed mass identity

\[
w(T)-w(S)=r,
\]

together with the first-scale total budget `D_C<2r`. These force a majority-by-deficiency property strong enough to compress all orientations to one parity class (even cycle) or to a Helly family after excluding the unique circular triple obstruction (odd cycle).

What has **not** been ported:

1. no theorem yet reduces every exact one-cycle barrier to the single-gap monochromatic model;
2. no theorem controls rows with several `U` intervals/components;
3. no stability theorem turns `d-q<=k-2` near-tight rows into `O_k(1)` perturbations of the exact cactus rows in a sign-compatible way;
4. no gluing theorem combines many cactus cycles/blocks while preserving a constant signed repair ratio.

Those are genuine remaining mathematical obligations, not bookkeeping gaps.
