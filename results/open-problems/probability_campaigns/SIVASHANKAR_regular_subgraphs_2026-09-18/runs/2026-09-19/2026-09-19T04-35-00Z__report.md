### 6.1 Common-negative majority theorem

Literal assumptions:

\[
V=W\dot\cup Z,\quad |W|=2m+1,\quad |Z|=m,
\]

and every `(m+1)`-subset `T⊂W` with `S=Z` is a positive first-scale barrier.

For each such `T`, positivity gives

\[
e(B[T])\le J:=\frac{k-2}{2}.
\]

Every edge of `B[W]` is counted in exactly `\binom{2m-1}{m-1}` such `T`, while there are `\binom{2m+1}{m+1}` rows. The ratio is

\[
\frac{\binom{2m+1}{m+1}}{\binom{2m-1}{m-1}}
=\frac{2(2m+1)}{m+1}.
\]

Hence

\[
e(B[W])\le (k-2)\frac{2m+1}{m+1}.
\]

Because `Delta(B)<=r`,

\[
e(W,Z)\le r|Z|=rm,
\]

so

\[
D_r(B)\ge s(W)
\ge r(m+1)-2(k-2)\frac{2m+1}{m+1}.
\]

Against the first-scale ceiling `2r-4k`, the margin is

\[
r(m-1)+4k-2(k-2)\frac{2m+1}{m+1}.
\]

For `m=1` this equals `k+6>0`. For `m>=2`, `(2m+1)/(m+1)<2`, so the margin is strictly larger than `r(m-1)+8>0`. The counting and capacity arithmetic check out under the theorem's literal partition assumptions.

**Scope caution:** this excludes the literal common-negative majority realization. It does **not** exclude arbitrary signed clutters with varying `S_i`, extra membership cells, or more complicated zero-state component incidence.

### 6.2 Triangle / literal blow-up exclusion

## 7. Cactus diagnosis: what the literature gives and what is still missing

For an exact nontrivial zero-slack row of the `x=s/r` witness, the preserved derivation forces `S,T` independent and every `U`-component to have exactly one boundary edge to `S` and one to `T`. ## 8. General-factor / delta-matroid diagnosis

Shao–Živný's current general-factor paper gives a precise warning rather than a solution. A symmetric degree constraint is a delta-matroid exactly when all gaps have length at most 1. Thus:

- `{0,2}` has only the single missing degree 1 and is compatible with that symmetric delta-matroid condition;
- `{0,k}` for `k>=4` has the missing block `1,2,...,k-1`, a gap of length `k-1>1`, and is outside that regime.

What this **does** say: generic exchange / matching-gadget conclusions that rely on the symmetric delta-matroid hypothesis cannot be imported without a new argument.

What this **does not** say: it does not prove the source conjecture false, does not establish computational hardness of the fixed-`k` near-regular class, does not imply an unbounded `g_k/LP_k` gap, and does not rule out a graph-specific cactus/flow theorem.

## 9. Counterexample-first diagnosis after the common-S exclusion

The literal common-negative majority obstruction is gone. A surviving first-scale counterexample would have to exploit **varying negative sides** and shared component incidence.
