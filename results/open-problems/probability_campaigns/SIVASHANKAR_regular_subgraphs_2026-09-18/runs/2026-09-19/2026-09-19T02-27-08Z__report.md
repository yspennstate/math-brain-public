# 5. B26 — sharpen the genuine graph-realizable barrier geometry

## 5.1 A new `3/2` barrier-balance lemma

For a component `C` of `B[U]`, write

\[
a_C:=e(C,T),\qquad z_C:=e(C,S).
\]

Then

\[
b=\sum_C a_C,
\]

and `q` is the number of components with `a_C` odd. Put

\[
E:=b-q\ge0.
\]

As in the previous run, `E` is even. Let `Q_1` be the set of `q`-components with `a_C=1`.

If `C\in Q_1` and `z_C=0`, then the unique edge from `C` to `T` is the unique edge leaving `C`, hence is a bridge of `B`, impossible. Therefore every `C\in Q_1` has at least one edge to `S`, and so

\[
c\ge |Q_1|.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every odd-`T` component not in `Q_1` has `a_C>=3` and contributes at least `a_C-1>=2` to `E=b-q`. Hence

\[
q-|Q_1|\le E/2,
\]

so

\[
|Q_1|\ge q-E/2.
\]

Combining with (5.1),

\[
c\ge q-E/2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Now define

\[
\Delta_d:=d_B(T)-d_B(S).
\]

Since the `S-T` edges cancel in the difference,

\[
\Delta_d=b-c+2m_T-2m_S.
\]

Using (5.2) and `m_S>=0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_d
&\le (q+E)-(q-E/2)+2m_T\\
&=\frac32E+2m_T.
\end{aligned}
\]

But

\[
L=d-q=(b-q)+2m_T=E+2m_T,
\]

so

\[
\frac32E+2m_T
=\frac32L-m_T
\le\frac32L.
\]

Therefore every disjoint pair in a bridgeless graph satisfies the new estimate

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_d\le\frac32(d-q).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

whenever the notation above applies. No asymptotics are used.

For a positive FIRST-SCALE barrier, `h=1` and

\[
s(T)-s(S)=r-\Delta_d,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
 s(T)-s(S)
 \ge r-\frac32(d-q).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Writing `d-q=2j`, this is

\[
s(T)-s(S)\ge r-3j.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Since `0<=j<=(k-2)/2`, the uniform consequence is

\[
\boxed{
 s(T)-s(S)\ge r-\frac32(k-2).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This strictly improves the previous first-scale lower bound `r-2k+4`.

## 5.2 Pairwise overlap improves from 8 to `k+6`

Let `(S_i,T_i)`, `i=1,2`, be positive FIRST-SCALE barriers, and write

\[
d_i-q_i=2j_i.
\]

By (5.5),

\[
s(T_i)\ge r-3j_i.
\]

Since `s` is a nonnegative modular weight and `D<=2(r-2k)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
s(T_1\cap T_2)
&\ge s(T_1)+s(T_2)-D\\
&\ge 2r-3(j_1+j_2)-2(r-2k)\\
&=4k-3(j_1+j_2).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 s(T_1\cap T_2)
 \ge 4k-3(j_1+j_2)
 \ge k+6.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The final inequality uses `j_i<=(k-2)/2`. In particular, the previous universal overlap `>=8` was not sharp; graph-realizable bridgeless barriers force at least `k+6` deficiency units of overlap. At the maximal-deficiency level `d-q=0` for both rows, the bound is `s(T_1\cap T_2)>=4k`.

## 5.3 Any three-way Helly failure must park order-`r` deficiency in the zero states

Take three positive FIRST-SCALE barriers `(S_i,T_i)`, and put `U_i=V\setminus(S_i\cup T_i)`, `d_i-q_i=2j_i`.

For each `i`,

\[
2s(T_i)+s(U_i)
=D+\bigl(s(T_i)-s(S_i)\bigr).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Assume

\[
T_1\cap T_2\cap T_3=\varnothing.
\]

Then every vertex belongs to at most two of the `T_i`, so

\[
\sum_{i=1}^3 s(T_i)\le2D.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Summing (5.8), applying (5.9), and then (5.5),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^3s(U_i)
&\ge \sum_{i=1}^3\bigl(s(T_i)-s(S_i)\bigr)-D\\
&\ge 3r-3(j_1+j_2+j_3)-D.
\end{aligned}
\]

Because `D<=2r-4k`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^3s(U_i)
\ge r+4k-3(j_1+j_2+j_3).
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Using `j_i<=(k-2)/2`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^3s(U_i)
\ge r-\frac{k}{2}+9
=R+\frac{3k}{2}+9.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

Thus a three-barrier family with empty common positive side cannot be an almost-pure `S/T` crossing. It must reuse a macroscopic amount of the entire first-scale deficiency budget through its zero states `U_i`.

This does not yet rule out a three-way crossing, because the same deficiency mass may occur in several `U_i`. But it identifies exactly where a genuine graph-realizable obstruction must live.

\text{for every }T\in\binom{W}{m+1}.
