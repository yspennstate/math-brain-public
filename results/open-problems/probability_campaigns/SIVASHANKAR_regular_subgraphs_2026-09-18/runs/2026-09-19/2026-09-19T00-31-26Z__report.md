# A. Exact blocker

Fix an even `k >= 4` and `r>2k`. For a finite connected bridgeless loopless graph `B` with `Delta(B)<=r`, let

\[
s(v):=r-d_B(v),\qquad D_r(B):=\sum_v s(v),
\]

and let

\[
g_k(B):=|V(B)|-f_k(B)
\]

be the minimum number of omitted vertices needed to leave a `k`-regular subgraph. For disjoint `S,T`, let

\[
\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
:=k|T|-k|S|-d_{B-S}(T)+q(S,T),
\]

where `q(S,T)` is the number of components `C` of `B-(S union T)` with `e(C,T)` odd. Define

\[
\operatorname{LP}_k(B)
:=
\min\left\{
\sum_v x_v:
0\le x_v\le1,\quad
k(x(T)-x(S))\ge\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
\ \forall S\cap T=\varnothing
\right\}.
\]

The exact remaining blocker is best written without a `0/0` ratio ambiguity:

\[
\boxed{
\exists C_k<\infty\ \forall r>2k\ \forall B\text{ connected, bridgeless, loopless},\ \Delta(B)\le r:
\qquad
g_k(B)\le C_k\operatorname{LP}_k(B).
}
\tag{BLOCKER}
\]

Equivalently, the supremum of `g_k(B)/LP_k(B)` over instances with `LP_k(B)>0` should be `O_k(1)`. The previous report proves the sharp fractional estimate

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{LP}_k(B)\le D_r(B)/r.}
\tag{FRAC}
\]

Thus `(BLOCKER)` implies

\[
g_k(B)\le C_k D_r(B)/r,
\tag{LOCAL-REPAIR}
\]

which is exactly the local theorem needed by the preserved bridge-tree reduction.

Indeed, delete the bridges of an original finite simple `r`-regular graph `G` and let its nontrivial bridge blocks be `B_i`. If the bridge tree has `c` bridge edges, then each bridge incidence contributes one unit of `r`-deficiency to its block, so

\[
\sum_i D_r(B_i)=2c.
\]

Applying `(LOCAL-REPAIR)` blockwise gives total local support loss at most `2C_k c/r`, while bridge-tree vertices/degenerate pieces are already handled by the preserved source/O–West counting. The O–West bridge estimate used in the first report gives `c=O(n/r)` in an `n`-vertex `r`-regular graph. Hence the total uncovered fraction is `O_k(r^{-2})`. Combined with Sivashankar's source lower bound, this proves Conjecture 5.1. Therefore the current obstruction is not another global bridge estimate: it is simultaneous integral rounding of the exact signed support barriers.

# C. Stress test of the signed-barrier formula and `x=s/r`

This recovery re-derived the two key identities rather than accepting them by inertia.

## C1. Parity independence under `{0,k}` deletion

For an omitted set `X`, define the target factor degree

\[
f_X(v)=\begin{cases}0,&v\in X,\\ k,&v\notin X.\end{cases}
\]

The Tutte `f`-factor bad-component parity for a component `C` of `B-(S\cup T)` depends on

\[
f_X(C)+e(C,T)\pmod 2.
\]

Because `k` is even, every value `f_X(v)` is even, so `f_X(C)` is even for every component and every `X`. Therefore the bad-component count is independent of `X` and is exactly the number of components with `e(C,T)` odd, namely `q(S,T)`.

The linear `f_X` terms change by exactly

\[
-k|X\cap T|+k|X\cap S|,
\]

so the `f_X`-factor inequalities are equivalent to

\[
k(|X\cap T|-|X\cap S|)\ge\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
\]

for every disjoint `S,T`. Minimizing `|X|` yields the exact formula for `g_k(B)`. No hidden parity dependence on `X` remains. This argument uses essentially that `k` is even; it is not an odd-`k` theorem.

## C2. Bridgeless inequality `b+c>=2q`

Fix disjoint `S,T` and put `U=V\setminus(S\cup T)`. Let

\[
b=e(T,U),\qquad c=e(S,U).
\]

Every component counted by `q` has an odd number, hence at least one, edge to `T`. Such a component cannot be all of `B`: it has a boundary edge to `T`. Since `B` is connected and bridgeless, the edge cut of every proper nonempty vertex set has size at least two. Thus each `q`-component contributes at least two edges to `S\cup T`. Different `U`-components have disjoint boundary-edge sets, so summing gives

\[
b+c\ge2q.
\]

This remains valid if `U` is disconnected. If `U=empty`, then `q=b=c=0`, so the inequality is trivially valid. If `q=0`, it says only `b+c>=0`, which is also valid.

## C3. Re-derivation of the `x=s/r` witness

Let

\[
m_T=e(B[T]),\qquad m_S=e(B[S]),
\]

\[
d=d_{B-S}(T)=b+2m_T,
\]

and

\[
\Delta_d=d_B(T)-d_B(S)=b-c+2(m_T-m_S).
\]

Then

\[
2(d-q)-\Delta_d
=b+c+2m_T+2m_S-2q\ge0.
\]

Hence, if `Delta_d>=0`,

\[
d-q\ge\Delta_d/2\ge(k/r)\Delta_d
\]

because `r>2k`. If `Delta_d<0`, then `q<=b<=d`, so `d-q>=0>(k/r)\Delta_d`. Therefore in all cases

\[
d-q\ge(k/r)\Delta_d.
\]

Since, with `h=|T|-|S|`,

\[
s(T)-s(S)=rh-\Delta_d,
\]

we get

\[
\frac{k}{r}(s(T)-s(S))
=kh-\frac{k}{r}\Delta_d
\ge kh-d+q
=\operatorname{def}_k(S,T).
\]

Thus `x_v=s(v)/r` is feasible for **all** signed barriers. Since `0<=s(v)<=r` when `Delta(B)<=r`, it also satisfies the box constraints `0<=x_v<=1`, and its objective is exactly `D_r(B)/r`.

Edge cases do not break the proof:

- `S=T=empty`: `def=0`, both sides are zero.
- `U=empty`: `q=b=c=0`; the internal-edge terms alone make the algebra valid.
- disconnected `U`: the cut argument is componentwise.
- `q=0`: no parity component is needed; the algebra still applies.

I therefore retain the signed-barrier formula and `(FRAC)`.

## C4. Stress test of the zero-slack characterization

Define

\[
\Sigma(S,T)
:=\frac{k}{r}(s(T)-s(S))-\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
=d-q-\frac{k}{r}\Delta_d.
\]

For `Delta_d<0`, `d-q>=0` makes `Sigma>0`. For `Delta_d>0`,

\[
\Sigma\ge(1/2-k/r)\Delta_d>0.
\]

Therefore zero slack forces `Delta_d=0` and `d=q`. Since `d=b+2m_T` and `q<=b`, this yields

\[
b=q,\qquad m_T=0.
\]

The equality `Delta_d=0` then gives

\[
c+2m_S=q.
\]

But `b+c>=2q` and `b=q`, so `c>=q`; hence

\[
c=q,\qquad m_S=0.
\]

When `S\cup T` is nonempty, connectedness now shows every `U`-component must meet `S\cup T`; since all `b=q` and `c=q` boundary edges are exhausted by the `q` components, every `U`-component is a `q`-component with exactly one edge to `T` and exactly one edge to `S`. Thus `S,T` are independent and every `U`-component is attached through a balanced two-edge cut. `S-T` edges remain unrestricted.

There is one **degenerate exception that should be stated explicitly**: for `(S,T)=(empty,empty)`, `Sigma=0` trivially, `U=V`, and it is false to say the sole connected `U`-component is a `q`-component. The previous report called its theorem a statement for a “nontrivial disjoint pair”; the safe formal condition is `S union T != empty`. With that condition, the characterization survives.

If `q=0` and `S union T != empty`, zero slack forces `b=c=m_S=m_T=0`. Connectedness then forces `U=empty`; this is a spanning bipartition-type equality case. If `S union T=V` from the start, the same `U=empty` case is already covered.

So the zero-slack result is retained with the explicit exclusion of the trivial empty pair. This is an exposition/quantifier correction, not metric movement.

# D. Conceptual attempt to refute bounded integrality gap

A genuine counterexample to `(BLOCKER)` in the source class would need a sequence of connected bridgeless near-`r`-regular blocks `B_j` with

\[
\operatorname{LP}_k(B_j)=O(1)
\quad\text{but}\quad
g_k(B_j)\to\infty.
\]

Because `(FRAC)` gives `LP<=D/r`, the most dangerous regime is `D=O(r)`: only `O(r)` total degree deficiency is available. Nevertheless an unbounded gap is not ruled out by scalar mass alone. In the first supercritical scale `D<2(r-2k)`, every positive barrier has `h=1` and consumes a net deficiency imbalance larger than `r-2k`, i.e. roughly more than half of the total deficiency mass. Abstract families of subsets each carrying more than half of a common fractional mass can still have large integral hitting number if their signs conflict. Therefore “all positive barriers are large” is not by itself a rounding theorem.

The graph structure makes a bad family much more constrained. Near zero slack, each hard set-pair must resemble a balanced two-edge-cut gadget: an independent `S`, an independent `T`, and components joining the two sides through one edge on each side. To force `g/LP` large, many such set-pairs would have to **cross incompatibly**, sharing the same small deficiency mass while making a vertex chosen positively for one `T` appear negatively in another `S`. A chain/cactus/network of balanced two-edge-cut gadgets is the natural conceptual object to test.

None of the existing examples realizes this conflict:

- `K_{r-t,r}` has one aligned equality barrier and `g=LP=D/r=t`; its integrality gap is one.
- Sivashankar/O–West balloons are exactly coverable away from bridge-tree vertices.
- the `K-e` support-failure example diagnoses the degree-sum relaxation, but is outside the near-`r` source regime and does not give bounded `D/r` with growing `g` in the present class.

Thus bounded integrality gap is **not refuted**, but neither is it supported merely by the existing calibrations. The missing object is a theorem excluding a large crossing family of balanced signed barriers, or an explicit construction showing such a family can occur.

# G. Nearest solved literature and exact missing uniformity

## G1. Belck–Tutte `f`-factor theory

Belck–Tutte gives an exact existence criterion for a prescribed integer degree function. It underlies the spanning theorem and the exact signed-barrier formula after fixing an omitted set `X`. What it lacks is an optimization theorem over the **choice of support** `X` with allowed degrees `{0,k}`. Applying the criterion separately for every `X` produces the signed family; it does not round that family simultaneously.

## G2. Qu–West / Schrijver generalized Tutte–Berge for `f`-bounded subgraphs

This theory exactly optimizes total degree in an `f`-bounded subgraph and therefore controls a degree-sum deficit. The B19 example shows why this is not enough: an extremely small degree deficit can coexist with no nonempty exact-`k` support. The missing quantity is vertex support with an all-or-none degree requirement, not total degree.

## G3. Lovasz parity `(g,f)` factors

Parity-factor theory handles interval/parity allowed degrees. The set `{0,k}` for even `k>=4` is not the full even interval `{0,2,...,k}`. Allowing intermediate even degrees changes the problem and again controls a relaxation rather than exact regular support.

## G4. Kiraly barrier / supermodularity and related uncrossing

Kiralyesque supermodular/barrier machinery explains why ordinary factor duals admit structured tight families. Frank–Jordan set-pair covering and Hirai-style skew-supermodular uncrossing similarly show that crossing set/set-pair systems can sometimes be laminarized and covered integrally. This is the nearest conceptual technique to the current blocker.

The missing step is exact matching: the present constraints are

\[
k(x(T)-x(S))\ge\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
\]

with a **negative coefficient on `S`**, box constraints on vertex variables, and a right-hand side derived from factor parity. No proof currently shows that this set-pair function is positively crossing supermodular / skew-supermodular in precisely the sense required by an integral covering theorem, nor that a transformation preserves objective and the `{0,k}` support interpretation. Generic mention of bisubmodularity is therefore not enough.

## G5. General-factor polyhedra / complexity

General-factor results emphasize that allowed degree sets with gaps behave differently from ordinary factor intervals. That is a warning against assuming integrality. The source class is much more structured—near regular, bridgeless, fixed even `k`, and asking an asymptotic support bound—so generic hardness does not refute `(BLOCKER)`. It does mean a proof must genuinely exploit the `r`-deficiency / bridgeless structure.

## G6. Sivashankar's solved `k=2` mechanism

For `k=2`, the source adds a loop at each deficient vertex and uses the 2-factor / b-factor polytope: original edges receive weight `2/r`, a loop at `v` receives weight `(r-d(v))/r`, and convexity yields an integral 2-factor using at most `D/r` loops. Deleting looped vertices then gives the desired support estimate.

This mechanism is exceptionally close to the fractional vector `x=s/r` found here. What it has that `k>=4` lacks is an auxiliary object—a single loop—that by itself supplies exactly the entire degree demand `2` at an omitted vertex in the factor formulation. For `k>=4`, a loop contributes only two degree units; allowing several loops or partial auxiliary degree naturally permits intermediate degrees and no longer encodes the all-or-none `{0,k}` support condition. The unsolved rounding problem is precisely the replacement for this missing `k=2` integrality gadget.

## G7. Sivashankar's omitted flow remark

The author reports a more involved fixed-even-`k` flow method giving `O_k(log r/r)`. Since the proof is omitted, its constants and exact obstruction cannot be imported. More importantly, the stated order is still too weak to imply the `r^{-2}` conjecture. It is relevant as an augmenting-flow language, not as closure.
