## 3. Preserved state needed in this run

Fix a finite connected bridgeless loopless graph `B` with `Delta(B)<=r`. Put

\[
s(v):=r-d_B(v),\qquad D:=D_r(B):=\sum_{v\in V(B)}s(v),
\]

and let

\[
g_k(B):=|V(B)|-f_k(B)
\]

be the minimum number of vertices omitted by a largest `k`-regular subgraph.

For disjoint `S,T`, write `U=V(B)\setminus(S\cup T)`, let `q(S,T)` be the number of components `C` of `B[U]` for which `e(C,T)` is odd, and put

\[
d:=d_{B-S}(T),\qquad
h:=|T|-|S|,
\]

\[
\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
:=kh-d+q(S,T).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The previous runs proved the exact support-deletion formula

\[
g_k(B)=\min\left\{|X|:
 k\bigl(|X\cap T|-|X\cap S|\bigr)
 \ge \operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
\ \text{for every disjoint }S,T
\right\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The parity term `q(S,T)` is independent of `X` because `k` is even.

Also preserved: every positive barrier `def_k(S,T)>0` satisfies

\[
h\ge1,
\qquad
\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
\le \frac{s(T)-s(S)-(r-2k)h}{2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore, in the selected Recovery-1 special case

\[
R:=r-2k,
\qquad
R<D\le2R,
\tag{FIRST-SCALE}
\]

every positive barrier has `h=1`.

Finally, the continuous signed relaxation

\[
LP_k(B):=\min\left\{\sum_vx_v:
0\le x_v\le1,
\quad k(x(T)-x(S))\ge\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
\ \forall S,T
\right\}
\tag{3.4}
\]

has the explicit feasible point `x_v=s(v)/r`, and hence

\[
LP_k(B)\le D/r.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The old `s/(r-2k)` all-signed-barrier claim remains withdrawn.

## 4. Counterexample-first: what a first-scale refutation would have to do

Before trying to round, I tried to make FIRST-SCALE false.

The complete-bipartite calibration does not do it. For `K_{r-t,r}` one has `D=rt` and `g_k=t` when `r-t>=k`. In FIRST-SCALE, `t=1` is possible and gives `g_k=1`, while `t=2` has `D=2r>2(r-2k)` and is already outside the first scale. Likewise, two separated copies of a one-unit obstruction cannot simply be placed side by side: the preserved spanning theorem says every genuinely separate bridgeless obstruction to a spanning `k`-factor costs more than `R=r-2k` units of deficiency, so two independent such obstructions would exceed the budget `D<=2R`.

Thus a counterexample with `g_k(B)->infinity` while `D=O(r)` cannot consist of many disjoint local obstructions. It would need many *overlapping signed barriers* reusing essentially the same deficiency mass, with vertices appearing positively in some `T` sides and negatively in other `S` sides. The calculations below make this requirement sharper.

I did not find a source-valid construction of that type. This is a failed refutation attempt, not evidence that FIRST-SCALE is true.

# 5. B12 — conserved parity and exact first-scale quantization

The most useful new fact in this run is that a first-scale positive barrier has a quantized, bounded defect from the zero-slack two-edge-cut picture.

## 5.1 Parity invariant

Fix disjoint `S,T` and let

\[
b:=e(T,U),\qquad m_T:=e(B[T]).
\]

Then

\[
d=b+2m_T.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Write the components of `B[U]` as `C_1,...,C_j`, and put `a_i=e(C_i,T)`. Then

\[
b=\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
q=|\{i:a_i\text{ odd}\}|.
\]

Modulo two, every even `a_i` contributes zero and every odd `a_i` contributes one. Hence

\[
b\equiv q\pmod2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Together with (5.1),

\[
\boxed{d-q\text{ is a nonnegative even integer}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is independent of FIRST-SCALE and uses only the definition of `q`.

## 5.2 Positive barriers in FIRST-SCALE have unit integral demand

Now impose FIRST-SCALE and assume `def_k(S,T)>0`. The preserved height localization gives `h=1`, so

\[
\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)=k-(d-q).
\tag{5.4}
\]

By (5.3), `d-q` is an even nonnegative integer. Since `k` is even and the right-hand side of (5.4) is positive,

\[
\boxed{
 d-q\in\{0,2,4,\ldots,k-2\},
\qquad
\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)\in\{2,4,\ldots,k\}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For an integral omitted set `X`, the number

\[
z_X(S,T):=|X\cap T|-|X\cap S|
\]

is an integer. Because `0<def_k(S,T)<=k`, the exact constraint

\[
k z_X(S,T)\ge\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)
\]

is therefore equivalent to

\[
\boxed{z_X(S,T)\ge1.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

So **every positive first-scale barrier has exactly the same integer demand: one signed unit**. The actual positive deficiency value `2,4,...,k` is irrelevant after integral rounding.

This is a genuine simplification of FIRST-SCALE: its positive half is a unit-demand signed transversal problem.

## 5.3 The `T` side is only `O(k)` edge-units away from the balanced template

Combining (5.1) with (5.5),

\[
(d-q)=(b-q)+2m_T\le k-2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{b-q\le k-2,\qquad m_T\le (k-2)/2.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Interpretation: except for at most `k-2` edge-units, every `T-U` incidence is the unique `T` edge of an odd `U`-component, and `T` itself has only `O(k)` internal edges. Thus a positive first-scale barrier is not an arbitrary large signed set-pair; its positive side is a bounded perturbation of the balanced two-edge-cut equality geometry found in the previous run.

This does **not** yet bound the `S` side: the asymmetry is real, and pretending that (5.8) also controls `S` would be an error.

## 5.4 A quantitative overlap theorem for positive `T` sides

The previous proof of `x=s/r` gave, for every disjoint `S,T`,

\[
2(d-q)-\Delta_d\ge0,
\qquad
\Delta_d:=d_B(T)-d_B(S).
\tag{5.9}
\]

For a positive first-scale barrier, (5.5) and (5.9) imply

\[
\Delta_d\le2(d-q)\le2k-4.
\]

Since `h=1`,

\[
s(T)-s(S)=r-\Delta_d\ge r-2k+4=R+4.
\tag{5.10}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{s(T)\ge R+4.}
\tag{5.11}
\]

Now take two positive first-scale barriers with positive sides `T_1,T_2`. Because `s` is a nonnegative modular vertex weight,

\[
s(T_1\cap T_2)
=s(T_1)+s(T_2)-s(T_1\cup T_2)
\ge s(T_1)+s(T_2)-D.
\]

Using (5.11) and `D<=2R`,

\[
\boxed{s(T_1\cap T_2)\ge 8.}
\tag{5.12}
\]

Thus **any two positive first-scale barriers share positive deficiency mass, quantitatively at least eight units**. In particular their `T` sides cannot be disjoint.

This is stronger than the earlier scalar statement that each positive `T` contains more than half of the deficiency mass. It still does not imply a common vertex for all positive `T` sides: a pairwise-intersecting hypergraph can have large transversal number. The missing input must use the graph/barrier structure, not just weighted majority.

# 6. B13 — try to uncross with a monotone potential

The target was to turn the pairwise-overlap theorem into a laminar or bounded-overlap core. The natural hope is that two crossing unit barriers can be replaced by simpler unit barriers while preserving the set of integral repairs.

## 6.1 What height localization alone does *not* prove

The signed system lives on disjoint pairs `(S,T)`, equivalently sign vectors in `{-1,0,1}^V`. Standard set-pair/bisubmodular meet/join operations preserve the total signed cardinality

\[
h=|T|-|S|
\]

in aggregate. Starting from two `h=1` pairs, an uncrossing can therefore still produce two height-one pairs. The fact that *every positive barrier has height one* does not by itself force one uncrossed pair to become nonpositive, nested, or simpler.

Likewise, (5.12) gives pairwise overlap of the `T` sides but no Helly theorem. The abstract family of sets carrying more than half of a common measure can have unbounded hitting number. Therefore neither the scalar deficiency budget nor pairwise intersection is a valid monotone potential by itself.

## 6.2 Exact place where a proof is missing

To justify a genuine uncrossing, one would need an inequality for the **exact rounded signed demand** under a specific set-pair meet/join, strong enough to say that replacing two crossing positive constraints does not weaken the integer system. I have not proved such an inequality.

The nearest literature statement I found is Király's weak/`lambda`-supermodularity framework for `f`-factor-type deficiencies on **essential valid pairs**, where the state includes an auxiliary collection of critical components and the meet/join operation is defined on that enriched state. That is not yet the same object as the bare disjoint pair `(S,T)` and rounded support-deletion demand used here. Importing that theorem without an exact identification would be a category error.

The previous zero-slack theorem suggests a plausible potential — number of crossing balanced two-edge-cut pieces plus the bounded excess `(b-q)+2m_T` — but I cannot currently prove it strictly decreases under a valid barrier replacement. No theorem is promoted here.

### B13 outcome

**Failure / precise obstruction.** The first-scale system has strong pairwise overlap, but no valid all-signed uncrossing inequality or strictly decreasing barrier potential has been established. The missing step is structural, not numerical.

# 7. B18 — exact signed set-pair language

This route did yield an exact reformulation that cleanly separates what the standard covering theorems would need to provide.

## 7.1 Integerized signed demand

For every disjoint pair `(S,T)`, not only positive barriers, define

\[
\rho_k(S,T):=\left\lceil \frac{\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)}{k}\right\rceil.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Since

\[
\operatorname{def}_k(S,T)=kh-(d-q),
\]

with integer `h,d,q`, the elementary identity `ceil(m-a)=m-floor(a)` for integer `m` gives

\[
\boxed{
\rho_k(S,T)
=h-\left\lfloor\frac{d-q}{k}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Because `z_X(S,T)` is integral, (3.2) is exactly equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
 g_k(B)=\min\left\{|X|:
 |X\cap T|-|X\cap S|\ge\rho_k(S,T)
\ \text{for every disjoint }S,T
 \right\}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Equation (7.3) is the cleanest current form of the local problem: it is a **signed set-pair covering problem** with one `{-1,0,+1}` column per vertex. In FIRST-SCALE, every positive row has rounded demand `rho=1` by (5.5)-(5.6).

## 7.2 Why Frank–Jordán does not apply directly

The Frank–Jordán theorem covers a nonnegative positively crossing supermodular set-pair demand by selecting arcs. A selected arc contributes either `0` or `1` to the coverage of a set-pair.

Here a selected vertex contributes

\[
\chi_{S,T}(v)=
\begin{cases}
+1,&v\in T,\\
-1,&v\in S,\\
0,&v\notin S\cup T.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The negative coefficient is not cosmetic. For example, a row with rounded demand `rho=0` is violated by a one-vertex set `X={v}` when `v\in S`, because its signed coverage is `-1`. A monotone arc-cover model would only see nonnegative coverage and would lose precisely this obstruction. Therefore the ordinary Frank–Jordán theorem cannot simply be cited as an integrality theorem for (7.3).

Similarly, standard integral bisubmodular-polyhedron results become relevant only after proving that the *exact* right-hand side in (7.3), with its parity-derived `q` term and the required box/objective structure, satisfies the appropriate bisubmodular or skew-supermodular hypotheses. That identification is not currently proved.

## 7.3 Why the source's `k=2` loop proof does not automatically solve even `k>=4`

The source's sharp `k=2` local lemma adds one loop at every vertex. A selected loop contributes degree two, exactly replacing the entire required degree of an omitted vertex. Edmonds' 2-factor polytope can then minimize the number of added loops.

For `k=2m>=4`, the naive analogue of adding `m` loops per vertex does **not** encode all-or-none omission. An auxiliary `k`-factor could take `j` of those loops for any `1<=j<m` and use `k-2j` ordinary edges at the same vertex. That is a partially covered vertex, which has no counterpart in the original `{0,k}` support problem. Linear loop cost therefore controls degree deficit but does not enforce support synchronization. This is the same support-vs-degree gap diagnosed earlier, now seen directly in the source's sharp method.

# 8. What the run changes mathematically

After this run, any such gap in FIRST-SCALE must occur despite all of the following simultaneous facts:

1. every positive constraint has the **same integer demand one**;
2. every positive barrier has `(b-q)+2e(B[T])<=k-2`, so its positive side is an `O(k)` perturbation of the balanced equality geometry;
3. any two positive `T` sides share at least eight units of deficiency mass;
4. the complete signed system is exactly the integer set-pair program (7.3);
5. ordinary monotone set-pair covering is insufficient because vertices can contribute `-1` on `S` sides.

Thus a first-scale counterexample would have to realize a large family of **pairwise-overlapping, almost-balanced, sign-conflicting unit barriers**. That is substantially more specific than “the LP might have a large integrality gap.”

I do not yet have either a construction of such a family in a graph or a theorem excluding it.
