## 6. B7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: exact excess decomposition

The last report isolated nonexact height-zero rows as the first uncontrolled layer. This run makes their finite-excess content exact.

For every component `C` of `B[U]`, define

\[
t_C=e(C,T),\qquad s_C=e(C,S).
\]

Let

\[
m_T=e(B[T]),\qquad m_S=e(B[S]).
\]

Let `q_T` be the number of components `C` with odd `t_C`, and `q_S` the number with odd `s_C`.

### Lemma 6.1 — exact one-sided excess identity

\[
\boxed{
 e_T:=\frac{d_{B-S}(T)-q_T}{2}
 =m_T+\sum_C\left\lfloor\frac{t_C}{2}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Likewise

\[
\boxed{
 e_S:=\frac{d_{B-T}(S)-q_S}{2}
 =m_S+\sum_C\left\lfloor\frac{s_C}{2}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

**Proof.** Write `b=e(T,U)=sum_C t_C`. Then

\[
d_{B-S}(T)=b+2m_T.
\]

Moreover

\[
q_T=\sum_C(t_C\bmod 2).
\]

Hence

\[
b-q_T
=\sum_C\bigl(t_C-(t_C\bmod2)\bigr)
=2\sum_C\left\lfloor\frac{t_C}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Substituting into `(d-q_T)/2` gives (6.1). Equation (6.2) is the same calculation with `S,T` reversed. ∎

### Corollary 6.2 — what fixed excess really controls

If `e_T=e`, then

- `m_T<=e`;
- at most `e` components of `B[U]` have `t_C>=2`;
- every other component that touches `T` has `t_C=1`.

Thus fixed `e=O_k(1)` controls the number of **T-side excess events**, but not a priori the `S`-side boundary.

### Theorem 6.3 — two-sided near-tight rows are cactus plus `O_k(1)` defects

Assume `(S,T)` is a nontrivial height-zero row and

\[
2e_T<k,
\qquad
2e_S<k.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Then:

1. `rho_k(S,T)=rho_k(T,S)=0`, so every feasible integral omitted set must satisfy
   \[
   |X\cap T|=|X\cap S|.
   \tag{6.4}
   \]
2. At most `e_T+e_S=O_k(1)` components have `t_C>=2` or `s_C>=2`.
3. `m_T+m_S<=e_T+e_S=O_k(1)`.
4. Every remaining `U`-component that meets `S\cup T` has exactly one edge to `T` and exactly one edge to `S`.

**Proof.** Height zero and `2e_T=d_{B-S}(T)-q_T<k` give

\[
\rho_k(S,T)=-\left\lfloor\frac{2e_T}{k}\right\rfloor=0,
\]

and similarly for the reverse row. Applying both inequalities gives (6.4).

By Lemma 6.1, every component with `t_C>=2` contributes at least one unit to `e_T`, so there are at most `e_T` such components; similarly at most `e_S` have `s_C>=2`. Also `m_T<=e_T` and `m_S<=e_S`.

On every component outside this exceptional collection, `t_C,s_C` belong to `{0,1}`. If `t_C=1,s_C=0`, that component has exactly one edge to `V(B)\C`; because distinct `U`-components have no edges between them, that unique boundary edge is a bridge, contradicting bridgelessness. The case `t_C=0,s_C=1` is symmetric. Connectedness excludes `t_C=s_C=0` for a component in a nontrivial row. Hence every nonexceptional component has `(t_C,s_C)=(1,1)`. ∎

This is the clean stability theorem the previous cactus route was missing **when both orientations are near-tight**: the row consists of exact 2-edge-cut cactus components plus only `O_k(1)` exceptional components/internal incidences.

## 7. B7 counterexample-first — one-sided finite excess does not give a finite defect object

The hoped-for stronger claim is false. Small `e_T` alone can coexist with a macroscopic opposite boundary even inside FIRST-SCALE.

Fix even `k>=4` and odd

\[
r\ge4k+1.
\]

Let

\[
B=K_{r-1,r}
\]

with bipartition `L,R`, where `|L|=r-1` and `|R|=r`.

Then:

- `B` is finite, simple, connected and bridgeless;
- every `v in L` has degree `r`;
- every `v in R` has degree `r-1`;
- `Delta(B)=r`;
- therefore
  \[
  D_r(B)=r.
  \tag{7.1}
  \]

Since `r>=4k`,

\[
r-2k<r\le2(r-2k),
\]

so `B` lies in FIRST-SCALE.

Choose `u in R` and set

\[
S=L,
\qquad
T=R\setminus\{u\},
\qquad
U=\{u\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

We have `|S|=|T|=r-1`, so `h=0`.

After deleting `S`, the vertices of `R` are independent. Thus

\[
d_{B-S}(T)=0,
\qquad
q_T=0,
\qquad
 e_T=0,
\qquad
\rho_k(S,T)=0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

But the unique `U` vertex has

\[
e(S,U)=r-1.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For the reverse row, after deleting `T`, every vertex of `S=L` is adjacent only to `u`, so

\[
d_{B-T}(S)=r-1.
\]

Because `r` is odd, `r-1` is even, hence the sole `U` component has even `S`-boundary and `q_S=0`. Therefore

\[
 e_S=\frac{r-1}{2}=\Theta(r).
\tag{7.5}
\]

So a height-zero row can have **exactly zero one-sided excess** while its reverse excess and opposite boundary are linear in `r`.

This family is **not** a counterexample to FIRST-SCALE rounding or to the source conjecture. It is a counterexample to the proposed B7 reduction `one-sided e=O_k(1) => finite O_k(1) boundary-incidence defect state`.
