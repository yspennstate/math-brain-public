## 2. New theorem: the exact uniform private hard-edge package

Let `G_n` be an `n x n` iid standard real Gaussian matrix. Write its singular values in increasing order as

\[
0\le s_1(G_n)\le s_2(G_n)\le\cdots\le s_n(G_n),
\]

and put

\[
\tau_n:=s_1(G_n),\qquad \sigma_{2,n}:=s_2(G_n),
\]

and

\[
a_n:=\sqrt2\,\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}.
\]

### Theorem P08-PRIVATE-LAGUERRE-UNIFORM-1

For every integer `n>=1` and every `x>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
0\le a_nx-\mathbb P(\tau_n\le x)
\le \frac{4n-3}{6}\,a_nx^3.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

For every `n>=2` and `0<=x<=r`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\tau_n\le x,\sigma_{2,n}\le r)
\le a_n b_n\,x r^3,
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

where

\[
b_n:=\frac{n-1}{3\sqrt2}
\frac{\Gamma((n+2)/2)}{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}
\le \frac{n^{3/2}}6.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the inherited target holds with a stronger one-point exponent `K=1` and a two-smallest exponent `K=3/2`:

\[
|\mathbb P(\tau_n\le x)-a_nx|
\le \frac{2n}{3}a_nx^3,
\]

and

\[
\mathbb P(\tau_n\le x,\sigma_{2,n}\le r)
\le \frac16 n^{3/2}a_n x r^3.
\]

The estimates are nonasymptotic.

## 3. Laguerre normalization

Let `lambda_1,...,lambda_n` denote the squared singular values of `G_n`. Their unordered joint density on `(0,infinity)^n` is

\[
\frac1{Z_n(-1/2)}
\exp\!\left(-\frac12\sum_i\lambda_i\right)
\prod_{i=1}^n\lambda_i^{-1/2}
\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `p>=0` and `alpha>-1`, define

\[
Z_p(\alpha)
:=\int_{(0,\infty)^p}
 e^{-\frac12\sum_i\lambda_i}
 \prod_i\lambda_i^\alpha
 \prod_{i<j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\,d\lambda.
\]

The Laguerre--Selberg integral gives

\[
Z_p(\alpha)
=2^{p(\alpha+1)+p(p-1)/2}
\prod_{j=1}^p
\frac{\Gamma(1+j/2)\Gamma(\alpha+1+(j-1)/2)}{\Gamma(3/2)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Two ratios will be used. First,

\[
2n\frac{Z_{n-1}(1/2)}{Z_n(-1/2)}
=\sqrt2\frac{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}
=a_n.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Second,

\[
\frac{Z_{n-2}(3/2)}{Z_{n-1}(1/2)}
=2^{-3/2}
\frac{\Gamma((n+2)/2)}{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Both are immediate cancellations in (3.2); (3.3) can also be recognized as the standard real square-Gaussian hard-edge density at zero.

## 4. Proof of the one-point cubic remainder

Let

\[
J_n(y):=
\int_{[y,\infty)^{n-1}}
 e^{-\frac12\sum_{j=2}^n\lambda_j}
 \prod_{j=2}^n\lambda_j^{-1/2}(\lambda_j-y)
 \prod_{2\le i<j\le n}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\,d\lambda_2\cdots d\lambda_n.
\]

By symmetry of the unordered eigenvalue density, choosing which eigenvalue is the minimum gives

\[
\mathbb P(\tau_n\le x)
=
\frac{n}{Z_n(-1/2)}
\int_0^{x^2}y^{-1/2}e^{-y/2}J_n(y)\,dy.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Put

\[
Z:=Z_{n-1}(1/2).
\]

Under the probability measure `mu` on `(0,infinity)^{n-1}` with density proportional to

\[
e^{-\frac12\sum\lambda_j}
\prod\lambda_j^{1/2}
\prod_{i<j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|,
\]

we can write

\[
\frac{J_n(y)}Z
=
\mathbb E_\mu\left[
\mathbf 1_{\{\lambda_{\min}\ge y\}}
\prod_{j=2}^n\left(1-\frac{y}{\lambda_j}\right)
\right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

This `mu` is exactly the eigenvalue law of a real Wishart matrix

\[
W=XX^T,
\qquad
X\in\mathbb R^{(n-1)\times(n+1)}
\]

with iid standard Gaussian entries. Hence the standard inverse-Wishart identity gives

\[
\mathbb E_\mu\operatorname{tr}(W^{-1})
=\frac{n-1}{(n+1)-(n-1)-1}=n-1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because all factors in (4.2) lie in `[0,1]` on `lambda_min>=y`,

\[
0\le 1-\frac{J_n(y)}Z.
\]

For the upper bound, first

\[
\mathbb P_\mu(\lambda_{\min}<y)
\le y\,\mathbb E_\mu\sum_j\lambda_j^{-1}
=(n-1)y,
\tag{4.4}
\]

since `1_{lambda_min<y} <= y sum_j lambda_j^{-1}`. Second, on `lambda_min>=y`,

\[
1-\prod_j\left(1-\frac y{\lambda_j}\right)
\le y\sum_j\lambda_j^{-1}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Combining (4.4)--(4.5),

\[
\boxed{
0\le 1-\frac{J_n(y)}Z\le2(n-1)y.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Also

\[
0\le1-e^{-y/2}\le y/2.
\]

Therefore

\[
0\le
1-e^{-y/2}\frac{J_n(y)}Z
\le \left(2(n-1)+\frac12\right)y.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Insert this in (4.1), use

\[
\int_0^{x^2}y^{-1/2}dy=2x,
\qquad
\int_0^{x^2}y^{1/2}dy=\frac23x^3,
\]

and use (3.3). The leading term is exactly `a_n x`, while the remainder is at most

\[
\frac{a_n}{2}\left(2(n-1)+\frac12\right)\frac23x^3
=\frac{4n-3}{6}a_nx^3.
\]

This proves (2.1). Notice that the argument also proves the useful global upper bound

\[
\mathbb P(\tau_n\le x)\le a_nx.
\tag{4.8}
\]

## 5. Proof of the two-smallest Janossy bound

Assume `0<=x<=r`. Let `y` denote the smallest squared singular value and `z` the second smallest. Choosing their ordered labels gives the factor `n(n-1)`. On the ordered region

\[
0\le y\le x^2,\qquad y\le z\le r^2,\qquad \lambda_j\ge z\quad(j\ge3),
\]

the Vandermonde factors involving the remaining eigenvalues satisfy

\[
(\lambda_j-y)(\lambda_j-z)\lambda_j^{-1/2}
\le \lambda_j^{3/2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Dropping the lower constraint `lambda_j>=z` after applying (5.1) only enlarges the integral. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(\tau_n\le x,\sigma_{2,n}\le r)
&\le
\frac{n(n-1)Z_{n-2}(3/2)}{Z_n(-1/2)}
\int_0^{x^2}y^{-1/2}
\int_y^{r^2}z^{-1/2}(z-y)\,dz\,dy\\
&\le
\frac{n(n-1)Z_{n-2}(3/2)}{Z_n(-1/2)}
\left(2x\right)\left(\frac23r^3\right)\\
&=
\frac{4}{3}
\frac{n(n-1)Z_{n-2}(3/2)}{Z_n(-1/2)}
\,x r^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Now factor the normalization through `a_n`:

\[
\frac{4}{3}
\frac{n(n-1)Z_{n-2}(3/2)}{Z_n(-1/2)}
=
a_n\frac{2(n-1)}3
\frac{Z_{n-2}(3/2)}{Z_{n-1}(1/2)}.
\]

Using (3.4) gives exactly

\[
a_n\,
\frac{n-1}{3\sqrt2}
\frac{\Gamma((n+2)/2)}{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}
=a_nb_n.
\]

Finally Gautschi's gamma-ratio inequality gives

\[
\frac{\Gamma((n+2)/2)}{\Gamma((n+1)/2)}
\le \sqrt{\frac{n+1}{2}},
\]

so

\[
b_n
\le \frac{(n-1)\sqrt{n+1}}6
\le\frac{n^{3/2}}6.
\]

This proves (2.2)--(2.3).
