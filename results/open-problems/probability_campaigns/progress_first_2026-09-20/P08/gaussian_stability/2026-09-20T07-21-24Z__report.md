## 1. Source target and inherited inputs

The source remains Bandeira--Dmitriev--Lucca--Nizic-Nikolac--Rodder, *Randomstrasse101: Open Problems of 2025*, arXiv:2603.29571, Open Problem 21, with Definition 10.1 and Conjecture 20 as context. For

\[
A_m\in\mathbb R^{(2m-1)\times m}
\]

with iid standard real Gaussian entries I use the dimensionally consistent definition

\[
\omega(A_m)=\min_{S:\operatorname{rank}(A_{S^c})<m}\sigma_m(A_S).
\]

Because a Gaussian matrix is full spark almost surely, `rank(A_{S^c})<m` is equivalent to `|S^c|<=m-1`. Adding rows can only increase the least singular value, so almost surely

\[
\boxed{
\omega(A_m)=\min_{|B|=m}s_{\min}(A_B).
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Write

\[
L_m=\binom{2m-1}{m},\qquad
 a_m=\sqrt2\,\frac{\Gamma((m+1)/2)}{\Gamma(m/2)},
\qquad
s_m(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{L_ma_m}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The inherited proved Gaussian inputs used below are exactly the ones already developed on P08 and in the historical P2 chain:

1. the one-square hard-edge estimate
   \[
   0\le a_m s-\Pr\{s_{\min}(G_m)\le s\}
   \le \frac{4m-3}{6}a_m s^3;
   \tag{1.3}
   \]
2. for two square minors at row-set distance `d`, the common-core coefficient
   \[
   \mathcal L_d(C)=\mathbb E_Z\sqrt{1+Z^T(CC^T)^{-1}Z},
   \]
   with exact mean `E L_d=a_m/a_d` and, uniformly for `theta m<=d<=(1-theta)m`,
   \[
   \frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal L_d)}{(\mathbb E\mathcal L_d)^2}
   =\frac{m-d}{2d(d-3)(m-1)}+O_\theta(m^{-3});
   \tag{1.4}
   \]
3. the block-sandwich/private-hard-edge transfer from `\mathcal L_d(C)` to a pair of square-minor hard-edge events;
4. the fixed-small-`d` logarithmic envelope and the mesoscopic product bound obtained from the same block inverse route. These bounds are product-scale up to polynomial factors and do not assume different minors are independent.

## 2. New threshold-uniform central-pair lemma

The earlier central-pair transfer was stated at fixed `lambda`. For almost-sure information one needs it uniformly when `lambda` grows or decays polynomially. The fixed-`lambda` restriction is not structural.

For a fixed pair `B,B'` of `m`-subsets let

\[
d=m-|B\cap B'|,
\]

and denote

\[
p_m(s)=\Pr\{s_{\min}(G_m)\le s\},
\qquad
P_{m,d}(s)=\Pr\{s_{\min}(A_B)\le s,\ s_{\min}(A_{B'})\le s\}.
\]

### Lemma P08-CENTRAL-PAIR-POLYWINDOW

Fix `theta in (0,1/2)` and `K<infinity`. Uniformly for

\[
\theta m\le d\le(1-\theta)m,
\qquad
m^{-K}\le\lambda\le m^K,
\qquad
s=s_m(\lambda),
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\frac{P_{m,d}(s)}{p_m(s)^2}
=1+rac{m-d}{2d(d-3)(m-1)}+O_{\theta,K}(m^{-3}).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
P_{m,d}(s)\le p_m(s)^2(1+C_{\theta,K}m^{-2}).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Condition on the common `(m-d) x m` Gaussian block `C`. The private `d` rows of the two completions are independent, hence

\[
P_{m,d}(s)=\mathbb E_C q_C(s)^2,
\tag{2.3}
\]

where `q_C(s)` is the one-completion conditional hard-edge probability.

The inherited block-sandwich proof gives, after division by `a_d s`,

\[
\frac{q_C(s)}{a_ds}=\mathcal L_d(C)+\mathcal E_{m,d,s}(C).
\tag{2.4}
\]

What matters here is the dependence of the explicit error ledger on `s`. Choose the same polynomial regularity cutoffs used in the fixed-`lambda` proof:

\[
\sigma_{\min}^+(C)\ge m^{-10},
\qquad
\sigma_2(G_d)\ge m^{-B},
\qquad
\text{all block slopes}\le m^B,
\tag{2.5}
\]

with `B` chosen after the requested moment order. On this event the deterministic sandwich changes its threshold by a factor `1+O(m^B s)`, while the square hard-edge expansion (1.3) contributes a relative error `O(m^C s^2)`. The double-hard-edge input contributes `O(m^{C-3B})`. The rectangular common-core bad event has probability

\[
\Pr\{\sigma_{\min}^+(C)<m^{-10}\}
\le(C\sqrt m\,m^{-10})^{d+1},
\tag{2.6}
\]

which is `exp(-c_theta m log m)`. The remaining polynomial bad events are made `O(m^{-J})` for arbitrary fixed `J` by increasing `B`.

Now, throughout the polynomial lambda window,

\[
s_m(\lambda)
\le \frac{m^K}{L_ma_m}
=\exp[-(\log4)m+O_K(\log m)],
\tag{2.7}
\]

so every term containing a positive power of `s` is smaller than `m^{-J}` for every fixed `J`. Repeating the inherited `L^4(C)` bookkeeping therefore gives, for arbitrary fixed `J`,

\[
\left\|\mathcal E_{m,d,s}\right\|_{L^4(C)}
\le C_{\theta,K,J}m^{-J}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

This is the only point where the old fixed-`lambda` formulation had to be changed.

Using (2.3)--(2.4), (2.8), the exact mean `E L_d=a_m/a_d`, and the one-square expansion (1.3),

\[
\frac{P_{m,d}(s)}{p_m(s)^2}
=\frac{\mathbb E\mathcal L_d(C)^2}{(\mathbb E\mathcal L_d(C))^2}
+O_{\theta,K}(m^{-3}).
\tag{2.9}
\]

Equation (1.4) now gives (2.1). QED.

**Important:** this is still a dependence calculation. No collection of minors has been declared independent.

## 3. Edge overlaps are exponentially sparse and only need polynomial pair control

For fixed `B`, the number of `B'` at distance `d` is

\[
N_m(d)=\binom md\binom{m-1}{d},
\qquad 0\le d\le m-1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus if `D_m` is the distance from a fixed `B` to a uniformly chosen `B'`, then

\[
\Pr(D_m=d)=\frac{N_m(d)}{L_m}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

`D_m` is hypergeometric with mean

\[
\mathbb ED_m=\frac{m(m-1)}{2m-1}=\frac m2+O(1).
\]

Hoeffding's inequality for sampling without replacement gives, for example,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left\{D_m\notin[m/4,3m/4]\right\}
\le C e^{-c m}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The inherited fixed-small-distance and mesoscopic block-inverse bounds are threshold-uniform in the present polynomial window because they are built from linear hard-edge upper bounds. Concretely, for `d>=2`,

\[
P_{m,d}(s)\le Cdm\,s^2+\text{superpolynomially smaller terms},
\tag{3.4}
\]

while for `d=1` the only extra loss is logarithmic,

\[
P_{m,1}(s)\le m^C s^2\log(e/s).
\tag{3.5}
\]

By (1.3), uniformly in the polynomial lambda window,

\[
p_m(s)\asymp a_ms\asymp\sqrt m\,s.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Hence every edge-overlap pair ratio is at most polynomial in `m`:

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{d\notin[m/4,3m/4]}
\frac{P_{m,d}(s)}{p_m(s)^2}
\le m^{C_K}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Combining (3.3) and (3.7), the entire edge-overlap contribution to the average pair ratio is `O_K(e^{-c m})`. This is why no high-order routed atlas is needed for the theorem below.

## 4. New theorem: second moment uniformly on a polynomial Poisson window

Define

\[
W_m(\lambda)
=\sum_{|B|=m}{\bf1}_{\{s_{\min}(A_B)\le s_m(\lambda)\}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then `omega(A_m)>s_m(lambda)` iff `W_m(lambda)=0`.

### Theorem P08-POLYWINDOW-SECOND-MOMENT

For every fixed `K<infinity`, uniformly for

\[
m^{-K}\le\lambda\le m^K,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EW_m(\lambda)=\lambda(1+e^{-\Omega_K(m)}),
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_2
\le (\mathbb EW_m(\lambda))^2
\left(1+\frac{C_K}{m^2}\right).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}W_m(\lambda)}{(\mathbb EW_m(\lambda))^2}
\le \frac{C}{\lambda}+\frac{C_K}{m^2}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

From (1.3), with `s=lambda/(L_m a_m)`,

\[
L_mp_m(s)
=\lambda+O\left(
L_m m a_m s^3
\right)
=\lambda\left[1+O\left(\frac{m\lambda^2}{L_m^2a_m^2}\right)\right].
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because `lambda` is polynomial and `L_m a_m=4^{m+o(m)}`, the relative error is exponentially small. This proves (4.2).

For the second factorial moment, average `P_{m,d}(s)/p_m(s)^2` against the exact distance weights `N_m(d)/(L_m-1)`. On the central window, Lemma P08-CENTRAL-PAIR-POLYWINDOW gives `1+O_K(m^-2)`. Outside it, (3.3) and (3.7) give `O_K(e^{-cm})`. Therefore

\[
\frac{\mathbb E(W_m)_2}{L_m(L_m-1)p_m(s)^2}
\le 1+\frac{C_K}{m^2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Since `L_m(L_m-1)<=L_m^2`, (4.3) follows. Finally

\[
\operatorname{Var}W_m
=\mathbb EW_m+\mathbb E(W_m)_2-(\mathbb EW_m)^2
\]

and (4.3) imply (4.4). QED.

A useful immediate consequence is the dependence-aware zero-count estimate

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{W_m(\lambda)=0\}
\le \frac{C}{\lambda}+\frac{C_K}{m^2},
\qquad 1\le\lambda\le m^K,
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

by Chebyshev. This is not Poisson-quality for growing `lambda`, but unlike the old fixed-apex `O(1/m)` failure term it is **summable in `m` once `lambda` grows slightly faster than `m`**.

## 5. New almost-sure near-Poisson-scale sandwich

Put all dimensions on one probability space by taking an infinite iid standard Gaussian array `(g_ij)_{i,j>=1}` and letting `A_m` be its first `2m-1` rows and first `m` columns. No independence between different `m` is needed below.

### Theorem P08-AS-POLYNOMIAL-SANDWICH

For every `eta>0`, almost surely there exists a finite random `m_0(eta)` such that for all `m>=m_0(eta)`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{m^{-1-\eta}}{L_ma_m}
\le \omega(A_m)
\le
\frac{m^{1+\eta}}{L_ma_m}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

For the lower bound set `lambda_m^-=m^{-1-eta}`. By Markov and (4.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\left\{\omega(A_m)\le s_m(\lambda_m^-)\right\}
&=\Pr\{W_m(\lambda_m^-)\ge1\}\\
&\le\mathbb EW_m(\lambda_m^-)
\le C m^{-1-\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The series in `m` converges.

For the upper bound set `lambda_m^+=m^{1+eta}` and choose a fixed polynomial window exponent `K>1+eta`. By (4.2), `E W_m(lambda_m^+)>=c m^{1+eta}` for large `m`, and by (4.4),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\left\{\omega(A_m)>s_m(\lambda_m^+)\right\}
&=\Pr\{W_m(\lambda_m^+)=0\}\\
&\le \frac{\operatorname{Var}W_m}{(\mathbb EW_m)^2}\\
&\le C m^{-1-\eta}+C_Km^{-2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This series also converges. The first Borel--Cantelli lemma applied to (5.2) and (5.3) proves (5.1). No cross-dimension independence is used. QED.

Since

\[
L_ma_m
=\frac{4^m}{2\sqrt\pi}(1+O(m^{-1})),
\tag{5.4}
\]

(5.1) is equivalently

\[
(2\sqrt\pi+o(1))m^{-1-\eta}4^{-m}
\le \omega(A_m)
\le
(2\sqrt\pi+o(1))m^{1+\eta}4^{-m}
\quad\text{a.s. eventually.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus this run proves the quantitative almost-sure envelope

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{m\to\infty}
\frac{|\log(4^m\omega(A_m))|}{\log m}
\le1
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

## 6. Direct source-normalized consequence

Let

\[
R_m=\max_{1\le i\le2m-1}\|a_i\|_2.
\]

Under the same infinite-array coupling,

\[
\frac{1}{m}\log R_m\to0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Indeed, the first row norm tends to infinity like `sqrt(m)` almost surely, so eventually `R_m>=1`. For any fixed `delta>0`, a chi-square exponential-moment bound gives

\[
\Pr\{R_m>e^{\delta m}\}
\le (2m-1)2^{m/2}\exp[-e^{2\delta m}/4],
\tag{6.2}
\]

which is summable; Borel--Cantelli and a countable sequence `delta downarrow0` give (6.1).

Combining (5.1) with (5.4) and (6.1) yields

\[
\boxed{
-\frac1m\log\frac{\omega(A_m)}{R_m}
\longrightarrow \log4
\quad\text{almost surely}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is a strict strengthening of the inherited Gaussian statement

\[
-\frac1m\log\frac{\omega(A_m)}{R_m}
\to\log4
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]

It directly confirms the proposed **exponential instability scale** for the iid real Gaussian ensemble in an almost-sure asymptotic sense under the natural coupling.
