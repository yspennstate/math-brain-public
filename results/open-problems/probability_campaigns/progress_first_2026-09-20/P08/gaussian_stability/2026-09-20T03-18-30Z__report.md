## 1. Source target and inherited bound

The source remains Afonso S. Bandeira, Daniil Dmitriev, Kevin Lucca, Petar Nizic-Nikolac and Almut Rodder, *Randomstrasse101: Open Problems of 2025*, arXiv:2603.29571, Open Problem 21, with Definition 10.1 and Conjecture 20 as context. For

\[
A_m\in\mathbb R^{(2m-1)\times m}
\]

with iid standard real Gaussian entries, the source stability quantity is

\[
\omega(A)=\min_{S:\operatorname{rank}(A_{S^c})<m}\sigma_m(A_S).
\]

The `<m` threshold is the dimensionally consistent form; the source display uses a conflicting dimension letter at that point.

For a Gaussian matrix in general position, an admissible set must have `|S|>=m`, and adding rows can only increase the least singular value. Hence almost surely

\[
\omega(A_m)=\min_{|B|=m}\sigma_{\min}(A_B).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Write

\[
L_m=\binom{2m-1}{m},\qquad
 a_m=\sqrt2\,\frac{\Gamma((m+1)/2)}{\Gamma(m/2)},
\qquad
s_m(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{L_ma_m},
\]

and

\[
W_m(\lambda)=\sum_{|B|=m}
\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma_{\min}(A_B)\le s_m(\lambda)\}}.
\]

Then

\[
\{L_ma_m\,\omega(A_m)>\lambda\}=\{W_m(\lambda)=0\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The inherited P08 chain now contains:

* `omega(A_m)=4^{-m+o_P(m)}` and explicit fixed-margin exponent tails;
* the exact one-square hard-edge estimate
  \[
  0\le a_nx-\Pr\{s_{\min}(G_n)\le x\}
  \le \frac{4n-3}{6}a_nx^3;
  \]
* the two-smallest bound
  \[
  \Pr\{s_{\min}(G_n)\le x,\ s_2(G_n)\le r\}
  \le \frac16 n^{3/2}a_n x r^3;
  \]
* the inherited saturated-atlas fixed-`k` chain to the candidate Gaussian Poisson law;
* the previous run's correlated-minor pair coefficient and fixed-`k` expansion
  \[
  \mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_k
  =\lambda^k\left(1+\binom{k}{2}m^{-2}+O_{k,\lambda}(m^{-3})\right).
  \tag{1.3}
  \]

The missing point in (1.3) was not another fixed-`k` lemma. It was growth control as `k` increases, because fixed-`k` factorial moments alone cannot be summed at `t=-1` to obtain a second-order formula for `Pr(W_m=0)`.

This run attacks that exact gap.

## 2. Exact refinement of the pair-overlap kernel

For two `m`-subsets, put

\[
D=m-|B\cap B'|.
\]

Under a uniformly chosen second subset before conditioning away `B'=B`,

\[
\Pr(D=d)=\pi_m(d)
=\frac{\binom md\binom{m-1}d}{\binom{2m-1}m},
\qquad 0\le d\le m-1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The explicit leading relative-variance kernel from the preceding report is

\[
g_m(d)=\frac{m-d}{2d(d-3)(m-1)},\qquad d\ge4.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The preceding report only needed

\[
\mathbb E g_m(D)=m^{-2}+O(m^{-3}).
\]

The exact hypergeometric law gives more.

### Proposition P08-PAIR-KERNEL-4

With the terms `d<=3` omitted (their total probability is `poly(m)4^{-m}`),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d=4}^{m-1}\pi_m(d)g_m(d)
=\frac1{m^2}+\frac{11}{m^3}+\frac{100}{m^4}
+O(m^{-9/2})+O(m^C4^{-m}).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Conditioning on `B'!=B` changes (2.3) only by an exponentially small amount.

### Proof

Let

\[
\Delta=D-\frac m2.
\]

The hypergeometric factorial moments give the following exact moments around `m/2`:

\[
\mathbb E\Delta=-\frac{m}{2(2m-1)},
\tag{2.4}
\]

\[
\mathbb E\Delta^2=\frac{m^2}{4(2m-1)},
\tag{2.5}
\]

\[
\mathbb E\Delta^3
=-\frac{m^2(3m-4)}{8(2m-3)(2m-1)},
\tag{2.6}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E\Delta^4
=\frac{m^3(3m-4)}{16(2m-3)(2m-1)}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

On the central window `|D-m/2|<=m/6`, every fifth derivative of `g_m` is `O(m^{-7})`. Hypergeometric concentration gives

\[
\Pr\{|D-m/2|>m/6\}\le e^{-c m},
\]

and `g_m(d)` is at most polynomial off the excluded singular values `d<=3`. Also `E|Delta|^5=O(m^{5/2})`. Therefore Taylor expansion through order four has expectation remainder `O(m^{-9/2})`, plus an exponentially small tail.

The five Taylor contributions are

\[
g_m(m/2)
=\frac1{m^2}+\frac7{m^3}+\frac{43}{m^4}+O(m^{-5}),
\]

\[
g_m'(m/2)\,\mathbb E\Delta
=\frac{3}{2m^3}+\frac{57}{4m^4}+O(m^{-5}),
\]

\[
\frac12g_m''(m/2)\,\mathbb E\Delta^2
=\frac{5}{2m^3}+\frac{123}{4m^4}+O(m^{-5}),
\]

\[
\frac16g_m'''(m/2)\,\mathbb E\Delta^3
=\frac{21}{4m^4}+O(m^{-5}),
\]

and

\[
\frac1{24}g_m''''(m/2)\,\mathbb E\Delta^4
=\frac{27}{4m^4}+O(m^{-5}).
\]

The `m^{-3}` coefficient is `7+3/2+5/2=11`, while the `m^{-4}` coefficient is

\[
43+\frac{57}{4}+\frac{123}{4}+\frac{21}{4}+\frac{27}{4}=100.
\]

This proves (2.3).

**Important limitation.** Equation (2.3) refines the *explicit leading kernel* `g_m`. It is not, by itself, the full `m^{-3}` coefficient of the pair probability, because the preceding analytic pair theorem still has an `O(m^{-3})` remainder. Thus `11` is a rigorously identified pair-channel contribution, not yet the complete third-order coefficient.

## 3. A new growing-moment estimate for the common-core environment

The route to a distribution-level correction is to let the factorial-moment order grow. The fixed-`k` proof can do this only if the environment multipliers are controlled in `L^q` with `q` growing.

For an `(m-d) x m` standard Gaussian matrix `C`, put

\[
S=CC^T,
\qquad
T=\operatorname{tr}S^{-1},
\qquad
U=\operatorname{tr}S^{-2},
\]

and

\[
\mathcal L(C)=\mathbb E_Z\sqrt{1+Z^TS^{-1}Z},
\qquad
Y(C)=\frac{\mathcal L(C)}{\mathbb E\mathcal L(C)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The preceding report proved the exact mean

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal L(C)=\frac{a_m}{a_d}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Lemma P08-ENV-LQ-GROWING

There are universal constants `c,C>0` such that, whenever `d>=16` and

\[
2\le q\le c d,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\|Y(C)-1\|_q
\le
C\left(
\frac{\sqrt q}{d}
+rac{m^{3/2}}{d^{5/2}}
\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular, if `d>=c_0 m`, then

\[
\|Y-1\|_q\le C_{c_0}\frac{\sqrt q}{m}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

Let `f(x)=sqrt(1+x)` and, conditionally on `C`, set

\[
Q=Z^TS^{-1}Z.
\]

Then `E_Z Q=T` and `Var_Z(Q)=2U`. Since

\[
-\frac14\le f''(x)\le0\qquad(x\ge0),
\]

Taylor's theorem and concavity give the deterministic sandwich

\[
0\le f(T)-\mathcal L(C)\le \frac{U}{4}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For the real inverse-Wishart law with deficiency `d`, the standard negative-moment recursion gives, uniformly for `q<=c d`,

\[
\left\|\bigl(\operatorname{tr}S^{-3}\bigr)^{1/2}\right\|_q
\le C\frac{m}{d^2},
\tag{3.6}
\]

and

\[
\|U\|_q\le C\frac{m^2}{d^3}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

These are the natural deficiency scalings: at proportional deficiency they reduce respectively to `O(m^{-1})` and `O(m^{-1})`. They can also be read directly from the general inverse-Wishart trace moments; the restriction `q<=c d` keeps every denominator `d-O(q)` uniformly away from zero.

The gradient identity

\[
\|\nabla_C T\|_F^2=4\operatorname{tr}S^{-3}
\tag{3.8}
\]

combined with the Gaussian `L^q` Poincare inequality yields

\[
\|T-\mathbb ET\|_q
\le C\sqrt q\frac{m}{d^2}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Also

\[
1+\mathbb ET
=1+\frac{m-d}{d-1}
=\frac{m-1}{d-1}
\asymp \frac md,
\tag{3.10}
\]

and the exact gamma-ratio mean (3.2) satisfies

\[
\frac{a_m}{a_d}\asymp\sqrt{\frac md}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Using

\[
|\sqrt{1+x}-\sqrt{1+y}|
=\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt{1+x}+\sqrt{1+y}},
\]

(3.9)--(3.11) show that the normalized fluctuation of `f(T)` is at most `C sqrt(q)/d`. The deterministic discrepancy (3.5), divided by (3.11) and estimated with (3.7), contributes at most

\[
C\frac{m^2/d^3}{\sqrt{m/d}}
=C\frac{m^{3/2}}{d^{5/2}}.
\]

Centering changes the bound by at most a factor two. This proves (3.3).

## 4. Logarithmic-order clean-sector expansion

The inherited saturated-atlas proof uses Venn charts for a labelled `k`-tuple of square minors. On its clean sector, all fresh defects are sums of Venn cells. If every cell is within a fixed factor of its balanced size, then the smallest fresh defect obeys

\[
d_*\ge c\frac{m}{2^k}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For

\[
K_m:=2\left\lfloor\frac{2\log m}{\log\log m}\right\rfloor,
\tag{4.2}
\]

we have `K_m=(4+o(1)) log(m)/log log(m)` and, uniformly for `k<=K_m`,

\[
2^{Ck}=m^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Applying (3.3) chartwise with `q<=k`, (4.1) gives for each normalized clean-sector environment multiplier `Y_i`

\[
\|Y_i-1\|_q\le m^{-1+o(1)},
\qquad 2\le q\le k\le K_m.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The point of (4.4) is that it turns the old opaque `O_k(m^{-3})` remainder into a uniform expansion.

### Theorem P08-CLEAN-LOGK

On the inherited saturated Venn-regular clean sector, uniformly for `3<=k<=K_m`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^k Y_i
=1+\sum_{1\le i<j\le k}
\mathbb E[(Y_i-1)(Y_j-1)]
+O(m^{-3+o(1)}).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently, after averaging the labelled pair distances,

\[
\boxed{
\text{clean normalized }k\text{-tuple factor}
=1+\binom{k}{2}m^{-2}+O(m^{-3+o(1)}).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Put `xi_i=Y_i-1`. Each `E xi_i=0`. Expanding the product exactly,

\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^k(1+\xi_i)
=1+\sum_{i<j}\mathbb E\xi_i\xi_j
+\sum_{r=3}^k\ \sum_{|J|=r}
\mathbb E\prod_{j\in J}\xi_j.
\tag{4.7}
\]

For a fixed `J` of size `r`, Holder and (4.4) give

\[
\left|\mathbb E\prod_{j\in J}\xi_j\right|
\le\prod_{j\in J}\|\xi_j\|_r
\le m^{-r+o(1)}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Because `k<=K_m=m^{o(1)}`,

\[
\sum_{r=3}^k\binom kr m^{-r+o(1)}
=O(m^{-3+o(1)}).
\tag{4.9}
\]

This proves (4.5).

For each labelled pair, the marginal overlap law is the hypergeometric law (2.1), up to the exponentially tiny no-replacement correction. The preceding pair theorem therefore gives

\[
\mathbb E\xi_i\xi_j=m^{-2}+O(m^{-3})
\]

on average over the regular pair geometry; excluding the exponentially rare nonregular pair distances changes this by an exponentially small amount. Since `k^2=m^{o(1)}`, summing the pair errors still gives `m^{-3+o(1)}`. This proves (4.6).

### What has actually changed

The fixed-`k` pair-additive expansion is no longer the bottleneck on the clean sector. The same structure now holds all the way to

\[
k\asymp\frac{\log m}{\log\log m},
\]

which is precisely the order needed by Bonferroni/inclusion-exclusion to resolve polynomial corrections to `Pr(W_m=0)`.

## 6. Conditional second-order survival law

The reason (5.1) is the right target is that it immediately converts the new clean-sector theorem into a distribution-level statement.

### Proposition P08-BONFERRONI-SECOND-ORDER

Assume `P08-ROUTED-CCI-LOGK`, so that for `k<=K_m+1`,

\[
\mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_k
=
\lambda^k\left(1+\binom{k}{2}m^{-2}\right)
+R_{m,k},
\tag{6.1}
\]

with

\[
|R_{m,k}|
\le \lambda^k m^{-3}(C_\lambda k)^k.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then, for each fixed `lambda>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{L_ma_m\,\omega(A_m)>\lambda\}
=e^{-\lambda}
\left(1+\frac{\lambda^2}{2m^2}\right)
+O_\lambda(m^{-3+o(1)}).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof

For integer-valued `W`, the two consecutive Bonferroni sums bracket `1_{W=0}`. Thus truncating inclusion-exclusion at `K_m` has error at most

\[
\frac{\mathbb E(W)_{K_m+1}}{(K_m+1)!}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Because

\[
K_m=(4+o(1))\frac{\log m}{\log\log m},
\]

Stirling gives

\[
\frac{\lambda^{K_m}}{K_m!}=m^{-4+o(1)}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The error part in (6.2), after division by `k!`, is

\[
m^{-3}\frac{(C_\lambda k)^k}{k!}
\le m^{-3}(eC_\lambda)^k\sqrt{k}
=m^{-3+o(1)}
\tag{6.6}
\]

uniformly for `k<=K_m+1`; summing `K_m=m^{o(1)}` such terms keeps the same order.

The main Poisson series gives `e^{-lambda}`. For the pair correction,

\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty
(-1)^k\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}\binom{k}{2}
=
\frac{\lambda^2}{2}e^{-\lambda}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The truncation tail in (6.7) is much smaller than `m^{-3}` by (6.5). Combining (6.4)--(6.7) proves (6.3).

Thus the full second-order survival law is no longer blocked by the clean Gaussian hard-edge analysis. It is blocked only by the uniform routed-sector constant ledger (5.1).
