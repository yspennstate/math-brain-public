## 2. New target: quantify the residual dependence rather than pretend independence

Let

\[
L_m:=\binom{2m-1}{m},\qquad
s_m(\lambda):=\frac{\lambda}{L_ma_m},
\]

and define the square hard-edge count

\[
W_m(\lambda)
:=\sum_{|B|=m}{\bf1}_{\{\sigma_{\min}(A_B)\le s_m(\lambda)\}}.
\]

If all square minors were independent, the first finite-size correction to Poisson would come only from the one-minor hard-edge CDF. The preceding cubic theorem shows that effect is actually exponentially tiny at `s_m`: since `a_m^2 asymp m`,

\[
\Pr(\sigma_{\min}(G_m)\le s_m)
=\frac{\lambda}{L_m}+O_\lambda(L_m^{-3}),
\]

so

\[
\mathbb EW_m(\lambda)=\lambda+O_\lambda(L_m^{-2}).
\]

Therefore the leading algebraic finite-`m` correction, if there is one, must come from dependence between overlapping minors. This run computes that correction.

## 3. Common-core representation for a pair of minors

Fix two distinct `m`-subsets `B,B'` of the `2m-1` rows and set

\[
d:=m-|B\cap B'|.
\]

Their common block has size `(m-d) x m`; write it as `C`, put

\[
S:=CC^T,\qquad H:=S^{-1},
\]

and let

\[
\mathcal L(C):=\mathbb E_Z\sqrt{1+Z^THZ},
\qquad Z\sim N(0,I_{m-d}).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is exactly the environment coefficient appearing in the inherited conditional hard-edge expansion. Conditional on `C`, the two sets of `d` private Gaussian rows are independent, so the pair dependence is carried by the fluctuation of `\mathcal L(C)`.

The old notes used the deterministic approximation

\[
\mathcal L_{\rm typ}(d,m)=\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{d-1}}
\]

and an `O(d^{-2})` relative-variance envelope. We now compute the first coefficient.

## 4. Exact mean of the common-core coefficient

### Lemma P08-COMMON-CORE-MEAN

For every `m>d>=1` for which `C` has full row rank almost surely,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_C\mathcal L(C)=\frac{a_m}{a_d}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

Let `n=m-d`. For `S=CC^T` with `C` an `n x m` standard real Gaussian matrix and independent `Z in R^n`, Hotelling's quadratic-form identity gives

\[
Q:=Z^TS^{-1}Z\stackrel d=\frac{X}{Y},
\]

where

\[
X\sim\chi_n^2,\qquad Y\sim\chi_{d+1}^2
\]

are independent. Hence

\[
B:=\frac{Y}{X+Y}\sim
\operatorname{Beta}\!\left(\frac{d+1}{2},\frac n2\right),
\qquad
1+Q=B^{-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_C\mathcal L(C)
&=\mathbb E\sqrt{1+Q}
=\mathbb E B^{-1/2}\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(d/2)\Gamma((m+1)/2)}
        {\Gamma((d+1)/2)\Gamma(m/2)}
=\frac{a_m}{a_d}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (4.1). \(\square\)

This identity is useful beyond asymptotics: the conditional hard-edge coefficient averages to the exact unconditional square-hard-edge coefficient, as it must.

## 5. New theorem: first relative-variance coefficient

### Theorem P08-COMMON-CORE-VAR-1

Fix `theta in (0,1/2)`. Uniformly for

\[
\theta m\le d\le(1-\theta)m,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_C(\mathcal L(C))}
     {(\mathbb E_C\mathcal L(C))^2}
=
\frac{m-d}{2d(d-3)(m-1)}
+O_\theta(m^{-3}).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, if `d/m -> alpha in (0,1)`, then

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal L)}{(\mathbb E\mathcal L)^2}
=
\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha^2}\,m^{-2}+O_\theta(m^{-3}).
\tag{5.2}
\]

At the mass-carrying overlap `alpha=1/2`, the coefficient is exactly `1` to first order:

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal L)}{(\mathbb E\mathcal L)^2}
=m^{-2}+O(m^{-3}).
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
T=\operatorname{tr}H,\qquad
U=\operatorname{tr}H^2,\qquad
V=\operatorname{tr}H^3.
\]

Conditionally on `C`, let

\[
Q=Z^THZ=T+\xi.
\]

The centered Gaussian quadratic-form identities are

\[
\mathbb E_Z\xi^2=2U,
\qquad
\mathbb E_Z\xi^3=8V,
\qquad
\mathbb E_Z\xi^4=12U^2+48\operatorname{tr}H^4.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For `f(x)=sqrt(1+x)`, Taylor expansion through order three gives

\[
\mathcal L(C)
=f(T)-\frac{U}{4(1+T)^{3/2}}
+\frac{V}{2(1+T)^{5/2}}
+R_C,
\tag{5.4}
\]

with

\[
|R_C|\le C U^2,
\tag{5.5}
\]

because `|f''''(x)|<=C(1+x)^(-7/2)` for `x>=0` and `tr(H^4)<=U^2`.

For the proportional rectangular Wishart law, standard negative-moment identities plus Gaussian Poincare give, uniformly in the stated range,

\[
\mathbb E|T-\mu|^4=O_\theta(m^{-4}),
\quad
\|U-\mathbb EU\|_2=O_\theta(m^{-2}),
\quad
\|V\|_2=O_\theta(m^{-2}),
\quad
\|U^2\|_2=O_\theta(m^{-2}),
\tag{5.6}
\]

where

\[
\mu:=\mathbb ET=\frac{m-d}{d-1}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

For completeness, the two derivative identities behind the Poincare bounds are

\[
\|\nabla_C T\|_F^2=4\operatorname{tr}S^{-3},
\qquad
\|\nabla_C U\|_F^2=16\operatorname{tr}S^{-5},
\]

and proportional-Wishart negative moments put their required fixed moments at the powers in (5.6).

Expanding `f(T)` around `mu` and using (5.4)--(5.6) therefore gives the `L^2` delta-method identity

\[
\mathcal L-\mathbb E\mathcal L
=f'(\mu)(T-\mu)+O_{L^2,\theta}(m^{-2}).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal L)
=f'(\mu)^2\operatorname{Var}(T)+O_\theta(m^{-3}).
\tag{5.9}
\]

The exact inverse-Wishart moments are

\[
\operatorname{Var}(T)
=
\frac{2(m-d)(m-1)}{d(d-1)^2(d-3)},
\tag{5.10}
\]

and

\[
1+\mu=\frac{m-1}{d-1}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Since

\[
f'(\mu)^2=\frac1{4(1+\mu)}
\]

and the exact mean (4.1), together with the uniform gamma-ratio expansion, gives

\[
(\mathbb E\mathcal L)^2
=(1+\mu)(1+O_\theta(m^{-1})),
\tag{5.12}
\]

we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal L)}{(\mathbb E\mathcal L)^2}
&=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(T)}{4(1+\mu)^2}
 +O_\theta(m^{-3})\\
&=\frac{m-d}{2d(d-3)(m-1)}+O_\theta(m^{-3}).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (5.1). \(\square\)

## 6. Quantitative transfer from the environment coefficient to the pair event

The inherited block sandwich and `WeightedPrivateHardEdge-L2k` were originally written only to get `o(1)` at the Poisson scale. The new explicit private Laguerre theorem lets their error budget be made polynomially negligible compared with the `m^{-2}` correction.

### Lemma P08-CENTRAL-PAIR-TRANSFER

Fix `theta in (0,1/2)`, fixed `lambda>0`, fixed moment order `k`, and any fixed `J`. For

\[
\theta m\le d\le(1-\theta)m
\]

and `s=s_m(lambda)`, the inherited conditional probability `q_C(s)` obeys

\[
\left\|
\frac{q_C(s)}{a_ds}-\mathcal L(C)
\right\|_{L^{2k}(C)}
=O_{\theta,k,\lambda,J}(m^{-J}).
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof budget

This is not a new independence assumption; it is a quantitative reading of the already proved block-sandwich route.

Choose polynomial regularity cutoffs, for example

\[
\sigma_{\min}^+(C)\ge m^{-10},
\qquad
\|H_{\rm private}R^{-1}\|\le m^{20},
\qquad
\sigma_2(G_d)\ge m^{-B},
\]

with `B` chosen after `J,k`.

* The rectangular common-core lower-tail estimate already used in the inherited route is
  \[
  \Pr(\sigma_{\min}^+(C)\le r)
  \le (C\sqrt m\,r)^{d+1}.
  \]
  At `r=m^{-10}` and `d>=theta m` this is superpolynomially small even after normalization by the exponentially small one-event probability.
* Polynomial Gaussian block-norm cutoffs have exponentially/superpolynomially small complements and can be enlarged with `J`.
* On the second-singular-value bad slice, the theorem from the previous P08 run gives
  \[
  \Pr(\tau_d\le x,\sigma_{2,d}\le m^{-B})
  \le \frac16 d^{3/2}a_dx\,m^{-3B},
  \]
  so relative to `a_dx` this is `O(m^{3/2-3B})`; choose `B` large.
* On the regular event, the deterministic block sandwich changes the private threshold by a relative factor `1+O(s m^C)`. Since `s_m(lambda) asymp 4^{-m}`, this error beats every power of `m`.
* The one-point cubic hard-edge remainder contributes `O(d x^2)` relatively, again exponentially small at the shifted threshold.

These are exactly the bad-event classes in the inherited `WeightedPrivateHardEdge-L2k -> BlockLinearization-L2k` proof, now with explicit private hard-edge constants. Raising the cutoffs as above gives (6.1) for arbitrary fixed `J`. \(\square\)

Consequently, for a central pair at distance `d`,

\[

\tag{6.2}

Equation (6.2) is the first explicit dependence correction for the correlated square minors. In particular, the correction is positive: the minors are asymptotically Poisson, but not independent at finite `m`.

## 7. Averaging over the exact overlap law

For a fixed `m`-subset `B`, if `B'` is another uniformly chosen `m`-subset of `[2m-1]`, the distance `D=m-|B cap B'|` has exact weight

\[
\pi_m(d)
=
\frac{\binom md\binom{m-1}d}
     {\binom{2m-1}m}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

It is a hypergeometric law with

\[
\mathbb ED=\frac{m(m-1)}{2m-1},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(D)
=
\frac{m^2(m-1)}{2(2m-1)^2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Define

\[
g_m(d):=\frac{m-d}{2d(d-3)(m-1)}.
\]

On any fixed central window, Taylor expansion around `D/m=1/2` using (7.2) yields

\[
\mathbb E_{\pi_m}g_m(D)
=\frac1{m^2}+O(m^{-3}).
\tag{7.3}
\]

The complement of a fixed central window has exponentially small hypergeometric mass. The inherited mesoscopic/fixed-distance product envelopes grow only polynomially/subexponentially there, so those classes contribute `o(m^{-3})` to the normalized pair average. The diagonal `B=B'` has weight `L_m^{-1}` and is separated from the falling-factorial count.

Combining (6.2)--(7.3) gives the new second-factorial-moment expansion

\[
\boxed{

\tag{7.4}

Since `EW_m=lambda+O(L_m^{-2})`, it follows that
