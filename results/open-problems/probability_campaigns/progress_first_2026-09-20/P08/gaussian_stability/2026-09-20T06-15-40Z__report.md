## 2. Exact Hotelling/Beta law for the fresh environment

The historical pair and fresh-sector proofs repeatedly meet the environment coefficient

\[
\mathcal L_d(C)
:=\mathbb E_Z\sqrt{1+Z^T(CC^T)^{-1}Z},
\tag{2.1}
\]

where

\[
C\in\mathbb R^{(m-d)\times m}
\]

is standard real Gaussian and `Z` is an independent standard Gaussian vector in `R^{m-d}`. Earlier P08 work proved

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal L_d(C)=\frac{a_m}{a_d}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and a proportional-deficiency `L^q` fluctuation estimate. For the routed proof one needs a statement that remains explicit as the extension order grows and that also identifies what happens near the small-deficiency boundary.

### Theorem P08-HOTELLING-LQ-EXACT

Let `1<=d<m`, put `n=m-d`, and define

\[
Y_d(C):=\frac{\mathcal L_d(C)}{a_m/a_d}.
\]

For every real `q>=1` with `q<d+1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E Y_d(C)^q
\le
\frac{\Gamma((d+1-q)/2)\Gamma((m+1)/2)}
     {\Gamma((d+1)/2)\Gamma((m+1-q)/2)}
\left(\frac{a_d}{a_m}\right)^q.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Moreover there is a universal `C` such that whenever

\[
1\le q\le\frac{d+1}{2},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\log\mathbb E Y_d(C)^q\le C\frac{q^2}{d},
\qquad
\|Y_d(C)\|_q\le \exp\left(C\frac qd\right).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

In particular, if `d>=2q`, then

\[
\boxed{\|Y_d(C)\|_q\le C_0}
\tag{2.5}
\]

for an absolute constant `C_0`, uniformly in `m,d,q`.

### Proof

Let

\[
S=CC^T,\qquad Q=Z^TS^{-1}Z.
\]

The standard Hotelling representation gives

\[
Q\stackrel d=\frac{X}{Y},
\qquad
X\sim\chi_n^2,\quad Y\sim\chi_{d+1}^2,
\]

with `X,Y` independent. Equivalently,

\[
U:=\frac1{1+Q}
\sim\operatorname{Beta}\left(\frac{d+1}{2},\frac n2\right).
\tag{2.6}
\]

For `q>=1`, conditional Jensen yields

\[
\mathcal L_d(C)^q
=\left(\mathbb E_Z(1+Q)^{1/2}\right)^q
\le \mathbb E_Z(1+Q)^{q/2}.
\]

Averaging in `C` and using (2.6),

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal L_d(C)^q
\le \mathbb E U^{-q/2}
=
\frac{\Gamma((d+1-q)/2)\Gamma((m+1)/2)}
     {\Gamma((d+1)/2)\Gamma((m+1-q)/2)}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

At `q=1`, equality before Jensen gives exactly

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal L_d(C)
=\frac{\Gamma(d/2)\Gamma((m+1)/2)}
       {\Gamma((d+1)/2)\Gamma(m/2)}
=\frac{a_m}{a_d},
\]

so dividing (2.7) by `(a_m/a_d)^q` proves (2.3).

For (2.4), put

\[
a=\frac{d+1}{2},\quad b=\frac{m-d}{2},\quad
M(t)=\log\mathbb E U^{-t}.
\]

Then

\[
M''(t)=\psi_1(a-t)-\psi_1(a+b-t)\le\psi_1(a-t).
\tag{2.8}
\]

If `0<=t<=q/2` and `q<d+1`, the elementary trigamma bound

\[
\psi_1(x)\le x^{-1}+x^{-2}
\]

shows

\[
M''(t)\le \frac{C}{d+1-q}.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Since `M(0)=0` and `M` is convex,

\[
M(1/2)\ge \frac12M'(0).
\]

Taylor's formula at zero therefore gives

\[
M(q/2)-qM(1/2)
\le \frac{q^2}{8}\sup_{0\le t\le q/2}M''(t)
\le C\frac{q^2}{d+1-q}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Under `q<=(d+1)/2`, the last denominator is comparable to `d`, proving (2.4). Taking `q<=d/2` gives (2.5). QED.

### Consequence for growing extension depth

If an `r`-step fresh route has active excesses `d_j>=2r`, then Holder and (2.5) give

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^rY_{d_j}
\le\prod_{j=1}^r\|Y_{d_j}\|_r
\le C_0^r.
}
\tag{2.11}
\]

Thus **large-excess fresh extensions have no hidden `m^{cr}` loss**. This is stronger and cleaner than repeatedly invoking proportional inverse-Wishart estimates.

## 3. Exact determinant tilting: a coarea Jacobian adds Gaussian degrees of freedom

The historical nonzero-fresh route contains normalized determinant/coarea weights and then bounds pseudoinverses under those weights by Holder. That is where factors such as `m^C` and, at effective excess one, `log(C/tau)` appeared. For Gaussian blocks the determinant weight should not be treated as an adversarial density: it exactly changes the Wishart degrees of freedom.

### Lemma P08-WISHART-TILT-SHIFT

Let

\[
X\in\mathbb R^{n\times(n+e)}
\]

have iid `N(0,1)` entries and put `S=XX^T`. For real `alpha` for which the normalizer is finite, define

\[
\frac{d\mathbb P_\alpha}{d\mathbb P}(X)
=\frac{\det(S)^\alpha}{\mathbb E\det(S)^\alpha}.
\]

Then under `P_alpha`,

\[
\boxed{S\sim W_n(n+e+2\alpha,I_n).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently, whenever `e+2alpha>1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\alpha\|X^+\|_F^2
=\mathbb E_\alpha\operatorname{tr}S^{-1}
=\frac{n}{e+2\alpha-1}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

In particular, for the coarea half-determinant tilt `alpha=1/2` and every `e>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{1/2}\|X^+\|_F^2=\frac ne.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

There is no logarithmic exceptional case at `e=1` after the determinant Jacobian is included.

### Proof

The Wishart density of `S` under the untitled Gaussian law is

\[
c_{n,e}\,\det(S)^{(e-1)/2}e^{-\operatorname{tr}S/2}\mathbf1_{S>0}.
\]

Multiplying by `det(S)^alpha` changes the exponent to

\[
\frac{e+2\alpha-1}{2},
\]

which is exactly the density of `W_n(n+e+2alpha,I_n)` after renormalization. This proves (3.1). The standard inverse-Wishart mean

\[
\mathbb ES^{-1}=\frac{I_n}{\nu-n-1},\qquad S\sim W_n(\nu,I_n),
\]

gives (3.2) and (3.3). QED.

### Enlarged-Gram version: the slope factor is often part of the same determinant

If `h` is an independent standard Gaussian vector in `R^n`, then the matrix determinant lemma gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{\det S}\sqrt{1+h^TS^{-1}h}
=\sqrt{\det(S+hh^T)}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus whenever a route chart writes a coarea Jacobian as a determinant factor times the familiar slope `sqrt(1+h^TS^{-1}h)`, those two pieces should be kept together. Writing

\[
\widetilde X=[X\;h],
\]

(3.4) is simply

\[
\sqrt{\det(\widetilde X\widetilde X^T)}.
\]

The normalized Jacobian tilt is therefore another exact Wishart shift on the enlarged Gaussian block, not an arbitrary Holder weight. This observation is important at the smallest effective excesses, where splitting the two factors artificially creates divergent inverse moments.

### Corollary P08-LOW-EXCESS-JACOBIAN

Assume the active residual Gram in a fresh completion is the Gaussian block carrying its coarea factor `det(S)^{1/2}`. Under the corresponding normalized determinant part of the Palm weight, for every `e>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{1/2}\left[\frac{f^2}{m}\|C_B^+\|_F^2\right]
\le \frac{f^2}{e}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since `f=e+delta` and `0<=delta<=2`,

\[
\frac{f^2}{e}
=e+2\delta+\frac{\delta^2}{e}
\le e+8.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore if the extension depth is `r` and the low-excess branch is defined by `e<2r`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{1/2}\left[\frac{f^2}{m}\|C_B^+\|_F^2\right]
\le 2r+8.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The old `e=1` logarithm, which at the hard-edge scale is of order `m`, is not intrinsic; it disappears once the candidate's own determinant Jacobian is incorporated before estimating the pseudoinverse.

The full Palm density can contain the slope factor from (3.4) and other already normalized chart factors. Equation (3.4) shows that the principal slope is best absorbed into the enlarged determinant before any Holder step. The previous run's oriented-beta estimate controls the remaining angular factors at `1+O(m^{-1+o(1)})` through `r<=K_m+1` when their active ambient dimension is `m-m^{o(1)}`.

### High/low excess split

For an `r`-step nonzero-fresh extension chain, split each active residual excess into

\[

* **High excess:** apply Theorem P08-HOTELLING-LQ-EXACT with `q=r`. The normalized environment coefficient costs at most an absolute constant in `L^r`, so a product of `r` such factors costs at most `C^r` by Holder.
* **Low excess:** `f=e+delta<=2r+2`. The candidate coarea determinant produces the exact Wishart shift, and the pseudoinverse error budget is only `O(r)` in first moment, with no power of `m` and no `log(1/s_m)`. The principal slope may be merged into the same enlarged determinant by (3.4).

## 5. Close-pair geometry is exponentially sparse through logarithmic order

There is also a useful uniform counting fact for the close/fixed-small-distance routes.

For two independent uniform `m`-subsets `B,B'` of `[2m-1]`, let

\[
D=m-|B\cap B'|.
\]

Then

\[
\Pr(D=d)
=\frac{\binom md\binom{m-1}d}{\binom{2m-1}m}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `r<=K_m+1` and let

\[
H_r=C_0^r
\]

for any fixed `C_0>1`. Since `H_r=m^{o(1)}`,

\[
\sum_{d\le H_r}\binom md\binom{m-1}d
\le \exp\{O(H_r\log m)\}
=\exp(o(m)).
\]

Using

\[
\binom{2m-1}m=\exp((\log4)m+O(\log m)),
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D\le H_r)
\le\exp\{-(\log4-o(1))m\}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

A union bound over at most `(K_m+1)^2` labelled pairs preserves the same exponential rate. Thus any route that is triggered by an actually close pair with threshold at most exponential in `r` has enormous counting slack. Combined with the inherited fixed-small-`D` / close-endpoint analytic envelopes, whose remaining endpoint constants are now at most subexponential after the saturated-atlas and beta transfers, this channel cannot be the source of a merely polynomial `m^{-3}` loss.

This is useful diagnostically: the unresolved `m^{-3}` in (1.1) must come from the non-close routed bookkeeping/seed, not from the genuinely close-pair geometry.
