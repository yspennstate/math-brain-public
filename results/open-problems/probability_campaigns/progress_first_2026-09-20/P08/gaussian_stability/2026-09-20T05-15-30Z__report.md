## 2. Dimension-sensitive beta inverse moments

### Lemma P08-ORIENTED-BETA

Let `U~Beta(a,b)` and let `q>=0`.  If

\[
a\ge2,\qquad q\le a,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\log \mathbb E U^{-q/2}
\le \frac{2bq}{a-q/2}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular, if `q<=a/2`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E U^{-q/2}\le \exp\!\left(\frac{4bq}{a}\right).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Set `s=q/2`.  The beta identity gives

\[
\mathbb E U^{-s}
=\frac{\Gamma(a-s)\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(a+b-s)}.
\]

Hence

\[
\log\mathbb E U^{-s}
=\int_0^s\bigl[\psi(a+b-t)-\psi(a-t)\bigr]dt.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For `x>=1`,

\[
\psi(x+b)-\psi(x)=\int_0^b\psi'(x+u)du.
\]

Using

\[
\psi'(y)=\sum_{n\ge0}(y+n)^{-2}\le y^{-2}+\int_0^\infty(y+t)^{-2}dt
\le \frac2y
\]

for `y>=1`, we obtain

\[
\psi(a+b-t)-\psi(a-t)\le \frac{2b}{a-t}.
\]

Integrating over `0<=t<=s` yields

\[
\log\mathbb E U^{-s}\le \frac{2bs}{a-s}
=\frac{bq}{a-q/2}
\]

and therefore (2.1), with the displayed constant `2` allowing the harmless `a-t<1` boundary convention uniformly.  Under `q<=a/2`, `a-q/2>=3a/4`, giving (2.2) after enlarging the universal constant.  QED.

The important feature is not the numerical constant.  It is the factor `bq/a`.  When the beta variable is the squared residual length after projecting a fresh normal direction against a low-dimensional previously exposed span, `a` is of order the active normal dimension while `b` is only the dimension already used.

### Conditional product form

Suppose `U_1,...,U_J` are exposed sequentially and, conditionally on the past,

\[
U_j\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a_j,b_j),
\]

with

\[
a_j\ge a_*,\qquad b_j\le B,\qquad q_j\le a_*/2.
\]

Repeated use of (2.2) and the tower property gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^J U_j^{-q_j/2}
\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{4B}{a_*}\sum_{j=1}^Jq_j\right).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is strictly stronger for the endpoint problem than the previous crude `C^{sum q_j}` estimate.

## 3. Crude exponential complexity is enough

The inherited saturated exact labelled incidence atlas has two structural properties already present in its fixed-`r` proof:

* endpoint supports are encoded by finitely many membership states per one of the `r` labelled candidates (historical notation such as `u123|v12|v3` is an example);
* an actual chart is nested/laminar, hence contains only `O(r)` active centers even though the universe of possible support labels is exponential in `r`.

The previous report already proved the sharper chart-count estimate `(Cr)^r`.  For the *analytic size* of one chart, the following deliberately crude bound suffices.

### Proposition P08-SATURATED-EXP-COMPLEXITY

There exists an absolute constant `C_0>1`, depending only on the finite endpoint-decoration alphabet of the inherited saturated construction, such that every order-`r` saturated zero-fresh endpoint chart admits bounds

\[
\boxed{
J_r\le C_0^r,\qquad
B_r\le C_0^r,\qquad
Q_r:=\sum_{j=1}^{J_r}q_j\le C_0^r,\qquad
\Delta_r\le C_0^r.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Here `J_r` is the number of residual angular beta variables after the scalar endpoint deficits have been extracted, `B_r` is the largest transverse beta shape, `q_j` are the remaining inverse angular charges, and `m-Delta_r` is a lower bound for the inherited active normal-block dimension.

#### Proof

A support label is a finite-state word on `r` candidate labels, so the total support universe has size at most `c_1^r` for a fixed `c_1`.  Saturation adds centers indexed by such supports but does not introduce a new continuum of combinatorial labels.

A nested chart uses at most `2r-1` laminar centers.  The original endpoint integrand is a finite product of labelled Gram/determinant/incidence factors indexed by support data.  Thus the number of primitive factors is at most `c_2^r`.  Each primitive determinant has degree at most `O(r)` in the planted directions.  Under a blow-up, the vanishing order of a polynomial along one exceptional divisor is at most its total degree.  Along at most `2r-1` nested blow-ups, the total pullback order contributed by one primitive factor is therefore `O(r^2)`.  Summing over at most `c_2^r` primitive factors gives

\[
Q_r\le O(r^2)c_2^r\le C_0^r
\]

after increasing `C_0`.

The same counting controls the number and codimension of support equations entering a saturated rank-defect center.  Even the wasteful bound "one independent normal condition per decorated support label" gives

\[
\Delta_r\le O(r)c_3^r\le C_0^r.
\]

Finally, every residual angular split is a projection against a span generated by previously exposed support normals.  Its transverse dimension is bounded by the same number of active support normals, hence `b_j<=C_0^r`, and the number of such angular variables is bounded by the number of chart centers / normal flags, hence also by `C_0^r`.  QED.

This proposition is intentionally not an attempt to optimize the inherited `O_r(1)`.  Its point is that a finite labelled saturated atlas cannot make those constants grow faster than exponentially once one works chartwise rather than counting arbitrary set families.

## 4. Uniform endpoint transfer through `K_m`

Set

\[
H_r=C_0^r.
\]

On an order-`r` endpoint chart, the inherited `NQSD -> LBV -> LC7` construction supplies active normal blocks of dimension at least

\[
m-\Delta_r\ge m-H_r.
\]

The scalar endpoint factors are already paid by the fixed-r deficits `b_j`, `c_j`, and pair-distance deficits recorded in the historical LC7 proof.  The remaining dangerous factors are inverse powers of squared residual lengths.  Their Gram--Schmidt beta laws have a first parameter bounded below by

\[
a_*\ge \frac{m-H_r-H_r}{2},
\]

and transverse parameter at most `B_r<=H_r`.

For `r<=K_m+1`,

\[
H_r=\exp(O(r))=m^{o(1)},
\]

so `a_*=(1/2+o(1))m`, while every `q_j<=Q_r<=H_r=o(m)`.  Applying (2.4) gives

\[
\log I_{m,r}^{\rm angular}
\le
C\frac{B_rQ_r}{m-2H_r}
\le
\frac{C H_r^2}{m}
=m^{-1+o(1)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
I_{m,r}^{\rm angular}=1+O(m^{-1+o(1)})
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

uniformly for `r<=K_m+1`.

This is the key progress of the run.  The previous target

\[
Q_r\le Cr
\]

is unnecessary.  Even the much weaker crude bounds

\[
Q_r,B_r,\Delta_r\le C_0^r
\]

are enough because the large beta shape is order `m`.

Combining (4.2) with the already proved nested-chart count `(Cr)^r`, the saturated zero-fresh endpoint continuation has total constant at most

\[
\boxed{
(Cr)^r\bigl(1+O(m^{-1+o(1)})\bigr)
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

through the full logarithmic range.  Thus **endpoint integrability itself cannot be the hidden `m^{cr}` loss in `P08-ROUTED-CCI-LOGK`.**

## 6. Failed / superseded route and lesson

The preceding run aimed to prove `Q_r<=Cr` by reopening every fixed-r wonderful-model chart.  That is unnecessary and risks spending many runs auditing constants that are far smaller than the ambient normal dimension.  The dimension-sensitive beta calculation shows why: an inverse power `q=m^{o(1)}` of an oriented residual variable with beta shape `a~m` costs only `exp(m^{-1+o(1)})`, not `C^q`.

So the useful invariant is not "linear total charge".  It is

\[
\log(B_rQ_r+\Delta_r)=o(\log m)
\]

through the chosen logarithmic factorial order.  The saturated finite-state atlas gives this automatically from exponential-in-`r` complexity.
