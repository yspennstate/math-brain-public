## 2. Determinant tilt and the exact Hotelling law

Let
\[
X\in\mathbb R^{n\times(n+e)},\qquad e\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\},
\]
be iid standard real Gaussian and set
\[
S=XX^T.
\]
For `alpha` with `e+2 alpha>-1`, define the normalized determinant tilt
\[
\frac{d\mathbb P_\alpha}{d\mathbb P}(X)
=\frac{\det(S)^\alpha}{\mathbb E\det(S)^\alpha}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The inherited Wishart-shift identity gives
\[
S\sim W_n(n+e+2\alpha,I_n)
\qquad\text{under }\mathbb P_\alpha.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Let `h` be an independent standard Gaussian vector in `R^n` and define the slope functional
\[
\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}(S)
:=\mathbb E_h\sqrt{1+h^TS^{-1}h}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Put
\[
r:=e+2\alpha,
\qquad
Y_{e,\alpha}:=\frac{\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}}
{\mathbb E_\alpha\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}}.
\]
The parameter `r` is the effective excess after determinant tilting.

### Theorem P08-TILTED-HOTELLING-MOMENTS
Assume `r>0`. Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\alpha\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}
=
\frac{\Gamma(r/2)\Gamma((n+r+1)/2)}
     {\Gamma((r+1)/2)\Gamma((n+r)/2)}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
Moreover, for every real `q>=1` satisfying
\[
q<r+1,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\alpha\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}^{\,q}
\le
\frac{\Gamma((r+1-q)/2)\Gamma((n+r+1)/2)}
     {\Gamma((r+1)/2)\Gamma((n+r+1-q)/2)}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed `q` and every fixed gap `delta>0`,
\[
q\le r+1-\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sup_{n\ge1}\sup_{r\ge q-1+\delta}
\mathbb E_\alpha Y_{e,\alpha}^{\,q}<\infty.
\tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof
Under (2.2), the standard Hotelling representation gives
\[
Q:=h^TS^{-1}h\stackrel d=\frac{U}{V},
\]
where
\[
U\sim\chi_n^2,\qquad V\sim\chi_{r+1}^2
\]
are independent. Equivalently,
\[
B:=\frac1{1+Q}
\sim\operatorname{Beta}\left(\frac{r+1}{2},\frac n2\right).
\tag{2.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_\alpha\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}
=\mathbb E B^{-1/2},
\]
which is exactly (2.4).

For `q>=1`, conditional Jensen gives
\[
\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}(S)^q
\le \mathbb E_h(1+Q)^{q/2}.
\]
Averaging and using (2.7),
\[
\mathbb E_\alpha\mathcal L_{e,\alpha}^{\,q}
\le \mathbb E B^{-q/2},
\]
and the beta integral gives (2.5), finite exactly when `q<r+1`.

Divide (2.5) by the `q`th power of (2.4). The resulting expression is a product of two gamma-ratio factors, one depending on `r` and one on `n+r`. On the region `r>=q-1+delta`, both stay a positive distance from their poles. Gautschi/Wendel gamma-ratio inequalities on the compact lower end and Stirling uniformly on the upper end give a bound depending only on `q,delta`, proving (2.6). QED.

The point is structural: **one determinant power `det(S)^{1/2}` increases the effective excess by exactly one**. Thus endpoint integrability can be read directly from the number of half-Jacobians accumulated on the same residual Gram block.

## 3. The exact third-moment threshold

In the coarea charts the determinant powers occur in half-integer units. Write
\[
\alpha=\frac h2,
\qquad h\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}.
\]
Then
\[
r=e+h.
\]
For `q=3`, Theorem P08-TILTED-HOTELLING-MOMENTS gives the sharp threshold
\[
3<r+1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\boxed{e+h\ge3.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Hence:

* if `e+h>=3`, the normalized slope has a **uniform third moment**;
* if `e+h=2`, the third moment is critical and logarithmic after truncation;
* if `e+h<=1`, a single slope factor is genuinely subcritical at order three and requires additional structure rather than a bare moment bound.

The first bullet is immediate from (2.6):
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{n\ge1}\sup_{e+h\ge3}
\mathbb E_{h/2}Y_{e,h/2}^3
\le C.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This already treats, for example, every block with `e>=2` carrying one coarea half-determinant, every block with `e>=1` carrying two such half-determinants, and every zero-excess block carrying at least three half-determinants on the same residual Gram.

## 4. Critical endpoint `e+h=2`: only a squared logarithm

The critical case is important because a bare third moment diverges, but the true hard-edge conditional probability is automatically capped. The cap turns the divergence into a polynomially harmless logarithm.

### Lemma P08-CRITICAL-TILTED-L3-CAP
Suppose `e+h=2`, `alpha=h/2`, and let
\[
Y=Y_{e,\alpha}.
\]
Then for `2<=q<3`, uniformly in `n`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\alpha Y^q\le \frac{C}{3-q}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Consequently, for every `T>=e`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\alpha\min(Y,T)^3
\le C\bigl(1+(\log T)^2\bigr).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof
When `e+h=2`, we have `r=2`, so (2.5) contains the factor
\[
\Gamma\left(\frac{3-q}{2}\right).
\]
The remaining gamma ratios, after normalization by (2.4), are uniformly bounded for `2<=q<3`. Since
\[
\Gamma(x)\le \frac Cx
\qquad (0<x\le1/2),
\]
we obtain (4.1).

For `y>=e`, choose
\[
q_y=3-\frac1{\log y}\in[2,3).
\]
Markov and (4.1) give
\[
\Pr(Y>y)
\le \frac{C\log y}{y^{3-1/\log y}}
\le \frac{Ce\log y}{y^3}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Using the layer-cake identity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\min(Y,T)^3
&=3\int_0^T y^2\Pr(Y>y)\,dy\\
&\le C+ C\int_e^T\frac{\log y}{y}\,dy\\
&\le C\bigl(1+(\log T)^2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (4.2). QED.

At the P08 threshold,
\[
p_m:=\Pr\{s_{\min}(G_m)\le s_m(\lambda)\}
=\frac{\lambda}{L_m}(1+e^{-\Omega(m)}),
\]
so the intrinsic saturation cap of a normalized conditional probability is at most
\[
T_m\le p_m^{-1}=\exp((\log4)m+O(\log m)).
\]
Therefore (4.2) yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\min(Y,T_m)^3=O_\lambda(m^2).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Thus a critical tilted endpoint costs only a fixed polynomial in `m`, not an exponential factor and not the old hard-edge-scale `1/s` loss.

## 5. New endpoint budget for the direct `k=3` route

The direct three-minor proof from the preceding run conditions on common Venn blocks and produces endpoint coarea factors. The historical fixed-order chart construction already supplies determinant half-Jacobians; the issue was whether small effective excess could make the third conditional moment explode at the exponentially small threshold.

The new theorem gives a simple branchwise invariant. For each active residual Gram block record
\[
(e,h),
\]
where `e` is its fresh rectangular excess before determinant weighting and `h` is the number of coarea half-determinants accumulated on that same block. Then:

\[
\boxed{
 e+h\ge3\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{uniform normalized }L^3,
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
\[
\boxed{
 e+h=2\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{at most }O(m^2)\text{ capped third moment at }s_m(\lambda).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The nontransverse Venn geometries in the direct triple decomposition have exponentially small set-count mass. Hence any branch whose remaining fixed-order chart factors are polynomial and whose active slope blocks satisfy `e+h>=2` contributes exponentially less than `m^{-3}` after geometry averaging. The earlier P08 fixed-order endpoint and private-hard-edge estimates already put all non-slope factors in this polynomial class. Therefore **the only endpoint configuration not killed by this argument is a residual block with**
\[
\boxed{e+h\le1.}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Since an actual coarea endpoint carries at least one half-Jacobian, the genuinely dangerous case is reduced to
\[
\boxed{e=0,\qquad h=1.}
\tag{5.4}
\]
This is precisely the single-Jacobian zero-excess configuration: there are no genuinely fresh normal directions and only one determinant shift has yet been accumulated on that residual Gram.

This is a sharper reduction than the preceding report's blanket `d_i in {0,1,2}` endpoint list. Small singleton defect by itself is not the correct analytic invariant. The correct local invariant is **fresh excess plus Jacobian count**.

I am not claiming that every `d_i=1,2` Venn endpoint has now been globally assembled automatically; the route must still identify its `(e,h)` after the deterministic block reductions. What is proved here is that once `e+h>=2` is visible, no superpolynomial third-moment loss remains, and once `e+h>=3` even the normalized third moment is uniformly bounded.

## 6. Why the last case is genuinely different

For `e=0,h=1`, we have `r=1`. The tilted Hotelling law has
\[
B\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,n/2),
\]
so the available moment range is only
\[
q<2.
\]
A third-moment estimate cannot be recovered from the single-block slope alone. Truncating at `T_m\asymp p_m^{-1}` can therefore cost an exponential factor and cannot be discarded merely by saying that the Venn geometry is exponentially rare.

So the next attack should not apply a stronger Holder inequality to this case. It should exploit extra structure specific to the zero-fresh cyclic chart:

1. either prove that a second determinant half-Jacobian acts on the **same** residual Gram before the critical slope is paid, moving the case from `e+h=1` to the logarithmic `e+h=2` regime;
2. or keep the slope and determinant together through the enlarged-Gram identity
   \[
   \sqrt{\det S}\sqrt{1+h^TS^{-1}h}
   =\sqrt{\det(S+hh^T)},
   \]
   and perform the three-body coarea integral before splitting factors;
3. or invoke the already established saturated zero-fresh chart only for this one residual configuration, rather than reopening the full all-orders atlas.

This isolates one concrete analytic object for the next run instead of treating all `d<=2` defects as equally singular.
