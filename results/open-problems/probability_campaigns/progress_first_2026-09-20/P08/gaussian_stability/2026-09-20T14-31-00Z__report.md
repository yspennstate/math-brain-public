## 3. One-half-Jacobian enlarged-Gram absorption

Let
\[
X\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}
\]
be standard Gaussian, set
\[
S=XX^T,
\qquad
J(X)=\sqrt{\det S}=|\det X|,
\]
and define the Gaussian slope functional
\[
\mathcal L(X)=\mathbb E_h\sqrt{1+h^TS^{-1}h},
\qquad h\sim N(0,I_n)
\]
independently of `X`.

The dangerous `e=0,h=1` chart factor is the **product**
\[
H_n(X):=J(X)\mathcal L(X),
\]
not the slope alone. For every invertible `X`, the matrix determinant lemma gives
\[
\det(S+hh^T)=\det(S)(1+h^TS^{-1}h).
\]
Hence pointwise
\[
\boxed{
H_n(X)
=
\mathbb E_h\sqrt{\det(S+hh^T)}
=
\mathbb E_h\sqrt{\det([X\ h][X\ h]^T)}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Thus the inverse-Gram singularity vanishes before any Holder step: the combined factor is just the expected Euclidean row-volume of an `n x (n+1)` standard Gaussian matrix.

### Theorem P08-ONEHALF-ABSORPTION-Lq
For every fixed real `q>=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\left\|\frac{H_n}{\mathbb EH_n}\right\|_q
\le C_q n^{(q-1)/4}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left(\frac{H_n}{\mathbb EH_n}\right)^3
\le C n^{3/2},
\qquad
\left\|\frac{H_n}{\mathbb EH_n}\right\|_3\le Cn^{1/2}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof
Let
\[
V_n(X,h)=\sqrt{\det([X\ h][X\ h]^T)}.
\]
By (3.1), `H_n=E_h V_n`. Jensen gives
\[
\mathbb E H_n^q\le \mathbb E V_n^q,
\qquad
\mathbb EH_n=\mathbb EV_n.
\tag{3.4}
\]
For an `n x (n+1)` standard Gaussian matrix, Bartlett decomposition gives
\[
V_n\stackrel d=\prod_{j=2}^{n+1}\chi_j,
\tag{3.5}
\]
with independent chi variables. Therefore
\[
\frac{\mathbb EV_n^q}{(\mathbb EV_n)^q}
=
\prod_{j=2}^{n+1}
\frac{\Gamma((j+q)/2)\Gamma(j/2)^{q-1}}
     {\Gamma((j+1)/2)^q}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
For fixed `q`, Taylor expansion of `log Gamma` (or uniform gamma-ratio inequalities) gives
\[
\log\frac{\Gamma((j+q)/2)\Gamma(j/2)^{q-1}}
              {\Gamma((j+1)/2)^q}
=
\frac{q(q-1)}{4j}+O_q(j^{-2}).
\tag{3.7}
\]
Summing from `j=2` to `n+1` yields
\[
\frac{\mathbb EV_n^q}{(\mathbb EV_n)^q}
\le C_q n^{q(q-1)/4}.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Combine (3.4) and (3.8), then take the `q`th root. This proves (3.2).

For `q=3` there is an exact telescoping formula. Indeed
\[
\frac{\mathbb E\chi_j^3}{(\mathbb E\chi_j)^3}
=
\frac{j+1}{2}
\left(
\frac{\Gamma(j/2)}{\Gamma((j+1)/2)}
\right)^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb EV_n^3}{(\mathbb EV_n)^3}
=
\frac{(n+2)!}
{2^{n+1}\Gamma((n+2)/2)^2}
\sim \frac{n^{3/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]
This gives (3.3). QED.

### Why this differs from the previous tilted-slope moment
Under the half-determinant probability tilt, the normalized slope alone has moment threshold `q<2` when `e=0`. That statement remains correct. What failed was **splitting** `J` and `L` and then demanding an `L^3` estimate on `L` by itself. In the actual coarea integrand the single half-Jacobian is present linearly with the slope. Absorbing them first changes the object from an inverse-Gram functional into an ordinary Gaussian volume. Only after that absorption do we use Holder, and the cost is polynomial.

## 4. Consequence for the zero-fresh cyclic triple chart

The inherited fixed-order `r=3` zero-fresh endpoint construction keeps the active half-Jacobian on the same residual Gram block as the associated slope before the final chart integral. This is also exactly the alternative proposed in the preceding P08 report: use

Apply Theorem P08-ONEHALF-ABSORPTION-Lq to every occurrence of the formerly dangerous `e=0,h=1` block. At order three there are only finitely many such blocks. After absorption, each normalized block has `L^3` norm at most `C m^{1/2}`. Therefore any product of the at most three dangerous blocks costs at most
\[
C m^{3/2}
\tag{4.1}
\]
under Holder. All other endpoint factors were already polynomial by the `e+h>=2` analysis and the inherited fixed-order private-hard-edge endpoint package. Consequently there is some fixed `C_0` such that on the entire nontransverse triple sector
\[
|\mathfrak K(B_1,B_2,B_3)|\le m^{C_0}
\tag{4.2}
\]
after the endpoint charts are summed.

Since the nontransverse Venn sector has combinatorial probability at most `C exp(-c m)`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\rm distinct}
\left[|\mathfrak K|\,;
\text{nontransverse}
\right]
\le m^{C_0}e^{-cm}

\tag{4.3}

Combining with the transverse bound (2.2),

\boxed{

This is the desired global connected-third-cumulant estimate for the direct fixed-order route. It is a complementary proof relative to the historical saturated-atlas `r=3` splice, not a replacement of that prior claim.

## 5. Third factorial moment

The inherited exact one-minor hard-edge expansion at the exponential threshold gives
\[
p_m:=\Pr(E_B)
=\frac{\lambda}{L_m}(1+e^{-\Omega_\lambda(m)}).
\tag{5.1}
\]
The established pair theorem gives
\[
\mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_2
=\lambda^2\left(1+\frac1{m^2}+O_\lambda(m^{-3})\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Let
\[
R_2(B_i,B_j)=\frac{\Pr(E_iE_j)}{p_m^2},
\qquad
R_3(B_1,B_2,B_3)=\frac{\Pr(E_1E_2E_3)}{p_m^3}.
\]
For a uniform ordered distinct triple, the exact connected identity gives
\[
R_3=R_2(1,2)+R_2(1,3)+R_2(2,3)-2+\mathfrak K.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Averaging, symmetry and (5.2) imply
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm distinct}R_2
=1+\frac1{m^2}+O_\lambda(m^{-3}),
\tag{5.4}
\]
because `(L_m)_2p_m^2=lambda^2(1+e^{-Omega(m)})`. With (4.4),
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm distinct}R_3
=1+\frac3{m^2}+O_\lambda(m^{-3}).
\tag{5.5}
\]
Finally `(L_m)_3p_m^3=lambda^3(1+e^{-Omega(m)})`, so
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_3
=\lambda^3\left(1+\frac3{m^2}+O_\lambda(m^{-3})\right).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Again: the repository already contained a historical saturated-atlas third-order closure. Equation (5.6) is a **new direct connected-cumulant derivation** that uses the current P08 pair theorem and the enlarged-Gram absorption rather than reopening the all-orders atlas.

## 6. Immediate distribution-level consequence

Let
\[
Y_m=L_ma_m\omega(A_m).
\]
Then
\[
\{Y_m\le\lambda\}=\{W_m(\lambda)\ge1\}.
\]
The first three Bonferroni inequalities, together with (5.1), (5.2) and (5.6), give for each fixed `lambda>0`
\[
\lambda-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}
-\frac{\lambda^2}{2m^2}
+O_\lambda(m^{-3})
\le
\Pr(Y_m\le\lambda)
\tag{6.1}
\]
and
\[
\Pr(Y_m\le\lambda)
\le
\lambda-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}+\frac{\lambda^3}{6}
+\frac{-\lambda^2/2+\lambda^3/2}{m^2}
+O_\lambda(m^{-3}),
\tag{6.2}

with the trivial truncation to `[0,1]` understood. Thus the proved CDF bracket now matches the first **three** terms of the candidate Poisson/`Exp(1)` expansion on the upper side, rather than only the first two factorial terms.

This is not a proof that `Y_m => Exp(1)`. Full convergence still requires control of all fixed factorial moments, or a different point-process/Stein argument.
