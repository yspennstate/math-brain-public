## 1. Source target and inherited input

The source remains Bandeira--Dmitriev--Lucca--Nizic-Nikolac--Rodder, *Randomstrasse101: Open Problems of 2025*, arXiv:2603.29571, Definition 10.1, Conjecture 20 and Open Problem 21. For iid standard real Gaussian

\[
A_m\in\mathbb R^{(2m-1)\times m},
\]

I continue to use the dimensionally consistent version of the source quantity

\[
\omega(A_m)=\min_{S:\operatorname{rank}(A_{S^c})<m}\sigma_m(A_S).
\]

Because Gaussian matrices are full spark almost surely,

\[
\omega(A_m)=\min_{|B|=m}s_{\min}(A_B)\qquad\text{a.s.}
\]

Set

\[
L_m=\binom{2m-1}{m},\qquad
 a_m=\sqrt2\,\frac{\Gamma((m+1)/2)}{\Gamma(m/2)},
\qquad
Y_m=L_ma_m\omega(A_m),
\]

and for `lambda>0`

\[
W_m(\lambda)=\sum_{|B|=m}
\mathbf 1_{\{s_{\min}(A_B)\le \lambda/(L_ma_m)\}}.
\]

Then

\[
\{Y_m\le\lambda\}=\{W_m(\lambda)>0\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The preceding runs proved, without assuming independent minors, that for every fixed `K<infinity`, uniformly for

\[
m^{-K}\le\lambda\le m^K,
\tag{1.2}
\]

with `mu_m(lambda)=E W_m(lambda)`, one has

\[
\mu_m(\lambda)=\lambda(1+O_K(e^{-c_Km}))
\tag{1.3}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_2
\le \mu_m(\lambda)^2\left(1+\frac{C_K}{m^2}\right).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Also, the exact square-Gaussian hard-edge estimate proved earlier gives for all `x>=0`

\[
\Pr\{s_{\min}(G_m)\le x\}\le a_mx.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Consequently the first-moment union bound is global:

\[
\boxed{\Pr\{Y_m\le\lambda\}\le\min\{1,\lambda\}\quad(\lambda>0).}
\tag{1.6}
\]

The previous report combined (1.3)--(1.4) with Paley--Zygmund and obtained the lower CDF bound `lambda/(1+lambda)`. The new observation is that integer-valuedness of `W_m` lets one use the strictly sharper Dawson--Sankoff quadratic minorant.

## 2. A sharp quadratic minorant for an integer count

For an integer `r>=1` and every integer `w>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf 1_{\{w>0\}}
\ge
\frac{2w}{r+1}-\frac{w(w-1)}{r(r+1)}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed the right side is a concave quadratic in `w`, equals `1` at `w=r,r+1`, is at most `1` on the intervening integer support, is at most `1` for `1<=w<=r`, and becomes no larger after `r+1`; at `w=0` both sides vanish. Equivalently,

\[
1-\left[\frac{2w}{r+1}-\frac{w(w-1)}{r(r+1)}\right]
=\frac{(w-r)(w-r-1)}{r(r+1)}\ge0
\]

for integer `w`.

Taking expectations gives the Dawson--Sankoff bound

\[
\Pr\{W>0\}
\ge
\frac{2\mathbb EW}{r+1}
-
\frac{\mathbb E(W)_2}{r(r+1)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For `lambda>0`, let

\[
r(\lambda)=\lceil\lambda\rceil
\]

(with either adjacent value allowed at integer `lambda`, where the formulas agree), and define

\[
\boxed{
D(\lambda)
:=
\frac{\lambda(2r(\lambda)-\lambda)}{r(\lambda)(r(\lambda)+1)}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus on `r-1<=lambda<=r`,

\[
D(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda(2r-\lambda)}{r(r+1)}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

## 3. New unconditional distribution bracket

### Theorem P08-DAWSON-SANKOFF-BRACKET

For every fixed `lambda>0`,

\[
\boxed{
D(\lambda)
\le
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\Pr\{Y_m\le\lambda\}
\le
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\Pr\{Y_m\le\lambda\}
\le
\min\{1,\lambda\}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Moreover, for each fixed `K`, uniformly on (1.2),

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{Y_m\le\lambda\}
\ge
D(\lambda)-\frac{C_K}{m^2}-C_Ke^{-c_Km}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Apply (2.2) to `W=W_m(lambda)` with `r=r(lambda)`. From (1.3)--(1.4),

\[
\Pr\{Y_m\le\lambda\}
\ge
\frac{2\mu_m}{r+1}
-
\frac{\mu_m^2(1+C_Km^{-2})}{r(r+1)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since `r>=lambda` and `r<lambda+1`, replacing `mu_m` by `lambda` costs only `O_K(e^{-c_Km})`, while the explicit pair-correlation correction costs

\[
\frac{C_Km^{-2}\mu_m^2}{r(r+1)}\le C'_Km^{-2}.
\]

This proves (3.2). Equation (1.6) supplies the upper CDF bound, and fixed `lambda` followed by `m->infinity` yields (3.1). QED.

### Strict improvement over Paley--Zygmund

On `r-1<=lambda<=r`, direct algebra gives

\[
D(\lambda)-\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}
=
\frac{\lambda\,[\lambda-(r-1)](r-\lambda)}{r(r+1)(1+\lambda)}
\ge0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The inequality is strict in the interior of every interval `(r-1,r)`. Thus this run strictly sharpens the previous unconditional CDF lower bound without any new independence assumption and without importing the candidate higher factorial moments.

For `0<=lambda<=1`, the first interval is especially simple:

\[
\boxed{D(\lambda)=\lambda-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the correct first two terms of the Poisson prediction `1-e^{-lambda}`.

## 4. New logarithmic-moment bounds at the exact hard-edge scale

The exact-scale tightness from the previous report was only in probability. The new bracket is strong enough to control the first absolute logarithmic moment.

Write

\[
A_m=(-\log Y_m)_+,
\qquad
B_m=(\log Y_m)_+.
\]

### 4.1 Small side: `Y_m<1`

By the global bound (1.6),

\[
\mathbb EA_m
=
\int_0^\infty \Pr\{Y_m\le e^{-t}\}\,dt
\le
\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\,dt
=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the reverse inequality, use (3.2) with, say, `K=3` on `0<=t<=3\log m`. Since `e^{-t}<=1`, (3.5) applies. The integrated `O(m^{-2})` error is `O((\log m)/m^2)=o(1)`, and truncating the limiting integral at `3\log m` loses only `O(m^{-3})`. Therefore

\[
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\mathbb EA_m
\ge
\int_0^\infty
\left(e^{-t}-\frac12e^{-2t}\right)dt
=
\boxed{\frac34}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac34
\le\liminf_m\mathbb E(-\log Y_m)_+
\le\limsup_m\mathbb E(-\log Y_m)_+
\le1.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.2 Large side: `Y_m>1`

From (3.2), for `1<=lambda<=m^3`,

\[
\Pr\{Y_m>\lambda\}
\le
1-D(\lambda)+O(m^{-2})+O(e^{-cm}).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\int_0^{3\log m}\Pr\{Y_m>e^t\}\,dt
\le
\int_1^{m^3}\frac{1-D(\lambda)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda+o(1).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The limiting integral has a closed form. On `[r-1,r]`, `r>=2`,

\[
\frac{1-D(\lambda)}{\lambda}
=
\frac1\lambda-
\frac{2r-\lambda}{r(r+1)}.
\]

Thus

\[
\int_{r-1}^{r}\frac{1-D(\lambda)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda
=
\log\frac r{r-1}
-
\frac{2r+1}{2r(r+1)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Summing from `r=2` to `R`, and using

\[
\frac{2r+1}{2r(r+1)}
=
\frac1{2r}+\frac1{2(r+1)},
\]

gives

\[
\sum_{r=2}^{R}
\left[
\log\frac r{r-1}
-
\frac{2r+1}{2r(r+1)}
\right]
=
\log R-H_R-\frac1{2(R+1)}+\frac54.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `H_R-log R -> gamma_E`, where `gamma_E` is the Euler--Mascheroni constant,

\[
\boxed{
\int_1^\infty\frac{1-D(\lambda)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda
=
\frac54-\gamma_E.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

It remains only to justify that the region above the polynomial window contributes `o(1)`. At `lambda=m^3`, (4.4) gives

\[
\Pr\{Y_m>m^3\}=O(m^{-2}).
\tag{4.9}
\]

By monotonicity the same bound holds until logarithmic height `2m`. On the other hand, for any fixed square minor `G_m`,

\[
Y_m=L_ma_m\omega(A_m)
\le
L_ma_m s_{\min}(G_m)
\le
\frac{L_ma_m}{\sqrt m}\|G_m\|_F.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Since `log(L_ma_m/sqrt(m))=(log4)m+O(log m)<1.5m` for large `m`, the tail `log Y_m>2m` forces `||G_m||_F>e^{m/2}`. The Frobenius norm is chi with `m^2` Gaussian degrees of freedom, so its tail beyond `e^{m/2}` is super-exponentially small, and

\[
\int_{2m}^{\infty}\Pr\{\log Y_m>t\}\,dt=o(1).
\tag{4.11}
\]

The intermediate interval `[3\log m,2m]` contributes at most `O(m^{-1})` by (4.9). Combining (4.5)--(4.11) proves

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb E(\log Y_m)_+
\le
\frac54-\gamma_E.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

### 4.3 Uniform integrability of the logarithmic hard-edge factor

From (4.1) and (4.12),

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb E|\log Y_m|
\le
1+\left(\frac54-\gamma_E\right)
=
\frac94-\gamma_E.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Numerically, `9/4-gamma_E` is about `1.6728`.

This is a genuine strengthening of mere tightness: the exact-scale logarithmic factor is uniformly integrable in `L^1`.

## 5. Expected logarithmic correction is now trapped in a nontrivial interval

(4.2) and (4.12) imply

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_E-\frac12
\le
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\mathbb E[-\log Y_m]
\le
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb E[-\log Y_m]
\le1.
}
\tag{5.1}

The lower endpoint is positive:

\[
\gamma_E-\frac12\approx0.0772.

The candidate `Exp(1)` hard-edge limit would give

\[
\mathbb E[-\log \mathrm{Exp}(1)]=\gamma_E,

## 6. Transfer to the source normalization and an `L^1` second-order theorem

Let

\[
R_m=\max_{1\le i\le2m-1}\|a_i\|_2,
\qquad
Z_m=
\frac{4^m\sqrt m}{2\sqrt\pi}
\frac{\omega(A_m)}{R_m}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The previous report proved `R_m/sqrt(m)->1` almost surely. Standard integration of the same chi-square union bound gives the slightly stronger fact needed here:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left|\log\frac{R_m}{\sqrt m}\right|
=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log m}{m}}\right)
=o(1).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Also Stirling gives

\[
\log\left[
\frac{4^m}{2\sqrt\pi L_ma_m}
\right]=O(m^{-1}).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\Delta_m:=\log\frac{Z_m}{Y_m}
=
\log\left[
\frac{4^m}{2\sqrt\pi L_ma_m}
\right]
+
\log\frac{\sqrt m}{R_m}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E|\Delta_m|=o(1).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently (4.13) and (5.1) transfer unchanged to `Z_m`:

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_m\mathbb E|\log Z_m|
\le\frac94-\gamma_E,
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_E-\frac12
\le
\liminf_m\mathbb E[-\log Z_m]
\le
\limsup_m\mathbb E[-\log Z_m]
\le1.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Now define the exact source-normalized exponent

\[
X_m=-\frac1m\log\frac{\omega(A_m)}{R_m}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The identity from the previous run is exact:

\[
T_m
:=
m(X_m-\log4)-\frac12\log m+\log(2\sqrt\pi)
=-\log Z_m.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Hence the new expectation theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left|
X_m-
\left(
\log4+
\frac{\log m}{2m}-
\frac{\log(2\sqrt\pi)}{m}
\right)
\right|
\le
\frac{\frac94-\gamma_E+o(1)}{m}.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

This upgrades the previous `O_P(m^{-1})` second-order localization to a quantitative **`L^1` localization at order `1/m`**.

It also yields a nontrivial bracket for the mean instability exponent:

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_E-\frac12
\le
\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 m\left[
\mathbb EX_m-
\log4-
\frac{\log m}{2m}+
\frac{\log(2\sqrt\pi)}{m}
\right]
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{m\to\infty}
 m\left[
\mathbb EX_m-
\log4-
\frac{\log m}{2m}+
\frac{\log(2\sqrt\pi)}{m}
\right]
\le1.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Under the candidate `Exp(1)` limit, the quantity in square brackets multiplied by `m` would converge to `gamma_E`; this run proves, unconditionally from the one- and two-minor theory, that every limiting mean correction must at least lie in `[gamma_E-1/2,1]`.

## 7. What changed, what did not, and next attack

### Inherited bound

The dependency-aware count had

\[
\mathbb EW_m(\lambda)=\lambda(1+o(1)),
\qquad
\mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_2
\le(\mathbb EW_m(\lambda))^2(1+O(m^{-2})),
\]

leading previously to the Paley--Zygmund CDF bracket and `O_P(m^{-1})` logarithmic localization.

### New proof/result

This run uses integer-valuedness of the hard-edge count to obtain the sharper Dawson--Sankoff lower envelope `D(lambda)`, proves the exact integral identity

\[
\int_1^\infty\frac{1-D(\lambda)}{\lambda}d\lambda
=\frac54-\gamma_E,
\]

and deduces

\[
\limsup\mathbb E|\log Z_m|\le\frac94-\gamma_E,
\]

hence the source-facing `L^1` second-order theorem (6.9) and the mean-correction bracket (6.10)--(6.11).

### Remaining gap

The limiting law of `Z_m` is still not proved. The candidate is `Exp(1)`, equivalently `T_m=-log Z_m` should converge to standard Gumbel. The all-orders saturated-atlas chain remains a **candidate full Gaussian proof**, not independently certified closure, and deterministic Conjecture 20 remains open.
