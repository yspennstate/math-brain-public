## 2. Uniform Venn-cell regularity through logarithmic order

Let `N=2m-1`.  Choose a labelled `k`-tuple `B_1,...,B_k` independently and uniformly from the `m`-subsets of `[N]`.  For `eta in {0,1}^k`, let

\[
C_\eta=\{x\in[N]: {\bf1}_{x\in B_j}=\eta_j\text{ for all }j\}.
\]

Put `p=m/N`.  Then

\[
\mu_\eta:=\mathbb E|C_\eta|
=Np^{|\eta|}(1-p)^{k-|\eta|},
\]

and uniformly for `k<=K_m+1`,

\[
\min_\eta\mu_\eta=m^{1-o(1)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem P08-VENN-LOGK

There are universal constants `c,C>0` such that, for every `2<=k<=K_m+1`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left\{
\max_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^k}
\left|\frac{|C_\eta|}{\mu_\eta}-1\right|>\frac14
\right\}
\le
Ck2^k\exp\left(-\frac{c m}{2^k k^2}\right).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

In particular the right side is

\[
\exp(-m^{1-o(1)})
\tag{2.3}
\]

uniformly for `k<=K_m+1`.

The same estimate holds after conditioning the labelled tuple to have distinct members, up to an additional `O(k^2/L_m)` term, which is exponentially small.

### Proof

Expose `B_1,...,B_k` sequentially.  Conditional on the first `j-1` sets, every current cell `C` of size `s` is split by `B_j`, and

\[
X:=|C\cap B_j|
\]

has a hypergeometric law with population size `N`, `s` marked objects, and sample size `m`.  Thus

\[
\mathbb E[X\mid C]=ps,
\]

and the standard multiplicative hypergeometric Chernoff bound gives, for `0<delta<=1/2`,

\[
\Pr\{|X-ps|>\delta ps\mid C\}
\le 2\exp(-c\delta^2 s).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Use the same

\[
\delta=\frac{1}{100k}
\]

at every split.  If all splits through level `j-1` are good, each level-`j-1` cell has size at least

\[
c_0N2^{-(j-1)}(1-\delta)^{j-1}
\ge c_1m2^{-k}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

There are fewer than `2^k` cells over all levels up to a harmless factor `k`.  A union bound using (2.4)--(2.5) therefore gives

\[
\Pr\{\text{some split is bad}\}
\le Ck2^k\exp\left(-\frac{c m}{2^k k^2}\right).
\]

On the complement, the multiplicative error along any root-to-leaf path is between `(1-delta)^k` and `(1+delta)^k`, both lying inside `[3/4,5/4]` for the chosen constant.  This proves (2.2).  Finally,

\[
2^{K_m}k^2=m^{o(1)},
\]

so the exponent in (2.2) is `m^{1-o(1)}`.  Conditioning on distinct subsets costs at most the collision probability `binom(k,2)/L_m`.  QED.

### Consequence

The old fixed-`k` phrase `exp(-c_k m)` can now be replaced, for the Venn-regularity part of the argument, by the explicit uniform rate

\[
\exp\left(-\frac{c m}{2^k k^2}\right).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus no hidden `m^{ck}` loss can come merely from making the Venn entropy split uniform for `k<=K_m`.

This does **not** by itself bound the hard-edge probability on an irregular tuple: the inherited routed/cluster estimates are still needed there.  The point is narrower and useful: the combinatorial entropy constant is no longer an unknown growing-`k` obstruction.

## 3. Counting the nested zero-fresh support atlas

The historical saturated zero-fresh proof is organized by nested quotient supports: `NQSD(r) -> LBV(r) -> LC7 -> LC1-LC7 -> endpoint chart estimate`.  A possible danger in sending `r=k-2` to infinity is that the number of nested charts could itself be superfactorial.

That danger can be removed abstractly.

### Lemma P08-NESTED-ATLAS-COUNT

Let `r>=2`.  The number of laminar/nested support families on `r` labelled leaves is at most

\[
2^{r-2}(2r-3)!!
\le (4r)^r.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If every node or edge carries one of `J` fixed route/chart decorations, the count is at most

\[
(C_J r)^r.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Any laminar family can be refined to a rooted full binary tree with the same `r` labelled leaves.  The number of rooted binary phylogenetic trees on `r` labelled leaves is `(2r-3)!!`.  Starting from such a binary refinement, a nonbinary nested family is obtained by contracting a subset of at most `r-2` internal edges.  This overcounts, which is harmless, and gives the first inequality in (3.1).  Since

\[
(2r-3)!!\le (2r)^r,
\]

(3.1) follows.  A rooted tree with `r` leaves has at most `2r-1` vertices, so `J`-valued local decorations contribute at most `J^{2r}`, which is absorbed into `(C_Jr)^r`.  QED.

### Interpretation for the inherited saturated route

The committed historical summary describes the difficult endpoint component specifically as a **nested quotient support** problem, and the final fixed-`r` proof uses a saturated exact labelled incidence atlas rather than enumerating arbitrary unrelated Venn charts.  Therefore the nested-support indexing itself has at most the admissible one-factorial scale `(Cr)^r`; there is no intrinsic `2^{2^r}` atlas explosion.

This is not yet a proof of (1.1), because a single chart could still carry a bad analytic constant.  It removes the chart-count obstruction.

## 4. Uniform beta-integrability engine for endpoint charts

The historical endpoint proofs repeatedly reduce angular coalescence to beta-type factors; for `r=2`, for example, the summary records the factor `(1-tau^2)^((d-5)/2)`.  The following lemma gives the needed growing-order control whenever the monomialized chart has only `O(r)` total negative angular charge and all active normal blocks retain linear dimension.

### Lemma P08-BETA-LOGK

Fix `c_0>0`.  Let

\[
U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,b),
\qquad a,b\ge c_0m.
\]

For `0<=q<=c_0m`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E U^{-q/2}\le \exp(C_{c_0}q),
\qquad
\mathbb E(1-U)^{-q/2}\le \exp(C_{c_0}q).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

More generally, suppose `U_1,...,U_s` are exposed sequentially and, conditionally on the past, each `U_j` has beta parameters at least `c_0m`.  If nonnegative exponents satisfy

\[
\sum_{j=1}^s q_j\le Cr,
\qquad r\le K_m+1,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^s U_j^{-q_j/2}
\le C_1^r,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

and the same holds with any mixture of `U_j` and `1-U_j`.

### Proof

For `s=q/2<a`, the beta identity gives

\[
\mathbb E U^{-s}
=\frac{\Gamma(a-s)\Gamma(a+b)}
       {\Gamma(a)\Gamma(a+b-s)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Taking logarithms and differentiating in `s`,

\[
\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathbb E U^{-s}
=\psi(a+b-s)-\psi(a-s).
\]

On `0<=s<=c_0m/2` with `a,b>=c_0m`, the digamma difference is bounded above by a constant depending only on `c_0`; for example this follows from the integral representation of `psi'` or the elementary bound `psi(y)-psi(x)<=log(y/x)+C/x`.  Integrating proves (4.1).  For (4.2), apply (4.1) conditionally one variable at a time and use the tower property.  QED.

### Why this matters

For `r<=K_m`, even a normal-block dimension of the form

\[
m-\Delta_r,
\qquad \Delta_r=\exp(O(r)),
\tag{4.4}
\]

is still `(1-o(1))m`, because `exp(O(K_m))=m^{o(1)}`.  Therefore the endpoint beta integrals cannot create an `m^{cr}` loss provided two explicit structural facts hold:

1. the total negative divisorial/angular charge of a chart is `O(r)`;
2. the `O_r(1)` dimension loss in the historical `NQSD(r)` statement grows at most exponentially in `r`.

Under those two facts, (3.2) and (4.2) make the total endpoint-chart cost at most `(Cr)^r`.

\[

\qquad

\[

=\frac{4^m}{2\sqrt\pi}
\left(1-\frac{3}{8m}+O(m^{-2})\right).
\tag{6.1}

Gaussian chi-square concentration and a union bound over `2m-1` rows give

\[
\log R_m-\frac12\log m=o_{\mathbb P}(1).

\[

\qquad E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1),

\[
-\log Y_m\Rightarrow G,

where `G` is standard Gumbel, `Pr(G<=x)=exp(-e^{-x})`.  Combining (6.1)--(6.3),

\[
\boxed{

\Rightarrow
G-\log(2\sqrt\pi).

\tag{6.4}

Equivalently,

in the distributional sense implied by (6.4).

This sharpens the interpretation of the source-normalized exponent: the leading limit is `log 4`, the deterministic first centering correction is `(log m)/(2m)`, and the next order is Gumbel if the candidate hard-edge Poisson law survives independent audit.
