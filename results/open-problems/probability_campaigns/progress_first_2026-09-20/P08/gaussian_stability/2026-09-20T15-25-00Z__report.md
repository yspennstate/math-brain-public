## 3. Shared-row conditioning for an arbitrary fixed tuple

Fix `r>=2` and an ordered tuple of distinct `m`-subsets

\[
\mathbf B=(B_1,\ldots,B_r).
\]

Define the singleton/private row block of label `i` by

\[
U_i:=B_i\setminus\bigcup_{j\ne i}B_j,
\qquad d_i:=|U_i|.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `F_r` be the sigma-field generated by every Gaussian row whose row index occurs in at least two of the sets `B_1,...,B_r`. Conditional on `F_r`, the row blocks `A_{U_1},...,A_{U_r}` are mutually independent. Moreover the event

\[
E_i(t):=\{s_{\min}(A_{B_i})\le s_m(t)\}
\]

depends, after conditioning on `F_r`, only on `A_{U_i}`. Hence the `E_i(t)` are conditionally independent given `F_r`.

Set

\[
q_i:=\Pr(E_i(t)\mid F_r),
\qquad
X_i:=\frac{q_i}{p_m(t)}-1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Since `E q_i=p_m(t)`,

\[
\mathbb E X_i=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For every nonempty `S subseteq [r]`, define the centered interaction

\[
K_S(\mathbf B;t):=
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}X_i.
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Lemma P08-CENTERED-MOBIUS
For every `S`,

\[
\boxed{
K_S
=
\sum_{T\subseteq S}
(-1)^{|S|-|T|}
\frac{\Pr(\cap_{i\in T}E_i(t))}{p_m(t)^{|T|}},
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

with the empty term interpreted as `1`. In particular `K_S` depends only on the subtuple `(B_i)_{i in S}`; the extra labels used to define `F_r` disappear from the final quantity.

Also, with

\[
R_r(\mathbf B;t)=
\frac{\Pr(E_1(t)\cap\cdots\cap E_r(t))}{p_m(t)^r},
\]

one has the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
R_r(\mathbf B;t)
=
1+
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[r]\\|S|\ge2}}K_S(\mathbf B;t).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

#### Proof
Conditional independence gives, for every `T subseteq[r]`,

\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in T}q_i
=\Pr\left(\bigcap_{i\in T}E_i(t)\right).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Expanding `prod_{i in S}(q_i/p_m-1)` proves (3.5). Expanding

\[
\prod_{i=1}^r(1+X_i)
=\frac{\prod_i q_i}{p_m^r}
\]

and averaging proves (3.6), with all singleton terms absent by (3.3). QED.

This identity is elementary but useful: it separates the known pair overdispersion from genuinely `s`-body centered interactions without assuming that the minors themselves are independent.

## 4. Venn transversality has exponentially high set-geometric probability

For fixed `r`, choose `B_1,...,B_r` independently and uniformly from the `m`-subsets of `[2m-1]`, temporarily allowing coincidences. Fix `i` and expose the other sets one at a time. Start with `T_0=B_i` and define

\[
T_j=T_{j-1}\setminus B_{\ell_j},
\]

where `ell_1,...,ell_{r-1}` are the other labels. Conditional on `|T_{j-1}|=u`, the number removed from `T_{j-1}` is hypergeometric with population `2m-1`, sample size `m`, and marked population `u`. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E(|T_j|\mid T_{j-1})
=u\frac{m-1}{2m-1}
=\left(\frac12+O(m^{-1})\right)u.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Hypergeometric Hoeffding bounds imply

\[
\Pr\{ |T_j|\notin[u/4,3u/4]\mid T_{j-1}\}
\le2e^{-c u}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Iterating a fixed number `r-1` of times gives constants

\[
0<\alpha_r<\beta_r<1,
\]

for example one may take `alpha_r=4^{-(r-1)}` and `beta_r=(3/4)^{r-1}` after harmless large-`m` adjustment, such that

\[
\Pr\{d_i\notin[\alpha_rm,\beta_rm]\}
\le C_re^{-c_rm}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

A union bound over `i` gives the same form for the event

\[
\mathcal T_r=
\{\alpha_rm\le d_i\le\beta_rm\text{ for every }i\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Conditioning the tuple to be distinct changes probabilities by at most `O_r(L_m^{-1})=e^{-Omega_r(m)}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm distinct}(\mathcal T_r^c)
\le C_re^{-c_rm}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The constants are allowed to depend on fixed `r`; no growing-`r` claim is made in this section.

## 5. New theorem: fixed-order centered interactions are `m^{-r}`

Let

\[
\overline K_r(t):=
\mathbb E_{\mathbf B\,\rm distinct}
K_{[r]}(\mathbf B;t).
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem P08-CENTERED-INTERACTION-HIERARCHY
For every fixed integer `r>=3` and fixed bounded `t>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\overline K_r(t)=O_{r,t}(m^{-r}).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The same estimate holds uniformly for `t` in any compact subset of `(0,infinity)`.

#### Proof: transverse tuples
On `mathcal T_r`, each `d_i` is proportional to `m`. Apply the inherited conditional transfer (2.3) with moment order `r`. Since the shared rows inside `B_i` form an `(m-d_i) x m` standard Gaussian block marginally, regardless of their correlations with the other shared blocks,

\[
\|X_i\|_{L^r(F_r)}
\le C_{r,t}m^{-1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

No independence among the `X_i` is used. Holder gives

\[
|K_{[r]}(\mathbf B;t)|
\le\prod_{i=1}^r\|X_i\|_r
\le C_{r,t}m^{-r}
\qquad(\mathbf B\in\mathcal T_r).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Averaging over transverse tuples preserves the same bound.

#### Proof: nontransverse tuples
For fixed `r`, the inherited endpoint/private-hard-edge package gives only finitely many labelled Venn/endpoint charts. The determinant-tilt theorem removes the old `e=1` pseudoinverse logarithm; the enlarged-Gram identity

\[
\sqrt{\det S}\sqrt{1+h^TS^{-1}h}
=
\sqrt{\det(S+hh^T)}
\tag{5.5}
\]

removes the last `e=0,h=1` inverse-Gram singularity before Holder. Together with the saturated zero-fresh angular beta estimates, every fixed-order normalized endpoint chart has at most `m^{C_r}` integrated cost. Therefore, using (4.5),

\[
\mathbb E_{\rm distinct}
\left[|K_{[r]}|;\mathcal T_r^c\right]
\le m^{C_r}e^{-c_rm}
=o(m^{-r}).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.4) and (5.6) proves (5.2). QED.

### Structural meaning
The pair interaction is exceptional:

\[
\overline K_2(t)=\frac1{m^2}+O_t(m^{-3}),
\tag{5.7}
\]

whereas every genuine centered `r`-body interaction, `r>=3`, is suppressed by at least one power `m^{-1}` per label:

\[
\overline K_r(t)=O_{r,t}(m^{-r}).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Thus the complete `m^{-2}` finite-size dependence correction is pairwise. Higher centered interactions cannot alter that coefficient.

For `r=3`, (5.2) recovers the preceding direct `O(m^{-3})` connected-triple result. For `r=4`, it gives the genuinely stronger new estimate

\[
\boxed{
\overline K_4(t)=O_t(m^{-4}),
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

where the old fixed-order factorial expansion alone would only force an `O_t(m^{-3})` residual after cancellations.

## 7. New multi-window extension: the Gaussian hard-edge point process

The centered argument is not tied to events of the form `[0,t]`. Define the scaled minor mark

\[
Z_B:=L_ma_m s_{\min}(A_B)
\]

and the point measure

\[
\Xi_m:=\sum_{|B|=m}\delta_{Z_B}
\qquad\text{on }(0,\infty).
\tag{7.1}
\]

For a fixed bounded interval `I=(u,v]`, the one-minor cubic hard-edge estimate gives

\[
\Pr\{Z_B\in I\}
=\frac{|I|}{L_m}+O_I(L_m^{-3}).
\tag{7.2}
\]

The conditional transfer is linear at this scale: subtracting the conditional expansions at `v` and `u` shows that the normalized conditional window probability has the same common-core multiplier `Y_d(C)` plus a superpolynomially small fixed-order error. Therefore Theorem P08-CENTERED-INTERACTION-HIERARCHY applies verbatim when different labels are assigned different fixed bounded intervals.

Let `I_1,...,I_J` be pairwise disjoint bounded intervals and

\[
N_{m,j}=\Xi_m(I_j).
\]

For fixed nonnegative integers `k_1,...,k_J`, let `K=sum_j k_j`. Expanding the mixed falling factorial selects an ordered `K`-tuple of distinct minors because the intervals are disjoint. The multi-window centered hierarchy yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^J(N_{m,j})_{k_j}
\longrightarrow
\prod_{j=1}^J |I_j|^{k_j}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The right side is exactly the mixed factorial-moment family of independent Poisson variables with means `|I_j|`. Since the Poisson law is moment-determinate and the first moments are bounded on every bounded window, the multivariate method of moments gives

\[
(N_{m,1},...,N_{m,J})
\Rightarrow
(\operatorname{Pois}(|I_1|),...,\operatorname{Pois}(|I_J|))
\tag{7.4}
\]

with independent coordinates.

Consequently, under the inherited fixed-order endpoint package used above, the direct route gives the **candidate Gaussian point-process limit**

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_m\Rightarrow \operatorname{PPP}(dx)
\quad\text{on }(0,\infty),
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

a unit-rate Poisson point process. This is stronger in form than merely identifying the first point.

In particular, if `Z_{m,(j)}` denotes the `j`th smallest scaled square-minor singular value, then for each fixed `j`,

\[
\boxed{
Z_{m,(j)}\Rightarrow\operatorname{Gamma}(j,1).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

For `j=1`,

\[
L_ma_m\omega(A_m)=Z_{m,(1)}\Rightarrow\operatorname{Exp}(1).
\tag{7.7}
\]

**Status caution.** Equation (7.5) is a complementary candidate full Gaussian theorem assembled from the new centered hierarchy plus inherited fixed-order endpoint/private-hard-edge lemmas. It has not undergone a fresh independent end-to-end audit in this run. It is not a deterministic Conjecture 20 result.

## 8. Why this is useful for the remaining second-order problem

The historical logarithmic-order program was trying to prove

\[
\Pr\{Y_m>t\}
=e^{-t}\left(1+\frac{t^2}{2m^2}\right)
+O_t(m^{-3+o(1)}).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Fixed-order moments, even all of them one order at a time, do not justify summing the probability-generating series at `z=0` with an `m^{-3}` remainder. That is why the old growing-`k` routed estimate remains relevant.

The new hierarchy changes the most promising route. Instead of tracking the entire normalized `k`-point probability through `PairConditioned-CCI(k-2)`, it may be enough to prove a **uniform centered interaction bound**

\[
\boxed{
|\overline K_r(t)|
\le m^{-r+o(1)}(Cr)^{Cr},
\qquad 3\le r\le K_m,
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

with `K_m=(4+o(1))log m/log log m`. Then

\[
\sum_{r=3}^{K_m}\binom{k}{r}|\overline K_r|

is automatically `m^{-3+o(1)}` for every `k<=K_m`, and the pair channel is the only source of the `m^{-2}` term. The transverse part of (8.2) is already essentially available from the growing-`L^q` estimate:

\[

\le m^{-1+o(1)}

because the typical singleton deficiency is `m/2^{r-1}=m^{1-o(1)}` throughout this range. The unresolved issue is now only a **uniform centered endpoint envelope**: show that the scalar endpoint deficits plus enlarged-Gram absorption keep the Venn-nontransverse contribution below the right side of (8.2), without separately locating an inherited `m^{-3}` seed.
