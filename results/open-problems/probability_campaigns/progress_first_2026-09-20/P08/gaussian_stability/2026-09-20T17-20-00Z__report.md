## 2. Zero-private cells force exponentially large one-label moments

Take three independently sampled uniform `m`-subsets `B_1,B_2,B_3` of `[2m-1]` and let
\[
U_1=B_1\setminus(B_2\cup B_3),\qquad d_1=|U_1|.
\]
Let `F_3` be the sigma-field generated by all Gaussian rows that occur in at least two of the three minors. On `{d_1=0}`, every row of `B_1` is already `F_3`-measurable. Hence
\[
q_1={\bf1}_{E_{B_1}},
\qquad
X_1=\frac{{\bf1}_{E_{B_1}}}{p_m}-1.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Because the Gaussian rows are independent of the set geometry, conditional on `{d_1=0}` the event `E_{B_1}` still has probability `p_m`. Therefore, for all sufficiently large `m` so that `p_m<=1/2`,
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm core}(|X_1|^3\mid d_1=0)
=p_m(p_m^{-1}-1)^3+(1-p_m)
\ge \frac1{8p_m^2}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Thus it remains only to estimate the geometry probability of `d_1=0`.

### Lemma P08-ZEROPRIVATE-GEOMETRY-LOWER
Along the even subsequence `m=2n`,
\[
\Pr(d_1=0)\ge R_n,
\]
where
\[
R_n=
\frac{\binom{2n}{n}\binom{2n-1}{n}\binom{3n-1}{n}}
{\binom{4n-1}{2n}^2},
\tag{2.3}
\]
and
\[
\frac1m\log R_n
\longrightarrow
-\log\frac{8}{3\sqrt3}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
The same exponential lower bound holds for a uniformly sampled ordered distinct triple.

#### Proof
Fix `B_1`. Let `D=B_1\setminus B_2`. Restrict to the event `|D|=n`. Since `|B_1|=2n` and its complement in `[4n-1]` has size `2n-1`,
\[
\Pr(|D|=n)
=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}\binom{2n-1}{n}}
{\binom{4n-1}{2n}}.
\]
Conditional on a particular such `D`, the event `D\subseteq B_3` implies `B_1\subseteq B_2\cup B_3`, equivalently `d_1=0`, and has probability
\[
\Pr(D\subseteq B_3)
=\frac{\binom{3n-1}{n}}
{\binom{4n-1}{2n}}.
\]
Multiplication gives (2.3). Stirling's formula gives
\[
\frac1m\log R_n
=\frac32H(1/3)-2\log2+o(1)
=-\left(3\log2-\frac32\log3\right)+o(1),
\]
which is (2.4). Passing to ordered distinct triples removes at most `O(L_m^{-1})=\exp[-(\log4+o(1))m]`, exponentially smaller than `R_n`. QED.

Combining (1.1), (2.2) and (2.4) gives the decisive obstruction:
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{\substack{m\to\infty\\m\ {m even}}}
\frac1m\log
\mathbb E_{\rm geometry,core}|X_1|^3
\ge \log(6\sqrt3)>0.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Indeed
\[
2\log4-\log\frac8{3\sqrt3}=\log(6\sqrt3).
\]
So the proposed global one-label `L^r` estimate (1.3?) fails by an exponential factor already for `r=3`.

This does **not** contradict the centered interaction bounds proved earlier. The point is exactly that endpoint cancellation is genuinely joint: one cannot take absolute one-label moments before exploiting centering.

## 3. Palm deletion removes the artificial rare-event blowup

The zero-private case has an exact cancellation identity that should be used before any Holder estimate.

### Lemma P08-PALM-DELETION
Suppose `d_1=0`, so (2.1) holds. For every integrable `F_3`-measurable random variable `H`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[X_1H]
=\mathbb E[H\mid E_{B_1}]-\mathbb EH.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
In particular, for the connected triple kernel,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(X_1X_2X_3)
=\mathbb E[X_2X_3\mid E_{B_1}]
-\mathbb E[X_2X_3].
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof
Since `X_1={\bf1}_{E_{B_1}}/p_m-1`,
\[
\mathbb E[X_1H]
=p_m^{-1}\mathbb E[{
\bf1}_{E_{B_1}}H]-\mathbb EH
=\mathbb E[H\mid E_{B_1}]-\mathbb EH.
\]
QED.

There is also a multi-label version. If a set `J` of labels have zero private rows and `H` is measurable with respect to the revealed core, then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[H\prod_{j\in J}
\left(\frac{{\bf1}_{E_j}}{p_m}-1\right)\right]
=
\sum_{T\subseteq J}
(-1)^{|J|-|T|}
\frac{\Pr(E_T)}{p_m^{|T|}}
\mathbb E[H\mid E_T],
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
where `E_T=cap_{j in T} E_j`, and the empty term is `E H`.

Equation (3.2) is the correct replacement for the false one-label `L^3` route. The apparent factor `p_m^{-1}` has disappeared completely: the endpoint object is a difference between a lower-order centered interaction under the hard-edge Palm law of `A_{B_1}` and the ordinary lower-order interaction.

## 4. Quantitative hard-edge Palm law for a square real Gaussian matrix

The Palm law in (3.2) can be identified explicitly.

Let `G` be an `m x m` iid standard real Gaussian matrix and let
\[
0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\cdots<\lambda_m
\]
be the ordered eigenvalues of `G^T G`, so `s_min(G)=sqrt(lambda_1)`. Let `nu_0` be the ordered eigenvalue law of
\[
W_0=Z^TZ,
\qquad Z\in\mathbb R^{(m+1)\times(m-1)}
\]
with iid standard Gaussian entries. Thus `nu_0` is the real Wishart/LOE law with dimension `p=m-1`, sample size `n=m+1`, and excess `n-p=2`.

### Theorem P08-GAUSSIAN-HARDEDGE-PALM
Fix `x>0`. Conditional on `lambda_1=x`, the law `nu_x` of `(lambda_2,...,lambda_m)` is absolutely continuous with respect to `nu_0`, with
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\nu_x}{d\nu_0}(\lambda)
=\frac{R_x(\lambda)}{a_m(x)},
\qquad
R_x(\lambda)=
{\bf1}_{\{\lambda_2>x\}}
\prod_{j=2}^m\left(1-\frac{x}{\lambda_j}\right),
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
where `a_m(x)=E_{nu_0}R_x`. Moreover, whenever `(m-1)x<1`,
\[
1-a_m(x)\le(m-1)x,
\tag{4.2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\|\nu_x-\nu_0\|_{TV}
\le \frac{(m-1)x}{1-(m-1)x}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Consequently, if `epsilon>0` and `m epsilon^2<=1/4`, then under the conditioning `s_min(G)<=epsilon`,
\[
\boxed{
\left(
\frac{s_{\min}(G)}\epsilon,
\lambda_2,...,\lambda_m
\right)
}
\]
is within total variation `C m epsilon^2` of
\[
\boxed{
(U,\mu_1,...,\mu_{m-1}),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
where `U~Unif[0,1]`, `(mu_1,...,mu_{m-1})~nu_0`, and the two are independent. The left and right singular-vector frames remain Haar and independent of the singular values exactly, because the Gaussian law and the conditioning event are bi-orthogonally invariant.

#### Proof
The ordered real Wishart eigenvalue density for a square Gaussian matrix is, up to normalization,
\[
\exp\left(-\frac12\sum_{j=1}^m\lambda_j\right)
\prod_{j=1}^m\lambda_j^{-1/2}
\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i).
\tag{4.5}
\]
Condition on `lambda_1=x`. The density of the remaining eigenvalues is therefore proportional to
\[
\exp\left(-\frac12\sum_{j=2}^m\lambda_j\right)
\prod_{j=2}^m\lambda_j^{-1/2}
\prod_{2\le i<j\le m}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)
\prod_{j=2}^m(\lambda_j-x)
{\bf1}_{\{\lambda_2>x\}}.
\]
Factoring `lambda_j-x=lambda_j(1-x/lambda_j)` turns the power `lambda_j^{-1/2}` into `lambda_j^{+1/2}`. But exponent `+1/2` is exactly the real Wishart exponent
\[
\frac{n-p-1}{2}=\frac12
\]
for `p=m-1,n=m+1`. This proves (4.1).

For every positive spectrum,
\[
1-R_x\le x\,\operatorname{tr}(W_0^{-1}).
\tag{4.6}
\]
Indeed, if `mu_min<=x`, then the right side is at least one. If every `mu_j>x`, use
`1-prod_j(1-u_j)<=sum_j u_j` with `u_j=x/mu_j`.
For a real Wishart matrix with `n>p+1`,
\[
\mathbb EW_0^{-1}=\frac{I_p}{n-p-1}.
\]
Here `n=m+1,p=m-1`, hence
\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(W_0^{-1})=m-1.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Taking expectations in (4.6) proves (4.2). If `a=E R_x`, then
\[
\|\nu_x-\nu_0\|_{TV}
=\frac12E_{\nu_0}\left|\frac{R_x}{a}-1\right|
\le\frac{1-a}{a},
\]
which gives (4.3).

Finally set `x=s^2`. In (4.5), the factor `x^{-1/2}dx` becomes `2 ds`, so the marginal density of `s` near zero is proportional to
\[
e^{-s^2/2}a_m(s^2).
\]
By (4.2), uniformly for `0<=s<=epsilon`, this is `1+O(m epsilon^2)`. Hence `s/epsilon` conditioned on `s<=epsilon` is `O(m epsilon^2)` in total variation from `Unif[0,1]`. Integrating (4.3) over that conditional `s` distribution gives (4.4). Bi-orthogonal invariance gives the angular statement. QED.

At the P08 threshold
\[
\epsilon=\epsilon_m(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{L_ma_m}=4^{-m+o(m)},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
m\epsilon_m(\lambda)^2
=\exp[-(2\log4+o(1))m].
}
\tag{4.8}
\]
Thus the hard-edge Palm replacement is exponentially more accurate than any algebraic order relevant to the `m^{-3}` program.

Equivalently, up to the exponentially tiny error in (4.8), conditioning a square Gaussian minor on its rare hard-edge event replaces it by a rank-`m-1` Gaussian Palm matrix whose nonzero squared singular values are those of an `(m+1)x(m-1)` Gaussian matrix, with independent Haar null directions; the rescaled vanishing singular value is uniform on `[0,1]`.
