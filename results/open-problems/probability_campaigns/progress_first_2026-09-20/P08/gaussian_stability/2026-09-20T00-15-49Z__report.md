## 3. New theorem

### Theorem P08-GAUSS-OMEGA-TAIL-1

Let `A_M in R^{(2M-1) x M}` have iid `N(0,1)` entries and let `R_M` and `X_M` be as above. For every fixed `epsilon in (0,log 4)`, there are constants `C_epsilon,c_epsilon>0` such that for all sufficiently large `M`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\!\left(X_M>\log4+\varepsilon\right)
\le C_\varepsilon M e^{-\varepsilon M/2}
+e^{-e^{c_\varepsilon M}},
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\!\left(X_M<\log4-\varepsilon\right)
\le \frac{C_\varepsilon}{M}
+C_\varepsilon\sqrt M\,e^{-\varepsilon M}
+C_\varepsilon M^{7/2}4^{-M}
+e^{-c_\varepsilon M}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\!\left(|X_M-\log4|>\varepsilon\right)
\le \frac{C_\varepsilon}{M}+C_\varepsilon e^{-c_\varepsilon M}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

after enlarging constants, and hence

\[
\frac{\omega(A_M)}{R_M}
=4^{-M+o_{\mathbb P}(M)}.
\]

Equivalently, the Gaussian exponential base in the source normalization is `1/4`.

The proof never assumes the candidate `M x M` minors are independent. The upper/existence side uses a maximally correlated fixed-apex family and averages its exact overlap geometry.

## 4. Source reduction: full spark turns `omega` into the square-row minimum

A Gaussian matrix is full spark almost surely. At `N=2M-1`, if `A` is full spark then

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{S^c})<M
\iff |S^c|\le M-1,
\]

so every admissible `S` has `|S|\ge M`. If `S\subset S'`, then

\[
A_{S'}^*A_{S'}-A_S^*A_S\succeq0,
\]

hence `sigma_M(A_{S'}) >= sigma_M(A_S)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\omega(A)=\min_{|S|=M}\sigma_M(A_S)
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

almost surely. This is the exact source-to-random-matrix reduction used below.

## 5. Lower envelope for `omega`: union bound plus a one-row distance lemma

This is the easy direction and does not use fake independence.

Let `G` be one square `M x M` iid standard real Gaussian matrix, with rows `r_1,...,r_M`, singular values `s_1>=...>=s_M`, and

\[
d_i:=\operatorname{dist}\bigl(r_i,\operatorname{span}\{r_j:j\ne i\}\bigr).
\]

The negative-second-moment identity gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^M s_j(G)^{-2}=\sum_{i=1}^M d_i^{-2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `s_M(G)<=t` and every `d_i>sqrt(M)t`, then the right side of (5.1) is strictly less than `1/t^2`, whereas the left side is at least `1/t^2`, a contradiction. Thus

\[
\{s_M(G)\le t\}\subseteq
\bigcup_{i=1}^M\{d_i\le\sqrt M\,t\}.
\]

Conditioned on all rows except `r_i`, the orthogonal direction to their span is deterministic and `d_i` is distributed as `|g|`, `g~N(0,1)`. For `sqrt(M)t<=1`,

\[
\mathbb P\{s_M(G)\le t\}
\le M\,\mathbb P\{|g|\le\sqrt M t\}
\le C M^{3/2}t.
\tag{5.2}
\]

There are

\[
L_M:=\binom{2M-1}{M}\le C\frac{4^M}{\sqrt M}
\]

square row minors. Combining (4.1), (5.2), and a union bound,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{\omega(A_M)\le t\}
\le C M4^M t
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

for the relevant small `t`.

Taking

\[
t_+:=e^{-(\log4+\varepsilon/2)M}
\]

gives

\[
\mathbb P\{\omega(A_M)\le t_+\}
\le C M e^{-\varepsilon M/2}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

This proves that the minimum is not exponentially smaller than the `4^{-M}` scale.

## 6. Constructive upper envelope: a correlated fixed-apex family

The nontrivial direction is to show that some minor is as ill-conditioned as the entropy scale predicts without pretending the minors are independent.

Fix one row, call it `a_0`. Let the remaining pool have `2M-2` rows. For every

\[
T\subset[2M-2],\qquad |T|=M-1,
\]

let `v_T` be a measurable choice of a unit vector spanning the one-dimensional nullspace of `A_T`. Define

\[
E_T(t):=\{|\langle a_0,v_T\rangle|\le t\},
\qquad
Z_t:=\sum_{|T|=M-1}\mathbf 1_{E_T(t)}.
\]

If `E_T(t)` occurs, then for the square minor `S=T\cup\{0\}`,

\[
\sigma_M(A_S)
\le\|A_Sv_T\|_2
=|\langle a_0,v_T\rangle|
\le t.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{Z_t>0\Longrightarrow\omega(A_M)\le t.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The number of candidates is

\[
L:=\binom{2M-2}{M-1}\asymp\frac{4^{M-1}}{\sqrt M}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since `a_0` is independent of `v_T` and Gaussian rotational invariance applies,

\[
p(t):=\mathbb P(E_T(t))=\mathbb P(|g|\le t),
\qquad
\mathbb EZ_t=Lp(t).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The candidates are **highly dependent**: every one contains the apex row and typical pairs share about half of their private rows. We now keep that dependence exactly.

## 7. Exact pair geometry from overlap conditioning

Fix two candidates `T,U`, and put

\[
D:=|T\setminus U|=|U\setminus T|\in\{0,1,\ldots,M-1\}.
\]

Their shared block `B=T\cap U` has `M-1-D` rows. Conditional on `B`, its kernel `W` has dimension `D+1` almost surely. Project the `D` private rows of `T` and the `D` private rows of `U` into `W`. The two projected blocks are independent standard Gaussian `D x (D+1)` matrices. Therefore their null lines are independent Haar lines in `W`.

Consequently, if

\[
\rho:=|\langle v_T,v_U\rangle|,
\]

then for `D>=1`

\[
\boxed{\rho^2\mid D\sim \operatorname{Beta}\left(\frac12,\frac D2\right).}
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is the key dependence-aware reduction. Given `v_T,v_U`, the two apex projections form a centered bivariate normal pair of unit variances and correlation of absolute value `rho`.

### 7.1 Pair bound for `D>=2`

For a bivariate standard normal with correlation `rho`, its density is bounded by

\[
\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
\]

Thus, for `0<t<=1`,

\[
\mathbb P(E_T(t)\cap E_U(t)\mid\rho)
\le \frac{2t^2}{\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Also

\[
p(t)=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\int_0^t e^{-x^2/2}\,dx
\ge \sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,t e^{-t^2/2}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Averaging (7.2) using (7.1), for `D=d>=2`,

\[
\frac{q_d(t)}{p(t)^2}
\le e^{t^2}K_d,
\tag{7.4}
\]

where

\[
K_d:=\mathbb E(1-\rho^2)^{-1/2}
=
\frac{B(1/2,(d-1)/2)}{B(1/2,d/2)}
=
\frac{\Gamma((d-1)/2)\Gamma((d+1)/2)}{\Gamma(d/2)^2}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The standard gamma-ratio estimate (with finitely many small `d` absorbed into the constant) gives

\[
\boxed{K_d\le1+\frac{C}{d},\qquad d\ge2.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

### 7.2 The singular `D=1` edge

When `D=1`, the two null lines are independent Haar lines in a two-dimensional plane. Writing their angle as `theta`, the density singularity is `1/|sin theta|`. Splitting the angle integral at `|sin theta|\asymp t` gives the elementary bound

\[
\boxed{q_1(t)\le C t^2\log(e/t),}
\tag{7.7}
\]

and hence, using (7.3),

\[
\frac{q_1(t)}{p(t)^2}\le C\log(e/t).
\tag{7.8}
\]

This logarithm is harmless because pairs with `D=1` are exponentially rare in the fixed-apex family.

## 8. Combinatorial averaging: typical pairs are far apart

For a fixed `T`, the number of `U` with replacement distance `D=d` is

\[
N_d=\binom{M-1}{d}^2.
\]

Therefore the overlap weights are

\[
w_d:=\frac{\binom{M-1}{d}^2}{\binom{2M-2}{M-1}},
\qquad
\sum_d w_d=1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Equivalently, `M-1-D` is hypergeometric with mean `(M-1)/2`. A standard sampling-without-replacement Hoeffding bound gives

\[
\mathbb P\{D<(M-1)/4\}\le e^{-cM}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d\ge2}\frac{w_d}{d}\le\frac{C}{M}+e^{-cM}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Moreover

\[
w_1
=\frac{(M-1)^2}{\binom{2M-2}{M-1}}
\le C M^{5/2}4^{-M}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Thus the dangerous two-dimensional overlap stratum is exponentially negligible even though its local correlation kernel has a logarithmic singularity.

## 9. Second moment and the `O(1/M)` failure probability

From (6.3),

\[
\frac{\mathbb EZ_t^2}{(\mathbb EZ_t)^2}
=
\frac1{Lp(t)}
+\sum_{d\ge1}w_d\frac{q_d(t)}{p(t)^2}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Using (7.4)--(7.8) and (8.3)--(8.4),

\[
\frac{\mathbb EZ_t^2}{(\mathbb EZ_t)^2}
\le
1+\frac{C}{M}+Ce^{-cM}
+C M^{5/2}4^{-M}\log(e/t)
+Ct^2
+\frac1{Lp(t)}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Paley--Zygmund in the form

\[
\mathbb P(Z_t=0)
\le
\frac{\operatorname{Var}Z_t}{(\mathbb EZ_t)^2}
\]

therefore yields the same right-hand excess.

Take

\[
t_-:=e^{-(\log4-\varepsilon)M}=4^{-M}e^{\varepsilon M}.
\]

For large `M`, `t_-<=1`, and (7.3) plus (6.2) gives

\[
Lp(t_-)
\ge c\frac{e^{\varepsilon M}}{\sqrt M}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Also `log(e/t_-)=O_\varepsilon(M)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{\omega(A_M)>t_-\}
\le
\frac{C_\varepsilon}{M}
+C_\varepsilon\sqrt M e^{-\varepsilon M}
+C_\varepsilon M^{7/2}4^{-M}
+e^{-c_\varepsilon M}.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

This is the promised explicit constructive upper envelope. It is obtained from a family of heavily overlapping minors, not from an independence surrogate.

## 10. Passing to the source normalization by the maximum row norm

For `R_M=max_i||a_i||_2`, standard chi-square tails imply, for each fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
\mathbb P\{R_M>e^{\varepsilon M/2}\}
\le e^{-e^{c_\varepsilon M}}
\tag{10.1}
\]

for large `M`. Also

\[
\mathbb P\{R_M<1\}
\le \mathbb P\{\|a_1\|_2<1\}
\le e^{-cM\log M}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

On the event

\[
\omega(A_M)\ge e^{-(\log4+\varepsilon/2)M},
\qquad
R_M\le e^{\varepsilon M/2},
\]

we have

\[
\frac{\omega(A_M)}{R_M}\ge e^{-(\log4+\varepsilon)M},
\]

which gives (3.1) by (5.4) and (10.1).

On the event

\[
\omega(A_M)\le e^{-(\log4-\varepsilon)M},
\qquad R_M\ge1,
\]

we have

\[
\frac{\omega(A_M)}{R_M}\le e^{-(\log4-\varepsilon)M},
\]

which gives (3.2) by (9.4) and (10.2). The theorem follows.

## 12. Failed/less useful attacks this run

Three routes were considered before settling on the fixed-apex second moment.

1. **Nearly independent disjoint minors.** Truly disjoint `M`-row minors are too few at `2M-1` total rows; forcing near-disjointness destroys the entropy `exp((log4)M)` needed for the `4^{-M}` scale.
2. **Constant-overlap code family.** A constant-weight code can reduce pair dependence, but any fixed positive separation constraint sacrifices an exponential fraction of the full binomial entropy unless tuned very delicately. The fixed-apex family keeps essentially the full entropy and makes the dependence analytically tractable instead.
3. **Treat all minors as independent.** Rejected outright. Typical candidates share order `M` rows, and adjacent candidates have a singular two-dimensional correlation kernel. The proof above explicitly stratifies by `D` and pays the `D=1` logarithm with its exponentially small combinatorial weight.
