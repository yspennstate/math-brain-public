\[
\omega(A_m)=\min_{S:\operatorname{rank}(A_{S^c})<m}\sigma_m(A_S).

\[
\omega(A_m)=\min_{|B|=m}s_{\min}(A_B).
\tag{1.1}

L_m=\binom{2m-1}{m},\qquad

W_m(\lambda)=\sum_{|B|=m}{\bf1}_{\{s_{\min}(A_B)\le s_m(\lambda)\}}.

## 3. New growing-`q` enlarged-Gram theorem

Let `X` be `n x n` standard Gaussian, `S=XX^T`, and
\[
H_n(X)=\sqrt{\det S}\;\mathbb E_h\sqrt{1+h^TS^{-1}h}.
\]
As proved previously,
\[
H_n(X)=\mathbb E_h V_n(X,h),
\qquad
V_n(X,h)=\sqrt{\det([X\ h][X\ h]^T)},
\tag{3.1}
\]
and Bartlett decomposition gives
\[
V_n\stackrel d=\prod_{j=2}^{n+1}\chi_j.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Lemma P08-ONEHALF-LQ-GROWING
There is a universal constant `C` such that for every `n>=2` and every real `q>=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\mathbb E V_n^q}{(\mathbb EV_n)^q}
\le Cq^2\log(en),
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
and consequently
\[
\boxed{
\left\|\frac{H_n}{\mathbb EH_n}\right\|_q
\le \left\|\frac{V_n}{\mathbb EV_n}\right\|_q
\le (en)^{Cq}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof
For a single chi factor define
\[
h_j(t)=\log\mathbb E\chi_j^t
=\frac t2\log2+\log\Gamma\left(\frac{j+t}{2}\right)-\log\Gamma\left(\frac j2\right).
\]
Then
\[
h_j''(t)=\frac14\psi_1\left(\frac{j+t}{2}\right).
\]
For `j>=2,t>=0`, the standard trigamma estimate `psi_1(x)<=x^{-1}+x^{-2}` gives
\[
0\le h_j''(t)\le \frac Cj.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Writing Taylor's formula with integral remainder at zero,
\[
h_j(q)-qh_j(1)
=\int_0^q(q-s)h_j''(s)\,ds
-q\int_0^1(1-s)h_j''(s)\,ds
\le \frac{Cq^2}{j}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Summing (3.6) over `j=2,...,n+1` and using (3.2) proves (3.3). Jensen in the auxiliary `h` variable gives `E H_n^q<=E V_n^q`, while `E H_n=E V_n`; taking the `q`th root gives (3.4). QED.

This is deliberately coarse. For fixed `q` the earlier sharp cost is only `n^{(q-1)/4}`. The point of (3.4) is that it stays explicit when `q` grows and its logarithm is only `O(q log n)`.

## 4. Uniform endpoint envelope is stretched-exponentially negligible

Set
\[
H_r=C_0^r
\tag{4.1}
\]
with `C_0` large enough to dominate the support, charge and normal-dimension complexities in the inherited saturated endpoint construction.

A logarithmic-order endpoint chart contains at most `H_r` primitive residual determinant/slope blocks after the finite support decorations are unfolded. The previously dangerous inverse-angular factors cost
\[
\exp(O(H_r^2/m))
\tag{4.2}
\]
by the oriented-beta theorem. Every zero-fresh `e=0,h=1` block is first enlarged as in (3.1). If all such blocks are estimated simultaneously by generalized Holder, one may use moment order at most `rH_r`; (3.4) then gives the deliberately wasteful bound
\[
\exp\{C rH_r^2\log m\}
\tag{4.3}
\]
for all enlarged-Gram factors combined. High-excess fresh factors cost `C^r`; low-excess determinant-tilted factors cost at most `(Cr)^{Cr}` under the inherited split; the nested chart count is at most `(Cr)^r`; and the Mobius expansion itself contributes at most `2^r` terms.

Therefore the complete normalized analytic cost of an endpoint chart can be bounded by
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{m,r}
\le
\exp\left\{C rH_r^2\log m+C r\log r+O(H_r^2/m)\right\}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
No optimization is intended.

For `r<=K_m+1`,
\[
H_r^2=C_0^{2r}=m^{o(1)},
\qquad
\log\mathcal A_{m,r}=m^{o(1)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
On the other hand, the first irregular Venn split pays, from (2.7),
\[
\exp\left(-\frac{cm}{2^rr^2}\right)
=\exp(-m^{1-o(1)}).
\tag{4.6}
\]
The same conclusion holds in the close/fixed-small-distance branches, where the inherited hypergeometric counting gives at least an exponential-in-`m` deficit before the subexponential analytic factor is paid.

Combining (4.4)--(4.6), and summing the `(Cr)^r` charts and `2^r` centered Mobius terms, gives the new uniform endpoint estimate
\[
\boxed{
\left|\overline K_r^{\rm endpoint}(\lambda)\right|
\le \exp(-m^{1-o(1)})
}
\tag{4.7}
\]
uniformly for
\[
3\le r\le K_m+1.
\]

**Scope note.** Equation (4.7) uses the inherited endpoint/private-hard-edge decomposition as a proved internal input; this run supplies the missing growing-moment control and the uniform entropy-versus-analytic-growth comparison. It is not a fresh independent audit of every historical chart identity.

## 5. The transverse centered interaction needs only `exp(Cr^2)`, not one-factorial growth

On `\mathcal T_r`, (2.8) and (2.5), with `q=r`, give
\[
\|X_i\|_r
\le \frac{C}{m}\left(\sqrt r\,2^r+2^{5r/2}\right)
\le \frac{C2^{3r}}m
\tag{5.1}
\]
through `r<=K_m+1`; the inherited clean-sector transfer errors are exponentially smaller than the displayed bound at the hard-edge threshold.

Holder therefore gives
\[
\left|K_{[r]}\right|
\le\prod_{i=1}^r\|X_i\|_r
\le
\left(\frac{C2^{3r}}m\right)^r.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Averaging and adding the endpoint estimate (4.7) yields the main new theorem.

### Theorem P08-CENTERED-LOGK
Uniformly for fixed bounded `lambda>0` and
\[
3\le r\le K_m+1,
\]
\[
\boxed{
|\overline K_r(\lambda)|
\le
m^{-r}\exp(Cr^2)
+\exp(-m^{1-o(1)}).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The previous report targeted the stronger-looking envelope
\[
m^{-r+o(1)}(Cr)^{Cr}.
\]
That strength is unnecessary. Because `r=o(log m)`, the quadratic exponential in (5.3) is still negligible relative to the factor `m^{-r}` and, crucially, is summable over interaction order.

Indeed put
\[
T_r(k)=\binom kr m^{-r}e^{Cr^2}.
\]
For `3<=r<k<=K_m+1`,
\[
\frac{T_{r+1}(k)}{T_r(k)}
\le \frac{k}{r+1}\frac{\exp(C(2r+1))}{m}
=m^{-1+o(1)}
\tag{5.4}
\]
uniformly in the stated range. Hence the `r=3` term dominates and
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=3}^k\binom kr|\overline K_r(\lambda)|
=O_\lambda\left(\frac{k^3}{m^3}\right)
+\exp(-m^{1-o(1)}).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
This is the exact summability statement needed for the second-order law.

## 6. Uniform factorial moments through Bonferroni order

Average the exact centered decomposition (2.4) over a uniform ordered distinct `k`-tuple. Using (2.2) for every pair and (5.5) for all higher interactions,
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm distinct}R_k
=
1+\binom{k}{2}\left(\frac1{m^2}+O_\lambda(m^{-3})\right)
+O_\lambda\left(\frac{k^3}{m^3}\right)
+e^{-m^{1-o(1)}}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Thus uniformly for `k<=K_m+1`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\rm distinct}R_k
=1+\binom{k}{2}\frac1{m^2}
+O_\lambda\left(\frac{k^3}{m^3}\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since `(L_m)_kp_m^k=lambda^k(1+e^{-Omega(m)})` uniformly for logarithmic `k`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(W_m(\lambda))_k
=\lambda^k\left[
1+\binom{k}{2}\frac1{m^2}
+O_\lambda\left(\frac{k^3}{m^3}\right)
\right],
\qquad k\le K_m+1.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This replaces the old `P08-ROUTED-CCI-LOGK` target by a direct centered-interaction proof. In particular, a separate historical `m^{-3}` route seed is no longer needed: the third and higher orders carry their own `m^{-r}` centered suppression on the regular sector, while the endpoint sector is stretched-exponentially negligible after the now-explicit analytic-growth bound.

## 7. Candidate second-order survival expansion

Because `K_m` is even, Bonferroni gives upper and lower bounds for `Pr(W_m=0)` using the partial sums through `K_m` and `K_m+1`. From Stirling,
\[
\frac{\lambda^{K_m+1}}{(K_m+1)!}
\le\left(\frac{e\lambda}{K_m+1}\right)^{K_m+1}
=m^{-4+o(1)}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Insert (6.3) into the two Bonferroni sums. The main series is
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-\lambda)^k}{k!}=e^{-\lambda}.
\tag{7.2}
\]
The pair correction is exact:
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-1)^k\lambda^k}{k!}\binom{k}{2}
=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}e^{-\lambda}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
The remainder is summable because
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{\lambda^k k^3}{k!}<\infty.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Consequently, under the inherited endpoint/private-hard-edge package used in Section 4,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{L_ma_m\omega(A_m)>\lambda\}
=e^{-\lambda}\left(1+\frac{\lambda^2}{2m^2}\right)
+O_\lambda(m^{-3}).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{L_ma_m\omega(A_m)\le\lambda\}
=1-e^{-\lambda}-\frac{\lambda^2e^{-\lambda}}{2m^2}
+O_\lambda(m^{-3}).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
The coefficient is positive in the survival function because the pair channel is overdispersed by `+m^{-2}`.

This is substantially stronger than the source-level exponential law and stronger than merely proving `L_ma_m omega(A_m)=>Exp(1)`: it identifies the first finite-size correction in the Gaussian hard-edge survival probability.
