## 3. A deterministic separated-tail lemma

### Lemma P08-SEPARATED-TAILS

Let `Z` be a real random variable with finite second moment. Suppose

\[
\Pr(Z\ge a)\ge p,
\qquad
\Pr(Z\le b)\ge q,
\qquad a>b.
\]

Write `g=a-b`. Then:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(Z)\ge pq\,g^2.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\mathbb E|Z-c|
\ge \min(p,q)\,g.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Finally every interval `I` with diameter `<g` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(Z\in I)\le1-\min(p,q).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

Let `Z'` be an independent copy of `Z`. Since

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z)=\frac12\mathbb E(Z-Z')^2,
\]

the two cross-events

\[
\{Z\ge a,Z'\le b\},\qquad
\{Z'\ge a,Z\le b\}
\]

have total probability at least `2pq`, and on either event
`|Z-Z'|>=g`. Hence (3.1).

For (3.2), if `c in [b,a]`, then

\[
\mathbb E|Z-c|
\ge p(a-c)+q(c-b)
\ge\min(p,q)(a-b).
\]

If `c<b`, the event `{Z>=a}` alone gives `E|Z-c|>=p g`; if `c>a`, the event `{Z<=b}` gives `E|Z-c|>=q g`.

For (3.3), an interval of diameter smaller than `g` cannot meet both half-lines `[a,infinity)` and `(-infinity,b]`. It must therefore omit at least one of two events with masses `p` and `q`. QED.

The lemma is elementary, but applied to the nontrivial dependent-minor CDF bracket it produces a source-facing statement that is not available from exponential-rate asymptotics.

## 4. New theorem: the exact hard-edge factor cannot concentrate

Put

\[
Z_m=-\log Y_m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

There are two convenient choices of thresholds.

### 4.1 Balanced quarter-mass tails

For `0<=lambda<=1`, (2.4) is exactly

\[
D(\lambda)=\lambda-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}.
\]

Choose

\[
\lambda_-:=1-\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\lambda_+:=\frac34.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
D(\lambda_-)=\frac14,
\tag{4.3}
\]

so by (2.3)

\[
\Pr\{Z_m\ge -\log\lambda_-\}
=\Pr\{Y_m\le\lambda_-\}
\ge\frac14-o(1).
\tag{4.4}
\]

By (2.5),

\[
\Pr\{Z_m\le-\log\lambda_+\}
\ge1-F_m(\lambda_+)
\ge\frac14.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The deterministic gap between these two tails is

\[
\boxed{
 g_*:=\log\frac{\lambda_+}{\lambda_-}
 =\log\left(\frac{3(2+\sqrt2)}4\right)
 \approx0.9402651048.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Applying Lemma P08-SEPARATED-TAILS gives

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{m\to\infty}
\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\mathbb E|Z_m-c|
\ge \frac{g_*}{4}
\approx0.2350662762.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

It also gives the concentration-function bound: for every fixed `r<g_*/2`,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{m\to\infty}
\sup_{c\in\mathbb R}
\Pr\{|Z_m-c|\le r\}
\le\frac34.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus no deterministic recentering can make the exact hard-edge logarithm converge to a point.

### 4.2 A stronger variance constant

For variance a different simple pair gives a larger explicit constant. Choose

\[
\lambda_1=\frac1{16},\qquad \lambda_2=\frac12.
\]

Then

\[
D(1/16)=\frac{31}{512},
\]

hence

\[
\Pr\{Z_m\ge\log16\}\ge\frac{31}{512}-o(1),
\qquad
\Pr\{Z_m\le\log2\}\ge\frac12.
\]

The gap is `log 8`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}(Z_m)
\ge
c_{\rm var}:=
\frac{31}{1024}(\log8)^2
\approx0.1309046786.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This is an unconditional quantitative nondegeneracy theorem for the exact scale `L_m a_m omega(A_m)`. It uses only the proven first-two-factorial-moment bracket and does not invoke the candidate Poisson limit.

## 5. New source-facing theorem: the 1/m fluctuation scale is optimal

Recall from (2.8)--(2.9) that

\[
T_m=m(X_m-c_m)=Z_m+\eta_m,
\qquad
\eta_m\to0\quad\text{in }L^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem P08-OPTIMAL-ONE-OVER-M-FLUCTUATIONS

The source-normalized logarithmic stability exponent has genuinely nonvanishing fluctuations on the `1/m` scale:

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 m^2\operatorname{Var}(X_m)
\ge
\frac{31}{1024}(\log8)^2
>0.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Together with the previous upper bound `Var(X_m)=O(m^-2)`, this yields

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(X_m)=\Theta(m^{-2}).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In addition, deterministic approximation cannot improve the `1/m` scale even in `L^1`:

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 m\inf_{b\in\mathbb R}\mathbb E|X_m-b|
\ge \frac{g_*}{4}>0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since the previous `L^2` upper bound gives `E|X_m-c_m|=O(1/m)`, we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{b\in\mathbb R}\mathbb E|X_m-b|=\Theta(m^{-1}).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Finally, the scaled exponent is uniformly anti-concentrated. For example,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{m\to\infty}
\sup_{u\in\mathbb R}
\Pr\left\{
\left|m(X_m-c_m)-u\right|\le\frac{g_*}{4}
\right\}
\le\frac34.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

### Proof

For variance, the standard deviation triangle inequality gives

\[
\left|\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(T_m)}
-\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Z_m)}\right|
\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\eta_m)}\to0.
\]

Combining with (4.9), and using `Var(T_m)=m^2 Var(X_m)`, proves (5.2). The previous report supplies the matching upper bound, proving (5.3).

For any deterministic `b`, set

\[
u=m(b-c_m).
\]

Then by (5.1),

\[
m\mathbb E|X_m-b|
=\mathbb E|Z_m+\eta_m-u|
\ge\mathbb E|Z_m-u|-\mathbb E|\eta_m|.
\]

Take the infimum over `u`, use (4.7), and note that `E|eta_m|->0` by `L^2` convergence. This proves (5.4). The upper bound in (5.5) follows by taking `b=c_m` and using the inherited uniform `L^2` bound.

For (5.6), choose the perturbation cutoff `g_*/8`. If `|eta_m|<=g_*/8` and

\[
|T_m-u|\le g_*/4,
\]

then

\[
|Z_m-u|\le3g_*/8.
\]

The latter interval has diameter `3g_*/4<g_*`, so by (4.4)--(4.5) and Lemma P08-SEPARATED-TAILS its probability is at most `3/4+o(1)`. Since `eta_m->0` in probability, (5.6) follows. QED.
