# 3. New theorem: the equal-ray Gibbs family reaches the `n^{-1/2}` moment scale in aggregate

### `P06-P07-GIBBS-CORRECT-SCALE-1 = PROVED PARTIAL / PRIME-n / PUBLICATION-NOVELTY-UNVERIFIED`

Fix an odd prime `n` and put

\[
H=\{1,\ldots,(n-1)/2\}.
\]

Let `(xi_a)_{a in H}` be independent Bernoulli-`1/2` variables and let

\[
D=\{a\in H:\xi_a=1\}.
\]

For `j in Z_n`, define

\[
c_a(j)=\cos(2\pi a j/n),\qquad
S_D(j)=\sum_{a\in D}c_a(j).
\]

For each fixed constant `c>=0`, define the random normalized partition function

\[
Z_D(c)=\frac1n\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_n}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{c}{\sqrt n}S_D(j)\right),
\]

and the associated Gibbs probability

\[
p_{D,c}(j)=\frac{\exp(-cS_D(j)/\sqrt n)}{n Z_D(c)}.
\]

Then, as `n -> infinity` through odd primes:

1. **Annealed partition asymptotic**
   \[
   \mathbf E_D Z_D(c)=\exp\!\left(\frac{c^2}{32}+O_c(n^{-1/2})\right).
   \]

2. **Self-averaging at the correct scale**
   \[
   \operatorname{Var}_D Z_D(c)=O_c(n^{-1}),
   \]
   and hence
   \[
   Z_D(c)=e^{c^2/32}+o_{\mathbf P}(1).
   \]

3. **Aggregate selected-frequency moment**
   \[
   \frac1{\sqrt n}\,\mathbf E_{p_{D,c}}S_D
   \xrightarrow{\mathbf P}-\frac c{16}.
   \]

4. Since `|D|/n -> 1/4` in probability, if
   \[
   \mu_a(c)=\mathbf E_{p_{D,c}}c_a(J),
   \]
   then
   \[
   \frac1{|D|}\sum_{a\in D}\mu_a(c)
   =-\frac{c/4+o_{\mathbf P}(1)}{\sqrt n}.
   \]

In particular, at **`c=4`**, the Gibbs family has the exact source-relevant **average** moment scale

\[
\frac1{|D|}\sum_{a\in D}\mu_a(4)
= -\frac{1+o_{\mathbf P}(1)}{\sqrt n}.
\]

This is not yet a feasible moment-polytope certificate because the individual moments need not be equal. It does prove that the `n^{-1/2}` radius is not blocked by the huge value `S_D(0)=|D|`: the Gibbs tilt suppresses that single singular point while the partition function remains order one and self-averaging.

## 4. Proof of the new theorem

Write `t=c/sqrt(n)` and `m=(n-1)/2`.

### 4.1 Exact Fourier sums for prime `n`

For every `j != 0 mod n`, multiplication by `j` permutes nonzero residues, and pairing `a` with `-a` gives

\[
\sum_{a\in H}c_a(j)=-\frac12.
\]

Also, using `cos^2 x=(1+cos 2x)/2` and the same permutation identity,

\[
\sum_{a\in H}c_a(j)^2=\frac{n-2}{4}.
\]

If `j,k !=0` and `k != +/- j`, then

\[
\sum_{a\in H}c_a(j)c_a(k)
=\frac12\sum_{a\in H}\{c_a(j-k)+c_a(j+k)\}
=-\frac12.
\]

These identities are the only place where primality is used.

### 4.2 First moment of `Z_D(c)`

For fixed `j !=0`, independence of the mask bits gives

\[
\mathbf E_D e^{-tS_D(j)}
=\prod_{a\in H}\frac{1+e^{-t c_a(j)}}2
=\exp\left[-\frac t2\sum_a c_a(j)
+\sum_a\log\cosh\frac{t c_a(j)}2\right].
\]

For fixed `c`, `|t c_a(j)|=O(n^{-1/2})`, so uniformly

\[
\log\cosh u=\frac{u^2}{2}+O(u^4).
\]

Since `sum_a c_a(j)^4=O(n)`, the total fourth-order remainder is `O_c(1/n)`. Therefore

\[
\log \mathbf E_D e^{-tS_D(j)}
=\frac t4+\frac{t^2}{8}\frac{n-2}{4}+O_c(n^{-1})
=\frac{c^2}{32}+O_c(n^{-1/2}).
\]

At `j=0`, `S_D(0)=|D|` and

\[
\mathbf E_D e^{-tS_D(0)}
=\left(\frac{1+e^{-t}}2\right)^m
=\exp\{-c\sqrt n/4+O_c(1)\},
\]

so the origin contributes exponentially less than the `n-1` nonzero points. Averaging over `j` proves item 1.

### 4.3 Second moment and self-averaging

For a generic pair `j,k !=0` with `k != +/-j`, put

\[
M_{jk}=\mathbf E_D e^{-t(S_D(j)+S_D(k))}.
\]

Using the identities above,

\[
\sum_a(c_a(j)+c_a(k))=-1,
\]

and

\[
\sum_a(c_a(j)+c_a(k))^2
=2\frac{n-2}{4}+2\left(-\frac12\right)
=\frac{n-4}{2}.
\]

The same `log cosh` expansion now gives

\[
\log M_{jk}=\frac t2+\frac{t^2(n-4)}{16}+O_c(n^{-1}).
\]

On the other hand, if `M_j=E exp(-tS_D(j))`, then

\[
2\log M_j=\frac t2+\frac{t^2(n-2)}{16}+O_c(n^{-1}).
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{M_{jk}}{M_jM_k}=1+O_c(n^{-1})
\]

for all generic pairs.

There are only `O(n)` nongeneric pairs (`j=k`, `j=-k`, or one index equal to zero). For `j=k !=0`, the same calculation with `2t` shows `M_{jj}=O_c(1)`. If one index is zero, then every summand `1+c_a(k)` in `S_D(0)+S_D(k)` is nonnegative, so for `c>=0`,

\[
e^{-t(S_D(0)+S_D(k))}\le1,
\]

and these pairs are also uniformly harmless. Thus the `O(n)` exceptional pairs contribute only `O_c(1/n)` after division by `n^2`, while the `n^2-O(n)` generic pairs factor up to relative `O_c(1/n)`. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Var} Z_D(c)=O_c(n^{-1}).
\]

Chebyshev proves item 2.

### 4.4 Differentiate by convexity, not by an unjustified interchange of random limits

Let

\[
F_D(c)=\log Z_D(c).
\]

For every fixed realization `D`, `F_D` is convex in `c`, being a log-sum-exp of affine functions. Item 2 implies, for every fixed real `u>=0`,

\[
F_D(u)\xrightarrow{\mathbf P}f(u):=u^2/32.
\]

Fix `c>0` and `0<eta<c`. Convexity gives the deterministic secant bounds

\[
\frac{F_D(c)-F_D(c-eta)}{eta}
\le F_D'(c)
\le\frac{F_D(c+eta)-F_D(c)}{eta}.
\]

For the three fixed points `c-eta,c,c+eta`, item 2 and a union bound make the right and left secants converge in probability to those of `f`. First let `n -> infinity`, then `eta ->0`. Since `f'(c)=c/16`,

\[
F_D'(c)\xrightarrow{\mathbf P}\frac c{16}.
\]

But direct differentiation at fixed `D` gives

\[
F_D'(c)
=-\frac1{\sqrt n}\mathbf E_{p_{D,c}}S_D.
\]

This proves item 3. Finally, `|D|` is binomial with mean `(n-1)/4`, so `|D|/n ->1/4` in probability; dividing item 3 by `|D|` proves item 4.

## 6. Exact probabilistic estimate that would turn this into a sharp certificate

Let

\[
\Phi_D(j)=(c_a(j))_{a\in D},\qquad
\mu_D(\lambda)=\mathbf E_{p_\lambda}\Phi_D,
\]

where

\[
p_\lambda(j)\propto \exp\langle\lambda,\Phi_D(j)\rangle.
\]

At the equal-ray point

\[
\lambda_0=-\frac4{\sqrt n}{\bf 1}_D,
\]

the theorem proves only the scalar identity

\[
\frac1{|D|}{\bf 1}^T\mu_D(\lambda_0)
=-\frac{1+o_P(1)}{\sqrt n}.
\]

A sufficient next theorem is a **samplewise transversality-plus-anisotropy estimate**: with probability `1-q_n`,

1. the residual to the equal target is controllable,
   \[
   r_D:=\mu_D(\lambda_0)+n^{-1/2}{\bf 1}_D,
   \]
   and lies inside a radius on which Newton/convex correction can operate; and
2. throughout the corresponding correction tube,
   \[
   \lambda_{\min}\operatorname{Cov}_{p_\lambda}(\Phi_D)\ge\kappa
   \]
   for an absolute `kappa>0`.

For example, if a radius `rho>0` can be fixed so that

\[
\|r_D\|_2\le \kappa\rho/2
\]

and the covariance lower bound holds for every `\|\lambda-\lambda_0\|_2\le rho`, strong convexity of the log-partition dual gives a `\lambda_*` in that ball with

\[
\mu_D(\lambda_*)=-n^{-1/2}{\bf 1}_D.
\]

That is an exact positive moment-polytope certificate at `delta_D>=1/sqrt(n)`.

To convert a high-probability sharp certificate into the **expectation** target without silently swapping quantifiers, it is enough to have

\[
q_n=o((\log n)^{-1/2}).
\]

Indeed the primary random-circulant paper gives the fallback high-probability bound `theta<=1+4 sqrt(n log n)` with failure at most `2/n`, while deterministically `theta<=n`. Therefore

\[
\mathbf E\theta
\le(1+o(1))\sqrt n
+O(q_n\sqrt{n\log n})
+O(1),
\]

and the middle term is `o(sqrt(n))` under the displayed failure estimate. Together with the standard lower bound, this yields the exact expectation asymptotic.

This is the precise probability-strength requirement; a finite SDP fit or merely `q_n=o(1)` is not automatically sufficient for the expectation statement.
