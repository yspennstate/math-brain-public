# 1. Iteration-generated Walsh words

Let the independent mask variables be the quotient Rademachers

\[
\epsilon_{[a]}\in\{-1,+1\},\qquad [a]=\{a,-a\},
\]

on odd `Z_n`, with the zero frequency excluded. The finite-model coefficient arrays generated by the positive square iteration are obtained from the following operations:

* cyclic convolution,
* multiplication by a selected-mask indicator,
* selected or global centering,
* scalar contraction,
* projection onto finitely many previously generated directions, and
* finite linear combinations.

Call a coefficient/moment expression an **iteration word of complexity at most `R`** if, after expanding centerings and projections, every summand contains at most `R` mask Rademachers and at most `R` cyclic additive constraints, with all constraint-matrix entries in `{-1,0,1}`.

For the square-Newton iteration, the words required through step `j` have complexity at most

\[
R_j\le a_0j+b_0
\tag{1.1}
\]

for absolute bookkeeping constants `a_0,b_0`; the conservative previous budget `8j+16` is more than sufficient.

The point of this section is to replace the old unspecified `O_R(1)` by an explicit degree dependence.

# 2. Explicit diagram count

## Lemma 2.1 — parity partitions

Consider the second moment of one normalized iteration word of complexity `R`. After fully expanding its two copies, a mask expectation survives only if every quotient label `[a]` appears with even total multiplicity.

The number of possible parity-identification patterns of the at most `2R` mask occurrences is at most

\[
(2R)^{2R}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For each such identification pattern, choosing the signs in quotient identities `a=+/-b` contributes at most

\[
2^{2R}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

After the identifications, all remaining cyclic constraints form an integer matrix of rank at most `2R`, with row Euclidean norm at most `\sqrt{2R}`. Hence every nonzero minor is bounded by Hadamard:

\[
|\det M|\le (2R)^R.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For odd composite `n`, a rank-`r` system modulo `n` can have an additional kernel multiplicity bounded by the gcd of `n` with an appropriate nonzero minor. Therefore the resonance multiplicity is at most

\[
\Delta_R:=(2R)^R.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Combining (2.1)--(2.4), there is an absolute `C_0` such that the total combinatorial/resonance factor is bounded by

\[
M_R:=\exp\{C_0 R\log(2R)\}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

This is deliberately coarse but explicit.

### Proof

The parity statement is exactly Walsh orthogonality. A partition of at most `2R` labeled occurrences can be encoded by assigning to each occurrence one of at most `2R` block labels, giving the crude bound `(2R)^{2R}`. Quotient signs give (2.2). Once a sign pattern is fixed, all equalities and cyclic convolution constraints are linear with coefficients in `{-1,0,1}`; Hadamard gives (2.3). Smith normal form over `Z/nZ` then bounds any extra solution multiplicity by the product of gcds of invariant factors with `n`, and hence by a nonzero rank minor; (2.4) is sufficient. Multiplying the bounds gives (2.5). ∎

# 3. Connected variance loses one free cyclic parameter

The fixed-degree reports repeatedly used the fact that disconnected two-copy diagrams cancel in a variance. Here the same statement is quantified.

## Lemma 3.1 — normalized connected variance

Let `W_R` be any normalized scalar energy/moment in the canonical iteration list of complexity at most `R`, normalized so that a generic disconnected second-moment diagram is order one. Then

\[
|\mathbf EW_R|\le M_R
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(W_R)
\le
{M_R\over n}\,n^{o_R(1)},
\tag{3.2}
\]

where the final factor can be taken equal to `1` for fixed `R`, and for growing `R` it is absorbed into a larger constant in (2.5) as long as

\[
R\log(2R)=o(\log n).
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

After subtracting `(E W_R)^2`, every surviving two-copy diagram has at least one mask identification or additive relation connecting the two copies. Relative to the disconnected dimension count, this removes at least one free cyclic parameter. Hence each connected pattern gains a factor `n^{-1}`. The only possible loss for odd composite `n` is the bounded-minor resonance multiplicity from Lemma 2.1. Summing over at most `M_R` patterns yields (3.2). Under (3.3), `Delta_R=exp(O(R log R))=n^{o(1)}`, so the same form persists for growing `R`. ∎

The same argument applies to every fixed scalar component of a vector word, and Parseval/Bessel converts coefficient-array `l2` energies to scalar time-domain moments exactly as in the quadratic and cubic reports.

# 4. Growing-degree simultaneous envelope

The number of canonical words actually generated by the iteration through complexity `R` is itself at most

L_R\le \exp\{C_1R\log(2R)\}

Let `R=R(n)` satisfy

\[
R\log(2R)=o(\log n).

Then, simultaneously for the entire canonical iteration-word list of complexity at most `R`, all normalized scalar moments and squared coefficient energies are at most

In particular, for every fixed `c>0`,

\tag{4.6}

### Proof

For each word, use Lemma 3.1 and Chebyshev at threshold `T_R`, with `C_2` larger than the constants in (3.1)--(3.2). The per-word failure is at most

n^{-1}\exp\{-cR\log(2R)\}\,n^{o(1)}.

Union over at most `L_R<=exp(C_1R log(2R))` canonical words and enlarge `C_2` once more. Under (4.2), all determinant/resonance factors remain `n^{o(1)}`. This proves (4.4). Equation (4.6) follows because `log log n * log log log n=o(log n)`. ∎

The earlier theorem was only: **for every fixed `R`, constants exist**. The new theorem allows `R` to grow as `O(log log n)` while preserving failure much smaller than the source-transfer requirement. This is exactly the depth scale needed by a superlinear tail.

# 5. What the raw `exp(O(R log R))` bound does *not* prove

This section is important because it prevents a false closure.

In the square-root escape lemma, one has

\[
\rho=1-\nu^2,
\qquad
\rho_+\asymp\sqrt\rho,
\tag{5.1}
\]

provided the current normalized skew/longitudinal errors satisfy

\[
\Gamma^2\le1+C\rho,
\qquad
|\kappa|\le C\rho,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and `rho` is small enough relative to `C`.

If one bounds the step-`j` stability constant only by the crude diagram envelope

\[
C_j\le \exp\{Cj\log(j+2)\},
\tag{5.3}
\]

then the direct Taylor proof certifies (5.1) only in a radius of the form

\[
\rho_j\le \exp\{-C'j\log(j+2)\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Indeed the remainder condition is of the form `C_j sqrt(rho_j)<=c_0`.

On the other hand, if the favorable recursion has a universal lower coefficient,

\[
\rho_{j+1}\ge c_0\sqrt{\rho_j},
\tag{5.5}
\]

and `x_j=log(1/rho_j)`, then

\[
x_{j+1}\le {x_j\over2}+O(1).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus after `j` steps

\[
x_j\lesssim 2^{-j}x_0+O(1).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Combining (5.4) and (5.7), the crude proof can only certify the near-boundary induction while

\[
2^{-j}x_0\gtrsim j\log j.
\tag{5.8}
\]

At the last such step the certified gap can still be as small as

\[
\rho_j\approx \exp\{-O(j\log j)\},
\tag{5.9}
\]

not an absolute source-independent constant.

**Conclusion.** The requested `M_R<=exp(CR log R)` estimate is true and useful, but by itself it does not close the uniform `epsilon->0` near-boundary bridge. A sharper invariant or a separate coarse-entry construction is genuinely required.

This is a mathematical obstruction of the present proof architecture, not evidence that the P07 statement is false.

# 6. Exact coarse superlinear decrement inequality

The positive square update itself becomes much stronger once its directional skew factor is bounded away from the boundary.

Work in a current whitened state as in the previous report:

\[
r=-\nu e,\qquad \rho=1-\nu^2,
\qquad
m_2=\mathbf E[XZ^2],\qquad
\Gamma=\|m_2\|_2.
\]

Let

\[
s=\sqrt{1-\nu^2},
\qquad
\beta={\nu\over1+s},
\qquad
A={\Gamma\beta\over2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Whenever `A<1/2`, the covariance-regularized square mixture from the earlier reports gives the exact decrement estimate

\[
\nu_+\le 2\sqrt{A(1-A)}\,\nu.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Because `beta<=nu` and `1-A<=1`, (6.2) immediately implies a new useful form:

## Lemma 6.1 — superlinear coarse tail

Whenever `A<1/2`,

\[
\boxed{
\nu_+\le \sqrt{2\Gamma}\,\nu^{3/2}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof

From (6.1),

\[
A\le {\Gamma\nu\over2}.
\]

Then

\[
2\sqrt{A(1-A)}\,\nu
\le2\sqrt A\,\nu
\le \sqrt{2\Gamma}\,\nu^{3/2}.
\]

∎

This is stronger than geometric contraction: once `nu` is small, each step raises its power from `1` to `3/2`.

# 7. Factorial diagram growth is beaten by the `3/2` power

Suppose that after entering a coarse basin the actual directional skew constants obey the crude iteration-generated bound

\[
\Gamma_j\le \exp\{C(j+1)\log(j+2)\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is the natural consequence of Theorem 4.1 for a linearly growing word degree.

Put

\[
y_j=-\log\nu_j.
\]

Lemma 6.1 gives

\[
y_{j+1}
\ge {3\over2}y_j-b_j,
\qquad
b_j:={1\over2}\log2+{C\over2}(j+1)\log(j+2).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Define the finite constant

\[
B_C:=\sum_{j=0}^\infty
\left({2\over3}\right)^{j+1}b_j<\infty.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Unrolling (7.2),

\[
y_k
\ge
\left({3\over2}\right)^k
\left[
y_0-
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
\left({2\over3}\right)^{j+1}b_j
\right].
\tag{7.4}
\]

Hence, if

\[
y_0>B_C+1,
\tag{7.5}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
y_k\ge (3/2)^k.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

To make the condition `A_j<1/2` self-consistent for all later steps, define also

\[
D_C:=
\sup_{j\ge0}
\left({2\over3}\right)^j
\left[C(j+1)\log(j+2)+\log2\right]<\infty.
\tag{7.7}
\]

If

\[
y_0>B_C+D_C+2,
\tag{7.8}
\]

then (7.4) yields

\[
y_j>C(j+1)\log(j+2)+\log2
\]

for every `j`, hence

\[
\Gamma_j\nu_j<1/2,
\]

which in turn implies `A_j<1/2`. Thus the superlinear recursion is self-sustaining.

## Proposition 7.1 — coarse tail to polynomially tiny decrement

Under (7.1) and the coarse-entry condition (7.8), after

\[
k=O(\log\log n)
\tag{7.9}
\]

further positive square steps,

\[
\boxed{\nu_k\le n^{-3}.}
\tag{7.10}
\]

Moreover the required word complexity is only

\[
R_k=O(\log\log n),
\tag{7.11}
\]

so Theorem 4.1 controls all iteration-generated coefficient words simultaneously with failure `n^{-1+o(1)}`.

### Proof

Choose `k` so that `(3/2)^k>=3 log n`; then (7.6) gives `y_k>=3 log n`. Equation (7.11) follows from the linear degree bookkeeping (1.1), and Theorem 4.1 applies. ∎

This is the first result on this line that makes an **`n`-dependent iteration depth** rigorous at the collision-counting level.

# 8. From tiny Newton decrement to the source one-sided inequalities

The source certificate does not need exact equality at a stronger target if we leave a fixed slack cushion.

Fix two depths

\[
0<d<d'<1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Construct toward the stronger target

\[
t'=-{d'\over\sqrt n}\mathbf 1_D.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Let `r=mu-t'` be the final selected-moment residual and `Sigma` its covariance. The Newton decrement is

\[
\nu^2=r^T\Sigma^{-1}r.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Because every cosine feature vector has Euclidean norm at most `sqrt(m)`, `m=|D|<=n/2`,

\[
\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)\le m\le n/2.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\|r\|_2^2\le \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)\nu^2
\le {n\over2}\nu^2.
\tag{8.5}
\]

If, for example, `nu<=n^{-3}`, then

\[
\|r\|_\infty\le\|r\|_2\le n^{-5/2}
\tag{8.6}
\]

for all large `n`. In particular,

\[
\|r\|_\infty<{d'-d\over\sqrt n}.
\tag{8.7}
\]

Hence every selected moment obeys

\[
\mu_a
\le -{d'\over\sqrt n}+{d'-d\over\sqrt n}
=-{d\over\sqrt n}.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Thus a coarse basin plus Proposition 7.1 would give a genuine one-sided source certificate after only `O(log log n)` tail steps. Exact equality is unnecessary.

The existing Fourier-primal dictionary then gives

\[
\vartheta(G_D)\le 1+{\sqrt n\over d}.
\tag{8.9}
\]

Taking `d=(1+epsilon)^{-1}` is the desired fixed-slack sharp certificate.
