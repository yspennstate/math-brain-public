## 2. Two monotone coordinates that make the waiting refinement transparent

Define

\[
\boxed{H(x):=\frac{S(x)}{A(x)},\qquad U(x):=\frac{\tau(x)}{A(x)}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

`U` is nondecreasing because `tau` is nondecreasing and `A` is nonincreasing.

The source's Lemma 5.4 proof gives a second monotonicity that is more important here. If `b` is an M-SERPT hill age and `a<=b`, then

\[
r_S(a)\le r_S(b).
\]

Corollary 5.5 is equivalently

\[
\frac{A(a)}{A(b)}\le\frac{S(a)}{S(b)},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{H(b)\le H(a).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus `H=S/A` decreases whenever the right endpoint is an M-SERPT hill age. In particular:

- `H` is nonincreasing along every M-SERPT hill interval;
- for every M-SERPT valley `(y,z]`, `H(z)<=H(y)`.

These two monotone coordinates are enough for the new charging theorem.

## 3. Exact identity on one M-SERPT chunk

### 3.1 M-SERPT hill chunk

On an M-SERPT hill interval, `y_MS(x)=x=z_MS(x)`. Therefore

\[
dQ_{MS}=\frac{f(x)\tau(x)}{A(x)^2}\,dx,
\]

while the `Xi` density is

\[
d\Xi=\frac{\lambda S(x)^2\tau(x)}{A(x)^3}\,dx.
\]

Since

\[
dH=\left(-\frac{f}{A}+\frac{\lambda S^2}{A^2}\right)dx,
\]

we get the exact identity

\[
\boxed{d\Xi-dQ_{MS}=U\,dH.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### 3.2 M-SERPT valley chunk

Let `(y,z]` be an M-SERPT valley. The exact monotone-SOAP waiting contribution of jobs in this block is

\[
Q_{MS}(y,z)
=[S(y)-S(z)]\frac{\tau(z)}{A(y)A(z)}
=[S(y)-S(z)]\frac{U(z)}{A(y)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The corresponding half-residence credit from `(1.5)` is

\[
\Xi(y,z)
=S(z)U(z)\left(\frac1{A(z)}-\frac1{A(y)}\right).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Subtracting `(3.2)` from `(3.3)` gives an exact telescoping form:

\[
\boxed{
\Xi(y,z)-Q_{MS}(y,z)
=U(z)[H(z)-H(y)].
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Because `H(z)-H(y)<=0`, this quantity need not be positive. The key is that its sign and magnitude are now controlled by the same increasing weight `U` on every M-SERPT chunk.

## 4. New theorem: a Gittins valley is paid by the M-SERPT half-credit

Fix one Gittins valley

\[
V=(u,v].
\]

By source Lemma 5.3, both Gittins hill boundaries are M-SERPT hill ages, and every M-SERPT hill/valley chunk inside `(u,v]` has right endpoint at most `v`.

Let `Xi_V` and `Q_{MS,V}` be the contributions of `(u,v]` to `Xi` and M-SERPT mean waiting, respectively. Summing `(3.1)` over M-SERPT hill portions and `(3.4)` over M-SERPT valleys gives

\[
\Xi_V-Q_{MS,V}
=
\sum_{H\subset V}\int_H U\,dH
+
\sum_{(y,z]\subset V}U(z)[H(z)-H(y)].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every increment of `H` appearing on the right side is nonpositive by `(2.2)`, while every multiplying value of `U` is at most `U(v)`. Therefore multiplying a negative increment by the smaller weight can only increase it, so the whole expression is bounded below by the terminal weight times the telescoped `H` increment:

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_V-Q_{MS,V}
\ge U(v)[H(v)-H(u)].
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The Gittins Proposition-4.7 lower-surrogate contribution on this valley is exactly

\[
\underline Q_{G,V}
=[S(u)-S(v)]\frac{\tau(v)}{A(u)A(v)}
=[S(u)-S(v)]\frac{U(v)}{A(u)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Add `(4.3)` to `(4.2)`. Since `H=S/A`, all left-end terms cancel and we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\Xi_V-igl(Q_{MS,V}-\underline Q_{G,V}\bigr)
\ge
\mathcal B_V,
}
\tag{GV}
\]

where the explicit **Gittins-valley endpoint reserve** is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_V
:=
S(v)\frac{\tau(v)}{A(v)}
\left(\frac1{A(v)}-\frac1{A(u)}\right)
\ge0.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the main theorem of the run. It has no finite-support/cardinality parameter. It directly compares the actual M-SERPT waiting refinement inside a Gittins valley with the exact half of the retained residence slack.

The earlier coarse Stieltjes rectangles are no longer needed for this comparison. Their central-plateau overcharge disappears automatically in the telescoping `H=S/A` identity.

## 5. Global waiting theorem

On a Gittins hill size, source Lemma 5.3 forces the age to be an M-SERPT hill size as well. Hence the Proposition-4.7 lower waiting integrand and the exact M-SERPT waiting integrand coincide there. There is no waiting mismatch on Gittins hills.

Let

\[
\Xi_{\mathcal H_G}
:=
\int_{\mathcal H_G}
\frac{\lambda S(x)^2\tau(x)}{A(x)^3}\,dx,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and define the explicit global endpoint reserve

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_G
:=
\Xi_{\mathcal H_G}
+\sum_{V\in\mathcal V_G}\mathcal B_V.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For a terminal Gittins valley take `S(v)=0` at `v=infinity`, so its endpoint term is zero.

Summing `(GV)` over Gittins valleys and adding the untouched hill credit yields

\[
\boxed{
Q_{MS}-\underline Q_G
\le
\Xi-\mathcal B_G
\le\Xi.
}
\tag{WAIT-CHARGE}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\Xi-\delta\ge\mathcal B_G\ge0.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is the rank-interval charging inequality sought by P01-B: every unit of excess M-SERPT waiting caused by refining the coarser Gittins hill partition is paid by the `Xi` half of the residence slack, with the explicit reserve `B_G` left over.

### Finite-support interpretation

For a finite-support law, M-SERPT consists of rank plateaus separated by record ages. Formula `(GV)` becomes a finite block identity/inequality and is independent of the number of records. Thus it complements R7-04 rather than reproducing its two-record algebra: P01-A proves the full response inequality on short finite record chains; `(WAIT-CHARGE)` controls the waiting-refinement part for an arbitrary chain length.

### Measure-theoretic scope

The derivation above is rigorous under the same continuous-density, piecewise-monotone presentation used in Sections 4--5 of the source, and its block form is rigorous for finite-support laws. The formulas are already in Stieltjes form, so the same argument should extend to locally finite mixed hill/valley geometries. I am **not** silently claiming that all endpoint/pathology details for an arbitrary Borel service law have been written here; that final general-measure extension remains a technical item for the full-source proof.

## 8. New all-load approximation bound: 3 instead of 5

Let

\[
x:=\frac{\underline Q_G}{\mu}\ge0.
\]

Combining `(HYBRID)` with both source lower bounds `(1.2)` yields

\[
\frac{T_{MS}}{T_G}
\le
\frac{2x+\ell}{\max\{x+1,\ell\}}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For fixed `ell`, maximize the right side over `x>=0`. The two denominator branches meet at `x=ell-1`.

- If `ell<=2`, the supremum is `2`.
- If `ell>=2`, the ratio increases up to `x=ell-1` on the `ell` branch and decreases after that on the `x+1` branch, giving

\[
\sup_{x\ge0}\frac{2x+\ell}{\max\{x+1,\ell\}}
=3-\frac2\ell.
\]

Thus the new source-level bound is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T_{MS}}{T_G}
\le
\begin{cases}
2,
&0\le\rho\le\rho_0,\\[1ex]
3-\displaystyle\frac{2\rho}{\log(1/(1-\rho))},
&\rho_0\le\rho<1.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{T_{MS}\le3T_G\quad\text{for every stable load in this source class}.}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The bound is strictly below 3 at every fixed `rho<1` and tends to 3 only as `rho->1`.

This would improve the 2020 paper's published all-load factor 5. Because this is a new internal theorem and has not yet received the independent review reserved for later, it should be treated as **PROVED PARTIAL pending independent review**, not as an externally established literature result.

## 9. The remaining full factor-two inequality is now much smaller

The exact master bound `(MASTER)` shows that it is sufficient to prove

\[
2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda
\ge
2\underline Q_G+\mathcal L
-2\max\{\underline Q_G+\mu,\mathcal L\}.
\]

The right side is exactly

\[
\min\{\mathcal L-2\mu,\ 2\underline Q_G-\mathcal L\}.
\]

Because the left side is nonnegative, the precise remaining sufficient inequality can be written

\[
\boxed{
2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda
\ge
\max\left\{0,
\min\{\mathcal L-2\mu,\ 2\underline Q_G-\mathcal L\}
\right\}.
}
\tag{EB}
\]

This is much narrower than the previous generic dual-reserve target: the waiting mismatch itself is already paid. What remains is only to show that the **unused endpoint reserve** `B_G` together with the `D1+D3` residence remainder covers the scalar gap between the two source Gittins lower bounds.

### Why not force the first branch alone

It would be tempting to replace `(EB)` by the stronger target

\[
2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda\ge\mathcal L-2\mu.
\]

Do not do this. The inherited Pareto analysis already showed that the finite-law-style first reserve `Q_MS+2mu-R_MS` can become negative for very heavy tails. The source also explains that Pareto laws near shape `alpha=1` make the logarithmic lower bound asymptotically tight. The `min` in `(EB)` is therefore essential: very-heavy-tail laws must be allowed to close through the `T_G>=mathcal L` branch rather than being forced through `T_G>=underline Q_G+mu`.
