## 1. Why the preceding route must be replaced

P01-B had proposed the strengthened sufficient inequality

\[
\Xi+\mathcal W\ge (c(\rho)-1)\underline Q_G
+\left(\frac{L(\rho)}2-1\right)E[X],
\tag{RC}
\]

where

\[
c(\rho)=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\rho}},\qquad
L(\rho)=\frac1\rho\log\frac1{1-\rho}.
\]

The newest P01-A report correctly refutes `(RC)` as a universal proof obligation. On every strict DMRL law, Gittins and M-SERPT are both FCFS, so the true approximation ratio is exactly one, but `Xi=0` and the global coefficient `c(rho)-1` leaves a false positive deficit at high load. In particular, mean-one Erlang-2 at `rho=0.9` refutes `(RC)` while satisfying `T_MS=T_G`.

The diagnosis is that the source's Lemma 5.6 introduces the global coefficient only after two relaxations: an AM-GM step inside each Gittins valley and then the replacement of the valley-specific quantity by its worst possible value `1-rho`. This run goes back to the source proof *before* those relaxations.

# 2. Exact local coefficient hidden in Lemma 5.6

Use the source notation

\[
S(x)=P(X>x),\qquad
A(x)=1-\lambda E[X\wedge x],
\qquad \rho=\lambda E[X].
\]

Fix one Gittins valley `(u,v]` with `S(u)>S(v)`. Define

\[
a:=\frac{A(v)}{A(u)},
\tag{2.1}
\]

and the source's auxiliary valley quantity

\[
g:=1-\rho+\rho\frac{S(v)}{S(u)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The proof of Lemma 5.6 constructs `x_*` in the valley so that its auxiliary `G` satisfies `G(x_*)=a`. Since the source has `G(v)=g` and `G(u)=1`, necessarily

\[
\boxed{g\le a\le1.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let `q(u,v)` denote the conditional M-SERPT waiting contribution on this valley divided by the Proposition-4.7 Gittins waiting lower contribution used in the source proof. Before AM-GM, equation (5.10) and the immediately following source inequality give

\[
q(u,v)
\le
\frac{2-a-g/a}{1-g}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The source next minimizes `a+g/a` by AM-GM and finally replaces `g` by `1-rho`, producing its global coefficient. We retain `(2.4)` instead.

Subtracting one gives the exact factorization

\[
\frac{2-a-g/a}{1-g}-1
=
\frac{1+g-a-g/a}{1-g}
=
\boxed{
\frac{(1-a)(a-g)}{a(1-g)}
}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Define the **local waiting-excess coefficient**

\[
\boxed{
\eta(u,v):=\frac{(1-a)(a-g)}{a(1-g)}.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

By `(2.3)`, `eta>=0`. Therefore the source proof yields the sharper valley estimate

\[
\boxed{q(u,v)\le1+\eta(u,v).}
\tag{LW}
\]

This is not a new queueing model or a finite-support specialization; it is the unrelaxed inequality already implicit in the source proof, now isolated in a form adapted to the factor-two problem.

# 3. The global factor is precisely the worst local extremum

For fixed `g in (0,1)`, write

\[
\eta(a,g)=\frac{1+g-a-g/a}{1-g}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial a}
=
\frac{-1+g/a^2}{1-g}.
\]

Hence the unique maximum on `g<=a<=1` is at

\[
a=\sqrt g,
\]

with value

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{\max}(g)=\frac{1-\sqrt g}{1+\sqrt g}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus

\[
1+\eta_{\max}(g)=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt g},
\]

which is exactly the source's post-AM-GM valley coefficient. Since `g>=1-rho`, maximizing once more gives

\[
1+\eta(u,v)\le\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\rho}}=c(\rho).
\]

This identifies the precise information lost by the published constant-factor proof: every valley is treated as though its load ratio satisfies the geometric-mean extremality `a=sqrt(g)` and its terminal auxiliary value satisfies the worst-load equality `g=1-rho`.

The local coefficient has two zero sets:

\[
\boxed{\eta=0\iff a=1\ \text{or}\ a=g.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The second zero set is the important one for the current project.

# 4. DMRL sanity check: the false penalty disappears exactly

P01-A proved that for a strict DMRL law both Gittins and M-SERPT collapse to FCFS. Its Gittins rank structure is one whole-line valley. For that valley

\[
u=0,\qquad v=\infty,
\]

so

\[
a=A(\infty)/A(0)=1-\rho,
\qquad
g=1-\rho.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\eta=0.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is exactly what the proof architecture should do: on a class where the actual policies coincide, the local waiting-excess penalty vanishes. The old global `(c(rho)-1)Q` term was entirely an artifact of maximizing over `a` and `g` after the valley calculation.

Thus the DMRL counterexample does not merely refute `(RC)`; it points to the correct variable to preserve.

# 5. Distributional interpretation through a Gittins-valley chord

The coefficient can be rewritten entirely in survival / integrated-tail data. Put

\[
p:=S(u)-S(v)>0,
\]

and define the valley chord slope

\[
\boxed{
k:=\frac{N(u)-N(v)}{S(u)-S(v)}
=\frac{\int_u^v S(x)\,dx}{p},}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `N(x)=E[(X-x)^+]`.

Because

\[
A(u)-A(v)=\lambda pk,
\]

we have

\[
a=1-\frac{\lambda pk}{A(u)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Also

\[
1-g=\rho\frac p{S(u)}
=\lambda E[X]\frac p{S(u)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Combining `(5.2)` and `(5.3)` gives

\[
a-g
=\lambda p\left(\frac{E[X]}{S(u)}-\frac{k}{A(u)}\right).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Substitution into `(2.6)` yields

\[
\boxed{
\eta(u,v)
=
\frac{\lambda p k}{A(v)}
\left(
1-\frac{kS(u)}{E[X]A(u)}
\right).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The bracket is nonnegative exactly because `a>=g`.

This form is useful for a general proof. The waiting excess on a Gittins valley is the product of

1. a **load-drop factor** `lambda p k/A(v)`, and
2. a **normalized chord/radial defect** `1-kS(u)/(E[X]A(u))`.

The second factor vanishes in the FCFS/DMRL whole-line case. It also makes the obstruction distributional rather than support-cardinality dependent, which is the correct object for P01-B.

# 6. Global refined waiting bound

Let `\underline Q_{G,V}` denote the unconditional Proposition-4.7 Gittins waiting lower contribution associated with a Gittins valley `V=(u,v]`. Define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\rm loc}
:=\sum_{V\in\mathcal V_G}
\eta_V\,\underline Q_{G,V}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a finite Gittins hill/valley decomposition, summing `(LW)` over valleys and using the source observation that M-SERPT has no positive waiting-ratio excess on Gittins hill sizes gives

\[
\boxed{
Q_{MS}\le \underline Q_G+\mathcal E_{\rm loc}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

For countably many rank intervals, the same inequality follows by monotone convergence because all contributions are nonnegative.

The published source bound is recovered by

\[
\mathcal E_{\rm loc}
\le(c(\rho)-1)\underline Q_G,
\]

but `(6.1)` retains the exact valley geometry and can be dramatically smaller. In particular it is zero on the DMRL/FCFS skeleton.

This is the main new arbitrary-law theorem of the run.

# 7. New master reduction using both independent Gittins lower bounds

Let

\[
\mu:=E[X],\qquad
L:=L(\rho)=\frac1\rho\log\frac1{1-\rho}.
\]

Retain the exact M-SERPT residence slack already derived in P01-B:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S:=Q_{MS}+L\mu-R_{MS}\ge0.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
T_{MS}=Q_{MS}+R_{MS}=2Q_{MS}+L\mu-\mathcal S.
\tag{7.2}
\]

There are two independent source lower bounds for Gittins:

\[
T_G\ge \underline Q_G+\mu,
\tag{7.3}
\]

from Proposition 4.7 plus `R_G>=E[X]`, and

\[
T_G\ge L\mu
\tag{7.4}
\]

from Proposition 4.10. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T_G\ge
\max\{\underline Q_G+\mu,\ L\mu\}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Insert `(6.2)` into `(7.2)`. A sufficient condition for the full factor-two conclusion is now

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S
\ge
2(\underline Q_G+\mathcal E_{\rm loc})+L\mu
-2\max\{\underline Q_G+\mu,\ L\mu\}.
}
\tag{MR}
\]

This is a strictly more adaptive target than `(RC)` or its global-coefficient replacement. It preserves both pieces of distributional information that the failed route discarded: the local valley coefficient and the stronger of the two source lower bounds.

Piecewise, `(MR)` is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S\ge
2\mathcal E_{\rm loc}+(L-2)\mu,
\quad
\underline Q_G\ge(L-1)\mu,
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S\ge
2\mathcal E_{\rm loc}+2\underline Q_G-L\mu,
\quad
\underline Q_G<(L-1)\mu.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

No claim is made here that `(MR)` has been proved universally. The advance is that it is the correct sharpened sufficient obligation after the DMRL refutation, rather than another globally over-relaxed condition known to fail for trivial reasons.

# 8. A new all-load arbitrary-law sufficient class

Because `mathcal S>=0`, `(7.7)` immediately gives a useful source-level theorem with no support restriction:

\[
\boxed{
\underline Q_G+\mathcal E_{\rm loc}
\le \frac{L(\rho)}2E[X]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_{MS}\le2T_G.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Indeed `(6.2)` gives `Q_MS<=L mu/2`, while Lemma 5.7 gives

\[
R_{MS}\le Q_{MS}+L\mu.
\]

Hence

\[
T_{MS}\le2Q_{MS}+L\mu\le2L\mu\le2T_G.
\]

This is an **all-stable-load theorem for an arbitrary-distribution class defined by a rank-integral condition**, not a heavy-traffic statement and not a finite-support result.

The complementary regime is now sharply isolated: only laws with

\[
\underline Q_G+\mathcal E_{\rm loc}>\frac{L\mu}{2}
\]

need a genuinely positive residence-slack lower bound. This is exactly where the previous `D1+2D2+D3` and contact-adaptive credits should be used.
