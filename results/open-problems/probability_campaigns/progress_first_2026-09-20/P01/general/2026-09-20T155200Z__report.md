## 1. Precise inherited inequality

Write

\[
S(x)=P(X>x),\qquad N(x)=E[(X-x)^+],
\]
\[
A(x)=1-\lambda E[X\wedge x]=c+\lambda N(x),\qquad c:=1-\rho,
\]
\[
\tau(x)=\frac\lambda2E[(X\wedge x)^2],\qquad \mu=E[X],
\]

and

\[
\mathcal L:=\int_0^\infty\frac{S(x)}{A(x)}dx
=\frac1\lambda\log\frac1c.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Previous P01-B work established the support-independent waiting refinement charge

\[
Q_{MS}-\underline Q_G\le \Xi-\mathcal B_G,
\tag{1.2}
\]

and the exact M-SERPT residence decomposition

\[
Q_{MS}+\mathcal L-R_{MS}=2\Xi+\Lambda,
\qquad \Lambda\ge0,
\tag{1.3}
\]

where `Lambda` is exactly the sum of the `D1+D3` portions of the M-SERPT valley slack. Hence

\[
T_{MS}\le 2\underline Q_G+\mathcal L-2\mathcal B_G-\Lambda.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The hill-survival Gittins residence lower bound is

\[
R_G\ge \mathcal R_G^{HS},
\tag{1.5}
\]

with

\[
\mathcal R_G^{HS}
=\int_{\mathcal H_G}\frac{S(a)}{A(a)}da
+\sum_{V=(u,v]\in\mathcal V_G}
\int_u^v\left[S(a)-S(v)+\frac{S(v)}{A(v)}\right]da.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Thus

\[
T_G\ge \underline Q_G+\mathcal R_G^{HS}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

The strongest one-branch inequality currently under attack is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L-2\mathcal R_G^{HS}
\le 2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda.
}
\tag{HS1}
\]

If `(HS1)` holds, `(1.4)` and `(1.7)` give `T_MS<=2T_G` immediately.

The previous run proved a local half-load theorem and a terminal-valley closure condition, but also exposed the key issue: a terminal valley can fail a purely local payment condition even when earlier rank intervals contain enough surplus. This run therefore switches from local terminal payment to **prefix compensation**.

## 2. New exact identity: `D1+D3` has an endpoint primitive

Let `(u,b]` be any M-SERPT valley. Put

\[
U=A(u),\quad V=A(b),\quad
H(x)=\frac{S(x)}{A(x)},\quad
T(x)=\frac{\tau(x)}{A(x)},
\]
\[
Y(x)=\frac1{A(x)},\qquad
P(x)=A(x)x+\tau(x).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The inherited exact valley terms are

\[
D_1=(H(u)-H(b))(T(b)-T(u)),
\tag{2.2}
\]

\[
D_3=\int_u^b(H(x)-H(b))\left(1-\frac{A(x)}U\right)dx.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The first part of `(2.3)` integrates exactly:

\[
\int_u^b H(x)\left(1-\frac{A(x)}U\right)dx
=
\frac1\lambda\left[
\log\frac UV-\frac{U-V}{U}
\right].
\tag{2.4}
\]

Indeed, `A'=-lambda S`, so `int S/A=(1/lambda)log(U/V)` and `int S=(U-V)/lambda`.

Next, from

\[
P'(x)=A(x)
\tag{2.5}
\]

and `P(x)=A(x)(x+T(x))`, one obtains the exact endpoint relation

\[
T(b)-T(u)
+
\int_u^b\left(1-\frac{A(x)}U\right)dx
=
P(b)\left(\frac1V-\frac1U\right).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Substituting `(2.4)--(2.6)` into `(2.2)--(2.3)` gives the new compact identity

\[
\boxed{
D_1+D_3
=
\frac1\lambda\left[
\log\frac UV-\frac{U-V}{U}
\right]
+H(u)[T(b)-T(u)]
-H(b)P(b)\left(\frac1V-\frac1U\right).
}
\tag{EP}
\]

No interior integral remains. The entire retained M-SERPT valley reserve is determined by endpoint values of `A,S,tau` and the endpoint age.

This identity is support-cardinality independent. It is valid for ordinary finite endpoints under the source's regular presentation, and the terminal form follows by monotone limiting with `V=c`, `H(b)=0`, and `T(b)=tau(infinity)/c` whenever the latter is finite (or in the extended nonnegative sense under truncation).

### Chain form

For a valley-only M-SERPT record chain

\[
a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_m<a_{m+1}=\infty,
\]

write `A_j=A(a_j)`, `H_j=H(a_j)`, `T_j=T(a_j)`, `P_j=P(a_j)`, `Y_j=1/A_j`, and take `A_{m+1}=c`, `H_{m+1}=0`. Summing `(EP)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda
=
\mathcal L
-
\frac1\lambda\sum_{j=0}^m
\frac{A_j-A_{j+1}}{A_j}
+
\sum_{j=0}^m H_j(T_{j+1}-T_j)
-
\sum_{j=0}^m H_{j+1}P_{j+1}(Y_{j+1}-Y_j).
}
\tag{CHAIN}
\]

Thus, in the finite-law geometry where hill intervals have zero Lebesgue measure, the logarithmic term in `(HS1)` cancels *exactly*. The remaining multi-record problem is an endpoint summation problem rather than an interior-integral problem. This is the correct interface with R7-04's finite record chain and with the P01-B Stieltjes program.

A useful warning also follows. Splitting one old valley `(u,b]` at an intermediate record `a` changes `Lambda` by

\[
\Delta\Lambda
= -\frac{(A_u-A_a)(A_a-A_b)}{\lambda A_uA_a}
+(H_a-H_u)(T_b-T_a)
+(H_bP_b-H_aP_a)(Y_a-Y_u).
\tag{2.7}
\]

The first term is strictly negative for a genuine split. Therefore a naive induction claiming that every inserted record monotonically increases the reserve cannot work. The proof must retain a prefix potential; this matches the empirical failure of purely local terminal payment in the previous run.

## 3. New prefix-compensation theorem: one common record closes with no terminal side condition

Consider the first nontrivial aligned arbitrary-law geometry:

- Gittins and M-SERPT have the same single internal envelope record at age `u`;
- their monotone envelopes are flat on `(0,u]` and on `(u,infinity)` (two common valleys, no positive-measure hill interval between them).

This includes the corresponding finite-support one-record topology, but the calculation itself does not use support cardinality.

Set

\[
U=A(u),\qquad s=S(u),\qquad t=\tau(u),\qquad d:=U-c=\lambda N(u).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For the hill-survival lower bound, the first valley contributes

\[
\frac{1-U}{\lambda}
+us\left(\frac1U-1\right),
\]

and the terminal valley contributes `d/lambda`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_G^{HS}
=\mu+us\left(\frac1U-1\right).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The only nonterminal Gittins endpoint reserve is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_G
=\frac{s t}{U}\left(\frac1U-1\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Apply `(EP)` to `(0,u]` and `(u,infinity)`. Using `A(0)=S(0)=1`, `tau(0)=0`, and `H(infinity)=0`, one obtains

\[
\Lambda_0
=
\frac1\lambda\left[
\log\frac1U-(1-U)
\right]
+\frac tU
-\frac{s}{U}(Uu+t)\left(\frac1U-1\right),
\tag{3.4}
\]

\[
\Lambda_\infty
=
\frac1\lambda\left[
\log\frac Uc-\frac{U-c}{U}
\right]
+\frac{s}{U}\left[
\frac{\tau(\infty)}c-\frac tU
\right].
\tag{3.5}
\]

Let

\[
\mathfrak Z
:=
2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda_0+\Lambda_\infty
-\left(\mathcal L-2\mathcal R_G^{HS}\right).
\tag{3.6}
\]

The logarithms cancel. The remaining algebra becomes transparent after the exact tail second-moment identity

\[
\tau(\infty)-t
=u(U-c)+\frac\lambda2E[(X-u)_+^2].
\tag{3.7}
\]

Writing `M_2(u)=E[(X-u)_+^2]`, exact simplification yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak Z
=
\frac{1}{2Uc\lambda}
\Bigl[
2c\{d^2+c(1-c)\}
+2U\lambda s u(1-c)
+2\lambda t\{c(1-s)+s\}
+\lambda^2sM_2(u)
\Bigr].
}
\tag{PC}
\]

Every displayed term is nonnegative, and the first bracket is strictly positive at every stable nonzero load because `0<c<1`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak Z>0.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

If the tail second moment is infinite, `(3.7)` may be read through truncation; for the finite-response regime the same nonnegative certificate is obtained by monotone passage. In particular, the proof of nonnegativity does not require using a finite upper bound on `M_2(u)`.

Therefore `(HS1)` holds in this entire two-valley aligned geometry, and combining `(1.4)` with `(1.7)` gives

\[
\boxed{
E[T_{MS}]\le 2E[T_G]
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

for every stable load for which the displayed response quantities are finite.

### Why this is stronger than the previous terminal theorem

The previous run required the local terminal condition

\[
2\lambda uS(u)+U(1+4c)\ge 3c
\]

to pay the terminal deficit from the terminal `D1+D3` alone. Formula `(PC)` shows that this local condition is unnecessary when the preceding common valley is retained: the first-valley endpoint reserve and its `D1+D3` contribution compensate any terminal shortfall automatically. The correct object is therefore a **prefix potential**, not a valley-by-valley nonnegative charge.

This is the first exact instance in the P01-B program where a locally underfunded post-half-load terminal block is closed by a preceding rank block with no load restriction.

## 4. Consequence for the general multi-record attack

The full conjecture is not proved. However, `(EP)` and `(PC)` materially narrow the remaining proof architecture.

For finite-support laws, R7-04 says a counterexample needs at least three strict internal M-SERPT records and a nontrivial prefix mismatch. Formula `(CHAIN)` now removes every `D3` interior integral from that longer chain. The only remaining variables are endpoint load levels `A_j`, endpoint hazard/survival ratios `H_j`, endpoint truncated-second-moment coordinates `T_j`, and the coarse Gittins record subsequence.

Moreover, `(2.7)` explains why simply inserting records one at a time cannot be justified by monotonicity: a genuine split creates a negative cross-load term. The one-record certificate `(PC)` shows what must replace that failed induction — a cumulative potential carrying earlier `B` and `D1+D3` surplus into the terminal suffix.

The next concrete target is therefore the first genuinely new case beyond both this theorem and R7-04's theorem: **one Gittins valley containing two or more skipped M-SERPT records in the post-half-load suffix**, with all `D1+D3` terms replaced by `(EP)`. The desired inequality should be written as a prefix sum in the endpoint variables before any atom-by-atom expansion.
