# New theorem: arbitrary-law factor two through rho ≈ 0.6261202890

## Theorem 1

Let `X` be any service distribution admissible under the source hypotheses, and let `rho<1`. Define

\[
t=-\frac12\log(1-\rho)
\quad\text{and}\quad
J(t)=e^{2t}-e^t+\frac{t}{\sinh t},
\]
with the continuous value `J(0)=1`.

There is a unique `t_*>0` satisfying

\[
J(t_*)=2.
\tag{5}
\]

If `0<=t<=t_*`, equivalently

\[
0\le \rho\le \rho_*:=1-e^{-2t_*},
\]
then

\[
\boxed{E[T_{MS}]\le2E[T_G].}
\tag{6}
\]

Numerically, only as a locator,

\[
t_*\approx0.4919105808473800,
\qquad
\boxed{\rho_*\approx0.6261202890192252.}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus Conjecture 8.1 is now proved for **every source-admissible service law** throughout an arbitrary-law load interval extending from the previous `~0.581862` threshold to `~0.626120`. This is not a finite-support theorem and not a heavy-traffic statement.

## Proof

If `E[T_G]=infinity`, (6) is vacuous, so assume it is finite and write

\[
T=E[T_G],\qquad
m=\frac{E[X]}T,\qquad
q=\frac{Q_G}T.
\]

Because `T=Q_G+R_G` and (4) gives `R_G>=E[X]`,

\[
q\le1-m.
\tag{8}
\]

Normalize the M-SERPT waiting and residence terms by `T`. From (1)--(3),

\[
\frac{Q_{MS}}T\le c q,
\]

and

\[
\frac{R_{MS}}T
\le
\min\{d m,\; c q+Lm\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{T_{MS}}T
\le c q+\min\{d m,cq+Lm\}.
\tag{9}
\]

For fixed `m`, the right side is increasing in `q`, so by (8),

\[
\frac{T_{MS}}T
\le
\min\{f_1(m),f_2(m)\},
\tag{10}
\]
where

\[
f_1(m)=c+(d-c)m,
\qquad
f_2(m)=2c+(L-2c)m.
\tag{11}
\]

We next work in the region eventually characterized by `J(t)<=2`. Put

\[
s=e^{-t}=\sqrt{1-\rho}.
\]

First, `d>c` for every `rho in (0,1)`, because

\[
d-c
=\frac{(1-s)(1+2s)}{s^2(1+s)}>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Second, in the target region we also have `L<=2c`. Indeed `J` will be shown increasing, and

\[
J(1)>e^2-e>2,
\]
so `J(t)<=2` implies `t<1`. For `0<t<=1`,

\[
1-e^{-t}\ge t-\frac{t^2}{2}\ge\frac t2,
\]
which gives

\[
t\le2(1-e^{-t}).
\]
This is exactly equivalent to

\[
L=\frac{2t}{1-e^{-2t}}\le\frac4{1+e^{-t}}=2c.
\tag{13}
\]

Thus on the target region, `f_1` is increasing and `f_2` is nonincreasing. Their unique intersection occurs at

\[
m_* = \frac{c}{d+c-L}>0,
\]
where positivity follows from `d>L` (the elementary logarithm inequality) and `c>0`. Hence, regardless of the actual feasible value of `m`,

\[
\min\{f_1(m),f_2(m)\}
\le H(\rho)
:=\frac{c(2d-L)}{d+c-L}.
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to characterize `H<=2`. Direct algebra gives

\[
H\le2
\iff
 d(1-s)-2+sL\le0.
\]
Since `s=e^{-t}`, `d=s^{-2}` and

\[
sL
=\frac{2te^{-t}}{1-e^{-2t}}
=\frac{t}{\sinh t},
\]
this is precisely

\[
H\le2
\iff
J(t)=e^{2t}-e^t+\frac{t}{\sinh t}\le2.
\tag{15}
\]

Finally we prove that (5) has exactly one positive solution. The continuous limit is `J(0)=1`, while `J(t)->infinity`. Moreover

\[
J'(t)
=e^t(2e^t-1)
+\frac{\sinh t-t\cosh t}{\sinh^2 t}.
\tag{16}
\]

For `t>0`,

\[
0<t\cosh t-\sinh t<\sinh^2 t.
\tag{17}
\]

The left inequality follows because the derivative of `t cosh t-sinh t` is `t sinh t>0` and the expression vanishes at zero. For the right inequality, `t<sinh t` implies

\[
t\cosh t-\sinh t
<\sinh t(\cosh t-1)
<\sinh^2 t,
\]
where the last inequality is `cosh t-1<sinh t`, equivalent to `1-e^{-t}>0`.

Thus the second term in (16) is greater than `-1`, while

\[
e^t(2e^t-1)>1
\]
for every `t>0`. Hence `J'(t)>0` strictly. There is exactly one `t_*` with `J(t_*)=2`, and (14)--(15) prove (6). QED.

# What changed structurally

The improvement is not a better estimate of a finite example. It comes from retaining the **lower envelope of two distinct arbitrary-law residence controls**:

\[
R_{MS}\le
\min\left\{
\frac{E[X]}{1-\rho},
Q_{MS}+L(\rho)E[X]
\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]

The first is strongest when normalized raw service `m=E[X]/T_G` is small; the second is strongest when it is large. Their crossing produces the scalar envelope `H(rho)`. The previous proof optimized only the first branch and therefore stopped at `rho≈0.581862`.

This also identifies a clean limitation of the present scalar route: `H(rho)>2` once `rho>rho_*`. That does **not** show Conjecture 8.1 fails, and it does not prove no other scalar inequality can work. It says only that the specific distribution-free information (1)--(4), combined by this exact two-branch optimization, ceases to certify factor two past `rho_*`. The remaining proof must exploit distribution-sensitive coupling, which is exactly where the inherited global charging inequality and P01-A's envelope-contact identities enter.

# Relation to the high-load bottleneck

The sufficient high-load inequality isolated last run remains

\[
\boxed{
R_{MS}-2E[X]
\le 2\underline Q_G-Q_{MS},
}
\tag{C}
\]

where

\[
\underline Q_G
=E\!\left[
\frac{\tau(z_G(X))}
{A(y_G(X))A(z_G(X))}
\right]
\le Q_G.
\]

The exact two-point example from the previous report refutes per-Gittins-block nonnegative charging, so (C) needs cross-block transport. P01-A has now shown in two distinct finite-law topologies that outward movement of a terminal atom past all earlier Gittins hills leaves the additive policy gap invariant and strictly **increases** factor-two slack. The consistent general picture is therefore:

1. early Gittins hills create charge;
2. a terminal valley may spend that charge and can be locally negative;
3. outward tail displacement beyond the last relevant hill is benign for factor-two slack;
4. a worst-case law should therefore be reducible toward envelope-contact configurations rather than controlled block by block.

This is motivation, not yet a general extremal-measure theorem.
