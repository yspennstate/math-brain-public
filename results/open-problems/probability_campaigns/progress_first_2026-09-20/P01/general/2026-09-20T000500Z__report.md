# New theorem: Conjecture 8.1 is true for every service law up to an explicit load threshold

## Theorem 1 (arbitrary-law factor-two region)

For every source-admissible service distribution and every stable load `rho` satisfying

\[
B(\rho):=
\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\rho}}
+
\frac{\displaystyle \frac1{1-\rho}-\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\rho}}}
{\displaystyle \frac1\rho\log\frac1{1-\rho}}
\le 2,
\tag{4}
\]

M-SERPT satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[T_{MS}]\le 2\mathbb E[T_G].}
\tag{5}
\]

Moreover `B(rho)` is strictly increasing on `(0,1)`. Hence there is a unique threshold `rho_*` with `B(rho_*)=2`, and

\[
\boxed{\rho_*\approx 0.5818619898077688.}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the source conjecture is proved, without any finite-support restriction, throughout `0<=rho<=rho_*`. The decimal in (6) is only a locator; the exact theorem is the root-defined condition (4).

### Proof

Since `A` is decreasing and `A(infinity)=1-rho`, (2) gives the distribution-free residence bound

\[
R_{MS}
\le \frac{\mathbb E[X]}{1-\rho}
=:d(\rho)\mathbb E[X].
\tag{7}
\]

Let `T=T_G`, and set

\[
e=\frac{\mathbb E[X]}{T}.
\]

From `R_G>=E[X]`,

\[
\frac{Q_G}{T}=1-\frac{R_G}{T}\le 1-e.
\tag{8}
\]

From (3),

\[
e\le \frac1{L(\rho)}.
\tag{9}
\]

Combining (1), (7), and (8),

\[
\frac{T_{MS}}T
=\frac{Q_{MS}+R_{MS}}T
\le c(\rho)(1-e)+d(\rho)e
=c(\rho)+(d(\rho)-c(\rho))e.
\tag{10}
\]

For `0<rho<1`, writing `s=sqrt(1-rho)` gives

\[
d-c=\frac1{s^2}-\frac2{1+s}
=\frac{(1-s)(1+2s)}{s^2(1+s)}\ge0.
\]

Therefore (9) turns (10) into

\[
\frac{T_{MS}}{T_G}\le B(\rho),
\tag{11}
\]

with `B` as in (4). This proves (5) whenever `B(rho)<=2`.

It remains to verify the claimed one-threshold structure. Put

\[
t=-\tfrac12\log(1-\rho),\qquad \sqrt{1-\rho}=e^{-t}.
\]

A direct simplification gives

\[
B(t)=\frac{2e^t}{e^t+1}
+\frac{e^{2t}-3+2e^{-t}}{2t}.
\tag{12}
\]

The first term is strictly increasing. For

\[
N(t)=e^{2t}-3+2e^{-t},
\]

we have `N(0)=N'(0)=0` and

\[
N''(t)=4e^{2t}+2e^{-t}>0.
\]

Hence `N` is strictly convex and, for `t>0`,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{N(t)}t
=\frac{tN'(t)-N(t)}{t^2}
=\frac{1}{t^2}\int_0^t uN''(u)\,du>0.
\]

Thus `B` is strictly increasing in `t`, equivalently in `rho`. Its continuous limit at `rho=0` is `1`, while `B(rho)->infinity` as `rho->1`, so there is one and only one root of `B=2`. Straight bisection/high-precision evaluation gives (6). QED.

### Why this is nontrivial relative to the source bound

The source's published all-distribution response-ratio curve is `4/(1+sqrt(1-rho))` in the low/mid-load branch, giving for example 2.5 only through load 0.64. The proof above combines the *waiting* coefficient with two separate Gittins lower constraints (`R_G>=E[X]` and `T_G>=L(rho)E[X]`) before maximizing. That avoids multiplying independent worst cases. It proves the conjectured constant 2 on a genuine arbitrary-law interval reaching approximately 0.582; this is not a three-atom theorem and not a heavy-traffic asymptotic statement.

# The high-load bottleneck: a precise global charging inequality

The theorem above also shows exactly why the remaining load range needs distribution-sensitive coupling rather than another scalar-in-`rho` estimate.

Define the source Proposition-4.7 Gittins waiting lower surrogate

\[
\underline Q_G
:=\mathbb E\left[
\frac{\tau(z_G(X))}{A(y_G(X))A(z_G(X))}
\right]
\le Q_G.
\tag{13}
\]

Because `R_G>=E[X]`, the following stronger inequality would settle the full source conjecture at every stable load:

\[
\boxed{
R_{MS}-2\mathbb E[X]
\le 2\underline Q_G-Q_{MS}.
}
\tag{C}
\]

Indeed, (C) implies

\[
T_{MS}=Q_{MS}+R_{MS}
\le2(\underline Q_G+\mathbb E[X])
\le2(Q_G+R_G)=2T_G.
\]

This is the concrete P01-B target for the high-load regime. Its interpretation is a rank-interval charging law: every unit by which M-SERPT residence exceeds twice raw service must be paid for by waiting-time slack left by Gittins's coarser hill/valley structure.

## A naive localization of (C) is false

A useful negative result is that (C) cannot be proved independently on each Gittins envelope block. Cross-block credit is genuinely necessary.

Take the exact two-point law

\[
P(X=1)=\frac{99}{100},\qquad P(X=10)=\frac1{100},
\qquad \rho=\frac23.
\tag{14}
\]

Here `p(b-a)=99*9/100>1`, so both increasing envelopes have a hill at age `1`; the coarse blocks for the lower-surrogate calculation are `(0,1]` and `(1,infinity]`. We have

\[
\mathbb E[X]=\frac{109}{100},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{200}{327},\qquad
A(1)=\frac{127}{327},\qquad A(\infty)=\frac13,
\]

and

\[
\tau(1)=\frac{100}{327},\qquad
\tau(\infty)=\frac{199}{327}.
\]

Therefore the common M-SERPT / Gittins-lower waiting charges on the two atoms are

\[
q_1=\frac{100}{127},\qquad q_{10}=\frac{597}{127}.
\]

For the terminal block alone, the conditional contribution to

\[
2(\underline Q_G+E[X])-(Q_{MS}+R_{MS})
\]

is

\[
q_{10}+20-\frac{10}{A(1)}
=
\frac{597+2540-3270}{127}
=-\frac{133}{127}.
\]

After multiplying by terminal mass `1/100`, its block charge is

\[
\boxed{-\frac{133}{12700}<0.}
\tag{15}
\]

So a theorem asserting nonnegative charge *separately on every Gittins block* is false.

But the first block contributes

\[
\frac{99}{100}(q_1+1)
=\frac{22473}{12700},
\]

and the total charge is

\[
\frac{22473-133}{12700}
=\boxed{\frac{1117}{635}>0}.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus the smallest exact test already forces a **prefix/global transport of slack from early ranks to a later tail block**. This is consistent with P01-A's new tail-location cancellation: terminal-tail displacement can consume previously created slack without changing the local additive policy gap.

This negative result is important because it rules out the most tempting per-valley proof of (C) before time is spent formalizing it.
