# 1. Setup

Fix an arrival rate `lambda>0` and a source law `X>=0` with

\[
\rho=\lambda E[X]<1,
\qquad E[X^2]<\infty.
\]

Write its survival function as `Fbar(a)=P(X>a)`. On ages with `Fbar(a)>0`, define the SERPT mean residual rank

\[
s(a):=r_S(a)=\frac{E[(X-a)^+]}{\bar F(a)}
=\frac{\int_a^\infty \bar F(t)\,dt}{\bar F(a)},
\]

and its increasing envelope

\[
m(a):=r_{MS}(a)=\max_{0\le b\le a}s(b).
\]

The 2020 source assumes a continuous density and piecewise-monotone SERPT/hazard ranks. For the convergence theorem below I use an explicit nondegeneracy condition on the M-SERPT record geometry.

### Definition (locally record-regular)

Call `X` **locally record-regular** if, on every compact age interval on which `Fbar>0`, the envelope `m` has finitely many valley components `(u_j,v_j)` and:

1. `m` is constant on each `(u_j,v_j)`;
2. `s(a)<m(a)` for every strict interior point `u_j<a<v_j`;
3. on every nontrivial compact interval contained in the complementary hill set, `m` is strictly increasing.

This allows ordinary hills and valleys but excludes a degenerate continuum of tangential record contacts. Under the source's continuity assumptions, all hill/valley boundaries have `X`-probability zero.

Choose sequences

\[
M_n\uparrow\infty,
\qquad h_n\downarrow0,
\qquad M_n/h_n\in\mathbb N,
\]

and define the finite-support **lower quantization**

\[
\boxed{
X_n:=h_n\left\lfloor\frac{X\wedge M_n}{h_n}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Then `0<=X_n<=X`, `X_n->X` almost surely and in `L^1` and `L^2`, and every `X_n` has finite support. The artificial atom at zero is harmless: zero-size jobs consume no service and can be removed by Poisson thinning; P01-A uses the same observation in its finite-law theorem.

# 2. New Lemma A: Gittins optimal mean response is monotone under stochastic service enlargement

### Lemma A

Suppose service laws `Y` and `X` admit a coupling with `0<=Y<=X` almost surely, and `lambda E[X]<1`. Then

\[
\boxed{E[T_G(Y)]\le E[T_G(X)].}
\tag{2}
\]

### Proof

Take a regular conditional law `K(y,dx)` of `X` given `Y=y` under the coupling. Consider a queue whose **physical** job sizes have law `Y`. We construct an admissible randomized comparison policy.

Run a virtual copy of the Gittins policy for law `X`. A physical job and its virtual counterpart arrive together and receive the same service until the physical job has accumulated its size `Y`. At that physical completion epoch, `Y` has been revealed by the completion itself. Independently sample a hidden virtual continuation `D>=0` from the conditional law of `X-Y` given `Y`. The virtual job remains present for another `D` units of service. Whenever virtual Gittins selects a still-physical job, serve that physical job. Whenever it selects a ghost continuation, the physical server may idle for that virtual service interval. The scheduling decision uses only ages, past completions and internal randomness; it never uses an unrevealed physical size.

By construction, the virtual total sizes are i.i.d. with marginal law `X`, so the virtual trajectory has exactly the law of an `M/G/1` queue with service law `X` under its Gittins policy. Every physical job completes after its first `Y` units of the service allocated to its virtual counterpart, and therefore completes no later than that virtual counterpart. Hence the comparison policy on the `Y` queue has mean response at most `E[T_G(X)]`.

True Gittins for law `Y` minimizes mean response among nonclairvoyant policies, so it cannot be worse than this randomized comparison policy. This proves (2). `square`

### Consequence for the quantizers

Since `X_n<=X` pathwise,

\[
\boxed{E[T_G(X_n)]\le E[T_G(X)]\quad\text{for every }n.}
\tag{3}
\]

This one-sided inequality is why lower quantization is useful: no continuity theorem for Gittins is needed in the final limiting argument.

# 3. New Lemma B: lower quantization preserves the SERPT rank locally

First truncate only. Put `Z_M=X\wedge M`. For `a<M`,

\[
s_M(a)
=\frac{E[(Z_M-a)^+]}{P(Z_M>a)}
=\frac{\int_a^M\bar F(t)dt}{\bar F(a)}.
\]

Fix a compact `[0,K]` on which `Fbar(K)>0`. Then for `M>K`,

\[
0\le s(a)-s_M(a)
=\frac{\int_M^\infty\bar F(t)dt}{\bar F(a)}
\le \frac{E[(X-M)^+]}{\bar F(K)}.
\tag{4}
\]

The right side tends to zero, so `s_M->s` uniformly on `[0,K]`.

Now quantize `Z_M` downward with mesh `h`, writing `Y=h floor(Z_M/h)` and taking `M/h` integral. At a grid point `a=kh<M-h`, continuity of the original source law below the terminal truncation atom gives

\[
Y>a\iff Z_M>a+h.
\]

On this event,

\[
Z_M-(a+h)\le Y-a\le Z_M-a.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
s_M(a+h)\le s_Y(a)\le s_M(a+h)+h.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Between consecutive grid points the conditioning set is unchanged and `s_Y` decreases with slope one, so (5), uniform continuity of `s_M` on a fixed compact, and (4) imply

\[
\boxed{s_{X_n}\longrightarrow s_X\quad\text{locally uniformly on }\{\bar F>0\}.}
\tag{6}
\]

The increasing-envelope operator is nonexpansive in sup norm:

\[
\left|\max_{b\le a}f(b)-\max_{b\le a}g(b)\right|
\le\sup_{b\le a}|f(b)-g(b)|.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{m_{X_n}\longrightarrow m_X\quad\text{locally uniformly}.}
\tag{7}
\]

# 4. New Lemma C: record-regularity turns rank convergence into hill/valley convergence

For any monotone-envelope SOAP rank let `y(x)` and `z(x)` denote the previous and next hill ages in the source's notation. Use `y_n,z_n` for M-SERPT under `X_n` and `y,z` for M-SERPT under `X`.

### Lemma C

If `X` is locally record-regular, then under the coupling (1), for `X`-almost every `x`,

\[
\boxed{
y_n(X_n)\to y(x),\qquad z_n(X_n)\to z(x).}
\tag{8}
\]

### Proof

There are two cases, excluding the finitely many local hill/valley boundaries, which have zero probability under the source's continuous-density assumption.

**Hill interior.** Suppose `x` lies in the interior of a true hill. For every sufficiently small `epsilon>0`, strict increase on the compact hill neighborhood gives

\[
m(x)-m(x-\epsilon)>0,
\qquad
m(x+\epsilon)-m(x)>0.
\]

By local uniform convergence (7), for all large `n` the envelope `m_n` cannot be constant across either interval from `x-epsilon` to `X_n` or from `X_n` to `x+epsilon`; otherwise its endpoint values would contradict one of the two strict gaps after an `o(1)` perturbation. Thus the plateau of `m_n` containing `X_n` has both endpoints within `epsilon` of `x`. Since `epsilon` is arbitrary, `y_n(X_n),z_n(X_n)->x=y(x)=z(x)`.

**Valley interior.** Suppose `x in (u,v)` for a true valley. Fix `epsilon>0` with `x in (u+epsilon,v-epsilon)`. By record-regularity and compactness,

\[
\eta:=m(u)-\max_{a\in[u+\epsilon,v-\epsilon]}s(a)>0.
\]

Local uniform convergence of both `s_n` and `m_n` implies that, for all large `n`, no point of `[u+epsilon,v-epsilon]` can set a new record: throughout that interval `s_n` remains at least (say) `eta/2` below the record level inherited from near `u`. Hence the approximating plateau containing `X_n` cannot acquire an interior hill there.

On the other hand, immediately to the hill side of each endpoint, true `m` changes strictly. The hill-interior argument therefore forces an approximating record point within `epsilon` of `u` and another within `epsilon` of `v`. Consequently the previous and next approximating hill ages for `X_n` lie in `(u-epsilon,u+epsilon)` and `(v-epsilon,v+epsilon)`, respectively. Letting `epsilon` decrease proves (8). `square`

The important point is that mere uniform convergence of monotone ranks would **not** be enough: tiny perturbations can break an exact plateau. The strict-interior record gap is what prevents that pathology.

# 5. New Theorem: M-SERPT response is continuous under the lower finite quantizers

The 2020 source's Propositions 4.7 and 4.8 are equalities for M-SERPT. In its notation, with

\[
A(a)=1-\lambda E[X\wedge a],
\qquad
\tau(a)=\frac\lambda2E[(X\wedge a)^2],
\]

we have

\[
E[Q_{MS}(X)]
=E\left[\frac{\tau(z(X))}{A(y(X))A(z(X))}\right],
\tag{9}
\]

\[
E[R_{MS}(X)]
=E\left[\frac{X}{A(y(X))}\right].
\tag{10}
\]

The source presents these formulas for its continuous setting; its paper explicitly says the discrete case was checked, and P01-A/R7-04 is already using the atom-compatible SOAP/Stieltjes form in its exact finite-law proofs. No new discrete SOAP formula is asserted here.

For `X_n`, define `A_n,tau_n` analogously. Because `X_n->X` in `L^1` and `L^2`, uniformly in the age argument,

\[
\sup_a|A_n(a)-A(a)|
\le\lambda E|X_n-X|\to0,
\tag{11}
\]

and

\[
\sup_a|\tau_n(a)-\tau(a)|
\le\frac\lambda2E|X_n^2-X^2|\to0.
\tag{12}
\]

Moreover `X_n<=X` implies `rho_n<=rho`, so

\[
A_n(a)\ge1-\rho>0
\]

for every `a,n`. Lemma C and (11)--(12) therefore give pointwise convergence of the waiting and residence integrands in (9)--(10) for `X`-almost every coupled sample.

The convergence is dominated uniformly. Indeed,

\[
0\le
\frac{\tau_n(z_n)}{A_n(y_n)A_n(z_n)}
\le
\frac{\lambda E[X^2]}{2(1-\rho)^2},
\tag{13}
\]

while

\[
0\le\frac{X_n}{A_n(y_n)}
\le\frac{X}{1-\rho}.
\tag{14}
\]

The right sides are integrable. Dominated convergence yields

\[
E[Q_{MS}(X_n)]\to E[Q_{MS}(X)],
\qquad
E[R_{MS}(X_n)]\to E[R_{MS}(X)].
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
E[T_{MS}(X_n)]\longrightarrow E[T_{MS}(X)].
}
\tag{15}
\]

This is the continuity statement that the one-sided reduction needs.

# 6. Finite-support reduction theorem

### Theorem (proved partial reduction)

Let `X` satisfy the 2020 source's continuous-law assumptions, `lambda E[X]<1`, `E[X^2]<infty`, and local record-regularity above. Then there exists a sequence of finite-support laws `X_n<=X` such that

\[
E[T_{MS}(X_n)]\to E[T_{MS}(X)]
\quad\text{and}\quad
E[T_G(X_n)]\le E[T_G(X)]\ \forall n.
\tag{16}
\]

Consequently:

1. **Finite-support sufficiency on this class.** If Conjecture 8.1 is true for every finite-support service law at every stable load, then it is true for every `X` in this class. Indeed,

\[
T_{MS}(X_n)\le2T_G(X_n)\le2T_G(X),
\]

and (15) permits `n->infty`.

2. **Counterexamples transfer to finite support.** If some `X` in this class is a strict source-valid counterexample,

\[
T_{MS}(X)>2T_G(X),
\]

put `eta=T_MS(X)-2T_G(X)>0`. For large `n`, (15) gives

\[
T_{MS}(X_n)>T_{MS}(X)-\eta/2
=2T_G(X)+\eta/2
\ge2T_G(X_n)+\eta/2.
\]

So every sufficiently fine lower quantizer is itself a finite-support counterexample.

This is an actual arbitrary-law **distributional reduction**, not a three-atom theorem.

# 7. Consequence of P01-A's newest theorem

P01-A has proved that every finite law with at most three positive support points satisfies the factor-two bound at every stable load. Combining that theorem with the reduction above gives:

> A strict counterexample in the record-regular finite-second-moment source class cannot be a genuinely continuum-only phenomenon. It would force finite-support counterexamples, and each sufficiently fine such counterexample must have at least **four positive support points**.

This does **not** prove that four points suffice to realize a worst case, and it does not close the source conjecture. It does justify the finite-law program mathematically: on this broad class, proving all finite-support laws is enough.

# 8. What remains before this becomes a full arbitrary-law reduction

Three gaps remain, and I am not hiding them behind the word "approximation":

1. **Degenerate record contacts.** Local record-regularity excludes exact tangencies/continua of contacts at a flat M-SERPT envelope level. Small discrete perturbations can break a tie and change an FCFS plateau into tiny ordered levels, so this is a genuine continuity issue, not bookkeeping.
2. **Infinite-second-moment laws.** The domination in (13) uses `E[X^2]<infty`. The source conjecture is not stated only for finite variance. Extending the reduction requires a truncation/uniform-integrability argument tailored to the SOAP waiting term, not simply FCFS domination.
3. **Finite-support endpoint.** P01-A currently proves all laws with at most three positive atoms, not all finite-support laws. The new reduction says exactly why its next four-atom transport problem matters; it does not solve that problem for P01-A.

The first gap is the most promising P01-B target. A way to remove it would be to construct lower finite approximants that **preserve every M-SERPT record plateau exactly**, e.g. by matching tail mass and integrated tail at record-contact ages, rather than ordinary uniform quantization. That is an extremal-measure/moment-matching problem and connects directly to P01-A's envelope-contact transport identities.
