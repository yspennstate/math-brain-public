# New Lemma 1: exact M-SERPT residence-slack decomposition

Define the global Lemma-5.7 slack

\[
\mathcal S:=Q_{MS}+K-R_{MS}\ge0.
\]

Partition the age line into M-SERPT hills and valleys as in the source. The slack has an explicit nonnegative decomposition.

## Hill contribution

On an M-SERPT hill, the source's exact derivatives are

\[
\frac d{dx}\Delta_Q
=\frac{\lambda x\bar F(x)^2}{A(x)^2}
 +\frac{2\lambda\bar F(x)^2\tau(x)}{A(x)^3},
\]

\[
\frac d{dx}\Delta_R
=\frac{\bar F(x)}{A(x)}
 +\frac{\lambda x\bar F(x)^2}{A(x)^2},
\qquad
\frac d{dx}\Delta_{\log}=\frac{\bar F(x)}{A(x)}.
\]

Hence there is not merely an inequality: the exact hill slack density is

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{dx}(\Delta_Q+\Delta_{\log}-\Delta_R)
=\frac{2\lambda\bar F(x)^2\tau(x)}{A(x)^3}.
}
\tag{H}
\]

## Valley contribution

Fix one M-SERPT valley `(y,z]`. Set

\[
F_y=\bar F(y),\quad F_z=\bar F(z),\quad
A_y=A(y),\quad A_z=A(z),\quad
\tau_y=\tau(y),\quad\tau_z=\tau(z).
\]

The source's exact waiting jump (its (5.13)) is

\[
\Delta_Q
=F_y\left(\frac{\tau_z}{A_yA_z}-\frac{\tau_y}{A_y^2}\right)
+F_z\left(\frac{\tau_z}{A_z^2}-\frac{\tau_z}{A_yA_z}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

The first term admits the exact split

\[
F_y\left(\frac{\tau_z}{A_yA_z}-\frac{\tau_y}{A_y^2}\right)
=
F_z\left(\frac{\tau_z}{A_z^2}-\frac{\tau_y}{A_yA_z}\right)+D_1,
\tag{2}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
D_1=
\left(\frac{F_y}{A_y}-\frac{F_z}{A_z}\right)
\left(\frac{\tau_z}{A_z}-\frac{\tau_y}{A_y}\right)\ge0.
}
\tag{3}
\]

The sign follows from source Corollary 5.5 at the hill endpoint `z`, together with monotonicity of `tau/A`.

The second term in (1) is

\[
\boxed{
D_2=
\frac{F_z\tau_z(A_y-A_z)}{A_yA_z^2}\ge0.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Now use the source's exact residence identity (5.15). In the step from (5.15) to (5.16), Corollary 5.5 replaces the integral integrand by a smaller one. The amount lost there is exactly

\[
\boxed{
D_3=
\int_y^z
\left(\frac{\bar F(x)}{A(x)}-\frac{F_z}{A_z}\right)
\left(1-\frac{A(x)}{A_y}\right)dx\ge0.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, expanding the integrand gives precisely

\[
\bar F(x)\left(\frac1{A(x)}-\frac1{A_y}\right)
-F_z\left(\frac1{A_z}-\frac{A(x)}{A_yA_z}\right),
\]

which is the difference between the exact integral in (5.15) and the Corollary-5.5 lower integrand used in (5.16).

The source's integrated upper bound from (5.16) is

\[
\Delta_R
\le
\Delta_{\log}
+F_z\frac{\tau_z-\tau_y}{A_yA_z}.
\tag{6}
\]

Because (5) is exactly the amount lost in that replacement, (6) is in fact

\[
\Delta_R
=
\Delta_{\log}
+F_z\frac{\tau_z-\tau_y}{A_yA_z}
-D_3.
\tag{7}
\]

Meanwhile, the lower term on the right of (2) exceeds the non-log term in (7) by **another copy of `D_2`**:

\[
F_z\left(\frac{\tau_z}{A_z^2}-\frac{\tau_y}{A_yA_z}\right)
-F_z\frac{\tau_z-\tau_y}{A_yA_z}
=D_2.
\tag{8}
\]

Substituting (2), (4), (7), and (8) into `Delta_Q+Delta_log-Delta_R` gives the exact valley identity

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_Q+\Delta_{\log}-\Delta_R
=D_1+2D_2+D_3.
}
\tag{V}
\]

This is the main new structural result of the run. The source only needed nonnegativity; the discarded slack is actually the sum of three explicit nonnegative credits, and the boundary credit `D_2` appears twice.

## Global form

Summing (H) and (V) over the M-SERPT hill/valley partition yields, for finite quantities and then by truncation/monotone limiting when appropriate,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S
=
\int_{\mathcal H_{MS}}
\frac{2\lambda\bar F(x)^2\tau(x)}{A(x)^3}\,dx
+\sum_{(y,z]\in\mathcal V_{MS}}(D_1+2D_2+D_3).
}
\tag{9}
\]

In particular define the basic rank credit

\[
\boxed{
\Xi:=
\int_{\mathcal H_{MS}}
\frac{\lambda\bar F(x)^2\tau(x)}{A(x)^3}\,dx
+\sum_{(y,z]\in\mathcal V_{MS}}
\frac{F_z\tau_z(A_y-A_z)}{A_yA_z^2}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Then (9) gives the sharpened arbitrary-law residence inequality

\[
\boxed{
R_{MS}\le Q_{MS}+K-2\Xi.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Unlike another scalar-in-rho estimate, `Xi` records the actual M-SERPT rank geometry. It is exactly the kind of credit that can move across a later negative tail block.

# New Lemma 2: preserve the waiting slack discarded by AM--GM

The source proof of Lemma 5.6 contains a second distribution-sensitive credit which is lost when it replaces its pre-AM--GM expression by the global coefficient `2/(1+sqrt(1-rho))`.

Fix a Gittins valley `(u,v]`. Let its Proposition-4.7 lower-surrogate waiting contribution be

\[
W_V=(\bar F(u)-\bar F(v))
\frac{\tau(v)}{A(u)A(v)}.
\tag{12}
\]

Use the source's auxiliary function on that valley

\[
\bar G_u(x)=1-\rho+\rho\frac{\bar F(x)}{\bar F(u)},
\qquad \bar G_u(u)=1.
\]

Put

\[
s_V=\bar G_u(v),
\qquad
a_V=\frac{A(v)}{A(u)}.
\]

Equation (5.3) says precisely that `a_V=Gbar_u(x_*)/Gbar_u(u)` for the balancing age `x_*`. Keeping the pre-AM--GM expression in (5.10) gives

\[
\frac{Q_{MS,V}}{W_V}
\le
\frac{2-a_V-s_V/a_V}{1-s_V}.
\tag{13}
\]

The source then applies AM--GM and obtains

\[
c_V:=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{s_V}}
=\frac{2-2\sqrt{s_V}}{1-s_V}.
\]

Therefore the discarded AM--GM credit is explicitly

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_V
:=W_V
\frac{a_V+s_V/a_V-2\sqrt{s_V}}{1-s_V}
= W_V\frac{(\sqrt{a_V}-\sqrt{s_V/a_V})^2}{1-s_V}
\ge0,
}
\tag{14}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{Q_{MS,V}\le c_V W_V-\Omega_V.}
\tag{15}
\]

Let `W_H` be the lower-surrogate contribution on Gittins hill sizes. There M-SERPT is also on a hill, and `Q_MS` equals that lower-surrogate contribution. Let

\[
c(\rho)=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\rho}}.
\]

Since `s_V>=1-rho`, we have `c_V<=c(rho)`. Summing (15) and the hill equality gives the refined waiting inequality

\[
\boxed{
Q_{MS}\le c(\rho)\,\underline Q_G-\mathcal W,
}
\tag{16}
\]

where `underline Q_G` is the source Proposition-4.7 Gittins waiting lower surrogate and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W=
(c(\rho)-1)W_H
+\sum_V\Big((c(\rho)-c_V)W_V+\Omega_V\Big)\ge0.
}
\tag{17}
\]

Thus both major inequalities used in the published factor-five proof have explicit, rank-sensitive slack terms which can now be carried instead of thrown away.

# New reduction: one explicit universal prefix-credit inequality would prove Conjecture 8.1

Combine (11) and (16):

\[
T_{MS}=Q_{MS}+R_{MS}
\le 2Q_{MS}+K-2\Xi
\le 2c(\rho)\underline Q_G-2\mathcal W+K-2\Xi.
\tag{18}
\]

The source lower bounds give

\[
T_G\ge \underline Q_G+E[X].
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore a sufficient arbitrary-law inequality for the full factor-two conjecture is

\[
\boxed{
\Xi+\mathcal W
\ge
(c(\rho)-1)\underline Q_G
+\left(\frac{L(\rho)}2-1\right)E[X],
}
\tag{RC}
\]

where

\[
L(\rho)=\frac1\rho\log\frac1{1-\rho}.
\]

Indeed, substituting (RC) into (18) gives

\[
T_{MS}\le2\underline Q_G+2E[X]\le2T_G.
\]

There is also an exact, slightly weaker-to-state version using the full slack `mathcal S` from (9):

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S+2\mathcal W
\ge2(c(\rho)-1)\underline Q_G+(L(\rho)-2)E[X].
}
\tag{RC-exact}
\]

The key point is that `(RC)` is **not** a finite-support statement. Every term is an explicit integral or hill/valley boundary term of the actual service law. It is the precise rank-interval charging inequality that the previous two P01-B runs were missing.

The remaining scalar deficit also has a useful age-local form:

\[
\boxed{
(L(\rho)-2)E[X]
=K-2E[X]
=\int_0^\infty \bar F(x)\left(\frac1{A(x)}-2\right)dx.
}
\tag{20}
\]

So even the only non-rank term in `(RC-exact)` is an age integral. Its integrand is negative while `A(x)>1/2` and positive only after the attained load crosses one half. This makes a genuine **prefix proof** possible: early hills can accumulate positive `Xi`, `Omega`, `D_1`, and `D_3` credit before a terminal high-load valley spends it.
