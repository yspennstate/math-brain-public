# 1. Exact tie-splitting problem

Write

`S(a)=P(X>a)`,

`N(a)=int_a^infty S(t) dt`,

`s(a)=N(a)/S(a)`

for the survival function, integrated tail and SERPT rank, and let

`A(a)=1-lambda E[min(X,a)] = 1-lambda int_0^a S(t)dt`,

`tau(a)=lambda/2 E[min(X,a)^2] = lambda int_0^a t S(t)dt`.

Consider one true M-SERPT valley `(u,v]`. Its envelope rank is constant, say `L`, and at its hill boundaries

`N(u)=L S(u)`, `N(v)=L S(v)`,

while throughout the valley

`N(x) <= L S(x)`.

Now suppose `c in (u,v)` is an **internal contact**:

`N(c)=L S(c)`.

This is precisely the kind of degenerate record contact excluded by the previous report's record-regularity hypothesis, and it is also the limiting mechanism behind R7-04's one-sided topology jump.

Compare two monotone SOAP rank topologies on the *same* service law:

1. unsplit: the whole `(u,v]` is one rank plateau;
2. split: `(u,c]` has the old plateau rank and `(c,v]` has an infinitesimally worse rank, so `c` becomes a hill age while all ordering outside `(u,v]` is unchanged.

Only jobs with sizes in `(u,v]` change their SOAP cutoffs.

# 2. New theorem: exact nonnegative rank-tie split charge

## Theorem 1 (single contact split)

For the split above, let `Delta_T(c;u,v)` be the change in mean response time (split minus unsplit). Then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_T(c;u,v)
=
\frac{A(u)-A(c)}{L A(u)A(c)}
\int_c^v \bigl(L S(x)-N(x)\bigr)\,dx
\ge 0.
}
\tag{SPLIT}
\]

It is strict exactly when `A(u)>A(c)` and the SERPT deficit `L S-N` is positive on a set of positive measure in `(c,v)`.

### Proof

For the unsplit valley, Propositions 4.7 and 4.8 give, for `x in (u,v]`,

\[
Q_0(x)=\frac{\tau(v)}{A(u)A(v)},
\qquad
R_0(x)=\frac{x}{A(u)}.
\]

After the split,

\[
Q_1(x)=\frac{\tau(c)}{A(u)A(c)},\quad
R_1(x)=\frac{x}{A(u)},
\qquad u<x\le c,
\]

and

\[
Q_1(x)=\frac{\tau(v)}{A(c)A(v)},\quad
R_1(x)=\frac{x}{A(c)},
\qquad c<x\le v.
\]

The contact identities imply

\[
A(u)-A(c)
=\lambda\int_u^c S
=\lambda L\bigl(S(u)-S(c)\bigr),
\]

\[
A(c)-A(v)
=\lambda L\bigl(S(c)-S(v)\bigr).
\]

Integrating the waiting-time difference over job sizes in the two pieces and simplifying gives the exact cancellation

\[
\Delta_Q
=-\frac{A(u)-A(c)}{A(u)A(c)}
\frac{\tau(v)-\tau(c)}{\lambda L}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Only the upper piece changes residence cutoff, hence

\[
\Delta_R
=\frac{A(u)-A(c)}{A(u)A(c)}
\int_c^v x f(x)\,dx.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since

\[
\tau(v)-\tau(c)=\lambda\int_c^v xS(x)\,dx,
\]

we have

\[
\Delta_T
=\frac{A(u)-A(c)}{A(u)A(c)}
\int_c^v x\left(f(x)-\frac{S(x)}L\right)dx.
\]

Set

\[
g(x)=L S(x)-N(x)\ge0.
\]

At both contact endpoints `c,v`, `g=0`, and almost everywhere

\[
g'(x)=-L f(x)+S(x).
\]

Therefore

\[
\int_c^v x\left(f-\frac SL\right)dx
=-\frac1L\int_c^v xg'(x)dx
=\frac1L\int_c^v g(x)dx,
\]

which proves `(SPLIT)`. `square`

This theorem explains the sign in P01-A's exact four-atom contact example: breaking an exact M-SERPT rank tie can create a positive discontinuous response penalty even though the underlying distribution changes continuously. The phenomenon is therefore not an anomalous four-atom artifact.

# 3. New rank-interval charging inequality: all refinements of one valley

Suppose a true valley `(u,v]` has ordered internal contact ages

\[
u=c_0<c_1<\cdots<c_k<v,
\qquad s(c_i)=L.
\]

Refine the plateau successively at `c_1,...,c_k`. Applying Theorem 1 inside the remaining upper plateau at each step gives

\[
\Delta_T^{\rm refine}
=
\sum_{i=1}^k
\frac{A(c_{i-1})-A(c_i)}{L A(c_{i-1})A(c_i)}
\int_{c_i}^v \bigl(LS-N\bigr)dx.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Every summand is nonnegative.

Moreover, because `0<=LS-N<=LS` and both `c_i` and `v` are contacts,

\[
\int_{c_i}^v(LS-N)dx
\le L\int_{c_i}^v S(x)dx
=\frac{L}{\lambda}\bigl(A(c_i)-A(v)\bigr).
\]

Thus

\[
\Delta_i
\le
\frac{(A(c_{i-1})-A(c_i))(A(c_i)-A(v))}
{\lambda A(c_{i-1})A(c_i)}
\le
\frac{A(c_{i-1})-A(c_i)}{\lambda A(c_{i-1})}.
\]

Using `1-z <= -log z`,

\[
\Delta_i
\le \frac1\lambda\log\frac{A(c_{i-1})}{A(c_i)}.
\]

The logs telescope, so **any finite contact refinement of one valley** obeys

\[
\boxed{
0\le \Delta_T^{\rm refine}
\le
\frac1\lambda\log\frac{A(u)}{A(v)}
=
\int_u^v \frac{S(x)}{A(x)}dx.
}
\tag{VALLEY-CHARGE}
\]

The right side is exactly the valley's logarithmic truncated-load budget appearing in the source's residence analysis. This is the requested kind of **rank-interval charging inequality**: every tie-breaking/topology cost inside a valley is paid by a single explicit source-level interval budget. It also controls countably many limiting refinements by monotone passage from finite sub-refinements.

A cruder single-contact corollary, useful for R7-04's contact-jump bookkeeping, is

\[
0\le J_c
\le
\frac{(A(u)-A(c))(A(c)-A(v))}{\lambda A(u)A(c)}
=
\frac1\lambda
\left(1-\frac{A(c)}{A(u)}\right)
\left(1-\frac{A(v)}{A(c)}\right).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This does **not** yet prove R7-04's desired `J_c <= 2T_G-T_MS`; it gives a universal local currency in which such jumps can be charged.

# 4. Consequence: degenerate M-SERPT contacts are safe for a one-sided finite-support reduction

The previous P01-B report used lower quantizers

\[
X_n=h_n\left\lfloor\frac{X\wedge M_n}{h_n}\right\rfloor,
\qquad M_n\uparrow\infty,\quad h_n\downarrow0,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and proved on every compact age interval with positive survival that

\[
s_n\to s
\quad\text{and}\quad
m_n:=\sup_{b\le a}s_n(b)\to m:=\sup_{b\le a}s(b)
\]

uniformly. It imposed a strict interior record gap so that approximating valleys could not split. `(SPLIT)` shows that this strict-gap assumption is unnecessary in the direction needed for Conjecture 8.1.

Indeed, fix a true valley `(u,v]` of level `L`. If an approximating hill age `c_n` stays a positive distance from the true valley boundaries and `c_n->c`, then necessarily

\[
s(c)=m(c)=L.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Otherwise `s(c)<=L-eta` for some `eta>0` on a neighborhood, and uniform convergence of both `s_n` and `m_n` forbids a new record there for all large `n`.

Thus every macroscopic topology defect that survives lower quantization converges to a **true contact point**. In the limit, the approximating M-SERPT topology is a refinement of the true plateau by contact points. Theorem 1 says every such refinement can only increase mean response; `(VALLEY-CHARGE)` handles an arbitrarily long sequence of contact refinements without a checker or a finite-contact assumption.

# 5. New reduction theorem: lower semicontinuity of M-SERPT under lower finite quantization

## Theorem 2 (source-class finite-support reduction)

Let `X` satisfy the 2020 paper's presentation assumptions: continuous density, stable load `rho<1`, and piecewise-monotone SERPT/hazard ranks (with the usual locally finite interpretation of piecewise monotonicity). Let `X_n` be the finite-support lower quantizers `(4.1)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
E[T_{MS}(X)]
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}E[T_{MS}(X_n)].
}
\tag{LSC-MS}
\]

No local record-regularity assumption is needed. No global `E[X^2]<infty` assumption is needed.

Together with the one-sided Gittins monotonicity lemma from the previous report,

\[
X_n\le X
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E[T_G(X_n)]\le E[T_G(X)],
\tag{MON-G}
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\text{factor two for every finite-support law}
\Longrightarrow
\text{factor two for every source-admissible continuous law}.
}
\tag{FS-REDUCE}
\]

### Proof of `(LSC-MS)`

The argument is local-to-global.

**Step A: bounded rank blocks.** Fix a compact block ending at a true hill age `K`, so every true M-SERPT valley intersecting the interior of the block is completed before `K`. On `[0,K]`, lower-quantizer convergence gives, uniformly,

\[
A_n\to A,
\qquad
\tau_n\to\tau.
\]

The second convergence needs only `L^1` convergence here because for `a<=K`,

\[
|(X_n\wedge a)^2-(X\wedge a)^2|
\le 2K|X_n-X|.
\]

On true hill interiors, uniform envelope convergence forces the approximating hill cutoffs to collapse to the same age, so the Proposition-4.7/4.8 conditional response formulas converge normally.

On a true valley, every persistent approximating hill lies asymptotically in the contact set by `(4.2)`. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, the limiting topology is therefore the original valley with an ordered contact refinement. The distributional quantities in the SOAP formulas converge at all such ages because the true law is atomless. Formula `(3.1)` shows that the limiting refined mean response contribution is at least the unsplit true-valley contribution. If the number of approximating splits is unbounded, `(VALLEY-CHARGE)` gives a uniform finite bound on all finite sub-refinements, and monotone passage in the refinement count preserves the same nonnegative comparison.

Summing the finitely many true hill/valley components in `[0,K]` gives

\[
\liminf_n E[T_{MS}(X_n);\,X_n\le K]
\ge E[T_{MS}(X);\,X\le K]
\tag{5.1}
\]

for every such completed hill cutoff `K`.

**Step B: unbounded sequence of hill cutoffs.** If true hill ages are unbounded, choose completed hill cutoffs `K_j->infty`. The total response contributions are nonnegative, so `(5.1)` and monotone convergence yield `(LSC-MS)`.

**Step C: an eventual terminal valley.** The only other case is that M-SERPT is eventually flat on `(u,infty)`. If `E[T_G(X)]=infty`, no strict factor-two counterexample can occur and there is nothing to transfer. If `E[T_G(X)]<infty`, the source's universal factor-five theorem implies `E[T_MS(X)]<infty`. But on a terminal M-SERPT valley Proposition 4.7 has `z(x)=infty` for tail jobs; positive tail mass together with `tau(infty)=infty` would force infinite M-SERPT waiting. Hence in this nontrivial case `E[X^2]<infty`. The lower quantizers then converge in `L^2`, so `tau_n(infty)->tau(infty)`, and the same contact-refinement argument extends to the final valley. This again yields `(LSC-MS)`.

This proves Theorem 2. `square`

### Factor-two transfer

Assume the factor-two conjecture has been proved for all finite-support laws. Each `X_n` is finite support and has load at most `rho`, so

\[
E[T_{MS}(X_n)]\le2E[T_G(X_n)]\le2E[T_G(X)].
\]

Taking `liminf` and applying `(LSC-MS)` gives

\[
E[T_{MS}(X)]\le2E[T_G(X)].
\]

Thus a strict arbitrary-law counterexample in the source class would force a strict finite-support counterexample. In particular, because P01-A has already proved the conjecture for all laws with at most three positive support points, any source-class counterexample must have finite-support shadows with at least four positive atoms.

# 6. Why this is different from the previous reduction

The previous theorem required two extra hypotheses solely to make every hill/valley boundary converge literally:

1. a strict interior record gap (`locally record-regular`), and
2. `E[X^2]<infty` for global domination.

The new direction is weaker and exactly what the conjecture needs: **lower semicontinuity**, not equality. Degenerate ties may cause the approximants' M-SERPT response not to converge to the tie-law response. Theorem 1 identifies the discontinuity and proves its sign is favorable:

\[
T_{MS}(\text{tie law})
\le
\liminf T_{MS}(\text{tie-broken approximants}).
\]

Nonnegativity plus compact localization removes the need for global second-moment domination; only the terminal-flat case invokes a second moment, and there it follows automatically whenever the comparison to finite `T_G` is nontrivial.

# 7. Interaction with P01-A / R7-04

R7-04's newest exact four-atom result showed that terminal-stretch slack is increasing inside stabilized cells but can drop at an M-SERPT record contact. Theorem 1 supplies the missing **generic symbolic jump mechanism** on the limiting law: a contact jump is an integral of the SERPT deficit `LS-N` against a positive load prefactor. It is therefore always nonnegative when a plateau is refined, and `(3.2)` / `(VALLEY-CHARGE)` give universal upper charges.

This does not duplicate R7-04's finite-support induction. It changes the general-proof burden:

- P01-B no longer needs to solve topology continuity for arbitrary laws first;

- if finite-support factor two is completed, Theorem 2 transfers it to the full continuous source class.
