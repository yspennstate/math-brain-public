## 1. Notation and the exact relaxation that should not be made

Write

\[
S(x)=P(X>x),\qquad N(x)=E[(X-x)^+]=\int_x^\infty S(t)\,dt,
\]
\[
A(x)=\bar\rho(x)=1-\lambda E[X\wedge x]
      =c+\lambda N(x),\qquad c:=1-\rho,
\]
\[
\tau(x)=\frac\lambda2 E[(X\wedge x)^2],\qquad
r(x):=r_S(x)=\frac{N(x)}{S(x)}.
\]

Fix a Gittins valley `V=(u,v]`. By source Lemma 5.3, both Gittins hill endpoints are M-SERPT hill ages. The source proof of Lemma 5.4 first establishes, for any M-SERPT hill age `b>=a`,

\[
\frac{A(b)}{A(a)}
\ge
\zeta+(1-\zeta)\frac{c}{A(a)},
\qquad
\zeta=\frac{S(b)}{S(a)}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Only after `(1.1)` does the paper use `A(a)<=1`, replacing `c/A(a)` by `c`. Section 8.1 explicitly points to this step as a source of avoidable looseness.

For the valley left endpoint `u`, do **not** make that replacement.

## 2. Local residual-load bridge

Define the valley-local bridge

\[
\boxed{
G_u(x):=
\frac{c}{A(u)}+
\left(1-\frac{c}{A(u)}\right)\frac{S(x)}{S(u)}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus `G_u(u)=1`. Equation `(1.1)` with `a=u` gives, for every M-SERPT hill age `b>=u`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A(u)}{A(b)}\le \frac1{G_u(b)}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is exactly the inequality used as source equation (5.2), but with the actual residual load at `u` retained.

The second key feature is that the density change of variables used in Lemma 5.6 survives unchanged. For a density presentation,

\[
-g_u(x):=G_u'(x)
= -\left(1-\frac c{A(u)}\right)\frac{f(x)}{S(u)},
\]
so, with `g_u(x)=-G_u'(x)>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{f(x)}{S(u)-S(v)}
=
\frac{g_u(x)}{G_u(u)-G_u(v)}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Hence every telescoping integral in source equations (5.8)--(5.10) goes through verbatim with `G_u` replacing the source's global bridge `1-rho+rho S(x)/S(u)`. In Stieltjes form `(2.3)` is simply the corresponding measure identity, so the algebra is not tied to finite support.

Put

\[
a:=\frac{A(v)}{A(u)},\qquad
\gamma:=G_u(v).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Because `v` is a Gittins hill and therefore an M-SERPT hill, `(2.2)` gives

\[
0<\gamma\le a\le1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

For the continuous source presentation, choose `x_*` with `G_u(x_*)=a`, exactly as the source chooses its equation-(5.3) point. Let

\[
y_*=y_{MS}(x_*),\qquad z_*=z_{MS}(x_*),
\]
\[
Y:=G_u(y_*),\qquad Z:=G_u(z_*).
\]
Then

\[
1\ge Y\ge a\ge Z\ge\gamma.
\tag{2.6}
\]

## 3. A sharpened valley theorem

Running the source three-piece calculation with `G_u` yields, before the final AM--GM maximization,

\[
q_V
\le
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\left(
\frac aY-a+1-\frac ZY+\frac Za-\frac\gamma a
\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The same factorization found in the preceding P01-B run now gives

\[
q_V\le 1+\eta_V^{\rm loc}-\chi_V^{\rm loc},
\tag{3.2}
\]
where

\[
\boxed{
\eta_V^{\rm loc}
:=\frac{(1-a)(a-\gamma)}{a(1-\gamma)}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\chi_V^{\rm loc}
:=\frac{(1-a/Y)(1-Z/a)}{1-\gamma}\ge0.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

As before, `0<=chi_loc<=eta_loc`. Define

\[
\varepsilon_V^{\rm loc}=\eta_V^{\rm loc}-\chi_V^{\rm loc}.
\]
Then the exact two-outer-gap decomposition is

\[
\boxed{
(1-\gamma)\varepsilon_V^{\rm loc}
=
\frac{(1-Y)(a-\gamma)}Y
+
\frac{(Y-a)(Z-\gamma)}{aY}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the previous outer-mismatch theorem survives, but its positive coefficient is now evaluated at the **actual local residual load**, not at the queue's initial load.

If the source's first `tau(z_MS(x))<=tau(v)` relaxation is also retained as a credit,

\[
C_{\tau,V}:=
\int_u^v
\frac{\tau(v)-\tau(z_{MS}(x))}
{A(y_{MS}(x))A(z_{MS}(x))}\,dF(x)\ge0,
\tag{3.6}
\]
then, with

\[
\underline Q_{G,V}:=
\frac{(S(u)-S(v))\tau(v)}{A(u)A(v)},
\]
we obtain

\[
\boxed{
Q_{MS,V}
\le
(1+\varepsilon_V^{\rm loc})\underline Q_{G,V}
-C_{\tau,V}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is a strict source-level improvement at every noninitial valley for which the old relaxation was strict.

## 4. Local-load conditional form: a direct realization of the source's Section 8 suggestion

Define the residual-load fraction at age `u`

\[
\theta(u):=1-\frac c{A(u)}
=\frac{A(u)-c}{A(u)}
=\frac{\lambda N(u)}{A(u)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
\gamma
=1-\theta(u)\left(1-\frac{S(v)}{S(u)}\right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
\theta'(u)=-\frac{c\lambda S(u)}{A(u)^2}\le0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

So the effective load parameter is monotone decreasing with age. This gives a quantitative version of the source's observation that the `A(u)<=1` relaxation cannot remain tight over many successive valleys.

Maximizing `(3.3)` over `a in [gamma,1]` gives

\[
\boxed{
q_V\le \frac{2}{1+\sqrt\gamma}
=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\theta(u)(1-S(v)/S(u))}}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The published source coefficient is recovered only after the two further relaxations

\[
\theta(u)\le\rho,
\qquad
1-S(v)/S(u)\le1.
\]

Thus the source's proposed idea of conditioning on valley mass can be sharpened to an exact local parameter: the product of **remaining normalized load** and **relative tail mass lost across that valley**.

## 5. New exact chord-defect identity

The local coefficient `(3.3)` has a much more revealing form.

Let

\[
p:=S(u)-S(v),
\qquad
I:=\int_u^v S(x)\,dx=N(u)-N(v),
\qquad
k_V:=\frac{I}{p}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus `k_V` is the time-per-completion chord over the valley endpoints. Since

\[
N(u)=N(v)+I,
\]
we have the exact identity

\[
r(u)S(u)=r(v)S(v)+k_Vp,
\]
so

\[
\boxed{
S(v)\,[r(v)-r(u)]
=p\,[r(u)-k_V].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Also

\[
1-a=\frac{\lambda I}{A(u)},
\]
\[
a-\gamma
=\frac{\lambda S(v)[r(v)-r(u)]}{A(u)},
\]
\[
1-\gamma
=\frac{\lambda r(u)p}{A(u)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Substituting `(5.2)--(5.3)` into `(3.3)` gives the exact factorization

\[
\boxed{
\eta_V^{\rm loc}
=
\frac{A(u)-A(v)}{A(v)}
\left(1-\frac{k_V}{r(u)}\right).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is the main structural gain of the run. The positive waiting penalty is not a mysterious global coefficient. It is the product of

1. the **local load drop** across the valley, and
2. the **relative Gittins-chord defect** against the left-end SERPT rank.

For a Gittins valley whose endpoint chord is the active Gittins stopping chord, the second factor is exactly the relative gap between the Gittins stopping rank and the SERPT terminal-stopping rank at the left endpoint. It vanishes in the aligned case.

Equation `(5.4)` is support-size independent.

## 6. The remaining waiting excess is a Stieltjes transport rectangle

Multiply `(5.4)` by the Gittins waiting surrogate contribution. Equivalently, using the middle representation in `(5.3)`, set

\[
\delta_V:=\frac{r(v)-r(u)}{r(u)}\ge0
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
B_V
:=
\frac{S(v)\tau(v)[A(u)-A(v)]}
{A(u)A(v)^2}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then exact algebra gives

\[
\boxed{
\eta_V^{\rm loc}\,\underline Q_{G,V}
=B_V\,\delta_V.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Now introduce the coordinate and weight

\[
\mathcal Y(x):=\frac1{A(x)},
\qquad
h(x):=\frac{S(x)\tau(x)}{A(x)}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Because

\[
\mathcal Y(v)-\mathcal Y(u)
=\frac{A(u)-A(v)}{A(u)A(v)},
\]
we get the especially simple form

\[
\boxed{
B_V=h(v)\,[\mathcal Y(v)-\mathcal Y(u)].
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore the entire uncredited local waiting defect is a right-endpoint Stieltjes rectangle times a relative SERPT-rank increment:

\[
\boxed{
\eta_V^{\rm loc}\underline Q_{G,V}
=
h(v)\,\Delta\mathcal Y_V\,
\frac{\Delta r_V}{r(u)}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Including the central-plateau and `tau` credits gives the global sharpened waiting statement

\[
\boxed{
Q_{MS}-\underline Q_G
\le
\sum_{V\in\mathcal V_G}
 h(v)\Delta\mathcal Y_V\frac{\Delta r_V}{r(u)}
-
\sum_V \chi_V^{\rm loc}\underline Q_{G,V}
-
\mathcal C_\tau.
}
\tag{GW-local}
\]

This is strictly more informative than the previous report's `E_out-C_tau`: every positive term is now an explicit product of two monotone-coordinate increments and one endpoint weight.

There is a second useful form because `theta=1-c mathcalY`:

\[
\boxed{
B_V\delta_V
=
\frac{h(v)}c\,[\theta(u)-\theta(v)]
\frac{r(v)-r(u)}{r(u)}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Thus the bad waiting term is a cross-increment between a monotone **decreasing residual-load potential** and an **increasing M-SERPT record rank**.

## 7. Exact match to the inherited residence-credit coordinate

The preceding P01-B residence decomposition defined the nonnegative half-credit

\[
\Xi
=
\int_{\mathcal H_{MS}}
\frac{\lambda S(x)^2\tau(x)}{A(x)^3}\,dx
+
\sum_{(y,z]\in\mathcal V_{MS}}
\frac{S(z)\tau(z)[A(y)-A(z)]}
{A(y)A(z)^2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Using `(6.4)` and

\[
d\mathcal Y(x)=\frac{\lambda S(x)}{A(x)^2}\,dx,
\]
its hill part is exactly

\[
\int_{\mathcal H_{MS}}h(x)\,d\mathcal Y(x),
\tag{7.2}
\]
while every M-SERPT valley contribution is exactly

\[
\boxed{
D_{2,(y,z]}
=h(z)\,[\mathcal Y(z)-\mathcal Y(y)].
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Compare `(7.3)` with `(6.5)`: **the new Gittins-valley penalty rectangle and the inherited M-SERPT residence-credit rectangle are the same geometric object in the same Stieltjes coordinate.** The difference is purely which partition supplies the right endpoint, plus the multiplicative relative-rank jump `delta_V`.

This is the distributional reduction I was looking for. The remaining queueing problem has been converted into a partition-transport problem:

> compare coarse Gittins-valley rectangles
> \[
> h(v)\Delta\mathcal Y_V\,\Delta r_V/r(u)
> \]
> against the M-SERPT hill/valley Stieltjes accumulation
> \[
> \int h\,d\mathcal Y
> \quad\text{and}\quad
> h(z)\Delta\mathcal Y,
> \]
> while retaining the already-positive central and `tau` credits.

This is exactly compatible with P01-A's newest last-record theorem: for finite laws, Gittins cannot skip the final M-SERPT record, so the partition mismatch is a **prefix** phenomenon rather than a terminal one.

4. **Exact transport identity.** Equations `(6.3)--(6.7)` rewrite the positive waiting penalty as a Stieltjes rectangle in `mathcalY=1/A`.
5. **Coordinate match to residence slack.** Equations `(7.2)--(7.3)` show the inherited residence credit lives in the identical Stieltjes geometry.

These are algebraic theorems; no new finite checker is needed because there is no finite enumeration or numerical certificate in the argument.

### Not proved

I have **not** proved the universal rectangle domination

\[
\sum_V h(v)\Delta\mathcal Y_V\frac{\Delta r_V}{r(u)}
\le \Xi+\text{available credits}.
\]

It would be false to identify `B_V` with a single `D_2` term in general: a Gittins valley may contain several M-SERPT hills and valleys. That tempting shortcut was explicitly rejected. The new theorem instead shows what must be transported across the two partitions.

I also do not claim Conjecture 8.1 is closed. The exact all-load arbitrary-law factor-two statement remains open in this repository pending this global transport step (or a different route).
