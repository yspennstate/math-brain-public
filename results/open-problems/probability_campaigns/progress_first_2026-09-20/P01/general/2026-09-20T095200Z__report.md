where

## 2. Two monotone coordinates

Define the normalized residual load

\[
\boxed{\theta(x):=1-\frac c{A(x)}=\frac{\lambda N(x)}{A(x)}}
\tag{2.1}
\]

and, at M-SERPT hill ages, the reciprocal SERPT rank

\[
\boxed{p(x):=\frac1{r(x)}=\frac{S(x)}{N(x)}}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Because `A=c+lambda N`, we have the exact inverse relations

\[
A(x)=\frac c{1-\theta(x)},
\qquad
N(x)=\frac{c\theta(x)}{\lambda(1-\theta(x))}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The function `theta` is nonincreasing. In the continuous source presentation,

\[
\boxed{
\theta'(x)
=-\frac{c\lambda S(x)}{A(x)^2}
=-\theta(x)(1-\theta(x))\,p_{raw}(x),
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
where `p_raw=1/r`.

Let

\[
m(x):=r_{MS}(x)=\sup_{0\le a\le x}r(a),
\qquad
\bar p(x):=\frac1{m(x)}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Then `m` is nondecreasing and `bar p` is nonincreasing. At every M-SERPT hill age, `m=r`, hence `bar p=p`. In particular, for every Gittins valley endpoint,

\[
\bar p(u)=p(u),\qquad \bar p(v)=p(v).
\tag{2.6}
\]

This gives a pair `(theta,bar p)` of globally monotone coordinates in which every outer Gittins/M-SERPT mismatch is a rectangle.

## 3. New theorem I: exact harmonic factorization of the local coefficient

Write

\[
\theta_u=\theta(u),\quad \theta_v=\theta(v),
\qquad
p_u=p(u),\quad p_v=p(v).
\]

Because `r(v)>=r(u)` at the Gittins-valley endpoints,

\[
\theta_u\ge\theta_v,\qquad p_u\ge p_v.
\]

Define two nonnegative dimensionless gaps

\[
\boxed{
a_V:=\frac{\theta_u-\theta_v}{1-\theta_u}}
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
b_V:=\theta_v\left(1-\frac{p_v}{p_u}\right).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1

The local coefficient `(1.1)` factors exactly as the harmonic product

\[
\boxed{
\eta_V=\frac{a_Vb_V}{a_V+b_V}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Moreover, if

\[
C_V:=\frac{\tau(v)p_u(1-\theta_u)}{\lambda c},
\tag{3.4}
\]
then

\[
\boxed{
\underline Q_{G,V}=C_V(a_V+b_V)}
\tag{3.5}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_V=C_Va_Vb_V
=\frac{\tau(v)\theta_v}{\lambda c}
(\theta_u-\theta_v)(p_u-p_v).}
\tag{RECT}
\]

### Proof

From `(2.3)`,

\[
\frac{A(u)-A(v)}{A(v)}
=\frac{\theta_u-\theta_v}{1-\theta_u}=a_V.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Also

\[
S(u)-S(v)
=p_uN(u)-p_vN(v)
=p_u[N(u)-N(v)]+N(v)(p_u-p_v).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Hence

\[
1-\frac{k_V}{r(u)}
=1-p_uk_V
=\frac{N(v)(p_u-p_v)}
{p_u[N(u)-N(v)]+N(v)(p_u-p_v)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Now

\[
a_V=\frac{\lambda[N(u)-N(v)]}{A(v)},
\qquad
b_V=\frac{\lambda N(v)(p_u-p_v)}{A(v)p_u}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Substitution of `(3.9)` into `(3.8)` gives

\[
1-p_uk_V=\frac{b_V}{a_V+b_V},
\]
which together with `(3.6)` proves `(3.3)`.

Using `(2.3)` in `(1.4)` and `(3.7)` yields `(3.5)`. Multiplying `(3.3)` and `(3.5)` gives `C_Va_Vb_V`, and substituting `(3.1)--(3.4)` gives `(RECT)`. QED.

This factorization is useful because it shows that the published AM--GM loss is literally a **cross-variation term**: one factor is residual-load drop and the other is reciprocal-rank drop. Neither factor alone causes waiting loss.

## 4. New theorem II: Stieltjes rectangle transport through the actual M-SERPT envelope

The rectangle in `(RECT)` can be decomposed along the monotone M-SERPT envelope rather than charged as a single endpoint object.

### Theorem 4.1 — rank-rectangle transport

For every Gittins valley `V=(u,v]`,

\[
\boxed{
(\theta_u-\theta_v)(p_u-p_v)
=
\int_{(u,v]}(\bar p(x)-p_v)(-d\theta(x))
+
\int_{(u,v]}(\theta(x)-\theta_v)(-d\bar p(x)).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
D_V=\frac{\tau(v)\theta_v}{\lambda c}
\left[
\int_{(u,v]}(\bar p-p_v)(-d\theta)
+
\int_{(u,v]}(\theta-\theta_v)(-d\bar p)
\right].}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The measure `-d bar p` is supported on M-SERPT hills; it vanishes on every strict M-SERPT valley. Thus the second term in `(4.2)` charges only actual M-SERPT rank increases, while the first charges residual-load transport across the whole Gittins valley.

### Proof

Set

\[
F(x)=(\theta(x)-\theta_v)(\bar p(x)-p_v).
\]

Both factors have bounded variation and are continuous in the source's density presentation, so Stieltjes integration by parts gives

\[
dF=(\bar p-p_v)d\theta+(\theta-\theta_v)d\bar p.
\]

At `x=v`, `F(v)=0`. By `(2.6)`,

\[
F(u)=(\theta_u-\theta_v)(p_u-p_v).
\]

Integrating from `u` to `v` and changing signs proves `(4.1)`. Equation `(4.2)` follows from `(RECT)`. The support statement follows from the definition of the nondecreasing envelope `m`: on an M-SERPT valley, `m` is constant and hence `bar p=1/m` is constant. QED.

This is the support-independent transport statement sought by P01-B. In the finite-law picture, a skipped prefix record creates a literal rectangle. In the arbitrary-law picture, the same rectangle is the sum of two positive Stieltjes areas traced by the continuous M-SERPT envelope.

## 5. Global mismatch-energy budget

Let `mathcal V_G` be the disjoint Gittins valleys. Because both coordinates are monotone, `(4.1)` gives a simple global area budget.

### Corollary 5.1

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{V\in\mathcal V_G}
(\theta_{u_V}-\theta_{v_V})(p_{u_V}-p_{v_V})
\le \frac\rho\mu,
\qquad \mu=E[X].}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Indeed, each integrand in `(4.1)` is bounded above by `bar p` and `theta`, respectively. Summing over disjoint valleys and enlarging to the full age line gives

\[
\sum_V\Delta\theta_V\Delta p_V
\le
\int_0^\infty \bar p(-d\theta)
+
\int_0^\infty \theta(-d\bar p).
\]

The right side is the total decrease of `theta bar p`. Since

\[
\theta(0)=\rho,\qquad
\bar p(0)=\frac1\mu,\qquad
0\le \bar p(x)\le\frac1\mu,\qquad
\theta(x)\downarrow0,
\]
we obtain `(5.1)`.

Combining `(RECT)` with `(5.1)` gives the global arbitrary-law waiting-defect estimate

\[
\boxed{
\sum_V\eta_V\underline Q_{G,V}
\le
\frac{1}{1-\rho}
\sup_{x\ge0}\{\tau(x)\theta(x)\}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

To see the cancellation, use `lambda mu=rho`:

\[
\frac{\sup(\tau\theta)}{\lambda c}\frac\rho\mu
=\frac{\sup(\tau\theta)}c.
\]

Since the actual coefficient in `(1.3)` is `epsilon_V<=eta_V` and `C_tau,V>=0`, `(5.2)` yields the support-independent theorem

\[
\boxed{
Q_{MS}
\le
\underline Q_G
+rac{\sup_x\tau(x)\theta(x)}{1-\rho}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equation `(5.3)` is not the conjecture, but it is a genuine distributional reduction: all support complexity has disappeared into one scalar transport energy `sup tau theta`.

## 7. New theorem III: Pareto laws force `K<0` at high stable load when `1<alpha<3/2`

Take the scale-one Pareto law

\[
P(X>x)=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le x<1,\\
x^{-\alpha},&x\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad 1<\alpha<2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Its mean is

\[
\mu=\frac\alpha{\alpha-1},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\rho(\alpha-1)}\alpha.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The raw SERPT rank is

\[
r(x)=\mu-x,\qquad 0\le x<1,
\]

and

\[
r(x)=\frac{x}{\alpha-1},\qquad x\ge1.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Hence M-SERPT has one valley from zero through `x=alpha`, followed by a hill:

\[
r_{MS}(x)=\mu\quad(0\le x\le\alpha),
\qquad
r_{MS}(x)=\frac{x}{\alpha-1}\quad(x\ge\alpha).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Put `c=1-rho`. For `x>=1`,

\[
A(x)=c+\frac\rho\alpha x^{1-\alpha},
\tag{7.5}
\]

and

\[
\tau(x)
=\lambda\left[
\frac12+\frac{x^{2-\alpha}-1}{2-\alpha}
\right].
\tag{7.6}
\]

Because the M-SERPT rank is monotone, the SOAP waiting/residence bounds used by the source are equalities. The valley pieces `x<=alpha` stay uniformly `O(1)` as `rho->1`. On the terminal hill,

\[
R_H
=\int_\alpha^\infty
\frac{\alpha x^{-\alpha}}{A(x)}\,dx.
\]

Since `A'(x)=-lambda x^{-alpha}`,

\[
\boxed{
R_H
=\frac\alpha\lambda
\log\frac{A(\alpha)}c
=\frac{\alpha^2}{\rho(\alpha-1)}
\log\frac1c+O(1).}
\tag{7.7}
\]

For waiting,

\[
Q_H
=\int_\alpha^\infty
\frac{\tau(x)}{A(x)^2}
\alpha x^{-\alpha-1}\,dx.
\tag{7.8}
\]

The constant part of `(7.6)` contributes only `O(1)`. For the leading `x^{2-alpha}` part, set

\[
y=x^{1-\alpha},\qquad d=\frac\rho\alpha.
\]

Then

\[
\alpha\int_\alpha^\infty
\frac{x^{1-2\alpha}}{(c+dx^{1-\alpha})^2}\,dx
=
\frac\alpha{\alpha-1}
\int_0^{\alpha^{1-\alpha}}
\frac{y}{(c+dy)^2}\,dy.
\tag{7.9}
\]

The last integral is elementary:

\[
\int\frac{y}{(c+dy)^2}dy
=\frac1{d^2}\left(
\log(c+dy)+\frac c{c+dy}
\right).
\]

Using `(7.6)` therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
Q_H
=\frac{\alpha^2}{\rho(2-\alpha)}
\log\frac1c+O(1).}
\tag{7.10}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
K=Q_{MS}+2\mu-R_{MS}
=
\frac{\alpha^2(2\alpha-3)}
{(2-\alpha)(\alpha-1)}
\log\frac1{1-\rho}
+O(1),
\qquad \rho\uparrow1.}
\tag{7.11}
\]

(The harmless factor `1/rho` in the separate logarithmic coefficients tends to one and is absorbed in the `O(1)` normalization of `(7.11)`.)

Therefore, for every

\[
\boxed{1<\alpha<\frac32,}
\]

there exists `rho_alpha<1` such that for all `rho in (rho_alpha,1)`,

\[
\boxed{Q_{MS}+2E[X]-R_{MS}<0.}
\tag{7.12}
\]

This is a counterexample to the **proof obligation** `(6.1)`, not to Conjecture 8.1. It proves that the positive `K_3` phenomenon established by P01-A cannot be promoted blindly from finite three-block laws to arbitrary service distributions.

## 8. The rectangle energy detects exactly the same heavy-tail threshold

For the same Pareto family, at any fixed stable load and `x->infinity`, equations `(7.5)--(7.6)` imply

\[
\theta(x)
\sim
\frac{\rho}{\alpha c}x^{1-\alpha},
\qquad
\tau(x)
\sim
\frac{\rho(\alpha-1)}{\alpha(2-\alpha)}x^{2-\alpha}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\tau(x)\theta(x)
\sim
\frac{\rho^2(\alpha-1)}
{\alpha^2(2-\alpha)c}
 x^{3-2\alpha}.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus the scalar transport energy in `(5.2)` is finite in the Pareto tail exactly on the `alpha>=3/2` side and diverges for `1<alpha<3/2`, the same threshold at which `(7.11)` changes sign.

This agreement is not a proof of Conjecture 8.1, but it is strong structural information: the reciprocal-rank transport route is naturally the **finite transport-energy** side of the problem, while very heavy tails require a different source lower bound.

## 9. Consequence for the general proof architecture

The previous finite-law-style master target

\[
Q_{MS}+2\mu-R_{MS}
\ge 2(\text{outer waiting defect})
\]

is now ruled out as a universal arbitrary-law statement. Any full proof must use more information about Gittins than the pair of lower bounds `Q_G>=underline Q_G` and `R_G>=mu` in the heavy-tail regime.

Therefore the next general attack should split the proof according to transport energy rather than according to support size:

1. **finite transport-energy regime:** use `(4.2)` to charge the two Stieltjes areas against the exact M-SERPT residence credit `Xi`, the retained `C_tau` waiting credit, and the unused `D1+D3` contact-adaptive reserve;

The Pareto calculation proves that this is not optional bookkeeping: a proof that discards the logarithmic lower bound cannot work on all source-admissible laws.
