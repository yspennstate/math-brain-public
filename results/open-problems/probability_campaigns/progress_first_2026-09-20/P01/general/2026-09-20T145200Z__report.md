## 1. Inherited exact master inequality and the precise missing universal inequality

Write

\[
S(x)=P(X>x),\qquad N(x)=E[(X-x)^+],
\]
\[
A(x)=1-\lambda E[X\wedge x]=c+\lambda N(x),\qquad c:=1-\rho,
\]
\[
\tau(x)=\frac\lambda2E[(X\wedge x)^2],\qquad \mu=E[X],
\]

and

\[
\mathcal L:=\int_0^\infty\frac{S(x)}{A(x)}dx
=\frac1\lambda\log\frac1c.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Let `Qbar_G` denote the source Proposition-4.7 Gittins waiting lower surrogate. Previous P01-B work proved the support-independent rank-refinement charge

\[
Q_{MS}-\underline Q_G\le \Xi-\mathcal B_G,
\qquad \mathcal B_G\ge0,
\tag{1.2}
\]

and the exact retained M-SERPT residence decomposition

\[
Q_{MS}+\mathcal L-R_{MS}=2\Xi+\Lambda,
\qquad \Lambda\ge0.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T_{MS}\le 2\underline Q_G+\mathcal L-2\mathcal B_G-\Lambda.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The previous run also proved the distribution-sensitive Gittins residence lower bound

\[
R_G\ge \mathcal R_G^{HS},
\tag{1.5}
\]

where, for Gittins hills `H_G` and valleys `V=(u,v]`,

\[
\mathcal R_G^{HS}
=
\int_{\mathcal H_G}\frac{S(a)}{A(a)}da
+
\sum_{V=(u,v]}
\int_u^v
\left[S(a)-S(v)+\frac{S(v)}{A(v)}\right]da.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Thus

\[
T_G\ge \underline Q_G+\mathcal R_G^{HS}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

The strongest natural one-branch target left by that result is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L-2\mathcal R_G^{HS}
\le 2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda.
}
\tag{HS1}
\]

If `(HS1)` holds, then `(1.4)` and `(1.7)` immediately give `T_MS<=2T_G`. The goal of this run was to understand exactly where the left side of `(HS1)` can be positive and to pay a nontrivial part of it.

## 2. Exact valley formula for the hill-survival deficit

Fix a Gittins valley

\[
V=(u,v],
\]

and abbreviate

\[
U=A(u),\qquad V_0=A(v),\qquad s_v=S(v),\qquad \ell=v-u.
\]

For a terminal valley take `v=infinity` and `s_v=0` in the limiting sense.

From `(1.6)`, the valley contribution to `R_G^{HS}` is

\[
\mathcal R^{HS}_V
=
\int_u^v S(x)dx
+\ell s_v\left(\frac1{V_0}-1\right).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because

\[
U-V_0=\lambda\int_u^v S(x)dx,
\tag{2.2}
\]

while

\[
\mathcal L_V
=\int_u^v\frac{S(x)}{A(x)}dx
=\frac1\lambda\log\frac U{V_0},
\tag{2.3}
\]

the exact local hill-survival deficit is

\[
\boxed{
D_V:=\mathcal L_V-2\mathcal R^{HS}_V
=
\frac1\lambda
\left[
\log\frac U{V_0}-2(U-V_0)
\right]
-2\ell s_v\left(\frac1{V_0}-1\right).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
D_V
=
\frac1\lambda\int_{V_0}^{U}\left(\frac1t-2\right)dt
-2\ell s_v\left(\frac1{V_0}-1\right).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

On a Gittins hill interval, the contribution to `L-2R_HS` is simply

\[
-\int_{\mathcal H_G}\frac{S(x)}{A(x)}dx\le0.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Hence `(2.5)` is an exact localization of every possible positive residence deficit.

## 3. Main new theorem I: positive deficit is confined to the post-half-load tail

The endpoint-survival term in `(2.5)` is nonpositive. Therefore

\[
D_V
\le
\frac1\lambda\int_{V_0}^{U}\left(\frac1t-2\right)dt.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If

\[
A(v)=V_0\ge\frac12,
\]
then `1/t-2<=0` throughout `[V_0,U]`, and therefore

\[
\boxed{D_V\le0.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

So **no Gittins valley ending before the residual-capacity coordinate `A=1/2` can create any positive deficit in `(HS1)`**. The only possible positive contribution comes from valleys whose right endpoint lies in the suffix `A<1/2`.

This gives a global scalar budget. The load-coordinate intervals `[A(v),A(u)]` of distinct Gittins valleys are disjoint up to endpoints because `A` is nonincreasing in age. Hence, after discarding the nonpositive hill terms and the nonpositive endpoint-survival terms,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L-2\mathcal R_G^{HS}
\le
\frac1\lambda\Psi(c),
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\Psi(c)
:=
\int_c^{1/2}\left(\frac1t-2\right)_+dt
=
\begin{cases}
0,&c\ge1/2,\\[1mm]
\log\dfrac1{2c}-1+2c,&0<c<1/2.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This statement has no support-cardinality parameter.

### Additive factor-two excess bound

Subtract `2(1.7)` from `(1.4)`. Exactly,

\[
T_{MS}-2T_G
\le
\mathcal L-2\mathcal R_G^{HS}
-2\mathcal B_G-\Lambda.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Combining `(3.3)` and nonnegativity of the reserves yields the all-load arbitrary-law additive estimate

\[
\boxed{
T_{MS}
\le
2T_G+rac{\Psi(1-\rho)}\lambda.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The point of `(3.6)` is not to replace the conjectured multiplicative factor two. Its useful content is structural: **every possible violation of factor two is paid entirely out of a one-dimensional post-half-load budget**. Before the age at which `A` drops below `1/2`, the new Gittins residence lower bound already pays the logarithmic term with room to spare.

The earlier P01-B theorem already proves exact factor two for every distribution through `rho=0.796812130020020...`, so `(3.2)--(3.6)` are most relevant to the genuinely high-load residual problem rather than as a new low-load threshold.

## 4. Main new theorem II: a common terminal valley is paid under an explicit source-level condition

The hardest scalar part of `(3.3)` is terminal-like. We can pay a broad terminal class directly from the inherited `Lambda=D1+D3` reserve.

Assume the terminal Gittins valley

\[
(u,\infty)
\]

is also one M-SERPT terminal valley, so there is no further M-SERPT record after `u`. Put

\[
U=A(u),\qquad c=A(\infty)=1-\rho,\qquad d:=U-c=\lambda N(u).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The terminal Gittins endpoint has zero survival, so `(2.4)` becomes

\[
\boxed{
D_\infty
=\frac1\lambda\left[\log\frac Uc-2d\right].
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the same M-SERPT terminal valley, the exact `D3` term from the residence decomposition is

\[
D_{3,\infty}
=
\int_u^\infty\frac{S(x)}{A(x)}
\left(1-\frac{A(x)}U\right)dx
=
\boxed{
\frac1\lambda\left[\log\frac Uc-\frac dU\right].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_\infty-D_{3,\infty}
=
\frac d\lambda\left(\frac1U-2\right).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

In particular, if `U>=1/2`, `D3` alone pays the entire terminal deficit.

For `U<1/2`, use the exact terminal `D1` term

\[
D_{1,\infty}
=
\frac{S(u)}U
\left(\frac{\tau(\infty)}c-\frac{\tau(u)}U\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because `U>c` and `tau(infinity)>=tau(u)`,

\[
D_{1,\infty}
\ge
\frac{S(u)}{Uc}\,[\tau(\infty)-\tau(u)].
\tag{4.6}
\]

Let `Y=(X-u)^+`. The truncated second-moment identity gives

\[
\tau(\infty)-\tau(u)
=
\lambda uN(u)+\frac\lambda2E[Y^2].
\tag{4.7}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz on the tail event gives

\[
E[Y^2]\ge\frac{N(u)^2}{S(u)}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Substituting `N(u)=d/lambda` into `(4.6)--(4.8)` yields

\[
\boxed{
D_{1,\infty}
\ge
\frac d{\lambda Uc}
\left[\lambda uS(u)+\frac d2\right].
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Combining `(4.4)` and `(4.9)` proves the following.

> **TERMINAL-VALLEY CLOSURE.** If a common Gittins/M-SERPT terminal valley starts at `u` and
> \[
> \boxed{
> 2\lambda uS(u)+U(1+4c)\ge3c,
> }
> \tag{TV}
> \]
> then
> \[
> \boxed{D_\infty\le D_{1,\infty}+D_{3,\infty}.}
> \tag{4.10}
> \]

A simple distribution-free sufficient condition for `(TV)` is

\[
\boxed{
U=A(u)\ge \frac{3c}{1+4c}.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

For `U>=1/2`, `(4.10)` follows already from `D3`; `(TV)` is only needed to push the proof deeper into the high-load tail.

## 5. An all-load structural factor-two theorem

The preceding pieces combine without any finite-support assumption.

> **THEOREM.** Assume the source's regular hill/valley presentation. Suppose:
>
> 1. every nonterminal Gittins valley `(a,b]` satisfies `A(b)>=1/2`; and
> 2. if Gittins has a terminal valley `(u,infinity)`, it is also an M-SERPT terminal valley and satisfies `(TV)`.
>
> Then, at every stable load,
> \[
> \boxed{E[T_{MS}]\le2E[T_G].}
> \]

**Proof.** By `(3.2)`, every nonterminal Gittins valley contributes nonpositively to `L-2R_G^{HS}`; Gittins hill pieces are also nonpositive by `(2.6)`. If there is no terminal valley, `(HS1)` holds trivially because its left side is nonpositive. If there is a terminal valley, `(4.10)` pays its entire positive deficit from the `D1+D3` part of `Lambda`. All remaining pieces of `Lambda` and all of `2B_G` are nonnegative. Thus `(HS1)` holds, and `(1.4),(1.7)` give factor two. QED.

This is a genuinely all-load arbitrary-law theorem for a structural class, not a heavy-traffic statement and not a three-atom theorem.

## 6. What this says about any remaining counterexample

The full conjecture is not proved. However, the general obstruction is now materially narrower.

Within the source presentation class, a counterexample to factor two must have **at least one** of the following features:

1. a nonterminal Gittins valley whose right endpoint lies beyond the half-load age, `A(v)<1/2`;
2. a terminal Gittins valley that contains a further strict M-SERPT record/hill, so it is not a common terminal valley;
3. a common terminal valley whose start lies so deep in the tail that `(TV)` fails.

For finite-support laws, R7-04 adds an independent restriction: any counterexample must also have at least **three strict internal M-SERPT record jumps**. Thus the remaining finite obstruction is not an arbitrary long chain anymore; the only chain pieces that can create an unpaid residence deficit are forced into the post-half-load suffix or into a deep terminal mismatch.

This is the intended interface with P01-A: R7-04 should continue handling exact finite mismatch chains, while P01-B now has a support-independent load-coordinate localization telling it which suffix of such a chain can possibly matter.

## 7. Remaining gap and next attack

The exact unresolved target remains `(HS1)` in the general case. The next useful attack is now sharply defined:

- fix one **nonterminal** Gittins valley with `A(v)<1/2`;
- decompose it into its internal M-SERPT hill/valley chain;
- prove a local inequality charging the positive part of `(2.5)` to that valley's endpoint reserve `2B_V` plus the internal `D1+D3` credits;
- if such a local inequality is false, preserve the smallest explicit source-valid counterexample to the local charge and use it to derive the correct prefix-coupled version.

The half-load theorem means no effort should be spent on Gittins valleys ending at `A>=1/2`: they are already harmless for the corrected general proof.
