## 2. New theorem: `(OLD-EB)` is false

Take the shifted Pareto family

\[
S(x)=(1+x)^{-\alpha},\qquad 1<\alpha<2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Its hazard rate is decreasing. Aalto--Ayesta--Righter imply that Gittins is FB for DHR service laws. Directly, the SERPT mean residual rank is

\[
r_S(a)=\frac{1+a}{\alpha-1},
\]

which is increasing, so M-SERPT is also FB. Thus on this family

\[
Gittins=M\text{-}SERPT=FB.
\tag{2.2}
\]

All ages are Gittins and M-SERPT hill ages. Consequently

\[
\underline Q_G=Q_G=Q_{MS},\qquad
\mathcal B_G=\Xi,\qquad \Lambda=0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The exact retained residence identity is

\[
2\Xi=Q_{MS}+\mathcal L-R_{MS}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The source's equation (8.1), based on Kamphorst--Zwart, gives as `rho -> 1`

\[
Q_{MS}\sim c_\alpha\mathcal L,
\qquad
R_{MS}\sim \alpha\mathcal L,
\qquad
c_\alpha:=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{2-\alpha}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda
\sim(c_\alpha+1-\alpha)\mathcal L.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Because `mu` is fixed while `mathcal L -> infinity`, the two positive branches on the right side of `(OLD-EB)` have asymptotic coefficients

\[
1,\qquad 2c_\alpha-1.
\tag{2.7}
\]

For every

\[
\boxed{\frac43<\alpha<\frac32,}
\tag{2.8}
\]

the minimum in `(2.7)` is strictly larger than the reserve coefficient in `(2.6)`. Indeed:

- if `4/3 < alpha <= sqrt(2)`, then `c_alpha<=1` and
  \[
  (2c_\alpha-1)-(c_\alpha+1-\alpha)
  =\frac{3\alpha-4}{2-\alpha}>0;
  \]
- if `sqrt(2) <= alpha < 3/2`, then `c_alpha>=1` and
  \[
  1-(c_\alpha+1-\alpha)
  =\frac{\alpha(3-2\alpha)}{2-\alpha}>0.
  \]

Hence, for every `alpha in (4/3,3/2)`, `(OLD-EB)` fails for all sufficiently high but still stable loads.

This is **not** a counterexample to Conjecture 8.1. In fact `(2.2)` makes the true response-time ratio exactly `1`. The failure proves that the old proof obligation was over-strong: the two coarse lower bounds `(1.3)` discard essential Gittins residence information precisely in a regime where the policies already coincide.

This also explains the earlier Pareto barrier for `Q_MS+2mu-R_MS`: it was a symptom of the same missing residence information, not evidence against factor two.

## 3. Pivot: derive a distribution-sensitive Gittins residence lower bound

Section 8.2 of the 2020 source explicitly identifies the lack of a useful lower bound on Gittins residence time beyond `E[R_G]>=mu` as a central obstacle. The 2018 SOAP theorem gives enough structure to obtain one.

For a single-class SOAP rank function `r`, let

\[
\bar r(a)=\sup_{0\le b\le a}r(b)
\]

be its increasing envelope, with hill set `H` and valleys `(u,v]` as in the 2020 source.

For a tagged job of size `x`, define its worst future rank at age `a<x` by

\[
r_x^{\rm worst}(a)=\sup_{a\le b<x}r(b),
\]

and let

\[
c_x(a)=\inf\{t\ge0:r(t)\ge r_x^{\rm worst}(a)\}
\tag{3.1}
\]

with the usual SOAP rank-bound convention at nonattained suprema.

In the single-class setting, the new-work random variable at that rank is `min(X,c_x(a))`. Therefore SOAP Theorem 5.5 gives the exact conditional residence formula

\[
\boxed{
E[R(x)]=\int_0^x\frac{da}{A(c_x(a))}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### 3.1 Hill ages

At almost every hill age `a`, the current rank is a new record:

\[
r(a)=\bar r(a)>\sup_{t<a}r(t).
\]

For every tagged size `x>a`,

\[
r_x^{\rm worst}(a)\ge r(a),
\]

so its first cutoff cannot occur before `a`:

\[
c_x(a)\ge a.
\]

Since `A` is decreasing,

\[
\frac1{A(c_x(a))}\ge\frac1{A(a)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### 3.2 Valley ages with survival to the next hill

Let `(u,v]` be a valley and `a in (u,v)`. If the tagged job survives beyond the valley, `x>v`, then because `v` is the next hill boundary, the future interval `[a,x)` contains ranks strictly above the plateau level (or their arbitrarily close right-limit representatives). No age below `v` attains such a rank. Hence

\[
c_x(a)\ge v,
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac1{A(c_x(a))}\ge\frac1{A(v)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If instead `a<x<=v`, we retain only the trivial bound `1/A(c_x(a))>=1`.

### 3.3 Averaging over job size

Tonelli applied to `(3.2)--(3.4)` gives the following.

> **HILL-SURVIVAL RESIDENCE LOWER BOUND.** For any single-class SOAP policy in the source's regular hill/valley presentation,
> \[
> \boxed{
> E[R]\ge \mathcal R_{HS}(r),
> }
> \tag{HS-R}
> \]
> where
> \[
> \boxed{
> \mathcal R_{HS}(r)
> =
> \int_{\mathcal H_r}\frac{S(a)}{A(a)}\,da
> +\sum_{(u,v]\in\mathcal V_r}
> \int_u^v
> \left[
> S(a)-S(v)+\frac{S(v)}{A(v)}
> \right]da.
> }
> \tag{3.5}
> \]
> Equivalently,
> \[
> \boxed{
> \mathcal R_{HS}(r)
> =\mu
> +\int_{\mathcal H_r}S(a)\left(\frac1{A(a)}-1\right)da
> +\sum_{(u,v]\in\mathcal V_r}
> (v-u)S(v)\left(\frac1{A(v)}-1\right).
> }
> \tag{3.6}
> \]

For a terminal valley set `S(v)=0` at `v=infinity`.

The theorem is support-cardinality independent. Formula `(3.5)` also makes its mechanism transparent: on a hill, every surviving tagged job pays at least the hill-age busy-period slowdown; inside a valley, jobs that survive all the way to the next hill pay at least the next-hill slowdown throughout that valley.

The displayed proof is rigorous under the same regular hill/valley presentation used by the source and SOAP. The Stieltjes/rank-bound formulation should extend to mixed laws; I am not claiming here that every pathological Borel endpoint convention has already been written out.

## 4. Apply `(HS-R)` to Gittins

Define

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_G^{HS}:=\mathcal R_{HS}(r_G).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
R_G\ge\mathcal R_G^{HS}\ge\mu,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and with Proposition 4.7,

\[
\boxed{
T_G\ge \underline Q_G+\mathcal R_G^{HS}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This lower bound interpolates correctly between the two extreme geometries that defeat any single scalar bound:

- if every Gittins age is a hill (for example the shifted-Pareto DHR/FB case),
  \[
  \mathcal R_G^{HS}=\int_0^\infty\frac{S}{A}=\mathcal L;
  \]
- if Gittins is FCFS, the rank envelope is one terminal valley and
  \[
  \mathcal R_G^{HS}=\mu.
  \]

Thus the new bound is strong precisely where Proposition 4.9 is weak, while remaining exact at the FCFS residence extreme.

## 5. Corrected master reduction

Combining the inherited M-SERPT master bound `(1.2)` with

\[
T_G\ge \max\{\underline Q_G+\mathcal R_G^{HS},\ \mathcal L\}
\tag{5.1}
\]

gives the distribution-sensitive ratio bound

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T_{MS}}{T_G}
\le
\frac{2\underline Q_G+\mathcal L-2\mathcal B_G-\Lambda}
{\max\{\underline Q_G+\mathcal R_G^{HS},\ \mathcal L\}}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

More importantly, factor two follows from the strictly weaker corrected reserve target

\[
\boxed{
2\mathcal B_G+\Lambda
\ge
\max\left\{0,
\min\{\mathcal L-2\mathcal R_G^{HS},\ 2\underline Q_G-\mathcal L\}
\right\}.
}
\tag{HS-EB}
\]

Unlike `(OLD-EB)`, `(HS-EB)` automatically survives the shifted-Pareto obstruction: in the all-hill case `R_G^{HS}=mathcal L`, so the first branch is `-mathcal L` and the right-hand side vanishes.

An immediate all-load arbitrary-law structural corollary is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_G^{HS}\ge\frac{\mathcal L}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_{MS}\le2T_G.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed `(1.2)` with nonnegative reserves gives `T_MS<=2Qbar_G+mathcal L`, while `(4.3)` gives `2T_G>=2Qbar_G+2R_G^{HS}`.

This is not a heavy-traffic result and has no finite-support assumption. It isolates the remaining hard laws as those with insufficient Gittins hill/survival residence credit **and** insufficient endpoint/residence reserve simultaneously.
