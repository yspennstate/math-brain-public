## 2. New extremal-measure theorem for an arbitrary M-SERPT record span

This section extends the finite record-envelope idea of R7-04 to the source's continuous-density / piecewise-monotone presentation without fixing the number of records.

Write

\[
S(x)=P(X>x),\qquad N(x)=E[(X-x)^+],
\]

\[
A(x)=c+\lambda N(x),\qquad c=1-\rho.
\]

Fix two M-SERPT hill/record ages `u<v` and consider the entire M-SERPT decomposition between them: continuous hill pieces are retained, and each strict M-SERPT valley is collapsed to one record block between its two hill endpoints. Put

\[
x_0=S(u),\quad y_0=A(u)-c,
\qquad
x_1=S(v),\quad y_1=A(v)-c,
\]

\[
A_0=A(u),\qquad A_1=A(v),\qquad
r_0=\frac{y_0}{x_0}.
\]

At M-SERPT record ages, `y/x=lambda r_S` is nondecreasing with age, while `y` is nonincreasing. Hence every intermediate record point `(x,y)` satisfies

\[
y\ge r_0x,
\qquad
y\ge y_1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Define the extremal lower envelope

\[
\phi(x):=c+\max\{r_0x,y_1\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Let

\[
\xi:=\frac{y_1}{r_0},\qquad
L:=x_0-\xi,\qquad R:=\xi-x_1.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then `x_1<=xi<=x_0`, `phi(x_0)=A_0`, and `phi(x_1)=A_1`.

### Theorem 2.1 — reciprocal-moment domination by a two-phase extremal record curve

Parameterize the record decomposition by survival mass `x` decreasing from `x_0` to `x_1`. Define the left reciprocal-load step/function `w(x)` as follows:

- on a collapsed M-SERPT valley block with left endpoint load `A_L`, set `w(x)=1/A_L` throughout that survival-mass block;
- on a continuous M-SERPT hill, set `w(x)=1/A(a(x))`.

Then pointwise

\[
1\le w(x)\le g(x):=\frac1{\phi(x)}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
M_1:=\int_{x_1}^{x_0}w(x)\,dx
\le I_1:=\frac1{r_0}\log\frac{A_0}{A_1}+\frac{R}{A_1}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
M_2:=\int_{x_1}^{x_0}w(x)^2\,dx
\le I_2:=\frac{L}{A_0A_1}+\frac{R}{A_1^2}.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

More generally, for every `p>0`,

\[
\int w(x)^pdx\le\int g(x)^pdx,
\]

where the right side is explicit:

\[
\int_{x_1}^{x_0}g(x)^pdx
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{A_0^{1-p}-A_1^{1-p}}{r_0(1-p)}+\frac{R}{A_1^p},&p\ne1,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle
\frac1{r_0}\log\frac{A_0}{A_1}+\frac{R}{A_1},&p=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.7}
\]

#### Proof

For any record endpoint with survival `x`, `(2.1)` gives

\[
A=c+y\ge c+\max\{r_0x,y_1\}=\phi(x).
\]

On a collapsed valley block with left survival endpoint `x_L`, `A_L>=phi(x_L)`. Since `phi` is nondecreasing in `x`, for every `x` in that block,

\[
\frac1{A_L}\le\frac1{\phi(x_L)}\le\frac1{\phi(x)}.
\]

The same inequality holds pointwise on a hill. This proves `(2.4)`, and integration gives the moment bounds. The integral of `phi^{-p}` splits at `xi`: on `[xi,x_0]`, `phi=c+r_0x`; on `[x_1,xi]`, `phi=A_1`. Direct integration gives `(2.7)`. QED.

### A coupled first/second moment inequality

The pointwise domination contains more information than separate bounds. Since `g>=w>=1`,

\[
g^2-w^2=(g-w)(g+w)\ge2(g-w).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
M_2\le I_2-2(I_1-M_1).
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

For a finite record chain, `M_1=P\bar w` and `M_2=sum_j p_jw_j^2`, so

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_w^2
\le I_2-2(I_1-P\bar w)-P\bar w^2.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

This is strictly more informative than treating the variance bound and the mean reciprocal load independently.

## 3. New continuum record-span coefficient: R7-04's `K>0` survives arbitrary record refinement

Define the reciprocal cross-load mass of the whole M-SERPT decomposition by

\[
\Sigma_M(u,v)
:=
\int_{\text{M-hills in }(u,v]}
\frac{dF(a)}{A(a)^2}
+
\sum_{(\alpha,\beta]\subset(u,v]\atop\text{M-valley}}
\frac{S(\alpha)-S(\beta)}{A(\alpha)A(\beta)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For each collapsed valley block, replace its true endpoint loads by the smaller envelope values `phi`. If the survival interval lies wholly in the linear part of `phi`,

\[
\frac{x_L-x_R}{\phi(x_L)\phi(x_R)}
=
\int_{x_R}^{x_L}\frac{dx}{\phi(x)^2}.
\]

The same identity holds in the constant part. If the block crosses `xi`, splitting once at `xi` can only increase the right-hand reciprocal sum, because the lower part then uses `A_1^2` instead of `phi(x_L)A_1`. Hill contributions satisfy the pointwise envelope bound. Summing gives

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_M(u,v)
\le
I_2
=
\frac{L}{A_0A_1}+\frac{R}{A_1^2}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `P=x_0-x_1`. Define the continuum factor-two record-span coefficient

\[
K(u,v):=\frac{2P}{A_0A_1}-\Sigma_M(u,v).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Exactly the same endpoint algebra as in R7-04 now gives

\[
\boxed{
K(u,v)
\ge
\frac{R}{A_0A_1}
+
\frac{L(c+r_0x_1)}{A_0A_1^2}
>0
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

for every nonempty record span.

This is the arbitrary-record / continuum analogue of the newest finite `K_{0,k}>0` theorem. It does not assume a bounded number of records and does not depend on support cardinality.

\tag{4.1}

The Proposition-4.7 Gittins waiting surrogate on the coarse valley is

\underline Q_{G,V}

Combining `(3.3)-(4.2)` yields

\boxed{

## 6. New physical prefix-moment constraint and correction to `B_*(u)`

The second missing compatibility condition comes from the fact that the prefix blocks are ordered by actual job size.

Let `b=a_m` be the endpoint age of the skipped prefix and define block mean coordinates

\[
m_j:=\frac{q_j}{p_j}
=\lambda E[X\mid\text{block }j].
\]

Every prefix job satisfies `X<=b`, hence

\[
0\le m_j\le \lambda b.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The endpoint identity

\[
y=\lambda E[X\wedge b]=u+\lambda br
\]

gives

\[
\lambda b=\frac{y-u}{r}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since the weighted mean of the `m_j` is `u/P`, feasibility of `(6.1)` immediately yields

\[
\boxed{0\le u\le Py.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

More importantly, the bounded-variable variance inequality gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_m^2
&:=\sum_jp_j\left(m_j-\frac uP\right)^2\\
&\le
P\frac uP\left(\frac{y-u}{r}-\frac uP\right)
=
\boxed{
\frac{u(Py-u)}{rP}.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This constraint is absent from the unconstrained square completion that produced `B_*(u)`.

To retain it, write

\[
\Gamma:=\sum_jp_j(w_j-\bar w)
\left(m_j-\frac uP\right).
\]

Then

\[
\sum_jw_jq_j=\bar w\,u+\Gamma,
\qquad
\sum_j\frac{q_j^2}{p_j}=\frac{u^2}{P}+\sigma_m^2,
\]

and Cauchy gives `Gamma<=sigma_w sigma_m`. In the R7 quadratic, the only block-distribution-dependent part is

\[
\frac C2\sigma_m^2-\Gamma.
\]

Let

\[
M(u):=\sqrt{\frac{u(Py-u)}{rP}}.
\]

Minimizing the last display over `0<=sigma_m<=M(u)` gives the sharpened scalar bound

\[
\boxed{
B(q)
\ge
B_*(u)
+
\frac C2
\left(
\frac{\sigma_w}{C}-M(u)
\right)_+^2.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Proof of `(6.5)`

For fixed `sigma_w`,

\[
\frac C2s^2-\sigma_ws
= -\frac{\sigma_w^2}{2C}
+\frac C2\left(s-\frac{\sigma_w}{C}\right)^2.
\]

The unconstrained minimum at `s=sigma_w/C` is exactly the negative variance term already present in `B_*(u)`. If the physical cap `s<=M(u)` excludes that minimizer, the distance-to-feasible-set square remains. This is `(6.5)`. QED.

At the saturation boundary `u=Py`, `M(u)=0`, so `(6.5)` restores

\[
\frac{\sigma_w^2}{2C}
\]

and cancels the entire negative variance term of `B_*(u)`. For the relaxed tuple `(1.4)`, this mandatory correction is

\[
\frac{\sigma_w^2}{2C}
=1.704593483573\ldots,
\]

turning the relaxed value `-1.022314...` into `+0.682279...`. Thus the apparent obstruction in Section 1 is exactly an artifact of allowing impossible prefix first-moment variance.

Equation `(6.5)`, together with the extremal reciprocal-moment constraints `(5.3)-(5.4)`, is the strongest current scalar interface between P01-A's arbitrary-record coefficient and P01-B's arbitrary-law prefix program.

## 7. What changed and what remains open

### Proved this run

1. **Endpoint-only obstruction identified.** The implication from the R7 lower bound on `K` plus its old variance bound to `B_*(u)>=0` is false on the relaxed endpoint domain, even after imposing `u<=Py`. This prevents wasting further runs on that insufficient relaxation.

2. **Extremal-measure reduction.** Every arbitrary M-SERPT record span is dominated, in all reciprocal-load moments, by the two-phase envelope
   \[
   \phi(x)=c+\max\{r_0x,y_1\}.
   \]
   The first and second reciprocal moments are explicitly `(2.5)-(2.6)`.

3. **Arbitrary-record `K>0`.** The newest finite R7 coefficient theorem extends to arbitrary record refinement in the source regularity class, with the same explicit positive lower margin `(3.4)`.

4. **Arbitrary-law merged-valley waiting reserve.** Any Gittins valley merging arbitrarily many/distributed M-SERPT records has the positive factor-two waiting reserve `(4.4)`.

5. **New joint reciprocal-moment constraint.** The physical record chain satisfies `(2.8)/(5.4)`, coupling `bar w` and `sigma_w` instead of treating them independently.

6. **New physical first-moment variance correction.** Actual prefix block means satisfy `(6.4)`, and the R7 scalar collapse sharpens to `(6.5)`.

### Still open

The full source conjecture is **not** proved. The current universal target remains `(VHS)`. On the finite skipped-prefix interface, the next exact question is whether the strengthened constraints

\[
K\ge 2P/a-I_2,
\qquad

\[

and

\[

+\frac C2\left(\frac{\sigma_w}{C}-M(u)\right)_+^2
\]

force nonnegativity on the entire physical high-load domain `a<1/2`. I have not proved that implication here.

The next attack should **not** return to fixed four- or five-atom enumeration. It should solve the remaining extremal problem over the two monotone moment coordinates `M_1,M_2` under the envelope `w(x)<=1/phi(x)` and the bounded prefix-mean variance `(6.4)`. The natural candidate extremizer is a two-phase/bang-bang record measure: `w=1` on an initial survival segment and `w=1/phi` on the terminal segment. If that extremizer can be proved, the remaining multi-record problem becomes two scalar cut locations rather than an arbitrary record chain. If it fails, the exact extremal tuple should be lifted back to a source-admissible record curve as a counterexample to the proposed scalar reduction, not to Conjecture 8.1 itself.
