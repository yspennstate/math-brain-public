## 1. Recall the exact quadratic field

Let
\[
M=\{1,\ldots,m\},\qquad m=(n-1)/2,
\]
and let
\[
\varepsilon_a=2\mathbf1_{\{a\in D\}}-1,\qquad
\xi_a=\mathbf1_{\{a\in D\}}=\frac{1+\varepsilon_a}{2}.
\tag{1.1}
\]
The `epsilon_a` are independent Rademachers. Extend them evenly to `Z_n` by `Y(0)=0`, `Y(k)=epsilon_[k]`, and set
\[
W_a=(Y*Y)(a).
\tag{1.2}
\]
The preceding report proved the exact design representation
\[
W_a=1+2\sum_{e\in E_a}\chi_e,
\qquad
\chi_{\{r,s\}}:=\varepsilon_r\varepsilon_s,
\tag{1.3}
\]
where `E_a` is a graph on `M` with
\[
|E_a|=m-1,
\qquad
|E_a\cap E_b|=1\quad(a\ne b),
\tag{1.4}
\]
and every unordered edge belongs to exactly two of the `E_a`. Hence, writing
\[
U_a:=W_a-1,
\tag{1.5}
\]
one has exactly
\[
\mathbb E U_a=0,\qquad
\mathbb E U_a^2=2n-6,\qquad
\mathbb E U_aU_b=4\quad(a\ne b).
\tag{1.6}
\]
The important point here is that the selected set `D` is generated by the **same** Rademachers as `W`; no independence between selection and quadratic score is assumed below.

For the radial field
\[
X_D(j)=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{a\in D}\cos(2\pi aj/n)
\tag{1.7}
\]
and every transverse selected-frequency vector `z` (`sum_{a in D}z_a=0`), the preceding report proved
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{q_0}[X_D^2f_z]
=\frac1{16(n-1)}\sum_{a\in D}z_aW_a.
}
\tag{1.8}
\]
This identity will now be used quantitatively on the selected coordinates themselves.

# 2. The selected quadratic energy has annealed limit `1/2`

Let
\[
d=|D|=\sum_a\xi_a,
\qquad
\overline W_D=\frac1d\sum_{a\in D}W_a
\]
when `d>0`, and define
\[
Q_D:=\sum_{a\in D}(W_a-\overline W_D)^2.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The event `d=0` is exponentially small and may be assigned `Q_D=0`.

## Theorem P07-B.21 — same-mask selected quadratic energy

As `n->infinity` through odd integers,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E_D Q_D}{n^2}\longrightarrow\frac12.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Moreover
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_D Q_D^2=O(n^4),
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
so there are absolute constants `c_0,c_1>0` such that for all sufficiently large odd `n`,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr_D\{Q_D\ge c_0n^2\}\ge c_1.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
Thus the selected transverse quadratic field is genuinely macroscopic on a nonvanishing fraction of source masks; it is not an artefact carried only by complementary frequencies.

### Proof

Write
\[
F:=\sum_a\xi_aU_a^2,
\qquad
S:=\sum_a\xi_aU_a.
\tag{2.5}
\]
Then exactly
\[
Q_D=F-\frac{S^2}{d}
\tag{2.6}
\]
on `d>0`.

First compute `E F`. Since `U_a` lies in the second Rademacher chaos, `U_a^2` contains only even Walsh degrees `0,2,4`. Multiplication by `epsilon_a` therefore produces only odd Walsh monomials after cancellations, all of mean zero. Hence
\[
\mathbb E[\varepsilon_aU_a^2]=0.
\tag{2.7}
\]
Using `xi_a=(1+epsilon_a)/2` and (1.6),
\[
\mathbb E[\xi_aU_a^2]
=\frac12(2n-6)=n-3.
\tag{2.8}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EF=m(n-3)
=\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}2
=\frac{n^2}{2}+O(n).
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

It remains to show that the selected empirical mean costs only `O(n)`. Decompose
\[
S=\frac12A+\frac12B,
\qquad
A:=\sum_aU_a,
\qquad
B:=\sum_a\varepsilon_aU_a.
\tag{2.10}
\]
From (1.6),
\[
\mathbb EA^2
=m(2n-6)+4m(m-1)=O(n^2).
\tag{2.11}
\]
For `B`, use (1.3):
\[
B=2\sum_a\sum_{e=\{r,s\}\in E_a}
\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_r\varepsilon_s.
\tag{2.12}
\]
If `a` is an endpoint of `e`, the monomial reduces to degree one; by the exact own-frequency incidence from the preceding report there are only `O(n)` such incidences. Otherwise it is a degree-three Walsh monomial. A fixed unordered triple can occur only `O(1)` times, because for a fixed edge `{r,s}` there are exactly two labels `a` for which `{r,s} in E_a`. Orthogonality of distinct Walsh monomials therefore gives
\[
\mathbb EB^2=O(n^2).
\tag{2.13}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{\mathbb ES^2=O(n^2).}
\tag{2.14}
\]

On the ordinary-density event `d>=n/8`,
\[
\frac{S^2}{d}\le\frac{8S^2}{n},
\]
so its expectation there is `O(n)`. On the complementary event, Chernoff gives probability `e^{-cn}`. Also Cauchy gives `S^2/d<=F`. Finally, since `F^2<=d\sum_a\xi_aU_a^4` and degree-two Bonami hypercontractivity gives
\[
\mathbb EU_a^4\le81(\mathbb EU_a^2)^2=O(n^2),
\]
one has
\[
\mathbb EF^2=O(n^4).
\tag{2.15}
\]
Thus the rare-density contribution is exponentially negligible by Cauchy-Schwarz, and altogether
\[
\mathbb E\frac{S^2}{d}=O(n).
\tag{2.16}
\]
Combining (2.6), (2.9), and (2.16) proves (2.2).

Since `0<=Q_D<=F`, (2.15) gives (2.3). Paley-Zygmund applied to `Q_D`, using (2.2)-(2.3), gives (2.4). `square`

### Why this matters

A tempting but invalid simplification would replace the selected coordinates by an independent half-sample of the field `W`. Theorem P07-B.21 does not do this. Equation (2.7) is a parity statement in the **same** source bits, and the only place where the selected mean enters is handled by the exact decomposition (2.10).

# 3. The radial Gibbs residual has a nonzero second-order transverse coefficient

Let `q_0` be uniform on `Z_n\setminus{0}` and define the radial Gibbs law
\[
\frac{dp_{\beta,D}}{dq_0}(j)
=\frac{e^{-\beta X_D(j)}}{A_D(\beta)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
For `a in D`, write
\[
m_a(\beta)=\mathbb E_{p_{\beta,D}}\phi_a,
\qquad
r_a(\beta)=m_a(\beta)-\frac1d\sum_{b\in D}m_b(\beta),
\tag{3.2}
\]
and
\[
V_D(\beta)=\|r_D(\beta)\|_2^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For transverse `z`, put `f_z=sum z_a phi_a`. The exact nonzero-site Gram identities give
\[
\mathbb E_{q_0}f_z=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{q_0}[X_Df_z]=0.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Therefore, differentiating the normalized exponential tilt at `beta=0`,
\[
\left.\frac{d}{d\beta}\mathbb E_{p_{\beta,D}}f_z\right|_{0}=0,
\tag{3.5}
\]
and
\[
\left.\frac{d^2}{d\beta^2}\mathbb E_{p_{\beta,D}}f_z\right|_{0}
=\mathbb E_{q_0}[X_D^2f_z].
\tag{3.6}
\]
Using (1.8), the transverse second derivative is therefore the explicit vector
\[
\boxed{
r_D''(0)
=\frac1{16(n-1)}
\left(W_D-\overline W_D\mathbf1_D\right).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
This is exact for every realized mask.

Since `r_D(0)=r_D'(0)=0`,
\[
V_D(\beta)
=\frac{\beta^4}{4}\|r_D''(0)\|_2^2+O_D(\beta^5)
\qquad(\beta\to0)
\tag{3.8}
\]
for each fixed finite `n`. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
V_D^{(4)}(0)
=\frac{3}{128(n-1)^2}Q_D.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]
Combining with Theorem P07-B.21 yields the annealed fourth-order coefficient
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb E_D V_D^{(4)}(0)
=\frac3{256}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]
Thus, in the sequential small-tilt sense,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_D V_D(\beta)
=\frac{\beta^4}{2048}+o_n(\beta^4)
}
\tag{3.11}
\]
at fourth order around `beta=0`. The coefficient `1/2048` is exactly one half of the total selected-plus-complement coefficient `1/1024` found earlier. The previous upper bound was therefore sharp at leading nonlinear order in the annealed expansion.

By (2.4) and (3.7), there are absolute `c,c'>0` such that
\[
\Pr_D\{\|r_D''(0)\|_2\ge c\}\ge c'
\tag{3.12}
\]
for all large odd `n`. Hence the second-order transverse residual is not disappearing samplewise on almost every source mask.

# 4. The `O(beta^2)` interpolation scale is necessary as well as sufficient

Let `H_D={z in R^D:sum z_a=0}` and define the transverse moment operator
\[
(T_Dh)_a
:=\mathbb E_{q_0}[h\phi_a]
-\frac1d\sum_{b\in D}\mathbb E_{q_0}[h\phi_b].
\tag{4.1}
\]
On `H_D`, the exact tight-frame identity from the earlier P07-B reports is
\[
T_DT_D^*=\lambda_n I,
\qquad
\lambda_n=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Therefore the minimum `L^2(q_0)` norm of an interpolant of a target `r in H_D` is
\[
\inf_{T_Dh=r}\|h\|_2
=\frac{\|r\|_2}{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Since `||h||_infty>=||h||_2`, every bounded interpolant obeys the same lower bound.

Define the pure quadratic interpolation constant
\[
K_D^{(2)}
:=\inf\{\|h\|_\infty:T_Dh=r_D''(0)\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Equivalently by duality and (1.8),
\[
\boxed{
K_D^{(2)}
=\sup_{z\in H_D\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{|\sum_{a\in D}z_aW_a|}
{16(n-1)\,\mathbb E_{q_0}|f_z|}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Then (3.7), (4.3), and Theorem P07-B.21 give
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E_D\bigl(K_D^{(2)}\bigr)^2
\ge\frac1{256}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Indeed
\[
(K_D^{(2)})^2
\ge
\frac{Q_D}{256(n-1)^2\lambda_n}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Also, by (2.4), `K_D^{(2)}` is bounded below by an absolute positive constant on a source event of absolute positive probability.

Now let `K_D(beta)` denote the exact bounded-interpolation norm of the full Gibbs transverse residual `r_D(beta)`. In finite dimension this is a norm of the target vector, so (3.7) and homogeneity imply for each fixed realized nontrivial mask
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{\beta\to0}
\frac{K_D(\beta)}{\beta^2}
=\frac12K_D^{(2)}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]
Consequently the desired upper scale `K_D(beta)=O(beta^2)` is **scale-sharp**: one cannot hope for a uniform `o(beta^2)` replacement. Quantitatively, the sequential annealed lower scale is
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb E_D\left[
\left(
\lim_{\beta\to0}\frac{K_D(\beta)}{\beta^2}
\right)^2
\right]
\ge\frac1{1024}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]
So the natural RMS coefficient is at least `1/32`.

This is useful strategically: the remaining task is not to make the quadratic leakage vanish. The correct goal is a **constant-factor bounded representation** of a genuinely order-one quadratic target.

# 5. A sharper theorem-sized target

The preceding report formulated the nonlinear collision-rich lemma `ACHL(beta,K_0)`. The new normalization isolates an even cleaner leading theorem.

## Missing lemma `QBI(C)` — quadratic bounded interpolation

Prove that for some absolute finite `C`,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr_D\{K_D^{(2)}>C\}=O(n^{-1/2}).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
By (4.6)-(4.9), this is the best possible scale: `C` cannot be replaced by a sequence tending to zero.

To upgrade `QBI` to the full `TRBI` needed for the source theta bound, it is enough to pair it with a small-tilt analytic stability estimate. One convenient exact formulation is: for some fixed `beta_0>0`, with `O(n^{-1/2})` failure,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0<\beta\le\beta_0}
\frac1{\beta^3}
\inf\left\{
\|h\|_\infty:
T_Dh=r_D(\beta)-\frac{\beta^2}{2}r_D''(0)
\right\}
\le C_3.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Then (5.1)-(5.2) imply
\[
K_D(\beta)\le\frac C2\beta^2+C_3\beta^3
\tag{5.3}
\]
for one sufficiently small fixed `beta`, i.e. `TRBI`. The preceding positive-certificate conversion then gives
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\vartheta(G_n)=O(\sqrt n),}
\tag{5.4}
\]
removing the entire published `sqrt(log log n)` loss.

The sharp leading constant `1` remains a separate stronger problem. P07-A's critical-tilt information-projection route identifies `beta=4` for that purpose; the present small-tilt theorem is aimed first at eliminating the iterated logarithm.
