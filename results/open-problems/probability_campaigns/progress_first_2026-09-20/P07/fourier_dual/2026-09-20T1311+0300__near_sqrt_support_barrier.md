## 1. Exact dual numerator: the affine pieces disappear

Let `n` be odd,
\[
M=\{1,\ldots,(n-1)/2\},\qquad D\subset M,
\]
with the source mask generated by independent fair bits `xi_a`, and let
\[
\phi_a(j)=\cos(2\pi aj/n),\qquad
X_D(j)=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{a\in D}\phi_a(j),\qquad j\ne0.
\]
Write `q_0` for the uniform probability on `Z_n\setminus\{0\}`. For `z=(z_a)_{a\in D}` with
\[
\sum_{a\in D}z_a=0,
\]
put
\[
f_z(j)=\sum_{a\in D}z_a\phi_a(j).
\]
The exact nonzero-site cosine Gram matrix gives
\[
\mathbb E_{q_0}f_z=0,
\tag{1.1}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_{q_0}[X_D f_z]=0.
\tag{1.2}
\]
Indeed the Gram matrix has constant diagonal `(n-2)/(2(n-1))` and constant off-diagonal `-1/(n-1)`, so its product with the all-ones vector is radial and is annihilated by `z\perp1`.

For fixed `beta>0`, recall
\[
A_D(\beta)=\mathbb E_{q_0}e^{-\beta X_D},
\qquad
h_{\rm raw}=A_D(\beta)^{-1}e^{-\beta X_D}-1+\beta X_D.
\]
Therefore, for every transverse `z`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{q_0}[h_{\rm raw}f_z]
=A_D(\beta)^{-1}\mathbb E_{q_0}[e^{-\beta X_D}f_z].
}
\tag{1.3}
\]
Also Jensen and `E_{q_0}X_D=-|D|/[\sqrt n(n-1)]` give the deterministic bound
\[
\boxed{A_D(\beta)\ge1.}
\tag{1.4}
\]
Thus sparse-witness control for the exact TRBI numerator reduces to the single smooth weight `e^{-beta X_D}`; the random normalization can only improve the dual ratio.

# 2. A general concentration lemma for smooth functions of the same-mask Fourier field

The central point is that the dependence between `X_D` and a test polynomial can be handled before taking any independence approximation.

For a `C^2` function `psi:R->R`, define for every positive frequency `a in M`
\[
R_a(D):=\mathbb E_{q_0}[\psi(X_D(J))\phi_a(J)].
\tag{2.1}
\]
Let
\[
L_1=\|\psi'\|_\infty,\qquad L_2=\|\psi''\|_\infty.
\]

## Theorem P07-B.16 — same-mask coordinate concentration

There are absolute constants `C,c>0` such that, for every odd `n>=5` and every `a in M`,
\[
|\mathbb E_D R_a(D)|
\le
\frac{C(1+|\psi(0)|+L_1)}{\sqrt n},
\tag{2.2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\Pr_D\{|R_a-\mathbb E R_a|>u\}
\le
2\exp\left(-\frac{cnu^2}{L_1^2+L_2^2}\right).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed `A>0`, with probability at least `1-O(n^{-A})`,
\[
\boxed{
\max_{a\in M}|R_a(D)|
\le
C_{A,\psi}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

### Proof of the mean bound

Fix `j ne0` and write `xi_b=1/2+eta_b`, where `eta_b` are independent symmetric `+-1/2` variables. The exact trigonometric sums are
\[
\sum_{b\in M}\phi_b(j)=-\frac12,
\qquad
\sum_{b\in M}\phi_b(j)^2=\frac{n-2}{4}.
\tag{2.5}
\]
Hence, as a random variable over the source mask,
\[
X_D(j)=-\frac1{4\sqrt n}
+\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{b\in M}\eta_b\phi_b(j),
\tag{2.6}
\]
with variance
\[
\sigma_n^2=\frac{n-2}{16n},
\tag{2.7}
\]
independent of `j`. Moreover
\[
\sum_{b\in M}\mathbb E\left|\frac{\eta_b\phi_b(j)}{\sqrt n}\right|^3
\le \frac{C}{\sqrt n}
\tag{2.8}
\]
uniformly in `j`. The Wasserstein/Berry--Esseen bound for sums of independent centered variables therefore gives a number
\[
\mu_{n,\psi}:=\mathbb E\psi\left(-\frac1{4\sqrt n}+\sigma_n Z\right),
\qquad Z\sim N(0,1),
\]
such that
\[
\sup_{j\ne0}
\left|\mathbb E_D\psi(X_D(j))-\mu_{n,\psi}\right|
\le \frac{CL_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{2.9}
\]
Since `E_{q_0}phi_a=-1/(n-1)`, multiplying by `phi_a(j)` and averaging over nonzero `j` yields
\[
|\mathbb E_D R_a|
\le \frac{|\mu_{n,\psi}|}{n-1}+\frac{CL_1}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{2.10}
\]
Finally `|mu_{n,psi}|<=|psi(0)|+CL_1`, proving (2.2).

### Proof of the concentration bound

Toggle one source bit `xi_b`. Let `D^{(b)}` denote the neighboring mask. Taylor's theorem gives, at every mask `D`,
\[
R_a(D^{(b)})-R_a(D)
=\pm\frac1{\sqrt n}
\mathbb E_{q_0}
[\psi'(X_D)\phi_a\phi_b]
+\rho_{ab}(D),
\tag{2.11}
\]
with
\[
|\rho_{ab}(D)|\le\frac{L_2}{2n}.
\tag{2.12}
\]
For the first terms, put
\[
h_D(j)=\psi'(X_D(j))\phi_a(j)
\]
on the nonzero sites and extend it by `h_D(0)=0`. Parseval on `Z_n` implies the Bessel estimate
\[
\sum_{b\in M}
\left|\mathbb E_{q_0}[h_D\phi_b]\right|^2
\le \frac{n}{n-1}\mathbb E_{q_0}|h_D|^2
\le 2L_1^2.
\tag{2.13}
\]
Combining (2.11)--(2.13), uniformly at every mask,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{b\in M}
|R_a(D^{(b)})-R_a(D)|^2
\le \frac{C(L_1^2+L_2^2)}{n}.
}
\tag{2.14}
\]
The Bernoulli-cube log-Sobolev / bounded-square-gradient inequality applied to (2.14) gives (2.3). A union bound over `|M|<(n/2)` then gives (2.4). `square`

### Why (2.14) is the useful dependence-aware point

A crude one-bit Lipschitz estimate is only `O(n^{-1/2})` per bit and would sum to an `O(1)` variance proxy. The Fourier Bessel step sums the **actual squared directional derivatives** first and recovers the correct `O(1/n)` proxy. No Fourier coordinate is treated as independent of another coordinate, and the mask generating `X_D` is exactly the same mask used in `R_a(D)`.

# 3. Exact lower bound for the `L^1` denominator of sparse transverse tests

Let `z\perp1` have support size
\[
s=|\operatorname{supp}z|.
\]
The tight-frame identity from the preceding report gives
\[
\mathbb E_{q_0}f_z^2
=\lambda_n\|z\|_2^2,
\qquad
\lambda_n=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Also
\[
\|f_z\|_\infty
\le\|z\|_1
\le\sqrt s\|z\|_2.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E f_z^2
\le\|f_z\|_\infty\mathbb E|f_z|
\]
gives the deterministic estimate
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{q_0}|f_z|
\ge \frac{\lambda_n}{\sqrt s}\|z\|_2.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
This weak bound is enough once (2.4) is used on the numerator.

## Corollary P07-B.17 — uniform sparse-witness exclusion for a smooth same-mask weight

On the event (2.4), simultaneously for every `z\perp1` supported on at most `s` frequencies,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{|\mathbb E_{q_0}[\psi(X_D)f_z]|}
{\mathbb E_{q_0}|f_z|}
\le
C_{A,\psi}\,s\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

By definition,
\[
\mathbb E[\psi(X_D)f_z]=\sum_a z_aR_a(D).
\]
Thus (2.4), Cauchy, and (3.3) yield
\[
|\mathbb E[\psi(X_D)f_z]|
\le\max_a|R_a|\,\|z\|_1
\le C_{A,\psi}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\sqrt s\|z\|_2,
\]
and division by (3.3) proves (3.4). `square`

The important point is that this is uniform over **all** supports and coefficients once the coordinate event (2.4) holds. There is no union bound over sparse supports.

# 4. Application to the exact Gibbs dual numerator

The function `psi(x)=e^{-beta x}` has unbounded global derivatives as `x->-infinity`, but the source field never reaches that regime with relevant probability. For fixed `j ne0`, Hoeffding applied to (2.6) gives
\[
\Pr_D\{|X_D(j)|>R\}\le 2e^{-cR^2}
\tag{4.1}
\]
for `1<=R<=c\sqrt n`. Hence, for sufficiently large fixed `C_0`,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr_D\left\{\max_{j\ne0}|X_D(j)|>C_0\sqrt{\log n}\right\}
=O(n^{-4}).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let
\[
R_n=C_0\sqrt{\log n}
\]
and choose a `C^2` truncation `psi_n` which agrees with `e^{-beta x}` on `[-R_n,R_n]`, is constant outside `[-R_n-1,R_n+1]`, and satisfies
\[
\|\psi_n'\|_\infty+\|\psi_n''\|_\infty
\le C_\beta e^{\beta(R_n+1)}
=:L_n.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Because `beta` is fixed,
\[
\boxed{L_n=n^{o(1)}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Apply Corollary P07-B.17 to `psi_n`. Combining (4.2)--(4.4), for every fixed `A` and with probability `1-O(n^{-A})` after increasing `C_0` and the concentration constant if necessary,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{z\perp1\\1\le |\operatorname{supp}z|\le s}}
\frac{|\mathbb E_{q_0}[e^{-\beta X_D}f_z]|}
{\mathbb E_{q_0}|f_z|}
\le
n^{o(1)}s\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Using (1.3)--(1.4), the same upper bound applies to the exact normalized Gibbs residual `h_raw`.

Now choose, for example,
\[
\boxed{
s_n:=\frac{\sqrt n}{L_n^2\log n}.}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Then
\[
s_n=n^{1/2-o(1)}
\tag{4.7}
\]
and (4.5) gives
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{z\perp1\\|\operatorname{supp}z|\le s_n}}
\frac{|\mathbb E[h_{\rm raw}f_z]|}
{\mathbb E|f_z|}
=o(1)
}
\tag{4.8}
\]
with arbitrarily high polynomial probability.

This is a genuine structural restriction on the exact bounded-interpolation dual problem.

# 5. Near-square-root support barrier

Recall the exact Hahn--Banach form of the interpolation cost from the preceding P07-B work:
\[
K_D(\beta)
=
\sup_{\substack{z\perp1\\z\ne0}}
\frac{|\mathbb E[h_{\rm raw}f_z]|}
{\mathbb E|f_z|}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Theorem P07-B.18 — any fixed-size obstruction is mesoscopic/dense

Fix `beta>0` and `kappa>0`. With probability tending to one faster than any prescribed inverse polynomial after constants are chosen as above, every transverse witness satisfying
\[
\frac{|\mathbb E[h_{\rm raw}f_z]|}
{\mathbb E|f_z|}\ge\kappa
\tag{5.2}
\]
must obey
\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{supp}z|
\ge n^{1/2-o(1)}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In particular, an `O(log n)` arithmetic run in the random frequency set, a fixed-size additive configuration, or any other sub-`n^{1/2-o(1)}` Fourier support cannot produce a fixed positive TRBI obstruction.

### Interpretation

This removes the main reason the global Kashin route looked dangerous. A dense Bernoulli frequency set does contain logarithmic arithmetic runs, and Dirichlet-type polynomials on such runs can have poor global `L^1/L^2` ratio. But the actual Gibbs numerator is generated by the same random mask, and Theorem P07-B.18 shows that its correlation with every such sparse polynomial is too small for those runs to matter. The difficult witnesses, if they exist, must be genuinely mesoscopic or dense.

# 6. The exact remaining lemma is now restricted to dense witnesses

The preceding report's `TRBI(beta,C)` asked for
\[
K_D(\beta)=O(\beta^2)
\tag{6.1}
\]
with sufficiently strong failure probability; that implication to an `O(sqrt(n))` theta bound was already proved there. By (4.8), for fixed sufficiently small `beta`, the sparse part of (5.1) is eventually `o(1)`, hence smaller than any prescribed fixed multiple of `beta^2`.

Therefore it now suffices to prove only the following dense-witness statement.

## Missing lemma `DWRL(beta)` — dense-witness residual leakage

For one sufficiently small fixed `beta>0`, with `s_n` as in (4.6), prove
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left\{
\sup_{\substack{z\perp1\\|\operatorname{supp}z|\ge s_n}}
\frac{|\mathbb E[h_{\rm raw}f_z]|}
{\mathbb E|f_z|}
>C\beta^2
\right\}
=O(n^{-1/2})
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
for the constant range required by the constructive interpolation theorem.

Then Theorem P07-B.18 supplies the complementary sparse range automatically, `TRBI` follows, and the existing positive-certificate argument gives
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\vartheta(G_n)=O(\sqrt n),}
\]
removing the published `sqrt(log log n)` factor. This still does not by itself prove the sharp leading constant `1`.

`DWRL` is substantially narrower than the previous global random-Fourier Kashin condition and narrower than `SGEL`: the dual witness is now known to involve almost square-root many selected frequencies.

# 7. Second-order form suggests the next dependence-aware attack

For small fixed `beta`, the exact cancellation gives formally and in `L^2(q_0)`
\[
h_{\rm raw}
=\frac{\beta^2}{2}
\left(X_D^2-\mathbb E_{q_0}X_D^2\right)
+O_{L^2}(\beta^3),
\tag{7.1}
\]
up to radial terms invisible to `z\perp1`. Consequently the leading residual vector is a centered quadratic convolution of the Bernoulli frequency mask: its `a`th coordinate counts signed pair relations `+-b+-c=+-a` in `D`, after centering and normalization.

Theorem P07-B.18 says the next proof does **not** need to control this quadratic chaos against arbitrary sparse additive configurations. The useful next target is the mesoscopic/dense class in (6.2), where a Hanson--Wright / random-convolution estimate has a chance to exploit averaging over at least `n^{1/2-o(1)}` coefficients of `z`.

A concrete next lemma would be: for the centered quadratic residual vector `r^(2)` obtained from the `beta^2` term in (7.1), prove uniformly for `z\perp1` with `|supp z|>=s_n`
\[
|\langle r^{(2)},z\rangle|
\le C\beta^2\,\mathbb E|f_z|
\tag{7.2}
\]
with `O(n^{-1/2})` failure, and then control the `O(beta^3)` remainder by the same large-support mechanism. This is now the preferred next attack; it is genuinely different from another clipping census.
