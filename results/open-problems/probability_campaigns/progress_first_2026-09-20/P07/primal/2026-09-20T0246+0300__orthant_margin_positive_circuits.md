# 2. Exact source-normalized primal section

Let `n` be odd and let `E\subset Z_n\setminus{0}` be the random symmetric edge-difference set, generated by independent fair choices on the `(n-1)/2` unordered pairs `{d,-d}`. Let `F` be the **unnormalized** DFT,

\[
F_{d,k}=e^{-2\pi i dk/n}.
\]

The circulant theta reduction in the primary paper gives the frequency primal

\[
\vartheta(G_E)=n\max y_0
\]

subject to

\[
y\in\mathbb R_+^n,\qquad \mathbf 1^Ty=1,\qquad (Fy)_d=0\quad(d\in E).
\tag{2.1}
\]

(The conjugate rows `d,-d` encode the real cosine/sine constraints. This is the source normalization; I do not replace it by an Erdős–Rényi or Paley model.)

Define the homogeneous real subspace

\[
L_E:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\mathbf 1^Tz=0,\ (Fz)_d=0\ \forall d\in E\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The uniform vector `u=n^{-1}1` is feasible. Thus every feasible point is `y=u+z` with `z\in L_E` and `z_i\ge-1/n`.

The key observation is that `L_E` is invariant under cyclic shifts of the **frequency coordinate**: shifting `z_k` multiplies `(Fz)_d` by a unit complex phase and hence preserves every zero constraint.

# 3. New exact theorem: theta is a one-sided orthant-margin ratio

For a real vector `z`, write

\[
z_+=(\max\{z_i,0\})_i,\qquad z_-=(\max\{-z_i,0\})_i.
\]

### Theorem P07-A.1 — exact orthant-margin identity

For every odd `n` and every symmetric edge-difference set `E`, with `L_E` as in (2.2),

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(G_E)
=1+\sup_{0\ne z\in L_E}
\frac{\|z_+\|_\infty}{\|z_-\|_\infty}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The denominator is nonzero for every nonzero `z\in L_E`, since `1^Tz=0`.

### Proof

Scale the affine displacement by writing

\[
x:=n(y-u).
\]

Then (2.1) is equivalent to

\[
x\in L_E,\qquad x_i\ge-1\ \forall i,
\]

and the objective is

\[
n y_0=1+x_0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Take any nonzero direction `z\in L_E`. Its negative part is nonzero. The largest positive scalar `t` such that `tz_i\ge-1` for every coordinate is

\[
t_{\max}=\frac1{\|z_-\|_\infty}.
\]

Hence the farthest feasible point on the ray generated by `z` has maximal positive coordinate

\[
\frac{\|z_+\|_\infty}{\|z_-\|_\infty}.
\]

Because `L_E` is cyclic-shift invariant, shift a coordinate attaining `||z_+||_infty` to coordinate zero without changing `||z_-||_infty` or feasibility. This produces a feasible point whose objective increment over `1` is exactly the displayed ratio.

Conversely, for any feasible `x` in (3.2),

\[
\|x_-\|_\infty\le1,
\]

so after shifting a maximum positive coordinate to zero its objective increment is at most the supremum in (3.1). Taking the maximum over the compact primal feasible polytope gives equality. ∎

### Why this is useful

The primary signed-RIP route has to control vectors regardless of sign. Equation (3.1) says the source problem only asks for the **largest positive peak relative to the largest negative excursion inside the random Fourier kernel**. Sparse signed kernel vectors are therefore not automatically obstructions; to inflate theta they must be *one-sidedly unbalanced*.

Define the exact random structural observable

\[
R_E:=\sup_{0\ne z\in L_E}\frac{\|z_+\|_\infty}{\|z_-\|_\infty}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then, with no asymptotics and no probability approximation,

\[
\boxed{\vartheta(G_E)=1+R_E.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus Conjecture 18 is equivalent to

\[
\mathbb E R_E=(1+o(1))\sqrt n.
\tag{3.5}
\]

This preserves the source's **expectation** statement: a high-probability bound is useful only with enough tail control to imply (3.5).

# 4. New deterministic sharp-constant sufficient modulus

Define

\[
\beta(E):=\inf_{0\ne z\in L_E}
\frac{\|z_-\|_2}{\|z_+\|_\infty}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Cyclic invariance lets us equivalently shift a largest positive coordinate to zero before taking the infimum.

### Lemma P07-A.2 — one-sided energy implies a theta upper bound

For every source instance,

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(G_E)\le 1+\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{\beta(E)}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

For every nonzero zero-sum `z`, at least one coordinate is positive, so `z_-` has at most `n-1` nonzero coordinates. Therefore

\[
\|z_-\|_2\le \sqrt{n-1}\,\|z_-\|_\infty.
\]

By (4.1),

\[
\|z_-\|_2\ge \beta(E)\|z_+\|_\infty.
\]

Combining,

\[
\frac{\|z_+\|_\infty}{\|z_-\|_\infty}
\le \frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{\beta(E)}.
\]

Take the supremum and apply (3.1). ∎

Consequently the **expectation-level** sufficient statement

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,\beta(E)^{-1}=1+o(1)
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

would immediately give

\[
\mathbb E\vartheta(G_E)\le(1+o(1))\sqrt n,
\]

which, combined with the already-known source lower bound, proves Conjecture 18.

This is not claimed to be necessary; it is a clean primal sufficient target. In particular it asks for a one-sided energy inequality, not an all-sign sparse RIP inequality.

### Equivalent conic-avoidance formulation

For `t>0` define the fixed-coordinate cone in the zero-sum hyperplane

\[
\mathcal C_t:=\{z\in\mathbf1^\perp:\ \|z_-\|_2\le t z_0\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The condition forces `z_0\ge0`; because `z\mapsto||z_-||_2` is convex, `C_t` is a convex cone. By cyclic invariance,

\[
\boxed{
\beta(E)<t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L_E\cap \mathcal C_t\ne\{0\}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed if the infimum in (4.1) is below `t`, shift a largest positive coordinate to zero; the converse is immediate since `z_0<=||z_+||_infty`.

So the sharp-constant program becomes a **random Fourier subspace versus a fixed one-sided convex cone** problem. This is the primal/probabilistic-geometry interface I propose to pursue. A useful next comparison theorem would transfer conic-avoidance estimates from an isotropic/Haar benchmark to the random half-Fourier row selection while preserving errors on the `o(1)` constant scale. I have not proved that comparison here.

# 5. Prime-order exact feasible certificates: every extreme point is a positive Fourier circuit

The previous theorem is valid for every odd `n`. On the prime subsequence one can say much more exactly.

Let `n=p` be an odd prime, let `r=|E|`, and let

\[
R:=\{0\}\cup E,
\qquad |R|=r+1.
\]

### Theorem P07-A.3 — prime positive-circuit parametrization

Every extreme point of the feasible polytope (2.1) has support exactly `r+1`.

For each support `T\subset Z_p` with `|T|=r+1`, define the square Fourier minor `F_{R,T}`. Chebotarev's theorem for the prime DFT makes every such minor invertible. The unique vector supported on `T` satisfying the equality constraints is

\[
y_T=(F_{R,T})^{-1}e_0,
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `e_0` is `1` in the row indexed by zero and `0` on the rows indexed by `E`. Extending by zero outside `T`, this vector is primal feasible **iff** every coordinate in (5.1) is nonnegative.

Conversely every extreme primal feasible point arises this way. Therefore the prime-order theta problem is exactly the finite optimization over **positive circuits**

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\vartheta(G_E)}p
=\max_{\substack{T\ni0,\ |T|=r+1\\ (F_{R,T})^{-1}e_0>0}}
\big[(F_{R,T})^{-1}e_0\big]_0.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

(Strict positivity holds on an extreme support; if a coordinate were zero, the actual support would be smaller.)

### Proof

Let `y` be any nonzero feasible point. Since `(Fy)_d=0` for every `d\in E`,

\[
|\operatorname{supp}(Fy)|\le p-r.
\]

The prime cyclic uncertainty theorem (equivalently the Chebotarev full-minor property) gives

\[
|\operatorname{supp}y|+|\operatorname{supp}(Fy)|\ge p+1.
\]

Hence

\[
|\operatorname{supp}y|\ge r+1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

On the other hand the feasible polytope is the probability simplex intersected with `r` independent real Fourier equalities; every extreme point has at most `r+1` positive coordinates. Combining with (5.3), every extreme point has exactly `r+1` support coordinates.

For a proposed support `T` of this size, the equality constraints are exactly

\[
F_{R,T}y_T=e_0.
\]

Chebotarev invertibility gives the unique solution (5.1). Positivity is precisely the remaining primal constraint. Since a linear objective attains its maximum at an extreme point, (5.2) follows. The uniform feasible vector has positive objective, so an optimizer necessarily contains frequency `0` in its support. ∎

### Explicit cofactor certificate

Cramer's rule turns (5.1) into a constructive certificate. For `t\in T`,

\[
\boxed{
 y_t=
 \frac{(-1)^{\sigma(t)}\det F_{E,T\setminus\{t\}}}
 {\det F_{R,T}},
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

with the usual row/column ordering sign `(-1)^{sigma(t)}`. Thus a support `T` is feasible exactly when these cofactors, after division by the common denominator, lie on the positive real ray and sum to one (the latter is already encoded by the zero row equation).

This is an exact **primal feasible-certificate construction**: choose `T`, compute its cofactor ray, and positivity certifies a valid theta point. It is finite for each prime but is not by itself an arbitrary-size theorem.

### Bordering identity exposing the conjectured `sqrt(p)` scale

Let `U=F/\sqrt p` be unitary. For a positive circuit support `T\ni0`, Cramer's rule gives

\[
y_0=p^{-1/2}
\frac{|\det U_{E,T\setminus\{0\}}|}
{|\det U_{R,T}|}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Jacobi's complementary-minor identity for a unitary matrix yields

\[
|\det U_{E,T\setminus\{0\}}|
=|\det U_{E^c,T^c\cup\{0\}}|,
\]

\[
|\det U_{R,T}|=|\det U_{R^c,T^c}|,
\qquad R^c=E^c\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\vartheta(G_E)}{\sqrt p}
=\max_{\text{positive circuits }T\ni0}
\frac{|\det U_{E^c,T^c\cup\{0\}}|}
{|\det U_{E^c\setminus\{0\},T^c}|}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The numerator is obtained from the denominator minor by bordering it with row `0` and column `0`. Equivalently the ratio is the modulus of the corresponding scalar Schur complement whenever the denominator minor is used as the base block.

Equation (5.6) is a second precise formulation of the sharp-constant question on the prime subsequence: the target constant `1` corresponds to controlling the **largest positivity-selected bordered Fourier-minor ratio**, not all Fourier minors indiscriminately.

This also makes clear why the signed sparse-kernel obstruction from the primary paper is not the whole story. On prime order, every extreme *positive* feasible point already has support exactly `r+1`, about half the coordinates in the dense model. What remains is quantitative **weight concentration inside dense positive circuits**.

\frac{\|z_+\|_\infty}{\|z_-\|_\infty}\right]

}
\tag{7.1}

A sufficient, more geometric obligation is (4.3). I will next pursue the route that is distinct from P07-B:

1. **Conic comparison / escape:** estimate the statistical geometry of `C_t` and seek a source-valid comparison between the random half-Fourier kernel `L_E` and an isotropic/Haar subspace specifically for intersection with this one-sided cone. The comparison must be strong enough at `t=1-o(1)` and at the expectation/tail level.
2. **Positive-circuit anti-concentration on primes:** use (5.6) to study only cofactor-sign-compatible supports, rather than all minors. Seek a first-moment / change-of-measure bound showing that positive circuits with bordered ratio `>1+eps` are exponentially suppressed after the positivity condition is imposed.
3. **Composite-order bridge:** determine whether a random symmetric half-row Fourier set is full spark or has a suitably strong *positive* spark property with high probability for general odd `n`. It is enough to rule out sparse **positive** circuits, not arbitrary signed dependencies.

If route (1) fails because Fourier kernels have a source-specific conic intersection not present in the isotropic benchmark, that obstruction itself is valuable: it would identify a mechanism capable of forcing a constant larger than `1` and hence a possible refutation route.
