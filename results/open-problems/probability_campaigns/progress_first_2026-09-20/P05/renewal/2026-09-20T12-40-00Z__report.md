## 2. Inherited pivotal Green representation

The previous P05 work proved the exact Poisson recurrence

\[
F_{a,b}
=\frac12+\frac12F_{a-1,b-1}
 +\frac14F_{a-1,b}+\frac14F_{a,b-1}
 +\frac14q_{a,b},
\tag{2.1}
\]

where `q_{a,b}` is the pivotal probability that the two opposite one-bit boundary insertion influences disagree.

The associated reverse Green walk starts at `(n,n)` and at each step decrements

\[
(1,1)\text{ with probability }\frac12,
\qquad
(1,0),(0,1)\text{ with probability }\frac14,
\tag{2.2}
\]

until one coordinate reaches zero. Let `tau_n` be the killing time,

\[
G_n(a,b)=\mathbf E\sum_{t<\tau_n}{\bf1}_{\{(A_t,B_t)=(a,b)\}},
\qquad
W_n=\sum_{a,b}G_n(a,b)=\mathbf E\tau_n,
\]

and define the difference-layer mass

\[
H_n(k)=\sum_{a-b=k}G_n(a,b).
\]

For `H_n(k)>0`, write

\[
\bar q_n(k)
=\frac{\sum_{a-b=k}G_n(a,b)q_{a,b}}{H_n(k)}.
\]

Then the global pivotal density is exactly

\[
p_n=\frac1{W_n}\sum_kH_n(k)\bar q_n(k),
\tag{2.3}
\]

and the previous evaluator identity gives

\[
\gamma_2=\frac23+\frac13\lim_{n\to\infty}p_n.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The preceding P05 run compressed `H_n(k)` to an exact one-dimensional binomial / simple-random-walk formula. The present run identifies its **full diffusive scaling limit**.

## 3. The Green lifetime has a deterministic macroscopic cutoff

Let

\[
D_t=A_t-B_t.
\]

One Green step changes `D_t` by `+1,0,-1` with probabilities `1/4,1/2,1/4`, so

\[
\mathbf E D_1=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(D_1)=\frac12.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Each coordinate separately is decremented at a step with probability `3/4`. Thus, before killing, the number of decrements of coordinate A in `t` steps is `Bin(t,3/4)`, and likewise for B.

### Lemma P05-R15.1 — macroscopic lifetime cutoff

\[
\boxed{\frac{\tau_n}{n}\longrightarrow\frac43\quad\text{in probability},}
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\frac{W_n}{n}\longrightarrow\frac43.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Fix `s<4/3` and `t=floor(sn)`. The mean number of decrements of either coordinate is `3t/4 <= (1-c)n` for a constant `c=c(s)>0`. A Chernoff bound therefore gives

\[
\Pr(\tau_n\le t)
\le2\Pr\{Bin(t,3/4)\ge n\}\to0.
\]

If `s>4/3`, then for the A-coordinate alone the mean number of decrements by time `floor(sn)` exceeds `n` by a positive linear fraction, and

\[
\Pr(\tau_n>t)
\le\Pr\{Bin(t,3/4)\le n-1\}\to0.
\]

This proves (3.2). Since `0<=tau_n<=2n-1`, uniform integrability is automatic and (3.3) follows. Equivalently one may sum the survival probabilities. QED.

The preceding stopped-martingale identity gives the stronger finite statement `W_n=(4/3)n-O(sqrt(n))`; only the limit is needed below.

## 4. New theorem: explicit limiting Green-layer law

Define a probability measure on the rescaled difference coordinate by

\[
\mu_n
:=\frac1{W_n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}H_n(k)\,\delta_{k/\sqrt n}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently, for bounded `f`,

\[
\int f\,d\mu_n
=\frac1{W_n}\sum_{t\ge0}
\mathbf E\left[f(D_t/\sqrt n){\bf1}_{\{t<\tau_n\}}\right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem P05-R15.2 — diffusive Green occupation limit

The measures `mu_n` converge weakly to the probability law `mu` obtained as follows:

1. choose `S` uniformly on `[0,4/3]`;
2. conditional on `S=s`, choose
   \[
   X\sim N(0,s/2).
   \]

That is,

\[
\boxed{
\int f(x)\,d\mu(x)
=\frac34\int_0^{4/3}
\mathbf E f\!\left(\sqrt{s/2}\,Z\right)ds,
\quad Z\sim N(0,1),
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

for every bounded continuous `f`.

#### Proof

Write `t=floor(sn)`. Unconditionally, `D_t` is a sum of `t` iid centered variables with variance `1/2`. Hence for every fixed `s>0`,

\[
D_{\lfloor sn\rfloor}/\sqrt n
\Rightarrow N(0,s/2).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Fix `delta>0`. Uniformly for macroscopic times `s<=4/3-delta`, the Chernoff estimate in Lemma R15.1 makes the killing probability exponentially small in `n`; hence the indicator of survival in (4.2) can be removed at asymptotically zero cost there. Uniformly for `s>=4/3+delta`, survival itself is exponentially small. The transition window

\[
|s-4/3|<\delta
\]

contains only `O(delta n)` summands, each bounded by `||f||_infty`, and hence contributes `O(delta)` after division by `W_n~4n/3`.

On the pre-cutoff interval, (4.4), a standard triangular-array/Riemann-sum argument, and boundedness of `f` give

\[
\frac1n\sum_{t<(4/3-\delta)n}
\mathbf E f(D_t/\sqrt n)
\longrightarrow
\int_0^{4/3-\delta}
\mathbf E f(\sqrt{s/2}Z)\,ds.
\]

Divide by `W_n/n ->4/3`, then let `delta downarrow0`. This proves (4.3). QED.

This theorem says that the two-dimensional killed Green kernel has a universal one-dimensional diffusive limit: away from a vanishing terminal layer, the occupation time is asymptotically uniform in macroscopic time, while the difference coordinate diffuses with variance rate `1/2`.

## 5. Closed-form density and strip mass

For `s>0`, `N(0,s/2)` has density

\[
\frac1{\sqrt{\pi s}}e^{-x^2/s}.
\]

Therefore `mu` has density

\[
g(x)=\frac34\int_0^{4/3}
\frac1{\sqrt{\pi s}}e^{-x^2/s}\,ds.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Evaluating the integral gives the explicit formula

\[
\boxed{
 g(x)
 =\sqrt{\frac3\pi}e^{-3x^2/4}
 -\frac32|x|\,\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt3|x|}{2}\right).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular

\[
g(0)=\sqrt{3/\pi}=0.977205023805839\ldots,
\qquad
\int x^2\,d\mu(x)=\frac13.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For `c>=0`, define the limiting outside-strip mass

\[
T(c):=\mu\{|x|>c\}.
\]

Because `P(|N(0,s/2)|>c)=erfc(c/sqrt(s))`,

\[
T(c)=\frac34\int_0^{4/3}
\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac c{\sqrt s}\right)ds.
\tag{5.4}
\]

A single integration by parts yields

\[
\boxed{
T(c)
=\left(1+\frac32c^2\right)
\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt3c}{2}\right)
-\sqrt{\frac3\pi}\,c\,e^{-3c^2/4}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus, for every fixed `c>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{W_n}\sum_{|k|>c\sqrt n}H_n(k)
\longrightarrow T(c).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The convergence can be given an elementary quantitative rate. Berry--Esseen for the iid increments of `D_t`, combined with Chernoff estimates outside an `n^{-1/4}`-wide macroscopic window around time `4n/3`, gives for each fixed `c>0`

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{W_n}\sum_{|k|>c\sqrt n}H_n(k)
=T(c)+O_c(n^{-1/4}).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

A proof sketch for (5.7): choose the transition window `|t-(4/3)n|<=n^{3/4}`. Its normalized contribution is `O(n^{-1/4})`. Outside it, survival or failure of survival differs from the deterministic cutoff by `exp(-Omega(sqrt(n)))`. For the unrestricted difference walk, the Berry--Esseen error at time `t` is `O(t^{-1/2})`; summed over `t<=2n` and normalized by `n` this contributes `O(n^{-1/2})`. The Riemann sum for `erfc(c/sqrt(s))`, continuously extended by zero at `s=0`, contributes a smaller error. Finally `W_n=(4/3)n+O(sqrt(n))`.

The key practical distinction from the preceding `sqrt(n log n)` strip is that **for any fixed permitted outside mass epsilon, only a constant times `sqrt(n)` is asymptotically necessary**. The logarithm is needed only when one insists that the tail itself vanish polynomially in `n`.
