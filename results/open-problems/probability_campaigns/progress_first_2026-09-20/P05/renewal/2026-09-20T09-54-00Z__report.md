## 2. Pivotal probability as a deterministic function of remaining lengths

For integers `m,n>=1`, condition independent fair binary strings `X_1^m,Y_1^n` on a mismatch at their first symbols. Define

\[
A=LCS(X_2^m,Y_1^n),\qquad
B=LCS(X_1^m,Y_2^n).
\]

As proved in the preceding run, `|A-B|` is always zero or one. Put

\[
\boxed{q_{m,n}:=\mathbf P(|A-B|=1\mid X_1\ne Y_1).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is now a scalar depending only on the two remaining source lengths. It is **not** an occupation probability coupled to a particular evaluator trajectory.

### Theorem P05-R12.1 — exact source-averaged Poisson equation

For every `m,n>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
F_{m,n}
=\frac12
+\frac12F_{m-1,n-1}
+\frac14F_{m-1,n}
+\frac14F_{m,n-1}
+\frac14q_{m,n}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
q_{m,n}
=4F_{m,n}-2-2F_{m-1,n-1}-F_{m-1,n}-F_{m,n-1}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

Condition on the first-symbol equality event.

With probability `1/2`, the first symbols match, and the ordinary binary LCS recursion gives exactly

\[
1+LCS(X_2^m,Y_2^n).
\]

With probability `1/2`, they mismatch. In that case

\[
LCS(X_1^m,Y_1^n)=\max\{A,B\}
=\frac{A+B+|A-B|}{2}.
\]

Conditioning on the mismatch does not alter the law of `X_2^m` or `Y_2^n`. Hence

\[
\mathbf E[A\mid X_1\ne Y_1]=F_{m-1,n},
\qquad
\mathbf E[B\mid X_1\ne Y_1]=F_{m,n-1}.
\]

Since `|A-B|` is a `{0,1}` variable, its conditional expectation is `q_{m,n}`. Substitution yields (2.2), and rearranging yields (2.3). QED.

Thus the expected-LCS table solves a linear discrete Poisson equation whose only nonlinear source is the scalar pivotal table `q`.

## 3. Pivotality is exactly disagreement of two boundary insertion gains

The source `q_{m,n}` has a more useful common-core representation.

Condition on the mismatch orientation `X_1=0,Y_1=1`; the opposite orientation follows by bit complementation. Put

\[
U=X_2^m,\qquad V=Y_2^n,
\qquad C=LCS(U,V).
\]

Define the two boundary insertion gains

\[
\alpha(U,V):=LCS(U,1V)-C,
\]

\[
\beta(U,V):=LCS(0U,V)-C.
\]

Adding one symbol changes LCS by at most one, so `alpha,beta` are both in `{0,1}`.

### Theorem P05-R12.2 — insertion-gain XOR identity

For every `m,n>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
q_{m,n}=\mathbf E\,|\alpha(U,V)-\beta(U,V)|.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, a mismatch is pivotal exactly when the two opposite one-letter boundary insertions on the common random core have different gain bits.

#### Proof

Under the orientation `0/1`,

\[
A=LCS(U,1V)=C+\alpha,
\qquad
B=LCS(0U,V)=C+\beta.
\]

Hence `|A-B|=|alpha-beta|`. Averaging over iid `U,V` gives (3.1). The `1/0` orientation is identical after complementing every bit. QED.

This is a cleaner finite-state target than an offline full LCS value: one needs to control disagreement of two one-bit *boundary influences* on the same core.

### Exact obstruction away from the diagonal

There cannot be a useful universal bound `q_{m,n}<=Q<1` over all aspect ratios. For `n=1`, condition on mismatch orientation `0/1`. Then the second deletion child is zero, while the first equals one iff `X_2^m` contains at least one `1`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
q_{m,1}=1-2^{-(m-1)}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

So `q_{m,1}->1`. Any pivotal certificate relevant to `gamma_2` must exploit the fact that the source-averaged evaluator spends asymptotically almost all of its time near the diagonal. The next section proves that quantitatively.

## 4. Explicit Green kernel for the Poisson equation

Consider the killed reverse walk on positive integer pairs. From `(a,b)` it moves to

\[
(a-1,b-1)\quad\text{with probability }1/2,
\]

\[
(a-1,b)\quad\text{with probability }1/4,
\qquad
(a,b-1)\quad\text{with probability }1/4,
\]

and stops when either coordinate reaches zero.

For start `(M,N)` and target `(a,b)` with `1<=a<=M`, `1<=b<=N`, put

\[
r=M-a,\qquad s=N-b.
\]

Because both coordinates decrease monotonically, the probability of ever visiting `(a,b)` is the finite exact kernel

\[
\boxed{
G_{M,N}(a,b)
=
\sum_{d=0}^{\min(r,s)}
\frac{(r+s-d)!}{d!(r-d)!(s-d)!}
\left(\frac12\right)^d
\left(\frac14\right)^{r+s-2d}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Here `d` is the number of diagonal decrements, `r-d` the number of first-coordinate-only decrements, and `s-d` the number of second-coordinate-only decrements.

### Theorem P05-R12.3 — exact Green representation

For all positive `M,N`,

\[
\boxed{
F_{M,N}
=
\sum_{a=1}^{M}\sum_{b=1}^{N}
G_{M,N}(a,b)
\left(\frac12+\frac14q_{a,b}\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (2.2) is exactly the one-step dynamic-programming equation for the killed reverse walk with local reward

\[
f(a,b)=\frac12+\frac14q_{a,b}
\]

and zero boundary values. Iterating until the finite stopping time gives the expected accumulated reward. Since the walk is coordinatewise monotone, an interior state can be visited at most once, so its expected occupation is precisely the hitting probability (4.1). Summing local rewards against these occupancies yields (4.2). QED.

This independently recovers the preceding randomized-evaluator identity but removes a delicate conditioning issue: after source averaging, the occupation weights are **fully explicit and source-independent**.

## 5. Exact Green average for the pivotal density

Now specialize to the diagonal start `(n,n)` and abbreviate

\[
G_n(a,b):=G_{n,n}(a,b),
\qquad
W_n:=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{b=1}^{n}G_n(a,b).
\]

`W_n` is the expected lifetime of the reverse walk.

The occupation-weighted pivotal density from the preceding report is exactly

\[
\boxed{
p_n=
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}G_n(a,b)q_{a,b}}
{\displaystyle\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}G_n(a,b)}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_2=\frac23+\frac13\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\sum G_nq}{\sum G_n}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The global random-optimization constant has now been reduced to a deterministic Green average of the two-parameter boundary-influence disagreement table `q_{a,b}`.

This is the principal structural movement of the run.

## 6. New localization theorem: only a diffusive neighborhood of the diagonal matters

Let `(A_t,B_t)` be the reverse walk started from `(n,n)`, let `tau_n` be its killing time, and put

\[
D_t=A_t-B_t.
\]

Before killing, the increments of `D_t` are iid with law

\[
\mathbf P(\Delta D=0)=\frac12,
\qquad
\mathbf P(\Delta D=1)=\mathbf P(\Delta D=-1)=\frac14.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbf E D_t^2=\frac t2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Also pathwise

\[
n\le\tau_n\le2n-1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For `w>=1`, define the fraction of Green occupation outside the width-`w` diagonal strip by

\[
R_{n,w}:=
\frac{\sum_{|a-b|>w}G_n(a,b)}{W_n}.
\]

### Theorem P05-R12.4 — explicit strip localization

For every `n,w>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
R_{n,w}
\le
\frac{(2n-2)(2n-1)}{4nw^2}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

The numerator equals the expected number of times before killing that `|D_t|>w`. Since `tau_n<=2n-1`, Chebyshev and (6.1) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{|a-b|>w}G_n(a,b)
&=\mathbf E\sum_{t<\tau_n}{\bf1}_{\{|D_t|>w\}}\\
&\le\sum_{t=0}^{2n-2}\mathbf P(|D_t|>w)\\
&\le\sum_{t=0}^{2n-2}\frac{t}{2w^2}\\
&=\frac{(2n-2)(2n-1)}{4w^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since `W_n=E tau_n>=n`, division yields (6.3). QED.

Thus if `w_n/sqrt(n)->infinity`, then `R_{n,w_n}->0`.

There is also a deterministic small-core bound inside the strip. Fix `h>=1`. Once a monotone path is at a state with

\[
\min(a,b)<h,
\qquad |a-b|\le w,
\]

one has `a+b<2h+w`. Each subsequent step decreases `a+b` by at least one, so the total number of visits in this small-core portion of the strip is at most `2h+w` pathwise. Consequently its Green mass divided by `W_n` is at most

\[
\frac{2h+w}{n}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Combining (6.3)--(6.4), define the central large-core region

\[
\mathcal C_n(h,w)
:=\{(a,b):\min(a,b)\ge h,\ |a-b|\le w\}.
\]

Its normalized Green mass is at least `1-epsilon_{n,h,w}`, where

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{n,h,w}
:=
\frac{(2n-2)(2n-1)}{4nw^2}
+\frac{2h+w}{n}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Since `0<=q<=1`, if

\[
q^-_{n,h,w}:=\inf_{(a,b)\in\mathcal C_n(h,w)}q_{a,b},
\qquad
q^+_{n,h,w}:=\sup_{(a,b)\in\mathcal C_n(h,w)}q_{a,b},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
q^-_{n,h,w}-\varepsilon_{n,h,w}
\le p_n\le
q^+_{n,h,w}+\varepsilon_{n,h,w}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

For example, taking

\[
h_n=\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor,
\qquad
w_n=\lfloor n^{3/4}\rfloor
\]

makes `epsilon_{n,h_n,w_n}->0`. Hence `gamma_2` is determined entirely by pivotal probabilities on **large, near-balanced cores**. The extreme-aspect-ratio behavior (3.2), although arbitrarily close to one, contributes asymptotically zero Green mass.

### Finite-certificate implication

Equation (6.6) is the arbitrary-length bridge needed for the next finite-state comparison. Any finite-state / transfer proof that certifies a uniform bound

\[
q_{a,b}\le Q
\]

for all sufficiently large near-balanced cores represented by its quotient immediately yields

\[
\boxed{\gamma_2\le\frac23+\frac Q3.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Likewise a uniform lower certificate `q_{a,b}>=Q` on the same central regime yields `gamma_2>=2/3+Q/3`. Thus a finite boundary-influence automaton need not control the highly unbalanced part of the full LCS table.

## 7. Sharp second-order normalization of the Green walk

The preceding report only required `E H_n=O(sqrt(n))`, where

\[
H_n:=|D_{\tau_n}|.
\]

The explicit walk now gives its exact first-order asymptotic constant.

### Theorem P05-R12.5 — terminal imbalance constant

\[
\boxed{
\frac{H_n}{\sqrt n}\Longrightarrow |N(0,2/3)|,
\qquad
\frac{\mathbf E H_n}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow\frac{2}{\sqrt{3\pi}}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
W_n=\mathbf E\tau_n
=\frac{4n}{3}
-\frac{4}{3\sqrt{3\pi}}\sqrt n
+o(\sqrt n).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

#### Proof

Let `C_t` be the total number of source coordinates consumed by time `t`. Its iid increments equal `2` with probability `1/2` and `1` with probability `1/2`, so `C_t/t->3/2` almost surely. At killing,

\[
C_{\tau_n}+H_n=2n.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The martingale `D_t^2-t/2`, stopped at bounded `tau_n`, gives

\[
\mathbf E H_n^2=\frac12\mathbf E\tau_n\le n.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Hence `H_n/n->0` in `L^2`, and (7.3) gives `C_{tau_n}/n->2` in probability. Because `n<=tau_n<=2n-1`, the ordinary strong law is uniform over the possible random indices, so

\[
\frac{C_{\tau_n}}{\tau_n}\to\frac32,
\qquad
\frac{\tau_n}{n}\to\frac43
\]

in probability.

The increments of `D_t` are iid, centered, bounded and have variance `1/2`. The standard Anscombe random-index CLT therefore yields

\[
\frac{D_{\tau_n}}{\sqrt n}\Longrightarrow N(0,2/3).
\]

Equation (7.4) gives uniform integrability of `H_n/sqrt(n)`, so absolute first moments converge, yielding the second part of (7.1).

Finally, Wald's identity for `C_t` and (7.3) gives the exact relation

\[
\frac32\mathbf E\tau_n=2n-\mathbf E H_n,
\]

which combined with (7.1) gives (7.2). QED.

This sharpens the evaluator normalization from a generic square-root error to an explicit universal coefficient.

## 8. Failed route recorded honestly: naive Harris/FKG monotonicity does not apply

The insertion-gain XOR representation suggests trying to prove positive association of `alpha` and `beta`. A tempting shortcut is to claim both gain indicators are coordinatewise monotone under the natural order “raise `U` bits from 0 to 1 and lower `V` bits from 1 to 0”, then invoke Harris/FKG.

That monotonicity is false.

Take the common core

\[
U=011,\qquad V=000.
\]

Then `LCS(U,V)=1` and

\[
\alpha(U,V)=LCS(011,1000)-1=0.
\]

Change only the first bit of `V` from `0` to `1`, obtaining `V'=100`. Still `LCS(U,V')=1`, but now

\[
\alpha(U,V')=LCS(011,1100)-1=1.
\]

Thus `alpha` can increase when a `V` bit is raised `0->1`; the naive product-order monotonicity needed by that FKG argument fails. No positive-correlation claim is made here. This failed pivot is worth preserving because it prevents a seductive but invalid one-line upper-bound argument.
