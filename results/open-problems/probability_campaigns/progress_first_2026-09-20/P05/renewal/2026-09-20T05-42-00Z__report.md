## 2. Inherited renewal identity and one-sided finite certificate

Let `G_i,H_i` be independent iid geometric run lengths

\[
\Pr(G_i=k)=\Pr(H_i=k)=2^{-k},\qquad k\ge1.
\]

For even `r`, define alternating run blocks

\[
A_r=0^{G_1}1^{G_2}\cdots1^{G_r},\qquad
B_r=0^{H_1}1^{H_2}\cdots1^{H_r},
\]

and

\[
b_r:=\mathbf E LCS(A_r,B_r),\qquad \rho_r:=\frac{b_r}{2r}.
\]

The first P05 renewal report proved

\[
\boxed{\gamma_2=\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_{2m}=\sup_{m\ge1}\rho_{2m}}
\tag{2.1}
\]

and the explicit deterministic-length comparison

\[
\boxed{
\left|\rho_r-\frac{a_{2r}}{2r}\right|
\le \sqrt{\frac2r}+\frac1r.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a cap `M`, let `G_i^(M)=min(G_i,M)`, `H_i^(M)=min(H_i,M)`, form `A_{r,M},B_{r,M}`, and set

\[
\rho_{r,M}:=\frac{\mathbf E LCS(A_{r,M},B_{r,M})}{2r}.
\]

The same report proved the exact cap sandwich

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{r,M}\le\rho_r\le\rho_{r,M}+2^{-(M-1)}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus `rho_{r,M}` is an exact rational finite computation and was already known to give a lower certificate for `gamma_2`. What was missing was a finite-size inequality in the **opposite direction** strong enough to turn the same finite computation into a two-sided certificate for the infinite constant.

## 3. External ingredient: Alexander's explicit finite-size inequality

K. S. Alexander, *The Rate of Convergence of the Mean Length of the Longest Common Subsequence*, Annals of Applied Probability 4(4) (1994), 1074--1082, Proposition 2.4, proves the following. If `n>=8` and positive `lambda,theta` satisfy

\[
\lambda^2 \ge
1+\frac{1}{2n\log(2n)}
+\frac{2\lambda}{\sqrt{2n\log(2n)}}
+\frac{\log(5.1\lambda)}{\log(2n)}
+\frac{\log\log(2n)}{2\log(2n)}
\tag{3.1}
\]

(the paper prints a strict sign on the first condition, which our margin below satisfies) and

\[
\theta^2\ge2+\frac{\log4}{\log(2n)},
\tag{3.2}
\]

then

\[
\frac{a_{2n}}{2n}
\ge \gamma_2-(2\lambda+\theta)
\sqrt{\frac{\log(2n)}{2n}}
-\sqrt{\frac{2\log2}{n}}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The useful observation for P05 is that one can choose fixed elementary values `lambda=theta=8/5` **uniformly for every n>=8**.

### Lemma P05-R8.1 — uniform explicit Alexander parameters

For every integer `n>=8`, Alexander's conditions (3.1)--(3.2) hold with

\[
\lambda=\theta=\frac85.
\]

#### Proof

Write `L=log(2n)`. First note the elementary bound `log 2>2/3`. Indeed,

\[
e=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac1{k!}
<\frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{j\ge0}4^{-j}
=\frac{49}{18}<\frac{11}{4},
\]

so `e^2<121/16<8`, hence `e^(2/3)<2` and therefore `log2>2/3`.
Consequently, for `n>=8`,

\[
L\ge\log16=4\log2>\frac83.
\tag{3.4}
\]

We bound the four correction terms in (3.1) separately.

First,

\[
\frac1{2nL}<\frac1{16(8/3)}=\frac3{128}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Second,

\[
\frac{2\lambda}{\sqrt{2nL}}
\le \frac{16/5}{\sqrt{128/3}}<\frac{49}{100},
\tag{3.6}
\]

because the square of the middle expression is exactly `6/25=0.24`, whereas `(49/100)^2=2401/10000=0.2401`.

Third, since `5.1 lambda = 8.16 = 8(51/50)`, `log(1+x)<x`, and `log2>2/3`,

\[
\frac{\log(5.1\lambda)}{L}
\le\frac{3\log2+\log(51/50)}{4\log2}
<\frac34+\frac{1/50}{8/3}
=\frac{303}{400}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Fourth, the elementary calculus maximum of `log x/x` on `(0,infinity)` is `1/e`, so

\[
\frac{\log L}{2L}\le\frac1{2e}<\frac3{16},
\tag{3.8}
\]

where the last inequality follows from `e>8/3` (already the partial sum through `1/3!` equals `8/3`, with positive remaining terms).

Therefore the complete right side of (3.1) is strictly smaller than

\[
1+\frac3{128}+\frac{49}{100}+\frac{303}{400}+\frac3{16}
=\frac{7867}{3200}
=2.4584375
<\frac{64}{25}=\lambda^2.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For (3.2),

\[
2+\frac{\log4}{L}
\le2+\frac{\log4}{\log16}
=\frac52
<\frac{64}{25}=\theta^2.
\tag{3.10}
\]

This proves both hypotheses uniformly. QED.

Substituting the fixed parameters into (3.3) gives, for every `n>=8`,

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_2\le\frac{a_{2n}}{2n}
+\frac{24}{5}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2n)}{2n}}
+\sqrt{\frac{2\log2}{n}}.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

## 4. Main new theorem: exact finite two-sided renewal certificate

### Theorem P05-R8.2 — explicit renewal/Alexander sandwich

For every **even** integer `r>=8` and every integer `M>=1`, define the exact rational finite quantity `rho_{r,M}` as in Section 2. Then

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{r,M}\le\gamma_2\le\rho_{r,M}+\varepsilon_{r,M},
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{r,M}=
\frac{24}{5}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2r)}{2r}}
+\sqrt{\frac{2\log2}{r}}
+\sqrt{\frac2r}
+\frac1r
+2^{-(M-1)}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\varepsilon_{r,M}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{whenever }r\to\infty\text{ through even integers and }M\to\infty.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus the binary Chvatal--Sankoff constant is the limit of an **explicit sequence of finite exact rational intervals** whose widths are known before the finite computations are performed.

#### Proof

The lower inequality in (4.1) is inherited from (2.1) and (2.3):

\[
\rho_{r,M}\le\rho_r\le\gamma_2.
\]

For the upper inequality, Alexander's explicit estimate (3.11) at `n=r` gives

\[
\gamma_2\le\frac{a_{2r}}{2r}
+\frac{24}{5}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2r)}{2r}}
+\sqrt{\frac{2\log2}{r}}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The deterministic-length / renewal comparison (2.2) implies

\[
\frac{a_{2r}}{2r}
\le\rho_r+\sqrt{\frac2r}+\frac1r,
\tag{4.5}
\]

and the cap sandwich (2.3) implies

\[
\rho_r\le\rho_{r,M}+2^{-(M-1)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Substituting (4.5)--(4.6) into (4.4) is exactly (4.1)--(4.2). Every term in (4.2) vanishes under (4.3). QED.

### Corollary P05-R8.3 — finite primal/dual certificates control arbitrary lengths

Fix even `r>=8` and `M`.

- Any exact finite **primal** certificate `L_{r,M}<=rho_{r,M}` immediately gives
  \[
  \gamma_2\ge L_{r,M}.
  \]
- Any exact finite **dual** certificate `U_{r,M}>=rho_{r,M}` immediately gives
  \[
  \gamma_2\le U_{r,M}+\varepsilon_{r,M}.
  \]

Therefore it is not necessary to enumerate every capped run-block pair exactly. ## 6. A correction to the preceding lookahead report

The preceding report's Lemma P05-R7.2 asserted lookahead monotonicity and gave a **pointwise Bellman-potential lift** as its proof. That particular proof should not be relied upon as written: in the `K+1` process the extra retained run category is conditioned/fixed, whereas the `K` Bellman operator averages the corresponding fresh category; after a transition that coordinate can enter the first `K` retained coordinates.
