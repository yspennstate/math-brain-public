## 2. Inherited pivotal Green formulation

The previous P05 reports reduce the source problem to pivotal probabilities

\[
q_{a,b}=\Pr(\alpha\ne\beta),
\]

where `alpha,beta` are the two opposite one-bit boundary-insertion influence bits. They proved the exact Poisson equation

\[
F_{a,b}
=\frac12+\frac12F_{a-1,b-1}
 +\frac14F_{a-1,b}+\frac14F_{a,b-1}
 +\frac14q_{a,b}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Its Green walk, started at `(n,n)`, decreases by

\[
(1,1)\text{ with probability }\frac12,
\qquad
(1,0),(0,1)\text{ with probability }\frac14,
\tag{2.2}
\]

and is killed when either coordinate reaches zero. Let `tau_n` be its killing time, let

\[
G_n(a,b)=\mathbf E\sum_{t<\tau_n}{\bf1}_{\{(A_t,B_t)=(a,b)\}},
\qquad
W_n=\sum_{a,b}G_n(a,b)=\mathbf E\tau_n,
\]

and let

\[
R_{n,w}:=\frac1{W_n}\sum_{|a-b|>w}G_n(a,b).
\]

The preceding run proved

\[
R_{n,w}
\le \frac{4(n-1)}n\exp\!\left(-\frac{w^2}{2n-2}\right).
\tag{2.3}
\]

That proof used only the pathwise worst-case lifetime `tau_n<=2n-1`. The present run removes that loss by identifying the exact survival geometry.

## 3. New exact identity: survival is a binomial/simple-walk event

At each reverse Green step introduce an indicator `I_i` for a diagonal decrement. Then

\[
\Pr(I_i=1)=\Pr(I_i=0)=\frac12.
\]

If `I_i=0`, independently choose an orientation sign `epsilon_i in {+1,-1}` with equal probabilities. Define

\[
C_t=\sum_{i=1}^t I_i,
\qquad
U_t=t-C_t,
\]

and let `S_u` be an ordinary simple symmetric random walk after `u` sign steps.

Conditional on `C_t=c`, the Green coordinate difference is exactly

\[
D_t:=A_t-B_t\ \stackrel d=\ S_{t-c}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The coordinate sum is deterministic given `(t,C_t)`:

\[
A_t+B_t=2n-(t+C_t),
\tag{3.2}
\]

because a diagonal move spends two coordinates and a non-diagonal move spends one. Since

\[
2\min(A_t,B_t)=A_t+B_t-|A_t-B_t|,
\]

we obtain the exact survival criterion

\[
\boxed{
 t<\tau_n
\iff
 t+C_t+|D_t|\le2n-2.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is a structural identity, not an asymptotic estimate.

### Theorem P05-R14.1 — exact one-dimensional Green compression

For every `n>=1` and integer `k`, define the total Green occupation of difference layer `k` by

\[
H_n(k):=\sum_{a-b=k}G_n(a,b).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
H_n(k)
=
\sum_{\substack{u\ge |k|\\u\equiv k\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
\frac{\binom{u}{(u+k)/2}}{2^u}
\sum_{c=0}^{\lfloor(2n-2-u-|k|)/2\rfloor}
\frac{\binom{c+u}{c}}{2^{c+u}},
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

with an empty inner sum interpreted as zero.

#### Proof

Write `u=t-c` for the number of non-diagonal moves. For fixed `t=c+u`,

\[
\Pr(C_t=c)=\frac{\binom{c+u}{c}}{2^{c+u}}.
\]

Conditional on these `u` non-diagonal moves,

\[
\Pr(S_u=k)=\frac{\binom{u}{(u+k)/2}}{2^u}
\]

when parity and range permit. By (3.3), a visit with difference `k` is alive exactly when

\[
2c+u+|k|\le2n-2.
\]

Summing its probability over all `u,c` gives (3.4). QED.

The inner sum is twice a negative-binomial CDF:

\[
\sum_{c=0}^C\frac{\binom{c+u}{c}}{2^{c+u}}
=2\Pr\{NB(u+1,1/2)\le C\},
\]

under the convention that `NB(r,p)` counts failures before the `r`-th success. Thus the full two-dimensional Green strip mass can be computed from a one-dimensional simple-walk / negative-binomial mixture.

An equivalent exact time representation is

\[
\Pr(t<\tau_n,\,|D_t|>w)
=
\sum_{c=0}^t \frac{\binom tc}{2^t}
\Pr\!\left(
 w<|S_{t-c}|\le 2n-2-t-c
\right).
\tag{3.5}
\]

This identity is useful for both exact finite computation and analytic tail estimates.

## 4. Sharper normalization from stopped martingales

The earlier evaluator work already supplies the stopped-martingale identities; here they are combined to obtain a useful explicit denominator for the new tail bound.

Let

\[
H_n:=|A_{\tau_n}-B_{\tau_n}|.
\]

The coordinate-sum process loses mean `3/2` per Green step, hence

\[
\boxed{
W_n=\mathbf E\tau_n
=\frac{4n}{3}-\frac23\mathbf EH_n.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The difference process has mean-zero increments of variance `1/2`, so optional stopping gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbf EH_n^2=\frac12W_n.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\mathbf EH_n\le\sqrt{W_n/2}.
\]

Putting `z=sqrt(W_n)` into (4.1) therefore yields

\[
z^2+\frac{2}{3\sqrt2}z-\frac{4n}{3}\ge0,
\]

and hence the explicit lower bound

\[
\boxed{
W_n\ge \underline W_n
:=
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{4n}{3}+\frac1{18}}
-\frac1{3\sqrt2}
\right)^2.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular `W_n=(4/3)n-O(sqrt(n))`, and (4.3) is already enough to replace the crude denominator `W_n>=n` in finite strip certification.

## 5. New theorem: localization at the typical lifetime rather than the worst lifetime

Fix `n>=2`, `w>=1`, and `s>=0`. Put

\[
T=\left\lceil\frac{4(n-1)}3+s\right\rceil,
\]

and assume `T<=2n-2` (the displayed choices below satisfy this automatically).

### Theorem P05-R14.2 — split Green-strip inequality

\[
\boxed{
R_{n,w}
\le
\frac{
2T\exp(-w^2/T)
+(2n-2-T)\exp\!\left(-\frac{9s^2}{4(n-1)}\right)
}{\underline W_n}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

For `1<=t<=T`, forget killing. The difference increment is distributed as the difference of two independent Bernoulli-`1/2` variables, equivalently one half of a `2t`-step simple symmetric walk. Its mgf is

\[
\mathbf Ee^{\theta D_1}=\cosh^2(\theta/2)\le e^{\theta^2/4},
\]

so Chernoff gives

\[
\Pr(|D_t|>w)\le2e^{-w^2/t}\le2e^{-w^2/T}.
\]

The contribution of these times is at most the first term in the numerator of (5.1).

For `t>=T+1`, survival (3.3) implies in particular

\[
C_t\le2n-2-t.
\]

Since `C_t~Bin(t,1/2)`, its lower-tail displacement from its mean is

\[
\delta_t
=\frac t2-(2n-2-t)
=\frac{3t}{2}-2n+2.
\]

Our choice of `T` gives `delta_t>=3s/2`. Hoeffding therefore yields

\[
\Pr(t<\tau_n)
\le
\Pr(C_t\le2n-2-t)
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{2\delta_t^2}{t}\right)
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{9s^2}{4(n-1)}\right),
\]

where `t<=2n-2` was used in the last step. There are at most `2n-2-T` such times. Divide the resulting occupation bound by `W_n>=underline W_n`. QED.

## 7. A weaker target for the next finite-state certificate: layer averages

The compression (3.4) suggests a useful relaxation of the previous pointwise program. For every layer with `H_n(k)>0`, define its Green-weighted pivotal average

\[
\bar q_n(k)
:=
\frac{1}{H_n(k)}
\sum_{a-b=k}G_n(a,b)q_{a,b}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then the global pivotal density is exactly

\[
\boxed{
p_n
=\frac1{W_n}\sum_k H_n(k)\bar q_n(k).}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Consequently, to prove an upper bound `p_n<=Q+error`, it is enough to control the **layer averages** `bar q_n(k)` for the Green-relevant differences `|k|<=w`; one need not necessarily prove a uniform pointwise bound on every `(a,b)` in the strip. This is strictly weaker as a certification target and matches the new one-dimensional kernel.

This observation does not itself bound `bar q_n(k)`. It identifies a smaller exact object for the next transfer/dual.
