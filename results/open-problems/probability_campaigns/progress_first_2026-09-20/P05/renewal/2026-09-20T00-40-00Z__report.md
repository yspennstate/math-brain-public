## 3. A finite-state regenerative alignment policy

Fix an integer cap `M>=2`. For a geometric run length `G`, define its **usable quota**

\[
Q_M(G)=\min(G,M).
\]

Its exact law on `{1,...,M}` is

\[
p_M(k)=
\begin{cases}
2^{-k},&1\le k<M,\\
2^{-(M-1)},&k=M.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The policy deliberately ignores every symbol beyond usable quota `M` in a long run; those ignored symbols are simply deleted when that source run is left. Hence every match made by the policy is a valid common subsequence of the original uncapped iid strings.

At a **same-phase epoch**, one source has an active same-symbol run with residual usable quota `x in {1,...,M}` and the other source exposes a fresh run with quota `y~p_M`. Match

\[
m=\min(x,y)
\]

symbols.

### Tie

If `x=y`, both active usable quotas are exhausted. Delete any ignored overflow, leave both source runs, and regenerate from two fresh opposite-symbol runs. For the one-dimensional state below, choose one of those fresh runs as the retained coordinate and denote its quota by `z~p_M`.

### Mismatch

If `x\ne y`, let

\[
d=|x-y|\ge1
\]

be the residual usable quota in the longer active run. The shorter source now exposes its next run, whose symbol is opposite to the residual symbol, with independent quota `c~p_M`.

The policy deletes the smaller crossing quantity and retains the larger:

\[
\boxed{z=\max(d,c).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

- If `d<=c`, delete the residual `d`; the longer source advances to a fresh run of the same symbol as the retained `c`.
- If `c<d`, delete the intervening run `c`; that source advances to a fresh run of the same symbol as the retained residual `d`.

Thus after every epoch the process returns to exactly the same structural form: one residual usable quota `z` paired with one fresh same-symbol run.

Crucially, **every epoch completes exactly two original source runs in total**. In the tie case it completes the two active runs. In the mismatch case it completes the shorter active run plus exactly one of (i) the longer active run or (ii) the newly exposed intervening run.

## 4. Exact M-state chain

The retained usable quota `X_t in {1,...,M}` is a Markov chain. Let

\[
r_M(x)=\sum_{y=1}^M p_M(y)\min(x,y).
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the expected number of matched symbols in the epoch given `X_t=x`.

The transition matrix is

\[
\boxed{
P_M(x,z)
=\sum_{y=1}^M p_M(y)
\left[
\mathbf 1_{\{x=y\}}p_M(z)
+
\mathbf 1_{\{x\ne y\}}
\sum_{c=1}^M p_M(c)
\mathbf 1_{\{z=\max(|x-y|,c)\}}
\right].
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every entry is rational. Moreover, for every `x,z`, the tie event `y=x` followed by a fresh reset to `z` has probability `p_M(x)p_M(z)>0`. Hence every entry of `P_M` is positive. The chain is irreducible and aperiodic and has a unique stationary law `pi_M`; because the linear system has rational coefficients, every component of `pi_M` is rational.

This replaces the previous `M^{2r}` block-pair certificate by an exact state space of size only `M` for this policy.

## 5. Main theorem: finite stationary certificate controls the original arbitrary-length problem

### Theorem P05-R3 — M-state regenerative lower certificate

For every `M>=2`, let `pi_M` be the unique stationary distribution of (4.2). Then

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_2\ge
\Lambda_M
:=\frac12\sum_{x=1}^M \pi_M(x)r_M(x).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, `Lambda_M` is an exact rational finite certificate obtained from an `M x M` rational linear system.

### Proof

The only nontrivial issue is the normalization: the Markov chain counts alignment epochs, while `gamma_2` is normalized by original source symbols. The fact that exactly two source runs finish per epoch makes the conversion exact asymptotically.

Augment the finite chain by an orientation bit `O_t in {A,B}` recording which physical source owns the retained quota `X_t`. On a tie reset, choose the orientation by an independent fair coin. On a mismatch, the orientation changes exactly according to which crossing quantity is retained. This gives a finite irreducible Markov chain on at most `2M` states. Its kernel is invariant under interchanging the two iid source strings. Therefore its unique stationary law is invariant under that swap.

Let `K_A(t),K_B(t)` be the numbers of complete original runs consumed from sources `A,B` by the end of `t` epochs. Pathwise,

\[
K_A(t)+K_B(t)=2t.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The run-count increments are bounded. By swap symmetry of the stationary augmented chain, their stationary means are equal. Together with (5.2), each stationary mean is exactly one run per epoch. The finite-state ergodic theorem therefore gives, from any initial state,

\[
\frac{K_A(t)}t\to1,
\qquad
\frac{K_B(t)}t\to1
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Now let `G_1,G_2,...` and `H_1,H_2,...` be the **original uncapped** iid geometric run lengths of the two strings. The ordinary strong law gives

\[
\frac{G_1+\cdots+G_n}{n}\to2,
\qquad
\frac{H_1+\cdots+H_n}{n}\to2
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

and hence, along the random subsequences `K_A(t),K_B(t)` which both tend to infinity,

\[
\frac{G_1+\cdots+G_{K_A(t)}}t\to2,
\qquad
\frac{H_1+\cdots+H_{K_B(t)}}t\to2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the policy consumes asymptotically `2t` original symbols from each source in `t` epochs; cap overflow has not been silently removed from the normalization.

Let `R(t)` be the number of matched symbols produced by the policy in its first `t` epochs. The reward is bounded by `M` and has conditional mean `r_M(X_t)`. Applying the ergodic theorem to the finite Markov-additive process yields

\[
\frac{R(t)}t\to
\bar r_M:=\sum_x\pi_M(x)r_M(x)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let `N_t` be the larger of the two original symbol-prefix lengths consumed by time `t`. By (5.4), `N_t/t ->2`. Every match produced by the policy is a valid common subsequence of the two prefixes of lengths at most `N_t`, so after padding the shorter consumed prefix inside its infinite source string,

\[
L(X_1^{N_t},Y_1^{N_t})\ge R(t).
\]

The Chvatal--Sankoff almost-sure/subadditive limit along the subsequence `N_t` is still `gamma_2`. Therefore

\[
\gamma_2
\ge
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{R(t)}{N_t}
=
\frac{\bar r_M}{2},
\]

which is (5.1). QED.

### Why this is the requested arbitrary-length bridge

The finite object is only the exact stationary solution of an `M`-state rational chain. The proof above converts it directly into a valid asymptotic lower bound for the **original uncapped iid binary strings of arbitrary length**. There is no Monte Carlo inference and no finite-`n` extrapolation.

## 8. What this teaches and the remaining gap

The certificate also identifies the limitation of the current one-residual state. The policy resolves every crossing immediately using only

\[
(d,c)=(\text{same-symbol residual},\text{opposite intervening run})
\]

and retains only `max(d,c)`. Exact computations as `M` grows rapidly stabilize near `0.74525`, far below `0.792665992`. Merely increasing the cap is therefore not the high-value next move.

The missing ingredient is **future-sensitive crossing resolution**. At a crossing, the true LCS may decide whether to sacrifice `d` or `c` using information in later runs. The next state must preserve enough lookahead to price those two branches rather than collapsing them immediately.
