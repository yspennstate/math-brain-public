## 3. New theorem: the entire two-bit boundary law is determined by four expected-LCS values

Write

\[
F=F_{m,n},\quad
F_L=F_{m-1,n},\quad
F_D=F_{m,n-1},\quad
F_0=F_{m-1,n-1}.
\]

Let

\[
p_{ij}^{m,n}:=\mathbf P(\alpha=i,\beta=j),\qquad i,j\in\{0,1\}.
\]

### Theorem P05-R13.1 — exact four-cell boundary-influence law

For every `m,n>=1`,

\[
\boxed{p_{00}^{m,n}=2(1+F_0-F),}
\tag{3.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{p_{10}^{m,n}=2F-F_D-F_0-1,}
\tag{3.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{p_{01}^{m,n}=2F-F_L-F_0-1,}
\tag{3.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{p_{11}^{m,n}=1+F_L+F_D-2F.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the apparently global joint distribution of the two opposite boundary influences requires no additional joint statistic once the four neighboring expected-LCS values are known.

#### Proof

By complement symmetry of the fair source, conditioning the newly inserted first bit of a random length-`n` string to be `1` does not change its expected LCS with an independent fair string: the conditional expectations for inserted bit `0` and inserted bit `1` are equal, and their average is the unconditional expectation. Hence

\[
\mathbf E\alpha=F_L-F_0.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Similarly

\[
\mathbf E\beta=F_D-F_0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Put `r=p_{11}`. Since `alpha,beta` are binary,

\[
q_{m,n}=\mathbf E|\alpha-\beta|
       =\mathbf E\alpha+\mathbf E\beta-2r.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Rearranging (2.1) gives

\[
q_{m,n}=4F-2-2F_0-F_L-F_D.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Substituting (3.5)--(3.8) into (3.7) yields

\[
r=1+F_L+F_D-2F,
\]

which is (3.4). Then

\[
p_{10}=\mathbf E\alpha-r,
\qquad
p_{01}=\mathbf E\beta-r,
\]

which give (3.2)--(3.3), and finally

\[
p_{00}=1-p_{10}-p_{01}-p_{11}=2(1+F_0-F).
\]

QED.

### Corollary P05-R13.2 — two-sided marginal-defect conservation

Define one-letter expected marginal gains

\[
d_x:=F-F_L,\qquad d_y:=F-F_D.
\]

Equation (3.4) becomes

\[
\boxed{p_{11}^{m,n}=1-d_x-d_y.}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{d_x+d_y\le1.}
\tag{3.10}
\]

This is not merely a bound: the slack in the local inequality is exactly the probability that **both** opposite boundary insertions gain one LCS symbol.

Likewise (3.1) says

\[
\boxed{p_{00}^{m,n}=2\bigl(1-(F-F_0)\bigr),}
\tag{3.11}
\]

so the deficit of the two-coordinate expected increment from its pathwise maximum one is exactly half the probability that neither insertion helps.

Equations (3.1)--(3.4) also imply the four local linear inequalities obtained from `p_ij>=0`. These can be imposed directly in any finite DP dual without carrying an auxiliary two-bit distribution.

### What this does and does not prove

This identity does **not** by itself improve the endpoint. In particular it does not prove positive correlation of `alpha` and `beta`; the preceding run already rejected a naive FKG/coordinate-monotonicity argument. The gain is exact state compression: the complete joint boundary law is now encoded by the local expected-LCS stencil `(F,F_L,F_D,F_0)`.

## 4. New theorem: exponential rather than Chebyshev localization of the pivotal Green kernel

The preceding report localized the deterministic Green average with a second-moment bound. The reverse walk started at `(n,n)` has steps

\[
(-1,-1)\text{ with probability }1/2,
\quad(-1,0),(0,-1)\text{ with probability }1/4.
\]

Before killing, its coordinate difference has iid increments

\[
\xi\in\{-1,0,1\},
\qquad
\mathbf P(\xi=0)=1/2,
\quad
\mathbf P(\xi=\pm1)=1/4.
\]

Let `tau_n` be the killing time and `D_t` the difference process. Let

\[
R_{n,w}
:=\frac{1}{W_n}
\sum_{|a-b|>w}G_n(a,b),
\qquad
W_n=\mathbf E\tau_n.
\]

### Theorem P05-R13.3 — exponential Green-strip bound

For every `n>=2` and `w>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
R_{n,w}
\le
\min\left\{1,
\frac{4(n-1)}{n}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{w^2}{2n-2}\right)
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

The one-step moment-generating function is exact:

\[
\mathbf E e^{\theta\xi}
=\frac12+\frac14(e^\theta+e^{-\theta})
=\frac{1+\cosh\theta}{2}
=\cosh^2(\theta/2).
\]

The elementary inequality `log cosh u <= u^2/2` gives

\[
\mathbf E e^{\theta\xi}\le e^{\theta^2/4}.
\]

For the unrestricted partial sum `S_t=xi_1+...+xi_t`, Chernoff optimization at `theta=2w/t` yields

\[
\mathbf P(|S_t|>w)\le2e^{-w^2/t}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Couple the killed difference walk to the same iid increment sequence. On `{t<tau_n}`, `D_t=S_t`. Also pathwise

\[
n\le\tau_n\le2n-1,
\]

so `W_n>=n` and only times `0<=t<=2n-2` can contribute. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{|a-b|>w}G_n(a,b)
&=\mathbf E\sum_{t<\tau_n}{\bf1}_{\{|D_t|>w\}}\\
&\le\sum_{t=1}^{2n-2}\mathbf P(|S_t|>w)\\
&\le2(2n-2)\exp\!\left(-\frac{w^2}{2n-2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Divide by `W_n>=n` to obtain (4.1). QED.

This strictly improves the previous polynomial localization

\[
R_{n,w}\le\frac{(2n-2)(2n-1)}{4nw^2}
\]

whenever a small tail error is required.

### Explicit inversion

For `0<epsilon<1`, it suffices to take

\[
\boxed{
w\ge
\sqrt{2(n-1)\log\!\frac{4(n-1)}{n\epsilon}}
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

to force `R_{n,w}<=epsilon`.

At `n=10^6`, the exponential certificate needs only

## 5. Consequence for finite pivotal certificates

Retain the preceding deterministic small-core bound: inside `|a-b|<=w`, the normalized Green mass with `min(a,b)<h` is at most

\[
\frac{2h+w}{n}.
\]

Combining it with (4.1), define

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon^{\exp}_{n,h,w}
:=
\frac{4(n-1)}n e^{-w^2/(2n-2)}
+\frac{2h+w}{n}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal C_n(h,w)
=\{(a,b):\min(a,b)\ge h,\ |a-b|\le w\}.
\]

At least `1-epsilon_exp` of normalized Green mass lies in this region (with the right side truncated at zero if the displayed error exceeds one).

Because `0<=q_{a,b}<=1`, if a finite/state-compressed argument proves on `C_n(h,w)` that

\[
Q_-\le q_{a,b}\le Q_+,
\]

then the exact Green pivotal average from the preceding report obeys

\[
\boxed{
Q_-(1-\varepsilon^{\exp}_{n,h,w})
\le p_n\le
Q_+ +(1-Q_+)\varepsilon^{\exp}_{n,h,w}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is a direct finite-certificate transfer: the uncontrolled part of the rectangle costs only its explicit Green mass, rather than forcing a global all-aspect-ratio bound on `q`.

A particularly useful vanishing-error choice is

\[
h_n=\lceil\log n\rceil,
\qquad
w_n=\left\lceil\sqrt{2(n-1)\log n}\right\rceil.
\]

Then

\[
\varepsilon^{\exp}_{n,h_n,w_n}
=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right),
\tag{5.3}
\]

so asymptotically all pivotal mass is confined to a band of width only `O(sqrt(n log n))` around the diagonal, with core depth only `O(log n)`.

The previous Chebyshev proof required a width `w_n/sqrt(n)->infinity` and, for polynomially small tail errors, could force widths as large as order `n`. The exponential estimate makes the intended boundary-profile transfer substantially smaller.
