## 2. Inherited phase-randomized renewal setup

Let

\[
G_1,G_2,\ldots\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\operatorname{Geom}(1/2),
\qquad
\Pr(G_i=k)=2^{-k},\quad k\ge1,
\]

and define the `r`-run endpoint

\[
T_r=\sum_{i=1}^rG_i.
\]

Let `S_r` be an independent copy. With a fair random initial phase, the alternating-run string stopped at `T_r` is exactly an iid fair binary string stopped at its `r`-th run endpoint. Put

\[
\bar\rho_r:=\frac{\mathbf E\,LCS(X_1^{T_r},Y_1^{S_r})}{2r}.
\]

The previous P05 phase-randomization theorem established

\[
\bar\rho_{2m}\uparrow\gamma_2
\quad\hbox{along the even-run superadditive subsequence,}
\]

and supplied a two-sided finite-to-infinite bridge. Its endpoint-comparison component used the deterministic prefix-table inequality

\[
\left|LCS(X_1^m,Y_1^n)-LCS(X_1^{m'},Y_1^{n'})\right|
\le \max\{|m-m'|,|n-n'|\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For completeness, (2.1) follows directly from monotone matchings. If both coordinates are extended, any optimal matching cannot simultaneously use an old-X/new-Y cross match and a new-X/old-Y cross match because those edges cross. Hence all matches lost when restricting to the smaller rectangle are charged to only one added side, plus new/new matches already charged to that same side. In the mixed-coordinate case, whichever rectangle has the larger LCS can lose at most the number of positions present only on its enlarged coordinate. This gives the `l_infinity` endpoint bound.

Applying (2.1) at the random and deterministic endpoints gives the exact pathwise estimate

\[
\left|
LCS(X_1^{T_r},Y_1^{S_r})-LCS(X_1^{2r},Y_1^{2r})
\right|
\le
\max\{|T_r-2r|,|S_r-2r|\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The preceding report bounded the expectation on the right by Cauchy--Schwarz. The present run removes that unnecessary relaxation.

## 3. New exact bridge term

Define

\[
A_r:=|T_r-2r|,
\qquad B_r:=|S_r-2r|,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\eta_r:=\frac1{2r}\mathbf E\max(A_r,B_r).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then (2.2) immediately yields the sharper exact comparison

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\bar\rho_r-
\frac{\mathbf E LCS(X_1^{2r},Y_1^{2r})}{2r}
\right|
\le\eta_r.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is not merely a renamed error term: `eta_r` is strictly smaller than the previous explicit upper bound at every nondegenerate `r` and is exactly computable from a one-dimensional negative-binomial law.

### Theorem 3.1 — exact one-dimensional series

Let

\[
F_r(j):=\Pr(A_r\le j).
\]

Since

\[
\Pr(T_r=t)=\binom{t-1}{r-1}2^{-t},\qquad t\ge r,
\tag{3.3}
\]

we have the finite expression

\[
F_r(j)=
\sum_{t=\max\{r,2r-j\}}^{2r+j}
\binom{t-1}{r-1}2^{-t}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

For two independent copies,

\[
\Pr(\max(A_r,B_r)<k)=F_r(k-1)^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\eta_r=
\frac1{2r}
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\left[1-F_r(k-1)^2\right].
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the exact endpoint error is a scalar negative-binomial series; no LCS dynamic program appears in its evaluation.

## 4. Exact rational finite certificate for `eta_r`

Equation (3.5) has an elementary rigorous tail bound. Fix `K>=r` and set

\[
S_{r,K}:=\sum_{k=1}^{K}[1-F_r(k-1)^2].
\]

By the union bound,

\[
\sum_{k>K}\Pr(\max(A_r,B_r)\ge k)
\le
2\sum_{k>K}\Pr(A_r\ge k)
=2\mathbf E(A_r-K)_+.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `K>=r`, the lower endpoint cannot contribute to `(A_r-K)_+`, because `T_r>=r`. Hence only the upper negative-binomial tail remains. Put

\[
t_0=2r+K+1,
\qquad
p_t=\Pr(T_r=t).
\]

The exact pmf ratio is

\[
\frac{p_{t+1}}{p_t}
=\frac{t}{2(t-r+1)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For `r>1` this ratio is decreasing in `t`. Consequently, with

\[
q_0:=\frac{t_0}{2(t_0-r+1)}<1,
\]

we have `p_{t_0+j}<=p_{t_0}q_0^j`, and therefore

\[
\mathbf E(A_r-K)_+
=\sum_{j\ge0}(j+1)p_{t_0+j}
\le
\frac{p_{t_0}}{(1-q_0)^2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (3.5), (4.1), and (4.3) gives the fully rational interval

\[
\boxed{
\frac{S_{r,K}}{2r}
\le\eta_r\le
\frac1{2r}\left(
S_{r,K}+\frac{2p_{t_0}}{(1-q_0)^2}
\right),
\qquad K\ge r\ge2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Every quantity in (4.4) is rational. The companion checker evaluates it using Python `Fraction`, not floating Monte Carlo.

For `K=r`, the exact certificate gives

\[
\eta_{100}<0.0797011737,
\tag{4.5}
\]

with omitted-tail budget below `7.812e-11`, and

\[
\eta_{1000}<0.0252285656,
\tag{4.6}
\]

with omitted-tail budget below `1.007e-78`.

## 5. Strict improvement over the inherited Cauchy bridge

The preceding bridge used

\[
\mu_r:=\mathbf E A_r
=\frac{r\binom{2r}{r}}{2^{2r-1}}
\]

and

\[
\beta_r:=
\frac{\mu_r+\sqrt{r-\mu_r^2/2}}{2r}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The origin of (5.1) is now transparent. Since `A_r,B_r` are iid,

\[
\mathbf E\max(A_r,B_r)
=\mu_r+\frac12\mathbf E|A_r-B_r|.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Also

\[
\mathbf E A_r^2
=\mathbf E(T_r-2r)^2
=2r,
\]

so

\[
\mathbf E(A_r-B_r)^2
=2(2r-\mu_r^2).
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives exactly the old estimate

\[
\frac1{2r}\mathbf E\max(A_r,B_r)\le\beta_r.
\]

But the inequality is strict: `|A_r-B_r|` is not almost surely constant. Hence

\[
\boxed{\eta_r<\beta_r\quad\text{for every }r\ge1.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus every earlier P05 phase-randomized finite-to-infinite certificate can be sharpened simply by replacing `beta_r` by `eta_r`; no assumption or policy changes.

Numerically, using the exact rational interval for `eta_r`,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}

1000&0.0252285655009&0.0273723652602&>0.0021437997592

The endpoint-comparison component alone is therefore already about `7.9%` tighter at these block sizes.

## 6. Sharp asymptotic constant

The exact series also identifies the correct first-order endpoint fluctuation constant.

Because `T_r` is the sum of `r` iid geometric variables of mean `2` and variance `2`,

\[
\frac{T_r-2r}{\sqrt{2r}}\Rightarrow Z\sim N(0,1).
\]

For two independent copies, the pair converges jointly to `(Z_1,Z_2)`. The family

\[
\max\left(
\frac{|T_r-2r|}{\sqrt{2r}},
\frac{|S_r-2r|}{\sqrt{2r}}
\right)
\]

is uniformly integrable because its square is bounded by the sum of the two squared normalized coordinates, whose expectations equal `2`. Hence expectations converge. A direct half-normal integral gives

\[
\mathbf E\max(|Z_1|,|Z_2|)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt r\,\eta_r\longrightarrow\sqrt{\frac2\pi}
=0.797884560802865\ldots .
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

By Stirling's formula, the old `beta_r` satisfies

\[
\sqrt r\,\beta_r\longrightarrow
\kappa:=
\frac1{\sqrt\pi}
+\frac12\sqrt{1-\frac2\pi}
=0.865594721042300\ldots .
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the sharp endpoint-fluctuation coefficient is smaller by the factor

\[
\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}}{\kappa}
=0.921776140041726\ldots,
\]

i.e. an asymptotic reduction of

\[
\boxed{7.8223859958\%}
\]

in this component of the renewal bridge.
