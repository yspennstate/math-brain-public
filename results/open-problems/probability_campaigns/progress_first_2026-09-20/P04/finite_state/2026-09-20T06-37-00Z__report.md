## 2. The diagonal finite-state factor class

Let `(W^(h))_(h in Z^d)` be independent copies of Bradley's 1994 binary nonstationary field. At physical site `k`, define

`V_k(j)=W_j^(k-j),  j in Z^d`.                                          (2.1)

At each fixed `k`, the coordinates of `V_k` use distinct independent layers, so `V_k` is an iid fair Rademacher vector.

For an arbitrary measurable finite-valued map

`f:{-1,+1}^{Z^d}->A`,                                                    (2.2)

define

`Y_k=f(V_k)`.                                                            (2.3)

Earlier progress-first work proved, exactly and without locality assumptions, that every such `Y` is strictly stationary and satisfies the full sigma-field independence (1.1). ## 3. Recalled parity-tree representation of one Bradley layer

Bradley's construction uses a norm-monotone bijection

`phi:N->Z^d`, with `||phi(n)||_infinity<n`,

and sup-norm spheres

`S_n={u:||u||_infinity=n}`.

The exact identity is

`W_(phi(n))=product_(u in S_n) W_u`.                                    (3.1)

Define `n(v)=phi^{-1}(v)` and the children of `v` by

`C(v)=S_(n(v))`.                                                        (3.2)

Every nonroot vertex `u` has the unique parent `phi(||u||)`, whose norm is strictly smaller. Thus this is a rooted tree on `Z^d`. In additive flip notation `e_v in F_2`, the homogeneous constraint is

`e_v = XOR_(u in C(v)) e_u`.                                            (3.3)

As proved in the immediately preceding report, Bradley's layer law is Haar on the compact sign group `K` satisfying these parity equations. Multiplication by any fixed `delta in K` therefore preserves the layer law.

The thin perturbations needed below are:

- for the root, one infinite descendant ray;
- for a nonroot target `v`, two infinite rays, one starting at `v` and one at any sibling of `v`.

Putting flip value `1` exactly on the ray(s) satisfies (3.3): every selected ray vertex has one selected child, while the common unselected parent of the two nonroot rays has exactly two selected children.

## 4. The pivotal-mask field

Fix one source layer `h`. At site `h+j`, the layer `W^(h)` occurs in exactly coordinate `j` of `V_(h+j)`. Define

`P_j^(h) = 1`

iff, with all other diagonal coordinates fixed, changing coordinate `j` from `-1` to `+1` changes the value of `f`.

This event depends only on layers other than `h`; in particular the entire mask

`P^(h)=(P_j^(h))_(j in Z^d)`                                             (4.1)

is independent of `W^(h)`.

Let

`q_j=P(P_j^(h)=1)`.                                                      (4.2)

The law is independent of the chosen layer label `h`.

### Lemma 4.1 — `q_j -> 0` at spatial infinity for every finite-valued measurable `f`

For every `epsilon>0`, there is a finite set `F subset Z^d` and an `F`-coordinate measurable function `g` with

`P_nu(f != g)<epsilon`,                                                  (4.3)

where `nu` is iid Rademacher product measure. If `j notin F`, flipping coordinate `j` leaves `g` unchanged. Hence

`q_j <= P(f(omega)!=g(omega)) + P(f(omega^j)!=g(omega^j)) < 2epsilon`.   (4.4)

The flip preserves product measure, so the two probabilities are equal. Since `F` is finite,

`lim_(R->infinity) sup_(||j||_infinity>=R) q_j = 0`.                    (4.5)

No summability of `(q_j)` is asserted or needed.

### Lemma 4.2 — the pivotal mask inherits exact Bradley halfspace one-dependence

For every coordinate `i` and integer `m`,

`σ(P_j^(h):j_i<m) independent of σ(P_j^(h):j_i>m+2)`.                  (4.6)

#### Proof

Fix another layer `g != h`. The background variable from layer `g` used at pivotal-mask site `j` is

`W_(h+j-g)^(g)`.                                                        (4.7)

If `j_i<m`, its layer-`g` coordinate satisfies

`(h+j-g)_i < h_i+m-g_i`.                                                (4.8)

If `j_i>m+2`, then

`(h+j-g)_i > h_i+m+2-g_i`.                                              (4.9)

Bradley's binary source layer has exact independence between those two coordinate halfspaces. This holds separately in every `g != h`, and the layers are mutually independent. Taking joins over all `g != h`, then passing to the smaller sigma-fields generated by the pivotal events, proves (4.6). QED.

This lemma is the extra structure that the preceding `ell^p` proof did not use.

## 5. Large independent child menus inside every parity-tree sphere

Let `v` be a parity-tree vertex and put

`n=n(v)=phi^{-1}(v)`, so its children are `S_n`.

Because `d>=2`, define a deterministic menu of children on the positive first-coordinate face by

`M(v) = {(n,4r,0,...,0): r in Z, |4r|<=n}`.                             (5.1)

Every point lies in `S_n`, and

`|M(v)| >= c n`                                                         (5.2)

for an absolute `c>0` once `n` is large.

The pivotal variables on this menu are **mutually independent**. Indeed, order them by their second coordinate. Consecutive second coordinates differ by at least `4`. By (4.6), the sigma-field generated by all earlier menu points is independent of the next point, and induction gives mutual independence.

There is also exact independence from previously inspected menus once the construction has reached large radius. If the previous menu had first coordinate `n_old`, then the chosen child has norm `n_old`, and norm-monotonicity of `phi` gives

`n_new = phi^{-1}(chosen child) >= (2n_old-1)^d+1`.                      (5.3)

For large `n_old`, `n_new>n_old+3`. Thus the entire next menu, lying on first-coordinate face `n_new`, is Bradley-separated from the sigma-field generated by all prior menus, whose first coordinates are at most `n_old`.

This yields an exact sequential experiment: at each sufficiently deep tree vertex, expose a linearly growing collection of **fresh independent pivotal bits**, independent of everything exposed at previous steps.

## 6. An eventually nonpivotal descendant ray exists with probability arbitrarily close to one

### Lemma 6.1 — adaptive open-ray lemma

Fix any parity-tree descendant subtree. For every `eta>0`, after discarding a sufficiently long deterministic finite prefix, one can construct measurably from the pivotal mask an infinite descendant ray such that

`P( every vertex of the remaining infinite ray is nonpivotal ) > 1-eta`. (6.1)

#### Construction and proof

Start at a sufficiently deep vertex `v_0`. At `v_t`, expose the menu `M(v_t)`. If at least one menu child is nonpivotal, choose the first such child in the fixed second-coordinate order as `v_(t+1)`. If all menu children are pivotal, declare a failure at this step and choose the first child anyway so the ray remains defined.

By Lemma 4.2 and Section 5, conditional on everything exposed previously, the pivotal variables in `M(v_t)` are mutually independent and have their unconditional one-site probabilities `q_u`.

Let

`eps(n)=sup_(||u||>=n) q_u`.

By (4.5), `eps(n)->0`. Once the start is deep enough, `eps(n_t)<=1/2` at every step. Hence the conditional probability that **every** child in the menu is pivotal is at most

`eps(n_t)^(|M(v_t)|) <= 2^(-c n_t)`.                                   (6.2)

By (5.3), the sequence `n_t` grows at least by repeated `d`-th powers. Therefore

`sum_t 2^(-c n_t)`                                                      (6.3)

can be made arbitrarily small by taking the initial deterministic prefix sufficiently long. A union bound over the sequential failure events gives (6.1).

On the no-failure event, every adaptively selected ray vertex after the discarded prefix is nonpivotal by construction. QED.

The proof uses no FKG, no domination theorem, no iid assumption on the full mask, and no summability of the influence profile. It uses only:

1. `q_j->0` from finite-valued measurability;
2. exact Bradley separation of the pivotal mask;
3. the enormous child spheres in Bradley's parity tree.

## 7. Random parity perturbations remain same-law

The adaptive ray in Lemma 6.1 is a random function of the pivotal mask, hence of **all layers other than `h`**. This is precisely what is needed.

Conditional on those other layers, the chosen one-ray/two-ray sign perturbation `delta` is a deterministic element of Bradley's parity group `K`. Since `W^(h)` is independent Haar on `K`,

`W^(h) -> delta * W^(h)`                                                (7.1)

preserves its conditional law given all other layers. Therefore replacing layer `h` by the randomly selected `delta*W^(h)` leaves the **joint law of all source layers unchanged** and produces a same-law output field `Y'`.

This conditional-Haar point is important: the perturbation may adapt to where `f` is nonpivotal without invalidating the self-coupling.

## 8. Main theorem — no finite-valued sitewise compression of Bradley's stationarization works

### Theorem 8.1

Let `A` be any finite alphabet and `f:{-1,+1}^{Z^d}->A` any measurable nonconstant function. Let

`Y_k=f((W_j^(k-j))_(j in Z^d))`                                        (8.1)

be the sitewise finite-state factor of Bradley's countable-layer diagonal stationarization.

Then `Y` is strictly stationary and lattice-halfspace one-dependent, but it is **not** measurable with respect to its own full spatial tail.

Thus replacing Bradley's nonatomic injective site encoder by **any** nonconstant finite-valued measurable statistic can never answer Question 10.

#### Proof

Nonconstancy gives a coordinate `j_0` with `q_(j_0)>0`. Put `h=-j_0`, so physical site `0=h+j_0` uses layer `h` in diagonal coordinate `j_0`.

If `j_0` is the parity-tree root, use one descendant branch. If not, choose a sibling `s` of `j_0` and use the standard two-branch parity perturbation: one branch descends from `j_0`, the other from `s`.

In each required descendant subtree, first choose a deterministic finite path long enough to reach the regime of Lemma 6.1. These finite prefixes will later be absorbed into one finite physical box. After that prefix, construct the adaptive ray from the pivotal mask.

Choose the starting depth so that each required adaptive ray fails with probability `<q_(j_0)/4` (for the root case one may use `<q_(j_0)/2`). Let `S` be the event that all required ray constructions succeed forever. Without any independence between the target pivotal event and the ray-success events,

`P( P_(j_0)^(h)=1 and S )`

`>= q_(j_0) - P(S^c) > q_(j_0)/2 > 0`.                                (8.2)

Let `delta` be the resulting random one-ray/two-ray parity perturbation, including the deterministic finite prefixes and the adaptive tails. By Section 7, perturbing only layer `W^(h)` by `delta` yields a same-law output field `Y'`.

On the positive-probability event in (8.2):

- `delta_(j_0)=-1` and `j_0` is pivotal, so `Y_0 != Y'_0`;
- every altered parity-tree coordinate on the adaptive infinite ray tails is nonpivotal, so **no output site corresponding to those infinitely many tail flips changes**;
- output changes can therefore occur only at the finitely many physical sites corresponding to the deterministic ray prefixes.

Hence

`0 < |{k:Y_k!=Y'_k}| < infinity`                                       (8.3)

with positive probability.

But `(Y,Y')` is a self-coupling of the same output law. If `Y` were own-tail deterministic, two same-law realizations agreeing outside a finite set must agree everywhere: choose a finite box containing their disagreement set and apply the common tail decoder for each coordinate to the identical exterior. Therefore every self-coupling of a tail-deterministic field satisfies

`P(0<|{k:Y_k!=Y'_k}|<infinity)=0`,                                     (8.4)

contradicting (8.3).

Thus `Y` cannot be own-tail deterministic. QED.

## 9. Scope and significance

This theorem strictly subsumes the preceding diagonal-factor obstructions:

- finite-coordinate/cylinder encoders;
- summable total influence;
- `ell^p` influence for any `p>1`;
- the planned regular weighted-threshold `ell^2\ell^1` construction;
- algebraic or nonlinear finite statistics of the diagonal vector, regardless of continuity, monotonicity, quasigroup structure, coding radius, or influence decay.

The only assumptions on the site encoder are **measurability, finite output alphabet, and nonconstancy**.

The theorem therefore closes the most direct interpretation of Bradley's open question:

> One cannot obtain the desired finite-state example merely by taking Bradley's exact countable-layer stationary construction and compressing each countable site vector through any finite-valued measurable map.

Bradley's nonatomic site encoding is not replaceable by a finite statistic even in a wildly nonlocal, infinite-influence way.

## 10. Why this is still not the full Bradley impossibility theorem

An arbitrary finite-state stationary field satisfying (1.1) need not admit Bradley's independent-layer diagonal representation (2.1). The present theorem uses two highly source-specific facts:

1. one removable source layer is independent Haar on Bradley's parity tree;
2. the output at site `h+j` sees that layer in exactly one input coordinate, so nonpivotality hides a parity-tree sign flip perfectly.

A fundamentally different finite-state construction could distribute its tail information without any such independent-layer decomposition. Therefore the source metric remains

`E_finite_tail = 0`.                                                     (10.1)

## 11. New conceptual obstruction for any positive construction

The result sharpens the architecture requirement considerably.

Bradley's original real-valued solution succeeds because every site retains every translated layer bit. A finite statistic loses enough information that, far down the parity tree, the pivotal mask becomes sparse (`q_j->0`). Exact halfspace independence then supplies huge menus of fresh independent nonpivotal descendants, and those descendants support a same-law hidden perturbation invisible to the finite output tail.

Thus a positive finite-state construction cannot merely imitate Bradley's independent translated layers with a more clever local compressor. It must **entangle stationarization and reconstruction at the field level**, so that there is no independent source component admitting an infinite same-law perturbation whose remote effect can be hidden by sparse pivotality.

This points away from sitewise compression and toward genuinely new stationary global architectures.
