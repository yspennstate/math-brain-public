## 2. Main theorem: exact independence forces orthogonal latent-gate information

The binary-gate lemma in the preceding run is the one-dimensional shadow of a more general Hilbert-space identity.

### Theorem 2.1 — latent-gate orthogonality

Let `G`, `L`, and `R` be standard Borel random elements. Assume

\[
L\perp\!\!\!\perp R\mid G
\tag{2.1}
\]

and also

\[
L\perp\!\!\!\perp R.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For square-integrable functions `f(L)` and `g(R)`, define the centered gate responses

\[
T_L f:=\mathbb E[f(L)\mid G]-\mathbb E f(L),
\qquad
T_R g:=\mathbb E[g(R)\mid G]-\mathbb E g(R).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal H_L:=\overline{\{T_L f:f\in L^2(L)\}},\qquad
\mathcal H_R:=\overline{\{T_R g:g\in L^2(R)\}}
\subset L^2_0(G).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal H_L\perp \mathcal H_R.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Equivalently, every gate statistic visible in the conditional law of the left side is orthogonal in `L^2(G)` to every gate statistic visible in the conditional law of the right side.

#### Proof

By conditional independence,

\[
\mathbb E[f(L)g(R)\mid G]
=\mathbb E[f(L)\mid G]\,\mathbb E[g(R)\mid G].
\]

Taking expectations gives

\[
\mathbb E[f(L)g(R)]
=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbb E(f(L)\mid G)\mathbb E(g(R)\mid G)\right].
\tag{2.6}
\]

Subtracting `Ef Eg` from both sides yields the exact covariance identity

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(f(L),g(R))
=
\langle T_Lf,T_Rg\rangle_{L^2(G)}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Unconditional independence (2.2) makes the left side zero for every square-integrable `f,g`, hence every generator of `H_L` is orthogonal to every generator of `H_R`. Closure gives (2.5). QED.

### Interpretation

A latent separator gate is allowed to affect both halfspaces without making them dependent only by placing the two families of conditional-law contrasts in **orthogonal components** of the gate. A truly common contrast cannot be visible from both sides.

This is stronger and more useful than checking pairwise covariance of one chosen statistic: (2.5) simultaneously controls *all* bounded events and all `L^2` observables on both sides.

## 3. Finite-state gate rank bound and the exact binary dichotomy

Suppose now that `G` has finite support of size `q` with every support point having positive probability. Then

\[
\dim L^2_0(G)=q-1.
\]

Define the **left gate rank** and **right gate rank**

\[
r_L:=\dim\mathcal H_L,\qquad r_R:=\dim\mathcal H_R.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Theorem 2.1 immediately gives

\[
\boxed{r_L+r_R\le q-1.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is an exact finite-alphabet information budget for any conditionally decoupled separator gate.

### Corollary 3.1 — binary gate

If `q=2`, then `L^2_0(G)` is one-dimensional, so two nonzero subspaces cannot be orthogonal. Therefore

\[
\boxed{L\perp G\quad\text{or}\quad R\perp G.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This recovers the previous binary latent dichotomy but now identifies the correct general invariant: not the number of gate states alone, but the dimensions of the gate-contrast spaces actually exposed to the two sides.

### Corollary 3.2 — one side reconstructs the gate

If `G` is measurable from `L`, then

\[
\mathcal H_L=L^2_0(G).
\]

Indeed every centered square-integrable function of `G` can be written as a centered function of `L`. By (2.5), `H_R={0}`, hence

\[
\mathbb E[g(R)\mid G]=\mathbb Eg(R)
\]

for every bounded `g`, i.e.

\[
\boxed{R\perp G.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus a gate recoverable from one separated halfspace must be completely invisible to the other halfspace in a conditionally independent latent architecture. In particular a nondegenerate gate cannot be recoverable from both sides.

### Sharpness for `q>=3`

The binary conclusion does **not** extend verbatim to larger gates. Take `G` uniform on `{0,1,2}` and let

\[
u=(-1,0,1),\qquad v=(1,-2,1),
\]

so `Eu(G)=Ev(G)=0` and `E[u(G)v(G)]=0`. For sufficiently small `epsilon>0`, conditionally on `G=g`, let `L,R` be independent Bernoulli variables with

\[
\Pr(L=1\mid G=g)=\tfrac12+\epsilon u_g,
\qquad
\Pr(R=1\mid G=g)=\tfrac12+\epsilon v_g.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then both side laws genuinely depend on `G`, but

\[
\Pr(L=1,R=1)=\mathbb E[(\tfrac12+\epsilon u_G)(\tfrac12+\epsilon v_G)]=\tfrac14,
\]

and the Bernoulli marginals are each `1/2`, so `L` and `R` are nevertheless independent. Here `r_L=r_R=1` and equality holds in (3.2): `1+1=q-1`.

Therefore the correct positive design principle for a multi-state hidden gate is **orthogonal contrast splitting**, not the false rule that one side must always be gate-blind.

## 4. Ordered gates: a shared monotone depth is impossible on both sides

The finite-mean/private-gate failure from the preceding report suggested replacing a local depth by a shared heavy-tailed separator depth `N`. The next theorem shows that the obvious version of this repair fails.

### Lemma 4.1 — strict monotone covariance

Let `N` be a nondegenerate random variable on a totally ordered standard Borel space. Let `a(N),b(N)` be bounded, nonconstant, nondecreasing functions modulo the law of `N`. Then

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(a(N),b(N))>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

If one is nondecreasing and the other nonincreasing, both nonconstant, the covariance is strictly negative.

#### Proof

Let `N'` be an independent copy. The identity

\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(a(N),b(N))
=
\mathbb E[(a(N)-a(N'))(b(N)-b(N'))]
\tag{4.2}
\]

is elementary. For two nondecreasing functions the integrand is nonnegative. Because each function is nonconstant on the support of `N`, there are positive-probability lower and upper portions of the support across which both functions strictly increase; on their product the integrand is strictly positive. Hence the expectation is positive. Replacing `b` by `-b` gives the opposite-monotonicity case. QED.

### Theorem 4.2 — no common monotone latent depth across an independent Bradley cut

Assume `L \perp R | N` and `L \perp R`, where `N` is a nondegenerate ordered gate. Suppose there are bounded side observables `f(L),g(R)` such that

\[
a(N):=\mathbb E[f(L)\mid N],
\qquad
b(N):=\mathbb E[g(R)\mid N]
\]

are both nonconstant monotone functions of `N` (in either common or opposite monotonic directions). Then the assumptions are impossible.

#### Proof

Theorem 2.1 / identity (2.7) requires

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(a(N),b(N))=0.
\]

Lemma 4.1 says this covariance is strictly positive or strictly negative. Contradiction. QED.

### Heavy-tail threshold specialization

Take the previous run's depth law

\[
\Pr(N\ge n)=1/n.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Suppose a separator-bundle construction exposes, on the left, any event `A` with gate-dependent probability

\[
\Pr(A\mid N)=p(N)
\]

where `p` is nonconstant nondecreasing in the depth, and on the right it exposes an event `B` with nonconstant monotone conditional probability `q(N)`. Then exact Bradley independence of the two sides cannot hold if revealing `N` otherwise decouples their source randomness.

In the most literal depth architecture, a visible activation indicator has

\[
\Pr(A_n\mid N)=\mathbf 1_{\{N\ge n\}}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Two opposite sides cannot both contain nontrivial visible threshold probes of the same `N`: the threshold functions are positively correlated.

This directly eliminates the naive repair

> one heavy-tailed separator depth chooses how many masks/checks are active on both sides, and conditional on that depth the sides use independent source randomness.

The depth may still control both sides only if its effect on at least one side is **not monotone-detectable**; the natural way is to one-time-pad or otherwise measure-preservingly scramble the *entire activation law*, not merely the payload bits.

## 5. Mirror-symmetric latent hierarchies must be exactly gate-blind

There is an even cleaner obstruction for symmetric separator designs.

### Theorem 5.1 — mirror-symmetric conditional law implies total gate invisibility

Assume (2.1)--(2.2). Suppose `L` and `R` have isomorphic state spaces and there is a fixed measurable isomorphism `J` such that, conditional on `G`,

\[
\mathcal L(J(L)\mid G)=\mathcal L(R\mid G)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{L\perp G\quad\text{and}\quad R\perp G.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

After identifying the two state spaces using `J`, the two conditional-law response spaces in (2.4) are equal:

\[
\mathcal H_L=\mathcal H_R.
\]

Theorem 2.1 also gives `H_L \perp H_R`. A Hilbert subspace orthogonal to itself is `{0}`. Thus every bounded conditional expectation from either side is constant in `G`, which is exactly independence from `G`. QED.

### Consequence for the proposed separator-bundle repair

A reflection-symmetric hierarchy node cannot reveal its depth, activation density, correlation pattern, or any other conditional-law statistic to either separated side if the two sides become independent once that node is exposed. It may still act on both sides **only through a perfectly law-invariant masking action**.

This is a much stronger design requirement than matching one-site marginals. The whole halfspace law, including all within-side correlations generated by the bundle, must be gate-independent.

## 6. What this does and does not rule out

### Newly ruled out

The following natural continuation of the previous run is now excluded.

1. Put a scalar heavy-tailed depth `N` on the omitted separator layer.
2. Let `N` have infinite reverse fanout by controlling infinitely many outputs on both sides.
3. Conditional on `N`, generate the left and right bundles from independent randomness.
4. Let larger `N` monotonically activate more visible checks/masks on both sides.

Steps 3--4 are incompatible with exact Bradley halfspace independence by Theorem 4.2. If the architecture is mirror-symmetric, Theorem 5.1 says even nonmonotone gate dependence is forbidden unless the complete side law is gate-invariant.

Thus **infinite fanout alone does not repair the local-gate defect**. A shared gate has to be secret-shared at the level of its entire conditional law.

### Surviving positive architectures

The full Bradley problem is not solved. Three qualitatively different possibilities survive.

**A. Perfectly gate-blind activation.** A shared infinite-fanout gate may act through measure-preserving permutations/one-time pads so that each separated halfspace has exactly the same law for every gate value. The two sides can still carry joint, pad-canceling information about the gate.

**B. Orthogonal contrast splitting.** A gate with at least three states, or a product gate with several independent components, may expose mutually orthogonal conditional-law contrasts to opposite sides as in (3.5). This is compatible with exact cut independence. A successful construction would have to arrange these contrast splittings simultaneously for every translated cut and every coordinate direction.

**C. No conditional-decoupling latent gate.** A future construction may be fundamentally non-latent: even after revealing the proposed hierarchy variables, the opposite sides remain conditionally dependent in a way whose mixture cancels exactly. Theorem 2.1 deliberately does not cover that more exotic class.

### Relation to Bradley's source

Nothing here weakens the known real-valued construction. Bradley's countable independent layers obtain cut blindness by a different mechanism: each layer separately already satisfies the halfspace independence, and the real coordinate packs all translated layers losslessly. The present theorem explains why a finite-state replacement based on a **single shared scalar hierarchy depth** cannot mimic that mechanism naively.

## 8. Next attack

The cleanest surviving positive route is now narrower and more concrete:

1. Replace scalar shared depths by a finite or countable collection of **orthogonally split gate components**.
2. Make each component infinite-fanout so that every pivotal gate refresh produces an infinite output avalanche.
3. Use measure-preserving masks so that for each Bradley cut the *entire* opposite-side conditional-law contrast spaces are orthogonal, preferably zero on one side.
4. Arrange pad-canceling relations across perpendicular directions so that every wrong finite filling is rejected by infinitely many remote checks.

The immediate mathematical subproblem for the next run is:

> **two-cut orthogonalization problem.** Can one build a finite-state translation-equivariant crossing gadget whose horizontal left/right gate-response spaces are orthogonal and whose vertical down/up gate-response spaces are orthogonal, while the joint four-side configuration still determines a nontrivial central finite secret and every genuinely pivotal gate has infinite reverse fanout?

A negative theorem should try to show that stationarity plus repeated translated cuts forces these orthogonal subspaces to overlap, which by Theorem 2.1 would force the overlap to vanish. A positive theorem should explicitly produce a finite crossing gadget with four compatible contrast subspaces and then lift it to a stationary infinite-fanout hierarchy.

That is the present proof frontier. The full source question remains open in this track.
