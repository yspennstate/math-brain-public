## 1. Exact source target, continuity, and a source-convention correction

The source target remains Richard C. Bradley, *Basic Properties of Strong Mixing Conditions. A Survey and Some Open Questions*, Probability Surveys 2 (2005), Section 8, Question 10, printed pp. 135–136, arXiv:math/0511078. Question 10 asks, for a given `d>=2`, whether there is a nondegenerate strictly stationary finite-state (even two-state) `Z^d` field having both properties of Bradley's preceding 1994 real-valued example: lattice-halfspace one-dependence and measurability modulo null sets with respect to its own full spatial tail.

The current primary-source check used the page image of Bradley's 1994 Proceedings paper, p. 593, rather than relying on OCR. The literal definitions there are

`G_(i,m)={k in Z^d : k_i <= m}`,

`H_(i,m)={k in Z^d : k_i >= m}`,

and lattice-halfspace one-dependence means

`σ(X_k:k in G_(i,m)) independent of σ(X_k:k in H_(i,m+2))`             (1.1)

for every coordinate `i` and integer `m`.

## 2. Exact sparse-product lemma at spacing 2

The first point is stronger than the spacing-4 formulation used in earlier reports because of the corrected source endpoints.

### Lemma 2.1 — `(2 Z)^d` is an iid skeleton

Let `X` be strictly stationary and satisfy (1.1). Then for every finite set

`F subset (2 Z)^d`,                                                     (2.1)

the family `(X_k)_(k in F)` is mutually independent. By stationarity all members have the same one-site law.

#### Proof

Induct on `|F|`. The claim is trivial for `|F|<=1`.

If `|F|>=2`, choose a coordinate `i` on which the points of `F` do not all agree. Their `i`-coordinates are even integers. Choose two consecutive occupied levels in increasing order, say the lower side has maximum `2a` and the upper side has minimum at least `2a+2`. Set

`F_-={k in F:k_i<=2a}` and `F_+=F\F_-`.

Both sets are nonempty. By (1.1) with `m=2a`,

`σ(X_k:k in F_-)` and `σ(X_k:k in F_+)`

are independent because `F_- subset G_(i,2a)` and `F_+ subset H_(i,2a+2)`. By the induction hypothesis, each subfamily is internally mutually independent. Independence of the two generated sigma-fields then makes the whole family mutually independent. ∎

### Corollary 2.2 — strict positive entropy density

Let the finite state space be `A`, `q=|A|`, and write

`h0=H(X_0)`.

If `X` is nondegenerate then `h0>0`. For boxes `B_n=[-n,n]^d cap Z^d`,

`H(X_(B_n)) >= |B_n cap (2 Z)^d| h0`.                                  (2.2)

Hence

`liminf_(n->infinity) H(X_(B_n))/|B_n| >= 2^(-d) h0 > 0`.              (2.3)

Thus every nondegenerate finite-state field satisfying Bradley's exact halfspace independence has **positive `Z^d` entropy density**, quantitatively at least `2^(-d) H(X_0)`.

No tail assumption was used in this lemma.

## 3. Subvolume deterministic presentations are impossible

The next observation turns (2.3) into a direct obstruction to finite-alphabet cocycle and cellular-automaton constructions.

### Theorem 3.1 — subvolume decoder obstruction

Let `X` be finite-state, strictly stationary, nondegenerate, and satisfy Bradley halfspace independence (1.1). Suppose there are finite sets `C_n` such that

`X_(B_n) is measurable with respect to X_(C_n)`                        (3.1)

and

`|C_n|=o(|B_n|)`.                                                       (3.2)

Then such an `X` cannot exist.

#### Proof

By (3.1),

`H(X_(B_n)) <= H(X_(C_n)) <= |C_n| log q = o(|B_n|)`.                  (3.3)

This contradicts the positive lower bound (2.3). ∎

### Corollary 3.2 — no finite-memory unique-filling cocycle / CA presentation

In particular, no source-valid finite-state candidate can have the following common deterministic local-rule structure: there is a fixed finite interaction range `r` and, for every large box, all symbols in the box are uniquely determined by a fixed-width incoming boundary/collar containing `O(n^(d-1))` sites.

This includes any proposed finite-state oriented cocycle or cellular-automaton spacetime presentation **once one has actually proved** that a box is uniquely fillable from `O(n^(d-1))` boundary data. It also includes corner-permutive / quasigroup-style local recurrences only in the cases where their defining recurrence gives such a surface-size unique-filling set.

The point is not merely that a particular linear or group law fails. Any nonlinear finite alphabet rule with only surface-order degrees of freedom has zero entropy density and therefore cannot even satisfy Bradley's halfspace independence, before tail determinism is considered.

This sharply narrows the positive local-rule program: a successful finite-state construction must retain **volume-order fresh entropy** while nevertheless making every finite erasure recoverable from the full exterior.

## 4. Quantitative remote-decoder rate

The same entropy lower bound gives a useful coding-rate statement that is independent of a specific presentation.

Let `D` be any finite set and suppose a decoder `phi(X_D)` estimates the entire box `X_(B_n)` with block error probability at most `epsilon`. Fano's inequality gives

`H(X_(B_n) | X_D) <= h_2(epsilon) + epsilon |B_n| log q`.               (4.1)

Therefore

`|D| log q >= I(X_(B_n);X_D)`
`              >= |B_n cap (2 Z)^d| h0 - h_2(epsilon) - epsilon |B_n| log q`.  (4.2)

For every fixed

`epsilon < 2^(-d) h0/(2 log q)`,                                      (4.3)

there is `c=c(d,h0,q,epsilon)>0` such that all sufficiently large boxes satisfy

`|D| >= c |B_n|`.                                                       (4.4)

Thus even an **approximate** finite decoder of a large box needs volume-order many observed symbols. Tail determinism can still use an infinite exterior, so (4.4) is not a contradiction by itself. Its role is architectural: no surface-rate exact or high-accuracy reconstruction mechanism can underlie a Bradley finite-state solution.

## 5. Main new inverse-limit theorem

The initial requested attack included inverse-limit/profinite coding. The following theorem rules out the most natural such route, even when the final finite-state observable is merely measurable and depends on infinitely many inverse-limit levels.

### Theorem 5.1 — zero-entropy inverse limits cannot produce a Bradley finite-state field

Let `Z` be a strictly stationary `Z^d` system generated by an increasing sequence of stationary factor systems

`Z^(1), Z^(2), ...`,                                                    (5.1)

in the sense that the sigma-field of the full `Z` configuration is generated by the union of the factor sigma-fields. Assume every finite stage has zero `Z^d` entropy density:

`h(Z^(m))=0 for every m`.                                               (5.2)

Let `X` be **any finite-alphabet stationary measurable factor** of `Z`; no continuity, finite radius, or finite-stage dependence is assumed for the factor map.

Then

`h(X)=0`.                                                               (5.3)

Consequently, a nondegenerate `X` satisfying Bradley halfspace one-dependence cannot be obtained this way.

#### Elementary proof tailored to the present problem

Let `A` be the finite output alphabet, `q=|A|`. Since the stage sigma-fields generate the full source sigma-field, and finite spatial cylinders generate each stage process, for every `epsilon>0` there exist a stage `m`, a finite physical set `F`, and an `A`-valued function of `(Z^(m)_j)_(j in F)`, call it `Y_0`, such that

`P(Y_0 != X_0) < epsilon`.                                              (5.4)

Translate the same rule to obtain a stationary field `Y=(Y_k)`. Since `Y` is a finite-window factor of `Z^(m)`, factor monotonicity of entropy gives

`h(Y)=0`.                                                               (5.5)

For any finite `Lambda`, chain rule and conditioning give

`H(X_Lambda | Y_Lambda)
 <= sum_(k in Lambda) H(X_k | Y_k)
 <= |Lambda| eta_q(epsilon)`,                                          (5.6)

where

`eta_q(epsilon)=h_2(epsilon)+epsilon log(q-1)`                          (5.7)

(with the obvious binary convention when `q=2`). Hence

`H(X_Lambda) <= H(Y_Lambda)+|Lambda| eta_q(epsilon)`.                   (5.8)

Take `Lambda=B_n`, divide by `|B_n|`, and let `n->infinity`. By (5.5),

`h(X) <= eta_q(epsilon)`.                                               (5.9)

Because `epsilon` is arbitrary and `eta_q(epsilon)->0`, (5.3) follows. ∎

### Corollary 5.2 — inverse limits of surface-determined finite stages are excluded

Suppose each stage `Z^(m)` is finite-state and its `n`-box is determined by at most

`C_m n^(d-1)`                                                           (5.10)

boundary/seed symbols. Then `h(Z^(m))=0`, so Theorem 5.1 applies.

Therefore **no measurable finite-state observation of the entire inverse limit can solve Bradley Question 10**, even if the observation is discontinuous and genuinely depends on infinitely many levels.

This rules out a broad profinite / hierarchical strategy:

1. build finite-stage deterministic cocycle, quasigroup, or CA systems with only surface-order degrees of freedom;
2. take a stationary inverse limit over finer and finer stages;
3. use an arbitrarily complicated finite-valued measurable observable of all levels.

The last step cannot restore the positive entropy density required by Bradley halfspace independence. The obstruction is at the dynamical entropy level, not continuity of the observation.

### Quantitative form

More generally, if `Z` is an inverse limit of stages with entropy densities `h_m`, then any finite-state factor `X` satisfies

`h(X) <= sup_m h_m`.                                                     (5.11)

A Bradley candidate therefore requires

`sup_m h_m >= 2^(-d) H(X_0)`.                                          (5.12)

For an unbiased binary candidate this lower bound is `2^(-d)` bits per lattice site. Hence a surviving inverse-limit construction must already carry a positive **volume-order entropy budget at finite stages**; a tower made only of zero-entropy deterministic skeletons cannot work.

## 6. Why this does not accidentally rule out Bradley's known real-valued construction

Bradley's 1994 stationary real-valued field is built from an independent translated copy of the nonstationary binary field for **every** translation index and then packs all corresponding bits into each real coordinate by a sigma-field-invertible nonatomic encoding.

A finite truncation of the translate family is not strictly stationary. Therefore Bradley's construction is not a stationary inverse limit of the zero-entropy finite-stage type excluded by Theorem 5.1. In fact its finite stationary entropy accounting is precisely where the finite-alphabet stationarization problem lives.

This distinction is important: Theorem 5.1 does not contradict the source example and does not prove the full finite-state impossibility theorem.

## 7. Failed positive route and what it teaches

I specifically tested the following new finite-alphabet idea rather than returning to admission/audit work:

- choose a finite quasigroup alphabet;
- impose a translation-covariant, corner-permutive local recurrence so that every finite hole can be uniquely filled from an exterior boundary;
- randomize the remote boundary in a stationary way;
- take either the resulting local system directly or an inverse limit of such systems.

The attractive part is that a quasigroup recurrence can avoid the linear/Frobenius relations that killed finite-group algebraic fields.

The failure is now exact and does **not** depend on linearity. If a box is uniquely determined by a fixed-thickness boundary, its entropy is `O(n^(d-1))`; Bradley halfspace independence demands `Omega(n^d)` entropy by Lemma 2.1. Taking an inverse limit of such zero-entropy stages does not help, even with a merely measurable finite output, by Theorem 5.1.

Thus the next positive construction cannot be a deterministic codimension-one evolution dressed up with a nonlinear alphabet. It must inject volume-order randomness at each finite scale while arranging global finite-erasure reconstruction through genuinely multi-directional constraints.

## 8. Remaining gap and next attack

The full source problem remains open in this track. The surviving possibilities include finite-state stationary systems with positive entropy density whose constraints are not surface-determined, not finite-range Markov, not Haar algebraic, and not zero-entropy inverse limits. Tail reconstruction would have to exploit volume-order multi-directional secret sharing.

The next attack should therefore target the following sharper statement:

> **Can exact Bradley halfspace independence plus finite alphabet force every finite-state tail-deterministic candidate to admit a positive-entropy decomposition into independent spacing-2 skeleton bits whose information cannot be recycled across all scales?**

A concrete route is to combine the exact `(2Z)^d` iid skeleton with tail determinism and derive a lower bound on the number of **independent finite-stage entropy sources** needed to reconstruct successively nested boxes. If those sources can be shown to remain disjoint across scales, their entropy demand would exceed finite per-site capacity and yield a full impossibility theorem. The obstacle is reuse: finite secret-sharing schemes can recycle the same remote symbols across many targets, so the next proof must control multi-target overlap, not merely one-box entropy.
