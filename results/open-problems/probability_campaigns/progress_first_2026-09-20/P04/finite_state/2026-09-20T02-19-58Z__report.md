## 3. First new route: a genuinely nonlinear finite-state cocycle comes very close

The previous reports concentrated on abelian/Haar algebraic shifts. A natural way out is to move to a **nonabelian** finite group, where the observed field is not a subgroup under pointwise multiplication and the Laurent-module obstruction no longer applies.

Let `G` be any nontrivial finite group. Let

`(U_i)_{i in Z}` and `(V_j)_{j in Z}`

be independent iid uniform `G`-valued sequences. Define, on `Z^2`,

`X_{i,j} = U_i V_j`.

This is a genuinely nonlinear/nonabelian separable cocycle when `G` is nonabelian.

### Proposition 3.1 — exact near-solution

The field `X` is:

1. finite-state and nondegenerate;
2. strictly stationary under all `Z^2` translations;
3. measurable with respect to its **own full spatial tail**;
4. but it is **not** Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependent.

#### Proof of 1–2

Finite state and nondegeneracy are immediate. Translation by `(a,b)` simply relabels the two iid sequences to `(U_{i+a})` and `(V_{j+b})`, so the joint law is unchanged.

#### Proof of exact own-tail determinism

Fix `(i,j)` and any finite deleted set `B subset Z^2`. Choose `k` and `ell` so that the three sites

`(i,ell)`, `(k,ell)`, `(k,j)`

all lie outside `B`; this is always possible because `B` is finite. Then, with no commutativity assumption,

`X_{i,ell} X_{k,ell}^{-1} X_{k,j}`

`= (U_i V_ell)(U_k V_ell)^{-1}(U_k V_j)`

`= U_i V_ell V_ell^{-1} U_k^{-1} U_k V_j`

`= U_i V_j`

`= X_{i,j}`.

Thus every coordinate is a deterministic function of three coordinates outside **every** prescribed finite deletion. Hence `X` is tail deterministic in Bradley's full spatial sense.

This is stronger than an asymptotic reconstruction statement: a deleted site is recoverable exactly from a remote rectangle.

#### Exact obstruction to Bradley independence

Fix two distinct column indices `a,b`. For every row `i`,

`X_{i,a}^{-1} X_{i,b}`

`= (U_i V_a)^{-1}(U_i V_b)`

`= V_a^{-1} V_b`.

Therefore the nonconstant random variable

`C_{a,b}:=V_a^{-1}V_b`

is measurable from **any single row**. In particular it is measurable from every nonempty left row-halfspace and every separated right row-halfspace. Since `V_a,V_b` are independent uniform, `C_{a,b}` is uniform on `G` and nonconstant.

Two independent sigma-fields cannot share a nonconstant random variable. Hence the Bradley halfspaces perpendicular to the first coordinate are not independent.

This identifies the exact failure: the cocycle achieves stationarity + finite state + full-tail determinism, but its repeated rectangle identities leave a **transverse cocycle invariant** visible from both opposite halfspaces.

### Why this is useful

This is a source-close nonlinear near-miss. It shows that abandoning abelian algebraic shifts does not make the tail condition difficult; the missing ingredient is to destroy every transverse common invariant while retaining enough repeated structure for exact finite-erasure reconstruction.

## 4. Broad nonlinear obstruction — no finite quasigroup / row-bijective separable cocycle

The failure above is not peculiar to group multiplication.

### Theorem 4.1

Let `B,C,A` be finite sets, let `(U_i)` and `(V_j)` be independent stationary sequences, and define

`X_{i,j}=F(U_i,V_j)`.

Assume that for every possible `u`, the map

`v -> F(u,v)`

is injective. If for some `a != b` the indicator `1{V_a=V_b}` is nonconstant, then **no two separated row-halfspaces can be independent**.

In particular, if `U_i,V_j` are nondegenerate iid variables on the same finite alphabet and `F` is a Latin-square / quasigroup operation, the resulting stationary finite-state array cannot satisfy Bradley's halfspace condition.

#### Proof

For every row `i`, injectivity in the second coordinate gives the exact equivalence

`X_{i,a}=X_{i,b}` iff `V_a=V_b`.

Hence

`E_{a,b}:=1{V_a=V_b}`

is measurable from the pair `(X_{i,a},X_{i,b})` in **every** row. Choose one row index in a left Bradley halfspace and another row index in the separated right halfspace. Then the same nonconstant `E_{a,b}` belongs to both halfspace sigma-fields. They therefore cannot be independent. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — the full finite quasigroup rank-one scheme fails in both directions

If `F:A x A -> A` is a quasigroup operation, then every left or right translation is a bijection. With nondegenerate iid `U,V`, Theorem 4.1 rules out independence across row cuts, and its transposed version rules out independence across column cuts.

Thus the entire obvious nonlinear replacement

`X_{i,j}=U_i * V_j`

is dead, even though group examples inside that class have perfect finite-erasure reconstruction.

### A useful design lesson

Any future finite-state cocycle must avoid a feature that all quasigroup masks have: **a relation on two transverse latent symbols that survives every row mask**. Equality already suffices. Adding row-dependent permutations or nonabelian conjugations does not fix this, because bijections still preserve equality. A successful masking family must destroy all such one-side-identifiable transverse relations, not merely hide the latent labels themselves.

## 5. Second new theorem — Bradley independence implies total directional strong mixing

The previous finite-phase obstruction assumed the phase components were ergodic under their finite-index stabilizer. That assumption is unnecessary.

### Theorem 5.1

Let `X` be any strictly stationary random field (state space arbitrary standard Borel) satisfying Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependence. Then for every nonzero `v in Z^d`, the single transformation `T_v` is strongly mixing:

`P(A intersect T_v^{-n} B) -> P(A)P(B)` as `n -> infinity`

for all field events `A,B`.

Consequently every nonzero lattice shift is ergodic, and the action of **every finite-index subgroup** `H <= Z^d` is ergodic.

#### Proof

First take cylinder events `A,B` depending on finite coordinate sets `S,T`. Choose a coordinate `i` with `v_i != 0`. For sufficiently large `n`, the finite sets `S` and `T+n v` are separated by more than Bradley's width-two gap in the `i`th coordinate. Thus there is an integer `m` such that one support lies in `G_{i,m}` and the other lies in `H_{i,m+2}`. Bradley independence gives

`P(A intersect T_v^{-n}B)=P(A)P(B)`

**exactly** for all sufficiently large `n`.

Finite-coordinate cylinders generate the field sigma-field. Approximate arbitrary events in probability by cylinders and use the usual four-error bound to pass to the limit. Hence `T_v` is strongly mixing.

If `H` has finite index, choose any nonzero `h in H`. An `H`-invariant event is `T_h`-invariant, and mixing of `T_h` makes its probability zero or one. Thus the `H`-action is ergodic. QED.

### Significance

Bradley's 1994 real-valued example can therefore be full-tail deterministic while **every nonzero one-dimensional lattice subaction is strongly mixing**. The obstruction is intrinsically multi-directional, not a hidden failure of ordinary directional mixing.

For P04 this theorem is operationally stronger: it kills every genuine periodic/compact global phase decomposition before tail determinism or finiteness of the alphabet is even used.

## 6. Main inverse-limit consequence — no genuine profinite / 2-adic global phase stationarization

The terminal D0038 handoff proposed a stationary dyadic/2-adic hierarchy. The previous report ruled out a finite detectable phase under an ergodicity assumption on phase components. Theorem 5.1 upgrades that to a much more general no-go.

### Theorem 6.1 — profinite phase is law-invisible

Let `K` be a profinite compact group with finite quotients `K_n`, and let a homomorphism

`rho: Z^d -> K`

define the translation action on a phase `U` by `U -> U+rho(t)`. Suppose `(X,U)` has a jointly stationary law under

`(X,U) -> (T_t X, U+rho(t))`,

and the `X`-marginal satisfies Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependence.

For a finite quotient `q_n:K->K_n`, put `J_n=q_n(U)` and

`H_n = ker(q_n o rho)`,

a finite-index sublattice (after restricting to the image if necessary).

Then the conditional law of `X` given `J_n=a` is the **same** for every phase class `a` of positive probability. Hence `X` is independent of every finite phase `J_n`. If the finite quotients generate the Borel sigma-field of `U`, then `X` is independent of the full profinite phase `U`.

#### Proof

Write

`mu = Law(X)`, `mu_a = Law(X | J_n=a)`.

For every `h in H_n`, translation by `h` leaves the phase class `a` fixed. Joint stationarity therefore makes each `mu_a` `H_n`-invariant.

By Theorem 5.1, `mu` is `H_n`-ergodic. Also

`mu = sum_a P(J_n=a) mu_a`.

For a phase class of positive weight `p_a`, `mu_a << mu` because `mu(E) >= p_a mu_a(E)`. Let `f_a=d mu_a/d mu`. `H_n`-invariance of both measures makes `f_a` `H_n`-invariant `mu`-a.s. Ergodicity forces `f_a` to be constant; because both measures have total mass one, that constant is one. Thus `mu_a=mu`.

So `X` is independent of `J_n` for every `n`. For nested finite quotients generating `sigma(U)`, martingale convergence of conditional expectations gives independence of `X` and `U`. QED.

### Corollary 6.2 — ordinary random dyadic-grid stationarization is not a live route

Take `K=Z_2^d` with Haar phase and the usual translation action. If a proposed finite-state construction uses a single random 2-adic origin/grid phase so that different residues modulo `2^n` induce genuinely different conditional laws of the observed field, then it **cannot** satisfy Bradley halfspace one-dependence.

If instead all finite phase residues induce the same observed law, then the observed field is independent of the entire 2-adic phase, so that phase is distributionally irrelevant and can be removed from the construction.

This removes a major branch of the previous positive program. A successful hierarchy cannot be a standard odometer/random-origin hierarchy with a global compact phase. It must use a **mixing local hierarchy with no nontrivial finite-index phase component**, or an architecture in which the hierarchy is auxiliary but completely law-invisible.

### What improved relative to the previous report

Before this run, the finite-phase obstruction required the phase measures to be ergodic under their stabilizer and treated only a finite orbit explicitly.

After this run:

- component ergodicity is no longer assumed;
- the argument applies to every finite quotient of a profinite phase;
- therefore a whole 2-adic/inverse-limit global phase is ruled out;
- the proof uses only Bradley independence and stationarity, so it is not tied to a particular finite alphabet or cocycle family.

## 7. Failed positive modification: random row permutations do not repair the cocycle

A natural reaction to Proposition 3.1 is to mask each row by an independent random permutation `pi_i` and set

`X_{i,j}=pi_i(V_j)`.

This makes the latent labels themselves unreadable from one row. It still fails immediately:

`X_{i,a}=X_{i,b}` iff `V_a=V_b`

because every `pi_i` is a bijection. Thus the column equality partition is a common random object visible from every row-halfspace.

The same failure survives nonabelian conjugation, left/right group multiplication, or any finite Latin-square mask. This is why the next attempt must abandon **bijective reuse of one global transverse latent sequence**, not merely change the algebra used to mask it.

## 10. Next mathematical attack

Do not repeat the abelian module, finite-phase, profinite-phase, or row/column Latin-square schemes.

The next positive attack should replace the dead 2-adic global phase by a **stationary mixing marker hierarchy**:

1. generate scale markers from a spatially mixing finite-state source rather than a single odometer origin;
2. use local perfect-secret-sharing gadgets so that every relation crossing a coordinate cut is one-time-padded by variables in the width-two separator;
3. force each site to participate in reconstruction gadgets at unbounded scales so every finite erasure is eventually bypassed;
4. test exact halfspace sigma-field independence before attempting full tail reconstruction.

The first exact subproblem is a two-scale gadget: construct a finite-alphabet random constraint system on a large rectangle whose left/right external sigma-fields are exactly independent, whose central symbol is reconstructible from the union of top/bottom plus separator data, and whose translated copies can overlap without creating a persistent equality/ratio invariant. If every overlap necessarily creates such a common invariant, promote that failure to a nonlinear obstruction theorem for mixing constraint networks.

A complementary negative route is to formalize the following broader principle suggested by this run: in any independent-latent hypergraph representation, if one latent variable has an identifiable influence on infinitely many sites on both sides of a Bradley cut, it induces common information and violates independence; if every latent influence is confined to bounded coordinate diameter, tail determinism should fail. Making the word **identifiable** exact without assuming linearity is the next plausible bridge toward a full impossibility theorem.
