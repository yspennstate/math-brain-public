## 2. Positive theorem: the local two-cut problem has a perfect finite solution

The correct local object is the standard additive `m`-of-`m` secret-sharing law, but its relevance here is stronger than the usual cryptographic statement: **every proper collection of directional shares has a law completely independent of the secret**. Hence every latent-gate response space from the preceding run vanishes identically.

### Theorem 2.1 — perfect `2d`-direction crossing gadget

Let `K` be any nontrivial finite abelian group and let `d>=2`. Let `S` be an arbitrary `K`-valued random variable. Independently of `S`, choose

\[
P_1,\ldots,P_{2d-1}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}(K),
\]

and define directional shares

\[
Y_j=P_j,\qquad 1\le j\le 2d-1,
\]

\[
Y_{2d}=S-\sum_{j=1}^{2d-1}P_j.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then:

1. the complete share vector determines the secret exactly,
   \[
   S=\sum_{j=1}^{2d}Y_j;
   \tag{2.2}
   \]
2. for every proper subset `J proper subset {1,...,2d}`, the vector `(Y_j)_{j in J}` is independent of `S`;
3. more strongly, conditional on any value of `S`, every `2d-1` shares are mutually independent uniform `K` variables; hence every smaller proper subcollection is also product-uniform;
4. in particular, after assigning the `2d` shares to the negative/positive sides of the `d` coordinate directions, each opposite pair is independent, and the complete law on either individual side is **exactly secret-blind**.

#### Proof

The reconstruction identity (2.2) is immediate from (2.1).

Fix `s in K`. The first `2d-1` shares are product-uniform by construction. If the omitted share is `Y_{2d}`, there is nothing more to prove. If instead the omitted share is, say, `Y_r=P_r` with `r<2d`, then for fixed `S=s` the map

\[
(P_1,\ldots,P_{2d-1})
\longmapsto
(Y_1,\ldots,\widehat{Y_r},\ldots,Y_{2d})
\]

is a bijection of `K^{2d-1}`: after the displayed shares are known, the missing pad is recovered uniquely from

\[
P_r=s-Y_{2d}-\sum_{j\ne r,\,j<2d}P_j.
\]

Therefore any `2d-1` shares are uniformly distributed on `K^{2d-1}` for each fixed `s`, and this law does not depend on `s`. Marginalizing proves the same statement for every proper subset. QED.

### `d=2`, binary instance

For `K=F_2`, let `A,B,C` be independent fair bits and let `S` be the central secret. Put

\[
L=A,\qquad R=B,\qquad D=C,\qquad U=S\oplus A\oplus B\oplus C.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then

\[
S=L\oplus R\oplus D\oplus U,
\]

while **every proper subcollection of `L,R,D,U` is independent of `S` and is itself a vector of independent fair bits**. In particular

\[
L\perp R,\qquad D\perp U,
\tag{2.4}
\]

and all four one-side latent response spaces relative to the gate `G=S` are zero.

Thus the preceding run's two-cut orthogonalization problem is solved positively at the local finite gadget level, and in the strongest possible way: no nonzero orthogonal contrasts are needed because every proper side law is exactly gate-invariant.

## 3. Infinite repetition of one secret: tail recovery is compatible with perfect cut blindness

The local gadget can be repeated countably many times for **one** secret without changing this conclusion. This matters because it shows that the difficulty is not tail reconstruction by itself; the difficulty is doing it for a positive-density stationary family of secrets with finite per-site state.

Let, for `t=1,2,...`,

\[
(Y_{t,1},\ldots,Y_{t,2d})
\]

be conditionally independent copies of the gadget in Theorem 2.1 sharing the same secret `S` and using fresh pads. Then:

- for any proper set of directions `J`, the whole countable directional bundle
  \[
  (Y_{t,j})_{t\ge1,\,j\in J}
  \]
  is product-uniform and independent of `S`;
- any complete `2d`-tuple at one level `t` recovers `S`;
- if the tuples are stored on pairwise distinct sites, deletion of an arbitrary finite set of storage sites leaves some complete tuple intact, so `S` remains exactly recoverable from the exterior.

Hence **perfect side blindness, infinite reverse fanout, and exact finite-erasure recovery are mutually compatible for a single zero-density secret**.

This eliminates a possible false obstruction: one cannot prove Bradley impossible merely by showing that a secret reconstructed from arbitrarily remote checks must become visible in the marginal law of one separated halfspace. The additive gadget is a counterexample to that local claim.

## 4. Tail rigidity under finite disagreements

The lift obstruction uses a coupling fact employed implicitly in earlier runs, recorded here in the exact form needed for random stationary routing.

### Lemma 4.1 — same-law finite-disagreement rigidity

Let `X` and `X'` be coupled finite-state random fields with the same law. Assume that law is own-tail deterministic in the sense of (1.2). If

\[
D:=\{k:X_k\ne X'_k\}
\]

is finite almost surely, then

\[
\boxed{X=X'\quad\text{a.s.}}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Fix a coordinate `v`. For every deterministic finite box `B_n` containing `v`, tail determinism gives a measurable function `phi_{v,n}` of the coordinates outside `B_n` such that

\[
X_v=\phi_{v,n}(X_{B_n^c})\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

Because `X'` has the same law, the same identity holds for `X'`. On the event `D subset B_n`, the two exterior configurations agree, hence `X_v=X'_v`. Since `D` is finite a.s., the events `{D subset B_n}` increase to probability one. Therefore `X_v=X'_v` a.s. for every `v`, and countability gives (4.1). QED.

## 5. Main new no-go theorem: finite-mean stationary incidence layers are irrelevant

The next theorem formalizes the exact obstruction to lifting Theorem 2.1 by assigning ordinary share slots to lattice sites.

### Setup

Let

\[
U=(U_x)_{x\in\mathbb Z^d}
\]

be an iid source layer with a nontrivial standard Borel single-site law, and let `R` be arbitrary stationary auxiliary randomness independent of `U`. Suppose a stationary finite-state field `X` is a measurable equivariant function of `(U,R)`.

Assume there is a stationary random incidence relation

\[
\mathcal I\subset\mathbb Z^d\times\mathbb Z^d
\]

measurable from `R` (so refreshing one `U_x` does not change `I`) such that for every output site `y`, the dependence of `X_y` on the entire source layer `U` is through only the incident coordinates

\[
I_y:=\{x:(x,y)\in\mathcal I\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

No linearity, group structure, locality, finite radius, or independence among the outputs is assumed. The sets `I_y` may contain arbitrarily remote source sites and may be random.

Put

\[
N_y:=|I_y|\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,\infty\}.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — finite-mean incidence stripping

If

\[
\mathbb E N_0<\infty
\tag{5.2}
\]

and the law of `X` is own-tail deterministic, then the iid layer `U` is **completely irrelevant** to `X`: there is a measurable equivariant map `Psi` such that

\[
X=\Psi(R)\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In particular, if `R` is absent, then `X` is almost surely constant.

#### Proof

For a source site `x`, define its reverse incidence set

\[
J_x:=\{y:(x,y)\in\mathcal I\}.
\]

By stationarity and the mass-transport principle,

\[
\mathbb E|J_0|
=
\mathbb E|I_0|
=
\mathbb E N_0
<\infty.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence `|J_0|<infinity` almost surely.

Refresh only `U_0`, replacing it by an independent copy `U'_0`, and leave all other source variables and `R` unchanged. Let `X'` be the resulting output. The refreshed source has the same law as `(U,R)`, so `X'` has the same law as `X`. Because the incidence geometry is unchanged and an output `X_y` can use `U_0` only when `y in J_0`,

\[
\{y:X_y\ne X'_y\}\subset J_0.
\]

The disagreement set is therefore finite almost surely. Lemma 4.1 forces

\[
X=X'\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus refreshing `U_0` has no effect on the output. By stationarity the same is true for every source coordinate `U_x`.

Fix an output coordinate `v`. Conditional on `R`, the variable `X_v` is therefore invariant modulo null sets under independent refresh of every single coordinate of the product source `U`. Equivalently, modulo the conditional product law it is measurable with respect to the complement of every finite subset of source coordinates, hence with respect to the iid source tail. Kolmogorov's zero-one law makes that tail trivial. Therefore `X_v` is `R`-measurable. Doing this simultaneously over the countable output lattice gives (5.3). QED.

### Corollary 5.2 — finitely many finite-mean product layers cannot support an own-tail code

Suppose `X` is generated from finitely many independent stationary iid source layers

\[
U^{(1)},\ldots,U^{(r)}
\]

and, for each layer `a`, there is a stationary incidence representation of the form above with finite mean arity

\[
\mathbb E N^{(a)}_0<\infty,
\]

whose routing is measurable from the other layers / auxiliary geometry and is stable under a one-site refresh of `U^{(a)}`. If `X` is own-tail deterministic, then the layers can be stripped one after another. Hence `X` is degenerate unless some additional non-product source remains.

This is a **geometry-free finite-mean theorem**: the incidences may be arbitrarily long-range and random. Only their stationary mean load matters.

## 6. Application to the perfect crossing gadget: direct stationary lifting is impossible

Consider trying to solve Bradley by putting one central secret at each lattice site and giving it infinitely many independent copies of the perfect directional gadget from Section 2, so that after any finite output erasure at least one complete `2d`-tuple remains available for each erased secret.

In any literal direct-share implementation, let `M(x,y)` be the number of share slots at output site `y` assigned to secret `x`. If every output site has at most `C<infinity` ordinary slots, then

\[
\sum_x M(x,y)\le C.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Stationarity and mass transport give

\[
\mathbb E\sum_y M(0,y)
=
\mathbb E\sum_x M(x,0)
\le C.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore a typical secret has only finitely many share incidences almost surely. It cannot have the infinitely many direct copies needed to survive deletion of all its finitely many share sites.

More generally, even if the number of ordinary share contributions stored at a site is random and finite almost surely, Theorem 5.1 shows that **finite mean load is still fatal**. If the secret/pad layer is genuinely pivotal, one must have

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E N_0=\infty.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the heavy-tail mechanism isolated in the preceding runs is not merely one convenient design choice inside this direct-incidence class: **infinite mean site arity is necessary**.

The borderline law used earlier,

\[
\Pr(N\ge n)=1/n,
\]

has exactly the required qualitative feature: `N<infinity` almost surely while `E N=infinity`. The present theorem explains why every bounded-load or integrable-load secret-sharing lift must fail before any Bradley cut calculation is even attempted.

## 7. What survives: superposition, not more local gadget design

Sections 2--6 move the frontier in a useful way.

The local crossing problem is solved: finite alphabets can hide a secret perfectly from every proper directional collection while allowing all directions jointly to recover it. Therefore continuing to search for a finite four-side gadget is wasted effort.

The stationary lift problem is the actual bottleneck. A positive construction cannot allocate finitely many ordinary share slots per output site to a positive-density family of secrets. It must instead do at least one of the following:

1. **infinite-mean finite-realization superposition:** every output symbol aggregates a finite number of secret/check contributions almost surely, but the number has infinite mean;
2. **non-incidence nonlinear compression:** one finite-state symbol simultaneously carries information about infinitely many potential secrets without decomposing into a finite-mean list of source incidences;
3. **non-product latent source:** avoid a decomposition into independently refreshable positive-density source layers to which Theorem 5.1 applies.

Only (1) currently has a concrete mechanism in this track: heavy-tailed self-marked routing. The remaining job is to combine that mechanism with the perfect additive directional masking of Theorem 2.1 while making the routing geometry itself cut-blind and tail-reconstructible.
