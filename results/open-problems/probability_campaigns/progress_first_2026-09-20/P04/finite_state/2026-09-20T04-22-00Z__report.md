## 1. Exact source target and continuity

Source: Richard C. Bradley, *Basic Properties of Strong Mixing Conditions. A Survey and Some Open Questions*, Probability Surveys 2 (2005), Section 8, Question 10, printed pp. 135–136, arXiv:math/0511078.

Bradley's literal 1994 definitions are retained. For `i in {1,...,d}` and `m in Z`,

`G_{i,m} = {k in Z^d : k_i < m}`,

`H_{i,m} = {k in Z^d : k_i > m}`,

and lattice-halfspace one-dependence means

`σ(X_k : k in G_{i,m})` is independent of `σ(X_k : k in H_{i,m+2})`

for every `i,m`. The full spatial tail is

`T_X = intersection_{S finite} σ(X_k : k notin S)`,

and tail determinism means every coordinate `X_k` is `T_X`-measurable modulo null sets.

## 3. Source entropy input retained from the previous run

Let the finite alphabet be `A`, `q=|A|`, and let

`h = H(X_0)`.

Nondegeneracy and strict stationarity imply `h>0`.

### Lemma 3.1 — the `4 Z^d` sublattice is iid

For every finite `F subset (4Z)^d`, the variables `(X_k)_{k in F}` are mutually independent and identically distributed. Hence

`H(X_F)=|F| h`.

Proof. Induct on `|F|`. If more than one point is present, choose a coordinate on which not all points agree. Distinct occupied levels in that coordinate are multiples of four, so split between two consecutive occupied levels `a<b` with `b>=a+4`. Put `m=a+1`. The left part lies in `G_{i,m}` because its coordinates are at most `a<m`; the right part lies in `H_{i,m+2}` because its coordinates are at least `a+4>m+2=a+3`. Bradley halfspace one-dependence separates the two sigma-fields. Apply induction inside each side. QED.

For `Lambda_n=[-n,n]^d intersect Z^d`,

`H(X_{Lambda_n}) >= |Lambda_n intersect (4Z)^d| h = Theta(n^d)`.

This volume-order lower bound is the contradiction engine below.

## 4. New abstract theorem: a box-uniform finite-type law cannot solve Bradley Question 10

The next theorem is the main advance of this run. It converts a large class of finite-alphabet algebraic/permutive candidates into the finite-range Markov class that the preceding run already showed is impossible. I give a direct proof so that the result does not depend on the older corollary as a black box.

### Definition 4.1 — box-uniform SFT law

Let `Y subset A^{Z^d}` be a nonempty subshift of finite type. A probability measure `mu` on `Y` is **box-uniform** if for every integer box `D=Lambda_N`, the marginal `mu_D` is the uniform probability distribution on the finite set `Y|D` of box patterns that occur in global points of `Y`.

Translation invariance is assumed when the resulting process is compared with Bradley's strictly stationary target.

### Theorem 4.2 — box-uniform SFT laws are finite-range spatial Markov

Let `Y subset A^{Z^d}` be an SFT and let `mu` be box-uniform. There exists a finite radius `rho` such that, for every finite box `Lambda`, the conditional law of `X_Lambda` given the entire exterior `X_{Lambda^c}` depends only on the finite shell

`S_rho(Lambda) = {z notin Lambda : dist_infinity(z,Lambda) <= rho}`.

Equivalently,

`X_Lambda` is conditionally independent of `X_{Lambda^c \ S_rho(Lambda)}` given `X_{S_rho(Lambda)}`.

#### Proof

Choose a finite defining window `W` for the SFT and choose `rho` so large that every translate of `W` meeting `Lambda` is contained in `Lambda union S_rho(Lambda)`.

Fix a large box `D` containing `Lambda union S_rho(Lambda)`. Because `mu_D` is uniform on `Y|D`, conditional on an occurring pattern `y` on `D\Lambda`, the law of `X_Lambda` is uniform on the set

`F_D(y) = {a in A^Lambda : a union y belongs to Y|D}`.

I claim `F_D(y)` depends on `y` only through its shell restriction `y|S_rho(Lambda)`.

Take one global configuration `x in Y` extending `y`; such an `x` exists because `y` is an occurring pattern. For a proposed filling `a`, splice `a` into `x` on `Lambda` and leave `x` unchanged outside `Lambda`; call the resulting full configuration `x^a`.

Every local defining window disjoint from `Lambda` is unchanged from `x` and is therefore legal. Every defining window meeting `Lambda` is contained in `Lambda union S_rho(Lambda)`, so whether it is legal is determined by `a` together with `y|S_rho(Lambda)`. Consequently `x^a in Y` if and only if all those finitely many boundary-crossing constraints are satisfied. The criterion is independent of the values of `y` farther away.

Thus `F_D(y)` is a shell function. Since the conditional law is uniform on this set, the conditional distribution of `X_Lambda` given `X_{D\Lambda}` is already measurable with respect to `X_{S_rho(Lambda)}` and is independent of the farther coordinates in `D`.

Let `D` increase to `Z^d`. The finite-exterior conditional probabilities form a martingale and converge to the conditional law given the full exterior. Because every finite stage beyond the shell is the same shell-measurable function, the limit is that same function. This proves the spatial Markov property. QED.

### Theorem 4.3 — no nondegenerate box-uniform SFT Bradley field

There is no nondegenerate, strictly stationary, finite-state, box-uniform SFT field that is simultaneously Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependent and tail deterministic.

#### Proof

Assume such a field exists. By Theorem 4.2, for some fixed `rho`,

`Law(X_{Lambda_n} | X_{Lambda_n^c})`

depends only on the external shell

`C_n = Lambda_{n+rho} \ Lambda_n`.

Tail determinism makes `X_{Lambda_n}` measurable with respect to `X_{Lambda_n^c}`, hence its full-exterior conditional law is a point mass. Since that conditional law is shell-measurable, `X_{Lambda_n}` is actually measurable with respect to `X_{C_n}`. Therefore

`H(X_{Lambda_n}) <= H(X_{C_n}) <= |C_n| log q = O(n^{d-1}).`

But Lemma 3.1 gives

`H(X_{Lambda_n}) >= |Lambda_n intersect (4Z)^d| h = Theta(n^d)`

with `h>0`. The two bounds contradict each other for large `n`. QED.

### What this theorem says conceptually

A uniform finite-type architecture has a finite Markov blanket: once the finite boundary shell is fixed, every interior filling compatible with the SFT has the same local counting weight. Tail determinism would then force the boundary shell to determine the entire box. Bradley independence simultaneously puts iid information at positive density inside the box. A surface-order shell cannot determine volume-order finite-alphabet entropy.

This is stronger than merely checking another algebraic example: it excludes an entire **measure architecture**.

## 5. Corollary: arbitrary finite (including nonabelian) group shifts with Haar measure are impossible

Let `K` be any nontrivial finite group, not assumed abelian. Let

`V <= K^{Z^d}`

be a closed shift-invariant subgroup, and let `mu_V` be Haar probability measure on `V`.

### Corollary 5.1

The Haar coordinate field of `V` cannot be a nondegenerate solution of Bradley Question 10.

#### Proof

Because `Z^d` is polycyclic and `K` is finite, the Schmidt/Salo finite-type theorem makes `V` an SFT.

For every finite box `D`, the coordinate projection

`pi_D : V -> V|D`

is a continuous group homomorphism onto the finite group `V|D`. The pushforward of Haar measure is Haar measure on that finite group, hence the uniform distribution on `V|D`. Thus `mu_V` is box-uniform. Theorem 4.3 applies. QED.

This strictly extends the earlier finite-abelian/F_p-linear obstruction: no commutativity, Laurent-module presentation, principal ideal, finite-dimensional linearity, or finite-type assumption needs to be imposed by hand.

### Independent structural proof: tail determinism kills homoclinic points

There is a useful second proof mechanism worth recording because it identifies the obstruction topologically.

Let

`Delta(V) = {v in V : supp(v) is finite}`

be the homoclinic subgroup. If the Haar field is tail deterministic, then `Delta(V)={e}`.

Indeed, suppose `v` is nontrivial and translate it spatially so `v_0 != e`. Let `F=supp(v)`. Left multiplication `L_v(x)=v x` preserves Haar measure and fixes every coordinate outside `F`. Since `X_0` is measurable with respect to the tail, in particular with respect to `sigma(X_k:k notin F)`, it is unchanged almost surely by `L_v`. But at coordinate zero,

`X_0(L_v x)=v_0 X_0(x)`,

which can never equal `X_0(x)` in a group unless `v_0=e`. Contradiction.

Now combine `Delta(V)={e}` with the SFT property. If `W` is a defining window and `rho` exceeds its `L-infinity` diameter, then agreement of two points `x,y in V` on the external shell `Lambda_{n+rho}\Lambda_n` forces agreement on `Lambda_n`: form `q=xy^{-1}`, truncate it to `Lambda_n`, and use the shell agreement to check every local SFT window; the truncation lies in `V` and has finite support, hence is the identity. Thus the shell determines the box and the same surface-versus-volume entropy contradiction follows.

This independent argument confirms that the nonabelian extension is not an artefact of the box-uniform formulation.

## 6. New nonlinear corollary: canonical measures on cellwise quasigroup shifts are impossible

The group-shift theorem still has an algebraic group operation. The next corollary moves beyond associativity.

Let `Q` be a finite quasigroup, and let `Y subset Q^{Z^d}` be a subshift closed under the cellwise quasigroup operations. Salo's 2026 avoshift result gives two facts relevant here:

1. such a cellwise quasigroup shift has equal one-site extension counts for arbitrary finite observed sets;
2. on the polycyclic group `Z^d`, such a shift is an SFT.

### Lemma 6.1 — equal extension counts produce a canonical box-uniform measure

For finite `C subset D subset Z^d`, the number of patterns in `Y|D` extending any fixed `p in Y|C` is independent of `p`.

Proof. Enumerate `D\C={s_1,...,s_r}`. At step `j`, the equal-extension-count property says every globally occurring pattern on `C union {s_1,...,s_{j-1}}` has the same number of extensions to `s_j`. Multiplying these constants over the finite sequence shows that the total extension count from `C` to `D` is independent of the initial pattern. QED.

Therefore the uniform measures on `Y|D` project to the uniform measures on `Y|C`. By Kolmogorov consistency they define a probability measure `mu_unif` on `Y`; shift invariance of `Y` makes `mu_unif` strictly stationary, and every finite marginal is uniform on the globally occurring patterns.

### Corollary 6.2

The canonical uniform measure `mu_unif` on a nondegenerate finite cellwise quasigroup shift cannot satisfy both Bradley halfspace one-dependence and tail determinism.

Proof. The shift is SFT and `mu_unif` is box-uniform, so Theorem 4.3 applies. QED.

This removes a genuinely nonlinear candidate class. In particular, replacing addition in an algebraic shift by a Latin-square/nonassociative quasigroup operation does not evade the finite-state obstruction **when the natural equal-count measure is used**.

Important scope label: this does **not** rule out every invariant measure supported on a quasigroup shift. A highly nonuniform invariant measure on the same support need not be box-uniform. Therefore this is PROVED PARTIAL, not a full D0038 impossibility theorem.

## 7. New permutive corollary: the natural TEP measure is impossible

Salo's 2022 TEP construction is even less algebraic: TEP subshifts include nonlinear examples that are not conjugate to group shifts. The cited theorem supplies a canonical invariant TEP measure `mu_TEP` whose marginal on every finite convex domain is uniform on the globally occurring patterns of that domain.

Integer boxes are convex, and a TEP subshift is an SFT. Hence `mu_TEP` satisfies exactly the box-uniform hypothesis of Theorem 4.3.

### Corollary 7.1

No nondegenerate TEP measure on a finite TEP subshift over `Z^d` can simultaneously be Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependent and tail deterministic.

This closes the most obvious nonlinear/permutive replacement for the previous Haar-algebraic route. It includes, but is not limited to, linear TEP examples such as Ledrappier-type systems; the statement also covers the genuinely nonlinear TEP examples for which no cellwise group structure exists.

Again the scope is a **natural-measure** theorem. It does not assert that every invariant measure supported by a TEP SFT is impossible.

## 8. Failed routes / lessons from this run

### 8.1 Merely changing the finite algebra did not help

Moving from finite fields to arbitrary finite nonabelian groups, then to cellwise quasigroups and TEP local constraints, initially looked like a way around the previous Laurent-module obstruction. The uniform-SFT theorem shows why the route still fails: the canonical measures inherit a finite shell specification, and tail determinism collapses volume information onto that shell.

### 8.2 SFT support alone is not yet ruled out

It would be incorrect to conclude that every finite-state SFT-supported stationary measure is impossible. The proof uses box-uniformity (or the corresponding finite-range conditional specification), not merely finite-type support. A singular/nonuniform invariant measure on an SFT can have long-range conditional dependence even though the support is locally defined.

### 8.3 The next positive construction must be noncanonical in a strong sense

Any surviving finite-alphabet algebraic/permutive construction must avoid its natural Haar/uniform/TEP law. It would need a stationary measure whose conditional law in a large box continues to depend on genuinely remote exterior information after the entire finite boundary shell is revealed. That is exactly the extensive anti-Markov requirement proved in the preceding report.

## 10. Next concrete attack

The algebraic/permutive **canonical-measure** branch is now broad enough that another variant of the same local-uniform idea would be repetition. The next attack should target the exact surviving loophole:

> Can a strictly stationary finite-state measure with Bradley halfspace one-dependence be supported on an SFT while having genuinely non-Markov, remote-dependent conditional specifications strong enough for own-tail reconstruction?

Two concrete approaches are now separated cleanly.

1. **Negative route — force local conditional regularity from exact halfspace independence.** For an SFT-supported candidate, compare conditional laws of the box under two far exteriors that agree on a thick shell. Use exact independence across coordinate halfspaces plus stationarity to try to show those conditional laws must coincide. If successful, Theorem 4.3's entropy contradiction becomes a full SFT-support impossibility theorem without any uniform-measure assumption.

2. **Positive route — abandon finite-type support.** Build an infinite-memory finite-alphabet inverse-limit code with no global phase and no finite Markov blanket. The previous peeling and screen lower bounds say its decoder must use linearly many remote probes for one site and volume-order far-exterior information for large boxes. The construction therefore has to implement multi-directional secret sharing at every scale, with the hierarchy itself invisible to each individual Bradley halfspace.

The next run should begin with route (1), because a successful shell-conditioning lemma would eliminate all finite-type symbolic constructions at once; if it fails for an explicit reason, that failure gives the specification a positive inverse-limit construction must realize.
