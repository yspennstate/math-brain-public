## 2. First new theorem: a finite symbol cannot be perfectly one-time-padded by infinitely many independent pads

There is a real measurability obstruction, but it is narrower than the preceding run's influence theorem.

### Theorem 2.1 — no countably infinite exact regular masking of one finite symbol

Let `A` be a finite set with `|A|>=2`, and let a finite group `G` act regularly on `A`. Let

\[
P_1,P_2,\ldots
\]

be iid uniform `G`-valued random variables, independent of an arbitrary random element `Z`. Suppose

\[
Y=f(Z,P_1,P_2,\ldots)\in A
\]

is measurable. Assume that for infinitely many indices `n`, changing only `P_n` acts on the output by the corresponding regular action, i.e. for every `g in G`,

\[
f(Z,P_1,\ldots,gP_n,\ldots)
=
 g\cdot f(Z,P_1,\ldots,P_n,\ldots)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then no such measurable `Y` exists.

#### Proof

Fix an index `n` for which (2.1) holds and condition on

\[
\mathcal F_{\neg n}:=
\sigma(Z,P_j:j\ne n).
\]

As `P_n` ranges uniformly over `G`, regularity of the action makes `Y` uniform on all of `A`, independently of `F_{\neg n}`. Thus

\[
\mathcal L(Y\mid\mathcal F_{\neg n})=
\mathrm{Unif}(A)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Now enumerate an infinite subsequence of masking indices as `n_1<n_2<...`. For any finite `r`, choose a masking index `n_s` outside `{n_1,...,n_r}`. Since

\[
\sigma(Z,P_{n_1},\ldots,P_{n_r})
\subset \mathcal F_{\neg n_s},
\]

(2.2) implies that `Y` is independent of `sigma(Z,P_{n_1},...,P_{n_r})` and uniform on `A`.

Let `r -> infinity`. The sigma-fields generated by `Z` and the first `r` masking pads increase to the sigma-field generated by `Z` and all masking pads. Conditional expectations therefore converge by the martingale convergence theorem to `1_{Y=a}` for each `a in A`, because `Y` is measurable from those variables. But every finite-stage conditional expectation equals `1/|A|`. Hence

\[
1_{\{Y=a\}}=1/|A|
\quad\text{a.s.},
\]

impossible for `|A|>=2`. QED.

### Binary specialization

For `A=G=Z/2Z`, (2.1) says that flipping any one of infinitely many pads flips the output bit. Finite parity has this property for finitely many pads; **countable parity is not a measurable Bernoulli-product observable**. The theorem formalizes exactly that obstruction.

This closes only the rigid architecture in which infinitely many pads each act *unconditionally* and transitively on one output symbol. It does not contradict the preceding influence theorem, because nonsummable influence can be obtained with pad actions that are rare and context-gated.

## 3. Main positive movement: heavy-tailed finite masking escapes Theorem 2.1 exactly

The natural escape works, and it works already with one output bit.

### Lemma 3.1 — finite-a.s. / infinite-mean gated parity

Let `N` be a positive integer-valued random variable with

\[
\Pr(N\ge n)=\frac1n,
\qquad n\ge1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\Pr(N=n)=\frac1{n(n+1)}.
\]

Then `N<infinity` almost surely but

\[
\mathbb E N=\sum_{n\ge1}\Pr(N\ge n)=\infty.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `R` be an arbitrary random element, let `P_n` be iid fair bits independent of `(R,N,B)`, and let

\[
\rho_n(R)\in\{0,1\}
\]

be arbitrary measurable summary bits. Put

\[
U_n:=P_n\oplus \rho_n(R).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let `B` be any additional random element independent of `R,(P_n)`, and define

\[
C:=a(B,N)\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}U_n.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then:

1. `C` is a legitimate measurable **single bit**, because only finitely many terms are used almost surely;
2. the full masked sequence `(U_n)_{n>=1}` is iid fair and independent of `R`, hence `C` carries no information about `R` beyond information already present in `(B,N)`;
3. if `(B,N)` is independent of `R`, then `C` is independent of `R` exactly;
4. resampling `P_n` flips `C` exactly on `{N>=n}`, so the individual resampling influence is

\[
I_n=\Pr(N\ge n)=\frac1n,
\tag{3.5}
\]

and therefore

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}I_n=\infty.
\tag{3.6}
\]

#### Proof

Conditional on `R`, the map

\[
(P_n)_n\mapsto(P_n\oplus\rho_n(R))_n
\]

is a coordinatewise translation of product Bernoulli measure. Therefore `(U_n)_n` is product Bernoulli and independent of `R`. Since `N<infinity` a.s., (3.4) is a finite parity and is measurable. Refreshing `P_n` changes exactly one included summand when `n<=N`, and changes no summand otherwise; hence (3.5). Summing (3.5) gives (3.6). QED.

### Strong bundle version

The point is not limited to a single ciphertext. Let `T` be finite or countable, let `N_t<infinity` a.s., and let

\[
C_t=F_t(B,(N_s)_{s\in T},U_1,U_2,\ldots)
\]

be any measurable finite-state bundle, with each `C_t` depending on only finitely many `U_n` almost surely if desired. If `(B,(N_s))` is independent of `R`, then the **entire bundle** `(C_t)_{t in T}` is independent of `R`, because the entire masked vector `U` is independent of `R`.

Thus exact cut blindness is compatible with a finite output alphabet, a finite actual mask count at every realization, and nonsummable expected influence. One does **not** need an illegal infinite parity.

### Before/after obligation

Before this run, the necessary conditions from the resampling theorem were qualitative:

- typical output must have nonsummable total source sensitivity;
- a pivotal pad cannot have merely finite reverse impact.

After Lemma 3.1, the first condition has an explicit sharp mechanism:

\[
I_n=1/n,
\qquad
\sum_n I_n=\infty,
\qquad
N<\infty\ \text{a.s.}
\]

So “finite state versus infinite total influence” is **not** itself an obstruction.

## 4. Stationary routing lemma: finite output degree but infinite reverse fanout can coexist

The second necessary condition can also be met by an explicit stationary incidence geometry.

### Lemma 4.1 — heavy-tailed stationary bipartite incidence

Fix distinct offsets

\[
z_1,z_2,\ldots\in\mathbb Z^d.
\]

At each output site `k in Z^d`, sample iid copies `N_k` of `N` from (3.1). Draw an incidence edge from output `k` to pad site

\[
v=k+z_n
\]

for every `1<=n<=N_k`.

Then:

1. the incidence rule is translation-equivariant, hence stationary;
2. every output has finite degree `N_k<infinity` almost surely;
3. `E deg_out(0)=infinity`;
4. every fixed pad site `v` has **infinite degree almost surely**.

#### Proof of (4)

For fixed `v`, the potential output attached through offset `z_n` is

\[
k_n=v-z_n.
\]

The sites `k_n` are distinct. The events

\[
E_n:=\{N_{k_n}\ge n\}
\]

are therefore independent, with

\[
\Pr(E_n)=1/n.
\]

Since `sum_n 1/n=infinity`, the second Borel--Cantelli lemma gives infinitely many `E_n` almost surely. Each such event contributes an incidence edge into `v`; hence `deg_in(v)=infinity` a.s. QED.

If iid pad bits `(P_v)` are placed at pad sites and an output takes the finite parity of its incident pads, then refreshing a single pad flips infinitely many outputs almost surely. Meanwhile the origin's total pad influence equals `E N_0=infinity`. Therefore this routing geometry satisfies **both** influence-side necessities from the previous run while every realized output parity remains finite.

This is a substantive narrowing of the construction problem: the influence constraints alone do not force an impossible countable product.

## 5. Exact defect of the naive routing construction: the local gate itself is fatal

The preceding routing lemma is **not** yet a Bradley construction. In the naive implementation the degrees `N_k` are supplied by independent local randomizers. Refreshing `N_0` changes at most the single output at the origin. If that refresh is genuinely pivotal, it gives a same-law coupling with a finite nonempty disagreement set, which own-tail determinism forbids.

So the heavy-tail mechanism cannot use a private local gate.

This isolates the new positive bottleneck:

> **the gate selecting the finite random mask set must itself have infinite reverse fanout, or must be completely output-irrelevant under every law-preserving local refresh.**

The former is the useful option. In geometric language, the stopping/depth variable should live on a separator bundle or hierarchy node that controls infinitely many output sites, rather than at one output site.

This is more precise than the previous generic requirement “use an infinite-arity aggregator.” The correct architecture is now:

- **finite realized arity** at each output;
- **infinite mean arity** (or another source of nonsummable influence);
- **infinite reverse fanout** for every genuinely pivotal pad and every genuinely pivotal gate;
- exact one-time-pad blindness across the relevant Bradley cut.

## 6. New cut-blind hierarchy lemma for a shared binary gate

There is also a useful obstruction on how such a shared gate may coordinate opposite halfspaces.

### Lemma 6.1 — binary latent dichotomy

Let `G in {0,1}` with `0<Pr(G=1)<1`. Let `L,R` be arbitrary standard Borel random elements. Suppose

\[
L\perp\!\!\!\perp R\mid G
\tag{6.1}
\]

and also

\[
L\perp\!\!\!\perp R.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then at least one side is blind to the gate:

\[
L\perp\!\!\!\perp G
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
R\perp\!\!\!\perp G.
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

Write `p=Pr(G=1)`, and let `mu_g`, `nu_g` be the conditional laws of `L`, `R` given `G=g`. Conditional independence gives

\[
\mathcal L(L,R)
=(1-p)\mu_0\otimes\nu_0+p\mu_1\otimes\nu_1.
\]

The product of the marginals is

\[
\mathcal L(L)\otimes\mathcal L(R)
=
\big((1-p)\mu_0+p\mu_1\big)
\otimes
\big((1-p)\nu_0+p\nu_1\big).
\]

Subtracting yields the signed product measure

\[
p(1-p)(\mu_1-\mu_0)\otimes(\nu_1-\nu_0).
\tag{6.4}
\]

By (6.2), (6.4) is zero. A tensor product of two nonzero finite signed measures is nonzero, hence either `mu_1=mu_0` or `nu_1=nu_0`. These are exactly the two alternatives in (6.3). QED.

### Relevance

A binary hierarchy/gate shared by both sides of a Bradley cut cannot be simultaneously visible in both conditional side laws if, conditional on that gate, the two sides decouple. Therefore a shared heavy-tail gate must be **cut-blind on at least one side** at every cut where conditional independence is used. This sharpens the earlier qualitative warning that a random hierarchy can become a forbidden common latent.

For a general latent `G`, the same calculation in `L^2(G)` says that the centered conditional-expectation subspaces contributed by the two sides must be orthogonal whenever conditional and unconditional independence both hold. The binary case has only one nonconstant latent direction, forcing the dichotomy above.

## 7. What is and is not solved

### Proved partial progress

This run proves four new structural facts relevant to the surviving construction route:

1. **No infinite exact regular masking:** one finite symbol cannot be unconditionally and transitively one-time-padded by infinitely many independent pads.
2. **Heavy-tail escape:** finite-a.s. random parity with `Pr(N>=n)=1/n` is measurable, finite-state, exactly cut-blind after one-time padding, and has nonsummable total pad influence.
3. **Stationary infinite-fanout routing:** a translation-equivariant incidence graph can give every output finite degree a.s. while every pad has infinite reverse degree a.s.; therefore both influence-side necessities can coexist.
4. **Binary shared-gate rigidity:** a binary latent that conditionally separates two sides of an exactly independent cut must be invisible to at least one side.

### Not solved

No full Bradley field has been constructed. The routing lemma by itself uses local iid gate variables `N_k`, and those variables violate tail rigidity because a local gate refresh affects only one output. Conversely Lemma 6.1 does not rule out an infinite-state or more intricately masked hierarchy whose conditional laws remain cut-blind.

Thus `E_finite_tail` remains `0 -> 0`.
