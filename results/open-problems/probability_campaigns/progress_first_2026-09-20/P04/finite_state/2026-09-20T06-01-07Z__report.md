## 3. Bradley's binary source is an explicit parity tree

The key new step is to use more of Bradley's Proposition 1 than the preceding runs did.

Bradley chooses a bijection

`phi : N -> Z^d`

with `||phi(n)||_infinity < n`, defines the sup-norm sphere

`S_n = {k in Z^d : ||k||_infinity = n}`,

and proves the exact parity identity

`W_(phi(n)) = product_(u in S_n) W_u`                                  (3.1)

for every `n>=1`.

This makes `Z^d` into a rooted tree. For every vertex `v`, let

`n(v) := phi^{-1}(v)`

and define its child set

`C(v) := S_(n(v))`.

Every nonroot vertex `u` belongs to the unique sphere `S_(||u||)`, so its parent is

`par(u) = phi(||u||)`.

Since `||phi(n)||<n`, parent norms strictly decrease. Hence repeated parents reach the root `0=phi(1)` and the sets `C(v)` partition the nonroot vertices.

Writing signs multiplicatively, (3.1) says exactly

`W_v = product_(u in C(v)) W_u`.                                       (3.2)

Equivalently, if `e_v in F_2` records whether a deterministic sign perturbation flips coordinate `v`, then an admissible perturbation satisfies

`e_v = XOR_(u in C(v)) e_u`                                             (3.3)

at every vertex.

### Lemma 3.1 — the law of `W` is Haar on the parity-tree constraint group

Let `K` be the subgroup of `{-1,+1}^{Z^d}` consisting of sign configurations satisfying (3.2) at every vertex. Bradley's construction is Haar measure on `K`. Consequently, for every deterministic `delta in K`,

`W  and  delta * W`

have the same law.

#### Proof

In Bradley's construction, on each sphere `S_n` all coordinates except the distinguished site `(n,0,...,0)` are chosen as independent fair signs, and the distinguished sign is then fixed by (3.1). Together with the root sign, those nondistinguished coordinates form independent fair group coordinates. Conversely, every configuration satisfying all equations (3.1) is uniquely determined by those free coordinates: recursively in increasing radius, each distinguished sign is recovered from its parent and the other signs on its sphere. Thus the law is the product Haar law transported through a group isomorphism onto `K`. Multiplication by a fixed `delta in K` merely flips a deterministic set of the free fair coordinates, leaving the product law invariant. QED.

This group observation is used only for the 1994 nonstationary **source layer**. It does not make the finite-state output `Y` an algebraic field; `f` remains arbitrary and nonlinear.

## 4. Pivotal masks for one Bradley layer

For a finite-valued measurable `f`, define the **flip influence**

`q_j := P_nu( f(omega) != f(omega with coordinate j flipped) )`,         (4.1)

where `nu` is iid fair Rademacher product measure on `{-1,+1}^{Z^d}`.

Nondegeneracy implies `q_j>0` for at least one `j`; otherwise `f` is invariant under every finite coordinate flip and therefore, by cylinder approximation / the product zero-one law, almost surely constant.

Fix a layer label `h`. At physical site `k=h+j`, the layer `W^(h)` appears in **exactly coordinate `j`** of the diagonal vector `V_(h+j)`. All other coordinates there use layers different from `h`.

Define the pivotal event

`P_j^(h) := { changing only coordinate j of V_(h+j) changes f(V_(h+j)) }`.

Because this event depends only on the other layer coordinates at that physical site, it is independent of the entire layer `W^(h)`, and

`P(P_j^(h)) = q_j`.                                                       (4.2)

Now multiply only layer `W^(h)` by a deterministic parity-tree perturbation `delta in K`, leaving every other layer unchanged. By Lemma 3.1 the resulting output field `Y'` has the same marginal law as `Y`. At site `h+j`:

- if `delta_j=+1`, nothing changes;
- if `delta_j=-1`, then `Y_(h+j) != Y'_(h+j)` exactly on `P_j^(h)`.

Thus the support of `delta`, intersected with the pivotal mask, is exactly the output disagreement set caused by this one-layer perturbation.

## 5. Two-ray parity perturbations

The tree equations admit exceptionally thin infinite perturbations.

### Lemma 5.1 — one-ray / two-ray codewords

Let `v` be any target vertex.

- If `v` is the root, any infinite descendant ray

`v=v_0, v_1, v_2, ...`, with `v_(t+1) in C(v_t)`,

supports a nonzero `delta in K`: set `delta=-1` exactly on that ray.

- If `v` is not the root, choose any sibling `s != v` with `par(s)=par(v)`. Choose infinite descendant rays `R_v` from `v` and `R_s` from `s`. Setting `delta=-1` exactly on `R_v union R_s` and `+1` elsewhere gives `delta in K` and flips `v`.

#### Proof

Work in the additive form (3.3). Along each selected ray, every selected vertex has exactly one selected child, so `1 = XOR(children)`. At the common parent of `v` and `s`, exactly two children are selected while the parent is unselected, so `0=1 XOR 1`. Every other unselected vertex has zero selected children. For the root-target case, the root is selected and has exactly one selected child. QED.

The significance is that to hide a flip of one layer coordinate from the finite-state output, it suffices to find two descendant rays whose *remote tails* contain very little pivotal mass.

## 6. Super-polynomial growth along Bradley descendant rays

The norm-monotone enumeration gives much more growth than was used in prior reports.

For `u in S_n`, all lattice points with sup norm `<n` occur earlier under `phi`, hence

`phi^{-1}(u) >= |[-(n-1),n-1]^d cap Z^d| + 1 = (2n-1)^d + 1`.           (6.1)

Therefore, if `v_(t+1) in C(v_t)=S_(n_t)` with `n_t=phi^{-1}(v_t)`, then

`n_(t+1) = phi^{-1}(v_(t+1)) >= (2 n_t - 1)^d + 1`.                     (6.2)

The branching degree at `v_t` is

`D_t := |C(v_t)| = |S_(n_t)| = (2n_t+1)^d - (2n_t-1)^d`,               (6.3)

so `D_t >= c_d n_t^(d-1)` for a positive constant `c_d`. Since `d>=2`, (6.2) makes `n_t` grow at least by repeated powers and therefore

`sum_t D_t^(-a) < infinity`                                             (6.4)

for every `a>0`.

This explosive branching is the mechanism that upgrades the previous `ell^1` obstruction to every `ell^p`, `p>1`.

## 7. Summable-ray lemma from `ell^p` pivotal mass

### Lemma 7.1

Assume `(q_j)_(j in Z^d) in ell^p(Z^d)` for some `p>1`. From **every** starting vertex `v_0` there exists a deterministic infinite descendant ray

`v_0,v_1,v_2,...`

such that

`sum_(t>=1) q_(v_t) < infinity`.                                        (7.1)

#### Proof

At `v_t`, put

`A_t := sum_(u in C(v_t)) q_u^p`,

and choose `v_(t+1)` among the `D_t=|C(v_t)|` children to minimize `q`. Then

`q_(v_(t+1)) <= (A_t/D_t)^(1/p)`.                                      (7.2)

The child sets encountered along one descendant ray are distinct spheres, hence disjoint. Therefore

`sum_t A_t <= sum_j q_j^p < infinity`.                                  (7.3)

By Hoelder,

`sum_t q_(v_(t+1))`

`<= (sum_t A_t)^(1/p) * (sum_t D_t^(-1/(p-1)))^((p-1)/p) < infinity`,   (7.4)

where the final finiteness is exactly (6.4). QED.

No independence assumptions on the pivotal events are used here. Only their one-site probabilities enter.

## 8. Main theorem — `ell^p`, `p>1`, diagonal factors cannot be tail deterministic

### Theorem 8.1

Let `A` be finite and let `f : {-1,+1}^{Z^d} -> A` be measurable. Form Bradley's diagonal factor

`Y_k=f(V_k)`

from independent translated copies of the 1994 binary nonstationary source as in Section 1. Assume `Y` is nondegenerate. If for some `p>1`

`sum_j q_j^p < infinity`,                                                (8.1)

where `q_j` is the flip influence (4.1), then `Y` is **not** measurable with respect to its own full spatial tail.

Since strict stationarity and exact lattice-halfspace one-dependence are automatic for this diagonal factor class, (8.1) rules out this entire `ell^p` subclass as solutions to Bradley Question 10.

#### Proof

Choose `j_0` with `q_(j_0)>0` and set `h=-j_0`. Then physical site `0=h+j_0` sees layer `h` exactly in diagonal coordinate `j_0`.

By Lemma 7.1 choose a descendant ray `R_0` from `j_0` with summable `q`-mass away from its first point. If `j_0` is not the parity-tree root, choose a sibling `s` and another summable descendant ray `R_1` from `s`. If `j_0` is the root, only `R_0` is needed. Let `delta in K` be the corresponding one-ray or two-ray perturbation from Lemma 5.1. In all cases `delta_(j_0)=-1` and

`sum_(j in supp(delta)) q_j < infinity`.                                (8.2)

Multiply only source layer `W^(h)` by `delta`, obtaining a same-law output field `Y'`.

Because (8.2) is a convergent series, choose finite initial pieces of the ray(s) so large that the remaining tail support `R_tail` satisfies

`sum_(j in R_tail) q_j < q_(j_0)/2`.                                   (8.3)

Choose a finite physical box `B` containing site `0` and every physical site `h+j` corresponding to the removed finite ray prefixes.

Consider the event

`E := P_(j_0)^(h)  intersect  intersection_(j in R_tail) (P_j^(h))^c`.

No independence is needed. The union bound and (4.2), (8.3) give

`P(E) >= q_(j_0) - sum_(j in R_tail) q_j > q_(j_0)/2 > 0`.              (8.4)

On `E`, site `0` changes because `delta_(j_0)=-1` and coordinate `j_0` is pivotal. Every site outside `B` agrees between `Y` and `Y'`: outside `B`, the only altered source-layer coordinates are those indexed by `R_tail`, and all of those coordinates are nonpivotal on `E`.

Hence on `E` the disagreement set

`D={k:Y_k != Y'_k}`

is nonempty and contained in finite `B`.

But `(Y,Y')` is a self-coupling of the same output law. If that law were own-tail deterministic, the tail-rigidity lemma from the preceding report would force

`P(0<|D|<infinity)=0`:

when two same-law configurations agree outside a finite box, every tail-measurable coordinate is decoded identically from that common exterior. Equation (8.4) contradicts this.

Therefore `Y` cannot be own-tail deterministic. QED.

## 9. What this newly excludes

The previous run only excluded `sum_j I_j(f)<infinity` (finite total resampling influence). The present theorem is strictly stronger.

Because flip influence and independent-resampling influence differ only by the fixed factor `2` for a binary input coordinate, Theorem 8.1 rules out every nonconstant finite-state diagonal factor whose influence sequence belongs to **any** `ell^p`, `p>1`.

Examples newly covered include influence profiles such as

`q_j ~ |j|^(-alpha)`

in any regime that is `ell^2` but not `ell^1`; these were not covered by the preceding theorem.

### Corollary 9.1 — the planned regular weighted-threshold route is dead

The previous report proposed testing

`f(omega)=1{sum_j a_j omega_j >= 0}`

with `a in ell^2 \ ell^1`, hoping that the nonsummable first-order influence could generate the required infinite avalanche.

If such a threshold is in an anti-concentrated regime with the standard bound

`q_j <= C |a_j|`                                                        (9.1)

for all `j` (for example, any concrete coefficient design for which this anti-concentration estimate is independently proved), then `q in ell^2` because `a in ell^2`. Theorem 8.1 therefore rules it out **even though its total influence may diverge**.

This is the important design correction: merely passing the previous `ell^1` test by arranging infinite total influence is not enough. A surviving diagonal compression must have an extraordinarily heavy pivotal profile:

`(q_j) notin ell^p  for every p>1`.                                     (9.2)

In particular, any future positive diagonal-factor attempt must deliberately leave the entire union `union_(p>1) ell^p`; ordinary square-summable influence architectures cannot work.

The anti-concentration implication (9.1) is stated conditionally here; this run does not claim it for every Rademacher series without checking the coefficient-specific concentration law.

## 10. Why the proof is source-specific and why it is not a full impossibility theorem

Three special features make Theorem 8.1 work:

1. Bradley's nonstationary binary source has the exact parity-tree identities (3.1).
2. The norm-monotone enumeration makes descendant branching explode according to (6.2).
3. The diagonal stationarization exposes exactly one bit of a fixed source layer in exactly one coordinate of each site's iid diagonal vector, so pivotal masks convert source-layer sign perturbations directly into output disagreements.

An arbitrary finite-state stationary Bradley candidate need not be a sitewise factor of this countable-layer architecture. Therefore Theorem 8.1 is **PROVED PARTIAL**, not a solution of Question 10.

The theorem nevertheless removes the most concrete positive route left by the previous run: regular infinite-influence compressions of Bradley's own construction.

## 11. Failed / unresolved extension

The proof would become a complete no-go for **all** diagonal factors if one could show that every finite-valued measurable Boolean/product-space function has `(q_j) in ell^p` for at least one `p>1`. I do **not** know such a theorem and do not assume it. Product-space functions can have infinite total influence, and the present argument leaves open extremely heavy pivotal tails satisfying (9.2).

The exact next diagonal-factor bottleneck is therefore:

> Can a finite-valued measurable `f` have a pivotal profile outside every `ell^p`, `p>1`, while its pivotal mask almost surely forms the repeated ray barriers required by tail reconstruction in Bradley's parity tree?

The parity-tree formulation is stronger than a raw influence condition. Own-tail determinism requires that a pivotal mask prevent **every** finite-support-hidden one-ray/two-ray perturbation at arbitrarily remote scales. A future run should attack that barrier property directly, using the additional dependence geometry of the pivotal field rather than only the marginals `q_j`.

## 12. Next attack

The next useful attack is **not** another checker or another weighted threshold. It is a percolation/cutset theorem for the pivotal mask.

For one resampled Bradley layer, the pivotal events `P_j^(h)` are generated by all the *other* independent Bradley layers. They inherit strong directional separation properties from those layers. The target is to prove that a finite-valued `f` cannot make this mask hit every descendant ray infinitely often unless some separated halfspace sigma-fields share nontrivial information, which would contradict the very Bradley independence that the diagonal construction otherwise preserves.

Concretely:

1. prove the exact halfspace-dependence law of the pivotal-mask field `P^(h)`;
2. formulate tail recovery as an `infinite cutset on every parity-tree ray` event;
3. use Bradley separation plus the enormous child spheres `S_n` to construct, with positive probability, two descendant rays containing only finitely many pivotal sites;
4. plug those rays into the one-layer Haar perturbation above to obtain the same finite-disagreement contradiction without any `ell^p` assumption.

If step 3 succeeds, it closes the entire Bradley-diagonal finite-state factor class. It would still be a proper subclass obstruction, not yet the full arbitrary-field Question 10.
