## 1. Exact source and continuity

Source: Richard C. Bradley, *Basic Properties of Strong Mixing Conditions. A Survey and Some Open Questions*, Probability Surveys 2 (2005), Section 8, Question 10, printed pp. 135–136, arXiv:math/0511078.

The source property inherited from Bradley (1994) is the following. For every coordinate `i in {1,...,d}` and integer `m`, put

`G_{i,m} = {k in Z^d : k_i < m}`,

`H_{i,m} = {k in Z^d : k_i > m}`.

The field is **lattice-halfspace one-dependent** when

`σ(X_k : k in G_{i,m})` is independent of `σ(X_k : k in H_{i,m+2})`

for every `i,m`. Its full spatial tail is

`T_X = intersection_{S finite} σ(X_k : k notin S)`.

It is **tail deterministic** when every coordinate `X_k` is `T_X`-measurable modulo null sets.

Question 10 asks whether, for `d>=2`, there is a nondegenerate strictly stationary **finite-state** (even two-state) field with both properties. Bradley's 1994 stationary example is real-valued/nonatomic and therefore does not settle the finite-state endpoint.

## 3. Basic entropy input: the 4Z^d sublattice is iid

The following fact was already implicit in the older D0038 work as the source of positive entropy density, but I record the exact proof because it is the input to the new theorems.

Let `A` be the finite state space, `q=|A|`, and

`h = H(X_0)`.

Nondegeneracy plus strict stationarity gives `h>0`.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact iid sparse sublattice

For every finite `F subset (4Z)^d`, the variables `(X_k)_{k in F}` are mutually independent and identically distributed. Consequently

`H(X_F)=|F| h`.

#### Proof

Identical distribution is strict stationarity. For independence, induct on `|F|`. If `|F|<=1` there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose a coordinate `i` on which not all points of `F` agree. The distinct `i`-coordinates are multiples of 4, so between two consecutive occupied levels there is a gap at least 4. Split `F=L union R` at such a gap, with

`max_{x in L} x_i = a`, `min_{x in R} x_i >= a+4`.

Set `m=a+1`. Then `L subset G_{i,m}` and every point of `R` has `i`-coordinate at least `a+4=m+3>m+2`, hence `R subset H_{i,m+2}`. Bradley halfspace one-dependence makes `σ(X_L)` and `σ(X_R)` independent. Apply the induction hypothesis separately inside `L` and `R`. This factors the full joint law. QED.

For the box

`Lambda_n = [-n,n]^d intersect Z^d`,

put

`N_n = |Lambda_n intersect (4Z)^d| = (2 floor(n/4)+1)^d`.

Then

`H(X_{Lambda_n}) >= N_n h`,

so the field has a source-forced positive entropy density at least `h/4^d` along these boxes.

## 4. New theorem: tail determinism forces extensive conditional information across every small screen

The prior handoff observed that positive volume entropy is compatible with tail determinism, so entropy positivity alone is not a contradiction. The missing step is to ask **where the information that reconstructs a box must sit after one conditions on a local boundary or other proposed screen**.

### Theorem 4.1 — extensive far-exterior conditional information

Assume `X` is finite-state, nondegenerate, strictly stationary, Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependent, and tail deterministic.

Let `Lambda subset Z^d` be finite, and let `C subset Lambda^c` be any finite set. Write

`R = Lambda^c \ C`.

Then

`I(X_Lambda ; X_R | X_C)`

`= H(X_Lambda | X_C)`

and therefore

`I(X_Lambda ; X_R | X_C)`

`>= H(X_Lambda) - H(X_C)`.

In particular, if `F subset Lambda intersect (4Z)^d`, then

`I(X_Lambda ; X_R | X_C) >= |F| h - |C| log q`.          (4.1)

#### Proof

Tail determinism means every coordinate is measurable with respect to

`T_X subset σ(X_k : k notin Lambda)`.

Since `Lambda` is finite, the whole finite vector `X_Lambda` is therefore measurable with respect to `X_{Lambda^c}`. Hence

`H(X_Lambda | X_{Lambda^c}) = 0`.

But `X_{Lambda^c}` is exactly `(X_C,X_R)`. The chain-rule identity for conditional mutual information gives

`I(X_Lambda;X_R | X_C)`

`= H(X_Lambda | X_C) - H(X_Lambda | X_C,X_R)`

`= H(X_Lambda | X_C)`.

Next,

`H(X_Lambda | X_C)`

`= H(X_Lambda) - I(X_Lambda;X_C)`

`>= H(X_Lambda)-H(X_C)`.

Because `X_C` takes values in `A^C`,

`H(X_C)<=|C| log q`.

Finally `H(X_Lambda)>=H(X_F)=|F|h` by Lemma 3.1. Combining these inequalities gives (4.1). QED.

### Box form

For `Lambda_n=[-n,n]^d intersect Z^d`, Theorem 4.1 gives, for every finite `C_n subset Lambda_n^c`,

`I(X_{Lambda_n}; X_{Lambda_n^c \ C_n} | X_{C_n})`

`>= N_n h - |C_n| log q`.                                (4.2)

Therefore if `|C_n|=o(n^d)`, then

`liminf_{n->infinity} (1/|Lambda_n|) I(X_{Lambda_n}; X_{Lambda_n^c \ C_n} | X_{C_n}) >= h/4^d > 0`.    (4.3)

The meaning is important: **after conditioning on any subvolume-size external screen, the remaining far exterior still carries a positive density of information about the entire interior.** A hypothetical finite-state Bradley solution is not merely non-Markovian; it must be extensively non-Markovian.

## 5. Main corollary: no finite-range Markov/Gibbs solution

### Corollary 5.1 — no finite-range Markov random field can answer Bradley Question 10

There is no nondegenerate finite-state strictly stationary field satisfying both Bradley properties and, in addition, a finite-range spatial Markov property.

More generally, there is no such field admitting finite external screens `C_n` with `|C_n|=o(n^d)` for which

`X_{Lambda_n}` is conditionally independent of `X_{Lambda_n^c \ C_n}` given `X_{C_n}`.

#### Proof

Conditional independence would make the left side of (4.2) equal to zero. But for `|C_n|=o(n^d)`, the right side is

`N_n h - |C_n| log q = Theta(n^d) > 0`

for all sufficiently large `n`. Contradiction. QED.

### Finite-range Gibbs/Markov specifications

For a standard finite-range Markov random field with interaction range `r`, take the external boundary shell

`C_n = Lambda_{n+r} \ Lambda_n`.

The spatial Markov property says the interior is conditionally independent of the exterior beyond that shell given the shell. But

`|C_n| = (2(n+r)+1)^d - (2n+1)^d = O(n^{d-1}) = o(n^d)`,

so Corollary 5.1 applies.

Thus **all finite-range finite-state Gibbs/Markov architectures are ruled out**, including hard-constraint Markov specifications; positivity/finite-energy is not needed. What fails is not merely a particular local rule but the existence of any surface-order Markov blanket.

### Stronger approximate-screen statement

Equation (4.2) also rules out approximate screening whenever the residual conditional mutual information is `o(n^d)`. For example, take a shell of thickness `r_n=o(n)`:

`C_n = Lambda_{n+r_n} \ Lambda_n`.

Then `|C_n|=o(n^d)`, and hence

`I(X_{Lambda_n}; X_{Lambda_{n+r_n}^c} | X_{C_n}) >= (h/4^d + o(1)) |Lambda_n|`.

So **even after revealing the entire sublinear-thickness boundary annulus, the genuinely far exterior must retain volume-order conditional information about the box**.

This is a useful discriminator for future positive constructions: any local hierarchy whose large box becomes approximately screened by a sublinear boundary layer cannot possibly solve D0038.

## 6. New exact peeling theorem: too few remote probes carry zero information

The previous reports proved only fixed-width corridor and efficient-local-coding obstructions. The next result is different: it applies to an arbitrary geometry of finitely many remote sites, even if they surround the origin in many directions.

For `R>=0`, write

`B_R = {k in Z^d : ||k||_infinity <= R}`.

### Lemma 6.1 — one Bradley-separated piece is informationally redundant

Let `D=A union B` be a finite set disjoint from `{0}`. Suppose for some coordinate `i,m`, either

`{0} union A subset G_{i,m}` and `B subset H_{i,m+2}`,

or the reversed orientation holds. Then

`I(X_0 ; X_D) = I(X_0 ; X_A)`.

#### Proof

Bradley halfspace one-dependence gives

`X_B independent of (X_0,X_A)`.

Hence

`I(X_0;X_D)=I(X_0;X_A)+I(X_0;X_B | X_A)`

and the second term is zero. QED.

### Theorem 6.2 — exact cardinality-distance barrier

Let `D subset B_R^c` be finite. If

`|D| < ceil((R+1)/3)`,

then

`I(X_0;X_D)=0`.

Equivalently, **any finite set of remote sites carrying even one positive amount of information about the origin must contain at least `ceil((R+1)/3)` sites once all of its sites lie outside the L-infinity radius-`R` box.**

This theorem uses only Bradley halfspace one-dependence; strict stationarity and tail determinism are not needed.

#### Proof

Start with `E=D`. If `E` is empty we are done. Otherwise choose any `x in E`. Because `E subset B_R^c`, some coordinate `i` satisfies

`|x_i| >= R+1`.

Consider the finite set of occupied `i`-coordinates

`P_i(E) = {0} union {y_i : y in E}`.

Suppose every consecutive pair of values in the ordered projection between `0` and `x_i` differed by at most 3. To reach absolute coordinate at least `R+1` from 0 would then require at least

`ceil((R+1)/3)`

nonzero occupied levels. Each occupied level requires at least one distinct point of `E`. This contradicts

`|E| <= |D| < ceil((R+1)/3)`.

Therefore there is a gap of at least 4 between two consecutive occupied levels of `P_i(E)`. Split `E=A union B` across that empty gap, choosing `A` to be the side containing the origin in projection and `B` the opposite nonempty side. If the last occupied level on the origin side is `a`, the first level on the other side is at least `a+4`. Setting `m=a+1` (or reversing orientation) gives exactly the geometry in Lemma 6.1. Hence

`I(X_0;X_E)=I(X_0;X_A)`

with `|A|<|E|`.

Repeat. Every peeling step strictly reduces the finite set and preserves mutual information with `X_0`. Eventually the set is empty. Thus

`I(X_0;X_D)=I(X_0; empty)=0`.

QED.

### Corollary 6.3 — tail reconstruction requires linearly many remote probes

Assume now finite state and tail determinism. For every `R` and every `epsilon>0`, there exists a finite `D subset B_R^c` with

`H(X_0 | X_D) < epsilon`.

Indeed `X_0` is measurable with respect to `σ(X_k:k outside B_R)`, which is generated by a countable increasing union of finite-coordinate sigma-fields, so finite-coordinate conditional entropies decrease to zero.

Choosing `epsilon<h=H(X_0)` makes `I(X_0;X_D)>0`, and Theorem 6.2 forces

`|D| >= ceil((R+1)/3)`.

Thus no hypothetical D0038 solution can recover even one symbol from a bounded number of arbitrarily remote probes. The number of probes required for any nontrivial prediction must grow at least linearly with the exclusion radius.

### Why this is stronger than the old fixed-width result

The old corridor argument excluded reconstruction through a fixed transverse window. Theorem 6.2 allows the probe set to use arbitrary directions, arbitrary transverse spread, and arbitrary geometry. Its only resource is cardinality. A small collection of remote sites can always be peeled across a Bradley gap until nothing remains.

This gives a concrete combinatorial shape constraint on any future decoder: it must contain enough sites to **bridge every large coordinate gap in stages of at most three lattice units**. In particular, multi-directional secret sharing cannot be implemented by a bounded number of far-away shares; the share geometry itself must become increasingly dense in coordinate projection as the reconstruction radius grows.

## 7. What the two new theorems say about the live construction program

The last run left a possible positive route: a nonlinear local/mixing hierarchy, not a global odometer phase, with unbounded-scale finite-erasure reconstruction.

The present results sharply narrow that route.

1. **No finite-range conditional specification.** If the hierarchy makes a box conditionally independent of the far exterior once a finite-thickness boundary is known, it is impossible.

2. **No asymptotically thin screening.** Even a boundary layer whose thickness is `o(n)` cannot absorb the needed dependence. The far exterior must retain `Theta(n^d)` conditional information.

3. **No bounded-share remote decoder.** Any finite approximation to the tail decoder for one symbol must use at least linear-in-distance many remote sites.

4. **The required dependence is therefore doubly nonlocal:** it must be extensive in information and geometrically spread across increasingly many coordinate levels, even though opposite separated halfspaces are exactly independent unconditionally.

This explains why ordinary Gibbs fields, SFT Markov measures, local cellular-automaton stationary measures with finite Markov blankets, and standard hierarchical block models with local boundary screening are poor positive candidates.

It does **not** prove the full source impossibility: conditional dependence can be created on volume scale by conditioning, and a genuinely non-Markov finite-state process can in principle maintain such dependence at all scales. The Burton–Denker–Smorodinsky phenomenon is a warning that finite-state symbolic systems can have unexpectedly rich bilateral tail structure.

## 8. Failed route this run: turn strong mixing into a Markov screen

A tempting shortcut was to combine the previous theorem that every nonzero shift is strongly mixing with finite alphabet and conclude that sufficiently distant exterior information is negligible after conditioning on a local shell. That inference is invalid.

Strong mixing is an **unconditional** separation statement. Theorem 4.1 shows that any D0038 solution would exhibit the opposite behavior conditionally: revealing a shell can leave, or induce, extensive dependence between the interior and remote exterior. Conditioning can create large secret-sharing correlations even when appropriate unconditioned halfspaces are independent.

So a proof of full impossibility cannot merely improve the unconditional mixing estimate. It must control **multi-directional conditional synergy**.

## 10. Next mathematical attack

The next attack should use Theorem 6.2 at **many target sites simultaneously** rather than merely one origin.

Take a positive-density sparse iid set of targets inside `Lambda_n`. Tail determinism gives a finite exterior decoder for their joint vector. For each target, repeatedly peel Bradley-separated subsets of the decoder while preserving the mutual information relevant to that target. The objective is to prove a **multi-target bridge-counting / mass-transport inequality** saying that one exterior site cannot efficiently serve the required gap-bridging cores of too many independent interior targets. If successful, the total number of required exterior sites in a controlled annulus would exceed its capacity, yielding the missing contradiction.

The hard point is overlap: different targets may reuse the same remote sites, and high-order secret sharing can make one site participate in many joint decoders. The next run should attack that overlap directly, probably via conditional entropy submodularity/Shearer inequalities indexed by translated sparse targets rather than by another local-coding ansatz.

If that route fails materially, the positive fallback is no longer a standard dyadic/Gibbs hierarchy; it must be an explicitly **non-Markov, volume-coupled nonlinear symbolic hierarchy** designed so that all sublinear screens retain extensive conditional dependence while every opposite Bradley halfspace remains exactly independent.
