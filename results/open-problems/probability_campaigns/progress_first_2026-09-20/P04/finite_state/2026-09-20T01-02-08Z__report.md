## 1. Exact source target and inherited state

Source: Richard C. Bradley, *Basic Properties of Strong Mixing Conditions. A Survey and Some Open Questions*, Probability Surveys 2 (2005), Section 8, Question 10, printed pp. 135–136, arXiv:math/0511078.

Bradley's 1994 definitions are the literal ones used here. For `i in {1,...,d}` and `m in Z`,

`G_{i,m}={k in Z^d:k_i<m}` and `H_{i,m}={k in Z^d:k_i>m}`.

The field is lattice-halfspace one-dependent when

`sigma(X_k:k in G_{i,m})` is independent of `sigma(X_k:k in H_{i,m+2})`

`T_X = intersection_{S finite} sigma(X_k:k notin S)`,

Bradley (1994) already constructs a two-state **nonstationary** field with both structural properties and then makes it stationary by packing one independent translated copy for every `j in Z^d` into each nonatomic real coordinate. That real-valued result is not a finite-state answer.

## 3. Main new theorem — no finite-abelian Haar algebraic solution

### Theorem 3.1

Let `A` be any finite abelian group and let

`V <= A^(Z^d)`

be a closed shift-invariant subgroup. Put Haar probability measure on `V`, and let `X` be the coordinate field.

If `X` is lattice-halfspace one-dependent in Bradley's exact sense and tail deterministic with respect to its own spatial tail, then `V` is trivial.

Equivalently:

> **No nondegenerate finite-state solution to Bradley Question 10 can be the Haar field of a closed shift-invariant subgroup over a finite abelian alphabet.**

This strictly extends the previous `F_p^q` theorem. It includes cyclic alphabets `Z/nZ` of arbitrary composite order, arbitrary finite products of cyclic groups, and every closed abelian group shift over such alphabets, without assuming a principal presentation, a finite-type presentation, or a finite coding radius.

The proof has three ingredients:

1. tail determinism forbids nonzero finitely supported elements of `V`;
2. for each prime `p`, this makes the `F_p`-module `M/pM` have Laurent rank zero;
3. Bradley halfspace independence makes the `p`-torsion module `M[p]` Laurent-torsion-free, while a generic-rank identity gives

   `rank(M[p]) = rank(M/pM)`.

Hence `M[p]=0` for every prime, forcing the entire dual module `M` to vanish.

## 4. Dual module setup

Let `M=V^` be the discrete Pontryagin dual of `V`. The ambient dual of `A^(Z^d)` is the direct sum of copies of `A^`, so `M` is a finitely generated module over the Laurent group ring

`Lambda = Z[u_1^(+-1),...,u_d^(+-1)]`.

Because `A` is finite, `M` has finite exponent. Thus there is an integer `N>=1` with `N M=0`.

For a prime `p`, write

`M[p] = {m in M : p m=0}`

and `M/pM` for the quotient. Both are naturally modules over

`R_p = F_p[u_1^(+-1),...,u_d^(+-1)]`.

For any finitely generated `R_p`-module `Q`, write

`rank_p(Q)=dim_{K_p}(Q tensor_{R_p} K_p)`,

where `K_p=Frac(R_p)`.

## 5. Tail determinism forces `rank_p(M/pM)=0`

### Lemma 5.1 — no finite-support group perturbation

If Haar(`V`) is tail deterministic, then `V` contains no nonzero finite-support element.

#### Proof

Suppose `0!=h in V` has finite support `S`. Choose a site-coordinate where `h` is nonzero. Haar measure is invariant under translation `x -> x+h`. Tail determinism says that coordinate is measurable from `X_{S^c}`. But `x` and `x+h` agree on `S^c` and disagree at the chosen coordinate. Applying the same exterior decoder before and after Haar translation gives a contradiction. QED.

This is the same finite-erasure argument as in the previous vector theorem and uses no linear structure beyond the compact-group translation.

### Lemma 5.2 — `dual(V[p]) = M/pM`

Let

`V[p]={x in V:p x=0}`.

Then `V[p]` is a closed shift-invariant subgroup of `A[p]^(Z^d)`, hence an `F_p`-linear algebraic field, and Pontryagin exactness gives

`dual(V[p]) ~= M/pM`.

#### Justification

Restriction of characters from `V` to `V[p]` is onto, and its kernel is the annihilator of `V[p]`. For compact abelian groups that annihilator is exactly `pM`; equivalently dualizing the multiplication-by-`p` sequence gives the cokernel `M/pM`.

### Corollary 5.3

For every prime `p`,

`rank_p(M/pM)=0`.

#### Proof

If `rank_p(M/pM)>0`, then the positive-rank lemma from the previous P04 report, applied to the `F_p`-linear group shift `V[p]`, produces a nonzero finitely supported element of `V[p]`. That element also lies in `V`, contradicting Lemma 5.1. QED.

Thus tail determinism makes the **top mod `p`** of the dual module Laurent-torsion for every `p`.

## 6. Generic-rank identity: top and socle have equal Laurent rank

The next lemma is the new module-theoretic bridge needed beyond elementary abelian alphabets.

### Lemma 6.1

For every prime `p`,

`rank_p(M[p]) = rank_p(M/pM)`.

#### Proof

Only the `p`-primary component of `M` contributes to either side, since multiplication by `p` is invertible on every `q`-primary component with `q!=p`. So replace `M` by its `p`-primary component and suppose `p^r M=0`.

Let

`Lambda_r=(Z/p^r Z)[u_1^(+-1),...,u_d^(+-1)]`.

Let `S` be the multiplicative set of Laurent polynomials in `Lambda_r` whose reductions modulo `p` are nonzero in `R_p`. Since `R_p` is a domain, `S` is multiplicatively closed. Localize:

`B=S^(-1)Lambda_r`, `N=S^(-1)M`.

Then

`B/pB ~= K_p`.

Moreover `p^r B=0`. Every quotient `p^j N / p^(j+1)N` is a finite-dimensional `K_p`-vector space because `N` is finitely generated over `B`. Hence `N` has finite `B`-module length.

Consider multiplication by `p` on `N`:

`0 -> N[p] -> N --p--> N -> N/pN -> 0`.

Additivity of finite module length gives

`length_B(N[p]) = length_B(N/pN)`.

Both endpoint modules are annihilated by `p`, so they are actually finite-dimensional vector spaces over `B/pB=K_p`, and their module lengths equal their `K_p` dimensions. Therefore

`dim_{K_p} N[p] = dim_{K_p} N/pN`.

Localization is exact, so

`N[p] = S^(-1)(M[p])`,

and

`N/pN = S^(-1)(M/pM)`.

These `K_p` dimensions are precisely the Laurent ranks over `R_p`. Hence

`rank_p(M[p]) = rank_p(M/pM)`. QED.

Combining with Corollary 5.3 gives

`rank_p(M[p])=0`

for every prime `p`.

## 7. Halfspace independence forces `M[p]` to be torsion-free

### Lemma 7.1

If Haar(`V`) is lattice-halfspace one-dependent, then for every prime `p`, the `R_p`-module `M[p]` has no nonzero Laurent-torsion element.

#### Proof

Fix `p`. Suppose `0!=m in M[p]` and that a nonzero Laurent polynomial `f in R_p` satisfies

`f m=0`.

Choose such an annihilator `f` with the minimum possible number of monomials. A one-monomial Laurent polynomial is a unit, so `f` has at least two monomials. Choose a coordinate direction `i` on which two exponent vectors in `supp(f)` differ, and split

`f=L+R`

between two consecutive occupied `i`-levels. Both `L,R` are nonzero and contain fewer monomials than `f`.

Because `m` has order `p`, the `R_p` action is well-defined on the cyclic module generated by `m`. In characteristic `p`, for every `n>=1`,

`f^(p^n)=L^(p^n)+R^(p^n)`,

with all exponent vectors dilated by the factor `p^n`. Since `fm=0`, also `f^(p^n)m=0`.

Minimality of `f` implies

`L^(p^n)m != 0` and `R^(p^n)m != 0`.

Indeed either vanishing would give a nonzero annihilator of `m` with fewer monomials.

Choose any finite-support ambient character representative `g` of the class `m`. Then `L^(p^n)g` and `R^(p^n)g` are finite-support representatives of the equal-and-opposite nonzero classes

`L^(p^n)m = -R^(p^n)m`.

Their supports separate linearly in the `i`th coordinate as `p^n -> infinity`. Choose `n` so large that the distance between the rightmost `i`-coordinate of the first support and the leftmost `i`-coordinate of the second support is at least `4`.

If the first support maximum is `a`, put `m_0=a+1`. Then the first character is measurable from

`sigma(X_k:k_i<m_0)`,

while the second representative of the **same nontrivial character of `V`** is measurable from

`sigma(X_k:k_i>m_0+2)`.

Bradley's two sigma-fields are independent. A random variable measurable with respect to both independent sigma-fields is almost surely constant. But a nontrivial character on a compact group is nonconstant under Haar measure. Contradiction.

Therefore `M[p]` is torsion-free over `R_p`. QED.

### Corollary 7.2

For every prime `p`, `M[p]=0`.

Indeed `M[p]` is a finitely generated torsion-free module over the domain `R_p`. If it were nonzero it would have positive Laurent rank. Lemma 6.1 and Corollary 5.3 instead give rank zero.

## 8. Completion of Theorem 3.1

If `M` were nonzero, its finite exponent would give some nonzero element of prime-power order. Multiplying by a suitable power of that prime would produce a nonzero element of `M[p]` for some `p`. Corollary 7.2 says every `M[p]` is zero. Hence `M=0`.

By Pontryagin duality, `M=0` implies `V` is the trivial compact group. The Haar coordinate field is degenerate.

This proves Theorem 3.1. QED.

### What this rules out

The negative class now includes every construction whose law is Haar measure on a closed shift-invariant subgroup over any finite abelian alphabet, including:

- all finite cyclic alphabets `Z/nZ`, including composite `n`;
- arbitrary finite products of cyclic groups;
- linear convolutional/cocycle kernels over finite abelian groups;
- principal and non-principal algebraic subshifts;
- finite-type and non-finite-type abelian group shifts;
- inverse limits that remain a single finite-abelian algebraic group shift at the observed level.

The full question remains open because a finite-state solution may be nonlinear, non-Haar, or based on a random hidden hierarchy rather than a fixed algebraic subgroup.

## 9. Second new theorem — finite-index phase randomization cannot preserve Bradley independence

The handoff and previous report left a tempting positive idea: construct a good nonstationary finite-state field with only finitely many spatial phases, then choose a uniform random phase to obtain strict stationarity. The next theorem rules out that entire stationarization mechanism under the natural ergodicity assumption on each phase.

### Theorem 9.1 — finite-phase obstruction

Let `A` be a finite alphabet. Let `H <= Z^d` be a finite-index sublattice, and let `nu` be an `H`-invariant, `H`-ergodic probability measure on `A^(Z^d)`. Suppose the full `Z^d` translation orbit of `nu` consists of `r>1` distinct measures

`nu_1,...,nu_r`.

Let

`mu = (1/r) sum_{j=1}^r nu_j`,

which is strictly stationary under `Z^d`.

Then for every coordinate direction `i` and every sufficiently separated left/right Bradley pair of halfspaces, the corresponding sigma-fields under `mu` are **not independent**.

In particular, one cannot stationarize a genuinely nonstationary finite-phase candidate by uniformly randomizing its phase while retaining Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependence.

#### Proof

Introduce a latent phase variable `J in {1,...,r}` with `P(J=j)=1/r`, and conditional law

`Law(X | J=j)=nu_j`.

Because the `nu_j` are distinct and finite in number, choose finitely many cylinder functions `f_1,...,f_s` whose expectation vectors

`a_j=(E_{nu_j}f_1,...,E_{nu_j}f_s)`

are all distinct.

Fix a left coordinate halfspace `L={k:k_i<m}`. For each `ell`, choose a tempered `H`-Følner sequence of large boxes translated farther and farther into `L`, so that every translate of the finite support of `f_ell` stays inside `L`.

The pointwise ergodic theorem for the `H` action gives, under `nu_j`, convergence of the empirical averages of each `f_ell` along that sequence to `E_{nu_j}f_ell`. Therefore the vector `a_j`, and hence `j`, is measurable from the restriction `X_L` almost surely.

Thus `J` is measurable with respect to the left-halfspace sigma-field.

Exactly the same construction, using translated `H`-Følner boxes running to `+infinity` in the `i`th coordinate, shows that `J` is also measurable with respect to the right-halfspace sigma-field `R={k:k_i>m+2}`.

If the two halfspace sigma-fields were independent, every random variable measurable with respect to both would be almost surely constant. But `J` takes `r>1` values with positive probability. Contradiction. QED.

### Consequence for the positive program

A successful finite-state stationarization cannot use a **detectable finite global phase**. In particular, simply redesigning Bradley's binary nonstationary construction to have a finite spatial period and then averaging the finitely many translates will not work if those periodic phases are ergodic under the period sublattice.

Any surviving hidden hierarchy must satisfy a stronger cut-blindness principle: the hierarchy/phase cannot be recoverable from either individual coordinate halfspace, even though the joint far exterior must reconstruct every erased finite block.

This sharpens the previous handoff's informal cut-blind hierarchy test into a proved obstruction for finite-index phase schemes.

## 10. Positive construction attempt made this run and why it failed

I explicitly tested the simplest new route suggested by the 1994 proof:

1. replace Bradley's distinguished-origin shell construction by a finite-period family of shell origins;
2. give each phase the same binary recursion up to translation;
3. choose the phase uniformly to recover full strict stationarity.

This avoids the impossible “uniform origin on `Z^d`” and uses only a finite alphabet. It also preserves tail reconstruction inside each phase.

Theorem 9.1 kills the route before one needs to optimize the shell recursion. The phase itself becomes statistically identifiable from either remote halfspace by averaging local cylinder frequencies over the period sublattice. The two opposite halfspaces therefore share the same nontrivial phase variable and cannot be independent.

So the failure is not specific to Bradley's exact shell enumeration: **any finite-period ergodic stationarization has the same latent-phase obstruction.**

## 12. Remaining gap

The full problem is now concentrated outside two large classes.

A positive solution, if one exists, must be simultaneously:

- genuinely nonlinear or non-Haar at the observed finite alphabet;
- not a fixed finite-abelian algebraic group shift;
- not a finite detectable phase mixture;
- not a fixed-width or efficient-local coding mechanism;
- globally nonlocal enough that every finite erasure is reconstructed;
- nevertheless **cut-blind** enough that no coordinate halfspace learns a common latent shared with its opposite halfspace.

A full impossibility proof would have to upgrade these class obstructions to arbitrary finite-state stationary measures. The missing bridge is still a multiscale theorem converting finite alphabet + exact opposite-halfspace independence into an exterior-decoupling property that forbids unlimited multidirectional secret-sharing synergy.

## 13. Next mathematical attack

Do **not** repeat the fixed linear module or finite-phase arguments.

The next constructive attack should use a genuinely infinite stationary hierarchy with phase invisible to each single halfspace. The concrete test is:

- use a 2-adic/dyadic random hierarchy in `d=2`;
- at each scale encode a center symbol through a nonlinear Latin-square / perfect-secret-sharing relation among several remote sectors;
- require that after marginalizing the separator slab, the law of each individual left/right halfspace is exactly independent of the hierarchy;
- ensure the joint exterior across widening transverse scales contains infinitely many independent reconstruction equations for every finite erased set.

The first proof obligation is not full tail reconstruction. It is the **single-scale cut-blindness lemma**: construct one nontrivial finite-alphabet block gadget whose hidden scale/phase is invisible to each of two opposite separated halfspace marginals but is recoverable jointly from at least three directional sectors. If no such gadget exists under translation-covariant reuse, that failure should be turned into a nonlinear finite-alphabet obstruction theorem.

The complementary negative attack is to generalize Theorem 3.1 from abelian Haar group shifts to a genuinely nonlinear class, preferably finite quasigroup/Latin-square shifts or stationary random constraint networks.
