## 2. First new result: two-cut incompatibility is false already for a binary bundle

The most important movement in this run is positive: the feared obstruction at the level of a single crossing bundle does **not** exist. A single two-state bundle can be perfectly masked against any finite number of independent directional source blocks simultaneously.

### Lemma 2.1 — multi-cut XOR bundle masking

Let `r>=1`. Let

\[
B,R_1,\ldots,R_r
\]

be mutually independent random elements. Let

\[
P_1,\ldots,P_r\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)
\]

be independent of all those blocks. For each `i`, let

\[
\rho_i(R_i)\in\{0,1\}
\]

be an arbitrary measurable bit, and define the one-time-padded directional mask

\[
U_i:=P_i\oplus\rho_i(R_i).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let `T` be any finite or countable index set, and let

\[
a_t(B)\in\{0,1\},\qquad t\in T,
\]

be arbitrary measurable base bits. Define the binary ciphertext bundle

\[
C_t:=a_t(B)\oplus U_1\oplus\cdots\oplus U_r,
\qquad t\in T.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Then for every `i`,

\[
\sigma\!\left(
 B,
 (R_j,P_j)_{j\ne i},
 (C_t)_{t\in T}
\right)
\perp\!\!\!\perp
\sigma(R_i).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus one binary bundle may simultaneously carry one perfectly hidden opposite-side summary for each of `r` coordinate cuts. The conclusion is exact sigma-field independence and remains true for countably infinite `T`.

### Proof

Fix `i`. Put

\[
M_i:=a_t(B)\oplus\bigoplus_{j\ne i}U_j
\]

for each bundle coordinate `t`; the whole vector `(M_{i,t})_(t in T)` is measurable from

\[
B,(R_j,P_j)_{j\ne i}
\]

and is independent of `(P_i,R_i)`.

Because `P_i` is a fair bit independent of `R_i`, the bit

\[
U_i=P_i\oplus\rho_i(R_i)
\]

is itself fair and independent of `R_i`. In fact the pair `(U_i,R_i)` has product law: for any admissible `r_i` and `u`, exactly one value of `P_i` gives `U_i=u`, with probability `1/2`.

Now

\[
(C_t)_{t\in T}=(M_{i,t}\oplus U_i)_{t\in T}
\]

is a measurable function of `U_i` and variables independent of `R_i`. Hence the sigma-field on the left of (2.3) is independent of `R_i`. For countable `T`, finite-dimensional independence extends to the generated sigma-field by the monotone-class theorem. QED.

### Why this is directly relevant to Bradley

For `d=2`, take `r=2`. One pad can be assigned to the omitted vertical separator layer and one to the omitted horizontal separator layer. Formula (2.2) shows that the **same binary output bundle** can be exactly blind to the right-side summary through the vertical pad and exactly blind to the upper-side summary through the horizontal pad. There is no algebraic need to enlarge the output alphabet merely to make the two directional masks coexist.

Therefore the next obstruction cannot simply say “horizontal and vertical one-time-pad actions are incompatible on a finite alphabet.” They are compatible already on the two-point alphabet.

## 3. Finite-group version: commuting actions give simultaneous exact masking

The same point is not peculiar to XOR.

### Lemma 3.1 — commuting-action multi-cut masking

Let finite groups `G_1,...,G_r` act on a finite set `A` by actions that commute pairwise. Let `P_i` be uniform on `G_i`, mutually independent and independent of mutually independent source blocks `B,R_1,...,R_r`. Let

\[
\rho_i(R_i)\in G_i,
\qquad
U_i:=P_i\rho_i(R_i).
\]

For any finite or countable family `a_t(B) in A`, define

\[
C_t:=(U_1\cdots U_r)\cdot a_t(B),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where the commuting actions make the order irrelevant. Then, for each `i`, the sigma-field generated by `B`, all `(R_j,P_j)` with `j ne i`, and the full bundle `(C_t)_t` is independent of `R_i`.

### Proof

For fixed `i`, `U_i=P_i rho_i(R_i)` is uniform on `G_i` and independent of `R_i`, because right multiplication by the fixed element `rho_i(r_i)` permutes `G_i`. All other acting variables and the base profile are independent of `R_i`. Therefore the entire bundle is a measurable function of variables independent of `R_i`. QED.

### Concrete nonabelian two-cut realization

Take a finite group `G` and let the output alphabet be `A=G`. Let one copy of `G` act by left multiplication and another by right multiplication. These two regular actions commute:

\[
L_g(R_h(x))=gxh=R_h(L_g(x)).
\]

Hence a crossing bundle may be written

\[
C_t=U_{\rm vert}\,a_t(B)\,U_{\rm horiz}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The vertical opposite-side summary can be absorbed into the common left factor and the horizontal opposite-side summary into the common right factor. Both are exactly one-time-padded. Taking, for example, `G=S_3` gives a genuinely nonabelian six-state local gadget. Taking `G=Z/2Z` reduces to Lemma 2.1 and shows that even two states suffice at the single-bundle compatibility level.

This positively resolves the **local two-cut compatibility subproblem** posed at the end of the preceding report. It does not yet assemble a stationary Bradley field.

## 4. Infinite fanout survives the earlier finite-disagreement obstruction

The bundle gadget is also compatible with the necessary reverse-influence condition discovered in earlier runs.

Take `T` infinite in Lemma 2.1. Refresh one separator pad `P_i` and leave every other source variable unchanged. Then `U_i` flips, hence

\[
C_t' = C_t\oplus1
\quad\text{for every }t\in T.
\]

Thus the single pad refresh changes infinitely many output coordinates deterministically if those bundle coordinates are realized at distinct spatial sites.

Likewise, for a regular group action in Lemma 3.1, multiplying one pad by a nonidentity group element changes every bundle coordinate. Therefore simultaneous multi-cut masking does **not** force the finite disagreement pattern that own-tail determinism forbids.

The local gadget consequently passes both tests that had been separately problematic:

1. exact cut blindness in more than one direction;
2. infinite reverse fanout under a pivotal separator-pad refresh.

The remaining difficulty is global assembly, not the crossing action itself.

## 5. Second new result: a general influence theorem for stationary factors of iid

The previous reports had several geometry-specific versions of the following phenomenon. The right abstraction is total resampling influence, which removes all metric/coding-radius assumptions.

Let `(U_v)_(v in Z^d)` be iid random variables on a standard Borel space. Let

\[
X=F(U)\in A^{\mathbb Z^d}
\]

be a measurable shift-equivariant factor with finite output alphabet `A`. For `z in Z^d`, let `U^{(z)}` denote the source obtained by replacing only `U_z` by an independent fresh copy, and define the origin resampling influence

\[
I_z:=\Pr\{F(U)_0\ne F(U^{(z)})_0\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — nondegenerate own-tail factors require infinite total resampling influence

If `X` is determined by its own full spatial tail and is nondegenerate, then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^d} I_z=\infty.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently, no nonconstant own-tail factor of iid can have summable single-source-site resampling influences.

### Proof

Refresh only the source variable at the origin and write

\[
X'=F(U^{(0)}),
\qquad
D:=\{k\in\mathbb Z^d:X_k\ne X'_k\}.
\]

Because the refreshed source has the same iid law, `X'` has exactly the same marginal field law as `X`.

By shift equivariance,

\[
\Pr(k\in D)
=
\Pr\{F(U)_k\ne F(U^{(0)})_k\}
=I_{-k}
\]

(up to the harmless shift-sign convention). Therefore Tonelli gives the exact identity

\[
\mathbb E|D|
=
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}\Pr(k\in D)
=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^d}I_z.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Assume the sum is finite. Then `|D|<infinity` almost surely.

Own-tail determinism has the following coupling rigidity property, already established in this campaign: two copies with the same marginal law cannot differ on a finite nonempty set. Indeed, on the event that their disagreement lies in a finite box `B`, the two fields agree on `B^c`; but every coordinate in `B` is measurable from the exterior `B^c` under the common tail decoder, so the two values must agree there as well. Hence

\[
\Pr(0<|D|<\infty)=0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since `|D|<infinity` almost surely under the summability assumption, (5.4) forces `D=emptyset` almost surely. Thus refreshing `U_0` never changes the output field.

Translation gives the same statement for refreshing any single source coordinate. In particular every `I_z=0`. A measurable function of an iid product that is almost surely invariant under refreshing each individual coordinate is measurable with respect to the iid source tail: invariance under every finite coordinate modification puts it in the intersection of the sigma-fields generated off finite sets. Kolmogorov's zero-one law makes that tail trivial. Therefore `X_0` is almost surely constant, contradicting nondegeneracy.

Hence the sum in (5.2) must diverge. QED.

### Stronger coupling conclusion

For a nonconstant own-tail factor, whenever a source-site refresh is genuinely pivotal,

\[
\Pr(|D|=\infty)>0,
\qquad
\Pr(0<|D|<\infty)=0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus a positive-probability source perturbation cannot create a merely large finite avalanche; it must create an infinite one.

## 6. Why Theorem 5.1 materially narrows bundle assembly

Theorem 5.1 is strictly geometry-free. It does not assume:

- a finite coding radius;
- a moment bound on a coding radius;
- an SFT/Markov support;
- a group law;
- finite-distance bundle edges;
- a linear or monotone output rule.

It only uses iid source refreshes, stationarity/equivariance, and summability of the actual change probabilities.

Consequently the following broad implementation classes are excluded for a tail-deterministic nondegenerate output:

1. an explicit finite number of iid pad/ciphertext slots per output site;
2. any stationary routing in which the origin output has finite expected number of pivotal iid source pads;
3. arbitrary long-range but summably sensitive nonlinear aggregates;
4. finite-range coding as a special case;
5. heavy-tailed geometric coding whenever its actual resampling influences are nevertheless summable.

This is stronger than the earlier finite-expected-coding-volume statement because enormous or infinite coding radius by itself is irrelevant: what matters is the **sum of pivotal refresh probabilities**.

For a successful iid-driven Bradley construction the influence graph must therefore be infinite in both directions in the mass-transport sense:

- refreshing one pivotal positive-density source pad must alter infinitely many outputs with positive probability;
- the output at a typical site must have nonsummable total sensitivity to the positive-density source pad field.

The first requirement was already visible from reverse fanout. The second is the new symmetric obstruction supplied by (5.3).

## 7. Explicit finite-slot infinite-bundle schemes are therefore impossible

A natural way to try to globalize Lemma 2.1 is to assign finitely many bundle-ciphertext slots to each lattice site, while every separator pad owns an infinite bundle of such slots elsewhere. Stationarity prevents this architecture when the bundle pads have positive spatial intensity.

Suppose, for example, that there are `M<infinity` explicit ciphertext slots per output site and each slot is a function of one designated iid separator pad together with same-side data. Then the origin output is directly sensitive to at most `M` designated pads, so

\[
\sum_z I_z\le M
\]

(up to replacing `M` by the finite expected number of genuinely pivotal designated pads). Theorem 5.1 forces degeneracy.

Equivalently, a mass-transport reading says that a positive-density field of pads cannot each own infinitely many distinct explicit finite-capacity output slots while each output carries only finitely many slots: finite incoming slot capacity gives finite mean outgoing incidence. Infinite fanout can survive only if the finite output symbol is a **genuine infinite-arity aggregate** of infinitely many bundle contributions, not a finite packed list of ciphertexts.

This is an important distinction. Lemma 2.1 says multi-cut one-time-pad algebra is locally compatible. Theorem 5.1 says that a full tail-deterministic stationary realization cannot simply tile space with finitely many such gadgets per site.

## 9. Failed constructions tested in this run

### 9.1 Dense row/column mask grid

The most obvious globalization of the commuting-action gadget is a rank-one grid, for example in the binary case

\[
X_{i,j}=A_{i+j}\oplus B_{i-j}
\]

or in a group form `X_(i,j)=U_(i+j) a_(i,j) V_(i-j)`. Without additional fresh noise, finite rectangle/plaquette invariants cancel the masks and create visible cross-halfspace relations; with iid uniform fresh noise at every site, the output becomes too close to iid and the tail reconstruction disappears. This does not yield a Bradley field.

This failure is instructive: infinite fanout indexed by zero-density diagonal families avoids the positive-density mass-transport count, but the shared diagonal masks themselves cross both members of Bradley-separated halfspaces and create recoverable global relations.

### 9.2 Finite number of bundle slots per site

This route is now ruled out rigorously by Theorem 5.1 rather than merely diagnosed. Any such positive-density iid-pad realization has summable origin influence and therefore cannot be both nondegenerate and own-tail deterministic.

## 10. Remaining gap and next attack

The surviving positive architecture is now much narrower and more explicit.

A full construction must combine:

1. the multi-cut binary/commuting-action mask of Lemma 2.1 or 3.1;
2. infinite reverse fanout for each pivotal separator pad;
3. **nonsummable forward influence** at each output, as forced by Theorem 5.1;
4. no positive-entropy global phase or diagonal latent visible from both members of any Bradley-separated halfspace pair;
5. enough remote mask-free bundle differences to kill every wrong finite filling and hence recover every finite erasure.

The next useful mathematical subproblem is no longer local two-cut compatibility; that is solved positively. It is an **infinite-arity aggregator problem**:

> Construct, or rule out, a finite-valued measurable aggregator of infinitely many commuting-action bundle masks which has nonsummable total resampling influence, is exactly cut-blind in each coordinate direction, and still leaves a countable family of pad-canceling relations from which every finite erasure is uniquely reconstructible.

A promising concrete test class is binary. One seeks a measurable Boolean aggregator `F` of infinitely many bundle contributions with

\[
\sum_z \Pr(F(U)\ne F(U^{(z)}))=\infty,
\]

while every Bradley halfspace sees each opposite-side source only through at least one fresh omitted-layer XOR mask. Weighted-threshold functions with square-summable but non-absolutely-summable coefficients meet the infinite-influence side in suitable anti-concentrated regimes, but an exact halfspace-independence proof for a stationary spatial assembly is still missing.

On the negative side, the most valuable next lemma would show that exact Bradley cut-blindness forces the total influence of any positive-density iid separator field to be summable at the output. Together with Theorem 5.1 that would exclude the entire iid-driven bundle program. No such implication is proved here.

The original Bradley problem therefore remains open, but the construction boundary moved: **crossing finite-alphabet masks are possible; the true obstruction is global infinite-arity assembly with bidirectionally infinite influence.**
