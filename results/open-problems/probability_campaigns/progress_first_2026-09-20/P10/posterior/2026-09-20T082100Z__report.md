## 3. Exact interaction decomposition

Write

\[
E(a)=\frac12F(a)-F(a/\sqrt2),
\]

and

\[
J(a,b)=K_*(p(a),p(b))-F(a/\sqrt2)-F(b/\sqrt2).
\]

Then exactly

\[
\boxed{H(a,b)=E(a)+E(b)-J(a,b).}
\]

The new useful fact is that `J` has a removable `a^2b^2` factor with a tight exact representation.

Let `r=p(a/sqrt(2))`, `s` be a relative permutation of `p(b/sqrt(2))`, `Z=r\cdot s`, and `t_i=r_i s_i/Z`. For the KL part,

\[
3Z D(t\|u)
=3\sum_i r_i s_i\{\log(3r_i)+\log(3s_i)-\log(3Z)\}.
\]

Averaging over the six relative permutations makes the first two pieces exactly `D(r||u)+D(s||u)`. There are two aligned permutations with

\[
3Z_A=1+ab
\]

and four misaligned permutations with

\[
3Z_M=1-ab/2.
\]

Therefore the KL interaction is

\[
J_D(a,b)=-g(ab),
\]

where

\[
g(t)=\frac13(1+t)\log(1+t)+\frac23(1-t/2)\log(1-t/2).
\]

Exact algebra for the cubic/quartic correction gives

\[
J_P(a,b)=a^2b^2\,\frac{P(a,b)}{72(ab-2)^3(ab+1)^3},
\]

where `P` is a 42-term polynomial over `Q(sqrt(2))`; its exact coefficient table is stored in the certificate. Thus

\[
\boxed{
J(a,b)=a^2b^2\,\widehat J(a,b),\qquad
\widehat J(a,b)=\frac{P(a,b)}{72(ab-2)^3(ab+1)^3}-\frac{g(ab)}{a^2b^2}.
}
\]

The last quotient is removable because

\[
\frac{g(t)}{t^2}=\sum_{n\ge2}
\frac{(-1)^n+2^{1-n}}{3n(n-1)}t^{n-2}.
\]

This representation is much tighter than direct interval evaluation of the Bayes-normalized kernel.

## 4. Exact edge-gap normalization and monotonicity

Directly,

\[
F(a)=\frac{1+2a}{3}\log(1+2a)
+\frac{2(1-a)}3\log(1-a)
+\frac{a^3}{3}-\frac{2a^4}{9}.
\]

The cubic correction cancels the entropy cubic term, so

\[
F(a)=a^2+\frac5{18}a^4+
\sum_{n\ge5}
\frac{(-1)^n2^n+2}{3n(n-1)}a^n.
\]

Hence

\[
E(a)=a^4\widehat E(a),
\]

with removable series

\[
\widehat E(a)=\sum_{n\ge4} f_n\bigl(\tfrac12-2^{-n/2}\bigr)a^{n-4},
\]

where `f_4=5/18` and `f_n=((-1)^n2^n+2)/(3n(n-1))` for `n>=5`. In particular `widehat E(0)=5/72`.

For the interval boxes away from the origin I also used a monotonicity consequence of the previously proved global edge theorem. Let

\[
G(a)=F'(a)/a\qquad(a\ne0).
\]

The prior ray proof gives `F'''(a)>=0` for `a>0` and `F'''(a)<=0` for `a<0`. Thus `F''` increases as one moves away from zero on either ray, and `G(a)`, being the average of `F''` between zero and `a`, also increases with `|a|` on each ray. Since

\[
E'(a)=\frac a2\left(G(a)-G(a/\sqrt2)\right),
\]

`E` is increasing on `[0,1]` and decreasing as `a` increases on `[-1/2,0]`. Therefore an interval lower bound for `E` is obtained exactly from the endpoint closest to zero.

## 5. Local Cauchy certificate and global adaptive certificate

Near the axes the direct expression `E(a)+E(b)-J(a,b)` has quartic cancellation. On boxes inside

\[
|a|,|b|\le1/20,
\]

the certificate instead checks the stronger normalized inequality

\[
\widehat J(a,b)\le2\sqrt{\widehat E(a)\widehat E(b)}.
\]

Because

\[
J=a^2b^2\widehat J,
\qquad E(a)=a^4\widehat E(a),
\]

this implies

\[
J(a,b)\le2\sqrt{E(a)E(b)}\le E(a)+E(b)
\]

by AM--GM. The local `widehat E` series is truncated with the explicit tail bound

\[
\left|\text{tail after }N\right|
\le
\frac{4(2r)^{N-3}}{N(N+1)(1-2r)},

The removable `g(t)/t^2` series is likewise enclosed by

\[
\left|\text{tail after }N\right|

\frac{r^{N-1}}{2N(N+1)(1-r)}.

## 7. Remaining gap and next attack

The immediate target is now the full arbitrary-orbit kernel inequality

\[
K_*(p,q)\le\frac{\Psi(p)+\Psi(q)}2
\qquad(p,q\in\Delta_2).
\]

Use the exact complex magnetization coordinate

\[
m=\sum_{k=0}^2p_k\omega^k,
\]

under which `S_3` acts dihedrally and the two-equal families are precisely the reflection axes. The next attack is to reduce each orbit representative to a fundamental Weyl sector and prove an **angular extremality theorem**: for fixed radial data, the KS gap is minimized on a reflection axis. If true, the theorem proved here would immediately lift to the whole simplex. Concretely:

1. parameterize `p` and `q` by `(r,theta)` and `(s,phi)` in a fundamental dihedral sector;
2. express the orbit-averaged gap in the basic `S_3` invariants `|m|^2` and `Re(m^3)` plus the entropy term;
3. differentiate in the angular invariants and test whether every interior angular critical point is a maximum/saddle rather than a minimum;
4. if angular extremality fails, isolate the first rigorous three-distinct counterexample and use it as the seed for the complementary below-KS reconstruction/fixed-point route rather than retuning another one-dimensional Lyapunov.

A proof of the global arbitrary-orbit kernel inequality, together with the already-proved global edge contraction, would be the first route in this track capable of reaching the binary ferromagnetic KS boundary globally rather than locally. It would still require the standard recursion/limit argument and independent review before being represented as full source closure.
