\[

Proof is elementary and piecewise only on the sign of `alpha+beta t^2`.

## 5. New finite-support theorem: two-orbit searches are impossible in general

The previous run showed only that the particular interior seed `p_int` cannot lie in an exactly closed two-orbit law at `lambda=0.706`. The correct statement is much stronger and does not depend on that seed.

Let `D_lambda` be the exact binary density-evolution map on an `S_3`-symmetric posterior law. For a child posterior `p`, write

\[
r=T_\lambda p=u+\lambda(p-u).
\]

For a relative permutation `sigma`, fusion produces

\[
F_{\lambda,\sigma}(p,q)_i
=
\frac{r_i\,s_{\sigma(i)}}{\sum_j r_j s_{\sigma(j)}}.
\]

For `0<lambda<1`, all edge coordinates are positive, so every relative-permutation class arising from a pair of positive-mass support orbits receives positive output mass. Hence a fixed law must be closed under all these orbit outputs.

### Lemma 5.1 — a three-distinct orbit produces five distinct self-fusion orbits

Suppose `p` has three distinct coordinates. Then `r=T_lambda p` also has three distinct positive coordinates; write them in decreasing order `a>b>c>0`.

- Identity relative permutation gives the orbit of `{a^2,b^2,c^2}`.
- Either 3-cycle gives the same orbit of `{ab,bc,ca}`.
- The three transpositions give the three two-equal orbits
  `{a^2,bc,bc}`, `{b^2,ac,ac}`, `{c^2,ab,ab}`.

The identity and 3-cycle outputs are distinct: their max/min ratios are `(a/c)^2` and `a/c`, respectively.

The three transposition outputs are pairwise distinct because their singleton/repeated ratios are

\[
R_a=\frac{a^2}{bc},\qquad
R_b=\frac{b^2}{ac},\qquad
R_c=\frac{c^2}{ab},
\]

and, for example, `R_a/R_b=(a/b)^3 !=1`; similarly for the other pairs. They are also distinct from the identity/cycle outputs because they have a repeated coordinate (or, in a degenerate geometric case, are uniform) while the identity/cycle outputs remain three-distinct.

Therefore one genuinely three-distinct support orbit forces **five distinct output `S_3` orbits** under its self-pair event.

### Lemma 5.2 — a nonuniform two-equal orbit forces two distinct two-equal outputs

Write a nonuniform two-equal posterior as

\[
p(x)=\left(\frac{1+2x}{3},\frac{1-x}{3},\frac{1-x}{3}\right),\qquad x\ne0.
\]

After the edge its distinguished/repeated ratio is

\[
R_\lambda(x)=\frac{1+2\lambda x}{1-\lambda x}.
\]

Aligned self-fusion has ratio `R_lambda(x)^2`, while a misaligned relative distinguished coordinate has ratio `1/R_lambda(x)`. These two orbits are distinct unless `R_lambda(x)^3=1`, which would force `x=0`. Thus any fixed law containing a nonuniform two-equal orbit must contain at least two distinct nonuniform two-equal orbit classes.

### Lemma 5.3 — two distinct two-equal orbits force a three-distinct output

The map `x -> R_lambda(x)` is strictly monotone for `lambda>0`:

\[
R_\lambda'(x)=\frac{3\lambda}{(1-\lambda x)^2}>0.
\]

Hence two distinct two-equal orbits have different edge ratios. Write their edge representatives as `(a,b,b)` and `(c,d,d)` with `a/b != c/d`. For a misaligned relative distinguished coordinate, the three unnormalized fused coordinates are

\[
(ad,\,bc,\,bd).
\]

They are all distinct: `ad=bc` would give `a/b=c/d`; `ad=bd` would give `a=b`; and `bc=bd` would give `c=d`. Therefore the cross event forces a genuinely three-distinct output orbit.

Combining the lemmas gives the new theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{For every }0<\lambda<1,
\text{ any nontrivial finite-support }S_3\text{-symmetric fixed posterior law}
\text{ for binary density evolution has at least five }S_3\text{ orbit classes.}
}
\]

Indeed, if the support already contains a three-distinct orbit, Lemma 5.1 forces at least five classes. If it contains only uniform/two-equal orbits, nontriviality gives a nonuniform two-equal orbit; Lemma 5.2 forces a second distinct two-equal class, and Lemma 5.3 then forces a three-distinct class, after which Lemma 5.1 applies.

This is a strict generalization of the previous `p_int`-specific two-orbit closure obstruction. It rules out **all** one-, two-, three-, and four-orbit exact reconstruction fixed-law ansatzes throughout the unresolved ferromagnetic interval. A finite-support reconstruction construction must begin with at least five orbit classes (and may well require more).
