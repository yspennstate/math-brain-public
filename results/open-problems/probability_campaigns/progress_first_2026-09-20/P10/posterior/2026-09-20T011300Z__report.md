## 4. New structural lemma: a cubic-corrected divergence has the exact KS edge contraction

The plain KL barrier is not the edge channel itself: it comes from finite-amplitude skewness of KL. There is a simple `S_3`-invariant correction that removes the cubic skew exactly.

Define

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(p):=D(p\|u)+\frac32\sum_{i=1}^3\left(p_i-\frac13\right)^3.
}
\]

### Lemma 2 (global edge contraction)
For every probability vector `p` on three symbols and every `0<=lambda<=1`,

\[
\boxed{\Phi(T_\lambda p)\le \lambda^2\Phi(p).}
\]

Also `Phi(p)>=0`, with equality only at `p=u`.

This contraction factor is exactly the local quadratic/Kesten--Stigum factor, globally across the whole simplex.

### Proof
Write `p=u+z`, `sum_i z_i=0`, and

\[
\phi(x)=x\log(3x)+\frac32\left(x-\frac13\right)^3,
\qquad
h(t)=\sum_i\phi\left(\frac13+t z_i\right).
\]

Then `h(0)=h'(0)=0`, `h(1)=Phi(p)` and `h(lambda)=Phi(T_lambda p)`. Direct differentiation gives

\[
\phi''(x)=\frac1x+9x-3,
\qquad
\phi'''(x)=9-\frac1{x^2}.
\]

First,

\[
\phi''(x)\ge 3
\]

for `x>0` by `1/x+9x>=6`, with equality only at `x=1/3`. Therefore `h''(t)>=0`, and `Phi>=0`.

Second,

\[
h'''(t)=\sum_i z_i^3\left(9-\frac1{(1/3+t z_i)^2}\right).
\]

For each coordinate, `z_i` has the same sign as `(1/3+t z_i)-1/3`; and `9-1/x^2` has exactly the same sign as `x-1/3`. Hence every summand in `h'''(t)` is nonnegative. Thus `h''(t)` is nondecreasing on `[0,1]`.

Using `h(0)=h'(0)=0`,

\[
\frac{h(t)}{t^2}=\int_0^1(1-s)h''(ts)\,ds.
\]

Since `h''` is nondecreasing, for `0<=lambda<=1`,

\[
\frac{h(\lambda)}{\lambda^2}
\le
\int_0^1(1-s)h''(s)\,ds
=h(1),
\]

which is the desired inequality.

### Why this matters
The Taylor expansion of KL around the uniform posterior has a cubic term that is responsible for the finite-amplitude edge contraction coefficient exceeding `lambda^2`. The added `+(3/2) sum (p_i-1/3)^3` cancels exactly that cubic term and, more strongly, gives the global inequality above. Therefore the edge step itself can be made **KS-sharp** by a natural low-order `S_3`-invariant Lyapunov correction.

## 5. The remaining obstruction is fusion, not the edge

One cannot simply replace KL by `Phi` in the old proof and reuse unconditional two-child subadditivity. That property is special to mutual information/KL.

There is an exact Bayes-plausible counterexample. Let the posterior law be uniform over the three permutations of

\[
p=(1/2,1/2,0).
\]

Its barycenter is `u`, so it is realizable as a posterior experiment. Write

\[
A=\Phi(1/2,1/2,0)=\log(3/2)-\frac1{24},
\]

and

\[
B=\Phi(1,0,0)=\log3+\frac13.
\]

Combining two independent copies conditioned on the same hidden root, equal excluded states occur with the appropriate joint weight and give posterior `(1/2,1/2,0)`, while distinct excluded states give a point mass. The resulting expected parent functional is

\[
\frac{A+B}{2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{A+B}{2}-2A
=
\frac12\log\frac89+\frac{11}{48}>0.
\]

Indeed `log(9/8)<1/8` gives the lower bound `>1/6`. So a universal fusion inequality `E Phi(parent)<=2 E Phi(child)` is false.

This failure is useful: the new functional has solved the **edge** side exactly, and the sole remaining question is whether the Potts-edge restriction plus permutation symmetry is strong enough to make the **two-child orbit-averaged fusion kernel** contract below KS.

## 6. Precise next inequality

For a symmetric posterior law, decompose it into `S_3` permutation orbits. For orbit representatives `p,q`, put

\[
r=T_\lambda p,\qquad s_\sigma=T_\lambda(\sigma q),
\]

and define the orbit-averaged two-child kernel

\[
K_\lambda(p,q)
=
\frac16\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}
3(r\cdot s_\sigma)
\,\Phi\!\left(
\frac{r\odot s_\sigma}{r\cdot s_\sigma}
\right).
\]

For any symmetric posterior law `nu`, the next-level expected `Phi` is the quadratic form `double integral K_lambda(p,q) dnu(p)dnu(q)`. Thus a global inequality of the form

\[
K_\lambda(p,q)
\le \frac{\rho_\lambda}{2}\bigl(\Phi(p)+\Phi(q)\bigr),
\qquad \rho_\lambda<1,
\]

would immediately prove binary-tree non-reconstruction at that `lambda`. Near the uniform point the sharp linearized value is `rho_lambda=2 lambda^2`, so this formulation retains the correct KS boundary instead of the plain-KL barrier.
