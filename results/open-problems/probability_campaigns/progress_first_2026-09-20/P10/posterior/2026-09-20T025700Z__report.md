## 2. New functional

Put `u=(1/3,1/3,1/3)` and `z_i=p_i-1/3`. Define

\[
\boxed{
\Psi(p):=D(p\|u)+\frac32\sum_{i=1}^3 z_i^3-\sum_{i=1}^3 z_i^4.
}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
\psi(x)=x\log(3x)+\frac32(x-1/3)^3-(x-1/3)^4,
\]

we have `Psi(p)=sum_i psi(p_i)`.

The quartic term is not cosmetic. Plain KL cannot support a pointwise two-child kernel proof beyond its already certified edge barrier, because if the second child is uninformative then the two-child kernel reduces exactly to the one-edge KL ratio. A successful pointwise Lyapunov therefore needs a one-edge functional that is already globally KS-sharp. The cubic correction achieved that; the quartic correction below preserves it while changing the finite-amplitude fusion geometry.

## 3. Theorem 1 — global KS-sharp one-edge contraction survives the quartic correction

### Statement
For every probability vector `p` on three symbols and every `0<=lambda<=1`,

\[
\boxed{\Psi(T_\lambda p)\le \lambda^2\Psi(p).}
\]

Moreover `Psi(p)>=0`, with equality only at `p=u`.

### Proof
Write `p=u+z`, `sum_i z_i=0`, and

\[
h(t)=\Psi(u+t z)=\sum_i\psi(1/3+t z_i),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]

Then `h(0)=h'(0)=0`, `h(1)=Psi(p)`, and `h(lambda)=Psi(T_lambda p)`.

First,

\[
\psi''(x)=\frac1x+9x-3-12(x-1/3)^2.
\]

A direct factorization gives

\[
\psi''(x)-\frac53
=\frac{(1-x)(12x^2-5x+1)}x.
\]

For `0<x<=1`, this is nonnegative: `1-x>=0`, `x>0`, and the quadratic `12x^2-5x+1` is strictly positive because its discriminant is `25-48=-23`. Hence

\[
\psi''(x)\ge\frac53,
\qquad
h''(t)=\sum_i z_i^2\psi''(1/3+t z_i)\ge\frac53\sum_i z_i^2.
\]

This already proves `Psi>=0`, strictly away from `u`.

It remains to prove that `h''` is nondecreasing. For `t>0`, set `x_i=1/3+t z_i`; then `sum_i x_i=1` and

\[
t^3h'''(t)=\sum_i F(x_i),
\]

where

\[
F(x):=(x-1/3)^3\psi'''(x)
=-\frac{(3x-1)^4(8x^2-3x-1)}{27x^2}.
\]

Let

\[
\alpha=\frac{3+\sqrt{41}}{16},
\]

the positive root of `8x^2-3x-1`. If all `x_i<=alpha`, then every `F(x_i)>=0`, hence `h'''(t)>=0`.

Suppose instead that one coordinate, say `a=x_1`, exceeds `alpha`. Then the other two satisfy

\[
x_2+x_3=1-a<1-\alpha<5/12.
\]

On `(0,5/12]`, `F` is convex. Indeed

\[
F''(x)
=-\frac{2(3x-1)^2}{9x^4}
\bigl(144x^4-27x^3-9x^2-3x-1\bigr),
\]

and the quartic in parentheses is negative on `[0,5/12]`. One exact way to see this is to put `x=(5/12)y`; its degree-four Bernstein coefficients on `0<=y<=1` are

\[
-1,\ -\frac{21}{16},\ -\frac{181}{96},\ -\frac{821}{256},\ -\frac{821}{576},
\]

all strictly negative.

Therefore Jensen gives

\[
F(x_2)+F(x_3)\ge2F((1-a)/2).
\]

A direct simplification yields

\[
F(a)+2F((1-a)/2)
=-\frac{(3a-1)^4Q(a)}{108a^2(1-a)^2},
\]

where

\[
Q(a)=36a^4-81a^3+51a^2-4a-4.
\]

The polynomial `Q` is strictly negative on `[0,1]`; for example, its degree-eight Bernstein coefficients are

\[
-4,-\frac92,-\frac{89}{28},-\frac{83}{56},-\frac{12}{35},-\frac5{28},-\frac{25}{28},-\frac{15}{8},-2,
\]

again all negative. Hence `sum_i F(x_i)>=0`, so `h'''(t)>=0` throughout the interior; boundary points follow by continuity. Thus `h''` is nondecreasing.

Finally, since `h(0)=h'(0)=0`,

\[
\frac{h(t)}{t^2}=\int_0^1(1-s)h''(ts)\,ds.
\]

For `0<=lambda<=1`, monotonicity of `h''` implies

\[
\frac{h(\lambda)}{\lambda^2}
\le\int_0^1(1-s)h''(s)\,ds=h(1),
\]

which proves `Psi(T_lambda p)<=lambda^2 Psi(p)`.

## 4. Theorem 2 — the orbit-averaged two-child kernel has strictly favorable quartic drift at KS

Let

\[
\lambda_*=1/\sqrt2.
\]

For orbit representatives `p,q`, put `r=T_{lambda_*}p` and `s_sigma=T_{lambda_*}(sigma q)`, and define the `Psi` fusion kernel

\[
K_*(p,q)=\frac16\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}
3(r\cdot s_\sigma)
\Psi\left(\frac{r\odot s_\sigma}{r\cdot s_\sigma}\right).
\]

Define the KS fusion gap

\[
H(p,q)=\frac12\bigl(\Psi(p)+\Psi(q)\bigr)-K_*(p,q).
\]

### Statement
If `p=u+x`, `q=u+y`, `sum_i x_i=sum_i y_i=0`, and

\[
A=\sum_i x_i^2,\qquad B=\sum_i y_i^2,
\]

then, as `(x,y)->(0,0)`,

\[
\boxed{
H(u+x,u+y)
=\frac1{32}\bigl(5A^2-2AB+5B^2\bigr)
+O((\|x\|+\|y\|)^5).
}
\]

Since

\[
5A^2-2AB+5B^2\ge4(A^2+B^2),
\]

there exists `delta>0` such that every nontrivial pair with `||x||+||y||<delta` satisfies

\[
K_*(u+x,u+y)<\frac12\bigl(\Psi(u+x)+\Psi(u+y)\bigr).
\]

Thus the quartic-corrected functional has a **strict local two-child contraction at the exact KS point**, not merely local linear stability.

### Proof of the expansion
The Taylor expansion of KL at the uniform point is

\[
D(u+x\|u)
=\frac32S_2-\frac32S_3+\frac94S_4+O(\|x\|^5),
\]

where `S_k=sum_i x_i^k`. Therefore the cubic term in `Psi` cancels and

\[
\Psi(u+x)=\frac32S_2+\frac54S_4+O(\|x\|^5).
\]

For three coordinates with `sum_i x_i=0`, the exact identity `S_4=S_2^2/2` gives

\[
\Psi(u+x)=\frac32A+\frac58A^2+O(\|x\|^5).
\]

The kernel is invariant under independent permutations of the coordinates of `x` and `y`, and symmetric under interchange of the two children. Consequently its fusion gap has no mixed degree-one invariant, and the quartic part must have the form

\[
c(A^2+B^2)+dAB.
\]

There is no cubic contribution: the only separate `S_3`-invariant cubics are pure in one child, and those vanish in the KS one-child gap because the local expansion of `Psi` has no cubic term.

To determine `c`, set `y=0`. The second child is then uninformative, so

\[
K_*(p,u)=\Psi(T_{\lambda_*}p).
\]

Using `lambda_*^2=1/2` and `lambda_*^4=1/4`,

\[
H(u+x,u)=\frac5{32}A^2+O(\|x\|^5),
\]

hence `c=5/32`.

To determine `d`, take

\[
x=y=t(2,-1,-1)/3,
\qquad A=B=\frac23t^2.
\]

There are only two relative permutation classes: the distinguished coordinate is aligned (weight `1/3`) or misaligned (weight `2/3`). Writing `a=lambda_* t`, the corresponding Bayes normalizers are exactly `3(r·r)=1+2a^2=1+t^2` and `3(r·sigma r)=1-a^2=1-t^2/2`. Expanding the two resulting posterior orbits gives

\[
\Psi(u+t(2,-1,-1)/3)
=t^2+\frac5{18}t^4+O(t^5),
\]

and

\[
K_*(u+tv,u+tv)
=t^2+\frac16t^4+O(t^5).
\]

Thus

\[
H(u+tv,u+tv)=\frac19t^4+O(t^5).
\]

Substituting `A=B=2t^2/3` into `c(A^2+B^2)+dAB` with `c=5/32` gives `d=-1/16`, proving the displayed expansion.

## 5. What this does and does not prove

This is **not** a full binary-tree non-reconstruction proof up to KS, and it is not substituted for a global contraction by invoking local stability. The source-valid proved endpoint remains `0.70237785`.

What changed is the obstruction. For plain KL, even the one-edge pointwise inequality fails before KS. For the previous cubic `Phi`, the one-edge step is KS-sharp but unconditional fusion fails at finite amplitude. For the new `Psi`, the one-edge step is still globally KS-sharp **and the exact orbit-averaged two-child kernel is strictly favorable in a whole neighborhood of the trivial posterior at KS**. Therefore any failure of this Lyapunov route must be a finite-amplitude failure away from `u`.

A non-certifying numerical diagnostic during this run did not find such a finite-amplitude kernel counterexample for several `lambda` values between `0.704` and the KS point; that observation receives no theorem credit and no source claim.

## 6. Exact next obstruction and next attack

The next target is now the compact finite-amplitude inequality

\[
\boxed{
K_*(p,q)\le\frac12\bigl(\Psi(p)+\Psi(q)\bigr)
\quad\text{for all }p,q\in\Delta_2.
}
\]

The local neighborhood of `(u,u)` is already handled by Theorem 2, so the next run should attack only its compact complement. A useful exact coordinate is the complex three-state magnetization

\[
m=\sum_{k=0}^2p_k\omega^k,\qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]

If two child likelihood messages have magnetizations `m,n`, their Bayes fusion is exactly

\[
\boxed{
m'=
\frac{m+n+\bar m\bar n}
{1+m\bar n+\bar m n}.}
\]

After a Potts edge this becomes

\[
F_\lambda(m,n)=
\frac{\lambda(m+n)+\lambda^2\bar m\bar n}
{1+2\lambda^2\operatorname{Re}(m\bar n)}.
\]

The six relative permutations act by `n -> omega^j n` and `n -> omega^j conjugate(n)`. This turns the four-real-dimensional orbit average into a dihedral rational/logarithmic inequality with explicit denominators. The concrete next attack is to use these coordinates plus boundary/curvature analysis to prove the global KS kernel inequality, or else isolate a rigorous finite-amplitude counterexample. A genuine counterexample would be useful rather than terminal: its extremal orbit should seed the complementary below-KS reconstruction/fixed-point construction.
