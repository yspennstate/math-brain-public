## 2. Complex magnetization invariants

Let

\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3},\qquad m=\sum_{k=0}^2 p_k\omega^k.
\]

Then

\[
p_k=\frac{1+2\operatorname{Re}(m\omega^{-k})}{3}.
\]

Writing `m=r e^{i theta}` and `z_i=p_i-1/3`, direct root-of-unity summation gives

\[
\sum_i z_i^2=\frac23|m|^2,
\qquad
\sum_i z_i^3=\frac29\operatorname{Re}(m^3),
\qquad
\sum_i z_i^4=\frac29|m|^4.
\]

Therefore the polynomial part of the new Lyapunov is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\Psi(p(m))
=D(p(m)\|u)+\frac13\operatorname{Re}(m^3)-\frac29|m|^4.
}
\]

This makes explicit that the first nonradial `S_3` invariant is `Re(m^3)=r^3 cos(3 theta)`.

At `lambda_*=1/sqrt(2)`, if the two child orbit representatives are `m,n` and `g` is a relative dihedral permutation of the second child, the edge-normalizer and fused magnetization are

\[
3Z_g=1+\operatorname{Re}(m\overline{g n}),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
F_*(m,gn)=
\frac{(m+gn)/\sqrt2+\overline m\,\overline{gn}/2}
{1+\operatorname{Re}(m\overline{gn})}.
}
\]

Thus the arbitrary-orbit KS kernel is a six-term dihedral average of a completely explicit rational/entropy expression. No one-dimensional axis reduction is needed to state it.

## 3. New exact KL harmonic decomposition

The entropy part of the weighted fusion kernel simplifies before any interval work. Let `r=T_*p`, `s=T_*q_g`, `Z=r.s`, and `t_i=r_i s_i/Z`. Then

\[
3Z D(t\|u)
=3\sum_i r_i s_i\{\log(3r_i)+\log(3s_i)-\log(3Z)\}.
\]

Averaging over the six relative permutations makes the first two terms exactly `D(T_*p||u)+D(T_*q||u)`. Consequently the entropy interaction is

\[
\boxed{
J_D(m,n)
=-\frac16\sum_{g\in D_3}
(1+c_g)\log(1+c_g),
\qquad c_g=\operatorname{Re}(m\overline{g n}).
}
\]

For `m=r e^{i theta}`, `n=s e^{i phi}`, put `R=rs`. The six `c_g` are

\[
R\cos(\theta-\epsilon\phi-2\pi j/3),
\qquad \epsilon\in\{\pm1\},\ j=0,1,2.
\]

For every integer `k>=0`, their exact orbit moment is

\[
\boxed{
M_k=
\frac{R^k}{2^k}
\sum_{\substack{0\le \ell\le k\\3\mid(k-2\ell)}}
\binom{k}{\ell}
\cos((k-2\ell)\theta)
\cos((k-2\ell)\phi).
}
\]

Since

\[
(1+t)\log(1+t)
=t+\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{(-1)^k}{k(k-1)}t^k,
\]

this diagonalizes the entire KL interaction into `D_3` angular harmonics. In particular

\[
M_2=\frac{R^2}{2},\qquad
M_3=\frac{R^3}{4}\cos3\theta\cos3\phi,\qquad
M_4=\frac{3R^4}{8},
\]

and

\[
M_6=R^6\left(\frac5{16}+\frac1{32}\cos6\theta\cos6\phi\right).
\]

This explains why the already-proved local quartic KS deficit is angularly benign: the dangerous angular structure begins in higher harmonics after the cubic correction has been incorporated. It also gives a replacement for the failed axis-extremality idea: try to control the interaction harmonic-by-harmonic against the edge gap.

## 4. Angular extremality is rigorously false

Take fixed radius

\[
|m|=\frac12.
\]

The two reflection-axis representatives in a fundamental Weyl sector are

\[
p_0=(2/3,1/6,1/6),
\qquad
p_{60}=(1/2,1/2,0).
\]

Now take the genuinely three-distinct interior point at `theta=pi/12`:

\[
\boxed{
p_{\mathrm{int}}=
\left(
\frac{1+(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/4}{3},
\frac{1+(\sqrt2-\sqrt6)/4}{3},
\frac{1-\sqrt2/2}{3}
\right).
}
\]

All three coordinates are positive and distinct, and it has the same magnetization radius `1/2`.

For the diagonal KS deficit

\[
H_*(p)=\Psi(p)-K_*(p,p),
\]

outward-rounded interval arithmetic gives

\[
H_*(p_0)\in
[0.022578930379063444356,\,0.022578930379072825740],
\]

\[
H_*(p_{\mathrm{int}})\in
[0.021259714927154005348,\,0.021259714927165107579],
\]

and

\[
H_*(p_{60})\in
[0.056579441073263236905,\,0.056579441073271452556].
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H_*(p_{\mathrm{int}})<
\min\{H_*(p_0),H_*(p_{60})\}.
}
\]

The certified gaps are at least `0.0013192154518983367` and `0.035319726146098129` respectively. Hence the proposed theorem “for fixed radial data the KS deficit is minimized on a reflection axis” is false. This is a refutation of the proposed reduction only, not of the Potts source statement and not of the global `Psi` kernel inequality.

\[

\[

[0.021992324163703780293,\,0.021992324163714521701].

So this explicit interior orbit is **not** an immediate one-orbit reconstruction witness: its expected `Psi` contracts substantially even in the unresolved interval. This is useful negative information: a below-KS reconstruction construction, if it exists, must use a genuinely distributional mixture of distinct orbit types (or a different observable), not merely one interior `S_3` orbit copied independently to both children.
