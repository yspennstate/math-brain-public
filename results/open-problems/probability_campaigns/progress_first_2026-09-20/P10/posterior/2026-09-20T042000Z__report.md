## 2. New theorem — full diagonal two-equal orbit family is KS-contracting

For

\[
-\frac12\le a\le1,
\qquad
p(a)=\left(\frac{1+2a}{3},\frac{1-a}{3},\frac{1-a}{3}\right),
\]

let `K_*` be the exact orbit-averaged two-child kernel at

\[
\lambda_*=\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]

### Theorem
For every `a in [-1/2,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
K_*(p(a),p(a))\le \Psi(p(a)).
}
\]

Equality holds only at `a=0`, i.e. at the uniform posterior.

Equivalently, a symmetric posterior law concentrated on a single `S_3` orbit of a two-equal vector has strictly favorable two-child `Psi` drift at the exact KS point unless it is already trivial.

This is stronger than the previous local theorem: it includes arbitrary finite amplitude all the way to the simplex boundary. It is still **PARTIAL** because the actual posterior law can mix many orbits, and the full pointwise kernel inequality requires arbitrary pairs `p,q`.

## 3. Exact one-dimensional reduction

Put

\[
F(a):=\Psi(p(a)).
\]

Direct substitution gives

\[
\boxed{
F(a)=\frac{1+2a}{3}\log(1+2a)
+\frac{2(1-a)}{3}\log(1-a)
+\frac{a^3}{3}-\frac{2a^4}{9}.
}
\]

After one KS edge, the two-equal parameter is `c=a/sqrt(2)`. In the six relative permutations of the two child orbits there are exactly two classes.

### Aligned distinguished coordinates
The Bayes normalizer is

\[
Z_A=\frac{1+a^2}{3},
\]

and the fused posterior is again two-equal with parameter

\[
A(a)=\frac{\sqrt2\,a+a^2/2}{1+a^2}.
\]

### Misaligned distinguished coordinates
The Bayes normalizer is

\[
Z_M=\frac{1-a^2/2}{3},
\]

and, up to coordinate permutation, the fused posterior is two-equal with parameter

\[
M(a)=-\frac{a}{\sqrt2+a}.
\]

Two of the six permutations are aligned and four are misaligned. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_*(p(a),p(a))
=\frac{1+a^2}{3}F(A(a))
+\frac{2-a^2}{3}F(M(a)).
}
\]

Define the one-dimensional KS gap

\[
H(a):=F(a)-\frac{1+a^2}{3}F(A(a))-\frac{2-a^2}{3}F(M(a)).
\]

The theorem is exactly `H(a)>=0` on `[-1/2,1]`, with equality only at zero.

## 4. Central interval: exact Taylor/Bernstein proof

The logarithmic part of `F` has an explicit power series. After the cubic cancellation built into `Psi`,

\[
F(a)=a^2+\frac5{18}a^4+\sum_{n\ge5}c_n a^n,
\qquad
c_n=\frac{(-1)^n2^n+2}{3n(n-1)}\quad(n\ge5).
\]

Let `F_6` be the exact truncation through degree six and let `H_6` be the corresponding expression obtained from the same aligned/misaligned formula. Exact symbolic cancellation gives

\[
\frac{H_6(a)}{a^4}
=\frac{P_{23}(a)}{2880\,(a+\sqrt2)^{11}(1+a^2)^5},
\]

where `P_23` is a degree-23 polynomial over `Q(sqrt(2))`.

On `[-1/20,1/20]`, transform `P_23` to the Bernstein basis. All 24 Bernstein coefficients are **exactly positive**, and the first is the smallest; the attached symbolic certificate checks these statements in `Q(sqrt(2))`. Using only the conservative rational bounds

\[
\sqrt2<\frac32,\qquad 1+a^2\le\frac{401}{400},
\]

gives the exact lower estimate

\[
H_6(a)\ge L a^4,
\qquad
L>0.0191591241715994328.
\]

For the omitted tail, for `n>=7`,

\[
|c_n|\le \frac{65}{8064}\,2^n.
\]

On `|a|<=1/20`, also

\[
|A(a)|\le\frac{61}{40}|a|,
\qquad
|M(a)|\le\frac{20}{27}|a|.
\]

Summing the three geometric tails (the direct term, aligned term and misaligned term) yields

\[
|H(a)-H_6(a)|
\le E|a|^7,
\]

with the exact certificate giving

\[
E(1/20)^3<0.0011300885293889521.
\]

Hence on the whole central interval

\[
\boxed{
H(a)\ge
(0.0191591241715994328-0.0011300885293889521)a^4>0
}
\]

for `a!=0`, while `H(0)=0`.

This part of the certificate is symbolic/exact apart from the printed decimal renderings of already-proved positive algebraic/rational quantities.

## 6. Why this is useful

The earlier theorem only said that the KS kernel is favorable in some unspecified neighborhood of `(u,u)`. A possible failure could therefore have occurred immediately at moderate amplitude along the most symmetric one-dimensional direction.

That possibility is now removed completely for the diagonal two-equal family. In particular:

1. The whole ray from the uniform posterior through `(1/2,1/2,0)` is safe at KS, including the boundary point that gave a finite-amplitude counterexample for the earlier cubic functional.
2. The whole ferromagnetic ray from uniform to a pure posterior is safe at KS.
3. Numerical optimization had repeatedly placed the smallest normalized gap near a diagonal two-equal point around `a≈-0.22208`; this theorem certifies that entire candidate bottleneck ray rather than merely sampling it. The numerical location itself receives no theorem credit.

The proved binary broadcast endpoint **does not move** from `0.70237785`, because an arbitrary posterior law is a mixture of orbits and the quadratic recursion samples two generally different orbit representatives.

## 7. Remaining gap and next attack

The next strictly stronger target is the two-parameter family

\[
\boxed{
K_*(p(a),p(b))\le\frac{F(a)+F(b)}2
\quad\text{for all }a,b\in[-1/2,1].
}
\]

This is already much closer to the actual quadratic posterior recursion: it would handle every symmetric posterior law supported on two-equal orbits, not merely a single orbit.

There is an explicit reduction available. The aligned class has

\[
Z_A=\frac{1+ab}{3},
\qquad
A(a,b)=\frac{(a+b)/\sqrt2+ab/2}{1+ab},
\]

while the four misaligned permutations have

\[
Z_M=\frac{1-ab/2}{3}
\]

and an explicit three-coordinate posterior obtained by normalizing

\[
(1+\sqrt2 a)(1-b/\sqrt2),\quad
(1-a/\sqrt2)(1+\sqrt2 b),\quad
(1-a/\sqrt2)(1-b/\sqrt2).
\]

The next run should attack this compact two-variable inequality by the same division of labor: exact multivariate Bernstein control near `(0,0)` using the already-known positive quartic form, then curvature/interval reduction on the finite-amplitude complement. If a rigorous counterexample occurs, use its orbit pair as the seed for the complementary below-KS reconstruction/fixed-point route rather than tuning another Lyapunov blindly.

After the two-equal-pair family, the remaining full problem is the genuinely four-real-dimensional compact orbit space. The complex magnetization / dihedral representation from the previous report remains the natural coordinate system for that final step.
