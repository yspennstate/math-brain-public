The exact KS value is `lambda_*=1/sqrt(2)`. The quartic Lyapunov functional is

## 2. Theorem — arbitrary negative two-equal pair contracts at KS

For

\[
p(a)=\left(\frac{1+2a}{3},\frac{1-a}{3},\frac{1-a}{3}\right),
\qquad -\frac12\le a\le 1,
\]

write

\[
F(a)=\Psi(p(a))
\]

and let `K_*` denote the exact orbit-averaged two-child kernel at `lambda_*=1/sqrt(2)`. Define the KS gap

\[
H(a,b):=\frac{F(a)+F(b)}2-K_*(p(a),p(b)).
\]

### Theorem
For every

\[
(a,b)\in[-1/2,0]^2,
\]

\[
\boxed{H(a,b)\ge0.}
\]

Equality occurs only at `(a,b)=(0,0)`.

Consequently, if a symmetric posterior law is supported on `S_3`-orbits of the negative two-equal family, then one exact binary-tree fusion step at KS does not increase expected `Psi`; it is strict for every nontrivial such law.

This is still **PARTIAL**: arbitrary posterior laws can mix positive and negative two-equal amplitudes and, after misaligned fusion, genuinely three-distinct-coordinate orbits.

## 3. Exact diagonal-average reduction

The previous theorem already gives

\[
H(a,a)\ge0\qquad(-1/2\le a\le0).
\]

It is therefore enough to prove that the off-diagonal pair is no worse than the average of the two diagonal pairs. Put

\[
Q(a,b):=H(a,b)-\frac{H(a,a)+H(b,b)}2
=\frac{K_*(p(a),p(a))+K_*(p(b),p(b))}{2}-K_*(p(a),p(b)).
\]

Set `x=-a`, `y=-b`, so `x,y in [0,1/2]`. Direct exact algebra, followed by the convergent logarithmic expansion on this negative ray, gives

\[
\boxed{Q(-x,-y)=(x-y)^2R(x,y)}
\]

with

\[
R(x,y)=R_P(x,y)-\frac12\sum_{n\ge2}c_n S_n(x,y)^2,
\]

\[
c_n=\frac{(-1)^n+2^{1-n}}{3n(n-1)},
\qquad
S_n(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}x^{n-1-k}y^k.
\]

Here `R_P` is the exact rational/algebraic contribution coming from the cubic/quartic correction and the Bayes normalizers. Its numerator over `Q(sqrt(2))` is stored exactly in the certificate rather than printed as a large coefficient table in this report.

The factor `(x-y)^2` is not a numerical guess: it is the exact cancellation forced by symmetry and the fact that `Q(a,a)=0`.

## 5. Distribution-level corollary

Let `mu` be any probability law on `[-1/2,0]`, and let the symmetric posterior law choose an amplitude `a~mu` and then a uniformly random permutation of `p(a)`. For independent child amplitudes `a,b~mu`, the theorem gives pointwise

\[
K_*(p(a),p(b))\le\frac{F(a)+F(b)}2.
\]

Integrating yields

\[
\iint K_*(p(a),p(b))\,d\mu(a)d\mu(b)
\le \int F(a)\,d\mu(a).
\]

Hence every symmetric posterior law supported on this entire continuum of negative two-equal orbits is KS-nonexpanding for the quartic Lyapunov functional. This is strictly stronger than the prior single-orbit diagonal theorem.

## 6. A failed extension that identifies the next obstruction

The successful proof used the stronger comparison

\[
H(a,b)\ge\frac{H(a,a)+H(b,b)}2.
\]

That comparison cannot simply be extended to all signs. At the explicit pair `(a,b)=(0,1)`, outward-rounded evaluation gives

\[
Q(0,1)\in[-0.14760024777103475,-0.14760024777103237],
\]

so the diagonal-average convexity is decisively false in the positive region even though the desired gap `H(0,1)` itself remains positive. Thus repeating the negative-ray factorization is the wrong global method.

A better normalization is

\[
E(a):=\frac12F(a)-F(a/\sqrt2),
\]

the one-child edge gap, and

\[
J(a,b):=K_*(p(a),p(b))-F(a/\sqrt2)-F(b/\sqrt2).
\]

Then exactly

\[
\boxed{H(a,b)=E(a)+E(b)-J(a,b).}
\]

The global edge theorem gives `E(a)>=0`, with equality only at zero. Near the origin,

\[
E(a)=\frac5{72}a^4+O(a^5),
\qquad
J(a,b)=\frac1{36}a^2b^2+O((|a|+|b|)^5),
\]

so the stronger Cauchy-type estimate

\[
J(a,b)\le2\sqrt{E(a)E(b)}
\]

has a comfortable local ratio `1/5` and would imply `H(a,b)>=0` by AM-GM. This removes the quartic degeneracy that makes direct interval evaluation near `(0,0)` inefficient.

## 7. Remaining gap and next attack

The next target remains the full two-equal square

\[
K_*(p(a),p(b))\le\frac{F(a)+F(b)}2,
\qquad a,b\in[-1/2,1].
\]

The negative-negative quadrant is now closed. The next attack should work only on the remaining positive-positive and mixed-sign regions using the `E/J` normalization above:

1. derive an exact factor/series representation for `J(a,b)/(a^2b^2)` and `E(a)/a^4` with removable limits at zero;
2. prove either the global Cauchy bound `J<=2 sqrt(E(a)E(b))` or a piecewise stronger bound on the mixed/positive regions by exact Bernstein plus interval arithmetic;
3. if a rigorous violation of the Cauchy bound appears, return to the weaker exact target `J<=E(a)+E(b)` rather than tuning another Lyapunov functional;
4. only after the full two-equal square is settled move to genuinely three-distinct orbit representatives in the complex-magnetization/dihedral coordinates.

A rigorous counterexample to the actual `H>=0` inequality, if found, should seed the complementary below-KS reconstruction/fixed-point route rather than another contraction ansatz.
