## 3. Global posterior recursion in mutual information

Let `X` be a uniform root symbol in `{1,2,3}` and let `Y_n` be the level-`n` observation below a vertex. Write

\[
P_n(Y_n)=(P(X=1\mid Y_n),P(X=2\mid Y_n),P(X=3\mid Y_n))
\]

for the posterior vector and `u=(1/3,1/3,1/3)`. Set

\[
I_n:=I(X;Y_n)=\mathbb E\,D(P_n\|u).
\]

Because the Potts channel is reversible with uniform stationary distribution, passing a posterior `p` through one edge toward the parent maps it exactly to

\[
T_\lambda p:=u+\lambda(p-u).
\]

Define the fixed-prior KL contraction coefficient

\[
\eta_3(\lambda):=
\sup_{p\in\Delta_3,\,p\neq u}
\frac{D(T_\lambda p\|u)}{D(p\|u)}.
\]

For either child subtree,

\[
I(X_{\rm parent};Y_{\rm child})
=\mathbb E D(T_\lambda P_n\|u)
\le \eta_3(\lambda) I_n.
\]

The two child observations `Y_1,Y_2` are conditionally independent given the parent. Therefore

\[
I(X;Y_1,Y_2)
=I(X;Y_1)+I(X;Y_2)-I(Y_1;Y_2)
\le I(X;Y_1)+I(X;Y_2),
\]

and hence the **global**, not local, recursion

\[
\boxed{I_{n+1}\le 2\eta_3(\lambda) I_n.}
\]

Consequently `2 eta_3(lambda)<1` implies exponential decay of mutual information and therefore non-reconstruction (Pinsker then forces the root-conditioned level laws together).

The remaining task is a genuine global simplex inequality; no stability-at-the-uniform-fixed-point substitution is being made.

## 4. The simplex inequality at `lambda=351/500`

I prove

\[
\boxed{
D(T_{351/500}p\|u)<\frac12D(p\|u)
\quad\text{for every }p\neq u.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Let `lambda=351/500`, `a=(1-lambda)/3`, `r(x)=a+lambda x`, and

\[
f(x)=r(x)\log(3r(x))-\frac12x\log(3x),
\]

with `0 log 0=0`. Then

\[
F(p):=D(T_\lambda p\|u)-\frac12D(p\|u)=\sum_{i=1}^3 f(p_i).
\]

For `x>0`,

\[
f''(x)=\frac{\lambda^2}{a+\lambda x}-\frac1{2x}.
\]

Its sign changes exactly once, at

\[
x_0=\frac{a}{2\lambda^2-\lambda}>\frac13.
\]

A boundary point cannot maximize `F`, because `f'(x)\to+\infty` as `x\downarrow0`, so moving an arbitrarily small mass from a positive coordinate into a zero coordinate increases `F`.

At an interior constrained maximizer, Lagrange multipliers give `f'(p_i)=constant`. Since `f'` first decreases and then increases, there are at most two possible coordinate values. Moreover two coordinates cannot both lie above `x_0`: along the tangent direction that transfers mass between those two coordinates, the second variation is the sum of two positive `f''` values, contradicting local maximality. If all coordinates lie below `x_0`, injectivity of the decreasing branch of `f'` forces the uniform point. Thus every non-uniform interior maximizer has, up to permutation,

\[
p=\left(x,\frac{1-x}{2},\frac{1-x}{2}\right),\qquad x>x_0>1/3.
\]

So the entire two-dimensional simplex problem reduces to one variable.

Put

\[
d(x)=x\log(3x)+(1-x)\log\!\left(\frac{3(1-x)}2\right),
\qquad
x_\lambda=\frac13+\lambda\left(x-\frac13\right).
\]

For the above vector, `D(p||u)=d(x)` and `D(T_lambda p||u)=d(x_lambda)`. It is enough to prove

\[
G(x):=d(x)-2d(x_\lambda)>0\qquad(1/3<x\le1).
\tag{2}
\]

Now

\[
G'(x)=\log\frac{2x}{1-x}-2\lambda\log\frac{2x_\lambda}{1-x_\lambda},
\]

and

\[
G''(x)=\frac{Q_\lambda(x)}{x(1-x)x_\lambda(1-x_\lambda)},
\]

where the denominator is positive and

\[
Q_\lambda(x)
=\lambda^2x^2+\frac{\lambda(1-4\lambda)}3x
+\frac{2-\lambda-\lambda^2}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]

At `lambda=351/500`, exact rational arithmetic gives

\[
Q(1/3)=0.0031982222\ldots>0,\quad
Q(0.4)=-0.0009139377\ldots<0,\quad
Q(0.49)=0.0004831826\ldots>0.
\]

Because `Q` is upward-opening, `G''` has exactly two roots, one in `(1/3,0.4)` and one in `(0.4,0.49)`, and `G''>0` for all `x>=0.49`.

Also `G(1/3)=G'(1/3)=0`. The exact logarithm enclosure described below certifies

\[
G'(0.49)<-3.09\times10^{-4},
\]

so after an initial increase `G` has one local maximum before `0.49`. Since `G''>0` after `0.49`, there is at most one later stationary point. It is bracketed by

\[
0.507896<x_*<0.507897,
\]

with

\[
G'(0.507896)<-4.05\times10^{-9},\qquad
G'(0.507897)>2.35\times10^{-8}.
\]

A rigorous rational Lipschitz enclosure on this one-micro-unit bracket gives

\[
\boxed{G(x_*)>7.69384529\times10^{-5}>0.}
\]

Finally `G(1)>0.173895`. Therefore the only minimum of `G` on `[1/3,1]` with value zero is the uniform endpoint `x=1/3`, proving (2), hence (1).

The ratio in the definition of `eta_3` extends continuously to `p=u`, where its value is `lambda^2=0.492804<1/2`. Compactness plus strict inequality away from `u` therefore yields

\[
\eta_3(351/500)<1/2.
\]

For fixed `p`, `D(u+lambda(p-u)||u)` is nondecreasing in `lambda>=0` (convexity along the ray from its minimum at `lambda=0`). Thus the same strict contraction holds for every `0<=lambda<=351/500`. Combining with the mutual-information recursion proves the theorem.

## 6. What failed / what this teaches

Before finding the KL Lyapunov, I tested two routes that are useful diagnostics but are not claims:

1. **Raw posterior second moment.** In the exact Fourier recursion for `q=3`, a two-child update has
   \[
   z' = \frac{a+b+\bar a\bar b}{1+2\Re(a\bar b)},\qquad a=\lambda z_1,\ b=\lambda z_2.
   \]
   The denominator allows finite-posterior configurations for which the raw second moment expands even below KS. So a pointwise `L^2` contraction is false and local KS stability is not a global proof.
2. **One-parameter Hellinger deficit.** Pairwise affinities tensorize cleanly across the two children, but the sharp pointwise Hellinger contraction already becomes too weak around the presently relevant binary interval. The KL functional succeeds because conditional independence gives the exact subtractive identity `I(X;Y1,Y2)=I(X;Y1)+I(X;Y2)-I(Y1;Y2)` while the edge map admits a sharper fixed-prior contraction.

The new route is therefore genuinely global posterior recursion, not a restatement of local stability.
