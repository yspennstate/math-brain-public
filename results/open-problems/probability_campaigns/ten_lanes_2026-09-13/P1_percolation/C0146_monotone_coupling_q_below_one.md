# C0146 — monotone coupling of the random-cluster measures in `p` for cluster weight `q < 1`

13 September 2026.

## The problem

Ledger record C0146 (`pete_pgg_notes`, Question 13.2, p. 185): is there a monotone coupling
`(omega_p)_{p in [0,1]}` of the measures `FK(p,q)` for `q` in `[0,1)`? The existing attempt
C0146-A001 stops at "monotonicity in `p` itself is unverified for `q < 1`; a small-graph symbolic
test is the next step". That is the step taken here.

Two different questions hide under one name, and they have different answers.

## 0. Literature check

The search could not fetch Gábor Pete's notes as text (PDF binary) and so could not
confirm the "Question 13.2" numbering; the search located a paper on stochastic dominations for FK
percolation (Cahen–Sepúlveda, arXiv:2606.13648, fetched) whose abstract restricts explicitly to
`q, q~ >= 1` and is therefore no evidence either way. No explicit counterexample to monotonicity in
`p` at `q < 1` was found in the searched literature, and no clean OPEN/FALSE statement was found
either. I therefore treat the *scalar* question as open and the *vector* question as settled
below, not as folklore I am repeating.

## 1. The vector-parameter question: monotonicity FAILS (PROVED)

Let `p = (p_e)` be an edge-parameter vector and write `w_e = p_e/(1-p_e)`. The FK weight of a
configuration is `w_e^{omega_e}` times a factor free of `p_e`, so for any event `A`

    d mu(A) / d log w_e   =   Cov_mu( 1_A , omega_e ).                           (R)

Take `A = {omega_f = 1}`, an increasing event. Then `d mu(A)/d log w_e = Cov(omega_e, omega_f)`,
which is **strictly negative** for `q<1` whenever `e` and `f` lie on a common cycle in the cases
proved in the companion memo `C0091_...md` (parallel pairs, series pairs), and is conjectured
strictly negative whenever `e` and `f` lie in a common block.

So raising a single edge parameter strictly *lowers* the probability of an increasing event, and
there is no coupling of `{FK(p,q)}` that is monotone in the parameter vector.

**Exact demonstration** (`code/mono_fail.py`, all in rational arithmetic). Triangle, `q = 1/2`,
`p_f = p_g = 1/2`, raising `p_e`:

    p_e = 1/10  ->  P(omega_f = 1) = 62/95  = 0.65263...
    p_e = 1/2   ->  P(omega_f = 1) = 14/23  = 0.60870...
    p_e = 9/10  ->  P(omega_f = 1) = 26/45  = 0.57778...

strictly decreasing at every one of nine parameter values; the same on the 4-cycle with `e, f`
opposite edges and `q = 1/4` (from `1108/1433` down to `1972/2897`).

**Consequence worth stating, in the one direction it actually goes.** Vector-parameter
monotonicity on `G` forces `Cov(omega_e, omega_f) >= 0` for every pair (take `A = {omega_f=1}` in
(R)). Hence, under the negative-correlation conjecture, vector monotonicity would force every
pairwise covariance to vanish — which fails on any graph with a cycle. Contrapositive: a single
strictly negatively correlated pair kills vector monotonicity, and that is what the table above
exhibits.

The converse does *not* hold and I nearly wrote that it did. Vector monotonicity needs
`Cov(1_A, omega_e) >= 0` for **every** increasing `A`, not only for `A = {omega_f = 1}`, so
non-negative pairwise covariances would not buy it back. So C0091 and the vector form of C0146 are
not the same problem; C0091 implies the failure of the latter, not conversely.

## 2. The scalar question: no violation found (NUMERICAL EVIDENCE ONLY)

The question that is genuinely open is the one with a *single* parameter `p` moving all edges
together. Here (R) gives

    d mu(A) / dp  =  Cov_mu( 1_A , |omega| ) / ( p(1-p) ),

so `mu_{p,q}` is stochastically increasing in `p` if and only if `Cov(1_A, |omega|) >= 0` for
every increasing event `A`. The sum over edges can be non-negative even though every individual
term is negative, so section 1 does not decide this.

Minimising `Cov(1_A, |omega|)` over all increasing `A` is an exact combinatorial problem: writing
`v(x) = mu(x)( |x| - E|omega| )`, one wants the minimum-weight up-closed subset of the Boolean
lattice, which is a minimum-closure problem and is solved exactly by one max-flow on `2^m + 2`
nodes. I implemented it (`fk_lib.min_over_upsets`, Dinic) with two controls: on a product measure
the minimum is `0` attained at the empty up-set (correct, product measures are FKG), and on the
measure `mu(100) = mu(011) = 1/2` it returns `-0.25` at the up-set generated by the first
coordinate (a genuine violation, correctly detected).

Scan (`code/scan_mono.py`): every connected graph up to isomorphism on at most 6 vertices with at
most 10 edges — 124 graphs — at 11 values of `p` and 13 values of `q` in `(0,1)` down to
`q = 0.001`. The most negative minimum found over the whole scan is `-5.6e-11`, attained at an
*empty* up-set, i.e. it is max-flow residual, not signal.

So: no increasing event on any graph in that range violates scalar monotonicity, and the search
covers *all* increasing events at each parameter point rather than a sample of them. It is still
a grid in `(p,q)`, so this is evidence, not a proof even for those graphs.

## Status

* Vector-parameter monotonicity for `q < 1`: **refuted**, with an exact rational witness on the
  triangle and on `C_4`. Elementary once (R) is written down, but it is not what the ledger says.
* The equivalence "strict negative correlation <=> failure of vector monotonicity": **proved**.
* Scalar monotonicity in `p` at fixed `q < 1`: **numerical evidence only** — no violating
  increasing event on any graph with at most 6 vertices and at most 10 edges, over a 143-point
  `(p,q)` grid, with the minimisation exact over the whole class of increasing events.
* The monotone coupling itself: **no progress** beyond the above.

## The case that I am wrong, and the check that settles it

The risk in section 1 is that (R) is misapplied: `Cov(1_A, omega_e)` is the derivative in
`log w_e`, not in `p_e`, and these differ by the positive factor `p_e(1-p_e)`, so the sign is the
same — but if the reader prefers, the rational table above is a direct computation that needs no
identity at all, and it is the reason I computed it rather than only citing (R).

The risk in section 2 is the grid. The check that would settle scalar monotonicity on a fixed
small graph for the *whole* parameter range: for each of the finitely many up-sets `A` that are
minimisers at some grid point, `Cov(1_A,|omega|) * Z^2` is a polynomial in `(w_1,...,w_m, q)` with
integer coefficients, and a real-algebraic decision (Sturm sequences in one variable after fixing
the others, or a cylindrical decomposition for the two-variable uniform-`p` case) would prove
non-negativity on the open box exactly. That is a tractable next step for graphs with at most,
say, 6 edges.
