# P0037 — a BK-type inequality for the weighted uniform spanning tree

13 September 2026.

## The problem

Let `T` be a weighted uniform spanning tree of a finite connected graph `G`
(`P(T) proportional to prod_{e in T} w_e`). For events `A, B` on `{0,1}^E`, the disjoint
occurrence `A box B` is the set of `omega` for which there are disjoint `S, T_0` contained in
`omega` with `[S] contained in A` and `[T_0] contained in B`. Conjecture (Lyons & Peres,
*Probability on Trees and Networks*): for **increasing** `A` and `B`,

    P[ T in A box B ]  <=  P[ T in A ] P[ T in B ].

Ledger record: P0037, `lyons_peres_20260819`, "Conjecture 4.10, printed p.125". The searches
below repeatedly surfaced the numbering **4.6**, not 4.10, and I could not fetch the book as text
to settle it; the statement is the same either way.

The existing campaign attempt P0037-A001 proves the case where `A` and `B` are both *principal*
(`A = [F]`, `B = [H]` with `F, H` disjoint fixed edge sets) from the determinantal formula plus
Hadamard–Fischer, and stops at "general increasing disjoint occurrence is a union over adaptive
witnesses".

## 1. A new case: `A` principal, `B` arbitrary (PROVED)

**Theorem.** Let `T` be a weighted uniform spanning tree of a finite connected graph. Let
`A = [K]` be a principal increasing event (`K` a fixed edge set) and let `B` be *any* increasing
event. Then `P(A box B) <= P(A) P(B)`.

*Proof.* Since `[S] contained in A` holds exactly when `S contains K`, the smallest and therefore
the best witness for `A` inside `omega` is `K` itself. Hence

    A box B  =  { K contained in T }  intersect  B_K,

where `B_K` is the increasing event generated by those minimal witnesses of `B` that are disjoint
from `K`. `B_K` is increasing and depends only on the coordinates in `E \ K`, while
`{K contained in T}` is increasing and depends only on the coordinates in `K`. These are disjoint
coordinate sets, so the negative association of the weighted spanning-tree measure (see the
citation note below) gives

    P(A box B)  <=  P(K contained in T) * P(B_K)  =  P(A) * P(B_K)  <=  P(A) P(B),

the last step because `B_K` is contained in `B`. QED

This strictly contains the both-principal case recorded in P0037-A001: there `B` had to be a fixed
edge set, here it is arbitrary. The mechanism is also the reason the general case is hard — as soon
as `A` has two incomparable minimal witnesses `K_1, K_2`, the union
`A box B = union_i ({K_i in T} intersect B_{K_i})` has to be bounded by `P(A)P(B)` and the union
bound gives `sum_i P(K_i in T)`, which overshoots `P(A)`.

**Machine check** (`code/bk_principal.py`): 55,964 instances — every connected graph up to
isomorphism on 3 or 4 vertices with at most 6 edges, two random integer weight vectors each, every
principal `A` over every forest `K`, and every `B` generated by one or two forest witnesses — all
in exact rational arithmetic, **0 violations**. The negative-association step it rests on was
tested separately on 1,424 disjoint fixed-set pairs: 0 violations.

## 2. Exhaustive search for a counterexample (NUMERICAL EVIDENCE ONLY — none found)

`code/bk_ust.py`. On the support of the spanning-tree measure an increasing event is determined by
its minimal witnesses, and those may be taken to be forests of `G`; the event's tree-set is then a
bitmask over spanning trees and `A box B` is the union over disjoint witness pairs. Searches run:

* All connected graphs up to isomorphism on 3 to 5 vertices with at most 7 edges (25 graphs), all
  antichains of at most 2 forest witnesses for each of `A` and `B`, uniform weights:
  **no violation**.
* The same with at most 3 witnesses: the run did not finish, so no result is claimed for it.

**Control on the box-product logic.** On the (non-spanning-tree) measure putting mass `1/2` on
`11` and `1/2` on `00`, with `A` generated by the first coordinate and `B` by the second, the same
code reports `P(A box B) = 1/2 > 1/4 = P(A)P(B)` — a true BK violation, correctly detected. On the
uniform product measure on `{0,1}^2` it reports equality, as Reimer requires. So the harness can
see a violation and does not manufacture one.

## The case that I am wrong

The theorem's weak point is the claim `A box B = {K in T} intersect B_K`. If `A = [K]` then any
witness `S` of `A` satisfies `S contains K`, so a pair of disjoint witnesses `(S, T_0)` with
`S contains K` yields the disjoint pair `(K, T_0)`; conversely `(K, T_0)` is itself such a pair.
The identity is therefore exact, not an inequality, and it is the step the 55,964-instance check
was built to catch if I had it backwards.

The second weak point is invoking negative association for the *weighted* spanning tree, and it is
sharper than I first wrote. **Feder and Mihail (1992) prove negative association for the UNIFORM
measure on the bases of a balanced matroid — not for arbitrary positive edge weights.** For the
weighted case the citation chain is

* Choe, Oxley, Sokal and Wagner, *Homogeneous multivariate polynomials with the half-plane
  property*, Adv. Appl. Math. 32 (2004) 88–187 (arXiv:math/0202034): the weighted spanning-tree
  generating polynomial is real stable for **every** positive edge-weight vector, so the measure
  is strongly Rayleigh; and
* Borcea, Brändén and Liggett, *Negative dependence and the geometry of polynomials*, JAMS 22
  (2009) 521–567 (arXiv:0707.2340): every strongly Rayleigh measure is **fully** negatively
  associated, i.e. `E[fg] <= E[f]E[g]` for increasing `f, g` on disjoint coordinate sets.

Lyons, *Determinantal probability measures*, Publ. Math. IHES 98 (2003) 167–212
(arXiv:math/0204325), is very likely an equally good direct citation via the determinantal
structure, but I have not confirmed at what generality (pairwise versus full negative association)
his statement is made.

**Provenance caveat, recorded because it matters:** the fetch tool failed partway through this
second lookup, so the bibliographic identifications above are corroborated across several
independent searches but the theorem *numbers* and abstract wording were not re-read from the
sources. The arXiv identifiers and titles are confirmed; the precise theorem numbers are not, and
I have therefore not quoted any. Anyone building on this should open arXiv:0707.2340 and
arXiv:math/0202034 and check the two statements before relying on them.

The distinction is not cosmetic: with pairwise negative correlation alone — which is what
Hadamard–Fischer on the transfer-current matrix gives, and what the ledger's own attempt A001 used
— the theorem above does **not** follow, because `B_K` is a general increasing event and not a
fixed edge set. Full negative association is exactly the strength the argument needs.

The concrete next check: run the search with three witnesses per side on 6-vertex graphs with
optimised edge weights, and — more promising — attack the two-witness union directly, where the
obligation is
`P(K_1 in T, B_{K_1}) + P(K_2 in T, B_{K_2}) - P(K_1 and K_2 in T, B_{K_1} or B_{K_2}) <= P(A)P(B)`,
a statement about two fixed sets and one increasing event in which every term is computable.
