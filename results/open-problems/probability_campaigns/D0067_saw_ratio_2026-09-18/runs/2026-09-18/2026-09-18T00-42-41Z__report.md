## 3. Frozen metric and admission baseline

Set

\[
r_n:=\frac{\sigma_{n+1}}{\sigma_n},
\qquad
A_{\rm SAW}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\log\frac{r_n}{\mu}\right|.
\]

The literal source question is equivalent to

\[
A_{\rm SAW}=0.
\]

The admission baseline is a genuine bound, not a fake zero:

\[
\boxed{0\le A_{\rm SAW}\le \log(3/\mu).}
\]

Proof of the upper bound: for `n>=1`, an endpoint of a square-lattice SAW has at most three legal next steps, because the preceding vertex is already occupied. Hence

\[
r_n\le 3.
\]

Kesten's theorem says

\[
r_n r_{n+1}
=\frac{\sigma_{n+2}}{\sigma_n}
\longrightarrow \mu^2.
\]

Since `r_{n+1}<=3`,

\[
\liminf r_n\ge \frac{\mu^2}{3}.
\]

Thus every subsequential limit of `r_n/mu` lies in

\[
[\mu/3,\,3/\mu],
\]

whose two endpoints are reciprocals. Taking logarithms gives the displayed bound. Using the source's rigorous lower bound `mu>=2.6256` only for orientation gives `log(3/mu)<0.134`; I do not freeze a decimal because the symbolic bound is cleaner.

# 4. B2 — attack the negation: scalar information permits a perfect parity oscillation

Before attempting a proof, test whether the obvious scalar ingredients are even capable of forcing the result.

Fix any

\[
0<a\le \log(3/\mu)
\]

and define the positive sequence

\[
\widetilde\sigma_n:=\mu^n c_n,
\qquad
c_n=
\begin{cases}
1,&n\text{ even},\\
e^a,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

Then `c_{m+n} <= c_m c_n` for every `m,n`:

- even + even: `1 <= 1`;
- even + odd or odd + even: `e^a <= e^a`;
- odd + odd: `1 <= e^{2a}`.

Therefore

\[
\widetilde\sigma_{m+n}\le
\widetilde\sigma_m\widetilde\sigma_n,
\]

so the model obeys the same submultiplicative inequality that gives the connective constant.

It also obeys the two-step ratio **exactly**:

\[
\frac{\widetilde\sigma_{n+2}}{\widetilde\sigma_n}=\mu^2.
\]

But its one-step ratio is

\[
\frac{\widetilde\sigma_{n+1}}{\widetilde\sigma_n}
=
\begin{cases}
\mu e^a,&n\text{ even},\\
\mu e^{-a},&n\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\]

and therefore never converges when `a>0`. The chosen bound on `a` even ensures the upper scalar constraint `r_n<=3`.

This is **not** a SAW counterexample: the numbers need not be integers and need not be realizable as walk counts. Its purpose is sharper. It proves that no argument using only

1. submultiplicativity,
2. existence of `mu`,
3. the exact/tending two-step ratio, and
4. the degree-three scalar upper bound

can settle Question 1.3. A proof must exploit additional geometry of the actual square-lattice SAW tree.

**B2 outcome:** counterexample to the naive proof route; source conjecture neither refuted nor proved.

# 5. B18 — say it in another language: the ratio is a one-site occupancy observable

Let `Sigma_n` be the set of `n`-step SAWs from the origin and let `P_n` be the uniform measure on `Sigma_n`.

For

\[
\omega=(\omega_0=0,\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_n),
\]

define its legal endpoint-extension count

\[
D_+(\omega)
:=
\#\{z:z\sim\omega_n,\ z\notin\{\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_n\}\}.
\]

Every `(n+1)`-step SAW has a unique `n`-step prefix, and every legal extension of an `n`-step SAW gives one `(n+1)`-step SAW. Hence, exactly,

\[
\sigma_{n+1}=\sum_{\omega\in\Sigma_n}D_+(\omega),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{r_n=\mathbb E_{P_n}[D_+].}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Now use reversal plus translation. Define

\[
(R\omega)_t:=\omega_{n-t}-\omega_n,
\qquad 0\le t\le n.
\]

`R` is an involutive bijection of `Sigma_n` onto itself. Under this map the old endpoint becomes the new origin, so `D_+(omega)` becomes the number of nearest neighbours of the new origin that were **never visited** by `R omega`.

Thus if

\[
D_0(\omega)
:=
\#\{z:z\sim0,\ z\notin V(\omega)\},
\]

then uniformity and the bijection give

\[
\boxed{r_n=\mathbb E_{P_n}[D_0].}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This turns the asymptotic enumeration question into a local spatial occupancy question.

Let `e_1=(1,0)` and

\[
p_n:=P_n(e_1\in V(\omega)).
\]

By the dihedral symmetry of `Z^2`, each of the four neighbours of the origin has the same visiting probability `p_n`. Since `D_0` is four minus the number of occupied neighbours,

\[
\boxed{r_n=4(1-p_n).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore Question 1.3 is exactly equivalent to

\[
\boxed{p_n\longrightarrow 1-\mu/4.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The event in (5.4) is not about a remote exponent or a whole-path scaling limit: it asks whether a uniform long SAW has an asymptotically well-defined probability of ever occupying one prescribed neighbour of its starting point.

This identity also pinpoints the missing odd-parity information. Kesten's surgery controls the product of two consecutive normalizers; Question 1.3 asks whether this single local occupancy probability itself stabilizes rather than alternating.

**B18 outcome:** exact source-equivalent reformulation, but not a proof of convergence.

# 6. B18 continued — an exact endpoint-view transfer operator

The same identity has a useful dynamical form.

For a path `omega`, define the endpoint-rooted visited set

\[
S(\omega):=\{\omega_t-\omega_n:0\le t\le n\}\subset\mathbb Z^2.
\]

Let `nu_n` be the law of `S(omega)` under `P_n`. Put

\[
D(S):=\#\{e\in\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2\}:e\notin S\}.
\]

Then (5.1) becomes

\[
\boxed{r_n=\int D(S)\,d\nu_n(S).}
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a legal neighbour `e notin S`, after extending the old endpoint by `e` and recentering at the new endpoint, the visited set becomes

\[
\Phi_e(S):=(S-e)\cup\{0\}.
\]

For any probability measure `nu` with positive `int D dnu`, define

\[
(T\nu)(f)
:=
\frac{
\displaystyle
\int\sum_{e\sim0,\ e\notin S}
 f(\Phi_e(S))\,d\nu(S)
}{
\displaystyle
\int D(S)\,d\nu(S)
}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Because every `(n+1)`-step path has exactly one `n`-step parent, the numerator counts each child once. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\nu_{n+1}=T\nu_n}
\tag{6.3}
\]

**exactly**, and the desired ratio `r_n` is exactly the normalizing constant of this nonlinear positive transfer.

This separates the problem into two pieces:

- Kesten controls the product of two successive normalizers;
- Question 1.3 asks whether the normalizer of the one-step transfer itself converges.

# 7. B29 — compactify: failure forces an alternating-normalizer orbit

Let

\[
\mathcal X:=\{S\subseteq\mathbb Z^2:0\in S\}
\]

with the product topology inherited from `{0,1}^{Z^2}`. This is compact. The observable `D(S)` depends only on the four neighbours of the origin and is continuous. Each branch `Phi_e` is continuous on the clopen set `{S:e notin S}`.

For finite SAWs, `D<=3`. Kesten's two-step ratio and `r_n<=3` give an eventual positive lower bound on `r_n`; for example, for all sufficiently large `n`, `r_n>=mu^2/6`. Hence `T` is continuous on the tail limit set relevant to the sequence `(nu_n)`.

Set

\[
d_n:=\log(r_n/\mu).
\]

Kesten gives

\[
d_n+d_{n+1}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The quantitative version recorded by Grimmett in 2021 even gives `d_n+d_{n+1}=O(n^{-1/3})` after using the eventual positive lower bound, but only convergence to zero is needed below.

Assume, for contradiction-search purposes, that

\[
A_{\rm SAW}>0.
\]

Choose a subsequence `n_k` such that

\[
d_{n_k}\to a,
\qquad |a|=A_{\rm SAW}>0.
\]

By compactness of probability measures on `X`, after a further subsequence

\[
\nu_{n_k}\Longrightarrow \nu_0.
\]

Continuity of `T`, (6.3), and (7.1) now imply recursively, for every fixed `j>=0`,

\[
\nu_{n_k+j}\Longrightarrow \nu_j:=T^j\nu_0,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\int D\,d\nu_j
=
\mu e^{(-1)^j a}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

So nonconvergence of the source ratio forces a genuine forward orbit of the endpoint-view transfer operator whose **normalizing constants alternate forever between two distinct reciprocal values**.

It is important not to overstate (7.2): it does **not** prove that `nu_{j+2}=nu_j`. The measures may drift under `T^2`. What is forced is an exact alternating normalizer along the entire limiting orbit. Equivalently, the closure of its even iterates is `T^2`-forward invariant with constant `D`-expectation `mu e^a`, while the odd side has constant `D`-expectation `mu e^{-a}`.

By reversal, the same statement can be read in the starting-point language (5.3): a failed ratio theorem produces two persistent compact regimes with different limiting probabilities of occupying a fixed neighbour of the origin.

Thus a source-level proof may be attacked through the following smaller-looking statement:

> **Alternating-normalizer exclusion problem.** Show that no orbit in the subsequential endpoint-view closure generated by actual square-lattice SAWs can satisfy (7.2) with `a != 0`.

This is still hard, but it is genuinely one-step and geometry-sensitive. The B2 stress test shows why this extra state-space information is necessary.

**B29 outcome:** exact compact obstruction/reduction; unrestricted metric unchanged.

## 8. What failed, and what a proof now has to use

The counterexample-first route rules out the tempting strategy “combine submultiplicativity with Kesten's two-step theorem.” An abstract sequence can satisfy those facts perfectly and still oscillate forever.

The transfer formulation identifies the extra ingredient a proof must exploit: geometry must prevent a nonzero alternating normalizer in the endpoint-view dynamics. Possible mechanisms include:

1. an odd-length endpoint surgery whose multiplicity cost is asymptotically neutral;
2. a monotonicity/entropy quantity for the size-biased transfer `T` that is strict unless the normalizer equals `mu`;
3. a local-limit uniqueness statement only for the nearest-neighbour occupancy observable, weaker than a full SAW scaling limit;
4. a two-count identity for paths that revisit a neighbour of the start, strong enough to compare consecutive `p_n`.

The simplest source-special case still open is already the literal square-lattice problem; there is no lower-dimensional or smaller-parameter version to discharge. The nearest solved statement is Kesten's two-step ratio theorem, whose parity-preserving surgery is exactly what fails to compare `n` and `n+1`.
