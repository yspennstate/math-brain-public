# B10 — derandomize: canonical endpoint-moving one-step switch

The previous local-surgery obstruction was exact: if a surgery keeps both attachment vertices fixed on the bipartite square lattice, its length change is even. The simplest way around that obstruction is therefore not a fancier fixed-attachment replacement; it is to **move an endpoint by one lattice edge**.

This gives an exact deterministic adjacent-length map. The only issue is global self-avoidance. The point of this run is that, after truncating the endpoint memory at depth \(M\), the failure class is *exactly* the old late-contact tail and nothing else.

## Terminal memory states

Fix \(M\ge1\). For an \(n\)-step SAW

\[
\omega=(\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_n),\qquad n\ge M,
\]

define its reversed terminal \(M\)-window

\[
x(\omega)=(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_M),
\qquad
x_j:=\omega_{n-j}-\omega_n.
\]

Thus \(x_0=0\), and \(x\) is an \(M\)-step self-avoiding nearest-neighbour path ending at the origin when viewed backward. Let \(X_M\) be the finite set of such windows.

For a lattice unit vector \(e\in\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2\}\), call \(e\) **\(M\)-locally admissible** for \(x\) if

\[
e\notin\{x_1,\ldots,x_M\}.
\]

If one appends the step \(e\), the new reversed window is determined completely by \(x\):

\[
\Phi_e(x)=
(0,-e,x_1-e,\ldots,x_{M-1}-e).
\]

Define the finite nonnegative transfer matrix \(A_M\) on \(X_M\) by

\[
(A_M)_{y,x}
=
\#\{e:\ e\text{ is \(M\)-locally admissible for }x,
\ \Phi_e(x)=y\}.
\]

For the square lattice the direction is already determined by the second vertex of \(y\), so the entries are in fact \(0\) or \(1\), but the counting definition is the useful one.

Let

\[
N_{n,M}(x):=\#\{\omega:\ |\omega|=n,\ x(\omega)=x\}.
\]

## Exact transfer-with-defect identity

For a locally admissible proposed extension \(e\), exactly one of two things happens:

* **legal:** \(\omega_n+e\) is not any vertex of \(\omega\), and appending it produces an \((n+1)\)-step SAW;
* **remote blocked:** \(\omega_n+e=\omega_{n-t}\) for a unique \(t>M\).

There are no other failures. In particular, local collisions were excluded by the definition of \(M\)-admissibility, and inverse multiplicity for a legal extension is exactly one because deleting the last edge recovers the parent.

Let \(B_{n,M}(y)\) count the remote-blocked marked proposals whose *would-be* local child window is \(y\). Then, componentwise,

\[
\boxed{
N_{n+1,M}=A_MN_{n,M}-B_{n,M}.
}
\tag{B10.1}
\]

### Proof

`A_M N_{n,M}` counts all pairs \((\omega,e)\) consisting of an \(n\)-step SAW and an \(M\)-locally admissible endpoint direction, grouped by the resulting local child window. Split those pairs into globally legal and remote-blocked proposals. The legal pairs are in bijection with \((n+1)\)-step SAWs via deletion of the last edge, and the blocked pairs are exactly \(B_{n,M}\). Subtracting the second class gives (B10.1). \(\square\)

Now let \(K_+\) be the number of the endpoint's three non-backtracking neighbours already occupied somewhere by the walk, and let \(K_+^{(M)}\) count those occupied within reversed depth at most \(M\). Set

\[
L_{n,M}:=\mathbb E_n[K_+-K_+^{(M)}].
\]

Each remote-blocked marked direction is exactly one occupied extra neighbour whose unique visit lies deeper than \(M\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|B_{n,M}\|_1=\sigma_n L_{n,M}.
}
\tag{B10.2}
\]

Writing

\[
q_{n,M}:=N_{n,M}/\sigma_n,
\qquad
b_{n,M}:=B_{n,M}/\sigma_n,
\qquad
r_n:=\sigma_{n+1}/\sigma_n,
\]

gives the exact normalized recurrence

\[
\boxed{
r_n q_{n+1,M}=A_Mq_{n,M}-b_{n,M},
\qquad
b_{n,M}\ge0,
\qquad
\|b_{n,M}\|_1=L_{n,M}.
}
\tag{B10.3}
\]

This is the desired endpoint-moving parity switch in finite-memory form. It changes length by one, it has inverse multiplicity one on successful moves, and its entire error is the already-identified remote-contact tail.

### Refutation check

The tempting stronger statement `B_{n,M}=0` is false for every fixed \(M\): the preserved delayed-contact family \(W_m\) produces endpoint neighbours whose first visit occurs after arbitrarily large odd depth. Thus the finite-memory transfer is not a proof by itself; the remote defect is genuine and exactly source-relevant.

# B25 — double-count both endpoints: exact two-ended adjacent-length identity

The one-ended transfer does not directly control

\[
S_n^{(M)}=K_0^{(M)}K_+^{(M)},
\]

because that observable sees both endpoints. The natural double count is to allow the parity switch at **either** endpoint.

Let \(Q_{n,M}\) denote the probability distribution, under the uniform \(n\)-step SAW, of the ordered pair of local endpoint windows

\[
(U_M,V_M)\in X_M\times X_M,
\]

where \(U_M\) is the forward window from the start and \(V_M\) is the reversed window from the terminal endpoint. Translation of a walk after prepending an edge does not alter either local shape except for the endpoint being updated.

On \(X_M\times X_M\), define

\[
T_M:=A_M\otimes I+I\otimes A_M.
\]

The first summand locally prepends a step at the start; the second locally appends a step at the end.

Let \(C_{n,M}\) be the unnormalized vector of two-ended state counts, so \(Q_{n,M}=C_{n,M}/\sigma_n\). Let \(R_{n,M}\) count all marked locally admissible start/end proposals that are remotely blocked, grouped by the would-be local child state.

Then the exact marked double count is

\[
\boxed{
2C_{n+1,M}=T_MC_{n,M}-R_{n,M}.
}
\tag{B25.1}
\]

### Proof

Every locally admissible marked proposal is either legal or remotely blocked, exactly as in B10. A legal terminal proposal has inverse obtained by deleting the last edge. A legal start proposal has inverse obtained by deleting the first edge and translating the remaining walk so that its new start is the origin. Conversely every \((n+1)\)-step rooted SAW has exactly two such marked parents: delete its first edge or delete its last edge. Therefore the legal part of \(T_MC_{n,M}\) is exactly \(2C_{n+1,M}\), and subtracting the blocked proposals proves (B25.1). \(\square\)

By reversal symmetry the expected late-contact count is the same at the two endpoints, hence

\[
\boxed{
\|R_{n,M}\|_1=2\sigma_nL_{n,M}.
}
\tag{B25.2}
\]

Normalize \(r_{n}=\sigma_{n+1}/\sigma_n\) and \(d_{n,M}:=R_{n,M}/\sigma_n\). Then

\[
\boxed{
2r_nQ_{n+1,M}=T_MQ_{n,M}-d_{n,M},
\qquad
\|d_{n,M}\|_1=2L_{n,M}.
}
\tag{B25.3}
\]

The total mass of \(T_MQ_{n,M}\) is therefore

\[
2(r_n+L_{n,M}).
\]

Define the **pure local proposal law**

\[
\widetilde Q_{n+1,M}:=
\frac{T_MQ_{n,M}}{2(r_n+L_{n,M})}.
\]

If \(L_{n,M}>0\), put \(\Beta_{n,M}:=d_{n,M}/(2L_{n,M})\); otherwise take \(\Beta_{n,M}\) arbitrary. Equation (B25.3) becomes the exact mixture identity

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde Q_{n+1,M}
=
\frac{r_n}{r_n+L_{n,M}}Q_{n+1,M}
+
\frac{L_{n,M}}{r_n+L_{n,M}}\Beta_{n,M}.
}
\tag{B25.4}
\]

Interpretation: choose a uniform \(n\)-SAW, mark one of its two endpoints and one of the locally admissible directions with the natural counting weight, and forget whether the target was globally occupied. The resulting local child law is a mixture of the true \((n+1)\)-SAW endpoint law and the remote-blocker law. The exact rejection probability is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{L_{n,M}}{r_n+L_{n,M}}.
}
\tag{B25.5}
\]

This is the requested global source-valid density identity: the unmatched mass is *precisely* remote-blocked endpoint contacts. No uncontrolled collision class and no unknown inverse multiplicity remain.

## Consequence for the finite-depth slack parity defect

For a local endpoint window \(x\), let \(\kappa_M(x)\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) be the number of extra endpoint neighbours represented within that window. On pairs define

\[
F_M(u,v):=\kappa_M(u)\kappa_M(v).
\]

Thus \(F_M=S^{(M)}\) and \(0\le F_M\le9\). From (B25.4),

\[
\left|
\mathbb E_{Q_{n+1,M}}F_M
-
\mathbb E_{\widetilde Q_{n+1,M}}F_M
\right|
\le
9\frac{L_{n,M}}{r_n+L_{n,M}}.
\tag{B25.6}
\]

Define the purely finite-memory local drift

\[
\Gamma_M
:=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\left|
\frac{\langle F_M,T_MQ_{n,M}\rangle}
     {\langle \mathbf 1,T_MQ_{n,M}\rangle}
-
\langle F_M,Q_{n,M}\rangle
\right|.
\tag{B25.7}
\]

The first term is exactly \(\mathbb E_{\widetilde Q_{n+1,M}}F_M\). Hence

\[
\delta_M
\le
\Gamma_M+
9\limsup_n\frac{L_{n,M}}{r_n+L_{n,M}}.
\tag{B25.8}
\]

Every square-lattice SAW has at most three one-step continuations, so \(r_{n+1}\le3\). Kesten's theorem gives \(r_nr_{n+1}\to\mu^2\); consequently, for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[
r_n\ge \frac{\mu^2}{6}.
\]

By reversal symmetry and the preserved exact lollipop-tail identification,

\[
\limsup_n L_{n,M}=\mathcal L_M.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\delta_M
\le
\Gamma_M+rac{54}{\mu^2}\,\mathcal L_M.
}
\tag{B25.9}
\]

This is a real reduction, but **not source closure and not frozen-metric movement**. It replaces the vague phrase “find an adjacent-parity coupling” by one finite-dimensional quantity \(\Gamma_M\) plus the already existing exact late-contact kernel. The remaining question is whether the normalized finite-memory transfer has enough aperiodic mixing to force \(\Gamma_M\) small in a way that survives \(M\to\infty\).

# B30 — one contact depth at a time: exact multiscale gluing obstruction

For each non-backtracking neighbour label \(\alpha\) of an endpoint, let

\[
\tau_+^\alpha(\omega)
\]

be the reversed path depth at which that neighbouring vertex was visited, with value \(\infty\) if it is unoccupied. Because \(\mathbb Z^2\) is bipartite, a finite \(\tau_+^\alpha\) is odd.

For fixed \(M\), the late-contact count is exactly

\[
K_+-K_+^{(M)}
=
\sum_\alpha \mathbf 1\{M<\tau_+^\alpha<\infty\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
L_{n,M}
=
\sum_\alpha\sum_{\substack{t>M\\ t\text{ odd}}}
\mathbb P_n(\tau_+^\alpha=t).
}
\tag{B30.1}
\]

The blocked vector itself decomposes by the unique first-contact depth:

\[
\boxed{
B_{n,M}
=
\sum_{\substack{t>M\\ t\text{ odd}}}B_{n,M;t},
}
\tag{B30.2}
\]

with disjoint marked classes. Thus every scale contributes honestly and exactly once.

If \(M'<M\), then

\[
\boxed{
L_{n,M'}-L_{n,M}
=
\sum_\alpha
\sum_{\substack{M'<t\le M\\ t\text{ odd}}}
\mathbb P_n(\tau_+^\alpha=t).
}
\tag{B30.3}
\]

So the obstruction to gluing finite-memory endpoint couplings across scales is not a new error term: it is precisely the mass in the first-contact shells. The earlier lollipop/polygon decomposition identifies the limsup tail of (B30.1) with the same averaged diagonal kernel \(\mathcal L_M\).

This gives a clean interpretation of the campaign's blocker:

* finite-memory one-step parity transport is now exact up to a rejected-proposal mass;
* the rejected-proposal mass is exactly late endpoint contact;
* increasing \(M\) removes shells one at a time;
* the only way this local coupling fails to converge to the true one-step law is mass escaping to contact depth infinity.

The new point is not that \(\mathcal L_M\) has been proved to vanish — it has not — but that **the parity-coupling obstruction and the late-contact obstruction are now the same defect in one exact transfer equation**, rather than two unrelated obligations.

## What this does and does not prove

The hoped-for inequality

\[
\delta_M\le C\mathcal L_{M-c}+o_M(1)
\]

is not yet proved, because the local finite-memory drift \(\Gamma_M\) in (B25.9) is uncontrolled. A purely local state law can in principle retain even/odd amplitude information under the normalized transfer unless one proves a mixing/aperiodicity theorem for the relevant finite-memory operator and controls its constants as \(M\) grows.

Accordingly:

* **proved this run:** exact finite-memory transfer (B10.1)–(B10.3), exact two-ended double count (B25.1)–(B25.5), exact slack reduction (B25.9), and exact shell decomposition (B30.1)–(B30.3);
* **not proved:** \(\Gamma_M=0\), any uniform-in-\(M\) contraction, \(\mathcal L_M\to0\), \(\delta_M\to0\), or \(A_{\rm SAW}=0\).
