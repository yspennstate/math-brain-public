# 4. B13 — obstacle inclusion is monotone, but worst-case shielding cannot decay exponentially

The first question is whether the run-9 obstacle kernel has a monotone quantity strong enough to force decay as the polygon gets larger.

## 4.1 Exact obstacle-inclusion monotonicity

Fix the same root `alpha`. For two finite obstacles `P,Q`, write

\[
P\preceq Q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
V(P)\setminus\{\alpha\}
\subseteq
V(Q)\setminus\{\alpha\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then, directly from the definition of the avoiding-tail kernel,

\[
\boxed{
P\preceq Q\Longrightarrow H_m(Q)\le H_m(P)
\quad\text{for every }m.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

So obstacle inclusion really is a monovariant: adding forbidden vertices can only remove tails.

This statement is exact but by itself weak for polygons, because two different simple polygon boundaries rarely form a useful nested chain as vertex sets. More seriously, even very large polygons can leave a completely unobstructed half-plane channel at the root.

## 4.2 A supporting-rectangle family

Take

\[
0=(0,0),\qquad \alpha=(0,1),
\]

and for `k>=1` let `P_k` be the rectangular polygon bounding `[0,k]x[0,1]`, rooted at the edge `{0,alpha}`. Thus every polygon vertex has nonnegative first coordinate.

A tail may start from `alpha` with the step

\[
(0,1)\to(-1,1)
\]

and thereafter remain in the strict left half-plane

\[
\{(x,y):x\le -1\}.
\]

Such a tail never meets `P_k` again, for **any** `k`.

Let `b_m^{\leftarrow}` denote any standard left-oriented bridge subclass of `m`-step square-lattice SAWs contained in that half-plane after its initial step. Classical Hammersley--Welsh/Kesten bridge theory, also summarized in Grimmett--Li, gives bridge connective constant equal to the square-lattice connective constant:

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}
\left(b_m^{\leftarrow}\right)^{1/m}=\mu.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
H_m(P_k)\ge b_m^{\leftarrow}
\tag{4.4}
\]

for every `k,m` (up to the irrelevant one-step convention in the bridge definition), while always `H_m(P_k)<=sigma_m`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{m\to\infty}
\left(\frac{H_m(P_{k(m)})}{\sigma_m}\right)^{1/m}=1
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

for **every** choice of widths `k(m)>=1`.

In particular choose `k(m)=m`. The polygon perimeter then tends to infinity linearly with the tail length, but the normalized obstacle penalty still has zero exponential rate.

## Proposition 4.1 — no uniform pointwise exponential shielding by perimeter

There is no constant `c>0` and threshold `N` such that every rooted polygon `P` of perimeter `L>=N` and every tail length `m>=N` satisfy a pointwise estimate of the form

\[
\frac{H_m(P)}{\sigma_m}
\le
\exp\{-c\min(L,m)\}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Apply (4.6) to `P_m` and `m`. The right side has `m`th root at most `e^{-c}`, whereas (4.5) has `m`th root tending to one. Contradiction. ∎

The same argument rules out any proposed worst-case obstacle theorem whose only mechanism is an exponential penalty proportional to a macroscopic function of perimeter. A large obstacle can be entirely on one side of a supporting line and leave a half-plane bridge channel untouched.

### What this does **not** rule out

Run 9's `Theta_{L,m}` is an **average over all rooted polygons of perimeter `L`**, weighted through the exact kernel. One exceptional rectangle among exponentially many polygons does not preclude average decay. Thus Proposition 4.1 is a no-go for the wrong quantifier order, not evidence against the actual averaged route.

Outcome of B13: **proved exact obstacle monotonicity and a worst-case-shielding no-go; no metric movement.**

# 5. B17 — the half-plane/bridge literature explains the no-go, but supplies no needed amplitude estimate

The number in Proposition 4.1 is not accidental. The classical half-plane theory already says that imposing a one-sided geometric restriction need not change the connective-constant exponential rate.

Grimmett--Li's survey records that, on graphs with a unimodular height function, the bridge constant equals the connective constant. On `Z^2` this is the ordinary coordinate height. Dyhr--Gilbert--Kennedy--Lawler--Passon construct/review the infinite half-plane SAW from the irreducible-bridge decomposition and identify the critical fugacity as `1/mu`. Thus the support-root rectangles in Section 4 preserve a full half-plane family of tails of exponential rate `mu`.

This has two consequences for D0067.

First, any finite-obstacle argument that hopes for a **uniform exponential loss** merely because an obstacle has large perimeter is structurally impossible. The correct missing information, if it exists, must live in a subexponential amplitude, in an average over obstacle shapes, or in a coupling between obstacle shape and the diagonal tail length.

Second, the standard bridge renewal relation is not itself a parity bridge. It controls half-plane walks at the critical scale but does not compare the full-plane coefficients at lengths `n` and `n+1`. The bipartite charge obstruction from run 6 therefore survives the half-plane deformation.

A bounded search also checked modern neighboring results. The 2025 theorem of Lehner--Lindorfer--Panagiotis obtains precise/periodic counting on graphs with infinitely many ends, but `Z^2` has one end and their finite-state decomposition does not port directly. The checked half-plane/strip literature gives renewal and limiting measures, not a theorem of the form

\[
\frac{H_m(P)}{\sigma_m}
\le F(P,m)
\]

with an `L`-summable average strong enough for the run-9 contact-depth series.

This search failure is **not** promoted to novelty or impossibility. It identifies the nearest available machinery and the exact missing strengthening.

Outcome of B17: **the half-plane bridge theory validates B13's obstruction and redirects the problem from worst-case exponential shielding to averaged diagonal amplitude control. No source closure.**

# 6. B27 — swap the quantifiers: exact averaged diagonal obstacle kernel

Run 9 phrased one sufficient route using a uniform-in-tail estimate on the shielding factor. Proposition 4.1 shows that worst-case pointwise control is too ambitious. Fortunately, the source problem does not require it.

For even `L>=4` and `m>=0`, define the exact rooted obstacle kernel

\[
Q_{L,m}^{\alpha}
:=
\sum_{P\in\mathcal R_L(\alpha)}H_m(P).
\tag{6.1}
\]

By the lollipop identity (3.9),

\[
Q_{L,m}^{\alpha}=N_{L+m-1,L-1}^{\alpha}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Rotational symmetry makes this independent of the chosen nearest-neighbour direction `alpha`, so write simply `Q_{L,m}`.

Now define the one-end omitted-contact count

\[
R_{n,M}:=K_0-K_0^{(M)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Each late extra-neighbour contact is uniquely identified by its first hit time `t>M`, and `L=t+1`. Therefore the exact lollipop decomposition gives, with no inequality,

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_n\,\mathbb E R_{n,M}
=
4\sum_{\substack{L>M+1\\L\ \mathrm{even}}}^{n+1}
Q_{L,n-L+1}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E R_{n,M}
=
\frac4{\sigma_n}
\sum_{\substack{L>M+1\\L\ \mathrm{even}}}^{n+1}
Q_{L,n-L+1}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is the quantifier swap: only the **diagonal** values

\[
(L,m)=(L,n-L+1)
\]

appear, and they appear only after summing over all rooted polygons. There is no need to control every `P`, every `m`, or `sup_m Theta_{L,m}`.

Define the averaged diagonal tail

\[
\mathcal L_M
:=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac4{\sigma_n}
\sum_{\substack{L>M+1\\L\ \mathrm{even}}}^{n+1}
Q_{L,n-L+1}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Then (6.5) says exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_M
=
\limsup_n\mathbb E[K_0-K_0^{(M)}].
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

So the run-8 late-contact quantity now has a complete polygon-obstacle representation.

## 6.1 Exact parity-versus-diagonal-kernel obstruction

Run 7/8 proved the algebraic estimate

\[
\varepsilon_M
\le 6\mathcal L_M.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Insert (6.8) into the exact parity-tail inequality (3.8). For every finite `M`,

\[
\boxed{
12\mu\sinh A_{\rm SAW}
\le
\delta_M+12\mathcal L_M.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
A_{\rm SAW}
\le
\operatorname{arsinh}
\left(\frac{\delta_M+12\mathcal L_M}{12\mu}\right)
\quad\text{for every }M.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

This gives a source-closing criterion stated entirely in terms of a finite-depth parity defect and the **averaged diagonal obstacle kernel**:

## Theorem 6.1 — diagonal-kernel closure criterion

If

\[
\boxed{
\inf_M\{\delta_M+12\mathcal L_M\}=0,
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{r_n\to\mu.}
\tag{6.12}
\]

This is only conditional because neither term in (6.11) is known to vanish.

## 6.2 What a counterexample must do

Let `a=A_SAW`. Rearranging (6.9),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_M
\ge
\left[\mu\sinh a-\frac{\delta_M}{12}\right]_+
\quad\text{for every }M.
}
\tag{6.13}
\]

Thus, if finite-depth parity stabilizes (`delta_M->0`) but `a>0`, then

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{M\to\infty}\mathcal L_M
\ge\mu\sinh a>0.
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

In words: a counterexample with asymptotically parity-stable local endpoint views must carry a fixed positive amount of probability mass in polygon-with-tail configurations whose first-contact perimeter escapes beyond every fixed scale.

Conversely, if the averaged diagonal obstacle tail is tight (`mathcal L_M->0`) but `a>0`, then (6.9) forces the full oscillation to remain visible in the finite-depth parity defect:

\[
\liminf_{M\to\infty}\delta_M
\ge12\mu\sinh a.
\tag{6.15}
\]

The source obstruction is therefore now exactly localized to two alternatives. The support-root rectangles from B13 show why one cannot eliminate the first alternative using a worst-case shielding theorem, but they do **not** construct the positive averaged diagonal mass demanded by (6.14).

Outcome of B27: **exact quantifier-swapped kernel and a sharper formulation of the remaining obstruction; no frozen-metric movement.**
