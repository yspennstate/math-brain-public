# 3. B11 — dualize the whole finite contact spectrum

The previous `Lambda_contact` was generated by one explicit countable family of prefix cylinders. The natural dual object is not another larger hand-written family; it is the complete sigma-algebra generated by **finite endpoint contact depths**.

## 3.1 Contact-depth variables

Fix the three extra neighbours of the start after deleting the first-step neighbour. For each such neighbour `alpha`, let

\[
\tau_0^\alpha:=\inf\{j\ge2:\omega_j=\alpha\}\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}.
\]

At the terminal endpoint, reverse the walk and define analogously `tau_+^beta` for the three extra neighbours of `omega_n` relative to its predecessor.

Then exactly

\[
K_0=\sum_\alpha {\bf 1}_{\{\tau_0^\alpha<\infty\}},
\qquad
K_+=\sum_\beta {\bf 1}_{\{\tau_+^\beta<\infty\}}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For an integer window `M>=1`, define

\[
K_0^{(M)}:=\sum_\alpha {\bf 1}_{\{\tau_0^\alpha\le M\}},
\qquad
K_+^{(M)}:=\sum_\beta {\bf 1}_{\{\tau_+^\beta\le M\}},
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
S_n^{(M)}:=K_0^{(M)}K_+^{(M)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

These are canonical observables: `S_n^(M)` is determined by the first `M` steps seen from each endpoint, and

\[
0\le S_n^{(M)}\le S_n^{(M+1)}\le S_n\le9.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Define the complete finite-depth slack constant

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{\rm depth}
:=
\sup_{M\ge1}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E S_n^{(M)}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

The supremum is a monotone limit in `M`. It contains every fixed finite endpoint-pattern lower bound, regardless of how that pattern is encoded.

## 3.2 Canonical metric theorem

### Theorem 3.1

For square-lattice SAW,

\[
0\le \Lambda_{\rm depth}
\le \liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E S_n
\le (3-\mu)^2,
\tag{3.6}
\]

and, with

\[
U_{\rm depth}:=\frac{\mu^2+9-\Lambda_{\rm depth}}6,
\tag{3.7}
\]

one has

\[
\limsup_n r_n\le U_{\rm depth},
\qquad
\liminf_n r_n\ge\frac{\mu^2}{U_{\rm depth}},
\tag{3.8}
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
A_{\rm SAW}
\le
\log\!\left(\frac{\mu^2+9-\Lambda_{\rm depth}}{6\mu}\right).
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

#### Proof

For every fixed `M`, (3.4) gives

\[
\liminf_n\mathbb E S_n^{(M)}
\le
\liminf_n\mathbb E S_n.

\[
\liminf_n\mathbb E S_n
=
\mu^2+9-6\limsup_n r_n.
\tag{3.10}

Therefore (3.5) and (3.10) imply

\[
\limsup_n r_n
\le
\frac{\mu^2+9-\Lambda_{\rm depth}}6
=U_{\rm depth}.

Kesten's theorem gives

\[
r_nr_{n+1}\longrightarrow\mu^2.
\tag{3.11}

Consequently `limsup r_n>=mu`; otherwise both adjacent ratios would eventually lie strictly below `mu`, contradicting (3.11). Thus `U_depth>=mu`. Also (3.11) and the upper bound on `limsup r_n` give

\liminf_n r_n\ge\frac{\mu^2}{U_{\rm depth}}.

The upper and lower bounds are reciprocal around `mu`, which yields (3.9). Finally `U_depth>=mu` is equivalent to

\Lambda_{\rm depth}\le\mu^2+9-6\mu=(3-\mu)^2.

This proves the theorem. ∎

# 5. B30 — one depth scale at a time

The previous section identifies the exact gluing problem. This section reduces it to a one-end late-contact tail and then tests the closest available polygon/closing estimates.

## 5.1 A one-end tightness criterion

Let

\[
R_{n,M}:=S_n-S_n^{(M)}\ge0.
\]

For numbers `0<=a<=A<=3` and `0<=b<=B<=3`,

\[
AB-ab=(A-a)B+a(B-b)
\le3(A-a)+3(B-b).
\]

Applying this with `A=K_0`, `a=K_0^(M)`, `B=K_+`, `b=K_+^(M)` gives

\[
R_{n,M}
\le
3\bigl(K_0-K_0^{(M)}\bigr)
+3\bigl(K_+-K_+^{(M)}\bigr).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Reversal symmetry makes the two expectations equal, hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E R_{n,M}
\le
6\,\mathbb E\bigl[K_0-K_0^{(M)}\bigr].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because there are only three extra root neighbours,

\[
K_0-K_0^{(M)}
=
\sum_{\alpha}
{\bf 1}_{\{M<\tau_0^\alpha<\infty\}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore the exact missing compactness estimate is

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{M\to\infty}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\sum_{\alpha}
\mathbb P_n(M<\tau_0^\alpha<\infty)=0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

If (5.4) held, (5.2) would imply that the canonical finite-depth slack captures the full `liminf E S_n`. This would eliminate the “mass at infinity” obstruction. It would **not by itself** prove `A_SAW=0`; one would still need to identify the resulting finite-depth limit with `(3-mu)^2` or otherwise identify the even and odd endpoint laws. This distinction is important.

## 5.2 Fixed first-contact depth and a polygon reduction

Fix one extra neighbour `alpha` of the origin and an odd integer `t`. Let `c_t(0,alpha)` denote the number of `t`-step SAWs from `0` to `alpha`. If a length-`n` SAW first reaches `alpha` at time `t`, its first `t` steps form one of these prefixes. For each such prefix, forgetting all avoidance constraints against the prefix leaves at most `sigma_{n-t}` possible continuations. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_n(\tau_0^\alpha=t)
\le
c_t(0,\alpha)\frac{\sigma_{n-t}}{\sigma_n}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let

\[
U_c:=\frac{\mu^2+9-\Lambda_{\rm contact}}6,
\]

the already certified pre-run upper bound on `limsup r_n`. For fixed odd `t`, `t-1` is even, so Kesten's two-step ratio theorem gives

\[
\frac{\sigma_{n-t}}{\sigma_{n-1}}\longrightarrow\mu^{-(t-1)}.
\]

The previous metric interval gives

\[
\limsup_n\frac{1}{r_{n-1}}
\le\frac{U_c}{\mu^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\frac{\sigma_{n-t}}{\sigma_n}
\le U_c\mu^{-(t+1)}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.5)--(5.6), for every **fixed** odd `t`,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\mathbb P_n(\tau_0^\alpha=t)
\le
U_c\,c_t(0,\alpha)\mu^{-(t+1)}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Closing the prefix by the missing edge from `alpha` to `0` produces a self-avoiding polygon of length `t+1`. The standard closing identity

\[
\mathsf W_t(\Gamma\text{ closes})
=
\frac{2(t+1)p_{t+1}}{\sigma_t}
\]

and fourfold symmetry of the possible neighbouring endpoints give

\[
\boxed{
c_t(0,\alpha)=\frac{t+1}{2}\,p_{t+1}.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Thus the fixed-depth estimate becomes

\[
\limsup_n\mathbb P_n(\tau_0^\alpha=t)
\le
\frac{U_c}{2}(t+1)p_{t+1}\mu^{-(t+1)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

## 5.3 Why the known polygon estimates do not glue the scales

The desired quantity (5.4) contains first-contact depths that may grow with `n`. Equations (5.6)--(5.9) are justified for each **fixed** `t`; they do not furnish a ratio estimate uniform in a growing `t`. This is the first gluing obstruction.

Even pretending that the fixed-`t` estimate could be uniformized, the known polygon estimates are not summable at the required strength. Madras's square-lattice estimate quoted by Hammond is

\[
p_m\le C m^{-1/2}\mu^m,
\]

which inserted into (5.9) gives only an order-`m^{1/2}` upper expression before the trivial probability cap. Hammond's stronger square-lattice polygon estimate

\[
p_m\le m^{-3/2+o(1)}\mu^m
\]

holds on a **full-density set** of even lengths, not all lengths, and after multiplication by the factor `m` in (5.8) it gives `m^{-1/2+o(1)}`, still not a summable all-length majorant. The all-length closing-probability bound `m^{-1/2+o(1)}` has the same scale problem.

So current published closing/polygon technology does not imply (5.4) by a direct union bound.

## 5.4 Dyadic formulation of the exact obstruction

For `M>=1`, decompose the late-contact event into dyadic shells

\[
2^jM<\tau_0^\alpha\le2^{j+1}M,
\qquad j=0,1,2,\ldots.
\tag{5.10}
\]

For each fixed `n`, the shell probabilities sum to at most one for a given neighbour, and all three neighbours contribute at most three. But taking `sup_n` before summing destroys that conservation:

\[
\sup_n\sum_j p_{n,j}
\le
\sum_j\sup_n p_{n,j},
\tag{5.11}
\]

and the right side need not be small or summable. A distribution whose mass moves to shell `j=j(n)` is the abstract point-at-infinity failure mode. The geometric `W_m` family proves that arbitrarily remote shells are genuinely realizable by proper SAW endpoint patterns.

Thus the exact missing theorem is now localized:

\[
\boxed{
\text{prove a parity-insensitive, uniform-in-}n\text{ tail bound for late first visits to the three extra endpoint neighbours.}
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is stronger than proving positivity for every fixed end pattern and different from another finite pattern enumeration.
