# B23 — put one odd-cycle defect into the category

## 1. Triangular augmentation with a diagonal activity

Write \(e_1=(1,0)\), \(e_2=(0,1)\). Add to the square lattice the diagonal edges
\[
\{x,x+e_1+e_2\},\qquad x\in\mathbb Z^2,
\]
and their reversals. At positive diagonal weight this is the usual degree-six triangular lattice in a linear coordinate representation.

For an \(n\)-step SAW \(\omega\) on this augmented graph, let \(d(\omega)\) be its number of diagonal edges and define
\[
C_n(z):=\sum_{\omega}z^{d(\omega)}
      =\sum_{k=0}^n a_{n,k}z^k,
\qquad z\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
a_{n,0}=\sigma_n,
\qquad
C_n'(0)=a_{n,1},
\]
so the first derivative at the bipartite point counts **exactly one** parity-breaking diagonal use. Multiple diagonal uses do not contaminate the derivative.

## 2. Exact one-defect expansion identity

Let \(\mathcal D_n\) be the set of augmented \(n\)-step SAWs with exactly one diagonal, so \(|\mathcal D_n|=a_{n,1}\). For \(\gamma\in\mathcal D_n\), let \(u,v\) be the endpoints of its unique diagonal. They have exactly two common square-lattice neighbours. Define
\[
E(\gamma):=\#\{\text{those two common neighbours not visited by }\gamma\}\in\{0,1,2\},
\]
and
\[
B_{n,j}:=\#\{\gamma\in\mathcal D_n:E(\gamma)=j\}.
\]
Let \(T^+_{n+1}\) denote the total number of marked two-edge turns among all square-lattice \((n+1)\)-step SAWs whose two-step displacement is \(\pm(e_1+e_2)\), i.e. precisely the turns that can be shortcut by the allowed diagonal.

There is an exact marked bijection:

- given \((\gamma,c)\), where \(\gamma\in\mathcal D_n\) and \(c\) is one of its unvisited common neighbours, replace the unique diagonal \(u\!\leftrightarrow\!v\) by \(u\!\to c\!\to v\); this yields a square-lattice \((n+1)\)-SAW with a marked allowed turn;
- conversely, shortcut a marked allowed turn \(u\!\to c\!\to v\) to the diagonal \(u\!\leftrightarrow\!v\).

Deleting the middle corner cannot create a repeated vertex, and expansion is legal exactly when the inserted corner was not already visited. Therefore
\[
\boxed{T^+_{n+1}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal D_n}E(\gamma)=B_{n,1}+2B_{n,2}.}
\]
Since
\[
a_{n,1}=B_{n,0}+B_{n,1}+B_{n,2},
\]
we also have the exact defect formula
\[
\boxed{2C_n'(0)-T^+_{n+1}=2B_{n,0}+B_{n,1}\ge0.}
\]
This is the precise correction to the optimistic idea that a one-diagonal walk is just a square walk with one turn shortened.

## 3. Counterexample to the naive expansion lemma

The correction is genuinely present at the smallest scales. For example,
\[
(0,0)\to(1,0)\to(0,-1)\to(1,-1)
\]
is a three-step augmented SAW whose middle edge is the diagonal \((-1,-1)\). The two square common neighbours of the diagonal endpoints are \((0,0)\) and \((1,-1)\), both already visited. Thus \(E(\gamma)=0\). So the statement "every one-diagonal SAW can be expanded to a square SAW" is false.

The exact checker enumerated the square and one-diagonal classes through \(n=8\). It verified
\[
T^+_{n+1}=B_{n,1}+2B_{n,2},
\qquad
2a_{n,1}-T^+_{n+1}=2B_{n,0}+B_{n,1}
\]
at every tested length, and explicitly found \(B_{n,0}>0\) from \(n=3\) onward. This is validation of the finite instances only; the displayed identities above are proved combinatorially for every \(n\).

## 4. Exact relation to the depth-3 obstruction

If a terminal two-edge square turn is shortcut to a final diagonal, the removed turn-corner is automatically one legal expansion corner. The *other* corner of that plaquette gives exactly the previous state split:

- other corner unvisited: open terminal state, and the one-defect walk has two legal expansions;
- other corner occupied by the immediately preceding plaquette vertex: terminal hook;
- other corner occupied farther back in the walk: remote-blocked state.

The last two both appear as a one-defect walk with only one legal expansion. Thus the diagonal defect cleanly packages the open-versus-occupied corner bit, but it **does not distinguish a local hook from a remote blocker**. The unbounded contact-depth hierarchy therefore survives the parity-breaking defect; it is not an artifact of the earlier depth-3 encoding.

# B5 — differentiate the parity-breaking activity

## 5. Weighted connective constant and elementary continuity at `z=0`

The weighted coefficients are submultiplicative:
\[
C_{m+n}(z)\le C_m(z)C_n(z),
\]
because splitting a weighted SAW after \(m\) steps gives a pair of weighted SAWs and the edge weights multiply. Hence
\[
\mu(z):=\lim_{n\to\infty}C_n(z)^{1/n}
       =\inf_{n\ge1}C_n(z)^{1/n}.
\]
For every \(z\ge0\), \(C_n(z)\ge C_n(0)=\sigma_n\), so \(\mu(z)\ge\mu\). Conversely, for every fixed \(N\),
\[
\mu(z)\le C_N(z)^{1/N}.
\]
Taking \(z\downarrow0\), then \(N\to\infty\), gives
\[
\boxed{\mu(z)\longrightarrow\mu\quad(z\downarrow0).}
\]
This finite-support argument is consistent with the much more general weighted-connective-constant continuity theorem of Grimmett--Li.

The finite-size susceptibility at the bipartite point is
\[
\chi_n:=\frac1n\left.\partial_z\log C_n(z)\right|_{z=0}
       =\frac{a_{n,1}}{n\sigma_n}.
\]
The exact B23 identity gives
\[
\chi_n\ge \frac{T^+_{n+1}}{2n\sigma_n}.
\]
Choose a proper internal Kesten pattern containing one designated allowed-orientation turn. The pattern theorem implies that, outside an exponentially smaller exceptional family, a square SAW contains linearly many translated copies of that pattern. Consequently there exists a constant \(c_{\rm pat}>0\), independent of \(n\), such that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
T^+_{n+1}\ge c_{\rm pat}\,n\,\sigma_{n+1},
\]
up to harmless adjustment of the constant, and therefore
\[
\boxed{\liminf_n\chi_n>0.}
\]
The first parity-breaking sector is therefore extensive; the defect activity is not a negligible boundary observable.

What is missing is convergence of \(\chi_n\), or a relation eliminating the blocked classes \(B_{n,0},B_{n,1}\). The exact correction above shows why differentiating at zero does not by itself yield \(r_n\): the derivative counts one-diagonal walks that may have no square expansion at all, and the obstruction is again remote occupancy.

# B6 — deformation to the non-bipartite point and a sharp no-go

At \(z=1\), the support is the ordinary triangular lattice. The classical ratio-limit theory, as summarized explicitly by Rechnitzer--Janse van Rensburg with reference to Madras--Slade, gives the one-step ratio limit on non-bipartite lattices such as the triangular lattice. Thus the two endpoints of the deformation have very different known status:

- \(z=0\): square lattice, source problem open;
- \(z=1\): triangular lattice, one-step ratio limit known.

The weighted connective constant \(\mu(z)\) is continuous as \(z\downarrow0\). However, the bounded literature search did **not** locate a coefficient-ratio theorem for every positive anisotropic weight \(z\), still less one uniform as \(z\downarrow0\).

More importantly, even granting such a fixed-\(z\) theorem would not be enough. The following scalar construction refutes that transfer principle.

## 6. Boundary-layer no-go lemma

Fix \(q>1\), and define
\[
b_n=\begin{cases}
1,&n\text{ even},\\
q,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(b_{m+n}\le b_mb_n\). Define positive polynomial partition functions
\[
X_n(z):=\mu^n\bigl(b_n+(1+z)^n\bigr),\qquad z\ge0.
\]
Each of the two summands is submultiplicative in \(n\), hence so is their sum (the product \(X_mX_n\) contains the two required diagonal terms plus positive cross terms).

At the bipartite point,
\[
X_n(0)=\mu^n(b_n+1),
\]
so
\[
\frac{X_{n+2}(0)}{X_n(0)}=\mu^2
\]
**exactly**, while the one-step ratios alternate between
\[
\mu\frac{q+1}{2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mu\frac{2}{q+1}.
\]
Thus the one-step ratio does not converge.

For every fixed \(z>0\), on the other hand, \((1+z)^n\) dominates the bounded parity term, so
\[
\boxed{\frac{X_{n+1}(z)}{X_n(z)}\longrightarrow\mu(1+z).}
\]
The exponential growth rate is \(\mu(1+z)\) for \(z>0\), equals \(\mu\) at \(z=0\), and is continuous at zero. Even the normalized derivative can retain the hidden parity:
\[
\frac{X_n'(0)}{nX_n(0)}=\frac1{b_n+1},
\]
which alternates.

Finally, at the critical boundary-layer scale \(z=c/n\),
\[
\frac{X_n(c/n)}{\mu^n}
=b_n+\left(1+\frac cn\right)^n
\longrightarrow b_n+e^c,
\]
so the even/odd distinction survives precisely on the \(z=\Theta(1/n)\) scale.

This is an **abstract scalar no-go, not a SAW counterexample**. It proves that the following package of information is logically insufficient to settle D0067:

1. submultiplicativity;
2. positive polynomial dependence on a parity-breaking activity;
3. exact/two-step ratio regularity at \(z=0\);
4. one-step ratio convergence for every fixed \(z>0\);
5. continuity of the weighted connective constant at \(z=0\).

A successful deformation proof needs genuinely SAW-specific **uniform coefficient control through the degenerating boundary layer \(z=O(1/n)\)** (or another theorem strong enough to exchange \(n\to\infty\) with \(z\downarrow0\)). Ordinary connective-constant continuity cannot do this.
