# 4. B1 — exact polygon-with-tail obstacle kernel

The crude fixed-depth estimate of run 8 counted a late contact by a polygon prefix and then replaced the remainder by an unrestricted SAW count. The information lost in that step is exactly that the remainder must avoid the polygon. Here that constraint is retained.

Fix a nearest neighbour `alpha` of the origin. For a SAW

\[
\omega=(\omega_0=0,\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_n),
\]

write

\[
\tau_\alpha(\omega)
:=\min\{j\ge1:\omega_j=\alpha\},
\]

with `infinity` if `alpha` is never visited. If `t>=3` and `tau_alpha=t`, bipartiteness forces `t` odd.

Let

\[
N_{n,t}^{\alpha}
:=\#\{\omega\in\mathrm{SAW}_n(0):\tau_\alpha=t\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Set

\[
L:=t+1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The prefix

\[
(0=\omega_0,\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_t=\alpha)
\]

plus the missing edge `{alpha,0}` is a simple square-lattice polygon of perimeter `L`. Conversely, deleting that root edge from such a rooted polygon gives exactly one prefix from `0` to `alpha` of length `t`.

Let `R_L(alpha)` denote the resulting finite set of rooted embedded polygons, with the root edge directed from `alpha` to `0`. For `P in R_L(alpha)`, let `H_m(P)` be the number of length-`m` SAWs starting at `alpha` whose subsequent vertices avoid every vertex of `P` other than their initial vertex `alpha`. Equivalently, `H_m(P)` is the number of tails that may be concatenated to the polygon prefix without violating self-avoidance.

## Theorem 4.1 — exact lollipop decomposition

For every fixed odd `t>=3` and every `n>=t`,

\[
\boxed{
N_{n,t}^{\alpha}
=
\sum_{P\in\mathcal R_L(\alpha)}H_{n-t}(P).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

Given a walk counted by the left-hand side, its first `t` edges form the unique rooted polygon `P` after adding `{alpha,0}`, and its final `n-t` edges form a tail counted by `H_{n-t}(P)`. Because the original walk is self-avoiding, the tail intersects the prefix only at `alpha`.

Conversely, a rooted polygon `P` together with a tail counted by `H_{n-t}(P)` concatenates to a unique `n`-step SAW whose first visit to `alpha` is exactly at time `t`. No prefix can visit `alpha` earlier because it is itself self-avoiding and ends at `alpha`. These two maps are inverse. ∎

This gives an exact one-end coefficient identity. Since `K_0` is the number of neighbours of the origin visited after time `1`,

\[
\sigma_n\mathbb E K_0
=
\sum_{\alpha\sim0}
\sum_{\substack{3\le t\le n\\t\ \mathrm{odd}}}
N_{n,t}^{\alpha}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Using `E K_0=3-r_n`,

\[
\boxed{
3\sigma_n-\sigma_{n+1}
=
\sum_{\alpha\sim0}
\sum_{\substack{3\le t\le n\\t\ \mathrm{odd}}}
\sum_{P\in\mathcal R_{t+1}(\alpha)}
H_{n-t}(P).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equation (4.5) is the desired category change: the one-step ratio is encoded exactly by a sum of polygon-with-tail kernels in finite polygon-complement domains. It is not a scalar renewal convolution because `H_m(P)` retains the full obstacle shape `P`. That shape dependence is precisely the information discarded by the earlier product bound.

A generating-function version follows formally. If

\[
H_P(z):=\sum_{m\ge0}H_m(P)z^m,
\]

then the coefficient of `z^n` in the right-hand side of

\[
\sum_{\alpha\sim0}
\sum_{\substack{L\ge4\\L\ \mathrm{even}}}
 z^{L-1}
\sum_{P\in\mathcal R_L(\alpha)}H_P(z)
\tag{4.6}
\]

is exactly `3 sigma_n-sigma_{n+1}` for `n>=1`. The unresolved issue is therefore a boundary/obstacle coefficient regularity problem, not merely polygon enumeration.

Outcome of B1: **new exact structural decomposition, but no source closure and no frozen-metric movement.**

# 5. B26 — convex/straight/concave roots and the exterior-channel identity

The exact kernel exposes a local distinction that the crude product estimate ignores. A long tail cannot leave a rooted polygon through every non-polygon edge: whether an edge points into the finite interior or the unbounded exterior is determined by the local turn at the root.

For a simple square-lattice polygon `P` and a vertex `v in P`, define

\[
k_{\rm out}(P,v)
:=\#\{\text{non-polygon nearest-neighbour edges from }v
\text{ entering the unbounded component of }\mathbb Z^2\setminus P\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

At a square-lattice polygon vertex there are only three local types:

- convex 90-degree corner: both unused edges point outside, so `k_out=2`;
- straight vertex: one unused edge points outside and one inside, so `k_out=1`;
- concave 270-degree corner: both unused edges point inside, so `k_out=0`.

Let `C(P), S(P), R(P)` be the numbers of convex, straight and concave vertices. For an orthogonal simple polygon,

\[
C(P)-R(P)=4,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
C(P)+S(P)+R(P)=L.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{v\in P}k_{\rm out}(P,v)
=2C(P)+S(P)
=L+4.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is the discrete curvature / exterior-channel identity.

## 5.1 Fixed root direction

Let `p_L` be the standard number of length-`L` square-lattice self-avoiding polygons up to translation. Counting directed polygon edges in a fixed lattice direction gives the standard closing identity

\[
|\mathcal R_L(\alpha)|
=c_{L-1}(0,\alpha)
=\frac{L}{2}p_L.
\tag{5.5}
\]

There are `2L` directed edges per polygon. Summing `k_out` over directed edges counts each vertex twice, so (5.4) gives total weighted mass `2(L+4)` per polygon. Rotation by the four lattice directions therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{P\in\mathcal R_L(\alpha)}
 k_{\rm out}(P,\alpha)
=
\frac{L+4}{2}p_L.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Equation (5.6) is exact and is stronger than merely saying that some rooted polygons are locally bad.

## 5.2 Long tails must leave through the exterior

Fix `L`. Because there are only finitely many rooted polygons in `R_L(alpha)`, there is a finite number `M_L` larger than the number of lattice vertices in the bounded interior of every such polygon.

A tail counted by `H_m(P)` cannot cross the polygon boundary: it cannot cross a lattice edge geometrically without meeting a boundary vertex, and all boundary vertices except its starting point `alpha` are forbidden. Thus, if its first step enters the bounded interior, it remains there. For `m>M_L`, such an interior tail is impossible by self-avoidance.

Consequently, for `m>M_L`, every tail begins along one of the `k_out(P,alpha)` exterior edges. For a prescribed first lattice direction, the total number of unrestricted `m`-step SAWs is exactly `sigma_m/4` by rotational symmetry. Therefore

\[
H_m(P)
\le
\frac{k_{\rm out}(P,\alpha)}4\,\sigma_m,
\qquad m>M_L.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Summing (5.7) with (5.6) and invoking the exact kernel (4.3) proves:

## Theorem 5.1 — exterior-channel lollipop bound

For fixed odd `t>=3`, with `L=t+1`, and all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
\boxed{
N_{n,t}^{\alpha}
\le
\frac{L+4}{8}p_L\,\sigma_{n-t}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Now use the current D0067 ratio interval. From the frozen bound,

\[
\limsup_j r_j\le U_{\rm depth},
\tag{5.9}
\]

and Kesten's product relation `r_j r_{j+1}->mu^2` gives

\[
\liminf_j r_j\ge\frac{\mu^2}{U_{\rm depth}}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

For fixed odd `t`,

\[
\frac{\sigma_{n-t}}{\sigma_n}
=
\frac1{r_{n-t}}
\frac{\sigma_{n-t+1}}{\sigma_n}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

The second factor spans the even offset `t-1`, so Kesten's theorem gives

\[
\frac{\sigma_{n-t+1}}{\sigma_n}
\longrightarrow
\mu^{-(t-1)}.
\tag{5.12}
\]

Combining (5.10)--(5.12),

\[
\limsup_n\frac{\sigma_{n-t}}{\sigma_n}
\le
U_{\rm depth}\mu^{-(t+1)}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Divide (5.8) by `sigma_n` to obtain

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb P_n(\tau_\alpha=t)
\le
\frac{L+4}{8}p_L U_{\rm depth}\mu^{-L}.

\tag{5.14}

Using `c_t(0,alpha)=Lp_L/2`, equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\mathbb P_n(\tau_\alpha=t)
\le
\frac{c_t(0,\alpha)}4
\left(1+\frac4L\right)
U_{\rm depth}\mu^{-L}.

\tag{5.15}
\]

The run-8 deletion estimate was

\[
\limsup_n\mathbb P_n(\tau_\alpha=t)
\le

\tag{5.16}

The ratio of the new right-hand side to (5.16) is

\tag{5.17}

Since `L>=4`, `U_depth <= (mu^2+9)/6`, and the rigorous square-lattice bound `mu>2.625622` is more than enough to make (5.17) strictly less than one, (5.14) is a genuine fixed-`t` sharpening of the previous bound.

\tag{5.19}
\]

Thus the curvature correction sharpens every fixed depth but does not resolve the uniform `M->infinity` obstruction.

## 5.3 The remaining shielding factor

\[
\Theta_{L,m}

\frac{
4\sum_{P\in\mathcal R_L(\alpha)}H_m(P)

\tag{5.20}

# 6. B10 — deterministic collision-free even-length compensation

Run 8's failed rerouting attack identified two difficulties: a replacement gadget may collide with a remote part of the shortened walk, and even if it does not, its inverse multiplicity may be uncontrolled. The first difficulty can actually be removed deterministically.

## Lemma 6.1 — every finite lattice SAW has a bounding-box side edge

Let `eta` be any nontrivial finite nearest-neighbour SAW in `Z^2`, and let `B(eta)` be its axis-aligned bounding rectangle. Then `eta` contains an edge lying along a side of `B(eta)`.

### Proof

Take a vertex of maximal `y`-coordinate. If such a vertex is an internal path vertex, its two path neighbours are distinct. There is only one nearest neighbour strictly below it, so the two path edges cannot both point downward; at least one is horizontal and lies along the top side of the bounding rectangle.

If every topmost vertex is an endpoint and no top-side edge occurs, apply the same argument to the bottom side. If top and bottom extrema consume the two endpoints, consider a rightmost vertex. If it is internal, one of its path edges lies vertically along the right side. If it is an endpoint, that endpoint is simultaneously a top or bottom extremum, hence a corner of the bounding rectangle; every nearest-neighbour edge from that corner that remains inside the rectangle lies along one of the two incident boundary sides. Degenerate one-dimensional bounding boxes are immediate. ∎

Fix a deterministic tie-breaking rule and choose one such boundary edge `A B`. Let `n_out` be its unit outward normal. For any integer `k>=1`, replace the single edge `AB` by

\[
A,\ A+n_{\rm out},\ldots,A+k n_{\rm out},
B+k n_{\rm out},\ldots,B+n_{\rm out},\ B.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This detour has length `2k+1`, so it adds exactly `2k` edges. Every new internal vertex lies strictly outside the old bounding rectangle, and therefore cannot collide with `eta`; the detour itself is simple.

## Corollary 6.2 — exact-length restoration after deleting a late-contact polygon

Deleting a `t`-step polygon prefix and replacing it by the direct root edge shortens the walk by

\[
t-1,
\]

which is even because `t` is odd. Put `k=(t-1)/2`. Applying (6.1) to a deterministically chosen boundary edge of the shortened walk restores exactly the original total length with no collision.

This removes **global collision avoidance** as a fundamental existence obstruction to length compensation.

However, it does **not** yield a useful counting injection. The shortened walk no longer remembers which rooted polygon prefix `P` was deleted. The rectangular hairpin depends only on the deficit `t-1`, not on the exponentially many possible polygon shapes. Distinct inputs with the same shortened tail therefore collapse onto the same compensated walk. To turn the construction into a useful inequality one must encode enough of `P` in the output while retaining controlled inverse multiplicity. Using a copy of `P` itself at a supporting boundary edge can preserve shape information only after choosing an extreme edge and handling the original root-edge mark; no bounded inverse multiplicity or entropy gain was proved in this run.

Thus the exact diagnosis is sharper than the previous one:

> Collision-free even-length compensation is easy; **information-preserving compensation with quantitatively controlled inverse multiplicity is hard.**

Outcome of B10: **deterministic collision problem solved at the existence level; no counting improvement sufficient for the all-depth tail.**
