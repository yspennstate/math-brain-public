# B2 — attack the negation through the exact one-defect susceptibility

Recall the triangular augmentation from the previous run: add diagonal edges with displacement \(\pm(e_1+e_2)\) and give each diagonal activity \(z\). Let
\[
C_n(z)=\sum_{k=0}^n a_{n,k}z^k,
\qquad a_{n,0}=\sigma_n,
\qquad C_n'(0)=a_{n,1}.
\]
For exactly-one-diagonal walks, write \(B_{n,j}\) for the number having exactly \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\) legal square-lattice expansions of their unique diagonal. Let \(T^+_{n+1}\) be the total number of marked square-lattice two-edge turns in \((n+1)\)-step SAWs whose displacement is \(\pm(e_1+e_2)\). The previous run proved exactly
\[
T^+_{n+1}=B_{n,1}+2B_{n,2},
\qquad
2a_{n,1}-T^+_{n+1}=2B_{n,0}+B_{n,1}.
\]
Define the normalized quantities
\[
\chi_n:=\frac{a_{n,1}}{n\sigma_n},
\quad
\tau_m:=\frac{T^+_m}{m\sigma_m},
\quad
\beta_n:=\frac{2B_{n,0}+B_{n,1}}{2n\sigma_n}\ge0.
\]
Then the one-defect identity becomes the exact susceptibility decomposition
\[
\boxed{
\chi_n=\frac{n+1}{2n}\,r_n\,\tau_{n+1}+\beta_n,
\qquad r_n:=\frac{\sigma_{n+1}}{\sigma_n}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
This equation makes the compensation problem explicit: an oscillation of \(r_n\) can be hidden only by parity dependence in the marked-turn density \(\tau_{n+1}\), the blocked-defect density \(\beta_n\), or both.

Assume for the rest of B2/B28 that \(A_{\rm SAW}=a>0\). Since \(r_n\) is already bounded above and away from zero, there exists a subsequence \(n_j\) and a number
\[
\rho=\mu e^{\varepsilon a}\ne\mu,
\qquad \varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\},
\]
such that \(r_{n_j}\to\rho\). Kesten's theorem gives
\[
r_n r_{n+1}=\frac{\sigma_{n+2}}{\sigma_n}\longrightarrow\mu^2,
\]
so recursively, for every fixed \(q\ge0\),
\[
r_{n_j+2q}\to\rho,
\qquad
r_{n_j+2q+1}\to\frac{\mu^2}{\rho}.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, for fixed \(k\),
\[
\frac{\sigma_{n_j+k}}{\sigma_{n_j}}
\longrightarrow
L_k(\rho):=
\begin{cases}
\mu^k,&k\text{ even},\\
\rho\,\mu^{k-1},&k\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
The shifted subsequence \(n_j+1\) has the same formula with \(\rho\) replaced by \(\mu^2/\rho\).

Equation (1) by itself does not contradict (2). Even if \(\chi_n\) converged, the blocked sector can in principle compensate the turn sector. The purpose of B28 is therefore to isolate a **clean parity-breaking sector** whose mass can be lower-bounded without any blocked expansion.

# B28 — pattern typicality in the dilute `z=c/n` defect window

## Proposition 1: fixed-k clean-shortcut coefficient lower bound

There exist constants \(\alpha>0\) and \(\lambda<\mu\), depending only on one fixed proper internal square-lattice pattern, such that for every fixed integer \(k\ge0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
 a_{n,k}\ge
 2^{-k}\Bigl(\sigma_{n+k}-\lambda^{n+k}\Bigr)
 \binom{\lfloor\alpha(n+k)\rfloor}{k}.
}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{a_{n,k}}{n^k\sigma_{n+k}}
\ge \frac{\alpha^k}{2^k k!}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

### Proof

Choose once and for all a proper internal Kesten pattern \(\mathcal P\) containing a distinguished oriented two-edge square turn
\[
u\to u+e_1\to u+e_1+e_2,
\]
so its two-step displacement is one of the allowed diagonals \(e_1+e_2\). Such a fixed-orientation proper pattern exists: one may place arbitrarily many translated copies in a sufficiently separated square-lattice SAW.

By Kesten's pattern theorem there are \(a_0>0\) and \(\lambda_0<\mu\) such that the number of \(N\)-step square-lattice SAWs containing fewer than \(a_0N\) translated occurrences of \(\mathcal P\) is at most \(\lambda_0^N\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), after harmless enlargement of \(\lambda_0\) if necessary.

The support of \(\mathcal P\) is finite. Hence each translated occurrence can overlap only a bounded number \(D\) of other translated occurrences, where \(D\) depends only on \(\mathcal P\). Greedily extracting a disjoint subfamily shows that every pattern-typical \(N\)-step walk has at least
\[
\alpha N,
\qquad \alpha:=\frac{a_0}{D+1}>0,
\]
pairwise vertex-disjoint distinguished turns, up to replacing \(\alpha\) by a slightly smaller constant to absorb integer parts.

Fix such a typical square SAW \(\omega\), and choose any \(k\) of those pairwise vertex-disjoint distinguished turns. At each chosen turn replace
\[
u\to c\to v,
\qquad v-u=e_1+e_2,
\]
by the single diagonal edge \(u\to v\). This deletes only the middle corner \(c\). Because the chosen turns are vertex-disjoint, doing all \(k\) shortcuts produces an \((N-k)\)-step self-avoiding walk on the augmented graph with exactly \(k\) diagonal edges. No new vertex is introduced, and no repeated vertex can be created by deleting vertices.

The resulting map from pairs
\[
(\omega,\{k\text{ selected disjoint turns}\})
\]
to augmented \((N-k)\)-step walks with exactly \(k\) diagonals has fibre size at most \(2^k\): given the image, its \(k\) diagonal edges are known, and each diagonal has at most two common square-lattice neighbours that could be inserted to recover a two-edge square turn. Thus there are at most \(2^k\) candidate square expansions; invalid expansions only reduce this number.

Therefore
\[
a_{N-k,k}\ge
2^{-k}(\sigma_N-\lambda_0^N)
\binom{\lfloor\alpha N\rfloor}{k}.
\]
Putting \(N=n+k\) gives (4), with \(\lambda=\lambda_0\). Since submultiplicativity gives \(\sigma_N\ge\mu^N\), the ratio \(\lambda^N/\sigma_N\le(\lambda/\mu)^N\) vanishes exponentially. For fixed \(k\),
\[
\frac1{n^k}\binom{\lfloor\alpha(n+k)\rfloor}{k}
\longrightarrow \frac{\alpha^k}{k!},
\]
which gives (5). QED.

This proposition uses only the **shortcut** direction from a square walk to a diagonal-defect walk. It is therefore immune to the `B0/B1` blocked-expansion obstruction. The factor \(2^k\) already accounts for all possible inverse corner choices.

## Proposition 2: parity is transported into the `z=c/n` boundary layer

Let \(n_j\) be a subsequence satisfying \(r_{n_j}\to\rho\ne\mu\) as in (2). For every fixed \(c\ge0\), define
\[
y:=\frac{\alpha\mu c}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{j\to\infty}
\frac{C_{n_j}(c/n_j)}{\sigma_{n_j}}
\ge
\cosh y+\frac{\rho}{\mu}\sinh y.
}
\tag{6}
\]
Along the shifted subsequence,
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{j\to\infty}
\frac{C_{n_j+1}(c/(n_j+1))}{\sigma_{n_j+1}}
\ge
\cosh y+\frac{\mu}{\rho}\sinh y.
}
\tag{7}
\]

### Proof

Fix \(K\). Positivity of all coefficients gives
\[
\frac{C_n(c/n)}{\sigma_n}
\ge
\sum_{k=0}^{K}
\frac{a_{n,k}}{n^k\sigma_n}c^k.
\]
Using Proposition 1 and (3), then taking \(j\to\infty\),
\[
\liminf_j\frac{C_{n_j}(c/n_j)}{\sigma_{n_j}}
\ge
\sum_{k=0}^{K}
\frac1{k!}\left(\frac{\alpha c}{2}\right)^kL_k(\rho).
\]
Now let \(K\to\infty\). The even terms sum to
\[
\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{y^{2m}}{(2m)!}=\cosh y,
\]
while the odd terms sum to
\[
\frac{\rho}{\mu}
\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{y^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}
=\frac{\rho}{\mu}\sinh y.
\]
This proves (6). Equation (7) is identical after replacing the first-step limit \(\rho\) by \(\mu^2/\rho\). QED.

If \(A_{\rm SAW}=a>0\) and \(\rho=\mu e^{a}\), the two rigorous lower envelopes differ by
\[
2\sinh(a)\sinh(y)>0
\qquad(c>0).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus a hypothetical square-lattice parity oscillation is not confined to the old endpoint-slack observable or to blocked defects: it is transmitted into a **clean, linearly abundant, independently shortcuttable pattern sector** at the singular activity scale \(z=c/n\).

This is genuine structural information, but it is not a contradiction. Equations (6)--(7) are lower bounds only. The remaining augmented walks, including blocked and multi-defect configurations not produced by the chosen clean pattern, can raise either parity subsequence. A proof by deformation therefore needs a parity-insensitive **upper** control, or an asymptotically exact cluster expansion, at `z=c/n`.

## Corollary: clean-sector susceptibility lower bounds

Taking \(k=1\) in (5) and using (3) yields along the same subsequences
\[
\liminf_j\chi_{n_j}\ge \frac{\alpha\rho}{2},
\qquad
\liminf_j\chi_{n_j+1}\ge \frac{\alpha\mu^2}{2\rho}.
\tag{9}
\]
Again, this does not force a difference of the full susceptibilities because the remaining one-defect sectors are positive and may differ by parity. It does show that any successful `z=c/n` argument cannot dismiss the first defect sector as negligible.

## Why the obvious Poisson completion is not yet a proof

The shortcut construction gives the correct factorial combinatorics for any **fixed** number of defects. To turn (6)--(7) into an asymptotically exact Poisson/cluster formula one would need uniform control as \(k\) grows, together with an upper bound on configurations whose diagonal defects are not generated by well-separated copies of the chosen pattern. Two obstructions remain:

1. an augmented walk can contain blocked diagonal edges (`B0/B1`) that do not admit the desired square expansion;
2. even for expandable defects, global square expansions can interact when many defects are present, so the simple `2^k` fibre bound is not an asymptotic enumeration formula.

The first obstruction is the same remote-occupancy phenomenon that appears in the depth-3 hook/open-corner analysis. The second is a many-defect inverse-multiplicity issue. I did not assume independence of pattern locations or promote the lower bound to a Poisson limit.

### Weighted connective constants

Grimmett--Li's weighted-SAW framework proves results about weighted connective constants / bridge constants and continuity with respect to weight functions. Those results control
\[
\lim_n C_n(z)^{1/n},
\]
not the amplitude-scale quantity
\[
\frac{C_n(c/n)}{\sigma_n}
\]
or adjacent coefficients uniformly as \(z\downarrow0\). The previous scalar boundary-layer example already proves that exponential-rate continuity alone cannot exchange the limits \(n\to\infty\) and \(z\downarrow0\).

### Yang--Baxter / integrable weighted SAW

Glazman--Manolescu prove universality of the critical fugacity and a half-plane two-point function under Yang--Baxter deformations of an integrable rhombic-face SAW model. This is substantially stronger than mere continuity inside that special model, but it still does not provide the required theorem here:

- their local state space is not the ordinary vertex-SAW on the square lattice plus a dilute diagonal edge;
- the Yang--Baxter weights lie on a special integrable manifold rather than the singular one-parameter odd-cycle defect used above;
- the stated observables are critical fugacity / half-plane two-point quantities, not unrestricted fixed-length adjacent coefficient ratios;
- the bounded search found no estimate uniform in a parity-breaking weight at the `1/n` scale that could be transplanted to (6)--(7).

Therefore the integrable route presently supplies an analogy, not the missing upper cluster control.

### Non-bipartite ratio-limit input

Classical summaries record one-step ratio convergence on non-bipartite lattices such as the triangular lattice while the square-lattice case remains open. The previous run showed abstractly that a fixed-positive-defect theorem is insufficient unless its constants are uniform through `z=O(1/n)`. The present run makes this sharper: under `A_SAW>0`, clean defect sectors already have distinct parity-sensitive lower envelopes at precisely that scale. Any successful port of the non-bipartite theorem must therefore control this boundary layer quantitatively; merely knowing each fixed `z>0` ratio limit cannot do so.
