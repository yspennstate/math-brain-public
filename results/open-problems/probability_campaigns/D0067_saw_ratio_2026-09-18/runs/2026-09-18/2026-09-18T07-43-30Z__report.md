# 3. B4 — a complete conditional one-step theorem

The previous run isolated late first-contact mass as one obstruction. That is not by itself enough: even if every endpoint contact is asymptotically visible in a finite window, an even/odd difference may persist **inside** that finite window. This section separates those two issues exactly.

For fixed `M`, define the omitted slack

\[
T_n^{(M)}:=S_n-S_n^{(M)}\ge0,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and the two asymptotic defect quantities

\[
\varepsilon_M
:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E T_n^{(M)},
\tag{3.2}
\]

\[
\delta_M
:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\left|\mathbb E S_{n+1}^{(M)}-\mathbb E S_n^{(M)}\right|.
\tag{3.3}
\]

`epsilon_M` measures mass escaping beyond depth `M`; `delta_M` measures the remaining **one-step parity defect in the finite endpoint view**.

## Theorem 3.1 — parity-tail closure criterion

If

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{M\ge1}\bigl(\delta_M+2\varepsilon_M\bigr)=0,
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\frac{\sigma_{n+1}}{\sigma_n}\to\mu.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular, it is sufficient that both

\[
\varepsilon_M\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\delta_M\longrightarrow0
\qquad(M\to\infty).
\tag{3.6}
\]

A stronger but conceptually simpler sufficient condition is: the uniform late-contact tail vanishes and every fixed finite-depth observable has asymptotically no `n` versus `n+1` expectation gap.

### Proof

From (0.1),

\[
\mathbb E S_{n+1}-\mathbb E S_n
=-6(r_{n+1}-r_n)+o(1).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Also, by (3.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\mathbb E S_{n+1}-\mathbb E S_n\right|
&\le
\left|\mathbb E S_{n+1}^{(M)}-\mathbb E S_n^{(M)}\right|\\
&\quad+
\mathbb E T_{n+1}^{(M)}+\mathbb E T_n^{(M)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Taking `limsup_n` gives

\[
6\limsup_n|r_{n+1}-r_n|
\le
\delta_M+2\varepsilon_M.
\tag{3.9}
\]

If (3.4) holds, then `r_{n+1}-r_n -> 0`. Kesten's theorem gives

\[
r_nr_{n+1}\to\mu^2.
\tag{3.10}
\]

The ratios are positive and bounded. Any convergent subsequence `r_{n_j}->x` has `r_{n_j+1}->x` by the difference convergence, while (3.10) then gives `x^2=mu^2`. Positivity forces `x=mu`. Every subsequential limit is therefore `mu`, proving (3.5). ∎

### What this theorem does and does not do

This is a genuine full proof **conditional on (3.4)**; it is not source closure because neither defect is known to vanish. Importantly, it shows that proving only the late-contact tail estimate proposed at the end of run 7 would still leave a separate parity problem. Conversely, proving local parity stabilization at every fixed depth would still leave the possibility that the entire oscillation escapes to depths increasing with `n`.

# 4. B24 — exact extremal obstruction when `A_SAW>0`

The two defects above are not merely sufficient conditions. A nonzero source-level oscillation forces a quantitatively fixed amount of defect into them.

Put

\[
x_n:=\log\frac{r_n}{\mu}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Kesten's two-step theorem is equivalent to

\[
x_n+x_{n+1}\to0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let

\[
a:=A_{\rm SAW}=\limsup_n|x_n|.
\tag{4.3}
\]

## Lemma 4.1 — the exact one-step oscillation amplitude

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|r_{n+1}-r_n|
=2\mu\sinh a.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Choose a subsequence along which `|x_n|->a`, then pass to a sign-constant subsequence. By (4.2), if `x_n->a` then `x_{n+1}->-a`, and vice versa. Hence along that subsequence

\[
|r_{n+1}-r_n|
\to
\mu|e^a-e^{-a}|=2\mu\sinh a,
\]

which gives the lower bound.

For the upper bound, take any subsequence realizing the limsup of the left-hand side and then a further subsequence on which `x_n->y`. By definition `|y|<=a`; (4.2) gives `x_{n+1}->-y`, so the corresponding difference tends to `2 mu sinh|y| <= 2 mu sinh a`. ∎

Combining Lemma 4.1 with (3.7) gives the exact slack oscillation amplitude

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n
\left|\mathbb E S_{n+1}-\mathbb E S_n\right|
=12\mu\sinh a.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Now apply (3.8).

## Theorem 4.2 — quantitative parity-tail dichotomy

For **every** finite depth `M`,

\[
\boxed{
12\mu\sinh A_{\rm SAW}
\le
\delta_M+2\varepsilon_M.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
A_{\rm SAW}
\le
\operatorname{arsinh}
\!\left(\frac{\delta_M+2\varepsilon_M}{12\mu}\right)
\qquad\text{for every }M.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus a nonzero one-step oscillation cannot hide from both diagnostics.

### Two sharp qualitative alternatives

If the omitted finite-depth tail is tight,

\[
\varepsilon_M\to0,
\]

then any `a>0` forces

\[
\liminf_{M\to\infty}\delta_M
\ge12\mu\sinh a.
\tag{4.8}
\]

So the full oscillation survives as a finite-depth even/odd endpoint-view difference.

Conversely, if finite-depth parity stabilizes,

\[
\delta_M\to0,
\]

then any `a>0` forces

\[
\liminf_{M\to\infty}\varepsilon_M
\ge6\mu\sinh a.
\tag{4.9}
\]

So a fixed positive amount of slack must remain at arbitrarily late contact depths.

## 4.3 Convert omitted slack into a one-end late-contact atom

Let

\[
R_{n,M}:=K_0-K_0^{(M)}
=\sum_\alpha {\bf1}_{\{M<\tau_0^\alpha<\infty\}},
\tag{4.10}
\]

and

\[
L_M:=\limsup_n\mathbb E R_{n,M}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The run-7 algebra gives

\[
S_n-S_n^{(M)}
\le
3(K_0-K_0^{(M)})+3(K_+-K_+^{(M)}).
\]

Reversal symmetry therefore yields

\[
\varepsilon_M\le6L_M.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Combining (4.6) and (4.12),

\[
\boxed{
L_M
\ge
\left[\mu\sinh a-\frac{\delta_M}{12}\right]_+.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Since `R_{n,M}<=3` and is positive exactly when at least one extra neighbour is first hit after depth `M`,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n
\Pr_n\{\exists\alpha:\ M<\tau_0^\alpha<\infty\}
\ge
\frac13
\left[\mu\sinh a-\frac{\delta_M}{12}\right]_+.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

Therefore, under finite-depth parity stabilization (`delta_M->0`), a hypothetical `A_SAW=a>0` creates a literal **atom at infinity** in the following operational sense: no matter how large the endpoint window is, with asymptotic probability at least approximately `mu sinh(a)/3`, some extra neighbour of the origin is visited only after leaving that window.

This is not a contradiction with known results, but it is substantially sharper than the previous statement that “mass may escape to infinity.” It quantifies exactly how much mass must escape if the local parity defect is absent.

# 5. B9 — randomized/rerouting attack on late contacts

The next question is whether the late-contact atom in (4.14) can be ruled out geometrically. I first tried to randomize a long prefix whose first return to an extra neighbour occurs at time `t`, with the hope of obtaining many valid reroutings. Before doing any entropy estimate, there is a simpler exact deletion map that should be extracted.

Fix one extra neighbour `alpha` of the origin and an odd `t` (bipartiteness forces odd `t`). Let

\[
c_t(0,\alpha)
\]

be the number of `t`-step SAWs from `0` to `alpha`. On the event `tau_0^alpha=t`, the first `t` steps form such a path; adding the missing edge `(alpha,0)` makes a self-avoiding polygon of length `t+1`.

## 5.1 Exact deletion map and a sharper fixed-`t` estimate

Delete the long `t`-step prefix and replace it by the single edge `0->alpha`, leaving the original tail after time `t` unchanged. The result is a self-avoiding walk of length

\[
n-t+1
\]

whose first edge is the prescribed edge `0->alpha`.

The map from an original walk to

- its deleted prefix, and
- its shortened walk

is injective. Conversely, not every such pair reconstructs a valid original walk because the inserted prefix may intersect the tail; ignoring this only enlarges the count. By square-lattice rotational symmetry, exactly one quarter of all nonzero-length SAWs have a prescribed first edge. Hence

\[
\#\{\omega\in\Sigma_n:\tau_0^\alpha=t\}
\le
\frac14 c_t(0,\alpha)\,\sigma_{n-t+1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_n(\tau_0^\alpha=t)
\le
\frac{c_t(0,\alpha)}4
\frac{\sigma_{n-t+1}}{\sigma_n}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because `t-1` is even, Kesten's two-step ratio theorem gives, for each **fixed** odd `t`,

\[
\frac{\sigma_{n-t+1}}{\sigma_n}
\longrightarrow
\mu^{-(t-1)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\Pr_n(\tau_0^\alpha=t)
\le
\frac14 c_t(0,\alpha)\mu^{-(t-1)}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Using the exact closing identity

\[
c_t(0,\alpha)=\frac{t+1}{2}p_{t+1},
\tag{5.5}
\]

this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\Pr_n(\tau_0^\alpha=t)
\le
\frac{t+1}{8}\,p_{t+1}\,\mu^{-(t-1)}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This improves the constant in the previous fixed-`t` tail estimate because it uses the exact prescribed-first-edge shortening rather than a one-step lower ratio bound. It does **not** alter the frozen `A_SAW` metric and it does not solve the uniform-in-`t` summation problem.

## 5.2 Why the random compensation idea still fails

The deletion saves `t-1` edges, an even number. This looks favorable because Kesten's parity-preserving pattern surgery can add length in increments of two. The attempted randomized strategy was:

1. delete the long polygonal prefix;
2. choose many separated insertable pattern locations in the shortened tail;
3. distribute `(t-1)/2` two-edge insertions among those locations;
4. hope the number of images beats the number of possible deleted prefixes.

The counterexample check shows why no theorem follows from this without a new global estimate. Total SAW counts already grow by approximately `mu^(t-1)` when an even length `t-1` is restored. The number of deleted prefixes grows at the same exponential scale; after cancellation, the remaining issue is precisely the polynomial scarcity of rooted polygons. A counting argument that genuinely made the late-contact series summable would therefore amount to a substantially stronger all-length polygon estimate, not a free consequence of the pattern theorem.

Indeed, (5.6) would be summable over large odd `t` if one had an all-length estimate strong enough that

\[
\sum_m m\,p_m\mu^{-m}<\infty.
\tag{5.7}
\]

A power-law bound `p_m <= C mu^m m^{-alpha}` would need `alpha>2`. The checked all-length result `alpha=1/2` is far weaker, and the `3/2-o(1)` result on a full-density set still leaves the weighted term of order `m^{-1/2+o(1)}` there and says nothing strong enough on the exceptional set. The snake-method closing estimate likewise does not provide the needed summable rooted-polygon tail.

There is a second obstruction: even if one conditions on many insertable local patterns in the shortened tail, inverse multiplicity and intersections with the deleted polygon must be controlled. A coiled walk can make a nominally local insertion collide with a distant portion of the body. The pattern theorem says walks with too few occurrences of a **fixed** proper pattern are exponentially rare globally, but it does not by itself supply a uniform bounded-to-one compensation map for the lollipop geometry above.

Outcome of B9: **useful exact fixed-`t` sharpening; no uniform tail theorem.** No random-rerouting claim is promoted beyond (5.1)--(5.6).
