# 4. B5 — add an endpoint-contact fugacity and differentiate

## 4.1 Boundary contact partition function

For real parameters `s,t`, define

\[
Z_n(s,t)
:=
\sum_{\omega\in\mathrm{SAW}_n}
\exp\{sK_0(\omega)+tK_+(\omega)\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

and its normalization

\[
M_n(s,t):=\frac{Z_n(s,t)}{\sigma_n}
=
\mathbb E_n e^{sK_0+tK_+}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since `0<=K_0,K_+<=3`,

\[
e^{-3(|s|+|t|)}\sigma_n
\le Z_n(s,t)
\le e^{3(|s|+|t|)}\sigma_n.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore every fixed endpoint fugacity has the same exponential growth constant:

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} Z_n(s,t)^{1/n}=\mu.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Differentiation at the unweighted point gives the exact boundary observables

\[
\partial_s M_n(0,0)=\mathbb E K_0=3-r_n,
\qquad
\partial_t M_n(0,0)=3-r_n,
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\partial_s\partial_t M_n(0,0)
=
\mathbb E[K_0K_+]
=
\mathbb E S_n.

\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus the source problem is equivalently a convergence question for one mixed derivative of a bounded **boundary** partition function.

Equation (4.3) implies

for every fixed `s,t`. Hence the limiting bulk free energy is completely flat in these endpoint fugacities. # 7. B12 — the bipartite parity charge of every fixed-endpoint surgery

Let

\[
\chi(x,y):=(-1)^{x+y}
\]

be the square-lattice bipartition character. Every nearest-neighbour step flips `chi`. Thus any lattice path from `u` to `v` of length `L` satisfies

\[
\boxed{(-1)^L=\chi(u)\chi(v).}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Two consequences are exact.

### 7.1 Fixed attachment vertices force even length change

If a surgery replaces a subpath from `u` to `v` by another subpath with the **same** attachment vertices, then both subpaths have the parity fixed by (7.1). Their lengths differ by an even integer. Therefore every finite local replacement that keeps both attachment vertices fixed is intrinsically parity-preserving.

In particular, all endpoint-contact cap opening/closing surgeries from run 5 fall under this theorem. To change the total SAW length by an odd number, a surgery must move at least one attachment endpoint to the opposite bipartition class (or otherwise change which vertices are being connected).

### 7.2 The negative fugacity is a gauge transform

For fixed vertices `u,v`, let

\[
G_{u,v}(z):=
\sum_{\gamma:u\to v\ \mathrm{SAW}} z^{|\gamma|}
\tag{7.2}
\]

as a formal power series. Since every contributing path has the same parity,

\[
\boxed{
G_{u,v}(-z)
=
\chi(u)\chi(v)\,G_{u,v}(z).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus on a bipartite lattice the points `z` and `-z` contain no independent information for a **fixed endpoint pair**; the sign is exactly a gauge factor. This rules out a tempting route in which one tries to remove the even/odd coefficient oscillation using only fixed-endpoint identities at `+1/mu` and `-1/mu`. Any successful parity-breaking identity must couple different endpoint color classes rather than remain inside one fixed-endpoint sector.

This does not solve Question 1.3, but it precisely characterizes the obstruction that an odd bounded-multiplicity surgery or analytic identity must overcome.

# 9. B6 — deform to the exactly solved honeycomb lattice

The purpose of this method was not to claim a honeycomb-only result as D0067 progress. It was to ask whether exact solvability there supplies the missing parity-breaking identity.

Duminil-Copin--Smirnov prove for the honeycomb lattice

\[
\mu_{\hexagon}=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}
\]

using a parafermionic observable at the critical step fugacity. This does not by itself settle a one-step coefficient ratio. The honeycomb lattice is also bipartite, so the parity character argument in Section 7 applies verbatim: every fixed-endpoint generating function again satisfies a sign/gauge identity of the form (7.3). Exact knowledge of the radius of convergence therefore does not identify the relative amplitude of the two coefficient parity classes.

A second natural comparison is the exact honeycomb **surface adsorption** fugacity of Beaton--Bousquet-Melou--de Gier--Duminil-Copin--Guttmann. That model attaches a fugacity to a number of boundary visits that can grow linearly with walk length and therefore can change bulk critical behavior. By contrast, the endpoint contacts `K_0,K_+` in Section 4 are bounded by 3 independently of `n`; (4.3) shows their fixed fugacities cannot move the bulk free energy at all. Consequently the adsorption identity is not directly a boundary-regularity theorem for the coefficient ratio studied here.

The deformation therefore isolates, rather than removes, the missing mechanism: one needs an identity or controlled switching operation that **couples opposite endpoint bipartition classes at the coefficient level**. The current parafermionic/radius information is parity-compatible and does not supply that coupling.

# 10. Counterexample checks against the proposed transfers

The user protocol requires attacking each transfer before promoting it.

1. **Bulk free-energy differentiation.** Counterexample mechanism: any bounded endpoint weight changes `Z_n` by only a multiplicative constant, so all such models have the same limiting free energy. Therefore smoothness/convexity of `n^{-1} log Z_n` cannot distinguish even and odd endpoint laws. This route is rejected as a closure argument.
2. **Fixed-endpoint odd surgery.** Equation (7.1) makes it impossible on every bipartite graph, independently of self-avoidance. Any proposed local replacement preserving its two attachment vertices has even length difference. This route is rejected unless it explicitly moves an attachment endpoint to the opposite color and controls global intersections/inverse multiplicity.
3. **Use exact honeycomb `mu` as a parity bridge.** The exact connective constant determines exponential growth but the bipartite gauge symmetry survives. A scalar sequence can still have the same radius/two-step behavior and different parity amplitudes; therefore exact `mu` alone is insufficient.

# 11. What remains open

The contact-spectrum theorem turns the remaining obstruction into a sharper form. Equation (3.4) says closure is equivalent to

\[
\mathbb E S_n\to(3-\mu)^2.
\tag{11.1}
\]

The explicit pattern spectrum proves

\[
\liminf_n\mathbb E S_n\ge\Lambda_{\rm contact}
\le(3-\mu)^2.
\tag{11.2}
\]

The gap is now

\[
\boxed{
(3-\mu)^2-\Lambda_{\rm contact}\ge0.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

A plausible interpretation is **mass escape in endpoint pattern depth**: every fixed finite proper two-end contact pattern has a positive limiting lower frequency, but the current theorem does not show that a finite or countable prefix-free inventory captures all slack uniformly in `n`. A parity-dependent amount of slack could, in principle, be carried by contacts whose first witnessing return occurs farther and farther from the endpoint as `n` changes. Eliminating exactly that escape would force equality in (11.2) and solve the source.

This is materially narrower than the original one-step ratio statement and is the next target; it is not itself closure.
