# 4. B8 — exact discretization at depth three

Let `omega=(omega_0,...,omega_n)` be a uniform `n`-step SAW. Recall that `K_0^(M)` counts extra neighbours of the start first visited by depth `M`, and `K_+^(M)` is the reversed endpoint analogue.

Because `Z^2` is bipartite, an extra neighbour of an endpoint can only be hit at an odd time. At depth three there is only one possible contact vertex. Define

\[
H_0(\omega)
:={\bf 1}_{\{\|\omega_3-\omega_0\|_1=1\}},
\]

and, at the terminal end,

\[
H_+(\omega)
:={\bf 1}_{\{\|\omega_n-\omega_{n-3}\|_1=1\}}.
\]

Self-avoidance makes these exactly the events that the first, respectively last, three edges run around three sides of one unit plaquette. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_0^{(3)}=H_0,
\qquad K_+^{(3)}=H_+,
\qquad S_n^{(3)}=H_0H_+.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

So the selected special case is not an abstract finite-state problem: it is exactly adjacent-parity convergence of a fixed two-end plaquette-hook cylinder.

Let

\[
h_n:=\mathbb P_n(H_0H_+)
=\mathbb E S_n^{(3)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\delta_3=\limsup_n|h_{n+1}-h_n|.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.1 The open-corner state and exact child bijection

For an `n`-step walk with last three vertices

\[
a=\omega_{n-2},\qquad b=\omega_{n-1},\qquad c=\omega_n,
\]

suppose the last two edges turn through 90 degrees. Let

\[
d:=a+(c-b),
\]

which is the fourth corner of that unit plaquette. Define `O_+` to be the event that the last two edges turn and `d` has **not** been visited by the walk.

On `O_+`, appending the unique edge `c->d` is legal. The child then has a terminal depth-three hook because `d` is adjacent to `a`. Conversely, deleting the last edge of any child with terminal hook leaves exactly such an open corner. The start-hook event is unchanged.

Thus deletion/addition is a genuine bijection:

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\omega\in\mathcal W_{n+1}:H_0H_+=1\}
=
\#\{\omega\in\mathcal W_n:H_0O_+=1\}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Let

\[
o_n:=\mathbb P_n(H_0O_+).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Dividing (4.4) by `sigma_{n+1}=r_n sigma_n` gives the exact adjacent-length balance

\[
\boxed{
h_{n+1}=\frac{o_n}{r_n}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\delta_3
=
\limsup_n\left|\frac{o_n}{r_n}-h_n\right|.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is the cleanest exact expression found for the selected recovery target.

## 4.2 Why the obvious four-state closure is not actually finite

Condition on `H_0`. The terminal two-step geometry falls into four useful categories:

1. `straight`: the last two edges are collinear;
2. `open`: they turn and the fourth corner is unvisited (`O_+`);
3. `hook`: they turn and the fourth corner is exactly `omega_{n-3}` (`H_+`);
4. `remote-blocked`: they turn and the fourth corner was visited earlier than `omega_{n-3}`.

The first three look local. The fourth is not: in reversed endpoint time, the fourth corner is an extra endpoint neighbour first hit at some odd depth at least five. Thus whether a local corner is `open` cannot be read from the last three path edges alone; it requires an occupancy bit that may have been set arbitrarily far back along the walk.

Equivalently, if `T_+` denotes “the last two edges turn” and `R_+` the remote-blocked case, then on every walk

\[
T_+=O_++H_++R_+,
\tag{4.8}
\]

with disjoint terms. Appending one step maps `O_+` to `H_+`, but determining the next `O_+` introduces a new local site whose occupancy may again come from arbitrarily remote history. The depth-three state transition therefore opens the same contact-depth hierarchy represented by `mathcal L_M`; it does not close to a finite Markov chain.

This is the first exact failure of the planned finite-state proof.

# 6. B7 — take parity subsequential limits and test whether known equations force equality

The event `H_0H_+` is a finite union of fixed two-end end-pattern pairs. Madras's theorem therefore supplies the parity-preserving regularity

\[
\frac{h_{n+2}}{h_n}\longrightarrow1
\tag{6.1}
\]

on the admissible large lengths, along with positive fixed-pattern frequencies. This is exactly the kind of information the 1988 abstract says is available; convergence as `n->infinity` is explicitly identified there as difficult/open.

Now take any subsequence `n_j` of one parity along which the bounded quantities converge, and write

\[
r_{n_j}\to\rho>0,
\qquad
h_{n_j}\to x,
\qquad
h_{n_j+1}\to y.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Kesten gives

\[
r_{n_j+1}\to\frac{\mu^2}{\rho},
\tag{6.3}
\]

and Madras's `n` versus `n+2` regularity gives

\[
h_{n_j+2}\to x.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Applying the exact child balance (4.6) at `n_j` and `n_j+1` yields, for corresponding subsequential limits of `o_n`,

\[
\boxed{
o_{n_j}\to \rho y,
\qquad
o_{n_j+1}\to\frac{\mu^2}{\rho}x.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

These equations do **not** force `x=y`. They are compatible with distinct even/odd two-end hook masses, provided the associated open-corner masses adjust according to (6.5). Even setting `rho=mu` does not remove that freedom: for sufficiently small positive `x!=y`, the abstract local balance `o_even=mu y`, `o_odd=mu x` stays within probability bounds.

This is not a SAW counterexample. It is a no-go statement for the attempted B7 closure: **Kesten two-step regularity + Madras two-step end-pattern regularity + the exact depth-three child bijection do not algebraically identify the two parity laws.**

The missing equation is precisely an `n` versus `n+1` parity mixer, or a theorem ruling out the remote-occupancy degree of freedom in `O_+`. That is the same structural obstruction already visible in the global blocker.
