## 4. Complete route-failure ledger

The point of this section is not to dismiss the preceding work. Several routes produced exact identities and strict quantitative improvements. The question is why none contains the missing mechanism in `(BLOCKER)`.

### Route 1 — scalar/submultiplicative information

**What was tried.** Combine submultiplicativity of `sigma_n`, Kesten's two-step limit, and the degree bound `r_n<=3`.

**What was learned.** The abstract sequence

\[
\widetilde\sigma_n=
\begin{cases}
\mu^n,&n\text{ even},\\
\mu^n e^a,&n\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\]

for a suitable `a>0` can satisfy the relevant scalar inequalities and exact two-step ratio while its one-step ratios oscillate.

**Precise failure.** Any proof using only scalar submultiplicativity, two-step asymptotics and the endpoint degree ceiling is logically incapable of excluding a parity amplitude. A genuinely SAW-geometric input is necessary.

### Route 2 — endpoint occupancy identity and nonlinear transfer

**What was tried.** Express

\[
r_n=4(1-p_n),
\]

where `p_n` is the probability that a fixed neighbour of the origin is visited, and formulate the exact endpoint-view transfer `nu_{n+1}=T nu_n` whose normalizer is `r_n`.

**What was learned.** Failure of the ratio limit produces a limiting forward orbit with alternating normalizers `mu e^{\pm a}`.

**Precise failure.** Compactness gives existence of a limiting orbit but no contraction, uniqueness, or parity mixing for `T`. The obstruction cannot be strengthened to a period-two measure orbit. The transfer formulation relocates the problem but does not break parity.

### Route 3 — two-end degree identity and covariance

**What was tried.** Count legal extensions at both endpoints and use endpoint delocalization. This gave

\[
\frac{\sigma_{n+2}}{\sigma_n}
=\mathbb E[D_0D_+]-\mathbb E C_n,
\qquad \mathbb E C_n\to0,
\]

hence `E[D_0D_+]->mu^2`, strict metric bounds, and

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(D_0,D_+)=\mu^2-r_n^2+o(1).
\]

**Precise failure.** The exact identity shows what an oscillation must look like — persistent sign-alternating long-range endpoint correlation — but no available theorem makes the two endpoint local degrees decorrelate in the fixed-length ensemble.

### Route 4 — odd length-changing local surgery

**What was tried.** Search for a bounded-to-one local replacement comparing lengths `n` and `n+1`.

**What was learned.** On bipartite `Z^2`, any two paths with the same attachment endpoints have lengths of the same parity. Equivalently, every fixed-endpoint local surgery changes length by an even integer.

**Precise failure.** A successful odd surgery must move an attachment endpoint or couple opposite endpoint-color sectors. Once the endpoint moves, global self-intersection and inverse-multiplicity control become the hard part. The obvious plaquette/cap surgeries stay parity-preserving.

### Route 5 — fixed Madras end patterns

**What was tried.** Use Madras's positive lower frequency for fixed admissible patterns, including prescribed patterns at both ends. Explicit trap patterns force endpoint slack and produced the first strict improvement of the metric bound.

**Precise failure.** Madras gives qualitative positivity pattern-by-pattern and `N` versus `N+2` regularity, not a uniform statement over pattern depth and not an `N` versus `N+1` coupling. A finite collection can force positive slack but cannot exhaust all slack.

### Route 6 — prefix-free trap spectra and all `D4` relative orientations

**What was tried.** Build an infinite prefix-free family of trapping patterns `T_k`, add all eight global orientations and all relative start/end orientations, and sum disjoint two-end cylinder masses by taking finite partial sums before `n->infinity`.

**What was learned.** This strictly improved the lower bound on endpoint slack without assuming a uniform `k`-bound.

**Precise failure.** The individual Madras lower frequencies may decay arbitrarily fast with pattern depth. Infinite positive mass is not the same as exhaustion of the actual slack distribution. The method cannot rule out mass at ever-growing first-contact depth.

### Route 7 — contact-level `U/V/T` families and endpoint-contact fugacity

**What was tried.** Refine patterns by whether they force `K>=1,2,3`, sum their weighted two-end contributions, and introduce

Z_n(s,t)=\sum_\omega e^{sK_0(\omega)+tK_+(\omega)}.

**What was learned.** A richer contact spectrum gives a further strict metric improvement.

**Precise failure.** Since `K_0,K_+` are uniformly bounded boundary observables, fixed `s,t` do not change the bulk free energy `log mu`. Bulk free-energy differentiability therefore has no leverage on the `O(1)` endpoint amplitudes that encode the parity oscillation.

**What was learned.** The inequality used in run 2 is LP-sharp for the abstract coupling; extremizers live on a small set of degree pairs.

**Precise failure.** `H_m(P)` depends on the full embedded shape and on the diagonal relation between polygon perimeter and remaining tail length. There is no known factorization or renewal equation for the full-plane kernel.

**What was learned.** This sharpens fixed-contact-depth constants and identifies interior-trapped root types that cannot support a long exterior tail.

**What was tried.** After deleting a long polygonal prefix, restore the lost even length by a canonical collision-free hairpin outside the remaining walk's bounding box.

**What was learned.** Existence of a collision-free compensation is not the fundamental obstruction.

**Precise failure.** The compensation forgets the deleted polygon. Many distinct input polygons can map to the same compensated walk, so inverse multiplicity grows with the deleted structure. No bounded-to-one injection results.

**What was learned.** Obstacle inclusion is monotone, but large supporting rectangles leave an unobstructed half-plane bridge channel of full exponential rate `mu`.

**Precise failure.** There cannot be a uniform pointwise exponential shielding penalty depending only on perimeter. The quantifier order must be averaged over polygons and evaluated on the coefficient diagonal.

**What was learned.** The missing scalar is a finite sum of local contact probabilities.

**Precise failure.** The natural opening/closing operation on a direct endpoint-contact polygon cap changes length by an even amount. The finite decomposition exposes the parity problem but does not solve it.

**What was tried.** Use Kesten's pattern theorem and Madras's `N,N+2` end-pattern regularity to force endpoint local laws to stabilize.

**Precise failure.** A sequence can have stable even and odd subsequential laws while being perfectly regular under a shift of two. Pattern typicality controls abundance, not the equality of the two parity sectors.

## 5. What a successful proof must contain that no route yet contains

The failure ledger separates the missing information into two genuinely different possibilities.

### Possibility A — a true `n` to `n+1` parity-coupling mechanism for finite endpoint views

For at least one growing sequence of finite depths `M`, and ideally for each fixed `M`, a proof needs to show that the finite endpoint-view law cannot retain distinct even/odd limits. In the present notation that means controlling

\[
\delta_M
=
\limsup_n
|E S_{n+1}^{(M)}-E S_n^{(M)}|.
\]

Kesten/Madras machinery is naturally two-step. Same-attachment surgeries are necessarily even. What is missing is a controlled mechanism that **moves between endpoint-colour sectors** without losing self-avoidance or bounded inverse multiplicity. It could be a finite-state transfer identity, a moving-end surgery, or a renewal coupling whose regenerative state includes the endpoint colour.

### Possibility B — an averaged diagonal polygon-tail tightness theorem

Instead of coupling parity locally, a proof may show that all endpoint slack is asymptotically witnessed at finite depth:

\[
\mathcal L_M\longrightarrow0
\qquad(M\to\infty).
\]

The important word is **averaged diagonal**. Run 10 proved that worst-case obstacle penalties are false. The needed theorem is about

\[
\sum_L Q_{L,n-L+1}
\]

under the fixed-length SAW normalization, not about every polygon separately. Such a theorem must exploit the dependence between polygon shape, root placement, and the avoiding tail strongly enough to beat the non-summable rooted-polygon bounds.

### Ideal mechanism

The most promising form of a full proof would control both effects at once: a renewal/regeneration decomposition with a state space carrying endpoint colour and finite endpoint geometry, whose renewal tail is tight and whose transition kernel is aperiodic. None of the current routes has such an aperiodic regenerative structure for full-plane square-lattice SAW.

## 7. Nearest solved literature and the ingredient it has that D0067 lacks

### Kesten's two-step ratio theorem

Kesten proves, on `Z^d`, that

\[
\frac{\sigma_{n+2}}{\sigma_n}\to\mu^2,
\]

indeed with the quantitative rate recorded by Grimmett's 2021 survey. The proof uses pattern surgery but preserves parity because `Z^d` is bipartite.

**What it has:** a robust abundance theorem plus surgery within a fixed parity class.

**What D0067 lacks:** a surgery with controlled inverse multiplicity that couples the two endpoint-colour/parity classes.

### Madras end-pattern regularity

Madras proves that a fixed admissible end pattern occurs with frequency bounded away from zero and that its frequency at `N+2` is asymptotically the same as at `N`, with analogous two-end statements.

**What it has:** stability of fixed local cylinders under a two-step shift.

**What D0067 lacks:** a theorem identifying the even and odd limits of those cylinder probabilities. Two-step regularity is compatible with two different parity limits.

### Dyhr--Gilbert--Kennedy--Lawler--Passon half-plane irreducible-bridge renewal

The half-plane model has a canonical height direction. Irreducible bridges concatenate into a genuine renewal structure, and at critical fugacity `1/mu` this yields an infinite half-plane SAW measure and strip constructions.

**What it has:** a canonical regenerative decomposition with a monotone boundary coordinate and a critical probability measure.

**What D0067 lacks:** a comparable full-plane regeneration point that is both canonical and aperiodic in walk length. The full-plane uniform fixed-length ensemble has no preferred height direction, and bipartiteness makes every fixed-endpoint replacement periodic mod 2. The half-plane renewal controls a boundary ensemble but does not furnish an `n` versus `n+1` fixed-length coefficient coupling.

This comparison is the reason the recovery action below targets the first nontrivial finite endpoint view instead of trying another global bridge estimate immediately.
