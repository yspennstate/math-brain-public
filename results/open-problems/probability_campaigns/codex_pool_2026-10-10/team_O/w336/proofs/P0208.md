# P0208: exact q-fold cycle calculation

Status: proof written by w336, independent KEEP pending. This extends the recorded q=2 campaign counterexample; it is not a new literature priority claim. The source's tau_star is a maximal commute time, not a maximal one-way hitting time. Relaxation here uses the rate-one continuous-time generator P-I.

Let m>=5, q>=2 and G=C_m[empty_q]. Its vertices are (i,a), with i modulo m and 1<=a<=q; two vertices are adjacent precisely when their first coordinates differ by one modulo m. G is simple, connected, 2q-regular, has n=mq vertices, and is not a cycle. Rotations, reflection of C_m and independent permutations inside each fiber act transitively on directed edges, so G is arc-transitive.

Fix target (0,1). Let a be the mean hitting time from a different vertex in fiber 0. Every visit to fiber 0 from elsewhere chooses a uniformly distributed vertex. If h_j is the mean from fiber j!=0, the quotient cycle's hitting-time equation gives

    h_j = j(m-j) + ((q-1)/q)a.

The identity j(m-j)=1+[(j-1)(m-j+1)+(j+1)(m-j-1)]/2 verifies that equation away from 0. From a twin of the target, a=1+h_1=m+((q-1)/q)a, so a=mq and h_j=j(m-j)+m(q-1). These values solve all first-step equations. A finite connected chain hits the target with finite mean, so uniqueness of the killed-chain linear system identifies them with the actual hitting times.

The graph automorphisms exchange any two vertices of a specified fiber separation, so hitting times are symmetric. For m>=5, floor(m^2/4)>m, hence the largest one-way hitting time is floor(m^2/4)+m(q-1), larger than the twin value mq. Consequently

    tau_star = 2 floor(m^2/4) + 2m(q-1),
    tau_0 = (m^2-1)/6 + m(q-1).

The second formula is the stationary mean hitting time of the fixed target, including its zero self-hitting time: sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j(m-j)=m(m^2-1)/6, and the remaining constant terms sum to m(q-1). A neighbor has mean hitting time m-1+m(q-1)=n-1.

P equals (A(C_m)/2) tensor (J_q/q). The fiber-constant subspace has eigenvalues cos(2*pi*k/m), 0<=k<m; its orthogonal complement has m(q-1) zero eigenvalues. Since cos(2*pi/m)>0 for m>=5, tau_2=1/(1-cos(2*pi/m)). This is the continuous-time relaxation time; for even m the non-lazy discrete-time absolute spectral gap vanishes, which is a different parameter.

For each fixed q>=2 and m tending to infinity,

    tau_star/n^2 -> 1/(2q^2),
    tau_2/n^2 -> 1/(2*pi^2*q^2),
    tau_star/(2*tau_0) -> 3/2.

Thus the same family refutes the three bounds recorded for Aldous-Fill Problem 7.27: O(n log n), O(n), and ratio 1+o(1). This statement relies on the cached problem's parameter definitions and does not establish whether the refutation was previously published.
