# P0087: stationary synchronization and covariance at f=c*r^2

Status: proof written; w346 KEEP pending. This proves a full stationary-law limit, stationary moments and a scalar generator limit under conditional independence. It does not prove the general network's functional diffusion limit or its initial layer.

Assume a fixed finite irreducible row-stochastic Q, its stationary probability row vector pi, p in [0,1]^d, c>0, r>0, f=c*r^2 and r+f<=1. Update

    Z'=(1-r-f)Z+r X+f p,

with conditionally independent X_i given Z, and P(X_i=1|Z)=(QZ)_i. There is a unique stationary law on [0,1]^d. Its mean is z*=f*((r+f)I-rQ)^(-1)p. Write pbar=pi.p and K=sum pi_i^2. As r decreases to zero, its stationary covariance satisfies

    Cov(Z) -> [K*pbar*(1-pbar)/(2c+K)]*1*1^T.

Proof of existence/uniqueness: the transition is Feller on a compact cube. Couple two states using the same independent uniforms for their Bernoulli coordinates. Because 1-r-f>=0, the weighted L1 distance has expected next value at most (1-f) times its present value; use pi Q=pi. Thus stationary laws are unique and the transition contracts in this Wasserstein distance. Taking expectations yields the displayed mean; the matrix is invertible because its eigenvalues have positive real part.

For synchronization, let D=diag(pi), S=pi.Z, and E_Q(z)=z^T D(I-Q)z=(1/2)sum pi_i Q_ij(z_i-z_j)^2. At stationarity, with Delta=r(X-Z)+f(p-Z),

    2r E[E_Q(Z)] = 2f E[<Z,p-Z>_D] + E[||Delta||_D^2]
                   <= 2f+(r+f)^2 = O(r^2).

Irreducibility makes E_Q(z)>=gamma*||z-1*(pi.z)||_D^2 for some fixed gamma>0: the finite symmetric quadratic form has kernel precisely the constants. Hence E||Z-1*S||_D^2=O(r). Also E[S]=pbar exactly, so z*_i tends to pbar.

The martingale innovation for S has conditional variance r^2 sum pi_i^2 (QZ)_i(1-(QZ)_i); conditional independence is used exactly here. Total variance at stationarity gives

    (2f-f^2) Var(S)=r^2 sum pi_i^2 E[(QZ)_i(1-(QZ)_i)].

Synchronization shows (QZ)_i-S tends to zero in L2. The variables are bounded, so for any subsequential limit V of Var(S), division by r^2 gives 2c V=K*(pbar*(1-pbar)-V). Its solution is unique. Every subsequence has that limit, and synchronization gives the full covariance limit.

For the scalar case Q=(1), stationarity gives the exact variance

    Var(Z)=r^2*p*(1-p)/(2f-f^2+r^2)
          =p*(1-p)/(1+2c-c^2*r^2).

For h in C^3([0,1]), the one-step scalar generator divided by r^2 converges uniformly to

    Lh(z)=c(p-z)h'(z)+(1/2)z(1-z)h''(z).

Indeed E[Delta]=c*r^2(p-z), E[Delta^2]=r^2*z(1-z)+c^2*r^4(p-z)^2, and |Delta|<=r+cr^2. Taylor's remainder, divided by r^2, is bounded by ||h'''||_infinity*r*(1+cr)^3/6, with the additional second-order drift term O(r^2). This identifies the candidate Wright-Fisher generator; path tightness and martingale-problem uniqueness are further steps.

Theorem (full stationary law). Under the same fixed-network assumptions and 0<pbar<1, the joint stationary law of Z converges weakly to Y*1, where

    Y ~ Beta(2c*pbar/K, 2c*(1-pbar)/K).

Proof: write S'=S+f*(pbar-S)+r*eta, where eta=pi.(X-QZ), E[eta|Z]=0, |eta|<=1 and E[eta^2|Z]=sum pi_i^2 (QZ)_i(1-(QZ)_i). For every polynomial h, the stationary Taylor identity divided by r^2 gives

    0=c E[(pbar-S)h'(S)]
      +(1/2)E[sum pi_i^2 (QZ)_i(1-(QZ)_i)*h''(S)] + O(r).

The drift-square contribution is O(r^2), and the third-order remainder is uniformly O(r). The proved L2 synchronization implies that the sum inside the second term differs from K*S*(1-S) by a quantity tending to zero in L1. Every subsequence has a further weakly convergent subsequence of S because [0,1] is compact. If its limiting moments are mu_k, use h(s)=s^k, k>=1, to obtain

    [2c+K*(k-1)]*mu_k = [2c*pbar+K*(k-1)]*mu_(k-1), mu_0=1.

These are exactly the moments of the displayed beta law: iterating gives the product of (2c*pbar/K+j)/(2c/K+j) for j=0,...,k-1, which also follows by integrating the beta density. A probability law on [0,1] is determined by its moments, since polynomials approximate continuous functions uniformly. Hence all subsequential limits agree and S converges weakly. Synchronization gives Z-S*1 tending to zero in probability, so the joint law converges to Y*1. If pbar=0 or 1, positivity of pi forces every p_i to be that endpoint and the unique stationary law is the corresponding point mass.

This proof uses all polynomial moments, not an inference from the first two moments. It proves convergence of stationary laws, not a limit for trajectories begun away from synchronization. Without conditional independence, the innovation variance changes. For example, Q=J_d/d, constant p, and one shared Bernoulli coin across all coordinates preserve a synchronized state and give the scalar beta parameters 2cp and 2c(1-p), rather than 2cdp and 2cd(1-p).

For reproducible finite checks, the exact covariance identity is

    V=B V B^T+r^2*diag(s*(1-s)-diag(Q V Q^T)),
    B=(1-r-f)I+rQ, s=Q z*.

Checks: the scalar exact formula; first four scalar stationary moments; exact polynomial generators; finite-network covariance systems; first four moments of the exact consensus Markov chain for Q=J_d/d at d=2,3,5; a common-coin control that changes the beta variance. The conditional-independence hypothesis is essential for K, the covariance formula and the stationary beta law.
