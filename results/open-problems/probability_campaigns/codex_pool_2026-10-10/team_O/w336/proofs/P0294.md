# P0294: a genuine workload benchmark with zero finite-N bias

Status: proof written; w346 KEEP pending. This is the d=1 random-routing special case, not a result for interacting least-of-d routing with d>=2.

Consider N rate-one workload servers, a Poisson input of rate N*lambda, independent uniformly chosen routing destinations, and i.i.d. nonnegative job sizes S independent of routing. Assume rho=lambda*E[S]<1 and E[S^2]<infinity. The N workload processes are independent M/G/1 workload processes when started in their product stationary law. For every N, the stationary mean of one server (and of the empirical mean workload) is

    lambda*E[S^2]/(2*(1-rho)).

Consequently its expansion in 1/N has exactly zero bias coefficient and zero remainder for this observable; stationary off-diagonal covariances are zero.

Proof: marked Poisson thinning produces N independent rate-lambda input processes with independent job marks. Each queue drains at rate one while positive and has upward jumps S. Its stability and stationary construction follow from the negative-drift reflected compound-Poisson input. The stationary idle mass is 1-rho by workload balance. For beta(s)=E[e^(-sS)] and L(s)=E[e^(-sW)], the bounded stationary generator test e^(-sW) gives

    0=s*(L(s)-(1-rho))+lambda*(beta(s)-1)*L(s),
    L(s)=(1-rho)*s/(s-lambda+lambda*beta(s)), s>0.

The second-moment assumption gives beta(s)=1-E[S]*s+E[S^2]*s^2/2+o(s^2). Expansion at zero gives L(s)=1-[lambda*E[S^2]/(2*(1-rho))]*s+o(s); the right derivative of a nonnegative variable's Laplace transform identifies its finite mean. Product stationarity proves all finite-N claims. This handles the actual empty-workload boundary, unlike a Gaussian OU control model.

For exponential service sizes of rate mu>lambda, P(W=0)=1-lambda/mu and P(W>x)=(lambda/mu)*exp(-(mu-lambda)*x) for x>=0, so the mean is lambda/(mu*(mu-lambda)). These formulas are a control for implementations of any proposed first-order correction. They do not justify setting the correction to zero under workload-dependent routing.

Checks: Laplace-transform generator identities, mean calculations for several service parameters, and exact Poisson-thinning algebra. General least-of-d bias remains unverified and unsolved here.
