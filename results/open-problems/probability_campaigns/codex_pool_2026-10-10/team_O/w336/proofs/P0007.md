# P0007: convex escape and an explicit no-ray corridor

Status: proofs written; w346 KEEP pending. Neither result constructs an all-ray trapping mirror configuration.

Lemma 1. Every unbounded open convex set C in R^d contains an unbounded straight ray starting at each x in C. Therefore, if a mirror-complement component is unbounded, open and convex, an origin in it has an unobstructed escaping launch direction and cannot have every reflected ray bounded.

Proof: choose r>0 with B(x,r) contained in C and y_k in C with |y_k-x| tending to infinity. A subsequence of d_k=(y_k-x)/|y_k-x| tends to a unit direction d. For fixed t>=0 and sufficiently large k, convexity gives a ball centered at x+t*d_k with radius (1-t/|y_k-x|)*r contained in C. Eventually this radius exceeds 3r/4 and the center is within r/4 of x+t*d, so B(x+t*d,r/2) is in C. This holds for every fixed t with the same subsequential direction d, proving the entire ray stays inside C. No collision occurs along it.

Lemma 2. C={(x,y): x>0 and |y-x^2|<1} is open, connected and unbounded but contains no unbounded straight ray.

Proof: (x,u)->(x,x^2+u) is a homeomorphism from (0,infinity) times (-1,1), proving the first three properties. For a ray (a+bt,c+dt), t>=0, with nonzero direction: b<0 eventually violates x>0; b=0 and d!=0 eventually violates the strip condition; b>0 makes y-x^2 a quadratic with leading coefficient -b^2, whose magnitude diverges. Thus all possible directions leave C in finite time.

This gives an explicit algebraic version of the previous spiral-corridor obstruction: unbounded connected vacancy alone does not supply a straight ray. The corridor is not represented as a permitted mirror arrangement, and absence of straight rays does not establish trapping of reflected rays. Convexity is a sufficient special-case condition, not a necessary one.

Checks: algebraic ray exit times for a finite angle grid, openness/connectedness via the explicit parametrization, and the nonconvex midpoint pair (1,1),(3,9), whose midpoint is outside C.
