# C0116: exact exponential cover-time oracle

Status: proof written; w346 KEEP pending. This is an exact finite-instance oracle, not a deterministic polynomial-time (1+epsilon) algorithm.

Let P be a finite irreducible rational transition matrix on n states. For a nonempty visited set S and current state u in S, let V_S(u) be the expected remaining number of steps until every state has been visited. Set V_full=0. For every proper S solve

    (I-P_SS)*V_S = 1 + [sum_{v outside S} P_uv*V_(S union {v})(v)]_(u in S).

Evaluate visited sets in descending cardinality. The exact answer starting at u is V_{ {u} }(u), and the worst-start cover time is their maximum. This takes O(2^n*n^3) rational arithmetic operations; no polynomial bit-complexity bound is claimed.

Proof: the right-hand values concern larger visited sets and are already known. First-step conditioning gives the equation. The killed matrix P_SS has spectral radius less than one: from each state in S, irreducibility provides an at-most-n-step positive-probability path to the complement; taking the minimum of finitely many such probabilities yields a geometric exit bound. Therefore I-P_SS is invertible and its inverse is sum_{k>=0}P_SS^k. This proves existence, uniqueness and the recursion by descending induction. Rational elimination gives exact rational expectations.

Independent controls for simple random walks:

* Complete graph K_n, n>=2: (n-1)*H_(n-1) from every start, because with k visited vertices the probability of a new one is (n-k)/(n-1).
* A path of L edges: expected cover time from i is L^2+i(L-i), by first hitting an endpoint and then the other. Hence worst-start cover time L^2+floor(L^2/4).
* Star with k leaves, k>=2: 2k*H_k-1 from the center and 2k*H_(k-1) from a leaf. Coupon trials are center-to-leaf excursions of length two except the final step. For k=1 both starts have cover time one; that boundary case must be handled separately.

The cached earlier attempt's GFF asymptotic discussion gives no effective finite-instance error bound or deterministic Gaussian-maximum algorithm. This oracle permits exact checks of proposed approximations on small graphs while keeping that unresolved requirement explicit. Its computational approach is classical and no novelty claim is made.

Checks: exact Fraction arithmetic against the three controls, and Bellman residuals on all connected labeled graphs up to four vertices. The checks deliberately keep the exponential algorithm outside any polynomial-time claim.
