## Uniform expansion, with both arrival marginals specified

Let M>0 be finite, eps>=0, and F be a stationary measurable nonnegative process with F <= M. Conditional on F, arrivals are a Poisson process with intensity eps F(ct), for c>0. All jobs require one unit of service and the server works at rate one. Assume q=eps M<1. Let a=E F(0).

Take a Poisson random measure on R x [0,q] with intensity dt dz, independent of F. Keep the point (t,z) in the Cox process exactly when z <= eps F(ct). Conditional on F this is a Poisson process with the required intensity; retaining all points gives a homogeneous Poisson process of rate q. These are the two required marginals.

For an arrival configuration with ages A at time zero, write

    V(A)=sup_{u>=0} (#{a in A: a<=u}-u)_+,
    T(A)=sum_{a in A} (1-a)_+,
    D(A)=V(A)-T(A).

Endpoint choices do not affect these values at a deterministic time almost surely. V is the stationary single-server workload, and T is the total residual work if every job had its own rate-one server.

**Configuration lemma.** D is nonnegative and nondecreasing when arrivals are added. To see nonnegativity, let 0<=a_1<=...<=a_k<1 be all recent ages. Then V>=k-a_k>=k-sum_i a_i=T. If k=0, T=0. To prove monotonicity, adding an age a>=1 leaves T unchanged and cannot lower V. If 0<=a<1, it is enough to prove V_new>=V_old+1-a. For any workload candidate b>=a, the arrival contributes one, so the new value is at least the old candidate plus one. For any b<a, the candidate at a in the augmented configuration is at least N_old(b)+1-a=(N_old(b)-b)+(1-a)+b. If V_old=0, the candidate at a is at least 1-a. Taking the supremum proves the desired inequality. Apply the finite argument on each bounded past interval and pass to increasing intervals. D therefore has the same monotonicity for the stable stationary configurations.

The dominating homogeneous workload V_q has finite moments of all orders. For completeness, choose theta>0 with q(e^theta-1)<theta, possible since q<1. For integer n, an exceedance of N(-u,0]-u over x for some u in [n,n+1] implies N(-(n+1),0]-n>x. Its probability is at most exp(-theta x+q(e^theta-1)) exp(-n[theta-q(e^theta-1)]). Summing over n gives an exponential tail. V<=V_q, and T has finite moments as it is bounded by the recent Poisson count.

Stationary square-workload balance is now integrable: the deterministic drift is -2V, and a unit-service arrival adds 2V+1. Conditioning the Cox compensator on F and the past gives

    E V = eps a/2 + eps E[F(0)V(0)].

Conditional Campbell's formula gives

    E[F(0)T(0)] = eps integral_0^1 (1-u) E[F(0)F(-cu)] du.

For homogeneous rate q, the same square balance gives E V_q=q/[2(1-q)], while Campbell gives E T_q=q/2. Thus E D_q=q^2/[2(1-q)]. The configuration lemma gives 0<=D<=D_q under the stated thinning coupling. Combining these facts yields, uniformly for every c>0,

    E V_c = eps a/2 + eps^2 integral_0^1 (1-u) E[F(0)F(-cu)] du + R_c,
    0 <= R_c=eps E[F(0)D_c] <= eps^3 M^3/[2(1-eps M)].

This proves, rather than assumes, the earlier packet's uniform remainder.

## Explicit increase under faster modulation

Let m=1024, theta=2pi/m, and let X_t be a stationary continuous-time Markov chain on Z/mZ which jumps only clockwise, at rate r=1/sin(theta). Set F(t)=2+cos(theta X_t). The uniform initial law is invariant; 1<=F<=3 and E F=2. The character eigenvalue r(e^{i theta}-1) has real part -delta and imaginary part 1, where delta=tan(pi/m). Orthogonality under the uniform law gives

    E[F(0)F(-s)] = 4+(1/2)e^{-delta s}cos(s).

Put J_delta(c)=integral_0^1 (1-u)e^{-delta cu}cos(cu)du. The proved expansion is

    E V_c=eps+2eps^2+(eps^2/2)J_delta(c)+R_c.

For J_0(c)=(1-cos c)/c^2, the inequality 1-e^{-x}<=x gives |J_delta(c)-J_0(c)|<=delta c/6. Also delta<pi/512: sin x<=x and cos x>=1/sqrt(2) for 0<=x<=pi/4 imply tan x<=sqrt(2)x<2x. With c_1=2pi and c_2=3pi, and the elementary bound pi^2<10,

    J_delta(c_2)-J_delta(c_1)
      >= 2/(9pi^2)-5delta pi/6
      > 1/45-25/1536 = 137/23040.

Take eps=1/10000, M=3. Since R_{c_2}>=0 and R_{c_1}<=27eps^3/[2(1-3eps)],

    (E V_{c_2}-E V_{c_1})/eps^2
      > 137/46080 - 27/19994
      > 137/46080 - 1/700
      = 2491/1612800 > 0.

All queues are stable. Faster modulation strictly raises the mean stationary workload for this construction, conditional on the independent auditor accepting the proof. This is a counterexample to the universal monotonicity clause, not a solution of the entire two-part problem.

## Exact sign test for the second-order coefficient

For continuous R on [0,infinity), Psi(c)=integral_0^1 (1-u)R(cu)du satisfies Psi'(c)=c^{-3}integral_0^c (2v-c)R(v)dv. Substitute v=cu and differentiate c^{-2}integral_0^c(c-v)R(v)dv. Psi is nonincreasing iff the displayed integral is nonpositive for all c>0. This says nothing by itself about the full workload outside light traffic.
