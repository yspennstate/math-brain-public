# P0133: unimodular winding constraint for a theta core

Status: proof of a structural necessary condition, awaiting w346. No limiting probability distribution is claimed. Continues the earlier cycle-rank-two calculation rather than repeating it as a distributional answer.

Let a finite connected graph be cellularly embedded in a torus and T be a primal spanning tree. The dual complement H consists of all dual edges whose primal edges are not in T. H is connected: a nontrivial partition of its dual vertices would give a nonempty primal separating boundary made entirely of T edges; such a boundary is an even edge subgraph and hence contains a cycle, impossible in T. Deleting the dual edges corresponding to T merges the original dual faces along a tree into a single topological disk. Thus H is a connected one-face cellular graph on the torus. Euler's formula gives cycle rank two.

The inclusion H -> torus induces an isomorphism on integral first homology. One way to see this is the cellular chain complex: there is one two-cell, and its attaching walk traverses every oriented graph edge once in each direction, so its boundary is zero in the abelian edge-chain group. Therefore H_1(torus,Z)=ker(boundary_1)=H_1(H,Z). In particular an integral graph cycle basis has winding vectors forming a basis of Z^2, with determinant +/-1.

Suppose additionally that, after pruning leaves and suppressing degree-two vertices, H has a theta core: three paths A,B,C between two branch vertices. Orient all three from the first branch vertex to the second and let their lifted displacements be D_A,D_B,D_C. The three loop windings are

    u=D_A-D_B, v=D_A-D_C, v-u=D_B-D_C,

expressed in units of the two torus periods. The cycles A-B and A-C form an integral basis of the graph's first homology. Hence det(u,v)=+/-1. All three winding vectors are primitive, and the determinant of every pair is +/-1. Up to orientation, the three slopes are a Farey triangle.

This is conditional on the theta-core topology. A bouquet core and differing definitions of edge-simple loops need separate treatment. The constraint alone supplies no weights for the limiting winding distribution and does not turn an arbitrary chosen cycle basis into the requested loop triple.

Source: P0133-A001 and Schramm Problem 6.2 in the cached 2006 paper. Exact finite-grid enumeration will separately test the homology-basis determinant without asserting a scaling limit.

## A bouquet witness under the literal no-repeated-edge convention

For every square torus grid of side L>=3, take the primal comb tree: in every horizontal row retain the L-1 nonwrapping horizontal edges, and in column zero retain the L-1 nonwrapping vertical edges. This is connected with L^2-1 edges and is a spanning tree.

Its dual complement has, after pruning leaves, one horizontal loop and one vertical loop meeting at a single dual vertex. To check this directly, the omitted horizontal wrap edges give the full vertical dual loop in column L-1. The omitted vertical edges give horizontal dual chains in every row, with a wrap edge only in row L-1. All chains in the other rows prune away; row L-1 supplies the second loop.

These two loops have disjoint edge sets and windings (1,0) and (0,1), after choosing orientations. Traversing either alone, or concatenating the first with the second in either orientation, gives four noncontractible closed trails with no repeated edges. Up to reversing the complete trail, their distinct homotopy windings are

    (1,0), (0,1), (1,1), (1,-1).

The concatenations repeat the common vertex but no edge. Thus, under the literal edge-simple definition given as 'no repeating edges' in the cached source, at least four classes occur for this finite-grid tree. A vertex-simple-cycle convention would give only the two individual loops. This requires reconciliation with the source's assertion of precisely three paths before turning it into a universal finite-grid statement. It is not a conclusion about the intended limiting distribution. The witness exists at arbitrarily fine grid sizes, but its probability may tend to zero.
