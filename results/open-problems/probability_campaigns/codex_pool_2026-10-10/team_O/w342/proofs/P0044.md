# P0044: canonical invariant monotone coupling on every tree network

Status: proof of a special case and a refuted route, awaiting w346. The general-network invariant coupling question remains unresolved here. The earlier amenable-action averaging argument remains valid; this special case does not require amenability.

Let G be a countable, connected, locally finite infinite tree with positive finite conductances. The free uniform spanning forest law is the deterministic configuration F=E(G): exhaust G by finite connected subtrees, each of which has exactly one spanning tree, its full edge set. Conductances do not change uniqueness, and every fixed edge eventually appears.

Let W have the wired uniform spanning forest law on the same network. Define the joint law by sampling this W and setting F=E(G) deterministically. Its W marginal is exactly WUSF, its F marginal is exactly FUSF, and W subset F holds for every outcome. The wired law is invariant under each conductance-preserving network automorphism: pushforward transports wired exhaustion measures to the measures on the transported exhaustion, which has the same wired weak limit. E(G) is also fixed by every automorphism. Thus the JOINT law is invariant under all network automorphisms.

This supplies the requested coupling for all locally finite tree networks, including the nonamenable regular tree. Nonamenability therefore cannot itself be an obstruction to invariant monotone coupling; it only prevents the particular Folner averaging proof.

More generally, if two laws on a countable edge set admit an inclusion coupling and have equal one-edge inclusion probabilities at every edge, then the coupled configurations coincide almost surely. Indeed P(e in F minus W)=P(e in F)-P(e in W)=0 for every e, and the countable union is null. If the common marginal is invariant, its diagonal coupling is invariant.

Source: P0044-A001's amenable averaging result and the cached 2026 Lyons-Peres Question 10.6. The cached statement already records invariant couplings on sofic Cayley groups (Lyons-Thom 2016); no claim that arbitrary networks are covered is made.
