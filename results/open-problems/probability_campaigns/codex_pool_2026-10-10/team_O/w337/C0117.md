# C0117: exact complete-graph scale, with the clock convention explicit

Label: proof of a special case and an interpretation check. Blind audit PENDING. The all-graph universal-constant problem remains unresolved.

Let m=n-1. Start simple random walk on K_n at a fixed vertex. When k vertices remain unvisited, the probability that the next step discovers one is k/m, regardless of which visited vertex is current. The strong Markov property gives independent waiting times G_k with geometric(k/m) laws on {1,2,...}. Thus

    tau_cov=sum_{k=1}^m G_k,
    E tau_cov=m H_m,
    Var(tau_cov)=m^2 H_m^(2)-m H_m.

For a target vertex different from the start, its discrete hitting time is geometric(1/m), so t_hit=m. In particular for K_6,

    Var(tau_cov)/t_hit^2=H_5^(2)-H_5/5=145/144>1.

This refutes a UNIT-CONSTANT interpretation of sd(tau_cov)<=t_hit. It does not refute a bound C t_hit for some universal C. The ratio tends to pi/sqrt(6), so any such discrete-time universal C must be at least pi/sqrt(6).

The primary paper states that it works in continuous time with mean-one exponential holding times (cached lines 426-430). In that convention the discovery waiting times are independent Exp(k/m), rather than geometric. The expectation and t_hit remain m H_m and m, while Var(tau_cov)=m^2 H_m^(2). Already K_3 gives variance/t_hit^2=5/4>1. This clock difference must not be lost when reusing a cover-time formula. The limiting standard-deviation ratio is again pi/sqrt(6).

Both conventions have a uniform exponential centered tail. For geometric G with success probability p, write K(lambda)=log E exp(lambda G). Then K''(lambda)=(1-p)exp(lambda)/[1-(1-p)exp(lambda)]^2. For |lambda|<=p/4, (1-p)exp(lambda)<=exp(-3p/4) and 1-exp(-3p/4)>=p/2 for 0<p<=1. Hence K''<=4/p^2. Integrating twice around zero gives

    log E exp(lambda(G-EG)) <= 2 lambda^2/p^2.

At lambda=theta/m, |theta|<=1/4 and p=k/m, independence implies

    log E exp(theta(tau_cov-E tau_cov)/m)
       <=2 theta^2 sum_{k=1}^m 1/k^2 <=4 theta^2.

The last inequality follows from sum_{k>=1}1/k^2<=2 by an integral bound. For independent Exp(k/m) gaps the centered cumulant second derivative after division by m is 1/(k-theta)^2<=4/k^2 for |theta|<=1/4, giving the same bound. Apply Chernoff at theta=1/4 and -1/4 to obtain, for s>=0,

    P(|tau_cov-E tau_cov|>=s t_hit)<=2 exp(1/4-s/4).

For K_n the mean cover time is the same at every starting vertex, so E tau_cov is also the source's t_cov. This settles the requested fluctuation scale for complete graphs only. No transfer from GFF comparison estimates to centered tails for arbitrary graphs is asserted.

The cumulant inequalities are proved above; the numerical values are checks, not a proof for all graphs.

Source: Ding, Lee and Peres, *Cover times, blanket times, and majorizing measures*, arXiv:1004.4371, Question 5.2, cached lines 3876-3879: "Is the standard deviation of tau_cov bounded by the maximal hitting time t_hit?"
