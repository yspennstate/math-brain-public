# P0209: uniform stationary normal approximation

Label: proof of a negative answer to the cached statement; blind audit PENDING.
Literature status: the supplied campaign already contains this negative construction. This is a proof completion and correction of its explanation, not a novelty claim or an independently accepted solution.

The cached Aldous-Fill 4.30 statement asks whether a chain-independent function psi(b,s), tending to zero for each fixed b as s tends to infinity, bounds the normal Kolmogorov distance of the stationary continuous-time integral S_t/(sigma sqrt(t)) when g has stationary mean zero, variance one and sup norm b, and s=t/tau_2. The exact cached locator is *Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs*, Open Problem 4.30, PDF page 137; no current online status was checked.

For an integer s >= 2 put A=1-s^(-2), epsilon=(1-A^2)/(5-4A), B=(5-4A)/(2-A), R=s^4 and t=s. Let Y be the stationary two-state chain with rates 0->1 equal to epsilon and 1->0 equal to 1-epsilon. Independently let Z be a stationary fair sign that flips at rate R/2. Set g(Y,Z)=AZ+B(Y-epsilon). These rational parameters give the same rare-state mechanism as the existing radical parameterization, while allowing exact arithmetic checks.

The product chain is finite, irreducible and reversible. Its generator eigenvalues are 0,-1,-R,-(R+1), so tau_2=1. Its stationary distribution is the product of Bernoulli(epsilon) and a fair sign. Directly, Eg=0. Since 1-epsilon=(2-A)^2/(5-4A), B(1-epsilon)=2-A, and

    A^2+B^2 epsilon(1-epsilon)=A^2+(1-A^2)=1.

Also B>0 and 0<epsilon<1/2. The largest absolute value of g is A+B(1-epsilon)=2: the other positive component is smaller and the largest negative absolute value is A+B epsilon <= A+B(1-epsilon). Thus b=2 at every s, exactly.

Orthogonality and independence give

    Cov(g(X_0),g(X_u)) = A^2 exp(-Ru)+B^2 epsilon(1-epsilon) exp(-u),
    sigma^2 = 2A^2/R+2B^2 epsilon(1-epsilon).

Let H_s be the event that Y starts at zero and never jumps before t=s. Its probability is (1-epsilon)exp(-epsilon s), which tends to one because epsilon is asymptotic to 2/s^2. On H_s the slow contribution to W_s=S_s/(sigma sqrt(s)) is the deterministic center c_s=-B epsilon sqrt(s)/sigma, which tends to zero. The normalized fast contribution V_s has mean zero and variance at most v_s=(2A^2/R)/sigma^2, which is asymptotic to 1/(2s^2). Chebyshev's inequality therefore proves W_s -> 0 in probability. All distributions here are exact; Z is not replaced by a Gaussian.

For a directly checkable Kolmogorov bound set delta_s=s^(-1/2), x_s=c_s+delta_s. Since x_s tends to zero,

    P(W_s <= x_s) >= P(H_s)-v_s/delta_s^2,
    d_K(W_s,N(0,1)) >= (1-epsilon)exp(-epsilon s)-s v_s-Phi(x_s) -> 1/2.

For the matching upper limit, fix eta>0. Convergence in probability to zero makes F_s(-eta) tend to zero and F_s(eta) tend to one. Monotonicity bounds the difference from Phi, outside [-eta,eta], by 1/2 plus a vanishing term and, inside that interval, by Phi(eta) plus a vanishing term. Send s to infinity then eta to zero to obtain limsup d_K <= 1/2. Consequently d_K tends to 1/2, so the proposed uniform bound cannot tend to zero even at b=2 and tau_2=1.

The exact finite-time second moment is

    E W_s^2 = {2A^2[s/R-(1-exp(-Rs))/R^2]
                 +2B^2 epsilon(1-epsilon)[s-1+exp(-s)]}/(sigma^2 s) -> 1.

There is no contradiction: W_s converges to zero in probability but W_s^2 is not uniformly integrable. This corrects the saved memory's assertion, "It does not - its standard deviation stays near 1". The variance argument in that sentence cannot disprove convergence in probability. The original September 6 proof and the stored attempt already gave the correct uniform-integrability explanation; this correction targets the later memory explanation. An independent audit of the full negative answer is still required.

The construction also survives a total-jump-rate bound of one: replace Q by Q/R and observe for time Rs. The maximum outgoing rate is at most 1/2+1/R<=1; relaxation time becomes R, the asymptotic variance rate becomes R sigma^2, and the integral has law R S_s. Thus its normalized law and the ratio time/relaxation time remain unchanged. This check closes the source's clock/rate convention boundary as in the original proof.
