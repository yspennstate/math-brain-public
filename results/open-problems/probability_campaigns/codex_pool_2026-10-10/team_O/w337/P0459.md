# P0459: concentration around the actual bilateral-preference mean

Label: proof of a sufficient finite-n bound and a mean-deficit lemma; blind audit PENDING. Full problem and sharp growth threshold remain unresolved.

Model: K_n has independent identically distributed continuous edge costs. Each vertex chooses its k cheapest incident edges, 1 <= k <= n-1. The bilateral preference graph LK(n,k) retains an edge exactly when both endpoints choose it. Write D_v for its degree and mu=E D_v. Exchangeability makes mu independent of v. Applying the common continuous distribution function reduces costs to independent uniform ranks (ties have probability zero).

For k<n-1 let F(u)=P(Bin(n-2,u)<=k-1). Conditional on the cost u of a fixed edge, the competing costs at its two endpoints are disjoint independent collections. Consequently

    mu=(n-1) integral_0^1 F(u)^2 du.

Let U,V be independent Beta(k,n-1-k) variables, the kth order statistics of n-2 independent uniforms. F(u)=P(U>u). Hence

    mu=(n-1) E min(U,V),
    k-mu=(n-1) E|U-V|/2
          <= sqrt(k(n-1-k)/(2n)) <= sqrt(k/2).

The equality follows from min(U,V)=(U+V-|U-V|)/2 and E U=k/(n-1). The inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz and Var U=k(n-1-k)/[(n-1)^2 n]. For k=n-1, mu=k exactly, so the same zero-deficit bound holds without a degenerate beta law. This proves a bound on the ACTUAL mean, rather than replacing it by k without justification.

For 0<eta<1 put a=sqrt(2k log(2n/eta)). Then

    P(max_v |D_v-mu| > 2a) <= eta.

Proof: if a>=k, |D_v-mu|<=k<=2a deterministically. Otherwise threshold costs at t=(k-a)/(n-1). The threshold degree B_v is Bin(n-1,t), with mean k-a. Bernstein's upper-tail inequality gives P(B_v>k)<=exp(-a^2/[2(k-a+a/3)])<=exp(-a^2/(2k)); the lower-tail Chernoff bound gives P(B_v<k-2a)<=exp(-a^2/[2(k-a)])<=exp(-a^2/(2k)) (and is trivial if k-2a<0). A union bound, without any independence assumption on vertex degrees, gives simultaneous k-2a<=B_v<=k with probability at least 1-2n exp(-a^2/(2k))=1-eta.

On this event every threshold edge is among the k cheapest edges at each endpoint, so it belongs to LK(n,k). Thus k-2a<=D_v<=k. The proved mean-deficit bound gives k-mu<=sqrt(k/2)<2a and mu<=k, so every D_v in that interval has |D_v-mu|<=2a. This completes the probability bound.

In particular k/log n -> infinity implies mu/k -> 1 and max_v|D_v-mu|/mu -> 0 in probability. To see the latter choose eta=1/n for each n, so a=sqrt(2k log(2n^2)) and a/k -> 0. The statement uses 1<=k<=n-1 and a common continuous edge law; arbitrary dependent edge costs are outside its scope.
