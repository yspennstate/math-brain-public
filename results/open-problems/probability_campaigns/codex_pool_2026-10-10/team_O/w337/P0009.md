# P0009: one added mirror can open a bounded trajectory

Label: proof refuting pathwise monotonicity, with exact routing certificates. Blind audit PENDING. Almost-sure localization for every positive iid mirror density remains unresolved.

Fix Manhattan orientations: even horizontal rows point east and odd rows west; even vertical columns point north and odd columns south. A directed state (x,y,a) records the vertex and the incoming axis a (horizontal or vertical). At a mirror the outgoing axis is switched; otherwise it is unchanged. Movement follows the orientation of that outgoing row or column.

The state map is a bijection. Each incoming edge has a unique predecessor vertex, and the local straight or switched routing is a permutation of the two incoming axes to the two outgoing axes. Therefore a bounded infinite trajectory is periodic from its first state: it visits finitely many directed states, and any later repeat can be pulled back by the inverse map to a return to the initial state. This explains why finite-orbit routing, not monotone edge deletion, is the relevant mechanism.

Place mirrors at exactly (-3,0),(0,0),(-3,3),(0,3), with no mirrors elsewhere. Start in incoming horizontal state at (0,0). The trajectory goes north along column 0, west along row 3, south along column -3 and east along row 0. It returns to its initial directed state after 12 steps and is localized.

Now add one mirror at (0,1). The trajectory's first step still goes from (0,0) to (0,1). The new mirror switches its incoming vertical axis to horizontal; row 1 points west. There are no other mirrors anywhere on row 1. The path therefore continues (-1,1),(-2,1),... indefinitely. Adding a mirror has changed a periodic trajectory into an unbounded one.

Thus localization is not an increasing event in the pointwise partial order on mirror configurations. This gives an explicit obstruction to borrowing a pathwise percolation monotonicity argument. These configurations have only finitely many mirrors and have probability zero under every iid density 0<q<1. Consequently the example proves neither nonmonotonicity of the iid localization probability as a function of q nor failure of the conjecture at any positive density. The distinction is essential.

At q=0 there is no turn and a ray travels indefinitely. At q=1 every vertex switches the axis; the trajectory has period four (check the two parities of row and column and both incoming axes). These endpoint checks also do not determine the interval 0<q<1.
