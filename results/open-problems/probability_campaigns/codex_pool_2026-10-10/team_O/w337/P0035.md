# P0035: positive speed on Cartesian products of regular trees

Label: proof of a special case extending the cached single-tree calculation. Blind audit PENDING. Arbitrary Cayley graphs remain unresolved; novelty is not claimed.

Let G be the Cartesian product of m infinite (b_i+1)-regular trees rooted at their chosen identities, with integers b_i>=2. Use the radial lambda-bias: an edge to a closer neighbor has transition weight lambda, and an edge to a farther neighbor has weight one. There are no same-level edges in this product. Suppose 0<lambda<min_i b_i.

Let R_i(n) be the distance in coordinate i and R(n)=sum_i R_i(n). If coordinate i is nonzero, its b_i farther edges have total weight b_i and its one closer edge has weight lambda. If it is zero, its b_i+1 edges all go farther and have weight one. The total graph degree D=sum_i(b_i+1) is finite. The conditional probability of updating any particular coordinate is bounded below by a positive constant depending only on D and lambda. For example min(1,lambda)(b_i+1)/[max(1,lambda)D] is a valid lower bound. Iterating the probability of no coordinate update shows every coordinate updates infinitely often almost surely.

At the successive update times of coordinate i, its radius is a birth-death chain: from a positive state it moves up with probability b_i/(b_i+lambda) and down with probability lambda/(b_i+lambda); from zero it moves to one. The embedded transition probabilities are unaffected by the random times at which other coordinates update. Starting at one, the probability of hitting zero is lambda/b_i<1, as follows by solving the finite gambler's-ruin equation with harmonic function (lambda/b_i)^r and sending the upper boundary to infinity. The strong Markov property makes the number of returns to zero finite almost surely. Each interval while a coordinate waits at zero is finite because of its positive update-probability bound. With finitely many coordinates there is therefore a finite random time after which ALL coordinates are positive.

After that time, putting B=sum_i b_i, the conditional probabilities for the total radius are

    P(delta R=+1 | past)=B/(B+m lambda),
    P(delta R=-1 | past)=m lambda/(B+m lambda).

Its conditional drift is the constant v=(B-m lambda)/(B+m lambda)>0. Subtract each conditional drift from the radius increment to obtain a martingale with bounded increments. Azuma and Borel-Cantelli give M_n/n -> 0 almost surely. The finitely many earlier drift terms contribute o(n), so

    R(n)/n -> (sum_i b_i-m lambda)/(sum_i b_i+m lambda) almost surely.

This proof does not use the last visit time as a stopping time; it uses the pathwise fact that only finitely many pre-constant drift terms occur. No unjustified optional-stopping or localization limit is involved.

If all b_i=b, the speed is (b-lambda)/(b+lambda). The product is a Cayley graph: each regular tree is the Cayley graph of a free product of b+1 involutions, and the coordinate generators give the product Cayley graph. Its exponential growth rate is b. For the growth claim, one coordinate gives a sphere-size lower bound proportional to b^r, while summing all m coordinate radii gives an upper bound C(r+1)^(m-1)b^r; balls have the same exponential rate. Thus this non-tree family satisfies the original range 1<lambda<b in full. For unequal b_i the growth rate is max_i b_i, while this proof only covers lambda<min_i b_i; it does not silently fill the intervening range.

Checks: p0035_check.py verifies exact conditional radial drift for all zero/nonzero coordinate patterns in seven tree-product families at four biases. Every pattern has drift at least the eventual value, and the all-nonzero pattern has that value exactly. Invalid inputs lambda>=min_i b_i are rejected. The balanced b=3, lambda=2 speed is 1/5 for every tested number of coordinates, while lambda=29/10 gives 1/59 and illustrates the near-critical limit.

Sources: Lyons and Peres, *Probability on Trees and Networks*, electronic version 19 August 2026, Question 3.11, printed page 91; original P0035 ledger caveat specifying radial conductances; cached single-tree attempt.
