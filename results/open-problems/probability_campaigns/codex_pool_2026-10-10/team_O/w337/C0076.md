# C0076: necessary normalization and a path-tightness obstruction

Label: proof of a conditional normalization lemma and a counterexample to an inference. Blind audit PENDING. The super-Brownian limit for critical unoriented percolation is not proved.

If the intrinsic survival/one-arm event A_n has c/n<=P(A_n)<=C/n for fixed positive c,C, and n^gamma P(A_n) converges to a finite strictly positive limit, necessarily gamma=1. For gamma<1 the upper bound tends to zero; for gamma>1 the lower bound tends to infinity. A two-sided order bound does NOT imply that n P(A_n) has a limit or identify its constant. It also does not prove that the hypotheses hold for every d>6 and every nearest-neighbor percolation model.

The scaled laws n P are finite only after suitable restrictions away from the zero excursion; their total mass on all paths is n. Any convergence to a canonical excursion measure must specify the restriction or a sigma-finite measure topology. It is not ordinary weak convergence of probability laws on all paths.

One-arm bounds and finite-dimensional convergence alone cannot supply tightness. Here is an explicit continuous-process obstruction on [1,2]. Let U be uniform on [5/4,7/4], n>=8, and put

    Y_n(t)=1+max(1-n|t-U|,0).

For any fixed time t, P(Y_n(t)!=1)<=4/n, and for k fixed times the union bound is at most 4k/n. Hence all fixed finite-dimensional distributions converge to the constant-one process. But at the peak t=U and endpoint t=U+1/n, the value difference is exactly 1. Therefore for every delta>0 and sufficiently large n the modulus of continuity at delta is at least 1 on EVERY path. The family is not tight in C([1,2]) with uniform topology, by the necessary compact equicontinuity condition.

The same spike also obstructs Skorokhod J1 tightness: the three times U-1/n,U,U+1/n exhibit an up-then-down oscillation of height one in an interval of length 2/n. Equivalently any proposed J1 subsequential limit, from the fixed-time distributions at a dense set of its continuity times, would be the constant-one path, and convergence in J1 to a continuous constant implies uniform convergence. Its supremum distance is identically one, a contradiction.

Now activate Y_n with an independent Bernoulli variable B_n of probability 1/n, setting it to the zero path otherwise. Survival at every time in [1,2] has probability exactly 1/n. Restricting n Law(B_n Y_n) to nonzero paths gives the above non-tight law with total mass one. Thus even exact one-arm normalization plus finite-dimensional convergence under the restricted scaled measure does not repair the missing path control. This toy process is not a percolation cluster and makes no claim against the conjectured limit itself.

The actual route still requires intrinsic-distance r-point information, enough control to identify distributions (including moment determinacy if moment methods are used), and a separate tightness/modulus or compact-containment argument in the specified measure-valued path topology. The source's number-of-pivotals two-point theorem cannot be silently replaced by an intrinsic-distance theorem. Spatial diffusivity v also needs the appropriate intrinsic two-point result, not only survival asymptotics.
