# C0130: bounded-cookie truncations give upper bounds, not a fixed finite state space

Label: proof of a reduction and refutation of the earlier finite-state claim; exact finite-interval values. Blind audit PENDING. General escape probability remains unresolved.

Consider a fixed deterministic environment on Z. Each site has at most M cookies with right-step probabilities in [epsilon,1-epsilon], for fixed epsilon>0; after these cookies its probability is 1/2. For the walk started at 1 with all positive-site cookies fresh, let q_L=P_1(T_L<T_0), L>=2, and q=P_1(T_0=infinity).

Stopping on first hitting 0 or L gives a finite chain with state (x,c_1,...,c_{L-1}), 1<=x<L and 0<=c_i<=M, where c_i is the number of departures from site i capped at M. There are at most (L-1)(M+1)^(L-1) transient states. Its boundary conditions are q(0,c)=0 and q(L,c)=1. At an interior state the equation is

    q(x,c)=p(x,c_x) q(x+1,c')+[1-p(x,c_x)]q(x-1,c'),

where c' increments c_x unless it is already M. This is exact and retains the visited-site history. The bound grows with L; a vector whose length can grow indefinitely is not a finite state space. The old attempt's phrase "a finite vector" cannot justify its claimed finite-state whole-half-line reduction.

The events {T_L<T_0} decrease with L. Uniform ellipticity implies absorption on every finite interval almost surely: from any interior state, the probability of taking enough consecutive left steps to hit 0 in the next L steps is at least epsilon^L, regardless of cookie history. Iterating this bound proves that remaining forever inside a fixed interval has probability zero. Thus on {T_0=infinity} the walk reaches every L almost surely, and

    q_L decreases to q.

Consequently each finite-interval calculation is an UPPER bound on half-line escape. Convergence without a quantitative tail estimate gives no certified positive lower bound on q. In a right-transient environment where trajectories from -1 tend to +infinity almost surely, a first step left must return through 0. The escape probability from 0 is then p(0,0) q. The same factor applied to q_L is only an upper bound; right transience is an additional hypothesis.

Two boundary cases can be checked separately. With all cookies fair, q_L=1/L and q=0. If every site's first cookie forces a right step, a walk started at 0 visits a new positive site at each step and never returns, so its escape probability is 1; this deterministic case does not satisfy the uniform-ellipticity hypothesis above.

Checks: c0130_check.py solves the exact rational boundary equations for two cookies of strength 4/5 and for fair cookies at L=2,...,8. It orders cookie vectors by consumed-cookie count and solves the remaining tridiagonal equations within each vector; no Monte Carlo or cookie-reset approximation is used. The initial positive-site state has all counters zero. The exact fair values 1/L and monotone upper bounds are verified.
