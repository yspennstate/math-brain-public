# P0336: opposite vanishing perturbations select distinct plans

Label: proof of a selection obstruction and exact small-grid checks. Blind audit PENDING. Selection by the original independent empirical samples remains unresolved.

Let the first marginal be uniform on [0,1]^2 and the second uniform on [2,3]x[0,1]. Put d=y_1-x_1 and h=y_2-x_2. Every coupling has d in [1,3] and E d=2. Since sqrt(d^2+h^2)>=d, the minimum Euclidean cost is 2, achieved by any coupling that preserves the vertical coordinate. Equality requires h=0 almost surely. This is the existing reduction, not a claim that the horizontal coupling has been determined.

For every epsilon>0 consider c_+=sqrt(d^2+h^2)+epsilon d^2. Jensen's inequality gives E c_+>=2+epsilon E d^2>=2+4epsilon. Equality is achieved by y=(x_1+2,x_2). Equality in both steps requires h=0 and d=2 almost surely, so this plan is the unique optimizer.

For c_-=sqrt(d^2+h^2)-epsilon d^2, write X=x_1 and U=y_1-2. Both X and U are uniform on [0,1], even under arbitrary dependence. Then

    E d^2=4+E(U-X)^2
          =4+1/6-2 Cov(U,X) <= 4+1/3=13/3.

Cauchy-Schwarz gives Cov(U,X)>=-1/12. Equality holds only when U-1/2=-(X-1/2) almost surely, hence U=1-X. Thus E c_->=2-(13/3)epsilon, with equality achieved only by y=(3-x_1,x_2). That plan is the unique optimizer for every epsilon>0. For 0<epsilon<1/3 the perturbed pointwise cost is also nonnegative, although nonnegativity is not needed for the comparison.

Both perturbations are within 9epsilon of Euclidean distance uniformly on these rectangles. Nevertheless their uniquely selected limits as epsilon decreases to zero are distinct: horizontal translation versus horizontal reversal. For example E(d-2)^2 is zero for the translation and 1/3 for the reversal. Therefore convergence of costs and marginals, or merely identifying the set of population optimizers, cannot choose the empirical limiting plan in the original question. The perturbations prove an obstruction to that inference; they are not substituted for the actual sampling mechanism.

The existing stability bound remains useful: sqrt(d^2+h^2)-d=h^2/[sqrt(d^2+h^2)+d], between h^2/(sqrt(10)+3) and h^2/2. Thus cost excess delta above the mean horizontal displacement bounds E h^2 by (sqrt(10)+3)delta. It forces equal vertical coordinates in a limiting optimizer but leaves the horizontal choice free.

The discrete reversal second moment uses its own grid marginal variance, not the continuum value 13/3.
