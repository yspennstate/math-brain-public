# C0022: a summable measurable separator is sufficient; different moments are not

Label: proof of a measurable separation criterion and counterexample to a moment-only route. Blind audit PENDING. Singularity of the percolation scaling-limit laws remains unresolved.

Let P and Q be laws on the same measurable space of compact subsets of a square. Suppose measurable events A_j, each defined from the observed compact set itself, satisfy

    sum_j [P(A_j^c)+Q(A_j)] < infinity.

The first Borel-Cantelli lemma gives P(A_j^c infinitely often)=0 and Q(A_j infinitely often)=0. Therefore A=liminf_j A_j is measurable, P(A)=1 and Q(A)=0. This proves P and Q are mutually singular. Independence of the events A_j is not required. The required events must be functions of the limiting random set, not functions of an unavailable discrete configuration or extra randomness.

A quantitative route is to find, at finer spatial scales, a set statistic T_j and threshold separating the laws with summable wrong-side probabilities. Merely obtaining different limiting distributions for T_j does not give these bounds. Likewise, any finite collection of count moments can differ between mutually absolutely continuous laws.

For an exact illustration on counts {0,1,2,3}, let P=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4) and Q=(5/24,9/24,3/24,7/24). Both laws have full support, so they are mutually absolutely continuous. They have the same first moment 3/2 and second moment 7/2, but their third moments are 9 and 37/4. Their total variation distance is 1/6. Thus even a third-moment difference after matching the first two moments does not establish singularity. The example can be realized by compact sets of these four possible cardinalities.

There is also a mandatory atom check. If both P and Q assign positive probability to the same compact set (in particular the empty set), their unconditioned laws cannot be mutually singular: any P-full event must contain that atom and so has positive Q probability. This is a conditional warning, not an assertion that both of the actual crossing-event laws have an empty atom under the convention in use. The crossing encoding and any conditioning on nonemptiness must be fixed first.

As a test of the criterion, for infinite iid binary sequences take P with success probability 1/4 and Q with success probability 3/4. Define A_j by fewer than half successes among the first 16j entries. Hoeffding gives each wrong-side probability at most exp(-2j), which is summable. The events overlap heavily but the same Borel-Cantelli proof separates the two laws. c0022_check.py verifies the finite errors by exact binomial sums and checks the count-moment example in Fraction arithmetic.
