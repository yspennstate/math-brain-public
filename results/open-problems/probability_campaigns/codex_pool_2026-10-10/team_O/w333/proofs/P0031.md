## The square-root threshold for exceptional first-passage clock time

Label: proof; independent audit pending.

Let tau_i be independent standard Brownian first hitting times of -1. For a deterministic exceptional-index set let m(N) count its first N indices and S_N=sum_(i=1 to m(N)) tau_i. For m(N)>0 and epsilon>0, P(S_N>epsilon N)=erf(m(N)/sqrt(2 epsilon N)). Thus S_N/N tends to zero in probability if and only if m(N)/sqrt(N) tends to zero. A zero density m(N)/N->0 alone is insufficient. If m(N)=O(N^(1/2-delta)) for some delta>0, then S_N/N->0 almost surely. These are clock statements, not a construction of a backward or two-sided Brownian law after a finite random shift.

Proof: By the strong Markov property, the sum of m independent first hitting times of -1 has the law of a Brownian first hitting time of -m. Brownian scaling and the reflection principle give the displayed erf formula. It tends to zero exactly when m(N)/sqrt(N) does. For the almost-sure assertion, use erf(x)<=2x/sqrt(pi) at N=2^k. The probabilities are O(2^(-delta k)) and summable. Borel-Cantelli and monotonicity of S_N extend the limit from dyadic N to every N. The earlier geometric-index result is included as a special case; the exact in-probability threshold is stronger.
