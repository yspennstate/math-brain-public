## Poisson branching extinction duality preserves the total-progeny rate

Label: proof; independent audit pending.

Let a Poisson(lambda) Galton-Watson process have lambda>1 and extinction probability q in (0,1), satisfying q=exp(-lambda(1-q)). Conditional on extinction its offspring law is Poisson(lambda q). If I(a)=a-1-log a, then I(lambda q)=I(lambda) exactly. For lambda=1+epsilon with epsilon decreasing to zero, lambda q=1-epsilon+(2/3)epsilon^2+O(epsilon^3). This is a branching-process mechanism for discrete duality; it does not prove that hypercube clusters after deleting the giant have this law.

Proof: If p_k is the original offspring law, conditioning the k independent descendant processes to become extinct gives p_k q^k/q. For Poisson(lambda), q=e^(lambda(q-1)) reduces this to Poisson(lambda q). Since log q=-lambda(1-q), I(lambda q)=lambda q-1-log lambda-log q=lambda-1-log lambda. Expanding the extinction equation gives 1-q=2epsilon-(8/3)epsilon^2+O(epsilon^3), hence the claimed dual mean. By Stirling, the Borel total-progeny mass has exponential rate I(a). A hypercube proof still needs uniform exploration and dependence estimates; the existing subcritical result must not be replaced by an unverified claim that its matching lower bound is missing.
