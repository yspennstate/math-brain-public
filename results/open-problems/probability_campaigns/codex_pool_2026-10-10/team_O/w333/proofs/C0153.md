## Finite energy obstructs full exterior wiring of every boundary vertex

Label: proof; independent audit pending.

Consider a finite simple graph with an FK(p,q) law, 0<p<1 and q>=1, with no imposed identification of vertices outside a chosen subgraph H. Suppose H has at least two distinct boundary vertices and boundary vertex v has k incident edges outside H. If xi is the partition of the boundary induced by open paths using only exterior edges, then P(xi is not the one-block wired partition)>=(1-p)^k. In a family of maximum degree D this is at least (1-p)^D>0. Therefore uniqueness of a giant component alone cannot justify an assertion that every fixed boundary vertex is wired together with probability tending to one.

Proof: An FK edge conditional open probability is p when its endpoints are already connected and p/(p+q(1-p)) otherwise. Both are <=p for q>=1. Exposing the k exterior edges incident to v in turn gives probability at least (1-p)^k that all are closed. On that event no exterior open path leaves v, so v cannot be identified with another boundary vertex. The domain Markov property gives an interior FK law with the random exterior partition xi, not automatically the fully wired finite-volume law. A correct limit proof must compare the induced exterior partitions or their effects on a smaller ball.
