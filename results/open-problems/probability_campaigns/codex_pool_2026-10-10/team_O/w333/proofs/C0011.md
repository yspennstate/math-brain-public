## Ray-Knight local-time identity retains an additive independent square

Label: proof of a correction to an earlier identity; audit pending.

In the normalized second Ray-Knight identity on a finite symmetric network pinned at a base vertex, the field identity in law is {L_(tau(t))^x + phi_x^2/2}_x =_d {(phi_x+sqrt(2t))^2/2}_x, with the left Gaussian field independent of the walk. It does not assert L_(tau(t))^x =_d (phi_x+sqrt(2t))^2/2. When Var(phi_x)=g>0 and t>0, the local-time marginal has mean t and an atom exp(-t/g) at zero, whereas the shifted Gaussian square has mean t+g/2 and no atom at zero. The omitted term in the earlier C0011 attempt invalidates that transfer step. This correction does not decide present openness of the full planar level-set problem.

Proof: For lambda>=0, independence on the left and the elementary Gaussian-square transform give E exp(-lambda L)=exp(-t lambda/(1+g lambda)). This is the Laplace transform of a compound Poisson sum with Poisson mean t/g and exponential jumps of mean g. It has mean t and zero atom exp(-t/g). Directly E[(phi+sqrt(2t))^2/2]=t+g/2, and a nondegenerate Gaussian hits a single value with probability zero. Thus the claimed equality after deleting the independent square is false.

## Square-root Chernoff rate for inverse-local-time marginals

Label: proof; independent audit pending.

Let L have Laplace transform E exp(-lambda L)=exp(-t lambda/(1+g lambda)), lambda>=0, where t>0 and g>0. For ell>=t, P(L>=ell)<=exp(-(sqrt(ell)-sqrt(t))^2/g). For 0<=ell<=t, P(L<=ell)<=exp(-(sqrt(ell)-sqrt(t))^2/g). At ell=0 the bound is exact: P(L=0)=exp(-t/g). This supplies a one-point rate, not joint spatial convergence of a local-time level set.

Proof: The transform identifies a compound Poisson law with exponential jumps, whose moment generating function is exp(t lambda/(1-g lambda)) for lambda<1/g. For ell>0 choose lambda=(1-sqrt(t/ell))/g in Markov/Chernoff; positive lambda controls the upper tail and negative lambda controls the lower tail. Its optimized exponent is -(sqrt(ell)-sqrt(t))^2/g. Handle ell=0 by lambda tending to minus infinity, or by the compound-Poisson zero atom.
