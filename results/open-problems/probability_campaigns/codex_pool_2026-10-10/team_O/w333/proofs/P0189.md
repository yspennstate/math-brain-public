For q >= 2 and gcd(p,q)=1, set omega=exp(2*pi*i*p/q), c=exp(pi*i/q), U e_k=omega^k e_k and V e_k=c e_(k-1 mod q). Then VU=omega UV, U^q=I, V^q=-I. A=U+V is nilpotent: each row of zI-U-V has only its diagonal and its cyclic off-diagonal entry. A contributing permutation either uses every diagonal or every cyclic entry; choosing one cyclic entry forces the next one until the cycle closes. Hence

    det(zI-A) = product_k(z-omega^k) - c^q = z^q-1-(-1) = z^q.

Cayley-Hamilton gives A^q=0. Thus its empirical eigenvalue measure is exactly delta_0. This is an exact calculation; numerical eigenvalues are unsuitable as a correctness check for this nonnormal example.

Let p_j/q_j tend to an irrational theta and q_j tend to infinity. In the quotient Q=product_j M_(q_j) / norm-null sequences, the classes of U_j,V_j satisfy the irrational rotation relation. Assume the usual simplicity of A_theta. Its universal unital homomorphism into Q is injective and isometric. For any fixed matrix-valued star-polynomial P, complete isometry gives

    limsup_j ||P(U_j,V_j)|| = ||P(u,v)||.

Apply this to every subsequence. If the norms did not converge to the right side, a subsequence separated from it would violate the same limsup equality. This proves norm convergence for every fixed matrix coefficient size.

Its normalized trace is zero when b != 0 (nontrivial cyclic shift), or when b=0,a != 0 (primitive-root geometric sum). The identity word has trace one and the phases converge. These are the canonical irrational-rotation trace values. With that faithful trace, the pair therefore converges strongly in both norm and star-moments.
