# P0127: exact line sampler and a failed growth route

Status: proved special case and exact counterexample, pending w347. Schramm, *Conformally invariant scaling limits: an overview and a collection of problems* (2006v2), Problem 2.9, asks for efficient approximate uniform lattice-tree sampling. Cached text lines 730-740 use trees with n vertices containing the origin. The prior campaign gave a finite but inefficient exact enumeration and warned that a uniform stationary measure does not imply polynomial mixing. The general higher-dimensional question remains unresolved by this packet; current literature status was not verified beyond these caches.

For Z, every n-vertex lattice tree containing zero is an interval [-L,n-1-L] for a unique L in {0,...,n-1}. Choose L uniformly to obtain the exact uniform rooted sampler. It uses O(log n) unbiased random bits in expectation with integer rejection sampling; a compact endpoint representation uses O(log n) bits. Listing all vertices takes O(n) word operations on a RAM with words of O(log n) bits, and its explicit coordinate encoding occupies O(n log n) bits. If trees are instead identified up to translation, there is just one such interval class. These representation and complexity conventions matter.

Uniform frontier growth is biased already at n=3 vertices, in every dimension d>=1. Put D=2d. Start at the origin, choose one of its D edges uniformly, then one of the 2(D-1) available boundary edges uniformly. There are 2D(D-1) equally likely ordered histories. A tree with the root at its center has two histories; a tree with the root at an endpoint has one. There are D(D-1)/2 center-rooted trees and D(D-1) endpoint-rooted trees, for a total of 3D(D-1)/2. Comparing their growth masses with reciprocal total tree count gives total variation exactly 1/6. Therefore this uncorrected growth rule cannot be called an approximately uniform sampler with error tending to zero merely because each local choice was uniform.

Exact enumeration checked d=1,2,3,4: 4,24,60,112 histories and 3,18,45,84 distinct trees; every total variation was 1/6. Audit the rooted-versus-translation convention, vertex-versus-edge count, frontier cardinality, history multiplicities and complexity claim. This counterexample concerns this growth rule, not all possible efficient samplers.
