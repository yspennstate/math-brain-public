# C0151: thinning, endpoint qualification, and literature status

Status: proof of a closure property and a conditional literature reduction, pending w347. The historical question is Szegedy's Conjecture 14.16 in Gabor Pete's cached *Probability and geometry on groups* notes, lines 14624-14628. The local intake's C0151-A001 says the maximum-density conjecture was refuted using Rahman-Virag and Ding-Sly-Sun, but also says those papers were not re-fetched. That is a literature lead, not a fresh primary-source verification. The source files supplied here did not contain either answer paper. Record the lead as Rahman and Virag, *Local algorithms for independent sets are half-optimal*, and Ding, Sly and Sun, *Maximum independent sets on random regular graphs*. Their precise theorem hypotheses and publication metadata must be checked before promoting the literature status.

Let I be an automorphism-equivariant factor-of-i.i.d. independent set on the d-regular tree, with root density a>0. An i.i.d. uniform label can measurably be split into independent uniform labels (for example split its binary digits, ignoring the null set of ambiguous expansions). Use one label family to construct I and the other as independent marks V_v. For any b in [0,a], retain v in I exactly when V_v<=b/a. This remains an equivariant factor of i.i.d., stays independent, and has root density a*(b/a)=b. Density zero is available as the empty set, including when a=0.

Consequently, if A is the supremum of attainable factor densities, every b<A is attainable: choose an attained a>b and thin. The attainable set contains [0,A) and is contained in [0,A]. Inclusion of A requires an attainment argument. Downward closure alone does not imply it; the abstract set [0,1) is a counterexample to that inference. The old sentence calling the attainable set automatically [0,alpha_factor] is therefore unjustified without its endpoint hypothesis. This does not assert nonattainment for this particular tree problem.

The literature-gap reduction is precise even before importing the two papers. Assume their large-d bounds give factor-density supremum A_d<=(1+epsilon)log(d)/d and attainable random-regular independent-set density at least (2-epsilon)log(d)/d for every sufficiently large d. Taking epsilon=1/4 gives 5/4<7/4 and disproves equality of the attainable sets. No claim about a fixed small d follows just from these asymptotics.

Small case: the radius-one factor that keeps a vertex when its label is the strict minimum among it and its d neighbors is independent and has density 1/(d+1). Exact enumeration for d=2,3,4,5 checked 6,24,120,720 label orders, respectively. Independent thinning gives half those densities. These are checks of a basic factor and its boundary behavior, not estimates of the optimal density or a proof of the cited asymptotics.

Audit checks: independent auxiliary labels, equivariance, the zero case, the distinction between supremum and maximum, and the exact scope of the literature gap. No solution of a new open problem is claimed.
