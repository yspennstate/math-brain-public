# C0058: colours alone lose vertex identity

Status: proved necessary-state correction, pending w347. Curien's cached *Peeling random planar maps* notes pose the site-percolation exploration question at text lines 4180-4181. The same notes explicitly allow nonsimple boundaries (lines 1458-1459 and 2112). The original attempt's assertion that Markov peeling exists exactly for bounded face degrees is unsupported; it also treats a colour word as sufficient without a simplicity hypothesis. No site threshold is computed here.

Site labels attach to vertices, not to boundary occurrences. Compare two boundary incidence patterns of length four: [a,b,a,c] and [a,b,c,d]. The first can arise as the boundary walk around a two-edge tree; the second around a square. Both may be bipartite planar-map boundaries and both have colour word UUUU before labels are exposed. Give distinct vertices independent Bernoulli(p) colours. In the first pattern, exposing occurrence zero also fixes occurrence two. In the second, occurrence two is still Bernoulli(p), independent of occurrence zero. After a black first exposure, its conditional black probability is respectively 1 and p. Thus a state recording only the word UUUU and its length cannot determine the next colour transition across these boundary configurations.

For p=1/2 the full four-occurrence colour laws have total variation 1/2; in general it is 2p(1-p). The shared-vertex law is supported on equality of bits zero and two; the distinct-vertex law puts probability 2p(1-p) on disagreement, and on the equality outcomes the shared law exceeds the distinct one. The exact checks at p=1/3,1/2,2/3 gave 4/9,1/2,4/9.

A state meant to give the conditional transition law for every full boundary configuration must therefore retain vertex identities or their incidence partition, or justify an averaging/lumping theorem that removes that information. The example rules out configurationwise lumping by the colour word alone; it does not rule out weak lumping under the special law of a particular exploration. This correction does not prove that a partition-and-colour state suffices for every geometry transition or threshold calculation. Adaptive revelation of independent labels preserves independence of distinct unseen vertices provided the reveal choice does not inspect hidden labels. Unbounded but finite face degrees permit finitely many new labels per step; they do not, by themselves, rule out a Markov description with a larger state. Conversely, bounded face degrees do not automatically bound the accumulated boundary length.

Audit the nonsimple-boundary convention, repeated-vertex correlations, the two conditional laws and the limited scope of the proposed state. This result refutes a compression assumption, not the existence of useful peeling algorithms.
