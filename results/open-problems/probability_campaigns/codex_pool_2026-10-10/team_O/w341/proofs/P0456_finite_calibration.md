# P0456: finite bilateral-preference calibration

Status: proof of boundary cases and a finite obstruction, pending w347. The cached primary source is Hossein Dabirian and Vijay Subramanian, *Connectivity of a Family of Bilateral Preference Random Graphs* (2025 cached version), introductory Open Problems, lines 103-120. It proves a first-order logarithmic threshold but leaves a smaller window. The model assigns one shared independent continuous weight per undirected edge, and both endpoints must rank an edge among their k lowest incident weights. This is not a model with independent preference orders at different vertices.

For k=1 and n>=2, the resulting graph is a matching. A specified edge {i,j} is mutually preferred exactly when its weight is the minimum among the 2n-3 edges incident to either endpoint. Its probability is 1/(2n-3), by exchangeability of independent continuous weights. Events for different mutually preferred neighbors of a fixed i are disjoint. Thus P(deg(i)=1)=(n-1)/(2n-3), P(i isolated)=(n-2)/(2n-3), and E[number isolated]=n(n-2)/(2n-3). Connectivity is impossible for n>2.

Absence of isolated vertices is not a deterministic characterization of connectivity. For n=6,k=2, give all six internal edges of two disjoint triples lower weights than all nine cross edges. Each vertex chooses its two triple neighbors, so the graph is two triangles and has minimum degree two. This ordering event has probability 1/binomial(15,6)=1/5005 under independent continuous weights. More generally two (k+1)-cliques give the same finite obstruction for n=2(k+1). The example does not prove that nontrivial components survive in the asymptotic fine window; such a claim would require its own probability estimate.

Exhaustive checks evaluated 2,173 (edge ordering,k) cases for n=2,3,4. At n=4,k=1 the isolated expectation is 8/5 and the probability of having no isolates is 1/5, while connectivity probability is zero. At n=4,k=2 the isolated expectation is 4/15 and connectivity probability is 11/15. The exact six-vertex two-triangle example is saved separately. The previous mean-matching route remains invalid: a central mean/CLT calculation does not control the isolation far tail or small components.

Audit the shared-weight assumption, incident-edge count 2n-3, disjointness of root matching events and the distinction between a finite obstruction and an asymptotic one. No window width or limiting connectivity probability is claimed.
