Lemma (exact constant scaling): on a finite network with Dirichlet boundary, use the unnormalized conductance Laplacian L, and define the DGFF covariance as L^(-1). Replacing every conductance c_e by a*c_e, a>0, replaces L by aL and covariance by L^(-1)/a. The centered Gaussian field therefore has exactly the law h/sqrt(a). Any already established deterministic constant-conductance level-set or extremal-process limit rescales by this same identity. This proves the constant-environment special case; it does not provide the arbitrary ergodic-environment limit.

Lemma (energy comparison): if lambda<=c_e<=Lambda on a fixed finite underlying network with unit-conductance Dirichlet Laplacian L_0, then lambda L_0 <= L_c <= Lambda L_0 as quadratic forms. All operators are positive definite when each interior component reaches the boundary. Inverting the positive-definite forms gives L_0^(-1)/Lambda <= L_c^(-1) <= L_0^(-1)/lambda. Proof of inversion: for any f, f^T L^(-1)f=sup_v (2f^Tv-v^TLv); increasing L decreases this supremum. Thus every linear functional has its variance between the corresponding two constant multiples of the standard DGFF variance. This comparison does not establish the extremal law or high-point joint structure.

Route audit: convergence of errors to zero on successive scales does not imply a bounded accumulated covariance error. The explicit error sequence e_j=1/j tends to zero but its partial sums diverge. A multiscale proof asking for an O(1) accumulated remainder must produce an appropriate summability or cancellation estimate; "ergodicity gives a local limit" alone supplies no such rate. This is a logical obstruction to the stored argument, not a constructed random-conductance counterexample to C0010.

Nor does dependence on local conductances by itself prove that the limiting cluster law must be a random law indexed by the observed environment: a marked/environment-averaged deterministic law could be possible. Determining the correct theorem statement requires a source and a proof.
