# C0146: monotone coupling on finite cactus graphs, with exact finite tests

Labels: proof of a graph-class special case; finite parameter tests on other graphs are numerical evidence only. Pending w347 KEEP. The arbitrary-graph q<1 problem, and any q=0 limiting convention, remain unresolved here.

Let G be a finite simple cactus graph (each edge belongs to at most one simple cycle) with free boundary conditions. Fix q>0. The FK(p,q) weight of an edge set A is p^|A| (1-p)^(|E|-|A|) q^k(A), where k(A) counts connected components including isolated vertices. There is a simultaneous monotone coupling for all p in [0,1].

Proof of factorisation: in a cactus, the cycle rank of A is exactly the number of original cycles fully present in A. Euler's identity gives k(A)=|V|-|A|+sum_cycles 1_{cycle subset A}. After removing the constant q^|V|, weights factor over bridge edges and the edge-disjoint cycle blocks. A bridge is Bernoulli with parameter t=p/(p+q(1-p)), which increases in p.

For a cycle of length m, the number K of open edges has mass proportional to binomial(m,k) w^k q^(1_{k=m}), with w=p/[q(1-p)]. Conditional on K=k its edge set is a uniform k-subset, independent of p. For p_2>p_1 the ratio of the two K-masses is a positive constant times (w_2/w_1)^k, increasing in k. This likelihood-ratio order implies stochastic order: multiplying a distribution by an increasing function makes the expectation of every increasing function larger, since their covariance is nonnegative (expand as the average of (f(i)-f(j))(h(i)-h(j))). Couple K_p by the inverse CDF using one uniform U for all p. Couple the uniform subsets by taking the first K_p edges of one uniform random permutation. Both choices are nested as p increases. Use independent U and permutations for different cycle blocks and independent bridge uniforms; the factorisation then gives the required FK marginal and inclusion simultaneously for all p. Endpoint p=0 is all closed and p=1 all open. This proof works also for 0<q<1 without using FKG.

This factorisation fails on theta graphs and K4 because cycles share edges. A separate exact finite-parameter experiment checks all increasing events at each tested (p,q) on those graphs. It uses d/dlogit(p) P(A)=Cov(1_A, |omega|). With integer-scaled weights W_omega, the derivative sign is the sign of sum_{omega in A} W_omega (|omega| Z-Z_1), where Z=sum W and Z_1=sum |omega|W. Minimising that sum over upward-closed subsets of the Boolean lattice is a minimum-cut problem: negative-cost vertices receive source arcs, positive-cost vertices receive sink arcs, and each one-edge inclusion implication receives a capacity larger than all finite costs. A negative optimum is an explicit increasing-event violation. Zero is expected when no negative derivative exists, since the empty and full events have derivative zero. Triangle cases are cross-checked by brute force over all event sets. These pointwise tests cannot prove monotonicity throughout the parameter domain or a simultaneous coupling on arbitrary graphs.

Source: C0146-A001, Pete, Probability and geometry on groups, Question 13.2, p.185. The previous attempt proposed a theta/K4 test but did not run it. Only positive q is treated mathematically; FK at q=0 requires a separate definition.
