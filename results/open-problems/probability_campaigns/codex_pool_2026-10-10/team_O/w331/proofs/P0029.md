# P0029: attainable peak sets and exact finite-grid controls

Label: proof of a support lemma plus exact finite numerical evidence, pending w347 KEEP. The source asks whether distance/n tends to zero, despite its informal repel wording; we retain the displayed question.

For a finite connected graph G, a subset S is the exact set of strict local maxima of some bijective vertex labeling if and only if S is nonempty and independent. Necessity: the globally largest label is a local maximum, and two adjacent vertices cannot both exceed each other. Sufficiency: give S the |S| largest labels. Order the other vertices by nonincreasing distance from S, giving nearer vertices larger labels; resolve equal-distance ties arbitrarily. Every nonroot has a neighbour one step closer to S, hence a neighbour with a larger label. Every root has only nonroot neighbours and hence is a peak. There are no other peaks.

Consequently any two nonadjacent prescribed vertices can be the two peaks with positive probability under a uniform random permutation. On square grids, both graph distance 2 (n>=3) and distance 2(n-1) are in the conditional support. Support alone cannot determine concentration or an asymptotic probability: rare configurations at macroscopic distance do not refute distance/n ->0, and close configurations do not prove it.

Exact permutation counts for the 2x2, 2x3 and 3x3 grids accompany this proof. They extend the prior 2x2 count of 8/24. They are finite controls and do not establish a limiting separation law. The remaining route is a quantitative bound on the weighted counts of acyclic orientations with two sinks (each orientation is weighted by its number of linear extensions), not merely existence of orientations.
