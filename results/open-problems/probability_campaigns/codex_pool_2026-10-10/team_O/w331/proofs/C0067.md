# C0067: comparable susceptibility values do not locate the derivative maximum

Label: proof of a counterexample to a calculus inference, pending w347 KEEP. The constructed functions are not percolation susceptibilities; this does not refute Open Problem 13.1.

For V>=1000 set a=V^(1/3), p_c=1/2, p_0=3/4, h=V^(-2), and f_V(p)=a exp(arctan(a(p-p_c))). This positive increasing C1 function has logarithmic derivative a/(1+a^2(p-p_c)^2), maximised at p_c. Let S(t)=0 for t<=-1, S(t)=1 for t>=1, and S(t)=1/2+3t/4-t^3/4 between -1 and 1. This is C1 and increasing, with S'(0)=3/4. Define g_V(p)=f_V(p) exp(S((p-p_0)/h)/10).

At every point f_V<=g_V<=exp(1/10) f_V. Yet the logarithmic derivative of g_V at p_0 is a/(1+a^2/16)+(3/40)V^2, whereas throughout |p-p_c|<=V^(-1/3) the perturbation derivative is zero and the logarithmic derivative is at most a. The maximum is therefore outside this window. The same holds outside every fixed K V^(-1/3) window for sufficiently large V. The perturbation preserves monotonicity and value bounds while changing derivative location drastically.

Thus two-sided plateau bounds for susceptibility itself cannot by themselves establish the location of the maximum of its logarithmic derivative. A precise sufficient missing input is an interior lower bound for this derivative that strictly beats a uniform exterior upper bound. The source's actual percolation structure may prohibit the constructed narrow bump; proving such derivative or pivotal control is the work still required. This attempt concerns p_c^(1); no assertion about the other definitions follows from it.
