# C0075: normalised moment measures give a precise conditional reduction

Label: proof of a general random-probability-measure lemma and a refuted finite-moment route, pending w347 KEEP. No percolation scaling limit is claimed proved.

Let S be Polish, let mu_n and mu be random Borel probability measures on S, and assume that for every integer r>=1 the deterministic probability measures E[mu_n^{tensor r}] converge weakly on S^r to E[mu^{tensor r}]. Then mu_n converges in distribution to mu in the space P(S) with its weak topology.

Proof of tightness: the first moment measures converge and hence form a uniformly tight family. Given epsilon>0, choose compact K_j with sup_n E[mu_n(K_j^c)] <= epsilon*2^(-2j), j>=1. Markov's inequality and a union bound show that with probability at least 1-epsilon, all inequalities mu_n(K_j^c)<=2^(-j) hold. The corresponding set A of measures is closed (K_j^c is open, so nu -> nu(K_j^c) is lower semicontinuous) and uniformly tight; Prokhorov makes A compact. Hence the laws of mu_n are tight.

Proof of uniqueness: any subsequential law limit has the same expectations of products of bounded continuous integrals nu(f_1)...nu(f_r) as mu, by the assumed moment-measure convergence. For a finite family of bounded f_i the integral vector lies in a compact box. Polynomial approximation on that box determines its distribution from all mixed moments. A countable family of bounded continuous functions determining the weak Borel structure on P(S) then determines the law of the random measure. Every subsequential limit therefore has law mu.

The normalisation matters. E[mu^{tensor r}](S^r)=1 for every r, not a factorially growing quantity. No Carleman bound is needed for these bounded evaluation vectors. Pointwise limits of characteristic functions of these probability moment measures imply their weak convergence if the limiting characteristic function is continuous at the origin; mass one alone is not a tightness proof (delta_n escapes on R).

Knowing only the first few moment measures is insufficient. On S={0,1}, set mu_P=(1-P)delta_0+P delta_1. Compare P uniform on [0,1] with P supported on {0,1/2,1} of weights {1/6,2/3,1/6}. They have the same moments through degree 3, so the first three moment measures agree, but E P^4 is 1/5 versus 5/24. Their fourth moment measures, and their laws, differ.

For the critical-cluster empirical measure n^(-1) sum_{x in C(0)} delta_{n^(-1/4)x}, conditioned on |C(0)|=n, apply the lemma only after checking convergence of every normalised moment measure to the corresponding ISE moment measure. Verifying those lace-expansion asymptotics, particularly the nearest-neighbour regime and the correct scaling constants, remains the substantive gap. A theorem about two or three point functions cannot be promoted to the required all-order statement.
