# P0021: boundary deletion need not disconnect a general planar zero-area continuum

Label: proof of a deterministic obstruction to a generic topological route, pending w347 KEEP. This is not a Brownian-trace counterexample.

Let K be the standard Sierpinski carpet in [0,1]^2, obtained by repeatedly deleting the open middle square from each retained square. It is compact and connected and has area zero, since the area of the stage-n approximant is (8/9)^n. Its complement has the deleted open squares as bounded connected components and the exterior of the unit square as its unbounded component.

Take x=1/4. Its ternary expansion is 0.020202...; therefore it belongs to the middle-third Cantor set and is not an endpoint of a complementary interval. The horizontal projection of every closed deleted square is contained in a closed middle-third gap from a finite stage, so none contains x. Consequently every point (1/4,y) with 0<y<1 lies in K and on none of the boundaries of any complementary component. The exterior component's boundary is the perimeter of the unit square and also misses this open vertical segment. After deleting all component boundaries, K therefore retains a connected nontrivial segment and is not totally disconnected.

Zero area, empty interior and connectedness of the original planar continuum cannot suffice to prove the Brownian assertion. Brownian-specific geometry must rule out this mechanism. The earlier time-set argument remains invalid too: a continuous image of a totally disconnected compact set can be connected (the Cantor-function example). Neither route settles the spatial remainder.
