# P0355: strict-discount example and a moment obstruction

Labels: proofs of special cases and a sufficient condition, pending w347 KEEP. These do not settle Conjecture 34 under Corollary 33 and condition (39). The source's exact hypotheses are not silently replaced.

The prior attempt uses the degenerate homogeneous recursion with N=2, eta=0, and xi_1=xi_2=1. Constant positive roots are then endogenous; strict discounts invalidate that particular constant example.

For a genuinely nondegenerate homogeneous strict-discount example, let X=(1/2) max(X_1,X_2), with no random innovations. For each a>0 the distribution F_a(x)=exp(-a/x), x>0, F_a(x)=0 for x<=0, is a fixed point: F_a(2x)^2=F_a(x). It is nonminimal, since the zero distribution is also a fixed point. A consistent invariant RTP exists: assign independent F_a variables at each finite boundary and propagate upward; the distributions at every fixed finite set of generations are unchanged when an extra boundary generation is added, so Kolmogorov consistency supplies the infinite process. All innovations are deterministic. A nondegenerate root cannot be measurable with respect to their trivial sigma-field, hence this RTP is nonendogenous. This establishes a special case supporting the nondegenerate direction, not a verified instance of every hypothesis of the source's parametrisation.

A separate sufficient condition explains why a simple contraction does not settle these heavy-tailed fixed points. Consider a deterministic b-ary invariant RTP with recursion X=g(xi;X_1,...,X_b), iid child variables with the root law, and the usual independence of innovations from descendant root variables. Assume |g(xi;x)-g(xi;y)| <= c max_i |x_i-y_i|, where 0<c<1. If E|X|^r<infinity for some r>0 and b*c^r<1, then the RTP is endogenous.

Proof: reveal the innovations down to depth n and independently sample two conditional continuations. Recursion gives |X-X'|^r <= c^(nr) sum_{|v|=n}|X_v-X'_v|^r. Taking expectations bounds this by (b*c^r)^n K, with finite K=E|X-X'|^r (use |x-y|^r <= max(1,2^(r-1))(|x|^r+|y|^r)). Thus the two conditional roots differ by more than epsilon with probability tending to zero. For every bounded Lipschitz test function h, their squared h-difference also tends to zero, so the expected conditional variance of h(X) tends to zero. The conditional expectations converge to the expectation given all innovations. A countable separating family of such h then shows that the full conditional root law is a point mass; equivalently X is measurable from all innovations.

In the F_a example, P(X>x)~a/x, so E X^r is finite exactly for 0<r<1, whereas 2*(1/2)^r<1 exactly for r>1. The two conditions never overlap. At r=1 the moment diverges and the contraction is critical. Thus this contraction cannot certify endogeny of the nonminimal example.
