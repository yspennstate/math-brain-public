# P0033: quantitative rederivation of the recorded AND/OR candidate

Label: proof of the following construction, pending w347 KEEP and source-statement audit. This is a continuation of the September candidate, not a novelty claim or an accepted solution.

Let GW have offspring D=2 with probability 3/5 and D=3 with probability 2/5. At a two-child vertex use AND; at a three-child vertex use OR. Independent Bernoulli(1/2) boundary spins at depth n induce a root spin with marginal 1/2, since (3/5)(1/2)^2+(2/5)(1-(1/2)^3)=1/2.

Let F_n reveal the offspring numbers above depth n, and q_n be the conditional probability of root spin 1 given F_n. Integrating one extra generation replaces each new subtree by an independent Bernoulli(1/2) spin, so q_n is a bounded martingale. Couple two root spins by the same finite tree and independent boundary spins. The child pair has marginal 1/2 and disagreement d_n=2 E[q_n(1-q_n)]. Distinct child pairs are independent, because the GW subtrees and their boundary spins are independent. AND of two pairs has disagreement d-d^2/2; OR of three has disagreement 3d/4-3d^2/4+d^3/4. Consequently

    d_(n+1) = (9/10)d_n - (3/5)d_n^2 + (1/10)d_n^3 <= (9/10)d_n,
    d_0 = 1/2,    E[q_n(1-q_n)] <= (1/4)(9/10)^n.

The inequality holds for 0<=d<=1 because the difference from (9/10)d is d^2(d-6)/10 <=0. Martingale convergence gives q_n -> q_infinity in L2 and almost surely. Taking expectations yields E[q_infinity(1-q_infinity)]=0, so q_infinity is a {0,1}-valued measurable function C(T), with probability 1/2 of each value. Since q_n=E[C(T)|F_n], predicting C by whether q_n is at least 1/2 has mean error E min(q_n,1-q_n)<=2 E[q_n(1-q_n)]<=0.5*(0.9)^n. Exact arithmetic verifies posterior variance <=1e-6 at depth 118 and this prediction-error bound <=1e-6 at depth 125. These are theoretical depth guarantees, not claims that an exponentially large tree prefix is cheap to enumerate. Taking limits in the polynomial recursions at every vertex shows that C obeys the AND/OR rule almost surely. There are countably many vertices; intersect their probability-one events. This construction ignores child ordering, so it descends to unlabelled rooted trees and is equivariant.

Unanimity preservation guarantees a child of the parent's colour: an AND=1 parent has all children 1, AND=0 has at least one 0; OR=0 has all 0, OR=1 has at least one 1. Choose uniformly among children with that colour. The transition weights sum to one and multiplying successive weights defines a consistent flow. The colour and the good set on which every descendant satisfies this binary limiting rule are Borel, because they are constructed from limits of finite-tree polynomials and countably many descendant conditions. To define a Borel rule on all infinite locally finite rooted trees, use the colour selector on this GW-full good set; elsewhere choose uniformly among children with infinite descendant subtrees. An infinite locally finite tree always has such a child, and this predicate is Borel and equivariant. Thus the fallback extends the flow rule without changing it GW-almost surely.

For any bounded measurable test function f of a child subtree, condition on the parent's degree and the entire child-colour vector. Independence of the GW subtrees implies that each eligible child has the distribution GW conditioned on C=c, where c is the parent colour. The eligible-child count depends only on this vector; averaging uniformly among them therefore has conditional expected f equal to E_GW[f(T) | C(T)=c]. Integrating over the parent colour, whose distribution is also Bernoulli(1/2), returns E_GW f(T). Thus GW itself is stationary, hence equivalent to GW.

Every transition preserves C. Since C is nonconstant with both values of mass 1/2, the stationary chain is not ergodic. The only claimed resolution is this precise construction under the flow convention in the cited source; prior art and the original source statement still require audit.
