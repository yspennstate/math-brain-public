# P0229: continuity and first moments do not give an integrable sampled supremum

Labels: proof of an obstruction in the class of continuous cost fields, and a conditional chaining reduction. Pending w347 KEEP. This is not a counterexample satisfying all SIRSN route axioms.

Let J>=1 have P(J=j)=2^(-j). Inside the unit disk choose disjoint balls centred at z_j=(1/2+2^(-j-2),0), radius r_j=2^(-j-5). Let psi_j be a continuous nonnegative bump supported in that ball, bounded by 1 and equal to 1 at its centre. Define B(z)=(2^J/J) psi_J(z), and L(z)=|z|+B(z). Each realised field is continuous, finite, has L(0)=0, and L(z)>=|z|. Since the supports are disjoint, E B(z)<=1/j at a point in support j and is zero elsewhere. Every support is at distance greater than 1/2 from the origin, so E L(z)<=3|z|. Nevertheless sup B=2^J/J and E sup B=sum_{j>=1}1/j=infinity. The supremum of L is finite almost surely but has infinite mean.

An infinite independent iid uniform sample is dense in the disk almost surely. By continuity its supremum of L equals the full supremum. Thus the infinite-sample version has the same failure. Truncating to J<=N gives expected bump supremum H_N, while the pointwise first-moment bound stays unchanged. The construction does not supply compatible routes or scale-invariant spatial-network geometry, so it refutes only an inference from continuity and individual mean-length bounds.

The previous stronger increment route remains valid under its stated assumptions: on a containing square suppose a jointly measurable L has one integrable anchor and E|L(x)-L(y)|^p<=C|x-y|^p for p>2. At dyadic level k there are O(2^(2k)) parent-child and neighbour edges of length O(2^(-k)). The expected maximum increment is bounded by the p-th root of the sum of p-th moments, hence O(2^(-k*(1-2/p))). The sum is finite. Dyadic telescoping, including neighbouring edges, gives a continuous modification and an integrable supremum. Stochastic continuity identifies this modification at each deterministic point; joint measurability and independence of the uniform sample permit Fubini, identifying it at each sampled point, and countability handles the whole sequence. The required increment estimate still has to be derived from a genuine route model.

Source: P0229 open-problem and attempt cards, citing Aldous, Scale-invariant random spatial networks, Open Problem 34, PDF p.50. This makes precise why the previous increment assumption is materially stronger than a first-moment hypothesis.
