# C0098: a decidable {3,4} subfamily and a corrected finite-perturbation claim

Label: proofs of boundary cases, pending w347 KEEP. The general five-probability algorithm is not obtained.

Use the standard Z2 sandpile convention: a site of height at least 4 topples by losing 4 and sending one chip to each of its four neighbours. A stable configuration has heights at most 3. Under any fair legal schedule, every deterministic configuration s>=3 with at least one site of height 4 is nonstabilising, and every site topples infinitely often.

Proof: if every site toppled only finitely often, its finite nonnegative integer odometer u would satisfy s+Delta u<=3, with Delta u(x)=sum_{y~x}u(y)-4u(x). Thus Delta u<=0 everywhere and Delta u<=-1 at a height-4 site. Every finite nonnegative superharmonic function on recurrent Z2 is constant: for simple random walk started at x and stopped on hitting y or at n, superharmonicity and nonnegativity give u(x)>=u(y) P_x(T_y<=n); recurrence makes this probability tend to 1. Interchanging x and y proves equality. Constancy contradicts the strict inequality at the seed. Hence some site must topple infinitely. Infinite topplings send infinitely many chips to every neighbour, and fairness then forces that neighbour to topple infinitely; connectedness propagates this to all sites.

For iid heights supported on {3,4}, if P(s=4)=0, the field is identically 3 and is already stable. If P(s=4)>0, almost surely there is a height-4 site (indeed infinitely many), so the preceding deterministic argument gives probability one of infinite toppling at every site. For rationally specified probabilities this gives a finite algorithm on that face by testing P(4)=0 exactly. The probability classification holds for arbitrary real probabilities; we do not claim a decidable zero test for an unspecified real-number encoding. Every law supported on {0,1,2,3} is also already stable.

In particular the stable background s=3 plus one chip at the origin is explosive. This corrects the prior attempt's blanket claim that a stable configuration plus a finite perturbation has answer zero. Such a claim needs extra restrictions on the background. The recurrence/superharmonic proof has no finite expected-odometer assumption and does not use uncertain density-threshold recollections.

Finite sink-box stabilisations are recorded only as numerical controls; they terminate because of boundary dissipation and are not a proof of the infinite-volume assertion. The main inputs are recurrence of the nearest-neighbour Z2 walk and the existence of a finite odometer if a fair legal evolution stabilises, both stated explicitly.

Source: C0098-A001, Levine-Peres sandpile problem (1), p.26, and the elementary least-action odometer relation. The deterministic counterexample is classical in nature; novelty is not claimed.
