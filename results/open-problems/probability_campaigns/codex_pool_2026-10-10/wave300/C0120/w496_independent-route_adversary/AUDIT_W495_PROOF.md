L1: Recomputed the two quantile-event bound directly. For Q(b)=1/m, b>=1 and q=b+1/b, Mills gives Q(q)/Q(b)>=(2/5)e^(-3/2)=a. The independent-copy variance identity gives Var(max G_i)>=(1-e^(-a))/(4 b^2)>=(1-e^(-a))/(8 log m). The upper integral is sum_i of the derivative-indicator covariance: Gaussian hypercontractivity gives m^(-(1-u)/(1+u)) after u=e^(-t), whose integral over [0,1] is at most 2/log m. The indicator functions are bounded, and the finite maximum is Lipschitz; weak derivatives and Sobolev approximation supply the covariance identity. No independent-prefix or limit-distribution assumption enters.

L2: The deterministic comparison |max_i(U_i+S_i)-max_i S_i|<=max_i|U_i| is correct. Each prefix has variance r. P(max_i|U_i|^2>x)<=min(1,2m e^(-x/(2r))) is a union bound, valid for the correlated prefixes. Splitting at 2r log(2m) gives exactly B=2r(log(2m)+1), not a product of separate tail bounds.

L3: Checked the graph indexing. The weighted internal levels are 0,...,r-1; the m private chains start at level r and each contain n=m vertices, for total path length L=n+r. Rooted binary-tree automorphisms act transitively on chains and preserve the joint iid law. Uniqueness holds almost surely, so the selector is equivariant and each chain is selected with probability 1/m. The coefficient at an internal level-k vertex is 2^(-k); the coefficient at any chain vertex is 1/m. Summing squares gives sum_{k=0}^{r-1}2^(-k)+mn/m^2=2-2/m+n/m=3-2/m. Integration by parts is for the original independent Gaussian coordinates. No step treats the different prefixes as independent.

L4: Let x=n/log m and c=(1-e^(-a))/8. Under B<=cx/4, the reverse L2 triangle inequality gives SD(M)>=sqrt(cx)-sqrt(B)>=sqrt(cx)/2, hence Var(M)>=cx/4. For the upper bound, Var(M)<=2 Var(A)+2 Var(M-A)<=4x+2B<6x because c<1/8. The condition eventually holds along m=2^r. A counterexample along the path lengths L=2^r+r is sufficient to refute a uniform geometry-free power bound. This is an asymptotic graph-family assertion, not a statement for every small r with the same explicit threshold.

L5: The factor beta^(-1) in the normalized free energy is essential. It makes coordinate derivatives equal to Gibbs occupation probabilities, rather than beta times those probabilities. Pointwise M-log(m)/beta<=F_beta<=M gives squared comparison error at most 2B+2(log(m)/beta)^2. For each fixed beta>0 this is O_beta(r^2), so the same eventual variance bounds hold. Transitivity concerns expected Gibbs chain mass, not conditional independence; each chain has expected mass 1/m. Summing the squared expected derivatives gives the same first-chaos variance. Total squared gradient is <=L because all occupations lie in [0,1] and their sum is L.

Boundary control: the beta qualifier cannot be dropped or made uniform down to zero. At beta=0 the continuous extension is the arithmetic mean of the path scores, whose variance is exactly 3-2/m. For each fixed finite graph, F_beta converges in L2 to that mean as beta decreases to zero (domination by the finite maximum absolute path score suffices). Thus a diagonal sequence beta_r>0 can make Var(F_beta_r) bounded. This is compatible with w495's fixed-beta assertion and prevents a uniform-in-beta reading.
