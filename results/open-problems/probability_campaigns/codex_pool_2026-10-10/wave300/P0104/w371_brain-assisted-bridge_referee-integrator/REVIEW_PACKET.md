### w369 verdicts

- **KEEP L0, with source scope recorded above.** If every interval of length `L` has mass at most `1-epsilon`, apply this to the length-`L` interval centered at `E X`. At least `epsilon` mass lies at squared distance `>L^2/4`; hence `Var(X)>=epsilon L^2/4`. This does not certify any new FPP variance exponent.
- **KEEP L1.** For disjoint events `X<=a` and `X>=b` of probability at least `p,q>0`, minimize `p(mu-a)^2+q(b-mu)^2` over `mu in [a,b]`; the minimum is `pq(b-a)^2/(p+q)`. Outside the interval, the one-sided bound is stronger. The proposed three-point equality law has mean `(pa+qb)/(p+q)` and attains equality. Applying this to Theorem 1 above recovers only a `c log ||x||` lower bound (if the variance is finite).
- **KEEP L2.** With an independent copy `X'`, `E(X-X')^2=2 sigma^2` and `E(X-X')^4<=16K sigma^4`. If an interval of width `ell` holds mass `1-p`, its product event holds mass at least `1-2p`; Cauchy-Schwarz gives `2 sigma^2<=ell^2+4 sqrt(2Kp) sigma^2`. At `p<=1/(32K)` the last coefficient is at most one, so `ell>=sigma`. Chebyshev supplies width `2sigma/sqrt(p)`.
- **KEEP L3, conditional bridge only.** Independently, for centered `U_n`, set `r_n=sqrt(Var U_n)/a_n`. If a subsequence has `r_n->0`, Chebyshev makes its weak limit degenerate. If `r_n->infinity`, Paley-Zygmund on `U_n^2` yields probability at least `1/(4K)` outside every fixed compact after scaling, contradicting tightness. Thus `r_n` stays within positive finite bounds. The fourth-moment cap then gives uniform integrability of scaled squares, so variances converge to the nonzero variance of the weak limit. For arbitrary deterministic centers, symmetrize with independent copies: `(X_n-X_n')/a_n` tends to `Z-Z'`, whose centered kurtosis is at most `(K+3)/2`. The centered argument applies and yields `Var(X_n)/a_n^2->Var(Z)`. No lattice input establishes the premises.
- **KEEP L4 as an abstract counterexample.** Its rare atom contributes `n^(-1/6)*n log n=n^(5/6) log n` to variance, so the variance exponent is `5/12`. Its probability `n^(-1/6)` vanishes, allowing weak `n^(1/3)` scaling. Writing `t=log n`, the normalized ordinary atom is `sqrt(t)e^(-t/6)<=sqrt(3/e)<2`; the three tail regimes in the proof establish the claimed uniform exponential bound at `sqrt(n/log n)`. This is not an iid FPP model.
