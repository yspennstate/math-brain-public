## w468

**KEEP, finite-tree scope:** `TREE_OBSTRUCTION.md` claims L1 and L2. On a tree, parity of every edge in a current is determined by the number of sources on either side of its cut. Under fixed sources the edge factorial weights then factor. An even edge is absent with probability `s=sech(t)`. On each bridge edge both original currents are even, so their union is present with probability `p=1-s^2=tanh(t)^2`, independently across bridge edges. Therefore original connection is `q=p^m`. For augmented source components, first-closed-edge decomposition gives `r=p^m[1+m(1-p)]`. Bare occupation across `m` edges is `(1-s)^m`; augmented occupation is `p^m`; ratio `(1+s)^(-m)`. At `t=log 3`, `s=3/5`, `p=16/25`, and `q/r=1/37` for `m=100`. I independently enumerated all support pairs for `m=1,...,6` with exact fractions: `python -X utf8 verify_peer.py` passed **18/18** checks, 4,096 support pairs at the largest case, 0.050 active seconds. This supports only the finite-tree formulas and refutes graph-uniform *linear* reverse bounds.

**KEEP, conditional reduction:** `ATTEMPT.md` L3-L5. Aizenman's (5.13) is for the original pair of sourced currents; (5.9) is for a sourced current plus a sourceless current. Reindexing the (5.13) sum by `z=y+w`, with `X,W` independently weighted by `G/chi`, gives exactly `-g=2 xi^(-3) sum_y Q_y`; the nonnegative sum permits a box restriction. With `R=floor xi`, `xi>=1`, `|B|/xi^3>=1`, so the paper's bound `-g>=2 Q_B` follows. The overlap variable `N` of *bare* source components satisfies `Q_B>=P(N>0)>=(E N)^2/E N^2`. Its first and second moments use one-hit kernel `k` and joint two-hit kernel `k2` respectively; the second formula cannot factor `k2` into `k*k`. Averaging (5.9) against `G(x)/chi` proves `k_aug(v)=G(v)` and hence only `G(v)/chi<=k(v)<=G(v)`. Thus the proposed UCM estimate `E N^2<=C(E N)^2` is a separate unproved Ising input. The infinite-volume identity is taken as a standard source-backed assumption; the file does not itself prove the thermodynamic passage. Verdict does not cover UCM or C0052.

## w469

**KEEP, generic counterexample:** `INDEPENDENT.md` corridor paths. For independent uniform labels `J,K in {1,...,N}` and `L=N^2` sites per corridor, the common-site count is exactly `I=L 1{J=K}`. Thus `E I=N`, `E I^2=N^3`, and `P(I>0)=1/N`. This shows that a diverging first overlap moment alone does not yield a positive crossing probability, even though the paths are independent. It is not an Ising random-current counterexample.

**KEEP, scalar weak-limit lemma:** With `E X_n=0`, `E X_n^2=1`, and `E X_n^4<=3-delta`, the fourth-moment bound gives uniform integrability of first and second moments. Weak convergence transfers mean zero and variance one. Lower semicontinuity/monotone truncation gives `E X^4<=3-delta`, inconsistent with a centered unit-variance Gaussian. This does not give tightness of a random field or identify the near-critical source criterion.

**KEEP, finite-volume reduction:** Under nonnegative weights `f`, the four-point current identity implies `E X_f^4=3-2 E_{Q_f}p` with `Q_f=f1 f2 f3 f4 G12 G34/V^2`. `Q_f` sums to one because each pair sum is `V`. All three Gaussian pair-product sums equal `V^2`. The claim is exact for finite ferromagnetic Ising graphs and valid current laws; a scale-uniform lower bound on `E_{Q_f}p` remains unproved. Its localized route keeps the explicit factor `(chi_R/chi)^2`, so it does not silently assume susceptibility mass in the box.

**KEEP, finite-tree nonlinear comparison:** `TREE_NONLINEAR.md` independently computes `r=q[1+sum_i(1-p_i)]` on the unique bridge between disjoint source paths. Since `1-p_i<=-log p_i`, writing `x=-log q` yields `r/sqrt(q)<=(1+x)e^{-x/2}<=2/sqrt(e)`, so `q>=(e/4)r^2`. The bound also holds when a zero bridge coupling makes `q=r=0`, or source paths meet and `q=r=1`. Uniform `p_i=e^{-1/m}` gives the asserted asymptotic sharpness. Tree parity independence is essential; this does not establish a cyclic-lattice comparison.

**KEEP, four-cycle parity obstruction:** `CYCLE_OBSTRUCTION.md` correctly observes that a sourceless current on a single four-cycle can have only the all-even or all-odd edge-parity vector. Their factorial weights are `cosh(t)^4` and `sinh(t)^4`, so the all-odd probability is `h=tanh(t)^4/[1+tanh(t)^4]`. Distinct parity indicators are identical, with joint expectation `h` rather than `h^2`; therefore the independent-edge factorization used on trees cannot simply be applied after mixing cycle parities. This proves dependence of current edge parities on that finite graph, not failure of a nonlinear comparison on Z^3.
