## Corrected original target and new method, 2026-10-10

Independently checked Grimmett, *Selected problems in probability theory*, Question 2.2, arXiv:2205.07318v1, printed p. 3. It asks for the rescaled weak limit of a **uniform n-step honeycomb SAW from the origin**. It leaves the rescaling and exact curve/SLE variant unspecified. The chordal domain ensemble discussed above is a related source problem and cannot be silently substituted for P0003.

For the actual fixed-length target, I switch to a coefficient-extraction route. Let c_n count n-step honeycomb SAWs from 0, let r_n be a candidate spatial scale, and for a bounded test functional f of the rescaled path define a_n(f)=sum_{|γ|=n} f(γ/r_n), C_f(z)=sum_{n>=0}a_n(f)z^n. Then E_n f=a_n(f)/c_n exactly. Try to obtain uniform complex-z singular estimates for C_f(z) and C_1(z), with a common leading exponent, so a transfer theorem identifies coefficient ratios and the fixed-length limit. The existing parafermionic identity controls a critical-fugacity observable at one fugacity; it supplies neither complex-neighborhood control nor the family of path functionals. This is a research reduction, not a solution.

**Single hardest open piece for this corrected route:** obtain an analytic continuation and singular expansion for C_f(z), uniformly enough for coefficient extraction for a convergence-determining class of rescaled path functionals. Even c_n's predicted two-dimensional exponent γ=43/32 remains conjectural in Grimmett's source. Any proof based only on chordal critical-fugacity partition ratios remains on a different ensemble unless it also proves a fixed-length transfer theorem.

**Exact coefficient calculation and diagnostic (not a SAW theorem).** With `C(z)=sum_n c_n z^n` and `C_f(z)=sum_n sum_{|γ|=n} f(γ/r_n) z^n`, the fixed-length expectation is exactly `[z^n]C_f(z)/[z^n]C(z)`. Knowing only the real-fugacity ratio as z approaches its critical value cannot settle coefficient ratios: take the abstract nonnegative sequences `c_n=1` and `a_n=1+epsilon*(-1)^n`, with `0<epsilon<1`. Then `C(z)=1/(1-z)`, `A(z)=1/(1-z)+epsilon/(1+z)`, so `A(z)/C(z)->1` as real `z->1-`, yet `a_n/c_n` alternates forever. The singularity at `z=-1` is invisible to that one-sided ratio but visible to coefficient extraction. This is a diagnostic for the needed complex-neighborhood control, not evidence of oscillation in actual SAW.
