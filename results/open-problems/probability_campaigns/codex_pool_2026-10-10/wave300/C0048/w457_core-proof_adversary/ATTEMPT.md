## Exact target and normalization

For integer d>=3 and q>=3, take the zero-field nearest-neighbor Potts Hamiltonian H=-sum_e 1{equal endpoint colors} on Z^d. Put p=1-exp(-beta). In a finite box with color-1 boundary, Edwards-Sokal gives

    mu^1(sigma_0=1)=1/q+(1-1/q)*phi^w(0 connected to the wired boundary).

Indeed, conditionally on the FK edges, the boundary component has color 1 and every other component receives a uniform independent color. Thus normalized magnetization is exactly wired-boundary connection probability. In infinite volume it is theta_w(p)=phi^w_p(0 connected to infinity). C0048 asks theta_w(p_c(d,q))>0 for every such d,q. Critical energy-density discontinuity is not being substituted for this statement.

Primary recovery: Duminil-Copin, *Order/disorder phase transitions: the example of the Potts model*, printed p.32, Conjecture 1. His simplex-spin convention rescales beta relative to the delta-spin convention above.

## I1. Exact local bounds and a counterexample to local-to-global inference

Let a(p,q)=p/[p+q(1-p)] with q>=1 and 0<p<1. On any finite graph and any FK boundary partition, condition on every edge other than e. The two endpoint classes are either already connected or distinct. Opening e then changes the component count by respectively 0 or -1. Taking the open/closed weight ratio proves

    P(e open | all other edges)=p, or a(p,q).

Consequently a<=P(e open | F)<=p for every sigma-field F generated by a subset of the other edges: this follows by the tower property, not independence. For any fixed simple n-edge path, expose the path edges in order. Conditional probabilities at each step stay in [a,p], including after the previous path edges were observed open, so the chain rule gives

    a^n <= P(all n path edges open) <= p^n.

For the nearest-neighbor box Lambda_N=[-N,N]^d with only its boundary wired, 0 connected to that boundary entails an open self-avoiding path whose first N edges are distinct and stay in the box. There are at most 2d(2d-1)^(N-1) possible prefixes. A union bound yields

    f_N(p):=phi^w_{Lambda_N,p,q}(0 connected to boundary)
        <= 2d*p*[(2d-1)*p]^(N-1).

Hence for d=3,q=3,p=1/10 every conditional edge-open probability is at least 1/28, yet f_N<=(3/5)*2^(-(N-1)) and theta_w(p)=0. This is an actual infinite-volume FK counterexample to the implication "uniformly positive local edge probabilities imply positive infinite-cluster probability." It does NOT refute C0048: p=1/10 is subcritical, not p_c. Criticality must supply a genuinely additional scale estimate.

One can also see why FKG cannot justify an upper product of marginals. On the free four-cycle at p=1/2,q=3, direct FK weight enumeration gives Z=258, each edge marginal 11/43, and all-four-open probability 1/86. Thus the joint probability exceeds (11/43)^4. A separately checked exact-arithmetic script will record the ratio. This example addresses upper-product misuse only; FKG does provide the lower product inequality for increasing events.

## I2. The valid limit step and the hardest remaining input

For the usual finite-volume FK construction, f_N(p) decreases in N. This uses the domain Markov property, monotonicity of boundary partitions for q>=1, and the fact that connection to a farther boundary entails connection to the nearer one. The limit is theta_w(p): for fixed n<N, write g_(N,n)=phi^w_{Lambda_N}(0 connected to boundary Lambda_n). Then theta_w<=lim_N f_N, because the inner marginal is dominated by fully wired Lambda_n. Conversely f_N<=g_(N,n), so lim_N f_N<=phi^w_p(0 connected to boundary Lambda_n) for every fixed n. Let n increase to infinity. Both inequalities give equality.

Every f_N is continuous in p on (0,1), and increasing in p. Therefore theta_w=inf_N f_N is right continuous: if p_j decreases to p, theta_w(p)<=theta_w(p_j)<=f_N(p_j); let j increase, then N increase. No exchange of an unproved pair of limits is needed.

This proves the following conditional reduction. If some delta>0 and epsilon>0 obey

    theta_w(p)>=delta for every p in (p_c,p_c+epsilon),

then theta_w(p_c)>=delta. Equivalently, it suffices to prove f_N(p)>=delta simultaneously for all N and all p in that right neighborhood. The same delta must survive the p-downarrow-p_c and N-to-infinity limits. Positivity separately for each p>p_c, with delta depending on p and tending to zero, proves nothing at p_c.

The hardest missing input for this route is a quantitative gap above zero in the ordered branch, in particular at q=3,d=3. This gap formulation is equivalent to critical ordering given the proved right-continuity step; it is an explicit isolation of the open input, not an easier theorem being claimed as progress. Mean-field discontinuity alone and high-dimensional bounds do not supply this input in every d>=3.
