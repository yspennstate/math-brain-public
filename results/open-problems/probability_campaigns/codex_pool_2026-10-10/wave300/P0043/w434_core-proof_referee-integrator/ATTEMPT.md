## Independent attempt on Proposition 8.1's no-strip case

Fix a small angular arc radius `zeta` and cover the unit circle by `M` signed arcs. Critical uniqueness and positive per-vertex survival imply that a sufficiently large finite seed meets the unique infinite cluster with probability exceeding `1-(eta/20)^M`. The no-bounded-projection-ray hypothesis makes any infinite cluster path leave every coordinate ellipse. For each ellipse, its exit path hits some arc with at least this probability. If every arc had hit probability at most `1-eta/20`, the decreasing arc-miss events and Harris's inequality would give probability at least `(eta/20)^M` of missing all arcs, contradiction. Thus every ellipse has a favored arc of hit probability above `1-eta/20`. This uses positive association, not independence of arc hits.

At the boundary ellipses `A(1,phi)=R_phi diag(U,W) R_phi^{-1}` with `U>>W`, every favored arc must lie near the minor-axis line. Otherwise choose `U_j→infinity`, bad angles `phi_j→phi_*`, and exit paths in an arc separated from that minor axis. Their major-axis displacement grows like `U_j` while their transverse displacement is at most `W+O(D_pi)`. For any fixed path length `l`, the initial `l` edges lie in a fixed finite ball. As `phi_j→phi_*`, sufficiently long initial segments lie in a fixed-width strip about `phi_*`; their event has probability at least `1-eta/20`. Let `l→infinity` and use local finiteness to obtain a strip ray with positive probability, contrary to the case hypothesis. This checks the necessary order: fix seed, then W, then take U large.

The winding step can be rederived without a continuity assumption on which favored arc is selected. Triangulate the matrix-parameter disk finely and attach to each vertex the unoriented line of an arbitrary favored signed arc. Suppose every neighboring pair of these lines differs by less than `pi/8` in projective angle. Doubling line angles maps them to the ordinary circle, with each principal edge increment of absolute value less than `pi/4`. On each triangular face the sum of its three principal increments is an integer multiple of `2pi` and has absolute value less than `3pi/4`, hence is zero. Summing faces makes total boundary winding zero. On the boundary, however, favored lines are within `pi/16` of the minor axis, whose projective angle is `phi+pi/2` as `phi` runs once modulo `pi`. Their doubled angles lie within `pi/8` of `2phi+pi`; with boundary mesh fine enough, principal increments track that reference and sum to `2pi`. Contradiction. Therefore some neighboring ellipses have favored lines separated by at least `pi/8`. This verifies the combinatorial-topological load bearing part of the no-strip case; endpoint-error constants, the strip case, and the final uniform all-orbit transfer still require separate checking before a KEEP on the full Proposition 8.1.

## Conditional audit of the adaptive exploration, Proposition 9.3

Assume the paper's Proposition 8.1, corridor separation Lemma 9.1, and fresh-collar transfer Proposition 9.2 with their stated parameters. The argument below checks the probabilistic implication to a subcritical infinite cluster; it does not prove those inputs.

For a tested site `v`, let `I` be the revealed history before its chosen incoming corridor is opened. Lemma 9.1 says the incoming corridor meets `I` only in its parent's already known endpoint region, while both outgoing corridors are fresh. The stored parent prediction therefore gives `P(A_v|I)>=1-delta`; intervening outside revelations leave incoming fresh bits independent and add only possible paths. In the prospective graph containing the incoming and one outgoing corridor, transfer through `L+1` relay pairs gives `P(A_v and miss child x | I)<=(L+1)kappa`. No product of relay success probabilities is used: a hit of the first and miss of the last contains a first adjacent hit/miss pair, so a union bound suffices.

After the incoming corridor is revealed, let `F` be the enlarged history and `q_x(F)` the conditional probability of hitting child `x` through its still fresh outgoing corridor. Conditional expectation gives `E[1_{A_v}(1-q_x(F))|I]<=(L+1)kappa`. Thus Markov and the two-child union bound give

`P(v fails | I) <= delta + 2(L+1)kappa/delta <= epsilon`.

This is valid for countable reveal domains: at any finite slot a finite decision string specifies a deterministic union of countable revealed edge sets, and conditional on their complete states the disjoint complement retains its product law. Decisions and predictions are measurable functions of revealed bits; computing a conditional probability is not itself a revelation. The claim still depends on the geometric assertion that the outgoing domain has no previously revealed edge.

For any fixed finite set `S` of nonroot sites, reveal in increasing layer order. At each specified tested site its failure probability conditional on the past is at most `epsilon`; a site never tested makes the joint event impossible. Iteration yields `P(all S tested and failed)<=epsilon^{|S|}`. If the good set is finite, the oriented boundary of its unit squares contains a closed directed trail through the root's west edge. A length-`ell` closed trail has `ell/2` up-or-left steps. Each such step crosses from a good site to a nongood child; that child was tested and failed. At most two steps can name the same child, so at least `ell/4` distinct failures are specified. There are at most `4^ell` candidate directed trails of length `ell` through the fixed root edge. Therefore `P(good set finite)<=sum_{ell>=1}4^ell epsilon^{ell/4}<1` by the paper's parameter choice. With positive probability infinitely many good marks occur at unbounded time coordinates. Each was reached by an actual open path from one finite wired seed set, so one seed lies in an infinite p'-open component. Since `p'<p_c`, this contradicts the threshold definition. This conditional implication is independently checked, while its upstream transfer and geometry remain review obligations.
