## w525: finite Gaussian interpolation

- **KEEP (finite Lemma 1).** Independently rederived $\nabla f_p=r_p$, $\nabla^2f_p=\operatorname{diag}(r_p)-r_pr_p^T$, and $\nabla^2F_\beta=\sum q_p\nabla^2f_p-\beta(\sum q_pr_pr_p^T-\bar r\bar r^T)$. Gaussian integration by parts for $X=C^{1/2}Z$ gives $m_\beta'(\xi)=\xi E(A_\beta-\beta B_\beta)$ for finite paths, positive deterministic weights, centered Gaussian $X$ with PSD $C$, $\beta>0$. $A_\beta=\frac12\sum_pq_p\sum_{i,j}r_{pi}r_{pj}\operatorname{Var}(X_i-X_j)\ge0$ and $B_\beta=\sum_pq_p(r_p-\bar r)^TC(r_p-\bar r)\ge0$. The sign of their difference is undetermined. No coordinate independence is used.
- **KEEP (finite Lemma 2).** For fixed finite $K$, $\min f_p-\log K/\beta\le F_\beta\le\min f_p$ uniformly. Integrating first and taking $\beta\to\infty$ gives the stated endpoint identity with error at most $2\log K/\beta$. This does not exchange a limit with a pointwise derivative.
- **KEEP (route-switch control).** With two singleton paths and independent standard normals, $E\log D_\xi=-\xi/\sqrt\pi$, $A_\beta=0$, $B_\beta=2q_1q_2$. Put $t=\beta\xi$ and $u=t(X_1-X_2)/2$; then $tEB_\beta=(2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{sech}^2(u)e^{-u^2/t^2}du\to1/\sqrt\pi$ for fixed $\xi>0$. Deleting $B$ gives the wrong derivative zero.
- **KEEP (finite Lemma 3).** $r_p^TCr_p\le\sum_i r_{pi}C_{ii}\le v$, so each path log-cost and its minimum are $\xi\sqrt v$-Lipschitz in $Z$. Gaussian Poincaré yields $\operatorname{Var}(\log D_\xi)\le\xi^2v$. Cantelli gives $|E\log D_\xi-M|\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}}$ for any median $M$; w526's looser $\sqrt{2\operatorname{Var}}$ is also safe.
- **KEEP (finite Lemma 4; no new LQG bound).** Since $A_\beta\le v$ and $B_\beta\ge0$, Lemma 1 gives $m_\beta'(\xi)\le \xi v$. Integrating from $a$ to $b$ and using the fixed-network soft-min error yields $E\log D_b-E\log D_a\le v(b^2-a^2)/2$. If $v_n/L_n\to1$ and the endpoint exponents exist, this only recovers the published lower bound $\lambda(b)-\lambda(a)\ge-(b^2-a^2)/2$; it does not improve $d_\gamma$. The $v_n/L_n\to1$ premise must be checked for the chosen field normalization.
- **KEEP only as conditional reduction; FIX any claim of a dimension formula.** If $v_n=O(L_n)$, the crossing median exponent exists, and $\log K_n/(\beta_nL_n)\to0$, the normalized integrated response equals the *difference of already-existing exponents*. It does not calculate either exponent. A discrete network needs a separately checked comparison to the exact LFPP metric and its $\varepsilon$ length factor.

## w526: independent adversary claims

- **KEEP C526.1 in its stated algebraic scope.** At $\gamma=1$, $Q=5/2$ and both $(d,\xi)=(3,1/3),(4,1/4)$ satisfy $\xi=\gamma/d$. This is no counterexample to metric axioms; it shows the two identities cannot evaluate $d$.
- **KEEP C526.2.** Gwynne–Pfeffer Theorem 1.4 requires deterministic $X$ or random $X$ independent of $h$. Equation (1.11) with deterministic $U$ gives $\dim_H^0(T_h^\gamma\cap U)=2-\gamma^2/2$; Corollary 1.7 gives $\dim_H^\gamma(T_h^\gamma\cap U)=d_\gamma$. Inserting this field-dependent set into the independent-set formula would give $\xi Qd_\gamma-\xi^2d_\gamma^2/2=\gamma Q-\gamma^2/2=2$, a false conclusion for every $\gamma>0$.
- **KEEP C526.3.** At $\gamma=\sqrt{8/3}$, $\xi=1/\sqrt6$, $Q=5/\sqrt6$, and a nonconstant metric geodesic has quantum dimension 1. The independent-set formula would give $5/6-1/12=3/4$. Its Euclidean image is connected with two different endpoints, hence a Euclidean 1-Lipschitz distance projection has an interval image and its Euclidean dimension is at least 1. The geodesic is field-dependent.
- **KEEP C526.4 and C526.5.** C526.4 agrees with the independently derived finite identity above. For C526.5, $N_n=2^{n^2}$ identical singleton costs $\xi Z$ and $\beta_n=L_n=n$ give $F_{\beta_n}=\xi Z-n\log2$; the normalized soft/hard expectation gap is exactly $\log2$. That bias is constant in $\xi$, so C526.5 alone does **not** disprove an integrated endpoint-difference reduction. The example is an abstract finite Gaussian family, not a planar LFPP network.
- **KEEP C526.6 (stronger integrated counterexample).** Let $M_n=2^{n^2}$, $L_n=\beta_n=n$, $M_n$ deterministic singleton paths of cost $e^n$, and one long path with cost $S_n(\xi)=M_n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{M_n}e^{\xi\sqrt n Z_i}$. At $\xi=0$, both hard and soft normalized expected log-costs tend to 0. At $\xi=2$, $ES_n=e^{2n}$ and $\operatorname{Var}(S_n)/(ES_n)^2=(e^{4n}-1)/M_n$, so Chebyshev implies $P(S_n<e^n)\to0$. Jensen gives $\log S_n\ge 2\sqrt n\bar Z$ and $E|2\sqrt n\bar Z|=2\sqrt{2n/(\pi M_n)}$. Therefore $E\min(cn,\log S_n)/n\to c$ for $c=1$ and $c=1-\log2$. The hard endpoint tends to 1; the soft endpoint is within $\log2/n$ of the latter minimum and tends to $1-\log2$. The finite interpolation identity makes its normalized integral have the same limit. This refutes dropping the uniform entropy-error premise in the **abstract** finite model, without challenging w525's stated conditional reduction, which includes that premise.

## Prior-route correction and remaining gap

Intake D0049-A001 says $d_\gamma=2Q/\xi$. This fails at $\gamma=\sqrt{8/3}$: $Q=5/\sqrt6$, $\xi=1/\sqrt6$, so it gives 10 instead of 4. The valid definition is $\xi=\gamma/d_\gamma$; it alone does not evaluate $d_\gamma$. No parent-problem solution follows from this packet.
