# P0297 proof attempt (working)

Exact target: determine d_gamma=dim_H(C,D_h) for gamma in (0,2), with canonical gamma-LQG metric D_h. Primary source: Gwynne--Miller, *Existence and uniqueness of the Liouville quantum gravity metric*, Problem 7.1, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00222-020-00991-6.

Hardest missing input: determine the LFPP crossing normalization exponent independently of d_gamma. The metric paper defines xi=gamma/d_gamma and proves (Corollary 1.11) that the median crossing normalization a_epsilon is regularly varying with exponent alpha=1-xi Q, Q=2/gamma+gamma/2. Algebra gives the proved conditional reduction d_gamma=gamma Q/(1-alpha). This is a reduction, not a dimension formula: alpha is precisely the unknown geometric exponent.

Attempt: test whether constant-field covariance and Gaussian area scaling identify alpha. They do not. For any p in (0,1), the snowflake metric D_p(x,y)=|x-y|^p on C has Hausdorff dimension 2/p. Set xi=gamma p/2 and D_{p,c}=e^{xi c}D_p, mu_c=e^{gamma c}Lebesgue measure. Then D_{p,c} scales under c exactly as an LQG metric does, and mu_c scales as LQG area does; xi*(2/p)=gamma holds for every p. Thus constant-shift algebra alone cannot select d_gamma. This countermodel does not satisfy the full LQG/GFF axioms, so it only refutes the proposed inference from those two covariance relations.

Detailed proof: t -> t^p is subadditive for 0<p<=1, so D_p is a metric. A set of D_p-diameter r has Euclidean diameter r^(1/p), hence H^s_{D_p} equals H^{ps}_{Euclidean} up to the standard normalization constant; dim_H(C,D_p)=2/p. Multiplying D_p by e^{xi c} multiplies its d-dimensional Hausdorff measure by e^{xi d c}=e^{gamma c}; choosing mu_0 proportional to H^d_{D_p} gives even exact measure covariance. Thus the family of covariance identities has a continuum of dimensions. This does not produce LQG metrics, because D_p for p<1 is not an LQG length metric.

Independent peer check complete: w516's L1 finite-Gaussian response identity has a nonnegative path-switching correction beta B_beta. The two-edge analytic calculation and independent quadrature in review_repro.py test that term. w517's O2 shows the selection issue at an exponent scale in a growing graph, but that graph is not the GFF planar crossing model.

Attempt on the hardest input H. A tempting finite shortcut H0 would replace the full-model switching term by the sum of isolated two-route weighted gap densities at zero, then seek their cutoff limit. H0 is false already for three Gaussian routes. With independent standard normals Z,U,V0 and log lengths f1=xi(Z+U), f2=xi(Z-U), f3=-M+xi V0, the isolated pair (1,2) density contribution is 2/(xi sqrt(2pi)). In the full soft minimum its contribution is exactly that amount times Phi(-M/(xi sqrt(2))), as independently checked in VERDICTS.jsonl:3R. At M=0 H0 overcounts that pair by a factor two. The missing factor is the chance that a tied pair beats every other route. A viable H would require competitive weighted near-tie laws and the within-path covariance occupation term, with bounds uniform in both path family and GFF cutoff; neither bound is proved here. This is a failed proof route, not an exact dimension result.
