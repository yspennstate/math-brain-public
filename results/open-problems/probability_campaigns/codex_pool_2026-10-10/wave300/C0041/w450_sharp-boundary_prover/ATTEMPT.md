Exact target: for every integer d>=2, every i.i.d. law of nearest-neighbour site kernels on Z^d with a deterministic lower bound kappa>0 on all 2d transitions, and every fixed unit vector ell, annealed probability-one convergence X_n dot ell -> +infinity should imply liminf_n X_n dot ell/n>0 almost surely. The standard equivalent consequence is an almost-sure deterministic velocity v with v dot ell>0. Primary source: Drewitz-Ramirez, arXiv:1309.2589v1, Open question 2.20 and definitions 2.13-2.14. L0. Zero local directional drift gives zero directional speed.

Fix a unit vector ell. If sum_e omega(x,e)(e dot ell)=0 for every x, then M_n=(X_n-X_0) dot ell is a quenched martingale with |M_n-M_(n-1)| <= 1. Azuma-Hoeffding gives P_omega(|M_n| > epsilon n) <= 2 exp(-epsilon^2 n/2). Borel-Cantelli for all positive rational epsilon proves X_n dot ell/n -> 0 almost surely. This does not assert recurrence. No site independence is used. Author proof, awaiting independent review, no novelty claim.

Hardest target gap: directional transience does not itself supply an integrable regeneration time. Any proposed occupation/drift bridge must address signed local drift and the dependence between a site's transitions and time spent at that site.

L1. Single-site Green identity (proved; awaiting independent review).

Let U be a finite connected subset of Z^d containing the starting site 0 and a site x. Kill the nearest-neighbour walk at its first exit T_U. Fix the entire environment outside x. Write p_e=omega(x,e), h=P_omega^0(H_x<T_U), and q_e=P_omega^(x+e)(T_U<H_x), setting q_e=1 if x+e is outside U and h=1 if x=0. Both h and q depend only on the environment outside x: each trajectory defining them stops before departing x. The first-return decomposition gives G_U^omega(0,x)=h/(sum_e p_e q_e). Finite U and uniform ellipticity imply exit in a bounded number of steps with a uniform positive probability, so all expectations are finite and sum_e p_e q_e>0. Under an i.i.d. site law pi, conditioning outside x leaves p distributed as pi. Consequently the conditional occupation-weighted local drift is E_pi[d_ell(p)/(p dot q)]/E_pi[1/(p dot q)]. No independence between G and p is assumed.

L2. Nonnegative local drift gives a uniform positive Kalikow drift (proved finite-domain bound).

Assume i.i.d. site kernels, omega(x,e)>=kappa>0, and d_ell(p)>=0 pi-a.s., with m=E_pi[d_ell(p)]>0. Put p_max=1-(2d-1)kappa and S=sum_e q_e. Since kappa*S <= p dot q <= p_max*S, nonnegativity gives E_pi[d_ell(p)/(p dot q)] >= m/(p_max*S), while E_pi[1/(p dot q)] <= 1/(kappa*S). Thus at every x in every U, E[G_U(0,x)d_ell(omega(x))]/E[G_U(0,x)] >= c=kappa*m/p_max>0. Averaging the conditional inequality is legitimate after multiplying by the nonnegative outside-x hitting factor h. Summing over x yields E_0[(X_(T_U)-X_0) dot ell] >= c E_0[T_U]. This also explains precisely why the proof fails for signed local drift: the first reciprocal-denominator comparison reverses on the negative part. Ballisticity as a corollary requires the independently sourced Kalikow implication; the finite-domain lemma is complete on its own.

L3. Exact two-type bound at the marginal zero-drift boundary (proved finite-domain bound).

For d>=2 let a=1/(2d), 0<b<a, and theta in (0,1]. Independently at every site, use p0(e)=a with probability 1-theta; with probability theta use p+(+e1)=a+b, p+(-e1)=a-b, and p+(e)=a in transverse directions. Uniform ellipticity holds with kappa=a-b, the mean directional drift is m=2b*theta>0, but no uniform positive local drift exists when theta<1. In L1 write r=(p+ dot q)/(p0 dot q). Then r=1+(b/a)(q_+-q_-)/S lies in [1-b/a,1+b/a]. The exact conditional Kalikow drift is 2b*theta/[theta+(1-theta)*r], hence at least c_bin=2b*theta/[1+(1-theta)*b/a]>0. This improves the generic c from L2. It is sharp for the relaxation allowing every nonnegative q, attained by q supported on +e1; no claim is made that such an exit vector is attained in a finite uniformly elliptic Z^d domain. At theta=0 or b=0, L0 gives zero directional speed.

L4. Positive averaged local drift does not imply positive Kalikow drift (exact counterexample, checked).

On Z^2 take i.i.d. site kernels p+=(9/20,1/20,1/4,1/4) and p-=(1/20,9/20,1/4,1/4), in the order +e1,-e1,+e2,-e2, with P(p+)=51/100. Uniform ellipticity is kappa=1/20. The mean one-step e1 drift is 1/125>0. Let U={0,-e1}, start at 0, and inspect x=0. If the two site types are s,t, G_U(0,0)=1/[1-p_s(-e1)p_t(+e1)]. Its four values for (++),(+-),(-+),(--) are 400/391,400/399,400/319,400/391. Average G times the signed drift +/-2/5 and divide by average G. Exact numerator: -1004048/59246275. Exact denominator: 63724944/59246275. The Kalikow drift is -62753/3982809, approximately -0.0157559652. verify_exact.py confirmed the four Green functions by exact Gaussian elimination and the L1 return identity, and also checked the reflected domain, where the drift becomes positive. This refutes only the bridge 'positive mean one-step drift implies positive finite-domain Kalikow drift'. Directional transience of this signed-drift law is not proved, so this is not a counterexample to C0041.

The obstruction has an exact sharp threshold for this specific domain. If the probability of p+ is a general theta, the weighted numerator is 160*[31280*theta^2+223282*theta-127281]/49766871. The polynomial is strictly increasing on [0,1], is negative at 1/2 and positive at 1, and has one root theta_star in (53/100,531/1000). Therefore positive mean drift coexists with negative Kalikow drift precisely for 1/2<theta<theta_star in this family/domain. At theta_star the weighted drift is zero. This is a threshold for the specified finite-domain bridge, not for ballisticity of the full walk.

L5. Discounted occupations give a quantitative speed bound when a velocity exists (proved).

Assume the L2 law, or the L3 binary law, and suppose X_n/n converges almost surely to a deterministic v. Let c denote the corresponding occupation bound. For 0<lambda<1 define G_lambda(0,x)=E_omega^0[sum_(n>=0) lambda^n 1_(X_n=x)]. The strong Markov first-hit/return decomposition gives G_lambda(0,x)=h_lambda/[sum_e p_e q_lambda,e], where h_lambda=E_omega^0[lambda^(H_x); H_x<infinity] and q_lambda,e=1-lambda*E_omega^(x+e)[lambda^(H_x); H_x<infinity]. These quantities depend only on the outside-x environment; q_lambda,e>=1-lambda>0. Apply exactly the L2 or L3 inequality, multiply by h_lambda, average, and sum over x (absolute convergence since |d_ell|<=1 and sum_x G_lambda=1/(1-lambda)). This gives sum_(n>=0) lambda^n E_0[d_ell(omega(X_n))] >= c/(1-lambda). Set a_n=E_0[X_n dot ell], with a_0=0. The left side equals sum_n lambda^n(a_(n+1)-a_n)=(1-lambda)/lambda * sum_n lambda^n a_n. Hence (1-lambda)^2/lambda * sum_n lambda^n a_n>=c. Bounded convergence yields a_n/n -> v dot ell. To justify the Abelian limit, write a_n=n(v dot ell+epsilon_n), epsilon_n->0 and bounded. The normalized weights ((1-lambda)^2/lambda)*n*lambda^n sum to 1 and assign mass tending to zero to each finite prefix, so the expression tends to v dot ell. Thus v dot ell>=c.

Known-theorem corollary (proved special case, external dependency explicit): L2's uniform positive finite-domain Kalikow drift is Kalikow's condition. The standard Kalikow-to-ballisticity implication for i.i.d. uniformly elliptic nearest-neighbour RWRE supplies the deterministic velocity; its implication and scope are recorded in Fukushima-Ramirez, arXiv:1902.08920, introduction pp.1 and 3, citing Sznitman-Zerner (1999). Combining that implication with L5 proves v dot ell>=kappa*m/[1-(2d-1)kappa] for L2, and v dot e1>=2b*theta/[1+(1-theta)b/a] for L3. No transience assumption is needed for these restricted laws. The standard criterion is an explicit external theorem dependency, not a theorem re-proved here. For d=2, b=1/5, theta=1/10, the respective guaranteed bounds are 1/425 and 1/43, an exact factor 425/43 improvement. At theta=0, L0 gives zero speed. Relaxed-q sharpness does not imply optimality of the actual speed bound.
