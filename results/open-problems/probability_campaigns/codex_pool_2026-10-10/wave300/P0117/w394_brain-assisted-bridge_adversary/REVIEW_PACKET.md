# P0117 adversarial review for w393 and w395

2026-10-10; direct proof audit by w394. ATTEMPT.md and claims C01-C04 were saved before reading w393/LEMMAS.md. No review of another review was performed. Independent referee for w394's own statements: w395, pending.

Exact working model: undirected nearest-neighbor Z^2; iid finite nonnegative weights with an atomless common law G. The shape-integrable working subclass additionally has E[min(t1,t2,t3,t4)^2]<infinity. The target is almost-sure absence of every injective doubly infinite path all of whose finite segments minimize full-lattice passage time. The intake's phrase "usual positive-speed regime" does not settle a moment assumption; the explicit working restriction is not claimed to be the entire question. Current openness is UNKNOWN.

Direct verdicts on w393/LEMMAS.md:

* L1: KEEP as a deterministic reduction under its stated positive finite weights. The witnesses minimize in B_n, rather than globally at fixed n. First-exit truncation is valid because a B_n competitor is also allowed in B_(n+1). For the converse, fix an arbitrary finite competitor Q after fixing a stabilized finite limiting segment; sufficiently large selected boxes contain Q. This order of quantifiers establishes global minimization. Thus the finite box event is measurable with respect to the inside-edge filtration. No claim of finite-scale global minimization is needed.
* L2: KEEP as a conditional survival lemma. For A_n=sum_(j<n)q_j and M_n=1_(E_n) exp(A_n), the displayed conditional inequality makes M_n a nonnegative integrable supermartingale since exp(q)(1-q)<=1 on [0,1]. Its almost-sure finite limit contradicts divergence on E_infinity. The filtration-measurability, monotone events, and random predictable lower bounds are exactly the needed premises. This does not multiply unconditional per-scale estimates.
* L3: KEEP under its additional support-near-zero hypothesis. The 8(n+1) perimeter edges lie outside the inside-edge filtration. Conditional on that filtration, the random threshold m/(8(n+1)) is fixed and the iid ring-edge probabilities multiply legitimately. A ring arc costs less than m/2, whereas a through-origin simple path costs at least 2m. The explicit bounds eventually decay at least as 2^(-8(n+1)) and so cannot meet L2's divergence premise. This says nothing about an upper bound on the true hazards.
* C1: KEEP as a probability counterexample, not an FPP environment. The finite product telescopes to (N+2)/(2(N+1)), with nonzero limit 1/2.

Most important remaining premise: a conditional estimate that kills every anchored candidate and has accumulated lower bounds diverging on infinite survival. No such estimate is supplied. The surviving proof is a collection of conditional/reduction results, not a proof of P0117.

Independent challenge to a possible substitution: The global event in w394 C02 has the same infinite intersection, but need not be an inside-edge cylinder event at a fixed radius. It must not replace w393's box event in L2 without a filtration argument. The concrete completions in verification/filtration_counterexample.json have identical inside B_1 data: global origin-hit pair counts are 16 and 0, while the box count is 16 in both. Mixing the two completions by a fair coin also gives an exact non-iid probability law for which that global event is not measurable in the inside-edge filtration.

Source-to-need comparison:

* Ahlberg-Hoffman, arXiv:1609.02447, Theorem 2.1 and A1: atomless iid weights with the finite squared minimum-of-four moment give origin-hit probability tending to zero for every deterministic pair of diverging endpoint sequences. Selecting maximizing deterministic pairs implies uniform q_R->0 on the finite boundary-pair family. It does not supply a summable hazard or a rate that controls the whole adaptive union.
* Dembin-Elboim-Peled, arXiv:2204.02332v5, Theorem 1.2: absolute continuity, finite exponential moment, and more than 40 limit-shape extreme points give a bound C log^2(D+2)/D^(1/16) for a fixed vertex hit, D the smaller endpoint distance. These assumptions are stronger than the target's working class. Even when available, summing over 32R^2-4R boundary pairs produces order R^(31/16) log^2 R, which gives no decaying bound.
* Their Theorem 1.3 controls the whole common-height family ((-n,s),(n,s)), s in Z, with order log^2 n/n^(1/24). This is already a restricted endpoint-union result. A bigeodesic through 0 is not known to yield endpoints with that common-height constraint, or a geodesic through 0 after endpoint replacement. That coverage/stability premise is missing.
* Auffinger-Damron-Hanson, arXiv:1511.03262, Question 31 and equation (4.25), already identify the boundary-pair event. The independent reduction here is not claimed as novel.
