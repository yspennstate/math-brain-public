All results below are author proofs awaiting independent checking by w395. None establishes P0117. The claims are recorded as proved special cases or conditional lemmas, not independently checked proofs.

L1. A finite, measurable exhaustion of anchored bigeodesics.

Assume deterministic finite strictly positive edge weights on the undirected nearest-neighbor lattice Z^2. Write B_n={x: ||x||_infinity<=n}, S_n={x: ||x||_infinity=n}, and T_n for shortest-path distance in the graph induced by B_n. Let E_n mean that some simple T_n-minimizing path between two distinct vertices of S_n passes through 0.

Then E_(n+1) is contained in E_n, each E_n depends only on the finitely many edges of B_n, and the intersection over n>=1 of E_n is exactly the event that a full-lattice bigeodesic passes through 0. This does not assume independence, uniqueness, a moment bound, a limit shape, or a direction.

Proof. If a witness exists at scale n+1, split it at 0 and stop each half at its first hit of S_n. The combined subpath lies in B_n. Every subpath minimizes in B_(n+1); any cheaper B_n competitor would also be a B_(n+1) competitor. Thus the combined path is a witness for E_n. Finiteness of B_n gives measurability.

A bigeodesic through 0 has two simple infinite halves. Each half exits every finite B_n; its first boundary hits give a simple globally minimizing subpath, hence an E_n witness. Conversely, choose a witness P_n for each n and orient it about 0. Each side has at least n edges. For every k, only finitely many oriented two-sided k-edge prefixes are possible. Successively extracting infinite subsequences makes every centered finite prefix constant. The compatible prefixes define a doubly infinite simple lattice path: every finite prefix was simple in the witnesses. Fix a finite segment and any competing finite full-lattice path Q between its endpoints. For sufficiently large n in the selected subsequence, the segment has stabilized and Q lies in B_n. The segment, as a subpath of P_n, has weight at most that of Q. This comparison against every finite Q proves global minimization of every segment. It explicitly prevents the error of confusing a box minimizer at one scale with a global minimizer.

Consequently, under any probability law of positive finite weights, P(bigeodesic through 0)=lim_n P(E_n). For a stationary law, showing this probability is zero rules out all bigeodesics by translating and taking a countable union over lattice vertices.

L2. A conditional survival criterion that permits dependence.

Let (F_n) be an increasing filtration, E_n decreasing F_n-measurable events, and q_n F_n-measurable random variables in [0,1]. Suppose P(E_(n+1)|F_n)<=1_(E_n)(1-q_n) almost surely. If sum_n q_n=infinity almost surely on E_infinity=intersection_n E_n, then P(E_infinity)=0.

Proof. Set A_1=0, A_n=sum_(j=1)^(n-1)q_j, and M_n=1_(E_n) exp(A_n). Then E[M_(n+1)|F_n] <= M_n exp(q_n)(1-q_n) <= M_n, because exp(q)(1-q)<=1 for 0<=q<=1. Each M_n is integrable, being bounded by exp(n-1). The nonnegative supermartingale convergence theorem gives a finite almost-sure limit. On E_infinity with divergent accumulated hazard, M_n tends to infinity, a contradiction. No unconditional per-scale bounds are multiplied and no independence of E_n is assumed.

Applying L2 to L1 uses F_n generated by edges inside B_n. The missing FPP estimate is a predictable lower bound on the conditional probability of killing all anchored candidates whose sum diverges along survival. An unconditional midpoint estimate is not this bound.

L3. A cheap boundary ring supplies positive but summable lower bounds.

In addition to L1, assume iid atomless nonnegative weights whose law satisfies G([0,a))>0 for every a>0. Given F_n, let m be the minimum of the four weights incident to 0; m is finite and strictly positive. At radius r=n+1, require all 8r edges around S_r to have weight less than m/(8r). These ring edges are independent of F_n. The conditional probability of the requirement is

    b_n = G([0,m/(8(n+1))))^(8(n+1)) > 0.

Any two S_r vertices can then be joined by a ring arc of at most 4r edges, with weight less than m/2. Every simple path through 0 has two incident edges, with total weight at least 2m. Thus E_(n+1) is impossible when the ring requirement holds, and P(E_(n+1)|F_n)<=1_(E_n)(1-b_n).

These specific witness probabilities are summable almost surely: atomlessness gives G([0,a)) tending to 0 as a decreases to 0; for each finite m, eventually G([0,m/(8(n+1))))<=1/2, so b_n<=2^(-8(n+1)). The finite prefix also has a finite sum. This proves the ring construction does not supply the divergent lower bound required in L2. It does not assert that the actual conditional killing hazards are summable.

L4. Escaping cheap rings refute a pointwise-limit shortcut.

For each integer M>=2, assign weight 1/(8M) to every edge of the square boundary S_M and weight 1 to every other lattice edge. There is no bigeodesic through 0 in this deterministic positive environment: the ring joins any two first-exit points using an arc of weight at most 1/2, whereas every through-0 segment pays at least 2 just on its two origin edges. Nevertheless these environments converge edge by edge to the all-one environment, which has a horizontal bigeodesic through 0. Moreover, their box events are exactly E_n=1 for n<M and E_n=0 for n>=M: below M all box edges have weight one, and at M the cheap ring kills; nesting handles larger n. Thus the limits of E_n in M and n need not commute. This is a deterministic logical counterexample, not a claim of an iid atomless FPP counterexample.

C1. Positive finite-energy killing probabilities alone do not imply extinction.

Let independent trials at stages n>=1 kill with probability q_n=1/(n+1)^2, and let E_n mean survival before stage n. All conditional kill probabilities are positive. Nevertheless, P(E_(N+1))=product_(n=1)^N (1-1/(n+1)^2)=(N+2)/(2(N+1)), tending to 1/2. This is a probability counterexample to an inference, not an iid planar FPP counterexample.

The explicit ring lower bounds above fail this requirement. No available midpoint or directional theorem has been shown to provide it.
