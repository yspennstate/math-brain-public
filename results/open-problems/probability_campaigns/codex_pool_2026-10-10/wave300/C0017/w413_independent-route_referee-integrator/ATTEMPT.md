## Route: domain perturbation and crossing stability

I will seek a quantitative crossing stability theorem under local lattice replacement. Couple square bond percolation and a solvable triangular site model using independent uniforms on a common mesh, replace the random variables and local connectivity rules one cell at a time, and telescope the crossing probability. For a given replacement, disagreement requires a pivotal configuration reaching the boundary arcs. Its probability should be bounded by an appropriate multi-arm event at the cell scale, with boundary variants near the marked points. If the summed signed effect tends to zero after a symmetry cancellation, Cardy's triangular-lattice limit would transfer to square bond percolation. Standard RSW bounds alone control only coarse crossing probabilities and cannot justify this cancellation. The load-bearing missing theorem is a sufficiently sharp and signed pivotal comparison across the two lattice laws, uniform in Jordan domains and marked boundary points. Neither a coupling nor a four-arm upper bound by itself closes it.

Before any computation, I will verify that the reference triangular model and a common cell replacement really define the same boundary event after interpolation. I will separately check whether existing universality results already settle or rule out this route. If I derive an exact finite-graph telescoping identity, I will mark it only as a finite-volume lemma, not as progress on the conformal limit until the summed error is controlled.

## Independent follow-up: spatially varying isoradial track exchange

The 2026 Duminil-Copin–Kozlowski–Krachun–Manolescu–Oulamara result, arXiv:2012.11672v2, Theorem 1.7, couples critical random-cluster configurations on *constant-angle* isoradial rectangular embeddings with a polynomial cluster-network error; q=1 includes square-bond percolation. This gives an independent route from w411's local gadget comparison and w412's Loewner martingale identification. Approximate a conformal map by a slowly varying field of local angle/scale changes; use track exchanges on mesoscopic blocks to transport the bond configuration while preserving quad crossings. A successful proof would need a coupling uniform over adjacent angle fields and enough quantitative control to sum block-interface errors, plus exact boundary normalization to Cardy's function.

Theorem 1.7 itself has a single constant angle; it cannot be multiplied over blocks or passed through a random slit domain without proving joint control of the couplings and boundary defects. The separate hardest open piece on this route is a spatially inhomogeneous track-exchange stability theorem with signed/conditional error control strong enough for all marked quads. Even that would establish conformal covariance before it identifies Cardy's particular value. I have no proof of either missing step, so this is a conjectural route only. The earlier continuum-identification idea overlaps w412's saved two-martingale work and is not claimed as distinct.

## 2024 claimed proof: bounded audit scope

Wang Zhou's arXiv:2409.03235v6 Theorem 2 claims interface convergence under Condition C. In the PDF, Condition C requires long straight boundary segments of length at least 1/log(1/δ) and perpendicular interior clearance, with geometric conditions near the Dobrushin endpoints. The author derives Cardy's formula as Corollary 1.1. I verified the theorem's *statement and hypotheses*, not the 115-page proof. In particular, I did not independently verify that the new edge-observable estimates survive exploration in random slit domains or that every domain approximation needed by the historical problem can be put under Condition C. The 2026 rotational-invariance paper's introduction still describes full conformal invariance as a further goal. Thus this preprint is a claimed resolution requiring dedicated mathematical review, not an established theorem in this packet.
