## Exact theorem comparison

Smirnov, *Conformal invariance in random cluster models I*, Theorem 2.2, states convergence of a normalized parafermionic observable for the square-lattice **FK-Ising** model: q=2, p=sqrt(2)/(1+sqrt(2)), Dobrushin boundary conditions, Caratheodory convergence of marked lattice domains, delta^(-1/2) F_delta -> (Phi')^(1/2) locally uniformly away from the boundary. The source's general-q extension is Conjecture 2.4. This theorem cannot be instantiated at independent square bonds, which are q=1, p=1/2. Its full hypothesis mismatch is q and p, and the conclusion is observable convergence for FK-Ising rather than the C0017 crossing plus interface statements. Source: https://www.unige.ch/~smirnov/papers/fk1, pp. 4-5.

Duminil-Copin, arXiv:1208.3787, Definition 3 and Proposition 4, gives for q in (0,4), at its self-dual critical parameter in a Dobrushin domain, the edge observable F(e)=E[exp(i sigma W(e,e_b)) 1_{e in gamma}] with sin(pi sigma/2)=sqrt(q)/2 and the local relation F(NW)-F(SE)=i(F(NE)-F(SW)). For q=1, sigma=1/3. The paper explicitly labels the general-q observable scaling limit Conjecture 1. The local relation is available; a common holomorphic limit, boundary identification, and SLE_6 convergence do not follow from it alone. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.3787, pp. 6, 9-10.

## Independent derivation and tests

I rederived w417-L1 by writing P_i=(nu_1,...,nu_i,mu_{i+1},...,mu_n). The difference E_{P_i}f-E_{P_{i-1}}f conditions on the other cells under H_i. For an exterior x with constant section pi -> f(x,pi), this is zero. For a nonconstant Boolean section, it is nu_i(B_x)-mu_i(B_x), with absolute value <= TV(mu_i,nu_i). Integrate under H_i and telescope. The product-law hypothesis supplies precisely this hybrid exterior; no conditional per-row bounds are multiplied. For f=AND of 3 bits, mu_i=Bernoulli(1/4), nu_i=Bernoulli(1/2), the true difference is 0.5^3-0.25^3=0.109375. The weighted bound is 0.25*(0.25^2+0.5*0.25+0.5^2)=0.109375, exactly sharp. The unweighted bound is 0.75, a measured factor 6.857 larger.

I independently checked w417-L2. If the three terminals cannot all connect in the deterministic graph, the target all-connected mass p>0 is impossible. Otherwise a spanning tree contains a minimal subtree joining them; a leaf of that subtree is one of the terminals, say c. Opening exactly the tree path between a and b and closing every other graph edge has strictly positive probability and yields the forbidden partition ab|c. For m fair edges that one configuration has probability 2^(-m), proving the claimed TV lower bound. This does not bar growing approximate or dependent gadgets. w417-L3 follows from t^2=1/2 and t[1-(1-t)^2]=1-1/(2sqrt(2)); the crossing bias is (1-1/sqrt(2))/2 = 0.1464466094. Its dependent-bit counterexample C1 is also correct by two-state enumeration.

I independently checked w418's abstract weak-CR example. With horizontal F(e)=conj(z_e), vertical F(e)=z_e, at each grid vertex F_E-F_W=delta and F_N-F_S=i delta, so WCR is exact. The four-edge average is Re z. On [0,1]^2, integral Re z dz=i, so the average is not holomorphic. `python -X utf8 verify_weak_cr.py` gave zero weak-CR residual and zero average error for grid n=1,2,4,8, with imaginary contour integral 1 each time. This is a counterexample to inference from the weak relation plus separate orientation equicontinuity, not a counterexample to the actual parafermionic observable. If all orientations converge locally uniformly to the same continuous f, then i delta times the sum of WCR over a grid rectangle converges to integral f dz=0; rectangular Morera gives holomorphicity. Coherence and boundary identification are the missing percolation-specific premises.

I rederived w418's endpoint-estimate trap. For three independent bits, f=majority, mu=Bernoulli(epsilon)^3, nu=Bernoulli(1-epsilon)^3, endpoint sensitivities are all 2epsilon(1-epsilon). Substituting them into L1 would give 6epsilon(1-epsilon)(1-2epsilon), which tends to zero, while the actual probability difference 1-6epsilon^2+4epsilon^3 tends to one. At epsilon=0.01 these are 0.058212 and 0.999404. The middle true hybrid sensitivity is (1-epsilon)^2+epsilon^2, so L1 itself remains valid and in fact sharp. Endpoint arm estimates cannot be silently used for hybrid laws.
