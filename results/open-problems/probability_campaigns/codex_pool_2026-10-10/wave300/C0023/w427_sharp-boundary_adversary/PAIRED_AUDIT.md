# Independent adversary verdict on the paired statements

Reviewed w426/STATEMENT.md and the claim rows after saving the independent boundary derivation. Verdict: KEEP L1, KEEP L2, KEEP B1, within their stated assumptions. No verdict here promotes a general gluing conjecture or C0023 to a theorem.

L1: On an open o-to-A path the deterministic separator v is reached. The open a_star-to-b path supplies its suffix from v to b. Concatenation gives o-to-b. The event inclusion holds under every edge law. The product probability lower bound additionally uses Harris association for two increasing events under independent edges. That assumption must stay attached to the probabilistic conclusion; it is not needed for the event inclusion.

L2: Deal first with different underlying forest components. A relay outside b's component gives minimum terminal probability zero. If all relays are in b's component and o is elsewhere, the o-to-A probability is zero. Otherwise all relevant vertices lie in one tree. For every relay a, the unique path from a meets the unique o-to-b path at a well-defined branch point v(a). Let v be the branch point nearest o and choose a_star attaining it. Every o-to-A path includes v, while the a_star-to-b path includes v. Apply L1. Zero-length paths (o=b, or a relay equal to an endpoint), ties, and deterministic edge probabilities cause no failure. A three-vertex path with relay at the middle gives equality and establishes the optimal coefficient 1.

B1: On o--a2--a1--b let the three edges have probabilities r,s,t and order the relays as (a1,a2). Selection of a2 forces the middle edge closed, so P(a2 connects to b | I=a2)=0 although the unconditional probability is st>0. The actual o-to-b probability is rst, and the incorrectly averaged marginal expression is rst(2-s). Its excess is rst(1-s)>0. At r=s=t=1/2 the two probabilities are 1/8 and 3/16. The original minimum-relay product is r*min(t,st)=rst, so the correct gluing inequality is attained exactly in the very example that refutes the stronger bridge.

Minimal missing premise for extending the forest proof: a deterministic separator witness is not supplied by independent edge laws. The four-cycle o--a--b--c--o with A={a,c} has no v and a_star satisfying L1. The direct o-to-a and o-to-c paths share only o, forcing v=o, whereas each possible a_star has a direct edge to b avoiding o. This refutes universal existence of that witness on cyclic graphs. At p=1/2 the true terminal probability is 7/16, the o-to-relay probability 3/4, and each relay-to-b probability 9/16; the correct product lower bound is 27/64<28/64. Thus failure of the separator witness is not failure of the product inequality.

The hardest remaining piece is a general dependence-aware gluing argument with constants uniform over the graph and relay count. An arbitrary-order selected relay cannot retain its unconditional continuation probability. The recent public preprint attempts such dependence control in its joint-gluing section, but this packet has not independently checked that entire proof or its downstream geometry.
