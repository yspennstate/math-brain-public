# C0023 proof checkpoint

The assigned target is no percolation at criticality on Z^d for 3 <= d <= 10. The primary mathematical convention is independent nearest-neighbor bond percolation, as in Kozma--Nitzan, arXiv:2401.12397v1, Section 1. Historical intake provenance is recorded separately in SOURCE_STATUS.json. Current openness is UNKNOWN; no success or novelty is claimed. Public comparator statements inspected on 2026-10-10 contain admitted placeholders, so they are not proof receipts.

Let theta_d(p) = P_p(|C(0)| = infinity) and p_c(d) = inf {p : theta_d(p) > 0}. The working target is theta_d(p_c(d)) = 0 for each integer 3 <= d <= 10.

## Lemma L0: finite-volume one-arm reduction

For n >= 1, let Lambda_n = [-n,n]^d intersect Z^d, let partial Lambda_n be its infinity-norm vertex boundary, and let A_n be the event that an open nearest-neighbor path connects 0 to partial Lambda_n. The path can be taken inside Lambda_n by stopping at its first boundary hit.

The events A_n decrease with n. Their intersection is exactly the event that C(0) is unbounded: existence of a connected vertex at arbitrarily large infinity-norm distance is equivalent to boundary crossings at every n. Every bounded lattice set is finite. Thus their intersection is {|C(0)| = infinity}. Probability continuity from above proves theta_d(p) = lim_n P_p(A_n) for each fixed p.

This is an elementary reduction, not a proof of critical nonpercolation.

## Prior-attempt digest and lane boundary

The historical C0023-A001 attempt tries to pass from half-space nonpercolation to the full lattice. Its own record names unproved half-space susceptibility and hyperplane gluing inputs. A full-lattice infinite cluster can break into finite pieces when boundary-crossing edges are removed, so half-space nonpercolation does not itself rule that cluster out. This packet does not reuse that unsupported implication.

Kozma--Nitzan, arXiv:2401.12397v1, Section 1, identifies the finite-size-criterion route and explains why a sprinkling argument applies above criticality. Its Conjecture 1 is a separate, general finite-graph inequality: P(0 connected to b) >= P(0 connected to A) min_{a in A} P(a connected to b). That inequality and the authors' reduction are not proved here. Our lane instead makes the dependence-range obstruction in a direct local-anchor argument explicit.

## Lemma L1: a valid dependence calculation

Let (X_z)_{z in Z^2} be a {0,1}-valued field. Assume strong R-dependence in the infinity norm: sigma-fields generated by two index sets at distance greater than the nonnegative integer R are independent. Suppose P(X_z=0) <= q for every z. Set K=(R+1)^2.

For any m distinct indices z_1,...,z_m,

    P(X_z1=...=X_zm=0) <= q^{ceil(m/K)}.

Partition Z^2 by the two coordinate residues modulo R+1. One of the K classes contains at least ceil(m/K) of the listed indices. All distinct members of that class are farther than R from one another. Strong R-dependence, applied inductively to one index versus the other indices, gives mutual independence inside that class. The all-bad event is contained in the all-bad event for this independent subfamily. Multiplying probabilities only for this subfamily gives the bound. Mere pairwise independence would not suffice for this proof.

## Lemma L2: an explicit local-block threshold

Under L1's assumptions, put t=8 q^{1/K}. If t<1, then

    P(0 lies in an infinite nearest-neighbor component of good sites)
       >= 1-q-9 t(1+t)/(1-t)^3.

In particular, q <= delta_R := 800^{-K} implies this probability is greater than 9/10.

Proof. If the origin is good but its nearest-neighbor good component is finite, its exterior vertex boundary contains a closed star-neighbor walk of bad sites with nonzero winding about the origin. To see the deterministic boundary fact, trace the exterior perimeter of the union of closed unit squares centered at the finite component. Each perimeter edge has an exterior square whose center is a bad nearest-neighbor of the component. Successive exterior centers coincide or are star-neighbors. Removing consecutive repetitions gives a closed star-walk around the component. Moving the traced perimeter through these exterior squares to their centers preserves winding about 0. Decompose this walk at repeated vertices; winding is additive, so one resulting vertex-simple cycle has nonzero winding. Its vertices are distinct bad sites. Unit star-edges between these sites cannot pass through the good lattice vertex 0.

For a cycle of m distinct sites with nonzero winding about 0, all vertices lie in [-m,m]^2: each coordinate's span is at most m, and both coordinate ranges contain 0. Overcount such cycles by choosing a starting point in this square and then m star-steps, with at most eight choices per step. There are at most (2m+1)^2 8^m possibilities. L1 bounds each cycle's all-bad probability by q^{ceil(m/K)} <= q^{m/K}. A union bound, including all m>=1 as an overcount, gives

    P(0 good and its good component finite)
       <= sum_{m>=1} (2m+1)^2 t^m
       <= 9 sum_{m>=1} m^2 t^m
       = 9 t(1+t)/(1-t)^3.

Add P(0 bad)<=q and take complements. At q<=800^{-K}, t<=1/100 and q<=1/800. The error is at most 1/800 + 9(1/100)(101/100)/(99/100)^3 < 1/10. This argument never multiplies overlapping block probabilities.

## Local anchor construction

Fix integer d>=2 and integers M>=L>=1. Embed coarse indices z=(z_1,z_2) in Z^d by c_z=L(z_1,z_2,0,...,0). Define

    S_z = c_z + [-L,L]^d intersect Z^d,
    D_z(M) = c_z + [-M,M]^d intersect Z^d,
    H=M+L.

Use the same fixed total order on the offsets of each seed S_z. Let h_z(L,M) be the first vertex of S_z that has an open path, using edges with both ends in D_z(M), to the vertex boundary of D_z(M); leave it undefined if there is no such vertex.

Let G_z(L,M) be the finite event that h_z and the four coarse nearest-neighbor anchors h_w are defined and, for each such w, h_z is connected to h_w inside D_z(H). Every anchor test used by this event is supported in D_z(H), since D_w(M) is contained there. For different good blocks the anchor h_w is the same globally defined finite rule, not a fresh incompatible choice.

Thus G_z uses only the N=d(2H)(2H+1)^{d-1} nearest-neighbor bonds internal to D_z(H). Its translates form a strong R-dependent field with

    R=floor(2H/L),  K=(R+1)^2.

Indeed, coarse index sets at infinity-norm distance greater than R have disjoint supporting lattice boxes, and hence disjoint supporting bond sets. Independence follows from the underlying product measure for whole sigma-fields.

If a nearest-neighbor coarse component of good indices is infinite, the selected anchors in that component all belong to one open lattice cluster. The anchors lie within distance L of their coarse centers, so anchors indexed by an unbounded set of coarse sites are unbounded. Their common open cluster is therefore infinite.

## Lemma L3: the certificate persists below its parameter

For fixed d,L,M, write q_p(L,M)=P_p(G_0(L,M)^c) and delta_R=800^{-(R+1)^2}. If p_0>0 and q_{p_0}(L,M)<delta_R, then there exists p_1<p_0 with theta_d(p_1)>0.

Proof. Couple the finitely many N supporting bonds with independent uniform random variables. For any event depending on those bonds, its indicators at parameters p and p' can differ only if at least one uniform lies between p and p'. A union bound gives

    |P_p(G_0)-P_p'(G_0)| <= N |p-p'|.

Choose epsilon=min(p_0/2, (delta_R-q_{p_0})/(2N))>0 and p_1=p_0-epsilon. Then q_{p_1}<delta_R. The support and compatibility properties hold at p_1 as well. L2 gives positive probability of an infinite good coarse component, and the anchor construction gives positive probability of an infinite lattice cluster. The latter event is a countable union over vertices. At least one vertex therefore has positive probability of belonging to an infinite cluster; lattice translation invariance gives theta_d(p_1)>0.

For the critical parameter this has the unconditional consequence

    q_{p_c(d)}(L,M) >= delta_{floor(2(M+L)/L)}
    for every finite M>=L>=1.

Here p_c(d)>0 follows, for example, from counting nonbacktracking paths: for p<1/(2d-1), the probability of an open self-avoiding path of length n from 0 is at most 2d p ((2d-1)p)^{n-1}, which tends to zero. A certificate with a strict margin at p_c would contradict the defining infimum. No assertion of a strict inequality is needed.

## Lemma L4: what a hypothetical infinite cluster actually supplies

Assume at a fixed p that there is almost surely exactly one infinite open cluster C_infinity. This is an explicit assumption; under a hypothetical theta_d(p_c)>0 it is supplied by product-measure ergodicity and the standard Burton--Keane uniqueness theorem. No independence of cluster-membership events is assumed.

For fixed L and a coarse z, as M tends to infinity the anchor h_z(L,M) stabilizes almost surely to the first vertex of S_z intersect C_infinity, if that intersection is nonempty; otherwise the anchor eventually becomes undefined. This holds because there are finitely many vertices in S_z. Each vertex outside C_infinity has a finite cluster, with a finite maximal radius. Each vertex in C_infinity reaches the boundary of every sufficiently large box around c_z; stopping its path at the first boundary hit gives the required internal path.

For the five seeds indexed by z and its four coarse neighbors, if all meet C_infinity then their stabilized anchors are connected by finitely many finite open paths. These paths eventually fit inside D_z(M+L). If a seed misses C_infinity, its anchor eventually is undefined. Consequently,

    1_{G_z(L,M)} -> 1_{all five seeds meet C_infinity}
    almost surely for each fixed L.

Bounded convergence gives

    lim_{M->infinity} q_p(L,M)
       = P_p(at least one of the five seeds misses C_infinity)
       <= 5 b_L,
    b_L=P_p(S_0 intersect C_infinity is empty).

The boxes S_0 increase to all of Z^d, and C_infinity exists almost surely, so b_L decreases to zero by probability continuity from above. Each translated seed has the same miss probability. Therefore

    lim_{L->infinity} lim_{M->infinity} q_p(L,M)=0.

This is a proved two-scale limit under the stated assumption. It is not a rate estimate, a bounded-aspect-ratio limit, or a dependence-matched certificate.

## Lemma L5: the diagonal argument does not follow from that limit

The numerical family below demonstrates a logical obstruction, not a percolation counterexample. For integers M>=L>=1 set

    q_fake(L,M) = 1             if M < L 2^L,
                  2^{-L}       if M >= L 2^L.

For fixed L it is nonincreasing in M and tends to 2^{-L}; hence its iterated limit is zero. Define R(L,M)=floor(2(M+L)/L) exactly as above. Nevertheless q_fake(L,M)>800^{-(R(L,M)+1)^2} for every allowed pair.

If M<L2^L, this follows from q_fake=1. Otherwise R>=2^{L+1}+2, so (R+1)^2>L; therefore 800^{-(R+1)^2}<2^{-(R+1)^2}<2^{-L}. Arbitrarily good iterated-limit accuracy can thus coexist with failure of every dependence-matched certificate.

## Single hardest remaining gap G

For each assigned d, derive from the hypothetical theta_d(p_c(d))>0 a finite pair M>=L>=1 for which

    q_{p_c(d)}(L,M) < 800^{-(floor(2(M+L)/L)+1)^2}.

Together with L3 this would contradict criticality and solve the target. L4 supplies only an iterated limit and L5 proves that this limit alone does not imply G. A sufficient, stronger input would be a justified high-probability estimate for G_0(L,aL) along a fixed integer aspect ratio a, since its dependence range would then be constant. Such same-scale connectivity is not proved here.

The attempted proof therefore stops at G. The reductions and the finite-dependent criterion are handwritten, author-checked draft lemmas; they are not independently checked proofs of the full target.
