No percolation at criticality on quasi-transitive graphs
OpenAI
September 24, 2026
Abstract
We prove that critical Bernoulli bond percolation has no infinite cluster on any infinite
connected locally finite quasi-transitive graph with critical probability less than one. This
resolves the bond form of the criticality conjecture of Benjamini and Schramm.

1 Introduction
Bernoulli bond percolation on an undirected graphG = (V,E )retains each edge independently with
probabilityp∈[0, 1]. Retained edges are called open. The open clusterCx of a vertexx consists of
the vertices joined tox by finite open paths, includingx itself. Writex↔Awhen Cx meets a set
A⊆V, andx↔ywheny∈Cx. WithP p denoting the product law, the critical probability is
pc(G) = inf{p∈[0,1] :Pp(there is an infinite open cluster)>0}.
A graph isquasi-transitiveif its automorphism group has finitely many orbits on vertices. This
includes every vertex-transitive graph and every Cayley graph of a finitely generated group with a
finite generating set.
The criticality problem asks whether an infinite cluster can first appear at the threshold itself.
Benjamini and Schramm formulated the general quasi-transitive conjecture in their systematic study
of percolation beyond Euclidean lattices [3, Conjecture 4].
Theorem 1.1.Let G be an infinite connected locally finite quasi-transitive undirected graph. If
pc(G)<1, then
Ppc(G)(there is an infinite open cluster) = 0.
Equivalently,P pc(G)(|Cx|=∞) = 0for every vertexx.
Theorem 1.1 resolves the Benjamini–Schramm criticality conjecture positively for Bernoulli
bond percolation. Its conclusion concerns all vertex orbits. The hypothesispc < 1is necessary:
at parameter one every edge is open, so an infinite connected graph percolates. In particular, the
theorem includes nearest-neighbor bond percolation onZd for everyd≥2; see Corollary 10.1.
