The exact target is nearest-neighbour **bond** percolation on Z^d, 3<=d<=10. Current openness remains UNKNOWN here because September 2026 formal proof claims have not been independently audited. No claim below is a solution of C0023.

The brain returned a sharpness card asserting subcritical exponential decay “right up to p_c.” The primary theorem says: for **each** p<p_c there is c(p)>0, and no bound at p=p_c is supplied. The retrieved result therefore cannot close the parameter-limit bridge. Kozma--Nitzan's Conjecture 3 asks for a uniform gluing modulus delta(epsilon) on **arbitrary finite independently edge-weighted graphs**; its Theorem 6 then implies theta(p_c)=0. A high marginal probability for each dependent block does not satisfy that finite-graph conjecture, and a block criterion must control its actual dependence range.

I independently checked w430's strongest load-bearing claim. Put a_n(p)=P_p(0 connects to the vertex boundary of Lambda_n), Lambda_n=[-n,n]^d. Duminil-Copin--Tassion define phi_p(S)=p sum over edges leaving S of P_p(0 connects inside S to the inside endpoint) and prove sup{p: some finite S containing 0 has phi_p(S)<1}=p_c. Because phi_p(S) is a finite polynomial, phi_{p_c}(S)<1 would also hold at some p>p_c. Thus phi_{p_c}(S)>=1 for every such S. For Lambda_n there are exactly 2d(2n+1)^(d-1) boundary edges, and every inside endpoint is on the vertex boundary. Hence phi_{p_c}(Lambda_n)<=p_c*2d(2n+1)^(d-1)*a_n(p_c), giving the lower bound in CLAIMS R02. For each n, a_n(p) is a finite polynomial: the first path hit of the box boundary occurs within Lambda_n. Consequently any exponential bound with positive rate uniform in p just below p_c would pass to p_c and violate the lower bound at large n.

For w429 L2 I also rederived the independent-color step: color the planar index lattice by both coordinates modulo r+1, giving K=(r+1)^2 colors. Any distinct sites in one color are separated by more than r, so the stated sigma-field independence gives joint independence for a finite subset. Any m bad sites include ceil(m/K) sites of one color, making its all-bad probability <=delta^(m/K). The exterior boundary of a finite nearest-neighbour good component containing 0 yields a bad star-connected circuit. Counting at most m*8^m circuits of length m by a positive-axis starting vertex gives error at most sum_m m(8delta^(1/K))^m. At delta<=64^(-K), this is <=8/49, and the origin-good probability is >=1-delta. Thus the claimed positive probability follows. This proves only a sufficient fixed-range criterion. It does not supply w429 L4's missing high-quality compatible blocks.

Peer w430 then supplied a stronger sufficient lemma, independently checked here. Tile the coordinate plane into squares of side s=r+1 and mark a tile good iff every site is good. Tiles at coarse distance at least two have original site distance at least s+1>r, so the tile field is setwise 1-dependent; its bad marginal is at most s^2 delta. The r=1 contour lemma gives percolation if s^2 delta<=64^-4. Adjacent fully good tiles give actual nearest-neighbour paths. This is a polynomial-in-range sufficient condition and improves w429's exponential-in-r threshold for r>=2. It is not a proof that critical percolation supplies such tiles or blocks.

Single hardest open step within this attempted route: from hypothetical theta_d(p_c)>0, construct **finite** block events with deterministic compatibility and quantitative bad probability delta satisfying delta*(r+1)^2<64^-4 for their actual dependence range r. Qualitative uniqueness and finite dependence at a growing scale do not yield it. A proof could instead supply Kozma--Nitzan's finite-graph gluing inequality, but neither peer has done so.
