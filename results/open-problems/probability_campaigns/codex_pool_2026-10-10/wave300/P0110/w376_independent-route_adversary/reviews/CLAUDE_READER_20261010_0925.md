## Referee report: the arbitrary-coupling consequence and its concrete constant

The correction under review is the final section ("coupling cannot erase a raw drift gap", with claim C008) and its numerical instantiation c0. I also checked the earlier blocks it relies on.

**Line-by-line check of C008**

1. **Hypotheses used.** The argument uses limsup U_n/n ≤ μ−c a.s. (from w375 L2) and V_n/n → μ a.s. These are properties of each sequence on its own original space. The union bound only needs the marginal probabilities P(U_n/n > μ−c+ε) and P(V_n/n < μ−ε) at each n. So any coupling, even one that changes with n, is legitimately allowed.
2. **Vanishing of the error terms.** Since limsup U_n/n ≤ μ−c a.s., the event {U_n/n > μ−c+ε} occurs only finitely often a.s. Its indicator therefore tends to 0 a.s., and bounded convergence gives P → 0. The V-term is handled the same way. This is valid.
3. **Union bound.** On the complement of both events, V_n/n ≥ μ−ε and U_n/n ≤ μ−c+ε. Hence (V_n−U_n)/n ≥ c−2ε, which is positive because ε < c/2. The inequality is correct and needs no independence.
4. **Meaning of μ.** The only coordinate-monotone path from 0 to ne1 is the straight axial path. Its cost divided by n tends to E t_e by the SLLN. So μ here must be E t_e (3/2 for Uniform[1,2]), not the FPP time constant. If μ were read as the time constant, L2's statement would say time constant ≤ time constant − c, which is false. The text defines μ implicitly through "directed axial cost / n → μ", so the argument is consistent. It would still be safer to write μ := E t_e explicitly.
5. **Concrete constant.**
   - The C004 event has 6 high edges and 8 low edges, each with probability 1/4, giving probability 4^(−14) = 1/268435456.
   - The cost gap is at least 6·(7/4) − 8·(5/4) = 10.5 − 10 = 1/2.
   - So delta ≥ (1/2)·4^(−14), and c = delta/(k+1) with k = 6 gives delta/7.
   - That yields c0 = 1/(14·4^14) = 1/3758096384, and the arithmetic checks out.
   - The rigour of this step depends on L2 actually defining delta as at least (gap)×P(event), with block length k+1 = 7. I cannot see L2's text, so this step is conditional on the audited L2.
6. **The example P(...≥ c0/2) → 1.** Take ε = c0/4, which is less than c/2. Then c−2ε ≥ c0 − c0/2 = c0/2. This is valid.
7. **Scope.** The scope is honest. The claim excludes raw o_P(n) coupling only, and makes no claim about centered fluctuations, exponents, or P0110.
