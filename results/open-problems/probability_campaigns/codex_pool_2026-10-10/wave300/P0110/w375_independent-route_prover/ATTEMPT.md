# P0110: bounded-disorder detours as an independent route

2026-10-10, w375. Outcome: four author-proved special-case lemmas; P0110 remains unproved. Current openness UNKNOWN; no novelty claim. Independent referee w377 has not yet checked these proofs.

The exact target is a nondegenerate iid undirected FPP law on Z^d, d>=2, with well-defined fluctuation and wandering exponents satisfying chi=2xi-1. The canonical P0110 card agrees with Auffinger-Damron-Hanson's Question 22, Section 4.3 of https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.03262. The survey's question is existential; the conditional scaling identity already has a published theorem. SOURCE_STATUS.json records source/version limitations.

For a precise sufficient route, define chi_a as the upper threshold such that every q>chi_a admits alpha>0 with sup_{v!=0} E exp(alpha*|T(0,v)-E T(0,v)|/|v|^q)<infinity. Define xi_a similarly with D(0,v)/|v|^q, where D is the maximum Euclidean deviation of the unique geodesic from [0,v]. Define chi_b as the lower threshold such that every q<chi_b has a uniformly positive lower bound on Var(T(0,v))/|v|^(2q) for all sufficiently large |v|, and xi_b similarly for E D(0,v)/|v|^q. Chatterjee's Theorem 1.1 assumes iid nearly-gamma weights with a moment generating function finite near zero in d>=2, and proves chi=2xi-1 if chi_a=chi_b=chi and xi_a=xi_b=xi. Its exponent equalities are not an established input for our chosen law.

Candidate law: Uniform[1,1+epsilon], epsilon>0, in dimension 2. Its bounded positive support implies that the minimum is attained: a directed path costs at most (1+epsilon)*|v|_1, so only paths with at most that many edges can improve it. There are finitely many such paths. Continuity and independence give uniqueness: two distinct finite paths differ on some edge, and conditioning on all other edges leaves a continuously distributed nonzero cost difference. A countable union covers all path pairs and endpoints. All exponential moments of a single weight exist; uniform laws belong to the nearly-gamma class in Chatterjee's stated examples. None of these elementary admissibility facts gives a scaling exponent.
