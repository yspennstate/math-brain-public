   \[
   \rho_C(x)=C^{-m}\prod_i\cos^2(\pi x_i/(2C))1_{(-C,C)^m},

   \]
   Thus `C=3 sqrt(2 pi)` gives `kappa=1/3` exactly.

2. **Translate containment and sign recovery.** For the open transform

   T_vK=((K-v)\cap(K+v))+\{tv:|t|<2\},

   convexity gives `T_v K subset (K-v) union (K+v)`. Iterating nonempty symmetric transforms and starting backward from `0` yields one Boolean sign at each step. No algorithmic claim is needed.

3. **Finite-step stability.** The Section 4 lift
   \[
   R(y,s)=\rho(y+sv)1_{|s|<3}/6

   has mass one. Fiberwise symmetric decreasing rearrangement contracts every horizontal L1 translation difference, so it does not increase the directional variations used by the induction.

4. **Retained-height identity.** The layer-cake calculation gives exactly
   \[
   \int |s|R^* =\frac32-\frac1{24}\int_0^6(6-h)\|\rho-\rho(\cdot+hv)\|_1dh.

   The BV translation inequality and `int_0^6 h(6-h)dh=36` give
   \[
   \int|s|R^*\ge\frac32-\frac32V_v(\rho).

   Thus `V_v<=1/3` makes height one available. The height-one section is exactly the open Banaszczyk transform.

5. **Prescribed-section/common-density step.** For every finite mixture

Slicing gives the averaged `H`-energy bound; convexity and Jensen at the mean absolute height bound the Cheeger value of the prescribed section. The upward-closed finite variation-vector set is convex, and separation forces a nonnegative separating vector, contradicting those mixture bounds. Coordinate directions supply BV compactness and lower semicontinuity, so the common finite-direction density is attained. 6. **Minkowski convexity analytic input.** I independently checked the normalization used in the primary source.
