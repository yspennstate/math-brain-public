# PROB10-09 idea-before-code — higher-rank central kernels

Frozen parent: iid BOND percolation on Cayley graphs of finitely generated nonamenable groups; prove `p_c<p_u`. This note proposes only a restricted-family theorem, hence PARTIAL.

## Primary route: replace the cyclic central fibre by an arbitrary finitely generated abelian central fibre

Let

`Q = Gamma_{g_1} * ... * Gamma_{g_r}`, `g_i>=2`,

with the union of the standard surface generating sets. Let

`1 -> A -> Gamma -> Q -> 1`

be a central extension with `A` finitely generated abelian. For the `i`th surface factor, let the lifted surface relator have central value `e_i in A`. Assume `e_1,...,e_r` generate `A`. Then the horizontal standard lifts alone generate `Gamma` (arbitrary central shears do not change the relator values because `A` is central).

The earlier cyclic-fibre proof should extend without any one-dimensional Fourier feature: Fourier transform on the discrete abelian group `A` gives a direct integral over its compact dual `A^`. Every fibre is a magnetic adjacency on `Q`, pointwise dominated by the untwisted adjacency. Hence

`||A_Gamma|| <= ||A_Q|| <= sum_i ||A_{Gamma_{g_i}}|| <= 4 sum_i sqrt(g_i)`.

On the critical side, Magnus Freiheitssatz gives a free subgroup of rank `2g_i-1` inside each surface factor from all but one standard generator. Their free product gives a free subgroup of rank

`k = sum_i (2g_i-1) = 2G-r`, `G=sum_i g_i`,

visible in the horizontal Cayley graph. Therefore

`p_c <= 1/(2k-1) = 1/(4G-2r-1)`.

Let `D=4G`, `q=D-1`, `rho<=4 sum sqrt(g_i)<=4 sqrt(rG)`, and `t=4G-2r-1`. The nonbacktracking root criterion will prove `beta_D(rho)<t` if

`(i) t>sqrt(q)` and `(ii) t+q/t > rho`.

The proposed analytic reduction is:

1. `t^2-q>0` for `G>=2r`.
2. `H_r(G)=t+q/t-4sqrt(rG)` is increasing on `G>=2r`, because
   `H' = 4 - 8r/t^2 - 2sqrt(r/G) > 4-8/25-sqrt(2)>0`.
3. At `G=2r`,
   `H_r(2r)=(6r-1)+(8r-1)/(6r-1)-4rsqrt(2)`;
   since `(8r-1)/(6r-1)>4/3` and `6-4sqrt(2)>0`, this is positive.

Thus every such horizontal-lift graph has `p_c<p_{2->2}<=p_u`.

### Frozen genuinely new graph for a quantitative certificate

Take `r=2`, `g_1=g_2=2`, `A=Z^2`, and relator vectors `e_1=(1,0)`, `e_2=(0,1)`. This is not the previous cyclic-centre graph. The horizontal graph has

`D=16`, `q=15`, `k=6`, `p_c<=1/11`, `rho<=8sqrt(2)`.

Try the rational nonbacktracking root cap `z=489/50`. If

`z^2>15` and `z+15/z>8sqrt(2)`,

then `p_u>1/z=50/489`, giving

`p_u-p_c > 50/489 - 1/11 = 61/5379 ~= 0.01134`.

The checker should certify these exact rational inequalities after squaring only positive quantities.

## Negation/scope test

The horizontal lifts generate the full central kernel exactly when the subgroup of `A` generated by the relator charges `e_i` is all of `A`. For `A=Z^m`, this is the primitive-lattice condition that the `m x r` Euler matrix has rank `m` and gcd of its maximal minors equal to one. If the generated subgroup has index `d>1`, the same horizontal labels generate only an index-`d` subgroup; that is an exact scope obstruction, not a counterexample to percolation.

## Three materially different methods compared

1. **Abelian-dual magnetic domination (primary):** extend the cyclic Fourier argument to arbitrary compact dual `A^`; expected to succeed and to give a full structural criterion.
2. **Finite-index/subgroup comparison:** pass to the subgroup generated by horizontal lifts when the Euler lattice is nonprimitive, prove a gap there, then try to transfer it to the full group. This is not enough for the requested full-group Cayley graph because `p_u` is not monotone in a way that supplies the needed strict comparison after adding cosets/generators. Record as a genuine obstruction rather than pretending the index issue disappears.
3. **Virtual central motion via surface relators:** coarse-grain relator words to build an `F_k x Z^m`-type subprocess. This pays powers `p^{4g_i}` per virtual central displacement and is quantitatively dominated by the direct horizontal free-tree witness; it is a backup mechanism, not the primary proof.
