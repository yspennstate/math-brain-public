## Prior-art correction before the new attack

Gaboriau--Tucker-Drob, *Approximations of standard equivalence relations and Bernoulli percolation at p_u* (C. R. Math. 354 (2016), Thm. 1.1), proves that if a nonamenable group is generated by two commuting infinite finitely generated subgroups, then for **every** Cayley graph, percolation at `p_u` is not in the uniqueness phase. In particular every finitely generated nonamenable group with infinite finitely generated centre satisfies their hypothesis by taking `H=G`, `K=<z>`.

That theorem is stronger than our restricted-family work with respect to the *boundary behaviour at `p_u`*, so no qualitative novelty is claimed merely from having infinite centre. It does **not by itself certify a strict threshold separation `p_c<p_u`**: `p_c=p_u` with no infinite cluster at the common critical value is logically compatible with “`p_u` is not in the uniqueness phase”. The quantitative strict inequalities below therefore remain a distinct target.

A current 10 Sep 2026 source (`arXiv:2609.11845`, Section 3.2 / its recorded open problem) still records the general Benjamini--Schramm nonamenability/nonuniqueness-phase conjecture as unresolved.

## Three genuinely different moves for this run

### Move A — cohomological / magnetic lift invariance (SUCCESS candidate)

Stop fixing canonical horizontal lifts. Work in an arbitrary central extension

`1 -> <z> ~= Z -> Gamma -> Q -> 1`

and allow **arbitrary central shears** of every quotient generator. Fourier transform in `<z>`. Each horizontal fibre is a magnetic copy of the quotient adjacency with unit-modulus phases, hence is diamagnetically dominated by the ordinary quotient adjacency. This should make the `p_u` lower bound uniform over all lift/shear choices.

If `k` quotient generators freely generate `F_k`, their arbitrary lifts still freely generate `F_k`; together with central `z` they give an exact `F_k x Z` subgroup. The full `Z`-fibre branching argument then gives a strict `p_c<1/(2k)`, also uniformly over lift/shear choices.

Proposed criterion. Let `d=|S_Q|`, `rho=||A_Q||`, `D=d+2`, `q=D-1=d+1`, `x=2k`, and let

`beta_D(R)=sqrt(q)` if `R<=2sqrt(q)`, otherwise `(R+sqrt(R^2-4q))/2`.

For `R=rho+2`, if

`x>sqrt(q)` and `R < x + q/x`,

then `beta_D(R)<x`, hence

`p_c(Gamma,S_lift union {z^+-1}) < 1/x < 1/beta_D(R) <= p_(2->2) <= p_u`.

This is a complete structural criterion, not merely the phrase “show p_c<p_(2->2)”.

### Move B — remove the explicit central generator (NEGATION / obstruction)

Try to prove the same statement when the generating set consists only of horizontally sheared lifts and omits `z`. The present mechanism breaks at an exact place: the freely projecting lifted generators alone give only `F_k`, not the edge-subgraph `F_k x Z`; the strict full-fibre improvement `p_c<1/(2k)` disappears and the immediate witness degrades to `p_c<=1/(2k-1)`. Central displacement hidden in relators is not represented by an edge-disjoint fibre exploration. Therefore this route does not yet approach arbitrary Cayley generating sets.

### Move C — arbitrary quotient Nielsen changes (NEGATION / obstruction)

Try to replace the standard surface quotient generators by an arbitrary finite quotient generating set. The lift-uniform Fourier step survives, but the two quantitative inputs do not: (i) there need not be a visible rank-`k` free subset among the generators, and (ii) Zuk's sharp standard-surface spectral bound no longer controls `rho` for an arbitrary quotient generating set. Thus an all-generating-set theorem requires a genuinely new quotient-side invariant, not another central-extension lemma.
