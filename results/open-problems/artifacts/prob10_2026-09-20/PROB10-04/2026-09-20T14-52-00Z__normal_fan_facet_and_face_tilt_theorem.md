## 1. Finite-support setting

Fix truncation `M`. For each variable block `(i,j)`, `1<=j<=i<=M`, let

\[
S_{ij}=\{(i,0,0)\}\cup\{(0,k,j-k):1\le k\le j\}.
\]

All corresponding source weights are strictly positive. The heavy-negative block is the segment

\[
H=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\}.
\]

Up to a fixed translation, the variable projected mean polytope is a positive weighted Minkowski sum of `H` and the polytopes `conv S_ij`. For a direction `xi=(a,b,c)`, the support of one light block is

\[
h_{ij}(\xi)=\max\{ai,\max_{1\le k\le j}[bk+c(j-k)]\}.
\]

The false-state score is affine in `k`, hence its maximum is attained at `k=j` if `b>=c` and at `k=1` if `b<c`.

Define the primitive wall family

\[
\mathcal W_V=\operatorname{prim}\Bigl(\{(0,1,-1)\}\cup
\{(i,-j,0),(i,-1,1-j):1\le j\le i\le M\}\Bigr),
\tag{1.1}
\]

where `prim` identifies nonzero scalar multiples with one primitive integer representative.

For the clause side, in each fixed syntactic-positive class `r`, let `T_r` be the finite set of projected clause states `(ps,ns1,ns2)`. Define

\[
\mathcal W_C=\operatorname{prim}\{s-t:s,t\in T_r,\ s\ne t,\ 0\le r\le3\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

## 2. Complete facet-normal theorem

### Theorem 2.1 — variable facets

Every outward facet-normal ray of the variable projected mean polytope is generated by

\[
\xi=w_1\times w_2\ne0
\tag{2.1}
\]

for two linearly independent walls `w_1,w_2 in W_V`.

Conversely, for any oriented primitive candidate `xi` obtained from (2.1), let `D_V(xi)` be the set of all differences between tied maximizing states in each Minkowski summand at direction `xi`. Then `xi` supports a facet if and only if

\[
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{span}D_V(\xi)=2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

For a positive weighted Minkowski sum `P=sum_b d_b P_b` with `d_b>0`, the exposed face satisfies

\[
F_P(\xi)=\sum_b d_b F_{P_b}(\xi).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Its affine-direction space is therefore the linear span of the affine-direction spaces of the exposed summand faces. Equivalently it is the span of differences of tied maximizing states in all summands.

If `xi` supports a facet in three dimensions, `F_P(xi)` has affine dimension two. Hence two linearly independent tied state differences `d_1,d_2` occur and satisfy `xi dot d_1=xi dot d_2=0`; therefore `xi` is parallel to `d_1 cross d_2`.

For the heavy-negative segment the only nonzero tied difference is `(0,1,-1)`. For a light block, if `b>c`, only the false endpoint `(0,j,0)` can maximize among false states, so a true/false tie has difference proportional to `(i,-j,0)`. If `b<c`, only `(0,1,j-1)` can maximize and a true/false tie has difference proportional to `(i,-1,1-j)`. If `b=c`, all false states tie and their direction is `(0,1,-1)`; any simultaneous true/false tie is already captured by either endpoint wall. Thus every possible tied difference needed for a facet lies in the wall family (1.1), up to nonzero scalar multiple. This proves the first statement.

For the converse, (2.3) shows that the exposed face has affine dimension exactly `rank span D_V(xi)`. A nonzero support direction exposes a facet exactly when that dimension is two, proving (2.2). QED.

### Theorem 2.2 — clause facets

Every outward facet-normal ray of the clause projected mean polytope is a nonzero cross product of two walls in `W_C`; an oriented candidate `xi` is a facet normal exactly when the span of tied maximizing clause-state differences at `xi` has rank two.

#### Proof

The clause projected mean polytope is a positive weighted Minkowski sum of the finite polytopes `conv T_r`. Apply (2.3). Any tied state difference in a summand is literally an element of the family used to define `W_C`. The same rank argument proves both claims. QED.

### Corollary 2.3 — exact integer compiler

All candidate normal rays and all facet/nonfacet decisions can be generated using integer arithmetic only:

1. primitive-normalize the finite wall vectors;
2. take primitive nonzero cross products of wall pairs;
3. test both orientations separately;
4. compare integer state scores and compute the rank of tied integer differences.

Testing both orientations is essential: the existence of a facet at `xi` does not imply a facet at `-xi`.

## 3. Complete feasible-region H-representation

Let `P_V` and `P_C` be the variable and clause projected mean polytopes, and let

\[
S=\{(u,v,w):0\le u\le\lambda_p,\ v\ge0,\ w\ge0,\ v+w\le\lambda_n\}.
\]

Let `F_V` and `F_C` be the complete oriented facet-normal lists from Theorems 2.1--2.2, and let `h_V,h_C` be the exact support functions already derived on the PROB10-04 route. Then

\[
\boxed{
P_V\cap P_C\cap S
=
\{y:\xi\cdot y\le h_V(\xi)\ \forall\xi\in F_V,
\ \zeta\cdot y\le h_C(\zeta)\ \forall\zeta\in F_C,
\ y\in S\}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

A full-dimensional polytope is the intersection of the closed halfspaces defined by its facets. Theorems 2.1--2.2 give all facet normals, while the support function gives the unique supporting offset in each such direction. Intersecting the two complete H-representations with `S` gives (3.1). No new inequality is required to describe an intersection: some input inequalities may become redundant, but every point satisfying all input inequalities lies in each constituent polytope and hence in their intersection. QED.

## 4. Quantitative finite-parameter approach to an entropy face

### Theorem 4.1 — face-tilt decomposition

Let

\[
A(\theta)=\log\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}w_s e^{\theta\cdot s},\qquad w_s>0,
\]

on a finite state set. Fix a nonzero direction `xi`; put

\[
h=\max_s\xi\cdot s,\qquad
F=\{s:\xi\cdot s=h\}.
\]

If `F` is not the whole state set, let

\[
\Delta=\min_{s\notin F}(h-\xi\cdot s)>0.
\]

For finite `eta`, define

\[
A_F(\eta)=\log\sum_{s\in F}w_s e^{\eta\cdot s},
\]

and

\[
R_0(\eta)=
\frac{\sum_{s\notin F}w_s e^{\eta\cdot s}}
{\sum_{s\in F}w_s e^{\eta\cdot s}}.
\]

Then for every `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
A(t\xi+\eta)=th+A_F(\eta)+\log(1+R_t(\eta)),}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
0\le R_t(\eta)\le R_0(\eta)e^{-t\Delta}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently, for every `y in conv F` (so `xi dot y=h`),

\[
\boxed{
A(t\xi+\eta)-(t\xi+\eta)\cdot y
=A_F(\eta)-\eta\cdot y+\log(1+R_t(\eta)),}
\tag{4.3}
\]

and the ambient finite tangent exceeds the corresponding face tangent by at most

\[
\boxed{R_0(\eta)e^{-t\Delta}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

Factor `e^{th}` from the face terms in the partition function. For every outside state, write

`e^{t xi dot s}=e^{th} e^{-t(h-xi dot s)}`.

This gives (4.1), and every outside gap is at least `Delta`, giving (4.2). If `y in conv F`, then `xi dot y=h`; subtract `(t xi+eta) dot y` from (4.1) to obtain (4.3). Finally `log(1+x)<=x` for `x>=0` gives (4.4). QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — integer incidence faces have unit spectral gap

If the state vectors and primitive normal `xi` are integral, every score `xi dot s` is an integer. Hence every positive score gap is at least one:

\[
\boxed{\Delta\ge1.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus, on every proper exposed face of the Diaz variable/clause incidence polytopes, finite normal tilt approaches the face-restricted entropy tangent at least as fast as `R_0(eta)e^{-t}`.

### Corollary 4.3 — weighted product blocks

For a positive weighted sum `A=sum_b d_b A_b`, apply Theorem 4.1 blockwise. On a common exposed face, the total leakage is bounded by

\[
\sum_b d_b R_{0,b}(\eta)e^{-t\Delta_b},
\]

with blocks having no outside states contributing zero. In the integral incidence setting every nonzero `Delta_b>=1`.

This supplies a constructive boundary mechanism complementary to the previous abstract compactness proof: one may recurse on exposed faces, choose finite tangential parameters there, and lift them to finite ambient dual parameters with explicit exponentially small leakage.
