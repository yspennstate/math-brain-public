## Route B — normal-fan facet compiler (primary)

### Proposed lemma B1: facet normals are cross-products of two maximizer walls

Let `P_V` be the variable projected mean polytope. If `xi` is an outward normal to a facet of `P_V`, the exposed Minkowski face has dimension two. Hence among the positively weighted summands there are two linearly independent tied state-difference vectors orthogonal to `xi`. By the endpoint observation above, those tied differences are members of `W_V` (up to nonzero integer multiples). Thus the ray `R_+ xi` is generated by a nonzero cross product of two elements of `W_V`.

Exactly the same argument applies to the clause polytope with `W_C`.

This gives a finite **complete** candidate list in dimension three; it is not just a sampled set of directions.

### Proposed lemma B2: exact facet test by exposed-direction rank

For a candidate oriented primitive integer normal `xi`, collect all differences between tied maximizing states in all positive-weight Minkowski summands. The exposed face is the Minkowski sum of the exposed summand faces, so its affine-direction space is the span of those tied differences. Consequently `xi` supports a facet iff that span has rank exactly two. Because all states and `xi` are integral, this test is exact integer arithmetic.

### Proposed lemma B3: the full common feasible region gets no new facet normals

Let `P=P_V cap P_C cap S`, where `S` is the obvious copy simplex. Once full facet H-representations of `P_V` and `P_C` are compiled, every facet inequality of `P` is one of the inequalities in the union of those two H-representations and the five copy-simplex inequalities. Intersecting polytopes can make existing inequalities redundant, but cannot create a new supporting hyperplane normal not present in a valid complete H-representation.

Thus a complete outer arrangement cell is described by:

1. finitely many compiled source-facet inequalities;
2. finitely many tangent-order inequalities `L_j<=L_k`.

Every three-dimensional cell extreme point is therefore an intersection of three hyperplanes drawn from this finite list, with lower-dimensional cases handled by rank.

## Route C — quantitative finite-parameter access to entropy faces

The previous compactness proof showed that finite tangents approximate boundary entropy but did not quantify the dual parameter needed. For one finite log partition

`A(theta)=log sum_s w_s exp(theta dot s)`,

fix an integer normal `xi`, put `h=max_s xi dot s`, face `F={s:xi dot s=h}`, and positive score gap

`Delta=min_{s notin F}(h-xi dot s)`.

For any finite tangential parameter `eta`, define

`A_F(eta)=log sum_{s in F} w_s exp(eta dot s)`.

Then for `theta=t xi+eta`, exactly

`A(theta)=t h + A_F(eta) + log(1+R_t(eta))`,

where

`0<=R_t(eta)<=R_0(eta) exp(-t Delta)`.

For any mean `y` on the exposed face (`xi dot y=h`),

`A(theta)-theta dot y = A_F(eta)-eta dot y + log(1+R_t(eta))`.

Hence a face-dual tangent can be lifted to a finite ambient tangent with explicit leakage at most

`log(1+R_0(eta)e^{-t Delta}) <= R_0(eta)e^{-t Delta}`.

In the Diaz incidence polytopes, primitive facet normals and state vectors are integral, so every nonzero score gap satisfies **Delta>=1**. Thus the approach to every exposed face is exponentially fast in the normal tilt, with no inverse-covariance assumption. Recursing on lower-dimensional faces gives a constructive alternative to the previous abstract boundary compactness argument.
