## Three materially different approaches considered before code

### A. General partition-lattice positivity for all `K_{m,n}`

For fixed `m`, processing one right-side vertex acts on the partition lattice `Pi_m`. The transfer has

`M_m=(m+1)I+N_m`, with `N_m^m=0`,

because every off-diagonal legal star transition strictly coarsens the terminal partition. Marked one- and two-edge counts are obtained by inserting marked transfer operators before powers of `M_m`.

The observed `m=3,4,5` Rayleigh numerators factor into positive-coefficient polynomials in `n` (with the same-right-vertex orbit carrying a factor `n-1`). A genuinely general proof would identify a positivity-preserving basis or Möbius/Whitney-number interpretation of every coefficient for arbitrary `m`. I do not currently have that sign interpretation; proving it would settle all complete bipartite graphs at once, but treating the observed coefficients as automatically positive would be unjustified.

### B. Exact sparse transfer at the next widths `m=6,7,8`

This is the chosen route. Bell numbers `B_6=203`, `B_7=877`, `B_8=4140` are still small enough for exact sparse partition transfer, while the theorem covers infinitely many 3-connected, non-series-parallel graphs for every right-side size `n`. Instead of dense matrix powers, compute the finite nilpotent coefficient vectors

`a_j = v N^j 1`,
`b_j = v E_0 N^j 1`,
`c_j = v E_{01} N^j 1`,
`l_j = v E_0 E_0 N^j 1`,
`d_j = v E_0 E_1 N^j 1`.

Then with `q=m+1`,

`Z/q^n = sum_j a_j binom(n,j)/q^j`,
`E/q^n = q^{-1} sum_j b_j binom(n-1,j)/q^j`,
`R/q^n = q^{-1} sum_j c_j binom(n-1,j)/q^j`,
`L/q^n = q^{-2} sum_j l_j binom(n-2,j)/q^j`,
`D/q^n = q^{-2} sum_j d_j binom(n-2,j)/q^j`.

Here `R,L,D` are the three pair orbits: same right vertex, same left vertex/different right vertices, and vertex-disjoint. Exact polynomial arithmetic can then test whether `E^2-ZR`, `E^2-ZL`, `E^2-ZD` admit manifestly positive power-basis certificates for all integer `n>=1` (or `n>=2` where needed). This is a theorem for the whole parameter range if the factors are exact and positive; it is not graph enumeration in `n`.

### C. Deletion–contraction / terminal-substitution closure

A scalar deletion–contraction recurrence for a newest spoke introduces the same marked minors/response data that the partition transfer already records. Two-terminal substitution is now developed independently in NM13-08 and does not decompose `K_{m,n}` once both sides have size at least 3, because these graphs are 3-connected. Re-entering that route here would overlap current owner work without a new closure mechanism. I therefore retain deletion–contraction only as a conceptual consistency check and do not claim progress from it.
