## Original/current source

Canonical outside target: sharp asymptotics for singularity of an iid Rademacher matrix. Repository locator: `F0001`, through Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, arXiv:2005.02797, Section 2, and the modern primary source

- Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney, *Singularity of discrete random matrices*, arXiv:2010.06554v2 / GAFA 31 (2021).

Current arXiv page checked 2026-09-17: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06554 . For iid uniform signs the source-matched conjectural main term remains

`P(det B_n=0) = (1+o(1)) n(n-1) 2^(1-n)`.

Jain–Sah–Sawhney prove the sharp elementary-mechanism formula for finite-support laws that are not uniform on their support. For laws uniform on their support they give the correct exponential scale and a sharp structured/compressible analysis, not the sharp polynomial prefactor. Their paper explicitly calls the uniform-support case the main remaining question and records unresolved issues in Irmatov's 2020 claimed Rademacher proof, including Theorem 3.

Two later primary/current papers were rechecked:

- Zeyan Song and Hanchao Wang, *Rank deficiency of Bernoulli random matrices for growing corank*, arXiv:2607.00495 (2026-07-01): `P(corank >= k)=(1-p+o(1))^(kn)` for `k=O(sqrt(log n))`; it does not determine the sharp corank-one Rademacher prefactor.
- Zach Hunter, Matthew Kwan, Lisa Sauermann, Mehtaab Sawhney, *On random matrices with large corank*, arXiv:2510.12933, now published in IMRN 2026(12), rnag126: `P(rank <= n-k) <= exp(-c n k)`; again not the sharp corank-one prefactor.

A bounded fresh 2026-09-17 search did not locate a later primary proof of the sharp iid nonsymmetric Rademacher prefactor. This is negative-search evidence only, not a worldwide novelty certificate.

## Important prior-art correction: Voigt–Ziegler 2006

A directly relevant source missed by the preceding affine-excess reports is:

- Thomas Voigt and Günter M. Ziegler, *Singular 0/1-Matrices, and the Hyperplanes Spanned by Random 0/1-Vectors*, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 15 (2006), 463–471, arXiv:math/0308050, DOI 10.1017/S0963548305007339.

Primary/current pages checked:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308050
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/abs/singular-01matrices-and-the-hyperplanes-spanned-by-random-01vectors/AB459BF8E20C1B3FFF0AADF989D65F55

They define `P_s(d)` as the singularity probability of a uniform `d x d` 0/1 matrix and `E_VZ(d)` as the expected number of cube vertices in the hyperplane spanned by a random independent `(d-1)`-set of 0/1-vectors. Their Theorem 1.1 states

`P_s(d) = ( 2^(-d) E_VZ(d) + d^2 2^(-d-1) ) (1+o(1))`.

They also prove the trivial lower bound `E_VZ(d) >= (d^2/2)(1+o(1))` from the fat coordinate/equality hyperplanes and state the strict equivalence between the conjectured singularity asymptotic and `E_VZ(d) ~ d^2/2`. They explicitly note that one may pass by symmetry to an affine version.

This means the conceptual core of run31's C285 — replacing sharp singularity by a basis-weighted random cube-hyperplane excess problem — is **not new**. C285 remains a valid exact collision-deleted/sign-gauge refinement with a convenient normalization, but it must not be presented as a novel source-equivalent geometric reduction. Any future write-up should cite Voigt–Ziegler prominently.

Voigt–Ziegler also already use strong-rank stratification and Littlewood–Offord bounds. Their proof shows that the lower-rank sectors are smaller, leaving exactly the expected hyperplane-vertex count as the hard rank-`d-1` quantity. This agrees with the modern higher-corank localization but predates it.

## Quantitative location where the JSS non-uniform proof loses the uniform case

The Jain–Sah–Sawhney PDF was inspected at Theorem 1.2/Corollary 1.3 and at the final deduction of Theorem 1.2. They fix a discrete law `p=(p_i)` and choose an auxiliary epsilon satisfying

`exp(2 epsilon - H(p)) < ||p||_2^2`.

The inequality is possible precisely because

`Delta(p) := H(p) + log ||p||_2^2 > 0`

for a non-uniform law, with equality exactly for a uniform law.

For the two-point sign law with mean/bias parameter `m`,

`P(+1)=(1+m)/2`, `P(-1)=(1-m)/2`,

one has

`Delta(m) = m^2/2 - 7 m^4/12 + 3 m^6/10 + O(m^8)`.

Thus the entropy margin in the existing non-uniform proof degenerates quadratically as the bias tends to zero. In the triangular window `m=m_n`, a proof step depending on an exponential separation from this gap has effective exponent at most order `n m_n^2`.

At the same time, the elementary equal/opposite row-pair mechanism is

`2^(-n)[(1+m^2)^n + (1-m^2)^n]`,

so convergence back to the uniform elementary mechanism requires `n m_n^2 -> 0`. These two requirements do not overlap for a direct use of the existing entropy-gap proof: the proof margin disappears precisely in the window where the biased elementary mechanism becomes the uniform one. This is a route barrier, not a proof that every possible deformation argument must fail.

## Run32 route conclusion

The most promising genuinely new ingredient found this run is to average a vanishing-bias product law over the full row/column sign-flip gauge before comparing it with the uniform law. For gauge-invariant events such as singularity with signed row/column collisions deleted, the averaged likelihood has a Fourier expansion only over Eulerian subgraphs of `K_{n,n}`; all degrees 1,2,3 vanish and the first nonconstant terms are 4-cycles. Run32 derives an exact cycle-space likelihood identity, a universal change-of-measure lower bound, and the subcritical second-moment limit at bias `m=t/sqrt(n)`. This does not yet close the conjecture, but it isolates a concrete triangular-array theorem that would be sufficient and shows quantitatively why the published fixed-law JSS argument does not currently provide it.
