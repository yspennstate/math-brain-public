## Assigned outside source

1. Historical lineage: J. Komlós, *On the determinant of (0,1) matrices*, Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 2 (1967), 7–21.
2. Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney, *Singularity of discrete random matrices*, arXiv:2010.06554, current arXiv version v2 dated 5 May 2021 / GAFA 31 (2021). Conjecture 1.1 states that singularity is asymptotically exhausted by zero rows/columns and equal/opposite row/column pairs. The paper proves the sharp full theorem when the entry law is not uniform on its support. For laws uniform on their support, including the uniform Rademacher law, it proves only exponential-scale precision for full singularity, while Theorem 1.5 sharply handles the almost-constant/low-entropy contribution. The introduction also records unresolved issues in the claimed Irmatov Rademacher resolution, including its Theorem 3.
3. Julian Sahasrabudhe, *Probabilistic combinatorics at exponentially small scales*, arXiv:2512.15077v1 (submitted 17 Dec 2025). Section 4 records Tikhomirov's `2^{-n+o(n)}` theorem and says that the precise iid `{−1,1}` asymptotic remains perhaps the central open problem in the area. The symmetric Bernoulli ensemble is treated separately immediately afterward.

For the uniform sign law, the zero-row mechanism is absent and each signed equal/opposite pair event has probability `2^{-n}`. Thus the source-normalized target used by R03 is

`P(det B_n=0)=(1+o(1))*2*n*(n-1)*2^{-n}= (1+o(1))*n*(n-1)*2^{1-n}`.

R03 retains the exact union `T_n` of all signed row/column pair events and the collision-subtracted metric

`r(n)=2^n P(det B_n=0, T_n^c)`.

The retained overlap calculation gives the matching lower asymptotic; source closure is equivalent to `r(n)=o(n^2)`.
