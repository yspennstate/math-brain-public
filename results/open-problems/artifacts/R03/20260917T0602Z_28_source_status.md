## Original/current source

1. Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney, *Singularity of discrete random matrices*, arXiv:2010.06554 / Geom. Funct. Anal. 2021. Conjecture 1.1 gives the elementary-mechanism asymptotic. For a uniform sign law, the zero-row term is absent and the two signed two-coordinate null events each have probability `2^{-n}`, so the specialization is
   `P(det B_n=0)=(1+o(1)) n(n-1) 2^(1-n)`.
   Their proved sharp theorem excludes laws uniform on their support; for uniform support they obtain only exponential-scale precision for full singularity, while sharply analyzing the compressible contribution.

2. Zeyan Song and Hanchao Wang, *Rank deficiency of Bernoulli random matrices for growing corank*, arXiv:2607.00495, submitted 2026-07-01. For iid Bernoulli(p) entries and `k=O(sqrt(log n))`, the abstract states
   `P(corank A >= k)=(1-p+o_n(1))^(kn)`.
   At `p=1/2`, fixed `k=2`, together with the standard sign/binary rank-preserving reduction, this makes the normalized corank-at-least-two sector `2^n P(corank>=2)=2^{-n+o(n)}`, hence negligible for R03's polynomial-scale normalized remainder.

3. Hunter–Kwan–Sauermann–Sawhney, *On random matrices with large corank*, IMRN 2026, rnag126, proves `P(corank>=k)<=exp(-c n k)` for iid sign matrices. This is useful structural support but its unspecified exponential constant alone is not the sharp R03 prefactor.

No primary-source resolution of the full iid sign prefactor was located in the fresh bounded search on 2026-09-17. Status remains OPEN.

## New primary source resolving run22's evidence gap at binary order nine

Dominik Beck, Zelin Lv, Aaron Potechin, *The Sixth Moment of Random Determinants for Arbitrarily Distributed Random Entries*, arXiv:2607.26857, submitted 2026-07-29.

The paper defines the exact counts `c_n(alpha,beta,gamma)` of binary matrices by number of ones and the two determinants `det B=beta`, `det(B+J)=gamma`. It states that the authors **obtained `c_n(alpha,beta,gamma)` through n=9** by an exhaustive Nauty/Traces computation on the Charles University HPC cluster. For n=9 they report approximately `1.93e15` arithmetic operations, `16,384` parallel CPU-hours, and about `96 GB` of intermediate data. They describe the exact enumeration: canonical representatives under `S_n x S_n`; `33,642,660` representatives at n=8 and `14,685,630,688` at n=9; generation of every 9x9 case by bordering each stored 8x8 representative with `2^17=131,072` row/column extensions; determinants accumulated by fraction-free Bareiss elimination.
