## Exact outside source and normalization

The assigned target remains intake `F0001`, Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, arXiv:2005.02797 / Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 2.2. The intake statement is

\[
p_n=(2+o(1))n^2 2^{-n}
\]

for an iid uniform Rademacher `n x n` matrix. This is asymptotically equivalent to

\[
\Pr(\det B_n=0)=(1+o(1))n(n-1)2^{1-n}.
\]

Let `T_n` be the exact union of equal/opposite signed row-pair and column-pair events. Retained R03 claim C225 pays their overlaps. The frozen remainder remains

\[
r(n)=2^n\Pr(\det B_n=0,T_n^c),
\]

with source closure equivalent to `r(n)=o(n^2)`.

## Current primary literature checked on 2026-09-17

1. Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah and Mehtaab Sawhney, *Singularity of discrete random matrices*, arXiv:2010.06554 / GAFA 31 (2021). Their sharp elementary-mechanism asymptotic is proved for finite-support laws that are **not uniform on their support**. For laws uniform on their support they obtain exponential-scale full singularity precision together with a sharp compressible-sector analysis. Uniform Rademacher remains outside the sharp-prefactor theorem.
2. Zeyan Song and Hanchao Wang, *Rank deficiency of Bernoulli random matrices for growing corank*, arXiv:2607.00495 (2026), determines `P(corank >= k)` for Bernoulli(p) in a growing-corank regime. It does not supply the Rademacher `k=1` polynomial prefactor.
3. Zach Hunter, Matthew Kwan, Lisa Sauermann and Mehtaab Sawhney, *On random matrices with large corank*, arXiv:2510.12933, proves `P(corank >= k) <= exp(-c n k)` for iid Rademacher matrices. This suppresses higher corank but not the sharp corank-one remainder.
4. A fresh bounded search on 2026-09-17 found no later primary paper claiming Vu's exact iid Rademacher asymptotic. This is dated negative-search evidence only, not a worldwide novelty certificate.

## Prior art for the new route

Run36 uses the classical Sárközy--Szemerédi Littlewood--Offord theorem: for `k` distinct real coefficients, the largest atom of a Rademacher sum is `O(k^{-3/2})`; the order is sharp up to constants. A convenient modern source is Hoi Nguyen, *A new approach to an old problem of Erdős and Moser*, arXiv:1112.0755, together with the Nguyen--Vu inverse Littlewood--Offord literature.

I also checked Ferber--Jain--Luh--Samotij, *On the counting problem in inverse Littlewood--Offord theory*, arXiv:1904.10425. It develops direct counting estimates for high-concentration coefficient vectors and random-matrix applications. I did not locate the exact run36 encoding in which `k` actual rows of an invertible sign basis determine the `k` distinct coefficient magnitudes and the remaining `d-k` rows pay the concentration factor. This bounded search is **not** a novelty certificate; any publication claim would require a dedicated literature audit.
