## Primary source and exact normalization

- Historical lineage: János Komlós, *On the determinant of (0-1) matrices*, Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 2 (1967), 7–21. Current Rutgers record: https://scholarship.libraries.rutgers.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/On-the-determinant-of-0-1-matrices/991031550043104646 .
- Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, arXiv:2005.02797, Section 2 / Conjecture 2.2: https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02797 .
- Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney, *Singularity of discrete random matrices*, arXiv:2010.06554 / GAFA 31 (2021), Conjecture 1.1: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06554 . The current arXiv abstract still states the sharp elementary-mechanism theorem for finite-support laws **not uniform on their support**; for laws uniform on their support, it gives only exponential-scale precision for full singularity plus a sharp compressible-vector analysis. Thus the uniform Rademacher law remains outside their sharp full theorem.

For iid signs, one specified pair of rows is equal or opposite with probability `2^(1-n)`. There are `binom(n,2)` row pairs and the same number of column pairs. Therefore the elementary first-order union contribution is

`2 * binom(n,2) * 2^(1-n) = n(n-1) 2^(1-n)`.

Retained R03-C225 handles the overlap correction and gives the matching lower asymptotic. Consequently the source-matched target is

`P(det B_n = 0) = (1+o(1)) n(n-1) 2^(1-n)`,

and with `T_n` the exact signed row/column collision union the frozen upper metric is

`r(n) = 2^n P(det B_n=0, T_n^c)`;

source closure is equivalent to `r(n)=o(n^2)`.
