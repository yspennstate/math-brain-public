# HARD7-04 — local-limit/lattice completion for the collared lower bound

This note fills the only delicate technical point in `2026-09-19T1430Z__vdh_collared_lower_bound.md`: the one-color two-dimensional local lower bound and the four-dimensional two-color factorization when the retained degree support is a triangular array.  It is part of the proof candidate, not an independent source-closure claim.

The argument is elementary Fourier/local-CLT technology.  For context, Coulson--Perarnau, *Largest component of subcritical random graphs with given degree sequence*, EJP 28 (2023), Section 2, explicitly develops lattice local-limit estimates for configuration-model exploration and cites Mukhin's multivariate lattice local-limit work (Theory Probab. Appl. 29 (1985), DOI 10.1137/1129047).  Here the fixed degree-1 atom makes the lattice issue simpler, while the fixed scaled cutoff makes the triangular-array Lindeberg estimate automatic.

## 1. Setup retained from the main proof

Fix `a>0`, retain `H_n={v:d_v<=a/epsilon_n}`, and let `k` lie in the logarithmic target window

\[
k\asymp \varepsilon_n^{-2}\log N_n,
\qquad N_n=n\varepsilon_n^3\to\infty.
\]

At the one-color saddle put

\[
x_v=r d_v e^{\theta d_v},\qquad p_v=\frac{x_v}{1+x_v},
\]

where `sum x_v=k`, `sum d_v x_v=2k-2`, and `theta~epsilon_n/R_*`.  The main proof already establishes

\[
\max_vp_v=O(\log N_n/N_n)=o(1),
\tag{1.1}
\]

\[
\sum_vp_v=k+o(\sqrt{k}),\qquad
\sum_vd_vp_v=2k-2+o(\sqrt{k}),
\tag{1.2}
\]

and, for the covariance matrix `Sigma_n` of

\[
(U,V)=\sum_v B_v(1,d_v),
\]

\[
k^{-1}\Sigma_n\to
\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&4+R_*\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det\Sigma_n=(R_*+o(1))k^2.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Also

\[
\frac{a/\varepsilon_n}{\sqrt{k}}=O((\log N_n)^{-1/2})\to0
\tag{1.4}
\]

and

\[
k^{-3/2}\sum_vp_v(1+d_v^3)=o(1).
\tag{1.5}
\]

For (1.5), `sum p_v d_v^3=O((k/n) sum d_v^4)=o(k/epsilon_n)` by the collar and `epsilon_n sqrt{k}~sqrt{log N_n}`.

The critical limiting size-biased degree law has mean 2 and variance `R_*>0`.  Hence the ordinary limiting law has positive mass at degree 1 and at at least one fixed degree `b>=3`.  Indeed, without degree 1 every positive size-biased degree is at least 2, so mean 2 would force degeneracy at 2.  Fix one such `b` and write `g=b-1`.

## 2. A finite cyclic smoothing lemma

We use the following elementary fact.

**Lemma 2.1.** Fix `g>=1`.  Let `J_{n,i}` be independent Bernoulli variables with `max_i P(J_{n,i}=1)->0`, and let `r_{n,i}` be arbitrary elements of `Z/gZ`.  Then there is a constant `c_g>0`, depending only on `g`, such that

\[
\liminf_n
\mathbb P\left(\sum_i r_{n,i}J_{n,i}=0\pmod g\right)\ge c_g.
\tag{2.1}
\]

**Proof.** Group the indices by residue and put `Lambda_{n,r}=sum_{i:r_i=r} p_i`.  There are only `g` residue classes.  Pass to a subsequence along which every `Lambda_{n,r}` converges in `[0,infty]`.  If all nonzero-residue limits are finite, the probability that none of the nonzero-residue variables is selected is

\[
\prod_{r_i\ne0}(1-p_i)
=\exp\{-\sum_{r_i\ne0}p_i+o(1)\}>0.
\]

If some residue classes have infinite intensity, let `H` be the subgroup generated by them.  Fourier inversion on the finite group shows that the sum of those high-intensity classes becomes uniform on `H`: every character nontrivial on `H` has modulus at most

\[
\exp\{-c_g\sum_{r:\chi(r)\ne1}\Lambda_{n,r}(1+o(1))\}\to0.
\]

The remaining finite-intensity classes have a positive limiting probability of selecting no vertex, so their sum lies in `H` with positive probability.  Consequently the total sum equals zero with positive limiting probability, at least that probability divided by `|H|`.  There are finitely many possible subgroups and boundary patterns.  Compactness of `[0,infty]^{g-1}` after the change `lambda -> lambda/(1+lambda)` therefore gives a strictly positive minimum `c_g`.  Since every subsequence has a further subsequence with this property, (2.1) follows.  `square`

The point of Lemma 2.1 is that small-probability degree classes cannot create a vanishing lattice penalty: either their total intensity stays bounded, in which case selecting none has positive probability, or it diverges, in which case they smooth modulo the subgroup they generate.

## 3. Complete proof of the one-color local lower bound

Split the Bernoulli variables into the degree-1 class, the degree-`b` class, and the rest.  Write their selected counts as `A`, `B`, and the rest contribution as `(U_0,V_0)`.  The first two classes contain `Theta(n)` vertices and each of their Bernoulli probabilities is `Theta(k/n)`.  Thus

\[
EA,EB=\Theta(k),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}A,\operatorname{Var}B=\Theta(k).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a fixed realization of the rest, the equations `U=k,V=2k-2` uniquely determine

\[
B=\frac{k-2-(V_0-U_0)}{b-1},
\qquad
A=k-U_0-B.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Choose sizes satisfying `k=2 mod g`.  The integrality condition in (3.2) is exactly

\[
V_0-U_0=0\pmod g.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The rest residue is a sum of independent Bernoulli residues `(d_v-1) mod g`, so Lemma 2.1 gives a fixed lower bound `c_g` for (3.3).

Moreover

\[
\operatorname{Var}U_0=O(k),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}V_0=O(k),
\tag{3.4}
\]

because `sum p_v=O(k)` and `sum p_v d_v^2=O((k/n)sum d_v^3)=O(k)`.  Taking a sufficiently large fixed `C`, Chebyshev's inequality makes

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(
|U_0-EU_0|+|V_0-EV_0|>C\sqrt{k}
\bigr)<c_g/2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence with probability at least `c_g/2` the rest simultaneously has the correct residue and lies within `C sqrt{k}` of its mean.  By (1.2), on this event the two integers in (3.2) lie within `O(sqrt{k})` of `EA,EB`.  Standard Stirling bounds for a binomial with `N=Theta(n)`, success probability `Theta(k/n)=o(1)`, and mean `Theta(k)` give uniformly in that central window

\[
\mathbb P(A=a)\ge c_1k^{-1/2},
\qquad
\mathbb P(B=b')\ge c_2k^{-1/2}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The degree-1 and degree-`b` classes are independent of the rest and of each other.  Conditioning on `(U_0,V_0)` and using (3.2)--(3.6) yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(U=k,V=2k-2)\ge c/k
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

uniformly for `k=2 mod g` in the target window.  This proves Lemma 4.1 of the main artifact without any hidden full-span assumption.

The same argument also shows why restricting to one residue class costs only a fixed factor: the size window contains `Theta(epsilon_n^{-2})` admissible values of `k`.

## 4. Four-dimensional factorization for the second moment

For two colors with target sizes `k,l` in the same short window, use at each retained vertex the three-category law with weights `1,x_v,y_v` from the main proof.  Let

\[
W_n=(U_1,V_1,U_2,V_2).
\]

Its mean differs from `(k,2k-2,l,2l-2)` by `o(sqrt{k})`.  Indeed every mutual-exclusion mean correction is at most `O(kl/n)`, and

\[
\frac{k^{3/2}}n=O((\log N_n)^{3/2}/N_n)\to0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The two diagonal covariance blocks equal the one-color covariance matrices plus `o(k)`.  The cross blocks are `o(k)`: count/count and count/degree entries are `O(kl/n)=o(k)`, while the degree/degree entry satisfies

\[
O\left(\frac{kl}{n^2}\sum_vd_v^4\right)
=o\left(\frac{kl}{n\varepsilon_n}\right)=o(k),
\tag{4.2}
\]

because `k/(n epsilon_n)=O(log N_n/N_n)`.  Thus, after normalization by `sqrt{k}`, the covariance matrix tends to the block-diagonal product of the two one-color limits.

The multivariate Lindeberg remainder vanishes by the same estimate as (1.5).  To see the local statement directly, apply Fourier inversion on `[-pi,pi]^4` and split the domain as follows.

1. In `O(k^{-1/2})` neighborhoods of the dual-lattice points, expand the log characteristic function to second order.  The cubic remainder is `o(1)` by (1.5), the mean displacement is `o(sqrt{k})` by (4.1), and the cross quadratic block is `o(k)` by (4.2).  Hence the Gaussian integral factors into the product of the two one-color Gaussian integrals up to `1+o(1)`.
2. Away from those neighborhoods, the positive-linear-size degree-1 and degree-`b` classes give exponential damping in every non-lattice direction.  At the finitely many residue directions where those two classes alone are periodic, split the remaining degrees by residue modulo `g`.  If a nontrivial residue class has diverging selected intensity, its characteristic factor damps that Fourier mode exponentially.  If all classes capable of detecting the mode have bounded intensity, then the two-color mutual-exclusion correction in that residue sector is `o(1)`, because `max_v(x_v+y_v)=o(1)`; the residue factor therefore equals the product of the two one-color residue factors up to `1+o(1)`.  This is the two-color version of Lemma 2.1.

It follows uniformly for admissible `k,l` that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{
\mathbb P(W_n=(k,2k-2,l,2l-2))
}{
\mathbb P((U_1,V_1)=(k,2k-2))
\mathbb P((U_2,V_2)=(l,2l-2))
}=1+o(1).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is exactly the local-probability ratio used between equations (6.3) and (6.4) in the main artifact.  Together with the explicit coefficient/pairing cancellation already written there, it gives the uniform factorial-moment ratio `1+o(1)`.

## 5. Random post-giant conditionalization

The deterministic theorem can be applied to the random residual degree sequence without an unspoken uniformity assumption by the subsequence principle.  Along any subsequence, the previously proved post-giant statements admit a further subsequence on which, almost surely,

\[
Q_n^{post}/\varepsilon_n\to-1,
\quad R_n^{post}\to R_*,
\quad L_n^{post}/n\to\mu,
\quad
\frac{\varepsilon_n}{n}\sum d_v^4e^{\lambda\varepsilon_nd_v}\to0,
\]

and the empirical third moments/limiting degree law converge as required.  Conditional on the revealed giant history, the remaining pairing is exactly uniform.  The deterministic theorem therefore makes the conditional lower-tail probability tend to zero almost surely along that further subsequence; dominated convergence gives the same unconditionally.  Since this holds for every starting subsequence, the full sequence converges.  Also the giant removes `o_p(n)` vertices, so replacing the residual vertex count by the original `n` changes `log(n epsilon_n^3)` only by `o_p(1)`.

This completes the local-limit and random-conditioning details used by the main proof candidate.
