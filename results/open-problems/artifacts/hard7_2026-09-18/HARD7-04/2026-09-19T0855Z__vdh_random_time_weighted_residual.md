# HARD7-04 — random-time weighted residual theorem for barely-supercritical configuration-model duality

**Status:** analytic route theorem / self-check, PARTIAL only. This note does **not** solve the source's second-largest-component question and does not claim novelty for the empirical-process inequality. Its purpose is to remove one concrete obstruction in the current HARD7-04 route: random-clock sampling noise in the first two degree moments of the putative residual graph.

## 1. Source regime and notation

Let `(d_i)_{i=1}^n` be a deterministic degree sequence, let `D_n` be the degree of a uniformly chosen vertex, and assume the finite-third-moment barely-supercritical regime used by the current `VDH-8.3-DUALITY` target:

\[
\mathbb E D_n^3\to \mathbb E D^3<\infty,
\qquad
\nu_n:=\frac{\sum_i d_i(d_i-1)}{\sum_i d_i}=1+\varepsilon_n,
\qquad
\varepsilon_n\downarrow0,
\qquad
\varepsilon_n n^{1/3}\to\infty,
\tag{1.1}
\]

with `\mathbb E D_n\to\mu>0` and a nondegenerate critical limiting law. Moment convergence in (1.1) gives uniform integrability of `D_n^3`; in particular

\[
d_{\max}^3/n\to0,
\qquad d_{\max}:=\max_i d_i.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Let `\xi_n<1` be the extinction probability of the size-biased excess branching process and set

\[
t_n^*:=-\log\xi_n.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The preceding HARD7-04 route theorem proves

\[
t_n^*=O(\varepsilon_n),
\qquad
1-g_n'(\xi_n)=\varepsilon_n+o(\varepsilon_n),
\tag{1.4}
\]

where `g_n` is the excess-degree pgf.

For each vertex `i`, take an independent clock

\[
E_i\sim \operatorname{Exp}(d_i),
\tag{1.5}
\]

with the convention `E_i=\infty` for `d_i=0`. This is exactly the one-vertex survival law generated by independent exponential half-edge clocks: `\mathbb P(E_i>t)=e^{-d_i t}`.

Define the weighted surviving sums

\[
A_n(t):=\sum_i d_i\mathbf 1\{E_i>t\},
\qquad
B_n(t):=\sum_i d_i(d_i-1)\mathbf 1\{E_i>t\},
\tag{1.6}
\]

and their deterministic means

\[
a_n(t):=\sum_i d_i e^{-d_i t},
\qquad
b_n(t):=\sum_i d_i(d_i-1)e^{-d_i t}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

The ratio `B_n/A_n` is the only degree statistic needed to test the criticality of a configuration model on the surviving vertex set.

## 2. A weighted half-line empirical-process inequality

We first isolate the probabilistic input.

**Lemma 2.1.** Let `Y_1,\dots,Y_n` be independent real random variables and let deterministic weights `w_i\ge0`. Then

\[
\mathbb E\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
\left|\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\bigl(\mathbf 1\{Y_i>t\}-\mathbb P(Y_i>t)\bigr)\right|
\le 4\left(\sum_{i=1}^n w_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

By the standard symmetrization inequality, with independent Rademacher signs `\epsilon_i`, the left side of (2.1) is at most

\[
2\,\mathbb E\sup_t\left|\sum_i\epsilon_i w_i\mathbf 1\{Y_i>t\}\right|.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Condition on the values `(Y_i)`. Order the indices by decreasing `Y_i`. As `t` varies, the set `{i:Y_i>t}` runs through prefixes of this fixed order (ties can be broken arbitrarily, with the tied block crossed at once; allowing intermediate prefixes only enlarges the maximum). Thus the conditional supremum in (2.2) is bounded by the maximum absolute partial sum of independent mean-zero increments `\epsilon_iw_i` in that order. The `L^2` maximal inequality gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\epsilon}\max_k\left|\sum_{j\le k}\epsilon_{\pi(j)}w_{\pi(j)}\right|
\le 2\left(\sum_iw_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Combining (2.2) and (2.3) proves (2.1). `square`

The point of (2.1) is that it is uniform over **all** clock times. A later random exploration stopping time therefore causes no optional-stopping problem here.

## 3. Uniform concentration at exactly the critical-window precision

**Proposition 3.1.** Under (1.1),

\[
\sup_{t\ge0}|A_n(t)-a_n(t)|=o_p(n\varepsilon_n),
\qquad
\sup_{t\ge0}|B_n(t)-b_n(t)|=o_p(n\varepsilon_n).
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

For `A_n`, Lemma 2.1 with `w_i=d_i` gives

\[
\mathbb E\sup_t|A_n(t)-a_n(t)|
\le 4\Bigl(\sum_i d_i^2\Bigr)^{1/2}=O(n^{1/2}),
\tag{3.2}
\]

since the third moments are bounded. Because `\varepsilon_n n^{1/3}\to\infty`,

\[
\frac{n^{1/2}}{n\varepsilon_n}
=\frac{1}{n^{1/2}\varepsilon_n}\to0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For `B_n`, use `w_i=d_i(d_i-1)`. Then

\[
\sum_iw_i^2
\le\sum_i d_i^4
\le d_{\max}\sum_i d_i^3.
\tag{3.4}
\]

By (1.2), `d_{\max}=o(n^{1/3})`, while `\sum_i d_i^3=O(n)`. Hence

\[
\left(\sum_iw_i^2\right)^{1/2}=o(n^{2/3}).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Another application of Lemma 2.1 yields

\[
\mathbb E\sup_t|B_n(t)-b_n(t)|=o(n^{2/3})=o(n\varepsilon_n).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Markov's inequality proves (3.1). `square`

### Scale diagnosis

The proof explains why the source's outside-critical-window condition is exactly natural for this route. The worst second-factorial-moment empirical noise allowed by finite third moments is `o(n^{2/3})`, whereas the drift signal is `n\varepsilon_n`. The condition `\varepsilon_n n^{1/3}\to\infty` is precisely what makes the drift signal asymptotically dominate that noise. At the `n^{-1/3}` critical scale this argument ceases to separate the two.

## 4. Random-time robustness and reflected drift

The preceding proposition is uniform in time, so the time may depend arbitrarily on the clocks and on other exploration randomness.

**Theorem 4.1 (random-time clock duality).** Let `T_n\ge0` be any random times, with no independence assumption, such that

\[
T_n-t_n^*=o_p(\varepsilon_n).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
\frac{B_n(T_n)}{A_n(T_n)}
=1-\varepsilon_n+o_p(\varepsilon_n),
\tag{4.2}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\left|\frac{1-B_n(T_n)/A_n(T_n)}{\varepsilon_n}-1\right|\xrightarrow{p}0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

The deterministic mean curves satisfy, uniformly in `t\ge0`,

\[
|a_n'(t)|\le\sum_i d_i^2=O(n),
\qquad
|b_n'(t)|\le\sum_i d_i^2(d_i-1)\le\sum_i d_i^3=O(n).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore (4.1) implies

\[
a_n(T_n)=a_n(t_n^*)+o_p(n\varepsilon_n),
\qquad
b_n(T_n)=b_n(t_n^*)+o_p(n\varepsilon_n).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Combining (4.5) with Proposition 3.1 gives the same relations with `A_n(T_n),B_n(T_n)` in place of the left sides.

Since `t_n^*=O(\varepsilon_n)` and `\sum d_i^2=O(n)`,

\[
a_n(t_n^*)=\sum_i d_i+O(n\varepsilon_n)=n\mu+o(n),
\tag{4.6}
\]

so the denominator is of order `n`.

Write `G_{0,n}(s)=n^{-1}\sum_i s^{d_i}`. At `e^{-t_n^*}=\xi_n`,

\[
\frac{b_n(t_n^*)}{a_n(t_n^*)}
=\frac{\sum_i d_i(d_i-1)\xi_n^{d_i}}
       {\sum_i d_i\xi_n^{d_i}}
=g_n'(\xi_n),
\tag{4.7}
\]

where the final equality uses the extinction equation `g_n(\xi_n)=\xi_n`. The preceding HARD7-04 parameter theorem gives `g_n'(\xi_n)=1-\varepsilon_n+o(\varepsilon_n)`. Dividing the `o_p(n\varepsilon_n)` numerator and denominator perturbations by the order-`n` denominator proves (4.2), hence (4.3). `square`

## 5. A one-condition reduction for the actual complement of the giant

Let `R_n=V\setminus C_1` be the actual residual vertex set after deleting the unique giant. Let

\[
S_n(T_n):=\{i:E_i>T_n\}.
\]

The full histogram need not be controlled if the following weighted symmetric-difference norm is controlled:

\[
W_n(T_n):=
\sum_{i=1}^n d_i^2\,
\left|\mathbf 1\{i\in R_n\}-\mathbf 1\{i\in S_n(T_n)\}\right|.
\tag{5.1}
\]

**Corollary 5.1.** If there exists a random `T_n` satisfying (4.1) and

\[
W_n(T_n)=o_p(n\varepsilon_n),
\tag{5.2}
\]

then the actual residual criticality parameter

\[
\widehat\nu_n^{\rm res}:=
\frac{\sum_{i\in R_n}d_i(d_i-1)}{\sum_{i\in R_n}d_i}
\tag{5.3}
\]

satisfies

\[
1-\widehat\nu_n^{\rm res}
=\varepsilon_n+o_p(\varepsilon_n).
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

Changing membership of vertex `i` changes the denominator in (5.3) by at most `d_i\le d_i^2` and the numerator by at most `d_i(d_i-1)\le d_i^2`. Thus (5.2) changes each of the two sums from its clock-surrogate value by `o_p(n\varepsilon_n)`. Apply Theorem 4.1 and the order-`n` denominator estimate. `square`

This is a substantive simplification of the residual-law obligation. It replaces control of the complete random residual histogram by one weighted mismatch estimate, (5.2), plus cut-time localization (4.1).

## 6. What is and is not removed

This note removes **clock-sampling noise** as the main obstruction to first-order dual drift. It also shows that dependence of the cut time on the exploration is harmless once its location is known at `o_p(\varepsilon_n)` precision.

It does **not** yet prove (5.2) for `R_n=V\setminus C_1`. That is now the sharp graph-topological obligation: control the degree-square mass of vertices awakened before the giant and any active/sleeping discrepancy at its termination. Nor does (5.4) by itself identify the scale of `C_2`; a source-matching proof still has to transfer an appropriate barely-subcritical largest-component theorem to the random residual degree sequence and handle the conditioning implicit in choosing the largest component.

## 7. Route value

The previous checkpoint said “control the random residual histogram.” The present theorem shows this was stronger than necessary. For the first-order discrete-duality parameter, the needed random information is compressed to

\[
\boxed{\quad
T_n-t_n^*=o_p(\varepsilon_n),
\qquad
W_n(T_n)=o_p(n\varepsilon_n).
\quad}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The next useful attack should therefore target the weighted mismatch `W_n`, especially the pre-giant explored components, rather than build another histogram checker.
