# P0157 — online drift stability, component budget, and exact marked-hazard structure in the actual configuration model

**Status.** R14 theorem-sized self-check / **PARTIAL** progress on van der Hofstad's finite-third-moment barely-subcritical configuration-model crossover problem. This note does **not** solve the source problem and does **not** claim novelty. It records three exact/quantitative facts for the actual pairing process that remove the artificial first-collision/birthday barrier from the previous one-root coupling route.

The main point is that one does not need pathwise agreement with an iid branching process in order to control one realized component. The correct state variable is the residual unseen-vertex measure. Its predictable offspring drift stays close to the original subcritical drift throughout every exploration window `b=o(ell delta_H)`, and the identity of each newly hit marked vertex is exactly degree-size-biased. What remains unresolved is the **clock**: proving, under the rare-event conditioning relevant to an extremal marked component, that the number of pairing opportunities generated by the currently discovered marked mass has the rank-one value needed for a full compressed-tail coupling.

## 1. Setup

Let `d=(d_1,...,d_n)` be a deterministic degree sequence with even total degree

\[
\ell:=\sum_{v=1}^n d_v,
\]

and let `CM(d)` be the configuration-model multigraph. Assume

\[
\nu:=\frac1\ell\sum_v d_v(d_v-1)<1,
\qquad
\sum_v d_v^3\le Cn,
\qquad
\ell\ge cn
\tag{1.1}
\]

for fixed constants `c,C>0`. Set `C_0:=C/c`.

Fix a deterministic marked set `H\subseteq[n]`, let `B=[n]\setminus H`, and define

\[
\delta_H
:=1-\frac1\ell\sum_{v\in B}d_v(d_v-1)>0.
\tag{1.2}
\]

We use the following **actual pairing exploration**. Start from any deterministic collection of `a_0` active half-edges incident to already exposed seed vertices. At each step choose one active half-edge by an arbitrary rule measurable with respect to the exploration history and reveal its uniformly paired partner. If the partner lies at a previously unseen bulk vertex `v\in B`, reveal `v` and activate its other `d_v-1` half-edges. If the partner lies in `H`, stop that branch at the mark. If the partner lies at an already exposed vertex, it creates no new active half-edges. The exploration stops when the active queue is empty.

This includes the absorbing-tail explorations used in the previous P0157 work. Importantly, the theorem below is insensitive to the rule used to choose the next active half-edge.

Let `F_t` be the history immediately before the `(t+1)`st pairing, `A_t` the active-half-edge count, and `tau:=inf{t:A_t=0}`. Let `V_t\subseteq B` be the bulk vertices first discovered during the first `t` pairings, and put

\[
D_t:=\sum_{v\in V_t}d_v(d_v-1).
\tag{1.3}
\]

For `t<tau`, let `Y_t` be the number of **new** active half-edges generated by the partner at step `t`; thus `Y_t=d_v-1` if the partner first discovers a bulk vertex `v`, and `Y_t=0` otherwise.

## 2. Exact residual-drift identity

### Proposition 2.1 — predictable offspring mean

For every `t<tau` with `2t+1<ell`, one has exactly

\[
\boxed{
 m_t:=\mathbb E[Y_t\mid\mathcal F_t]
 =\frac{\ell(1-\delta_H)-D_t}{\ell-2t-1}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

At time `t`, every half-edge of every still-unseen bulk vertex is unpaired. The partner of the chosen active half-edge is uniform over the `ell-2t-1` available half-edges. If the partner belongs to an unseen bulk vertex `v`, there are exactly `d_v` possible partner half-edges and the exploration creates `d_v-1` new active half-edges. Hence

\[
\mathbb E[Y_t\mid\mathcal F_t]
=\frac{1}{\ell-2t-1}
\sum_{v\in B\setminus V_t}d_v(d_v-1).
\]

Using

\[
\sum_{v\in B}d_v(d_v-1)=\ell(1-\delta_H)
\]

and the definition of `D_t` proves (2.1). `square`

The formula already shows why the previous birthday barrier was artificial. Repeated bulk labels and cycle closures are not coupling failures. They simply fail to contribute to the unseen-vertex numerator and are absorbed into the residual state `D_t`.

## 3. The depletion process is only linear in the explored budget

### Proposition 3.1 — expected residual-measure depletion

For every deterministic `b\le ell/4`, extending the exploration by zero increments after `tau`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E D_b\le 2C_0 b.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently, if `b=b_n` satisfies

\[
 b=o(\ell\delta_H),
\tag{3.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le b\wedge\tau}
\frac{|m_t-(1-\delta_H)|}{\delta_H}
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

At step `t<b`, the increment `D_{t+1}-D_t` equals `d_v(d_v-1)` if the partner first discovers an unseen bulk vertex `v`, and is zero otherwise. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[D_{t+1}-D_t\mid\mathcal F_t]
&=\frac{1}{\ell-2t-1}
\sum_{v\in B\setminus V_t}d_v^2(d_v-1)\\
&\le\frac{\sum_v d_v^3}{\ell-2b-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Because `b\le ell/4`, the denominator is at least `ell/2-1`; for large `n`, using `sum d_v^3\le Cn` and `ell\ge cn` gives the uniform bound `2C_0+o(1)`. Absorbing the harmless finite-`n` rounding into `2C_0` gives (3.1).

From (2.1), for every `t\le b\wedge tau`,

\[
|m_t-(1-\delta_H)|
\le
\frac{(2b+1)+D_b}{\ell-2b-1}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The deterministic first term divided by `delta_H` tends to zero under (3.2). By Markov's inequality and (3.1),

\[
\frac{D_b}{\ell\delta_H}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

Substitution into (3.4) proves (3.3). `square`

### Interpretation

The residual process remains subcritical with essentially the **same drift** on the whole window `b=o(ell delta_H)`. This is parametrically larger than the `o(sqrt ell)` window forced by the previous pathwise iid coupling. No collision-free hypothesis is used.

## 4. A component-exploration budget beyond the birthday scale

The upper drift needed for a stopping-time budget is even simpler: because `D_t\ge0`, it does not require the third-moment bound.

### Theorem 4.1 — actual-CM budget for one absorbing exploration

Assume only `delta_H>0`. Let `b` satisfy

\[
2b+1\le \frac{\ell\delta_H}{4}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then, for every adapted choice of the next active half-edge,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\tau\wedge b]\le \frac{2a_0}{\delta_H},
\qquad
\mathbb P(\tau>b)\le\frac{2a_0}{\delta_H b}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, whenever

\[
a_0=o(\ell\delta_H^2),
\tag{4.3}
\]

one may choose a deterministic budget `b_n` with

\[
\frac{a_0}{\delta_H}=o(b_n),
\qquad
b_n=o(\ell\delta_H),
\tag{4.4}
\]

and then

\[
\boxed{\mathbb P(\tau>b_n)\to0.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof

From (2.1) and `D_t\ge0`,

\[
 m_t\le \frac{\ell(1-\delta_H)}{\ell-2t-1}.
\]

Under (4.1), uniformly for `t<b`, the right-hand side is at most `1-\delta_H/2` (the stated constant is deliberately conservative). When the chosen active half-edge is paired, the active queue loses that half-edge; if the partner is another already active half-edge, the queue loses one additional half-edge. Hence always

\[
A_{t+1}-A_t\le -1+Y_t.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore, for `t<tau\wedge b`,

\[
\mathbb E[A_{t+1}-A_t\mid\mathcal F_t]
\le -\delta_H/2.
\]

The stopped process

\[
A_{t\wedge tau\wedge b}+\frac{\delta_H}{2}(t\wedge tau\wedge b)
\]

is a supermartingale. Taking expectations and using nonnegativity of `A` yields

\[
\mathbb E[\tau\wedge b]\le 2a_0/\delta_H.
\]

Since `{tau>b}` implies `tau\wedge b=b`, Markov's inequality gives the second bound in (4.2). The existence of `b_n` satisfying (4.4) is exactly the scale separation (4.3), and then (4.5) follows. `square`

### Why this matters for P0157

Under the finite-third-moment source regime, `d_max=O(n^{1/3})`, `ell=Theta(n)`, and `n delta_n^3 -> infinity`, so

\[
 d_{\max}=o(\ell\delta_n^2).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus a component exploration started from any deterministic single maximal-degree root fits, with high probability, into a budget satisfying `b=o(ell delta_n)`. This does **not** make the statement selection-safe for the largest component: the scalar-matched and staircase examples show that the extremal component can be selected precisely by rare multi-hub coalescence. The value of Theorem 4.1 is narrower: it proves that the finite-population exploration itself is not forced to leave the residual-drift window merely because it exceeds `sqrt ell` pairings.

## 5. Exact online law of newly discovered marks

Let

\[
U_t:=\{h\in H: h\text{ has not yet been hit by time }t\},
\qquad
S_t:=\sum_{h\in U_t}d_h.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proposition 5.1 — exact marked hazard and exact size-biased identity

At every step `t<tau`, conditional on `F_t`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\text{next partner is in }U_t\mid\mathcal F_t)
=\frac{S_t}{\ell-2t-1}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Moreover, conditional on the event that the next partner lies in an unseen marked vertex,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\text{new mark is }h\mid\mathcal F_t,\text{ a new mark is hit})
=\frac{d_h}{S_t},
\qquad h\in U_t.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Until a marked vertex `h` is first hit, none of its `d_h` half-edges has been exposed by this component exploration. Hence all `d_h` are present in the uniform partner pool. Summing over unseen marks gives (5.2), and division by that total gives (5.3). `square`

### Consequence: the label problem is solved exactly; the clock problem remains

Equation (5.3) says that **given that a new tail vertex is discovered, its identity is already the exact degree-size-biased law** required by a rank-one exploration. No asymptotic independence argument is needed for the label choice.

Equation (5.2) shows that the only nontrivial ingredient left for a component-level rank-one comparison is the random number of pairing opportunities generated between successive marked discoveries. If a currently discovered marked mass `W` generated

\[
(1+o_p(1))\frac{W}{\delta_H}
\]

pairing opportunities in a selection-safe way, then the integrated hazard for an unseen mark `h` would be on the scale

\[
\frac{W d_h}{\ell\delta_H},
\]

which is exactly the rank-one kernel already identified by the earlier one-root and Steiner calculations. The difficulty is that the extremal marked component is selected by rare coalescence, so an unconditional preselected-root law is insufficient.

## 7. Exact remaining obstruction

The previous recovery asked for a component-level online rank-one comparison. This note proves two major pieces of that architecture:

1. the residual offspring drift is stable throughout `b=o(ell delta_H)`;
2. each newly discovered mark has the exact degree-size-biased identity law.

The remaining obstruction is now sharply isolated as a **selection-safe clock theorem**. In one useful form:

> Conditional on the evolving marked skeleton of a rare component that can compete for the maximum, show that the total number of bulk pairing opportunities generated by each discovered marked mass is asymptotic to that mass divided by `delta_H`, uniformly over the skeleton sizes relevant to the extreme component.

Equivalently, one needs a probability-ratio/contiguity statement for the **clock increments**, not for every individual pairing. The scalar-matched family shows why the conditioning cannot be ignored: a preselected hub is typically ordinary even when the largest component is created by an atypical cluster of many hubs.

A fresh worker should therefore start from the exact online representation above and attack the clock under rare-event conditioning (for example by a Doob transform, spine construction, or a skeleton-first switching argument), rather than rebuilding the one-root iid coupling.
