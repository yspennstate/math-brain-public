# R06 / I0056 — idea before archival checker: optimize one exposed frontier instead of taking a whole ball

Source: Russell Lyons and Yuval Peres, *Probability on Trees and Networks*, Question 6.20 / intake I0056.

The frozen H_{5,5} metric is already width zero. This note concerns the still-open source-valid mixed-face graph T=r{4,5} and does not redefine the fixed metric.

The preceding run proves the all-radius frontier anatomy for graph-distance spheres in T. Every v in S_n has exactly one of three (arrival, fraternal, departure) types

A=(1,0,3),  B=(1,1,2),  C=(2,0,2).

A whole metric ball is therefore an unnecessarily symmetric test set. At a fixed exposed sphere S_n, let W be a chosen subset of frontier vertices and set K=B_{n-1} union W. Then every unchosen S_n vertex remains in the outer boundary (it has a parent in B_{n-1}), while the only boundary created beyond S_n is the set N^+(W) of departure neighbours of W in S_{n+1}. Hence exactly

|K|=|B_{n-1}|+|W|,
|partial_V K|=|S_n|-|W|+|N^+(W)|.

This turns one step of the infinite isoperimetric problem into a finite bipartite neighbourhood optimization on S_n -> S_{n+1}.

Method #5 (add a parameter): treat W as the parameter in the exact rational objective

R_n(W)=(|S_n|-|W|+|N^+(W)|)/(|B_{n-1}|+|W|).

Method #8 (discretize): test the three globally proved frontier types A,B,C separately and in unions before attempting a full minimum-cut/closure problem.

Method #26 (hunt the sporadic case): the shell recurrence says round balls have an asymptotic ratio worse than the current finite witness, so look for a finite-radius frontier type whose child-overlap makes a genuinely better non-round set.

The archival checker should re-use the exact Coxeter development over Z[sqrt(2),phi], independently recompute S_17/S_18 and the A/B/C types, form W={type-B vertices in S_17}, compute its exact departure-neighbour union, and verify the resulting rational boundary ratio by integer arithmetic. It should also compare the seven nonempty unions of A,B,C so that selecting B is not merely an unexplained choice among the coarse orbit types.
