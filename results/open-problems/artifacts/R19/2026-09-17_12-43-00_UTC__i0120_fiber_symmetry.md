## 1. Problem and reason for changing the algorithm

The active source case is `LRC(14)`: for every nonzero integer tuple
`u=(u_1,...,u_14)`, find a real time `t` such that

\[
\|t u_i\|\ge \frac1{15},\qquad 1\le i\le14.
\]

The previous run proved that if a primitive counterexample satisfies a fixed
short Beck--Everett relation, then the prime-gate lift search may remain on
that exact relation hyperplane at every level.  A primitive relation removes
one raw lift dimension: a multiplier-`c` fiber has `c^13`, not `c^14`,
relation-compatible lifts.

The next question is whether the remaining `c^13` branches have exact
symmetries that can be quotiented *before* witness testing.  They do.  There
are two independent sources:

1. permutations among unmarked coordinates having the same current residue;
2. units modulo the lifted modulus that are congruent to `1` modulo the
   current modulus, hence act inside one projection fiber.

Both preserve the relation and both clauses of properness.  The resulting
quotient is exact, not a heuristic pruning rule.

Throughout Sections 2--5, write

\[
Q=lp
\]

for the current modulus, where `p` is the prime gate and `l` is the current
lift level.  The most important support-two relation is written

\[
w_2\equiv a w_1\pmod Q,
\]

with `a=4` for the hardest pilot ratio found at `p=37,41,43`.  Since all
Beck--Everett support-two coefficients are at most `31`, every prime gate
`p>31` is coprime to `a`.

## 2. Method #14: the stabilizer group of a relation-marked seed

Mark coordinates `1,2` as the two coordinates in the relation.  Among the
remaining twelve coordinates, partition indices according to equality of
their residues modulo `Q`.  Let the multiplicities be

\[
m_1,\ldots,m_s,\qquad m_1+\cdots+m_s=12.
\]

The group

\[
G_w=S_{m_1}\times\cdots\times S_{m_s}
\]

permutes only unmarked coordinates inside equal-residue blocks.

### Theorem R19-LR6 — exact stabilizer quotient for a support-two relative lift

Fix an integer multiplier `c>=1`.  Among all lifts modulo `cQ` that project
to `w` modulo `Q` and still satisfy

\[
w'_2\equiv a w'_1\pmod{cQ},
\]

the number of `G_w`-orbits is exactly

\[
\boxed{
  c\prod_{j=1}^s {m_j+c-1\choose c-1}.
}
\]

The raw relation-compatible fiber has size `c^13`.  It is therefore enough
to test one representative of each of the displayed orbits for witness
properness and gcd properness.

#### Proof

Write each lift uniquely as

\[
w'_i=w_i+Qq_i,\qquad q_i\in\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z.
\]

Since `w_2-a w_1` is divisible by `Q`, write

\[
w_2-a w_1=Qh.
\]

The lifted relation is equivalent to

\[
h+q_2-aq_1\equiv0\pmod c,
\]

so `q_1` is arbitrary and then

\[
q_2\equiv aq_1-h\pmod c
\]

is forced.  Thus the marked pair contributes exactly `c` possibilities.
The twelve unmarked lift digits are otherwise free.

Inside a block of `m_j` equal current residues, the permutation group
`S_{m_j}` acts on the `m_j` lift digits.  Its orbit is determined exactly by
the histogram

\[
(h_{j,0},\ldots,h_{j,c-1}),\qquad
h_{j,r}\ge0,\quad \sum_r h_{j,r}=m_j.
\]

There are

\[
{m_j+c-1\choose c-1}
\]

such weak compositions.  Different current residue blocks can never merge
at modulus `cQ`, because equality modulo `cQ` would imply equality modulo
`Q`.  Hence the block orbit counts multiply, and the marked factor `c`
gives the formula.

It remains only to justify quotienting.  Both source properness clauses are
symmetric under coordinate permutations.  The relation involves only the
two marked coordinates, so `G_w` also preserves relation compatibility.
Therefore all members of one `G_w`-orbit have the same proper/improper
status.  Testing one representative per orbit is complete.  QED.

### Corollary R19-LR6a — exact generating function by support size

Suppose a level-one support contains the two marked residue classes and has
size `r`.  The other `r-2` support classes must occur at least once among the
twelve unmarked coordinates, while the two marked residue classes may occur
zero or more additional times among those twelve coordinates.  Summing the
orbit count in R19-LR6 over every admissible multiplicity pattern gives the
exact aggregate number of canonical multiplier-`c` branches per such
support:

\[
\boxed{
A_{r,c}
=c\,[z^{12}]
(1-z)^{-2c}\Big((1-z)^{-c}-1\Big)^{r-2}.
}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\sum_{m\ge0}{m+c-1\choose c-1}z^m=(1-z)^{-c},
\]

while a support class required to occur uses the same series with the
constant term removed.  This formula can be used directly by an exact
branch-budget estimator without enumerating multiplicity vectors.

## 3. Method #5: add the lift multiplier as a real parameter and differentiate

The stabilizer quotient is most useful when repeated residues occur.  This
can be quantified exactly, rather than guessed from small computations.

For a fixed unmarked multiplicity vector
`m=(m_1,...,m_s)` with total `12`, define for integer `c>=1`

\[
O_m(c)=c\prod_j {m_j+c-1\choose m_j}
\]

(the orbit count from R19-LR6) and

\[
R_m(c)=\frac{c^{13}}{O_m(c)}
=\frac{c^{12}}{\prod_j {m_j+c-1\choose m_j}}
\]

(the exact raw-to-canonical compression factor).
The binomial coefficient has the polynomial continuation

\[
{m+c-1\choose m}
=\frac{c(c+1)\cdots(c+m-1)}{m!},\qquad c>0.
\]

### Theorem R19-LR7 — stabilizer compression is monotone in the lift multiplier

For real `c>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dc}\log R_m(c)
=rac{12}{c}
-\sum_{j=1}^s\sum_{q=0}^{m_j-1}\frac1{c+q}
\ge0.
}
\]

The inequality is strict for every `c>0` iff at least one unmarked residue
is repeated (`m_j>=2` for some `j`).  Moreover

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{c\to\infty}R_m(c)=\prod_j m_j!.
}
\]

#### Proof

Taking logarithms of the displayed product continuation gives

\[
\log R_m(c)
=12\log c+\sum_j\log(m_j!)
-\sum_j\sum_{q=0}^{m_j-1}\log(c+q).
\]

Differentiation yields the claimed identity.  There are exactly
`sum_j m_j=12` reciprocal terms in the double sum, and every one is at most
`1/c`.  Therefore the derivative is nonnegative.  Equality can hold only
when every reciprocal term equals `1/c`, which means every occurring `q`
is zero, equivalently every `m_j=1`.  If some `m_j>=2`, a term with `q>=1`
is strictly smaller than `1/c`, giving strict positivity.

Finally,

\[
{m_j+c-1\choose m_j}
=\frac{c^{m_j}}{m_j!}(1+O(c^{-1})),
\]

and the exponents sum to twelve, so the ratio tends to
`prod_j m_j!`.  QED.

### Interpretation

The support-two ratio `a` does **not** enter the branch-count formula at all.
Thus the previous empirical distinction between the easy `(2,1)` ratio and
the hard `(4,1)` ratio is caused by witness-cover geometry, not by the
number of relation-compatible lifts.  The algebraic branching cost is
universal over relations of the form `u_2=a u_1`.

The theorem also says that larger lift multipliers make duplicate-residue
stabilizers *more*, not less, valuable.  For all-distinct unmarked residues
`R_m(c)=1`; there is no hidden gain to claim.  This cleanly identifies the
case where a different symmetry is needed.

## 4. A second group acting: units that fix the projection fiber

The current public finite-checking framework uses global multiplication by a
unit as an equivalence at prime level.  There is a smaller unit group that
acts **inside a single lift fiber**, and it can be used at every later stage.

For a prime multiplier `r`, put `N=rQ` and define

\[
K_{Q,r}
=\{b\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times:
 b\equiv1\pmod Q\}.
\]

Multiplication by `b in K_{Q,r}` leaves every lifted tuple unchanged after
projection modulo `Q`.

### Lemma R19-LR8 — fiber-kernel units preserve the exact search predicate

The group `K_{Q,r}` preserves:

1. projection to the current seed modulo `Q`;
2. every homogeneous integer relation `m.w'=0 mod N`;
3. the witness-properness clause;
4. the subset-gcd properness clause.

Hence it acts on the relation-compatible improper lift fiber.

#### Proof

Projection is immediate from `b=1 mod Q`.  Homogeneity gives
`m.(b w')=b(m.w')=0 mod N`.

For witness properness, multiplication by a unit permutes the time residues:
if `t=s/N` witnesses `w'`, then `t'=b^{-1}s/N` witnesses `b w'`, and
conversely.  Thus the union-of-bad-times predicate is invariant.

For the gcd clause, `b` is coprime to `N` and therefore to the lift level.
Multiplication by `b` cannot change any gcd of the lift level with a subset
of the speed coordinates.  QED.

### Theorem R19-LR9 — exact gauge reduction for prime lifts

Let `r` be prime.

**First occurrence (`r` does not divide `Q`).**  Fix one coordinate `j`.
As its lift digit `q_j` ranges over `F_r`, the lifted coordinate
`w_j+Q q_j` ranges bijectively over `F_r` modulo `r`.  The group
`K_{Q,r}` is naturally `F_r^*` and has exactly two orbits on these `r`
possibilities:

- the unique lift divisible by `r`;
- all `r-1` lifts not divisible by `r`.

Therefore, after applying the simultaneous unit action to the whole tuple,
a complete search needs only **two gauge cases** for that coordinate rather
than `r` digit cases.

**Repeated occurrence (`r` divides `Q`).**  Suppose the chosen current
coordinate satisfies `r` not dividing `w_j`.  Then the `r` units

\[
b_s=1+Qs,\qquad s\in\mathbb F_r,
\]

belong to `K_{Q,r}` and act transitively on the `r` lift digits of coordinate
`j`.  Every orbit has a unique representative with `q_j=0`.  Thus the
fiber-kernel action removes an exact factor `r` from the search.

Finally, if the current seed is improper at a level whose lift factor is
divisible by `r`, then some coordinate with `r` not dividing `w_j` always
exists.  In fact the subset-gcd clause would already declare the seed proper
if all but at most one coordinates were divisible by `r`.  Hence every
improper seed has at least two nondivisible coordinates, so the repeated
prime gauge can always be applied.

#### Proof

If `r` does not divide `Q`, the Chinese remainder theorem identifies the
kernel of reduction from units modulo `rQ` to units modulo `Q` with
`F_r^*`.  Modulo `r`, it acts on the lifted coordinate by multiplication.
The multiplicative group has exactly the two stated orbits on `F_r`: zero
and the nonzero elements.

Now assume `r|Q`.  Each `b_s=1+Qs` is a unit modulo `rQ`, is `1 mod Q`, and
the `r` choices are distinct.  Writing a lift as `w_j+Qq_j`, multiplication
by `b_s` changes its digit modulo `r` to

\[
q_j+s w_j,
\]

because the remaining term contains a factor `Q` and vanishes modulo `r`.
Since `w_j` is nonzero modulo `r`, translation by `s w_j` is transitive and
free on `F_r`.  Choosing `s=-q_j w_j^{-1}` gives the unique representative
with digit zero.

For the last assertion, if at most one coordinate were nonzero modulo `r`,
remove that coordinate (or any coordinate if none is nonzero).  Every
remaining coordinate would share the divisor `r` with the lift level, which
is exactly the subset-gcd properness clause.  Contraposition gives the
claim.  QED.

## 5. Consequence for the current record-style lift pipeline

The reductions stack without changing the mathematical gate.

- The exact Beck--Everett relation first changes a raw multiplier-`c` fiber
  from `c^14` to `c^13`.
- Equal-residue stabilizers then replace the relation-compatible fiber by the
  R19-LR6 orbit representatives.
- On a **first** prime-`r` lift, fiber-kernel units reduce one chosen digit
  from `r` possibilities to two orbit types (divisible / nondivisible).
- On every **repeated** prime-`r` lift of an improper seed, fiber-kernel units
  remove a full factor `r` by fixing one nondivisible coordinate's lift digit
  to zero.

In particular, in a repeated binary squeeze every stage after the first
binary occurrence gets an additional exact factor-two quotient on top of
the relation's factor-two reduction.  At a first terminal `r=7` stage, the
marked/selected coordinate has only two unit-orbit types rather than seven
raw digit cases.  This is a structural reduction independent of whether the
specific `(4,1)` gate ultimately closes.

## 6. Method #19: exact relative-orbit lift algorithm

A sound implementation can use the following state and transition.

**State.**  Keep the two relation-marked coordinates distinguished.  Keep
all other current residues grouped by exact equality modulo `Q`, with their
multiplicities.  Retain only improper relation-compatible states.

**Transition by multiplier `c`.**

1. Choose the marked digit `q_1`; compute the forced `q_2` from
   `q_2=a q_1-h mod c`.
2. For each unmarked equality block of size `m`, enumerate weak histograms
   `(h_0,...,h_{c-1})` rather than `c^m` labeled digit assignments.
3. If `c` is prime, quotient by `K_{Q,c}` using R19-LR9.  At a repeated prime
   choose a deterministic nondivisible coordinate and gauge its digit to
   zero; at a first prime retain the two divisible/nondivisible gauge types.
4. Construct one tuple for each resulting orbit representative.
5. Apply the exact witness-cover test and subset-gcd test; discard proper
   states.
6. For a further lift, regroup unmarked coordinates by their **new exact
   residues modulo `cQ`**.  Do not keep an old stabilizer after a block has
   split.

### Correctness

Every operation above is a quotient by an explicitly proved group action
that preserves projection, the exact relation, and properness.  Conversely,
R19-LR6 enumerates every orbit of equal-residue permutations, and R19-LR9
supplies representatives for the fiber-kernel unit orbits.  Therefore every
raw relation-compatible improper branch has a representative in the
algorithm, while no discarded orbit can contain a counterexample branch.
The algorithm decides exactly the same relative eventual-properness gate as
the unquotiented `c^13` search.
