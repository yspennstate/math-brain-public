## Exact outside source and scope

The target is the classical Lonely Runner Conjecture in the stationary-runner integer-speed convention:

> `LRC(k)`: for every nonzero integer speed tuple `(v_1,...,v_k)`, there exists real `t` such that `||t v_i|| >= 1/(k+1)` for every `i`.

This is the usual `k+1`-runner conjecture after one runner is made stationary. The original lineage is Wills (1967) / Cusick; current primary sources checked here are:

- Touch Sungkawichai and Tanupat Trakulthongchai, *Eleven, twelve, and thirteen lonely runners*, arXiv:2604.23906v2 (2026-09-01).
- Jaan Allikvere, *Fourteen lonely runners*, arXiv:2609.02604v1 (2026-09-02).
- Matthias Beck and Samuel Everett, *Lonely Runner Relations*, arXiv:2609.06259v1 (2026-09-05).

Allikvere proves `LRC(13)`, i.e. the full classical conjecture for **14 total runners**. Thus the queue text saying the largest verified case was 7 and that 8 was open is stale and must not be used as a baseline.

The admitted next finite source case is therefore

`LRC(14)` = **15 total runners**, with exact threshold `1/15`, for the full class of integer speed tuples in the standard convention.

A restricted congruence-family result for fifteen runners was also found in a September 2026 AI-paper venue; it explicitly says it is not the general fifteen-runner case and earns no source-case credit here.
