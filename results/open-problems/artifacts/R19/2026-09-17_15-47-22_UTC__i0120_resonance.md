## 2. Method #12 — the conserved object is a projective denominator-111 class

The useful invariant is not a coordinate residue. It is the entire blocking configuration modulo `111`, modulo multiplication by units.

### R19-LR8 — coprime projective persistence

Let `c>=1` satisfy

\[
\gcd(c,111)=1.
\]

Let `c^{-1}` denote the inverse of `c` modulo `111`, and define coordinatewise

\[
r^{(c)}\equiv c^{-1}r_0\pmod{111},
\qquad 0\le r^{(c)}_j<111,
\]
then

\[
Y^{(c)}=2c\,r^{(c)}.
\]

Then:

1. `Y^(c)` is a lift of `Y_0` from modulus `222` to modulus `222c`;
2. it preserves the relation
   \[
   Y^{(c)}_2\equiv2Y^{(c)}_1\pmod{222c};
   \]
3. it has no threshold-`1/15` grid witness at modulus `222c`.

#### Proof

Since `c r^(c) = r_0 (mod 111)`,

\[
Y^{(c)}-Y_0=2(c r^{(c)}-r_0)
\]
is coordinatewise divisible by `222`. This is (1).

Equation (1.2) is preserved by multiplication by the unit `c^{-1}` modulo `111`, so

\[
r^{(c)}_2-2r^{(c)}_1=111m
\]
for some integer `m`. Multiplication by `2c` gives

\[
Y^{(c)}_2-2Y^{(c)}_1=222cm,
\]
which is (2).

Finally, at numerator `a`,

\[
\left\|\frac{aY^{(c)}_j}{222c}\right\|
=
\left\|\frac{a r^{(c)}_j}{111}\right\|.
\]
As `a` ranges modulo `111`, multiplication by `c^{-1}` simply permutes the numerator classes in (1.1). Thus their common good set remains empty. This proves (3). \(\square\)

### Full `e_5=2` lift

The core theorem already forces a no-witness descendant of the twelve-coordinate core. To lift the full fourteen-coordinate boundary state, put the first twelve coordinates equal to `5Y^(c)`. Because

\[
5Y^{(c)}-5Y_0=10(c r^{(c)}-r_0)
\]
is divisible by `1110`, this is a lift of the first twelve coordinates of `T` to modulus `1110c`.

For each of the two exceptional coordinates choose

\[
z_j^{(c)}\equiv z_j\pmod{1110},
\qquad (z_1,z_2)=(1,557),
\]
with

\[
\gcd(z_j^{(c)},1110c)=1.
\]
Such a choice always exists: for every prime divisor of `c` not already dividing `1110`, the arithmetic progression `z_j+1110k` has exactly one forbidden residue of `k` modulo that prime; choose an allowed residue for each prime and combine them by the Chinese remainder theorem. Primes already dividing `1110` are harmless because the base residues are units modulo `1110`.

The twelve-coordinate core still blocks every numerator, while the two exceptional coordinates are units for every prime dividing the new level `30c`; therefore the gcd-proper clause also fails after every single-coordinate deletion. Hence there is an improper relation-compatible descendant at every multiplier `c` coprime to `111`.

### Consequence

For this seed, **no terminal multiplier coprime to `111=3*37` can close the descendant tree**. In particular, arbitrary products of `2`, `5`, `7`, and any other primes different from `3` and `37` cannot eliminate this projective obstruction. Any successful terminal step on this branch must eventually use a multiplier sharing a prime factor with `111`, namely `3` or `37`.

This is the conserved quantity sought by method #12.

## 4. Method #25 — count the five phase completions

The next lemma converts a core witness into full witnesses on an `e_5=2` face.

### R19-LR9 — five-phase completion lemma

Let `M` be a positive integer and let

\[
y=(y_1,\ldots,y_{12})
\]
have a threshold-`1/15` grid witness at numerator `a` modulo `M`:

\[
\left\|\frac{a y_j}{M}\right\|\ge\frac1{15}
\quad(1\le j\le12).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let `z_1,z_2` be nonzero modulo `5`, and form the fourteen-coordinate state

\[
v=(5y_1,\ldots,5y_{12},z_1,z_2)
\]
at modulus `5M`.

Then at least three of the five numerators

\[
A_s=a+sM,\qquad s=0,1,2,3,4,
\]
are full threshold-`1/15` witnesses for `v`.

#### Proof

For a core coordinate,

\[
\frac{A_s(5y_j)}{5M}
=
\frac{a y_j}{M}+s y_j,
\]
so its phase modulo one is independent of `s`; all twelve core inequalities (4.1) therefore survive for every `s`.

For an exceptional coordinate `z`, its five phases differ by

\[
\frac{z}{5}
\]
modulo one. Since `z` is nonzero modulo `5`, these are exactly five equally spaced points on the circle, with spacing `1/5`. The bad arc

\[
\{x:\|x\|<1/15\}
\]
has total length `2/15<1/5`, so it contains at most one of those five points. Each exceptional coordinate can therefore exclude at most one value of `s`. Two exceptional coordinates exclude at most two values, leaving at least three full witnesses. \(\square\)

### Contrapositive

On an `e_5=2` boundary state of this form, full no-witness behavior forces the divided twelve-coordinate core itself to have no witness. Thus the obstruction is controlled entirely by the core, and factor-5 padding cannot hide a core witness.

## 5. Resonance: factor `3` kills the complete relation-preserving fiber

R19-LR8 says that multipliers coprime to `111` cannot terminate the branch. The first genuinely different experiment is therefore a multiplier sharing the denominator, namely `3`.

Lift `Y_0` from modulus `222` to modulus

\[

\]
A lift is

\[

\qquad b_j\in\{0,1,2\}.

The relation

\[

\[
(b_1,b_2)\in\{(0,1),(1,0),(2,2)\},

\[

relation-preserving factor-3 core lifts.

### The binary orbit is killed by the same resonant prime

For the binary projective state `Y_n`, divide a factor-3 lift by `2^{n+1}`. This identifies its normalized core with a lift of `r_n` modulo `333`. Multiplication by the unit `2^n` modulo `333` is a bijection from these relation-preserving lifts to the `n=0` relation-preserving lifts, and it merely permutes numerator classes. Thus the same minimum witness count `24` applies to every member of the 36-cycle. R19-LR9 again gives at least `72` full witnesses after the factor-3 step.

So arbitrary binary persistence does **not** imply eventual non-properness: the whole canonical binary projective orbit is destroyed in one resonant factor-3 lift.

## 6. Method #13 — no scalar monovariant, but an arithmetic termination principle

I looked for a scalar quantity that increases under every lift. The existing branch data rule out the most obvious candidate: raw improper descendant counts are not monotone in the needed direction (earlier binary levels can branch from one survivor to several). Divisibility defects `e_q` also do not by themselves force monotonicity under a change of lift prime.

The useful replacement is arithmetic rather than scalar:

- multipliers **coprime** to the core denominator preserve a projective blocking orbit;
- a multiplier sharing a prime with that denominator can change the projective denominator and may terminate the orbit;
- for this exact seed the first resonant prime `3` does terminate the complete relation-preserving fiber.

This is not a universal theorem for all harmful relation shapes. It is the precise mechanism exposed by this seed.
