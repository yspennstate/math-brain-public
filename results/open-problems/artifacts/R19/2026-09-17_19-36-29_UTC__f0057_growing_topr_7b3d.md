## 1. Source inputs, isolated

Work after Janson's equivalent exponential tilt, so the random allocation satisfies

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)\overset d=
(Z_{n,1},\ldots,Z_{n,n})\mid\{S_n=m\},
\qquad S_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n Z_{n,i},
\tag{1.1}
\]

where the `Z_{n,i}` are iid with law `p_n`.

There are two source regimes.

1. **Fixed-law regime.** `p_n=p=L(\xi)`, `m=\lambda n+o(\sqrt n)`, `0<\lambda\le\nu`, and `\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}\xi<\infty`.
2. **Tuned regime.** In the `\xi^{(n)}` version, `p_n=L(\xi^{(n)})`, with `E Z_{n,1}=m/n`, `p_n\Rightarrow p`, common lattice span, and the source's uniform exponential-tail/local-CLT control.

The proof of Theorem 18.7 supplies a deterministic cutoff `U_n` such that

\[
U_n=o(\sqrt n),\qquad
\Pr(Y_{(1)}>U_n)=o(1),\qquad
n\Pr(Z_{n,1}>U_n)=o(1).
\tag{1.2}
\]

In the tuned regime Janson in fact takes `U_n=O(\log n)`.

The same proof, using Lemma 13.1 and Remark 13.2, yields for each fixed `q`, uniformly over supported `k_1,\ldots,k_q\le U_n`,

\[
\Pr(Y_1=k_1,\ldots,Y_q=k_q)
=(1+o(1))\prod_{a=1}^q p_n(k_a).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Only `q=1,2` will be needed below.

Remark 13.2 is crucial: the triangular-array local CLT continues to hold under uniform square integrability, and the centering may differ from the target integer by `o(\sqrt n)`. Thus the direct-conditioning argument below does not introduce a third-moment assumption.

## 2. The theorem

Let `r_n` be positive integers such that

\[
r_n\to\infty,
\qquad
r_n U_n=o(\sqrt n).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Write

\[
\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}=(Y_{(1)},\ldots,Y_{(r_n)}),
\qquad
\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)}=(Z_{n,(1)},\ldots,Z_{n,(r_n)}),
\tag{2.2}
\]

where the second vector contains the top `r_n` order statistics of the iid reference sample.

### Theorem G

Under the exact source assumptions of Theorem 18.7, for every sequence `r_n` satisfying (2.1),

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}\!\left(
 \mathcal L(\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}),
 \mathcal L(\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)})
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The same conclusion holds in the source's tuned `\xi^{(n)}` version, with `\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)}` formed from iid `\xi^{(n)}` variables. Since there `U_n=O(\log n)`, in particular one may take any

\[
r_n=o\!\left(\frac{\sqrt n}{\log n}\right).
\tag{2.4}
\]

For the fixed-law version, (2.3) at least gives a deterministic diverging `r_n` because `U_n=o(\sqrt n)`.

## 3. Why the previous proof appeared fixed-dimensional

The preceding direct-conditioning proof used a rare cloud of bounded expected size `D`, then sent `D\to\infty` only *after* `n\to\infty`. That is enough for every fixed number of top ranks, but it does not visibly control a growing vector.

The exact obstruction to letting the cloud grow is not the number of cloud configurations. It is the shift in the bulk sum after the cloud is revealed. A cloud of `q` values, each at most `U_n`, can move the residual target by order `qU_n`. The local CLT remains in its central window precisely while

\[
qU_n=o(\sqrt n).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This identifies the correct scale on which the old algorithm can be pushed.

## 4. Insert a slowly growing cloud intensity

Set

\[
A_n:=\frac{\sqrt n}{U_n}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

By (1.2), `A_n\to\infty`; by (2.1), `r_n=o(A_n)`. Choose any deterministic integer sequence `D_n` satisfying

\[
r_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
D_n=o(A_n).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For example, after harmless integer rounding,

\[
D_n=\sqrt{r_nA_n}
\tag{4.3}
\]

works. Thus

\[
D_n\to\infty,
\qquad
D_nU_n=o(\sqrt n).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Define the threshold

\[
h_n:=\min\{h:\ n\Pr(Z_{n,1}\ge h)\le D_n\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The cutoff property `nP(Z_{n,1}>U_n)=o(1)` implies `h_n\le U_n+1` for all large `n`; moreover `h_n\to\infty`, since `D_n/n\to0` and the limiting source law has unbounded support (`\omega=\infty`).

Let

\[
T_n:=\{h_n,h_n+1,\ldots,U_n\},
\qquad
a_n:=p_n(T_n).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Then

\[
na_n\le D_n.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Let `\eta_n` be the point cloud of iid observations that fall in `T_n`, with multiplicity.

## 5. Growing-cloud likelihood-ratio lemma

Let

\[
p_n^c(k):=\frac{p_n(k)\mathbf 1_{\{k\notin T_n\}}}{1-a_n}
\tag{5.1}
\]

be the complement law and write `\mu_n=E_{p_n}Z`, `\mu_n^c=E_{p_n^c}Z`.

Because `a_n\le D_n/n` and every removed value is at most `U_n`,

\[
|\mu_n^c-\mu_n|
\le \frac{a_n(|\mu_n|+U_n)}{1-a_n}
=O\!\left(\frac{D_nU_n}{n}\right),
\tag{5.2}
\]

so

\[
n|\mu_n^c-\mu_n|=o(\sqrt n).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Similarly, using the uniformly bounded second moments supplied by the source regime,

\[
\left|E_{p_n^c}Z^2-E_{p_n}Z^2\right|
=O\!\left(\frac{D_nU_n^2}{n}\right)+o(1)=o(1),
\tag{5.4}
\]

because

\[
\frac{D_nU_n^2}{n}
=\left(\frac{D_nU_n}{\sqrt n}\right)
 \left(\frac{U_n}{\sqrt n}\right)\to0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The laws `p_n^c` converge to the same nondegenerate limit law as `p_n` and are uniformly square-integrable. In the fixed-law case this follows by deleting and renormalizing a tail set; in the tuned case it follows from the source's uniform exponential-tail bound.

The lattice span is eventually unchanged. Since `w_0>0`, choose finitely many fixed support points whose gcd is the source span `d`. Since `h_n\to\infty`, none is deleted for large `n`; hence `p_n^c` has the same span `d`.

### Lemma 5.1 — growing-cloud uniform likelihood ratio

Let

\[
K_n:=\lfloor 2D_n\rfloor.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\nu:\,|\nu|\le K_n}
\left|
\frac{\Pr(S_n=m\mid\eta_n=\nu)}{\Pr(S_n=m)}-1
\right|\to0.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

#### Proof

If a cloud configuration `\nu` contains `q` points with total value `s(\nu)`, then, conditionally on that exact cloud, the remaining `n-q` coordinates are iid with law `p_n^c`. Therefore

\[
\Pr(S_n=m\mid\eta_n=\nu)
=\Pr(S_{n-q}^c=m-s(\nu)).
\tag{5.8}
\]

For `q\le K_n`,

\[
s(\nu)\le qU_n\le 2D_nU_n=o(\sqrt n).
\tag{5.9}
\]

Also `q=o(n)`. The deviation of the numerator target from its complement-law mean is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(m-s(\nu))-(n-q)\mu_n^c\\
&=(m-n\mu_n)+(q\mu_n-s(\nu))
 +(n-q)(\mu_n-\mu_n^c).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.10}
\]

The first term is `o(\sqrt n)` in the fixed-law source regime and is zero in the tuned centered regime. The second is `O(D_nU_n)=o(\sqrt n)` uniformly in all allowed configurations, and the third is `o(\sqrt n)` by (5.3). Thus

\[
(m-s(\nu))-(n-q)\mu_n^c=o(\sqrt n)
\tag{5.11}
\]

uniformly for `|\nu|\le K_n`.

Apply Janson's Lemma 13.1 and Remark 13.2 to the triangular array with row law `p_n^c` and row length `n-q`. Equations (5.4), uniform square integrability, common span, and (5.11) give

\[
\Pr(S_{n-q}^c=m-s(\nu))
=\frac{d+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2 n}}
\tag{5.12}
\]

uniformly over all such configurations. The source denominator has the same asymptotic.

For completeness, the uniformity in (5.12) does not require a new quantitative local-CLT theorem. If it failed, choose a violating sequence `q_n,\nu_n`. Along that single sequence, `q_n=o(n)`, the row laws `p_n^c` satisfy the same convergence/UI/span hypotheses, and the target deviation is `o(\sqrt n)` by (5.11); Remark 13.2 then forces the ratio to one, a contradiction. Dividing by the denominator proves (5.7). ∎

## 6. Total variation of the entire growing cloud

Bayes' formula and Lemma 5.1 give

\[
\sup_{|\nu|\le K_n}
\left|
\frac{\Pr(\eta_n=\nu\mid S_n=m)}{\Pr(\eta_n=\nu)}-1
\right|\to0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Under the iid law, `N_n:=|\eta_n|` is binomial with mean at most `D_n`. Hence a Chernoff bound gives

\[
\Pr(N_n>K_n)\to0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let `P_n` and `Q_n` denote respectively the iid and conditioned laws of `\eta_n`, and let `A_n=\{|\eta_n|\le K_n\}`. If the supremum in (6.1) is `\varepsilon_n`, then

\[
|Q_n(A_n)-P_n(A_n)|\le\varepsilon_n,
\tag{6.3}
\]

so `Q_n(A_n^c)\le P_n(A_n^c)+\varepsilon_n=o(1)`. On `A_n`, the total variation contribution is at most `\varepsilon_n/2`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}\bigl(
 \mathcal L(\eta_n\mid S_n=m),
 \mathcal L(\eta_n)
 \bigr)\to0.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is the key new strengthening of the previous fixed-`D` cloud lemma: the expected cloud size itself tends to infinity, subject only to the central-window constraint `D_nU_n=o(\sqrt n)`.

## 7. There are enough values at the boundary

By minimality in (4.5),

\[
n\Pr(Z_{n,1}\ge h_n-1)>D_n.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let

\[
C_n^Z:=\#\{i:h_n-1\le Z_{n,i}\le U_n\},
\qquad
\Lambda_n:=E C_n^Z.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since `nP(Z_{n,1}>U_n)=o(1)`, (7.1) yields

\[
\Lambda_n>D_n-o(1)\to\infty.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Because `r_n=o(D_n)`,

\[
\Pr(C_n^Z<r_n)\to0
\tag{7.4}
\]

by a standard binomial lower-tail bound.

Now define the analogous conditioned count

\[
C_n^Y:=\#\{i:h_n-1\le Y_i\le U_n\}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The source's one-coordinate uniform ratio (1.3) gives

\[
E C_n^Y=(1+o(1))\Lambda_n,
\tag{7.6}
\]

while the two-coordinate ratio gives

\[
E(C_n^Y)_2=(1+o(1))\Lambda_n^2.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Var}(C_n^Y)
=o(\Lambda_n^2)+O(\Lambda_n),
\tag{7.8}
\]

and hence

\[
\frac{C_n^Y}{\Lambda_n}\overset p\longrightarrow1.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Since `r_n/\Lambda_n\to0`, this gives

\[
\Pr(C_n^Y<r_n)\to0.
\tag{7.10}
\]

This step is why only the already-source-proved `q=1,2` fixed-coordinate local ratios are needed; no growing-order factorial-moment theorem is being assumed.

## 8. Recover the first `r_n` ranks from the cloud

Define a deterministic map `\Phi_n` on finite multisets supported in `T_n`: list the atoms in decreasing order with multiplicity and, if fewer than `r_n` atoms are present, pad the missing positions with the single boundary value `h_n-1`.

On the event

\[
G_n^Y:=\{Y_{(1)}\le U_n,\ C_n^Y\ge r_n\},
\tag{8.1}
\]

we have exactly

\[
\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}=\Phi_n(\eta_n^Y).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Indeed, every value between `h_n-1` and `U_n` that is not in the cloud `T_n` equals exactly `h_n-1`; thus padding handles arbitrary ties correctly. The same identity holds for the iid vector on

\[
G_n^Z:=\{Z_{n,(1)}\le U_n,\ C_n^Z\ge r_n\}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

By (1.2), (7.4), and (7.10),

\[
\Pr((G_n^Y)^c)+\Pr((G_n^Z)^c)\to0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Total variation contracts under deterministic maps, so (6.4) and (8.2)–(8.4) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
&d_{\rm TV}\!\left(
 \mathcal L(\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}),
 \mathcal L(\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)})
 \right)\\
&\le
 d_{\rm TV}\bigl(
 \mathcal L(\eta_n\mid S_n=m),
 \mathcal L(\eta_n)
 \bigr)
 +\Pr((G_n^Y)^c)+\Pr((G_n^Z)^c)
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
\]

This proves Theorem G. ∎
