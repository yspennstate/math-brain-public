# F0057 — sublinear-dimensional joint total variation for the extreme order statistics

## 0. Scope, source and status

Source: Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), Theorem 19.7 and Problem 19.10 (Theorem 18.7 / Problem 18.10 in the arXiv numbering).

The source asks whether, under the assumptions of Theorem 19.7,

\[
d_{\rm TV}(Y_{(j)},\xi_{(j)})\to0
\]

for every fixed `j`. The source proves this for `j=1`, proves Kolmogorov convergence for every fixed `j`, and explicitly says it expects a joint finite-dimensional extension. Earlier R19 runs already produced a source-wide fixed-`j` proof, a finite-dimensional joint proof, and then a growing-dimensional theorem under the sufficient condition `r_n U_n=o(sqrt n)`, where `U_n=o(sqrt n)` is Janson's maximum cutoff.

This artifact replaces the **worst-case cloud** argument in that last strengthening by a **typical-cloud likelihood-ratio** argument and proves the much stronger conclusion

\[
\boxed{r_n=o(n).}
\]

No stronger moment condition, compact support, no-tie hypothesis, or changed balls-in-boxes model is introduced.

The theorem below is proved for the main fixed-`xi` regime of Theorem 19.7, namely `m=lambda n+o(sqrt n)`, `0<lambda<=nu`, `Var xi<infinity`, and also for Janson's exactly tuned `xi^(n)` version. I do **not** claim here that the growing-dimensional strengthening transfers to fixed `xi` under the source's separate weaker `m/n=lambda+o(1/log n)` clause; fixed `j` does, but a growing joint vector would require a quantitative comparison between the tuned and untuned product tails.

## 1. Source facts used, and only those facts

After Janson's equivalent exponential tilt, the allocation law has the exact conditioning representation

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)\overset d=(Z_{n,1},\ldots,Z_{n,n})\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\qquad S_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n Z_{n,i}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

There are two regimes used below.

### 1.1 Fixed-law main regime

Here `Z_{n,i}` have the fixed law `p=L(xi)`,

\[
m_n=n\mu+o(\sqrt n),\qquad \mu=E\xi,\qquad 0<\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}\xi<\infty,
\tag{1.2}
\]

and the support has lattice span `d`. Janson's Theorem 19.7 assumes `w_0>0` and `omega=infinity`, hence the support is unbounded.

### 1.2 Exactly tuned regime

Here `Z_{n,i}` have law `p_n=L(xi^(n))` with

\[
E Z_{n,1}=m_n/n,
\tag{1.3}
\]

and the source proof gives `p_n => p`, a common lattice span, variance converging to `sigma^2>0`, and uniform exponential-tail control in the parameter range of the tuned version. In particular the family is uniformly square-integrable.

### 1.3 Local CLT and uniform concentration input

Janson's Lemma 14.1 and Remark 14.2 give the lattice local CLT for triangular arrays. Remark 14.2 explicitly allows target centering errors `o(sqrt n)` and relaxes the uniform third-moment condition to uniform square integrability. It also gives the uniform-in-target upper bound.

Thus in either regime,

\[
P(S_n=m_n)=\frac{d+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2 n}}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The same local CLT applies to any triangular row law obtained below by deleting a tail set whose probability and second-moment mass both tend to zero, provided the lattice span remains `d`.

### 1.4 A maximum cutoff

The proof of Theorem 19.7 constructs a deterministic `U_n` such that

\[
P(Y_{(1)}>U_n)=o(1),\qquad nP(Z_{n,1}>U_n)=o(1).
\tag{1.5}
\]

In the fixed-law proof, Janson takes `U_n=o(sqrt n)` using only `E xi^2<infinity`. In the tuned version, he takes `U_n=O(log n)`. The new proof below needs only (1.5), not the rate of `U_n`.

## 2. New theorem

For an integer sequence `r_n` satisfying

\[
1\le r_n=o(n),
\tag{2.1}
\]

write

\[
\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}=(Y_{(1)},\ldots,Y_{(r_n)}),
\qquad
\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)}=(Z_{n,(1)},\ldots,Z_{n,(r_n)}).
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem S — sublinear extreme-vector equivalence

Under the main fixed-law assumptions of Janson's Theorem 19.7,

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}\!\left(
 \mathcal L(\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}),
 \mathcal L(\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)})
 \right)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every }r_n=o(n).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The same conclusion holds in Janson's exactly tuned `xi^(n)` version, with the iid comparison vector formed from the tuned law `p_n`.

This strictly strengthens the preceding R19 theorem `r_n U_n=o(sqrt n)`: the cutoff `U_n` disappears from the admissible rank scale.

## 3. The key change: expose a typical tail cloud, not every possible tail cloud

Let

\[
D_n:=\sqrt{n(r_n+1)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
D_n\to\infty,\qquad \frac{D_n}{n}\to0,
\qquad \frac{r_n}{D_n}\to0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Choose the largest integer threshold `h_n` satisfying

\[
nP(Z_{n,1}\ge h_n)\ge D_n.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This exists for all large `n`. Since `D_n/n->0` while the limiting law has unbounded support,

\[
h_n\to\infty.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Moreover, by (1.5),

\[
nP(Z_{n,1}\ge U_n+1)=o(1)<D_n
\]

for large `n`, so maximality in (3.3) gives

\[
h_n\le U_n.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Define the tail band

\[
T_n:=\{h_n,h_n+1,\ldots,U_n\},
\qquad a_n:=P(Z_{n,1}\in T_n).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Because `h_n->infinity`,

\[
a_n\to0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Let `\eta_n` be the **labelled** cloud

\[
\eta_n:=\{(i,Z_{n,i}):Z_{n,i}\in T_n\}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The labels are only a proof device; the order-statistic map below discards them.

Write

\[
N_n:=|\eta_n|,
\qquad W_n:=\sum_{(i,z)\in\eta_n} z.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Since `a_n->0`,

\[
N_n/n\overset p\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.10}
\]

The old proof demanded a local CLT uniformly over **all** configurations with `N_n` below a deterministic budget and therefore bounded `W_n` by `N_nU_n`. The new proof instead identifies the exact centered residual shift and shows that it is `o_p(sqrt n)` automatically.

## 4. The hidden centering identity

Let

\[
M_n:=E[Z_{n,1}\mathbf 1_{\{Z_{n,1}\in T_n\}}],
\qquad \mu_n:=E Z_{n,1},
\tag{4.1}
\]

and let `p_n^c` be the law conditioned to avoid the cloud band,

\[
p_n^c(k):=\frac{p_n(k)\mathbf 1_{\{k\notin T_n\}}}{1-a_n},
\qquad
\mu_n^c:=E_{p_n^c}Z
=\frac{\mu_n-M_n}{1-a_n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For a cloud with size `N_n` and total `W_n`, the remaining `n-N_n` variables must sum to `m_n-W_n`. Define its displacement from the complement-law mean:

\[
\Delta_n:=(m_n-W_n)-(n-N_n)\mu_n^c.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The crucial identity is

\[
\boxed{E\Delta_n=m_n-n\mu_n.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed,

\[
EW_n=nM_n,\qquad EN_n=na_n,
\]

and

\[
(1-a_n)\mu_n^c=\mu_n-M_n,
\]

so

\[
E\Delta_n
=m_n-nM_n-(n-na_n)\mu_n^c
=m_n-n\mu_n.
\]

Thus deleting the entire tail cloud introduces **no first-order recentering error at all**. The expected tail mass cancels exactly.

Next,

\[
\Delta_n-E\Delta_n
=-(W_n-EW_n)+\mu_n^c(N_n-EN_n).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Delta_n)
\le nE\!\left[(Z_{n,1}-\mu_n^c)^2
\mathbf 1_{\{Z_{n,1}\in T_n\}}\right].
\tag{4.6}
\]

The means `mu_n^c` remain bounded. Since `T_n subseteq [h_n,infinity)` and `h_n->infinity`, finite second moment in the fixed-law regime, and uniform square integrability in the tuned regime, imply

\[
E\!\left[(Z_{n,1}-\mu_n^c)^2
\mathbf 1_{\{Z_{n,1}\in T_n\}}\right]=o(1).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Delta_n)=o(n).
\tag{4.8}
\]

In the fixed-law main regime, `m_n-nmu_n=o(sqrt n)`; in the tuned regime it is zero. Combining with (4.4)-(4.8),

\[
\boxed{\Delta_n=o_p(\sqrt n).}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This is the mechanism missed by the preceding worst-case estimate `|W_n|<=N_nU_n`.

## 5. The complement law remains inside Janson's local-CLT class

Because `a_n->0` and the removed set tends to infinity,

\[
p_n^c\Rightarrow p,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{p_n^c}Z\to\sigma^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The family `p_n^c` is uniformly square-integrable: conditioning on an event of probability `1-o(1)` cannot destroy the UI of the original family, and the deleted second-moment mass tends to zero by (4.7).

The lattice span is eventually unchanged. Choose finitely many fixed support points whose differences generate the source span `d`; because `h_n->infinity`, all of them remain outside `T_n` for large `n`. In the tuned tilt, the support itself is unchanged.

Thus `p_n^c` satisfies Lemma 14.1 / Remark 14.2 with the same limiting variance and span.

## 6. Likelihood ratio tends to one on a typical cloud set

Let `P_n` be the iid law of the labelled cloud `eta_n`, and let `Q_n` be its law under the conditioned allocation `S_n=m_n`.

For a feasible labelled cloud configuration `nu`, let

\[
q(\nu):=|\nu|,
\qquad s(\nu):=\sum_{(i,z)\in\nu}z,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
\Delta(\nu):=(m_n-s(\nu))-(n-q(\nu))\mu_n^c.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Conditionally on the exact labelled cloud, every remaining coordinate is iid with law `p_n^c`. Therefore the Radon-Nikodym derivative is exactly

\[
L_n(\nu):=\frac{dQ_n}{dP_n}(\nu)
=\frac{
P_{p_n^c}(S_{n-q(\nu)}=m_n-s(\nu))
}{P_{p_n}(S_n=m_n)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

By (3.10) and (4.9), there exists a deterministic `epsilon_n downarrow 0` such that the typical set

\[
A_n:=\left\{
q(\nu)\le\epsilon_n n,
\quad |\Delta(\nu)|\le\epsilon_n\sqrt n
\right\}
\tag{6.4}
\]

satisfies

\[
P_n(A_n)\to1.
\tag{6.5}
\]

I claim

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\nu\in A_n}|L_n(\nu)-1|\to0.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

If not, select a violating sequence `nu_n in A_n`. Along that sequence,

\[
n-q(\nu_n)\sim n,
\qquad
(m_n-s(\nu_n))-(n-q(\nu_n))\mu_n^c=o(\sqrt n).
\tag{6.7}
\]

The triangular row laws `p_n^c` satisfy convergence, common span, positive limiting variance and uniform square integrability by Section 5. Remark 14.2 therefore gives

\[
P_{p_n^c}(S_{n-q(\nu_n)}=m_n-s(\nu_n))
=\frac{d+o(1)}{
\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2(n-q(\nu_n))}}
=\frac{d+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2n}}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The denominator has the same asymptotic by (1.4), contradicting the assumed violation. This proves (6.6).

Now use `E_{P_n}L_n=1`. From (6.5)-(6.6),

\[
Q_n(A_n)=E_{P_n}[L_n\mathbf 1_{A_n}]\to1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\begin{aligned}
2d_{\rm TV}(P_n,Q_n)
&=E_{P_n}|L_n-1|\\
&\le \sup_{A_n}|L_n-1|
+P_n(A_n^c)+Q_n(A_n^c)\to0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}(P_n,Q_n)\to0.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

This is an equivalence-of-ensembles statement for the **entire tail cloud**, even though its expected cardinality may diverge much faster than `sqrt n/U_n`.

## 7. The tail cloud contains far more than `r_n` points

By the definition of `h_n`,

\[
nP(Z_{n,1}\ge h_n)\ge D_n.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Using the maximum cutoff (1.5),

\[
\Lambda_n:=E_{P_n}N_n
=nP(h_n\le Z_{n,1}\le U_n)
\ge D_n-o(1).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\Lambda_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{r_n}{\Lambda_n}\to0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Under the iid law, `N_n` is binomial. Chebyshev's inequality, for example, gives

\[
P_n(N_n<r_n)\to0.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Because the **whole cloud law** converges in total variation by (6.11), the identical event transfers immediately:

\[
Q_n(N_n<r_n)\to0.
\tag{7.5}
\]

No separate growing-order factorial-moment calculation is needed.

## 8. Recover the top `r_n` vector from the cloud

Let `Top_{r_n}` be the deterministic map that discards labels and returns the largest `r_n` cloud values in decreasing order whenever the cloud has at least `r_n` points.

On

\[
G_n^Z:=\{Z_{n,(1)}\le U_n,\ N_n\ge r_n\},
\tag{8.1}
\]

all top `r_n` iid sample values belong to `T_n`, and

\[
\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)}=Top_{r_n}(\eta_n).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Similarly, under the conditioned law, on

\[
G_n^Y:=\{Y_{(1)}\le U_n,\ N_n\ge r_n\},
\tag{8.3}
\]

we have

\[
\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}=Top_{r_n}(\eta_n).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Equations (1.5), (7.4) and (7.5) show

\[
P((G_n^Z)^c)+P((G_n^Y)^c)\to0.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Total variation contracts under deterministic maps. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
&d_{\rm TV}\!\left(
\mathcal L(\mathbf Y_n^{(r_n)}),
\mathcal L(\mathbf Z_n^{(r_n)})
\right)\\
&\le d_{\rm TV}(Q_n,P_n)
+P((G_n^Y)^c)+P((G_n^Z)^c)
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.6}
\]

This proves Theorem S. ∎

## 9. Why the three new Section-B moves mattered

### #1 CHANGE THE CATEGORY

The problem was no longer treated as a direct order-statistic comparison. It was recast as **equivalence of ensembles for a tail point cloud**. The top-r vector is only a deterministic projection at the end.

### #12 FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED

The hidden conserved quantity is the mean removed tail mass. The identity

\[
E\Delta_n=m_n-n\mu_n
\]

shows that deleting the cloud causes no new first-order shift: the mean of the removed cloud and the mean change of the complement law cancel exactly. Only an `o_p(sqrt n)` fluctuation remains.

### #28 REPLACE FOR ALL WITH FOR TYPICAL

The previous proof controlled the residual local CLT for **every** cloud configuration up to a cardinality bound, forcing the crude worst-case estimate `W_n<=N_nU_n` and hence `r_nU_n=o(sqrt n)`. The new proof requires the likelihood ratio to be close to one only on a `P_n`-probability `1-o(1)` set. Uniformity on that typical set follows from a subsequence application of the source local CLT, and the complement automatically has vanishing conditioned mass because `E_P L=1`.

## 10. Counterexample / boundary pressure

The argument genuinely needs `r_n=o(n)` in order to choose a tail level `h_n->infinity` containing many more than `r_n` points while still deleting only `o(n)` coordinates. At a positive linear rank fraction the relevant threshold is generally a fixed quantile, so the deleted law need not approach the original law and the second-moment tail error need not vanish. I do **not** claim here that every linear fraction fails, only that the sublinear scale is the natural universal range of this argument.

A useful neighboring analogy is equivalence-of-ensembles theory: local or vanishing-fraction observables can be asymptotically canonical, while extensive observables retain the global constraint. This is motivation only, not an imported theorem.
