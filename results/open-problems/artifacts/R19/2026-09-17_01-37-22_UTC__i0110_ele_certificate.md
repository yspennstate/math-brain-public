# R19 / I0110 — Entropic Lift-Extraction certificate

Status: **INTERNAL COMMON-CERTIFICATE CANDIDATE / PARTIAL for Salez Problem 2.1.**

This note isolates one deterministic cutoff implication and a quenched-derandomization lemma. It is intended as the common target for the source families, not as a claim that Salez Problem 2.1 is closed. The abstraction itself is not claimed novel: several of the source papers explicitly describe an entropic-time paradigm, and Hermon--Olesker-Taylor explicitly use a conditioned/modified L2 calculation. The point here is to state a single version that can be checked model-by-model without putting total variation mixing itself into the hypotheses.

## 1. Deterministic ELE certificate

For each `n`, let `(P_{n,t})_{t>=0}` be a finite Markov semigroup with stationary law `pi_n`, and fix the source-relevant initial law. Write `p_{n,t}` for the resulting law at time `t` and

\[
d_n(t):=\|p_{n,t}-\pi_n\|_{\rm TV}.
\]

An **Entropic Lift-Extraction (ELE) certificate** consists of times

\[
t_n^-<t_n^+,
\qquad \frac{t_n^+}{t_n^-}\to1,
\]

and, separately at the two times, an auxiliary probability space `(Omega_n^\pm,q_n^\pm)`, a map

\[
\Phi_n^\pm:\Omega_n^\pm\to X_n,
\]

and a typical set `E_n^\pm subset Omega_n^\pm`, with the following properties. The lifts/maps must be specified by the model architecture (cover/local-limit exploration, quasi-tree, generator-count lattice, backbone/stopping construction, or another explicit model-generated lift), rather than chosen after inspecting `d_n(t)`.

### ELE-L: pre-entropic compression

There are numbers `eps_n^-, eta_n^- -> 0` such that

\[
\|p_{n,t_n^-}-(\Phi_n^-)_\#q_n^-\|_{\rm TV}\le \eta_n^-,
\]

\[
q_n^-(E_n^-)\ge1-\varepsilon_n^-,
\qquad
\pi_n\bigl(\Phi_n^-(E_n^-)\bigr)\le\varepsilon_n^-.
\]

This is the source-coding/AEP half: before the entropic threshold, almost all auxiliary mass projects into a set with negligible stationary mass.

### ELE-U: post-entropic smooth extraction

There are numbers `eps_n^+, eta_n^+ -> 0` such that

\[
\|p_{n,t_n^+}-(\Phi_n^+)_\#q_n^+\|_{\rm TV}\le \eta_n^+,
\qquad
m_n:=q_n^+(E_n^+)\ge1-\varepsilon_n^+.
\]

Define the pushed-forward subprobability

\[
r_n(x):=\sum_{\omega\in E_n^+:\ \Phi_n^+(\omega)=x}q_n^+(\omega),
\qquad \sum_x r_n(x)=m_n.
\]

The extraction condition is the centered weighted collision bound

\[
\boxed{
D_n:=\sum_{x\in X_n}
\frac{\bigl(r_n(x)-m_n\pi_n(x)\bigr)^2}{\pi_n(x)}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{ELE-U}
\]

This is an L2/collision requirement on the *structurally specified projected typical lift*. It is not an assumption that the chain is already close in total variation.

## 2. R19-I0110-C008 — ELE deterministically implies cutoff

**Theorem.** If a deterministic sequence of finite Markov chains has an ELE certificate, then

\[
d_n(t_n^-)\to1,
\qquad
d_n(t_n^+)\to0.
\]

Since total variation distance to stationarity is nonincreasing in time and `t_n^+/t_n^- -> 1`, the sequence exhibits cutoff in the source-relevant initial-law sense.

### Proof

Set

\[
\widehat p_n^-:=(\Phi_n^-)_\#q_n^-,
\qquad
A_n:=\Phi_n^-(E_n^-).
\]

Because every point of `E_n^-` maps into `A_n`,

\[
\widehat p_n^-(A_n)\ge q_n^-(E_n^-)\ge1-\varepsilon_n^-.
\]

The coupling/representation error gives

\[
p_{n,t_n^-}(A_n)\ge1-\varepsilon_n^--\eta_n^-.
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n(t_n^-)
&\ge p_{n,t_n^-}(A_n)-\pi_n(A_n)\\
&\ge1-2\varepsilon_n^--\eta_n^-\longrightarrow1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

For the upper bound put

\[
\widehat p_n^+:=(\Phi_n^+)_\#q_n^+.
\]

Write `s_n:=\widehat p_n^+-r_n`; this is a nonnegative subprobability of mass `1-m_n`. Then

\[
\widehat p_n^+-\pi_n
=(r_n-m_n\pi_n)+(s_n-(1-m_n)\pi_n).
\]

The total variation norm between two subprobabilities having the same mass `a` is at most `a`. Therefore

\[
\|s_n-(1-m_n)\pi_n\|_{\rm TV}\le1-m_n.
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|r_n-m_n\pi_n\|_{\rm TV}
&=\frac12\sum_x|r_n(x)-m_n\pi_n(x)|\\
&\le\frac12
\left(\sum_x\pi_n(x)\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_x\frac{(r_n(x)-m_n\pi_n(x))^2}{\pi_n(x)}\right)^{1/2}\\
&=\frac12\sqrt{D_n}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n(t_n^+)
&\le \eta_n^++\|\widehat p_n^+-\pi_n\|_{\rm TV}\\
&\le \eta_n^++\varepsilon_n^++\frac12\sqrt{D_n}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Equations (1)--(2), the monotonicity of `d_n`, and `t_n^+/t_n^- -> 1` give cutoff. `QED`

## 3. R19-I0110-C009 — annealed collision estimates derandomize to quenched ELE

Many of Salez's nine examples are random environments. The upper certificate is often first proved after averaging over the random graph/generators. The following elementary step turns that into the deterministic property required on a high-probability set of realized environments.

**Lemma.** Let `Z_n` be the random environment. Suppose the ELE data are measurable functions of `Z_n` and all nonnegative defects

\[
S_n(Z_n):=\varepsilon_n^-(Z_n)+\eta_n^-(Z_n)+
\varepsilon_n^+(Z_n)+\eta_n^+(Z_n)+D_n(Z_n)
\]

satisfy

\[
\mathbb E S_n\to0,
\]

while `t_n^+/t_n^- -> 1` deterministically (or in probability with a deterministic high-probability envelope). Then there are deterministic `delta_n -> 0` such that

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(S_n>\delta_n\bigr)\to0.
\]

Consequently the realized deterministic chain has an ELE certificate with high probability, and hence cutoff with high probability.

### Proof

If `a_n:=E S_n`, take `delta_n=sqrt(a_n)` when `a_n>0` and `delta_n=0` otherwise. Markov gives

\[
\mathbb P(S_n>\delta_n)\le a_n/\delta_n=\sqrt{a_n}\to0.
\]

On the complementary event all five nonnegative defects are at most `delta_n`, so C008 applies to that deterministic realized chain. `QED`

This proves the asymptotically-high-probability quantifier. It does **not** silently upgrade it to pathwise almost sure eventual validity. If a model supplies summable failure probabilities, the usual Borel--Cantelli upgrade is available.

## 4. Why this is not the raw collision criterion that failed last run

The previous R19 run showed on `G=(Z/2Z)^d` with `k=d+s` that the raw, unconditioned H2/collision threshold is strictly later than the Shannon threshold. ELE-U deliberately permits an AEP/typical truncation *before* the collision estimate. The discarded mass is charged explicitly by `1-m_n`; it is not hidden. This is exactly the move that lets a Shannon-scale typical law be extracted in L2 while rare lift atoms no longer dominate the second moment.

## 5. Source-family matching obtained this run

This section records **coverage of the same certificate definition**, not a claim that Problem 2.1 is solved and not a novelty claim for the individual ingredients.

### [52] Hermon--Olesker-Taylor — random Abelian Cayley graphs: MATCH

Primary source: arXiv:2102.02809v2.

- Structural lift: the walk is represented by the coordinate-count walk `W` on `Z^k` and the random-generator projection `w -> w . Z`.
- ELE-L: their entropic-time lower bound is a typical-set/support-size argument.
- ELE-U: they explicitly condition `W` on a typical set and perform a **modified L2 calculation**; their Lemma 2.6 is exactly of the truncated collision form, and their Proposition 2.9 proves the conditioned L2 defect is `o(1)` in expectation. They then invoke Markov to obtain the quenched high-probability statement.
- Narrow window: their entropy growth/concentration controls the two sides of the entropic threshold.

Thus [52] is a direct ELE instance, not merely an analogy.

### [61] Hermon--Sly--Sousi — graph plus random perfect matching: MATCH

Primary source: arXiv:2008.08564 / Ann. Probab. 50 (2022), 203--240. Later primary-family recap: Baran--Hermon--Sarkovic--Sousi, PTRF 2024.

- Structural lift: the source paper constructs an auxiliary random quasi-tree and identifies cutoff time by when the quasi-tree walk entropy reaches `log |V_n|`.
- ELE-L: entropy/information concentration on the quasi-tree gives a high-probability pre-entropic part supported on too little stationary mass.
- ELE-U: the proof conditions/truncates to the typical quasi-tree event, obtains an L2 bound at entropic time, then uses the constant-order relaxation/Poincare step over an `o(log n)` extra window to make the collision defect vanish.
- The theorem gives window `O(sqrt(log n))` while the mixing time is order `log n`, so the ELE clock condition holds.

The source paper itself describes entropic-time cutoff through an auxiliary process as a recurring paradigm; ELE is a formalization of that architecture, not a claim that the architecture was previously unnoticed.

### [16] Berestycki--Lubetzky--Peres--Sly — sparse random graphs from a typical start: MATCH

Primary source: arXiv:1504.01999 / Ann. Probab. 46 (2018).

- Structural lift: the local Poisson--Galton--Watson tree and its harmonic measure.
- ELE-L: harmonic measure at depth `k` concentrates on about `exp(d k)` effective states; before `(nu d)^{-1} log n` the projected mass therefore occupies negligible stationary mass.
- ELE-U: their introduction states that the upper bound hinges on an L2 estimate at the entropic time; in the expander core, a further `O(sqrt(log n))` (or `sqrt(log n)(log log n)^2` with degree-1/2 corrections) spectral-smoothing period drives the law to equilibrium.
- Their cutoff window is `log^{1/2+o(1)} n`, while the location is order `log n`.

This is ELE in the source's typical-start scope. It is not promoted to a stronger worst-start statement here.

### [22] Bordenave--Lacoin and [80] Lubetzky--Sly: RETAINED MATCHES

These were the two already credited source cases before this run. Bordenave--Lacoin use an infinite transitive cover, Shannon--McMillan--Breiman entropic compression, an explicit backbone/stopping construction, and spectral L2 extraction. Their criterion applies to random regular graphs, covering the [80] family in the already-recorded sense. No new credit is claimed for the same two cases; they are recorded here because they satisfy the *same* ELE certificate now used for [16], [52], and [61].

### Not yet credited: [14], [21], [13], [62]

This run does not award coverage by analogy. The nonbacktracking, directed/nonreversible, two-community, and finite-community papers still need an exact source-level ELE map with the same four obligations: structural lift, pre-entropic compression, post-entropic truncated collision extraction, and a sublinear clock window.

## 6. Metric consequence and caveat

Under the explicit certificate interpretation above, the common-property coverage count moves

\[
\boxed{M_9:2/9\longrightarrow5/9}
\]

for `[22],[80],[52],[61],[16]`.

This is **internal certificate coverage**, not closure of Salez Problem 2.1. The main scope risk is semantic: Salez asks for a "structural property" of the random graphs. ELE is a structural proof certificate involving an explicitly specified auxiliary lift/extractor. An independent audit must decide whether that is sufficiently intrinsic to count as the intended kind of structural property rather than merely an abstraction of existing proofs. Until that audit, status remains **PARTIAL / COMMON-CERTIFICATE CANDIDATE**.
