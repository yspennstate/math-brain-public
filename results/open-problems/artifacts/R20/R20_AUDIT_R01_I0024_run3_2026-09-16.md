## 2. Claim C: centered state-to-stationarity Gram-flow formula — PASS

Let `B` be an orientation incidence matrix on non-loop edges, `C=diag(c_e)` with `c_xy=pi_x P(x,y)`, and `L=BCB^T=Pi(I-P)`. A centered state-to-stationarity family is a matrix `Psi` whose column `psi^x` obeys

`B psi^x=e_x-pi`, and `Psi pi=0`.

Set `G=Psi^T C^{-1} Psi`. For any `h` with `pi^T h=0`, define

`theta_h=Psi Pi h`.

Then

`B theta_h=(I-pi 1^T)Pi h=Pi h`,

and

`theta_h^T C^{-1}theta_h=h^T Pi G Pi h`.

Putting `z=Pi^{1/2}h`, the energy quotient is the Rayleigh quotient of

`A=Pi^{1/2}G Pi^{1/2}`.

Because `Psi pi=0`, one has `A sqrt(pi)=0`; hence the supremum over centered `h` is exactly `lambda_max(A)`.

For every such `h`, Thomson's principle lower-bounds this energy by the minimum energy of a flow with divergence `Pi h`. Solving

`L u=Pi h`, equivalently `(I-P)u=h` modulo constants, and expanding `h` in a `pi`-orthonormal eigenbasis of the reversible `P` gives minimum-energy quotient

`sum_{i>=2} a_i^2/(1-lambda_i) / sum_{i>=2}a_i^2`,

whose supremum is

`1/(1-lambda_2)=tau_2`.

The electrical family

`psi_*^x=C B^T L^+(e_x-pi)`

produces the minimum-energy flow simultaneously for every demand `Pi h`, so equality is attained. Therefore

`tau_2 = inf_Psi lambda_max(Pi^{1/2}G_Psi Pi^{1/2})`.

I find no hidden minimax exchange: the lower bound is pointwise in every demand and the single electrical family attains all of those pointwise minima simultaneously.

## 3. Claim D: derivation of Aldous–Fill path bounds — PASS

The run-3 path symmetrization is valid: replacing the ordered path `gamma_xy` by the 50/50 mixture of `gamma_xy` and the reverse of `gamma_yx` makes the expected pair flow antisymmetric while replacing each directed-edge load by the arithmetic mean of the two opposite directed loads. Therefore the maximum directed congestion cannot increase.

For the resulting antisymmetric line-integral kernel `D_xy=-D_yx`, define

`g_x=sum_y pi_y D_xy`.

Then `pi(g)=0`, and direct symmetrization gives the exact identities

`E[D_XY(g_X-g_Y)]=2 Var_pi(g)`,

`E[(g_X-g_Y)^2]=2 Var_pi(g)`.

Cauchy–Schwarz yields

`Var_pi(g) <= (1/2) E[D_XY^2]`.

That factor `1/2` is essential. Applying pathwise Cauchy–Schwarz and then collecting the two orientations of every physical edge cancels this `1/2`, leaving precisely the directed-edge congestion normalization used by Aldous–Fill. Replacing resistance length by ordinary path length gives the second displayed source bound; taking expected loads gives the randomized-path version. I find no extra factor of two and no missing `w`/`w_e` normalization in the run-3 derivation.
