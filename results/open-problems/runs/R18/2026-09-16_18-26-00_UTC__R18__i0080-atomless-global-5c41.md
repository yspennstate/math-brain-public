## 5.1 The witness family

Fix
\[
B=\frac1{10},
\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac1{100},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac85,
\qquad
d=\frac{19}{20}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For parameters
\[
0<b\le B,
\qquad
\ell\ge100,
\qquad
p=\frac{\kappa}{\ell},
\tag{5.2}
\]
define \(\nu=\nu_{b,\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}\) by its logarithmic density:
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d(\log_*\nu)}{dx}
=
\frac{1-p}{\ell b}\mathbf1_{[-\ell b,0]}(x)
+
\frac{p}{\varepsilon b}\mathbf1_{[-\varepsilon b,0]}(x).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is a probability density because its two displayed pieces have masses
\(1-p\) and \(p\). Since \(p\le2/125<1\), both coefficients are positive.

On the logarithmic line the density is zero to the left of \(-\ell b\), constant on
\([-\ell b,-\varepsilon b)\), jumps upward on
\([-\varepsilon b,0]\), and drops to zero after \(0\). It is therefore unimodal with mode \(0\).

Thus \(\nu\) is log-unimodal with multiplicative mode \(1\).

It is atomless and, under \(u=e^x\), has the ordinary Lebesgue density
\[
\frac{d\nu}{du}
=
\frac1u\left[
\frac{1-p}{\ell b}\mathbf1_{[e^{-\ell b},1]}(u)
+
\frac{p}{\varepsilon b}\mathbf1_{[e^{-\varepsilon b},1]}(u)
\right].
\tag{5.4}
\]
Hence it is absolutely continuous and compactly supported in
\[
[e^{-\ell b},1]\subset(0,\infty).
\tag{5.5}
\]

  \qquad
  k'(x)\ge c_-|x|^{-p}\quad(x\le-R).
  \]

For \(k_s(x)=s^{-1}k(x/s)\) and a probability law \(\mu\) with
\[
M_p=\int|y|^p\,d\mu(y)<\infty,
\]
define
\[
G_s(x)
=

=
\int k(x-y/s)\,d\mu(y).
\]

For \(r\ge\epsilon\), define
\[
m_+(r)
=
\min_{u\in[r/2,3r/2]}(-k'(u)),
\qquad
m_-(r)
=
\min_{u\in[-3r/2,-r/2]}k'(u).
\]
Strict sign on compact ranges and the polynomial tail lower bound for large
\(r\) imply
\[
c_*=
\inf_{r\ge\epsilon}
r^p\min\{m_+(r),m_-(r)\}
>0.
\]

For \(x\ge\epsilon\), on
\[
A_x=\{|y|\le sx/2\}

one has \(x-y/s\in[x/2,3x/2]\). Markov gives
\[
q_x:=\mu(A_x^c)
\le
\frac{2^pM_p}{s^px^p}.
\]
If
\[

\ge

\frac{4(2^pM_p)}{\epsilon^p},

G_s'(x)>0
\quad(x\le-\epsilon).

For sufficiently large \(s\), this is at most \(-c_0/2\).

Hence \(G_s'\) is positive to the left of \(-\epsilon\), strictly decreases across the central interval from a positive to a negative value, and remains negative to the right of \(+\epsilon\). It has exactly one zero. Thus \(G_s\), and therefore \(\mu*k_s\), is unimodal.
