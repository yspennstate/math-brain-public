## 3. Method #9 — RANDOMIZE: F0016 / Vu Conjecture 11.5 (rank resilience)

### Exact source target

The intake records Van Vu's combinatorial random-matrix survey, Conjecture 11.5, **Rank Resilience**. For an iid Rademacher `n x n` matrix `M_n`, `Res(M_n)` is the minimum number of entry sign changes needed to make the matrix singular; the conjecture is

`Res(M_n) = (1/2 + o(1)) n` with probability `1-o(1)`.

The intake already records the later Ferber–Luh–McKinley resilience theorem for rectangular Rademacher matrices as a strong partial result; it does not close the exact square source statement.

### New-method diagnostic

Randomization exposes why an obvious probabilistic attack cannot touch the hard quantifier. For every **fixed** sign-flip mask `S in {+1,-1}^{n x n}` independent of `M_n`, the entrywise product

`S ⊙ M_n`

has exactly the same iid Rademacher law as `M_n`. The same remains true if `S` is randomized independently of `M_n`. Therefore an argument of the form “choose a random set of about `n/2` entries to flip and show the resulting matrix is nonsingular/singular with some probability” is distributionally blind: it sees an untouched iid Rademacher matrix.

The source is difficult precisely because the corruption mask is **adaptive**, chosen after observing `M_n`. Any successful proof must control a worst-case data-dependent family of masks of size below `(1/2-o(1))n`; random independent masks erase the mechanism rather than reveal it.

This is a useful route-killing invariant, not a source solution. The square conjecture remains an asymptotic adversarial theorem with no short finite closure certificate recovered here.

**Disposition:** `F0016 = OPEN-looking / HARD / NOT CLOSABLE for R15; no claim`.

## 4. Method #14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: F0003 / sharp singularity exponent for fixed-d random regular graphs

### Exact source target and known obstruction

The source is Vu's survey question on the singularity probability of the adjacency matrix of a uniformly random fixed-`d` regular graph. The intake attempt identifies the standard lower-bound mechanism: a pair of vertices with identical neighborhoods (“twins”). For one unordered pair, the probability is of order `n^{-d}`; there are `Theta(n^2)` pairs, yielding the conjectural source scale

`P(A_{n,d} singular) = n^{-d+2+o(1)}`.

Nonsingularity itself has much stronger later results; the **sharp exponent** is the residual target.

### Symmetry reduction and what it does not give

The natural `S_n` action by vertex relabeling is transitive on unordered pairs. Thus the `n^{2-d}` lower bound is an orbit-counting phenomenon: one local singularity template, multiplied by the size of its pair orbit. This makes the intended extremal mechanism unusually concrete.

But that same quotient shows the missing theorem. To prove the sharp exponent it is not enough to count the twin orbit; one must show that **every other kernel-vector / local-dependency orbit contributes `o(n^{2-d})` in total**. Those other mechanisms range from small-support signed relations to distributed kernel vectors. Existing fixed-degree switching and anti-concentration technology does not collapse them to a finite orbit list whose total can currently be certified at the required scale.

A finite enumeration of small `n`, or even a complete classification of bounded-support kernel vectors, would remain PARTIAL because the source quantifies over the full asymptotic singularity event.

**Disposition:** `F0003 = OPEN-looking / HARD / NOT CLOSABLE for R15; no claim`.

## 5. Method #22 — READ THE FAILURES: F0005 / determinant size and limiting law for a fixed-d random regular graph

### Exact source target

Vu's Question 6.7 asks to estimate the determinant of the adjacency matrix `A_{n,d}` for fixed `d` and find its limiting law.

There is substantial neighboring progress: cycle-count methods give limiting laws for sufficiently smooth linear eigenvalue statistics of random regular graphs, and newer fixed-degree work gives very strong eigenvalue rigidity and edge universality. A bounded current search did not recover a theorem that gives the source-matching limiting law for the adjacency determinant itself.

### Why the successful neighboring machinery stops

Formally,

`log |det A| = sum_i log |lambda_i|`.

The test function `log|x|` is singular at the spectral point `x=0`. This is exactly where the smooth-linear-statistics machinery ceases to be routine. Strong global rigidity or edge universality does not by itself control:

1. the rare event `lambda_i = 0` (exact singularity),
2. microscopic eigenvalues near zero whose contribution to `log|det A|` is large,
3. uniform integrability of the singular statistic.

Thus the failure is not “we need another moment computation”; the source lives at a singular interior spectral point and requires zero-energy local information plus sufficiently sharp lower-tail control on the least absolute eigenvalue. A recent log-determinant method for a Bethe-Hessian / non-backtracking object is not the same determinant and cannot be substituted.

That obstruction makes the full question a genuine asymptotic spectral-law problem, not a finite CLOSABLE endpoint.

**Disposition:** `F0005 = OPEN-looking / HARD / NOT CLOSABLE for R15; no claim`.
