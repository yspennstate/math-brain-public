# 4. Recovery idea before proof: the a16 "infinite-mean renewal" is a finite-intensity Palm defect in local time

The a18 stuck report identified the lack of a canonical Palm coordinate as the main positive-route obstruction. The a16 candidate unexpectedly **does** have such a coordinate.

Recall its pair-boundary regrouping. Write `R_m=S_{2m}`. Between `R_m` and `R_{m+1}` the process is standard Brownian motion started at 0, run until it first hits `+1`, and then continued until its first return to 0. Equivalently, in Itô excursion language at level 0, one pair consists of

1. every excursion before the first positive excursion whose supremum reaches 1; and
2. that successful excursion **in full**, through its return to 0.

Thus pair boundaries are completion times of successive `+1`-reaching positive excursions.

In physical time the pair duration has infinite expectation. That made Remark 7.6 look promising. But in **local-time parameter** the successful excursions form a finite-intensity homogeneous Poisson process. Anchoring the path immediately after one successful excursion is therefore exactly a Palm bias of a stationary marked Poisson excursion process. Palm bias is shift-couplable away in local time.

This is the hidden coordinate that the a16 route had not used.

# 5. R15-C19 — finite-intensity excursion-Palm pastings are 2BM

**STATUS: PROVED, using standard Itô excursion theory plus published Palm/shift-coupling.**

Let `E` be the Brownian excursion space and `n` Itô excursion measure. Let `A subset E` be measurable with

\[
0<n(A)<\infty.
\tag{C19.1}
\]

Let

\[
\Pi=\sum_i \delta_{(\ell_i,e_i)}
\]

be the stationary marked Poisson excursion process on `R x E` with intensity

\[
d\ell\,n(de).
\]

Thin it to

\[
\Pi_A=\sum_{i:e_i\in A}\delta_{\ell_i},
\]

a homogeneous Poisson process on the local-time line of finite positive intensity `lambda=n(A)`.

Let `Q_A^0` be the Palm law of the full marked excursion process with respect to `Pi_A`, and reconstruct a continuous path by concatenating excursions in increasing local-time order, placing physical time 0 at the **end** of the forced A-excursion at local-time label 0.

Then the reconstructed process is 2BM.

## Proof

### Step 1: exact Palm form

By the Poisson Palm/Slivnyak theorem, under `Q_A^0` the marked configuration has the law

\[
\delta_{(0,e_0)}+\Pi',
\tag{C19.2}
\]

where

- `e_0` has law `n(de | A)`;
- `Pi'` is an ordinary Poisson random measure with intensity `dell n(de)`;
- `e_0` and `Pi'` are independent.

So away from local-time label 0 the excursion field is exactly the ordinary stationary Brownian excursion PPP; the sole bias is the forced A-excursion at 0.

### Step 2: remove the Palm bias by a local-time shift

Palm inversion / continuous-time shift coupling for stationary random measures gives a finite random local-time shift `H` such that

\[
\theta_H(\delta_{(0,e_0)}+\Pi')
\stackrel d=\Pi.
\tag{C19.3}
\]

One way to state the imported result is: the Palm version and stationary version of this finite-intensity marked Poisson environment are shift-couplable; equivalently, an allocation/cycle-stationarity construction balances the Palm point with Lebesgue local time. This is precisely the setting of Thorisson's continuous-time shift-coupling theory and the diffuse-random-measure Palm/allocation machinery of Last--Mörters--Thorisson.

No physical-time finite-mean assumption is needed: the shift acts on the **local-time axis**, where the A-points have finite positive intensity.

### Step 3: convert the local-time shift into a finite physical random time

For every finite local-time displacement `H`, the corresponding inverse-local-time displacement is finite almost surely. The one extra A-excursion in (C19.2) also has finite lifetime almost surely. Hence `H` determines an almost surely finite physical zero time `S` in the reconstructed path.

Under the target law on the right side of (C19.3), label 0 is almost surely not an excursion atom. Therefore physical time `S` is an ordinary zero separating the negative and positive excursion PPPs.

### Step 4: the shifted physical path is two-sided Wiener

Under ordinary `Pi`, the excursions with local-time labels `ell>0` and `ell<0` are independent Poisson random measures with the Brownian Itô intensity. Concatenating the positive labels produces a standard Brownian motion forward from S; concatenating the negative labels in reverse order produces an independent standard Brownian motion backward from S.

Therefore

\[
(X_{S+t}-X_S)_{t\ge0},
\qquad
(X_{S-t}-X_S)_{t\ge0}
\]

are independent standard Brownian motions. Thus the reconstructed process is 2BM. `square`

## Scope note

The theorem is a no-go theorem for a **counterexample mechanism**, not a solution of Problem 7.2. It requires an actual finite-intensity excursion-Palm coordinate. The source hypotheses do not supply such a coordinate for an arbitrary FBM+BBM with one independent seam.
