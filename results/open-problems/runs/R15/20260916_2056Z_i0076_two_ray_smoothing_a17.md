# 3. Move #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: two-ray uniform-phase smoothing

The previous run a15 proved one-sided uniform-phase smoothing: adding one independent uniform phase after an arbitrary anticipative root makes the future law absolutely continuous with respect to one-sided Wiener measure.

The stronger statement below keeps a **full finite amount of past together with the entire future**, and, using BBM, gives the complementary ray at the **same randomized root**.

## R15-PR2 — finite-backward-window / infinite-forward-ray smoothing

**Status: PROVED.**

Let X be any FBM. Let T be any a.s.-finite real random time; T may depend arbitrarily on the whole path and need not be a stopping time. Fix `L>0`, enlarge the probability space by an independent

`U ~ Uniform[0,L]`,

and define on `[-L,infinity)`

`Y_t = X_{T+U+t}-X_{T+U}`.

Let `P_L^+` denote the restriction of two-sided Wiener law to the rooted path space `C([-L,infinity))`. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Law}(Y)\ll P_L^+.}
\tag{PR2+}
\]

### Complete proof

Choose FBM witnesses `S_n -> -infinity` and set

`B^(n)_s = X_{S_n+s}-X_{S_n}`, `s>=0`.

For each n, `B^(n)` has exactly standard one-sided Wiener law. Put

`E_n={S_n<T-L}`.

Because T is finite and `S_n -> -infinity`, `union_n E_n` has probability one.

On `E_n`, define `A_n=T-S_n>L`. Then for every `t>=-L`,

\[
Y_t=B^{(n)}_{A_n+U+t}-B^{(n)}_{A_n+U}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Write uniquely

\[
A_n+U=M_n L+R_n,
\qquad M_n\in\{1,2,\ldots\},\quad R_n\in[0,L).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The crucial point is that although `A_n` can be an arbitrary anticipative function of the original randomness, adding the independent uniform U makes

\[
R_n=(A_n+U)\bmod L
\]

uniform on `[0,L)` and independent of the entire original sigma-field. In particular `R_n` is independent of `B^(n)`.

Let `N` be any Borel subset of `C([-L,infinity))` with `P_L^+(N)=0`. For each deterministic integer `m>=1`, on `E_n cap {M_n=m}`, (3.1) is the recentered Brownian path at time `mL+R_n`, restricted to `[-L,infinity)`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P(Y\in N, E_n,M_n=m)\\
&\qquad\le
\mathbb P\!\left(
(B^{(n)}_{mL+R_n+t}-B^{(n)}_{mL+R_n})_{t\ge-L}\in N
\right)\\
&\qquad=
\frac1L\int_0^L
P_L^+(N)\,dr=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The equality uses only: `R_n` is independent uniform, `B^(n)` has Wiener law, and for every deterministic `mL+r>=L`, recentering ordinary Brownian motion there and retaining times `[-L,infinity)` has exactly the restriction of two-sided Wiener law.

Summing (3.3) over the countable set `m>=1` gives

`P(Y in N,E_n)=0`.

Finally `union_n E_n` has probability one, so `P(Y in N)=0`. This proves `(PR2+)`. `QED`

## Backward complement at the same randomized root

If X is BBM as well, apply `(PR2+)` to the FBM `\widehat X_s=X_{-s}` with base time `-T-L` and independent phase `U'=L-U`. Since

`(-T-L)+(L-U)=-T-U`,

the resulting root in the original X-coordinate is again exactly `T+U`. After time reversal, we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Law}igl((X_{T+U+t}-X_{T+U})_{t\le L}\bigr)
\ll P_L^- ,
}
\tag{PR2-}
\]

where `P_L^-` is two-sided Wiener law restricted to `C((−infinity,L])` with root value zero.

Thus for the **same** randomized root `T+U`, the full-path law `nu_L` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\nu_L|_{\mathcal F_{[-L,\infty)}}\ll
P|_{\mathcal F_{[-L,\infty)}},
\qquad
\nu_L|_{\mathcal F_{(-\infty,L]}}\ll
P|_{\mathcal F_{(-\infty,L]}}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This uses no stopping-time assumption and, notably, does not even use the Problem-7.2 independence hypothesis.

## What PR2 does and does not prove

PR2 rules out any obstruction visible from either entire one-sided ray once a finite overlap across the randomized root is retained. The only possible singular obstruction to global two-sided Wiener absolute continuity is therefore a **genuine two-remote-tail joining**: a relation coupling arbitrarily remote negative and positive times while being invisible on either complementary ray separately.

This is a strict strengthening of a15, but it does **not** prove P3. Absolute continuity on each of two complementary marginal sigma-fields does not imply absolute continuity on their join. The elementary analogue is a diagonal coupling of two continuously distributed coordinates: each coordinate marginal is Lebesgue-absolutely-continuous, while the joint law on the diagonal is singular with respect to product Lebesgue measure.

At the original Problem-7.2 seam the two halves are independent, so the remaining question is now sharply localized: can FBM+BBM plus that original product structure rule out a singular tail joining after re-rooting?

# 4. Move #15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: the excursion-count parameter is a gauge, not a new invariant

I tried to break the symmetry of the a16 alternating-dual candidate without changing its Brownian/Bessel mechanism.

For a sign `eps in {+1,-1}` and integer `r>=1`, let `W_eps^(r)` be Brownian motion started at 0 and stopped at the first hit of `eps` occurring in the **r-th excursion from 0 that reaches eps**. Equivalently, recursively alternate: hit eps, return to 0, and repeat, stopping at the r-th hit of eps.

Its last-zero decomposition has the same form as a16:

`W_eps^(r) = C_eps^(r) concat eps U`,

where the terminal ascent U is the usual BES(3) ascent to 1, independent of the prefix, and the prefix is reversible. Excursion-Poisson reversal gives the same prefix reversibility used in a16: conditional on the local-time label of the r-th eps-reaching excursion, reflecting the earlier local-time interval preserves the marked excursion process, and Ito excursion measure is time-reversal invariant.

This appears at first to give a free label r that could make the alternating boundaries path-identifiable. It does not.

By the strong Markov property,

\[
\boxed{
W_+^{(r)}\ \stackrel d=\
W_+^{(1)}\oplus W_-^{(1)}\oplus W_+^{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_+^{(1)},
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

with exactly `2r-1` elementary alternating pieces; the analogous identity holds for `W_-^(r)`.

Therefore replacing the a16 elementary pieces by arbitrary deterministic excursion-count labels `r_k` merely **rebrackets the same underlying alternating Brownian pieces**. It does not create a new unmarked path law or a recoverable marker. The attempted asymmetry is gauge, not geometry.

This kills a tempting candidate-development route: increasing the number of successful excursions inside a piece cannot by itself certify non-2BM or identify the renewal skeleton.
