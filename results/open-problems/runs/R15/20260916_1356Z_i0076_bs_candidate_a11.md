# Exact source definitions and hypothesis

From Definition 2.1 of the source:

- `X` is an **FBM** if there are random times `S_n -> -infinity` a.s. such that for every `n`, `(X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n})_{t>=0}` is standard Brownian motion.
- `X` is a **BBM** iff `(X_{-t})_{t in R}` is an FBM.
- `X` is **2BM** if there exists a random time `S` such that `(X_{S+t}-X_S)_{t>=0}` and `(X_{S-t}-X_S)_{t>=0}` are independent standard Brownian motions.

Problem 7.2 asks exactly:

> Assume `X` is an FBM and BBM and there exists a random time `T` such that the processes `(X_{T+t}-X_T)_{t>=0}` and `(X_{T-t}-X_T)_{t>=0}` are independent. Do these assumptions imply that `X` is 2BM?

Crucially, Problem 7.2 does **not** assume either half at `T` is Brownian. Problem 7.5 is excluded from this track.

# New result I0076-R15-L1: the canonical seam of the frozen candidate is not independent

**Claim.** For the Burdzy–Scheutzow Theorem-6.1 candidate, at the canonical embedding time `T=M_0=0`, the two half-path processes are not independent. In fact the obstruction is already present in the first copied discrete block.

**Proof.** In the first stage of Theorem 6.1 the source fixes

`a_1=-1`, `b_1=1`,

and after choosing the deterministic integer `d_1>=1` it samples the fresh right block

`(V_2,...,V_{d_1+1})`

as independent fair coin tosses. It then defines the left block by the exact copy rule

`V_k = V_{k-a_1+d_1+b_1+1}` for `k in [a_1-d_1,a_1-1]`.

With `a_1=-1,b_1=1`, this is

`(V_{-d_1-1},...,V_{-2}) = (V_2,...,V_{d_1+1})`

coordinatewise.

In the Brownian embedding of Remark 2.6(i), `M_0=0`. Starting from a skeleton time, the corresponding half-path recovers the skeleton exit signs by successive first exits from the current integer level: on each embedded piece the Brownian prefix stays inside the unit interval until the terminal signed Bessel/excursion segment reaches the next integer. Hence the future half-path from `M_0` measurably determines `V_0,V_1,V_2,...`, while the past half-path measurably determines `V_{-1},V_{-2},...` (up to the deterministic reversal sign convention, which does not affect measurability).

Therefore the fair coin

`U := V_2 = V_{-d_1-1}`

is measurable with respect to **both** the future-half and past-half sigma-fields at `T=0`. The event `A={U=+1}` has probability `1/2`. If those two half sigma-fields were independent, then because `A` belongs to their intersection we would have

`P(A)=P(A cap A)=P(A)^2`,

forcing `P(A)` to be `0` or `1`, contradiction. Thus the canonical seam does not satisfy the Problem-7.2 independence hypothesis. `□`
