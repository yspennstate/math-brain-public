### 5.3 Midpoint multiplicities and planar four-caps

For each `m notin A`, let `r_m` be the number of unordered pairs `{x,y} subset A` whose midpoint is `m`.

In characteristic 3 the midpoint relation is

`m=-(x+y)`.

Because `A` is a cap, the midpoint of every pair lies outside `A`, and distinct pairs with the same midpoint are disjoint. Therefore

`sum_{m notin A} r_m = C(288,2)=41328`.                           (2)

Two distinct pairs with the same midpoint form a planar 4-cap. Conversely, every planar 4-subset of a cap has exactly one pairing into two pairs with the same midpoint. Thus if `q(A)` is the number of planar 4-subsets of `A`,

`q(A) = sum_{m notin A} C(r_m,2)`.                                (3)

Now count incidences between affine hyperplanes and 4-subsets of `A`.

A nonplanar 4-subset spans affine dimension 3 and lies in

`(3^(7-3)-1)/2 = 40`

affine hyperplanes.

A planar 4-subset spans affine dimension 2 and lies in

`(3^(7-2)-1)/2 = 121`

affine hyperplanes.

Hence

`sum_K C(h_K,4) = 40 C(288,4) + 81 q(A)`.                         (4)

### 5.6 Independent recovery of 111-rigidity, with prior-art label

**Lemma.** Every 111-cap `C` in `AG(6,3)` is a 112-cap minus one point.

This result was already announced by Thackeray at BCC 2021, so it is not claimed as new here.

**Proof.** Choose `p in C` and let `D=C-{p}`, a 110-cap. By Thackeray Theorem 3.9, there is a 112-cap `H` with

`D = H - {u,v}`

for two points `u,v in H`.

Suppose `p notin H`. The secant profile above says that every external point of `H` is the midpoint of at least 10 pairwise disjoint `H`-pairs. Removing only `u,v` can destroy at most two of those disjoint pairs, so at least eight pairs remain entirely inside `D`. One such pair together with its midpoint `p` is a collinear triple in `C`, contradiction.

Therefore `p in H`. Since `p notin D`, it is one of `u,v`, and `C` is `H` minus the other point. QED.

Consequently every 111-cap `C` has an anchor `a` in its ambient 6-flat, with

**55 complete antipodal pairs of `C` centered at `a`.**            (6)

The anchor is not in `C`.

### 5.8 One anchor supports at most 15 different 111-slices

Fix a complement point `a`.

Translate `a` to the origin. Each antipodal pair `{x,-x}` of `A` centered at `a` gives one projective point `[x] in PG(6,3)`. Let `S_a` be this set of pair-directions.

`S_a` is a projective cap. Indeed, if three of its projective points were collinear, after choosing signs one of the third directions would be represented by `u+v` or `u-v`; because both signs of every represented direction lie in `A`, three affine cap points would then sum to zero, giving a forbidden collinear triple.

Thus the known projective bound gives

`|S_a| <= m_2(PG(6,3)) <= 136`.                                  (8)

Now suppose the same anchor `a` belongs to `s` different 111-hyperplane slices.

For each such hyperplane `K_i`, its 111-cap contributes exactly 55 antipodal pairs centered at `a`. Therefore

`B_i := S_a intersect P(dir K_i)`

has exactly 55 projective points.

For two distinct hyperplanes `K_i,K_j`, their direction spaces intersect in a 5-dimensional vector space, whose projectivization is `PG(4,3)`. Hence

`|B_i intersect B_j| <= m_2(PG(4,3)) = 20`.                       (9)

For each `x in S_a`, let `d_x` be the number of the `B_i` containing `x`. Then

`sum_x d_x = 55s`

and, by (9),

`sum_x C(d_x,2) <= 20 C(s,2)`.                                   (10)

By Cauchy and (8),

`sum_x C(d_x,2)
 = (sum_x d_x^2 - 55s)/2

Combining (10) and (11) gives

`((55s)^2/136 - 55s)/2 <= 20 C(s,2)`.

For `s>0`, this simplifies exactly to

`(305/136)s <= 35`,

so

`s <= 952/61 < 16`.

Therefore

**one anchor supports at most 15 different 111-slices.**           (12)
