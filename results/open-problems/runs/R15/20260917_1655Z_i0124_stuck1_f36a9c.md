## 6. Method #27 — swap the quantifiers: one cap must realize all directions at once

The formal MacWilliams witness proves only an existential statement of the form

This is the useful quantifier swap. The next object is not another scalar histogram; it is a globally compatible labeling of the dual projective space.

Let two independent affine linear forms be `u,v`. Define the `3 x 3` codimension-two point-count matrix

\[
M_{ij}=|C\cap\{u=i,\ v=j\}|,\qquad i,j\in\mathbf F_3.
\]

Its nine entries are cap sizes in affine 5-flats, so every entry is at most the known five-dimensional maximum 45. The four projective directions in the pencil spanned by `u` and `v` are

\[
[u],\ [v],\ [u+v],\ [u-v].
\]

Their three-slice counts are respectively the row sums, column sums, and the two families of mod-3 diagonal sums of `M`. Thus one `3 x 3` matrix simultaneously constrains **four** one-direction types.

This observation is elementary and is not recorded as a new theorem; it is the exact compatibility datum that the previous one-variable relaxations discarded.
