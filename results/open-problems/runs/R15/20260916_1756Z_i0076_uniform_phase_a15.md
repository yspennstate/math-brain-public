# New-method attacks

## Move #9 — RANDOMIZE: add one independent phase to the anticipative offset

The discrete P2 proof worked because the offset from a certified random-walk origin to the independent seam is integer-valued: one unions over countably many deterministic shifts. Continuous time fails because an arbitrary anticipative real offset can select a Wiener-singular rooted law.

The new idea was to randomize only the **fractional part** of that offset by a single external `U ~ Unif[0,1]`. The integer part may remain arbitrarily anticipative. This completely restores the countable proof and yields the theorem below.

## Move #22 — READ THE FAILURES: splitting times explain why the continuous obstruction is real

Jacobsen's splitting-time theory, extending Williams-type Brownian decompositions, explicitly treats random times at which past and future become independent even though the post-time process may have a different Markov law. Its discussion also warns that continuous splitting times cannot generally be approximated monotonically by countable-range splitting times. This is exactly the point at which the discrete R15-D1 proof stops porting to `R`.

So a positive proof cannot silently smuggle in a stopping-time or countable-range approximation. Conversely, a counterexample can plausibly exploit a genuinely anticipative phase selector.

## Move #14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: the remaining defect is relative phase, not one-sided singularity after smoothing

The relevant group is real re-rooting of path space,

`(theta_s x)(t)=x(t+s)-x(s)`.

Run a14 reduced 2BM to equality with two-sided Wiener law on this action's invariant sigma-field. What remains is a two-sided **relative-phase/gluing** obstruction: smoothing the two halves separately is not the same as shifting the original two-sided path by one real number.

# R15-PR1 — uniform-phase smoothing theorem for an arbitrary FBM

**Status: PROVED.**

### Theorem

Let `X=(X_t)_{t in R}` be an FBM in the sense of Burdzy--Scheutzow. Let `T` be any a.s.-finite real random time. It need not be a stopping time and may depend on the entire two-sided path and all existing auxiliary randomness.

Extend the probability space by an independent random variable

`U ~ Uniform[0,1]`.

Define the future process rooted at the independently randomized phase

`F^{T,U}_t = X_{T+U+t}-X_{T+U}`, `t>=0`.

Then

`Law(F^{T,U}) << W_+`,

where `W_+` is standard one-sided Wiener measure on `C_0([0,infinity))`.

### Proof

Let `S_n -> -infinity` a.s. be FBM witness times and put

`B^(n)_t = X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n}`, `t>=0`.

For each fixed `n`, `B^(n)` has exactly the one-sided Wiener law `W_+`.

Let `N` be a Borel subset of one-sided path space with `W_+(N)=0`. Define

`E_n={S_n<T}`.

Because `S_n -> -infinity` and `T` is finite, `E_n` occurs eventually a.s.; in particular `union_n E_n` has probability one.

On `E_n`, set

`A_n=T-S_n >=0`.

Then pathwise

`F^{T,U} = theta^+_{A_n+U} B^(n)`,

where

`(theta^+_a b)(t)=b(a+t)-b(a)`, `t>=0`.

Write

`M_n=floor(A_n+U)` and `R_n={A_n+U} in [0,1)`.

The key point is that `A_n` may be arbitrarily anticipative, but the fractional part `R_n` is uniform and independent of the entire original sigma-field. Indeed, conditional on all original randomness, `A_n=a` is fixed and `{a+U}` is uniform on `[0,1)`. Hence `R_n` is independent of `B^(n)`.

For each deterministic integer `m>=0`,

`P(F^{T,U} in N, E_n, M_n=m)`

is at most

`P(theta^+_{m+R_n} B^(n) in N)`.

Using independence of `R_n` and `B^(n)` and deterministic shift-invariance of Brownian future laws,

`P(theta^+_{m+R_n}B^(n) in N)`
`= integral_0^1 W_+((theta^+_{m+r})^{-1}N) dr`
`= integral_0^1 W_+(N) dr`
`=0`.

Summing over the countable values `m=0,1,2,...` gives

`P(F^{T,U} in N, E_n)=0`.

Finally, since `union_n E_n` has probability one,

`P(F^{T,U} in N)=0`.

This holds for every Wiener-null Borel set `N`, hence

`Law(F^{T,U}) << W_+`. `QED`

### What the proof does and does not use

It uses only the FBM witness property and one fresh independent uniform random variable. It does **not** use:

- BBM;
- seam independence;
- a stopping-time assumption;
- any independence between `T`, the FBM witnesses, and the Brownian witness paths.

The proof is the continuous analogue of the discrete countable-offset proof after independently randomizing only the fractional phase.

# R15-PR1C — independent phase smoothing of both halves gives an artificial Wiener-dominated glue

**Status: PROVED, but explicitly NOT a physical re-rooting of X.**

Now use the actual Problem-7.2 seam `T`, so

`F_t=X_{T+t}-X_T`,
`G_t=X_{T-t}-X_T`

are independent.

Take independent uniforms `U_+,U_-`, independent also of `(F,G)`. Define

`F'_t = F_{U_++t}-F_{U_+}`,

`G'_t = G_{U_-+t}-G_{U_-}`.

R15-PR1 and R15-PR1B give

`Law(F') << W_+`,
`Law(G') << W_+`.

Because `(F,U_+)` and `(G,U_-)` are independent, `F'` and `G'` remain independent. Hence the artificial two-sided glue obtained by putting `G'` on negative time and `F'` on positive time has law

`nu << W_- tensor W_+ = P_2BM`.

By the shift-coupling compiler P1 from run a14, this artificial glued path is shift-couplable to two-sided Wiener law.

However, the artificial glue is **not** obtained by one real re-rooting of the original `X`: it independently deletes a random initial interval from each side of the source seam and then reglues the surviving tails. Therefore this does not prove Problem 7.2.

It gives a sharp reduction: after one independent phase smoothing on each side, all remaining obstruction is carried by the finite central splice / relative phase between the two independently selected tails.

# Why this does not move P3

P3 asks to remove the continuous anticipative-offset obstruction under the **exact** source hypotheses, with no external alteration of the physical path.

R15-PR1 proves that arbitrary one-sided singularity disappears after adding one independent uniform phase. R15-PR1C even makes the independently smoothed two-sided glue Wiener-dominated. But the two uniforms create two separate one-sided shifts. They do not define one physical time shift of `X`.

A re-rooting-invariant event can in principle depend on the **relative phase** of structures visible in the two tails. Independent phase smoothing can destroy such an invariant. An abstract periodic-marker model demonstrates the logical possibility: both half-laws can be separately phase-smoothed to stationary laws while the unrandomized glue has a deterministic relative phase visible in a translation-invariant relation between the two marker grids.

Thus P3 remains open. The missing theorem must control relative phase using the FBM+BBM structure itself, not merely each half separately.

# Counterexample-first explorations this run

I did not abandon the counterexample route merely because the positive discrete analogue succeeded.

### 1. Infinite-mean strongly decomposable Brownian pieces

Remark 7.6 of the source points directly at strongly decomposable infinite-mean constructions. I tested the simplest reversible candidate: concatenate independent Brownian pieces stopped at independent positive durations. That cannot refute 7.2. At every piece boundary the future and past are independent Brownian continuations, so a common 2BM origin already exists.

### 2. Brownian splitting-time pieces

A non-stopping Brownian splitting time can give independent pre/post fragments with a non-Brownian post law (Williams/Jacobsen-type decomposition), which is exactly the kind of seam Problem 7.2 permits. But using one such split inside a genuinely two-sided Brownian path is useless: the old two-sided Brownian origin remains elsewhere, so the resulting process is still 2BM as a process law.

To obtain a counterexample one would need an **infinite identifiable pasting** of such splits that destroys every 2BM origin while retaining separate FBM and BBM origin sequences. I found no verified construction of that type this run.

### 3. Offset reversible tilings

I tried an alternating-piece picture in which forward Brownian blocks and backward Brownian blocks have offset boundaries, while the source seam lies inside a forward block. For blocks split into two independent aggregate pieces, the route collapses: if an offset reverse pairing has Brownian law, independence makes the same-block reverse pairing have the same law, producing a common Brownian boundary and hence a 2BM origin. The only apparent escape is anticipative dependence of the Brownian witness origin on the opposite tail -- again the exact continuous obstruction identified by the splitting-time literature.

So the counterexample search and the positive search point to the same bottleneck: **anticipative relative phase**.

# Nearest solved analogue and what it has that Problem 7.2 lacks

The full discrete analogue R15-D1 is solved because integer offsets reduce a path-dependent seam selection to a **countable** union of deterministic null events.

The closest continuous technology is splitting-time / Palm / unbiased-shift theory. Those works provide either:

- a structural characterization of selected times by conditional independence / Markov post-laws; or
- a mass-stationary allocation rule that balances a random measure and produces an unbiased Brownian shift.

Problem 7.2 lacks exactly such a balance law for the source seam `T`. FBM+BBM supplies Brownian origins on both sides, but no theorem yet forces their relative phase at the independent seam to be mass-stationary.
