# Exact source definitions and hypothesis

From Definition 2.1 of Burdzy–Scheutzow:

- `X=(X_t)_{t in R}` is **FBM** if there are random times `S_n -> -infinity` a.s. such that for each `n`, `(X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n})_{t>=0}` is standard Brownian motion.
- `X` is **BBM** iff `(X_{-t})_{t in R}` is FBM.
- `X` is **2BM** if there exists a random time `S` such that `(X_{S+t}-X_S)_{t>=0}` and `(X_{S-t}-X_S)_{t>=0}` are independent standard Brownian motions.

Problem 7.2 asks:

> Assume that `X` is an FBM and BBM and there exists a random time `T` such that `(X_{T+t}-X_T)_{t>=0}` and `(X_{T-t}-X_T)_{t>=0}` are independent. Do these assumptions imply that `X` is 2BM?

The independence hypothesis alone does **not** say either half at `T` is Brownian.

# I0076-R15-L2 — a finite-list common-word lemma

**Status: PROVED.**

Let `A` and `B` be independent random elements. Let `U` be uniform on a finite alphabet of size `N`; `U` may be arbitrarily dependent on `(A,B)` and on extra randomness. Suppose `L_A(A)` and `L_B(B)` are random lists, each of cardinality at most `K`. If an event `E` has the property

`E subseteq {U in L_A(A) intersection L_B(B)}`,

then

`P(E) <= K / sqrt(N)`.                                      (L2.1)

## Proof

For each alphabet symbol `u`, set

`p_u=P(u in L_A(A))`, `q_u=P(u in L_B(B))`.

Since `A` and `B` are independent,

`P(u in L_A(A), u in L_B(B)) = p_u q_u`.

Also `P(U=u)=1/N`. Hence

`P(E,U=u) <= min(1/N, p_u q_u)`.

For nonnegative numbers,

`min(1/N,p_u q_u) <= sqrt(p_u q_u/N)`.

Summing and applying Cauchy–Schwarz,

`P(E)`
` <= N^(-1/2) sum_u sqrt(p_u q_u)`
` <= N^(-1/2) sqrt((sum_u p_u)(sum_u q_u))`.

But the expected cardinalities of both lists are at most `K`, so

`sum_u p_u = E|L_A| <= K`,
`sum_u q_u = E|L_B| <= K`.

This proves (L2.1). `□`

The useful point is that `U` need **not** be independent of either half. The only independence used is between the two half-data objects `A` and `B`.

# I0076-R15-L3 — the Theorem-6.1 discrete candidate has no independent integer seam

**Status: PROVED.**

Let `Z` be the discrete process constructed in Theorem 6.1, with increments

`V_k=Z_{k+1}-Z_k in {-1,+1}`.

There is **no integer-valued random time `S`** such that the two increment-half processes

`A=(V_{S+k})_{k>=0}`

and

`B=(V_{S-1-k})_{k>=0}`

are independent.

Equivalently, there is no random integer cut at which the cumulative future and past processes of `Z` are independent, even without asking either half to be simple random walk.

## Proof

At stage `n` of Theorem 6.1 the source chooses deterministic integers `c_n,d_n` with

`P(|a_n| or b_n >= c_n) < 1/n^2`,                    (6.1)

and

`(4c_n+1)^2 2^(-d_n) < 1/n^2`.                      (6.1')

It then generates the fresh right block

`U_n=(V_{b_n+1},...,V_{b_n+d_n})`

as `d_n` independent fair coins and copies it coordinatewise to

`(V_{a_n-d_n},...,V_{a_n-1})`.

Thus `U_n` is uniform on an alphabet of size

`N_n=2^(d_n)`.

Assume for contradiction that an integer random time `S` has independent half-increment sequences `A,B` as above.

Define

`E_n={a_n<S<b_n and |a_n| or b_n < c_n}`.

The construction has `a_{n+1}<a_n<0<b_n<b_{n+1}` with integer endpoints, hence `a_n -> -infinity` and `b_n -> +infinity` pathwise. Therefore, for every finite integer random `S`,

`P(a_n<S<b_n) ->1`.

Together with (6.1),

`P(E_n)->1`.                                           (L3.1)

On `E_n`, the right copied word occurs in the future half `A` beginning at relative offset

`i_n=b_n+1-S`.

Because `a_n<S<b_n` and `|a_n|,b_n<c_n`,

`1 <= i_n <= 2c_n`.

The left copy occurs in the past half in reverse order. Indeed, if

`j_n=S-a_n`,

then

`(B_{j_n},...,B_{j_n+d_n-1})`

is the reversal of `U_n`, and again

`1 <= j_n <=2c_n`.

From `A` alone make the list `L_A(A)` of all length-`d_n` words beginning at offsets `1,...,2c_n`. From `B` alone make `L_B(B)` by taking the reversals of all length-`d_n` words beginning at those same candidate offsets. Both lists have size at most

`K_n=2c_n`.

On `E_n`,

`U_n in L_A(A) intersection L_B(B)`.

Apply L2 with `N=2^(d_n)` and `K=2c_n`:

`P(E_n) <= 2c_n 2^(-d_n/2)`.

But (6.1') implies, after taking square roots,

`(4c_n+1)2^(-d_n/2) < 1/n`,

so in particular

`P(E_n) < 1/n`.                                      (L3.2)

Equations (L3.1) and (L3.2) contradict each other. Therefore no such random integer `S` exists. `□`

## Relation to source Proposition 6.2

Proposition 6.2's discrete argument rules out an independent cut when the **future half is additionally simple symmetric random walk**. L3 removes that marginal-law assumption for the particular Theorem-6.1 discrete candidate. It is candidate-specific, not a general theorem about all FRW+BRW processes.

# I0076-R15-L4 — no random skeleton time can solve the missing Brownian obligation

**Status: PROVED for skeleton-valued times in the frozen Brownian embedding.**

Let `M_k` be the skeleton times in Remark 2.6(i). There is no integer-valued random `S` such that

`T=M_S`

makes the two Brownian half-processes

`(X_{T+t}-X_T)_{t>=0}` and `(X_{T-t}-X_T)_{t>=0}`

independent.

## Proof

Starting at a skeleton time `M_S`, each embedded forward segment remains strictly within distance `1` of its current skeleton level until it reaches the next skeleton level at distance exactly `1`. Therefore the future half-path alone recovers successively the signs

`V_S,V_{S+1},...`

by taking successive first exits of distance `1` from the current recovered skeleton level.

Going backward, the time-reversible Brownian/Bessel pieces used in Remark 2.6 have the analogous property: the past half-path alone recovers

`V_{S-1},V_{S-2},...`

(up to a deterministic sign/reversal convention, irrelevant for sigma-fields).

Hence independence of the two continuous half-paths at `M_S` would imply independence of the discrete half-increment sequences in L3. L3 says that is impossible. `□`

Thus the frozen candidate's missing witness, if it exists at all, must occur strictly away from the source's identifiable skeleton seams. In particular, changing from the canonical `M_0` to another random skeleton origin cannot rescue the candidate.
