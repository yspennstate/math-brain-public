## 2. New-method attack #5 — parameterize the 288 proof by N

The previous no-288 proof suggested that the mechanism should be viewed as a family indexed by the hypothetical cap size \(N\), rather than as a one-off 288 argument. I therefore deformed the exact incidence/midpoint calculation from

\[

and asked where the argument actually breaks.

Let \(C\subset AG(7,3)=\mathbb F_3^7\) be an \(N\)-cap, first with \(N\in\{287,286\}\). There are 1093 hyperplane directions and 3279 affine hyperplanes. Write

\[
h_K=|C\cap K|.
\]

A point lies in 1093 hyperplanes, a pair in 364, and every triple of cap points is affinely independent and lies in 121 hyperplanes. Consequently

\[
\sum_K h_K=1093N,
\]

\[
\sum_K\binom{h_K}{2}=364\binom N2,
\qquad
\sum_K\binom{h_K}{3}=121\binom N3.
\]

Center at \(\mu=N/3\), putting \(x_K=h_K-\mu\), and define

\[
D_2(N)=\sum_Kx_K^2,
\qquad
D_3(N)=\sum_Kx_K^3.
\]

Exact algebra gives

\[
D_2(287)=\frac{545300}{3},
\qquad
D_3(287)=-\frac{23012234}{9},

and

\[
D_2(286)=\frac{543686}{3},
\qquad
D_3(286)=-\frac{22768174}{9}.

These are exact rationals, not numerical estimates.

The dimension-6 maximum gives \(c\le112\). Moreover, `b+c>=222` is impossible: Thackeray Proposition 4.1(b) would give \(a\le55\), hence \(b+c=N-a\ge231\), whereas \(b+c\le224\) from \(b,c\le112\). Therefore

## 5. New-method attack #13 — midpoint energy is the monovariant

Every hyperplane slice with at least 110 cap points supplies a high-multiplicity midpoint.

Indeed, choose any 110 points from such a slice. They form a 110-cap in `AG(6,3)`, so Thackeray Theorem 3.9 embeds them in a 112-cap obtained by adding two cap points. Potechin proved that 112-caps in `AG(6,3)` are unique up to affine equivalence. The standard doubled-Hill representative has an anchor which is the midpoint of 56 antipodal cap pairs; this exact midpoint profile was already independently executed in the preceding R15 certificate. Deleting two cap points destroys at most two of those pairs. Therefore every `>=110` slice has an anchor \(m\) with at least

\[
\boxed{54}
\]

complete antipodal cap pairs centered at \(m\). Since those pair endpoints belong to \(C\), the anchor itself is outside \(C\).

Fix one exterior anchor \(m\). The directions of all antipodal cap pairs centered at \(m\) form a projective cap \(S_m\subset PG(6,3)\): three collinear projective directions would allow signs giving three collinear points of \(C\). Hence the published bound gives

\[
r_m=|S_m|\le136.
\tag{10}
\]

Suppose \(s\) large hyperplanes choose the same anchor \(m\). From each choose exactly 54 of its pair-directions. Two distinct hyperplanes through \(m\) intersect projectively in `PG(4,3)`, so two chosen direction sets meet in at most

\[
m_2(4,3)=20.
\]

If \(d_x\) is the number of chosen hyperplanes containing direction \(x\), then

\[
\sum_xd_x=54s,
\qquad
\sum_x\binom{d_x}{2}\le20\binom s2.
\]

Cauchy therefore gives

\[
(54s)^2
\le
r_m\sum_xd_x^2
\le
r_m\bigl(54s+20s(s-1)\bigr),
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{

\left\lceil\frac{54^2s}{54+20(s-1)}\right\rceil.

\tag{11}
\]

At \(s=24\), the right side is 137, contradicting (10). Thus

\[
\boxed{\text{one anchor supports at most 23 large slices}.}
\tag{12}
\]

This converts the forced number of large slices into forced high midpoint energy `Q(C)`.
