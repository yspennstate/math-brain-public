## 2. Idea before code — Method #3: prove something stronger

The previous run already exposed the hazard-rate identity for the pointwise arc ratio. This run asks which part of arc-transitivity is actually necessary.

### R05-I0030-C5 — averaged-neighbor monotonicity on every finite connected regular graph

**STATUS: PROVED. NOVELTY UNVERIFIED.**

Let `G` be any finite connected `d`-regular simple graph with `d>=1`, not assumed vertex-transitive. Let

\[
P=A/d,\qquad H_t=e^{t(P-I)}.
\]

Fix a vertex `x` and define

\[
a_x(t)=H_t(x,x),
\]

and the normalized average over its neighbors

\[
\bar r_x(t)
=
\frac{1}{d\,a_x(t)}
\sum_{y\sim x}H_t(x,y).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\bar r_x(t)\text{ is strictly increasing for all }t\ge0,}
\tag{C5.1}
\]

with

\[
\bar r_x(0)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}\bar r_x(t)=1.
\]

#### Proof

Because `H_t` commutes with `P`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_x'(t)
&=(H_t(P-I))_{xx}\\
&=\frac1d\sum_{y\sim x}H_t(x,y)-a_x(t).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\bar r_x(t)=1+\frac{a_x'(t)}{a_x(t)}.
}
\tag{C5.2}
\]

Put `L=I-P`. Since `G` is regular and undirected, `L` is real symmetric and positive semidefinite. Let `mu_x` be its local spectral measure at `delta_x`. Then

\[
a_x(t)=\langle\delta_x,e^{-tL}\delta_x\rangle
=\int e^{-\lambda t}\,d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

Define the tilted probability measure

\[
d\nu_{x,t}(\lambda)
=
\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{a_x(t)}d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

Differentiating (C5.2),

\[
\boxed{
\bar r_x'(t)
=\frac{a_xa_x''-(a_x')^2}{a_x^2}
=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{x,t}}(\lambda).
}
\tag{C5.3}
\]

This variance is strictly positive at every finite `t`: connectedness gives positive local mass `1/|V|` at eigenvalue `0`, while `L_{xx}=1` forces positive local mass on the positive spectrum. Hence C5.1.

At `t=0`, all off-diagonal heat entries vanish, so `bar r_x(0)=0`. As `t->infinity`, connectedness gives `H_t(x,y)->1/|V|` for every `y`, hence `bar r_x(t)->1`. QED.

### Why C5 is source-relevant

On an arc-transitive graph the stabilizer of `x` is transitive on the neighbors of `x`; therefore all `H_t(x,y)`, `y~x`, are equal. Hence `bar r_x(t)` is exactly the frozen pointwise arc ratio `r_{xy}(t)`.

Thus the source conclusion is the edge-homogeneous specialization of a theorem valid on **every regular graph after averaging over the neighbor sphere**. This is a different and stronger route into Open Problem 7.30 than extending a finite census.

The mechanism is elementary and closely related to the classical reliability fact that mixtures of exponentials have decreasing failure rate (Proschan 1963; Mi 1998). I make no novelty claim for C5.

### R05-I0030-C6 — pointwise adjacent monotonicity on every finite connected 1-walk-regular graph

**STATUS: PROVED. NOVELTY UNVERIFIED.**

Let `G` be a finite connected 1-walk-regular graph. Equivalently for present purposes, `G` is regular and for every integer `m>=0`,

- `(A^m)_{xx}` is independent of the vertex `x`, and
- `(A^m)_{xy}` is independent of the edge `x~y`.

Then for every edge `x~y`,

\[
\boxed{
 t\longmapsto \frac{H_t(x,y)}{H_t(x,x)}
 \text{ is strictly increasing from }0\text{ to }1.
}
\tag{C6.1}
\]

#### Proof

The power series

\[
H_t=e^{-t}\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{(t/d)^m}{m!}A^m
\]

shows that there are scalar functions `a(t),b(t)` such that

\[
H_t(x,x)=a(t)\quad\text{for every }x,
\]

and

\[
H_t(x,y)=b(t)\quad\text{for every edge }x\sim y.
\]

Applying C5 at any vertex,

\[
\bar r_x(t)=\frac{b(t)}{a(t)}.
\]

C5 gives strict increase from `0` to `1`. QED.

Every arc-transitive graph is 1-walk-regular, so C6 contains the frozen R05 theorem. The 1-walk-regular condition itself is standard graph-theoretic prior art; recent work such as Godsil--Sun--Zhang (2025) and Fan--Wang--Zhang (arXiv:2606.10404) uses it extensively. I found no source explicitly stating C6 as a heat-ratio theorem, but a bounded negative search is not evidence of novelty.

## 4. Method #15: deliberately break the symmetry — known sharpness boundary

The stronger theorem also identifies which symmetry is doing the work. I searched for a source-valid failure after weakening arc/edge homogeneity.

Regev--Shinkar, *A Counterexample to Monotonicity of Relative Mass in Random Walks*, ECP 21 (2016), arXiv:1506.08631, give a **weighted Cayley graph** whose relative-mass ratio is not monotone. Their weighted lamplighter construction has two edge types: hypercube moves and lamp toggles. They choose

- `u`: lamplighter at the origin, all lamps off;
- `v`: lamplighter at the origin, only the origin lamp on.

By their definition these two vertices differ by one toggle edge, so **`u` and `v` are adjacent**, yet for suitable `t1<t2`,

\[
r_{u,v}(t_1)>r_{u,v}(t_2).
\]

This is a known result, not an R05 counterexample.

It gives a useful sharpness message: vertex-transitivity/Cayley symmetry alone does not force pointwise **adjacent** normalized-heat monotonicity once edge types/weights break the neighbor-sphere homogeneity. It does **not** refute Aldous--Fill 7.30, whose frozen R05 class is unweighted arc-transitive, and it does not conflict with C5 because C5 concerns the neighbor average for the uniform regular walk.

The same paper converts its example to an unweighted Cayley graph while retaining an all-pairs failure, but its displayed lifted failing pair is not used here as an adjacent-pair claim.
