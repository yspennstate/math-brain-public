### R05-I0030-C2 — uniform laziness is exactly a time deformation

**STATUS: PROVED.**

For `0<theta<=1`, replace simple random walk by its uniformly lazy version

\[
P_\theta=(1-\theta)I+\theta P.
\]

The rate-one continuous-time generator is

\[
P_\theta-I=\theta(P-I),
\]

hence exactly

\[
H_t^{(\theta)}
=\exp(t(P_\theta-I))
=H_{\theta t}.
\]

Therefore for an arc `x~y`,

\[
r_\theta(t)
:=\frac{H_t^{(\theta)}(x,y)}{H_t^{(\theta)}(x,x)}
=r(\theta t).
\]

Using the retained spectral-variance formula for `r'`,

\[
\partial_t r_\theta(t)
=\theta\,\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\theta t}}(\lambda)>0,
\]

and, for `t>0`,

\[
\partial_\theta r_\theta(t)
=t\,\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\theta t}}(\lambda)>0.
\]

Thus the frozen conclusion is not an artefact of choosing the non-lazy rate normalization: uniform laziness only rescales time. This is a normalization robustness statement, not a new source target.

## 3. Method #11 — dualize to a reliability hazard / two-copy spectral energy

This was the most useful conceptual route of the run because it exposes how elementary the positivity mechanism actually is and therefore sharpens the prior-art warning.

Let

\[
a(t)=H_t(x,x),\qquad b(t)=H_t(x,y),\qquad x\sim y.
\]

Arc-transitivity gives vertex-constant diagonal and edge-constant adjacent entry. The heat equation gives the exact bridge

\[
a'(t)=b(t)-a(t),
\qquad
r(t)=\frac{b(t)}{a(t)}=1+\frac{a'(t)}{a(t)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `mu_x` be the local spectral measure of

\[
L=I-P\succeq0
\]

at `delta_x`. Then

\[
a(t)=\int e^{-\lambda t}\,d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

Define the exponentially tilted probability measure

\[
d\nu_t(\lambda)
=\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{a(t)}d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

The reliability-theory hazard of this (possibly defective, because of the zero eigenvalue) finite mixture of exponentials is

\[
h(t)=-\frac{a'(t)}{a(t)}
=\mathbb E_{\nu_t}\lambda.
\]

Thus (3.1) is simply

\[
\boxed{r(t)=1-h(t).}
\tag{R05-I0030-C3a}
\]

Mixtures of exponentials have decreasing failure rate; here the complete proof is one line:

\[
h'(t)=-\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_t}(\lambda).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{r'(t)=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_t}(\lambda)>0.}
\tag{R05-I0030-C3b}
\]

Strictness holds at every finite `t` because on a connected nontrivial finite graph the local spectral measure has positive mass at eigenvalue `0` and positive mass on the positive spectrum.

There is also an exact dual/two-copy form:

\[
\boxed{
 r'(t)=
 \frac{1}{2a(t)^2}
 \iint (\lambda-\mu)^2e^{-t(\lambda+\mu)}
 \,d\mu_x(\lambda)d\mu_x(\mu).
}
\tag{R05-I0030-C3c}
\]

This representation makes positivity manifest without a graph coupling.

## 4. Method #23 — assume a stronger shape than the proposer asked for, and break it

The source asks only for **some** stronger monotonicity. A natural temptation after proving increasing `r(t)` is to expect a clean shape such as global concavity. That stronger statement is false on a graph satisfying every original frozen graph hypothesis.

Differentiating the tilted variance once more gives

\[
r''(t)=-\kappa_3(\nu_t),
\]

where `kappa_3` is the third centered cumulant. At `t=0`, for a simple `d`-regular graph,

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_x}\lambda=L_{xx}=1,
\]

and therefore

\[
\kappa_3(\mu_x)
=[(L-I)^3]_{xx}
=-\frac{(A^3)_{xx}}{d^3}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
r''(0)=\frac{(A^3)_{xx}}{d^3}
=\frac{2\,\tau_x}{d^3},
}
\tag{R05-I0030-C4}
\]

where `tau_x` is the number of triangles containing `x`.

Thus every source-valid arc-transitive graph containing a triangle has **strictly convex** adjacent heat ratio at time zero. In particular, on `K_3`,

\[
r''(0)=\frac{2}{2^3}=\frac14>0.
\]

So the invented strengthening “the adjacent normalized heat ratio is globally concave” is false even for `K_3`. This does **not** refute Aldous–Fill 7.30 and does **not** weaken the frozen monotonicity theorem; it delineates the theorem's shape.

For context, the genuinely stronger **all-pairs** normalized-heat monotonicity is known to fail on a Cayley graph: O. Regev and I. Shinkar, *A counterexample to monotonicity of relative mass in random walks*, ECP 21 (2016), DOI `10.1214/16-ECP4392`, arXiv:1506.08631. That counterexample addresses the later Peres all-pairs question; I found no source establishing that their counterexample is arc-transitive, so it is not imported as an AF7.30 counterexample. Price proves all-pairs monotonicity for finite weighted Abelian Cayley graphs (ECP 22 (2017), arXiv:1612.07306). Nica, Kubo–Namba, and Liu–Zhang treat further stronger all-pairs classes.

## 7. Independent R06 audit

Newest R06 package read:

It contains a new concrete theorem for the tetrapentagonal medial graph `T=M(H_{5,4})`:

\[
\Phi_E(T)\ge\sqrt3-1.
\tag{R06-I0056-MEDIAL-LOWER-01}
\]

I independently audited this lower bound.

Let `S` be a finite edge set of the primal quadrangulation `R=H_{5,4}`, viewed as a vertex set in its medial graph. Let `B=|partial_E^T S|`. For every primal face, record the cyclic 0/1 word of selected boundary edges. The number of transitions around all faces is exactly `B`. If `Q` is the number of partial faces, each partial 4-cycle has at least two transitions, so `Q<=B/2`.

Let `F` be the number of full faces. In the dual `R*=H_{4,5}`, every outer neighbor of the set of full faces is partial. The retained/audited exact dual vertex Cheeger constant is

\[
h_*=2/\sqrt3.
\]

Hence

\[
F\le Q/h_*\le B/(2h_*).
\]

The R06 closure adds any outside medial vertex with at least two selected medial neighbors. In a 4-regular graph this changes edge boundary by `4-2r<=0`. Its preliminary positive lower bound forces finite termination. At a terminal set no partial primal quadrilateral may have pattern `1110` or `1010` up to rotation; hence every partial face has either one selected edge or two adjacent selected edges. Thus each partial face has exactly two transitions and at most two selected edges, so

\[
B=2Q,
\]

and, double-counting selected edge/face incidences,

\[
2|S|
\le4F+2Q
\le\frac{2B}{h_*}+B
=(\sqrt3+1)B.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{B}{|S|}\ge\frac2{1+\sqrt3}=\sqrt3-1.
\]

The closure never increases boundary and only enlarges the set, so the same lower bound holds for the original set. I found no gap in this argument at its stated scope.
