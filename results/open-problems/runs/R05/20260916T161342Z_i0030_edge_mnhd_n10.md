## 4. Method #18: the edge theorem is a 1-walk-regular spectral-variance identity

This is a **mechanism observation, not a new source target and not a novelty claim**.

A graph is 1-walk-regular when, for every `m`, the diagonal entries of `A^m` are constant and the entries of `A^m` on edges are constant. Standard graph literature records that every vertex- and edge-transitive graph is 1-walk-regular, hence every arc-transitive graph is.

For any finite connected `d`-regular 1-walk-regular graph define

\[
a(t)=H_t(x,x),\qquad b(t)=H_t(x,y)\quad(x\sim y).
\]

The exponential power series and 1-walk-regularity make `a,b` independent of the chosen vertex/edge. The Kolmogorov equation gives

\[
a'(t)=b(t)-a(t),
\]

so

\[
r(t):=\frac{b(t)}{a(t)}=1+(\log a(t))'.
\]

Let `mu_x` be the local spectral measure of the normalized Laplacian `L=I-P` at `delta_x`. Then

\[
a(t)=\int e^{-\lambda t}\,d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

Introduce the exponentially tilted probability measure

\[
d\nu_t(\lambda)=\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{a(t)}\,d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

Then the edge heat ratio has the exact probabilistic form

\[
\boxed{
 r(t)=1-\mathbb E_{\nu_t}\lambda,
 \qquad
 r'(t)=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_t}(\lambda).
}
\]

For a finite connected nontrivial graph, the local spectral measure has positive mass at `0` and positive mass away from `0`, so the tilted variance is strictly positive at every finite time. This is the previous proof compressed to a one-line monovariant.
