## 2. Method #20 — why the regular-graph upgrade fails

The preceding run proved that for every finite connected `d`-regular graph,
\[
\bar r_x(t)
=
\frac{d^{-1}\sum_{y\sim x}H_t(x,y)}{H_t(x,x)}
\]
is strictly increasing. The natural but invalid next step would infer monotonicity for each individual neighboring ratio.

Fix any such graph and starting vertex `x`. Define
\[
q_z(t)=\frac{H_t(x,z)}{H_t(x,x)},\qquad
\bar r_x(t)=\frac1d\sum_{y\sim x}q_y(t).
\]
The heat equation gives the exact nonlinear normalized evolution
\[
\boxed{
q_z'(t)=\frac1d\sum_{w\sim z}q_w(t)-q_z(t)\bar r_x(t).
}
\tag{R05-I0030-C8}
\]
Indeed, with `h_z=H_t(x,z)` and `a=H_t(x,x)`,
\[
h_z'=d^{-1}\sum_{w\sim z}h_w-h_z,\qquad
a'/a=\bar r_x-1,
\]
and division gives C8.

For neighbors of `x`, put
\[
\delta_y(t)=q_y(t)-\bar r_x(t).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\sum_{y\sim x}\delta_y(t)=0\quad\text{for every }t.}
\tag{C8.1}
\]
Also, connectedness gives `q_y(0)=0` and `q_y(t)->1`, so
\[
\boxed{\int_0^\infty q_y'(t)\,dt=1\quad\text{for every neighbor }y.}
\tag{C8.2}
\]
Meanwhile
\[
\bar r_x'(t)=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{x,t}}(\lambda)>0.
\]
Thus positivity is guaranteed only after averaging. An individual neighbor may have a negative derivative interval and compensate elsewhere. Arc/edge homogeneity kills all deviations `delta_y` identically; regularity alone does not.

**R05-I0030-C8: PROVED.** It is an exact evolution/conservation identity, not a novelty claim.
