## 2. Idea before code — #8 discretize the continuous-time mechanism

The previous run rewrote the continuous-time result as a spectral-order statement. The natural opposite move is to ask what survives in **discrete time**, where the non-lazy walk can have negative spectrum and periodicity. Instead of changing the source target, this tests exactly which part of continuization carries the monotonicity.

For a finite connected arc-transitive `d`-regular graph put

\[
S=A/d.
\]

For a laziness parameter `h in [0,1)` define

\[
Q_h=hI+(1-h)S.
\]

For an arc `x~y`, let

\[
a_m=(Q_h^m)_{xx},\qquad
b_m=(Q_h^m)_{xy},\qquad
r_m^{(h)}=b_m/a_m.
\]

Arc-transitivity makes `a_m` independent of `x` and `b_m` independent of the arc.

The key one-step identity is

\[
\boxed{a_{m+1}=h a_m+(1-h)b_m.}
\tag{C14.1}
\]

Indeed, `Q_h` is a polynomial in `S`, so it commutes with `S`, and

\[
(SQ_h^m)_{xx}
=\frac1d\sum_{z\sim x}(Q_h^m)_{zx}=b_m.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
r_m^{(h)}=
\frac{a_{m+1}/a_m-h}{1-h}.
}
\tag{C14.2}
\]

Thus the entire adjacent-ratio question becomes monotonicity of the consecutive return-moment quotient `a_{m+1}/a_m`.

## 3. #13 find what only increases — Hausdorff moment log-convexity

### R05-I0030-C14 — sharp lazy discrete-time analogue

**STATUS: PROVED.**

**Theorem.** Let `G` be a finite connected nontrivial arc-transitive graph, and let

\[
Q_h=hI+(1-h)A/d.
\]

If

\[
\frac12\le h<1,
\]

then for every arc `x~y`,

\[
\boxed{
m\longmapsto
\frac{(Q_h^m)_{xy}}{(Q_h^m)_{xx}}
\text{ is non-decreasing for }m=0,1,2,\ldots .
}
\tag{C14.3}
\]

For `h>1/2` the increase is strict at every step. The universal threshold `1/2` is sharp: for every `0<h<1/2`, the arc-transitive graph `K_2` violates monotonicity already from `m=1` to `m=2`.

### Proof

Since the eigenvalues of `S=A/d` lie in `[-1,1]`, the eigenvalues of `Q_h` lie in

\[
[2h-1,1].
\]

For `h>=1/2`, `Q_h` is positive semidefinite. Let `mu_x` be its local spectral measure at `x`. Then

\[
a_m=(Q_h^m)_{xx}=\int_{[0,1]} q^m\,d\mu_x(q).
\tag{C14.4}
\]

Hence `(a_m)` is a Hausdorff moment sequence. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\boxed{a_{m+1}^2\le a_m a_{m+2}.}
\tag{C14.5}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\left(\int q^{m+1}\,d\mu_x\right)^2
\le
\left(\int q^m\,d\mu_x\right)
\left(\int q^{m+2}\,d\mu_x\right).
\]

All `a_m` are positive because the stationary eigenvalue `q=1` has local mass `1/|V|`. Thus (C14.5) is equivalent to

\[
\frac{a_{m+1}}{a_m}
\le
\frac{a_{m+2}}{a_{m+1}}.
\]

Equation (C14.2) now proves (C14.3).

If `h>1/2`, every spectral value of `Q_h` is strictly positive. The local measure contains the stationary atom `q=1` with mass `1/|V|` and, because the graph is nontrivial, also has positive mass on at least one distinct eigenvalue `q<1`. Therefore equality in Cauchy--Schwarz is impossible and (C14.5) is strict for every `m`; hence `r_{m+1}^{(h)}>r_m^{(h)}`.

At the boundary `h=1/2`, zero spectral values can cause equality after a finite step, so the correct universal assertion is non-decrease.

For sharpness, take `K_2`. Its `Q_h` eigenvalues are

\[
1,\qquad c=2h-1.
\]

Thus

\[
a_m=\frac{1+c^m}{2},\qquad
b_m=\frac{1-c^m}{2},
\qquad
r_m=\frac{1-c^m}{1+c^m}.
\tag{C14.6}
\]

If `0<h<1/2`, then `-1<c<0`, and

\[
r_1-r_2
=
\frac{2c(c-1)}{(c+1)(c^2+1)}>0.
\tag{C14.7}
\]

So `r_2<r_1`. This proves that no smaller universal laziness threshold works even inside the source class. `QED`
