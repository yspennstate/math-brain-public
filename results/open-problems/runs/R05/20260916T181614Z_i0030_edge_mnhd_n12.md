`https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch7.S3.html`.
Aldous–Fill Proposition 7.29 is explicitly continuous-time random walk on a distance-regular graph. It states that if

\[
P_v(X_t=w)=q(t,d(v,w)),
\]

then (i) `d -> q(t,d)` is non-increasing for fixed `t`, and (ii)

\[
 t\longmapsto q(t,d)/q(t,0)
\]

is non-decreasing for fixed distance `d`. Immediately afterward Open Problem 7.30 asks:

> Does random walk on an arc-transitive graph have some monotonicity property stronger than that of Corollary 7.3?

Corollary 7.3 was also rechecked at
`https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch7.S1.html`: for a symmetric reversible chain,

\[
P_i(X_{2t}=j)\le P_i(X_{2t}=i),
\]

with the analogous inequality for every time after continuization.

The canonical intake row `I0030` records exactly the same source question and still labels it `open_per_source`.

The frozen R05 source-matching statement remains:

> Let `G` be a finite simple connected arc-transitive graph, let `P=A/d` be simple random walk, and let `H_t=e^{t(P-I)}`. For every arc `x~y`,
> \[
> r_{xy}(t)=H_t(x,y)/H_t(x,x)
> \]
> is strictly increasing on `[0,infinity)`, with `r_{xy}(0)=0` and `r_{xy}(t)->1`.

The analytic theorem is already PROVED in the initial R05 report and has an independent R04 mathematical audit. The proof is retained, not re-derived: arc-transitivity gives vertex-constant `a(t)=H_t(x,x)` and edge-constant `b(t)=H_t(x,y)`, the heat equation gives

\[
a'(t)=b(t)-a(t),
\qquad
b(t)/a(t)=1+(\log a(t))',
\]

and the local spectral measure of `I-P` gives

\[
(\log a(t))''=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_t}(\lambda)>0
\]

for every finite `t` on a connected nontrivial graph.

### #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

Rather than merely check more highly symmetric examples, I looked inside the complete order-12 arc-transitive class for graphs that are **not distance-regular**, because those are source-valid adversaries lying genuinely outside Proposition 7.29's hypothesis.

This succeeded. In Marston Conder's order-12 census, graphs 2, 3, and 5 are not distance-regular. Exact root-1 distance-two witnesses are:

- graph 2: vertices 3 and 4 have intersection triples `(1,2,1)` and `(2,2,0)`;

- graph 5: vertices 4 and 5 have triples `(4,2,0)` and `(3,3,0)`.

Here the triple records the numbers of neighbors at distances `d-1,d,d+1` from the root. Since the triples differ at the same distance, distance-regularity fails exactly.

These are therefore legitimate tests of the actual AF7.30 intermediate regime, not disguised Proposition 7.29 cases. No counterexample to the frozen adjacent-arc monotonicity exists among them; the exact order-12 checker verifies their graph hypotheses and the walk-count identity underlying the theorem.

### #14 FIND THE GROUP ACTING

I did not enumerate the `2^66` labeled simple graphs on 12 vertices. Conder's primary online census is explicitly a **complete list of all connected symmetric (arc-transitive) graphs of order 2 to 30**, constructed from the database of all transitive permutation groups of degree at most 30 in Magma. Thus the group action quotients the order-12 search to exactly **11 isomorphism classes**.

For each of those 11 representatives the local checker then proves arc-transitivity without enumerating the full automorphism group: fix one ordered edge `(x,y)`, mark its endpoints with two distinct colors, and for every target ordered edge `(u,v)` perform an exact colored graph-isomorphism test. Existence of such an isomorphism is exactly existence of an automorphism sending `(x,y)` to `(u,v)`.

This avoids both blind all-graph enumeration and factorial enumeration of automorphism groups such as `Aut(K_12)`.

### #21 ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE

The algebraic structure behind the proof is the standard class of **1-walk-regular graphs**. Current literature defines these as graphs for which every `A^m` has constant diagonal and constant entries on edges, and explicitly notes that every vertex-and-edge-transitive graph is 1-walk-regular. Examples can be 1-walk-regular without being vertex-transitive, so this is a genuine algebraic weakening of symmetry.

The frozen proof only uses precisely those two heat-kernel constancies. In that language,

\[
r'(t)=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_t}(\lambda),
\]

so the mechanism is a tilted spectral variance identity on a 1-walk-regular edge, not a distance-partition coupling. This observation does **not** change the frozen AF7.30 target and is not presented as novel; it is a prior-art warning that the mechanism may be folklore in the 1-walk-regular community.
