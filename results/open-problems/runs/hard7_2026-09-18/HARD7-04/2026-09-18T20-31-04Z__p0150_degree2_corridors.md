## 2. Frozen quantitative object

Let `N_2(n)` be the number of degree-two vertices in the deterministic degree sequence and

`ell_n = sum_v d_v`.

Under empirical-degree convergence let

`p_2=P(D=2)`,  `mu=E[D]`,  `q=2p_2/mu`.

The heavy-tailed source assumptions rule out `D=2` almost surely, hence `q<1`.

Let `L_n` be the largest number of consecutive degree-two vertices on any simple path in the configuration model. Define

`C_2^+ := inf{c>0 : P(L_n <= c log n) -> 1}`.

This is not a replacement for the source theorem. It measures one canonical possible GHP obstruction: a macroscopic thin corridor invisible to finite typical-distance samples.

## 3. Method #2 — attack the negation first

I first tried to make the weakest-assumption GHP statement fail by manufacturing a macroscopic, low-mass, degree-two pipe. Such a pipe is the simplest graph-specific realization of the generic topological phenomenon recorded in the old P0150 attempt: typical sampled distances can miss a long thin appendage while Hausdorff distance detects it.

This attack **fails** in the source model for an exact reason: the configuration-model pairing makes consecutive degree-two continuation geometrically unlikely. The failure is itself a useful theorem.

## 4. Method #19 — turn the pairing into an exact exploration algorithm

For all large `n`, define

`q_n := 2N_2(n)/(ell_n-1)`.

Follow an oriented half-edge through fresh degree-two vertices. After `j` successful fresh degree-two hits, the active half-edge has `ell_n-2j-1` possible partners. At most `2N_2-2j` of them can belong to fresh degree-two vertices. Since eventually `2N_2<=ell_n-1`,

`(2N_2-2j)/(ell_n-2j-1) <= 2N_2/(ell_n-1)=q_n`.

The cross-multiplied slack is exactly

`2j(ell_n-1-2N_2) >= 0`.

There are at most `ell_n` oriented degree-two starting half-edges. Therefore, for every integer `r>=1`,

`P(L_n >= r) <= ell_n q_n^(r-1)`.

Because `q_n->q<1` and `ell_n=O(n)`, every

`c > 1/(-log q)`

satisfies

`P(L_n > c log n) -> 0`.

The proof, including the `q=0` case, is written in full in the artifact named above. This is not simulation and uses no unproved independence assumption: it is a conditional pairing-exposure estimate.

## 5. Method #7 — take the source scaling limit

At the heavy-tailed critical metric scale

`eta=(tau-3)/(tau-1)>0`,

the logarithmic bound immediately yields

`n^{-eta} L_n -> 0`

in probability.

I then checked that this statement has direct GHP content rather than being only a path-count estimate. Deterministically suppress every maximal degree-two chain of a connected component to a weighted edge of equal graph length. Equip the suppressed graph with the push-forward of the original vertex measure. Distances among unsuppressed vertices are unchanged. Every suppressed vertex is within distance at most `L(C)` of a surviving endpoint. Consequently the original component and its degree-two-suppressed version have GHP distance at most `L(C)` under the max-type convention (and at most `2L(C)` under a conservative sum convention).

Thus degree-two suppression changes every source-rescaled component by `o_P(1)` at scale `n^{-eta}`.

There is also a structural corollary. If a simple path `P` contains `B(P)` non-degree-two vertices, its degree-two vertices form at most `B(P)+1` blocks, so

`|V(P)| <= B(P)+(B(P)+1)L_n`.

Hence a path of length at least `delta n^eta` must contain

`Omega(n^eta/log n)`

non-degree-two vertices with high probability. A macroscopic tentacle therefore cannot be a sparse-junction or degree-two pipe.

## 6. What this changes about the source problem

The generic thin-spike obstruction from the old intake attempt is too coarse for this random graph. One important class of thin spikes is rigorously absent under only empirical degree/mean convergence plus `q<1`; the strong stochastic-domination assumption is not needed for this part.

The source's remaining difficulty is therefore more specific and more genuinely branching: a bad macroscopic low-mass fringe must contain on the order of `n^eta/log n` non-degree-two vertices. The next proof must control **branching fringe height / survival**, not merely long degree-two pipes. This aligns exactly with the 2026 source's statement that the stronger hypothesis is crucial in the existing proof for survival probabilities of critical clusters.

This is substantive narrowing, but it does **not** prove GHP convergence and does **not** show the strong hypothesis is unnecessary in every argument.
