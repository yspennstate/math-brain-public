## 5. B2 — attack the negation first: can the first moment live on rare bad hosts?

A V-witness consists of two internally disjoint paths from a central hub to two leaf hubs, each arm having length in `[m,2m]` and all internal vertices degree three. Let `w` be a fixed witness and let `R` be its total number of path edges, so `2m<=R<=4m`.

Condition on all `R` matching pairs of `w`. The residual matching is uniform. The witness leaves

\[
A_0=(3d-4)+(R-2)=3d+R-6\le4m^2
\]

unpaired half-edges incident to its skeleton. Explore the remainder of its host component from this queue and truncate at

\[
T=\left\lfloor\ell\delta/64\right\rfloor.
\]

Conditioning on the witness removes three hubs and `R-2` degree-three vertices from the unseen set. Their contributions to `d_v(d_v-2)` are nonnegative, so the standard active-half-edge drift becomes at least as subcritical as before. Uniformly up to `T`,

\[
\mathbb E[\Delta A_t\mid\mathcal F_t]\le-\delta/2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathbb E(\tau\wedge T)\le\frac{2A_0}{\delta}=O(m^3),
\qquad
\mathbb P(\tau>T)=O(m^{-8}).
\]

Before any new hub is found, the per-pairing hazard of hitting one of the other `M-3` hubs is at most

\[
\frac{2Md}{\ell}=O(m^{-5}),
\]

so

\[
\mathbb P\{\text{a fourth hub joins the host}\mid w\}=O(m^{-2}).
\]

The bounded normalized third moment also gives a uniform conditional second-moment bound for queue increments. Applying the square-queue drift yields

\[
\mathbb E\sum_{t<\tau\wedge T}A_t=O(m^5),
\]

and therefore the collision/cycle probability is

\[
\mathbb P\{\text{host of }w\text{ has a cycle}\mid w\}=O(m^{-7}).
\]

Call `w` **good** if its host is acyclic and contains exactly its three witness hubs. Uniformly,

\[
\mathbb P(w\text{ bad}\mid w\text{ occurs})=O(m^{-2}).
\]

Hence, if `Z_m^g` counts good ordered witnesses,

\[
\mathbb E(Z_m-Z_m^g)=O(m^{-2})\mathbb EZ_m=O(m^{-1}),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathbb EZ_m^g\sim c_*m.}
\]

**B2 outcome: successful.** The negation that the linear first moment is carried by rare cyclic/four-hub hosts is quantitatively eliminated.

## 7. B18 — say it in another language: component occupancy gives a 0-or-2 identity

The key simplification is to stop classifying all possible pairs of overlapping witnesses and instead quotient by the **host component**.

A good witness lives in a tree component containing exactly three hubs. The unique paths in a tree imply that its central hub and two arms determine the unique minimal three-hub subtree. That host contributes exactly two ordered good witnesses: the two possible orders of the leaf hubs. Neither leaf can be a witness center, because the path from that leaf to the opposite leaf passes through the third hub and therefore has a hub, rather than a degree-three vertex, in its interior.

Let `D_m^g` count ordered pairs of good witnesses lying in distinct components. Pointwise,

\[
(Z_m^g)^2=2Z_m^g+D_m^g.
\]

Different components have vertex-disjoint skeletons, hence `D_m^g<=D_m^0`. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E[(Z_m^g)^2]
\le2\mathbb EZ_m^g+(1+o(1))(\mathbb EZ_m)^2
=(1+o(1))(\mathbb EZ_m^g)^2.
\]

Paley--Zygmund / the elementary second-moment inequality gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbb P(Z_m^g>0)\to1.}
\]

Combining this with the preceding four-hub exclusion yields

\[
\boxed{\mathbb P(H_{\max}=3)\to1.}
\]

**B18 outcome: successful.** Recasting witness overlap as component occupancy removes the full same-component overlap taxonomy and closes the exact stress-family boundary theorem.
