## 3. A nonuniform Craig-Jensen lemma

Craig's identity gives, for `u>=0`,

\[
\bar\Phi(u)
=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{2\sin^2\theta}\right)d\theta.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Put

\[
s=u^2,\qquad b(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\sin^2\theta}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For an angular cell `I=[a,b]` with `0<a<b<=pi/2`, define

\[
\overline b_I
=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b b(\theta)d\theta
=\frac{\cot a-\cot b}{2(b-a)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since `x -> exp(-s x)` is convex for `s>=0`, Jensen gives

\[
\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b e^{-s b(\theta)}d\theta
\ge e^{-s\overline b_I}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

It is convenient to normalize angle by `theta=(pi/2)x`, `0<=x<=1`. A cell `[a,b]` in the normalized `x` coordinate has Craig mass

\[
w(a,b)=\frac{b-a}{2}
\tag{3.5}
\]

and exact mean rate

\[
\beta(a,b)
=\frac{\cot(\pi a/2)-\cot(\pi b/2)}{\pi(b-a)}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore, for any disjoint retained cells away from zero and any rational majorants `\widehat\beta_j >= \beta(a_j,b_j)`,

\[
\boxed{
\bar\Phi(u)\ge
\sum_j w_j e^{-\widehat\beta_j u^2},
\qquad u\ge0.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(u)\le 1-\sum_j w_j e^{-\widehat\beta_j u^2},
\qquad u\ge0.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Discarding a Craig cell is safe because its integrand is nonnegative; it merely sacrifices its positive contribution to the lower bound on `barPhi`.
