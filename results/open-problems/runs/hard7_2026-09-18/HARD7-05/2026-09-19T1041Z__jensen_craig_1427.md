## 3. B12/B19: the Jensen-Craig finite-mixture hierarchy

For `u>=0`, Craig's identity is

\[
\bar\Phi(u)=1-\Phi(u)
=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{u^2}{2\sin^2\theta}\right]d\theta.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Write

\[
s=u^2,\qquad b(\theta)=\frac1{2\sin^2\theta}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `I=[a,b]` be any angular cell with `0<a<b<=pi/2`, and let

\[
\overline b_I=\frac1{b-a}\int_a^b b(\theta)d\theta.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Because `x -> e^{-s x}` is convex for every `s>=0`, Jensen gives

\[
\frac1{b-a}\int_a^b e^{-s b(\theta)}d\theta
\ge e^{-s\overline b_I}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The mean rate is explicit:

\[
\boxed{
\overline b_I=\frac{\cot a-\cot b}{2(b-a)}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Therefore every finite partition of a subinterval away from zero gives a positive exponential minorant of the Gaussian tail.

There is also a useful refinement theorem. Suppose `I` is split into cells `I_1,...,I_m`, with masses `p_j=|I_j|/|I|` and mean rates `\overline b_j`. Then

\[
\overline b_I=\sum_j p_j\overline b_j.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Hence, again by convexity,

\[
\sum_j p_j e^{-s\overline b_j}
\ge e^{-s\overline b_I}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

So **partition refinement can only improve the Craig-Jensen minorant**. This is the conserved-barycenter mechanism from B12.

For equal-angle partitions, put

\[
\theta_j=\frac{j\pi}{2N},\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,N.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Discard the singular first cell `[0,theta_1]`. Every remaining cell has mass `1/(2N)` in (3.1), and its mean rate is

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{N,j}=\frac N\pi
\left(\cot\frac{j\pi}{2N}-\cot\frac{(j+1)\pi}{2N}\right),
\qquad 1\le j\le N-1.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\bar\Phi(u)\ge \frac1{2N}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}e^{-\beta_{N,j}u^2}
}
\qquad(u\ge0).
\tag{3.10}
\]

The discarded cell has Craig mass `1/(2N)`, so its total contribution is at most `1/(2N)`. Combined with (3.7), a standard `delta`-split argument shows that nested refinements with the first cell shrinking to zero converge pointwise to the exact Craig integral for every fixed `u>0`: first fix `delta>0`, use ordinary Riemann/Jensen refinement on `[delta,pi/2]`, bound `[0,delta]` by `delta/pi`, and then send `delta` to zero.
