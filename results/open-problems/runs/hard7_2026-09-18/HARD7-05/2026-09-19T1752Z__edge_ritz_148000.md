### 4.1 One-step rotation for `mu`

Let `(X,Y)` be centered standard bivariate Gaussian with correlation `4/5`. With independent standard Gaussians `(A,D)`,

\[
X={3A+D\over\sqrt{10}},
\qquad
Y={3A-D\over\sqrt{10}}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
X+Y={6A\over\sqrt{10}},
\tag{4.4}
\]

so the edge condition is simply `A<0`. Consequently

\[
\langle f,Tf\rangle
=\mathbb E\!\left[f(X)f(Y)\mathbf1_{\{A<0\}}\right],
\tag{4.5}
\]

which expands into products of ordinary Gaussian moments in `D` and exact half-line Gaussian moments in `A`. The latter are

\[
\mathbb E[A^{2m}\mathbf1_{A<0}]={ (2m-1)!!\over2},
\qquad
\mathbb E[A^{2m+1}\mathbf1_{A<0}]
=-{2^m m!\over\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus `mu` reduces to algebra over rational numbers, square roots and `pi`, all enclosed by the exact checker.

### 4.2 Two-edge rotation for the residual

Let `(X,Y,Z)` be a stationary AR(1) Gaussian triple with

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Cov}(Y,Z)=\frac45,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(X,Z)=\frac{16}{25}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Set

\[
A={X+Y\over\sqrt{2(1+r)}},
\qquad
B={Y+Z\over\sqrt{2(1+r)}},
\qquad r=\frac45.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Then `A,B` are standard Gaussian with exact correlation

\[
\rho_{AB}={1+r\over2}=\frac9{10},
\tag{4.9}
\]

and the two edge events are `A<0,B<0`. Gaussian regression gives the exact representation

\[
X={84A-30B\over19\sqrt{10}}+E,
\qquad
Z={-30A+84B\over19\sqrt{10}}+E,
\tag{4.10}
\]

where

\[
E\sim N\!\left(0,{1\over19}\right)
\tag{4.11}
\]

is independent of `(A,B)`. Hence

\[
\langle f,T^2f\rangle
=\mathbb E\!\left[f(X)f(Z)\mathbf1_{\{A<0,B<0\}}\right].
\tag{4.12}
\]

It remains to evaluate bivariate Gaussian quadrant moments. Write

\[
Q_{m,n}=\mathbb E[A^mB^n\mathbf1_{\{A>0,B>0\}}],
\qquad c={9\over10},\quad s^2=1-c^2={19\over100},\quad a={c\over s}={9\over\sqrt{19}}.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Define

\[
B_0={s\over2\sqrt{2\pi}},
\quad B_1={s^2\over2\pi},
\quad B_n=(n-1)s^2B_{n-2},
\tag{4.14}
\]

and

\[
A_0={1\over4}+{1\over2\pi}\arcsin c,
\quad
A_1={1\over2\sqrt{2\pi}}+aB_0,
\quad
A_n=(n-1)A_{n-2}+aB_{n-1}.
\tag{4.15}
\]

Then integration by parts gives

\[
Q_{0,n}=A_n,
\tag{4.16}
\]

\[
Q_{1,n}=cA_{n+1}+sB_n,
\tag{4.17}
\]

and, for `m>=2`,

\[
Q_{m,n}=cQ_{m-1,n+1}+(m-1)s^2Q_{m-2,n}.
\tag{4.18}
\]

By symmetry,

\[
\mathbb E[A^mB^n\mathbf1_{\{A<0,B<0\}}]
=(-1)^{m+n}Q_{m,n}.
\tag{4.19}
\]

Equations (4.10)-(4.19) therefore evaluate every term of (4.12) without numerical quadrature.
