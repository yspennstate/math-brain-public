## 5. The exact truncated-normal polynomial recurrence

Let `(X,Y)` be a stationary centered bivariate Gaussian pair of correlation `r`, and put

\[
s=\sqrt{1-r^2},\qquad q(y)=\Phi(ry/s),\qquad p(y)=\phi(ry/s).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Conditionally on `Y=y`, `X` is Gaussian with mean `ry` and variance `s^2`. Define

\[
J_n(y)=\mathbf E[X^n1_{\{X>0\}}\mid Y=y].
\tag{5.2}
\]

The initial terms are

\[
J_0=q,
\qquad
J_1=ry\,q+s\,p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For every integer `n>=2`, Gaussian integration by parts gives the exact recurrence

\[
\boxed{J_n(y)=ry\,J_{n-1}(y)+(n-1)s^2J_{n-2}(y).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Indeed, if `g_y(x)` is the `N(ry,s^2)` density, then

\[
(x-ry)g_y(x)=-s^2g_y'(x).
\]

Multiplying by `x^{n-1}` and integrating on `(0,\infty)`, the boundary term vanishes for `n>=2`, so

\[
J_n-ryJ_{n-1}
=-s^2\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}g_y'(x)\,dx
=(n-1)s^2J_{n-2}.
\]

For `n=1` the nonzero boundary term at zero is exactly `sp`, giving (5.3).

It follows inductively that for every `n`,

\[
J_n(y)=Q_n(y)q(y)+P_n(y)p(y)
\tag{5.5}
\]

with explicit polynomials `Q_n,P_n`. This is the structural step that removes the degree-two bottleneck: all needed matrix entries at any fixed polynomial degree reduce to scalar Gaussian moments.
