## 5. Finite Ritz compression of the killed AR(1) operator

Because `0<r<1`, the OU operator `P_r` is positive self-adjoint on `L^2(gamma)`. Hence its compression `K` in (3.3) is positive self-adjoint. Write its eigenvalues in decreasing order

\[
\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\lambda_3\ge\cdots\ge0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Consider the three-dimensional subspace

\[
E=\operatorname{span}\{1,x^2,x^4\}\subset L^2((0,\infty),\gamma).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let `e_i(x)=x^{2i}`, `i=0,1,2`, on the positive half-line, and define

\[
G_{ij}=\langle e_i,e_j\rangle,
\qquad
M_{ij}=\langle e_i,Ke_j\rangle.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The generalized Ritz values `nu_1>=nu_2>=nu_3` are the roots of

\[
Mv=\nu Gv.
\tag{5.4}
\]

By the min--max principle,

\[
\boxed{\lambda_j\ge\nu_j,\qquad j=1,2,3.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The Gram matrix is elementary:

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}
\frac12&\frac12&\frac32\\
\frac12&\frac32&\frac{15}{2}\\
\frac32&\frac{15}{2}&\frac{105}{2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

To compute `M`, let `(X,Y)` be standard bivariate Gaussian with correlation `r`; then

\[
M_{ij}=\mathbb E[X^{2i}Y^{2j}1_{\{X>0,Y>0\}}].
\tag{5.7}
\]

Put

\[
R=r^2,\qquad s^2=1-R,\qquad
P=\Pr(X>0,Y>0)=\frac14+\frac{\arcsin r}{2\pi},
\qquad
h=\frac{rs}{2\pi}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — exact truncated-moment matrix

The matrix `M` is

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
P & P+h & 3P+(5-2R)h\\
P+h & (1+2R)P+3h & (3+12R)P+(13+2R)h\\
3P+(5-2R)h & (3+12R)P+(13+2R)h &
(9+72R+24R^2)P+(55+50R)h
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

**Proof.** Condition on `Y=y>0`. If

\[
J_m(y)=\int_0^\infty x^m\frac1s\phi\!\left(\frac{x-ry}{s}\right)dx,
\]

one integration by parts gives

\[
J_0(y)=\Phi\!\left(\frac{ry}{s}\right),\qquad
J_1(y)=ryJ_0(y)+s\phi\!\left(\frac{ry}{s}\right),
\]

and, for `m>=2`,

\[
J_m(y)=ryJ_{m-1}(y)+(m-1)s^2J_{m-2}(y).
\tag{5.10}
\]

For

\[
A_m=\int_0^\infty y^{2m}\phi(y)\Phi\!\left(\frac{ry}{s}\right)dy,
\]

a second integration by parts, together with

\[
\phi(y)\phi(ry/s)=\frac1{2\pi}e^{-y^2/(2s^2)},
\]

gives

\[
A_0=P,\qquad
A_m=(2m-1)A_{m-1}
+2^{m-1}(m-1)!s^{2m-2}h.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Substituting (5.10)--(5.11) into

\[
M_{ij}=\int_0^\infty y^{2j}\phi(y)J_{2i}(y)dy
\]

for `0<=i,j<=2` and using `s^2=1-R` yields (5.9). This is algebraic; no quadrature enters. `square`
