## 4. Exact four-cycle trace

The earlier loop-trace representation states, for integer `n>=2`,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(T_r^n)
=\frac{1}{1-r^n}
\Pr_{\rm loop}\{X_i+X_{i+1}<0\ \forall i\pmod n\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where the centered cyclic Gaussian loop has covariance

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_{i+d})
=\frac{r^d+r^{n-d}}{1-r^n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For `n=4`, write

\[
S_i=X_i+X_{i+1},\qquad i\pmod4.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then identically

\[
S_0-S_1+S_2-S_3=0,
\qquad	ext{equivalently}\qquad
S_0+S_2=S_1+S_3.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence the two alternating strict sign patterns

\[
(-,+,-,+),\qquad (+,-,+,-)
\tag{4.5}
\]

are impossible. Gaussian boundaries have probability zero in the three-dimensional support of `(S_0,S_1,S_2,S_3)`.

Let

\[
a=\Pr(S_0,S_1,S_2,S_3<0).
\tag{4.6}
\]

By cyclic symmetry and global sign symmetry, let `b` denote the probability of any specified exactly-three-negative pattern (and therefore also any specified exactly-one-negative pattern), and let `c` denote the probability of any specified adjacent exactly-two-negative pattern. The fourteen possible strict sign patterns partition probability one, so

\[
2a+8b+4c=1.
\tag{4.7}
\]

On the other hand, for the adjacent pair event

\[
p:=\Pr(S_0<0,S_1<0),
\tag{4.8}
\]

the compatible sign patterns give

\[
p=a+2b+c.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Subtracting half of (4.7) from twice (4.9) yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{a=2p-\frac12}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Now `(S_0,S_1)` is a centered nondegenerate bivariate Gaussian pair. If its correlation is `rho`, the standard Gaussian orthant formula gives

\[
p=\frac14+\frac{\arcsin\rho}{2\pi}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{a=\frac{\arcsin\rho}{\pi}}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

For the four-step loop, put

\[
g_0=\frac{1+r^4}{1-r^4},\qquad
 g_1=\frac{r+r^3}{1-r^4},\qquad
 g_2=\frac{2r^2}{1-r^4}.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Then

\[
\rho
=\frac{g_0+2g_1+g_2}{2(g_0+g_1)}
=\frac{(1+r)(1+r^2)}{2(1+r^3)}.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Combining (4.1), (4.12), and (4.14) proves the exact general four-cycle formula

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(T_r^4)
=\frac{1}{1-r^4}
\frac1\pi
\arcsin\!\left(\frac{(1+r)(1+r^2)}{2(1+r^3)}\right).
}
\tag{4.15}
\]

\[

\tag{4.16}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{

=\frac{625}{369}\frac{\arcsin(41/42)}{\pi}.

\tag{4.17}
\]

This is an analytic identity, not a numerical orthant approximation.
