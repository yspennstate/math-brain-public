## 5. Why softening can reuse the hard-edge fourth-trace cap

Write `k_r(x,y)` for the AR transition density relative to `phi`. Expanding the trace gives

\[

\phi(dx_i)k_r(x_i,x_{i+1})w_{r,s}(x_i,x_{i+1}),

\tag{5.2}
\]

Without the four `w` factors, the integral is `tr(P_r^4)=1/(1-r^4)`. Normalize the resulting cyclic Gaussian measure and call it `nu_r`. Its vertex covariance depends only on cyclic distance `d` and is

\[

\quad(d=0,1,2,3),
\tag{5.3}
\]

where for `d=0` this reads `(1+r^4)/(1-r^4)`. This is also obtained directly by inverting the circulant precision matrix of `sum_i (x_{i+1}-rx_i)^2/(1-r^2)`.

Under `nu_r`, put

\[
S_i=\frac{X_i+X_{i+1}}{\sqrt{2(1+r)}}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Every covariance `Cov(S_i,S_j)` is positive: the variance is `(G_0+G_1)/(1+r)>0`; for adjacent edges the covariance is `(G_0+2G_1+G_2)/(2(1+r))>0`; for opposite edges it is `(G_1+G_2)/(1+r)>0`.

Introduce independent standard normals `E_i` and

\[
V_i=\sqrt{s}\,S_i+\sqrt{1-s}\,E_i.

Similarly,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(K_{r,1}^4)
=\frac1{1-r^4}\Pr(S_0<0,\ldots,S_3<0).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Let `v=Var(S_i)`. For `i!=j`, standardization gives

\[
\operatorname{Corr}(V_i,V_j)

because all displayed covariances are positive and `0<s<1`. The standardized vectors have equal unit variances, so Slepian's comparison for the common zero threshold implies the orthant probability in (5.6) is at most that in (5.7). This proves (5.1). QED.
