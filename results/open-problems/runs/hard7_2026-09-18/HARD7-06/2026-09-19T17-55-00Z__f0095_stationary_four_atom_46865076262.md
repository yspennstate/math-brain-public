### Proof

The committed checker uses only Python integers and `fractions.Fraction` for every sign decision.

Condition on the total Poisson degree `n`. For every `0<=n<=28`, enumerate every four-part composition `(k1,k2,k3,k4)` and its exact multinomial weight. For each state, the vertex factor `S(N)` is an exact positive rational at most 1.

For vertex logarithms, scale each rational ratio into `[1/2,1]` by an exact power of two and use

`log z = 2 atanh((z-1)/(z+1))`.

For the resulting nonpositive atanh argument, truncation gives a one-sided upper bound; the power-of-two correction uses a separate rational lower bound on `log 2`. Every logarithmic bound is rounded outward on a `2^-128` dyadic lattice.

All omitted Poisson terms `n>28` have `log S(N)<=0`, so dropping the tail raises the vertex expectation and is one-sided in the required direction. The negative retained Poisson series is multiplied by a rigorous lower bound for `exp(-d_*)`, obtained from an upper Taylor/geometric bound on `exp(d_*)`, which again preserves an upper bound on the vertex term.

For the edge term, all 16 support-pair factors `R(x,y)` are positive rationals. The same atanh expansion with explicit remainder gives rigorous lower bounds for their logarithms. Since the functional subtracts `(d/2)` times this edge expectation, those lower bounds produce an upper bound on the total functional.
