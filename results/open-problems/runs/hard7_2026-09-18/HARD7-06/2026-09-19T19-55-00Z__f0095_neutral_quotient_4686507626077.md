## 4. B3 — prove a stronger exact quotient instead of optimizing the neutral mass numerically

For `q=3`, write the Ayre--Coja-Oghlan--Greenhill trial functional as

\[
\Sigma_d(a)=V_d(a)-\frac d2Q(a),
\tag{4.1}
\]

where, for a Poisson point process `N` of intensity `d a`,

\[
V_d(a)=\mathbb E\log S(N),
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
S(N)=3\prod_{x\in N}\frac{2+x}{3}
-3\prod_{x\in N}\frac{1+2x}{3}
+\prod_{x\in N}x,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
Q(a)=\iint\log R(x,y)\,a(dx)a(dy),
\qquad
R(x,y)=1-\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}3.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The boundary point `x=1` is exactly neutral:

\[
\frac{2+1}{3}=\frac{1+2}{3}=1,
\qquad R(1,y)=1.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Let `b` be a probability law on `[0,1)` and put

\[
a=m b+(1-m)\delta_1,
\qquad 0<m\le1,
\qquad \lambda=dm.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Define

\[
V_b(\lambda)=\mathbb E\log S(N_{\lambda b}),
\qquad
Q_b=\iint\log R(x,y)\,b(dx)b(dy).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Deleting every neutral Poisson point leaves `S` unchanged, while every edge pair involving `1` contributes `log 1=0`. Therefore, **exactly**,

\[
\boxed{\Sigma_d(a)=V_b(\lambda)-\frac{\lambda^2}{2d}Q_b.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This removes the neutral mass from the distributional optimization. If `V_b(lambda)<0` and `Q_b<0`, define

\[
\boxed{D_b(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^2Q_b}{2V_b(\lambda)}>0.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

If `D_b(lambda)>=lambda`, then for every `d>D_b(lambda)` the admissible law

\[
a_d=\frac\lambda d b+\left(1-\frac\lambda d\right)\delta_1
\tag{4.10}
\]

has the same active Poisson intensity `lambda` and satisfies

\[
\Sigma_d(a_d)
=V_b(\lambda)-\frac{\lambda^2}{2d}Q_b
<0,
\tag{4.11}
\]

because equality holds at `d=D_b(lambda)` and `Q_b<0`. Thus the full neutral-atom family reduces to minimizing the scalar quotient (4.9) over active shapes and active intensities.

This is stronger than the preceding run's observation that `x=1` can be integrated out of one fixed checker: it is an exact variational reduction for the whole neutral-boundary class.

## 5. B6 — deform from the exactly neutral endpoint and prove the scalar minimum is interior

The quotient could still have been useless if its optimum lived at `lambda=0` or `lambda=infinity`. For every finite active support bounded away from `1`, neither happens.

First observe directly from (4.3):

\[
S(N)=1\qquad\text{for }|N|=0,1,2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For three active marks,

\[
\boxed{
S(\{x,y,z\})
=1-\frac29(1-x)(1-y)(1-z).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence, for a finite active law not concentrated at `1`,

\[
V_b(\lambda)=-c_b\lambda^3+O(\lambda^4),
\tag{5.3}
\]

where

\[
c_b=-\frac16\,\mathbb E\log\left(1-\frac29\prod_{i=1}^3(1-X_i)\right)>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because `Q_b<0`,

\[
D_b(\lambda)
\sim \frac{-Q_b}{2c_b}\frac1\lambda
\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(\lambda\downarrow0).
\tag{5.5}
\]

For the opposite endpoint, suppose the active support lies in `[0,z_*]` with `z_*<1`. For a realization with `n` active points let

\[
A=\prod\frac{2+x}{3},\quad
B=\prod\frac{1+2x}{3},\quad
C=\prod x.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then `C<=A`, and

\[
\frac BA
=\prod\frac{1+2x}{2+x}
\le r_*^n,
\qquad
r_*:=\frac{1+2z_*}{2+z_*}<1.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore, for every `n>=1`,

\[
3(1-r_*)A\le 3(A-B)+C=S(N)\le4A.
\tag{5.8}
\]

So `log S(N)` differs from `sum log((2+X_i)/3)` by a uniformly bounded quantity, and Poisson averaging yields

\[
V_b(\lambda)
=\lambda\,a_b+O(1),
\qquad
a_b:=\mathbb E_b\log\frac{2+X}{3}<0.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus

\[
D_b(\lambda)
\sim \frac{Q_b}{2a_b}\lambda
\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(\lambda\to\infty).
\tag{5.10}
\]

Consequently every finite active shape bounded away from `1` has at least one **interior** global minimizer of the neutral quotient. The previous four-atom stiffness is therefore not an artifact of a boundary mass parameter: the active intensity must solve an interior balance condition.

## 6. B13 — the derivative is exactly the averaged contact defect

Poisson differentiation gives

\[
V_b'(\lambda)
=\mathbb E_{N,Y\sim b}
[F(N+\delta_Y)-F(N)],
\qquad F=\log S.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For fixed active shape `b`, differentiating (4.9) yields

\[
\frac{D_b'(\lambda)}{D_b(\lambda)}
=\frac2\lambda-rac{V_b'(\lambda)}{V_b(\lambda)}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

At `d=D_b(lambda)` let `m=lambda/d`. From (4.9),

\[
mQ_b=\frac{2V_b(\lambda)}\lambda.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{D_b'}{D_b}
=-\frac{C_b(\lambda)}{V_b(\lambda)},
\qquad
C_b(\lambda):=V_b'(\lambda)-mQ_b.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The quantity `C_b(lambda)` is exactly the `b`-average of the mass-insertion contact potential introduced in run 23, because under `a=m b+(1-m)delta_1` the edge part against an active point is `m int log R(x,y)b(dy)`.

Since `V_b(lambda)<0`,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{sgn}D_b'(\lambda)=\operatorname{sgn}C_b(\lambda).}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus neutral-mass optimization has a genuine monovariant: move `lambda` in the direction opposite the averaged contact imbalance until `C_b=0`. The current route no longer needs a generic fourth-weight optimizer.
