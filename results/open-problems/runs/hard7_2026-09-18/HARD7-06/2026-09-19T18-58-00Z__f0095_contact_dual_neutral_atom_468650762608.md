## 4. B11 — dualize the ACG functional into a contact problem

For `q=3`, write the ACG trial functional for a probability law `a` on `[0,1]` as

`Sigma_d(a) = E F(N_a) - (d/2) Q(a)`,

where `N_a` is a Poisson point process of intensity `d a`,

`F(N)=log S(N)`,

`S(N)=3 prod_{u in N}((2+u)/3) - 3 prod_{u in N}((1+2u)/3) + prod_{u in N}u`,

and

`Q(a)=E_{X,Y~a} log R(X,Y)`,

`R(x,y)=1-(1-x)(1-y)/3`.

For a mass-transfer perturbation

`a_epsilon=(1-epsilon)a + epsilon delta_x`,

Poisson add-one differentiation gives

`d/d epsilon|_0 E F(N_{a_epsilon})`
` = d E[F(N_a+delta_x)-F(N_a)]`
`   - d E_{Y~a} E[F(N_a+delta_Y)-F(N_a)]`.

Also

`d/d epsilon|_0 Q(a_epsilon)`
` = 2 E_{Y~a}log R(x,Y) - 2 Q(a)`.

Define the contact potential

`Psi_a(x) := E[F(N_a+delta_x)-F(N_a)] - E_{Y~a} log R(x,Y)`.

Then the first variation is exactly

`D Sigma_d(a)[delta_x-a] = d ( Psi_a(x) - E_{Y~a} Psi_a(Y) ).`      (4.1)

This changes the support search from generic nonlinear fitting to a one-dimensional dual contact condition. At a measure-level local minimum, admissible mass insertion requires the right side of (4.1) to be nonnegative for every `x`; support points with positive movable mass must lie on the contact level.

## 5. B14 — exploit the exact neutral boundary contact

The special point `x=1` is exactly neutral:

`(2+1)/3=(1+2*1)/3=1`, and `1=1`,

so

`S(N+delta_1)=S(N)`

for every finite multiset `N`. In addition,

`R(1,y)=1`

for every `y`. Consequently

`Psi_a(1)=0` exactly.                                                (5.1)

`Psi_a(x_i)=0`,                                                     (5.2)
