The exact external source remains Alan Frieze's ICM 2014 chromatic-number conjecture (intake `F0095`):

> **Conjecture:** There exists a sequence `d_k`, `k >= 2`, such that w.h.p. `chi(G_{n,d/n}) = k` for `d_{k-1} < d < d_k`.

HARD7-06 freezes the first small unresolved colourability-location case `k=3`. The source-level obligation is the existence of a constant transition location, not merely one-sided numerical bounds.

The upper-bound engine remains Peter Ayre, Amin Coja-Oghlan and Catherine Greenhill, *Lower Bounds on the Chromatic Number of Random Graphs*, Combinatorica 42 (2022), 617--658, arXiv:1812.09691, especially equations (1.9)--(1.10) and Theorem 1.3. For a probability law `a` on `[0,1]`, they define `Sigma^*_{d,q}(a)` and

\[
d_q^*:=\inf\{d>0:\inf_a\Sigma^*_{d,q}(a)<0\},
\]

and prove for `q>=3` that

\[
d>d_q^*\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \chi(G(n,d/n))>q\quad\text{w.h.p.}
\]

Their paper records the one-atom certificate `Sigma^*_{4.697,3}(delta_{0.25})<0`. The 2024 Coja-Oghlan--Krieg--Lawnik--Scheftelowitsch discussion still identifies `4.03` as the constructive lower endpoint and `4.697` as the then-current rigorous upper endpoint. A bounded fresh search dated 2026-09-19 for ordinary sparse Erdos--Renyi 3-colourability at `4.697`, `4.687`, and `4.6865`, plus 2025--2026 follow-ups, did not recover a published rigorous ordinary-model endpoint below the HARD7-06 run-11 value. This is bounded negative evidence, not an exhaustive novelty certificate.

The current canonical claim is still owned by HARD7-06. No posted, settled, or duplicate-equivalent claim was found in the repository readback.

### Proof

For arbitrary incoming parameters `alpha_h in (0,1]`, interpret message `h` as forbidding no colour with probability `alpha_h`, and otherwise forbidding one uniformly chosen colour. Inclusion--exclusion gives exactly

\[
3\prod_h\frac{2+\alpha_h}{3}
-3\prod_h\frac{1+2\alpha_h}{3}
+\prod_h\alpha_h
\]

for the probability that not all three colours have been forbidden. Therefore, for every `D,K`, not merely those checked by the finite loop,

\[
0<S_{D,K}\le1,
\qquad \log S_{D,K}\le0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus truncating the Poisson sum after `D=24` deletes only non-positive contributions and gives an **upper bound** on the first expectation in `Sigma^*`.

For every positive rational argument the checker encloses the logarithm by power-of-two scaling to `1/2<=z<=2` and the exact identity

\[
\log z
=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{t^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N,
\qquad t=\frac{z-1}{z+1},
\]

with

\[
|R_N|\le
\frac{2|t|^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-t^2)},
\qquad N=22.
\tag{5.5}
\]

It lower-bounds `e^{-d}` by the reciprocal of an upper Taylor bound on `e^d`. Because the truncated Poisson-log sum is negative, multiplying by that lower bound again gives an upper bound on the first ACG term. For the edge term, it takes lower bounds on the three logarithms; subtraction by `d/2` reverses them into an upper bound for the full functional.
