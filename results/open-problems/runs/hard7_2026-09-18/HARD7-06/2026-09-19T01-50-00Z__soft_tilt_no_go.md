## 3. New route before code: exact soft messages plus a tunable local size tilt

Run 5 showed that exact free-tree cluster counts cannot be represented by the alphabet `{0,1,f}`: even a path produces infinitely many Fibonacci likelihood ratios. So carry a positive variable-to-factor count pair `(Z_0,Z_1)` and its log-likelihood

\[
X=\log(Z_1/Z_0).
\]

For a NAE-3 factor whose two child cavity log-likelihoods are `X,Y`, put

\[
p=\frac{e^X}{1+e^X},\qquad q=\frac{e^Y}{1+e^Y}.
\]

Conditioning on the root bit gives the exact factor-to-root likelihood ratio

\[
\widehat R(X,Y)=\frac{1-pq}{p+q-pq},
\]

or equivalently

\[
\phi(X,Y):=\log \widehat R(X,Y)
=\log\frac{1+e^X+e^Y}{e^X+e^Y+e^{X+Y}}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The corresponding two-root-state local normalization is

\[
S(X,Y):=(1-pq)+(p+q-pq)=1+p+q-2pq.
\tag{3.2}
\]

An exact tree variable update at degree `d` sums the `d-1` incoming factor log-likelihoods.

The first deliberately simplified `1RSB-like` experiment is therefore: before applying each factor map, independently reweight an iid child pair `(X,Y)` by `S(X,Y)^lambda`, where `lambda in [0,1]`. This keeps a genuine local normalization coordinate rather than discarding it. But it is **not** the Nam--Sly--Sohn 1RSB measure: it omits variable-node normalization correlations, the full distribution of free-tree structures, and global cluster weights. The point of the calculation is to decide whether this simpler route can even be locally stable.

## 4. Exact auxiliary theorem: every physical local tilt still expands a two-point perturbation

Let

\[
\mu_t=\tfrac12\delta_t+\tfrac12\delta_{-t},\qquad t>0.
\]

For equal-sign children, (3.1) gives outputs `-a(t)` and `+a(t)`, where

\[
a(t):=\log\frac{e^t+2}{2+e^{-t}}>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For opposite-sign children, the output is zero. Writing

\[
p_t=(1+e^{-t})^{-1},\qquad c_t=p_t(1-p_t),
\]

(3.2) gives the two local normalizations

\[
S_s=1+2c_t \quad\text{(same signs)},\qquad
S_o=2-2c_t \quad\text{(opposite signs)}.
\]

Under the factor-local `S^lambda` tilt, the total probability of a nonzero factor message is

\[
w_\lambda(t)=\frac{S_s^\lambda}{S_s^\lambda+S_o^\lambda}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

At `d=6`, five independent incoming factor messages are summed, so the output variance is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X')=5w_\lambda(t)a(t)^2.
\]

Relative to `\operatorname{Var}(X)=t^2`, define

\[
\Gamma_\lambda(t):=5w_\lambda(t)\left(\frac{a(t)}t\right)^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proposition

For every `t>0` and every `lambda in [0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_\lambda(t)>1.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{\lim_{t\downarrow0}\Gamma_\lambda(t)=\frac{10}{9}}
\tag{4.5}
\]

for every fixed `lambda in [0,1]`.

Thus this entire one-parameter factor-local size-tilt family cannot make the unbiased soft-message origin variance-contracting, even on the very special symmetric two-point perturbations.

### Proof

Since `0<c_t<1/4`, one has `S_s<S_o`. Hence for `0<=lambda<=1`,

\[
w_\lambda(t)\ge w_1(t).
\]

Put

\[
z=\tanh(t/2).
\]

Then `c_t=(1-z^2)/4`, so

\[
w_1(t)=\frac{3-z^2}{6}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore it is enough to prove

\[
\frac{a(t)}t>
q(t):=\sqrt{\frac{6}{5(3-z^2)}}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Differentiate (4.1). With `z_u=tanh(u/2)`,

\[
a'(u)=\frac{e^u}{e^u+2}+\frac1{1+2e^u}
=\frac{2(z_u^2+3)}{9-z_u^2}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The last expression is strictly increasing for `u>0`. Splitting the integral of `a'` into three equal pieces therefore gives the strict lower Riemann bound

\[
\frac{a(t)}t>
L(t):=\frac13\left(a'(0)+a'(t/3)+a'(2t/3)\right).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Set `x=e^{t/3}>1`. Exact simplification gives

\[
L(t)=\frac{2N(x)}{9D(x)},
\]

where

\[
N(x)=16x^6+31x^5+47x^4+55x^3+47x^2+31x+16,
\]

\[
D(x)=(x+2)(2x+1)(x^2+2)(2x^2+1),
\]

while

\[
q(t)^2=\frac{3(x+1)^2(x^2-x+1)^2}{5(x^6+4x^3+1)}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

After clearing the positive denominator, the numerator of `L(t)^2-q(t)^2` is the palindromic polynomial

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(x)={}&1232x^{18}+400x^{17}-2216x^{16}-10456x^{15}-13404x^{14}
+18108x^{13}+35905x^{12}\\
&+101492x^{11}+154820x^{10}+136826x^9+154820x^8+101492x^7
+35905x^6+18108x^5\\
&-13404x^4-10456x^3-2216x^2+400x+1232.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.11}
\]

Let `y=x+x^{-1}=2+u`, `u>=0`. Palindromicity yields

\[
P(x)/x^9=Q(y),
\]

and exact substitution gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q(2+u)={}&1232u^9+22576u^8+170504u^7+672792u^6+1416876u^5
+1313508u^4\\
&-134595u^3-723654u^2+478953u+708588.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.12}
\]

This is positive for every `u>=0` by two elementary bounds. If `0<=u<=1`, use `u^2,u^3<=u` and discard the positive higher terms:

\[
Q(2+u)\ge708588+(478953-723654-134595)u\ge329292>0.
\]

If `u>=1`, the only negative terms are absorbed by the `u^4` term:

\[
\begin{aligned}
1313508u^4-134595u^3-723654u^2
&=u^2(1313508u^2-134595u-723654)\\
&\ge455259u^2>0,
\end{aligned}
\]

and all remaining terms in (4.12) are nonnegative. Thus `P(x)>0`, so `L(t)^2>q(t)^2`; both sides are positive, hence by (4.9),

\[
a(t)/t>L(t)>q(t).
\]

Using (4.6)--(4.7),

\[
\Gamma_1(t)>1,
\]

and `w_lambda>=w_1` gives (4.4) for every `lambda in [0,1]`.

Finally `a(t)/t -> 2/3` and `w_lambda(t)->1/2` as `t->0`, so

\[
\Gamma_\lambda(t)\to5\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac49=\frac{10}{9}.
\]

This proves the proposition. `□`
