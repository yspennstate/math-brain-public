## 3. The ACG `q=3` functional in point-process form

For a probability law `a` on `[0,1]`, let `N_a` be a Poisson point process on `[0,1]` with intensity measure `d a`. Equivalently, `D~Poisson(d)` and, conditional on `D`, the points `alpha_1,...,alpha_D` are iid with law `a`.

For `q=3`, put

\[
A(z)=\frac{2+z}{3},\qquad
B(z)=\frac{1+2z}{3},
\]

\[
S(N)=3\prod_{x\in N}A(x)-3\prod_{x\in N}B(x)+\prod_{x\in N}x,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
R(x,y)=1-\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}3.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The ACG functional is

\[
\Sigma_d(a)
=\mathbb E\log S(N_a)
-\frac d2\iint\log R(x,y)\,a(dx)a(dy).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For the finite-support laws used here all logarithms are integrable with ample Poisson tails, so the directional differentiations below can be justified directly by expanding the finite Poisson series.

## 4. B15 — break the finite-support ansatz on purpose

Previous endpoint work searched two- and three-atom families. That can miss the true minimiser even if the finite-dimensional optimiser has converged. Instead, start from an arbitrary finite-support law `a` and deliberately add a new support point `z` not already present.

Let `nu` be any signed finite measure of total mass zero such that `a_t=a+t nu` remains a probability measure for sufficiently small one-sided `t`. For

\[
F(N):=\log S(N),
\]

Poisson differentiation gives

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mathbb EF(N_{a_t})
=d\int \mathbb E\left[F(N_a+\delta_z)-F(N_a)\right]\nu(dz).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because `nu([0,1])=0`, the `-F(N_a)` term integrates to zero. Differentiating the quadratic edge term gives

\[
-d\int\left[\int\log R(z,y)\,a(dy)\right]\nu(dz).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define the **contact potential**

\[
h_a(z)
:=\mathbb E\log S(N_a+\delta_z)
-\int\log R(z,y)\,a(dy).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then the full first variation is the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
D\Sigma_d(a)[\nu]
=d\int h_a(z)\,\nu(dz).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

In particular, if `x` is an atom of `a` with positive mass, the feasible mass-transfer direction `nu=delta_z-delta_x` yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0+}\Sigma_d(a+t(\delta_z-\delta_x))
=d\,[h_a(z)-h_a(x)].}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Consequence: exact KKT contact condition

If a finite-support law `a` is a local minimiser against arbitrary support creation/mass transfer, then every support point `x` must be a global minimiser of the scalar function `h_a`:

\[
\boxed{
h_a(z)\ge h_a(x)\quad\text{for every }z\in[0,1]
\text{ and every positive-mass support point }x.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

All positive-mass support points therefore have the same contact value. This is a necessary condition only; the ACG functional is not shown convex, so (4.6) by itself is not a global-optimality certificate.

### The useful simplification is one-dimensional

For a realised point multiset `N`, write

\[
P_A=\prod_{x\in N}A(x),\qquad
P_B=\prod_{x\in N}B(x),\qquad
P_0=\prod_{x\in N}x.
\]

Insertion of one point is **affine** in the new coordinate:

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(N+\delta_z)
&=3P_AA(z)-3P_BB(z)+P_0z\\
&=(2P_A-P_B)+z(P_A-2P_B+P_0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Likewise

\[
R(z,y)=\frac{2+y+(1-y)z}{3}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus `h_a` is not an opaque functional over probability measures; it is a one-variable difference of log-affine mixtures:

\[
\boxed{
h_a(z)
=\mathbb E\log(C_N+D_Nz)
-\int\log\frac{2+y+(1-y)z}{3}\,a(dy),}
\tag{4.9}
\]

where

\[
C_N=2P_A-P_B,\qquad D_N=P_A-2P_B+P_0.
\]

For `z in [0,1]` the arguments in (4.9) are positive by the same probabilistic interpretation used in the exact ACG certificates.

This is the substantive B15 outcome: breaking the support restriction does **not** create another high-dimensional variational problem. The missing first-order condition is a scalar global inequality on `[0,1]`.

## 5. B19 — make the variational search an actual descent algorithm

Identity (4.5) gives a constructive support-generation procedure rather than another blind finite-support optimisation.

### Column-generation scheme

Starting with a finite-support law `a`:

1. Compute or rigorously enclose the scalar contact potential `h_a(z)` on all `z in [0,1]`.
2. Let `x` be a positive-mass support point with maximal contact value among the current support and seek a point `z_*` with smaller contact value.
3. If one certifies
   \[
   h_a(z_*)<h_a(x),
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
   then (4.5) proves that transferring a sufficiently small positive mass from `x` to `z_*` strictly lowers `Sigma_d`.
4. Add `z_*` to the support, optimise the one-dimensional transfer amount exactly or with a rigorously bracketed line search, then repeat.
5. A legitimate first-order stopping certificate is not “the finite grid found nothing”; it is a verified continuum inequality (4.6).

This is analogous to column generation / exchange algorithms in optimal design: the current support is the restricted master problem, while minimising `h_a(z)` is the pricing problem. The analogy is methodological; I make no novelty claim for column generation itself.

### Second variation and the line-search obstruction

The same Poisson calculus gives an exact Hessian. For a zero-mass signed direction `nu`, let

\[
\Delta_zF(N)=F(N+\delta_z)-F(N).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
D^2\Sigma_d(a)[\nu,\nu]
=d^2\iint\mathbb E\left[\Delta_z\Delta_yF(N_a)\right]\nu(dz)\nu(dy)
-d\iint\log R(z,y)\,\nu(dz)\nu(dy).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This tells the next run exactly what must be controlled after finding a negative contact gap: the first derivative guarantees a nonempty descent interval, and (5.2) controls how far the new atom can be moved before curvature reverses the gain.

Differentiating (4.9) also gives

\[
h_a'(z)
=\mathbb E\frac{D_N}{C_N+D_Nz}
-\int\frac{1-y}{2+y+(1-y)z}\,a(dy),
\tag{5.3}
\]

\[
h_a''(z)
=-\mathbb E\frac{D_N^2}{(C_N+D_Nz)^2}
+\int\frac{(1-y)^2}{(2+y+(1-y)z)^2}\,a(dy).
\tag{5.4}
\]

The two terms in (5.4) have opposite signs. Therefore the desired continuum pricing problem is not reduced to a trivial convexity argument. This is the precise failure mode of the obvious “just minimise `h` by endpoint checking” shortcut.

**B19 outcome:** the infinite-dimensional support search is reduced to a reproducible one-dimensional pricing problem plus a one-dimensional line search, with an exact descent theorem. What is still missing is a rigorous global enclosure of `h_a(z)-min_{x in supp(a)} h_a(x)` on the continuum and, beyond first-order stationarity, a global argument ruling out nonlocal measure directions.
