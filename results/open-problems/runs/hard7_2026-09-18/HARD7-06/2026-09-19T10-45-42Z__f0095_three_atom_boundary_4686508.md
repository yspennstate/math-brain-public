## 3. The q=3 ACG functional used in this run

I keep the same exact `q=3` specialization used in the previous certified upper-bound runs. For a probability law `a` on `[0,1]`, let `D~Poisson(d)` and let `alpha_1,...,alpha_D` be iid from `a`. Define

\[
A(z)=\frac{2+z}{3},\qquad B(z)=\frac{1+2z}{3},
\]

\[
S(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_D)
=3\prod_{h=1}^D A(\alpha_h)
 -3\prod_{h=1}^D B(\alpha_h)
 +\prod_{h=1}^D\alpha_h,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
R(\alpha,\beta)=1-\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}3.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The functional evaluated by the exact certificate is

\[
\Sigma^*_{d,3}(a)
=
\mathbb E\log S(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_D)
-\frac d2\,\mathbb E\log R(\alpha,\beta).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For every incoming tuple in `[0,1]^D`, `S` is a probability and therefore

\[
0<S\le1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Ayre--Coja-Oghlan--Greenhill define the corresponding variational threshold `d_3^*`; their theorem gives

\[
d>d_3^*\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \chi(G(n,d/n))>3\quad\text{w.h.p.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus any explicit law with `Sigma^*_{d,3}(a)<0` proves `d_3^*<=d` and yields a rigorous non-3-colourability endpoint at every strictly larger average degree.

## 4. B5 — add a parameter and differentiate: probe the two-atom law in the full measure space

The preceding finite-support searches were optimizing *inside* a two-atom family. That can hide a descent direction that changes the support itself. I therefore perturbed an arbitrary law `a` toward a new atom `z`:

\[
a_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)a+\varepsilon\delta_z.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Write `N` for a Poisson point process on `[0,1]` with intensity `d a`. Differentiating the Poisson expectation in the signed intensity direction `d(\delta_z-a)` gives the formal first variation of the vertex term,

\[
 d\left[
 \mathbb E\log S(N\cup\{z\})
 -\int \mathbb E\log S(N\cup\{x\})\,a(dx)
 \right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

The edge term is quadratic in `a`, so its derivative is

\[
-d\left[
\int \log R(z,y)\,a(dy)
-
\iint \log R(x,y)\,a(dx)a(dy)
\right].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore the Gateaux direction tested numerically was

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_a(z)=d\Big(&\mathbb E\log S(N\cup\{z\})
-\int \mathbb E\log S(N\cup\{x\})a(dx)\\
&-\int\log R(z,y)a(dy)
+\iint\log R(x,y)a(dx)a(dy)\Big).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a useful diagnostic because a genuine interior minimizer over probability measures must not possess a negative feasible first variation.

Applied to the previous nearly optimal two-atom law, an exploratory floating-point scan did **not** point to a new interior support location. The descent signal accumulated at the endpoint `z=1`. ## 7. What the new route says mathematically
