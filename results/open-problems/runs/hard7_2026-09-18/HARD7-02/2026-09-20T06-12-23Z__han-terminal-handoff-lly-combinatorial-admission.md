## 11. B2 — attack the negation first: zero columns do not refute weak ESD when d diverges

Let `Z_n` be the number of zero columns of `M_n`. For a fixed column `j`, each of the `n` independent rows excludes `j` with probability `1-d/n`. Hence

`P(column j is zero) = (1-d/n)^n <= e^{-d}`

and

`E[Z_n/n] = (1-d/n)^n <= e^{-d}`.

Therefore, for every sequence `d(n)->infinity`,

`Z_n/n -> 0`

in `L1`, hence in probability.

Thus the classical zero-column mechanism genuinely makes `M_n` singular below its logarithmic threshold, but it creates only `o_P(n)` zero columns whenever `d->infinity`. It cannot by itself contradict **weak ESD** convergence to the circular law.

This distinction matters because requiring nonsingularity of the unshifted matrix is stronger than the source ESD statement in sublogarithmic regimes.

## 12. B6 — deform from iid Bernoulli: exact projected isotropy

Let `R=(R_1,...,R_n)` be one random row and `p=d/n`. Then

`E R_i=p`,

`Var(R_i)=p(1-p)`,

and for `i != j`,

`E[R_i R_j] = d(d-1)/(n(n-1))`,

so

`Cov(R_i,R_j) = -p(1-p)/(n-1)`.

Consequently the complete covariance matrix is exactly

`Cov(R) = p(1-p) * n/(n-1) * (I - J/n)`.

Under the source normalization `sqrt(d(1-d/n)) = sqrt(n p(1-p))`, the centered normalized row therefore has covariance

`(1/(n-1)) * (I - J/n)`.

Hence on the deterministic hyperplane `1^perp`, the row is **exactly isotropic at second order**, with covariance `1/(n-1)` times the identity, for every `d`. The fixed-row-sum dependence is visible as the single null direction `1`, not as a covariance anisotropy that deteriorates when `d` becomes sparse.

This does not prove circular-law universality: the hard issue is discrete small-ball/log-integrability, not covariance. But it removes a tempting false obstruction and suggests projecting away the rank-one mean/constraint before comparing to the iid sparse Bernoulli model.
