## 2. Input retained from the preceding run: join majorization

The preceding report proved the route theorem `R02-UC-MAJ`. If `L` is a finite join-semilattice of size `m`, `U_1,...,U_r` are iid uniform on `L`, and `J_r=U_1\vee\cdots\vee U_r`, then, writing the atom probabilities of `J_r` in increasing order as

\[
p_{(1)}\le\cdots\le p_{(m)},
\]

one has

\[
\sum_{k=1}^s p_{(k)}\le (s/m)^r
\qquad(1\le s\le m).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `r=2`, the chain comparator is

\[
q_k=\frac{2k-1}{m^2},\qquad
Q_s:=\sum_{k\le s}q_k=\frac{s^2}{m^2}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The previous theorem says only that every semilattice join law lies on the more concentrated side of the chain law. Method #15 asks whether the hypothetical **sub-half marginal condition** forces a quantitative distance from the chain extremizer. It does.

## 3. Method #15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: sub-half marginals force a quantitative chain escape

### R02-UC-RANK-GAP (complete proof; novelty UNVERIFIED)

Let `F` be a finite union-closed family of size `m>=2`, and suppose every ground element has frequency at most `c m`, where

\[
0\le c<\frac12.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Choose **any** linear extension

\[
A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_m
\]

of inclusion, and let `I_s={A_1,...,A_s}`. Let `X,Y` be iid uniform on `F`, and put `J=X\cup Y`. Define the escape count

\[
E_s=
|\{(A,B)\in I_s^2:A\cup B\notin I_s\}|.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then

\[
\Pr(J\in I_s)=\frac{s^2-E_s}{m^2},
\tag{3.3}
\]

and the cumulative chain deficit obeys

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}
\left[
\left(\frac{s}{m}\right)^2-\Pr(J\in I_s)
\right]
\ge
(1-2c)\left(1-\frac1m\right).
}
\tag{RANK-GAP}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{m^2}\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}E_s
\ge
(1-2c)\left(1-\frac1m\right).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

So a hypothetical Frankl counterexample with all frequencies `<m/2` cannot be even approximately chain-like in the **integrated rank-escape** sense: a positive constant fraction of ordered-pair mass, integrated over every prefix cut, must jump above its current inclusion rank.

### Proof

Every prefix `I_s` of a linear extension is a lower ideal. Therefore `J in I_s` implies `X,Y in I_s`. Conversely, among the `s^2` ordered pairs from `I_s^2`, exactly `E_s` have union outside `I_s`. This proves (3.3).

It remains to lower-bound `sum_s E_s`. First bound the number of ordered comparable pairs.

Let

\[
N_\subseteq=|\{(A,B)\in F^2:A\subseteq B\}|.
\]

For each nonempty `A`, choose one element `x in A`. Every `B superset A` contains `x`, so by (3.1) there are at most `cm` possible `B`. If the empty set belongs to `F`, it has exactly `m` supersets. Hence, uniformly whether or not the empty set is present,

\[
N_\subseteq\le cm(m-1)+m.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The set of ordered comparable pairs is the union of the relations `A subseteq B` and `B subseteq A`; their intersection consists exactly of the `m` diagonal pairs. Thus

\[
N_{\rm comp}
\le 2[cm(m-1)+m]-m
=2cm(m-1)+m.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore the number of **ordered incomparable pairs** is at least

\[
N_{\rm inc}
\ge m^2-[2cm(m-1)+m]
=(1-2c)m(m-1).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Now take an ordered incomparable pair `(A_i,A_j)`. Union-closure gives `C=A_i union A_j in F`, and incomparability makes `C` strictly larger than both inputs. Therefore in every linear extension

\[
\operatorname{ind}(C)>\max(i,j).
\]

At the prefix

\[
s=\max(i,j),
\]

both inputs lie in `I_s` but their union does not. Hence this ordered incomparable pair contributes at least once to `sum_s E_s`. Consequently

\[
\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}E_s\ge N_{\rm inc}
\ge(1-2c)m(m-1).
\]

Divide by `m^2` and use (3.3). QED.

### Rank-variable form

If `R(A_k)=k`, then `R(X)` and `R(Y)` are independent uniform ranks on `{1,...,m}`. Let

\[
M=\max(R(X),R(Y)).
\]

Since `R(J)>=M`, and the tail-sum identity converts the prefix escape probabilities into mean rank excess, `(RANK-GAP)` is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[R(J)]-\mathbb E[M]
\ge
(1-2c)\left(1-\frac1m\right).
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

This is the useful asymmetry statement: **sub-half marginals force the join to overshoot the chain maximum by a definite integrated rank amount, for every linear extension.**

I make no novelty claim for this elementary counting mechanism. The result is registered as a route theorem only.

## 4. Quantitative entropy consequence — and the precise reason it still does not move the metric

Let the atom probabilities of `J=X union Y` be sorted increasingly as

\[
p_1\le\cdots\le p_m,
\]

and let `q_k=(2k-1)/m^2` be the chain comparator. Put

\[
P_s=\sum_{k\le s}p_k,
\qquad
Q_s=\sum_{k\le s}q_k=\frac{s^2}{m^2},
\qquad
D_s=Q_s-P_s.
\]

Because the sum of the `s` smallest atom probabilities is at most the mass of **any** `s`-element subset, in particular `I_s`, equations (3.2)-(3.3) give

\[
D_s
\ge
Q_s-\Pr(J\in I_s)
=
\frac{E_s}{m^2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now use the exact identity, with base-two logarithms,

\[
H(q)-H(p)
=
D_{\rm KL}(p\|q)
+
\sum_{k=1}^m(p_k-q_k)\log_2q_k.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The first term is nonnegative. Summation by parts and `P_s-Q_s=-D_s` yield

\[
\sum_{k=1}^m(p_k-q_k)\log_2q_k
=
\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}D_s
\log_2\frac{q_{s+1}}{q_s}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H(q)-H(J)
\ge
\frac1{m^2}
\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}
E_s\log_2\frac{2s+1}{2s-1}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Combining `(RANK-GAP)` with the smallest weight, attained at `s=m-1`, gives the explicit stability estimate

\[
\boxed{
H(J)
\le
H(q_{m,2})-
(1-2c)\left(1-\frac1m\right)
\log_2\frac{2m-1}{2m-3}.
}
\tag{R02-UC-ENT-STAB}
\]

This is stronger than the preceding run's bare chain entropy extremality under the hypothetical sub-half marginal assumption.

But it also diagnoses the obstruction sharply:

\[
\log_2\frac{2m-1}{2m-3}=\Theta(m^{-1}).
\]

Thus the **guaranteed** extra entropy deficit obtained from mere incomparability/rank escape is only `O((1-2c)/m)`. The previous chain deficit itself is only `O(1)` for fixed number of unions. Neither is on the `Omega(log m)` scale needed to change the asymptotic constant through the current entropy framework.

So this theorem does **not** move `c_UC`. It tells us exactly what a successful strengthening must add: not just many incomparable pairs, but a mechanism forcing substantial **rank distance** between `A,B` and `A union B`, or an equivalent weighted escape estimate whose logarithmic weights do not all collapse near the top of a linear extension.

No exact-arithmetic checker is needed here: these are theorem-sized symbolic inequalities with the complete proof on the page, not a finite enumeration claim.

## 5. Method #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

The rank-gap proof survives several standard translations, and each language clarifies a different missing ingredient.

### 5.1 Join-semilattice / lattice language

A union-closed family is a finite join-semilattice under inclusion. `R02-UC-RANK-GAP` says that a hypothetical sub-half-marginal counterexample has a positive density of incomparable ordered pairs, and every such pair forces the join to cross at least one lower-ideal boundary in every linear extension.

Bouchard's 2025 lattice formulation develops necessary conditions for a minimum counterexample in terms of join-irreducibles and principal filters. That makes a natural next question: can those minimum-counterexample conditions force **long** rank jumps, rather than merely one-step jumps? If yes, the weighted sum in (4.4) could become much larger.

### 5.2 Horn / Boolean-model language

Lozin--Zamaraev identify union-closed families with OR-closed models of appropriate Horn/dual-Horn Boolean structure and prove Frankl for several Boolean-function classes. In this language a linear extension is an ordering of models compatible with bitwise implication, while `(3.2)` counts pairs of early models whose coordinatewise OR escapes the early prefix.

The useful statistic is therefore not a single coordinate entropy but a **prefix escape profile of the OR map**. This suggests asking for structural lower bounds on OR-rank expansion in Boolean classes not already covered by known Horn subclasses.

### 5.3 Maximal-stable-set graph language

Bruhn--Charbit--Schaudt--Telle give an equivalent graph formulation: every nontrivial graph should have adjacent vertices each occurring in at most half of its maximal stable sets; the unresolved core can be restricted to bipartite graphs. Their later/random-bipartite work shows that typical bipartite graphs almost satisfy the desired property.

This language warns against expecting a purely symmetric extremizer. A hypothetical counterexample must organize the maximal-stable-set measure so that every edge is protected by a heavy endpoint, while the union-closed language simultaneously forces many incomparable set pairs. Translating the rank-jump statistic into maximal-stable-set transitions may expose a graph expansion quantity that is invisible to one-letter entropy.

None of these translations is claimed new. The point of #18 is route selection: the missing object appears to be **join/OR expansion across many ordered cuts**, not another scalar marginal optimization.

## 7. What failed, and what this run actually learned

The preceding run proposed accumulating the `O(1)` chain entropy deficit across conditional union-closed fibers. This run stress-tested the idea in the more basic unconditional setting.

The good news is that the sub-half marginal assumption really does force a quantitative global departure from the chain: `(RANK-GAP)` gives an integrated Lorenz/rank gap of at least

\[
(1-2c)(1-1/m).
\]

The bad news is that Shannon entropy weights the last rank cuts only by `Theta(1/m)`. With no control on **where** the escape occurs, the guaranteed extra entropy deficit collapses to `O(1/m)`. Therefore a bare “many incomparable pairs” theorem or a bare “every non-chain has strict entropy loss” theorem cannot move the universal constant.

This is a useful failure diagnosis, not a metric improvement.

A route that can actually beat the baseline must prove at least one of the following kinds of strengthening:

- a weighted rank-jump lower bound
  \[
  \frac1{m^2}\sum_{A\parallel B}
  \log\frac{R(A\cup B)}{\max(R(A),R(B))}
  \ge \eta(c)>0;
  \]
- an `Omega(log m)` accumulated deficit over a hierarchy of conditional semilattice fibers;
- or a nonuniform/weighted join inequality whose deformation parameter converts minimum-counterexample structure into an extensive information loss.

At present none is proved.
