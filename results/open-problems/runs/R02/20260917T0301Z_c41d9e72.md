## 2. Recovered baseline and the covering-code language

Write

\[
M_n:=\max_{A\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times n}}\operatorname{disc}(A).
\]

For a sign row `r` and signing `x`, after identifying signs with binary words,

\[
|r\cdot x|
=
 n-2\min\{d_H(r,x),d_H(-r,x)\}.
\]

Thus the `n` rows together with their complements form an antipodal binary code `C` of size at most `2n`, and

\[
\operatorname{disc}(A)=n-2R(C),
\]

where `R(C)` is its covering radius. Equivalently,

\[
M_n
=
 n-2\min\left\{R:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there is a complement-closed binary code of length }n,\\
\text{size at most }2n,\text{ and covering radius }R
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This exact dictionary is the useful dual language for the present run.

The inherited small-order campaign established exact `M_n` for `2<=n<=11` and an explicit `9 x 9` discrepancy-five matrix, giving the older `5/3` bound. The preceding R02 report upgraded the durable explicit lower bound to `7/sqrt(17)`.

## 3. Method #11 DUALIZE — exact global optimality at order 17

### R02-I0095-C002 — exact order-17 optimum

**STATUS: PROVED.**

\[
\boxed{M_{17}=7.}
\tag{C002}
\]

### Proof

Suppose some `17 x 17` sign matrix had discrepancy at least 9. Then for **every** signing `x` at least one row `r_i` satisfies

\[
|r_i\cdot x|\ge9.
\]

By the Hamming identity above, `x` is then within Hamming distance at most 4 of `r_i` or `-r_i`. Therefore the 34 row/complement words would cover the entire 17-dimensional cube by Hamming balls of radius 4.

A radius-four ball in `Q_17` has cardinality

\[
V(17,4)
=\sum_{j=0}^4\binom{17}{j}
=3214.
\]

Even if all 34 balls were disjoint, their union could have at most

\[
34\,V(17,4)
=34\cdot3214
=109276
<131072
=2^{17}
\]

points. This is impossible. Hence every `17 x 17` sign matrix has a signing with every row sum of absolute value `<9`. Since a sum of 17 signs is odd, this gives

\[
M_{17}\le7.
\]

The preceding exact witness has discrepancy 7, so `M_17=7`. `square`

The finite arithmetic is independently present in this run's exact checker.

## 5. Method #19 MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM — exact XOR-cover coordinate descent

The covering-code formulation permits a search directly in the quotient by complementation rather than over sign matrices.

Normalize an antipodal codeword by fixing one coordinate, so the universe is `F_2^(n-1)`. For a target radius `r`, the folded ball centered at zero is

\[
S_{n,r}
=
\{x:\operatorname{wt}(x)\le r\text{ or }\operatorname{wt}(x)\ge n-r\}.
\]

A center `c` covers the XOR translate `c+S_{n,r}`. The construction problem is therefore:

\[
\text{choose exactly }n\text{ translates of }S_{n,r}\text{ covering }F_2^{n-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

I implemented a deterministic exact-search heuristic with two stages:

1. **greedy:** repeatedly choose the center covering the most currently uncovered quotient points;
2. **coordinate descent:** remove one center at a time and replace it by the exact best center against the current uncovered set.

The best-center step is not sampled: it is computed simultaneously for every center by exact XOR convolution using the Walsh-Hadamard transform.

The three first metric-improving even-order targets tried were:

```text
n=18, radius 5: |S|=12616
  greedy uncovered = 6392
  coordinate-local uncovered = 4800

n=20, radius 6: |S|=60460
  greedy uncovered = 5348
  coordinate-local uncovered = 4072

n=22, radius 7: |S|=280600
  greedy uncovered = 2676
  coordinate-local uncovered = 2399
```

At `n=22`, the residual is only

\[

of the quotient cube, but it is still nonzero. The archived output explicitly says that a nonzero residual is **not** an impossibility proof.
