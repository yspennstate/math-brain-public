## 2. Method #3 — prove something stronger: a join-majorization theorem

The previous run proved an elementary multi-letter total-correlation inequality by using only the weak fact

\[
H(X^{(1)}\cup\cdots\cup X^{(r)})\le \log_2|F|.
\]

That discards almost all of the semilattice structure. This run replaces it by an exact distributional comparison, not merely an entropy estimate.

### R02-UC-MAJ (complete argument; novelty UNVERIFIED)

Let `L` be a finite join-semilattice of size `m>=2`. Let

\[
U_1,\ldots,U_r\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(L),\qquad r\ge2,
\]

and let

\[
J_r=U_1\vee\cdots\vee U_r.
\]

Write the `m` atom probabilities of `J_r`, sorted increasingly, as

\[
p_{(1)}\le p_{(2)}\le\cdots\le p_{(m)}.
\]

Then for every `s=1,...,m`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{(i)}\le \left(\frac{s}{m}\right)^r.
}
\tag{UC-MAJ}
\]

Equivalently, the law of `J_r` majorizes the law of the maximum of `r` iid uniform points on an `m`-element chain. If

\[
q_k=\frac{k^r-(k-1)^r}{m^r},\qquad k=1,\ldots,m,
\]

then `q_1<...<q_m`, `sum_{k<=s}q_k=(s/m)^r`, and `(UC-MAJ)` says that the increasing Lorenz partial sums of `J_r` are no larger than those of `q`.

Consequently, by Schur concavity,

\[
\boxed{H(J_r)\le H(q_{m,r}).}
\tag{UC-ENT}
\]

For Shannon entropy, equality in `(UC-ENT)` holds if and only if `L` is a chain.

### Proof

Choose any linear extension

\[
x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_m
\]

of the partial order on `L`, and let

\[
I_s=\{x_1,\ldots,x_s\}.
\]

Every prefix of a linear extension is a lower ideal. If `J_r in I_s`, then every input satisfies `U_j <= J_r`; because `I_s` is a lower ideal, every `U_j` also belongs to `I_s`. Hence

\[
\{J_r\in I_s\}\subseteq\{U_1,\ldots,U_r\in I_s\},
\]

and therefore, by independence and uniformity,

\[
\Pr(J_r\in I_s)\le\left(\frac{|I_s|}{m}\right)^r
=\left(\frac{s}{m}\right)^r.
\tag{1}
\]

The sum of the `s` smallest atom probabilities is at most the probability mass of **any** `s`-element subset, in particular `I_s`. Thus

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{(i)}
\le\Pr(J_r\in I_s)
\le\left(\frac{s}{m}\right)^r,
\]

which is `(UC-MAJ)`.

On an `m`-element chain, join is maximum, so

\[
\Pr(J_r=x_k)=\left(\frac{k}{m}\right)^r-
\left(\frac{k-1}{m}\right)^r=q_k,
\]

and its increasing prefix sums attain equality `(s/m)^r`. Therefore the law of `J_r` majorizes `q_{m,r}`, and Shannon entropy, being Schur-concave, gives `(UC-ENT)`.

For equality, strict Schur concavity forces the sorted atom-probability vector of `J_r` to equal `q_{m,r}`. Then the leftmost and rightmost terms in

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_{(i)}
\le \Pr(J_r\in I_s)
\le (s/m)^r
\]

are equal for every `s`, so equality holds throughout. The event on the left side of (1) is a subset of the event on the right; since the underlying iid law gives every tuple positive mass, equality of probabilities means that whenever all `U_j` lie in `I_s`, their join also lies in `I_s`. Thus every prefix `I_s` is join-closed.

If `L` were not a chain, choose incomparable `x,y`. Their join `z=x\vee y` is strictly above each. In every linear extension both `x` and `y` occur before `z`; the prefix ending at the later of `x,y` contains `x,y` but not `z`, contradicting join-closure. Hence `L` is a chain. The converse is immediate from the maximum calculation. QED.

I register this as a **route theorem**, not as movement in `c_UC`. I have not found the exact statement in the bounded prior-art search below, but novelty is UNVERIFIED and no publication claim is made.

## 3. Method #12 — the conserved object is every lower ideal, not a coordinate marginal

The proof exposes the stronger invariant that the previous entropy route missed. For **every lower ideal** `I` of a finite join-semilattice under uniform iid inputs,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(J_r\in I)\le \left(\frac{|I|}{m}\right)^r.
}
\tag{LI}
\]

This is a whole-poset constraint. It does not mention an element/coordinate of a set family, binary entropy, or any chosen coupling parameter. It says that an upward operation (join) can enter a lower ideal only if every input was already there.

For a union-closed family, ordered by inclusion, union is exactly the join. Thus `(LI)` supplies `m-1` simultaneous nested constraints after any linear extension. Passing from those constraints to majorization is what produces the strict entropy loss below `log m`.

This is the useful answer to method #12: the route should conserve/order **ideal mass**, rather than continue optimizing only one-coordinate marginals.

## 4. Method #27 — swap the quantifiers, then recover entropy as a corollary

The old statement was effectively:

> for the particular random variable `Z_r`, find an entropy upper bound.

The stronger statement is:

> for **every** lower ideal `I` of **every** finite join-semilattice, control `Pr(J_r in I)`.

Once this universal family of inequalities is proved, entropy follows automatically by majorization. This quantifier swap matters because it creates a reusable structural object that can survive conditioning on blocks of coordinates, whereas the scalar entropy inequality loses that information immediately.

For a union-closed family `F`, set `m=|F|`, take uniform `X`, iid copies `X^(1),...,X^(r)`, and

\[
Z_r=X^{(1)}\cup\cdots\cup X^{(r)}.
\]

Define the exact chain deficit

\[
\delta_{m,r}=\log_2m-H(q_{m,r})>0.
\]

The theorem gives

\[
\boxed{H(Z_r)\le \log_2m-\delta_{m,r}.}
\tag{2}
\]

In exact finite form, with `d_k=k^r-(k-1)^r`,

\[
\delta_{m,r}
=\frac1{m^r}\sum_{k=1}^{m}d_k\log_2d_k-(r-1)\log_2m.
\]

For fixed `r` and `m->infinity`, a Riemann-sum calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{m,r}\longrightarrow
\delta_r=\log_2 r-\frac{r-1}{r\ln2}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed the scaled chain-maximum distribution converges to the Beta(`r`,1) density `f(t)=r t^{r-1}`, whose differential entropy is `-ln r+(r-1)/r` nats. For `r=2`,

\[
\delta_2=1-\frac1{2\ln2}\approx0.27865248\text{ bits}.
\]

## 5. Strict strengthening of the previous multi-letter route

The preceding R02 report used total-correlation data processing to prove

\[
(r-1)\log_2m
\le
\sum_i\left[rh_2(p_i)-h_2(1-(1-p_i)^r)\right],
\]

where `p_i=P(i in X)`.

Replacing only the crude entropy step `H(Z_r)<=log m` by `(2)` gives the strictly stronger inequality

\[
\boxed{
(r-1)\log_2m+\delta_{m,r}
\le
\sum_i\left[rh_2(p_i)-h_2(1-(1-p_i)^r)\right].
}
\tag{R02-UC-r+}
\]

The proof is the same data-processing proof as before except that

\[
TC(Z_r)=\sum_i h_2(1-(1-p_i)^r)-H(Z_r)
\]

is now bounded below using `(2)`.

This is a real structural strengthening: even the chain cannot make the union of iid uniform members uniform, and every non-chain has strictly more entropy loss than the chain extremizer.

## 7. A concrete next route: conditional union-closed fibers

There is an immediate reason the lower-ideal theorem may survive where the current single-letter method saturates.

Fix a coordinate block `S` and a trace `w` on `S`. The fiber

\[
F_w=\{A\in F:A\cap S=w\}
\]

is itself union-closed: if `A,B` have the same trace `w`, then `(A union B) cap S=w`, and `A union B` belongs to `F`.

Therefore `R02-UC-MAJ` applies **inside every nonempty prefix fiber**, with `m` replaced by `|F_w|`. This opens a genuinely multi-letter direction: condition on a block, apply exact semilattice entropy loss fiberwise, and then account for the trace entropy. The current single-letter ceiling results do not by themselves rule out a proof that accumulates structural deficits over many nontrivial fibers.

I do not claim such accumulation yet. The next run should first try to prove or refute a quantitative statement of the form

\[
\sum_w P(W=w)\,\delta_{|F_w|,2}
\ge \eta(c)\,H(W)
\]

or a related block-information inequality under the hypothesis that every ground element has frequency at most `c<1/2`. A counterexample to any naive form is just as useful, because it will identify what structural statistic has to accompany fiber size.
