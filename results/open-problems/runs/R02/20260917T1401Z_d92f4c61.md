## 4. (c) What a successful proof must contain that no route so far contains

A successful continuation needs **one genuinely new transfer/amortization principle**. In operational form, at least one of the following must be supplied:

1. **Scale-distributed escape:** a proof that under `p_x<c<1/2`, join escape cannot concentrate only near the top of every linear extension/lower-ideal chain; a positive amount must occur over enough logarithmic scales to yield a size-uniform information gain.
2. **Fiber transfer:** a statement that when one conditions/deletes/splits on a coordinate, either the child fiber retains a quantitatively useful sub-half condition or the act of losing that condition itself pays a definite entropy/rank cost. This would make recursion amortizable.
3. **A conserved potential:** a nonnegative potential `P(F)` for which each decomposition step either earns a definite entropy gain or decreases `P` by a controlled factor, so a depth-`O(log |F|)` recursion cannot pass through many free steps.
4. **A genuinely non-single-letter dual certificate:** constraints on whole-set pair/tuple laws that survive Moffat/Costa–Sadhu's pointwise ceiling and prevent the dual mass from hiding on cheap cuts.

Nothing proved so far contains one of these in a form that is uniform in `|F|`.

The nearest solved problem is Tian's **empty-set-free height-at-most-four theorem** (arXiv:2608.25147, 2026-08-25).

What that proof environment has:

- finitely many inclusion layers;
- critical elements tied to near-top structure;
- coatoms and avoidance fibers with bounded-depth normal forms;
- coordinate deletion producing a concrete matching-defect obstruction;
- enough layer control to convert local structure into a global frequency conclusion.

What the unrestricted problem lacks:

- a bounded number of layers;
- a guarantee that conditioning/deletion preserves the useful marginal inequality;
- a finite normal form for the residual fibers;
- a transfer theorem preventing entropy/rank deficit from dissipating as one passes between many layers.

Jiang's global-coupling result is the nearest method-level analogue: it does create a positive finite-size entropy gain, but explicitly not one that remains uniform as family size grows. Together these two neighboring results point to the same missing object: **a size-uniform transfer of local structure into global information gain**.
