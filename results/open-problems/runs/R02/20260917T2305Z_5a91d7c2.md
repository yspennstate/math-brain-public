## 3. Idea before code: quantify exactly what rho-mixing plus bilateral reconstruction would have to hide

The previous five runs established that one-scale secret sharing is too flexible to contradict rho-mixing. A uniform secret can be recovered from two shares even when each share individually has zero HGR correlation with the secret and the two shares are themselves independent.

The useful question is therefore not whether bilateral determinism causes ordinary pairwise dependence. It is:

> **How much conditional left-right dependence must be created when the central block is revealed, if each remote side individually becomes HGR-invisible to that block?**

This leads to a finite inequality that can be proved exactly and then lifted to any hypothetical D0031 witness.

No checker was written before the idea: the claims below are analytic identities/inequalities over arbitrary finite variables, not a finite exhaustive classification.

## 4. Method #8 — DISCRETIZE: the exact finite secret-sharing proxy

Let `S,L,R` be finite-valued random variables. Think of `S` as a central block and `L,R` as finite windows cut out of the remote left and right tails.

Suppose first that

\[
H(S\mid L,R)=0,
\qquad
S\perp L,
\qquad
S\perp R.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then the elementary interaction-information identity

\[
I(S;L,R)
=
I(S;L)+I(S;R)+I(L;R\mid S)-I(L;R)
\tag{4.2}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
I(L;R\mid S)-I(L;R)=H(S).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus a perfectly hidden secret that is exactly reconstructed jointly forces the **largest possible increase** in left-right dependence when the secret is revealed.

The bound is sharp in the strongest possible sense. Let `G` be a finite abelian group, let `S,L` be independent Haar-uniform on `G`, and set

\[
R=L+S.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then `S` is independent of each share, `L` and `R` are themselves independent, and `S=R-L`; hence

\[
I(L;R)=0,
\qquad
I(L;R\mid S)=\log_2|G|=H(S).
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is ordinary additive secret sharing / one-time-pad structure; no novelty claim is made. Its importance here is diagnostic: **a finite one-cut discretization of D0031 cannot possibly yield an impossibility theorem from pairwise HGR alone.** At one scale the desired behavior is not merely possible; it is extremally possible with zero unconditional maximal correlation.

The route must therefore use the nested stationary consistency of these secret-sharing relations across arbitrarily remote cuts.

**#8 outcome:** no metric movement. The smallest finite proxy is completely understood and shows that any finite obstruction must include overlap/stationarity across more than one cut, not just one center plus two shares.

## 5. Method #11 — DUALIZE: HGR controls finite-secret mutual information through the singular spectrum

The exact bridge needed for the process problem is a standard but useful finite-alphabet inequality. I write the proof because it fixes the constants and makes clear what HGR does and does not control.

### Lemma R02-D31-HGRMI — a finite-secret HGR-to-information bound

Let `S` have support size `m>=2`, let `Y` be arbitrary, and let

\[
r:=\rho(\sigma(S),\sigma(Y))
\]

be their Hirschfeld–Gebelein–Rényi maximal correlation. Then, with base-two logarithms,

\[
\boxed{
I(S;Y)
\le
\log_2\!\left(1+(m-1)r^2\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

Discard zero-probability values of `S`, write `p_s=P(S=s)>0`, and define the centered likelihood-ratio kernel

\[
K(s,y)=\frac{P(S=s\mid Y=y)}{p_s}-1.
\]

On centered `L^2`, the conditional-expectation operator

\[
T:L_0^2(S)\to L_0^2(Y),
\qquad
Tf=E[f(S)\mid Y],
\]

has kernel `K` and operator norm

\[
\|T\|_{op}=r.
\]

Its rank is at most `m-1`. Its Hilbert–Schmidt norm is exactly the chi-square dependence:

\[
\|T\|_{HS}^2
=
\chi^2(P_{S,Y}\Vert P_S P_Y).
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `sigma_1,...,sigma_{m-1}` are its nonzero singular values, each is at most `r`, hence

\[
\chi^2(P_{S,Y}\Vert P_SP_Y)
=
\sum_j\sigma_j^2
\le
(m-1)r^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Finally KL divergence is bounded by Rényi divergence of order two:

\[
I(S;Y)
=D(P_{S,Y}\Vert P_SP_Y)
\le
\log_2\!\left(1+\chi^2(P_{S,Y}\Vert P_SP_Y)\right),
\]

which together with (5.3) proves (5.1). `□`

This is standard maximal-correlation / chi-square / strong-data-processing machinery; related information-theoretic prior art includes Asoodeh–Alajaji–Linder (2015) and Li–El Gamal (2014/2016). I make no novelty claim for (5.1).

### Proposition R02-D31-SYN — approximate reconstruction forces near-maximal negative co-information

Let `S,L,R` be finite-valued, let `|supp(S)|=m`, and suppose

\[
H(S\mid L,R)\le\varepsilon.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Set

\[
r_L=\rho(S,L),\qquad r_R=\rho(S,R).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
I(L;R\mid S)-I(L;R)
\ge
H(S)-\varepsilon
-
\log_2(1+(m-1)r_L^2)
-
\log_2(1+(m-1)r_R^2).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

From (4.2),

\[
I(L;R\mid S)-I(L;R)
=I(S;L,R)-I(S;L)-I(S;R).
\]

The reconstruction assumption gives

\[
I(S;L,R)=H(S)-H(S\mid L,R)\ge H(S)-\varepsilon.
\]

Apply Lemma R02-D31-HGRMI separately to `(S,L)` and `(S,R)`. `□`

Equivalently, the three-way co-information

\[
I(S;L;R):=I(L;R)-I(L;R\mid S)
\]

satisfies

\[
I(S;L;R)
\le
-H(S)+\varepsilon+	ext{the two HGR leakage terms}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Since the difference on the left of (5.5) can never exceed `H(S)`, the secret-sharing example (4.4) shows the estimate is exactly sharp at zero leakage.

**#11 outcome:** no source decision, but the obstruction is now dualized into a quantitative spectral statement. Small one-sided HGR does not kill bilateral reconstruction; it forces the reconstruction information into an almost maximally synergistic conditional channel.

## 6. Method #28 — TYPICAL rather than ALL: lift the finite inequality to every hypothetical D0031 witness

Assume for this section that a D0031 witness `X=(X_i)_{i in Z}` exists on a finite alphabet of size `q`.

Fix `k>=0` and define the central secret

\[
S_k:=X_{[-k,k]}.
\]

For `n>k` and `M>n`, define finite remote windows

\[
L_{n,M}:=X_{[-M,-n]},
\qquad
R_{n,M}:=X_{[n,M]}.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal G_n
=
\sigma(X_i:i\le -n)\vee\sigma(X_i:i\ge n).
\]

Bilateral determinism means the full process sigma-field equals the double tail

\[
\bigcap_{r\ge1}\mathcal G_r.
\]

Hence `S_k` is measurable with respect to **every** `G_n`, not merely asymptotically. The finite-window sigma-fields generated by `(L_{n,M},R_{n,M})` increase to `G_n` as `M->infinity`. Because `S_k` is finite-valued, martingale convergence gives

\[
H(S_k\mid L_{n,M},R_{n,M})\longrightarrow0
\qquad(M\to\infty).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus exact infinite-tail reconstruction can be replaced, at any prescribed error tolerance, by a **finite typical decoder**. This is the correct finite reduction; demanding exact reconstruction from a fixed finite window would be unnecessarily strong.

On the other hand the central block and either remote window are separated by distance at least `n-k`. Monotonicity and stationarity of maximal correlation therefore give

\[
\rho(S_k,L_{n,M})\le \rho_X(n-k),
\qquad
\rho(S_k,R_{n,M})\le \rho_X(n-k).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The support size of `S_k` is at most

\[
m_k\le q^{2k+1}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Combining (5.5), (6.1), and (6.2) yields the following necessary signature.

### Theorem R02-D31-EXTSYN — extensive conditional synergy is necessary

For every fixed `k`, every `n>k`, and every `epsilon>0`, there exists a finite `M>n` such that

\[
\boxed{
I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M}\mid S_k)-I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M})
\ge
H(S_k)-\epsilon
-2\log_2\!\left(1+(q^{2k+1}-1)\rho_X(n-k)^2\right).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently, for every `k` and every `eta>0`, all sufficiently large `n` admit a finite `M=M(n,k,eta)` with

\[
\boxed{
I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M}\mid S_k)-I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M})
\ge H(S_k)-\eta.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

At the same time, the same choice of sufficiently large `n` makes each one-sided leakage arbitrarily small:

\[
I(S_k;L_{n,M}),\ I(S_k;R_{n,M})\to0
\quad\text{uniformly in }M
\tag{6.6}
\]

for fixed `k`, by (5.1)–(6.2).

So any D0031 witness must repeatedly realize an almost perfect two-share secret-sharing geometry: each remote side individually reveals asymptotically nothing about the central block in the strongest finite-secret HGR sense, while the two sides jointly recover it and revealing the center increases their mutual dependence by essentially the full central entropy.

### The entropy gain is necessarily extensive in block length

A nondegenerate finite-state rho-mixing process has strictly positive entropy rate `h(X)>0`.

Indeed, if `h(X)=0`, then

\[
H(X_0\mid X_{-1},X_{-2},\ldots)=0,
\]

so `X_0` is measurable from the entire past. By stationarity and induction every `X_n`, `n>0`, is measurable from `sigma(X_i:i<=0)`. But `X_n` is also measurable from `sigma(X_i:i>=n)`. Since `X_n` is nondegenerate, a nonconstant centered function of `X_n` belongs to both `L^2` spaces, forcing

\[
\rho_X(n)=1
\]

for every `n`, contradicting rho-mixing.

For a stationary finite-alphabet process, block entropy therefore obeys

\[
H(S_k)\ge (2k+1)h(X).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Hence (6.5) strengthens to

\[
\boxed{
I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M}\mid S_k)-I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M})
\ge (2k+1)h(X)-\eta.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

for suitable arbitrarily remote finite windows.

This is the cleanest structural result obtained in the six D0031 runs: **a finite-state rho-mixing bilateral witness must contain volume-order, near-maximal negative interaction information at arbitrarily remote scales.** Pairwise HGR disappears; higher-order conditional synergy remains extensive.

It is still only a necessary condition. The one-time-pad example proves that (6.8) is not contradictory at a single scale. What remains absent is a theorem showing that one finite-state stationary process cannot realize this near-maximal synergy consistently for nested central blocks and arbitrarily many cuts.

**#28 outcome:** no `q_witness` movement. Replacing exact finite-window reconstruction by typical finite-window reconstruction yields a rigorous process-level condition, but the missing obstruction is now explicitly a **multiscale consistency theorem for near-perfect secret sharing**.

## 7. Why this is a better handoff than another local gadget

The previous report asked whether a two-scale finite-state repair gadget with `rho_large<1` exists. This run shows that a generic two-share/two-scale support search is not the right bottleneck unless it encodes stationarity and nested overlap:

- one-cut perfect secrecy + exact recovery is attained exactly by a one-time pad;
- HGR smallness only suppresses the first/second-singular-value channel from the center to either side;
- bilateral determinism pushes the information into **conditional dependence between the two sides**, and a hypothetical witness must make that conditional interaction essentially `H(S_k)` at every scale;
- because `h(X)>0`, that hidden interaction grows linearly with the central block length.

A future reassignment should therefore not enumerate isolated secret-sharing tables. It should attack the **compatibility of a nested family of nearly extremal secret-sharing tables under shifts**.

A plausible next invariant is the co-information defect

\[
\Delta_{k,n,M}
:=H(S_k)-\bigl[I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M}\mid S_k)-I(L_{n,M};R_{n,M})\bigr]\ge0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

D0031 would require, for every fixed `k`,

\[
\inf_{M>n}\Delta_{k,n,M}\to0
\qquad(n\to\infty).
\tag{7.2}
\]

The missing theorem would show that finite alphabet + stationarity + shift consistency impose a nonzero cumulative defect across a growing family of overlapping `S_k`, or else give a construction that makes those defects vanish. This is considerably more specific than “find a better marker hierarchy.”

### (b) Routes tried and the precise failure of each

The six-run durable route ledger is:

1. **Finite quantization of Bradley 1986:** rho-mixing survives a coordinatewise finite factor by data processing, but bilateral determinism need not survive factorization.
2. **Finite-radius / beta-mixing parent factors:** excluded as a positive class because absolute regularity gives trivial double tail.
3. **One-dimensional finite-field linear/Haar subshifts:** excluded in the tested closed linear class; proper shift-invariant linear constraints give a finite two-sided recurrence and nonmixing periodic structure, while the full shift is iid with trivial double tail.
4. **Zero-sum / parity secret sharing:** locally HGR-perfect, but a single randomized global phase used to stationarize a block tiling becomes a common tail variable and forces rho one.
5. **Additive multilevel sharing:** telescopes exactly to one giant parity check, so it does not create genuinely new multiscale structure.
6. **Finite-generator reformulation:** Ornstein–Weiss supplies finite bilaterally deterministic generators abstractly, but no quantitative HGR control for the resulting generator was recovered.
7. **Finite-horizon four-state module:** for every finite `D`, `X_i=(U_i,U_{i-D}+U_{i+D})` repairs every erased interval of length at most `D` and has rho zero beyond `2D`; however as `D->infinity` its local law converges to iid and the repair maps escape to infinity.
8. **Rare marker thinning:** insufficient for HGR. If left/right recognize the same rare marker with small relative disagreement, normalized indicator correlation is near one even when the marker probability tends to zero.
9. **One-cut finite support search:** now shown to be intrinsically non-obstructive by exact secret sharing. The real issue is nested shift-consistency, not the existence of a good finite table.

### (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

A **negative** solution needs a multiscale finite-alphabet theorem forbidding a stationary nested family of asymptotically perfect two-share secret-sharing relations. In the language of this run, it must prove that the co-information defects (7.1) cannot all vanish across overlapping central blocks and increasingly remote cuts when the one-sided HGR operator norms tend to zero.

A **positive** solution needs exactly the opposite object: a fixed finite alphabet and a shift-consistent hierarchy whose one-sided conditional-expectation operators contract to zero, while the pair of remote sides jointly carries every central block through almost maximally synergistic conditional channels. Any scale/phase information itself must remain HGR-blind to each individual side.
