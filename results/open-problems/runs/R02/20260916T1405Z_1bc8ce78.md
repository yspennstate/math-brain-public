## 4. R02-KLS-X002 — the softmax `log n` drift is attained on an explicit product law

**Status: DISPROVED ROUTE, not a refutation of KLS.**

Let `Y` be any centered variance-one one-dimensional log-concave law with third central moment

\[
\tau=\mathbb E Y^3\ne0,
\]

and let `X=(Y_1,...,Y_n)` have iid coordinates. At the isotropic state `A=I`, independence gives covariance-noise matrices

\[
T_k=\tau E_{kk}.
\]

For the Klartag--Lehec softmax potential, along `A+epsilon T_k`,

\[
\Phi_\beta(I+\varepsilon T_k)
=1+\frac1\beta
\log\big(e^{\beta\tau\varepsilon}+n-1\big).
\]

Hence exactly

\[
D\Phi_\beta[I][-I]=-1,
\qquad
D^2\Phi_\beta[I][T_k,T_k]
=\beta\tau^2\frac{n-1}{n^2}.
\]

The stochastic-localization generator at this state is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L\Phi_\beta(I)
=-1+\frac{\tau^2\beta}{2}\left(1-\frac1n\right).
}
\tag{X002.1}
\]

But

\[
\Phi_\beta(I)=1+\frac{\log n}{\beta}.
\]

To start below the stopping level `2`, one must have `beta>log n`. Therefore, for every fixed nonzero `tau`, the **actual** softmax drift is `Omega(log n)` already at the isotropic starting state.

So one cannot remove the logarithm in Klartag--Lehec Lemma 5.2 merely by replacing their upper bound on `c_t` by a universally `O(1)` statewise estimate while keeping the same softmax potential. Such an estimate is false.

Moreover, in any argument whose usable time is controlled by `(initial gap)/(constant times beta)`, the tuning itself is optimal at their choice. Writing `L=log n`,

\[
\frac{2-\Phi_\beta(I)}{\beta}
=\frac{1-L/\beta}{\beta}
=\frac{\beta-L}{\beta^2},
\]

whose unique maximum for `beta>L` is at

\[
\boxed{\beta=2L=2\log n.}
\tag{X002.2}
\]

This does not rule out a subtler softmax argument using future negative drift/path information. It rules out the direct `statewise drift sharpening` route.

### Uniformly strongly log-concave exact example

This is not an artefact of the one-sided exponential law. Let `U` have density

\[
f_U(x)=c\begin{cases}
e^{-x^2/2},&x<0,\\
e^{-2x^2},&x\ge0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
c=\frac23\sqrt{\frac2\pi}.
\]

Its potential is globally convex and has curvature at least `1` away from the joining point. Exact half-Gaussian integration gives

\[
\mathbb EU=-\frac12\sqrt{\frac2\pi},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(U)=\frac{3\pi-2}{4\pi},
\]

and after centering and variance-normalizing,

\[
\boxed{
\tau
=-\frac{\sqrt2(\pi+4)}{(3\pi-2)^{3/2}}\ne0.
}
\tag{X002.3}
\]

The normalized law is uniformly strongly log-concave with a fixed dimension-free curvature. Thus X002 occurs even in a very regular product subclass. A standard smoothing of the single join preserves fixed positive curvature and nonzero third moment if a `C^infty` positive-density approximation is desired.

## 5. R02-KLS-C003 — the cubic Stieltjes root has dimension-free generator at every isotropic starting law

**Status: PROVED, local-in-state intermediary lemma.**

The previous run's cubic-resolvent identity suggests replacing softmax by an adaptive Stieltjes-type barrier. Define `z=z(A)` near `A=I` by

\[
F(A,z)
:=\operatorname{Tr}\left[Q^3-Q\right]=0,
\qquad
Q=A(zI-A)^{-1},
\]

and put `ell=log z`. At `A=I` the unique finite root is `z=2` (`Q=I`).

Let `H` be symmetric and write

\[
S_1=\operatorname{Tr}H,
\qquad
S_2=\operatorname{Tr}H^2.
\]

For

\[
f(\lambda,z)
=h\!\left(\frac\lambda{z-\lambda}\right),
\qquad h(q)=q^3-q,
\]

the exact derivatives at `(lambda,z)=(1,2)` are

\[
f_\lambda=4,\quad f_z=-2,\quad
f_{\lambda\lambda}=32,\quad f_{\lambda z}=-18,\quad f_{zz}=10.
\]

Implicit differentiation gives

\[
Dz_I[H]=\frac{2}{n}S_1,
\]

\[
D^2z_I[H,H]
=\frac{16}{n}
\left(S_2-\frac{S_1^2}{n}\right),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
D\ell_I[H]=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}H}{n},
}
\tag{C003.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
D^2\ell_I[H,H]
=\frac8n\operatorname{Tr}H^2
-\frac9{n^2}(\operatorname{Tr}H)^2.
}
\tag{C003.2}
\]

Now let the current law be **any isotropic log-concave law**, with third-moment covariance-noise matrices

\[
(T_k)_{ij}=\mathbb E[X_iX_jX_k].
\]

The covariance SDE at `A=I` is

\[
dA=\sum_kT_kdW_k-I\,dt.
\]

The martingale quadratic variation of `ell` is

\[
\frac{d\langle\ell\rangle}{dt}
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_k(\operatorname{Tr}T_k)^2.
\]

Letwin's quadratic-form theorem with `M=I` implies

\[
\sum_k(\operatorname{Tr}T_k)^2
=\left|\mathbb E[(|X|^2-n)X]\right|^2
\le\operatorname{Var}(|X|^2)
\le8n.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\langle\ell\rangle}{dt}\le\frac8n.
}
\tag{C003.3}
\]

For the drift, C003.2 gives

\[
\mathcal L\ell(I)
=-1
+\frac4n\sum_k\|T_k\|_F^2
-\frac9{2n^2}\sum_k(\operatorname{Tr}T_k)^2.
\]

For each fixed `k`, duality and Letwin's theorem yield

\[
\|T_k\|_F^2\le8.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
-1-\frac{36}{n}
\le \mathcal L\ell(I)
\le31.
}
\tag{C003.4}
\]

So the cubic barrier has a genuinely dimension-free initial drift and even `O(1/n)` initial martingale quadratic variation for **every** isotropic log-concave starting law. This is qualitatively better than the softmax barrier X002.

It is only a one-state theorem; it does not improve the fixed metric.

## 6. R02-KLS-X003 — a uniform statewise drift theorem for the cubic barrier is also false

**Status: DISPROVED ROUTE, with a well-conditioned strongly-log-concave product family.**

The natural next guess would be to extend C003.4 uniformly over all stopped states with `||A||_op<=2`. That is false if the only assumptions retained are log-concavity/strong log-concavity and covariance control.

Let

\[
q_* = 1/\sqrt3,
\qquad
m_*=-h(q_*)=\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}.
\]

For an integer `r`, choose `R>1` by

\[
h(R)=r m_*.
\]

Take `r` roots equal to `R` and `F=r^2` roots equal to `q_*`. Then

\[
r h(R)+Fh(q_*)=0
\]

exactly. Set `z=1` and

\[
\lambda_R=\frac{R}{1+R},
\qquad
\lambda_*=\frac{q_*}{1+q_*}.
\]

Thus every covariance eigenvalue lies in the fixed interval

\[
\lambda_*\le\lambda_i<1.
\]

Use independent copies of the standardized split-Gaussian law from X002.3 and set `X_i=sqrt(lambda_i)Y_i`. The product law remains uniformly strongly log-concave with a dimension-free curvature, and its whitened third tensor is diagonal,

\[
\tau_{ijk}=\tau\,1_{\{i=j=k\}},
\qquad \tau\ne0.
\]

Let

\[
e(q)=(3q^2-1)q(1+q),
\]

\[
\Gamma(q)=6q[q(1+q)]^2
+(3q^2-1)q(1+q)(1+2q).
\]

Since `e(q_*)=0`, the cubic-root denominator is `E=r e(R)`. Exact second implicit differentiation in each diagonal covariance-noise direction gives the Hessian Itô contribution

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_r
={}&\frac{\tau^2\lambda_R}{2e(R)}
\left[\Gamma(R)\left(1-\frac1r\right)
+\Gamma(q_*)-e(R)\right]\\
&+\frac{\tau^2 r\lambda_*\Gamma(q_*)}{2e(R)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{X003.1}
\]

The finite-variation covariance drift contributes exactly

\[
D\ell[-A^2]=-\lambda_R.
\]

As `r->infinity`,

\[
R^3-R=r m_*,
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma(R)}{e(R)}\sim4R,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\frac{I_r}{R}\to 2\tau^2,
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal L\ell}{R}\to2\tau^2>0.
}
\tag{X003.2}
\]

The dimension is `N=r+r^2`, hence the drift diverges like a positive constant times `N^(1/6)`, even though the covariance condition number stays bounded and the law is uniformly strongly log-concave.

At the same states the exact martingale quadratic variation is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\langle\ell\rangle}{dt}
=\frac{\tau^2\lambda_R}{r}
=\Theta(N^{-1/2}),
}
\tag{X003.3}
\]

so this is deterministic curvature, not large noise.

The statement does **not** prove that these states are reached with appreciable probability by stochastic localization started from isotropy. It refutes a uniform stopped-state lemma based only on strong log-concavity plus a bounded covariance spectrum. Any successful cubic-barrier proof must exploit a dynamical/reachability property of the actual localization path, or add a compensator.

## 7. What this says about the actual `a=1/2` loss

The run narrows the obstruction substantially.

1. The published softmax barrier has a real `Theta(log n)` statewise drift on an explicit isotropic product law. R02-C001 cannot simply be inserted into equation (40) to make that drift `O(1)`.
2. A cubic Stieltjes root avoids this at the isotropic state universally (C003).
3. But the same cubic root does not admit a uniform dimension-free statewise drift bound even on well-conditioned uniformly strongly-log-concave products (X003).

Therefore the missing ingredient is not another unconditional covariance inequality. It is a **path-sensitive control**: show that high-curvature cubic-root states such as X003 are sufficiently inaccessible/rare before a constant time when the process starts isotropic, or construct a compensator whose drift cancels this curvature.

The metric remains `a=1/2`; no exponent improvement is claimed.
