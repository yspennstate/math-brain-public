## 2. Method #18 — topological-group translation and a new closure/open-subgroup reduction

The permutation topology on `Aut(G)` gives a cleaner formulation of the remaining algebraic obstruction.

Let

```text
L := closure(Gamma) <= Aut(G),
H := closure(A) <= L.
```

Because `Gamma` is transitive, `L` is transitive. Because `A` has finitely many vertex orbits, `H` is quasi-transitive. Also `Aut(G')` is closed in the permutation topology (failure to preserve an edge multiplicity is witnessed on a finite pair of vertices), so from `A <= Aut(G')` we obtain

```text
H <= Aut(G').
```

This yields the following additional source-relevant partial theorem.

### Proposition 2.1 (open closure case)

If `H=closure(A)` is open in `L=closure(Gamma)`, then

```text
mu(G) < mu(G').
```

### Proof

Fix a vertex `rho` and put `K=Stab_L(rho)`. In the permutation topology `K` is compact open. Since `H` has finitely many vertex orbits, there are finitely many `gamma_1,...,gamma_r in L` with

```text
L = union_{j=1}^r H gamma_j K.
```

Hence the left quotient `H\L` is compact: it is the union of finitely many compact images of the sets `gamma_j K`. If `H` is open, `H\L` is also discrete. A compact discrete space is finite, so `[L:H]<infinity`. The finite-index case of Grimmett--Li's strict augmentation theorem, applied to the transitive group `L` and subgroup `H <= Aut(G')`, gives `mu(G)<mu(G')`. QED.

This is slightly stronger than simply assuming `[Gamma:A]<infinity`: a nonclosed `A` can have infinite index while its closure is an open finite-index subgroup. I make no novelty claim; this is an elementary topological reduction.

Consequently any counterexample may be replaced by one with `A` closed, cocompact and **non-open** in a closed transitive subgroup of `Aut(G)`. If the closure is normal, the normal case of the source theorem applies as well. The hard algebraic regime is therefore a genuinely non-open, non-normal cocompact subgroup, not merely an arbitrary quasi-transitive subgroup.

## 3. Method #25 — count left and right phases twice

The previous report introduced

```text
iota_A(d) = [A : A intersect d A d^{-1}].
```

There is an exact opposite-side identity

```text
[A : A intersect d^{-1} A d]
 = [d A d^{-1} : A intersect d A d^{-1}].
```

Thus, when both sides are finite, the ratio of the two indices is the usual left/right commensuration (Hecke/modular) imbalance. This makes precise what a naive mass-transport argument would need to control.

I tried to use this identity to force either finite right phases or exponential phase entropy. It does **not** do so. The reason is geometric and decisive: these indices count group cosets, whereas the SAW surgery counts distinct finite witness placements. With infinite vertex stabilizers, infinitely many cosets may differ only outside the finite witness ball. In a regular tree, for example, the pointwise stabilizer of a finite ball is already infinite. Hence infinite group-phase index can live entirely in an invisible stabilizer tail and need not create even two distinct local surgeries.

So an unweighted double count of group elements would silently identify algebraic multiplicity with geometric multiplicity. That is false in precisely the residual infinite-stabilizer regime. Any future phase-counting proof must quotient by the stabilizer of the *actual finite witness* before assigning entropy.

## 4. Method #3 — stronger dichotomy attempted and the exact failure

The stronger statement I tried was:

> either one right phase contains a positive linear fraction of the witness occurrences along a full-growth family of SAWs, or fragmentation among infinitely many phases itself supplies enough entropy to force strict connective-constant growth.

The first branch is enough: the 2014 surgery proof then works without a globally finite phase set. The second branch, however, does not follow from infinite algebraic phase index. If a length-`n` SAW has occurrence counts `k_1,...,k_r` over right phases, the published surgery in one conjugate augmentation only exploits one `k_i`. Even if

```text
sum_i k_i >= a n,
```

linear fragmentation can have `max_i k_i = O(1)`. Then

```text
sum_i 2^{c k_i}
```

is only polynomial in `n`, not exponentially larger, and no connective-constant gap follows. Combining surgeries from different phases would place the resulting walk in different conjugate augmentations, not in the single graph `G'` whose connective constant is being compared.

This is the exact reason the proposed "infinite phase = entropy" route fails. It is not repaired by counting more group representatives because, as Section 3 explains, representative multiplicity may be hidden in stabilizers and need not correspond to new geometric walks.
