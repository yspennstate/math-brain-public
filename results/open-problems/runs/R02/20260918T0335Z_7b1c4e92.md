### Theorem B.1 — arbitrary quasi-transitive augmentation of a regular tree

Let `T_D` be the infinite `D`-regular tree, `D >= 2`. Let `A <= Aut(T_D)` act quasi-transitively on vertices. Let `G'` be a locally finite graph on the same vertex set, obtained from `T_D` by adding at least one edge (possibly parallel to a tree edge), and assume

`A <= Aut(G')`.

Then

`mu(G') > mu(T_D) = D-1`.

No normality of `A`, no finite index, no freeness, no finite stabilizers, no finite-coset property, and no irreducibility assumption on a quotient non-backtracking matrix are required.

Thus **Question 4.10 has an affirmative answer whenever the base graph is a regular tree**, including non-normal graph-of-groups actions with infinite vertex stabilizers.

The proof occupies the next sections.

## C. #11 DUALIZE — finite non-backtracking orbit operator

Put `b = D-1`. Since `A` is quasi-transitive and `T_D` is locally finite, `A` has finitely many orbits on oriented tree edges. Let `S` be this finite set of oriented-edge orbits.

For `s,t in S`, choose a representative oriented edge `(u,v)` of `s` and define

`M_{s,t}`

as the number of oriented edges `(v,w)` in orbit `t` with `w != u`. This is well defined: changing the representative by an element of `A` bijects all admissible continuations. Every row sum equals

`sum_t M_{s,t} = b`.

An `n`-step SAW on a tree is exactly a non-backtracking path, so products of entries of `M` count orbit-coded tree SAWs exactly.

The crucial observation is not irreducibility of `M` (which can fail badly for nonunimodular actions), but a property of **every closed strongly connected component**.

### Lemma C.1 — every closed component sees every unoriented edge orbit

Let `C` be a closed strongly connected component of the directed support graph of `M`. Then, for every `A`-orbit `O` of unoriented tree edges, at least one of the two orientations of `O` belongs to `C`.

#### Proof

Let `V_C` be the set of `A`-vertex-orbits that occur as the terminal vertex-orbit of some state in `C`.

First, if an oriented state `s` belongs to `C`, then both its initial and terminal vertex-orbits lie in `V_C`: the terminal orbit does by definition, while strong connectivity gives a predecessor `p in C` with `M_{p,s}>0`, and the terminal orbit of `p` is the initial orbit of `s`.

Now take a vertex-orbit `X in V_C` and an unoriented edge-orbit `O` incident to `X`. Orient `O` away from `X`, and call the oriented state `e`. Choose `s in C` terminating at `X`.

If `e` is not the reverse state of `s`, then `M_{s,e}>0`; closedness gives `e in C`.

If `e` is the reverse state of `s` but there are at least two actual outgoing edges in state `e`, then after excluding the one actual back-edge there remains an admissible continuation, again `M_{s,e}>0`, so `e in C`.

The only remaining case is that `e` is the reverse of `s` and has local multiplicity one. Then the opposite orientation `bar(e)=s` already lies in `C`. In all cases `O` has an orientation represented in `C`, and the vertex-orbit at the other end of `O` also belongs to `V_C` (directly if `e in C`; in the last case because the initial orbit of `s=bar(e)` lies in `V_C`).

Hence `V_C` is closed under adjacency in the finite connected quotient on vertex-orbits. Therefore `V_C` is the full vertex-orbit set, and the same argument covers every unoriented edge-orbit. QED.

This lemma is the structural replacement for the irreducibility assumption in the previous R02 tree argument.

### Lemma C.2 — a chosen oriented edge orbit occurs linearly in a full-growth family

Fix a closed component `C` and a state `q in C`. There exist `c>0`, `theta<b`, and a fixed finite prefix from the chosen root such that the number of length-`n` tree SAWs extending that prefix and containing fewer than `cn` occurrences of state `q` is `O(theta^n)`.

#### Proof

The restriction `M_C` is irreducible and has every row sum `b`, hence Perron root `b`. For `0<z<1`, form `M_C(z)` by multiplying every entry whose target state is `q` by `z`. Since `q` has an incoming positive entry in the irreducible component, `M_C(z) < M_C` in at least one positive entry, so strict Perron--Frobenius monotonicity gives

`rho(M_C(z)) < b`.

A path with at most `cn` visits to `q` has weight at least `z^{cn}` in the tilted count. Therefore its ordinary count is at most

`z^{-cn} O(rho(M_C(z))^n)`.

Choose `c>0` small enough that `z^{-c} rho(M_C(z)) < b`.

From any initial oriented edge state, the finite support digraph reaches at least one closed component. Fix one actual finite tree path from the root entering `C`. Once inside `C`, closedness keeps all orbit-state continuations in `C`, and each actual edge has exactly `b` non-backtracking continuations. Thus this fixed-prefix family itself has exponential growth `b`, and the estimate applies. QED.

## D. #15 BREAK THE SYMMETRY — use an actual `A`-cycle, not a `Gamma`-phase

Pick one added edge `f` of `G'`. Since `A <= Aut(G')` and preserves `T_D`, the whole orbit `Af` consists of added edges.

Let the endpoints of `f` be `x,y`, and let

`P = [x=v_0,v_1,...,v_ell=y]`

be the unique tree geodesic between them. If `f` is parallel to a tree edge then `ell=1`; otherwise `ell>=2`. The edge `f` together with `P` is a cycle `C_f` of length `ell+1` (a 2-cycle in the parallel case).

Choose one tree edge `e` of `P`. By Lemma C.1, a closed component `C` contains one orientation `q` of the unoriented `A`-orbit of `e`. Reverse the chosen orientation of `e` if needed so that `q` is its orbit.

By Lemma C.2 there is a full-growth family of tree SAWs with linearly many occurrences of oriented edges in orbit `q`. For every such actual occurrence `g`, choose `a_g in A` with `a_g e = g`. Then

`a_g C_f = a_g P union {a_g f}`

is an **actual cycle of `G'`**, not a `Gamma`-image living in a varying conjugate augmentation. This is exactly where the tree route escapes the finite-coset phase obstruction.

## E. #30 ONE SCALE / ONE DIRECTION — bounded cycle surgery

Consider one tree SAW `pi` from the full-growth family and one anchor occurrence `g=a_g e`.

Both `pi` and `a_g P` are simple paths in a tree. Therefore their intersection is a connected path. It contains the anchor edge `g`. Write this maximal intersection segment as `S_g`, with

`1 <= s_g := |E(S_g)| <= ell`.

In the cycle `a_g C_f`, the complementary arc between the endpoints of `S_g` has length

`ell+1-s_g`

and contains the added edge `a_g f`. Every internal tree vertex of that complementary arc lies in `a_g P \ S_g`, hence is not on `pi` by maximality of the intersection. Therefore replacing `S_g` by the complementary arc produces another SAW, now on `G'`.

This is the key tree fact: **no pattern-occurrence phase and no freeness of `A` is needed; connected intersection of tree geodesics makes the local surgery automatically clean.**

### E.1. Linear many disjoint surgeries

The cycle `a_g C_f` lies in a uniformly bounded ball around its anchor edge (radius at most `ell`). From `cn` anchor occurrences, greedily retain anchors separated by more than `4ell+4` steps along the tree geodesic `pi`. This leaves at least `c_1 n` anchors for some `c_1>0`, and the corresponding cycles are pairwise vertex-disjoint.

For each retained anchor, `s_g` belongs to the finite set `{1,...,ell}`. Hence for every `pi` at least `c_1 n/ell` retained anchors have the same value `s`. Partitioning the full-growth family by this chosen value and passing to an infinite subsequence of `n`, there is a fixed `s` for which the relevant class still has exponential growth `b^n`.

Put

`delta = ell+1-2s`.

Every replacement of one of these anchors changes the length by exactly `delta`.

### E.2. Injectivity

Take any subset `H` of the selected disjoint anchors and perform all corresponding replacements. The result is a SAW `psi` in `G'`.

The map `(pi,H) -> psi` is injective. Indeed, `pi` uses only tree edges, so every non-tree edge of `psi` is one of the inserted edges `a_g f`. For any such edge, its endpoints determine its unique tree geodesic `a_g P`. In `psi`, the maximal subpath of the cycle `a_g P union {a_g f}` containing the inserted edge is precisely the complementary arc used in the surgery; replacing it by the missing tree segment recovers `S_g`. The selected cycles are disjoint, so these inverse operations do not interfere. After expanding every inserted edge-cycle in this way one recovers the unique original tree SAW `pi`, and the set of inserted edges recovers `H`.

The same argument covers the parallel-edge case because edges are distinguished in the source multigraph convention.

### E.3. Entropy beats bounded length distortion

Let `k_n >= c_2 n` be the number of selected anchors of the fixed type `s`, and choose `j_n=floor(eta n)` of them, where `eta>0` is small and `eta<c_2`.

All outputs then have the same length

`m_n = n + j_n delta`.

By injectivity,

` sigma_{m_n}^{G'} >= |Good_n| * binom(k_n,j_n) `,

where `|Good_n|^{1/n} -> b` along the chosen subsequence. Using Stirling's formula and writing `H(p)=-p log p-(1-p)log(1-p)`,

` log mu(G') >= [log b + c_2 H(eta/c_2)] / [1+eta delta] `

(up to the immaterial floor errors).

If `delta <= 0`, the right side is strictly larger than `log b` for every sufficiently small positive `eta`.

If `delta > 0`, then

`c_2 H(eta/c_2) / eta -> +infinity` as `eta -> 0`,

so for sufficiently small `eta`,

`c_2 H(eta/c_2) > eta delta log b`.

Again the displayed lower bound is strictly larger than `log b`. When `b=1`, the positive entropy term directly gives `mu(G')>1`.

Hence in all cases

`mu(G') > b = D-1 = mu(T_D)`.

This proves Theorem B.1. QED.

## F. Why this is a genuine extension of the previous R02 tree result

The preceding report's tree argument assumed a **free cocompact** action and an irreducible finite quotient non-backtracking matrix. Theorem B.1 allows:

- arbitrary finite number of `A`-vertex orbits;
- nontrivial and infinite stabilizers;
- reducible non-backtracking orbit operators (including nonunimodular drift);
- arbitrary added-edge orbits, not just patterns whose quotient words occur recurrently in a chosen finite quotient;
- parallel edges or genuine chords.

The proof also identifies why the naive `"the whole chord path occurs with positive density"` route was unnecessarily strong. One only needs **one tree edge orbit on the chord geodesic** to recur. The maximal-intersection surgery then converts that single edge encounter into a valid use of the whole added chord.

A useful sanity model is the end-stabilizer action on `T_3`: its oriented-edge orbit chain is reducible, and a sibling-turn geodesic pattern can be transient in the dominant downward state. The theorem still forces strict augmentation because it anchors surgery at one recurrent constituent edge, rather than requiring the full sibling-turn word to recur. No finite enumeration is needed for this point.

## G. What still blocks the full source

For a general transitive graph `G`, an `n`-step SAW and a translated shortest path `aP` can meet in several disconnected pieces, and vertices of `aP` near the anchor in graph distance can be visited at widely separated times along the SAW. Replacing the maximal intersection is then not automatically a bounded-length surgery. This is exactly the geometric obstruction that the 2014 proof handles with its hierarchy of local cycle-visit events; its final finite-coset step then creates the separate algebraic phase obstruction.

The tree theorem removes the algebraic obstruction completely in the unique-geodesic category, leaving a sharper residual question:

> Can one prove that, in a full-growth family of SAWs on a transitive graph, a positive density of actual `A`-cycle anchors have **bounded temporal collision span**, or else use the repeated near-collisions themselves to obtain the required entropy gain?

This is now the most concrete route toward the full source. It uses the actual `A`-orbit of the added edge from the start and therefore avoids the left/right `Gamma`-phase problem if the collision step can be controlled.
