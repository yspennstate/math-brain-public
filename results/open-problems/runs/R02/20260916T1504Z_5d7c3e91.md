## 4. R02-KLS-C004 — exact product localization reaches covariance operator norm >2 by time `O(1/log n)` with probability tending to one

**Status: PROVED for one explicit isotropic product log-concave family. This is PARTIAL / a method obstruction, not a proof or refutation of KLS.**

### 4.1 Initial law and posterior realization

Let

\[
U=E-1,\qquad E\sim \mathrm{Exp}(1),
\]

so `U` is supported on `[-1,infinity)`, has mean zero, variance one, and log-concave density

\[
\rho(u)=e^{-(u+1)}\mathbf 1_{\{u\ge-1\}}.
\]

Let

\[
\mu_0=\nu^{\otimes n},\qquad \nu=\mathcal L(U).
\]

Then `mu_0` is isotropic and log-concave. It is an easy KLS family: one-dimensional Poincare tensorizes, so this example is not a KLS counterexample.

Use the standard filtering realization of the simplified stochastic localization. Sample

\[
X\sim\mu_0
\]

and an independent standard Brownian motion `B_t` in `R^n`, and observe

\[
Y_t=tX+B_t.
\]

Conditionally on `Y_t=y`, Bayes' formula gives

\[
d\mu_{t,y}(x)
\propto
\exp\left(\langle y,x\rangle-\frac t2|x|^2\right)d\mu_0(x).
\]

Under the innovation Brownian motion, this posterior process is the usual simplified Lee--Vempala/Eldan stochastic localization. Thus fixed-time covariance events can be computed from this posterior realization.

Because `mu_0` is a product law and the likelihood factorizes, `mu_{t,y}` remains a product law. Consequently its covariance matrix is diagonal:

\[
A_t=\operatorname{diag}\big(v_t(Y_{t,1}),\ldots,v_t(Y_{t,n})\big),
\]

where `v_t(y)` is the variance of the scalar posterior on `u>=-1` with density

\[
\rho_{t,y}(u)
=
Z_{t,y}^{-1}
\exp\left(-(1-y)u-\frac t2u^2\right)
\mathbf 1_{\{u\ge-1\}}.
\tag{C004.1}
\]

### 4.2 A fixed observation interval forces variance >2 for all sufficiently small `t`

Let

\[
K=\left[\frac13,\frac25\right].
\]

At `t=0`, for every `y<1`, the density (C004.1) is just a shifted exponential with rate `1-y`, hence

\[
v_0(y)=\frac1{(1-y)^2}.
\]

Therefore on `K`,

\[
\min_{y\in K}v_0(y)
=v_0(1/3)
=\frac94
>2.
\tag{C004.2}
\]

I now justify the small-`t` passage uniformly, rather than using pointwise convergence. For `y in K`, on `u>=0`,

\[
\exp\left(-(1-y)u-\frac t2u^2\right)
\le e^{-3u/5},
\]

while on `-1<=u<=0` the density factor and its first two polynomial moments are uniformly bounded. Hence the normalizing integral and the first two raw moment integrals converge uniformly on `K` as `t downarrow 0` by dominated convergence. The limiting normalizer is bounded away from zero on compact `K`, so the variance also converges uniformly:

\[
\sup_{y\in K}|v_t(y)-v_0(y)|\longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus there is an absolute `t_0>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
v_t(y)>2
\quad\text{for every }0<t<t_0,\ y\in[1/3,2/5].
}
\tag{C004.3}
\]

### 4.3 Extreme-value event across coordinates

Set

\[
t_n=\frac1{10\log n}.
\]

For coordinate `i`, define

\[
E_i
=
\left\{
X_i\in[-1,0],\quad
B_i(t_n)\in[1/3+t_n,\,2/5]
\right\}.
\]

If `t_n<1/15`, then on `E_i`, since `t_nX_i in[-t_n,0]`,

\[
Y_{t_n,i}=t_nX_i+B_i(t_n)
\in[1/3,2/5].
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`, also `t_n<t_0`; hence by (C004.3),

\[
E_i\implies (A_{t_n})_{ii}>2
\implies \|A_{t_n}\|_{op}>2.
\tag{C004.4}
\]

The events `E_i` are iid. Also

\[
\mathbb P(X_i\in[-1,0])=1-e^{-1}.
\]

For `t=t_n`, the Gaussian density is decreasing on the positive interval in the definition of `E_i`, hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\big(B_t\in[1/3+t,2/5]\big)
&\ge
(1/15-t)(2\pi t)^{-1/2}
\exp\left(-\frac{(2/5)^2}{2t}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Eventually `t_n<=1/30`, so the interval length is at least `1/30`. Moreover

\[
\frac{(2/5)^2}{2t_n}
=\frac45\log n.
\]

Therefore for an absolute `c>0`,

\[
p_n:=\mathbb P(E_i)
\ge
c\sqrt{\log n}\,n^{-4/5}.
\tag{C004.5}
\]

In particular

\[
np_n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

By independence,

\[
\mathbb P(\text{no }E_i)
=(1-p_n)^n
\le e^{-np_n}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Combining with (C004.4),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(\|A_{1/(10\log n)}\|_{op}>2\right)
\longrightarrow1.
}
\tag{C004.6}
\]

Since `A_0=I` and the localization covariance paths are continuous, for

\[
\tau_2
=
\inf\{t\ge0:\|A_t\|_{op}>2\},
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(\tau_2\le\frac1{10\log n}\right)
\longrightarrow1.
}
\tag{C004.7}
\]

This completes the proof.

## 5. R02-KLS-X004 — uniform operator-norm covariance survival beyond order `1/log n` is the wrong target

**Status: DISPROVED ROUTE, not a refutation of KLS.**

The current Letwin/Klartag--Lehec architecture controls KLS through a time interval on which a covariance barrier remains bounded. A natural attempt after Letwin makes the quadratic-form parameter dimension-free is to try to extend a universal event such as

\[
\sup_{0\le s\le t}\|A_s\|_{op}\le2
\]

to times `t` asymptotically larger than `1/log n`.

C004 rules out that strategy for the **standard** stochastic-localization covariance operator norm, uniformly over the original KLS class. Even the easy isotropic product shifted-exponential law satisfies

\[
\tau_2=O_{\mathbb P}(1/\log n),
\]

and specifically (C004.7).

Thus the residual logarithm is not merely a softmax-potential proof artefact. The actual covariance maximum develops an extreme-coordinate spike on the same order `1/log n`. The mechanism is transparent: there are `n` independent scalar posterior variances, and a Gaussian observation of size `O(1)` has probability `n^{-c}` at time `c'/log n`; the maximum sees one of them.

This does **not** contradict KLS. The starting measure is a product law with dimension-free spectral gap. It says instead that any proof which insists on keeping the raw covariance operator norm below a fixed absolute threshold for substantially longer than `1/log n` cannot be the full KLS proof.

It also does not contradict Guan's covariance-tail inequalities: those estimates have their own constants and threshold/exponent statements. C004 is a direct exact statement about the maximum-coordinate covariance event for one explicit localization flow.

The missing ingredient must therefore be one of the following genuinely different mechanisms:

- tolerate rare/high covariance directions rather than stop at their first appearance;
- average or weight the covariance spectrum rather than control its maximum;
- exploit a compensating transport/filtering structure after a coordinate becomes large;
- or abandon the standard covariance-survival transfer entirely.

This is a sharper obstruction than X002/X003: it is a **trajectory reachability theorem**, not just a bad static covariance state.
