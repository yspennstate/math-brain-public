## 3. Method #14 — the group action: a finite-stabilizer reduction

Let `rho` be a vertex and put

`K = Stab_Gamma(rho)`.

Because `Gamma` is transitive, `V` is naturally `Gamma/K`. The `A`-orbits on `V` are therefore the double cosets

`A \ Gamma / K`.

Thus `A` is quasi-transitive exactly when there are finitely many double cosets; equivalently, for some `gamma_1,...,gamma_r in Gamma`,

`Gamma = union_{i=1}^r A gamma_i K`.                                      (3.1)

### Proposition 3.1 — finite vertex stabilizers force finite index

Assume `|K| < infinity`. If `A <= Gamma` acts quasi-transitively on `G`, then

`[Gamma:A] < infinity`.

#### Proof

By (3.1),

`Gamma = union_i union_{k in K} A gamma_i k`.

Each `A gamma_i k` is a right coset of `A`. There are at most `r |K|` of them. Hence the number of right cosets of `A` in `Gamma` is finite, so `[Gamma:A] < infinity`. `□`

### Corollary 3.2 — Question 4.10 is affirmative for finite-stabilizer transitive actions

Under the hypotheses of Question 4.10, if the chosen transitive action `Gamma -> Aut(G)` has a finite vertex stabilizer, then

`mu(G) < mu(G')`.

#### Proof

Proposition 3.1 gives `[Gamma:A] < infinity`. Apply Grimmett--Li Theorem 4.7(b) (equivalently the finite-coset-property theorem of the 2014 paper). `□`

This includes all cases where the transitive action is discrete with finite point stabilizers; in particular, for a regular/Cayley action the stabilizer is trivial. It also shows that **any counterexample to Question 4.10 must live in the infinite-stabilizer regime** for every transitive action being used in the source formulation.

I do not claim this observation is new; it is an elementary consequence of the source theorem plus the double-coset description of quasi-transitivity. It is nevertheless a useful exact reduction that was not recorded in `D0074-A001`.

## 4. Method #20 — why the obvious source theorem stops

The 2014 Grimmett--Li paper defines the finite coset property by the existence of finitely many `nu_i in Gamma` such that

`V = union_i nu_i A rho`.

By contrast, plain `A`-quasi-transitivity gives finitely many **A-orbits**

`V = union_i A gamma_i rho`.

Normality lets one swap `A gamma_i` with `gamma_i A`; finite index supplies finitely many ordinary cosets directly. Without either condition, these are genuinely different requirements. Proposition 3.1 says a finite point stabilizer collapses the distinction because a finite double-coset decomposition contains only finitely many ordinary right cosets. Therefore the remaining source difficulty is sharply localized:

`A\Gamma/K finite,   |K| = infinity,   but no finite-coset property is known.`      (4.1)

A canonical model of (4.1) is a cocompact lattice acting on a regular tree while `Gamma` is the full tree automorphism group: `A` has finitely many vertex orbits but infinite index because `Gamma` has infinite compact vertex stabilizers.

That is where I used method #2: try to turn this model into a counterexample. Instead, the simplest tree version is still strictly monotone, by a direct finite-state pattern argument.

## 5. Method #2 — attack the negation on a residual tree-lattice model

The following theorem is deliberately stated in a clean subclass in which the quotient non-backtracking shift is finite and irreducible. It is independent of Proposition 3.1 because the ambient full tree automorphism group has infinite vertex stabilizers and a cocompact free lattice can have infinite index.

### Lemma 5.1 — positive density of a fixed word in an irreducible finite non-backtracking shift

Let `H` be a finite connected graph whose directed non-backtracking matrix is irreducible, and let `p` be a fixed admissible non-backtracking word of length `ell`. Suppose the underlying non-backtracking shift has growth rate `lambda` (for a finite `d`-regular quotient below, `lambda=d-1`). Then there exist constants `c>0` and `theta<lambda` such that the number of length-`n` non-backtracking words containing fewer than `c n` pairwise interior-disjoint occurrences of `p` is `O(theta^n)`.

#### Proof

Pass to the `(ell-1)`-block presentation of the finite non-backtracking shift. Let `M` be its irreducible adjacency matrix; its spectral radius is `lambda`. Fix `t in (0,1)` and multiply the transition that completes the word `p` by `t`, leaving all other admissible transitions unchanged. Call the resulting nonnegative matrix `M_t`. We have `0 <= M_t <= M` and `M_t != M`. Since `M` is irreducible, strict Perron--Frobenius monotonicity gives

`rho(M_t) < rho(M) = lambda`.                                           (5.1)

If `N_p(w)` is the number of (possibly overlapping) occurrences of `p` in a word `w`, the corresponding weighted path product is `t^{N_p(w)}`. Hence, for every `epsilon>0`, finite-dimensional matrix growth gives

`sum_{|w|=n} t^{N_p(w)} <= C_epsilon (rho(M_t)+epsilon)^n`.             (5.2)

For words with `N_p(w) <= alpha n`, because `0<t<1`,

`t^{N_p(w)} >= t^{alpha n}`.

Therefore their number is at most

`C_epsilon [ t^{-alpha}(rho(M_t)+epsilon) ]^n`.

By (5.1), choose `epsilon>0` and then `alpha>0` sufficiently small that the bracket is strictly below `lambda`. Finally, from `N_p(w)` occurrences one can greedily select at least `N_p(w)/ell` occurrences with disjoint interiors. Replacing `alpha` by `alpha/ell` proves the claim. `□`

### Theorem 5.2 — strict augmentation for a free cocompact regular-tree action

Let `T_d` be the `d`-regular tree, `d>=3`. Let `A <= Aut(T_d)` act freely on vertices and without edge inversions, with finite quotient `H=A\T_d`. Assume the directed non-backtracking matrix of `H` is irreducible (for the standard finite connected loopless `d`-regular quotient this is the usual Hashimoto irreducibility condition). Let `G'` be a locally finite graph on the same vertex set, containing `T_d` properly, with `A <= Aut(G')`. Then

`mu(G') > mu(T_d) = d-1`.                                               (5.3)

#### Proof

Choose one added edge `e={x,y}`. Let

`P=(x=v_0,v_1,...,v_ell=y)`

be the unique tree geodesic between its endpoints. If the augmentation is simple then `ell>=2`; if `e` is parallel to an old edge, the same argument below works with `ell=1` and is even simpler.

Project `P` to `H`; because the quotient map is locally a covering map under the stated free/no-inversion hypotheses, the projection is an admissible non-backtracking word `p` of length `ell`. Every occurrence of `p` in the projection of a tree SAW lifts to an `A`-translate of `P`: if an occurrence starts at `z` with the same quotient vertex as `x`, then `z=ax` for a unique `a in A`, and uniqueness of path lifting forces the lifted segment to be `aP`. The corresponding added edge `ae` belongs to `G'`.

An `n`-step SAW on the tree is exactly a non-backtracking tree path. From a fixed first directed edge there are exactly `(d-1)^{n-1}` such continuations. By Lemma 5.1, after decreasing a constant `c>0` if necessary, all but `O(theta^n)`, `theta<d-1`, of these paths contain at least

`k_n = floor(c n)`

pairwise interior-disjoint lift occurrences of `P`. Call them good paths and, for each good path, choose canonically the leftmost `k_n` such occurrences.

Fix

`j_n = floor(k_n/2)`.

For any good tree path `w` and any `j_n`-element subset `S` of its chosen occurrences, replace each selected `ell`-edge geodesic segment `aP` by the single added edge `ae`. The resulting walk in `G'` is self-avoiding: its vertex sequence is obtained from the original simple tree path by deleting the interiors of disjoint intervals and joining each pair of surviving endpoints by the added chord. Its length is

`m_n = n - j_n(ell-1)`.                                                 (5.4)

The map `(w,S) -> modified walk` is injective. Indeed, every added edge used by the modified walk is visible as an edge of `E(G')\E(T_d)` from the selected `A`-orbit; expanding it back to its unique tree geodesic reconstructs both `w` and the chosen subset `S`.

Consequently, up to an irrelevant fixed initial-edge factor,

`sigma_{m_n}^{G'} >= ((d-1)^n - O(theta^n)) * binom(k_n,j_n)`.           (5.5)

Stirling's formula gives

`(1/n) log binom(k_n,j_n) -> c log 2`,

while

`m_n/n -> 1 - c(ell-1)/2`.

Choose the `c` supplied by Lemma 5.1 smaller if necessary so the last limit is positive. Because `A` acts quasi-transitively on `G'`, the connective-constant limit exists. Taking the subsequence `m_n` in (5.5) yields

`log mu(G') >= [ log(d-1) + c log 2 ] / [ 1 - c(ell-1)/2 ]`.

The right-hand side is strictly larger than `log(d-1)`. Hence (5.3). `□`

### Scope / novelty warning

Theorem 5.2 is a restricted positive result, not a solution of Question 4.10. Its finite-state occurrence argument is a standard Perron--Frobenius/pattern mechanism, and the source's earlier finite-coset theorem may already subsume some free-lattice quotients. I did **not** find a source stating exactly Theorem 5.2 in the bounded search, but that is not a novelty certificate. The value here is the rigorous elimination of the most obvious infinite-stabilizer tree counterexample route.
