## 4. New derivation R02-KLS-C001 -- cubic resolvent tensor bound

**Status: PROVED at the stated scope. Literature novelty: UNVERIFIED.**

Let `nu` be any centered log-concave probability law on `R^n` with positive-definite covariance `A`. For an orthonormal eigenbasis of `A`, write

`T_k = E[ Z Z^T Z_k ]`, where `Z = X-E X`.

Fix `z > ||A||_op` and set

`R=(zI-A)^(-1)`, `Q=AR`.

Let `q_i` be the eigenvalues of `Q`, so `q_i=lambda_i/(z-lambda_i)>0`. Define the positive fixed-`z` covariance-Ito tensor term

`I_z = sum_k Tr(R T_k R T_k R)`.

Then

**R02-KLS-C001.**

`I_z <= 8 Tr(Q^3) = 8 sum_i q_i^3`.                                                       (C001)

### Proof

Whiten: `Y=A^(-1/2) Z`. Then `Y` is isotropic log-concave and its third central tensor

`tau_ijk = E[Y_i Y_j Y_k]`

is fully symmetric. In the eigenbasis of `A`,

`(T_k)_ij = sqrt(lambda_i lambda_j lambda_k) tau_ijk`.

Writing `r_i=(z-lambda_i)^(-1)`, direct expansion gives

`I_z = sum_{i,j,k} r_i^2 r_j lambda_i lambda_j lambda_k tau_ijk^2`

`    = sum_{i,j,k} q_i q_j q_k [(1+q_i)/(1+q_k)] tau_ijk^2`,                               (4.1)

because `1+q_i=z r_i`.

Since `tau_ijk^2` is invariant under all six permutations of `(i,j,k)`, symmetrize the coefficient. For positive `a,b,c`, put

`Omega(a,b,c) = (abc/6) sum_{u != v in {a,b,c}} (1+u)/(1+v)`.

We claim

`Omega(a,b,c) <= (a^3+b^3+c^3)/3`.                                                        (4.2)

Indeed,

`(1+a)/(1+b)+(1+b)/(1+a) = 2 + (a-b)^2/[(1+a)(1+b)]`.

Using `(1+a)(1+b)>=ab` in the three reciprocal pairs,

`Omega <= abc + [c(a-b)^2+a(b-c)^2+b(c-a)^2]/6 =: U`.

The exact polynomial identity

`6[(a^3+b^3+c^3)/3-U]`

`= [a^3+b^3+c^3+3abc-sum_sym a^2 b] + [a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc]`

is the sum of Schur's degree-three inequality and AM--GM, hence is nonnegative. This proves (4.2).

Therefore

`I_z <= (1/3) sum_{i,j,k} (q_i^3+q_j^3+q_k^3) tau_ijk^2`

`    = sum_i q_i^3 sum_{j,k} tau_ijk^2`.                                                   (4.3)

For each fixed `i`, let `M_i=(tau_jki)_{j,k}`. For any symmetric matrix `M`,

`<M,M_i>_F = E[(Y^T M Y - Tr M) Y_i]`.

Cauchy--Schwarz, isotropy `E Y_i^2=1`, and Letwin's Theorem 1.2 give

`|<M,M_i>_F|^2 <= Var(Y^T M Y) <= 8 ||M||_F^2`.

Taking the Frobenius dual supremum yields

`sum_{j,k} tau_ijk^2 = ||M_i||_F^2 <= 8`.

Substitution in (4.3) proves (C001). QED.

This uses the *full symmetry* of the physical third central tensor before applying any operator-norm or Ky-Fan relaxation.

## 6. Adversarial test R02-KLS-X001 -- root balance does not control the spectral edge

A tempting stronger step would be to infer `q_max=O(1)` from the cubic root balance and then control every resolvent term through `||Q||_op`. That is false.

For any integer `m>=2`, let

`N_m = 8(m^3-m)/3`,

which is an integer because `m^3-m` is divisible by 3. Take one root `q_0=m` and `N_m` roots equal to `1/2`. Since

`h(m)=m^3-m`, `h(1/2)=-3/8`,

the balance `sum h(q_i)=0` holds exactly, while `q_max=m -> infinity` and the total dimension is `n=1+N_m=Theta(m^3)`.

This is realized by an explicit centered log-concave product law. Set `z=1`, `lambda_i=q_i/(1+q_i)`, and take independent

`Z_i = sqrt(lambda_i) (E_i-1)`, `E_i ~ Exp(1)`.

Then `Cov(Z)=diag(lambda_i)`, and the whitened third tensor is diagonal with `tau_iii=2`. Consequently the physical tensor term is exactly

`I_z = 4 sum_i q_i^3`.

Here

`sum_i q_i^3 = (4m^3-m)/3 <= n`,

so `I_z/n -> 2`, even though `q_max~n^(1/3)` diverges.

Thus **a proof route that replaces the cubic tensor energy by a global resolvent-edge norm can manufacture a dimension loss that is absent in the physical tensor contraction.** This is the concrete failed covariance route recorded this run.
