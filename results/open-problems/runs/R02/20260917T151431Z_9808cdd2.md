## 3. #6 DEFORM TO A SOLVED POINT — measured deletion instead of uniform child deletion

Let `F` be a finite union-closed family on ground set `U`, let

\[
m=|\mathcal F|,
\]

and let `X` be uniform on `F`. For `S\subseteq U`, define the coordinate-deletion map

\[
\pi_S(A)=A\setminus S,
\]

its image support

\[
\mathcal G_S=\pi_S(\mathcal F),
\]

and the natural pushforward law

\[
\mu_S=\mathcal L(\pi_S X).
\]

For `B\in G_S`, let

\[
f_S(B)=|\{A\in\mathcal F:\pi_S(A)=B\}|
\]

be the fiber size.

### R02-I0121-C009 — measured deletion transfer theorem

**STATUS: PROVED on the page. Novelty: UNVERIFIED.**

The following hold for every `S`.

1. `G_S` is union-closed and `pi_S` is a join homomorphism:
   \[
   \pi_S(A\cup A')=\pi_S(A)\cup\pi_S(A').
   \]
2. Every surviving coordinate marginal is conserved exactly. For `z\notin S`,
   \[
   \Pr_{\mu_S}(z\in B)=\Pr(z\in X).
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
3. Conditional on `B=pi_S(X)`, the original set `X` is uniform on its fiber, hence
   \[
   H(\mu_S)
   =\log_2 m-\mathbb E_{B\sim\mu_S}\log_2 f_S(B).
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
4. If `u_S` denotes the uniform law on `G_S`, then
   \[
   D_2(\mu_S\|u_S)
   =\log_2|\mathcal G_S|-\log_2m
   +\mathbb E_{\mu_S}\log_2 f_S(B).
   \tag{3.3}
   \]
   Equivalently,
   \[
   \log_2\frac{m}{|\mathcal G_S|}
   =\mathbb E\log_2 f_S(B)-D_2(\mu_S\|u_S).
   \tag{3.4}
   \]
5. For each fixed outside trace `B`, the hidden fiber
   \[
   \mathcal F_B=\{A\cap S:A\in\mathcal F,\ \pi_S(A)=B\}
   \]
   is itself union-closed on `S`.

#### Proof

For (1), deletion distributes over union. Thus the image of a union-closed support is union-closed.

For (2), if `z` is not deleted, then

\[
1_{\{z\in\pi_S(X)\}}=1_{\{z\in X\}}
\]

pointwise.

For (3), because `X` is uniform on `m` members and every member in a fixed fiber has the same prior mass, the conditional law of `X` given `B` is uniform on a fiber of size `f_S(B)`. Therefore

\[
H(X\mid B)=\mathbb E\log_2 f_S(B).
\]

Since `B` is a deterministic function of `X`,

\[
\log_2m=H(X)=H(B)+H(X\mid B),
\]

which gives (3.2).

Equation (3.3) is the standard identity

\[
D_2(\mu_S\|u_S)=\log_2|G_S|-H(\mu_S),
\]

combined with (3.2), and (3.4) is a rearrangement.

Finally, if `Q_1,Q_2` are hidden traces in the same fiber over `B`, then the corresponding members are `B\cup Q_1` and `B\cup Q_2`. Their union is

\[
B\cup(Q_1\cup Q_2)\in\mathcal F,
\]

and has the same outside trace `B`. Thus `Q_1\cup Q_2` lies in the hidden fiber. QED.

### Why this matters

The previous recursion failed because uniformization changed the marginals. C009 says the natural measured child stays inside a closed category:

\[
\boxed{\text{union-closed support + probability measure + the same surviving marginal cap}.}
\]

What is lost is only uniformity, and that loss is exactly the fiber entropy in (3.2). This is the first valid transfer invariant R02 has found that survives arbitrary coordinate deletion.

## 4. #25 COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE — a block hidden-OR entropy inequality

The previous theorem identifies the right category. The next step is to carry the union operation through a deleted block without throwing away its hidden information.

Let `X^(1),X^(2)` be iid uniform on `F`. For a block `S`, write

\[
B_j=\pi_S(X^{(j)}),\qquad
Q_j=X^{(j)}\cap S,
\]

and

\[
W=B_1\cup B_2,\qquad O=Q_1\cup Q_2.
\]

Then

\[
(W,O)=X^{(1)}\cup X^{(2)}.
\]

For a coordinate with marginal `p`, define the usual binary-OR information function

\[
\Phi(p)=2h_2(p)-h_2(2p-p^2).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Also define

\[
e_S:=H(Q_1\mid B_1)
\]

and the **hidden OR entropy**

\[
\beta_S:=H(O\mid B_1,B_2).
\]

### R02-I0121-C010 — block deletion / hidden-OR transfer inequality

**STATUS: PROVED on the page. Novelty: UNVERIFIED.**

For every block `S`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{z\notin S}\Phi(p_z)
\ge
\log_2m-2e_S+\beta_S.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

By C009,

\[
H(B_1)=\log_2m-e_S.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Regroup the two iid copies coordinatewise on the surviving coordinates. The total correlation of the input blocks is

\[
2\sum_{z\notin S}h_2(p_z)-2H(B_1).
\]

Applying the deterministic OR map separately to every surviving coordinate cannot increase total correlation. Therefore

\[
\sum_{z\notin S}h_2(2p_z-p_z^2)-H(W)
\le
2\sum_{z\notin S}h_2(p_z)-2H(B_1),
\]

or equivalently

\[
\sum_{z\notin S}\Phi(p_z)
\ge 2H(B_1)-H(W).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Now `(W,O)=X^(1) union X^(2)` is supported on `F`, so

\[
H(W,O)\le\log_2m.
\]

Consequently

\[
H(W)
=H(W,O)-H(O\mid W)
\le \log_2m-H(O\mid W).
\]

Because `W` is a deterministic function of `(B_1,B_2)`, conditioning on `(B_1,B_2)` is more informative than conditioning on `W`, hence

\[
H(O\mid W)\ge H(O\mid B_1,B_2)=\beta_S.
\]

Thus

\[
H(W)\le\log_2m-\beta_S.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Combine (4.3)--(4.5) to obtain (4.2). QED.

### Interpretation

The term

\[
-2e_S+\beta_S
\]

is exactly the amount of hidden-block information that survives the two-copy OR operation. This is the quantity that the previous uniform-child recursion discarded. If one can find a sequence of blocks for which this term, together with the removed-coordinate `Phi` mass, yields a positive amount **per logarithmic scale**, the route can escape the known single-letter ceiling.

This theorem does not yet prove such accumulation.

## 5. Exact one-coordinate specialization and the critical free-factor dichotomy

Take `S={p}`. Let

- `rho=d_F(p)/m` be the frequency proportion of `p`;
- `k_p` be the number of deletion fibers containing **both** `B` and `B union {p}`;
- `alpha=k_p/m`.

A projection fiber has size one or two. Classify the bases into

- `L_p`: only `B` occurs;
- `U_p`: only `B union {p}` occurs;
- `C_p`: both occur.

Then

\[
|C_p|=k_p,
\quad
\frac{|L_p|}{m}=1-\rho-\alpha,
\quad
\frac{|U_p|}{m}=\rho-\alpha,
\quad
\Pr(B\in C_p)=2\alpha.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Given the projected base, the hidden bit is respectively `0`, `1`, or a fair Bernoulli bit. Hence

\[
e_{\{p\}}=2\alpha.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let

\[
h_*:=h_2(3/4),\qquad d_*:=1-h_*>0.
\]

The hidden OR bit is random only for an `(L,C)` or `(C,L)` pair, where it is fair, and for a `(C,C)` pair, where it is Bernoulli `3/4`. Therefore

\[
\beta_{\{p\}}
=4\alpha(1-\rho-\alpha)+4\alpha^2h_*
=4\alpha(1-\rho)-4d_*\alpha^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Substituting (5.2)--(5.3) into C010 gives:

### R02-I0121-C011 — exact one-coordinate deletion inequality

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{z\ne p}\Phi(p_z)
\ge
\log_2m-4\alpha\rho-4d_*\alpha^2.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is an exact theorem for every coordinate of every finite union-closed family.

### Critical coordinate

Suppose `p` is critical, `rho=1/2`. Then

\[
\sum_{z\ne p}\Phi(p_z)
\ge
\log_2m-2\alpha-4d_*\alpha^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The ordinary two-copy entropy inequality, if one simply removes the `p` contribution, pays

\[
\Phi(1/2)=2-h_*=1+d_*.
\]

The improvement supplied by the deletion structure is exactly

\[
\Gamma(\alpha)
=(1+d_*)-2\alpha-4d_*\alpha^2
=(1-2\alpha)(1+d_*+2d_*\alpha).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\Gamma(\alpha)>0\quad\text{for every }\alpha<1/2.}
\]

At the endpoint `alpha=1/2`, every projection fiber is double. Equivalently,

\[
\mathcal F=\mathcal G_{\{p\}}\times\{0,1\}
\]

in the `p` coordinate: `p` is a genuine free Boolean factor. So a critical coordinate gives an exact dichotomy:

\[
\boxed{
\text{either `p` is a free factor, or deleting `p` gives a strictly positive structural entropy gain.}
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

No novelty claim is made for the underlying entropy identities; the point is that they give the missing quantitative transfer term in the R02 route.

## 6. #25 again — collision fibers are directional hypercube edges

The same quantity `k_p` has a completely combinatorial interpretation.

A double fiber is exactly an internal edge of the induced hypercube graph on `F` in direction `p`. Hence

\[
k_p=\#\{\{A,A\triangle\{p\}\}:A,A\triangle\{p\}\in\mathcal F\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The number of members whose `p`-toggle leaves the family is

\[
b_p=m-2k_p.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For a critical coordinate,

\[
1-2\alpha=\frac{b_p}{m},
\]

so (5.6) becomes

\[
\Gamma(\alpha)
=\frac{b_p}{m}\,(1+d_*+2d_*\alpha).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the new entropy gain is, up to a bounded factor, exactly the **directional edge-boundary density** of `F` in the critical coordinate.

Counting the same object over coordinates gives

\sum_p k_p=e(Q_U[\mathcal F]),

the number of internal hypercube edges induced by the family. This points to hypercube edge-isoperimetry / stability as a possible way to amortize many deletion gains: near-zero transfer gain in many directions forces the family to be close to a Boolean product in many directions.

## 7. #30 ONE SCALE / LAYER AT A TIME — port Tian's height-four theorem into the double-fiber layer

Now specialize to the nearest unresolved source-relevant laboratory.

Assume `F` is an **empty-set-free union-closed family of height at most five**, with

\[
|\mathcal F|=2t,
\]

and no element occurring in more than `t` members. Let `p` be a critical element, so its frequency is exactly `t`.

Put

\[
k=|C_p|,
\qquad
s=t-k.
\]

Criticality and the fiber decomposition give

\[
|L_p|=|U_p|=s,
\qquad
|\mathcal G_{\{p\}}|=2t-k=t+s.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The common double-fiber family `C_p` has two crucial properties.

1. `C_p` is union-closed, because it is the intersection of the `p`-absent base family and the base family of `p`-present members.
2. `C_p` is empty-set-free. If the empty base were double, then `emptyset` itself would belong to `F`.
3. `C_p` has height at most four. Indeed, if
   \[
   B_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq B_h
   \]
   is a chain in `C_p`, then
   \[
   B_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq B_h\subsetneq B_h\cup\{p\}
   \]
   is a chain of length `h+1` in `F`.

Therefore Tian's proved height-four theorem applies to `C_p` whenever `k>0`.

### R02-I0121-C012 — height-four witness must be absorbed by the singleton defect

**STATUS: PROVED assuming Tian's published height-four theorem. PARTIAL for Frankl.**

There exists `z != p` whose incidence in `C_p`, say `c_z`, satisfies

\[
c_z>\frac{k}{2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Let `r_z` be the number of the `2s` singleton fibers in `L_p union U_p` whose base contains `z`. Since every double fiber containing `z` contributes two members of `F`,

\[
d_F(z)=2c_z+r_z\le t=k+s.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Hence

\[
r_z\le k+s-2c_z.
\]

Using the integer consequence of (7.2):

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
k\text{ even}:& r_z\le s-2,\\[2mm]
k\text{ odd}:& r_z\le s-1.
\end{array}}
\tag{7.4}
\]

#### Proof

If `k` is even, (7.2) gives `c_z >= k/2+1`, so `2c_z>=k+2`; substitute in (7.3). If `k` is odd, `c_z >= (k+1)/2`, so `2c_z>=k+1`. QED.

### Consequences

- `k=t` (a free factor) is impossible in an empty-set-free height-five counterexample: then `s=0`, but (7.4) is impossible. This also follows directly because the height-four witness in `C_p` would lift with doubled frequency to more than `t` members of `F`.
- Therefore every critical coordinate in such a counterexample has `alpha=k/(2t)<1/2`, so the entropy gain `Gamma(alpha)` in (5.6) is strictly positive.
- If `s=1` and `k` is even, (7.4) is already impossible. If `s=1` and `k` is odd, every height-four-abundant witness of `C_p` must avoid both singleton defect bases.

This is a concrete port of the solved height-four endpoint into the height-five deletion layer. It does **not** finish height five; it says where the remaining defect must hide.

## 8. Recovery of Tian's matching-defect identity in the same notation

For completeness, the uniform projection child has

\[
|G_{\{p\}}|=2t-k.
\]

For `z!=p`, write

\[
d_F(z)=t-\delta_z,\qquad \delta_z\ge0,
\]

and let `c_z` again be the number of double fibers containing `z`. Uniformizing the projection counts every double fiber only once, so

\[
d_{G_{\{p\}}}(z)=d_F(z)-c_z=t-\delta_z-c_z.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
d_{G_{\{p\}}}(z)-\frac{|G_{\{p\}}|}{2}
=\frac{k}{2}-\delta_z-c_z.

\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus a coordinate becomes strictly abundant after **uniformizing** the deleted child exactly when

\[
\delta_z+c_z<\frac{k}{2}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

This is the matching-defect phenomenon that the preceding stuck run was trying to port. The measured child avoids the artificial frequency inflation entirely: under its natural pushforward law the same surviving coordinate still has probability

\[
\frac{t-\delta_z}{2t}\le\frac12.

So the two views fit together:

- Tian's uniform child encodes the obstruction in the defect `k/2-delta_z-c_z`;
- the R02 measured child preserves marginals and encodes the same obstruction as fiber entropy / nonuniformity.

This is the useful bridge produced by the run.
