## 4. Method #22 first: what the old failure teaches

The source's own nonergodic example fails exactly at **equivalence**. This suggests replacing a stationary measure supported on exceptional tree events by a **full-measure measurable partition** of GW into invariant classes, each of whose conditional GW laws is stationary. If the classes cover GW, their stationary mixture can be GW itself.

That observation leads to a general lemma.

### Conservative-interpretation lemma

Let GW be supported on locally finite trees with no leaves. Suppose there is an isomorphism-invariant Borel color

`c(T) in {0,1}`

such that:

1. `0 < GW(c=1) < 1`;
2. for GW-a.e. tree, the parent color is the color of at least one child;
3. the color of the parent is a deterministic symmetric function of the child colors.

Define a local transition rule by choosing uniformly among children having the same color as the parent. (Off the GW-full hereditary good set, use any Borel equivariant infinite-child fallback; this does not change the GW statements.) Products of these local transition probabilities define a consistent equivariant flow rule.

Write `GW_i = GW(. | c=i)`. Conditional on the root degree and the vector of child colors, the child subtrees are independent and a child of color `i` has law exactly `GW_i`. Uniform selection among the same-color children therefore returns law `GW_i`, regardless of the degree/color vector. Averaging gives

`GW_i K = GW_i`,  i=0,1.

Hence

`GW = pi_0 GW_0 + pi_1 GW_1`

is stationary for the associated Markov kernel. The transition preserves `c`, so `{c=1}` is a nontrivial invariant event. Thus the stationary chain with initial law GW is nonergodic. Since its stationary law is **GW itself**, equivalence is automatic.

So Question 17.18 reduces to constructing one nontrivial endogenous conservative color.

## 5. Method #2: attack the universal claim with an explicit color

Take a Galton–Watson law with

`P(D=2)=3/5`,   `P(D=3)=2/5`.

There are no leaves. At a vertex with two children use the Boolean rule AND; at a vertex with three children use OR. Both rules are conservative: an AND output is the color of some child, and an OR output is the color of some child.

It remains to prove that this recursion produces a nontrivial color that is a measurable function of the **uncolored tree**, rather than requiring extra randomness at infinity.

For a depth-`n` truncation, give level-`n` vertices independent Bernoulli(1/2) temporary colors and propagate them upward by the AND/OR rules. Let `q_n(T)` be the conditional probability that the root output is 1 given the uncolored truncation through depth `n` (degrees below depth `n` exposed, degrees at level `n` not yet exposed). Then `q_0=1/2`.

The one-step mean map is

`F(p) = (3/5)p^2 + (2/5)[1-(1-p)^3]`,

and

`F(1/2)=1/2`.

Because the root formula is read-once/multilinear in the boundary probabilities and the newly exposed offspring variables at distinct boundary vertices are independent, `(q_n)` is a bounded martingale in the tree filtration. Therefore

`q_n -> q_infinity`

almost surely and in `L^2`, with `E q_infinity=1/2`.

### Exact second moment

Let

`r_n = E[q_n^2]`.

Equivalently, use two independent temporary boundary colorings of the same uncolored truncation. For each child pair, both marginals are 1/2 and `P(11)=r_n`; therefore `P(00)=r_n` as well.

- For a 2-child AND, both root outputs are 1 with probability `r_n^2`.
- For a 3-child OR, inclusion–exclusion gives probability
  `1-1/8-1/8+r_n^3 = 3/4+r_n^3`.

Hence

`r_0=1/4`,

`r_{n+1}=3/10 + (3/5)r_n^2 + (2/5)r_n^3 =: R(r_n)`.

The increment factors exactly as

`R(r)-r = (r-1/2)[(2/5)r^2+(4/5)r-3/5]`.

For `1/4 <= r < 1/2`, both factors are negative, so the increment is positive. Also `R` is increasing, `R(1/4)=11/32`, and `R(1/2)=1/2`, so `[1/4,1/2]` is invariant. The only fixed point in that interval is `1/2`; the other positive root of the quadratic factor is `(sqrt(10)-2)/2 > 1/2`. Therefore

`r_n ↑ 1/2`.

By `L^2` convergence,

`E[q_infinity(1-q_infinity)] = 1/2 - E[q_infinity^2] = 0`.

Thus

`q_infinity in {0,1}` almost surely.

Set `c(T)=q_infinity(T)` on the full-measure set. The finite-depth recursions are

- `q_{n+1}(T)=q_n(T_1)q_n(T_2)` when `D=2`;
- `q_{n+1}(T)=1-prod_{i=1}^3(1-q_n(T_i))` when `D=3`.

Passing to the limit proves that `c` obeys the AND/OR recursion. Since `E c=1/2`, both color classes have GW probability `1/2`.

This verifies all hypotheses of the conservative-interpretation lemma.

## 6. Method #14: the group action / equivariance obligation

The source requires equivariant flow rules, so this cannot be treated as cosmetic bookkeeping.

Every `q_n` depends only on the rooted isomorphism type of the depth-`n` tree: AND and OR are symmetric in the children and boundary Bernoulli(1/2) colors are exchangeable. Hence the pointwise limit color `c` is rooted-isomorphism invariant wherever it is defined. Uniform choice among children with the same invariant color is equivariant under every rooted automorphism/permutation of the children.

Define the local transition at every subtree by the same rule; on exceptional configurations with no eligible same-color infinite child, choose uniformly among infinite children. The resulting product measure on rays is Borel, equivariant, and automatically consistent under conditioning on any positive-mass child. On the GW support here every child subtree is infinite and the exceptional branch is used with probability zero.

Thus the constructed object is a flow rule in the literal source sense, including the allowed zero weights.

## 7. Stationarity and nonergodicity — source conclusion

Let `GW_i=GW(.|c=i)`. Given the root offspring number and the child-color vector, the child subtrees are independent and child `j` has conditional law `GW_{c_j}`. If the parent has color `i`, the conservative AND/OR rule ensures at least one child has color `i`; the flow selects uniformly among those children. Conditional on every such degree/color vector, the selected subtree has law `GW_i`. Therefore

`GW_i K = GW_i`,  i=0,1.

Since `GW(c=i)=1/2`,

`GW K = (1/2)GW_0K+(1/2)GW_1K = GW`.

The stationary measure is exactly GW, hence certainly equivalent to GW. But the chain never changes color, so

`A={T:c(T)=1}`

is invariant and `GW(A)=1/2`. The stationary Markov chain is not ergodic.

Therefore the source universal implication is false:

**There exists a flow rule with a stationary measure equivalent to GW whose associated Markov chain is nonergodic.**

So the mathematical answer to Lyons–Peres Question 17.18 is **NO**.
