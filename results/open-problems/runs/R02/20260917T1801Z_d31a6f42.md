## 2. Exact source target

## 5. B10 — DERANDOMIZE: reduce the positive direction to tail-preserving finite quantization

The first new route is to start from the **already solved real-valued side** rather than constructing rho-mixing from scratch.

For sigma-fields \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\), write

\[
\rho(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
=\sup\{|\operatorname{Corr}(F,G)|:
F\in L^2(\mathcal A),\ G\in L^2(\mathcal B),
\operatorname{Var}F,\operatorname{Var}G>0\}.
\]

For a stationary process \(Y\), let

\[
\rho_Y(n)=\rho\bigl(\sigma(Y_k:k\le0),\sigma(Y_k:k\ge n)\bigr).
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — coordinate factors cannot increase rho

Let \(Y=(Y_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z}\) be strictly stationary and let \(f\) be measurable. Put

\[
X_k=f(Y_k).
\]

Then for every \(n\),

\[
\boxed{\rho_X(n)\le \rho_Y(n).}
\]

More generally, if

\[
X_k=f(Y_{k-r},\ldots,Y_{k+r}),
\]

then for \(n>2r\),

\[
\boxed{\rho_X(n)\le \rho_Y(n-2r).}
\]

**Proof.** For the coordinate factor,

\[
\sigma(X_k:k\le0)\subseteq\sigma(Y_k:k\le0),\qquad
\sigma(X_k:k\ge n)\subseteq\sigma(Y_k:k\ge n).
\]

Maximal correlation is monotone under restriction of either sigma-field, giving the first inequality. For an \(r\)-block factor,

\[
\sigma(X_k:k\le0)\subseteq\sigma(Y_k:k\le r),\qquad
\sigma(X_k:k\ge n)\subseteq\sigma(Y_k:k\ge n-r),
\]

and stationarity gives the second inequality. ∎

### Consequence

Take Bradley's 1986 real-valued rho-mixing bilaterally deterministic process \(Y\). **Every finite coordinatewise quantization**

\[
X_k=f(Y_k),\qquad |f(\mathbb R)|<\infty,
\]

is automatically strictly stationary and rho-mixing, and is nondegenerate whenever \(f(Y_0)\) is nonconstant.

So the positive source problem can be sharpened to a single missing property:

\[
\boxed{
\text{find a finite quantizer }f\text{ for the known real-valued witness such that }f(Y)
\text{ remains bilaterally deterministic.}
}
\]

This is a better target than rebuilding all four requirements simultaneously.

There is an important caveat: bilateral determinism **does not pass automatically to factors**. Although \(Y_0\) is measurable from the remote two-sided \(Y\)-tail, the remote \(f(Y)\)-tail can discard exactly the information used by that decoder. Thus Lemma 5.1 is a genuine reduction, not a solution.

I make no novelty claim for monotonicity of maximal correlation under factors; it is elementary. The new-method value here is the route selection: preserve rho for free and spend all construction effort on a tail-preserving finite quantizer.

## 6. B23 — DO WHAT THE PROPOSER DID NOT EXPECT: one-cut rho=0 is compatible with exact joint reconstruction

The natural intuition behind D0031 is that exact reconstruction from the two remote sides should force correlation across the cut. At a **single scale**, that intuition is false in the strongest possible way.

The D0038 neighboring campaign had already identified finite parity/secret-sharing as the reason one-scale Shannon arguments are too weak. I do **not** claim that underlying observation as new. Here I recast it in the exact HGR/maximal-correlation language relevant to D0031.

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group and let \(S,U\) be independent and uniform on \(G\). Define

\[
L=U,\qquad R=U+S.
\]

Then for every \(a,b\in G\),

\[
\Pr(L=a,R=b)=|G|^{-2},
\]

so \(L\) and \(R\) are independent and therefore

\[
\boxed{\rho(\sigma(L),\sigma(R))=0.}
\]

Nevertheless

\[
\boxed{S=R-L}
\]

is reconstructed exactly from the two shares jointly.

Thus exact two-sided recovery of a nontrivial finite secret is compatible with **zero** maximal correlation between the individual sides.

There is also a standard product/tensorization observation. If a share pair \((L,R)\) is independent of a base pair \((A,B)\), then

\[
\rho\bigl(\sigma(A,L),\sigma(B,R)\bigr)
=
\max\{\rho(\sigma(A),\sigma(B)),\rho(\sigma(L),\sigma(R))\}.
\]

For the one-time-pad shares above this equals the base rho.

This does **not** yet encode an existing central symbol of the base process: making the secret a function of the base destroys the independence hypothesis used in the product identity. Nor does repeating the gadget independently at infinitely many scales solve stationarity or finite-alphabet consistency; cross-scale relations immediately appear.

**B23 outcome.** A NO proof cannot come from any one-cut information inequality saying “joint reconstruction forces positive HGR correlation.” It must use the simultaneous consistency of the reconstruction constraints over **all scales and all shifts**. Conversely, a YES construction should try to distribute reconstruction information by balanced shares whose one-sided marginals reveal essentially no common mode.

No novelty is claimed for one-time-pad secrecy or HGR tensorization.

## 7. B26 — HUNT THE SPORADIC CASE: exclude two large construction classes completely

The third attack asks whether a witness could hide in the simplest finite-state classes. Two broad-looking classes fail for exact structural reasons.

### 7.1 No nondegenerate finite-radius factor of an absolutely regular parent

Recall that absolute regularity (beta-mixing) is stronger than rho-mixing in a different direction and, crucially, Bradley records that beta-mixing implies triviality of the double-tail sigma-field.

Let \(Y\) be a stationary beta-mixing process and let

\[
X_k=f(Y_{k-r},\ldots,Y_{k+r})
\]

be a finite-radius factor. Exactly as in Lemma 5.1, monotonicity of beta under sub-sigma-fields gives

\[
\beta_X(n)\le \beta_Y(n-2r)\to0.
\]

Hence the double tail \(\mathcal T_d(X)\) is trivial. If \(X\) were bilaterally deterministic, then

\[
\sigma(X)=\mathcal T_d(X)
\]

(up to null sets), so every coordinate would be almost surely constant. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No nondegenerate finite-radius factor of a beta-mixing process can solve D0031.}
}
\]

In particular this excludes ordinary finite-state irreducible aperiodic Markov chains and finite-radius observations of such chains, including the standard primitive finite-state HMM route.

This is not a source solution: rho-mixing does **not** imply beta-mixing, and Bradley's target is designed precisely in the gap between such dependence notions.

### 7.2 No one-dimensional finite-field linear/Haar witness

Let \(H\le \mathbb F_q^{\mathbb Z}\) be a closed shift-invariant **\(\mathbb F_q\)-linear** subspace, and let \(X\) have Haar law on \(H\).

I claim that no nondegenerate such \(X\) can be both rho-mixing and bilaterally deterministic.

#### Case 1: \(H=\mathbb F_q^{\mathbb Z}\)

Haar measure is the iid product measure. Its double tail is trivial, so a nondegenerate iid process is not bilaterally deterministic.

#### Case 2: \(H\) is proper

Because \(H\) is a proper closed linear subspace of the compact product space, its continuous annihilator is nonzero. Continuous linear functionals on \(\mathbb F_q^{\mathbb Z}\) have finite support. Thus there are integers \(a<b\) and coefficients

\[
c_a,\ldots,c_b\in\mathbb F_q,
\qquad c_a c_b\ne0,
\]

such that every \(x\in H\) satisfies, for every \(n\in\mathbb Z\),

\[
\sum_{i=a}^b c_i x_{n+i}=0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Because both endpoint coefficients are nonzero, (7.1) determines \(x_{n+b}\) from the preceding \(b-a\) coordinates and also determines \(x_{n+a}\) from the following \(b-a\) coordinates. Hence an element of \(H\) is determined by any block of \(b-a\) consecutive symbols. Therefore

\[
|H|\le q^{b-a}<\infty.
\]

The shift is a permutation of this finite set, so for some \(L\ge1\),

\[
T^L=I\quad\text{on }H.
\]

If the Haar process is nondegenerate, \(X_0\) is nonconstant and

\[
X_{mL}=X_0\qquad\text{a.s. for all }m\in\mathbb Z.
\]

For any requested separation \(n\), choose \(mL\ge n\). Then the past sigma-field contains the nonconstant variable \(X_0\), while the future sigma-field contains the identical variable \(X_{mL}\). Consequently

\[
\rho_X(n)=1.
\]

Thus \(X\) is not rho-mixing.

Combining the two cases,

\[
\boxed{
\text{No nondegenerate Haar process on a closed one-dimensional finite-field linear subshift solves D0031.}
}
\]

This rules out the most tempting stationary linear parity-code realization of the secret-sharing idea. I make no novelty claim for the underlying algebraic-dynamics facts; the point is that they give an exact no-go for this campaign route.

## 8. What changed conceptually

The fixed witness metric did not move, but the admissible construction space is now much narrower and the positive attack has a more precise interface.

A successful YES witness cannot be any of the following simple objects:

- a finite-radius factor of an absolutely regular parent;
- an ordinary finite-state primitive Markov/HMM process;
- a Haar process on a one-dimensional closed finite-field linear subshift;
- a single-scale secret-sharing gadget pasted independently at each cut.

At the same time, there is no one-scale HGR obstruction to exact two-sided decoding.

The most plausible positive architecture is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{nonlinear + infinite-memory + finite-alphabet + multiscale, with balanced reconstruction shares.}
}
\]

There are two especially concrete ways to enter it.

1. **Quantize Bradley 1986 without destroying the double-tail decoder.** Rho is automatically preserved by Lemma 5.1; only tail-preserving finite quantization has to be engineered.
2. **Deform a Bernoulli generator.** Ornstein–Weiss show that bilaterally deterministic finite generators occur abundantly inside Bernoulli systems, while Burton–Denker–Smorodinsky give a finite-state strongly mixing bilaterally deterministic process. The missing quantitative requirement is to make the coding stages HGR-safe, rather than merely rare in probability. Small partition distance is not enough, because maximal correlation can be carried by rare normalized events.

A realistic next target is an iterative coding lemma in which a finite-state generator is modified to make a larger central block recoverable from farther two-sided tails while controlling an operator-norm cost

\[
\rho_{j+1}(n_j)\le \rho_j(n_j)+\varepsilon_j,
\qquad \sum_j\varepsilon_j<\infty.
\]

If such a stage can be implemented by balanced/permutation or one-time-pad-like recoding, its limit could be both bilaterally deterministic and rho-mixing. If every such reconstruction stage necessarily has unit HGR cost, that would instead point toward a NO theorem.
