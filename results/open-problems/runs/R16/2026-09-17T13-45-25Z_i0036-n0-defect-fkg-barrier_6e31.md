## 3. Method #6 — deform to the solvable point `n=0`: the exact defect-sector theorem

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite graph of maximum degree at most three. Let `a,b` be two degree-one terminals. For an edge set `omega subset E`, let `partial omega` denote the set of vertices of odd degree in the occupied subgraph. Let `ell(omega)` be the number of cycle components. Define

\[
Z_G^{a,b}(n,x)
:=
\sum_{\partial\omega=\{a,b\}}
 n^{\ell(\omega)}x^{|\omega|},
\]

and

\[
Z_G^{\varnothing}(n,x)
:=
\sum_{\partial\omega=\varnothing}
 n^{\ell(\omega)}x^{|\omega|}.
\]

### Theorem R16-I0036-C07(a) — exact `n=0` defect-sector reduction

Every `omega` with `partial omega={a,b}` decomposes uniquely into

1. one simple self-avoiding `a-b` path `gamma`, and
2. a collection of vertex-disjoint cycle components disjoint from `gamma`.

Every `omega` with empty defect set is a disjoint collection of cycles. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
Z_G^{a,b}(0,x)
=
S_G^{a,b}(x)
:=
\sum_{\gamma:a\to b\ \mathrm{SAW}}x^{|\gamma|},
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{Z_G^{\varnothing}(0,x)=1.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

More generally, for every integer `j>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{j!}\,\partial_n^jZ_G^{a,b}(0,x)
=
\sum_{\substack{\partial\omega=\{a,b\}\\\ell(\omega)=j}}
 x^{|\omega|},
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

with the analogous formula in the empty sector.

### Proof

All nonterminal occupied degrees are even and at most three, hence are `0` or `2`. Since `a,b` have ambient degree one and are the only odd vertices, their occupied degree is exactly one. Starting from `a`, follow the unique occupied edge. At every nonterminal vertex there is exactly one continuation unless the path terminates. The component cannot close into a cycle before reaching `b`: a tail attached to a cycle would create a degree-three vertex, which would be odd. Hence it reaches `b`, and the component is a simple `a-b` path. Every remaining occupied component has every vertex of degree two and is therefore a cycle. This decomposition is unique by connected components. The empty-defect statement is the same degree argument without the open component. Equations (3.1)--(3.3) follow by reading off the coefficient of `n^j`. `QED`

Thus the finite unmarked loop measure at `n=0` is trivial, while the two-defect sector is exactly the weighted SAW generating function. This already says that a proof which simply takes `n downarrow 0` in an unmarked loop crossing probability is deforming the wrong object.

## 4. The first derivative is a positive path-loop interaction observable

Write

\[
A(n):=Z_G^{a,b}(n,x),\qquad
B(n):=Z_G^{\varnothing}(n,x),\qquad
R(n):=\frac{A(n)}{B(n)}.
\]

Let

\[
A(n)=A_0+nA_1+O(n^2),\qquad
B(n)=1+nB_1+O(n^2).
\]

By (3.1), `A_0=S_G^{a,b}(x)`. A one-loop two-defect configuration is uniquely a pair `(gamma,C)` consisting of a SAW `gamma` and a simple cycle `C` vertex-disjoint from `gamma`. Therefore

\[
A_1
=
\sum_{\gamma}x^{|\gamma|}
\sum_{C:C\cap\gamma=\varnothing}x^{|C|},
\]

whereas

\[
B_1=\sum_Cx^{|C|}.
\]

Since

\[
R'(0)=A_1-A_0B_1,
\]

we obtain the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
-R'(0)
=
\sum_{\gamma:a\to b\ \mathrm{SAW}}
 x^{|\gamma|}
\sum_{C:C\cap\gamma\ne\varnothing}x^{|C|}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{R'(0)\le0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Dividing by `R(0)=S_G^{a,b}(x)>0` gives

\[
\boxed{
-\partial_n\log R(n)\big|_{n=0}
=
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{SAW}_{a,b,x}}
\left[
\sum_{C:C\cap\gamma\ne\varnothing}x^{|C|}
\right].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The right side is positive, phase-free, and geometric. It measures how many closed-loop polymers are excluded by the open SAW. This is exactly the kind of positive invariant-sector information that the preceding 48-phase Fourier no-go theorem showed cannot come from nontrivial winding characters alone.

The identity is elementary polymer-gas differentiation and is very plausibly folklore in some form. A bounded prior-art search found rigorous small-`n` loop `O(n)` work (for example Lorenzo Taggi, arXiv:1806.09360) and the standard `O(n)->0` SAW correspondence, but I did not locate this exact finite-domain derivative identity used as a route to the fixed-lattice SAW scaling problem. **Publication novelty is UNVERIFIED; no novelty claim is made.**

## 5. Method #29 — compactify correctly: on a cylinder the derivative controls SAW length

The previous run proposed putting the strip on a cylinder to restore exact cyclic symmetry. The `n=0` analysis shows a necessary correction: keep two degree-one defects. If one compactifies only the unmarked closed-loop model and then sets `n=0`, the finite-volume measure becomes empty and loses the SAW entirely. The correct compact object is the defect ratio `R(n)`.

Consider a finite honeycomb cylinder/torus piece in which every ordinary honeycomb edge lies on two hexagonal faces, with two pendant terminal edges creating degree-one terminals `a,b`. Let `gamma` be an `a-b` SAW of length `L`. Apart from the two pendant edges, there are `L-2` honeycomb edges. Counting incidences `(path edge, adjacent hexagonal face)` gives `2(L-2)` incidences. Any hexagonal face contains at most six path edges. Hence at least

\[
\frac{L-2}{3}
\]

distinct elementary hexagons intersect `gamma`. Each elementary hexagon is a length-six cycle and contributes `x^6` to the inner sum in (4.3). Therefore

\[
\sum_{C:C\cap\gamma\ne\varnothing}x^{|C|}
\ge
\frac{x^6}{3}(L-2).
\]

Combining with (4.3) gives the explicit positive-sector estimate

\[
\boxed{
-\partial_n\log R(n)\big|_{n=0}
\ge
\frac{x^6}{3}
\left(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{SAW}_{a,b,x}}|\gamma|-2\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{SAW}_{a,b,x}}|\gamma|
\le
2+\frac{3}{x^6}
\left[-\partial_n\log R(n)\big|_{n=0}\right].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is a concrete trivial-sector coupling: a bound on one `n`-derivative of the defect ratio yields a bound on a genuine SAW geometric moment. It is not yet an annulus-crossing estimate and therefore does not solve I0036, but it is substantially more targeted than adding more Fourier modes.

The same cylinder also retains a winding class for the open path. Thus a plausible compactified state space is now: **open-path winding sector + positive interaction derivative**, rather than the unmarked loop measure + nontrivial parafermionic modes.

## 7. A generic exact barrier to uniform quasi-FKG continuation

The source's necessity remark supplies, for parameters outside the FKG region and in particular for fixed `0<n<1`, a finite-domain pair of configurations for which the lattice-condition ratio is strictly below one. Abstract that as follows.

Let `mu` be a strictly positive probability mass function on a finite distributive configuration lattice and define

\[
Q_\mu(\sigma,\tau)
:=
\frac{\mu(\sigma\vee\tau)\mu(\sigma\wedge\tau)}
{\mu(\sigma)\mu(\tau)}.
\]

### Theorem R16-I0036-C07(b) — tensorization of an FKG defect

If some finite witness has `Q_mu(sigma,tau)=r<1`, then for the `m`-fold product measure and repeated configurations,

\[
Q_{\mu^{\otimes m}}
(\sigma^{\otimes m},\tau^{\otimes m})
=r^m.
\]

Hence no positive constant `c` independent of `m` can satisfy a multiplicative quasi-FKG inequality

\[
\mu(\sigma\vee\tau)\mu(\sigma\wedge\tau)
\ge
c\,\mu(\sigma)\mu(\tau)
\tag{7.1}
\]

uniformly over a product-closed family containing all tensor powers of that witness.

### Proof

Join and meet act coordinatewise on the product lattice and product probabilities multiply. Therefore the four probability factors in `Q` each factor over coordinates, giving exactly `r^m`. Since `r<1`, `r^m -> 0`. `QED`

Applied to the DGPS necessity statement, this certifies a route obstruction: **one cannot rescue their FKG/domain-gluing method below `n=1` merely by replacing exact FKG with a fixed domain-independent multiplicative loss and iterating it across scales.** A local loss can tensorize into an exponential loss.

Scope qualification: this theorem does **not** prove that the actual connected SAW defect sector has no other positive-association or switching principle. It rules out a specific deformation architecture: continuation of the known local FKG lattice condition with a uniform multiplicative slack. A successful `n=0` route must use a different structure, such as switching in defect sectors, a signed/algebraic identity with a positive remainder, or a geometry-dependent estimate which does not tensorize this way.
