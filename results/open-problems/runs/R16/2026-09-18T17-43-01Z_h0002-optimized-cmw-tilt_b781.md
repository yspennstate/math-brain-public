### Sharpened fixed-vertex exploration lemma

Let `0<u<=1`. Let `H` be a graph, let `Lambda>0`, and let `w in [0,1]^{V(H)}` satisfy

`sum_{y in N_H(x)} w(y) >= Lambda w(x)`

for every `x`. Let `p >= (1+u)/Lambda`. Fix `v` and let `C_v` be its component in `H_p`. Put

`gamma := u/6`,

`b := (1+u)(1-gamma) = (6+5u-u^2)/6`,

and

`Phi(u) := (b-1)^2/(4b)
          = u^2(5-u)^2 / [24(6+5u-u^2)]`.

Then for every `C>=0`,

`P( |C_v| <= gamma Lambda and w(C_v) >= C ) <= exp(-Phi(u) C)`.

In particular, when the Perron weight is normalized by `w(v)=1`,

`P( |C_v| > (u/6)Lambda ) >= 1-exp(-Phi(u))`.

### Proof

Follow CMW's exploration proof of Lemma 3.1(b) up to the final invocation of their Lemma 2.5. On the event

`|C_v| <= gamma Lambda`, `w(C_v)=W >= C`,

the same small-set expansion estimate used by CMW gives, for the total queried weight `Q`,

`Q >= (1-gamma)Lambda W`,

while the total weight of successful queries is at most `W`.

CMW Lemma 2.5(2) allows an arbitrary concentration parameter `a in [0,1/2]`. Therefore

`a*(successful queried weight) - p*a*(1-a)*Q`

is at most

`a W - (1+u)a(1-a)(1-gamma)W`

`= - F_u(a) W`,

where

`F_u(a) := a[(1+u)(1-a)(1-gamma)-1]`

`          = a(b-1) - b a^2`.

Hence whenever `F_u(a)>0`, CMW Lemma 2.5(2) gives

`P( |C_v| <= gamma Lambda and w(C_v)>=C ) <= exp(-F_u(a) C)`.

The function `F_u` is a concave quadratic. Its unique maximizer is

`a_* = (b-1)/(2b)`.

Since `b>1`, one has

`0<a_*<1/2`,

so this maximizer is admissible for CMW Lemma 2.5 with no boundary loss. Substitution gives

`F_u(a_*) = (b-1)^2/(4b) = Phi(u)`.

This proves the sharpened bound. ∎

## 6. Method #28 — typical quotient cuts do not replace the adversarial cut condition

I also tested the most tempting weakening of H2: require expansion only for a typical random cut of the seed-component quotient instead of for every macroscopic cut.

That route fails for a simple structural reason. Consider a quotient graph consisting of two dense pieces with no edge between them, e.g. `K_r dotcup K_r`. A uniformly random bipartition of quotient vertices crosses `Theta(r^2)` internal edges with overwhelming probability, so a strong **typical-cut** statistic can look excellent. But the deterministic cut separating the two cliques has exactly zero capacity. That is precisely the kind of cut selected adversarially by the event that sprinkling fails to make a giant.

Thus a statement about most random cuts cannot by itself replace the all-relevant-cuts control needed by this proof architecture. A future weakening has to control the cuts generated by connected-component partitions, or supply a different dynamical/reservoir mechanism that prevents a bad deterministic bottleneck from being selected. This failed route is recorded because it narrows what a genuine H2 replacement must accomplish.
