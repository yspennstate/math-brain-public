# 4. The five-part stuck diagnosis

## (a) Exact blocking statement

By Salez's sharp varentropy theorem for sparse fast-mixing chains, the source conjecture would follow from, and in this expander setting is equivalent to, the following information-concentration statement. For every bounded-degree vertex-transitive expander sequence `(G_n)`, every fixed `epsilon in (0,1)`, root `o_n`, heat-kernel law `mu_{n,t}(x)=P_{t}(o_n,x)`, and `t_n=t_mix^{(n)}(epsilon)`, prove

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_{X\sim\mu_{n,t_n}}
\left[
\log\bigl(|V_n|\mu_{n,t_n}(X)\bigr)
\right]
=o\!\left(\log^2 |V_n|\right).
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

The previous Hecke lift shows that this is unchanged if the walk is lifted from `G_n=Gamma_n/H_n` to its symmetric `H_n`-bi-invariant convolution walk upstairs. The group language therefore does not remove the estimate; it isolates it.

A sufficient, more geometric recovery target comes from Pedrotti--Salez's July 2026 local-product theorem. If `gamma(mu)` denotes the Poincare constant with the equilibrium law replaced by `mu`, then for bounded-degree expanders, where `t_mix=Theta(log |V|)`, it would be enough to prove on the relevant mixing window a quotient-sensitive bound of the form

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{\mathrm{mix},n}\,\log|V_n|\to\infty,
\qquad
\gamma_{\mathrm{mix},n}:=
\inf_{t\in[t_{\rm mix}(1-\epsilon),\,t_{\rm mix}(\epsilon)]}
\gamma(\mu_{n,t}).
}
\tag{LP}
\]

Pedrotti--Salez explicitly note that the restricted local Poincare constant can replace the global one in their proof, and also suggest relaxed long-time subcommutation `psi(t)<=C exp(-2 kappa t)` as the route beyond negative curvature. Neither estimate is presently known for arbitrary transitive expanders.

## (b) Routes tried and exact failure of each

### Route 1: products / explicit new families

Fixed Cartesian products preserve cutoff and hence cannot refute the conjecture. Synchronous direct/tensor products yield an `O(sqrt(log N))` window and escape several standard sufficient classes, but they remain structured decomposable examples. They moved the family metric to its optimal exponent `1/2` but do not address arbitrary transitive expanders. The two required outside audits of the new-family package are still absent.

### Route 2: ordinary curvature

The previous report computed an explicit girth-at-least-five test direction with

\[
\kappa_{BE}(o)\le-(d-2)/d<0.
\]

Thus positive Bakry--Emery curvature cannot be the universal reason transitive expanders cut off: high-girth Ramanujan examples already exhibit cutoff while having negative local curvature in this normalization.

### Route 3: lift vertex transitivity to a group and invoke group information concentration

The exact lift `G=Gamma/H` preserves the full information-content law, but it produces an `H`-bi-invariant orbital measure, not a conjugacy-invariant measure. Hermon--Huang--Pedrotti--Salez's dimension-free varentropy theorem assumes conjugacy invariance, and their trajectory transformation uses it essentially. The support of the lifted measure has size `d|H|`, while `|H|` can grow although quotient degree `d` is fixed. A support-size bound upstairs is therefore not a quotient-uniform theorem.

### Route 4: use stationarity/spectral gap alone

The classical product condition is false in general, and Pedrotti--Salez's theorem explains the missing object: one needs a non-equilibrium Poincare constant, not the stationary spectral gap. Spectral expansion gives `t_mix=Theta(log N)` but does not control the heat-kernel-weighted bottlenecks entering `gamma(mu_t)`.

### Route 5: global subcommutation from time zero

Pedrotti--Salez define the optimal `psi(t)` in

\[
\Gamma P_t f\le \psi(t) P_t\Gamma f.
\]

The negative-curvature calculation shows that infinitesimal control at `t=0` can be bad. Their own Remark 1 identifies the natural repair: absorb bad short-time behavior in a prefactor `C>1` and seek positive exponential decay only at larger times. No route so far supplies such a uniform large-time gradient estimate on arbitrary transitive expanders.

## (c) What a proof must contain that no route currently contains

Every successful general proof now appears to need a **quotient-sensitive smoothing statement** that uses transitivity and expansion but is insensitive to stabilizer size, local negative curvature, and lack of conjugacy invariance. Two equivalent-looking formats are:

1. direct information concentration (BLOCK), or
2. a mixing-window local Poincare / mesoscopic gradient estimate strong enough for (LP).

The missing ingredient cannot merely be stationary expansion, a fixed-time local statement, or a representation with many group atoms. It must act at the moving time scale `t=Theta(log N)` and control the evolving heat kernel itself.

## (d) Simplest special case still unproved by these routes

The simplest source-valid subclass not covered by the present R16 machinery is:

> **arbitrary fixed-degree Cayley expanders with symmetric generating sets, without assuming conjugacy invariance, Ramanujan spectrum, long-range expansion, or a special building/cover construction.**

This removes stabilizers (`H={e}`) entirely while retaining the central obstruction: changing one increment in a product of generators conjugates the remaining suffix, and without conjugacy invariance the endpoint-information law can change substantially. A bounded literature refresh found many special Cayley-expander cutoff theorems (abelian/nonnegative-curvature cases, Ramanujan complexes/classical groups, conjugacy-invariant walks), but not a theorem covering all bounded-degree Cayley expanders. This is a bounded search, not a novelty certificate.

## (e) Nearest solved problem and the ingredient it has that I0112 lacks

There are two especially close solved mechanisms.

1. **Hermon--Huang--Pedrotti--Salez, _Concentration of information on discrete groups_ (arXiv:2409.16869).** For conjugacy-invariant group walks they obtain a dimension-free varentropy estimate. The proof has a measure-preserving trajectory transformation available precisely because the jump law is invariant under conjugation, together with nonnegative Bakry--Emery curvature. Arbitrary Cayley/Schreier expanders lack both properties.

2. **Pedrotti--Salez, _The local product condition implies cutoff_ (arXiv:2607.05345).** They prove a universal window bound in terms of the non-equilibrium local Poincare constant and explicitly propose long-time relaxed subcommutation as a way to survive negative curvature. Arbitrary transitive expanders lack a known way to estimate that local constant at the `Theta(log N)` mixing scale.

The second mechanism is the more promising recovery because it is designed to tolerate negative curvature and does not require reversibility or a chain rule.

# 5. The three required new-method attacks

## Method #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: from a transitive graph to an evolving weighted electrical network

For simple random walk with generator `L=P-I`, and heat-kernel law `mu_t`, the local Poincare energy can be viewed as the Dirichlet form of a time-dependent reversible weighted network. Formally,

\[
\int \Gamma f\,d\mu_t
=
\sum_{\{x,y\}\in E}
\frac{\mu_t(x)+\mu_t(y)}{2d}\,(f(x)-f(y))^2.
\]

Thus estimating `gamma(mu_t)` is not primarily a group-representation problem: it is a weighted conductance/Poincare problem for edge weights

\[
c_t(x,y)=\frac{\mu_t(x)+\mu_t(y)}{2d}.
\]

This change of category clarifies the obstruction. Uniform edge expansion of the underlying unweighted graph does **not** by itself lower-bound the conductance of every weighted cut, because `mu_t` may put most of its mass deep inside a set while assigning little mass to its boundary shell. To make this route work one needs a heat-kernel regularity statement—e.g. a quantitative control on how fast `mu_t` can decay across successive boundary shells—at times of order `log N`. Ordinary expansion alone supplies the number of boundary edges, not their `mu_t`-weights.

This route therefore did not move `W_exp`, but it yields a sharper recovery target than another curvature lemma: prove a quotient-uniform weighted Cheeger/Poincare inequality for the specific evolving weights `c_t`, not for arbitrary measures.

## Method #7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: local weak limits and the asymptotic equipartition property

A bounded-degree transitive expander sequence has subsequential rooted local limits. Uniform expansion forces every such infinite limit to be nonamenable in the finite-set isoperimetric sense. On a fixed nonamenable transitive/group-like limit, the random walk has positive entropy rate, and the classical Shannon--McMillan/Avez philosophy gives concentration of information after long time.

The tempting proof would therefore be:

`finite transitive expanders -> nonamenable transitive local limit -> AEP on the limit -> transfer back at t ~ log N -> cutoff`.

The failure is exact: Benjamini--Schramm/local convergence controls each **fixed** radius/time, whereas the source problem needs a moving time

\[
t_n\asymp \log |V_n|\to\infty.
\]

No compactness argument used so far gives uniform AEP/varentropy estimates up to this moving scale. The missing theorem would have to be a **uniform quantitative Shannon--McMillan theorem over all degree-`D` transitive graphs with a fixed nonamenability/spectral-expansion parameter**, with an error strong enough at times comparable to `log |V|`. Without such uniformity, passing to a local limit loses exactly the scale on which mixing occurs.

This is the cleanest explanation found so far for why the infinite-volume entropy picture does not automatically settle the finite conjecture.

## Method #26 — HUNT THE SPORADIC CASE: look where the symmetry is weakest

The easy transitive cases are too symmetric: abelian Cayley walks, conjugacy-invariant walks, Ramanujan/building examples, and product constructions all come with extra algebraic or geometric structure. A useful sporadic test must instead have:

- vertex transitivity but several edge/stabilizer suborbits;
- a noncommutative Hecke algebra or an arbitrary non-normal Cayley generating set;
- spectral expansion but no long-range-expander certificate;
- no representation that makes the increments independent after a gauge change.

A single small vertex-transitive graph cannot move an asymptotic cutoff metric, so a brute-force census of isolated sporadic graphs would be bookkeeping rather than research. The right finite computation is instead to search small Cayley/Schreier examples for failure of a **candidate quotient-sensitive local-Poincare inequality**, and only then seek an infinite expander family carrying the same local obstruction. No source-valid candidate inequality has yet survived enough analytic scrutiny to justify that computation, so I deliberately did not manufacture a checker this run.
