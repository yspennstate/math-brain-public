## 3. #5 ADD A PARAMETER — a complete logarithmic Moran phase diagram

The previous knife-edge set is one member of a one-parameter family. Making that parameter explicit isolates the exact logarithmic scale where ordinary Riesz capacity changes from zero to positive.

### R16-I0043-C07 — deterministic `p`-Moran phase theorem

Fix `alpha in (0,1)` and `p in R`. Choose an integer `N=N(alpha,p)` large enough that

\[
q_n(p):=2^{-1/\alpha}
\left(\frac{N+n}{N+n-1}\right)^{p/\alpha}<\frac12
\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `p<=0` this is automatic after any fixed sufficiently large `N`; for `p>0`, the ratio in parentheses decreases with `n`, so it suffices to impose (3.1) at `n=1`.

Starting from `[0,1]`, replace every level-`n-1` interval by two endpoint children of relative length `q_n(p)`. Let `K_p` be the limiting separated binary Moran set. Its level-`n` cylinder length is

\[
\ell_n(p)
=\prod_{j=1}^nq_j(p)
=2^{-n/\alpha}
\left(\frac{N+n}{N}\right)^{p/\alpha}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{\dim_HK_p=\alpha\quad\text{for every }p\in\mathbb R.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H^\alpha(K_p)=
\begin{cases}
0,&p<0,\\
\text{finite and positive},&p=0,\\
\infty,&p>0,
\end{cases}}
\tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cap}_\alpha(K_p)>0
\iff p>1.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the family has an exact deterministic logarithmic transition:

- `p<0`: zero critical Hausdorff measure;
- `p=0`: finite positive critical Hausdorff measure;
- `0<p<=1`: infinite critical Hausdorff measure but zero `alpha`-Riesz capacity;
- `p>1`: positive `alpha`-Riesz capacity.

After an affine embedding, every `K_p` lies in any prescribed `[a,b] subset (0,infinity)`.

### Proof of (3.3)

The contraction ratios are uniformly bounded above by some `q<1/2` and below by a positive constant depending on `(alpha,p,N)`. Hence the construction has uniform strong separation. From (3.2),

\[
-\log\ell_n(p)
=\frac n\alpha\log2
-\frac p\alpha\log\frac{N+n}{N},
\]

so

\[
\frac{n\log2}{-\log\ell_n(p)}\longrightarrow\alpha.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The level covers give the upper bound on dimension. The fair Bernoulli measure, assigning mass `2^{-n}` to every level-`n` cylinder, together with uniform separation, gives the matching lower mass-distribution bound for every exponent `s<alpha`. Hence `dim_HK_p=alpha`.

### Proof of (3.4)

The level-`n` cover has total `alpha`-cost

\[
2^n\ell_n(p)^\alpha
=\left(\frac{N+n}{N}\right)^p.
\tag{3.7}
\]

If `p<0`, (3.7) tends to zero, proving `H^alpha(K_p)=0`.

If `p=0`, (3.7) is identically one, giving finiteness. Uniform separation and the fair measure give `mu(B(x,r))<=C r^alpha`, so the mass-distribution principle gives positivity.

If `p>0`, the fair measure satisfies, at cylinder scales,

\[
2^{-n}
=\left(\frac N{N+n}\right)^p\ell_n(p)^\alpha.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Since `n` is comparable with `log(1/r)` uniformly at small scales and a ball meets only `O(1)` comparable cylinders, (3.8) yields

\[
\mu(B(x,r))
\le C\frac{r^\alpha}{[\log(e/r)]^p}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Therefore the Hausdorff measure for the gauge

\[
h_p(r)=\frac{r^\alpha}{[\log(e/r)]^p}
\]

is positive. For a cover by sets of diameter at most `delta`,

\[
\sum_i(\operatorname{diam}U_i)^\alpha
\ge [\log(e/\delta)]^p
\sum_i h_p(\operatorname{diam}U_i),
\]

so `H^alpha(K_p)=infinity`.

### Proof of the zero-capacity half of (3.5)

Let `nu` be any probability measure on `K_p`, let `C_{n,j}` be the `2^n` level-`n` cylinders, and set

\[
a_n:=\ell_n(p)^{-\alpha}
=2^n\left(\frac N{N+n}\right)^p.
\tag{3.10}
\]

For independent `X,Y~nu`, let `E_n` be the event that `X,Y` lie in the same level-`n` cylinder. Since a common level-`n` cylinder has diameter at most `ell_n`, Tonelli and the nested-cylinder decomposition give

\[
I_\alpha(\nu)
\ge
\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n-a_{n-1})\,\mathbb P(E_n).
\tag{3.11}
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\mathbb P(E_n)
=\sum_{j=1}^{2^n}\nu(C_{n,j})^2
\ge2^{-n}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Put

\[
b_n:=\left(\frac N{N+n}\right)^p.
\]

Then the level contribution in (3.11)--(3.12) is

\[
T_n(p):=(a_n-a_{n-1})2^{-n}
=b_n-\frac12b_{n-1}.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Because `b_{n-1}/b_n -> 1`, for all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
T_n(p)\ge c_p b_n
\tag{3.14}
\]

for some `c_p>0`. The series `sum_n b_n` diverges exactly when `p<=1`. Therefore `I_alpha(nu)=infinity` for **every** probability measure `nu` on `K_p` whenever `p<=1`, so

\[
\operatorname{Cap}_\alpha(K_p)=0,
\qquad p\le1.
\tag{3.15}
\]

### Proof of the positive-capacity half of (3.5)

Let `mu` be the fair Bernoulli measure. If two independent `mu`-points first split after `n` common binary digits, the split event has probability `2^{-(n+1)}`. Uniform strong separation gives a constant `g>0` such that their distance is at least `g ell_n(p)`. Hence

\[
I_\alpha(\mu)
\le C\sum_{n\ge0}2^{-n}\ell_n(p)^{-\alpha}
=C\sum_{n\ge0}\left(\frac N{N+n}\right)^p.
\tag{3.16}
\]

The final series converges exactly when `p>1`, proving positive capacity and completing (3.5). QED.

**Label.** Complete deterministic potential-theory calculation. The Moran/capacity mechanism is classical in spirit; no publication-novelty claim is made.

## 5. #16 COMPUTE SMALL CASES — exact scale of second-moment failure

The fair-measure truncated energy in (3.16) is governed by

\[
J_m(p):=\sum_{n=0}^m
\left(\frac N{N+n}\right)^p.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The first three integer cases already display the entire transition:

### `p=0`

\[
J_m(0)=m+1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The canonical second-moment denominator grows linearly.

### `p=1`

\[
J_m(1)
=N\sum_{k=N}^{N+m}\frac1k
=N\bigl(H_{N+m}-H_{N-1}\bigr)
=N\log m+O_N(1).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The preceding run's knife-edge `K_0` is exactly this `p=1` member: the second-moment loss is only logarithmic.

### `p=2`

\[
J_m(2)
=N^2\sum_{k=N}^{N+m}\frac1{k^2}
\le N^2\sum_{k=N}^\infty\frac1{k^2}<\infty.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The second moment becomes uniformly bounded.

More generally,

\[
J_m(p)\asymp
\begin{cases}
m^{1-p},&p<1,\\
\log m,&p=1,\\
1,&p>1,
\end{cases}
\tag{5.5}
\]

with constants depending on `(N,p)`.

Because the Fakhry--Zhan two-point Green estimate is sharp up to constants at fixed `n=2`, the canonical Paley--Zygmund construction on the level-`m` approximation loses precisely the factor `J_m(p)`: for `p>1` it yields a scale-uniform positive lower bound, at `p=1` only an order `1/log m` lower bound, and for `p<1` a polynomially vanishing lower bound. This is **the exact scale at which the current second-moment route loses the bound**.

This calculation is symbolic and exact; I did not manufacture a finite checker for identities (5.2)--(5.5).

### What the finite higher-point literature says, and does not say

Jokela--Jarvinen--Kytola prove exact order-refined amplitudes for arbitrarily large finite marked sets. Their same-side case is especially relevant here:

- all marked points on the same side have only the outward-increasing visit order at leading scale;
- the complete `N`-point correlation equals that one ordered amplitude;
- fusing successively visited same-side points multiplies the leading amplitude by the gap to the power
  \[
  1-\frac8\kappa=-\alpha

  and reduces the problem to `N-1` points.

Thus the hierarchical exponent seen in the Moran energy calculation is not an artefact of the two-point estimate; it is exactly the exponent present in finite higher-point fusion. What is still missing is a bound uniform enough in the **number of marked cells** to let `N -> infinity` along a critical interval tree. Fakhry--Zhan's theorem explicitly allows its comparison constants to depend on `n`, and JJK's exact formulas are finite-`N` formulas rather than an `N`-uniform tree estimate. This is the unresolved quantifier interchange.
