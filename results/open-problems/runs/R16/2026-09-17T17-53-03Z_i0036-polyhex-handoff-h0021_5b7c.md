## 4. #24 FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT — a five-hexagon strip with exponentially many invisible swap decorations

Consider a horizontal chain of five fused hexagons in the honeycomb lattice. It is convenient to describe it as two zig-zag rails with six vertical column vertices on each rail. Between consecutive column vertices, each rail edge is subdivided once; the vertical rung at each column joins the two rails. Thus each cell is the six-cycle

\[
T_i-U_i-T_{i+1}-B_{i+1}-V_i-B_i-T_i,
\qquad i=0,1,2,3,4.
\]

Attach pendant boundary terminals

\[
a\!-\!T_0,\qquad b\!-\!B_0,\qquad d\!-\!T_5,\qquad c\!-\!B_5.
\]

The four **interior** rungs are

\[
e_i=T_iB_i,\qquad i=1,2,3,4.
\]

Let `R_top` be the top rail path from `a` to `d`, and `R_bot` the bottom rail path from `b` to `c`. Their union is an adjacent-connectivity target pair, which I denote by

\[
t_0=(R_{\rm top},R_{\rm bot}).
\]

Now choose any odd subset

\[
S\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}.
\]

Construct `P_S:a->c` and `Q_S:b->d` by starting `P_S` on the top rail and `Q_S` on the bottom rail, moving monotonically from left to right, and whenever a selected interior rung `e_i` is reached, making **both** paths traverse that rung in opposite directions and exchange rails. Because `|S|` is odd, the endpoints are swapped: `P_S` ends at `c` and `Q_S` ends at `d`. Thus `(P_S,Q_S)` has the opposite boundary connectivity.

### Proposition R16-I0036-POLYHEX-01

For every odd `S` above:

1. `P_S` and `Q_S` are individually self-avoiding paths in the finite honeycomb strip;
2. their mod-two edge support is independent of `S`:

\[
E(P_S)\triangle E(Q_S)=E(R_{\rm top})\cup E(R_{\rm bot});
\tag{4.1}
\]

3. the support in (4.1) has no cycle component — it is exactly two disjoint boundary-to-boundary paths;
4. the total source/target length difference is

\[
|P_S|+|Q_S|-|t_0|=2|S|.
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

Between successive vertical columns, `P_S` and `Q_S` occupy opposite rails. Hence each horizontal/zig-zag rail edge is used by exactly one of the two source paths, and collectively the two source paths use every rail edge exactly once. At a selected rung, both paths traverse the same rung in opposite directions; at an unselected rung neither path uses it. Monotone progress in the column coordinate prevents either individual path from returning to an old vertex, so both are self-avoiding.

In the mod-two symmetric difference, every selected rung occurs twice and cancels. Every rail edge and every pendant terminal edge occurs once and remains. This proves (4.1), and the right side is visibly the two rail paths, hence cycle-free. Finally each selected rung contributes one extra edge to `P_S` and one extra edge to `Q_S` relative to the rail target, yielding (4.2). `QED`.

This construction is the extremal object the previous recovery did not account for: the erased/reconstruction data can consist of **independent local rail swaps that are completely invisible in the mod-two skeleton**.
