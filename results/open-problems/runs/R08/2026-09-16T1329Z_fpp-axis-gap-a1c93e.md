## R08-X001 — exact shared-bypass obstruction to naive independent surgery

**Status: PROVED route obstruction. It does not refute the source problem.**

For every integer \(m\ge1\) and rational \(\varepsilon>0\), set \(L=m+1\) and consider the nearest-neighbor ladder subgraph
\[
\{0,\dots,L\}\times\{0,1\}\subset\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Give every horizontal edge weight \(1\). Give the two endpoint vertical rungs weights \(\varepsilon/2\), and every internal vertical rung weight
\[
H=10L+10+\varepsilon.
\]
Let \(s=(0,0)\), \(t=(L,0)\), and mark the first \(m\) bottom horizontal edges.

The bottom rail is the unique \(s\)-\(t\) geodesic, with cost \(L\). The only competitive bypass avoiding any one marked bottom edge is
\[
(0,0)\to(0,1)\to(L,1)\to(L,0),
\]
with cost \(L+\varepsilon\). Any route using an internal vertical rung costs more than \(H>L+\varepsilon\).

Therefore deleting **any one** of the \(m\) marked edges changes the unique passage time from \(L\) to \(L+\varepsilon\). Every marked edge has the same deletion margin \(\varepsilon\), but all \(m\) margins are generated by one common bypass.

Consequently the implication

> “positive density of geodesic edges with positive deletion margins”  
> \(\Rightarrow\) “independent/factorized local detour gains”

is false even for finite planar nearest-neighbor square-lattice graphs with positive rational weights.

This is the precise obstruction that a positive-density surgery argument must overcome: one must control overlap/preimages of bypasses or extract a jointly usable family, not multiply one-edge margin probabilities.

## R08-L001 — the exact uniform-limit obligation for a strict gap

**Status: PROVED reduction.**

Let \(u,v\in\mathbb Z^2\). Suppose there are random lattice paths \(P_n\) from \(0\) to \(n(u+v)\), a constant \(\gamma>0\), and deterministic \(\eta_n=o(n)\) such that
\[
\mathbb E\,T(P_n)
\le
\mathbb E\,T(0,nu)
+
\mathbb E\,T(nu,n(u+v))
-\gamma n+\eta_n.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\mu(u+v)\le\mu(u)+\mu(v)-\gamma.}
\tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Since first-passage time is the minimum path cost,
\[
T(0,n(u+v))\le T(P_n).
\]
Taking expectations and using (1),
\[
\mathbb E T(0,n(u+v))
\le
\mathbb E T(0,nu)
+
\mathbb E T(nu,n(u+v))
-\gamma n+\eta_n.
\]
Translation invariance gives
\[
\mathbb E T(nu,n(u+v))=\mathbb E T(0,nv).
\]
For every fixed integer vector \(x\), the integrable stationary subadditive process \(T(0,nx)\) satisfies
\[
\frac1n\mathbb E T(0,nx)\longrightarrow\mu(x)
\]
(the usual Kingman/L1 time-constant conclusion; Exp(1) has finite first moment). Divide the previous inequality by \(n\) and let \(n\to\infty\). Since \(\eta_n/n\to0\), (2) follows. \(\square\)

For the frozen pair \(u=e_1,v=e_2\), any construction proving (1) with \(\gamma>0\) immediately gives
\[
\Delta_{12}\ge\gamma.
\]

So the true surgery target is not “many locally improvable edges.” It is a **linear expected jointly usable saving** after all bypass conflicts and adaptive selection costs have been charged.
