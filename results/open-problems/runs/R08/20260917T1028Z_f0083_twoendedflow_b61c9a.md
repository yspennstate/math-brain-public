## 3. B6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE: exact active-ball characterization

The preceding report's territorial-radius test can be upgraded from a one-way certificate to an exact reduction in the all-sated finite-radius regime.

Fix appetite `alpha>0`, a discrete center set `Xi`, and an allocation `psi` in which every center is sated. For a center `xi`, define

`R_psi(xi) = ess sup { |x-xi| : psi(x)=xi }`.

Assume these radii are finite. For a radius family `R=(R_xi)`, define the **active nearest-center cell**

`C_xi(R) = { x : |x-xi| < R_xi and |x-xi| < |x-eta| for every eta != xi with |x-eta| < R_eta }`.

Because `Xi` is countable, the union of pairwise Euclidean bisectors is Lebesgue-null, so ties can be ignored.

### Lemma 1 — stable all-sated allocations are exactly radius fixed points

**(a) Necessity.** If `psi` is stable, all centers are sated, and all `R_psi(xi)<infinity`, then, up to a null set,

`psi^{-1}(xi) = C_xi(R_psi)`

for every center `xi`.

**Proof.** Let `x` be a normal site and suppose `|x-xi| < R_psi(xi)`. By the definition of essential supremum, there is territory of `xi` at distances strictly larger than `|x-xi|`; hence `xi` covets `x`. If `x` were unclaimed, it would desire `xi`, giving an unstable pair. If `x` were assigned to `eta` with `|x-eta|>|x-xi|`, it would again desire `xi`, also giving an unstable pair. Therefore any site lying in at least one active ball must be assigned to the nearest active center. Conversely a territory point of `xi` lies at distance at most `R_psi(xi)` a.e.; boundary spheres are null. This gives the claimed equality a.e. `square`

**(b) Sufficiency.** Conversely, suppose a finite radius family `R` satisfies

`Leb(C_xi(R)) = alpha` for every `xi`.

Allocate `C_xi(R)` to `xi`, leave sites outside all active cells unclaimed, and ignore the null tie/boundary set. Then the resulting allocation is stable.

**Proof.** Every center is sated. If a site `x` desires a center `xi`, then either `x` is unclaimed or its assigned center is farther than `xi`. If `|x-xi|<R_xi`, the nearest-active rule would instead allocate `x` to `xi` or to an even closer active center, contradicting desire. Hence desire implies `|x-xi|>=R_xi`. But all territory of `xi` lies inside `B(xi,R_xi)`, so `xi` cannot covet `x`. Thus no unstable pair exists. `square`

Therefore, within this regime, F0083 becomes the concrete nonlinear fixed-point problem

`V_xi(R) := Leb(C_xi(R)) = alpha,   xi in Xi`.

Two distinct radius solutions `R` and `S` whose active cells differ on positive measure give two stable allocations immediately.

This matters because it removes the previous report's vague “common reservoir” language. The background does **not** need to be filled: sites outside all active balls can remain unclaimed because every center is sated. The construction problem is now exactly to produce two solutions of a geometric volume system.

## 5. B15 + B30 — break symmetry and solve the flow one layer at a time

The replacement is a two-ended, nonstationary layered graph with **cubic Euclidean growth**, bounded edge lengths, exact conservation at every vertex, uniformly summable directed-path capacities, and no source/sink defect.

### 5.1 Centers

For `n in Z`, let

`m_n = |n|+1`,

and define the layer

`V_n = { (n,i,j) : 0 <= i,j < m_n } subset R^3`.

Set

`Xi_* = union_{n in Z} V_n`.

This set is uniformly discrete: distinct centers have Euclidean distance at least `1`.

Its growth is exactly cubic up to constants. Layer `+/-k` has `(k+1)^2` centers. All its centers lie in the ball of radius `sqrt(3) k + O(1)`, while every center in that layer has norm at least `k`. Consequently

`#(Xi_* cap B(0,R)) = Theta(R^3)`.

So the source's necessary “greater than quadratic” growth is met at the first polynomial exponent above the obstruction, rather than by the previous exponential crowding.

### 5.2 The exact one-dimensional refinement coupling

For `m>=1`, partition `[0,1)` in two consecutive ways:

`I_i^m = [i/m,(i+1)/m),        0<=i<m`,

`I_a^{m+1} = [a/(m+1),(a+1)/(m+1)), 0<=a<m+1`.

Define

`t_m(i,a) = Leb(I_i^m cap I_a^{m+1}).`

Then exactly

`sum_a t_m(i,a)=1/m`,

`sum_i t_m(i,a)=1/(m+1)`.

Also `t_m(i,a)>0` only for `a=i` or `a=i+1`, so the index displacement is at most one.

### 5.3 Positive layers: refinement flow

For `n>=0`, put `m=n+1`. From `v=(n,i,j)` to `w=(n+1,a,b)` set

`q(v,w)=t_m(i,a)t_m(j,b)`

when this product is positive.

The total outgoing capacity of every level-`n` vertex is

`(1/m)^2`,

and the total incoming capacity of every level-`n+1` vertex is

`(1/(m+1))^2`.

Since there are `m^2` vertices at level `n`, total flow across every positive interface is exactly `1`.

### 5.4 Negative layers: run the same coupling backwards

For `n<=-1`, put `m=|n|+1`, so layer `n` has `m^2` vertices while layer `n+1` has `(m-1)^2`. Define

`q((n,i,j),(n+1,a,b)) = t_{m-1}(a,i)t_{m-1}(b,j)`.

Now each level-`n` vertex again has outgoing capacity `1/m^2`, and each level-`n+1` vertex receives `1/(m-1)^2`.

At level `0` there is one vertex. It receives total flow `1` from layer `-1` and sends total flow `1` to layer `+1`. Hence **every vertex of the full two-ended directed graph has incoming capacity equal to outgoing capacity**.

This removes the binary scaffold's root-volume defect exactly.

### 5.5 Bounded Euclidean edges

Whenever `t_m(i,a)>0`, the indices differ by at most one. Therefore every directed edge changes each coordinate by at most one, and

`length(edge) <= sqrt(3)`.

Thus the exact flow lives on a bounded-edge Euclidean graph without sacrificing uniform discreteness.

### 5.6 Uniform ray summability

For `n>=0`, every positive-layer edge satisfies

`q(e) <= 1/(n+2)^2`.

For `n<=-1`, every edge satisfies

`q(e) <= 1/(|n|+1)^2`.

Any directed path increases the layer index by one at each step. Therefore, uniformly over **all** directed paths and all starting layers,

`sum_{e on path} q(e) <= 2 sum_{r=2}^infinity 1/r^2 = 2(zeta(2)-1) < infinity.`

This is the exact analogue of the source's Lemma 12 ray-summability condition.

### 5.7 Finite-energy transience on the positive half

The positive half begins at the unique level-zero vertex. The same capacities form a unit flow from that root to infinity. At level `n`, total capacity is `1`, so

`sum_{e between n and n+1} q(e)^2 <= (max_e q(e)) sum_e q(e) <= 1/(n+2)^2`.

Hence the total flow energy is at most

`sum_{n>=0} 1/(n+2)^2 < infinity`.

By the standard finite-energy criterion, the positive-half graph is transient; hence the full undirected graph is transient as well.

`sum_{e into v} epsilon q(e) = sum_{e out of v} epsilon q(e)`,

## 7. The exact obstruction still not solved

One cannot simply place an isolated tiny “transfer ball” near each graph edge and declare it moved. The active-ball characterization shows why: if a center's farthest claimed point is at radius `R_xi`, then **every** site inside that active radius must either be assigned to that center or to an even closer active center. Arbitrary holes between a private core and a remote transfer blob create unstable pairs.

So the next object is not a collection of disconnected reservoirs. It is a pair of complete active-ball Voronoi systems.

A useful local formula is now available. If active balls have no triple-overlap in a neighborhood of an edge `uv`, the volume equation at `u` is

`V_u(R) = Vol(B(u,R_u)) - sum_{v~u} Leb{ x in B(u,R_u) cap B(v,R_v) : |x-v|<|x-u| }`,

with analogous equations at every vertex. The transfer terms are ordinary Euclidean lens/cap volumes. The flow scaffold says exactly what the *difference* of these edge terms should be between two solutions.

The high-value next move is therefore to deform into a thin-overlap regime, linearize this infinite radius system, and ask whether the derivative is a weighted graph Laplacian whose transient two-ended geometry admits a nontrivial bounded boundary-at-infinity mode. That is a precise possible mechanism for two radius fixed points; it is not yet proved.
