# 3. The flat unimodal witness

For `0<r<1/4`, define

`nu_r = r delta_0 + (1-r) 1_(0,1)(x) dx.`                         (3.1)

This is a probability measure and is unimodal with mode `0` in the exact Hasebe--Ueda sense: the only atom is at the mode, the density is zero to the left, constant on `(0,1)`, and zero after `1`.

Its Cauchy transform is

`G_r(z) = r/z + (1-r) log(z/(z-1)).`                              (3.2)

On the upper boundary of the open interval `(0,1)`,

`G_r(x+i0) = A_r(x) - i beta_r`,

where

`A_r(x) = r/x + (1-r) log(x/(1-x))`,
`beta_r = pi(1-r).`                                                 (3.3)

An exact derivative identity is

`A_r'(x) = (x-r)/(x^2(1-x)).`                                      (3.4)

Let

`F_r(x)=1/G_r(x+i0) in C^+.`                                       (3.5)

We will use the three ordered density points

`x_1=r`, `x_2=2r`, `x_3=1/2`,                                    (3.6)

and the corresponding transform points

`z_j=F_r(x_j)`.

From (3.4),

`A_r'(r)=0`,
`A_r'(2r)=1/[4r(1-2r)]`,
`A_r'(1/2)=4-8r.`                                                   (3.7)

The flat density is the key design choice: the first-order perturbation will not have to beat any pre-existing slope.

# 4. A quantitative Harnack margin; one explicit r works

Write

`z_3=X_3+iY_3`, `z_2=X_2+iY_2`.

For a positive harmonic function `h` on the upper half-plane normalized by `h(z_3)=1`, Harnack's inequality gives

`h(z_2) >= L_H := (1-rho)/(1+rho)`,

where

`rho = |(z_2-z_3)/(z_2-conj(z_3))|.`                              (4.1)

The exact cross-ratio identity

`|z_2-conj(z_3)|^2-|z_2-z_3|^2 = 4Y_2Y_3`

implies the useful crude bound

`L_H >= Y_2 Y_3 / |z_2-conj(z_3)|^2.`                              (4.2)

Take from now on the explicit rational value

`r = 1/1000.`                                                       (4.3)

At `x_3=1/2`,

`A_r(1/2)=2r`,
`Y_3=beta_r/(4r^2+beta_r^2).`

At `x_2=2r`, write

`M_r=-A_r(2r) = -1/2 + (999/1000) log 499`,
`Y_2=beta_r/(M_r^2+beta_r^2).`                                    (4.4)

Since both `|z_2|` and `|z_3|` are at most `1/beta_r`, (4.2) gives

`L_H >= beta_r^4 / [4(4r^2+beta_r^2)(M_r^2+beta_r^2)].`            (4.5)

The exact checker certifies a simple rational lower estimate. Using the classical bounds

`3 < pi < 22/7`

and

`log 499 < 7`

(the latter follows already from the exact partial exponential sum
`sum_{n=0}^7 7^n/n! = 47273/72 > 499`), one gets

`L_H > 80676485676081 / 2090000000000000 > 3/100.`                  (4.6)

Meanwhile

`A_r'(2r)=125000/499`,
`A_r'(1/2)=499/125`,                                                (4.7)

and exactly

`(3/100) A_r'(2r) > A_r'(1/2).`                                   (4.8)

Thus there is a strict first-order margin already at the completely explicit witness parameter `r=1/1000`.

# 5. Move 11 — DUALIZE through the local Voiculescu transform

Let `mu` now be an **arbitrary non-Dirac probability measure**. No moments are assumed.

For every probability law, the Voiculescu transform `phi_mu` is defined on

`Omega_mu = union_{alpha>0} Gamma_{alpha,beta_mu(alpha)}`

and takes values in `C^- union R`. Moreover `phi_mu(z)=o(z)` nontangentially at infinity.

For `c>0`, let `D_c mu` denote dilation by `c`. Then

`phi_{D_c mu}(z)=c phi_mu(z/c).`                                   (5.1)

Set

`S_c(z)=-phi_{D_c mu}(z).`                                         (5.2)

Its domain is

`Omega_c = c Omega_mu
         = union_{alpha>0} Gamma_{alpha,c beta_mu(alpha)}.`         (5.3)

Two facts are crucial.

### 5.1 The local domains exhaust the upper half-plane

For every compact `K subset C^+`, choose `alpha` larger than

`max_{z in K} |Re z|/Im z`.

Then for sufficiently small `c`,

`K subset Gamma_{alpha,c beta_mu(alpha)} subset Omega_c.`           (5.4)

Hence `Omega_c` exhausts `C^+` as `c downarrow 0` in the compact-set sense.

### 5.2 The perturbation is small and has a strict Pick sign

For compact `K subset C^+`, nontangential `phi=o(z)` and (5.1) give

`sup_{z in K} |S_c(z)| -> 0.`                                      (5.5)

Also `Im S_c >=0` on `Omega_c`. In fact it is **strictly** positive for non-Dirac `mu`. If `Im S_c` vanished at an interior point, the nonnegative harmonic function `Im S_c` would attain its minimum zero there, hence vanish identically on the connected transform domain. Then `S_c` would be a real-valued analytic function, hence constant by the open-mapping theorem. A constant Voiculescu transform is a Dirac law, contradiction.

For completeness, the sign can also be seen directly from the reciprocal Cauchy transform. For every probability law,

`Im F_mu(w) >= Im w`,                                               (5.6)

because

`|G_mu(w)|^2 <= integral |w-t|^{-2} dmu(t)`

by Cauchy--Schwarz, while

`-Im G_mu(w) = Im(w) integral |w-t|^{-2} dmu(t)`.

If `z=F_mu(w)`, then

`Im phi_mu(z)=Im(w-z)<=0`,

with strict inequality for non-Dirac laws. Thus the local sign is not an FID hypothesis.

# 6. Normalize the local Pick perturbation

Recall the three fixed transform points `z_1,z_2,z_3` from Section 3.

The full-half-plane Harnack lower in (4.6) is strictly larger than `3/100`. Choose a bounded connected domain

`D compactly contained in C^+`

containing small neighborhoods of `z_1,z_2,z_3` and so large that its Harnack lower constant from `z_3` to `z_2` is still strictly larger than `3/100`. Such a `D` exists by exhausting `C^+` with increasing bounded domains; the corresponding hyperbolic/Harnack constants converge to the upper-half-plane value.

By (5.4), for all sufficiently small `c`,

`D subset Omega_c.`                                                 (6.1)

Define

`a_c = Re S_c(z_3)`,
`eta_c = Im S_c(z_3) >0`,

and

`H_c(z) = [S_c(z)-a_c]/eta_c.`                                     (6.2)

Then on `D`,

`H_c:D -> C^+`,
`H_c(z_3)=i`,                                                       (6.3)

and Harnack gives

`Im H_c(z_2) > 3/100.`                                              (6.4)

Also `eta_c ->0` by (5.5). The family `{H_c}` is locally bounded on compact subsets of `D`: this follows from the Harnack inequality for the imaginary part together with the analytic normalization `H_c(z_3)=i` (equivalently, compose with conformal charts and the Cayley transform). Cauchy estimates give uniform derivative bounds on slightly smaller neighborhoods of the three points.

Now center the small candidate by a real translation:

`lambda_c = D_c mu boxplus delta_{a_c}.`                            (6.5)

Its local Voiculescu transform is

`phi_{lambda_c}(z) = -eta_c H_c(z)`                                (6.6)

on `D`.

If `mu` were FSU then every dilation and translation of `mu`, hence every `lambda_c`, would also be FSU. We will contradict this using the single fixed unimodal witness `nu_r`.

# 7. Local subordination expansion

Let

`rho_c = lambda_c boxplus nu_r`

and write

`w_c(z)=F_{rho_c}(z)`.

On the local inverse branch, the additive free-convolution identity gives

`w_c(z)
 = F_{nu_r}( z - phi_{lambda_c}(w_c(z)) )
 = F_{nu_r}( z + eta_c H_c(w_c(z)) ).`                              (7.1)

At `eta=0`, the solution is

`w_0(z)=F_{nu_r}(z)`.

On a complex neighborhood of each interior point `x_j`, the upper boundary branch of `F_{nu_r}` is analytic. The derivative with respect to `w` of the left side minus the right side of (7.1) is exactly `1` at `eta=0`. Therefore the analytic implicit-function theorem applies.

The physical free-convolution branch agrees with this local branch for small `c`: for `Im z>0`, `lambda_c -> delta_0` weakly, hence `F_{lambda_c boxplus nu_r}(z)->F_{nu_r}(z)`; uniqueness of the IFT branch identifies them, and analytic continuation gives the boundary values at the three real interior points.

Because the normalized maps `H_c` form a locally bounded family, the IFT remainder is uniform in `c` at the three fixed points. Differentiating (7.1) at zero and inverting yields

`G_{rho_c}(x_j+i0)
 = G_r(x_j+i0)
 + eta_c G_r'(x_j+i0) H_c(F_r(x_j))
 + O(eta_c^2).`                                                      (7.2)

On `(0,1)`,

`G_r'(x+i0)=A_r'(x)`

is real. Therefore the local density satisfies

`p_c(x_j)
 = (1-r)
 - [eta_c/pi] A_r'(x_j) Im H_c(z_j)
 + O(eta_c^2).`                                                      (7.3)

The three first-order coefficients now have the required shape.

At `x_1=r`,

`A_r'(x_1)=0`,                                                       (7.4)

so

`p_c(x_1)=(1-r)+O(eta_c^2).`

At `x_2=2r`, (6.4) and (4.7) give

`A_r'(x_2) Im H_c(z_2)
 > (3/100)(125000/499).`                                             (7.5)

At `x_3=1/2`, the normalization `H_c(z_3)=i` gives

`A_r'(x_3) Im H_c(z_3)=499/125.`                                   (7.6)

By the exact rational inequality (4.8), the coefficient in (7.5) is strictly larger than the coefficient in (7.6), and it is strictly positive. Since `eta_c>0` and tends to zero, (7.3)--(7.6) imply that for all sufficiently small `c`,

`p_c(r) > p_c(2r) < p_c(1/2).`                                     (7.7)

Because

`r < 2r < 1/2`,                                                     (7.8)

this is a strict three-point valley. No unimodal density can have such a valley: if its mode lies at or left of the middle point it cannot rise afterward; if its mode lies at or right of the middle point it cannot have fallen from the first point.

Thus

`lambda_c boxplus nu_r`

is not unimodal. But `nu_r` is unimodal, contradicting FSU of `lambda_c`, hence contradicting FSU of `mu`.

Since `mu` was an arbitrary non-Dirac probability law, C005 is proved.

# 9. Move 22 — READ THE FAILURES / why the preceding limit strategy was weaker

Before noticing the local-transform fact for arbitrary laws, I explored the stronger FID-only theorem and Pata's domain-of-partial-attraction result.

Pata (1996) proves that any nondegenerate affine subsequential limit of normalized free iid sums is freely infinitely divisible, and conversely every freely infinitely divisible law has a nonempty domain of partial attraction. This would have implied that an FSU law can have no nondegenerate normalized subsequential free-sum limit once all non-Dirac FID laws are excluded.

But that route does **not** by itself settle the source question: the existence of a nondegenerate partial-attraction limit is a property of the starting law, not a universal compactness theorem for every probability law. Classical partial-attraction theory likewise contains laws outside all useful partial-attraction domains.

The failure exposed what was unnecessarily global in the limit approach. The actual obstruction is already visible in the **local** Voiculescu transform of every law near infinity. Dilation moves that local cone down to the fixed witness points, and Harnack transfers a nonzero local Pick perturbation from one point to another. This is precisely why Move 11 succeeds without any infinite-divisibility assumption.

So Move 22 did not create a second theorem; it identified and removed the incorrect extra global compactness obligation.
