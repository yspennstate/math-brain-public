## 4. B7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: why the two known limits do not deform to the source

Durrett--Swindle's theorem adds stirring at rate `epsilon^{-2}` and proves coexistence when `epsilon` is sufficiently small, using convergence to a reaction--diffusion PDE and a traveling-wave/block argument. The source problem has **zero stirring**, corresponding to the singular opposite endpoint `epsilon=infinity`. ## 5. B16 — COMPUTE EVERY SMALL CASE: finite-block closure lemma

### Lemma 5.1 — one-point block certificate implies an interval

Consider a protected finite block with `N_v` site adsorption clocks, `N_e` nearest-neighbor O2-pair clocks, and time horizon `T`. Let `E` be any good-block event determined by the Harris construction in that block (including the source's instantaneous-reaction resolution and any `p`-independent random tie breakers). Then for all `p,p' in (0,1)`,

\[
\boxed{
|\mathbb P_p(E)-\mathbb P_{p'}(E)|
\le T\left(N_v+\frac{N_e}{2}\right)|p-p'|.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

Use one dominating rate-1 Poisson process at each protected site and accept a CO arrival when an attached uniform mark `U` satisfies `U<=p`. For each protected nearest-neighbor edge use a dominating rate-`1/2` Poisson process and accept an O2 arrival when `U<=1-p`; its accepted rate is `(1-p)/2=q/4`, exactly the source normalization. Couple parameters `p,p'` with the same clocks and uniforms. A site mark changes acceptance status with probability `|p-p'|`, and an edge mark does the same with probability `|p-p'|`. The expected numbers of potentially discrepant marks are respectively `TN_v|p-p'|` and `(T N_e/2)|p-p'|`. If no acceptance decision differs, the two graphical evolutions are identical because reaction resolution uses the same additional `p`-independent tie breakers. A union bound gives the probability that the coupled graphical restrictions differ, which bounds the total variation distance and hence the difference of probabilities of `E`. QED.

Thus if a finite certificate at `p_0=1/2` has

\[
\mathbb P_{1/2}(E)\ge \frac{63}{64}+\eta
\qquad (\eta>0),
\]

then the same certificate has probability strictly greater than `63/64` throughout the explicit nondegenerate interval

\[
\left|p-\frac12\right|<
\frac{\eta}{T(N_v+N_e/2)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

So the source's existential **interval** quantifier does not require checking a continuum of `p` values: one strict finite-block certificate at one rational anchor automatically opens into an interval.

### Lemma 5.2 — crude explicit 1-dependent threshold

Let the translated good-block variables form a 1-dependent oriented site field on the usual two-child oriented square lattice and suppose every site is good with probability at least `1-epsilon`. If

\[
\boxed{\epsilon<1/64,}
\tag{5.3}
\]

then the good blocks percolate with positive probability (and, from a fully seeded bottom level, with positive asymptotic block density).

#### Proof

Use the standard dual-barrier/Peierls representation of failure of an oriented open crossing. A dual closed barrier of length `m` has at most four choices at each continuation step, so there are at most `4^m` candidate barriers of length `m` from a fixed anchoring neighborhood. Along any such barrier, choose every third closed block. Those selected blocks are pairwise at dependency distance greater than one, hence independent under 1-dependence. Therefore the probability of a specified length-`m` closed barrier is at most

\[
\epsilon^{\lceil m/3\rceil}.
\]

The total barrier bound is consequently dominated geometrically by

\[
\sum_{m\ge M} 4^m\epsilon^{m/3}.
\]

When `epsilon<1/64`, the ratio `4 epsilon^{1/3}` is strictly less than one, so the probability of arbitrarily long blocking barriers tends to zero. Hence an infinite oriented good path has positive probability. Translation invariance gives a positive density of connected good blocks when the bottom level is fully seeded. QED.

The constant `63/64` is deliberately crude; nothing in the source depends on optimizing it.

### Lemma 5.3 — good-block percolation gives source coexistence

Require the deterministic good-block implication to reproduce a finite pattern `S` containing at least one CO and at least one O in each forward block. Start the particle system from a periodic tiling of incoming copies of `S`, randomized by a uniform spatial shift over one period. On the event that a block is connected to the seeded bottom level by good blocks, the deterministic block implication gives a copy of `S` at the corresponding block time. By Lemma 5.2, for `epsilon<1/64` these connected blocks have positive spatial density.

The process has locally finite graphical rates, so each occurrence of a type at a block time has a uniformly positive probability of persisting for a short deterministic time interval. Therefore the space-time Cesaro averages of the laws have uniformly positive one-site densities of both types. Compactness of `{0,1,2}^{Z^2}` gives a weak subsequential limit; the usual Krylov--Bogoliubov argument for the Feller process makes the limit stationary. Randomizing the initial periodic shift and spatially averaging if necessary makes it translation invariant. Both species retain positive density, hence this is a coexistence stationary law in the source sense.

Combining Lemmas 5.1--5.3 proves:
