## 4. Method #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: collision-free corank one as a bordered inverse-bilinear problem

The bounded sparse modular families of runs18--27 keep improving finite `n` but are exponentially negligible for fixed support type. The asymptotic obstruction is collision-free **small-corank dense/unstructured singularity**. I therefore stop parameterizing null vectors and rewrite the real-corank-one sector in Schur-complement language.

Let

\[
\mathcal R_m^{(1)}=
\{B\in\mathcal G_m:\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}B=m-1\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `B in R_m^(1)`, let `s_L(B)` and `s_R(B)` be the support sizes of nonzero left and right real null vectors. Since `B in G_m`,

\[
\boxed{s_L(B),s_R(B)\ge3.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed support one would force a zero row or column; support two would make two nonzero Boolean rows or columns proportional, hence equal.

Fix an invertible binary `(m-1) x (m-1)` matrix `A`. Let its column set and row set be denoted `Col(A)` and `Row(A)`. Define

\[
\Xi^\circ(A)=
\#\Bigl\{(b,c)\in\{0,1\}^{m-1}\times\{0,1\}^{m-1}:\
 b\notin\{0\}\cup\operatorname{Col}(A),\
 c\notin\{0\}\cup\operatorname{Row}(A),\
 c^TA^{-1}b\in\{0,1\}
\Bigr\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The exclusions in (4.3) are not arbitrary cleanup. They are exactly the border realizations of the trivial row/column mechanisms. If `b=0`, singularity forces the final bit `d=0`, making the new column zero. If `b=Ae_j`, singularity forces `d=c_j`, making the new column equal column `j`. The two row exclusions are the transpose analogues.

So after deleting those obvious completions, the residual corank-one problem is the two-sided inverse-bilinear small-ball event

\[
\boxed{c^TA^{-1}b\in\{0,1\}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a dense-sector formulation, unlike the bounded-support null-plane catalog.
