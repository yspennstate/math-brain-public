## 2. Method #4 — prove a weaker statement completely: few-level dense normals are superexponentially negligible

Run31/C285 reduced the dense corank-one problem, after paying the JSS structured sector and higher corank, to the excess number of cube points on a hyperplane generated by a random invertible sign basis. Write `d=n-1`. For an invertible sign matrix

\[
R\in\{\pm1\}^{d\times d},
\]
put

\[
w_R=R^{-1}{\bf1},\qquad
C(w_R)=\{x\in\{\pm1\}^d:\langle x,w_R\rangle=1\},
\qquad M(w_R)=|C(w_R)|.
\]

Retained C285 says that, after excluding signed-unit collision cases and the paid structured sector, source closure is equivalent at polynomial scale to proving that the mean excess `M(w_R)-d` is `o(d^2)`.

The Hadamard examples in run29 show that a fixed dense normal can have `M(w)` of Littlewood--Offord size `Theta(2^d/sqrt(d))`, so pointwise anticoncentration is false. The new parameter is therefore the **coefficient complexity**

\[
\boxed{\kappa(w):=\#\{|w_i|:w_i\ne0\},}
\tag{2.1}
\]

the number of distinct nonzero absolute coefficient magnitudes.

Fix the retained JSS density constant `delta>0` and define, for an integer `K`,

\[
\mathcal W_{d,K,\delta}
=\{w:|\operatorname{supp}w|\ge\delta d,\ \kappa(w)\le K\}.
\]

### R03-C290 — coefficient-complexity cutoff

**STATUS: PROVED.** Let `R` be uniform over invertible `d x d` sign matrices. For every fixed `epsilon>0`, uniformly for

\[
K\le d^{1/2-\epsilon},
\]
we have

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\Big[(M(w_R)-d)_+\,
\mathbf1_{\{w_R\in\mathcal W_{d,K,\delta}\}}\Big]
\le \exp\big(-(\epsilon+o(1))d\log d\big).
}
\tag{C290}
\]

In particular the entire dense, few-level sector contributes `o(1)`, vastly below the `o(d^2)` target.

### Proof

Fix a support/sign/magnitude-class assignment with at most `K` nonzero magnitude classes. There are at most

\[
(2K+1)^d
\tag{2.2}
\]

such labeled assignments: each coordinate is zero or belongs, with either sign, to one of `K` classes.

After flipping cube coordinates to absorb the coefficient signs, write the positive class magnitudes as

\[
a=(a_1,\ldots,a_k),\qquad k\le K,
\]

with class sizes `m_1,...,m_k`. A cube point is seen by the section equation only through its block-sum vector

\[
z=(z_1,\ldots,z_k),\qquad
z_j\in\{-m_j,-m_j+2,\ldots,m_j\}.
\]

Thus

\[
Z=\prod_{j=1}^k Z_j,
\qquad |Z|\le(d+1)^k.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For each `z in Z`, the equality `a dot z=1` is a hyperplane in `a`-space. The equality pattern

\[
\{z\in Z:a\cdot z=1\}
\]

is determined by the affine flat obtained by intersecting all hyperplanes through `a`. Such a flat in `R^k` is determined by at most `k` of those hyperplanes. Consequently the number of distinct section patterns for the fixed coordinate assignment is at most

\[
\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{|Z|}{j}
\le(|Z|+1)^k
\le(d+2)^{K^2}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Combining (2.2)--(2.4), the number of possible dense cube-section patterns with coefficient complexity at most `K` is bounded by

\[
\boxed{N_{d,K}\le(2K+1)^d(d+2)^{K^2}.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Now fix such a normal with support size `s>=delta d`. On the support, after the sign flips, the equation `w dot x=1` is one positive weighted subset-sum level. Two subsets on the same level cannot strictly contain one another. By Sperner's theorem,

\[
M(w)
\le 2^{d-s}\binom{s}{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}
\le C_\delta\frac{2^d}{\sqrt d}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

For a fixed section `C` of size `M`, at most `M^d` ordered sign-row tuples lie in `C`, hence at most `M^d` invertible sign bases do. Because an invertible `R` satisfying `Rw=1` determines `w` uniquely, summing over section patterns does not miss an extra multiplicity. If `I_d` denotes the number of invertible `d x d` sign matrices, Tikhomirov's theorem gives

\[
I_d=(1-o(1))2^{d^2},
\]

so for all large `d`, `I_d>=2^{d^2-1}`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{d,K}
&:=\mathbb E[(M(w_R)-d)_+\mathbf1_{\mathcal W_{d,K,\delta}}]\\
&\le
2(2K+1)^d(d+2)^{K^2}
\left(C_\delta\frac{2^d}{\sqrt d}\right)^{d+1}2^{-d^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Taking logarithms,

\[
\log E_{d,K}
\le d\log(2K+1)+K^2\log(d+2)
-\frac{d+1}{2}\log d+d\log2+O_\delta(d).
\tag{2.8}
\]

If `K<=d^(1/2-epsilon)`, then

\[
d\log(2K+1)
\le\left(\frac12-\epsilon\right)d\log d+O(d),
\]

and

\[
K^2\log(d+2)=o(d\log d).
\]

Substitution into (2.8) yields

\[
\log E_{d,K}\le-(\epsilon+o(1))d\log d,
\]

which proves C290. QED.
