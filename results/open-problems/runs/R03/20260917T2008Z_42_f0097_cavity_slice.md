## 2. Frozen F0097 metric

Let `F_k(n,m)` denote the standard proper random `k`-CNF with `n` variables and `m` independent uniformly sampled proper clauses. Put

\[
p_k(n,m)=\Pr(F_k(n,m)\text{ is satisfiable}).
\]

By monotonicity in `m`, define the median threshold location

\[
\theta_{k,n}
:=\frac1n\min\{m\in\mathbb N:p_k(n,m)\le 1/2\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Friedgut's sharp-threshold theorem says that for every fixed `k>=3` this is a sharp threshold sequence; the remaining source issue is whether it converges. Therefore the natural source-valid oscillation quantity is

\[
\boxed{\Omega_k:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\theta_{k,n}
-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\theta_{k,n}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For fixed `k`, uniform-threshold existence is equivalent to `Omega_k=0`. F0097 asks this for every fixed `k>=3`. The first unresolved case is `k=3`, so R03 freezes

\[
\boxed{\text{metric}=\Omega_3,\quad\text{target }0.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The rigorous `3.52` lower and `4.4898` upper bounds imply

\[
\boxed{0\le\Omega_3\le0.9698.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is **metric initialization from prior literature**, not a new R03 improvement. The stagnation count for F0097 starts at zero rather than inheriting F0001's six-run streak.

## 3. Method #2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

Assume the negation of the `k=3` source statement:

\[
\Omega_3>0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then the median threshold sequence has at least two subsequential limits separated by a fixed `delta>0`. A first possible contradiction route is to prove very strong stability under changing `n` by one variable. This looks tempting because adding one variable affects only `O(1)` clauses at fixed density.

The obstruction is exact and elementary: even a bounded sequence satisfying

\[
|a_{n+1}-a_n|=O(1/n)
\]

need not converge. For example

\[
a_n=c+A\sin(\log n)
\tag{3.2}
\]

has `|a_{n+1}-a_n|=O(1/n)` but

\[
\limsup a_n-\liminf a_n=2A.
\]

Thus **any route whose only output is adjacent-size continuity, even at the natural `1/n` density scale, cannot rule out F0097's negation**. A proof needs either a summable drift bound or a genuinely mesoscopic/multiplicative-scale contraction.

Outcome #2: the negation survives all purely local-in-`n` continuity estimates. This tells us what the next method must beat.

## 4. Method #6 — DEFORM TO A SOLVED POINT: an exact one-variable cavity identity

Deleting/assigning one variable transforms pure `k`-SAT into a common `(n,m-T)` `k`-SAT core plus a bounded number of random `(k-1)`-clauses. For `k=3` the perturbation is therefore random **2-clauses**, a solved CSP globally. The point is to determine exactly what this deformation buys.

### C298. Exact cavity comparison in the proper-clause model

Let `F_k(n+1,m)` be standard proper random `k`-SAT and distinguish the last variable `x`. Let `T` be the number of clauses containing `x` (with either sign). Then

\[
T\sim\operatorname{Bin}\!\left(m,\frac{k}{n+1}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Set

\[
s_k:=1-2^{-(k-1)},\qquad a_k:=\frac{1+s_k}{2}=1-2^{-k}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then for every `n,m,k>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[
 p_k(n,m-T)\bigl(2a_k^T-s_k^T\bigr)
\right]
\le p_k(n+1,m)
\le
\mathbb E\!\left[p_k(n,m-T)\right].
}
\tag{C298}
\]

In particular, monotonicity in the clause count gives the explicit finite-`n` lower transfer

\[
\boxed{
p_k(n+1,m)\ge A_{k,n,m}\,p_k(n,m),
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
A_{k,n,m}
=2\left(1-\frac{k}{(n+1)2^k}\right)^m
-\left(1-\frac{k}{(n+1)2^{k-1}}\right)^m.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

If `m/(n+1)->c`, then

\[
A_{k,n,m}\to
A_k(c):=2e^{-kc/2^k}-e^{-kc/2^{k-1}}>0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For `k=3`, across the conservative rigorous density window `3.52<=c<=4.4898`, this limiting transfer factor is bounded below by approximately `0.3369` (the decimal is explanatory only, not a finite certificate).

### Proof of C298

Condition on `T=t`. The `m-t` clauses not containing `x` form an independent copy

\[
G\sim F_k(n,m-t).
\]

The `t` clauses containing `x` split by the sign of the `x`-literal:

\[
T_+\sim\operatorname{Bin}(t,1/2),\qquad T_-=t-T_+.
\]

After assigning `x=True`, one sign-class disappears because those clauses are satisfied by the `x` literal; the opposite sign-class becomes `T_-` independent random proper `(k-1)`-clauses on the old `n` variables. Assigning `x=False` swaps the roles. Call the two collections `H_+` and `H_-`.

Hence the exact satisfiability event is

\[
\{G\wedge H_+\text{ SAT}\}\cup\{G\wedge H_-\text{ SAT}\}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The upper bound follows immediately: either event implies that `G` itself is satisfiable, so conditional on `T=t` the probability is at most `p_k(n,m-t)`.

For the lower bound, when `G` is satisfiable choose a deterministic canonical satisfying assignment `y(G)` (say lexicographically first). A uniformly random proper `(k-1)`-clause is satisfied by this fixed assignment with probability

\[
s_k=1-2^{-(k-1)}.
\]

Therefore, conditional on `G` satisfiable and on `(T_+,T_-)`, the probability that this **same** assignment `y(G)` extends to at least one value of `x` is

\[
s_k^{T_+}+s_k^{T_-}-s_k^t.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Averaging the sign split,

\[
\mathbb E[s_k^{T_+}\mid T=t]
=\left(\frac{1+s_k}{2}\right)^t=a_k^t,
\]

and by symmetry the same holds for `T_-`. Thus (4.7) averages to

\[
2a_k^t-s_k^t.
\]

Multiplying by `p_k(n,m-t)` and averaging `T` proves the lower inequality in C298.

Finally, because `m-T<=m` and satisfiability probability decreases with the clause count,

\[
p_k(n,m-T)\ge p_k(n,m).
\]

Taking the binomial probability generating function in the lower inequality gives

\[
\mathbb E[a_k^T]
=\left(1-\frac{k}{n+1}(1-a_k)\right)^m
=\left(1-\frac{k}{(n+1)2^k}\right)^m,
\]

and analogously for `s_k`, proving (4.3)--(4.4). Equation (4.5) is the elementary exponential limit. QED.

### Source-slide literal-with-replacement convention

Frieze's slides say to choose the `k` literals independently. For the one distinguished variable `x`, a clause contains two or more occurrences of the underlying variable `x` with probability `O_k(n^{-2})`; with `m=O(n)`, the probability that any clause has such a multiple `x`-occurrence is `O_k(n^{-1})`. On the complement of that event the same one-variable decomposition and proof hold (the old literals may repeat, but a fixed assignment falsifies all `k-1` independent literals with probability exactly `2^{-(k-1)}`). Thus C298 has an `O_k(1/n)` additive one-step error in the literal-with-replacement convention at fixed density. I do **not** claim finite-n identity of the two global models.

### What the deformation does *not* give

At the `3`-SAT transition, `A_3(c)` is only a positive constant, around `0.34--0.46` over the conservative rigorous window. Therefore tracking a **single canonical satisfying assignment** through one cavity step loses a constant factor. Iterating over `Theta(n)` newly exposed variables would lose exponentially much probability. The fact that the inserted constraints are 2-clauses is not enough: they act on the highly correlated near-critical 3-SAT solution set, not on a fresh random 2-SAT instance.

The 2026 Chatterjee et al. paper makes this diagnosis especially concrete: below the Gibbs-uniqueness threshold one can rigorously control the number of satisfying assignments, but extending such control to the threshold would likely resolve the uniform-threshold question. C298 isolates the same missing object as a **cavity survival probability of the solution set**.

Outcome #6: proved a concrete exact finite-n comparison, but it does not move `Omega_3` below the literature baseline. Status remains PARTIAL.

## 5. Method #27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS

The source asks for a threshold for **every fixed `k`**. Rather than carry that universal quantifier through every argument, freeze the first unresolved case `k=3` and ask only

\[
\boxed{\Omega_3=0.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is a strictly weaker target and would be PARTIAL relative to F0097, but it is source-valid and removes the irrelevant large-k regime already solved by Ding--Sly--Sun.

C298 supplies strong local consistency between sizes `n` and `n+1`. Combining it with Friedgut's sharpness can turn a fixed positive satisfiability probability into a statement that a density lies within the vanishing threshold window. What it **cannot** do is sum those local errors over all sizes: §3's `sin(log n)` obstruction shows why. Therefore a source-closing `k=3` proof via this route needs one of the following genuinely stronger statements:

1. **mesoscopic cavity stability:** compare threshold locations between `n` and `(1+epsilon)n` with an error that contracts uniformly as `epsilon` varies;
2. **solution-set survival:** show that near the threshold the union over many satisfying assignments boosts the one-step extension probability from the single-assignment factor `A_3(c)` to `1-o(1)` in a quantitatively summable way;
3. **a monotone/invariant potential across cavity steps:** a quantity tied to threshold location whose total variation is finite, ruling out logarithmically slow oscillation.

This is more precise than “prove concentration” or “improve a coupling”: it says exactly what type of statement must defeat the explicit slowly-oscillating-sequence obstruction.

Outcome #27: the all-`k` source has been reduced operationally to a concrete first unresolved metric and a mesoscopic stability obligation. No source closure is claimed.
