## 2. Method #5 — ADD A PARAMETER: balanced relation support sizes `(s,t)`

The run18 2-plane lower bound used only planes generated by sparse projective null lines of support three or four. Instead of adding another isolated local type, parameterize a relation line by its support size.

For `s>=3`, call a projective line **balanced of support s** if, on its support, a representative has coefficients `+1,-1` with the two multiplicities as equal as possible. Modulo global sign, the number of balanced lines on one fixed support is
\[
L_s=
\begin{cases}
{ s\choose \lfloor s/2\rfloor},&s\text{ odd},\\[1mm]
\frac12 {s\choose s/2},&s\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.1}
\]
For an odd prime with no modular wrap at this support scale, one such equation cuts the Boolean cube in exactly
\[
{ s\choose\lfloor s/2\rfloor}
\tag{2.2}
\]
patterns on its support; (2.2) is just Vandermonde after choosing equally many selected `+` and `-` coordinates. The exact checker verifies (2.2) for `3<=s<=12`.

Now choose disjoint supports `S,T` of sizes
\[
3\le s\le t,\qquad s+t\le m,
\]
and one balanced line `v` on `S` and `w` on `T`. Let
\[
V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_p}\{v,w\}.
\]
The disjoint-support condition is the key structural gain.

## 3. Method #12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: the projective support spectrum

Every nonzero vector in `V` has one of exactly three support types:

- `alpha v`, support `s`;
- `beta w`, support `t`;
- `alpha v+beta w` with both coefficients nonzero, support exactly `s+t`.

There is no cancellation between disjoint supports. Thus the multiset of projective support sizes in `P(V)` is
\[
\boxed{\{s,t,\underbrace{s+t,\ldots,s+t}_{p-1\text{ times}}\}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
This conserved spectrum solves two bookkeeping problems without inclusion--exclusion across plane families.

First, it makes the generating pair intrinsic: the only two projective lines in `V` whose support is smaller than `s+t` are precisely the two generators. If `s=t`, they are the unique two support-`s` lines and are recovered unordered. Hence distinct choices below do not accidentally generate the same plane.

Second, if
\[
\max(s,t)\ge5,
\tag{3.2}
\]
then `V` has at most one projective line of support at most four. Every old run18 plane was generated by **two distinct** support-three/four lines and therefore contains at least two such projective lines. So every plane satisfying (3.2) is automatically disjoint from the entire previously credited run18 family.

This is the decisive proof device in the new metric improvement.

The number of distinct planes of type `(s,t)` is therefore exactly
\[
\boxed{
N_{m;s,t}=
\begin{cases}
{m\choose s}{m-s\choose t}L_sL_t,&s<t,\\[1mm]
\frac12 {m\choose s}{m-s\choose s}L_s^2,&s=t.
\end{cases}}
\tag{3.3}
\]
The factor `1/2` occurs only when the two support sizes are equal, because then choosing the two disjoint support/line pairs in the opposite order is the same plane.

## 4. Method #8 — DISCRETIZE: count collision-free matrices plane-first

Fix one such plane `V` and define
\[
S_V=(V^\perp\cap\{0,1\}^m)\setminus\{0\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Choosing `m` ordered distinct vectors from `S_V` as columns automatically gives nonzero, pairwise-distinct columns and ensures that `V` lies in the left nullspace.

To enforce the row part of `\mathcal G_m`, let
\[
H_i=\{x:x_i=0\},\qquad
H_{ij}=\{x:x_i=x_j\}.
\]
The event that row `i` is zero means every selected column lies in `H_i`; the event that rows `i,j` coincide means every selected column lies in `H_{ij}`. Therefore the union bound gives the rigorous lower count
\[
\boxed{
g(V)=
(|S_V|)_m
-\sum_i(|S_V\cap H_i|)_m
-\sum_{i<j}(|S_V\cap H_{ij}|)_m.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
When negative, replace it by zero. No independence assumption is used.

If a matrix has modular left-nullity `d`, then it contributes one incidence for every 2-plane in its nullspace, i.e. `{d\brack2}_p`. Thus counting `(A,V)` pairs plane-first is **exactly the correct multiplicity** for `M_2`; a matrix may occur for several planes without causing an overcount error.

Permutation symmetry makes `g(V)` depend only on `(m,p,s,t)` for the canonical balanced disjoint pair. Combining (3.3) and (4.2) gives the all-dimension new lower-bound family
\[
\boxed{
\Delta M_{2,p,m}
\ge
\sum_{\substack{3\le s\le t,\ s+t\le m\\\max(s,t)\ge5}}
N_{m;s,t}\,[g_{p,m;s,t}]_+.
}
\tag{C258}
\]

## 7. What the three new methods taught us

### #5 ADD A PARAMETER — succeeded locally, exposes a hierarchy

The support pair `(s,t)` organizes a whole family of structured modular null planes. ### #12 FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED — succeeded and was essential

The projective support spectrum (3.1) is the invariant that makes the new family safe to add. Without it, a larger `M_2` number could silently double-count planes already credited in run18. The spectrum proves both uniqueness of the generating pair and disjointness from the old support-three/four family.

### #8 DISCRETIZE — succeeded quantitatively, but does not solve the asymptotic

The exact `m=9,10` catalogs moved the fixed remainder. However, adding ever more fixed sparse-support types is not a plausible route by itself to `r(n)=o(n^2)`: the dominant unresolved issue is still the mass of real-nonsingular matrices that are singular modulo the chosen prime.

The next useful step is therefore not another one-off support classification. It is to **sum the `(s,t)` hierarchy asymptotically and couple it to a growing modulus**. The plausible split is:

1. sparse/high-concentration modular normals, controlled by support/entropy or inverse Littlewood--Offord structure and paid by higher Grassmann incidences;
2. dense modular normals, for which a growing prime should suppress accidental determinant divisibility.

What is missing is a collision-conditioned determinant-residue or inverse-Littlewood--Offord theorem strong enough to show that the second class is `o(n^2 2^{-n})` after the exact trivial collisions are removed. Existing exponential-error finite-field universality is not automatically at the required `2^{-n}` prefactor scale.

Write `d^G=d^H+r`, where `H` is a connected spanning subgraph of `G`, `q=|E(G)\setminus E(H)|`, and therefore
\[
\sum_v r_v=2q.
\]
Rayleigh monotonicity gives `R_G(u,v)<=R_H(u,v)`, and for connected unweighted `H`, `R_H(u,v)<=n-1`. In the multiplicative degree--Kirchhoff formula
\[
K(G)=\frac1{2m_G}\sum_{u<v}d^G_ud^G_vR_G(u,v),
\]
the `d^H d^H` term is exactly `2m_H K(H)`. The two cross terms are bounded by
\[
(n-1)(2q)(2m_H)=4qm_H(n-1),
\]
and the `rr` term by
\[
(n-1)\sum_{u<v}r_ur_v\le2q^2(n-1).
\]
Division by `2m_G` gives exactly R04-C4.1 and then C4.2.
