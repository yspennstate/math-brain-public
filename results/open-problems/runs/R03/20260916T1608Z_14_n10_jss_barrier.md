## 4. R03-C240 — sign/binary normalization and a fully certified n=10 remainder

**Status: PROVED, self-contained. Metric-moving finite result.**

### 4.1 Exact normalization identity

Let `B` be an `n x n` sign matrix. Define

\[
N_{ij}=B_{ij}B_{i1}B_{1j}B_{11}.
\]

Then `N` is obtained from `B` by multiplying rows and columns by signs, so singularity is unchanged, and the first row and first column of `N` are all `+1`.

Write the lower-right `(n-1)x(n-1)` block of `N` as `C` and set

\[
A=\frac{1-C}{2}\in\{0,1\}^{(n-1)\times(n-1)}.
\]

Subtract the first row of `N` from every other row. The first column becomes `(1,0,...,0)^T` and the lower-right block becomes `C-J=-2A`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\det N=(-2)^{n-1}\det A.}
\tag{C240.1}
\]

Every sign matrix has a unique normalized representative `N`, and each normalized representative has exactly `2^{2n-1}` sign preimages under row/column sign flips. Therefore the induced `A` is uniform on all binary `(n-1)x(n-1)` matrices and

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(B_n\text{ singular})
=
\Pr(A_{n-1}^{0/1}\text{ singular over }\mathbb R).
}
\tag{C240.2}
\]

The archived checker exhaustively verifies this identity for every sign matrix through order four, including the exact determinant relation (C240.1).

### 4.2 Finite-field lower certificate for real invertibility

If a binary matrix is invertible over `F_2`, its integer determinant is odd, hence nonzero over `R`. The number of invertible `m x m` matrices over `F_2` is

\[
|GL(m,2)|=\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(2^m-2^i).
\]

Combining with C240.2 gives, for every `n>=2`, the fully explicit source-independent bound

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(B_n\text{ singular})
\le
1-\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1-2^{-j}).
}
\tag{C240.3}
\]

For `n=10`, `m=9`, the exact checker computes

\[
|GL(9,2)|
=699612310033197642547200
\]

and hence

\[
\Pr(B_{10}\text{ singular})
\le
\frac{25003673060507}{35184372088832}.
\]

Therefore the frozen remainder metric has the executable-certified value

\[
\boxed{
b(10)
\le
\frac{18818920154267}{34359738368}
=547.7026615485956\ldots .
}
\tag{C240.4}
\]

This is deliberately not claimed sharp. It is, however, a complete exact-arithmetic proof of a previously unmeasured finite-domain value and therefore moves the fixed metric.

## 6. R03-X27 — naive diagonalization of JSS Theorem 3.8 does not reach polynomial prefactor

**Status: DISPROVED ROUTE, not a refutation of any JSS theorem.**

For Rademacher entries, `H(p)=log 2`. JSS Theorem 3.8 bounds the unstructured contribution by

\[
\exp((\epsilon-\log2)n)
\]

for **each fixed** `epsilon>0`, with constants and the threshold `n_0` depending on `epsilon`.
After multiplying by `2^n`, this leaves

\[
e^{\epsilon n}.
\]

For that term itself to be `o(n^2)`, one would need a sequence `epsilon_n` satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_n n-2\log n\to-\infty.
}
\tag{X27.1}
\]

For example `epsilon_n=(log n)/n` would be enough at the level of the displayed error.

But substituting such an `epsilon_n` into the published fixed-`epsilon` theorem is a quantifier error. More concretely, the actual proof of JSS Lemma 2.10 chooses `lambda=lambda(xi,epsilon)` and then enforces

\[
\gamma<\lambda^2.
\tag{X27.2}
\]

Its equation (2.7) contains the factor

\[
(e/\lambda)^{2k\lambda\ell}.
\]

For Rademacher `k=2`, and the lemma has

\[
\ell\ge(1-\gamma)n>\frac89n
\]

because `lambda<1/3` and `gamma<lambda^2`. To make this factor at most `e^{\epsilon n/10}`, as the published proof explicitly does, one must have

\[
4\lambda\ell\log(e/\lambda)
\le\frac{\epsilon n}{10},
\]

so in particular

\[
\lambda\log(e/\lambda)
\le\frac{9\epsilon}{320}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda\le\frac{9\epsilon}{320}.
\tag{X27.3}
\]

Consequently the proof's guaranteed robust-step fraction obeys

\[
\gamma n
<\lambda^2n
\le
\frac{81}{102400}\epsilon^2n.
\tag{X27.4}
\]

At the polynomial-prefactor scale `epsilon_n=A log n/n`, the right-hand side is

\[
O\!\left(\frac{\log^2n}{n}\right)\to0.
\]

Thus the **literal parameter-selection mechanism used in the published proof loses its linear robust-step reservoir completely before the entropy slack reaches polynomial scale**. This explains precisely why Theorem 3.8 cannot simply be diagonalized by writing `epsilon=epsilon_n`.

This does not rule out a new quantitative proof. It identifies the part that must be changed.

## 7. R03-C241 — polynomial-slack preprocessing lemma in the balanced Rademacher slice

**Status: PROVED intermediate lemma. This is not the source problem and does not count as closure.**

The preceding obstruction is partly an artifact of forcing JSS Lemma 2.10 into a fixed-`epsilon` linear-step format. Re-running its product argument at polynomial entropy slack gives a nontrivial exact replacement.

### Statement

Assume `n>=4` is even and work in the exact balanced Rademacher multislice

\[
s_+=s_-=n/2.
\]

Use the same averaging-sequence and drop definitions as JSS Lemma 2.10, but set

\[
\lambda=\frac1n,
\qquad
\Gamma=\frac1{2n^2},
\qquad
R=\Gamma L.
\]

Suppose

\[
N\le2^n n^{-A}
\]

and for some starting point

\[
f_{\mathcal A,s,n}(t)\ge\frac{L}{N\sqrt n},
\qquad L\ge1.
\]

Let `J` be the set of `lambda`-robust steps which are not `R`-drops. Then

\[
\boxed{
|J|
\ge
A-\frac{11}{2}
-
\frac{\log(6e^{9/2})}{\log n}.
}
\tag{C241.1}
\]

Since `|J|` is an integer, the ceiling of the positive part may be taken on the right.

### Proof

Repeat JSS equations (2.2)--(2.6); these identities do not require `epsilon` to be fixed. With `k=2`, `ell=n`, the exact-balanced multinomial coefficient, and the choices above, their product inequality becomes

\[
\frac{L}{N\sqrt n}
\le
(1+\Gamma/\lambda)^n\lambda^{-|J|}
\binom{n}{n/2}^{-1}
(1-\lambda)^{-n}
(e/\lambda)^{4\lambda n}.
\tag{C241.2}
\]

Now use, for `n>=4`,

\[
(1+\Gamma/\lambda)^n
=
(1+1/(2n))^n\le e^{1/2},
\]

\[
\lambda^{-|J|}=n^{|J|},
\]

\[
\binom{n}{n/2}^{-1}
\le(n+1)2^{-n},
\]

\[
(1-1/n)^{-n}\le4,
\]

and

\[
(e/\lambda)^{4\lambda n}=(en)^4.
\]

The assumption `N<=2^n n^{-A}` gives

\[
\frac{L}{N\sqrt n}
\ge L n^{A-1/2}2^{-n}.
\]

Cancel `2^{-n}` in (C241.2), use `n+1<=3n/2`, and obtain

\[
L n^{A-1/2}
\le
6e^{9/2}n^{5+|J|}.
\]

Since `L>=1`,

\[
n^{A-11/2-|J|}\le6e^{9/2},
\]

which is exactly (C241.1). QED.

### Meaning and limitation

This shows that the entropy-product stage itself **can** be run at polynomial slack: `N=2^n/n^A` forces a controlled number of robust non-drops without any fixed positive `epsilon`.

It does **not** yet give the required unstructured matrix bound. The next JSS ingredient, Lemma 2.6 / equation (2.8), has a constant

\[
C_{2.6}(\lambda/2,\ldots)
\]

whose dependence was only needed for fixed `lambda` in the published theorem. With `lambda=1/n`, that dependence must be quantified, and the later decrement/subsampling step must be re-run with only `O(A)` guaranteed non-drops rather than `Theta(n)` of them.

So C241 is a genuine partial repair of X27, but the source conjecture remains OPEN.

### Covariance split

R04 partitions the circular band into consecutive blocks of sizes `m_j<=W+1` and sets

\[
a_j^2=\frac{m_j}{2(2W+1)}.
\]

A standard GOE block of size `m_j` has diagonal variance `2/m_j` and off-diagonal variance `1/m_j`. Hence `a_j V_j` contributes exactly

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\text{diag})=(2W+1)^{-1}=v,
\]

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\text{within-block offdiag})=\frac v2.
\]

The target band covariance has diagonal variance `v` and band off-diagonal variance `v`, so the coordinatewise residual variances are `0` on block diagonals, `v/2` on within-block off-diagonals, `v` on inter-block band coordinates, and `0` outside the band. They are nonnegative and supported on independent Gaussian upper-triangular coordinates. Thus the distributional Gaussian split in R04-C4 is correct.

### Uniform envelope

Conditional on C3, the relevant quantity is

\[
\Lambda_{N,W}
=
\frac{\sqrt{2(2W+1)}}{N}\sum_j\sqrt{m_j}.
\]

In the non-complete case put `s=W+1`, write

\[
N=s(k+x),\qquad k\ge2,\quad0\le x<1,
\]

and `y=sqrt(x)`. Direct algebra gives

\[
\Lambda_{N,W}
=
\sqrt{4-2/s}\,\frac{k+y}{k+y^2}.
\]

For fixed `y in [0,1]`, `(k+y)/(k+y^2)` is decreasing in `k`, hence the maximum is at `k=2`. Also `sqrt(4-2/s)<2`, so

\[
\Lambda_{N,W}
\le2\frac{2+y}{2+y^2}.
\]

The derivative numerator is `2-4y-y^2`, whose root in `[0,1]` is `y=sqrt(6)-2`. Substitution gives

\[
\max\Lambda_{N,W}
\le1+\frac{\sqrt6}{2}.
\]

The complete case gives `Lambda=sqrt(2)`, which is smaller. Thus R04-C4's improved envelope is correct, conditional on C3. **Audit PASS at this explicit scope.**
