## 3. Common exact-nullity-two completion lemma

Work over `F_11` with `m=10`. A projective sign-relation line of support `s` is represented by a nonzero vector whose nonzero coordinates are all `+/-1`, normalized so its first nonzero coordinate is `+1`.

For a two-plane `V <= F_11^{10}`, put

\[

\]

All planes admitted below satisfy:

1. `V` contains no projective vector of support `1` or `2`;
2. no sign-line pair in `V` lies in any previously corrected sparse-plane family;
3. their generator union has exactly seven coordinates.

Let `U` be the seven-coordinate union. The remaining three Boolean coordinates are free. Write

\[
H_V=V_U^\perp\cap\{0,1\}^{7},
\qquad h_{\rm loc}=|H_V|.
\]

If `H_V` spans the five-dimensional local factor `V_U^perp`, define `q_V` to be the largest intersection of `H_V` with a proper projective hyperplane in that factor. Then globally

\[
h=|C_V|=8h_{\rm loc},
\qquad
Q=\max(8q_V,h/2).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed a proper global hyperplane either has nonzero local restriction, in which case it captures at most `8q_V` points, or has zero local restriction and cuts the free three-cube in at most half its points.

Choose the first eight columns greedily to form an ordered basis of `V^perp`. After `j` independent columns have been chosen, their span contains at most `2^j` Boolean points and, because it is still proper, at most `Q` points of `C_V`. Hence the number of choices is bounded below by

\[
\boxed{
B(V)=\prod_{j=0}^{7}\bigl(h-\min(2^j,Q)\bigr).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The ninth column can then be chosen nonzero and different from the eight basis columns in at least `h-9` ways; the tenth in at least `h-10` ways. Thus `V` certifies at least

\[
\boxed{
E(V)=B(V)(h-9)(h-10)

\tag{3.3}
\]

ordered binary matrices.

Every such matrix lies in the retained collision-free binary class `G_10`: the ten columns are nonzero and pairwise distinct by construction, while a zero row would put a support-one vector in the left null plane and two equal rows would put a support-two vector there, both excluded. Its columns span `V^perp`, so its left modular nullspace is **exactly** `V`; different planes therefore yield disjoint matrix sets.
