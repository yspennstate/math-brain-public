### L. Bilinear Schur-core formulation

Fix a nonzero cofactor and write

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}C&a\\ b^T&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad C\in\{0,1\}^{(d-1)\times(d-1)}\text{ invertible}.

\[

## 5. (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

A source-closing proof now needs a **counting theorem, not merely an anticoncentration theorem**.

The missing object can be stated at the Schur-core level. Set `m=d-1`. For each invertible binary core `C in {0,1}^{m x m}`, let

\[
I_\circ(C)
:=\#\Big\{(a,b)\in\{0,1\}^m\times\{0,1\}^m:
 b^TC^{-1}a\in\{0,1\},
\text{ and the induced left/right null profiles satisfy the retained source filters}
\Big\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the maximally generic special case, the filters require `(-C^{-1}a,1)` and `(-C^{-T}b,1)` to be full and to have pairwise-distinct absolute coordinates, together with the pivot/collision deletions.

The source-scale task is an aggregate estimate of the shape

\[
\sum_C I_\circ(C)
=o\!\left(d^2 2^{d^2-d-1}\right),
\tag{5.2}
\]

not a uniform pointwise claim that every `C` has tiny incidence. There are about `2^{m^2}` possible cores, while each core has `2^{2m}` possible `(a,b)` pairs. Consequently the required average incidence density is on the scale

\[
\operatorname{poly}(d)2^{-d},
\tag{5.3}
\]

which explains why polynomial Littlewood--Offord estimates stop far too early.

What no R03 route currently supplies is a tail estimate for the **number of binary invertible cores** supporting unusually large `I_\circ(C)`, all the way down to the exponentially small scale (5.3). The needed theorem would trade incidence richness for scarcity of cores. It must use the special algebraic fact that the bilinear coefficient matrix is an **inverse of a binary invertible matrix**, plus simultaneous constraints on `C^{-1}a` and `C^{-T}b`; a theorem for an arbitrary bilinear coefficient matrix is too weak.

This is the missing matrix-level entropy source.

## 7. (e) Nearest solved problems and what they have that this endpoint lacks

There are two relevant notions of “nearest”; both point to the same missing resource.

### Source-nearest accepted theorem: Jain--Sah--Sawhney

For non-uniform finite-support entry laws, JSS proves the full elementary-mechanism asymptotic with exponentially small relative error in the nontrivial sector. Its bulk proof has a strict distributional entropy/norm gap. At uniform Rademacher that gap is exactly absent. R03's deformation route already confirmed that simply sending a bias to zero does not preserve the needed margin.

**Resource present there and absent here:** an external entropy gap supplied by the one-entry law.

### Mechanism-nearest solved framework: bilinear/counting inverse Littlewood--Offord

Costello's bilinear Littlewood--Offord theory classifies forms whose atom probability is substantially above the generic polynomial scale as close to degenerate; see arXiv:0902.1538. Ferber--Jain--Luh--Samotij's counting inverse-LO framework, arXiv:1904.10425, replaces pointwise structure by counting exceptional coefficient vectors and has been used to derive exponential-type singularity bounds in other discrete matrix models.

**Resource present there and absent here:** a concentration threshold high enough to trigger a usable structural/counting classification. R03 needs an analogous counting statement down at the exponentially small density `poly(d)2^{-d}` and specifically over inverse binary cores `C^{-1}`.

That scale mismatch is why another application of ordinary bilinear LO is not the next action. The only plausible port is the **counting philosophy**: stratify cores by incidence density and show that richer cores are exponentially rarer.
