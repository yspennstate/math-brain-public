## 3. Method #10 DERANDOMIZE — a deterministic exact-corank-two family

Take two disjoint three-coordinate supports. On each support choose a balanced projective relation line of type
\[
(1,1,-1).
\]
Let `v,w in F_p^m` be the two chosen relation vectors and put
\[
V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_p}\{v,w\}.
\]
The supports are disjoint, so the `p+1` projective lines in `V` have support spectrum
\[
\boxed{\{3,3,\underbrace{6,\ldots,6}_{p-1\text{ times}}\}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
In particular `V` contains no nonzero vector of support one or two, and its two support-three projective lines are intrinsic. Therefore distinct unordered pairs of disjoint support-three relation lines give distinct planes.

On a fixed three-set there are exactly three projective `(1,1,-1)` relation lines, according to which coordinate has the exceptional sign. Hence the number of distinct planes of this type is
\[
\boxed{
N_m=\frac92\binom m3\binom{m-3}3.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let
\[
C_V:=V^\perp\cap\{0,1\}^m.
\]
For one balanced support-three relation, exactly three of the eight Boolean assignments solve the relation. Since the two relations have disjoint supports,
\[
\boxed{|C_V|=h_m:=9\,2^{m-6}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The goal is not merely to choose columns in `C_V`; it is to force the selected columns to **span all of** `V^perp`. That makes the left nullspace exactly `V`, hence modular corank exactly two.

## 4. Method #13 FIND WHAT ONLY INCREASES — greedy rank growth gives a product certificate

The monovariant is the dimension of the span of the columns already selected.

Use the elementary cube/subspace lemma retained from run23: if `W <= F_p^m` has dimension `j`, then
\[
|W\cap\{0,1\}^m|\le2^j.
\tag{4.1}
\]
This follows by projecting injectively to `j` pivot coordinates.

Since `dim V^perp=m-2`, choose its first `m-2` matrix columns greedily from `C_V`, always increasing span dimension. After `j` independent columns have been chosen, their `j`-dimensional span contains at most `2^j` Boolean points. Therefore the next column has at least
\[
h_m-2^j
\]
choices. Consequently the number of possible ordered Boolean bases in the first `m-2` column slots is at least
\[
\boxed{
B_m:=\prod_{j=0}^{m-3}(h_m-2^j).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

After fixing such a basis, there remain at least
\[
h_m-1-(m-2)=h_m-m+1
\]
Boolean vectors in `C_V` that are nonzero and not among the chosen basis columns. Choose the `(m-1)`st column from those, then the final column from the remaining `h_m-m` vectors. Thus every fixed plane `V` generates at least
\[
\boxed{
B_m(h_m-m+1)(h_m-m)
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
ordered binary matrices.

Every matrix generated by (4.2)--(4.3) lies in `G_m`. Indeed its columns span `V^perp`. If row `i` were zero, then `e_i` would annihilate the full column span and hence belong to `V`, contradicting (3.1), which has no support-one line. If rows `i,j` were equal, then `e_i-e_j in V`, contradicting the absence of support-two vectors in (3.1). The columns themselves are nonzero and pairwise distinct by construction.

Moreover its left nullspace over `F_p` is **exactly** `V`, because its columns span `V^perp`. Hence `d_p(A)=2` exactly.

For a fixed `V`, the ordered matrix columns uniquely determine the choices in the construction; there is no multiplicity. Across different `V`, a matrix cannot be counted twice because its exact two-dimensional left nullspace is unique.
