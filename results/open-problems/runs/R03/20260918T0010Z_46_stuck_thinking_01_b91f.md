## 4. Method #22 — READ THE FAILURES

The literature failures line up with the same obstruction rather than supplying a hidden black box.

**Carenini TR26-145.** The new `O(n/log n)` window is exactly the strongest all-fixed-`k` result found in the fresh check. Its proof architecture is sharp-threshold booster extraction followed by local-to-random replacement. Runs44--45 were already attacking the two places where an extra logarithm could plausibly appear. C302 says that merely making the replacement subexponential is insufficient if the booster strength itself remains `e^{-Theta(K)}` and the last layer comparison remains universal.

**Abbe--Montanari concentration.** Their 2014 theorem assumes an unsatisfiability probability `O(1/log(n)^{1+epsilon})`. This makes clear why one additional positive power of `log n` is qualitatively different from the present `O(n/log n)` window. Their interpolation gives concentration once the probability input is strong enough; it does not manufacture that stronger small-`k` probability input.

**Ding--Sly--Sun.** The nearest solved version of the same source is sufficiently-large-`k` random k-SAT. Their method does not prove a generic local-to-global Fourier transfer. Instead it changes the random variable used in the moment argument and reduces the resulting optimization to tree recursions. The 2026 CPC paper explicitly emphasizes that largeness of `k` is used critically in the large-`k` line to obtain concentration and expansion properties. At `k=3` there is no corresponding small parameter available in the current proof technology.

**Random 2-SAT.** The exact threshold is tied to the giant-component transition in the implication digraph. That graph-theoretic order parameter is a genuine global invariant whose critical behavior is tractable. Random 3-SAT has no known analogous one-dimensional percolation order parameter at its satisfiability transition. Porting the 2-SAT proof therefore fails at the category level, not because of a missing constant.

Outcome of #22: the present obstruction is not an overlooked standard theorem. The missing ingredient must either (i) create a new `k=3` order parameter that charges mesoscopic solution correlation, or (ii) reweight the solution/booster measure so that the correlation becomes visible to an existing local energy.

## 5. Method #21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE

The closest *methodological* match found in the fresh search is Carenini's 30 August 2026 **ranked spreadness** paper. It studies exactly the shape “a distribution on small local witnesses must be exposed by a random sample”, and its ranked-spread condition removes dependence on the maximum witness width. This is relevant because runs44--45 also produce a distribution of essential local booster witnesses and then need to replace a structured witness by random native information.

But the theorem's quantitative dependence exposes a new obstruction rather than solving ours. In the one-sided testing application, average query size `d` and rejection gap `delta` require an integer parameter

\[
c>d/\delta.
\]

For an R03 essential booster, the only currently guaranteed strength after extraction is of the form

\[
\delta\asymp e^{-O(K)},\qquad d=O(K).
\]

A literal black-box substitution therefore asks for `c` exponential in `K`, so the associated sampling exponent is useless for creating a `K >> log n` threshold argument. Ranked spreadness removes **width** dependence, but not the exponentially small **event-mass** dependence that is fatal here.

This comparison is useful because it isolates what a successful port would have to change: normalize *inside the conditioned booster event* before applying a spread/kernel theorem, and only then decondition. If the global factor `beta_X(B)` is paid before kernel extraction, nothing is gained. I do not claim that this conditioning/deconditioning maneuver works; testing exactly whether it can avoid reintroducing `beta` is a concrete future route.

Outcome of #21: the closest new local-to-random technology has the right geometry but the wrong dependence on the rare booster mass. That is a precise parameter mismatch, not a vague lack of machinery.

## 6. Method #5 — ADD A PARAMETER

C306 shows that the obstruction can be organized by Fourier level. For a satisfiable formula `F`, write

\[
E_j(F)=\sum_{|T|=j}\widehat f_F(T)^2,
\qquad f_F=2^n\mu_F.
\]

Introduce the continuous noise-scale profile

\[
\Psi_F(\rho)=\sum_{j\ge1}E_j(F)\rho^j,\qquad 0\le\rho\le1.
\]

This is only a diagnostic parameterization in the present report; no new inequality is claimed. The exact discrete statistic from C306 is

\[
\mathcal C_r(F)=
\sum_{j=1}^r
\frac{\binom rj}{\binom nj}E_j(F).
\]

Thus run43 controls the first three coefficients through `C_3`, while run45 says a booster can force a nontrivial value of the hypergeometric transform at a growing `r`. The generic obstruction is now transparent: a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients may be tiny in its first three coefficients and still become large because of coefficients at degree `j>3`.

What a successful 3-CNF argument would need is a **dynamical restriction on the growth of this profile under clause exposure**, not merely another endpoint estimate. A representative form would be a differential or multiscale inequality preventing `Psi_F` from accumulating `e^{-O(r)}` mass at mesoscopic degrees without paying detectable bounded-degree energy. No such inequality was located in the current literature, and standard hypercontractivity runs in the wrong logical direction for this purpose unless one first proves an additional “globality” or CNF-specific structural hypothesis.

Outcome of #5: the blocker can be viewed as a missing scale-propagation law for the Fourier/noise profile. This gives the next run an object that varies continuously with scale rather than another isolated lemma at one value of `r`.

## 7. (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

2. its loss is `e^{o(r)}` (or stronger) on the relevant rare booster event, rather than `e^{-Theta(r)}` or `n^{-Theta(r)}`;

The most plausible missing object is therefore a **multiscale potential** attached to the conditioned near-critical solution set, whose clause-exposure drift charges the full Fourier/noise profile rather than only levels 1--3. ## 8. (d) Simplest special case still unproved

The first collision-rich booster class not covered by C301 has a particularly simple factor graph. Take an essential clean 3-CNF booster `B={C_1,...,C_t}` whose clause-intersection graph is a tree, where every new clause shares exactly one variable with the previous union, contributes two new variables, and no variable belongs to more than two clauses. Then

\[
|\operatorname{supp}B|=2t+1,
\qquad
q(B)=3t-(2t+1)=t-1.
\]

So it is **minimally collision-rich**: C301's orbit condition already fails by a factor of roughly `n^{-(t-1)}`, but there are no dense cycles, repeated high-degree variables, or complicated collision cores to blame.

The simplest still-unproved R03 special case is:

If even this tree-like class cannot be controlled, a general collision-rich booster theorem is not currently realistic. Conversely, a proof here would reveal whether the missing mesoscopic-to-local mechanism is already present on factor trees or truly requires cyclic cores.
