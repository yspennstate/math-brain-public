## 2. Source normalization and current primary literature

The outside source remains Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, arXiv:2005.02797 / *Probability Surveys* 18 (2021), Section 2, Conjecture 2.2. For an `n x n` iid uniform sign matrix,

\[
p_n=(2+o(1))n^2 2^{-n}.
\]

The elementary equal/opposite row-or-column lower bound is

\[
p_n\ge (4-o(1)){n\choose2}2^{-n},
\]

so the retained target

\[
\boxed{\Pr(\det B_n=0)=(1+o(1))n(n-1)2^{1-n}}
\]

is exactly equivalent at the conjectured precision.

Let `T_n` be the exact union of all equal/opposite signed row-pair and column-pair events. The frozen R03 remainder metric is

\[
\boxed{r(n):=2^n\Pr(\det B_n=0,T_n^c),}
\]

and source closure is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{r(n)=o(n^2).}
\]

Primary-source check on 2026-09-18:

1. Jain--Sah--Sawhney, arXiv:2010.06554 / GAFA 2021, proves the sharp elementary-mechanism asymptotic for finite-support laws not uniform on their support. For the uniform-support endpoint, including iid Rademacher signs, it retains only exponential-scale precision.
2. Tikhomirov, *Annals of Mathematics* 191 (2020), gives the logarithmic/exponential-rate result `(1/2+o(1))^n`, not the polynomial prefactor.
3. Song--Wang, arXiv:2607.00495v2 (16 Jul 2026), obtains sharp exponential growing-corank asymptotics in the `{0,1}` Bernoulli model for `k=o(sqrt(log n))`; it does not supply the Rademacher `k=1` polynomial prefactor studied here.

\[
\boxed{q_V\le h_{\rm loc}-1.}
\tag{C328}

## 7. Concrete obstruction in the 2025 Blinovsky preprint

Because arXiv:1511.05283v7 now explicitly claims the full theorem and a second asymptotic term, current-source verification required checking the displayed proof rather than simply repeating the older literature status.

In its dense `R_3` reduction, equation (22) displays, for every tail sign pattern `b`, an inequality of the form

\[
\rho(\bar v_{b,r})\le \rho(v),
\qquad
\rho(a):=\Pr_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^{d}}(\varepsilon\cdot a=0),
\tag{7.1}
\]

where the construction folds tail coefficients into one retained coefficient.

As displayed, this universal first inequality is false. Take

\[
v=(1,1,2).
\]

Then exact enumeration gives

\[
\rho(1,1,2)=\frac{2}{8}=\frac14.
\]

Folding the tail coefficient `2` into the second coefficient with sign `b=-1` gives `(1,1-2)=(1,-1)`, for which

\[
\rho(1,-1)=\frac24=\frac12>\frac14.
\]

The checker archives this exact eight-state / four-state counterexample. The conceptual reason is that folding corresponds to conditioning on a tail sign pattern; a conditional concentration may exceed the unconditional average. A correct identity/inequality must control an **average or moment over `b`**, not every fiber pointwise.

This does **not** prove Blinovsky's theorem false. It does mean the displayed proof cannot be used as an accepted closure certificate without repairing this step or showing that an unstated restriction rules out the counterexample. Therefore R03 remains **PARTIAL / source not established as closed**.
