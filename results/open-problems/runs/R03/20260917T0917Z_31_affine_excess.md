## 3. Method #14 — quotient the row-sign gauge

Run30 left the source-level object in the basis-weighted two-level section form. Write

\[
d=n-1,
\qquad
S(w):=\{a\in\{\pm1\}^d:\langle a,w\rangle\in\{\pm1\}\},
\qquad N(w):=|S(w)|.
\]

For an invertible sign matrix `A` and a sign vector `b`, put

\[
w=A^{-1}b.
\]

The rowwise sign group `\{\pm1\}^d` acts by simultaneously multiplying row `i` of `A` and coordinate `i` of `b`. Quotient it explicitly. Let

\[
D_b:=\operatorname{diag}(b),
\qquad
R:=D_bA.
\]

Then `R` is again an invertible sign matrix and

\[
Rw=D_bAw=D_bb=\mathbf1,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
w=R^{-1}\mathbf1.
\]

Conversely, for every invertible sign matrix `R` and every `b\in\{\pm1\}^d`, the pair

\[
A=D_bR,\qquad b
\]

maps back to the same `R` and the same `w=R^{-1}\mathbf1`. Thus the gauge map `(A,b)\mapsto R` is **exactly `2^d`-to-one**.

Define the one-sided affine cube section

\[
C_R:=\{x\in\{\pm1\}^d:\langle x,w_R\rangle=1\},
\qquad
w_R:=R^{-1}\mathbf1,
\qquad
M_R:=|C_R|.
\]

The rows of `R` are `d` linearly independent points of `C_R`, so `M_R\ge d`. Also

\[
S(w_R)=C_R\sqcup(-C_R),
\qquad
N(w_R)=2M_R.
\]

This removes an artificial two-sided symmetry from C283: the basis-weighted law `\mu_d` is exactly the law of `w_R=R^{-1}\mathbf1` when `R` is uniform over invertible `d\times d` sign matrices. Equivalently, `C_R` is the unique affine hyperplane section of the sign cube through the `d` random basis rows of `R`.

This is not merely a change of notation. The two trivial mechanisms become geometric coordinates:

1. **row collision:** the `d` forced section points are precisely the rows of `R`; deleting signed row collisions is exactly the subtraction `M_R-d`;
2. **column collision with the distinguished column:**
   \[
   w_R=\pm e_j
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \text{column }j\text{ of }R\text{ is identically }\pm1.
   \]
   This is exactly a collision of column `j` with the deterministic all-ones column after sign normalization.

\[
N(w_R)-2d=2(M_R-d).

\[
\bar q_n
=
\frac{I_d\,2^{d+1}}{2^{d^2+2d}}
\mathbb E_R[(M_R-d)\mathbf1_{\rm admissible}]
=
 p_d^{\rm inv}2^{1-d}
\mathbb E_R[(M_R-d)\mathbf1_{\rm admissible}],

## 6. Method #20 — why the obvious hyperplane-counting shortcut still fails

The affine formulation led directly to older threshold-function literature that is closer to the obstruction than the generic inverse-LO references.

Irmatov's 1996 paper *Estimates for the number of threshold functions* states that asymptotic saturation of the Schlaefli threshold-function upper bound is equivalent to the proportion tending to one of collections of `n-1` sign vectors whose span contains no other sign-cube points beyond their signed generators.

After the gauge normalization above, that is the same geometric phenomenon as the event

\[
M_R=d.
\]

But C285 shows that the Rademacher source requires a **mean-excess / uniform-integrability** statement, not merely a 0/1 typicality statement:

\[
\mathbb E[(M_R-d)\mathbf1_{\rm nontriv}]=o(n^2).
\]

A statement such as `P(M_R=d)->1` is not by itself enough, because `M_R` can be exponentially large on rare sections. The retained Hadamard-core obstruction from run29 provides explicit hyperplanes with section size of order `2^d/sqrt(d)`. Thus an argument that only proves that exceptional hyperplanes are rare, without paying their section size, can still miss the source scale.

This gives a concrete explanation for what any threshold-function route must add: **a weighted tail bound**, not another unweighted typical-section statement. I do not claim that this is the precise technical gap in Irmatov 2020; JSS only state that unresolved issues exist, including Theorem 3, and that a slight modification appears to give impossibly good error terms.

A second attempted import was Ferber--Jain--Luh--Samotij's counting inverse Littlewood--Offord theorem. Their paper states that its main counting theorem is effective down to concentration

\[
\exp(-c\sqrt{n\log n}).
\]

The affine source scale in C285 corresponds to sections having only polynomially many extra vertices, i.e. concentration of order

\[
\operatorname{poly}(n)2^{-n},
\]

which is exponentially smaller. Therefore FJLS is important machinery but cannot be applied as a black box at the final scale.
