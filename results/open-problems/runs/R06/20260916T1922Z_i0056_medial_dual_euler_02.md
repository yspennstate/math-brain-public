## 4. Method #12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: the full-face Euler budget

Take any finite nonempty vertex set

\[
S\subset V(T).
\]

For a lower-bound proof we may reduce to a connected component, since the edge-boundary ratio of a disconnected set is a size-weighted average of its component ratios. We may then fill every finite component of `T\setminus S`: filling a finite hole removes crossing edges and creates no new edge to the unbounded complement, while increasing `|S|`. Thus a lower bound for connected hole-filled sets implies the same lower bound for arbitrary finite sets.

Put

\[
n=|S|,\qquad B=|\partial_E^T S|,
\]

and let `e` be the number of edges of the induced plane graph `T[S]`.
Since `T` is 4-regular,

\[
4n=2e+B,
\qquad e=2n-B/2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For connected hole-filled `S`, every bounded face of the induced plane graph is one ambient square or one ambient pentagon of `T`. Indeed an unselected vertex inside a bounded face would be a finite complement component, and an omitted ambient edge joining selected endpoints would be present because the graph is induced.

Let `F_4,F_5` denote the numbers of full square and full pentagonal ambient faces. Euler's formula gives

\[
n-e+(F_4+F_5)=1.
\]
Using (4.1),

\[
\boxed{
F_4+F_5=n-B/2+1.
}
\tag{R06-I0056-MEDIAL-EULER-01}
\]

This is the conserved quantity missing from the previous local closure argument: **small medial edge boundary forces many completely filled ambient faces of the two types**.

## 7. Method #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: the ball upper bound matches the edge-homogeneous growth parameter

\[
