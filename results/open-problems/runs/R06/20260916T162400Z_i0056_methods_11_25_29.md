## 3. New-method attack #11 — DUALIZE

### Idea before calculation

Question 6.20 has two clauses. The first has both primal and dual regularity. The second only assumes primal transitivity, so the dual generally becomes quasi-transitive rather than regular. I tried to use planar duality to turn the remaining vertex-boundary optimization into a regular dual edge problem.

### What works in the co-regular case

Lyons--Peres' Section 6 duality argument works especially cleanly when both G and G^dagger are regular. Hole-filling lets one restrict the edge optimization to connected simply connected sets, Euler's formula couples the primal and dual boundary counts, and transitivity of the dual closes the extremal relation. This is precisely why the regular/co-regular clause is unusually rigid.

### Exact obstruction in the more-general transitive clause

If G is vertex-transitive but its face degrees have more than one orbit, then G^dagger is not regular. The regular-dual scalar optimization is replaced by an orbit-weighted problem. The identity used in the regular/co-regular proof no longer returns a single unweighted constant Phi_E or Phi_V without extra data.

I therefore did **not** obtain an exact mixed-face constant by dualizing. The useful reduction is structural: the obstruction is not planarity itself but dual irregularity. A plausible next formulation is a finite-orbit weighted dual Cheeger problem for a canonical quasi-transitive dual; that would have to be proved equivalent to the source normalization before it could move Question 6.20.

### New positive consequence obtained while dualizing

Oh's 2020 theorem supplies a sharp edge lower bound without requiring the dual to be regular. Combining that result with the previously proved closure theorem produces a source-relevant extension beyond the co-regular branch; see Section 6 (`R06-I0056-CLOSURE-02`).

**Verdict for method #11:** exact mixed-face constant not obtained; the dual route identifies dual irregularity as the missing datum, but Oh's primal edge lower bound salvages an exact equality Phi_V=Phi_E for a large mixed-face regime.

## 4. New-method attack #25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE

### Incidence identity

For a finite vertex set K in a d-regular graph, let

\[
r_x=|N(x)\cap K|\qquad(x\in\partial_VK).
\]

Then the same crossing incidences can be counted either by edges or by outer boundary vertices:

\[
|\partial_EK|=\sum_{x\in\partial_VK} r_x,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
|\partial_EK|-|\partial_VK|
=\sum_{x\in\partial_VK}(r_x-1).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This identity is exactly the `concavity mass' absorbed by the R06 multiplicity-two closure algorithm. It also explains why terminal closure sets satisfy equality of the two boundaries: all surviving outside vertices have r_x=1.

### Attempted curvature count

I next tried to replace Oh's edge inequality by a direct Euler/face-incidence count. If an induced planar region had girth at least 5, the standard planar estimate would give

\[
|E(K)|\le \frac53(|K|-2),
\]

and therefore in a d-regular ambient graph

\[
|\partial_EK|=d|K|-2|E(K)|
\ge \left(d-\frac{10}{3}\right)|K|+\frac{20}{3}.
\]

This misses the closure threshold `(d-3)|K|` by one third in the coefficient.

More importantly, **minimum face degree at least 5 in the ambient tessellation does not justify an ambient girth-at-least-5 assumption**: short separating cycles may surround several faces. The missing face incidences appear on the region's internal holes/separating boundary components. Thus a naive local Euler count silently drops exactly the boundary terms that the source's plane embedding allows.

For minimum face degree 6 and under additional no-short-separating-cycle hypotheses, the analogous elementary planar count reaches the threshold, but those added hypotheses are not part of Question 6.20. I therefore do not use this as a source theorem.

**Verdict for method #25:** the double count (4.1) cleanly identifies the edge/vertex gap, but bare finite-region curvature counting is too weak at face degree 5 and is not source-valid without controlling separating cycles. Oh's Gauss--Bonnet-strength edge inequality supplies precisely the missing global curvature information.

## 5. New-method attack #29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY

### Geometric interpretation of the previous closure theorem

The multiplicity-two closure can be viewed as filling every finite concavity visible from at least two incident boundary edges. Starting from K, repeatedly add any outside vertex with at least two neighbours in the current set. The edge expansion hypothesis forces this procedure to terminate after finitely many additions. The terminal set has a boundary-edge to boundary-vertex bijection.

This is the discrete version of completing the finite region before reading its boundary at infinity: finite holes and local indentations are absorbed, and the remaining boundary is one-incidence-thick.

The key point is that the argument is not planar. Its exact theorem, already independently checked by R05, is:

### `R06-I0056-CLOSURE-01` (retained)

Let G be an infinite simple d-regular graph. If

\[
\Phi_E(G)\ge d-3,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\Phi_V(G)=\Phi_E(G).}
\]

The proof uses the monovariant edge boundary under multiplicity-two filling and the terminal boundary bijection. It does not use an assumed shape of an optimizer.

### New source-scope extension: mixed faces with minimum face degree five

The points-at-infinity viewpoint suggested not insisting on co-regularity. The closure theorem only needs a large enough **edge** expansion constant. Oh already supplies such an edge lower bound from minimum local curvature data.

This yields the new theorem below.

## 6. `R06-I0056-CLOSURE-02` — vertex and edge constants coincide for every regular plane tessellation with face degrees at least five

**Label: PROVED mathematical theorem, audit-gated / novelty UNVERIFIED.**

### Statement

Let G be an infinite simple d-regular tessellation of the plane. Assume every face has finite degree at least 5. Then

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_V(G)=\Phi_E(G).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

No co-regularity or transitivity is required for this equality statement.

### Proof

If d=3, then

\[
d-3=0,
\]

while trivially

\[
\Phi_E(G)\ge0.
\]

Therefore `R06-I0056-CLOSURE-01` applies directly and gives

\[
\Phi_V(G)=\Phi_E(G).
\]

Now suppose d>=4. By Oh's sharp lower isoperimetric theorem for infinite plane tessellations with vertex degrees at least p and face degrees at least q, applied with

\[
p=d,\qquad q=5,
\]

we have

\[
\Phi_E(G)\ge
\Phi(d,5)
=(d-2)\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{3(d-2)}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The admissibility condition is satisfied because

\[
\frac1d+\frac15\le\frac14+\frac15<\frac12.
\]

Compute exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(d,5)^2-(d-3)^2
&=(d-2)^2-\frac{4(d-2)}3-(d-3)^2\\
&=2d-5-\frac{4(d-2)}3\\
&=\frac{2d-7}{3}>0
\end{aligned}
\]

for every d>=4. Hence

\[
\Phi_E(G)>d-3.
\]

`R06-I0056-CLOSURE-01` now gives

\[
\Phi_V(G)=\Phi_E(G).
\]

This proves (6.1). `square`

### Source relevance

For the first clause of Question 6.20, the regular/co-regular f>=5 graphs are a special case, recovering the previous exact formula after inserting the known exact regular-tessellation edge constant.

For the second clause, any **vertex-transitive** plane tessellation whose face degrees are all at least 5 now has only one isoperimetric constant to determine:

\[
\boxed{\Phi_V(G)=\Phi_E(G).}
\]

This does **not** determine their common numerical value in a genuinely mixed-face transitive graph. Therefore the full source remains PARTIAL.

A further clean corollary is that every infinite simple cubic graph satisfies Phi_V=Phi_E, independently of planarity, since the closure threshold is zero. This follows from CLOSURE-01 and is not separately counted as metric movement.

### Why this is a real extension rather than a relabeling

Haslegrave--Panagiotis settle exact regular/co-regular outer-vertex constants at face degrees 3 and 4. Oh settles the edge inequality. CLOSURE-02 uses the edge inequality plus the new edge-to-outer-vertex closure mechanism to cover **non-co-regular** tessellations with minimum face degree 5. It is a reduction/equality theorem for the more-general clause, not an exact mixed-face formula.
