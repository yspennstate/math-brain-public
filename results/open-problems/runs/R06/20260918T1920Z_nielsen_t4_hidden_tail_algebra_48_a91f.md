## 5. Method #7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: a hidden tail coin invisible to every finite prefix

Let

\[
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N},
\]

let `C` be its clopen algebra, and let `Q` be fair-coin measure, so

\[
Q([s])=2^{-|s|}.
\]

Define the Borel tail event

\[
A=\{\omega:\omega_n=1\text{ for infinitely many }n\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then `A` is a dense `G_delta`. Its complement consists of the eventually-zero paths,

\[
A^c=\{\omega:\omega_n=0\text{ for all sufficiently large }n\},
\]

which is countable and dense. Density on both sides is the key: no finite prefix decides membership in `A`.

Let

\[
\mathcal A=\operatorname{alg}(\mathcal C\cup\{A\}),
\tag{5.2}
\]

the Boolean algebra generated by all clopens and this one tail event.

### Lemma 5.1 — unique two-coordinate normal form

Every `E in A` has a unique representation

\[
E=E(C,D):=(A\cap C)\cup(A^c\cap D),
\qquad C,D\in\mathcal C.
\tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

Existence follows by Boolean induction: relative to the partition `A,A^c`, finite Boolean operations act separately on the clopen coordinates.

For uniqueness, suppose `E(C,D)=E(C',D')`. Intersecting with `A` gives

\[
A\cap(C\triangle C')=\varnothing.
\]

But `C triangle C'` is clopen. If it were nonempty it would be a nonempty open set and hence would meet the dense set `A`, contradiction. Thus `C=C'`. Intersecting with `A^c` and using density of `A^c` similarly gives `D=D'`. QED.

Equivalently, `A` is Boolean-isomorphic to `C x C` through `E(C,D) -> (C,D)`.

## 6. Method #15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: a continuum of exact relative-CHT charges

For every parameter

\[
p\in(0,1),
\]

define

\[
P_p(E(C,D)):=pQ(C)+(1-p)Q(D).
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 — hidden-tail algebra theorem

For every `p in (0,1)`, `P_p` has all of the following properties on the algebra `A`.

1. `P_p` is a finitely additive probability.
2. `P_p` agrees with `Q` on every clopen, so every nonempty cylinder has strictly positive `P_p`-probability.
3. For every `E in A`, its posterior process stabilizes after a finite event-dependent depth and takes eventual values only in
   \[
   \{0,p,1-p,1\}.
   \]
   Consequently the exact CHT identity holds for every `E in A` and every threshold `delta in (0,1)` **relative to this algebra**.
4. The clopen approximation defect of the tail event is exactly
   \[
   \boxed{\inf_{C\in\mathcal C}P_p(A\triangle C)=\min(p,1-p)>0.}
   \tag{6.2}
   \]
5. Hence the family `{P_p:0<p<1}` gives uncountably many distinct relative-CHT/non-approximation examples. At `p=1/2`, the defect in (6.2) is exactly the global theoretical maximum `1/2`.

#### Proof

**Finite additivity.** By Lemma 5.1, the map `E(C,D) -> (C,D)` is a Boolean-algebra isomorphism from `A` to `C x C`. Formula (6.1) is the convex combination of `Q` on the two coordinates, so it is normalized, nonnegative and finitely additive.

**Cylinder positivity.** A clopen `K` has the normal form `E(K,K)`, so

\[
P_p(K)=pQ(K)+(1-p)Q(K)=Q(K).
\tag{6.3}
\]

In particular, for every finite word `s`,

\[
P_p([s])=2^{-|s|}>0.
\]

**Finite stabilization of every event in the algebra.** Fix `E=E(C,D)`. Since `C,D` are clopen, there is an `m` such that both belong to the finite-information algebra `F_m`.

Let `B` be any length-`n` cylinder with `n>=m`. Then `B` lies in exactly one of the four clopen regions

\[
C\cap D,\quad C\setminus D,\quad D\setminus C,\quad (C\cup D)^c.
\]

Using (6.1) on `E cap B`, while `P_p(B)=Q(B)`, gives

\[
P_{p,n}(E)|_B=
\begin{cases}
1,&B\subset C\cap D,\\
p,&B\subset C\setminus D,\\
1-p,&B\subset D\setminus C,\\
0,&B\subset(C\cup D)^c.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus for every `n>=m` the posterior is the same `F_m`-measurable function

\[
g_E=1_{C\cap D}+p1_{C\setminus D}+(1-p)1_{D\setminus C}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Fix `delta in (0,1)` and let

\[
C_n(E,\delta)=\{P_{p,n}(E)>\delta\}.
\]

For all `n>=m`, (6.5) makes this threshold set equal to one fixed clopen set `H_delta in F_m`. Therefore the total first-hitting open is already finite:

\[
O(E,\delta)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}C_n(E,\delta)
=\bigcup_{n=1}^{m}C_n(E,\delta).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Equivalently, every first-hitting piece `D_n(E,delta)` is empty for `n>m`. Hence

\[
P_p\!\left(\bigcup_{n\ge1}D_n(E,\delta)\right)
=\sum_{n\ge1}P_p(D_n(E,\delta))
\]

is just a finite-additivity identity. This is precisely CHT for every event available in the algebra `A`.

**Exact approximation defect.** For any clopen `K`,

\[
A\triangle K=(A\cap K^c)\cup(A^c\cap K)=E(K^c,K).
\]

Therefore

\[
P_p(A\triangle K)
=pQ(K^c)+(1-p)Q(K)
=p+(1-2p)Q(K).
\tag{6.7}
\]

If `p<=1/2`, the right side is minimized at `K=empty`, yielding `p`; if `p>=1/2`, it is minimized at `K=Omega`, yielding `1-p`. This proves (6.2). Since `P_p(A)=p`, different parameters give distinct charges. At `p=1/2`, (6.7) is identically `1/2` for **every** clopen `K`. QED.

### Scope warning

This theorem is intentionally **not** labeled a solution of Nielsen Theorem 4. The source domain is the entire Borel sigma-algebra `B`, whereas `A=alg(C union {A})` is only a proper Boolean algebra. Indeed `C` is countable, hence `A` is countable, while the Borel sigma-algebra of Cantor space has cardinality continuum.

The theorem is nevertheless source-relevant: it shows that Assumption 1, exact posterior conditioning, exact CHT, and maximal failure of approximation are mutually compatible at every finite Boolean stage. The obstruction is genuinely the sigma-completion step, not a finite-prefix inconsistency.

## 7. Horn--Tarski removes finite additivity and positivity from the remaining obstruction

Every such extension still satisfies Nielsen's Assumption 1, because its cylinder values remain

Every such extension also retains

\[
\inf_{C\in\mathcal C}\widetilde P_p(A\triangle C)=\min(p,1-p),
\tag{7.1}
\]

because all events `A triangle C` already lie in the original algebra and their probabilities are fixed by (6.7).

In particular, for `p=1/2`, **every** finite-additive Borel extension has

\[
D(\widetilde P_{1/2})=\frac12.
\tag{7.2}
\]

#### Proof

Horn--Tarski extends a finitely additive probability on a subalgebra to the larger Boolean algebra `B`. Cylinder positivity and (7.1) are values on the old subalgebra and therefore survive unchanged. The universal bound `D(P)<=1/2`, together with the witness `A` in (7.1), gives equality (7.2). QED.

## 8. Method #21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: extension theory versus conglomerability

The nearest solved literature clarifies exactly what (7.3) adds beyond standard finite-additive theory.

### 8.1 Horn--Tarski gets all the way to the Borel domain, but not to CHT

The extension theorem is purely Boolean/linear. It preserves every old event value and positivity, but it imposes no sequential-continuity condition on the newly generated first-hitting unions. Therefore a generic Horn--Tarski extension is not evidence for CHT.

This explains why the old Nielsen proof failed in a stronger guise: fixing enough topological values can overdetermine the extension, while unconstrained finite-additive extension is easy. The live problem is to impose only the **endogenous** countable-additivity constraints generated by posterior hitting times.

### 8.2 General nonconglomerability does not decide the source

Schervish--Seidenfeld--Kadane's classical result says that a merely finitely additive probability is nonconglomerable for some countable partition. CHT, however, asks for countable additivity only on partitions of the form

\[
\{D_n(B,\delta)\}_{n\ge1}
\]

coming from first crossings of that same probability's posterior process. The bad partition guaranteed abstractly by nonconglomerability need not be realizable as any such conditional-hitting partition.

So the gap can be stated cleanly:

> **general finite-additive extension** is available, and **some countable partition must misbehave**, but the source asks whether all of the very special posterior-generated partitions can nevertheless behave correctly.

This is the closest literature-shaped formulation I found this run. It suggests studying realizability of countable partitions as posterior first-hitting partitions rather than manufacturing another ad hoc ultrafilter.
