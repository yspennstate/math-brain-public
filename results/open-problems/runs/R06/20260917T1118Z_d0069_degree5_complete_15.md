## 4. Idea before code — method #3: prove a stronger weighted degree-four statement

For either residual pattern, replace each parallel class of `Q` by one simple edge. Call the resulting simple skeleton `H`. In both cases:

- `H` is infinite, connected, simple, vertex-transitive and `4`-regular;
- each skeleton edge `e` carries multiplicity `m(e)` inherited from `Q`;
- a skeleton SAW `e_1...e_n` corresponds to exactly `prod_i m(e_i)` edge-copy-distinct Q-SAWs.

Consequently the Q critical SAW mass at `z=1/2` is exactly the weighted skeleton mass with edge factor

`w(e)=m(e)/2`.                                                  (4.1)

The useful stronger target is not a separate connective-constant estimate for each multiplicity pattern. It is the following weighted branching lemma.

### Weighted blue-branch lemma

Let `H` be an infinite simple vertex-transitive 4-regular graph. Along an extendable even SAW of length `L=2n`, let `b_s in {0,1,2}` be Grimmett--Li's number of blue alternative edges at position `s`. Their condition-Pi double count, specialized to `Delta=4`, gives

`sum_{s=0}^{L-1} b_s >= L`.                                   (4.2)

The artificial root position has `b_0<=2`. Deleting it, for the `N=L-1` genuine transition positions,

`sum b_s >= L-2 = N-1`.                                      (4.3)

If `n_j` is the number of genuine positions with `b_s=j`, then

`n_0+n_1+n_2=N`,

`n_1+2 n_2 >= N-1`,

hence

`n_2-n_0 >= -1`.                                              (4.4)

Suppose now that at every extendable prefix, whenever the chosen infinite continuation has `b` blue alternatives, the **total critical weight of the chosen child plus those `b` blue children** is at least

`F(0)=1/2,   F(1)=1,   F(2)=2`.                              (4.5)

Then every full path satisfies

`prod_s F(b_s)=2^(n_2-n_0)>=1/2`.                             (4.6)

To turn this pathwise statement into a total-mass statement, let `S(eta)` be the sum of the critical weights of all extendable children of prefix `eta`, and normalize

`p(eta->eta')=w(eta,eta')/S(eta)`.

For all paths of a fixed depth sharing a fixed first edge,

`sum_paths prod w = E_p[prod S(eta_s)]`.                      (4.7)

Since `S(eta_s)>=F(b_s)` along each realized path, (4.6) forces the right side to be at least `1/2` (up to the fixed positive first-edge factor). Thus the weighted critical generating function has a positive amount of mass at infinitely many depths and diverges. By (4.1), `mu(Q)>=2`.

This is the stronger object sought by method #3: both residual multiplicity patterns will be reduced to the same three local lower bounds (4.5).

## 5. Why (4.2) is legitimate here

The source proof is used at exactly one point. For a simple 4-regular vertex-transitive graph, Grimmett--Li prove condition Pi. In their proof of Theorem 4.3, for every extendable `2n`-step SAW they colour an alternative edge blue precisely when the current prefix followed by that edge has an infinite SAW extension. Their equations (5.2)--(5.3) give

`sum b_s >= n(Delta-2)`.

For `Delta=4`, this is `sum b_s >= 2n=L`, exactly (4.2). Thus `1+b_s` is the number consisting of the chosen continuation plus its extendable blue alternatives. The weighted argument above does not assume every nonbacktracking child is extendable; it lower-bounds the sum over whatever `1+b_s` extendable children actually exist by taking the lightest possible such subset.

## 7. Residual quotient `[3,1,1,1]`: method #12, find the conserved potential

Direct critical weights now fail the same universal test. The unique triple-edge class is an invariant perfect matching in Q, so in the skeleton H every vertex has exactly one edge of type `T` (triple) and three edges of type `S` (single).

If an incoming edge is T, the three remaining candidates are all S and have raw weight `1/2`. The one-child mass would be only `1/2`, but if incoming is S, the candidates are `T,S,S` with raw weights `3/2,1/2,1/2`. There is a state imbalance: the heavy triple edge compensates for the light all-single state one step later.

The conserved object is a two-state multiplicative potential. Set

`h(T)=2/3,   h(S)=1`.                                         (7.1)

For a transition whose outgoing edge has type `j` and incoming edge has type `i`, replace the raw critical factor `w` by the h-transformed factor

`w_tilde = w * h(j)/h(i)`.                                   (7.2)

Along any path these factors telescope:

`prod w_tilde = (prod w) * h(final)/h(initial)`.              (7.3)

Since `2/3 <= h <=1`, transformed and original path masses differ by a uniformly bounded factor. Divergence of one critical generating mass is therefore equivalent, for this purpose, to divergence of the other.

Now inspect the two incoming states.

### Incoming T

All three candidate children are S. Each transformed weight is

`(1/2)*(1)/(2/3)=3/4`.

The lightest `1,2,3` child sums are

`3/4, 3/2, 9/4`,

which dominate `1/2,1,2`.

### Incoming S

The candidates are one T and two S. Their transformed weights are

T child: `(3/2)*(2/3)/1 = 1`,

S child: `(1/2)*1/1 = 1/2`,

S child: `1/2`.

The lightest `1,2,3` child sums are exactly

`1/2, 1, 2`.

Thus the h-transformed extendable-SAW tree satisfies the same universal local bound (4.5). Applying the normalized-tree argument gives a transformed critical suffix mass at least `1/2` at every relevant even depth. Equation (7.3) converts it back to original critical mass with loss at most the fixed factor `2/3`. Therefore the original Q critical generating function diverges at `z=1/2`, and

`mu(Q)>=2`.                                                    (7.4)

The h-transform is the essential new mechanism of this run. It packages the alternating deficit/surplus between triple-entering and singleton-entering states into a telescoping invariant rather than trying to prove an impossible one-step raw bound.
