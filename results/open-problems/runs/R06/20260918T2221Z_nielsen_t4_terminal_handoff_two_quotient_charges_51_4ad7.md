## 6. Method #12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: two quotient charges preserve every cylinder marginal

Let `C` denote the clopen algebra of Cantor space.

### 6.1 A category charge `mu_0`

Let `M` be the ideal of meager Borel subsets of `Omega`, and consider the quotient Boolean algebra

\[
\mathcal B/\mathcal M.
\]

The clopen algebra embeds injectively in this quotient. Indeed, if two clopen sets `C_1,C_2` are equal modulo a meager set, then their symmetric difference is both clopen and meager. A nonempty clopen subset of Cantor space is a nonempty open Baire space and is not meager in `Omega`; hence the symmetric difference must be empty.

Therefore

\[
q([C]) := Q(C),\qquad C\in\mathcal C,
\]

is a well-defined finitely additive probability on the embedded clopen subalgebra of `B/M`.

By the classical Horn--Tarski extension theorem, `q` extends to a finitely additive probability `\bar\mu_0` on all of `B/M`. Pull it back to the Borel algebra:

\[
\mu_0(E):=\bar\mu_0([E]_{\mathcal M}).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mu_0(C)=Q(C)\quad(C\in\mathcal C)},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\mu_0(M)=0\quad\text{for every meager Borel }M}.
\]

Since `D` is countable, it is meager. Hence

\[
\mu_0(D)=0,\qquad \mu_0(A)=1.
\]

The extension theorem used here is standard, not claimed new. A fresh literature check recovered Horn--Tarski, *Measures in Boolean algebras*, Trans. AMS 64 (1948), 467--497, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-1948-0028922-8`; a modern statement appears as Theorem A.10 in the Cambridge article *On Accuracy and Coherence with Infinite Opinion Sets*: a partial measure on a subset of an algebra extends to a finitely additive probability on the full algebra.

### 6.2 A dense-countable charge `nu_0`

Every subset of countable `D` is Borel in `Omega`. On `P(D)` define the proper ideal

\[
\mathcal I
:=\{S\subset D:\exists\text{ closed }K\subset D\text{ (closed in }\Omega)\text{ with }S\subset K\}.
\]

It is an ideal because a finite union of closed subsets of `D` is again a closed subset of `D`. It is proper because `D` itself is dense and not closed in `Omega`, so no closed `K\subset D` can contain all of `D`.

Now consider the traces `C\cap D` of clopen sets. The trace map is injective modulo `I`. Suppose

\[
(C_1\triangle C_2)\cap D\in\mathcal I.
\]

Put `B=C_1\triangle C_2`, which is clopen. If `B` were nonempty, density of `D` would give

\[
\overline{B\cap D}=B.
\]

But membership in `I` would give a closed `K\subset D` with `B\cap D\subset K`, hence

\[
B=\overline{B\cap D}\subset K\subset D,
\]

contradicting density of `A` in every nonempty clopen set. Therefore `B=empty`, so `C_1=C_2`.

It follows that

\[
q_D([C\cap D]_{\mathcal I}) := Q(C)
\]

is a well-defined finitely additive probability on the trace-clopen subalgebra of `P(D)/I`. Horn--Tarski again extends it to a state `\bar\nu_0` on all of `P(D)/I`. Pull this back first to `P(D)` and then to Borel subsets of `Omega`:

\[
\lambda(S):=\bar\nu_0([S]_{\mathcal I}),
\qquad
\nu_0(E):=\lambda(E\cap D).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\nu_0(C)=Q(C)\quad(C\in\mathcal C)},
\]

\[
\boxed{\nu_0(D)=1,\quad \nu_0(A)=0},
\]

and, crucially,

\[
\boxed{\nu_0(F)=0\quad\text{for every closed }F\subset D}.
\]

Thus the fair-coin finite-information marginals are conserved by **two radically different** full-Borel finitely additive charges.

\mu_N(A)=1,\ \mu_N(D)=0,

\[
\mu(E):=\lim_{N\to\mathcal U}\mu_N(E),

## 10. Exact CHT on a broad Boolean algebra inside the full Borel sigma-algebra

The finite-flip symmetry gives a second strong property.

Call a Borel event `T` **finite-modification invariant** if

\[
gT=T\qquad\forall g\in G.
\]

### Proposition 10.1 — invariant events have constant posteriors

For every such `T`, every `n`, and every length-`n` cylinder `[s]`,

\[
\boxed{P(T\cap[s])=P(T)\,2^{-n}}.\tag{10.1}
\]

**Proof.** `G_n` acts transitively on the `2^n` level-`n` cylinders. Since both `T` and `P` are invariant, `P(T\cap[s])` is the same for every length-`n` word `s`. Summing over the level-`n` cylinder partition yields total mass `P(T)`, proving (10.1). QED.

Since `P([s])=2^{-n}`, the posterior of `T` is therefore

\[
P_n(T)(x)=P(T)
\]

for every `n,x`. For any threshold `delta`, its hitting union is either `Omega` (if `P(T)>delta`) or `empty` (otherwise). Hence CHT holds trivially for every finite-modification-invariant Borel event.

In particular, `A` and `D` themselves have identically constant posterior `1/2` at every finite information level.

### Proposition 10.2 — exact CHT on the Boolean algebra generated by clopens and invariant Borel events

Let

\[
\mathfrak A_{\rm inv}
:=\operatorname{alg}\bigl(\mathcal C\cup\{T\in\mathcal B:gT=T\ \forall g\in G\}\bigr).

\[
\Omega=C_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_m

\[

Choose `N` so that every `C_j` is measurable at level `N`. For any `n\ge N`, each level-`n` cylinder `B` lies in one `C_j`, and then

\[

By (10.1),

\[
\frac{P(E\cap B)}{P(B)}=P(T_j).
