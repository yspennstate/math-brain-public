## 2. Original source scope and exact conventions

The current Lyons--Peres online text states Question 6.20:

> What is `Phi_V(G)` for regular, co-regular plane graphs `G`? What are `Phi_E(G)` and `Phi_V(G)` for more general transitive, plane graphs `G`?

Source: https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/prbtree/book_pb.pdf (Question 6.20 in Chapter 6).

Lyons--Peres define the **outer vertex boundary**
\[
\partial_V K:=\{x\notin K:\exists y\in K,\ x\sim y\}
\]
and, in the unweighted convention used here,
\[
\Phi_V(G)=\inf_{\varnothing\ne K\subset V,\ K\text{ finite}}
\frac{|\partial_VK|}{|K|}.
\]
Thus this run does **not** use inner boundary, edge boundary, or a normalized-by-degree Cheeger convention.

For a regular/co-regular plane graph, `d` is the common vertex degree and `d^dagger` (also written `f`) is the common face degree/codegree. The canonical regular hyperbolic tessellation `H_{d,f}` exists in the hyperbolic regime
\[
(d-2)(f-2)>4
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac1d+\frac1f<\frac12.
\]

The current online Lyons--Peres update records Haslegrave--Panagiotis for codegrees 3 and 4 and records Oh (2020) for Question 6.21. It displays an apparent sign typo in the definition of `alpha_d`: the primary Haslegrave--Panagiotis published PDF has
\[
\alpha_d=\frac{d-6+\sqrt{(d-2)(d-6)}}2,
\]
not the displayed minus-root in the current book update. The primary paper explicitly says `alpha_7` is the golden ratio, which also forces the plus root. I use the primary-paper convention.

Primary partial resolution:
- John Haslegrave and Christoforos Panagiotis, *Site percolation and isoperimetric inequalities for plane graphs*, Random Structures & Algorithms 58 (2021), 150--163, DOI `10.1002/rsa.20946`, first online 25 June 2020.
- Their Theorems 4--5 determine the vertex isoperimetric constant for the regular triangular (`f=3`) and quadrangular (`f=4`) hyperbolic tessellations and explicitly state that these answer Lyons--Peres Question 6.20 for all `d^dagger=3,4` cases.

Those solved cases are excluded from this R06 target.

## 4. Counterexample-first: exact finite boundary constructions

Before applying a universal lower bound, I searched exact small outer-vertex-boundary constructions.

### 4.1 Girth check

The `{5,5}` tessellation has girth 5. Here is a self-contained planar count.

Suppose a simple cycle `C` of length `m<5` encloses `n_i` interior vertices and `f_i` pentagonal faces. Let `e` be the number of edges in the closed disk. Euler and face-incidence counting give
\[
n_i+m-e+f_i=1,
\qquad
5f_i=2e-m,
\]
so
\[
3f_i=2n_i+m-2.
\]
Every interior vertex has all five incident edges in the disk, and every boundary vertex has disk degree at least 2. Hence
\[
2e\ge 5n_i+2m.
\]
But `2e=5f_i+m`, so
\[
\frac{10n_i+8m-10}{3}\ge \frac{15n_i+6m}{3},
\]
which implies
\[
m\ge 5+\frac52 n_i\ge5,
\]
a contradiction. Thus no 3- or 4-cycle exists.

### 4.2 Exact local candidates

For `K={v}`,
\[
|\partial_VK|/|K|=5.
\]

For two adjacent vertices, each endpoint has four neighbors outside the pair and the two external neighbor sets are disjoint by girth at least 5, giving
\[
|\partial_VK|/|K|=8/2=4.
\]

For the five vertices of one facial pentagon, each face vertex has three edges leaving the face. No outside vertex can meet two face vertices: two vertices of a 5-cycle are at cyclic distance 1 or 2, so such a common outside neighbor would create a 3- or 4-cycle. Therefore all 15 outside neighbors are distinct and
\[
|\partial_VK|/|K|=15/5=3.
\]

These exact small constructions did **not** beat the layered face-ball construction below.

## 5. Rigorous upper bound from exact face-ball constructions

Use Yusuke Higuchi and Tomoyuki Shirai, *Isoperimetric Constants of (d,f)-Regular Planar Graphs*, Interdisciplinary Information Sciences 9 (2003), 221--228, DOI `10.4036/iis.2003.221`.

They define face-centered layered balls `B_n^*`, with:
- `V_n^*`: vertices first appearing at layer `n`;
- `v_n^*=|V_n^*|`;
- `S_n^*`: layer-`n` vertices having a neighbor in the previous ball;
- `u_n^*`: the total number of inward edges from `S_n^*`.

For `f>=4`, their Remark 2.2(3) states that every vertex of `S_n^*` has **exactly one** neighbor in the previous ball. Their recurrences are
\[
u_n^*=D v_{n-1}^*-u_{n-1}^*,
\qquad
v_n^*=(DF-1)v_{n-1}^*-F u_{n-1}^*,
\]
with `D=d-2`, `F=f-2`, `u_0^*=0`, `v_0^*=f`.

For `H_{5,5}`, `D=F=3`, hence
\[
\boxed{
u_n=3v_{n-1}-u_{n-1},
\qquad
v_n=8v_{n-1}-3u_{n-1},
\quad
u_0=0,\ v_0=5.}
\]

Let
\[
K_n:=V(B_n^*).
\]
I need the **outer vertex boundary**, not Higuchi--Shirai's edge boundary. Here the conversion is exact: if `x` is outside `K_n` and adjacent to `y in K_n`, then an incident face containing edge `xy` meets `B_n^*`, so `x` first appears in `V_{n+1}^*` and has an inward neighbor; hence `x in S_{n+1}^*`. Conversely every vertex of `S_{n+1}^*` is outside `K_n` and adjacent to it. Thus
\[
\boxed{\partial_VK_n=S_{n+1}^*.}
\]
Because `f=5>=4`, each vertex of `S_{n+1}^*` contributes exactly one inward edge, so
\[
\boxed{|\partial_VK_n|=u_{n+1}^*.}
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{|\partial_VK_n|}{|K_n|}
=\frac{u_{n+1}}{\sum_{k=0}^n v_k}.
\]

The executed exact recurrence gives:

| n | `|K_n|` | `|∂_V K_n|` | exact ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 5 | 15 | `3` |

| 2 | 320 | 720 | `9/4` |

| 5 | 103680 | 231840 | `161/72` |
| 6 | 710645 | 1589055 | `843/377` |
| 7 | 4870845 | 10891545 | `2207/987` |

Higuchi--Shirai equation (2.8) gives the exact limiting unnormalized edge-boundary/vertex ratio of these same face balls as
\[
\sqrt{\frac{D(DF-4)}F}.
\]
At `D=F=3`, this is
\[
\sqrt5.
\]
Since on these balls edge-boundary size equals outer-vertex-boundary size, the explicit finite sequence proves
\[
\boxed{\Phi_V(H_{5,5})\le\sqrt5.}
\tag{R06-I0056-U}
\]

This upper bound comes from exact finite boundary constructions, not from a universal estimate.
