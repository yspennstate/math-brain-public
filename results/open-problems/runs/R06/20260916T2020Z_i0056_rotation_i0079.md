I independently audited its concrete Lemma `R07-HOLDER-L`:

> For every `gamma>1/2`, standard Brownian motion is almost surely nowhere left-Hölder of exponent `gamma` with finite local constant.

### Audit proof

Fix `gamma>1/2`, an integer horizon `M`, and integer constant bound `K`. Choose an integer `N` with

\[
N(\gamma-1/2)>1.
\]

If a left-Hölder point `s in (0,M]` existed with constant at most `K`, then for every sufficiently fine dyadic mesh `n=2^m`, the `N` consecutive mesh increments immediately to the left of `s` would all have magnitude at most

\[
2K((N+1)/n)^\gamma.
\]

For a fixed block, the normalized increments `sqrt(n) Delta_j` are independent standard normals. Boundedness of the Gaussian density gives probability at most

\[
C_{K,N,\gamma}\,n^{-N(\gamma-1/2)}
\]

for all `N` increments to be that small. There are `O(Mn)` possible blocks, so on the dyadic sequence the probability that any qualifying block exists is at most

\[
C'2^{-m(N(\gamma-1/2)-1)},
\]

which is summable. Borel--Cantelli rules out qualifying blocks at all sufficiently fine dyadic scales, contradicting the consequence of a left-Hölder point. A countable union over `M,K` proves the lemma.
