## 4. Method #5 — add/track the parameter: Schramm's fixed-q tuning is the near-critical window

Schramm parameterizes the perturbation implicitly by a macroscopic crossing probability `q`. Modern work parameterizes it explicitly by

\[
p(\eta)=p_c+\lambda r(\eta),
\]

where `r(eta)` is the pivotal/near-critical scale and `lambda` is fixed.

The important conceptual move is to regard `q` not as a qualitatively different model but as a coordinate on the same near-critical one-parameter family: choosing a nontrivial fixed macroscopic crossing probability prevents the model from escaping either to the critical `q=1/2` slice or to a degenerate super/subcritical regime. Thus the source question should be compared against the `lambda`-indexed near-critical scaling limit, not against ordinary SLE6 alone.

I am **not** recording an unproved exact formula `q <-> lambda` here. The admissible conclusion from the checked literature is the weaker and sufficient one: the source's tuning is finite-size/near-critical-window tuning, and the Garban--Pete--Schramm `lambda` family is the modern framework in which that question is treated.

## 5. Method #21 — who else has this shape: Nolin--Werner and Garban--Pete--Schramm

### 5.1 Nolin--Werner: subsequential massive interfaces are genuinely non-SLE6

Pierre Nolin and Wendelin Werner, *Asymmetry of near-critical percolation interfaces*, JAMS 22 (2009), prove that in the nondegenerate intermediate near-critical regime, any subsequential scaling limit of the interface is singular with respect to SLE6 while retaining Hausdorff dimension 7/4.

This answers an important qualitative part of Schramm's question: the `q != 1/2` limit, if nontrivial, is not simply the critical SLE6 law.

### 5.2 Garban--Pete--Schramm: unique near-critical configuration scaling limit

Christophe Garban, Gabor Pete and Oded Schramm, *The scaling limits of near-critical and dynamical percolation*, JEMS 20 (2018), prove that for each fixed `lambda`, the near-critical percolation configuration has a scaling limit in the Schramm--Smirnov quad-crossing space, and construct the entire `lambda`-indexed Markov process. Their paper explicitly says that the Camia--Fontes--Newman near-critical construction problem is answered at the configuration level.

The earlier GPS interface-measure paper also describes the sequel as refining Nolin--Werner by showing that the near-critical interface has an actual scaling limit, calling the object **massive SLE6**. A 2014 talk by Gabor Pete likewise states that the near-critical percolation interface scaling limit exists and calls it massive SLE6.

These are strong reasons not to admit I0037 as if the 2006 source question were untouched.

## 6. Method #22 — read the failure: exact curve topology / Loewner characterization is a real residual

The later literature itself records an important caveat, and this is why I am **not** simply relabeling the entire source question KNOWN without qualification.

In the JEMS paper, GPS prove that the **trace as a closed set** of the near-critical interface is measurable from the near-critical continuum configuration. But they explicitly say they have **not proved measurability of the interface as an ordered curve**; consequently the Nolin--Werner subsequential curve limits were, in that exact curve sense, “not exactly known yet to be an actual limit” in the exposition they give there.

They separately formulate a conjectural Loewner driving description for massive chordal SLE6, of the schematic form

\[
dW_t=\sqrt6\,dB_t+dA_t,
\]

with a self-interacting monotone drift, and emphasize that making the proposed drift rigorous is challenging.

So there are three levels that must not be conflated:

1. **near-critical configuration** in quad-crossing topology — rigorous unique scaling limit (GPS);
2. **interface trace as a closed set** — measurable from that continuum configuration;
3. **interface as an ordered curve / explicit Loewner driving law** — a finer issue, with GPS explicitly recording a measurability/driving-law residual.

A useful older GPS paper and a Pete conference description use the informal sentence “the near-critical interface does have an actual scaling limit (massive SLE6)”. The later JEMS text makes clear that the precise topology/ordering matters. The correct admission response is therefore to freeze the residual before assigning work, rather than call the source fully open or fully solved based on one sentence.

A bounded fresh search dated 2026-09-17 for later primary work on `near-critical percolation interface`, `massive SLE6`, `curve scaling limit`, `Loewner drift`, and variants did not surface a newer primary paper proving the full explicit massive-SLE6 driving law.
