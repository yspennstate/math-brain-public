## 3. Source normalization and retained product baseline

For a positive target law `mu` on `{-1,+1}^n`, set

\[
r_i^\mu(x)=\frac{\mu(x^i)}{\mu(x)},
\qquad
u_i^\mu(x)=\log r_i^\mu(x).
\]

As in the preceding report, normalize the three source rates so the uniform target has coordinate flip rate `1/n`:

\[
c_{G,i}^\mu(x)=\frac1n\frac{2e^{\nu_i^\mu(x)}}{1+e^{\nu_i^\mu(x)}},
\]
\[
c_{M,i}^\mu(x)=\frac1n\min\{1,e^{\nu_i^\mu(x)}\},
\]
\[
c_{S,i}^\mu(x)=\frac1n e^{\nu_i^\mu(x)/2}.
\]

Let

\[
\nu_n=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Ber}(p_{i,n}),
\qquad
\eta\le p_{i,n}\le1-\eta
\tag{3.1}
\]

for fixed `eta>0`. The previous report proved that each of the three product chains has cutoff at the unique `t_{J,n}` satisfying

\[
S_{J,n}(t):=\sum_i e^{-2a_{J,i,n}t}=1,
\tag{3.2}
\]

where the coordinate relaxation rates satisfy

\[
\frac{m_\eta}{n}\le a_{J,i,n}\le\frac{M_\eta}{n}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus `t_{J,n}=Theta_eta(n log n)` and the product cutoff window is `O_eta(n)`. I import that proved baseline rather than rederive it.

For a full-support target `pi_n`, define

\[
h_n(x)=\log\frac{\pi_n(x)}{\nu_n(x)},
\qquad
\Delta_n=\max_{x,i}|h_n(x^i)-h_n(x)|.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\Delta_n
=
\max_{x,i}
\left|
\log\frac{r_i^{\pi_n}(x)}{r_i^{\nu_n}(x)}
\right|.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The preceding C001 theorem required `n log n Delta_n -> 0`. The goal of this run was to understand exactly where that linear loss came from.

# R01-I0113-C002 — path-entropy transfer: the interaction threshold improves from linear to quadratic

**STATUS: PROVED in this report as a mathematical theorem; source-level closure remains PARTIAL pending independent source-fit/prior-art audit.**

Assume (3.1) and

\[
\boxed{
 n\log n\,\Delta_n^2\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{C002.1}
\]

Then each of the Gibbs, Metropolis and square-root samplers with invariant law `pi_n` exhibits worst-case total-variation cutoff at the same first-order center `t_{J,n}` as its product reference, with `O_eta(n)` cutoff window in the usual two-limit sense.

In particular, the admissible perturbation scale improves from

\[
\Delta_n=o((n\log n)^{-1})
\]

to

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_n=o((n\log n)^{-1/2}).
}
\tag{C002.2}
\]

The proof has two independent entropy comparisons: one static, one on path space.

## 4. Static entropy comparison: stationary laws are already close under `n Delta^2 -> 0`

The naive argument in C001 used the cube diameter to obtain `osc(h_n)<=n Delta_n`, hence an `O(n Delta_n)` density comparison. That is much too expensive because it treats all coordinate increments as if they could align deterministically.

Under the product reference `nu_n`, bounded differences give a quadratic estimate instead. Let

\[
m_n=E_{\nu_n}h_n.
\]

The Doob martingale exposing the coordinates one by one has increments whose ranges are at most `Delta_n`. Hoeffding's lemma therefore yields

\[
\log E_{\nu_n}\exp\{h_n-m_n\}
\le \frac18\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{osc}_i(h_n)^2
\le \frac{n\Delta_n^2}{8}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because `exp(h_n)=d pi_n/d nu_n`, normalization gives

\[
1=E_{\nu_n}e^{h_n},
\]

hence

\[
0=m_n+\log E_{\nu_n}e^{h_n-m_n}.
\]

Consequently

\[
D(\nu_n\|\pi_n)
=-m_n
\le\frac{n\Delta_n^2}{8}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Pinsker gives the uniform static bound

\[
\boxed{
\|\pi_n-\nu_n\|_{\rm TV}
\le\frac{\sqrt n\,\Delta_n}{4}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus (C002.1) already implies stationary-law closeness.

## 5. Dynamic entropy comparison: use the likelihood ratio before coupling the endpoints

The previous common-jump coupling bounded the endpoint error by `O(t Delta_n)`. At the natural time `t=Theta(n log n)`, that forces the old linear condition. This is the exact failure identified by Method #20: a first-order coupling counts every small rate perturbation as an irreversible mismatch.

Relative entropy of path laws is second order in the log-rate error.

Fix a sampler `J`, an initial state `x`, and a time `t`. Let `P^{pi}_{x,[0,t]}` and `P^{nu}_{x,[0,t]}` denote the continuous-time path laws generated by the `pi_n` and `nu_n` rates, started from the same state.

For the three rate-response functions

\[
g_G(u)=\frac{2e^u}{1+e^u},
\qquad
g_M(u)=\min\{1,e^u\},
\qquad g_S(u)=e^{u/2},
\tag{5.1}
\]

we have

\[
|\log g_J(u)-\log g_J(v)|\le |u-v|.
\tag{5.2}
\]

(The Lipschitz constants are actually `1`, `1`, and `1/2`.) Therefore (3.5) implies, for every state and coordinate,

\[
\left|
\log\frac{c_{J,i}^{\pi_n}(x)}{c_{J,i}^{\nu_n}(x)}
\right|
\le\Delta_n.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The reference product rates have uniformly bounded total exit rate: there is `B_eta<infinity` with

\[
\sup_{n,x,J}\sum_i c_{J,i}^{\nu_n}(x)\le B_\eta.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For completeness, the continuous-time likelihood ratio gives the exact path-space KL identity

\[
D(P^{\pi}_{x,[0,t]}\|P^{\nu}_{x,[0,t]})
=
E_{P^\pi}
\int_0^t
\sum_i
\left[
 c_i^\pi\log\frac{c_i^\pi}{c_i^\nu}
-c_i^\pi+c_i^\nu
\right](X_s)\,ds.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This follows directly by writing the jump-path likelihood as the product of the jump intensities times the exponential of minus the integrated total intensity, then taking expectation; the compensator turns the jump sum into the first integral term.

Put

\[
u=\log(c_i^\pi/c_i^\nu).
\]

Then the corresponding bracket in (5.5) is

\[
c_i^\nu F(u),
\qquad
F(u)=e^u u-e^u+1.
\]

Since

\[
F(u)=\int_0^u v e^v\,dv,
\]

we have for every real `u`

\[
0\le F(u)\le\frac12e^{|u|}u^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.3)--(5.6),

\[
D(P^{\pi}_{x,[0,t]}\|P^{\nu}_{x,[0,t]})
\le
\frac12 B_\eta e^{\Delta_n}t\Delta_n^2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Data processing from paths to the endpoint and Pinsker therefore give

\[
\boxed{
\sup_x
\|P^{\pi}_{J,t}(x,\cdot)-P^{\nu}_{J,t}(x,\cdot)\|_{\rm TV}
\le
\frac{\Delta_n}{2}
\sqrt{B_\eta e^{\Delta_n}t}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is the square-root accumulation that the direct coupling misses.

## 6. Transfer of the cutoff profile

For every fixed real `A`, the times

\[
t=t_{J,n}+An
\]

are `O_eta(n log n)`. Under (C002.1), (5.8) gives

\[
\sup_x
\|P^{\pi}_{J,t}(x,\cdot)-P^{\nu}_{J,t}(x,\cdot)\|_{\rm TV}
=o(1),
\tag{6.1}
\]

uniformly for each fixed `A`. Also (4.3) gives

\[
\|\pi_n-\nu_n\|_{\rm TV}=o(1).
\tag{6.2}
\]

By the triangle inequality,

\[
\left|
\|P^{\pi}_{J,t}(x,\cdot)-\pi_n\|_{\rm TV}
-
\|P^{\nu}_{J,t}(x,\cdot)-\nu_n\|_{\rm TV}
\right|=o(1)
\tag{6.3}
\]

uniformly in the initial state for each fixed `A`.

The retained product theorem says that, as first `n->infinity` and then `A->infinity`, the product distance tends to `1` at `t_{J,n}-An` and to `0` at `t_{J,n}+An`. Equation (6.3) transfers both limits to the interacting target. This proves C002.

### Adversarial checks of the proof

1. **Negative log-rate perturbations:** (5.6) is valid for `u<0` as well, because then `F(u)=-\int_u^0 ve^v dv <= u^2/2`.
2. **State-dependent rates:** (5.5) already allows state dependence; no independence of jump clocks under `pi_n` is assumed.
3. **Different stationary laws:** endpoint closeness alone is insufficient; this is why the separate static KL bound (4.2) is necessary.
4. **The product counterexample X001 is not contradicted:** it violates the fixed marginal non-degeneracy (3.1), even though `Delta_n=0` when one takes `nu_n=pi_n`.
5. **No almost-sure statement is inferred:** all bounds are deterministic inequalities between finite-state probability laws.

# R01-I0113-C003 — quantifier swap: one transfer theorem covers a whole class of odds-response samplers

Let `g:R->(0,infinity)` satisfy

\[
g(0)=1,
\qquad
g(u)=e^u g(-u),
\tag{C003.1}
\]

so that

\[
c_{g,i}^\mu(x)=\frac1n g(\log r_i^\mu(x))
\tag{C003.2}
\]

is reversible with respect to `mu`, and suppose `log g` is `L_g`-Lipschitz. For any fixed finite family `G` of such responses, let `L=max_{g in G}L_g`. On the compact product-reference odds interval forced by (3.1), positivity and log-Lipschitz continuity give uniform upper/lower bounds on rates, and therefore the product coordinate relaxation rates remain between constant multiples of `1/n`. The retained product cutoff proof consequently applies to every `g in G`.

Moreover,

\[
\left|\log\frac{c_{g,i}^{\pi}(x)}{c_{g,i}^{\nu}(x)}\right|
\le L\Delta_n.
\]

Repeating (5.5)--(5.8) with `L Delta_n` in place of `Delta_n` gives

\[
\sup_x\|P^{\pi,g}_t(x,\cdot)-P^{\nu,g}_t(x,\cdot)\|_{TV}
\le
\frac{L\Delta_n}{2}
\sqrt{B_{\eta,G}e^{L\Delta_n}t}.
\tag{C003.3}
\]

The static stationary comparison is unchanged. Hence the same single condition (C002.1) implies cutoff simultaneously for every `g` in the finite family.

The three Problem-2.7 responses satisfy (C003.1), with log-Lipschitz constants at most `1`; therefore C003 contains C002.

## 8. Attempted dimension-free curvature route and exact obstruction

The strongest desired next step would replace a vanishing `Delta_n` by an `O(1)` Dobrushin/spectral-independence condition. I tried to splice Salez Theorem 4.7 with the July 2026 Pedrotti--Salez local-product theorem.

The source itself supplies a promising common rate-influence quantity

\[
\delta_i(x)=c_i(x)-\sum_{j\ne i}(c_j(x^i)-c_j(x))_+.
\tag{8.1}
\]

If all `delta_i>=0`, Theorem 4.7 gives non-negative sectional curvature for **general** coordinate-flip MCMC and a positive MLSI lower bound when the deltas have a margin. This applies to Gibbs and Metropolis as well as square-root at the sectional/MLSI level.

The July 2026 sharp window theorem, however, gets its immediate deterministic-start estimate `gamma_* >= kappa` from **Bakry--Emery** curvature. In Salez's Theorem 4.7, the displayed Bakry--Emery estimate is special to the square-root choice `c_i=sqrt(pi(x^i)/pi(x))`; the general Gibbs/Metropolis part only supplies sectional curvature and MLSI. Therefore the two imported results do **not** by themselves provide a dimension-free common all-three cutoff criterion.

This is the current concrete obstruction:

\[
\boxed{
\delta_i^J(x)\ge c/n\ \forall J\in\{G,M,S\}
\quad\not\stackrel{\text{known imported theorems}}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
\rho_J\ge0\ \forall J.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

A successful dimension-free route now has three plausible options: prove a Bakry--Emery comparison for Gibbs/Metropolis under a sampler-invariant influence matrix; control the new non-equilibrium `gamma_*` directly from sectional curvature/contractive coupling; or establish the needed `gamma_* t_mix -> infinity` by a local-product argument specific to single-site dynamics.

This is more informative than simply trying to sharpen C002 by another Taylor estimate.
