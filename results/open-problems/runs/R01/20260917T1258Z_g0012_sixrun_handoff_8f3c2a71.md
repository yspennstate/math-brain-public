## 3. Mandatory stuck diagnosis before rotation

### (a) Exact blocking statement

All routes now reduce to the same missing degree-free statement. One sufficient form is:

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{G:\,|V(G)|=n,\,\delta(G)\ge3\\ e\in\vec E(G)}}
\Pr_e\!\left(X_0,\ldots,X_k\ \text{are distinct}\right)
\xrightarrow[k/\sqrt n\to\infty]{}0.
}
\tag{B}
\]

The R01 chain has proved several source-valid restricted versions of (B), but it still lacks a **vertex-scale pack-or-overlap lemma** that controls many degree scales and high normalized cross-flux without replacing `n` by `m` or inserting a degree-dependent constant.

### (b) Routes tried and the precise point of failure

1. **Counterexample search / complete bipartite and hub geometries.**  `K_{a,b}` satisfies a direct square-root birthday estimate even for extreme imbalance. The natural layered `{3,D}` forward-bias construction also fails: persistent forward bias forces exponential layer growth. These kill obvious counterexamples, but do not prove the general theorem.

2. **Irregular plan representation.**  Independent uniform local derangements reproduce every simple NBW path with exactly its correct product weight, so regularity is not needed merely to represent the walk by plans. The failure occurs later, in the packing of deviations.

3. **Heat-bath / local derangement cancellation.**  Replacing a forced local derangement by a reversible heat-bath removes the irrelevant `R_d` completion multiplicity. This yields a degree-free regular theorem and the earlier biregular result. It does not remove the global directed-edge volume `2m` from the non-returning packing step.

4. **Global packing via raw deviations.**  For a simple base path `rho`, the number of available raw deviations is
   \[
   W(\rho)=\sum_i(d(v_i)-2).
   \]
   Edge-disjoint deviation segments give a bound proportional to `2m/(L W(rho))`. In a regular graph, `2m` and `W` cancel to the required `n/(Lk)` scale; in a sparse-corridor/dense-reservoir graph they need not.

5. **Homogeneous-degree localization.**  Erdős–Kelly regular completion plus a random-time window yields a degree-independent regular bound and controls paths with few degree changes. It cannot control a path that changes degree scales often.

6. **Adaptive factorial crossing tail.**  For a cut `U union W`, on the whole-path simplicity event, distinct queried vertices give
   \[
   \Pr(E,Y\ge r)\le e_r(q)\le Q^r/r!.
   \]
   This proves the source square-root law for two-degree graphs with bounded normalized high-to-low cross-flux. It becomes ineffective when that normalized cross-flux diverges.

7. **Marker-preserving tail splicing.**  For two simple NBW paths, valid tail swaps preserve the exact product path weight. This removes the apparent degree-ratio Jacobian. The unresolved part is a bounded-multiplicity global switching that turns many overlaps of deviation trajectories into *single-path* self-intersections rather than merely another pair of simple paths.

8. **Bounded-degree literature.**  Alon's old draft and the Bencs et al. theorem control bounded maximum degree (at a polylogarithmic loss in the available statements), while Dozier controls fixed regular degree sharply. None supplies a reduction from arbitrary high degrees to those settings.

No route above is being rebranded as an unrestricted theorem.

### (c) What a full proof would have to contain that no route yet contains

A full proof needs one of two genuinely new ingredients:

- a **degree-free pack-or-overlap theorem**: for the family of length-`L` external deviation trajectories from a simple base plan, either enough trajectories are vertex-disjoint to force an `O(n/L)` packing bound, or their vertex overlaps can be converted, by a weight-preserving switching of uniformly bounded multiplicity, into returning/self-intersecting deviations; or
- an entirely different **vertex-block birthday inequality** for the directed-edge NBW operator that sees the `n` vertex blocks rather than the `2m` oriented-edge states.

The present R01 work has local pieces of both approaches, but not the missing global inequality.

The simplest residual already appears inside the two-degree world:

G\text{ has degree set }\{3,D\},
\qquad
A(G)=\frac{e(V_3,V_D)}{D-1}\to\infty.
\tag{S}

The preceding bounded-cross-flux theorem handles `A(G)=O(1)`. Thus even (S), with arbitrarily many alternating `3 <-> D` switches, remains a clean minimal laboratory for a future reassignment.

- **Dozier 2026:** exact `sqrt(n)` for fixed-degree regular graphs. Its plan proof packs non-returning deviation segments using `nd` directed edges and divides by `(d-2)k`; regularity makes the degree factors cancel.

The missing full-source ingredient is exactly uniform control when no maximum degree, regularity or expansion assumption is available.

## 4. Method #7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: Poissonize path length to repair the tail-swap mismatch

The previous two-path switching route had a basic bookkeeping defect: if simple paths `P,Q` of equal deterministic length meet at times `i,j`, swapping tails produces new paths of lengths `i+(k-j)` and `j+(k-i)`, so individual fixed lengths are not preserved when `i != j`.

A natural way around that is to pass to a grand-canonical / geometrically stopped ensemble. Give a finite NBW path `P` weight

\[
z^{|P|}\,w(P),
\qquad 0<z<1,
\]

where `w(P)` is its ordinary NBW path probability. For a pair `(P,Q)`, a tail swap at an arbitrary common vertex preserves the **total length** and therefore exactly preserves

\[
z^{|P|+|Q|}w(P)w(Q).
\]

This removes the unequal-time Jacobian completely. Under the simple-path conditional law, two independent paths of typical length `ell` have expected vertex-set overlap at least of order `ell^2/n` by Cauchy–Schwarz, so the grand-canonical ensemble is a plausible setting for a true two-path birthday switching.

**Why this route did not close the problem in this run.** A first mutual intersection of two individually simple paths can be tail-swapped into two other individually simple paths (the standard theta-shaped configuration). Thus overlap plus weight preservation alone does not inject into the complement of simplicity. One still needs a second operation — likely reversing one of the two external prefixes — that converts an overlap of two deviation orbits into a return to the original base path with uniformly bounded reconstruction multiplicity. I did not obtain that bounded-multiplicity statement, so no theorem is claimed from this route.

This is nevertheless a sharper handoff than the fixed-length version: **Poissonization solves the length mismatch; only the simple-pair-to-single-self-intersection conversion remains.**

## 5. Method #17 — LOOK FOR YOUR NUMBERS SOMEWHERE ELSE: the older Alon draft and Markov-chain birthday machinery

The genuine gap is the degree-unbounded regime.

The classical birthday-paradox-for-Markov-chains literature suggests a second translation: the NBW is a Markov chain on oriented edges with **uniform stationary measure** because its transition matrix is doubly stochastic. But the naive state-space cardinality is `2m`, and black-box collision theorems then see `sqrt(m)` rather than the required `sqrt(n)`. The right abstraction therefore has to remember that the collision event is a collision of **vertex blocks** of oriented edges.

This literature transfer did not itself move `alpha_univ`, but it identifies the correct target theorem: a partitioned-state birthday bound for the special NBW operator, not a generic state-collision theorem.

## 6. Method #29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY: degree compactification and the exact local operator boundary

Write the oriented-edge transition operator as

\[
Q((u,v),(v,w))=\frac1{d(v)-1}\mathbf 1_{\{w\ne u\}},
\]

and let `R` reverse orientation. The elementary block calculation

\[
(QR f)(u,v)
=
\frac1{d(v)-1}
\sum_{w\sim v,\,w\ne u} f(w,v)
\]

shows why simply declaring high degree to be the obstruction is misleading. On the incoming-edge block at a vertex `v`, `QR` is the matrix

\[
A_v=\frac{J-I}{d(v)-1}.
\]

Its constant direction has eigenvalue `1`, while every orthogonal direction has eigenvalue `-1/(d(v)-1)`. Thus the nonconstant local singular value is exactly

\[
\rho_v=\frac1{d(v)-1}\in(0,1/2].
\]

Compactifying by allowing the boundary value `rho_v=0` corresponds to `d(v)=infinity`; at that boundary **local memory is maximally contracted**, not preserved. So a bad high-degree family cannot be bad merely because a single local derangement has too many states. The surviving obstruction sits in the `n`-dimensional collection of vertex-block constant directions and how orientation reversal transports mass between those blocks.

I do **not** promote this elementary factorization as a new publication result; closely related non-backtracking operator decompositions are standard. Its value here is diagnostic. It says that any operator proof must control repeated projections onto moving vertex-block constant subspaces. No such degree-free collision inequality was completed here.

The natural next operator target for a future reassignment is therefore:

> derive a birthday/collision inequality for the sequence of vertex-block projections generated by `Q=(block\ contraction)R`, exploiting the uniform `<=1/2` contraction off the block constants while measuring collisions in only `n` blocks.
