## 4. #1 CHANGE THE CATEGORY — independently reduce switching to a Laplace-ratio problem

The most useful category change is from a randomly switched planar dynamical system to a deterministic transform problem.

On Malrieu's quadrant `\theta\in(-\pi/2,0)`, set

\[
t=-\tan\theta>0,\qquad \phi(t)=t-t^{-1},\qquad u_r(t)=rH(\theta;r).
\]

Because

\[
2\cot(2\theta)=t-t^{-1}
\]

and `\sec^2(y)dy=-ds` under `s=-\tan y`, the source formula becomes exactly

\[
H(t;r)=e^{-r\phi(t)}\int_0^t e^{r\phi(s)}\,ds,
\]

hence

\[
u_r'(t)=r\left[1-(1+t^{-2})u_r(t)\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Using the source's quadrant symmetries and the exact Jacobian `d\theta=-dt/(1+t^2)`, the normalization and Lyapunov numerator reduce to

\[
J(r)=\int_0^\infty \left[1+(t^{-2}-1)u_r(t)\right]dt,
\]

\[
K(r)=\int_0^\infty\left[\frac{t}{1+t^2}-\frac{u_r(t)}t\right]dt,
\qquad G(r)=\frac{K(r)}{J(r)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now set

\[
x=t-t^{-1},\qquad
 t(x)=\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2+4}}2,
\qquad
a(x)=\frac{t(x)^2}{1+t(x)^2}
      =\frac12\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+4}}\right).
\]

Since `dx/dt=1+t^{-2}`, equation (4.1) becomes

\[
\frac{du}{dx}=r(a-u),
\]

with the causal solution

\[
u_r(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{-q}a(x-q/r)\,dq.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Pairing `x` and `-x`, using `t(-x)=1/t(x)` and `a(-x)=1-a(x)`, and then writing `q=r\delta`, gives the exact ratio

\[
G(r)=\frac{\mathcal L k(r)}{\mathcal L j(r)},
\qquad
\mathcal L f(r)=\int_0^\infty e^{-r\delta}f(\delta)\,d\delta,
\tag{4.4}
\]

where

\[
k(\delta)=\int_0^\infty
\frac{a(x+\delta)-a(x-\delta)}{\sqrt{x^2+4}}\,dx,
\tag{4.5}
\]

\[
j(\delta)=\int_0^\infty
\left[1-\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+4}}
\big(a(x+\delta)+a(x-\delta)-1\big)\right]dx.
\tag{4.6}
\]

I checked the signs, normalization factor and orientation in this reduction independently. In particular, no hidden reciprocal of `r` enters (4.4); the source's displayed generator really uses jump rate.

**Outcome of #1:** the stochastic problem becomes a precise deterministic statement about one ratio of one-sided Laplace transforms. This change of category exposes the global shape mechanism cleanly.

## 5. The input quotient has exactly one turn

\[

\[

\tag{5.1}
\]

\[
j''(\delta)=2(b*b)(\delta)>0,
\tag{5.2}
\]

with

\[
k(0)=0,\qquad j(0)=\pi,\qquad j'(0)=0.
\]

The only delicate point in (5.1) is that `w` is not integrable. That does **not** invalidate the radial-decreasing convolution step: truncate `w` on centered finite intervals, apply the one-dimensional interval-overlap/layer-cake argument to each truncation, and pass monotonically. Hence `k'` is nonincreasing on `[0,\infty)`, so

\[
k''(\delta)\le0.
\]

Define

\[
h(\delta)=\frac{k(\delta)}{j(\delta)},
\qquad
q(\delta)=k'(\delta)j(\delta)-k(\delta)j'(\delta)=j(\delta)^2h'(\delta).
\]

Then

\[
q'(\delta)=k''(\delta)j(\delta)-k(\delta)j''(\delta)<0
\qquad(\delta>0),
\tag{5.3}
\]

because `j>0`, `k>0`, `j''>0` and `k''\le0`. Also

\[
q(0)=k'(0)j(0)=\frac{\pi^2}{8}>0.

At infinity, `k(\delta)=\log\delta+O(1)` while `j(\delta)=\delta+O(1)`, so `h(\delta)\to0`; near zero, `h(\delta)\sim\delta/8>0`. Therefore `q` cannot stay nonnegative forever. Since it is strictly decreasing, it crosses zero exactly once. Thus there exists a unique `\delta_*>0` with

\[

h'(\delta_*)=0,\qquad

\tag{5.4}

This is the exact input one-turn property needed later.

## 7. #27 SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS — a self-contained level-set proof

Rather than directly differentiating `G(r)` for all `r`, fix a horizontal level.

Let

\[
h_*:=h(\delta_*),\qquad 0<c<h_*.
\]

Set

\[
\phi_c(\delta)=k(\delta)-cj(\delta)
=j(\delta)(h(\delta)-c).
\]

By (5.4), there are exactly two zeros

\[
0<\alpha(c)<\delta_*<\beta(c)<\infty
\]

and the strict sign pattern is

\[
\phi_c:\quad -,+,-.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Its Laplace transform is

\[
F_c(r):=\mathcal L\phi_c(r)
=\mathcal L j(r)\,[G(r)-c].
\tag{7.2}
\]

Because `\mathcal L j(r)>0`, positive roots of `F_c` are exactly the positive solutions of `G(r)=c`.

### 7.1 Independent two-sign-change lemma

Assume for contradiction that `F_c` has three distinct positive zeros

\[
0<r_1<r_2<r_3.
\]

Choose a nonzero coefficient vector `(A_1,A_2,A_3)` so that

\[
Q(\delta)=\sum_{i=1}^3 A_i e^{-r_i\delta}
\]

vanishes at both `\alpha` and `\beta`. Such a vector exists because these are two homogeneous linear equations in three unknowns.

A nonzero combination of the three exponentials has at most two zeros counting multiplicity. Indeed, factor `e^{-r_1\delta}` and put

\[
y=e^{-(r_2-r_1)\delta},
\qquad
p=\frac{r_3-r_1}{r_2-r_1}>1.
\]

The remaining factor is

\[
f(y)=A_1+A_2y+A_3y^p,
\]

whose derivative

\[
f'(y)=A_2+pA_3y^{p-1}
\]

has at most one positive zero. Generalized Rolle therefore gives at most two zeros of `f`, counting multiplicity. Since `Q` already vanishes at the two distinct points `\alpha,\beta`, those are its only roots and both are simple.

Thus `Q` changes sign exactly at `\alpha,\beta`; after multiplying all `A_i` by `-1` if necessary, `Q` has the same `-,+,-` sign pattern as `\phi_c`. Hence

\[
Q(\delta)\phi_c(\delta)>0
\]

except at the two zero points, and absolute convergence is automatic because `Q` is a finite positive-rate exponential combination while `\phi_c` grows at most linearly/logarithmically. Therefore

\[
\int_0^\infty Q(\delta)\phi_c(\delta)\,d\delta>0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

But the assumed transform zeros give

\[
\int_0^\infty Q\phi_c
=\sum_{i=1}^3 A_iF_c(r_i)=0,
\tag{7.4}
\]

a contradiction. Thus for every `0<c<h_*`,

\[
\#\{r>0:G(r)=c\}\le2.
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is a complete special-case proof of the needed Laplace Descartes principle; it does not depend logically on the 2025 Karp--Vishnyakova--Zhang theorem.

### 7.2 Two crossings force one connected hill

For each finite `r>0`, the probability measure

\[
d\mu_r(\delta)=
\frac{e^{-r\delta}j(\delta)}{\mathcal Lj(r)}\,d\delta
\]

has strictly positive density and

\[
G(r)=\int h(\delta)\,d\mu_r(\delta).
\]

Since `h<h_*` except at one point,

\[
0<G(r)<h_*
\tag{7.6}
\]

for every finite `r`.

The endpoint limits proved below imply that for any `c>0`, every component of

\[
S_c=\{r>0:G(r)>c\}
\]

is bounded away from both `0` and infinity. If a nonempty `S_c` had two components, their four boundary points would be four distinct solutions of `G(r)=c`, contradicting (7.5). Hence every nonempty strict superlevel set is one interval.

This one-dimensional quasiconcavity forces `G` to be nondecreasing up to a maximizer and nonincreasing afterward. `G` is real analytic on `(0,\infty)` because the Laplace integrals and all `r`-derivatives converge locally uniformly. A nontrivial plateau would therefore force global constancy. Since `G>0` inside and tends to zero at both ends, there is exactly one global maximizer `r_*`, and the order monotonicity is strict:

\[
G(r_1)<G(r_2)\quad(0<r_1<r_2\le r_*),
\]

\[
G(r_1)>G(r_2)\quad(r_*\le r_1<r_2<\infty).
\tag{7.7}
\]

### 7.3 Exclude stationary inflections away from the mode

Strict order monotonicity alone does not exclude an isolated stationary inflection. Suppose `r_0\ne r_*` and `G'(r_0)=0`. Let `c=G(r_0)`. By (7.6), `0<c<h_*`, so `\phi_c` has exactly the two sign changes `\alpha<\beta`.

Because `G` is analytic and strictly monotone through `r_0`, the first nonzero Taylor term of `G-G(r_0)` has odd order at least three. Hence

\[
F_c(r)=\mathcal Lj(r)[G(r)-c]
\]

has a zero of multiplicity at least three at `r_0`. Differentiating under the integral gives

\[
\int_0^\infty \delta^m e^{-r_0\delta}\phi_c(\delta)\,d\delta=0,
\qquad m=0,1,2.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Now take

\[
P(\delta)=-(\delta-\alpha)(\delta-\beta).
\]

`P` has exactly the same `-,+,-` sign pattern as `\phi_c`, so

\[
\int_0^\infty P(\delta)e^{-r_0\delta}\phi_c(\delta)\,d\delta>0.
\tag{7.9}
\]

But `P` has degree two, so the three moment identities (7.8) force that same integral to equal zero. Contradiction.

Therefore no off-mode stationary point exists. Differentiability plus (7.7) yields

\[
G'(r)>0\quad(0<r<r_*),
\qquad
G'(r)<0\quad(r>r_*),
\]

and Fermat gives `G'(r_*)=0`.

**Outcome of #27:** swapping the universal derivative problem for a family of fixed-level zero-count problems gives a short, self-contained global proof and independently validates the package's most delicate shape-transfer step.
