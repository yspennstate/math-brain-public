## 4. Method #2 — counterexample-first: complete bipartite graphs fail decisively as a counterexample

The most obvious unbounded-degree test family is `K_{a,b}`, including the highly imbalanced regime `a=3`, `b=n-3`. It does not threaten the conjecture. In fact the birthday estimate is elementary and uniform in the degree ratio.

### Proposition 4.1 — exact simple-walk probability on `K_{a,b}`

Let the starting oriented edge go from the `A` side (`|A|=a`) to the `B` side (`|B|=b`), and let the walk have `k` edges total. Put

\[
t=k-1,\qquad r=\left\lceil\frac t2\right\rceil,
\qquad s=\left\lfloor\frac t2\right\rfloor .
\]

If `r<a` and `s<b`, then

\[
\Pr(\text{simple})
=
\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{j-1}{a-1}\right)
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left(1-\frac{j-1}{b-1}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

If either `r>=a` or `s>=b`, the probability is zero. Consequently, for `t>=4`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(\text{simple})
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{(t-2)^2}{2(n-2)}\right),
\qquad n=a+b .}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the full square-root conclusion holds uniformly over **all** complete bipartite graphs with both parts of size at least three, even when the degree ratio diverges.

### Proof

After the fixed first edge, the walk alternately chooses new vertices from `A` and `B`. Whenever it is at a `B` vertex, the immediately previous `A` vertex is already excluded by non-backtracking, so when `j-1` other `A` vertices have also appeared before, the probability that the next `A` choice is new is

\[
1-\frac{j-1}{a-1}.
\]

The analogous factor on the other side is `1-(j-1)/(b-1)`. Multiplying the conditional probabilities gives (4.1).

Using `log(1-x) <= -x`,

\[
\log\Pr(\text{simple})
\le
-\frac{r(r-1)}{2(a-1)}
-\frac{s(s-1)}{2(b-1)}.
\]

By Cauchy–Schwarz in Engel form,

\[
\frac{r(r-1)}{a-1}+\frac{s(s-1)}{b-1}
\ge
\frac{\big(\sqrt{r(r-1)}+\sqrt{s(s-1)}\big)^2}{n-2}.
\]

For `t>=4`, both `r,s>=2` and `sqrt(q(q-1)) >= q-1`, hence the numerator is at least

\[
((r-1)+(s-1))^2=(t-2)^2.
\]

Substitution proves (4.2). QED.

This rules out the simplest “one tiny side / one huge side” hub geometry. In particular, **degree imbalance alone is not the mechanism of a counterexample**.

The recurring obstruction is edge balance: sustained forward drift across many undirected layers requires the layer sizes or interface degrees to compensate, and the construction runs out of vertices rapidly. ## 5. Method #18 — directed-edge language and an exact irregular plan representation

### Lemma 5.1 — NBW on directed edges is doubly stochastic on every undirected graph

Let `\vec E` be the oriented edges and

\[
Q((u,v),(v,w))=\frac{1}{d(v)-1},\qquad w\ne u.
\]

For a fixed target directed edge `(v,w)`,

\[
\sum_{u\sim v,\,u\ne w}Q((u,v),(v,w))
=(d(v)-1)\frac1{d(v)-1}=1.
\]

Rows also sum to one, so `Q` is doubly stochastic. Hence the uniform measure on oriented edges is stationary for NBW **without any regularity assumption**.

Its head-vertex marginal is

\[
\rho(v)=\frac{d(v)}{2m}.
\]

Therefore two independent stationary head samples collide with probability

\[
\sum_v\rho(v)^2\ge\frac1n.
\]

So irregular stationary marginals cannot make an independent birthday experiment slower than the uniform `1/n` collision scale. The hard part is temporal dependence/non-mixing from an arbitrary starting edge.

### Lemma 5.2 — local-derangement plans represent the simple-walk event exactly at variable degrees

For every vertex `v`, let `D_v` be the set of derangements of its incident neighbours. Choose independently and uniformly `pi_v in D_v`. Given an incoming oriented edge `(u,v)`, let the plan send it to `(v,pi_v(u))`. Fix a starting edge and iterate the resulting plan.

Then for every `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm plan}(\text{the first }k\text{ edges form a simple path})
=
\Pr_{\rm NBW}(\text{the first }k\text{ edges are simple}).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

Fix any particular simple path

\[
(v_0,v_1),(v_1,v_2),\ldots,(v_{k-1},v_k).
\]

Its internal vertices `v_1,...,v_{k-1}` are distinct. For each internal vertex `v_i`, symmetry of uniform derangements gives

\[
\Pr\big(\pi_{v_i}(v_{i-1})=v_{i+1}\big)=\frac1{d(v_i)-1}.
\]

Because the internal vertices are distinct, these constraints involve independent plan coordinates. Thus the plan probability of this particular simple path is

\[
\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac1{d(v_i)-1},
\]

which is exactly its NBW probability. Summing over all simple paths proves (5.1). QED.

This corrects the narrow diagnosis in `G0012-A001`: **no degree-ratio Jacobian is needed merely to represent simple NBWs by plans**. Moreover, the local operation `pi_v -> pi_v^{-1}` preserves the uniform law on `D_v` for each `v`, regardless of `d(v)`. Thus inversion itself also has no degree-ratio Jacobian. What remains nontrivial is whether Dozier's *whole deviation involution and its counting measure* can be normalized uniformly when the number of available deviations varies with the visited degrees.

I make no novelty claim for Lemmas 5.1–5.2; they are the correct language for the next attack.

## 6. Method #20 — the exact point where the regular plan proof stops

The regular proof has a non-returning-deviation packing step. The same geometry can be written in variable degrees and exposes the obstruction exactly.

For a simple plan path `v_0,...,v_k`, define its available trajectory-deviation count

\[
W(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(d(v_i)-2).
\tag{6.1}
\]

At internal vertex `v_i` there are `d(v_i)-2` alternative non-backtracking outgoing edges distinct from the one used by the base path. If a deviation is non-returning and remains simple for `L` further steps, the last `L` **oriented edges** of two distinct such trajectory deviations are disjoint by the same injective-plan argument used in the regular case. Since the graph has `2m` oriented edges,

\[
N_{\rm nonreturn,L\text{-}simple}(\rho)
\le \frac{2m}{L}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently the direct irregular analogue of the regular packing fraction is only

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_{\rm nonreturn,L\text{-}simple}(\rho)}{W(\rho)}
\le
\frac{2m}{L\,W(\rho)}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

In a `d`-regular graph, `2m=nd` and `W=(d-2)(k-1)`, so choosing `L` of order `n/k` makes (6.3) small and still permits `L=o(k)` exactly when `k^2 >> n`. This is Dozier's square-root mechanism.

For unrestricted minimum-degree-three graphs, (6.3) demands roughly

\[
L\gg \frac{m}{W(\rho)}
\quad\text{while the final switching needs}\quad
L\ll k,
\]

hence the naive port needs

\[
\boxed{k\,W(\rho)\gg m.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Minimum degree gives only `W(\rho)>=k-1`, while `m` can be much larger than `n`. Therefore the **global edge count is the wrong packing volume**. This, not a generic “degree ratios break reversal” statement, is the first clean obstruction.

There is a second degree-sensitive bookkeeping issue later in the regular proof: if one fixes a deterministic contingency derangement, the number of possible preimages contains the derangement number `R_d`. With variable unbounded degrees that constant is no longer uniform. A likely repair is to replace fixed contingencies by a local heat-bath resampling of `pi_v` conditioned on changing the used outgoing edge; the uniform derangement law is then the natural reference measure. I did not promote this to a lemma because the full reverse multiplicity estimate is not yet closed.

### What the next proof must contain

A successful port now needs one of two genuinely new ingredients:

1. **Local packing:** replace the global `2m` in (6.2) by the size of the oriented-edge region actually reachable by the first `L` steps of deviations, and prove that this local volume is `O(n)` in the weighted sense relevant to `W(\rho)`; or
2. **Shared-high-degree switching:** if many deviation trajectories use the same high-degree vertices via different oriented edges, exploit that sharing itself to manufacture collisions rather than paying degree as packing capacity.

This is substantially sharper than the inherited “unbounded degrees are hard” obstruction.
