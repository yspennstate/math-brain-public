## 2. Preserved lacunary critical law and the idea written before validation

Write

\[
B(k)=((m-1)k-1)m^k.
\]

Choose

\[
d_j=2^{-j},\qquad p_j=\frac{d_j}{B(K_j)+1},\qquad
\mathbf P(X_0=K_j)=p_j,
\]

with

\[
\mathbf P(X_0=0)=1-\sum_jp_j.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_j B(K_j)p_j=\sum_j(d_j-p_j)=1-\sum_jp_j,
\]

so (1.1) holds exactly. Also `E[X_0m^{X_0}]<infinity` because `K_jm^{K_j}p_j <= C_m d_j`.

The earlier construction set

\[
L_j=j,\qquad n_j=K_j-L_j,
\qquad b_j=1-(1-p_j)^{m^{n_j}}, \tag{2.1}
\]

and chose the `K_j` so remote that, at time `n_j`, survival was asymptotically caused by seeing a `K_j` atom among the `m^{n_j}` leaves. One such atom forces `X_{n_j}>=L_j`, and the resulting conditioned laws are not tight.

The new idea, written down before any checking in this run, was:

> Do not sample the counterexample only at the single observation time `n_j`. Center time at `n_j`, let the offset vary in a growing window, and ask whether the entire survival pulse has a deterministic local profile. If so, compare its one-step growth with the universal upper bound `q_{n+1}<=m q_n`.

This uses no finite computation; the needed validation is analytic.

## 3. #7 TAKE THE LIMIT — a growing-window pulse profile

Let

\[
q_n:=\mathbf P(X_n>0),\qquad
R_j:=\lfloor j/2\rfloor,\qquad
\eta_j:=2^{-j}. \tag{3.1}
\]

The recursive choice of the `K_j` may be strengthened, without changing the source domain or the exact critical identity, so that all earlier conclusions remain true and in addition the following two uniform isolation conditions hold.

### Old atoms

Let `X^{<j}` denote the recursion whose initial law keeps only the atoms `K_i`, `i<j`, and moves all remaining mass to zero. Its critical defect is

\[
\sum_{i<j} d_i-1=-\sum_{i\ge j}d_i<0, \tag{3.2}
\]

so this finite-support law is strictly subcritical. For finite-support strictly subcritical data, the standard subcritical estimate gives constants `C_j,c_j>0` such that

\[
\mathbf P(X_n^{<j}>0)\le C_je^{-c_jn}. \tag{3.3}
\]

Because an exponential in `K_j` beats the `1/K_j` size of the current pulse, enlarge `K_j` until

\[
C_j e^{-c_j(n_j-R_j)}
\le \eta_j m^{-R_j}b_j. \tag{3.4}
\]

The same enlargement can simultaneously preserve the previous strengthening `n_j^2b_j -> infinity`.

### Future atoms

After `K_j` is fixed, choose every later `K_ell` so large that, for each already fixed `j`,

\[
m^{n_j+R_j}p_\ell
\le
\eta_j\,2^{-(\ell-j)}m^{-R_j}b_j,
\qquad \ell>j. \tag{3.5}
\]

At stage `ell` this is only a finite set of old constraints, and `p_ell ->0` as `K_ell->infinity`, so the induction closes. Summing over `ell>j` shows that at every depth `n_j+r` with `|r|<=R_j`, the probability that any future atom appears is at most

\[
\eta_j m^{-R_j}b_j. \tag{3.6}
\]

### Current atom

For `|r|<=R_j`, let

\[
b_{j,r}:=1-(1-p_j)^{m^{n_j+r}}. \tag{3.7}
\]

Set `lambda_j=m^{n_j}p_j`. From the exact form of `p_j`,

\[
\lambda_j\asymp_m \frac{d_jm^{-j}}{K_j}. \tag{3.8}
\]

Moreover

\[
\max_{|r|\le R_j} m^{n_j+r}p_j
=m^{R_j}\lambda_j
\ll_m \frac{2^{-j}m^{-j/2}}{K_j}\longrightarrow0. \tag{3.9}
\]

Hence the elementary rare-event expansion is uniform throughout the whole window:

\[
b_{j,r}=(1+o(1))m^{n_j+r}p_j
=(1+o(1))m^r b_j,\qquad |r|\le R_j, \tag{3.10}
\]

where the `o(1)` is uniform in `r`.

A single `K_j` leaf at depth `n_j+r` forces

\[
X_{n_j+r}\ge K_j-(n_j+r)=j-r\ge j-R_j>0. \tag{3.11}
\]

Thus `q_{n_j+r}>=b_{j,r}`. Conversely, if there is no current or future atom, survival can only come from the old finite-support truncation. By (3.4)--(3.6), uniformly for `|r|<=R_j`,

\[
q_{n_j+r}
\le b_{j,r}+2\eta_jm^{-R_j}b_j. \tag{3.12}
\]

Since (3.10) implies `b_{j,r}>=(1-o(1))m^{-R_j}b_j`, the error in (3.12) is `o(b_{j,r})` uniformly. Therefore:

### Theorem R01-D0057-C003 — uniform survival pulse

For the refined source-valid critical law above,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|r|\le R_j}
\left|\frac{q_{n_j+r}}{m^rb_j}-1\right|\longrightarrow0,
\qquad R_j=\lfloor j/2\rfloor\longrightarrow\infty.
} \tag{3.13}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{|r|\le R_j}
\mathbf P\!\left(X_{n_j+r}\ge j-r\mid X_{n_j+r}>0\right)
\longrightarrow1.
} \tag{3.14}
\]

The second display follows because the current-atom event has probability `b_{j,r}` and forces (3.11), while the whole survival probability is `(1+o(1))b_{j,r}` uniformly.

This is a genuine strengthening of the old one-time subsequence statement: a single critical law has longer and longer time windows on which the whole survival pulse and its escaping conditioned mass are asymptotically explicit.

## 4. #12 FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED — maximal one-step growth and a pulse invariant

For every initial law and every `n`, survival at generation `n+1` requires at least one of the `m` children to be positive. Therefore

\[
q_{n+1}
\le 1-(1-q_n)^m
\le m q_n. \tag{4.1}
\]

So `m` is the absolute largest possible one-step multiplicative growth factor for the survival probability.

From (3.13), uniformly for `-R_j<=r<R_j`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{q_{n_j+r+1}}{q_{n_j+r}}\longrightarrow m.
} \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the counterexample does not merely violate the expected slow critical behavior: on windows whose length tends to infinity, it asymptotically saturates the universal upper growth bound at every step.

There is also a simple conserved quantity inside the dominant single-spike mechanism. The residual height drops by one when the observation time advances by one, while the number of available leaves grows by a factor `m`. Combining (3.10),

\[
m^{j-r}b_{j,r}
=(1+o(1))m^j b_j
\quad\text{uniformly for }|r|\le R_j. \tag{4.3}
\]

This is the local pulse invariant: **height weight lost by erosion is exactly compensated by leaf multiplicity.** It explains why the survival probability can grow at the maximal factor `m` even though the weighted critical mass of the pulse stays essentially unchanged.

The invariant also clarifies why this mechanism still does not refute source Conjecture 4(b). From (3.8),

\[
n_jm^j b_j\asymp_m d_j=2^{-j}\longrightarrow0. \tag{4.4}
\]

The same is true uniformly over the `R_j=o(n_j)` window after replacing `m^jb_j` by `m^{j-r}b_{j,r}`. Thus the isolated pulse is enormous on the `q_n` / `n^{-2}` scale but invisible on the natural `n(G_n(m)-1)` lower-bound scale. This is a structural limitation of this particular isolated-critical-budget attack, not evidence that Conjecture 4(b) is true.

## 5. #27 SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS — from one bad subsequence to an expanding bad window

The earlier refutation only needed

\[
\exists n_j\to\infty:\quad
\mathbf P(X_{n_j}\ge j\mid X_{n_j}>0)\to1.
\]

The strengthened construction swaps the order of the time-offset quantifiers:

\[
\boxed{
\exists\text{ one critical law and }R_j\to\infty
\text{ such that the pulse asymptotics hold for every }|r|\le R_j.
} \tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, the one-step ratio has the extremal opposite behavior from the geometric-Yaglom prediction over an expanding interval. The earlier R01 reduction showed that the source geometric limit would force

\[
\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_n}\longrightarrow1. \tag{5.2}
\]

Here instead, along all interior points of the expanding pulse windows,

\[
\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_n}=m+o(1). \tag{5.3}
\]

This makes the obstruction much sharper: it is not a single accidental spike in one marginal but a mesoscopic episode of maximally amplifying survival produced by a remote critical atom.
