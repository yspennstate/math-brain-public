## 3. Preserve the source normalization

For a positive target law `mu` on `{−1,+1}^n`, write

\[
r_i^\mu(x):=\frac{\mu(x^i)}{\mu(x)}.
\]

Equation (2.13) specifies the rates only up to a global proportionality factor. I fix the factor by the source's own normalization sentence: when `mu` is uniform, all three implementations reduce to simple random walk on the `n`-cube, i.e. each coordinate flips at rate `1/n`.

Thus the three generators use

\[
c_{G,i}^\mu(x)=\frac1n\frac{2r_i^\mu(x)}{1+r_i^\mu(x)},
\tag{3.1G}
\]
\[
c_{M,i}^\mu(x)=\frac1n\min\{1,r_i^\mu(x)\},
\tag{3.1M}
\]
\[
c_{S,i}^\mu(x)=\frac1n\sqrt{r_i^\mu(x)}.
\tag{3.1S}
\]

A global deterministic rescaling would not change the existence of cutoff, but (3.1) makes the comparison to the source hypercube normalization exact.

# R01-I0113-X001 — dependency alone is insufficient: an independent target can destroy square-root cutoff

**STATUS: PROVED. This refutes a class of candidate conditions, not Salez Problem 2.7.**

For `n>=2`, let

\[
\nu_n
=
\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)
\otimes
\operatorname{Ber}(p_n)^{\otimes(n-1)},
\qquad
p_n=\frac{1}{1+4^{n^2}}.
\tag{X001.1}
\]

Here `Ber(p)` denotes the `{−1,+1}` law assigning `+1` probability `p`.
The coordinates are exactly independent, so every inter-coordinate dependence measure that vanishes on product laws is identically zero.

Consider the square-root sampler (3.1S). Because the target is product, the chain is a product of independent two-state chains.

For coordinate 1, the two flip rates are `1/n` and `1/n`, hence its relaxation rate is

\[
a_{1,n}=\frac2n.
\tag{X001.2}
\]

For every `i>=2`, the odds are

\[
\frac{p_n}{1-p_n}=4^{-n^2},
\]

so the two square-root flip rates are exactly

\[
\frac{2^{-n^2}}n,
\qquad
\frac{2^{n^2}}n,
\]

and hence

\[
a_{i,n}=\frac{2^{n^2}+2^{-n^2}}n.
\tag{X001.3}
\]

A two-state chain with stationary law `q` and total relaxation rate `a` has

\[
P_t(x,\cdot)-q=e^{-at}(\delta_x-q),
\]

and consequently its worst-case total-variation distance is at most `e^{-at}`; for the balanced first coordinate it is exactly `(1/2)e^{-at}`.

Let `d_n(t)` be the worst-case TV distance of the full square-root chain. Projection to the first coordinate gives, for every fixed `s>0`,

\[
 d_n(sn)\ge \frac12e^{-2s}.
\tag{X001.4}
\]

For product measures, TV is at most the sum of coordinate TVs, so

\[
 d_n(sn)
\le
\frac12e^{-2s}
+(n-1)\exp\{-s(2^{n^2}+2^{-n^2})\}.
\tag{X001.5}
\]

The second term tends to zero. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 d_n(sn)\longrightarrow \frac12e^{-2s}
\qquad(s>0).
}
\tag{X001.6}
\]

In particular, for every fixed `epsilon in (0,1/2)`, monotonicity and (X001.6) give

\[
\frac{t_{\rm mix}^{(n)}(\epsilon)}n
\longrightarrow
\frac12\log\frac1{2\epsilon}.
\tag{X001.7}
\]

For example,

\[
\frac{t_{\rm mix}(1/8)}{t_{\rm mix}(1/4)}\to2,
\]

so there is no cutoff.

### Exact consequence

Any proposed sufficient condition that is automatically satisfied by **every product target**, without any marginal non-degeneracy/effective-dimension requirement, cannot imply cutoff simultaneously for the three Problem-2.7 samplers: X001 is already product, yet the square-root sampler has a smooth one-coordinate limiting profile.

This does **not** refute the source problem. It identifies a necessary design feature of any successful common condition: it must prevent the square-root implementation from accelerating almost every coordinate away while only finitely many slow coordinates remain.

This was Section-B move #2.

# 4. Move #5 — parameterize the product reference and identify the right non-degeneracy quantity

Let

\[
\nu_n=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Ber}(p_{i,n}),
\qquad
\eta\le p_{i,n}\le1-\eta
\tag{4.1}
\]

for a fixed `eta in (0,1/2)`.

For each of the three implementations the `nu_n` chain factors into independent two-state coordinates. Let `a_{J,i,n}` denote the total relaxation rate of coordinate `i`, with `J in {G,M,S}`. Directly from (3.1),

\[
a_{G,i,n}=\frac2n,
\tag{4.2G}
\]

\[
a_{M,i,n}
=
\frac{1}{n\max\{p_{i,n},1-p_{i,n}\}},
\tag{4.2M}
\]

and

\[
a_{S,i,n}
=
\frac{1}{n\sqrt{p_{i,n}(1-p_{i,n})}}.
\tag{4.2S}
\]

Hence there are constants

\[
0<m_\eta\le M_\eta<\infty
\]

such that, simultaneously for all three implementations,

\[
\frac{m_\eta}{n}\le a_{J,i,n}\le\frac{M_\eta}{n}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is exactly what X001 violated: there the square-root `a_{i,n}` were unbounded multiples of `1/n` on `n-1` coordinates.

For each `J`, define

\[
S_{J,n}(t):=\sum_{i=1}^n e^{-2a_{J,i,n}t}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and let `t_{J,n}` be the unique solution of

\[
S_{J,n}(t_{J,n})=1.
\tag{4.5}
\]

From (4.3),

\[
\frac{n\log n}{2M_\eta}
\le t_{J,n}\le
\frac{n\log n}{2m_\eta}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus the natural product reference has a `Theta(n log n)` center and an `O(n)` candidate window for every implementation.

# R01-I0113-C001 — a common strong weak-interaction condition implies cutoff for all three samplers

**STATUS: PROVED as a mathematical theorem in this report. Source-level closure is NOT yet claimed: the hypothesis is deliberately strong and needs independent source-fit/prior-art audit.**

## The common condition

For a target `pi_n` with full support, suppose there exists a product reference `nu_n` as in (4.1) and define

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_n
:=
\max_{x\in\{-1,1\}^n}\max_{1\le i\le n}
\left|
\log\frac{r_i^{\pi_n}(x)}{r_i^{\nu_n}(x)}
\right|.
}
\tag{C001.1}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
h_n(x):=\log\frac{\pi_n(x)}{\nu_n(x)},
\]

then

\[
\Delta_n=\max_{x,i}|h_n(x^i)-h_n(x)|.
\tag{C001.2}
\]

Thus `Delta_n` is the sup norm of the discrete gradient of the **interaction correction** to a non-degenerate product law; one-site fields have been absorbed into `nu_n`.

Assume

\[
\boxed{
 n\log n\,\Delta_n\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{C001.3}
\]

Then, with the source normalization (3.1), each of the Gibbs, Metropolis and square-root samplers with stationary law `pi_n` exhibits worst-case total-variation cutoff. More precisely, sampler `J` has cutoff at the same first-order center `t_{J,n}` from (4.5), with mixing window `O_\eta(n)`.

This is one common explicitly stated weak-interaction hypothesis for all three implementations.

## Proof, Step 1 — the product references have cutoff

Fix one implementation `J` and suppress `J,n` from notation. For coordinate `i`, let

\[
z_i(t):=e^{-a_i t}.
\]

If the product chain starts from `x`, then its `i`th coordinate law at time `t` is

\[
\mu_{i,t}
=\nu_i+z_i(t)(\delta_{x_i}-\nu_i).
\tag{C001.4}
\]

### Upper bound

For a two-state coordinate,

\[
\chi^2(\mu_{i,t}\|\nu_i)
=z_i(t)^2\left(\frac1{\nu_i(x_i)}-1\right)
\le K_\eta z_i(t)^2,
\tag{C001.5}
\]

where

\[
K_\eta:=\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}.
\]

Chi-square tensorizes exactly for products:

\[
1+\chi^2(\mu_t\|\nu)
=
\prod_i\left(1+\chi^2(\mu_{i,t}\|\nu_i)\right)
\le
\exp\{K_\eta S_n(t)\}.
\tag{C001.6}
\]

Hence

\[
d_{\rm TV}(\mu_t,\nu)
\le
\frac12\sqrt{e^{K_\eta S_n(t)}-1}.
\tag{C001.7}
\]

At `t=t_n+An`, (4.3) and `S_n(t_n)=1` imply

\[
S_n(t_n+An)\le e^{-2m_\eta A}.
\tag{C001.8}
\]

Therefore the worst-case TV distance tends uniformly to zero as first `n->infty` and then `A->infty`.

### Lower bound

Choose the initial state coordinatewise to be a less likely stationary state, so

\[
|x_i-p_i|=\max\{p_i,1-p_i\}\ge\frac12.
\]

Let `sigma_i=sign(x_i-p_i)` and define the time-dependent test statistic

\[
T_t:=\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i z_i(t)(X_i-p_i).
\tag{C001.9}
\]

Under stationarity,

\[
E_\nu[T_t]=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(T_t)\le\frac14 S_n(t).
\tag{C001.10}
\]

Under the law at time `t` started from `x`, (C001.4) gives

\[
E_x[T_t]
=
\sum_i z_i(t)^2|x_i-p_i|
\ge\frac12S_n(t),
\tag{C001.11}
\]

while independence still gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}_x(T_t)\le\frac14S_n(t).
\tag{C001.12}
\]

Use the event

\[
A_t=\{T_t>S_n(t)/4\}.
\]

Chebyshev applied to (C001.10)--(C001.12) yields

\[
\nu(A_t)\le\frac4{S_n(t)},
\qquad
P_x(A_t)\ge1-\frac4{S_n(t)}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}(P_t(x,\cdot),\nu)
\ge1-\frac8{S_n(t)}.
}
\tag{C001.13}
\]

At `t=t_n-An`, (4.3) gives

\[
S_n(t_n-An)\ge e^{2m_\eta A}.
\tag{C001.14}
\]

Hence the worst-case TV distance tends uniformly to one as first `n->infty` and then `A->infty`.

Combining (C001.7)--(C001.14), every product reference sampler has cutoff at `t_n`, with an `O_\eta(n)` window. This elementary proof is included for self-containment; product-chain cutoff theory is classical and is not claimed as novel.

## Proof, Step 2 — `pi_n` is uniformly close to `nu_n`

From (C001.2), the oscillation of `h_n` across the cube is at most its diameter times the edge Lipschitz constant:

\[
\operatorname{osc}(h_n)\le n\Delta_n.
\tag{C001.15}
\]

Because

\[
E_{\nu_n}[e^{h_n}]=1,
\]

we must have

\[
\min h_n\le0\le\max h_n.
\]

Together with (C001.15),

\[
|h_n(x)|\le n\Delta_n
\quad\text{for all }x.
\tag{C001.16}
\]

Consequently

\[
\|\pi_n-\nu_n\|_{\rm TV}
\le
\frac12\left(e^{n\Delta_n}-1\right)
=o(1).
\tag{C001.17}
\]

## Proof, Step 3 — all three generators are uniformly close

Write the normalized rate maps in the log-odds variable `u=log r`:

\[
g_G(u)=\frac{2e^u}{1+e^u},
\qquad
 g_M(u)=\min\{1,e^u\},
\qquad
 g_S(u)=e^{u/2}.
\tag{C001.18}
\]

Their logarithms satisfy

\[
\left|(\log g_G)'(u)\right|=\frac1{1+e^u}\le1,
\tag{C001.19G}
\]

\[
\log g_M(u)=\min\{0,u\}
\quad\text{is 1-Lipschitz},
\tag{C001.19M}
\]

and

\[
(\log g_S)'(u)=\frac12.
\tag{C001.19S}
\]

Therefore (C001.1) implies, for all three implementations simultaneously,

\[
e^{-\Delta_n}
\le
\frac{c_{J,i}^{\pi_n}(x)}{c_{J,i}^{\nu_n}(x)}
\le
e^{\Delta_n}.
\tag{C001.20}
\]

Because the product marginals are in `[eta,1-eta]`, the total exit rate of every `nu_n` sampler is bounded by a constant `B_eta` independent of `n,x,J`:

\[
\sup_{n,x,J}\sum_i c_{J,i}^{\nu_n}(x)\le B_\eta<\infty.
\tag{C001.21}
\]

Hence

\[
\sup_x\sum_i
|c_{J,i}^{\pi_n}(x)-c_{J,i}^{\nu_n}(x)|
\le
B_\eta(e^{\Delta_n}-1).
\tag{C001.22}
\]

Couple the two continuous-time chains from the same initial state by using common jumps at the minimum of the corresponding rates until their first disagreement. While they agree, the total disagreement hazard is bounded by the right side of (C001.22). Therefore

\[
\sup_x
\|P_{J,t}^{\pi_n}(x,\cdot)-P_{J,t}^{\nu_n}(x,\cdot)\|_{\rm TV}
\le
B_\eta(e^{\Delta_n}-1)t.
\tag{C001.23}
\]

## Proof, Step 4 — transfer the product cutoff

Let `d_{J,n}^{pi}(t)` and `d_{J,n}^{nu}(t)` denote the worst-case TV distances to their own equilibria. By the triangle inequality, (C001.17) and (C001.23),

\[
|d_{J,n}^{\pi}(t)-d_{J,n}^{\nu}(t)|
\le
B_\eta(e^{\Delta_n}-1)t
+
\frac12(e^{n\Delta_n}-1).
\tag{C001.24}
\]

For fixed `A`, (4.6) gives

\[
t_{J,n}\pm An=O_\eta(n\log n).
\]

The common hypothesis (C001.3) makes the right side of (C001.24) tend to zero uniformly at those times. Therefore the upper and lower cutoff bounds from Step 1 transfer unchanged:

\[
\lim_{A\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 d_{J,n}^{\pi}(t_{J,n}+An)=0,
\tag{C001.25}
\]

\[
\lim_{A\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}
 d_{J,n}^{\pi}(t_{J,n}-An)=1.
\tag{C001.26}
\]

Since `n=o(t_{J,n})` by (4.6), this is total-variation cutoff for sampler `J`.

The argument was uniform in `J in {G,M,S}`. Thus the same condition (C001.3) proves cutoff for all three source implementations.

\[
\boxed{M_3=3\text{ at the mathematical-candidate level.}}
\]

QED.

## 5. Why C001 is genuinely a dependency condition rather than "pi is uniform"

The product reference `nu_n` may have arbitrary site-dependent fields subject only to the fixed non-degeneracy `eta<=p_i<=1-eta`. Those one-site fields are absorbed into `nu_n`.

The quantity

\[
 h_n=\log(\pi_n/\nu_n)
\]

is exactly the residual interaction potential, and `Delta_n` is its largest single-coordinate discrete derivative. Therefore C001 permits non-product laws; it requires that their interaction correction be uniformly weak at the scale `o(1/(n log n))`.

X001 explains why some marginal regularity cannot simply be omitted: even `h_n=0` identically does not force square-root cutoff if almost all product marginals become degenerate fast enough.

This is the outcome of Section-B move #18: the useful language is not an undifferentiated Dobrushin number alone, but **log-odds perturbation relative to a non-degenerate product reference**.
