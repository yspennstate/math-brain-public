## 6. Move #30 — ONE DIRECTION AT A TIME

I rewrote the existing proof in the spectral demand basis to see whether the multi-demand optimization really “glues” or whether a hidden minimax step remains.

Let

\[
S=\Pi^{-1/2}L\Pi^{-1/2}
\]

and take an orthonormal eigenbasis

\[
S u_k=\mu_k u_k,
\qquad
0=\mu_1<\mu_2\le\cdots,
\qquad
u_k:=\mu_k^{-1}.
\]

For `k>=2`, define the zero-sum demand

\[
b_k=\Pi^{1/2}u_k.
\]

Because `u_k` is orthogonal to `sqrt(pi)`, `1^Tb_k=0`, and

\[
b_j^T\Pi^{-1}b_k=\delta_{jk}.
\]

Route each direction electrically:

\[
\theta_k=R_*b_k=CB^TL^+b_k.
\]

A solution of `Lx=b_k` is

\[
x_k=\frac1{\mu_k}\Pi^{-1/2}u_k,
\]

up to an additive constant. Since every `b_j` is zero-sum, that gauge constant disappears. Therefore the electrical-energy cross term is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle\theta_j,\theta_k\rangle_{C^{-1}}
&=b_j^TL^+b_k\\
&=b_j^Tx_k\\
&=\frac{\delta_{jk}}{\mu_k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{SD}
\]

Thus in the stationary-orthonormal spectral demand basis the optimal flow-energy Gram matrix is literally

\[
\operatorname{diag}(\mu_2^{-1},\ldots,\mu_n^{-1}).
\]

Taking its largest eigenvalue gives

\[
\max_{k\ge2}\mu_k^{-1}
=\mu_2^{-1}
=(1-\lambda_2)^{-1}
=\tau_2.
\]

This is not a new theorem beyond the previous right-inverse proof; it is a one-direction-at-a-time decomposition of the same argument. But it isolates *why* there is no minimax obstruction: the single electrical routing operator simultaneously sends the stationary spectral demand directions to energy-orthogonal flows, and each direction attains its own Thomson optimum. They therefore glue without loss.

**Outcome of #30:** the source-facing mechanism is now especially transparent.
