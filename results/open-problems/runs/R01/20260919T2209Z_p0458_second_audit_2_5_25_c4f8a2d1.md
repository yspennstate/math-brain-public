## 5. #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST — stress the two seams

The most plausible failure mode is not a generic algebra error; it is an uncovered asymptotic sequence where one of `k` and `n-k` diverges arbitrarily slowly, or a mismatch between the fixed-complement and interior scales.

### 5.1 Interior seam

Let `X` be the `(k+1)`st root order statistic among `n-1` independent uniforms and `S` the root's `k` preferred neighbors. Conditional on `(X,S)`, the selected root scores are iid `Uniform(0,X)` and all non-root edge scores retain their independence. For `i in S`, the `k`th order statistic among the `n-2` non-root scores at `i` is

\[
T_i\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,n-k-1),
\]

and the root edge is rejected precisely when `U_i >= T_i`. Thus, with an independent copy `T`,

\[
q_n(x)=\frac1x\mathbb E[(x-T)_+],
\qquad
\mathbb E[H\mid X]=kq_n(X).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For two selected vertices `i!=j`, their non-root stars share exactly the edge `(i,j)`, with score `W`. Conditional on `(X,W)`, the remaining variables entering their rejection indicators are disjoint. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j\mid X)=\operatorname{Var}(p_X(W)\mid X).
\]

Deleting `W` leaves `Bin(n-3,u)` competitors below a root score `u`. Changing `W` changes the rejection event only on the pivotal count `k-1`, so

\[
\operatorname{osc}(p_X)
\le \frac1X
\int_0^1 {n-3\choose k-1}u^{k-1}(1-u)^{n-k-2}\,du
=\frac1{(n-2)X}.
\]

Popoviciu gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j\mid X)
\le \frac1{4(n-2)^2X^2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

On

\[
G_n=\{X\ge k/(2n)\},
\]

the total pair-covariance term is `O(1)`. Also

\[
\Pr(G_n^c)
=\Pr\!\left(\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,k/(2n))\ge k+1\right)
\le e^{-(\log2-1/2)k},
\]

which follows directly from exponential Markov with base `2`. Further,

\[
kq_n(X)\mathbf1_{G_n}
\le 2n\,\mathbb E(|X-T|\mid X),
\]

and the beta means/variances give expectation `O(s_n+1)`. Since both `k` and `n-k` diverge,

\[
s_n^2=\frac{k(n-k)}n\to\infty
\]

even if the smaller side diverges arbitrarily slowly. Hence

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(H\mid X)=o(s_n^2),
\qquad
\frac{H-kq_n(X)}{s_n}\to0
\quad\text{in }L^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

This survives the adversarial cases `k=log log n`, `n-k=log log n`, and all intermediate switching rates; no hidden proportional-density assumption enters.

Set

\[
p_n=k/n,
\quad \sigma_n=s_n/n,
\quad U_n=(X-p_n)/\sigma_n,
\quad V_n=(T-p_n)/\sigma_n.
\]

The slight mean offsets of `X` and `T` from `p_n` are `o(\sigma_n)` whenever both beta shapes diverge. Their variances are asymptotic to `\sigma_n^2`, and their standardized fourth moments stay bounded. Thus `U_n,V_n` converge to independent standard normals. If

\[
g_n(u)=\mathbb E[(u-V_n)_+],
\]

then Wasserstein-1 convergence gives `sup_u|g_n(u)-g(u)|->0`, and the exact identity

\[
\frac{kq_n(X)}{s_n}=\frac{k}{nX}g_n(U_n)
\tag{5.4}
\]

has prefactor `k/(nX)->1`. The good-event bound plus fourth-moment control supplies square uniform integrability; the exponentially small complement contributes at most `(k/s_n)^2e^{-ck}=o(1)`. Hence (5.4) converges with its first two moments, not merely in distribution. Together with (5.3), this proves the interior theorem at the full stated scope.

## 6. #5 ADD A PARAMETER — use r as the interpolation variable

For fixed `r`, the root endpoint process converges to a rate-one Poisson process on `(0,infinity)`, and

\[
A_{n,r}\Rightarrow A_r\sim\Gamma(r,1).
\]

The limiting rejection mark probability at location `y` is

\[
p_r(y)=e^{-y}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{y^j}{j!}
=\Pr(\Gamma(r,1)>y).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The only dependence between finitely many candidate marks is through their finitely many mutual edges. Delete those edges. The remaining endpoint stars are disjoint, and an internal deleted edge can affect a candidate with threshold `y<=L` only when it exceeds that root-edge threshold, an event of probability at most `L/n`. For `m` candidates the total error is `O(m^2L/n)`, uniformly on `[0,L]^m`. Thus compact-window marking becomes independent.

For truncation removal, rejection implies that after deletion of the unique shared edge a candidate still has at most `r-1` larger remaining edges. Hence, for fixed `r` and large `n`,

\[
\Pr(J_i=1\mid Y_i=y)
\le e^{-y/2}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{y^j}{j!},
\tag{6.2}
\]

and for two candidates the joint probability is bounded by the product of the corresponding one-point envelopes after deleting their common edge. This product is integrable on `(0,infinity)^2`. Therefore both the first moment and the second factorial moment pass through the compact-window limit. This supplies the variance convergence needed for standardization.

Conditional on `A_r=a`, independent thinning gives

\[
H_r\mid A_r=a\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda_r(a)),
\]

with

\[
\Lambda_r(a)
=\int_a^\infty p_r(y)\,dy
=e^{-a}\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}(r-\ell)\frac{a^\ell}{\ell!}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Now let `r` itself tend to infinity. For iid `A_r,G_r~Gamma(r,1)`,

\[
\Lambda_r(A_r)=\mathbb E[(G_r-A_r)_+\mid A_r].
\]

Set `U_r=(A_r-r)/sqrt(r)` and `V_r=(G_r-r)/sqrt(r)`. Gamma CLT plus bounded fourth moments gives Wasserstein-1 convergence of `V_r` and square uniform integrability of `U_r`. Consequently

\[
\frac{\Lambda_r(A_r)}{\sqrt r}
=
\mathbb E[(V_r-U_r)_+\mid U_r]
\Rightarrow g(-Z)\stackrel d=g(Z)
\]

with first-two-moment convergence. The conditional Poisson noise is negligible because

\[
\frac1r\mathbb E[(H_r-\Lambda_r(A_r))^2]
=\frac{\mu_r}{r}=O(r^{-1/2})\to0.
\]

Thus

\[
H_r/\sqrt r\Rightarrow g(Z)
\]

with first-two-moment convergence. This is a genuine distributional/moment glue, not merely a matching of means. Since for `k=n-1-r`, the interior scale has `s_n^2=k(n-k)/n\sim r+1`, the fixed-complement normalization also matches the interior scale asymptotically.
