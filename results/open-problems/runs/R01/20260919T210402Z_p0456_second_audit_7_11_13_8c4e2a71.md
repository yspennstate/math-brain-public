## 4. #11 DUALIZE — binomial rejection probabilities become a beta/order-statistic saddle

Fix the one-root clean problem after all theorem-approved bounded exclusions. If an offered edge has weight `x`, let

\[
q_h(x)=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(N,x)\ge h).
\]

Let `B_h` be the `h`th order statistic of `N` independent uniforms. Then

\[
B_h\sim \operatorname{Beta}(h,N-h+1),
\qquad
q_h(x)=\Pr(B_h\le x).
\]

Consequently

\[
Q_h(x):=\int_0^x q_h(u)\,du
      =\mathbb E[(x-B_h)_+].
\]

This is the clean dual form: the binomial tail is replaced by a positive-part transform of one beta random variable.

For the first `p` ordered proposal deadlines, rank integration and one integration by parts give the exact functional

\[
M_{p,h}
=\frac{(m)_{p+1}}{p!}
  \int_0^1 Q_h(x)^p(1-x)^{m-p-1}\,dx.
\]

Write

\[
\mu=\mathbb EB_h=\frac{h}{N+1}.
\]

If `B_h` is replaced by its mean, then `Q_h(x)` is replaced by `(x-\mu)_+`, and the integral evaluates **exactly**:

\[
\begin{aligned}
M^{(0)}_{p,h}
&=\frac{(m)_{p+1}}{p!}
  \int_\mu^1 (x-\mu)^p(1-x)^{m-p-1}\,dx\\
&=\frac{(m)_{p+1}}{p!}(1-\mu)^m B(p+1,m-p)\\
&=(1-\mu)^m.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here `(m)_{p+1}=m!/(m-p-1)!`, so the beta-function factor cancels exactly. In the source regime `m=N+O(k)`, `h=k+O(1)`, `k=\log n+O(1)`,

\[
\log M^{(0)}_{p,h}
=m\log(1-\mu)
=-h+O(k^2/n),
\]

hence

\[
M^{(0)}_{p,h}=e^{-h}(1+o(1)).
\]

The possible failure mode is that beta fluctuations could change this by a macroscopic factor. They do not. The beta standard deviation is `Theta(sqrt(k)/n)`, while the saddle lies at distance `Theta(k/n)` above `mu`. Splitting at a fixed fraction of that saddle displacement gives: below the split the normalized beta integral loses `exp(-Theta(k))`; above it, the positive-part correction

\[
\mathbb E[(B_h-x)_+]
\]

is `exp(-Theta(k))` relative to `x-\mu`. Thus the true integral satisfies

\[
M_{p,h}=e^{-h}(1+o_C(1))
\]

for the clean fixed-defect parameters used by the theorem.

I also checked the theorem's bounded-omission extension. Deleting `O(1)` arbitrary proposal deadlines and lowering required competitor quotas by `O(1)` only inserts a polynomial factor in `k` into the ordered-deadline integral. Therefore the uniform upper bound remains

\[
\exp[-k+o(k)].
\]

This is the exact scale needed after finite interaction defects are exposed. No favorable-rank assumption is used.

**Adversarial check:** Jensen gives `Q_h(x) >= (x-\mu)_+`, so mean replacement has the correct lower direction; the proof's nontrivial task is the matching upper estimate near the saddle. The beta concentration scales above make that upper comparison valid. I found no sign or normalization reversal.

\[

If `k_n-log n -> +infinity`, fix `M>0` and choose an integer comparison quota `q_n` with

\[
M\le q_n-\log n\le M+1.
\]

For all large `n`, `k_n>=q_n`. The fixed-window theorem (with fixed `C=M+1`) yields

\[
\liminf_n\Pr(LK(n,k_n)\text{ connected})
\ge e^{-e^{-M}},
\]

up to the harmless integer-rounding improvement in the exponent. Sending `M->infinity` gives connectivity probability tending to one.

If `k_n-log n -> -infinity`, use an integer `q_n` with

\[

and `k_n<=q_n` eventually. Monotonicity and the fixed-window law give an upper bound tending to at most `e^{-e^M}`, which goes to zero as `M->infinity`.
