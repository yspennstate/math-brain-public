## 4. New claim R01-I0024-C — centered state-to-stationarity Gram-flow formula

**Status: PROVED as an equivalent source-facing formulation.**

A centered state-to-stationarity flow family is a family

`Psi=(psi^x)_{x in V}`

satisfying

`B psi^x=e_x-pi` for every `x`,

and

`sum_x pi_x psi^x=0`.

The centering condition is harmless: for any feasible family the weighted mean is a circulation, so subtracting that same circulation from every `psi^x` preserves all divergences and does not change any centered aggregate demand flow.

Define the energy Gram matrix

`G_Psi(x,y)=(psi^x)^T C^{-1} psi^y`.

Then `G_Psi` is positive semidefinite and `G_Psi pi=0`.  Therefore

`A_Psi := Pi^(1/2) G_Psi Pi^(1/2)`

is positive semidefinite and kills `sqrt(pi)`.

The new exact characterization is

\[
\boxed{
\tau_2
=
\inf_{\Psi}
\lambda_{\max}\!\left(
\Pi^{1/2}G_\Psi\Pi^{1/2}
\right),
}
\tag{C}
\]

where the infimum is over centered state-to-stationarity flow families.

The electrical family

\[
\boxed{
\psi_*^x=CB^TL^+(e_x-\pi)
}
\tag{C*}
\]

attains the infimum.

### Complete proof

Let `h` satisfy `pi(h)=0`, and for a feasible family define

`theta_h=sum_x pi_x h_x psi^x = Psi Pi h`.

Its divergence is

`B theta_h = sum_x pi_x h_x(e_x-pi)=Pi h`,

because `sum_x pi_x h_x=0`.

Its energy is

`theta_h^T C^{-1}theta_h = h^T Pi G_Psi Pi h`.

With `z=Pi^(1/2)h`, the quotient by `h^T Pi h` is exactly the Rayleigh quotient of `A_Psi` on `sqrt(pi)^perp`.  Since `A_Psi sqrt(pi)=0`, its supremum is simply `lambda_max(A_Psi)`.

Thomson's principle for the zero-sum demand `b=Pi h` gives

`theta_h^T C^{-1}theta_h >= (Pi h)^T L^+ (Pi h)`.

Taking the supremum over nonzero centered `h` gives

`lambda_max(A_Psi) >= tau_2`.

For the electrical family (C*),

`theta_h = CB^T L^+ Pi h`,

which is the minimum-energy flow for the demand `Pi h` for every centered `h` simultaneously.  Hence every Rayleigh quotient is the exact Thomson minimum and

`lambda_max(A_{Psi_*})=tau_2`.

This proves (C).

The continuous-time analog is identical with `L=-Pi Q` and edge conductance `pi_x q_xy`; the value is the inverse of the smallest positive eigenvalue of `-Q` in the reversible Hilbert space.

### Why C is useful for source fit

The displayed characterization is literally an infimum over a finite family of flows from each state to stationarity.  Its scalar objective is the largest eigenvalue of their energy Gram matrix.  It is equivalent to the prior right-inverse formula but no explicit outer `sup_h` remains in the source-facing theorem statement.

## 5. New claim R01-I0024-D — the exact formula directly yields Aldous–Fill Theorem 4.32 and Corollary 4.33

**Status: PROVED.**

This addresses the motivation written immediately next to Open Problem 3.44.

### 5.1 Weighted-graph normalization

Use Aldous–Fill's weighted graph notation.  Let undirected edge weights be `w_e`, vertex weights `w_x=sum_y w_xy`, total directed weight

`w=sum_x w_x`,

and

`pi_x=w_x/w`.

Our conductance is

`c_e=w_e/w`.

For an ordered path `gamma_xy`, Aldous–Fill define

`r(gamma_xy)=sum_{e in gamma_xy} 1/w_e`.

They prove

\[
\tau_2\le
w\max_{\vec e}
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y r(\gamma_{xy})1_{\{\vec e\in\gamma_{xy}\}},
\tag{AF1}
\]

and

\[
\tau_2\le
w\max_{\vec e}\frac1{w_e}
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y |\gamma_{xy}|1_{\{\vec e\in\gamma_{xy}\}}.
\tag{AF2}
\]

### 5.2 Symmetrize paths without increasing either directed congestion

Start from arbitrary ordered deterministic paths.  For each ordered pair `(x,y)`, let `Gamma_xy` be the random path which is

- `gamma_xy` with probability `1/2`, and
- the reversal of `gamma_yx` with probability `1/2`.

Couple `Gamma_yx` as the reversal of `Gamma_xy`.  Then the path-flow distribution is reversal-symmetric.

For any directed edge `e`, its new resistance-weighted load is

`L_e^sym=(L_e+L_reverse(e))/2`,

so

`max_e L_e^sym <= max_e L_e`.

The same identity holds for the length-weighted load in (AF2).  Thus symmetrization does not worsen the claimed Aldous–Fill constants.

Let `phi^{xy}` be the expected signed unit flow of `Gamma_xy` in a fixed physical orientation.  Reversal symmetry gives

`phi^{yx}=-phi^{xy}`.

Set

`psi^x=sum_y pi_y phi^{xy}`.

Then

`B psi^x=e_x-pi`,

and `sum_x pi_x psi^x=0`, so this is an admissible family for claim C.

### 5.3 The missing factor 1/2 is an exact antisymmetric-projection identity

Fix an arbitrary physical edge current `f`, and set the edge voltage/co-vector

`xi_e=f_e/c_e=w f_e/w_e`.

Let

`D_xy=E[ integral_{Gamma_xy} xi ]`.

Then `D_xy=-D_yx`.  The weighted adjoint state function of the routing operator is

`g_x=sum_y pi_y D_xy`.

In particular `pi(g)=0`.

For any antisymmetric kernel `D`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_x\pi_x g_x^2
\le
\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_yD_{xy}^2.
}
\tag{P}
\]

Proof: if `Var_pi(g)=0` there is nothing to show.  Otherwise

`E[D_XY(g_X-g_Y)]=2 E[g_X^2]`,

while

`E[(g_X-g_Y)^2]=2E[g_X^2]`.

Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives

`4 Var(g)^2 <= E[D_XY^2] * 2 Var(g)`,

which is (P).

This factor is exactly what is needed to reconcile the physical undirected edge energy with Aldous–Fill's directed-edge congestion normalization.

### 5.4 Derivation of AF1

Jensen over the random path and pathwise Cauchy–Schwarz give

`D_xy^2 <= E[(integral_Gamma xi)^2]`

and

`(integral_Gamma xi)^2 <= r(Gamma) sum_{e in Gamma} w_e xi_e^2`.

Insert this into (P), exchange the edge and pair sums, and use reversal symmetry.  For a physical undirected edge the two directed path loads are equal, so the factor `2` from adding the two orientations cancels the factor `1/2` in (P).  Hence

`Var_pi(g) <= (max_directed L_e^sym) sum_{undirected e} w_e xi_e^2`.

But

`f^T C^{-1}f = (1/w) sum_e w_e xi_e^2`.

Therefore the Hilbert-space adjoint norm of this path routing satisfies

\[
\|R_\Gamma\|_{\Pi^{-1}\to C^{-1}}^2
\le
w\max_{\vec e}L_e^{sym}
\le
w\max_{\vec e}L_e.
\]

Claim C gives

`tau_2 <= ||R_Gamma||^2`,

so (AF1) follows exactly.

### 5.5 Derivation of AF2 and randomized-path Corollary 4.33

Instead use

`(integral_Gamma xi)^2 <= |Gamma| sum_{e in Gamma} xi_e^2`.

The same projection and orientation cancellation yield

`Var_pi(g) <= sum_e xi_e^2 L_e^{sym,length}`

and hence

`Var_pi(g) <= [max_e L_e^{sym,length}/w_e] sum_e w_e xi_e^2`.

Thus

\[
\|R_\Gamma\|^2
\le
w\max_{\vec e}\frac{L_e^{sym,length}}{w_e}
\le
w\max_{\vec e}\frac{L_e^{length}}{w_e},
\]

which is exactly (AF2).

Nothing in this argument requires the original paths to be deterministic: starting with arbitrary random `Gamma_xy`, symmetrize its law with the reversal of `Gamma_yx` and repeat the Jensen step.  This gives Aldous–Fill Corollary 4.33 with the expected length-load

`E |Gamma_xy| 1_{e in Gamma_xy}`.

Thus the exact flow characterization does what the source explicitly requested: the known distinguished-path inequalities are obtained by selecting a concrete feasible flow-routing family and bounding its exact Gram/operator cost.
