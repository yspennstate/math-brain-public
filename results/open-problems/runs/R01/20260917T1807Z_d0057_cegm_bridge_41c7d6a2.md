## 3. Method #4 — prove a weaker statement completely: CEGM's profile implies the m=2 Yaglom law

The following implication is elementary but important because it converts the 1984 formal profile into exactly the modern conditional-law target.

### Proposition R01-D0057-C004 — CEGM-to-Yaglom bridge

Let `m=2`, let

\[
F_N(s)=\mathbf E[s^{X_N}],\qquad q_N=\mathbf P(X_N>0)=1-F_N(0),
\]

and suppose that for every fixed `s in [0,1)` the critical iterates satisfy

\[
F_N(s)-1=
\frac{8(s-1)}{N[N(2-s)+4]}\,(1+o(1)),
\tag{3.1}
\]

with the same statement at `s=0`. Then

\[
q_N\sim \frac4{N^2}
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal L(X_N\mid X_N>0)
\Longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
\mathbf P(Y=k)=2^{-k},\quad k\ge1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Set `s=0` in (3.1). Then

\[
F_N(0)-1
\sim -\frac8{N(2N+4)}
\sim -\frac4{N^2},
\]

which proves (3.2).

The conditional probability generating function is

\[
H_N(s)
:=\mathbf E[s^{X_N}\mid X_N>0]
=\frac{F_N(s)-F_N(0)}{q_N}.
\]

For fixed `0<=s<1`, (3.1) gives

\[
F_N(s)-1\sim \frac{8(s-1)}{N^2(2-s)},
\qquad
F_N(0)-1\sim-\frac4{N^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
H_N(s)
\longrightarrow
\frac{\frac{8(s-1)}{2-s}+4}{4}
=\frac{s}{2-s}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

But

\[
\frac{s}{2-s}=\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}s^k,
\]

so convergence of the probability generating functions on `[0,1)` identifies the limiting law and proves (3.3). `square`

### Consequence

For `m=2`, a rigorous replacement for CEGM's smoothness-in-`N` step would simultaneously give the exact survival constant `4` and the geometric Yaglom law in the finite-third-weighted-moment regime. The simplest two-point test law from STUCK-1,

\[

\]

lies squarely inside CEGM's finite-derivative setting.

This is a materially better target than adding more lacunary counterexample lemmas: the old paper already supplies the candidate scaling function and identifies the precise step that was not rigorous.

## 4. Method #18 — exact conditional recursion as a projective shift plus a collision error

The second result is an exact operator formulation valid for every `m>=2`. It explains why conditional tightness is difficult and pinpoints the quantity that must be regularized.

Let `X` have survival probability

\[
q=\mathbf P(X>0),
\]

and conditional positive law `mu`. Let `Y` have law `mu`, and put

\[
a:=\mu([2,\infty)).
\]

Take `m` iid copies of `X` and define

\[
X'=\left(\sum_{i=1}^m X_i-1\right)_+,
\qquad q'=\mathbf P(X'>0),
\]

with conditional positive law `mu'`.

Define

\[
A_m(q):=m q(1-q)^{m-1}
\]

and

\[
B_m(q):=1-(1-q)^m-mq(1-q)^{m-1}
       =\mathbf P\{\operatorname{Bin}(m,q)\ge2\}.
\]

For `k>=1`, let

\[
B_{m,k}(q,\mu)
:=\mathbf P\left(\#\{i:X_i>0\}\ge2,
                 \sum_iX_i\ge k+1\right).
\]

Then `0<=B_{m,k}<=B_m(q)`.

### Proposition R01-D0057-C005 — exact projective-shift identity

For every `k>=1`,

\[
q'=A_m(q)a+B_m(q),
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\mu'([k,\infty))
=
\frac{A_m(q)\,\mu([k+1,\infty))+B_{m,k}(q,\mu)}
     {A_m(q)a+B_m(q)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

If `a>0`, define the decrement/conditioning operator `S` by

\[
(S\mu)([k,\infty))
:=\frac{\mu([k+1,\infty))}{a}.
\]

Then

\[
\left|\mu'([k,\infty))-(S\mu)([k,\infty))\right|
\le \frac{B_m(q)}{q'}
\le {m\choose2}\frac{q^2}{q'}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Partition according to the number of positive children. If exactly one child is positive, the event `X'>=k` is precisely that the unique positive value is at least `k+1`. This contributes

\[
A_m(q)\mu([k+1,\infty)).
\]

The contribution from at least two positive children is exactly `B_{m,k}`. This proves (4.2). For `k=1`, at least two positive children automatically have sum at least `2`, so `B_{m,1}=B_m`; also the one-positive contribution is `A_m(q)a`. Hence (4.1).

Write `u=\mu([k+1,\infty))`, so `0<=u<=a`. Subtracting `u/a` from (4.2) gives the exact numerator

\[
\frac{a B_{m,k}-uB_m}{a q'}.
\]

Both `aB_{m,k}` and `uB_m` lie in `[0,aB_m]`, so the absolute value is at most `B_m/q'`. Finally, `B_m(q)` is the probability of at least two successes among `m` Bernoulli-`q` trials, and the union bound over pairs gives

\[
B_m(q)\le {m\choose2}q^2.
\]

This proves (4.3). `square`

### Interpretation

When `q'` is comparable to `q`, the nonlinear conditioned dynamics is an `O_m(q)` perturbation of the simple shift `S`. The fixed points of `S` are exactly the geometric laws

\[
\mu_a(k)=(1-a)a^{k-1},\qquad k\ge1.
\]

Indeed, a fixed point has tail recursion `T_{k+1}=aT_k`, `T_1=1`.

Equation (4.1) also shows how the source parameter is selected. If `q->0` and `q'/q->1`, then

\[
1=\lim \frac{q'}q
=\lim m(1-q)^{m-1}a,
\]

because `B_m(q)/q=O_m(q)`. Therefore

\[
a\to\frac1m,
\]

and the corresponding shift fixed point is exactly

\[
\mu(k)=\left(1-\frac1m\right)\left(\frac1m\right)^{k-1}
=\frac{m-1}{m^k}.
\]

So the operator language reduces the source's Yaglom selection mechanism to the same regularity issue already seen in the earlier ratio criterion: rule out anomalous dips in `q_{n+1}/q_n`.

## 5. Method #20 — the exact place the truncation algorithm loses the proof

STUCK-1 proposed porting Chen--Shi truncation/dominability to the conditioned law. Proposition C005 shows the precise obstruction.

An unconditioned error of size `delta_n` becomes roughly `delta_n/q_n` after conditioning. In the recursion itself the entire multi-positive-child error is only `O_m(q_n^2)` before conditioning, but after conditioning its natural bound is

\[
O_m\left(\frac{q_n^2}{q_{n+1}}\right).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus an unconditioned domination estimate is insufficient unless it survives division by the next survival probability. To make the shift perturbation summably or uniformly small, one needs an **anti-dip estimate** such as

\[
q_{n+1}\ge c q_n
\tag{5.2}
\]

on the relevant scales, and ultimately enough regularity to force

\[
\frac{q_{n+1}}{q_n}\to1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The modern sustainability theorem `q_n=n^{-2+o(1)}` is not, by itself, a proof of (5.3): a slowly varying exponent allows sparse local oscillations. The old CEGM Appendix III encountered the same issue in a different coordinate. Their rigorous estimates control averages and correlations of the critical iterates, but the final profile (2.3) is obtained only after imposing smoothness in the iteration variable.

This is the exact failure requested by method #20. The next useful theorem is not another tail truncation bound; it is a discrete regularity theorem that prevents critical iterates from making sufficiently sharp local excursions.
