## 4. Move #14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING

There are two relevant group actions, and they clarify exactly what kind of object the candidate formula is.

### 4.1 Cycle-space gauge group

Let the edge-flow Hilbert space carry the energy inner product

\[
\langle\theta,\eta\rangle_E=\theta^T C^{-1}\eta.
\]

The feasible linear routings are right inverses of

\[
B:\mathbb R^E\to\mathcal H_0.
\]

If `R_0` is one right inverse, every other one is

\[
R_0+K,
\qquad K\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal H_0,\ker B).
\]

Thus the additive group

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal H_0,\ker B)
\]

acts freely and transitively on the feasible routing space. This is the precise `cycle-flow gauge freedom`: two routings differ only by a demand-dependent circulation.

The electrical section `R_*` is the unique section landing in the energy-orthogonal complement of `ker B`. Equivalently, (I0024-RI) is the problem of finding the least-norm linear section of the Hilbert quotient

\[
\mathbb R^E/\ker B\cong\mathcal H_0.
\]

This language does **not** create a new mathematical theorem beyond the previous Loewner-minimality observation. Its value is diagnostic: it identifies the exact prior-art category to search. The abstract least-norm-section statement is standard Moore--Penrose Hilbert geometry, so any paper must be modest about mechanism novelty. The source-specific content is the stationary `Pi^{-1}` demand geometry and the identification of the resulting section norm with `tau_2`.

### 4.2 Chain automorphism group

If a group `Gamma` acts by automorphisms of the reversible chain, it acts isometrically on demand and edge-flow spaces and commutes with `B`. For any feasible routing `R`, average its conjugates over `Gamma`. The average remains a right inverse, and convexity/unitary invariance of the operator norm implies that its cost is no larger. Therefore symmetric chains may be analyzed within the `Gamma`-equivariant routing subspace.

This does not help the already-proved universal inequality, but it gives a source-facing interpretation on vertex-transitive examples: the exact flow optimizer belongs to the symmetry commutant and decomposes along the same irreducible spectral blocks as the Markov operator. It also explains why searching only symmetric scalar congestion statistics can miss the answer: the relevant invariant is an operator on the nonconstant representation space, not one scalar load per edge.

**Outcome of #14:** it increases prior-art caution but sharpens the exact source-specific object: a stationary-weighted least-norm section of the divergence quotient, with an equivariant reduction on symmetric chains.

## 6. Move #29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY

The source domain is irreducible finite reversible chains. A natural test is whether the formula behaves correctly on the boundary where irreducibility is lost.

For a reducible reversible chain, the support graph has at least two components. Then

\[
\operatorname{im} B
=
\{b:\text{the sum of }b\text{ is zero separately on each component}\},
\]

so there is no right inverse of `B` on the full global zero-sum space `H_0`. With the standard convention `inf(empty set)=+infinity`, the right side of (I0024-RI) is `+infinity`. At the same boundary, eigenvalue `1` has multiplicity at least two, hence `lambda_2=1` and `tau_2=+infinity`. Thus the exact formula compactifies correctly to the reducible boundary without changing normalization.

The blow-up can be seen quantitatively before reaching the boundary. Let `S` be nontrivial, put

\[
a=\pi(S),\qquad
b_x=\pi_x(1_S(x)-a),
\]

so `1^Tb=0` and

\[
b^T\Pi^{-1}b=a(1-a).
\]

Any flow with divergence `b` has net flux `a(1-a)` across the cut. If

\[
Q(S,S^c)=\sum_{e\in\partial S}c_e,
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz on the cut gives

\[
E(\theta)\ge\frac{a^2(1-a)^2}{Q(S,S^c)}.
\]

Therefore every routing has operator cost at least

\[
\frac{a(1-a)}{Q(S,S^c)},
\]

which diverges as a positive-mass cut closes. This is exactly the same indicator-test lower bound visible from the spectral Rayleigh quotient.

This boundary calculation is not needed for the finite irreducible proof. It is a robustness test: the flow formula has the correct point at infinity and the correct cut-scale divergence as the chain approaches reducibility. A formula produced by an accidental finite-dimensional normalization would have been vulnerable here.

**Outcome of #29:** the candidate survives the natural compactification and identifies the same bottleneck blow-up on both the flow and spectral sides.
