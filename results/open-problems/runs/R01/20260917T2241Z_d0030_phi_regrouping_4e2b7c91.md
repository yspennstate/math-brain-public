## 4. Idea before code: replace the infinite counterexample by a three-variable phi-regrouping problem

The previous run proved a finite-range scale-gadget compiler: if independent stationary finite-range processes can have summably small ordinary phi costs while retaining a fixed positive rho-star value at their own growing scales, then their independent product gives a full negative answer to Bradley Question 2.

The main question was therefore how to manufacture one scale gadget.

Bradley's 2011 rho-only triple suggests a much more local formulation. Let

\[
V=(V_1,V_2,V_3)
\]

be an arbitrary random triple and define

\[
\delta_1(V)
:=\phi\!\left(\sigma(V_3),\sigma(V_1,V_2)\right),
\]

\[
\delta_2(V)
:=\phi\!\left(\sigma(V_3,V_2),\sigma(V_1)\right),
\]

and

\[
R(V)
:=\rho\!\left(\sigma(V_1,V_3),\sigma(V_2)\right).
\]

The local negative program is now:

\[
\boxed{
\delta_1(V_j),\delta_2(V_j)\to0
\quad\text{while}\quad
R(V_j)\ge c>0.
}
\tag{LOCAL}
\]

If `(LOCAL)` is possible sufficiently rapidly, the next proposition turns it into exactly the finite-range scale gadgets needed by the previous compiler.

## 5. Method #8 — exact one-dimensional moving-window compiler

**Proposition 5.1 (one-dimensional triple-to-scale compiler).**
Let `V=(V1,V2,V3)` be a random triple. Let

\[
W_j=(W_{j,1},W_{j,2},W_{j,3}),\qquad j\in\mathbb Z,
\]

be iid copies of `V`. Fix an integer `L>=1`. Let `Phi` be a measurable encoding having a measurable inverse on its image, and define

\[
Y_k
=\Phi\!\left(W_{k-L,1},W_{k,2},W_{k+L,3}\right),
\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then:

1. `Y` is strictly stationary and `2L`-dependent;
2. its ordinary one-step phi coefficient satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\phi_Y(1)
\le
1-(1-\delta_1(V))^L(1-\delta_2(V))^L
\le
L\bigl(\delta_1(V)+\delta_2(V)\bigr);
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

3. its interlaced maximal-correlation coefficient satisfies

\[
\boxed{\rho_Y^*(L)\ge R(V).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

**Proof.** Strict stationarity follows immediately from the iid shift-invariance of `(W_j)` and the translation-covariant definition (5.1).

Each `Y_k` is measurable with respect to the three base triples indexed by

\[
\{k-L,k,k+L\}.
\]

The entire past `sigma(Y_k:k<=0)` therefore uses only `W_j` with `j<=L`, while the future `sigma(Y_k:k>=n)` uses only `W_j` with `j>=n-L`. These collections are disjoint for `n>2L`; hence `Y` is `2L`-dependent.

For the cut between times `0` and `1`, fix one base index `j`. The three components of `W_j` occur at the three observation times

\[
j-L,\qquad j,\qquad j+L
\]

in the order `W_{j,3}`, `W_{j,2}`, `W_{j,1}`.

- For `j=1-L,...,0`, the past sees `(W_{j,3},W_{j,2})` and the future sees `W_{j,1}`. The local phi cost is `delta_2(V)`.
- For `j=1,...,L`, the past sees `W_{j,3}` and the future sees `(W_{j,2},W_{j,1})`. The local phi cost is `delta_1(V)`.
- Every other base triple lies wholly on one side of the cut and contributes zero dependence across that cut.

Because the base triples are independent, Bradley's independent-join phi inequality gives

\[
\phi_Y(1)
\le
1-(1-\delta_1)^L(1-\delta_2)^L.
\]

Using `1-(1-a)^L(1-b)^L <= L(a+b)` for `a,b in [0,1]` proves (5.2).

For (5.3), choose the interlaced index sets

\[
S=\{-L,L\},\qquad T=\{0\}.
\]

Their separation is exactly `L`. By invertibility of the coordinate encoding, `sigma(Y_{-L},Y_L)` contains `W_{0,3}` and `W_{0,1}`, while `sigma(Y_0)` contains `W_{0,2}`. Therefore any centered square-integrable test functions witnessing the maximal correlation of `(W_{0,1},W_{0,3})` with `W_{0,2}` are admissible test functions for these larger sigma-fields. Hence

\[
\rho\!\left(\sigma(Y_{-L},Y_L),\sigma(Y_0)\right)
\ge
R(V),
\]

and taking the supremum in the definition of `rho-star` gives (5.3). QED.

This proposition is the main conceptual progress of the run. It turns Bradley Question 2's negative direction into a sharply local three-variable problem.

## 6. Method #24 — the finite extremal quantity

For `c in (0,1]`, define

\[
\eta(c)
:=
\inf\left\{
\max\{\delta_1(V),\delta_2(V)\}:
R(V)\ge c
\right\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

where the infimum runs over all probability spaces and all random triples `V`.

This quantity cleanly separates two possibilities.

**Corollary 6.1.** If `eta(c)=0` for some fixed `c>0`, then Bradley Question 2 has a negative answer.

**Proof.** Choose any sequence `L_j -> infinity`, for example `L_j=2^j`. From `eta(c)=0`, choose triples `V^(j)` such that

\[
R(V^{(j)})\ge c,
\qquad
\max(\delta_1(V^{(j)}),\delta_2(V^{(j)}))
\le \frac{2^{-j}}{2L_j}.
\]

Apply Proposition 5.1 independently at scale `L_j`. The resulting process `Y^(j)` is `2L_j`-dependent,

\[
\phi_{Y^{(j)}}(1)\le2^{-j},
\qquad
\rho_{Y^{(j)}}^*(L_j)\ge c.
\]

Choose the triples even smaller, e.g. replace `2^{-j}` by `4^{-j}`, if desired; the ordinary phi costs are summable. The finite-range scale-gadget compiler proved in the preceding R01 report then forms the independent product over `j`, yielding a strictly stationary phi-mixing sequence with `rho-star(L_j)>=c` for all `j`. Thus it is not rho-star-mixing. QED.

So the negative direction has now been reduced to the following finite/local question:

\[
\boxed{\text{Is }\eta(c)=0\text{ for some }c>0?}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Bradley's 2011 Lemma 2.2 proves the exact analogue of (6.2) with the two `phi` coefficients replaced by the corresponding `rho` coefficients. The phi version is genuinely stronger and is not supplied by that paper.

Conversely, proving `eta(c)>0` for every `c>0` would rule out this entire moving-window counterexample architecture. It would **not** by itself prove the full Bradley source question, because an arbitrary phi-mixing process need not decompose into these iid triple gadgets.

## 7. Method #16 — exact binary small-case extremization

Only after obtaining the local quantity (6.1), I ran an exact rational finite search to see what the smallest binary models do.

### Search space

Take `V1,V2,V3 in {0,1}` and restrict to laws whose eight atom probabilities have common denominator `D=10`. There are

\[
{10+8-1\choose8-1}=19448
\]

count vectors. I discarded only laws for which `V2` is degenerate, because then the normalized HGR coefficient is undefined/trivial.

For each law I computed exactly, with Python `fractions.Fraction`:

\[
\delta_1=\phi(\sigma(V_3),\sigma(V_1,V_2)),
\qquad
\delta_2=\phi(\sigma(V_3,V_2),\sigma(V_1)),
\]

and

\[
R^2
=\rho^2(\sigma(V_1,V_3),\sigma(V_2)).
\]

Since `V2` is Bernoulli,

\[
R^2
=
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[V_2\mid V_1,V_3])}
     {\operatorname{Var}(V_2)},
\]

which is exact rational arithmetic.

### Exact result

Under the local budget

\[
\max(\delta_1,\delta_2)\le\frac1{10},
\]

there are exactly `316` feasible nondegenerate laws, and

\[
\boxed{\max R^2=\frac19,\qquad \max R=\frac13.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

There are exactly `24` maximizing count vectors. In lexicographic state order

\]

for which

\[
\delta_1=\frac1{10},\qquad \delta_2=0,
\qquad R^2=\frac19.

## 8. A complete square-root family: what the small scan is seeing

For every even integer `D>=2`, let `V1` be constant and let `(V2,V3)` have law

\[
\Pr(V_2=0,V_3=1)=\frac1D,
\]

\[
\Pr(V_2=1,V_3=0)=\frac12,
\]

\[
\Pr(V_2=1,V_3=1)=\frac12-\frac1D,
\]

and `P(V2=0,V3=0)=0`.

Then `V3` is fair and `P(V2=0)=1/D`. Since `V1` is constant,

\[
\delta_2=0,
\qquad
\delta_1=\phi(\sigma(V_3),\sigma(V_2)).
\]

Conditioning on `V3=1` changes `P(V2=0)` from `1/D` to `2/D`, while conditioning on `V3=0` changes it to `0`. For a binary target this is already the extremal event, so

\[
\boxed{\delta_1=\frac1D.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

For two Bernoulli variables, maximal correlation is the absolute Pearson correlation. Directly,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(1_{\{V_2=0\}},1_{\{V_3=1\}})
=\frac1{2D},
\]

\[
\operatorname{Var}(1_{\{V_2=0\}})
=\frac1D\left(1-\frac1D\right),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(1_{\{V_3=1\}})=\frac14.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R^2=\frac{1}{D-1}
=\frac{\delta_1}{1-\delta_1}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus this elementary family has

\[
R\asymp\sqrt{\delta_1}.
\]

It gives increasingly small sequential phi only by forcing the interlaced HGR to zero. In particular it **does not** solve (6.2). But it identifies the natural scale that any genuine fixed-`R` construction must beat.

The `D=10` member has `R^2=1/9` and explains the exact finite-grid optimum found above, up to relabeling of the constant coordinate.

## 9. What this run rules out and what remains

The previous run already ruled out two coarse counterexample mechanisms:

- a persistent global/common phase, because a nontrivial event common to the two separated sigma-fields forces phi at least `1/2`;
- independent repetition of weak HGR channels, because Csaki–Fischer tensorization takes a supremum rather than amplifying maximal correlation.

This run shows that a much more sophisticated mechanism nevertheless has a precise finite target. A negative solution can be obtained from a sequence of **three-variable regrouping gadgets** with two sequential phi coefficients tending to zero but fixed interlaced HGR.

The present binary lattice and the elementary square-root family do not produce such a gadget. They instead suggest that, at least in the smallest state spaces, the HGR gain tracks a square root of the phi budget.

The decisive remaining local problem is therefore one of these two mutually informative outcomes:

1. **Negative route:** construct growing-alphabet triples `V^(j)` with `delta_1,delta_2 -> 0` and `R>=c>0`. Proposition 5.1 plus the preceding scale compiler then gives a full source counterexample.
2. **Positive obstruction to this architecture:** prove a dimension-free inequality

\[
R(V)\le F(\max\{\delta_1(V),\delta_2(V)\}),
\qquad F(t)\to0,
\tag{9.1}
\]

for all random triples. This would show `eta(c)>0` and kill the most concrete negative architecture, though more work would still be required to derive the full source YES answer for arbitrary stationary processes.

A useful first target is whether some universal constant `C` satisfies

\[
R(V)^2\le C\bigl(\delta_1(V)+\delta_2(V)\bigr).
\tag{9.2}
\]

The square-root family shows that an exponent better than `1/2` for `R` versus phi is impossible in this level of generality. I have **not** proved (9.2).
