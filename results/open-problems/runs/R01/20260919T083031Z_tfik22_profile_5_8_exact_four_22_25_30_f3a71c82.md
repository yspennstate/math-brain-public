## 3. Method #30 — localize first to the five degree-4 vertices

Let

\[
A=\{v:d(v)=4\},\qquad |A|=5,
\]

and let `B=V\setminus A`, so `|B|=8` and every vertex in `B` has degree 3. Put

\[
H=G[A],\quad x=e(H),\quad y=e(A,B),\quad z=e(G[B]).
\]

Counting degrees on the two parts gives

\[
2x+y=20,\qquad y+2z=24,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{y=20-2x,\qquad z=x+2.}
\]

Since `G` is triangle-free, `H` is a triangle-free graph on five vertices. There are exactly 14 unlabeled possibilities; the checker enumerates all 14 directly from the finite graph atlas.

For each `b\in B`, set

\[
S_b=N(b)\cap A.
\]

Triangle-freeness implies `S_b` is an independent set of `H`, and the degree constraints on `A` are exactly

\[
\boxed{\#\{b\in B:i\in S_b\}=4-d_H(i)\quad(i\in A).}
\]

Thus the cross-edge layer becomes a finite multiset problem on independent subsets of a five-vertex graph rather than an enumeration of all 13-vertex graphs.

## 4. Method #25 — count the pair-deletion core twice

Fix two nonadjacent vertices `a,b\in A`. Write

- `r_a=d_H(a)`, `r_b=d_H(b)`;
- `t_{ab}=|N_B(a)\cap N_B(b)|`;
- `c_{ab}=|N_H(a)\cap N_H(b)|`.

Delete `a,b`. Exactly `8-r_a-r_b-t_{ab}` distinct degree-3 vertices in `B` are adjacent to at least one of them and become degree at most two; suppressing/removing those vertices can only preserve planarity-equivalence of the remaining core. Each common `A`-neighbor of `a,b` also loses two incident edges and is forced into the same low-degree reduction. Starting from 22 edges and removing the eight edges incident with `a,b`, the reduced core has at most

\[
\boxed{
6+r_a+r_b+t_{ab}-c_{ab}
}
\]

edges. Additional low-degree cascades only decrease this number.

Therefore, whenever

\[
6+r_a+r_b+t_{ab}-c_{ab}\le 8,
\]

the reduced core is planar (a nonplanar simple graph needs at least 9 edges); restoring subdivisions/leaves preserves planarity, so `G-a-b` is planar. Thus `G` is 2-apex and consequently not intrinsically knotted.

This gives a rigorous cheap filter before any topological classification.
