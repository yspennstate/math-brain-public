## 4. Candidate exact source-resolution theorem

### Theorem R01-I0024-A — all-pairs flow formula

Let `P` be a finite irreducible reversible discrete-time Markov chain on state space `V`, with stationary distribution `pi`. Let

\[
E=\{\{x,y\}:x\ne y,\ P(x,y)>0\}.
\]

Choose one orientation of every undirected edge. Define the Markov conductance

\[
c_e=\pi_xP(x,y)=\pi_yP(y,x)>0,
\]

let `B` be the vertex-edge incidence matrix, and let

\[
C=\operatorname{diag}(c_e),\qquad
\Pi=\operatorname{diag}(\pi_x).
\]

An **admissible all-pairs flow family** is an antisymmetric family

\[
\Phi=(\phi^{xy})_{x,y\in V},
\]

with `phi^{xx}=0`, `phi^{yx}=-phi^{xy}`, and

\[
B\phi^{xy}=e_x-e_y.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a function `h:V->R` with `pi(h)=0`, define the combined flow

\[
\Theta_\Phi(h)
:=
\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y(h_x-h_y)\phi^{xy}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define the flow-family cost

\[
\mathcal C(\Phi)
:=
\sup_{\substack{h\ne0\\ \pi(h)=0}}
\frac{
\Theta_\Phi(h)^TC^{-1}\Theta_\Phi(h)
}{
 h^T\Pi h
}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\tau_2=\inf_{\Phi}\mathcal C(\Phi).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The infimum is attained by the electrical all-pairs flows

\[
\boxed{
\phi_*^{xy}
=C B^T L^+(e_x-e_y),
\qquad
L:=BCB^T=\Pi(I-P),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `L^+` is the Moore–Penrose inverse.

Thus the proved uniform multiplicative distortion of this candidate characterization relative to relaxation time is exactly

\[
\boxed{C=1}.
\]

### Continuous-time version

For a finite irreducible reversible continuous-time chain with generator `Q`, set

\[
c_{xy}=\pi_xq_{xy},\qquad L=-\Pi Q.
\]

With the same definitions (4.1)–(4.3),

\[
\inf_\Phi\mathcal C(\Phi)=\lambda_2^{-1}=\tau_2,
\]

where `lambda_2` is the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of `-Q` in `L^2(pi)`.

## 5. Full proof

### Lemma 5.1 — every all-pairs family routes the demand `Pi h`

For every admissible `Phi` and every `h` with `pi(h)=0`,

\[
\boxed{B\Theta_\Phi(h)=\Pi h.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

**Proof.** By (4.1),

\[
B\Theta_\Phi(h)
=
\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y(h_x-h_y)(e_x-e_y).
\]

Fix a coordinate `z`. The terms with `x=z` contribute

\[
\frac12\pi_z\sum_y\pi_y(h_z-h_y)
=\frac12\pi_zh_z
\]

because `sum_y pi_y h_y=0`. The terms with `y=z` contribute the same quantity. Hence the `z` coordinate is `pi_z h_z`, proving (5.1). ∎

### Lemma 5.2 — Thomson minimum for an arbitrary zero-sum demand

Let `b` satisfy `sum_x b_x=0`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\min_{B\theta=b}\theta^TC^{-1}\theta
=b^TL^+b.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The unique minimum-energy flow is

\[
\theta_*(b)=CB^TL^+b.
\tag{5.3}
\]

**Proof.** The underlying graph is connected, so

\[
\operatorname{range}(L)=\mathbf 1^\perp.
\]

Thus `LL^+b=b`. Set `u=L^+b` and `theta_*=CB^Tu`. Then

\[
B\theta_*=BCB^Tu=Lu=b.
\]

For any other feasible `theta`, write `theta=theta_*+eta`, where `B eta=0`. The energy splits orthogonally:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta^TC^{-1}\theta
&=\theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*
+2u^TB\eta
+\eta^TC^{-1}\eta\\
&=\theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*+\eta^TC^{-1}\eta
\ge \theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally

\[
\theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*
=u^TBCB^Tu
=u^TLu
=b^TL^+b.
\]

This proves (5.2)–(5.3). ∎

### Lemma 5.3 — the worst minimum flow energy is exactly `tau_2`

For `h` with `pi(h)=0`, put `b=Pi h`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{h\ne0\\\pi(h)=0}}
\frac{b^TL^+b}{h^T\Pi h}
=\tau_2.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

**Proof.** Define the symmetric conjugate

\[
S:=\Pi^{-1/2}L\Pi^{-1/2}
=I-\Pi^{1/2}P\Pi^{-1/2}.
\]

Its eigenvalues are

\[
0,\ 1-\lambda_2,\ldots,1-\lambda_n,
\]

and its null vector is `sqrt(pi)`. Put

\[
z=\Pi^{1/2}h.
\]

Then `pi(h)=0` is exactly `z perp sqrt(pi)` and

\[
h^T\Pi h=z^Tz.
\]

Let `u` be any solution of

\[
Lu=\Pi h.
\]

Writing `v=Pi^{1/2}u`, the equation becomes

\[
Sv=z.
\]

Since `z` is orthogonal to the kernel, the relevant solution is `v=S^+z` up to an irrelevant kernel vector. Consequently

\[
(\Pi h)^TL^+(\Pi h)
=z^TS^+z.
\]

On `sqrt(pi)^perp`, the largest eigenvalue of `S^+` is

\[
\frac1{1-\lambda_2}=\tau_2.
\]

Taking the Rayleigh quotient proves (5.4). ∎

### Lower bound in Theorem R01-I0024-A

Fix any admissible family `Phi`. By Lemma 5.1, `Theta_Phi(h)` is feasible for demand `b=Pi h`. Lemma 5.2 therefore gives, pointwise in `h`,

\[
\Theta_\Phi(h)^TC^{-1}\Theta_\Phi(h)
\ge
(\Pi h)^TL^+(\Pi h).
\]

Divide by `h^T Pi h`, take the supremum over centered nonzero `h`, and apply Lemma 5.3:

\[
\mathcal C(\Phi)\ge\tau_2.
\]

Hence

\[
\inf_\Phi\mathcal C(\Phi)\ge\tau_2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Attainment by one fixed electrical all-pairs family

For every ordered pair define

\[
\phi_*^{xy}
=CB^TL^+(e_x-e_y).
\]

It is antisymmetric. Since `e_x-e_y` has coordinate sum zero,

\[
B\phi_*^{xy}
=LL^+(e_x-e_y)
=e_x-e_y,
\]

so it is admissible.

Its combined flow is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_{\Phi_*}(h)
&=
CB^TL^+
\left[
\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y(h_x-h_y)(e_x-e_y)
\right]\\
&=CB^TL^+\Pi h,
\end{aligned}
\]

where the bracket equals `Pi h` by Lemma 5.1's algebra. But this is exactly the Thomson minimizer `theta_*(Pi h)` from Lemma 5.2. Therefore, for every centered `h` simultaneously,

\[
\Theta_{\Phi_*}(h)^TC^{-1}\Theta_{\Phi_*}(h)
=(\Pi h)^TL^+(\Pi h).
\]

Lemma 5.3 now gives

\[
\mathcal C(\Phi_*)=\tau_2.
\]

Together with (5.5),

\[
\boxed{
\inf_\Phi\mathcal C(\Phi)=\tau_2.
}
\]

This proves the discrete-time theorem. Replacing `L=Pi(I-P)` by `L=-Pi Q` gives the identical continuous-time argument. ∎

## 6. Why this differs from the known path-congestion bound

The source's distinguished/random-path method applies Cauchy–Schwarz commodity-by-commodity and then controls the maximum edge load. That necessarily discards cancellation/cross terms.

The exact formula instead first combines all pair flows linearly according to the test function:

\[
\Theta_\Phi(h)
=\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y(h_x-h_y)\phi^{xy},
\]

and only then squares its total electrical energy. The cross terms are exactly what allows the electrical all-pairs family to realize the inverse Laplacian and hence the exact Poincaré constant.

Equivalently, this is the finite-dimensional statement that the inverse spectral gap is the square of the minimum operator norm of a right inverse of the weighted divergence. That abstract Hilbert-space principle explains why the proof is short and why close relatives occur in Babuška–Aziz theory.
