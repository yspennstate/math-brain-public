## 3. New formulation R01-I0024-B — minimum-norm flow-routing right inverse

**STATUS: PROVED. Frozen metric stays `C=1`.**

Let `P` be a finite irreducible reversible discrete-time Markov chain on finite state space `V`, with stationary law `pi`.

Orient every non-loop edge once and define

\[
c_{\{x,y\}}=\pi_xP(x,y)=\pi_yP(y,x)>0.
\]

Let `B` be the vertex-edge incidence matrix,
\[
C=\operatorname{diag}(c_e),
\qquad
\Pi=\operatorname{diag}(\pi_x).
\]
Then
\[
L:=BCB^T=\Pi(I-P).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The equality includes chains with self-loops: loops do not appear as flow edges, and the diagonal of `BCB^T` is
\[
\sum_{y\ne x}\pi_xP(x,y)
=\pi_x(1-P(x,x)),
\]
which is exactly the diagonal of `Pi(I-P)`.

Let the zero-sum demand space be
\[
\mathcal H_0=\{b\in\mathbb R^V:\mathbf1^Tb=0\}.
\]
Give it the weighted norm
\[
\|b\|_{\Pi^{-1}}^2=b^T\Pi^{-1}b.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Give edge flows the electrical energy norm
\[
\|\theta\|_{C^{-1}}^2=\theta^TC^{-1}\theta.
\tag{3.3}
\]

A **linear flow-routing operator** is a linear map
\[
R:\mathcal H_0\to\mathbb R^E
\]
such that
\[
BRb=b
\quad\text{for every }b\in\mathcal H_0.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Define
\[
\|R\|_{\mathrm{route}}^2
=
\sup_{0\ne b\in\mathcal H_0}
\frac{(Rb)^TC^{-1}(Rb)}
     {b^T\Pi^{-1}b}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Theorem R01-I0024-B

\[
\boxed{
\tau_2
=
\inf_{\substack{R:\mathcal H_0\to\mathbb R^E\\BR=I_{\mathcal H_0}}}
\|R\|_{\mathrm{route}}^2 .
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The infimum is attained by the electrical routing operator
\[
\boxed{
R_*=CB^TL^+\big|_{\mathcal H_0},
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
where `L^+` is the Moore–Penrose inverse.

Thus the uniform multiplicative distortion is exactly
\[
\boxed{C=1}.
\]

### Continuous-time form

For a finite irreducible reversible continuous-time chain with generator `Q`, put
\[
c_{xy}=\pi_xq_{xy},
\qquad
L=-\Pi Q.
\]
With exactly the same routing definition,
\[
\inf_R\|R\|_{\mathrm{route}}^2
=
\lambda_2^{-1}
=
\tau_2,
\]
where `lambda_2` is the smallest positive eigenvalue of `-Q` in `L^2(pi)`.

## 4. Full proof of R01-I0024-B

### Lemma 4.1 — Thomson for arbitrary demand

For every `b in H0`,
\[
\boxed{
\min_{B\theta=b}\theta^TC^{-1}\theta
=
b^TL^+b.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The minimizer is
\[
\theta_*(b)=CB^TL^+b.
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.**
Since the underlying undirected graph is connected,
\[
\operatorname{range}(L)=\mathbf1^\perp=\mathcal H_0.
\]
Thus `LL^+b=b`. Put
\[
\theta_*=CB^TL^+b.
\]
Then
\[
B\theta_*=LL^+b=b.
\]
If `theta` is any other feasible flow, write
\[
\theta=\theta_*+\eta,
\qquad
B\eta=0.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta^TC^{-1}\theta
&=\theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*
+2(L^+b)^TB\eta
+\eta^TC^{-1}\eta\\
&=\theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*+\eta^TC^{-1}\eta\\
&\ge\theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally, more explicitly,
\[
(CB^TL^+b)^TC^{-1}(CB^TL^+b)
=
b^TL^+BCB^TL^+b
=
b^TL^+LL^+b
=
b^TL^+b.
\]
This proves (4.1). ∎

### Lemma 4.2 — the worst minimum routing energy is `tau_2`

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\ne b\in\mathcal H_0}
\frac{b^TL^+b}{b^T\Pi^{-1}b}
=
\tau_2.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

**Proof.**
Write
\[
b=\Pi h.
\]
Then `1^T b=0` iff
\[
\pi^Th=0,
\]
and
\[
b^T\Pi^{-1}b=h^T\Pi h=\|h\|_\pi^2.
\]

Let
\[
S=\Pi^{-1/2}L\Pi^{-1/2}
=
I-\Pi^{1/2}P\Pi^{-1/2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
This is symmetric. Its eigenvalues are
\[
0,\;1-\lambda_2,\ldots,1-\lambda_n,
\]
and its null vector is `sqrt(pi)`.

Put
\[
z=\Pi^{1/2}h.
\]
The centering condition is
\[
z\perp\sqrt\pi.
\]
Choose
\[
v=S^+z,
\qquad
u=\Pi^{-1/2}v.
\]
Then
\[
L u
=
\Pi^{1/2}S\Pi^{1/2}\Pi^{-1/2}v
=
\Pi^{1/2}Sv
=
\Pi^{1/2}z
=
\Pi h=b.
\]
Any two solutions of `Lu=b` differ by a constant vector, and `b^T1=0`; hence
\[
b^TL^+b=b^Tu.
\]
Therefore
\[
b^TL^+b
=
z^TS^+z.
\]
Also
\[
b^T\Pi^{-1}b=z^Tz.
\]
Thus (4.3) is exactly the top Rayleigh quotient of `S^+` on
`sqrt(pi)^perp`, namely
\[
\frac1{1-\lambda_2}=\tau_2.
\]
∎

### Lower bound for every routing operator

Fix any right inverse `R`. For each demand `b`, `Rb` is feasible. By Lemma 4.1,
\[
(Rb)^TC^{-1}(Rb)
\ge
b^TL^+b.
\]
Taking the quotient and supremum gives, by Lemma 4.2,
\[
\|R\|_{\mathrm{route}}^2
\ge\tau_2.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Hence
\[
\inf_R\|R\|_{\mathrm{route}}^2\ge\tau_2.
\]

### Electrical routing attains equality

For
\[
R_*=CB^TL^+,
\]
Lemma 4.1 says pointwise in every demand
\[
(R_*b)^TC^{-1}(R_*b)=b^TL^+b.
\]
Therefore Lemma 4.2 gives
\[
\|R_*\|_{\mathrm{route}}^2=\tau_2.
\]
Together with (4.5), this proves (3.6). ∎

## 6. Exact equivalence with the prior all-pairs-flow theorem

The previous R01 theorem used an antisymmetric family of unit pair flows
\[
B\phi^{xy}=e_x-e_y,
\qquad
\phi^{yx}=-\phi^{xy},
\]
and
\[
\Theta_\Phi(h)
=
\frac12
\sum_{x,y}
\pi_x\pi_y(h_x-h_y)\phi^{xy}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This section proves that formulation is neither narrower nor broader than R01-I0024-B.

### From all-pairs flows to a routing right inverse

Define
\[
\psi^x=\sum_y\pi_y\phi^{xy},
\]
and for `b in H0`,
\[
R_\Phi b=\sum_x b_x\psi^x.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since
\[
B\psi^x
=
\sum_y\pi_y(e_x-e_y)
=
e_x-\pi,
\]
we have
\[
BR_\Phi b
=
\sum_xb_x(e_x-\pi)
=
b
\]
because `sum_x b_x=0`.

If
\[
b=\Pi h,
\qquad \pi(h)=0,
\]
then antisymmetry gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_\Phi(h)
&=
\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_yh_x\phi^{xy}
-\frac12\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_yh_y\phi^{xy}\\
&=
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_yh_x\phi^{xy}\\
&=
R_\Phi(\Pi h).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the all-pairs cost is exactly the operator cost of `R_Phi`.

### From a routing right inverse to all-pairs unit flows

Conversely, given `R` with `BR=I_H0`, define
\[
\phi_R^{xy}=R(e_x-e_y).
\tag{6.4}
\]
Then
\[
B\phi_R^{xy}=e_x-e_y
\]
and linearity gives
\[
\phi_R^{yx}=-\phi_R^{xy}.
\]

For this family,
\[
\psi_R^x
=
\sum_y\pi_yR(e_x-e_y)
=
R(e_x-\pi).
\]
Therefore for zero-sum `b`,
\[
R_{\Phi_R}b
=
\sum_xb_xR(e_x-\pi)
=
R(b).
\tag{6.5}
\]

So the two feasible classes induce exactly the same routing operators and exactly the same infimum.

This equivalence materially strengthens the source fit of the prior theorem: the result is not an artifact of a special electrical all-pairs ansatz.
