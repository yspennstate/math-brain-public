## 2. Exact blocking statement

The previous run removed the local derangement reverse-multiplicity loss and proved the square-root scale for every bipartite `(a,b)`-biregular graph. The remaining arbitrary-degree obstruction can be stated in one formula.

For a `k`-simple base plan/path `rho=(v_0,...,v_k)`, let

\[
W(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(d(v_i)-2).
\]

The unweighted non-returning packing gives only

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_{\rm nonreturn,\,L\text{-simple}}(\rho)}{W(\rho)}
\le
\frac{2m}{L\,W(\rho)}.
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

Regularity or biregularity converts `2m/W(rho)` into `O(n/k)`. In a general graph a simple trajectory may remain in a sparse corridor while `m` is inflated by a remote dense reservoir, so `(BLOCK)` can be arbitrarily weaker than the source-scale estimate.

What a full proof still needs is a **degree-free local replacement** for `(BLOCK)`: either a reachable-volume packing or a switching that turns overlaps of local deviation trajectories into enough bad plans with bounded reverse multiplicity, so that the relevant volume is `O(n)` rather than global `2m`.

## 3. Method #5 — add a parameter: degree-weighted deviations do not remove the remote-volume obstruction

I tested a one-parameter family before trying another lemma on the existing route. At an internal path vertex put

\[
a_i=d(v_i)-2\ge1,
\]

and give each raw alternative at index `i` weight proportional to `a_i^{\theta-1}`. The total mass assigned to index `i` is then `a_i^\theta`, with normalization

\[
Z_\theta(\rho)=\sum_i a_i^\theta.
\]

This interpolates between:

- `theta=1`: the raw-deviation measure used in the previous plan proof;
- `theta=0`: equal total mass per path index, uniformly split among its `d(v_i)-2` alternatives;
- `theta<0`: deliberate downweighting of high-degree path vertices.

The route fails for a structural reason, not a bad choice of `theta`. The non-returning segment packing still counts ambient oriented-edge capacity. Even after reweighting, a crude bound has the form

\[
\text{good weighted mass}
\lesssim
\frac{2m}{L}\,
\frac{\max_i a_i^{\theta-1}}{Z_\theta(\rho)}.
\]

For `theta<=1`, a path of degree-three vertices has `Z_theta(rho)\asymp k` while a disjoint dense reservoir can make `m\asymp n^2`; for `theta>1`, the maximum single-deviation weight itself becomes uncontrolled. Differentiating the logarithm of `Z_theta` at `theta=0` only produces the path statistic `sum log a_i`; it contains no information about remote edge volume and therefore cannot cancel a reservoir that the path has not reached.

**Outcome of #5:** no metric movement. A per-path scalar reweighting cannot by itself repair a proof whose packing denominator is global `m`. The next successful route must localize the volume or exploit intersections rather than merely reweight existing deviations.

## 4. Method #12 — find what is conserved: exact boundary/chord decomposition

A useful diagnostic identity does survive and clarifies what `W(rho)` measures. Let

\[
S=\{v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}\}
\]

for a simple path prefix, let `B=|partial_E S|` be its edge boundary, and let `C` be the number of chords of the induced subgraph on `S`, i.e. the number of internal edges of `G[S]` in excess of the `k-1` path edges. Handshaking gives

\[
\sum_{x\in S}d(x)=2|E(S)|+B,
\qquad |E(S)|=(k-1)+C.
\]

Therefore the path excess satisfies the exact bookkeeping identity

\[
\boxed{
W(\rho)=B-d(v_0)+2C.
}
\tag{FLUX}
\]

Equivalently, in incremental form, if `b_t` is the number of already visited neighbors of `v_t` other than its predecessor and `B_t` is the boundary of the visited prefix, then

\[
d(v_t)-2=(B_t-B_{t-1})+2b_t.
\]

This exposes a genuine dichotomy:

1. if the chord term is large, many local alternatives point back into the visited set and direct collision switchings should be available;
2. if the chord term is small but `W` is large, almost all excess degree is boundary flux to still-unvisited vertices, so the proof must show that many outward excursions cannot remain mutually disjoint for long inside only `n` vertices.

The identity does **not** solve the second case. `B` itself can be order `n^2`, and the global oriented-edge packing simply recreates `(BLOCK)`. So conservation identifies the correct place to split the argument but does not yet supply the degree-free overlap-to-collision switching.

**Outcome of #12:** no metric movement. The missing information is not another scalar invariant; it is how outward boundary flux from many path positions overlaps in vertex space.

## 5. Method #24 — extremal object: sparse corridor plus dense reservoir is the proof obstruction, and pair-crossing almost repairs it

The extremal geometry for the **current proof**, not necessarily for the conjecture itself, is now clear: a low-degree corridor carrying the simple base trajectory attached through a small interface to a very dense reservoir. The corridor keeps

\[
W(\rho)=\Theta(k)
\]

while the reservoir can make `m` quadratic in its size. Dozier's fixed-degree theorem says that a genuinely bounded-degree corridor cannot itself sustain anomalously long simplicity; therefore the reservoir inflation is evidence that global `m` is the wrong certificate, not evidence for a counterexample.

I then tried to replace oriented-edge packing by **vertex-intersection packing**. For each path index and each alternative first edge, follow the base plan for `L` steps. If these clean probes were pairwise vertex-disjoint, `kL` visited probe vertices would force `kL\lesssim n`. Thus at the source scale `L\asymp n/k`, many probes must meet in vertex space. A first intersection between a later probe and an earlier probe gives an appealing switching picture: follow the later probe to the meeting vertex, reverse the earlier excursion, and return to an already visited base vertex.

This is the first route in this run that attacks the reservoir obstruction directly rather than changing constants. It nearly gives the desired local-volume replacement, but it hits a precise local information-loss barrier at the splice vertex.

### 5.1 Exact local cardinality obstruction at a splice

Let `D_d` be the derangements of a `d`-element neighbor set and let

\[
R_d=|D_d|,
\qquad
R'_d=|\{\pi\in D_d:\pi(q)=p\}|
\]

for fixed distinct `q,p`. Symmetry gives

\[
R_d=(d-1)R'_d.
\tag{5.1}
\]

At a probe intersection one arrives through neighbor `q` and wants to leave through the predecessor `p` on the excursion to be reversed. Any local surgery that simply **forces** `pi(q)=p` maps an unrestricted `R_d` local source states into only `R'_d` target states. Thus the average reverse multiplicity is at least `d-1`. This is a counting obstruction, not an artifact of the particular heat-bath implementation.

The changed-image heat bath from the preceding run makes this transparent: choosing one prescribed new image has total mass `1/(d-2)`. It is degree-free only when the set of acceptable splice outputs itself has order `d`.

Therefore the naive pair-crossing proof does not yet establish an unrestricted exponent. At a high-degree intersection visited by only two probe prefixes, the splice loses exactly the degree factor we were trying to remove.

### 5.2 Why this is still a better obstruction than global `m`

The obstruction is now **local**. It says a successful proof must do one of the following:

- create many acceptable splice outputs at a high-degree meeting vertex, so the `d-1` local states are not compressed into one target condition;
- carry enough reversible auxiliary information through the switching to recover the old local image;
- or use a multi-probe / multiscale construction in which a high-degree failed splice immediately creates many new probes, forcing another overlap at a smaller effective scale.

That is much more specific than "irregular degrees break the proof."

### 5.3 Extended-path route worth trying next

There is one concrete route that survives this run but is **not yet a claim**. Instead of encoding all alternatives inside one derangement plan, augment a simple base NBW with independent NBW continuation trees from many path positions. A family of `L`-step clean continuations cannot all be vertex-disjoint once `kL>n`. Reversal of an NBW segment uses the same internal factors `1/(d(v)-1)`, so a path-space tail-switch may preserve probability weights without forcing one prescribed derangement image at a high-degree splice. This is precisely the kind of category change that could evade (5.1). I have not completed the inverse map / bounded-preimage argument, so no exponent improvement is claimed.

**Outcome of #24:** no metric movement, but the former `m`-scale obstruction has been reduced to a specific high-degree splice-information problem.

## 7. What a proof must contain that none of the current routes contains

A source-level proof now needs **one genuinely degree-free local information mechanism**. Concretely, it must provide a way to turn the fact that many outward `L`-step continuations live in only `n` vertices into a switching/probability comparison whose reverse multiplicity is bounded independently of the degree at a meeting vertex.

The missing object can be stated as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{vertex-overlap mass}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{collision-switch mass with }O(1)\text{ (or summably weighted) local reverse multiplicity}.
}
\tag{NEEDED}
\]

The existing regular/biregular proof supplies the left-hand side indirectly through uniform edge balance; Bencs et al. supply a bounded-degree version through their forward-set tree and a global maximum-degree cap. Neither supplies `(NEEDED)` for unbounded irregular degrees.

Their general theorem gives `O_\Delta(sqrt(n) log n)` expected self-intersection under fixed maximum degree. What G0012 lacks is a replacement when `Delta` grows with `n`.

This is a useful shape analogue for chains with uniform stationary distribution. The G0012 directed-edge chain is also uniform-stationary, but arbitrary slow/localized geometry means a generic stationary-chain theorem does not automatically imply a vertex collision at the source scale from a prescribed start. The needed extra structure is the graph/local-routing reversal embodied in `(NEEDED)`.
