## 3. Preserved owner proof from the preceding run

Write

\[
B(k)=((m-1)k-1)m^k,\qquad k\ge2.
\]

Choose positive `d_j` with `sum_j d_j=1`, remote integers `K_j`, and

\[
p_j:=\frac{d_j}{B(K_j)+1},\qquad
\mathbf P(X_0=K_j)=p_j,\qquad
\mathbf P(X_0=0)=1-\sum_jp_j. \tag{3.1}
\]

Then exactly

\[
\sum_j B(K_j)p_j=\sum_j(d_j-p_j)=1-\sum_jp_j,
\]

so (C) holds; moreover `E[X_0m^{X_0}]<infinity` because `K_jm^{K_j}p_j <= C_m d_j`.

The preceding run fixed

\[
d_j=2^{-j},\qquad L_j=j,\qquad n_j=K_j-L_j,
\]

and

\[
b_j:=1-(1-p_j)^{m^{n_j}}, \tag{3.2}
\]

the probability that at least one `K_j` atom appears among the `m^{n_j}` leaves of the depth-`n_j` recursion tree. The `K_j` are chosen recursively so that old atoms have already died with probability `o(b_j)` and future atoms occur with probability `o(b_j)` at scale `n_j`. One `K_j` leaf forces

\[
X_{n_j}\ge K_j-n_j=L_j.
\]

Thus the previous proof obtained

\[
\mathbf P(X_{n_j}\ge L_j)\ge b_j,
\qquad
q_{n_j}:=\mathbf P(X_{n_j}>0)\le (1+2\varepsilon_j)b_j,
\quad \varepsilon_j=2^{-j}, \tag{3.3}
\]

and therefore

\[
\mathbf P(X_{n_j}\ge L_j\mid X_{n_j}>0)\to1,
\qquad L_j\to\infty. \tag{3.4}
\]

This refutes Conjecture 2 under the bare source hypothesis.

The idea written down *before* any validation in this run was: **add one more diagonal requirement to the same recursive choice, forcing the individual critical bursts to be large on the natural `n^{-2}` scale.** No code is required because the new statement is analytic and the inequalities below are exact.

## 4. Method #3 — prove something stronger: force arbitrary `n^2` burst amplitude

The new requirement is

\[
A_j:=n_j^2 b_j\longrightarrow\infty, \tag{4.1}
\]

and in fact one may prescribe any deterministic `A_j^* -> infinity` and choose `K_j` so that `n_j^2b_j >= A_j^*`.

### Lemma 4.1 — the burst can be made arbitrarily large on the `n^{-2}` scale

For fixed `j`, as `K=K_j -> infinity` with `L_j=j` and `n=K-j`,

\[
b_j\asymp_{m,j}\frac{d_j}{K},
\qquad
n^2b_j\asymp_{m,j} d_j K. \tag{4.2}
\]

In particular `n^2b_j -> infinity` as `K_j -> infinity`.

#### Proof

Put `N=m^n=m^{K-j}`. Since

\[
B(K)+1=(((m-1)K-1)m^K)+1,
\]

we have, for `K>=2`,

\[
\frac{d_j}{(m-1)K m^K}
\le p_j
\le \frac{2d_j}{(m-1)K m^K}. \tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{d_jm^{-j}}{(m-1)K}
\le Np_j
\le \frac{2d_jm^{-j}}{(m-1)K}. \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus `Np_j -> 0`. For all sufficiently large `K`, `Np_j<=1`; using

\[
1-(1-p)^N\le Np,
\]

and, for `Np<=1`,

\[
1-(1-p)^N\ge 1-e^{-Np}\ge \tfrac12Np,
\]

we obtain

\[
\frac{d_jm^{-j}}{2(m-1)K}
\le b_j
\le \frac{2d_jm^{-j}}{(m-1)K}. \tag{4.5}
\]

Since `n=K-j~K`, (4.2) follows. `□`

### Compatibility with the old/future diagonalization

This extra requirement does not compete with the preceding construction.

- For fixed previously selected atoms, the finite-support truncation is strictly subcritical and its survival probability is exponentially small in `n`; hence it is eventually much smaller than `b_j`, which is only order `1/K_j`.
- The condition `n_j^2b_j>=A_j^*` is satisfied by taking `K_j` large.
- Once `K_1,...,K_j` are fixed, every future atom probability `p_ell` can be made arbitrarily small by taking `K_ell` large. Impose for each `ell>j`
  \[
  m^{n_j}p_ell\le \varepsilon_j2^{-(\ell-j)}b_j. \tag{4.6}
  \]
  At stage `ell` there are only finitely many earlier constraints, so all can be met simultaneously. Summing (4.6) yields total future-leaf contamination at scale `n_j` at most `\varepsilon_jb_j`.

Thus (3.3)--(3.4) and the new condition (4.1) hold for one and the same critical law.

## 7. Consequence: bare generating-function Conjecture 4(a) fails for every `s in (0,m)\{1}`

### Case `1<s<m`

\[

\ge (s^{L_j}-1)\mathbf P(X_{n_j}\ge L_j)

Thus

\[

 n_j^2\bigl(\mathbf E[s^{X_{n_j}}]-1\bigr)

} \tag{7.1}
\]

This contradicts the finite `n^{-2}` coefficient asserted in Conjecture 4(a).

### Case `0<s<1`

Let

\[
H_j(s):=\mathbf E[s^{X_{n_j}}\mid X_{n_j}>0].

From (3.4),

\[
H_j(s)
\le \mathbf P(X_{n_j}<L_j\mid X_{n_j}>0)+s^{L_j}
\longrightarrow0. \tag{7.2}

Since

\[
1-\mathbf E[s^{X_{n_j}}]

\boxed{

Again the magnitude is incompatible with a finite constant times `n^{-2}`. Hence the single lacunary law refutes Conjecture 4(a) for every fixed `s in (0,m)\{1}`.

## 8. Method #21 — compare the shape with the stable/heavy-tail literature

The stable critical literature explains why this counterexample does not merely contradict a known regular-heavy-tail theorem. Chen--Shi study regularly varying weighted tails and obtain a different critical universality regime when the standard integrability condition fails. The present mechanism is more singular: it stores the exact critical mass in isolated, successively remote atoms and chooses observation times just before each atom has been completely eroded by the recursion.

The useful comparison is therefore not a novelty claim but a structural one:

- regular heavy tails spread critical mass over all large scales;
- this construction concentrates a summable sequence of critical masses on isolated scales;
- the recursion converts each isolated atom into a separate survival burst;
- strict subcriticality of every finite truncation lets one diagonalize those bursts so that old and future scales are negligible.

## 9. Method #30 — one scale at a time reveals exactly why the third weighted moment blocks this mechanism

The source itself warns that an assumption like

\[
\mathbf E[X_0^3m^{X_0}]<\infty \tag{9.1}
\]

may be necessary. The present construction gives a clean reason that the warning is natural.

For one atom `K_j`, (4.3) also yields

\[
K_j^3m^{K_j}p_j
\ge \frac{d_jK_j^2}{m-1}. \tag{9.2}
\]

On the other hand, using `b_j<=m^{n_j}p_j`, `n_j<=K_j`, and the upper bound in (4.3),

\[
n_j^2b_j
\le \frac{2d_jK_jm^{-L_j}}{m-1}. \tag{9.3}
\]

Therefore any isolated-atom burst of this exact critical-mass type satisfying `n_j^2b_j -> infinity` forces `d_jK_j -> infinity`, and then its individual contribution in (9.2) diverges even faster. In particular the construction necessarily has

\[
\mathbf E[X_0^3m^{X_0}]=\infty. \tag{9.4}
\]

Conversely, if the same atomic parametrization were required to satisfy the finite third weighted moment, then

\[
\sum_j d_jK_j^2<\infty,
\]

so `d_jK_j -> 0`, and (9.3) forces every isolated single-atom `n^{-2}` burst amplitude to vanish. Thus this *specific* lacunary mechanism cannot refute the strengthened finite-third-moment conjecture.

That does not prove the strengthened conjecture. It sharply separates what has been refuted from what remains mathematically plausible.
