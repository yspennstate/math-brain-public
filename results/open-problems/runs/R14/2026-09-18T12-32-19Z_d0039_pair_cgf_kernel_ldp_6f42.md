## 7. Method #10 — DERANDOMIZE: the random phase disappears from the logarithmic-time action

Let \(m_0\) be fixed and sufficiently large, condition on the sigma-field through index \(2m_0\), and write

\[
S_M=L_{2M}-L_{2m_0}.
\]

Iterating (6.5) by conditional expectation gives, for every fixed real \(t\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\mathbb E\bigl[e^{tS_M}\mid\mathcal F_{2m_0}\bigr]
&=
\sum_{m=m_0+1}^{M}
\left(
\frac{\frac34t+\frac14t^2}{m}
+O_t(m^{-3/2})
\right)\\
&=
\left(\frac34t+\frac14t^2\right)\log M+O_{t,m_0}(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Hence the tail endpoint satisfies a conditional speed-`log M` LDP with cumulant

\[
\psi(t)=\frac34t+\frac14t^2
\]

and rate

\[
\psi^*(x)=\boxed{(x-\tfrac34)^2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

More is true and is needed for the kernel.

For \(0\le s\le1\), set

\[
m_M(s)=\left\lfloor m_0^{\,1-s}M^s\right\rfloor
\]

and

\[
Y_M(s)=
\frac{L_{2m_M(s)}-L_{2m_0}}{\log M}.
\]

For a partition \(0=s_0<\cdots<s_k=1\), repeated use of the **uniform** conditional estimate (6.5) gives, for arbitrary \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\),

\[
\frac1{\log M}
\log
\mathbb E
\exp\!\left\{
\log M
\sum_{i=1}^k
t_i\bigl(Y_M(s_i)-Y_M(s_{i-1})\bigr)
\right\}
\longrightarrow
\sum_{i=1}^k
(s_i-s_{i-1})\psi(t_i).
\tag{7.3}
\]

The limiting cumulant is finite, differentiable and steep on all of
\(\mathbb R^k\). Thus every finite-dimensional increment vector has the corresponding Gärtner--Ellis LDP.

For completeness, exponential tightness in path space follows from the same estimate rather than being assumed. On a logarithmic-time interval of width \(\delta\), the compensated exponentials obtained from (6.5) are positive supermartingales. Doob's maximal inequality applied with \(t>0\) and \(t<0\) gives

\[
\mathbb P\left(
\sup_{|s-s'|\le\delta}
|Y_M(s)-Y_M(s')-\tfrac34(s-s')|>\eta
\ \middle|\ \mathcal F_{2m_0}
\right)
\le
\exp\{-c(\eta,\delta)\log M+O(1)\},
\]

with \(c(\eta,\delta)\to\infty\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\). A finite cover of \([0,1]\), followed by the projective-limit theorem, yields the conditional path LDP with good rate

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J(y)
=
\int_0^1\left(y'(s)-\frac34\right)^2\,ds
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

for absolutely continuous \(y\) with \(y(0)=0\), and \(+\infty\) otherwise.

This is the derandomization step: at logarithmic speed the projective phase no longer appears in the action.

## 8. Method #7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: from Prüfer path to the positive Christoffel kernel

At \(E=0\), Breuer's definition gives the exact identity

\[
R_n^2
=
a_n^2p_n(0)^2+\mu_n^2p_{n+1}(0)^2,
\tag{8.1}
\]

hence

\[
\frac{R_n^2}{\mu_n^2}
=
q_np_n(0)^2+p_{n+1}(0)^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For integers \(M<N\), summing (8.2),

\[
T_{M,N}:=
\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}\frac{R_n^2}{\mu_n^2}
=
\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}
\left(q_np_n^2+p_{n+1}^2\right).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Let

\[
\Delta_{M,N}:=\max_{M\le n<N}|\log q_n|.
\]

Then

\[
e^{-\Delta_{M,N}}
\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}(p_n^2+p_{n+1}^2)
\le T_{M,N}\le
e^{\Delta_{M,N}}
\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}(p_n^2+p_{n+1}^2).
\tag{8.4}
\]

The pair sum differs from the corresponding tail Christoffel kernel by at most a factor two. Also
\(\mu_n^2/n\) is bounded above and below by positive deterministic constants for large \(n\), so \(T_{M,N}\) has the same logarithmic exponent as

\[
\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}\frac{R_n^2}{n}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

### Tail coefficient deviations are exponentially removable

Since \(a_n^2\sim\Gamma(2n,1)\),

\[
\mathbb P(q_n<N^{-\epsilon})
\le N^{-2n\epsilon+O(n)}
\]

for fixed \(\epsilon>0\), while the upper Gamma tail
\(\mathbb P(q_n>N^\epsilon)\) is super-polynomial in \(N\). Therefore

\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac1{\log N}
\log
\mathbb P(\Delta_{M,N}>\epsilon\log N)
\le -2M\epsilon+O_M(1/\log N).
\tag{8.6}
\]

Letting \(M\to\infty\) after the LDP bounds makes this an exponentially good approximation at arbitrary finite rate level. The same argument controls the single odd step between paired even indices, so passing from the paired path \(Y_M\) to all \(R_n\) changes no speed-`log N` rate.

### The logarithmic-sum functional

If \(y\) is a logarithmic-time amplitude path, then

\[
\sum_{n}\frac{R_n^2}{n}
\]

is a Riemann sum against \(d(\log n)\). On the \(\log N\) scale,

\[
\frac1{\log N}\log\left(
1+\sum_{n=M}^{N}\frac{R_n^2}{n}
\right)
\longrightarrow
F(y):=\max\{0,\sup_{0\le s\le1}y(s)\}.
\tag{8.7}
\]

The upper bound follows from harmonic mass \(O(\log N)\); for the lower bound, take a small logarithmic-time interval on which \(y\) is within \(\eta\) of its supremum. Its harmonic mass is order \(\log N\), so it has zero exponent and recovers the supremum. Uniform-topology continuity of \(F\) then allows contraction of (7.4).

The tail-kernel rate is therefore

\[
I_{\rm bulk}(x)
=
\inf\{\mathcal J(y):F(y)=x\}.
\tag{8.8}
\]

This variational problem is elementary but I write the missing inequality explicitly. If \(x>0\) and \(y\) first reaches \(x\) at time \(\tau\), then

\[
\int_0^\tau (y'-\tfrac34)^2ds
\ge
\frac{(x-\frac34\tau)^2}{\tau}.
\]

To keep the path from exceeding \(x\) after \(\tau\), the least additional cost is attained by the constant continuation and is

\[
\left(\frac34\right)^2(1-\tau).
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal J(y)
\ge
\frac{x^2}{\tau}-\frac32x+\frac{9}{16},
\]

which is minimized over \(0<\tau\le1\) at \(\tau=1\). The straight path \(y(s)=xs\) attains equality. At \(x=0\), the constant path \(y\equiv0\) gives the same formula. Hence

\[
\boxed{
I_{\rm bulk}(x)=
\begin{cases}
(x-\frac34)^2,&x\ge0,\\
+\infty,&x<0.
\end{cases}}
\tag{8.9}
\]

## 9. Finite-prefix branch and the unconditioned kernel

The preceding tail LDP conditions on a finite initial block. The previous R14 report proved that the **unconditioned** kernel has an exact moment wall at \(t=2\), caused by \(a_1\).

Here the finite-prefix contribution can be incorporated exactly at logarithmic scale.

For every fixed \(M\), the finite transfer vector, \(K_M\), and \(R_M^2\) are rational functions of finitely many Gaussian \(b_j\)'s and positive \(a_j\)'s. The only denominator with the smallest near-zero power is \(a_1\). Since at beta 4

\[
\mathbb P(a_1<\epsilon)
=\Theta(\epsilon^4),
\tag{9.1}
\]

a factor \(a_1^{-2}\) has logarithmic tail cost \(2h\) for a polynomial boost \(N^h\).

Precisely, if \(C_M\) denotes any fixed finite-prefix norm large enough to dominate \(1+K_M+R_M^2\), then:

1. for every \(t<2\), \(\mathbb E C_M^t<\infty\), because the negative-moment threshold for \(a_j^{-2t}\) is \(t<2j\);
2. Markov followed by \(t\uparrow2\) gives
   \[
   \limsup\frac1{\log N}\log P(C_M>N^h)\le-2h;
   \]
3. on a positive-probability compact event for all coefficients except \(a_1\), with \(b_1\) bounded away from zero and no finite transfer cancellation,
   \(C_M\ge c a_1^{-2}\), giving the matching lower bound
   \[
   \liminf\frac1{\log N}\log P(C_M>N^h)\ge-2h.
   \]

Thus the positive finite-prefix boost has exact boundary rate

\[
J_0(h)=2h,\qquad h\ge0.
\tag{9.2}
\]

A polynomial **suppression** of the finite transfer cannot be cheaper. For a fixed product of invertible \(2\times2\) transfer matrices,

\[
\sigma_{\min}(M)\ge\frac{|\det M|}{\|M\|}.
\]

On compact coefficient sets the transfer and its inverse are uniformly bounded. To obtain an \(N^{-h}\) contraction one must therefore drive at least one \(q_j\) polynomially toward zero (cost at least \(2j h_j\), with minimum index \(j=1\)) or make a Gaussian / chi coefficient polynomially large, which is super-polynomial. The resulting coarse boundary cost is at least \(2|h|\). Since the bulk drift is \(3/4<1\), a negative boundary shift never improves the minimization below.

The uniform-in-phase tail LDP means that no additional phase cost is paid when the prefix is large: the phase resulting from the rare prefix is arbitrary but (6.5) is uniform over it.

Consequently the unconditioned kernel rate is the infimal convolution

\[
I(x)
=
\inf_{0\le h\le x}
\left[
2h+I_{\rm bulk}(x-h)
\right],
\qquad x\ge0.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Differentiating the objective shows the interior optimizer is

\[
h=x-\frac74
\]

when \(x>7/4\); otherwise \(h=0\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
I(x)=
\begin{cases}
(x-\frac34)^2,&0\le x\le\frac74,\\[2mm]
2x-\frac52,&x\ge\frac74,\\[2mm]
+\infty,&x<0.
\end{cases}}
\tag{9.4}
\]

The two branches meet with value \(1\) and derivative \(2\), exactly matching the finite-prefix critical moment \(t_c=2\).

## 10. The level-2 theorem

### Theorem R14-D0039-LDP

For the beta-Hermite semi-infinite Jacobi model at \(\beta=4\), let \(\mu_4\) be the spectral measure at the first coordinate, let \(p_k\) be its first-kind orthonormal polynomials, and set

\[
K_N(0,0)=\sum_{k=0}^Np_k(0)^2,
\qquad
X_N=\frac{\log K_N(0,0)}{\log N}.
\]

Then \((X_N)\) satisfies a large-deviation principle with speed \(\log N\) and good rate function

\[
\boxed{
I(x)=
\begin{cases}
(x-\frac34)^2,&0\le x\le\frac74,\\
2x-\frac52,&x\ge\frac74,\\
+\infty,&x<0.
\end{cases}}
\]

That is, for every Borel set \(A\),

\[
-\inf_{x\in A^\circ}I(x)
\le
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\frac1{\log N}\log P(X_N\in A)
\]

and

\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac1{\log N}\log P(X_N\in A)
\le
-\inf_{x\in\overline A}I(x).
\]
