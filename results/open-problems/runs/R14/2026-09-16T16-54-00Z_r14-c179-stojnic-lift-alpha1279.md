### 6.1 Stojnic Theorem-4 condition and sign audit

The PDF's equations (24)--(28) should be read from the typeset source, not OCR: OCR drops some minus signs.  Starting from the displayed identity

`min_{||lambda||=1,lambda>=0}[-g^T lambda-kappa 1^T lambda]`

one obtains

`max_{gamma>0}[- f_1(g,kappa)/(4 gamma) - gamma]`,

where

`f_1(g,kappa)=sum_i (max(g_i+kappa,0))^2`.

Consequently the large-`n` scalar appearing in Theorem 4 is

`I_per(c,alpha,kappa) = sup_{gamma>0}[-gamma + (alpha/c) log E exp{- c (max(G+kappa,0))^2/(4 gamma)}]`.

The signs are also forced by the paper's later parameter `p=1+c/(2 gamma)` and by the finite numerical optimizer `gamma=0.2922<c/2` at `kappa=-1`; a positive quadratic exponential would diverge there.

The spherical term is

`I_sph(c)=ghat-(1/(2c)) log(1-c/(2 ghat))`,

`ghat=(c+sqrt(c^2+4))/4`.

Theorem 4 states that if

`min_{c>=0}[-c/2 + I_sph(c)+I_per(c,alpha,kappa)] < 0`,

then the unit-sphere system is infeasible with overwhelming probability.

The source explicitly separates this rigorous theorem from its numerically optimized tables and warns that the latter can carry finite-precision error.

Therefore it suffices to prove, at one exact `c`, that the entire `gamma` supremum keeps the displayed objective below zero.

### 6.2 Exact scalar transform at `kappa=-1`

Set

`Y=(G-1)_+^2`.

For `t=c/(4 gamma)` and

`s=(1+2t)^(-1/2)`,

we have

`gamma(s)=c s^2/[2(1-s^2)]`.

Complete the square on the tail integral:

`L(s):=E exp[-tY]`

`= Phi(1) + s exp[-(1-s^2)/2] barPhi(s)`.

Indeed,

`int_1^infinity phi(g) exp[-t(g-1)^2] dg`

`= s exp[-(1-s^2)/2] barPhi(s)`.

Define

`u(s)=1-L(s)=barPhi(1)-s exp[-(1-s^2)/2]barPhi(s)`.

Then

`I_per(c,alpha,-1)=sup_{0<s<1} J(s)`,

where

`J(s)=- c s^2/[2(1-s^2)] + (alpha/c) log(1-u(s)).`

No numerical stationarity assumption remains.

### 6.3 Monotonicity needed for a global supremum certificate

Write the inverse Mills ratio

`r(s)=barPhi(s)/phi(s)`.

Because

`exp[-(1-s^2)/2] phi(s)=phi(1)`,

we can write the tail contribution as

`A(s)=s phi(1) r(s)`.

Since

`r'(s)=s r(s)-1`,

`A'(s)=phi(1)[(1+s^2)r(s)-s]`.

For `s>0`, integration by parts gives the strict Mills lower bound

`barPhi(s) > phi(s) s/(1+s^2)`:

`barPhi(s)=phi(s)/s-int_s^infinity phi(t)/t^2 dt`

and the last integral is strictly smaller than `barPhi(s)/s^2`.

Therefore `A'(s)>0` and

`u'(s)<0`.

This is the differentiation step that makes a rigorous interval cover of the entire dual scalar possible.
