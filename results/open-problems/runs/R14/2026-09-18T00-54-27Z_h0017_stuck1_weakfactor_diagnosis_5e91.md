## 5. Method #20 reused under Section I: why the weak-factor purification inference is invalid

The previous point-to-hyperplane route suggested: each finite-volume object is a measurable function of the iid environment, so perhaps a weak limit should remain an ergodic factor of iid and therefore cannot carry the non-ergodic global endpoint phase coin.

That inference is false in general on an amenable lattice. A standard Følner-majority example gives the exact obstruction.

Let `(xi_z)` be iid fair `{-1,+1}` spins on `Z^2`, let `B_n=[-n,n]^2`, and set

`Y_n(omega)=1{ sum_{z in B_n} xi_z > 0 }`

(with any fixed tie convention). Consider the translation-covariant process

`Z_n(v)=Y_n(T_v omega)`.

For every `n`, `Z_n` is a factor of the iid field, hence its law is ergodic. But for each fixed translation `v`, the boxes `B_n` and `B_n+v` differ on only `O(n)` sites while each contains `Theta(n^2)` sites. Writing the two sums as a common overlap sum plus their two boundary-difference sums shows

`P(Z_n(v) != Z_n(0)) -> 0`.

For example, choose threshold `n^(3/4)`: the overlap sum has standard deviation `Theta(n)`, so its probability of lying in an interval of width `O(n^(3/4))` tends to zero, while each boundary-difference sum has variance `O(n)` and exceeds `n^(3/4)` with probability tending to zero. A union bound handles any fixed finite set of translations. Meanwhile `P(Y_n=1)->1/2`.

Hence the finite-dimensional distributions of `Z_n` converge to a process that is a **single fair global coin repeated at every site**. The limit is non-ergodic although every approximating process is an ergodic factor of iid.

This is not a new FPP theorem; it is a standard sanity-check mechanism showing exactly why “finite-volume factor + weak convergence => factor/ergodic limit” cannot purify the Damron--Hanson endpoint mixture. To make that route work one needs a *geodesic-specific property stronger than weak factor convergence* (for example, a uniform spatial mixing/tail-localization estimate or actual almost-sure stabilization), and no R14 route has produced such a property.
