### 3.2 This recovery: #9 RANDOMIZE

I tried to use randomization as an **analytic decomposition device**, not as a replacement for the worst-case source supremum.

The exact factorization

\[
f_*(t)=|1-e^{it}|^2h(t),
\qquad
h(t)=\frac1{\log(2e/|1-e^{it}|)},
\tag{3.1}
\]

has a useful scale-mixture identity. If

\[
\lambda(t)=|1-e^{it}|^2,
\qquad C=4e^2,
\]

then `0<=lambda<=4<C` and algebraically

\[
h(t)
=\frac{2}{\log(C/\lambda(t))}
=2\int_0^\infty C^{-s}\lambda(t)^s\,ds,
\tag{3.2}
\]

hence

\[
f_*(t)=2\int_0^\infty C^{-s}\lambda(t)^{1+s}\,ds.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This suggests randomizing or square-function decomposing an arbitrary coefficient vector across fractional Sobolev scales `s`, then trying to control the cross term between separated components by Khintchine/Cotlar almost-orthogonality.

The route fails at the normalization step. For each fixed noninteger `s>0`, the spectrum `lambda^{1+s}` has a non-even algebraic zero, so the earlier Helson--Sarason/VMO theorem says it does **not even satisfy ordinary** `r(n)->0`. Thus one cannot prove a useful separated-subspace angle bound scale-by-scale and integrate it. Positivity of the quadratic forms in `(3.3)` controls energies after integration, but normalized cross angles do not commute with a positive mixture: a bad near-null direction may depend on `s` and the denominator is itself integrated. Random signs can bound Hilbert--Schmidt or average interactions between components, but Bradley's `r^*` is an operator norm and an adversary can select the coherent sign pattern.

A second randomization attempt decomposed `Q` and `S` into connected interval components and randomized component signs. This again gives an average square-function quantity, while `(2.1)` takes the supremum after normalization. The missing estimate is a deterministic Cotlar-type bound that survives arbitrarily many components and arbitrarily small Gram eigenvalues. Randomization by itself does not supply it.

**Outcome of #9: useful scale-mixture viewpoint, but no source movement.**

#### (ii) Treil--Volberg: complete regularity and weighted operator theory

Treil and Volberg, *Completely regular multivariate stationary process and the Muckenhoupt condition* (arXiv:math/9712279; Pacific J. Math. 190 (1999)), give necessary-and-sufficient weighted/operator conditions for **ordinary past/future complete regularity**, including matrix-valued processes. Their formulation turns complete regularity into asymptotic orthogonality in a weighted `L^2` space and connects it to local Muckenhoupt/Carleson behavior.

That machinery is very close in language to `(2.1)` but still has a global past/future order. Bradley's interlaced coefficient allows arbitrarily alternating finite sets. The missing theorem is not another scalar Muckenhoupt criterion; it is an extension from one ordered cut to an unbounded number of alternating cuts with constants independent of the number of cuts.

#### (iii) Pushnitski--Yafaev: logarithmic Hankel singularities

Pushnitski and Yafaev, *Sharp estimates for singular values of Hankel operators* (arXiv:1412.0551), prove sharp power-scale singular-value bounds for Hankel matrices whose entries behave like

\[
O\!\left(j^{-1}(\log j)^{-\alpha}\right).
\]

Their later *Best rational approximation of functions with logarithmic singularities* (arXiv:1601.00882) combines Hankel singular-value asymptotics with the Adamyan--Arov--Krein theorem to obtain BMO rational-approximation asymptotics for logarithmic singularities.

This is the nearest analytic technology to the residual `-\log\log`/logarithmic scale in `(1.1)`. It is promising for **ordinary** past/future projection tails or approximation numbers. However, singular values of one Hankel operator do not directly control the supremum over all arbitrary interlaced coordinate compressions in `(2.1)`. To port the method, one needs an interlaced localization principle showing that every separated Toeplitz cross-angle can be decomposed into Hankel pieces with an operator-norm summation that has no component-count loss.

No such theorem surfaced in the bounded search.

**Outcome of #21: the missing mechanism can now be named precisely — an interlaced Hankel/Cotlar localization theorem, or a Utev-rate verification for the special weight. Neither is presently proved here.**
