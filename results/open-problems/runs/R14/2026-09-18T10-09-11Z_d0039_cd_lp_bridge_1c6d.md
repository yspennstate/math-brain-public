## 3. Exact source and current-literature status

The source is Bálint Virág, *Operator limits of random matrices*, ICM 2014 / arXiv:1804.06953, Section 4, Question 1. Its semi-infinite beta-Hermite Jacobi matrix `J_beta` has spectral measure `mu_beta` at the first coordinate. Virág recalls that the degree-n orthogonal-polynomial zeros have the n-point beta-Hermite law and that Breuer--Forrester--Smilansky proved

`dim_H(mu_beta) = (1-2/beta)^+`.

The source asks whether `mu_beta` and a Gaussian multiplicative cascade measure with the same Hausdorff dimension have the same fractal / multifractal spectrum.

The 2026 Chhaibi--Najnudel JEMS theorem is an exact GMC identification for the **circular** beta ensemble / OPUC spectral measure, not Virág's beta-Hermite Jacobi measure. A bounded fresh search on 2026-09-18 again found no later primary theorem closing the beta-Hermite question.

The important new source relative to the old intake attempt is Gaultier Lambert and Elliot Paquette, *Bulk asymptotics of the Gaussian beta-ensemble characteristic polynomial*, arXiv:2508.01458 (2025). Their starting random Jacobi matrix is

`A = tridiag(a_k,b_k,a_k)`,  `b_k ~ N(0,2)`,  `a_k ~ chi_{beta k}`,

independently, so `beta^{-1/2} A` is precisely the nested beta-Hermite Jacobi operator relevant to Virág's `mu_beta` (up to the source's harmless indexing convention). ## 5. Method #2 — attack the negation first

I first tried to find a structural reason the beta-Hermite and matched-cascade spectra must differ.

### 5.1 Atomic / zero-dimensional regime

For `beta<2`, Virág records that `mu_beta` is pure point. This is not by itself a clean refutation because “the Gaussian multiplicative cascade with the same Hausdorff dimension” is convention-sensitive at dimension zero: critical and supercritical cascade normalizations have different limiting objects, and the source does not fix a convention narrowly enough to turn this into an honest counterexample. I therefore do **not** label the source refuted.

### 5.2 Support geometry

`mu_beta` lives on the real line with unbounded support whereas the standard cascade is often normalized on a compact interval. This also does not refute equality of the local multifractal spectrum: smooth localization / coordinate changes do not make support size itself a multifractal invariant.

### 5.3 Non-Gaussian chi noise: a useful warning, not a refutation

Let the off-diagonal entries of `J_beta=beta^{-1/2}A` be

`c_j = a_j/sqrt(beta)`,  with `a_j^2 ~ chi^2_{beta j}`,

and define the exact normalization factor

`Q_n := n! / prod_{j=1}^n c_j^2 = beta^n n! / prod_{j=1}^n a_j^2`.

For fixed real `t<beta/2`, gamma moments give

`E Q_n^t = prod_{j=1}^n [ (beta j/2)^t Gamma(beta j/2 - t) / Gamma(beta j/2) ]`.

Using

`log Gamma(x-t)-log Gamma(x)+t log x = t(t+1)/(2x)+O_t(x^{-2})`,

we obtain the exact scaled cumulant limit

`lim_{n->infty} (1/log n) log E Q_n^t = t(t+1)/beta`,  `t<beta/2`.

The finite right endpoint `t=beta/2` is caused entirely by the first chi variable. Consequently a naive **annealed** speed-`log n` Gartner--Ellis argument sees finite-prefix rare events and is not automatically the quenched multifractal calculation one wants. After conditioning on / discarding a fixed initial segment, the moment domain expands with the starting index and the log-scale quadratic bulk survives. This is evidence against using raw disorder tails as the spectrum, but it is not evidence against Virág's conjectural equality.

**Outcome of #2:** no honest source counterexample. The counterexample search instead identifies a trap: finite-prefix annealed tails must be separated from the tail-Jacobi fluctuations that can govern almost-sure level sets.

## 6. Method #18 — exact Christoffel--Darboux bridge to Lambert--Paquette

This is the metric-moving step.

Index the semi-infinite Jacobi matrix by `0,1,...` and write

`J_beta(k,k)=d_k`,  `J_beta(k-1,k)=J_beta(k,k-1)=c_k>0`.

Let `J_beta^(n)` be the leading `n x n` minor and

`D_n(E) := det(E I_n - J_beta^(n))`,  `D_0=1`.

Let `p_n` be the orthonormal polynomials of `mu_beta`, normalized with positive leading coefficient. From the Jacobi recurrence,

`p_n(E) = D_n(E) / C_n`,  where  `C_n := prod_{j=1}^n c_j`.

Define the first-kind polynomial norm / Christoffel kernel

`K_n(E,E) := sum_{k=0}^n p_k(E)^2`.

### Proposition (exact boundary-data formula)

For every real `E` and every `n>=0`,

`K_n(E,E) = c_{n+1}[p'_{n+1}(E)p_n(E)-p_{n+1}(E)p'_n(E)]`

and hence

`K_n(E,E) = [D'_{n+1}(E)D_n(E)-D_{n+1}(E)D'_n(E)] / C_n^2`.   (6.1)

For Lambert--Paquette's normalization

`hatPhi_n(E) = D_n(E)/sqrt(n!)`

and `c_j=a_j/sqrt(beta)`, this becomes the completely explicit identity

`K_n(E,E)`
` = [beta^n n! sqrt(n+1) / prod_{j=1}^n a_j^2]`
`   * [hatPhi'_{n+1}(E)hatPhi_n(E)-hatPhi_{n+1}(E)hatPhi'_n(E)]`.   (6.2)

In particular, the Wronskian in brackets in (6.2) is strictly positive for real `E`.

### Proof

The orthonormal Jacobi recurrence can be written

`E p_k(E)=c_{k+1}p_{k+1}(E)+d_k p_k(E)+c_k p_{k-1}(E)`,

with `p_{-1}=0`, `p_0=1`. Apply this recurrence at `x` and `y`, multiply crosswise by `p_k(y)` and `p_k(x)`, and subtract. One obtains

`(x-y)p_k(x)p_k(y)`
` = c_{k+1}[p_{k+1}(x)p_k(y)-p_k(x)p_{k+1}(y)]`
`   - c_k[p_k(x)p_{k-1}(y)-p_{k-1}(x)p_k(y)]`.

Summing from `k=0` to `n` telescopes, since the `k=0` lower boundary vanishes. Therefore, for `x != y`,

`sum_{k=0}^n p_k(x)p_k(y)`
` = c_{n+1}[p_{n+1}(x)p_n(y)-p_n(x)p_{n+1}(y)]/(x-y)`.

Let `y->x=E`. This gives the first displayed formula. Substituting `p_n=D_n/C_n` and `C_{n+1}=C_n c_{n+1}` cancels the boundary coefficient and yields (6.1). Finally Lambert--Paquette equation (1.25) is `D_n=sqrt(n!) hatPhi_n`, while `C_n^2=beta^{-n} prod_{j=1}^n a_j^2`; substitution yields (6.2). Positivity follows because the left side is a sum of squares with `p_0=1`. QED.

### Why this matters

The old route treated `sum_{k<=n}|p_k(E)|^2` as a path observable requiring simultaneous control of all intermediate `k`. Formula (6.2) removes that obligation **exactly**. The positive norm is boundary data: two consecutive characteristic polynomials and their energy derivatives, plus an explicit gamma-product normalization.

It also removes the most obvious oscillatory singularity. Individual real characteristic polynomials contain a `cos(phase)` factor and can be arbitrarily close to zero; the positive Wronskian in (6.2) cannot inherit an uncancelled `log|cos|` singularity. This is the concrete source-relevant reason `B_spectrum` moves from 0 to 1.

Prior-art label: the Christoffel--Darboux identity is classical (and Lambert--Paquette's Appendix B already differentiates a related Prüfer phase). The contribution of this run is the route identification and exact normalization match to Virág's `J_beta`, **not a claim that Christoffel--Darboux is new**.

## 7. Method #30 — freeze `beta=4, E=0` and isolate the exact remaining failure

At `beta=4`, Virág's known Hausdorff dimension is `1-2/beta=1/2`, so this is the first clean nontrivial test case.

Lambert--Paquette Theorem 4.4, in the same infinite Jacobi coupling, gives for fixed unscaled `mu`

`hatPsi_n(mu) = -1/4 log n + i*pi*n/2 + i*sqrt(n) mu`
`                 + beta^{-1/2} hatM_{n,m}(mu) + Omega_n^(m)(mu)`,

with `Omega_n^(m)(mu)` converging in probability, together with microscopic finite-dimensional convergence under shifts `mu + pi lambda/sqrt(N)` to the stochastic-sine process. Their equation (4.14) then recovers the characteristic polynomial `D_n(mu)` from this complex phase.

This is much closer to D0039 than the old intake knew. But it does **not** yet give level 2 of the metric. Three precise obligations remain.

### (A) Derivative / Wronskian control at large-deviation strength

Equation (6.2) needs derivatives of two consecutive characteristic polynomials. Finite-dimensional convergence of microscopic ratios cannot simply be differentiated. Lambert--Paquette explicitly note in the proof of the microscopic phase limit that the coupling used there has **no explicit rate of convergence**. For a speed-`log n` LDP one needs local-uniform or direct Wronskian control whose failure probability is exponentially negligible on the `log n` scale, not merely convergence in probability.

### (B) Joint, not independent, treatment of the gamma product and Wronskian

The prefactor

`Q_n = beta^n n! / prod a_j^2`

has an explicit gamma-product cumulant structure, but the same `a_j` enter the transfer-matrix martingale and hence the Wronskian. One may not add independent rate functions. A correct proof needs the joint conditional log-mgf (or an exponential-equivalence decomposition) for

`log Q_n + log W_n(E)`.

### (C) Fixed-energy LDP is still not a multifractal spectrum

Even a complete `E=0` rate function is only a one-point disorder statement. Source progress at level 3 requires uniform energy control strong enough to estimate Hausdorff dimensions of exceptional level sets and then compare them with the matched cascade parabola. This is where a covering argument / energy process LDP enters.

**Outcome of #30:** the old “path norm” obstruction is gone, but it is replaced by a sharper one: obtain an exponential-rate derivative/Wronskian version of the Lambert--Paquette local characteristic-polynomial asymptotics and then make it energy-uniform.
