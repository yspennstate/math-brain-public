## 1. Idea before code and the three attacks

The mathematical idea was written before checker code at

`artifacts/prob10_2026-09-20/PROB10-02/20260920T0742Z_r17_reward_flux_idea_3c71.md`

(creation commit `6d64bd46461aa7b992d23eaafae2818d9ba33e26`).

The motivating observation is that the preceding R17 transfer estimates paid for the **time until two strip chains coalesce**. That can be vastly larger than the actual difference of their cumulative rewards. The three attacks were:

1. **Reward-flux / coboundary attack.** For one ordered gradient defect, derive the exact difference of one-step rewards as a telescoping central-cut indicator plus a one-time left-exit flux.
2. **Raw-symbol sensitivity attack.** Show that changing one raw source symbol in the initial strip can change the cumulative future strip reward by at most one, even though that symbol is compared against several future `v` bits while it remains in the window.
3. **Strong `m`-span attack.** Test whether the resulting general `2m` state-span bound can in fact be cut to `m`. Exact small-width common-word enumeration supports this for `m=2,4`, but this run does not contain a proof, so the `m` version is recorded only as evidence.

Attacks 1 and 2 succeed as general theorems. Attack 3 remains unresolved.

## 2. Exact genuine-source strip recursion

Fix strip half-width `w` and set
\[
m=2w.
\]
A formal state is
\[
s=(x,g),\qquad x,g\in\{0,1\}^m.
\]
Let
\[
V_0=0,\qquad V_k=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i\quad(1\le k\le m).
\]
For a fresh actual source pair `(u,v)`, put
\[
a_i={\bf1}_{\{x_{i+1}=v\}}\quad(0\le i<m),
\qquad
a_m={\bf1}_{\{u=v\}}.
\]
The new boundary profile is the max-plus/LCS recursion
\[
Q_0=\max\{V_1,V_0+a_0\},
\tag{2.1}
\]
\[
Q_k=\max\{Q_{k-1},V_k+a_k,V_{k+1}\},
\qquad1\le k<m,
\tag{2.2}
\]
\[
Q_m=\max\{Q_{m-1},V_m+a_m\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
The next raw window and gradient are
\[
x'=(x_2,\ldots,x_m,u),
\tag{2.4}
\]
\[
g'_k=Q_k-Q_{k-1},\qquad1\le k\le m,
\tag{2.5}
\]
and the one-step diagonal reward is
\[
\rho(s;u,v)=Q_w-V_w\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2.6}
\]
The same `v` occurs in every `a_i`, so (2.1)--(2.6) are the true dependent LCS strip, not an independent-match surrogate.

The equivalent carrier recursion from the preceding reports is
\[
d_0=0,
\qquad
d_i=(1-g_i)\bigl(d_{i-1}\vee{\bf1}_{\{x_i=v\}}\bigr),
\tag{2.7}
\]
\[
g'_i=g_{i+1}+d_{i+1}-d_i\ (i<m),
\qquad
g'_m=(1-d_m){\bf1}_{\{u=v\}}.
\tag{2.8}
\]
The inherited one-defect theorem says that under a common source update, two ordered fronts differing in one gradient bit either coalesce or still differ in exactly one gradient bit.

## 3. Attack 1 succeeds: an exact reward-flux identity

Take one raw window `x` and two gradients
\[
g\le h=g+e_j,
\qquad g_j=0,
\tag{3.1}
\]
where coordinates are one-based. Let `V^-`,`V^+` be their cumulative profiles and let `Q^-`,`Q^+` be the outputs of (2.1)--(2.3) under the **same** source pair `(u,v)`. Then
\[
V_k^+-V_k^-={\bf1}_{\{k\ge j\}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Define the central-cut indicator of the incoming defect by
\[
I(e):={\bf1}_{\{j\le w\}}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
If the output still has one gradient defect at coordinate `j'`, set
\[
I(e')={\bf1}_{\{j'\le w\}};
\]
if it is absorbed, set `I(e')=0`. Finally define the left-boundary profile flux
\[
\ell:=Q_0^+-Q_0^-.
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — one-defect reward is a coboundary plus left-exit flux

For every formal edge state (3.1) and every genuine source pair `(u,v)`,
\[
\boxed{
\rho(h;u,v)-\rho(g;u,v)
=I(e')+\ell-I(e).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Moreover `ell in {0,1}`. If the defect survives, `ell=0`; if it is absorbed, the output profile difference is the constant `ell`, so `ell=1` is exactly left absorption and `ell=0` right absorption.

#### Proof

Monotonicity of the LCS recursion gives
\[
0\le Q_k^+-Q_k^-\le1
\]
for every `k`, because the incoming profiles differ pointwise by at most one. By the inherited one-defect theorem, the output gradients either agree or differ at a unique coordinate.

If the output gradients differ at `j'`, then from (2.5)
\[
(Q_k^+-Q_k^-)-(Q_{k-1}^+-Q_{k-1}^-)
=h'_k-g'_k={\bf1}_{\{k=j'\}}.
\]
The profile difference is nonnegative and at most one, so necessarily
\[
Q_k^+-Q_k^-={\bf1}_{\{k\ge j'\}}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
In particular `ell=0` and
\[
Q_w^+-Q_w^-=I(e').
\tag{3.7}
\]

If the output gradients agree, the same difference equation shows that `Q_k^+-Q_k^-` is constant in `k`, equal to its value `ell` at `k=0`. Since the profile difference lies in `{0,1}`, `ell in {0,1}` and
\[
Q_w^+-Q_w^-=\ell.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Using the reward definition (2.6) and the incoming profile step (3.2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho(h;u,v)-\rho(g;u,v)
&=(Q_w^+-Q_w^-)-(V_w^+-V_w^-)\\
&=(Q_w^+-Q_w^-)-I(e).
\end{aligned}
\]
Substituting (3.7) in the surviving case and (3.8) in the absorbed case gives (3.5). QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — one initial gradient defect costs at most one total reward

Drive the two chains by any common future source word of arbitrary finite length `N`. Let `e_t` be the surviving defect when it exists. Sum (3.5) over time. The `I(e_t)` terms telescope. The left-exit term can occur at most once because it appears only when the unique defect is absorbed. Therefore
\[
R_N(h)-R_N(g)
=I(e_N)-I(e_0)+L_N,
\tag{3.9}
\]
where `L_N in {0,1}` indicates whether left absorption occurred by time `N`. Hence
\[
\boxed{
|R_N(h)-R_N(g)|\le1
\quad\text{for every common future word and every }N.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]
This bound is independent of the defect's lifetime. A defect may survive for a long time, but it cannot accumulate an arbitrarily large reward discrepancy.

This is the central methodological change of the run: for finite-horizon reward bias, the right object is the **reward flux**, not the coalescence time.

## 4. Attack 2 succeeds: one raw source bit also costs at most one

The gradient theorem does not by itself compare arbitrary formal states because their raw windows can differ. The required raw-symbol estimate is also pathwise.

### Theorem 4.1 — one-symbol raw-window Lipschitz bound

Fix an initial gradient `g`, a future source word of length `N`, and two initial raw windows `x,\tilde x` differing at exactly one coordinate. Then
\[
\boxed{
|R_N(x,g)-R_N(\tilde x,g)|\le1.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Unroll (2.1)--(2.6) for `N` source updates into the ordinary directed LCS dynamic-programming graph on the corresponding finite strip. Boundary vertices on the initial cross-section carry the fixed values `V_k`; horizontal and vertical steps have weight zero; a diagonal step has weight one exactly when the associated `X`-symbol and `Y`-symbol agree. The endpoint value minus the initial central value is precisely the cumulative reward `R_N`.

Changing one coordinate of the initial raw window changes the label of exactly one `X`-symbol in this unrolled graph. It may therefore change several **candidate** diagonal-match edges, because that symbol is compared against several `Y`-symbols before it leaves the strip. However every directed monotone alignment path uses a given `X`-symbol in at most one diagonal step. Consequently the weight of every fixed path changes by at most one when that single raw symbol is flipped.

The maximum of a family of numbers each changed by at most one changes by at most one. The initial boundary values `V_k` are identical because `g` is fixed. Thus the endpoint optimum, hence `R_N`, changes by at most one, proving (4.1). QED.

This argument is precisely dependence-preserving: the repeated comparisons involving the changed source symbol are not independent random variables; they are deterministic functions of one shared source symbol, and the monotone-path constraint controls them all at once.

## 5. Full-state pathwise oscillation and the new exact transfer

Interpolate between arbitrary formal states
\[
s=(x,g),\qquad t=(y,h)
\]
by flipping their differing raw bits one at a time and then their differing gradient bits one at a time. Theorem 4.1 bounds each raw flip by one; Corollary 3.2 bounds each gradient flip by one (reverse the ordered pair when necessary). Therefore, for every common future source word and every horizon `N`,
\[
\boxed{
|R_N(s)-R_N(t)|
\le d_H(x,y)+d_H(g,h)
\le2m.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is deterministic and does not require the two chains to coalesce.

Let
\[
F_N(s)=\mathbf E_sR_N.
\]
Taking expectations in (5.1) gives
\[
\operatorname{span}F_N
:=\max_sF_N(s)-\min_sF_N(s)
\le2m.
\tag{5.2}
\]
The inherited mismatch-reset/synchronizing theorem gives a unique recurrent class and stationary law `pi_w` for the finite width-`w` strip. Let `gamma_w` be its stationary mean one-step reward. A chain started from `pi_w` is stationary, so
\[
\sum_s\pi_w(s)F_N(s)=N\gamma_w.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Since a convex combination lies between the minimum and maximum of `F_N`, (5.2)--(5.3) prove:

### Theorem 5.1 — linear deterministic finite-horizon-to-stationary transfer

For every strip half-width `w`, `m=2w`, every formal starting state `s`, and every horizon `N`,
\[
\boxed{
\left|\mathbf E_sR_N-N\gamma_w\right|
\le2m=4w.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbf E_sR_N-4w}{N}
\le\gamma_w\le
\frac{\mathbf E_sR_N+4w}{N}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This removes all exponential reset/coalescence constants from the finite-horizon bias estimate.

## 6. Attack 3: stronger `m`-span is supported in small cases but unproved

The interpolation proof loses a factor two by paying separately for the `m` raw bits and `m` gradient bits. A natural stronger conjecture is
\[
\operatorname{span}R_N(\cdot;\omega)\le m
\tag{6.1}
\]
for every fixed future source word `omega` and every horizon `N`.

The exact checker exhaustively propagates common-word reward differences for all formal state pairs at `m=2` and `m=4`. It obtains:

- `m=2`: maximum absolute difference reaches `2` by horizon `2` and remains `2` through horizon `6`;
- `m=4`: maximum absolute difference reaches `4` by horizon `4` and remains `4` through horizon `10`.

Thus (6.1) is sharp in the tested widths and horizons. **This is evidence only.** I did not find a proof that combines raw-symbol and gradient discrepancies without double counting, so the theorem stated in Section 5 remains the rigorous `2m` bound.

A proof of (6.1) would improve (5.4) by another factor two, but it would still be a finite-to-stationary transfer and would not alone determine `gamma_2`.
