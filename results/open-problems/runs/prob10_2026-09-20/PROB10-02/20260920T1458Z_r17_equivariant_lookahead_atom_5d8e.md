## 2. The genuine-source finite-lookahead process

Fix `k>=1`. A state is
\[
s=(x,y)\in\{0,1\}^k\times\{0,1\}^k,
\]
where `x` and `y` are the next `k` unconsumed letters of the actual independent iid fair source strings `X` and `Y`.

For a window `x=x_1\cdots x_k` and bit `u`, write
\[
\sigma_u x=x_2\cdots x_ku.
\]

A deterministic policy obeys the following source-valid rules.

### Equal heads

If `x_1=y_1`, match the two leading letters. Reward and consumption are
\[
r=1,\qquad \Delta C_X=\Delta C_Y=1.
\]
Reveal independent fresh source bits `u,v` and move to
\[
(\sigma_u x,\sigma_v y),\qquad (u,v)\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{0,1\}^2.
\]

### Mismatching heads

If `x_1\ne y_1`, choose one source to discard from.

- X-action: `r=0`, `(Delta C_X,Delta C_Y)=(1,0)`, reveal a fresh iid X-bit `u`, and move to `(sigma_u x,y)`.
- Y-action: `r=0`, `(Delta C_X,Delta C_Y)=(0,1)`, reveal a fresh iid Y-bit `v`, and move to `(x,sigma_v y)`.

Every reward-one operation is therefore an actual common-subsequence match. Reusing a source bit automatically induces all of the correct match dependence. There is no independent match-indicator field.

Put the average source consumption
\[
c=\frac{\Delta C_X+\Delta C_Y}{2}
=\begin{cases}
1,&x_1=y_1,\\
1/2,&x_1\ne y_1.
\end{cases}
\]

A policy is **swap equivariant** if swapping `(x,y)` swaps X/Y actions. Equal-head matching is already invariant.

## 3. Bellman lower certificate

### Theorem 3.1 — finite-state average-consumption certificate

Fix a deterministic finite-window policy and `rho in R`. Suppose there is a bounded potential `h` such that for every state `s`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf E_s\left[r-\rho c+h(S_1)-h(s)\right]\ge0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Then for every bounded stopping time `tau`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf E R_\tau\ge \rho\,\mathbf E A_\tau-\operatorname{osc}(h),
\qquad A_\tau=\frac{C_X(\tau)+C_Y(\tau)}2.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

The process
\[
Z_t=R_t-\rho A_t+h(S_t)-h(S_0)
\]
is a submartingale by (3.1). Optional stopping at bounded `tau` gives `E Z_tau>=0`. Since
\[
h(S_\tau)-h(S_0)\le\operatorname{osc}(h),
\]
rearrangement gives (3.2). QED.

The missing issue is that average consumption is not yet common prefix length: if one source is discarded much faster than the other, `A_tau` may overstate the smaller consumed prefix. The next theorem gives a completely proved balance transfer; no stationary/mixing assertion is assumed.

## 4. A policy-independent synchronizing source atom

### Theorem 4.1 — `5k`-step common atom

Let `P` be the state kernel of **any deterministic lookahead policy that always matches equal heads**. Let
\[
z=(0^k,0^k),\qquad m=5k,\qquad \delta_k=2^{-10k}.
\]
Then for every state `s`,
\[
\boxed{P^m(s,\{z\})\ge\delta_k.}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{span}(P^m f)\le(1-\delta_k)\operatorname{span}(f)
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
for every real function `f` on the finite state space. In particular the chain has a unique stationary law.

#### Proof

We construct, separately from every starting state, a fixed-probability event in the still-unrevealed source bits. The choices below are adaptive only in the harmless deferred-decisions sense: whenever the policy requests a fresh iid bit, prescribe a definite value determined by the already exposed history. Each prescribed fair bit has conditional probability `1/2`.

**Stage 1: create one fully alternating window.** Whenever a fresh bit on a side is requested, prescribe the next bit of a private alternating stream on that side. Every decision advances at least one source coordinate. After `2k-1` decisions the total number of X/Y advances is at least `2k-1`; hence by the pigeonhole principle at least one side has advanced at least `k` times. Its entire current `k`-window therefore consists of its prescribed alternating stream. Continued prescriptions keep that window alternating whenever it moves.

**Stage 2: flush the lagging window.** Once one side, say X, is fully alternating, the other side advances within at most two decisions. Indeed, if the policy advances Y at a mismatch, this is immediate. If instead it advances the alternating X-window, the new X head is the opposite of its old head. Because the previous heads mismatched and the alphabet is binary, the new X head equals the still-frozen Y head. The next decision is therefore a forced equal-head match and advances Y as well. Each of the at most `k` further Y advances costs at most two decisions. Thus both windows are alternating by time at most
\[
(2k-1)+2k=4k-1.
\]

**Stage 3: align phases.** There are only two binary alternating words of length `k`. If the two alternating windows agree, they are already diagonal. If they are opposite, their heads mismatch. The policy advances one side; shifting an alternating window and appending the continuing alternating bit changes it exactly to the opposite phase. Hence after at most one more decision the two windows are identical. This occurs by time at most `4k`.

**Stage 4: drive the diagonal to zeros.** From a diagonal state, equal heads force joint matching. Prescribe every subsequently revealed bit on both sides to be `0`. Equality is preserved after each joint shift, and after `k` such shifts the state is exactly `(0^k,0^k)`. If the state is reached early, continue prescribing zeros; `(0^k,0^k)` then remains fixed under these prescribed equal-head transitions until exact time `5k`.

At most two fresh source bits are revealed per decision. Therefore the entire prescribed event uses at most `10k` fair bits and has conditional probability at least `2^{-10k}`. This proves (4.1).

For (4.2), write
\[
P^m(s,\cdot)=\delta_k\,\delta_z+(1-\delta_k)Q_s
\]
for probability measures `Q_s`. The common atom cancels when comparing two starting states, giving the span contraction. A finite Markov kernel whose fixed power contracts span by a strict factor has a unique stationary law. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — swap symmetry forces balanced stationary consumption

If the policy is swap equivariant, the unique stationary law `pi` is invariant under swapping X and Y. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\pi(\Delta C_X-\Delta C_Y)=0.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Swapping coordinates conjugates the transition kernel to itself. Hence the swapped image of any stationary law is stationary. Uniqueness from Theorem 4.1 forces equality with its swapped image. The one-step consumption difference is antisymmetric under the swap. QED.

## 5. Explicit finite-to-infinite balance error

Define
\[
b(s)=\mathbf E_s[\Delta C_X-\Delta C_Y].
\]
For a deterministic policy `b(s)` is actually one of `-1,0,1`, so
\[
\operatorname{span}(b)\le2.
\]
For a swap-equivariant policy, Corollary 4.2 gives `pi b=0`.

### Lemma 5.1 — bounded Poisson solution

There is a solution of
\[
\boxed{g-Pg=b}
\tag{5.1}
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
G_k:=\operatorname{osc}(g)\le\frac{10k}{\delta_k}=10k\,2^{10k}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

Because `pi b=0`, take
\[
g=\sum_{t\ge0}P^t b.
\]
The common-atom contraction yields, for `m=5k`,
\[
\operatorname{span}(P^{jm+r}b)
\le2(1-\delta_k)^j,
\qquad0\le r<m.
\]
The series therefore converges modulo constants, and telescoping gives `g-Pg=b`. Summing spans gives
\[
\operatorname{osc}(g)
\le 2m\sum_{j\ge0}(1-\delta_k)^j
=\frac{2m}{\delta_k}
=\frac{10k}{\delta_k}.
\]
QED.

Let
\[
D_t=C_X(t)-C_Y(t).
\]
Then
\[
M_t:=D_t+g(S_t)-g(S_0)
\]
is a martingale, because (5.1) cancels the conditional drift of `D_t`. Its increments obey
\[
|M_{t+1}-M_t|\le1+G_k.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For `n>=1`, stop at
\[
\tau_n=\inf\{t:\max(C_X(t),C_Y(t))=n\}.
\]
Every decision consumes at least one source symbol, while at `tau_n` both consumptions are at most `n`; hence
\[
\tau_n\le2n.
\tag{5.4}
\]
The martingale square-function estimate and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\mathbf E|M_{\tau_n}|
\le\sqrt{2n}\,(1+G_k).
\]
Because `D_tau=M_tau-(g(S_tau)-g(S_0))`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf E|D_{\tau_n}|
\le \sqrt{2n}\,(1+G_k)+G_k.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
At `tau_n`, one source has consumed exactly `n` symbols and the other `n-|D_tau|`, so
\[
A_{\tau_n}=n-\frac{|D_{\tau_n}|}{2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Every collected reward is a true matched pair among the first `n` letters of both source strings. Thus
\[
R_{\tau_n}\le LCS(X_1^n,Y_1^n).
\tag{5.7}
\]
Combining (3.2), (5.5), (5.6), and (5.7) proves the explicit finite-prefix theorem.

### Theorem 5.2 — proved finite-to-infinite transfer

For every swap-equivariant deterministic `k`-lookahead policy with a potential satisfying (3.1),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbf E LCS(X_1^n,Y_1^n)}n
\ge
\rho
-\frac{\rho}{2n}\Bigl(\sqrt{2n}(1+G_k)+G_k\Bigr)
-\frac{\operatorname{osc}(h)}n,
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
where
\[
G_k=10k\,2^{10k}
\]
is a valid explicit choice. Hence
\[
\boxed{\gamma_2\ge\rho.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This is the required analytical transfer. A finite Bellman calculation or simulation is **not** being promoted directly to the asymptotic constant.
