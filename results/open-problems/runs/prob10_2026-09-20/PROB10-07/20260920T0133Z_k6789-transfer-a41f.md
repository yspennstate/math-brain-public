## 4. Transfer lemma for arbitrary fixed width

Fix `m>=1`, label the left part `[m]={0,...,m-1}`, and process the `n` right vertices one at a time. A partial forest induces a set partition `pi` of `[m]`: two left terminals are in the same block exactly when they are already connected by the processed forest.

For the next right vertex choose a spoke subset `S subseteq [m]`. Since the existing graph is a forest, adding this star creates a cycle iff two points of `S` already lie in the same block of `pi`. Therefore

\[
S\text{ is legal}\iff |S\cap B|\le1\quad\text{for every block }B\in\pi.
\tag{4.1}
\]

When legal, all blocks hit by `S` merge and all other blocks remain unchanged. Let `M_m` be the exact transfer matrix indexed by `Pi_m`, counting all legal subsets `S` that produce each next partition. Empty `S` and the `m` singleton subsets preserve every state, so every diagonal entry equals

\[
q:=m+1.
\]

Every other transition strictly reduces the number of blocks. Hence, setting

\[
N_m:=M_m-qI,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{N_m^m=0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently for every nonnegative integer `k`,

\[
M_m^k=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{k}{j}q^{k-j}N_m^j.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is a combinatorial identity, not a fitted recurrence.

## 5. Marked operators and the only three edge-pair orbits

Let `v` be the row vector supported on the discrete partition and let `1` be the all-ones column vector. Let `E_0` denote the transfer restricted to legal spoke sets containing terminal `0`, `E_1` the analogous operator for terminal `1`, and `E_{01}` the operator requiring both `0,1`.

Write:

- `Z` = total number of forests of `K_{m,n}`;
- `E` = number containing one fixed edge;
- `R` = number containing two fixed edges sharing their right endpoint;
- `L` = number containing two fixed edges sharing their left endpoint but having different right endpoints;
- `D` = number containing two vertex-disjoint fixed edges.

Automorphisms of `K_{m,n}` show these are all possible distinct-edge pair types. Exact transfer gives

\[
Z=vM_m^n\mathbf1,
\qquad
E=vE_0M_m^{n-1}\mathbf1,
\qquad
R=vE_{01}M_m^{n-1}\mathbf1,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and, for `n>=2`,

\[
L=vE_0E_0M_m^{n-2}\mathbf1,
\qquad
D=vE_0E_1M_m^{n-2}\mathbf1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Define the exact nilpotent coefficient vectors

\[
a_j=vN_m^j\mathbf1,
\quad b_j=vE_0N_m^j\mathbf1,
\quad c_j=vE_{01}N_m^j\mathbf1,
\]
\[
\ell_j=vE_0E_0N_m^j\mathbf1,
\quad d_j=vE_0E_1N_m^j\mathbf1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Using (4.3), after dividing by `q^n`,

\[
\frac Z{q^n}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_j\frac{\binom nj}{q^j},
\tag{5.4}
\]

\[
\frac E{q^n}=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}b_j\frac{\binom{n-1}j}{q^j},
\qquad
\frac R{q^n}=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_j\frac{\binom{n-1}j}{q^j},
\tag{5.5}
\]

and

\[
\frac L{q^n}=\frac1{q^2}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\ell_j\frac{\binom{n-2}j}{q^j},
\qquad
\frac D{q^n}=\frac1{q^2}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_j\frac{\binom{n-2}j}{q^j}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus each normalized Rayleigh numerator is an exact rational polynomial in `n`:

\[
\delta_R=\frac{E^2-ZR}{q^{2n}},\qquad
\delta_L=\frac{E^2-ZL}{q^{2n}},\qquad
\delta_D=\frac{E^2-ZD}{q^{2n}}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The source probability inequality has the same sign because `Z>0` and

\[
\Pr(e)\Pr(f)-\Pr(e,f)=\frac{E^2-ZQ}{Z^2}.
\tag{5.8}
\]
