## 2. Exact source statement and before/after metrics

For a finite simple graph `G=(V,E)`, let `\mathcal F(G)` be the family of all acyclic edge subsets, with all component counts mixed and isolated vertices allowed. For distinct edges `e,f`, define

\[
Z=|\mathcal F(G)|,\qquad Z_e=|\{F:e\in F\}|,\qquad Z_{ef}=|\{F:e,f\in F\}|.
\]

Then

\[
\Pr(e)\Pr(f)-\Pr(e,f)=\frac{Z_eZ_f-ZZ_{ef}}{Z^2}.
\]

Thus the source target is exactly

\[
\Delta_G(e,f):=Z_eZ_f-ZZ_{ef}\ge0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is the uniform measure on **all forests**, not a spanning-tree law, random MST, random-cluster law conditioned on connectivity, or fixed-component ensemble.

Before this run, PROB10-07 had proved `K_{1,2,n}` for every `n>=1` and complete bipartite `K_{a,b}` for `min(a,b)<=30`. The complete-all-graphs order metric was and remains 9.

### Theorem `PROB10-07-TRIPARTITE-SUM5`

For every integer `n>=1`, every distinct edge pair is strictly negatively correlated under the uniform-all-forests measure on each of

\[
\boxed{K_{1,3,n},\quad K_{2,2,n},\quad K_{1,4,n},\quad K_{2,3,n}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Equivalently, for each of these four graph families and every distinct `e,f`,

\[
\boxed{\Delta_G(e,f)>0.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Combining (2.2) with the immediately preceding PROB10-07 theorem for `K_{1,2,n}` and permuting the three parts gives the structural corollary:

> Every complete tripartite graph `K_{a,b,c}` whose two smallest part sizes have sum at most five satisfies strict pairwise negative edge correlation for uniform forests.

This is a restricted structural-family theorem only. The arbitrary finite-graph conjecture remains OPEN/PARTIAL.

## 3. Three materially different routes compared before coding

### A. Deletion-contraction quadratic — exact but already blocked

For a third edge `g`, write `I_G=A+y_gB`, with `A=I_{G\setminus g}` and `B=I_{G/g}` in matroid notation. Then for tested `e,f\ne g`,

\[
\Delta_G^{e,f}(y_g)=\Delta_A^{e,f}+C_{A,B}^{e,f}y_g+\Delta_B^{e,f}y_g^2,
\]

where

\[
C_{A,B}^{e,f}=A_eB_f+B_eA_f-AB_{ef}-BA_{ef}.
\]

P14/HARD10-01 already isolated that this mixed coefficient has no universal sign. Endpoint Rayleigh positivity alone therefore does not close the induction. Repackaging that blocker would not be a new route, so this identity was used only as a falsification check.

### B. False-twin closure — scalar invariant is insufficient

Repeated vertices in `K_{a,b,n}` are false twins, suggesting an induction by cloning. But after one clone, future acyclicity depends on the complete connectivity partition induced on the common neighborhood. A scalar Rayleigh gap is not a closed state variable. No general false-twin preservation theorem is assumed.

### C. Fixed-core Bell-partition transfer — succeeds

Freeze the first two parts, of sizes `a,b`, and process the `n` vertices in the third part sequentially. Put `m=a+b`. A processed forest induces a set partition `pi` of the `m` fixed terminals. For a new repeated vertex `r`, choose a spoke set `S` into the terminals.

**Acyclicity criterion.** The extension is acyclic iff `S` meets every block of `pi` in at most one terminal. If two members of `S` are already in the same block, the existing path between them together with their two spokes through `r` forms a cycle. Conversely, if `S` meets distinct blocks, the star at `r` joins distinct tree components and cannot create a cycle. When legal, all blocks met by `S` merge; for `|S|<=1`, the partition is unchanged.

This criterion is exact and yields a finite transfer on the Bell-partition state space.

## 4. Transfer lemma and proof

Let `M_m` be the transfer matrix indexed by set partitions of the `m` fixed terminals. For every partition state, the empty spoke set and the `m` singleton spoke sets preserve the state, so every diagonal entry is exactly `m+1`. Every other legal spoke set merges at least two current blocks. Hence

\[
M_m=(m+1)I+N_m,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where every nonzero transition of `N_m` strictly decreases the number of blocks. A partition has at most `m` blocks. Any product of `m` strict block-count decreases is therefore zero, so

\[
N_m^m=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The binomial theorem is valid because `I` commutes with `N_m`, and (4.2) truncates it exactly:

\[
M_m^x=(m+1)^x\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{x}{j}\left(\frac{N_m}{m+1}\right)^j,
\qquad x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For `m=4`, the state count is `B_4=15` and the checker verifies `N_4^4=0`, `N_4^3\ne0`. For `m=5`, the state count is `B_5=52` and it verifies `N_5^5=0`, `N_5^4\ne0`.

## 5. Marked-edge reduction and exhaustiveness

Fix an unordered edge-pair orbit under permutations within the two fixed parts and within the repeated part. Expose the minimum number `k\in\{0,1,2\}` of repeated vertices needed to contain the marked pair. Enumerate exactly all acyclic subsets of the fixed `K_{a,b}` core together with these `k` distinguished stars. This gives four row vectors

\[
v_0,v_e,v_f,v_{ef}
\]

indexed by terminal partitions, according as no marked edge, only `e`, only `f`, or both are required.

If `x=n-k` repeated vertices remain, then

\[
Z=v_0M_m^x\mathbf1,\quad
Z_e=v_eM_m^x\mathbf1,\quad
Z_f=v_fM_m^x\mathbf1,\quad
Z_{ef}=v_{ef}M_m^x\mathbf1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By (4.3), each count is `(m+1)^x` times an exact rational polynomial in `x` of degree at most `m-1`. Thus

\[
\Delta_G(e,f)=(m+1)^{2x}R_{\mathcal O}(x),
\tag{5.2}
\]

with `R_O` an exact rational polynomial of degree at most `2m-2`.

The checker generates the automorphism group from all permutations of each fixed part, additionally swaps the fixed parts when `a=b`, and permutes the distinguished repeated vertices. It canonicalizes every unordered pair of present edges, so the resulting orbit list is exhaustive rather than hand-selected. The orbit counts are
