## 6. Positive Hermite expansion and closed endpoint moments

Let `He_n` denote the probabilists' Hermite polynomials, so

\[
\mathbb E[He_m(Z)He_n(Z)]=n!\,1_{m=n}.
\]

Define

\[
d_n=\mathbb E[g_a(Z)He_n(Z)],
\qquad w_n=\frac{d_n^2}{n!}\ge0,
\qquad m=d_0=\mathbb E g_a(Z).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Mehler's identity for the correlated standard pair in (5.5), together with the `L^2(phi)` Hermite expansion, gives

\[
\boxed{p_4=m^2+\sum_{n\ge1}q^n w_n}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\mathbb E g_a(Z)^2=m^2+\sum_{n\ge1}w_n.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Since all `w_n` are nonnegative and `0<q<1`, for every integer `M>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
 p_4\le (1-q^{M+1})m^2+q^{M+1}\mathbb Eg_a(Z)^2
 +\sum_{n=1}^M(q^n-q^{M+1})w_n.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This tail bound has no cancellation and is therefore suited to outward interval arithmetic.

Two quantities in (6.4) have exact cone formulas. Introduce independent standard normals `Z,E,E_1,E_2`.

First,

\[
m=\Pr\{Z>0,|E|<aZ\}
=\boxed{\frac{\arctan a}{\pi}},
\tag{6.5}
\]

because the event is a planar wedge of angular width `2 arctan a`.

Second,

\[
\mathbb E g_a(Z)^2
=\Pr\{Z>0,|E_1|<aZ,|E_2|<aZ\}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The direction of `(E_1,E_2,Z)` is uniform on the sphere. The solid angle of this square cone is

\[
\Omega_a=\int_{-a}^a\int_{-a}^a
\frac{dx\,dy}{(1+x^2+y^2)^{3/2}}.
\]

Integrating first in `x` gives

\[
\Omega_a=4a\int_0^a\frac{dy}{(1+y^2)\sqrt{1+a^2+y^2}}.
\]

With `y=tan t` and then `z=sin t`, this becomes

\[
\Omega_a=4\arcsin\frac{a^2}{1+a^2}
=4\arctan\frac{a^2}{\sqrt{1+2a^2}}.
\]

Dividing by `4pi`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E g_a(Z)^2
=\frac1\pi\arctan\frac{a^2}{\sqrt{1+2a^2}}.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Finally the finite Hermite coefficients need no numerical quadrature. The identity

\[
(\phi He_{n-1})'=-\phi He_n
\]

and `g_a(0)=0` give, for `n>=1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n
&=\int_0^\infty (2\Phi(ax)-1)He_n(x)\phi(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_0^\infty 2a\phi(ax)\phi(x)He_{n-1}(x)\,dx\\
&=\boxed{\frac{a}{\pi}\int_0^\infty
He_{n-1}(x)e^{-(1+a^2)x^2/2}\,dx}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.8}
\]

After expanding `He_{n-1}`, every integral is an explicit half-Gaussian moment. Since `a^2=A` is rational, the first finitely many `w_n` require only rational arithmetic, `pi`, and square roots, all enclosed outward in the checker.

\[
\varepsilon^2\ge(\lambda_1-\mu)(\mu-\beta).
