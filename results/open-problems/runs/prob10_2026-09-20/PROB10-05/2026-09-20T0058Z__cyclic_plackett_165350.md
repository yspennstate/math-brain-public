## 4. New theorem: a quantitative cyclic orthant decrement

For `c in [b,a]`, define

\[
\Sigma(c)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&a&c&a\\
a&1&a&c\\
c&a&1&a\\
a&c&a&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P(c)=\Pr_{\Sigma(c)}(X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3<0).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The eigenvalues of `Sigma(c)` are

\[
1+2a+c,\quad 1-c,\quad1-2a+c,\quad1-c.
\]

On `[b,a]` all are positive; at the only nontrivial lower endpoint,

\[
1-2a+b=\frac{903083}{170952323}>0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence the path stays strictly positive definite.

### Lemma 4.1 — special Plackett derivative

For `c in [b,a]`,

\[
P'(c)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1-c^2}}
\left[\frac14+\frac{1}{2\pi}\arcsin q(c)\right],
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
q(c)=\frac{c(1+c)-2a^2}{1+c-2a^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

**Proof.** Vary first only the covariance entry `rho_{02}` (and symmetrically `rho_{20}`), keeping the other matrix entries fixed. For a Gaussian density, differentiating with respect to this off-diagonal covariance parameter gives the mixed spatial derivative `partial_{x_0} partial_{x_2}` of the density. Integrating over the negative orthant and integrating by parts in `x_0,x_2` leaves the bivariate marginal density of `(X_0,X_2)` at `(0,0)` multiplied by the conditional negative-orthant probability of `(X_1,X_3)` given `(X_0,X_2)=(0,0)`. Thus

\[
\frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_{02}}
=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-c^2}}
\Pr(X_1,X_3<0\mid X_0=X_2=0).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The conditional variance of each remaining coordinate is

\[
1-\frac{2a^2}{1+c},
\]

and their conditional covariance is

\[
c-\frac{2a^2}{1+c}.
\]

Therefore their conditional correlation is exactly (4.4), and the centered bivariate Gaussian sign formula gives

\[
\Pr(X_1,X_3<0\mid X_0=X_2=0)
=\frac14+\frac{\arcsin q(c)}{2\pi}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Along `Sigma(c)` both opposite correlations `rho_{02}` and `rho_{13}` vary with unit speed. By cyclic symmetry the two partial derivatives are equal, so adding them yields (4.3). `QED`
