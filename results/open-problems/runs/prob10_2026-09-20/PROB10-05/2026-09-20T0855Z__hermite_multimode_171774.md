## 5. Exact Hermite matrix identity for the soft-edge operator

Let `(U,V)` be standard bivariate Gaussian with `Corr(U,V)=r`. Introduce an independent standard Gaussian `E` and define

\[
W=\sqrt{s}\,\frac{U+V}{\sqrt{2(1+r)}}+\sqrt{1-s}\,E.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then `W` is standard and

\[
\operatorname{Corr}(U,W)=\operatorname{Corr}(V,W)=\sqrt c,
\qquad
c=s\frac{1+r}{2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Conditioning on `(U,V)` in (5.1) gives exactly the soft-edge factor in (3.7), so for probabilists' Hermite polynomials `He_n`,

\[
\langle He_i,KHe_j\rangle
=\mathbb E[He_i(U)He_j(V)1_{\{W<0\}}].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Condition on `W=w`. The conditional means of `U,V` are both `sqrt(c)w`, their conditional variances are `1-c`, and their conditional covariance is `r-c`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\left[e^{tU-t^2/2+zV-z^2/2}\mid W=w\right]\\
&\quad=\exp\left(\sqrt c(t+z)w-\frac c2(t+z)^2+rtz\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Expanding the first factor with the Hermite generating function and the second as `exp(rtz)` gives, with `n=i+j-2k`,

\[
\boxed{
\langle He_i,KHe_j\rangle
=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(i,j)}
\frac{i!j!}{k!(i-k)!(j-k)!}
r^k c^{n/2}
\mathbb E[He_n(W)1_{\{W<0\}}].}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The half-Gaussian Hermite moments are elementary. Since

\[
\frac d{dx}\bigl(\phi(x)He_{n-1}(x)\bigr)=-\phi(x)He_n(x),
\]

for `n>=1`,

\[
\mathbb E[He_n(W)1_{\{W<0\}}]
=-\phi(0)He_{n-1}(0).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E[He_0(W)1_{\{W<0\}}]=\frac12,
\quad
\mathbb E[He_{2m}(W)1_{\{W<0\}}]=0\ (m\ge1),
\tag{5.7}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E[He_{2m+1}(W)1_{\{W<0\}}]
=-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}(-1)^m\frac{(2m)!}{2^m m!}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Let `h_n=He_n/sqrt(n!)`. Equations (5.5)--(5.8) imply the exact parity identity

\[
\boxed{
\langle h_i,Kh_j\rangle=0\quad(i\ne j,\ i+j\ \text{even}),
\qquad
\langle h_i,Kh_i\rangle=\frac{r^i}{2}.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

All opposite-parity entries are a rational multiple of the single scalar

\[
\tau=\sqrt{\frac{c}{2\pi}}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

This is the structural fact exploited below.
