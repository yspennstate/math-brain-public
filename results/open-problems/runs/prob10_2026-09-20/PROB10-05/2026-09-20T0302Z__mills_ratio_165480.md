### 4.2 Exact resolvent identity

Let

\[

\]

and let the Gaussian Mills ratio be

\[
m(x)=\frac{1-\Phi(x)}{\phi(x)}

\tag{4.7}

Using

\[
\frac1{1+qY^2}=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}e^{-qtY^2}\,dt

and Tonelli,

\[
\mathbb E\frac1{1+qY^2}
=
\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-t}}{\sqrt{1+2qt}}\,dt.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Substitute `u=sqrt(1+2qt)` and then `v=xu`; since `x=q^{-1/2}`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\frac1{1+qY^2}
&=x^2e^{x^2/2}\int_1^\infty e^{-x^2u^2/2}\,du\\
&=xe^{x^2/2}\int_x^\infty e^{-v^2/2}\,dv\\
&=x m(x).
\end{aligned}\tag{4.9}
\]

This identity is exact.

### 4.3 A self-contained Mills-ratio upper bound

Define

\[
u(x)=\frac4{3x+\sqrt{x^2+8}},\qquad x>0.\tag{4.10}
\]

The Mills ratio satisfies the Riccati equation

\[
m'(x)=xm(x)-1.\tag{4.11}
\]

A direct differentiation gives

\[
u'(x)-(xu(x)-1)
=
\frac{x^3-x^2\sqrt{x^2+8}+6x-2\sqrt{x^2+8}}
{3x^3+5x^2\sqrt{x^2+8}+24x+4\sqrt{x^2+8}}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

The denominator is positive. The numerator is negative because

\[
x(x^2+6)<(x^2+2)\sqrt{x^2+8},\tag{4.13}
\]

and both sides are positive, while squaring yields the exact identity

\[
(x^2+2)^2(x^2+8)-x^2(x^2+6)^2=32>0.\tag{4.14}
\]

Hence

\[
u'-(xu-1)<0.\tag{4.15}
\]

Set `d=u-m`. Then

\[
(d e^{-x^2/2})'
=e^{-x^2/2}[u'-(xu-1)]<0.\tag{4.16}
\]

Moreover `m(x)<=1/x` follows immediately from

\[
m(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{-xt-t^2/2}\,dt,
\]

and `u(x)=O(1/x)`, so `d(x)e^{-x^2/2}->0` as `x->infinity`. Integrating (4.16) from `x` to infinity gives

\[
0-d(x)e^{-x^2/2}<0,
\]

hence `d(x)>0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{m(x)<\frac4{3x+\sqrt{x^2+8}}}\qquad(x>0).\tag{4.17}
\]

\[
\boxed{
