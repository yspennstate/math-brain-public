## 2. New theorem

**Theorem.** For iid Bernoulli **bond** percolation on `G_{3,4}`,

\[
 p_c(G_{3,4})<\frac{13}{251}
 < \frac1{5+\sqrt{13}+\sqrt{11+10\sqrt{13}}}
 \le p_{2\to2}(G_{3,4})\le p_u(G_{3,4}).
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 p_u(G_{3,4})-p_c(G_{3,4})
 >
 \frac1{5+\sqrt{13}+\sqrt{11+10\sqrt{13}}}
 -\frac{13}{251}
 }
\]

and the right-hand side equals

\[
0.0128682522819065558971977389988681286864\ldots>0.
\]

In particular, this standard Cayley graph has a nonempty nonuniqueness phase.

### Proof, part A: a free-subgroup percolation comparison

The standard surface presentation is a one-relator presentation whose cyclically reduced relator involves every displayed generator. By the classical Magnus Freiheitssatz, the subgroup generated by any proper subset omitting one generator is free on the remaining generators. Therefore

\[
\langle a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,a_3\rangle\cong F_5
\quad\text{inside }\Gamma_3,
\]

and

\[
\langle c_1,d_1,c_2,d_2,c_3,d_3,c_4\rangle\cong F_7
\quad\text{inside }\Gamma_4.
\]

Using only these subgroup-generator edges, the product Cayley graph contains a copy of

\[
T_{10}\square T_{14},
\]

where `T_d` denotes the `d`-regular tree. Hence monotonicity under adding edges gives

\[
p_c(G_{3,4})\le p_c(T_{10}\square T_{14}).
\]

Orient the `T_{14}` factor away from a root. Each non-root vertex then has `q=13` forward children. Use the `T_{10}` factor as the vertical fiber. The recovered June cluster-branching proposition says the following: for a transitive vertical graph `H`, put

\[
\varphi_H(p)=\mathbb E_p\bigl[1-(1-p)^{|C_H(o)|}\bigr].
\]

If `q varphi_H(p)>1`, the oriented child-fiber exploration dominates a supercritical Galton--Watson process and `T_{q+1} square H` percolates.

For `H=T_{10}` we do not need its full cluster law. Inspect only the root and its ten vertical neighbors. For a fixed forward child fiber, activation occurs if either

1. the horizontal child edge at the root is open, or
2. for at least one of the ten vertical neighbors `v`, both the edge `o-v` and the horizontal child edge at `v` are open.

These eleven tests involve disjoint edge sets. Therefore

\[
\varphi_{T_{10}}(p)
\ge L(p):=1-(1-p)(1-p^2)^{10}.
\]

At

\[
p_0=\frac{13}{251},
\]

the executed exact checker gives

\[
13L(p_0)-1
=
\frac{1697895400814478356431156797270480026164660356}
{247256746938901389708907980902953941770470013130251}>0.
\]

Since `L` is a polynomial, this strict inequality also holds at some `p'<p_0`. The cluster-branching comparison therefore gives

\[
p_c(T_{10}\square T_{14})<p_0=\frac{13}{251},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{p_c(G_{3,4})<13/251.}
\]

This proof uses only independent bond variables and an embedded Cayley subgraph; no numerical percolation simulation enters the claim.

### Proof, part B: adjacency norm of the full surface-product Cayley graph

Write the genus-three factor adjacency as

\[
A_3=A_{F_5}+B_3,
\]

where `A_{F_5}` keeps the five free generators and inverses and `B_3` keeps the omitted generator `b_3^{\pm1}`. Across all cosets of `F_5`, `A_{F_5}` is a disjoint union of copies of `T_{10}`, so

\[
\|A_{F_5}\|=2\sqrt{10-1}=6.
\]

The omitted-generator graph has degree two, hence `||B_3||<=2`. Therefore

\[
\|A_3\|\le8.
\]

Likewise, in genus four the seven retained generators give disjoint copies of `T_{14}`, and the omitted generator contributes norm at most two, so

\[
\|A_4\|\le2\sqrt{13}+2.
\]

Cartesian-product adjacency is

\[
A=A_3\otimes I+I\otimes A_4,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\|A\|\le R:=10+2\sqrt{13}.}
\]

### Proof, part C: the nonbacktracking two-point operator bound

Let `N_n(x,y)` be the number of length-`n` nonbacktracking walks on a `D`-regular graph, viewed as a kernel/operator. The standard nonbacktracking recurrence gives

\[
N_n=P_n(A),
\]

and the recovered June proof yields

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|N_n\|_{2\to2}^{1/n}
\le \beta(D,\|A\|),
\]

where

\[
\beta(D,r)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{D-1},&r\le2\sqrt{D-1},\\[2mm]
\dfrac{r+\sqrt{r^2-4(D-1)}}2,&r>2\sqrt{D-1}.
\end{cases}
\]

The function is increasing in `r`, so the norm bound above gives

\[
\limsup_n\|N_n\|^{1/n}
\le\beta(28,R).
\]

Here `R^2>108`, and direct simplification gives

\[
\beta(28,R)
=5+\sqrt{13}+\sqrt{11+10\sqrt{13}}
=:\beta_*.
\]

For every `p<1/beta_*`, the operator series `sum_{n>=0} p^n N_n` converges in `ell^2 -> ell^2` norm. The two-point connectivity kernel satisfies entrywise

\[
\tau_p(x,y)
\le \sum_{n\ge0}p^n C_n(x,y)
\le \sum_{n\ge0}p^n N_n(x,y),
\]

because every open connection has a self-avoiding witness path and every self-avoiding path is nonbacktracking. For any finitely supported `f`, entrywise positivity then gives the `ell^2` norm domination for the two-point operator. Hence `T_p` is bounded for every `p<1/beta_*`, i.e.

\[
\boxed{p_{2\to2}(G_{3,4})\ge1/\beta_*.}
\]

Finally, the standard transitive-percolation inequality gives

\[
p_{2\to2}\le p_u.
\]

To compare with the percolation upper bound, the exact checker verifies `beta_*<50/3`. For completeness, the radical inequality is sign-safe and reduces as follows:

\[
5+\sqrt{13}+\sqrt{11+10\sqrt{13}}<\frac{50}{3}
\]

is equivalent after isolating the last square root and squaring to

\[
300\sqrt{13}<1243,
\]

which follows from

\[
13\cdot300^2=1{,}170{,}000
<1{,}545{,}049=1243^2.
\]

Thus

\[
\frac{13}{251}\beta_*
<\frac{13}{251}\frac{50}{3}
=\frac{650}{753}<1,
\]

so `13/251<1/beta_*`. Combining the three inequalities proves the theorem. `square`

## 3. Structural extension proved in this run

The explicit example is an instance of the following reusable criterion.

**Proposition (subgraph + nonbacktracking operator criterion).** Let `X` be an infinite connected `D`-regular Cayley graph. Suppose there are numbers `p_0 in (0,1)` and `R` and an infinite subgraph `H subset X` such that

1. `p_c(H)<p_0`;
2. `||A_X||_{2->2}<=R`;
3. `p_0 beta(D,R)<1`.

Then

\[
p_c(X)<p_0<\frac1{\beta(D,R)}\le p_{2\to2}(X)\le p_u(X),
\]

and therefore

\[
p_u(X)-p_c(X)>\frac1{\beta(D,R)}-p_0>0.
\]

**Proof.** Adding the edges of `X` to bond percolation on `H` can only increase connectivity, so `p_c(X)<=p_c(H)<p_0`. The same nonbacktracking series argument from Part C, now with `R` in place of the exact norm, yields `p_{2->2}(X)>=1/beta(D,R)`. Hypothesis 3 is exactly `p_0<1/beta(D,R)`, and the general inequality `p_{2->2}<=p_u` finishes the chain. `square`

This proposition is a **proved structural criterion / synthesis**, not a claim that it is new in the literature. Its complete mathematical scope is exactly the three displayed hypotheses; it does not assume hyperbolicity, planarity, or amenability of a factor.

The surface-group proof shows one systematic way to instantiate it: use a Magnus free subgroup to obtain a product-of-trees subgraph for hypothesis 1, while keeping all surface-group edges in the operator estimate for hypotheses 2--3. This is the requested extension beyond a single finite computation.

## 4. Negation tests and comparison of three methods

### Method #2 — attack the negation / reject fake novelty

I first tested obvious product families against current known classes. A product of regular trees is not a fresh target: products with trees are already among the established `L^2`-boundedness/nonuniqueness classes. The chosen group `Gamma_3 x Gamma_4` is instead a direct product of two infinite surface groups. It contains `Z^2`, so it is not Gromov hyperbolic; its standard Cartesian Cayley graph has 4-cycles, so high-girth theorems do not apply; neither factor is amenable, so tree-times-amenable product results are not the statement proved here.

A 2025 current reference that materially changes the landscape is Choi--Seo, arXiv:2508.08932, which proves the conjecture for **every Cayley graph of every acylindrically hyperbolic group**. A nontrivial direct product of two infinite groups is not acylindrically hyperbolic, so that theorem does not absorb this example. A current search snippet from that work explicitly points to direct products of free groups as a still-open-type obstruction; this was one reason to test a direct product of surface groups rather than another negatively curved single factor.

### Method #21 — subgroup comparison

The successful percolation-side move is the `F_5 x F_7` subgroup comparison. It turns the surface relators from an obstacle into an advantage: the Freiheitssatz gives a large tree-product subgraph while the full ambient graph remains outside the hyperbolic class. This yields the strict exact upper bound `p_c<13/251`.

### Method #18 — nonbacktracking/operator language

The successful two-point-side move is the full-graph nonbacktracking recurrence. It yields

\[
p_u\ge p_{2->2}\ge1/\beta_*=0.06466108096716552\ldots.
\]

As a diagnostic that nonbacktracking control is quantitatively meaningful, keep the coarser comparison point `p*=3/50`. The same norm upper bound gives the ordinary all-walk/resolvent lower bound only

\[
1/R=0.05810203018900044\ldots<3/50,
\]

so that coarse all-walk estimate cannot bridge `p*=3/50`. In contrast

\[
1/\beta_*=0.06466108096716552\ldots>3/50.
\]

The exact checker certifies the all-walk failure at that point by

\[
(3/50)(10+2\sqrt{13})>1
\iff 3\sqrt{13}>10
\iff 117>100.
\]

After the initial idea was written, the cluster calculation itself sharpened `3/50` down to `13/251`; at that sharper point even the coarse all-walk bound is enough for mere positivity, while the nonbacktracking bound nearly doubles the quantitative certified gap. Thus the NB method is not cosmetic: it gives the stronger tracked lower bound.
