Their paper is important neighboring progress but does not, in the stated theorem checked here, supply the fixed-standard-generator inequalities proved below.

3. **Terlov--Timar, arXiv:2502.02560.** This is a weighted/nonunimodular theory and does not state the fixed standard-Cayley theorem below.

4. **Choi--Seo, arXiv:2508.08932.** They prove nonuniqueness for every Cayley graph of every acylindrically hyperbolic group. The groups below have infinite centre and are not acylindrically hyperbolic. One quick structural reason is that in a non-elementary acylindrical action, an infinite-order central element commuting with a loxodromic element would itself lie in the virtually cyclic loxodromic elementary subgroup; since it is central, the whole group would centralize it, forcing the group to be virtually cyclic, a contradiction.

The present family uses a nonzero central-extension class `e!=0`; its frozen standard Cayley graph is not the Cartesian product `Cay(Gamma_g) square Z`. Peres's theorem therefore does not directly supply the result below.

The groups below satisfy that algebraic hypothesis, but the result below concerns a specific, frozen, natural standard generating set and gives an explicit operator certificate. Thus the theorem below is not a restatement of their existential generating-set result.

## 5. Exact subgroup lemma: `T_{4g-2} square Z` sits inside the standard graph

**Lemma 5.1.** For every `g>=2` and every `e!=0`, the subgroup

\[
H_g=\langle a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_{g-1},b_{g-1},a_g,z\rangle
\le E_{g,e}
\]

is isomorphic to

\[
F_{2g-1}\times\mathbb Z.
\]

**Proof.** In the quotient surface group `Gamma_g`, Magnus Freiheitssatz applied to the one-relator presentation says that the subgroup generated by the displayed `2g-1` surface generators is freely generated by them. Since `z` is central, there is a natural surjective homomorphism

\[
F_{2g-1}\times\mathbb Z\to H_g
\]

sending the free generators to the chosen lifts and the integer generator to `z`.

Suppose a word `w z^n` maps to the identity. Projecting to `Gamma_g` gives `w=1` inside the free subgroup. Because those quotient generators freely generate that subgroup, `w` freely reduces to the empty word. The same cancellations occur literally among their chosen lifts, so the lifted word itself is the identity. Hence `z^n=1`. The kernel in (4.2) is infinite cyclic, so `z` has infinite order and `n=0`. The map is injective. ∎

Using only the corresponding generator edges, Lemma 5.1 gives the graph inclusion

\[
\boxed{
T_{4g-2}\square\mathbb Z\subseteq C_{g,e}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
p_c(C_{g,e})\le p_c(T_{4g-2}\square\mathbb Z)
\le p_c(T_{4g-2})
=\frac1{4g-3}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The middle inequality simply drops the `Z` edges; it will be sharpened for `g=2`.

## 6. Exact genus-two critical witness from the full `Z` fibre

For `g=2`, (5.1) gives `T_6 square Z` as a subgraph. Orient the tree away from a root, so each non-root tree vertex has five forward children.

At a reached tree vertex, explore the entire open bond cluster of the seed in its `Z` fibre. Let `N` be the size of this one-dimensional open cluster. For each of the five forward child fibres, declare the child activated if at least one horizontal edge from the parent fibre cluster to that child fibre is open. Conditional on `N`, these five activations are independent with probability

\[
1-(1-p)^N.
\]

Different individuals in the oriented exploration use disjoint child fibres and fresh edges, so this embeds a Galton--Watson process with mean offspring

\[
5\,\phi_{\mathbb Z}(p),
\qquad
\phi_{\mathbb Z}(p)=\mathbb E[1-(1-p)^N].
\tag{6.1}
\]

For one-dimensional bond percolation, write `N=1+L+R`, where `L,R` are independent geometric random variables with

\[
\mathbb P(L=k)=(1-p)p^k,\qquad k\ge0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[(1-p)^N]
&=(1-p)\left((1-p)\sum_{k\ge0}[p(1-p)]^k\right)^2\\
&=\frac{(1-p)^3}{(1-p+p^2)^2},
\end{aligned}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\phi_{\mathbb Z}(p)
=1-\frac{(1-p)^3}{(1-p+p^2)^2}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

At

\[
p_0=\frac{31}{200},
\]

the executed exact checker gives

\[
5\phi_{\mathbb Z}(p_0)-1
=\frac{6499484}{1208327121}>0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the embedded Galton--Watson process is supercritical. Since the left side of (6.3) is continuous and has strict positive slack, it remains positive for some `p<p_0`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 p_c(C_{2,e})<\frac{31}{200}=0.155
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

for every `e!=0`.

This is a theorem-sized probabilistic argument; the finite checker validates only the rational arithmetic in (6.3).

## 7. Central Fourier decomposition and uniform magnetic domination

We next control the adjacency norm of the full standard graph.

Choose any set-theoretic section of the quotient map (4.2), so an element of `E_{g,e}` can be represented by a quotient vertex together with an integer central coordinate. Right multiplication by a lifted surface generator changes the quotient vertex by the corresponding surface generator and changes the central coordinate by an integer cocycle. Fourier transform in the central `Z` coordinate decomposes the horizontal adjacency operator into a direct integral

\[
A_{\mathrm{hor}}
\cong
\int_{0}^{2\pi}^{\oplus} H_\theta\,\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},
\tag{7.1}
\]

where `H_theta` is the ordinary surface adjacency with unit-modulus phases attached to its oriented edges. The inverse-generator pairing makes `H_theta` self-adjoint.

For every finitely supported `f` on the quotient Cayley graph,

\[
|H_\theta f|(x)
\le
A_{\Gamma_g}|f|(x)
\tag{7.2}
\]

pointwise, because each magnetic coefficient has modulus one. Hence

\[
\|H_\theta f\|_2
\le
\|A_{\Gamma_g}|f|\|_2
\le
\|A_{\Gamma_g}\|_{2\to2}\|f\|_2,
\]

and therefore

\[
\|H_\theta\|_{2\to2}
\le
\|A_{\Gamma_g}\|_{2\to2}
\quad\text{for every }\theta.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The two central generator edges `z,z^{-1}` become the scalar operator `2 cos(theta) I`. Thus every fibre of the full adjacency satisfies

\[
\|H_\theta+2\cos\theta\,I\|
\le
\|A_{\Gamma_g}\|+2,
\]

and taking the essential supremum in the direct integral gives the uniform, Euler-class-independent estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|A_{C_{g,e}}\|_{2\to2}
\le
\|A_{\Gamma_g}\|_{2\to2}+2.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This proves the negation test from the earlier idea artifact: the central twist cannot enlarge the horizontal norm beyond the untwisted surface norm.

## 8. Nonbacktracking transfer to `p_{2->2}`

For completeness, the operator step is restated self-containedly.

Let `G` be a simple `D`-regular graph with adjacency operator `A`, and put `Q=D-1`. Let `N_n(x,y)` be the number of length-`n` nonbacktracking walks from `x` to `y`. Then

\[
N_0=I,\qquad N_1=A,\qquad N_2=A^2-DI,
\]

and for `n>=3`,

\[
N_n=A N_{n-1}-Q N_{n-2}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Hence `N_n=P_n(A)` for explicit real polynomials. If `R>=||A||`, the exponential growth of these polynomials on `[-R,R]` is at most

\[
\beta_D(R)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt Q,&R\le2\sqrt Q,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{R+\sqrt{R^2-4Q}}2,&R>2\sqrt Q.
\end{cases}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|N_n\|_{2\to2}^{1/n}
\le\beta_D(R).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Every open connection contains an open self-avoiding, hence nonbacktracking, path. The union bound gives entrywise

\[
T_p(x,y):=\mathbb P_p(x\leftrightarrow y)
\le
\sum_{n\ge0}p^n N_n(x,y).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Since all kernels are nonnegative, entrywise domination yields operator-norm domination after replacing an input vector by its absolute value. Thus the series in (8.4) converges in `l^2` operator norm whenever `p beta_D(R)<1`, and

\[
\boxed{
 p_{2\to2}(G)\ge\beta_D(R)^{-1}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

For an infinite transitive Cayley graph,

\[
p_{2\to2}(G)\le p_u(G).
\tag{8.6}
\]

Indeed, for `p>p_u`, uniqueness of the infinite cluster and FKG give

\[
T_p(x,y)
\ge
\mathbb P(x\leftrightarrow\infty,\ y\leftrightarrow\infty)
\ge\theta(p)^2>0
\]

uniformly in `x,y`. A kernel bounded below by a positive constant on an infinite vertex set is not a bounded operator on `l^2`; for example apply it to normalized indicators of finite sets. This proves (8.6).

1. a large explicit free subgroup whose chosen generators sit literally inside the frozen generating set, giving a quantitative `p_c` witness; and
2. a central Fourier decomposition reducing the full adjacency norm to a controlled quotient magnetic operator.

A general solution would need a replacement for **both** pieces, or a completely different bridge from nonamenability to an `l^2` two-point gap. Merely restating `p_c<p_{2\to2}` as the missing lemma would not be progress.

A concrete next internal direction, still within this conjecture, is to ask whether the magnetic-domination argument survives for broader amenable normal fibres (for example finitely generated abelian kernels of rank `k`) together with a quotient that has a certified adjacency norm and a free-lift subgroup. The exact obstruction is then no longer the central character scalar `2 cos(theta)`: a noncentral semidirect action couples quotient and fibre frequencies, so one needs a matrix-valued or crossed-product analogue of (7.4). That is the first genuinely new missing mechanism, not another numerical recheck of the current family.
