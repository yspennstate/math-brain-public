Three genuinely different routes were compared there.

1. **Abelian-dual magnetic domination — SUCCESS.** Fourier transform on the compact dual of an arbitrary finitely generated abelian central kernel produces magnetic quotient operators dominated pointwise by the untwisted quotient adjacency. This is the route proved below.
2. **Finite-index subgroup transfer — OBSTRUCTION.** If the Euler lattice is nonprimitive, the horizontal labels generate a finite-index (or, with free missing directions, infinite-index) subgroup. A gap on that subgroup does not automatically give the requested gap for the full-group graph after additional labels are inserted: the critical threshold decreases under added edges, while the uniqueness threshold does not have a monotonicity strong enough to preserve the required strict comparison. The index defect therefore cannot be waved away.
3. **Virtual central motion through surface relators — QUANTITATIVELY INFERIOR.** One can simulate central displacements by demanding an entire length-`4g_i` surface relator path to be open, but this costs factors `p^(4g_i)` and is dominated by the free-subgroup critical witness in the present regime.

The negation/scope test is exact: the horizontal lifts generate the whole extension iff the subgroup of `A` generated by the surface relator charges is `A` itself. For `A=Z^m`, Smith normal form gives the equivalent maximal-minor gcd criterion above.

## 4. The higher-rank central-extension theorem

Let

`Q = Gamma_{g_1} * ... * Gamma_{g_r}`, `r>=1`, `g_i>=2`,

where `Gamma_g` is the closed orientable genus-`g` surface group with its standard inverse-closed generating set of cardinality `4g`.

Let

`1 -> A -> Gamma -> Q -> 1`                                      (4.1)

be a central extension, where `A` is finitely generated abelian. Choose inverse-compatible lifts of all standard quotient generators. For the `i`th surface factor, write `e_i in A` for the value in the central kernel of its standard surface relator under these lifts. Assume

`<e_1,...,e_r> = A`.                                             (4.2)

Let `S` be only the chosen horizontal lifts and their inverses, and let

`X = Cay(Gamma,S)`.

Put

`G0 = sum_i g_i`,
`D = 4 G0`,
`q = D-1 = 4G0-1`,
`k = sum_i(2g_i-1)=2G0-r`,
`t = 2k-1 = 4G0-2r-1`.

**Theorem 4.1 (abelian-central horizontal-lift gap).** Under (4.1)–(4.2),

`p_c(X) <= 1/t < p_{2->2}(X) <= p_u(X)`.                        (4.3)

In particular `p_c(X)<p_u(X)`.

The conclusion is uniform over the central extension class and over arbitrary central shears of the selected horizontal lifts, provided (4.2) holds.

### 4.1 Horizontal generation

Let `F` be the free group on the horizontal labels. Its quotient onto `Q` has normal kernel generated by the `r` surface relators (one from each free-product factor). In the central extension, each such relator evaluates to `e_i`. Because the kernel `A` is central, conjugates of the relators contribute the same central values. Hence the intersection of the subgroup generated by the horizontal lifts with `A` is exactly the subgroup generated by `e_1,...,e_r`.

Assumption (4.2) therefore implies that the horizontal lifts generate all of `Gamma`. Conversely, if the `e_i` generate a proper subgroup of `A`, the horizontal lifts miss the complementary kernel cosets. This proves both the generation statement and the scope obstruction.

Central shears do not alter `e_i`: if `a,b` are replaced by `a c`, `b d` with `c,d in A`, then centrality gives `[ac,bd]=[a,b]`.

### 4.2 Critical side: a visible free tree

For the standard one-relator presentation

`Gamma_g=<a_1,b_1,...,a_g,b_g | prod_j [a_j,b_j]=1>`,

Magnus' Freiheitssatz implies that deleting any one generator appearing in the cyclically reduced relator leaves a free subgroup of rank `2g-1` on the remaining generator symbols. Therefore the union of these free subsets over the free-product factors freely generates

`F_k`, `k=sum_i(2g_i-1)=2G0-r`,

inside `Q`.

The corresponding chosen lifts also freely generate `F_k`: a nontrivial reduced word equal to the identity upstairs would project to a nontrivial reduced word equal to the identity in `Q`.

Thus `X` contains the standard `2k`-regular tree generated by these directions as an edge-subgraph. Bond percolation on that tree has threshold `1/(2k-1)`, and adding edges can only decrease `p_c`. Hence

`p_c(X) <= 1/(2k-1)=1/t`.                                      (4.4)

### 4.3 Adjacency norm: Fourier transform on the full compact dual of `A`

Choose a set-theoretic section of `Q` in `Gamma`. Then as Hilbert spaces

`ell^2(Gamma) ~= ell^2(Q) tensor ell^2(A)`.

Fourier transform the central `A` coordinate. Since `A` is discrete abelian, `ell^2(A)` decomposes over its compact Pontryagin dual `A^`. The horizontal adjacency becomes a direct integral

`A_X = int_{chi in A^}^{oplus} A_chi dchi`,

where each `A_chi` is the quotient adjacency with unit-modulus magnetic phases coming from the extension cocycle and chosen lifts.

For every `f` and every character `chi`, pointwise

`|A_chi f| <= A_Q |f|`.                                        (4.5)

Therefore

`||A_chi|| <= ||A_Q||`, and hence `||A_X|| <= ||A_Q||`.         (4.6)

Now write `A_Q=sum_i B_i`, where `B_i` is convolution by the standard generators of the `i`th surface subgroup. Restricting the regular representation of `Q` to `Gamma_{g_i}` gives a direct sum of copies of the regular representation of that subgroup over left cosets, so

`||B_i|| = ||A_{Gamma_{g_i}}|| <= 4 sqrt(g_i)`                 (4.7)

by Żuk's standard-surface random-walk bound. Thus

`rho:=||A_X|| <= 4 sum_i sqrt(g_i) <= 4 sqrt(r G0)`.            (4.8)

The last step is Cauchy–Schwarz.

This argument uses no one-dimensional property of a cyclic centre.

### 4.4 Nonbacktracking transfer

For a `D`-regular graph let `N_n` be the length-`n` nonbacktracking walk operator. Then

`N_0=I`, `N_1=A`, `N_2=A^2-DI`,

and for `n>=3`,

`N_n=A N_{n-1}-(D-1)N_{n-2}`.                                 (4.9)

For a spectral value `lambda` of `A`, the exponential roots satisfy

`u^2-lambda u+q=0`, `q=D-1`.

If `z>sqrt(q)` and

`z+q/z > ||A||`,                                               (4.10)

then every such root has modulus strictly below `z`. By the spectral theorem,

`limsup_n ||N_n||^(1/n) < z`.                                  (4.11)

Every open connection contains a shortest open path, hence a nonbacktracking one. The union bound gives entrywise

`T_p(x,y) <= sum_{n>=0} p^n N_n(x,y)`.                          (4.12)

Consequently `T_p` is bounded on `ell^2` whenever `p` is below the reciprocal of the nonbacktracking exponential rate. Thus, writing that rate as `beta`,

`p_{2->2}(X) >= 1/beta`.                                      (4.13)

Finally `p_{2->2}<=p_u`: for `p>p_u`, the unique infinite cluster has positive density `theta(p)`, and FKG gives

`T_p(x,y)>=theta(p)^2` for every `x,y`; an infinite constant-lower-bounded column is not in `ell^2`.

It remains to prove `beta<t`.

### 4.5 Uniform inequality `beta<t`

Recall

`t=4G0-2r-1`, `q=4G0-1`, `G0>=2r`,

and `rho<=4sqrt(rG0)`.

First, `t>sqrt(q)`. Indeed the left side is increasing in `G0`, and at `G0=2r`,

`t^2-q=(6r-1)^2-(8r-1)=36r^2-20r+2>0`.                        (4.14)

Second define

`H_r(G)=t+q/t-4sqrt(rG)`.

Using `dt/dG=dq/dG=4` and `t-q=-2r`,

`H_r'(G)=4-8r/t^2-2sqrt(r/G)`.                                 (4.15)

For `G>=2r`, `t>=6r-1` and

`8r/t^2 <= 8/25`, `2sqrt(r/G)<=sqrt(2)<3/2`.

Therefore

`H_r'(G)>4-8/25-3/2>0`.                                       (4.16)

So the minimum is at `G=2r`. There,

`H_r(2r)=(6r-1)+(8r-1)/(6r-1)-4r sqrt(2)`.

Since

`(8r-1)/(6r-1)>4/3`

and `sqrt(2)<3/2`,

`H_r(2r)>(6-4sqrt(2))r+1/3>0`.                                (4.17)

Hence

`t+q/t > 4sqrt(rG0) >= rho`.                                  (4.18)

By the root criterion (4.10), `beta<t`. Combining with (4.4), (4.13) and `p_{2->2}<=p_u` proves Theorem 4.1.

## 7. What remains open and the next genuinely different route

The universal gap is still exactly the difficult one: arbitrary finitely generated nonamenable groups and arbitrary finite Cayley generating sets need not admit (i) a visible free subset with a tree threshold witness, (ii) an amenable central fibre giving magnetic quotient domination, or (iii) a quotient adjacency norm separated from the free-subgroup branching threshold.

Within the current central-extension lane, Theorem 4.1 is now essentially complete for standard surface-free-product horizontal sets and arbitrary finitely generated abelian central fibre. Another run that merely changes the Euler matrix or centre rank would be bookkeeping rather than new mathematics.

The next nonduplicating method should therefore attack **generator stability**: replace the visible-free-subset bound by a quotient-side invariant that survives a controlled Nielsen/finite-generator perturbation, while keeping the compact-dual magnetic domination. Concretely, one promising target is a theorem of the form

`free-subgroup critical witness + magnetic quotient spectral norm + bounded generator distortion => p_c<p_u`

with explicit distortion constants, followed by a family of nonstandard Cayley sets not covered by the standard horizontal theorem. If this cannot beat the threshold, the exact obstruction should be recorded rather than reverting to another centre-rank variant.
