## 4. General horizontal-lift central-extension criterion

Let

\[
1\longrightarrow \langle z\rangle\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow \Gamma \overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow} Q
\longrightarrow1
\tag{4.1}
\]

be a central extension. Let `S_Q` be a finite inverse-closed generating set of `Q`, with

\[
D:=|S_Q|,
\qquad
\rho:=\|A_{Q,S_Q}\|_{2\to2}.
\]

Choose inverse-compatible lifts `\widetilde S_Q` and assume that these lifts generate all of `Gamma`. Let

\[
X=\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma,\widetilde S_Q),
\tag{4.2}
\]

so `X` is `D`-regular and contains no separately adjoined central generator.

Assume that `S_Q` contains `k>=2` generator pairs represented by elements

\[
t_1,\ldots,t_k
\]

that freely generate `F_k`. Put

\[
q:=D-1,
\]

and define the standard nonbacktracking exponential root bound

\[
\beta_D(\rho)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt q,&\rho\le2\sqrt q,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{\rho+\sqrt{\rho^2-4q}}2,&\rho>2\sqrt q.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.3}
\]

**Theorem 4.1 (horizontal-lift strict-gap criterion).** If

\[
\beta_D(\rho)<2k-1,
\tag{4.4}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
 p_c(X)\le\frac1{2k-1}
 <\frac1{\beta_D(\rho)}
 \le p_{2\to2}(X)\le p_u(X).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular `p_c(X)<p_u(X)`.

### Proof of the critical-side inequality

Let `\widetilde t_i` be the chosen lifts. If a nonempty freely reduced word in `\widetilde t_i^{+-1}` were the identity in `Gamma`, its projection would be the corresponding nonempty freely reduced word in `t_i^{+-1}`, contradicting freeness in `Q`. Hence the lifts themselves freely generate `F_k`.

Using only the corresponding generator edges, `X` therefore contains an exact copy of the `2k`-regular tree `T_{2k}` as an edge-subgraph through each coset. Bond percolation on `T_{2k}` has critical probability `1/(2k-1)`, so adding all remaining edges can only lower the critical threshold:

\[
 p_c(X)\le\frac1{2k-1}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof of lift-uniform adjacency domination

Choose a section of `Q` in `Gamma` and identify

\[
\ell^2(\Gamma)\cong\ell^2(Q)\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb Z).
\]

Fourier transform the central coordinate. For almost every character `theta`, the lifted horizontal adjacency becomes a magnetic quotient operator `A_theta`: every quotient generator edge receives a unit complex phase determined by the extension cocycle and the chosen lift. Therefore, pointwise,

\[
 |A_\theta f|\le A_{Q,S_Q}|f|.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Taking `ell^2` norms gives

\[
 \|A_\theta\|\le\rho
\]

for every fibre, hence the direct-integral adjacency of `X` obeys

\[
 \boxed{\|A_X\|\le\rho.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is independent of the cocycle and of arbitrary central shears of the chosen lifts.

### Proof of the nonbacktracking transfer

Let `N_n` be the length-`n` nonbacktracking walk kernels on a `D`-regular graph. They satisfy

\[
N_0=I,\qquad N_1=A,
\qquad N_2=A^2-DI,
\]

and, for `n>=3`,

\[
N_n=A N_{n-1}-(D-1)N_{n-2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

By the spectral theorem and (4.8), the exponential growth of `\|N_n\|` is at most `beta_D(rho)` (possible polynomial prefactors do not alter the radius of convergence).

Every open connection from `x` to `y` contains a shortest open path, and such a path is nonbacktracking. The union bound therefore gives entrywise

\[
T_p(x,y)=\mathbb P_p(x\leftrightarrow y)
\le\sum_{n\ge0}p^nN_n(x,y).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Consequently `T_p` is bounded on `ell^2` whenever

\[
p\,\beta_D(\rho)<1,
\]

so

\[
p_{2\to2}(X)\ge\beta_D(\rho)^{-1}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Finally `p_{2\to2}\le p_u`: for `p>p_u`, uniqueness of the infinite cluster and FKG imply

\[
T_p(x,y)\ge \mathbb P_p(x\leftrightarrow\infty,\,y\leftrightarrow\infty)
\ge\theta(p)^2>0
\]

for all `x,y`. A kernel bounded below by a positive constant on an infinite graph is not a bounded `ell^2` operator. Combining (4.6), (4.11), and (4.4) proves (4.5). `square`

## 5. Surface-central amalgams without a central generator

For `i=1,...,r`, let `g_i>=2` and `e_i in Z`, and define

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,\mathbf e}
=\left\langle
 z,\ a_{i,j},b_{i,j}
\;\middle|\;
 z\text{ central},\quad
 R_i:=\prod_{j=1}^{g_i}[a_{i,j},b_{i,j}]=z^{e_i}
 \ (1\le i\le r)
\right\rangle.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Killing `z` gives

\[
Q_{\mathbf g}
\cong \Gamma_{g_1}*\cdots*\Gamma_{g_r},
\tag{5.2}
\]

the free product of the closed orientable surface groups.

For arbitrary integers `m_{i,j},n_{i,j}`, replace the horizontal lifts by

\[
 a_{i,j}z^{m_{i,j}},\qquad b_{i,j}z^{n_{i,j}},
\]

together with their inverses. Since `z` is central,

\[
[a_{i,j}z^{m_{i,j}},b_{i,j}z^{n_{i,j}}]
=[a_{i,j},b_{i,j}],
\tag{5.3}
\]

so every surface relator still evaluates to `z^{e_i}`.

### Exact generation lemma

Let `H` be the subgroup generated by all these horizontal sheared lifts and put

\[
d:=\gcd(|e_1|,\ldots,|e_r|),
\]

with the convention `d=0` if all `e_i=0`.

Then

\[
\boxed{H\cap\langle z\rangle=\langle z^d\rangle.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

**Proof.** The inclusion `\langle z^d\rangle\subseteq H` follows from Bezout when `d>0`, because every `z^{e_i}=R_i` lies in `H`; for `d=0` the left side is trivial. Conversely, let a horizontal word represent `z^n`. Its projection to (5.2) is trivial. In the free group on the horizontal labels, the kernel of the presentation map to the free product (5.2) is the normal closure of the disjoint surface relators `R_i`. Hence the word is a product of conjugates of `R_i^{+-1}`. In `mathcal E`, every such conjugate evaluates to `z^{+-e_i}` because `z` is central. Therefore `n` is an integer combination of the `e_i`, so `d|n`. This proves (5.4). `square`

The horizontal lifts surject onto the quotient `Q_{\mathbf g}`. Since `mathcal E=H\langle z\rangle`, (5.4) implies

\[
[\mathcal E:H]=d\quad(d>=1),
\]

and the index is infinite when `d=0`. Thus the horizontal lifts generate the **full group exactly when**

\[
\boxed{d=1.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the promised exact obstruction outside the primitive-Euler scope.

## 6. Uniform strict gap for every primitive Euler vector

Assume now `d=1`. Put

\[
G:=\sum_{i=1}^r g_i,
\qquad
H_\sqrt{}:=\sum_{i=1}^r\sqrt{g_i},
\qquad
k:=\sum_{i=1}^r(2g_i-1)=2G-r.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The horizontal Cayley graph has degree

\[
D=4G,
\qquad q=D-1=4G-1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

By Magnus Freiheitssatz, in each standard surface factor one may omit one of the `2g_i` positive standard generators and obtain a free subgroup of rank `2g_i-1`. In the free product quotient their union freely generates

\[
F_k,
\]

so Theorem 4.1 has

\[
t:=2k-1=4G-2r-1.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For the quotient adjacency, write `A_Q=sum_i A_i`, where `A_i` is the adjacency contributed by the `i`th surface-factor generators. Right multiplication by that factor preserves its left cosets, so `A_i` is a direct sum of copies of the standard genus-`g_i` surface adjacency. By (1.1),

\[
\|A_i\|\le4\sqrt{g_i}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\rho:=\|A_Q\|\le R:=4\sum_i\sqrt{g_i}=4H_\sqrt{}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

It remains to show

\[
\beta_D(R)<t
\tag{6.5}
\]

uniformly.

### Step 1: `t>sqrt(q)`

For fixed `r`, `G>=2r`. The quantity

\[
t^2-q=(4G-2r-1)^2-(4G-1)
\]

is increasing in `G` throughout this range. At the minimum `G=2r`,

\[
t^2-q=36r^2-20r+2>0
\qquad(r\ge1).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Hence `t>sqrt(q)`.

### Step 2: the root-test inequality

For every integer `g>=2`, define

\[
\delta(g)=4g-2-4\sqrt g.
\]

Both `4g-2` and `4sqrt(g)` are positive, and

\[
(4g-2)^2-16g
=4\bigl(4(g-1)^2-3\bigr)>0,
\tag{6.7}
\]

so

\[
\delta(g)>0.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Since

\[
q=t+2r,
\]

we obtain the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
t+\frac qt-R
&=\sum_{i=1}^r(4g_i-2-4\sqrt{g_i})-1+\frac qt\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^r\delta(g_i)+\frac{2r}{t}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Now `h(x)=x+q/x` is strictly increasing on `(sqrt(q),infty)`. If `R<=2sqrt(q)`, then `beta_D(R)=sqrt(q)<t`. If `R>2sqrt(q)`, the larger characteristic root `beta=beta_D(R)` is the unique point above `sqrt(q)` with `h(beta)=R`; by (6.9), `R<h(t)`, hence `beta<t`. This proves (6.5).

Applying Theorem 4.1 yields the complete restricted-family theorem:

**Theorem 6.1.** For every `r>=1`, every `g_i>=2`, every primitive Euler vector `e in Z^r` with `gcd(|e_i|)=1`, and every arbitrary integer central shear of every standard horizontal generator, the horizontal lifts alone form a Cayley generating set of `mathcal E_{g,e}` and

\[
\boxed{
 p_c
 \le \frac1{4G-2r-1}
 <\frac1{\beta_{4G}(4\sum_i\sqrt{g_i})}
 \le p_{2\to2}\le p_u.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

No explicit central generator edge is present.
