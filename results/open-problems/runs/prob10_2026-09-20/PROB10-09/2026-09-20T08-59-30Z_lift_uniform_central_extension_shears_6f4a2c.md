## 4. General lift-uniform central-extension criterion

Let

\[
1\longrightarrow \langle z\rangle\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow \Gamma \overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow} Q
\longrightarrow1
\tag{4.1}
\]

be a central extension. Let `S_Q` be a finite inverse-closed generating set of `Q`, with no involution/collision issues, and write

\[
d:=|S_Q|,\qquad \rho:=\|A_{Q,S_Q}\|_{2\to2}.
\]

Choose **arbitrarily** one inverse-compatible lift of every generator in `S_Q`, giving `\widetilde S_Q`, and let

\[
X=\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma,\widetilde S_Q\cup\{z,z^{-1}\}).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus `X` is `D=d+2` regular. Suppose `S_Q` contains inverse pairs generated by elements

\[
t_1,\ldots,t_k
\]

that freely generate a free subgroup `F_k`, with `k>=2`.

Set

\[
q:=D-1=d+1,\qquad R:=\rho+2,
\]

and

\[
\beta_D(R)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt q,&R\le2\sqrt q,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{R+\sqrt{R^2-4q}}2,&R>2\sqrt q.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.3}
\]

**Theorem (lift-uniform strict-gap criterion).** If

\[
2k>\sqrt q
\quad\text{and}\quad
R<2k+\frac{q}{2k},
\tag{4.4}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
 p_c(X)<\frac1{2k}<\frac1{\beta_D(R)}
 \le p_{2\to2}(X)\le p_u(X).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular `p_c(X)<p_u(X)`. The conclusion is uniform over the central extension cocycle and over **all** inverse-compatible lifts of the frozen quotient generating set.

### Proof: the lifted free subgroup is exactly `F_k x Z`

Let `\widetilde t_i` be the chosen lifts. A nonempty freely reduced word in the `\widetilde t_i^{+-1}` cannot equal the identity: its projection is the corresponding nonempty reduced word in the free basis `t_i`, hence nontrivial. Thus the lifts freely generate `F_k`.

If a word `w(\widetilde t_1,...,\widetilde t_k)` lies in `<z>`, then its projection is trivial in the quotient free subgroup. Freeness reduces `w` to the empty word by inverse cancellations, and the identical cancellations among the chosen lifts give `w=1` already in `Gamma`. Hence

\[
\langle\widetilde t_1,...,\widetilde t_k\rangle\cap\langle z\rangle=\{1\}.
\]

Since `z` is central,

\[
\langle\widetilde t_1,...,\widetilde t_k,z\rangle\cong F_k\times\mathbb Z.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Using only those generator edges, `X` therefore contains the exact edge-subgraph

\[
T_{2k}\square\mathbb Z.
\tag{4.7}
\]

### Proof: strict critical bound from the entire `Z` fibre

Root the `T_{2k}` factor and write

\[
b:=2k-1\ge3
\]

for the number of forward children of a non-root tree vertex. At a newly activated tree fibre, expose the full open cluster of its seed inside the vertical `Z` line. Its size is

\[
N=1+L_-+L_+,
\]

where `L_-` and `L_+` are independent with

\[
\mathbb P(L=m)=(1-p)p^m,\qquad m\ge0.
\]

For a given forward child fibre, the probability that at least one of the `N` horizontal candidate edges is open is `1-(1-p)^N`. Different forward children use disjoint edges. Choosing the first open crossing deterministically before exposing the child's new vertical cluster yields an honest Galton--Watson process with mean

\[
\mu(p)=b\left(1-\mathbb E[(1-p)^N]\right).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Put `s=1-p`. Since

\[
\mathbb E[s^L]=\frac{s}{1-ps},
\]

we have

\[
\mathbb E[s^N]=\frac{s^3}{(1-ps)^2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

At

\[
p_0=\frac1{b+1}=\frac1{2k},
\]

one gets

\[
\mathbb E[s^N]
=\frac{b^3(b+1)}{(b^2+b+1)^2}.
\]

The inequality `mu(p_0)>1` is exactly

\[
(b-1)(b^2+b+1)^2>b^4(b+1),
\]

whose difference is

\[
f(b):=b^3-b^2-b-1.
\]

Now `f(3)=14>0`, and

\[
f'(b)=3b^2-2b-1=(3b+1)(b-1)>0\qquad(b\ge3).
\]

Thus `mu(p_0)>1` for every `k>=2`. By continuity, the retained branching process is still supercritical for some `p<p_0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{p_c(X)<\frac1{2k}.}
\tag{4.10}
\]

### Proof: arbitrary lifts cost no more than the quotient norm

Choose a section `Q -> Gamma` and identify

\[
\ell^2(\Gamma)\cong\ell^2(Q)\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb Z).
\]

Fourier transform the central coordinate. For almost every `theta`, the horizontal lift adjacency becomes a magnetic quotient operator `A_theta`: every quotient edge receives a unit-modulus phase determined by the extension cocycle and the chosen lift. Consequently, pointwise,

\[
|A_\theta f|\le A_{Q,S_Q}|f|.
\]

Hence

\[
\|A_\theta f\|_2\le\rho\|f\|_2,
\qquad
\|A_\theta\|\le\rho.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The two central edges contribute the scalar `2 cos(theta)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\|A_X\|\le\rho+2=R.}
\tag{4.12}
\]

This is the key lift-uniform statement: arbitrarily large central shears alter phases but not the norm bound.

### Proof: nonbacktracking transfer and the scalar criterion

For a `D`-regular graph with adjacency norm at most `R`, the standard nonbacktracking polynomial recurrence gives growth exponent at most `beta_D(R)` in (4.3). Since every open connection has a self-avoiding, hence nonbacktracking, witness path,

\[
T_p\le\sum_{n\ge0}p^nB_n
\]

entrywise, and the right side converges in `ell^2 -> ell^2` norm whenever `p beta_D(R)<1`. Thus

\[
p_{2\to2}(X)\ge\beta_D(R)^{-1}.
\tag{4.13}
\]

The standard transitive-graph comparison gives `p_{2->2}<=p_u`.

Let `x=2k`. If `R<=2sqrt(q)`, the first condition in (4.4) gives `beta=sqrt(q)<x`. If `R>2sqrt(q)`, the larger characteristic root is `beta`, the smaller root is below `sqrt(q)`, and

\[
x^2-Rx+q
=x\left(x+\frac qx-R\right)>0.
\]

Because `x>sqrt(q)`, `x` lies to the right of the larger root. Hence again `beta<x`. Combining with (4.10), (4.13), and `p_{2->2}<=p_u` proves (4.5). `square`

## 5. Application: arbitrary central shears and arbitrary Euler vectors in surface amalgams

For `r>=2`, `g_i>=2`, and now **arbitrary** integers `e_i`, define

\[
\Gamma_{\mathbf g,\mathbf e}
=\left\langle z,a_{i,j},b_{i,j}
\;\middle|\;
 z\text{ central},\quad
 \prod_{j=1}^{g_i}[a_{i,j},b_{i,j}]=z^{e_i},\ i=1,...,r
\right\rangle.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For nonzero `e_i` this is the standard oriented circle-bundle surface-group extension; for `e_i=0` the corresponding factor is `Gamma_{g_i} x Z`. Amalgamating the common fibre preserves the infinite central subgroup. Killing `z` gives

\[
Q=\Gamma_{g_1}*\cdots*\Gamma_{g_r}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Now choose **arbitrary integers** `u_{i,j},v_{i,j}` and use the Cayley generating set

\[
S(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)=
\{(a_{i,j}z^{u_{i,j}})^{\pm1},
  (b_{i,j}z^{v_{i,j}})^{\pm1}\}_{i,j}
\cup\{z^{\pm1}\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

These are arbitrary central shears of every standard quotient generator; there is no bound on the shear exponents.

Write

\[
G:=\sum_i g_i,\qquad H:=\sum_i\sqrt{g_i},\qquad
k:=\sum_i(2g_i-1)=2G-r.
\tag{5.4}
\]

By Magnus Freiheitssatz, after omitting one standard surface generator in each factor, the corresponding quotient generators freely generate `F_k` inside the free product (5.2). Section 4 shows that **their arbitrary sheared lifts**, together with `z`, still give `F_k x Z`. Therefore

\[
p_c<\frac1{2k}=\frac1{4G-2r}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For the quotient adjacency, each surface-factor contribution preserves its cosets. Zuk's standard surface bound gives

\[
\|A_{\Gamma_{g_i}}\|\le4\sqrt{g_i},
\]

so by the triangle inequality

\[
\rho\le4H.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus `D=4G+2`, `q=4G+1`, and `R<=4H+2`.

It remains only to verify the scalar criterion uniformly. Since every `g_i>=2`,

\[
H\le\frac G{\sqrt2},
\qquad
R\le R_0:=2\sqrt2\,G+2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Also `G>=2r`, hence

\[
x:=2k=4G-2r\ge3G.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For `r>=2`, `G>=4`, so `x>sqrt(4G+1)`. Consider

\[
P(t)=t^2-Rt+(4G+1).
\]

Because `x>=3G>R_0/2` and `t^2-R_0t` is increasing on this range,

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(x)
&\ge x^2-R_0x+4G+1\\
&\ge(3G)^2-R_0(3G)+4G+1\\
&=(9-6\sqrt2)G^2-2G+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.9}
\]

The exact inequality

\[
9-6\sqrt2>\frac12
\]

follows by squaring `17/2>6sqrt(2)`, reducing to `289>288`. Therefore

\[
P(x)>\frac12G^2-2G+1\ge1
\qquad(G\ge4).
\tag{5.10}
\]

This is precisely the root-side inequality in Section 4, and hence

\[
\boxed{
 p_c(\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma_{\mathbf g,\mathbf e},S(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)))
 <p_u(\operatorname{Cay}(\Gamma_{\mathbf g,\mathbf e},S(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)))
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

for **every** `r>=2`, every `g_i>=2`, every Euler vector `e in Z^r`, and every integer shear vector `(u,v)`.

The all-zero Euler vector includes a split product with the central line; mixed/nonzero Euler vectors include non-split central extensions. Qualitative `p_u`-boundary nonuniqueness for groups with infinite centre is already within Gaboriau--Tucker-Drob's theorem; (5.11) is the strict-threshold, quantitative statement supplied here for this explicit unbounded family of Cayley generating sets.
