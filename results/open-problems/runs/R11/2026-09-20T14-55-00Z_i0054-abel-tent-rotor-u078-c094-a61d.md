# 6. Abel mixing lemma

Consider one visible carrier and ignore inactive/active labels temporarily. Every proposal is active with probability `q_N`, independently of its position in the proposal stream. Hence, starting from any incoming phase, the number `K` of proposals up to and including the next active one has

\[
\Pr(K=r)=q_N(1-q_N)^{r-1},\qquad r\ge1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a continuous 1-periodic function `f`, define

\[
(A_qf)(x)=\sum_{r\ge1}q(1-q)^{r-1}f(x+r\omega).
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Lemma U078.1

For irrational `omega`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{x\in\mathbb T}
\left|(A_qf)(x)-\int_0^1f(u)\,du\right|
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(q\downarrow0)
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

for every continuous periodic `f`.

### Proof

For the Fourier character `e_m(x)=e^{2\pi i m x}`, `m!=0`,

\[
A_qe_m(x)
=e^{2\pi i m x}
\frac{q e^{2\pi i m\omega}}
{1-(1-q)e^{2\pi i m\omega}}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Since irrationality gives `e^{2 pi i m omega} != 1`, the denominator tends to a nonzero number, so the right side tends uniformly in `x` to zero. Constants are fixed. Every trigonometric polynomial therefore satisfies (6.3). Given continuous `f`, choose a trigonometric polynomial `P` with `||f-P||_infty<epsilon`. Both `A_q` and Lebesgue averaging have operator norm one on `L^infty`, hence

\[
\sup_x|A_qf(x)-\textstyle\int f|
\le2\epsilon+\sup_x|A_qP(x)-\textstyle\int P|.
\]

Let `q->0` and then `epsilon->0`. This proves the lemma. `square`

A deterministic offset is absorbed into `x`; therefore (6.3) is automatically uniform over all `a_{N,s}`.
