## 2. Idea before code; what failed; the three moves

The previous run's C046 forced the squarefree polynomial to have exactly six irreducible factors. The natural next question is whether that artificial fixed-factor conditioning can be removed.

The first candidate is the genuinely ordinary one: sample a uniform squarefree degree-`N` polynomial and use **all** of its irreducible factors as the children. This fails for a structural reason. Squarefree polynomial factorization over a fixed finite field is a logarithmic combinatorial structure with parameter one. For a fixed visible set partition with block sizes `n_1,...,n_r`, the all-factor proposal has the Ewens(1) limit

\[
 p(n_1,\ldots,n_r)
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{\prod_a (n_a-1)!}{b!},
 \qquad b=\sum_an_a.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus on three labels

\[
 p(1|2)\to\frac12,
 \qquad
 p(1|2|3)\to\frac16.
\]

After pair normalization, the direct ternary rate tends to `1/3`, whereas CTCS has no direct ternary jump. So merely dropping the six-factor condition destroys the CTCS tangent.

The repair is **pointing**: use one irreducible factor against its complement. This kills every higher-way event exactly while retaining the harmonic degree intensity which generates the beta kernel.

The actual moves were:

- **#8 — discretize.** I turned the pointed-factor intensity into an honest finite raw-step hierarchy with a vanishing exact mesh and a legal attempt probability, rather than proving only a continuous generator identity.
- **#20 — find why the obvious algorithm fails.** The all-factor unconditioned polynomial has an Ewens(1) visible partition and hence an unavoidable ternary rate `1/3` after pair normalization. Pointing one factor is exactly the repair.
- **#25 — count the same thing twice.** The total proposal intensity is both the mean number of irreducible factors of a uniform squarefree polynomial and the normalized cardinality of the finite factor-incidence set. The same factor incidences also give the split rates either by direct pair counting or by grouping incidences by factor degree.

## 3. Exact squarefree and pointed-factor counts over F_2

Let `I_2(m)` be the number of monic irreducible degree-`m` polynomials over `F_2`. As usual,

\[
 I_2(m)=\frac1m\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)2^{m/d}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `S_N` be the number of squarefree monic degree-`N` binary polynomials. The Euler product is exact:

\[
 \sum_{N\ge0}S_Nz^N
 =\prod_g(1+z^{\deg g})
 =\frac{1-2z^2}{1-2z},
\tag{3.2}
\]

where the product is over monic irreducibles. Therefore

\[
 S_0=1,\qquad S_1=2,\qquad
 \boxed{S_N=2^{N-1}\quad(N\ge2).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Fix one irreducible `g` of degree `m`. The number of squarefree degree-`n` cofactors not divisible by `g` is

\[
 B_{n,m}
 :=[z^n]\frac{\sum_{r\ge0}S_rz^r}{1+z^m}
 =\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor n/m\rfloor}(-1)^aS_{n-am}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence the exact number of factor incidences `(f,g)` in which `f` is squarefree monic of degree `N` and `g|f` is an irreducible factor of degree `m` is

\[
 \boxed{A_{N,m}=I_2(m)B_{N-m,m}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Put

\[
 A_N=\sum_{m=1}^NA_{N,m},
 \qquad
 \kappa_N=\frac{A_N}{S_N}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the #25 double count:

\[
 \boxed{
 \kappa_N
 =\frac1{S_N}\sum_{f\in\mathcal S_N}K(f)
 =\mathbb E[K(F_N)],
 }
\tag{3.7}
\]

where `K(f)` is the number of irreducible factors and `F_N` is uniform on the squarefree degree-`N` polynomials. Equivalently, differentiating the marked Euler product

\[
 \prod_g(1+u z^{\deg g})
\]

at `u=1` produces exactly the same factor-incidence count `A_N`.

Two elementary bounds are enough for the path construction. First,

\[
 I_2(m)\le \frac{2^m}{m}.
\]

Second, `B_{N-m,m}\le S_{N-m}`. Using (3.3), for every `1\le m\le N`,

\[
 \boxed{
 W_{N,m}:=\frac{A_{N,m}}{S_N}\le\frac2m,
 }
\tag{3.8}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \kappa_N\le2H_N=O(\log N).
 }
\tag{3.9}
\]

The standard logarithmic-structure asymptotic in fact gives `\kappa_N\sim\log N`, but the upper bound is all the discretization proof needs.

## 4. Bulk harmonic intensity without fixed factor count

The crucial point is that the exact pointed-factor rate `W_{N,m}` has the same bulk `1/m` asymptotic as permutation cycles, despite the absence of any conditioning on `K(f)`.

Let `n=N-m`. From (3.4),

\[
 B_{n,m}=S_n-B_{n-m,m}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

If `m,n\to\infty`, then from (3.3)

\[
 \frac{B_{n,m}}{S_n}=1+O(2^{-m})
\tag{4.2}
\]

uniformly when both `m` and `n` are a positive fraction of `N`; when `m>n`, the equality `B_{n,m}=S_n` is exact. Also (3.1) gives

\[
 I_2(m)2^{-m}=\frac1m+O(2^{-m/2}).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently, uniformly for every fixed `epsilon>0` and
`epsilon N\le m\le(1-epsilon)N`, 

\[
 \boxed{
 W_{N,m}=\frac1m(1+o(1)).
 }
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the arithmetic replacement for the fixed-component logarithmic-assembly tangent in U025.

## 5. U026 — the pointed-factor beta-kernel theorem

For a specified visible unordered binary partition with block sizes `i,j>=1`, `b=i+j`, conditional on a selected factor of degree `m`, uniform allocation of the `N` labels to the factor slots gives the exact hypergeometric probability

\[
 s_{N,m}(i,j)
 =\frac{(m)_{\underline i}(N-m)_{\underline j}
 +(m)_{\underline j}(N-m)_{\underline i}}
 {(N)_{\underline b}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define the exact pointed-factor rate

\[
 \boxed{
 q_N(i,j)=\sum_{m=1}^N W_{N,m}s_{N,m}(i,j).
 }
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Bulk

For `m/N=x` in `[epsilon,1-epsilon]`, (4.4) and fixed `i,j` give

\[
 W_{N,m}s_{N,m}(i,j)
 =\frac1N\left[
 x^{i-1}(1-x)^j+x^{j-1}(1-x)^i
 \right]+o(N^{-1})
\tag{5.3}
\]

uniformly. The bulk sum is therefore a Riemann sum.

### Small selected factors

For `m\le epsilon N`, (3.8) and the falling-factorial bound give, for a constant depending only on `i,j`,

\[
 W_{N,m}s_{N,m}(i,j)
 \le \frac{C_{i,j}}m
 \left[(m/N)^i+(m/N)^j\right].
\]

Thus

\[
 \sum_{m\le epsilon N}W_{N,m}s_{N,m}(i,j)
 =O_{i,j}(epsilon^i+epsilon^j).
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Factors whose complement is small

Writing `n=N-m\le epsilon N`, now `m\ge N/2` and (3.8) gives `W_{N,m}=O(N^{-1})`. Hence

\[
 \sum_{N-m\le epsilon N}W_{N,m}s_{N,m}(i,j)
 =O_{i,j}(epsilon^{i+1}+epsilon^{j+1}).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let `N\to\infty`, then `epsilon\downarrow0`. From the bulk Riemann sum,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_N(i,j)
 &\longrightarrow
 \int_0^1\left[
 x^{i-1}(1-x)^j+x^{j-1}(1-x)^i
 \right]dx\\
 &=B(i,j+1)+B(j,i+1)\\
 &=\boxed{B(i,j)
 =\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This proves the structural claim

\[
 \boxed{\texttt{R11-I0054-U026 = PROVED\_AUTHOR\_SIDE}.}
\]

Unlike U025, **no fixed factor count is imposed**. The price of removing that conditioning is only that one irreducible factor is pointed and split against its complement.

## 6. C047 — the factor-pointed squarefree binary-polynomial hierarchy

For each `N`, let

\[
 \mathcal I_N=
 \{(f,g): f\in F_2[x]\text{ monic squarefree},\ \deg f=N,
 \ g\text{ monic irreducible},\ g\mid f\}.
\]

Thus `|\mathcal I_N|=A_N`.

A **pointed proposal** samples `(f,g)` uniformly from `\mathcal I_N`. Conditional on `m=deg g`, choose a uniform `m`-subset `A` of `[N]`; this is the selected factor's label box. The proposal is the binary partition `{A,A^c}`. Equivalently, one can first allocate the labels uniformly among all factor-degree boxes of `f` and retain only the pointed factor versus all other factors.

Set

\[
 \delta_N=\min\{N^{-2},(2\kappa_N)^{-1}\},
 \qquad
 a_N=\delta_N\kappa_N\le\frac12.
\tag{6.1}
\]

C047 starts from the one-block partition of `[N]`. In every raw step, independently for each current nonsingleton block `B`:

1. attempt with probability `a_N`;
2. on an attempt draw a fresh pointed proposal `{A,A^c}`;
3. replace `B` by the nonempty sets `B\cap A` and `B\cap A^c`;
4. if one intersection is empty the attempt is invisible;
5. children are processed only from the next raw step.

One raw step has physical length `delta_N`.

This is a finite **discrete** model. It uses the complete unconditioned family of squarefree degree-`N` binary polynomials; there is no condition `K(f)=R`. Every effective split is binary by construction.

## 7. Exact hidden-label lumping and whole-path convergence

\[

summed over `m`, i.e. exactly `q_N(i,j)` from (5.2). U026 therefore gives

\[
 Q_N^{(k)}\longrightarrow Q_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}
\tag{7.1}

entrywise on every fixed finite partition state space. There are no multifurcating one-block transitions at any `N`.

 P_N^{(k)}

\tag{7.2}
