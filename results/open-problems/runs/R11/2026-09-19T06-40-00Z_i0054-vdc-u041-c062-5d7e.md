## 2. Move #20 first: why exact total occupation is not enough

The natural continuation of C061 is to say: if a deterministic schedule uses the right finite environment types in the right total proportions, then its average generator is correct, so the CTCS limit should follow.

That statement is false without a **local time-scale condition**.

If two environment generators `A` and `B` are each used for half of a macroscopic interval, but every `A` step is placed first and every `B` step second, then the limiting process sees the time-inhomogeneous generator

\[
Q(t)=\begin{cases}A,&0\le t<1/2,\\B,&1/2\le t\le1,\end{cases}
\]

not the homogeneous average `(A+B)/2`. For noncommuting finite-state generators the endpoint kernels already differ; even in commuting one-jump examples the path law and jump-time law differ. Thus an exact global count can be useless if discrepancy is stored in a macroscopic burst.

This identifies the real obligation omitted by the naive averaging algorithm:

> **the schedule must become well mixed on physical windows whose length tends to zero.**

That is the condition formalized in U041.

## 3. Move #5: introduce the schedule-discrepancy parameter and differentiate in it

Fix a finite visible restriction `[k]`. Let `S_k` be its finite partition state space. Suppose the `N`th discrete hierarchy evolves in microsteps of physical duration `delta_N`, and at microstep `t` its one-step transition kernel is

\[
P_{N,t}^{[k]}
=I+\delta_N A_{N,t}^{[k]}+R_{N,t}^{[k]}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Assume, in any fixed matrix norm on the finite space,

\[
\|A_{N,t}^{[k]}\|\le C_{N,k},
\qquad
\|R_{N,t}^{[k]}\|\le c_k\delta_N^2 C_{N,k}^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Choose a block length `W_N` and write

\[
\tau_N=W_N\delta_N,
\qquad
B_{N,r}^{[k]}
=\frac1{W_N}\sum_{t=rW_N}^{(r+1)W_N-1}A_{N,t}^{[k]}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let `\bar Q_N^{[k]}` be a target averaged generator, and define the local schedule-discrepancy parameter

\[
\eta_{N,k}(T)
:=
\sup_{0\le r\tau_N\le T}
\|B_{N,r}^{[k]}-\bar Q_N^{[k]}\|.
\tag{3.4}
\]

To see why this is the right parameter rather than an arbitrary norm, interpolate

\[
H_{r,\theta}
=(1-\theta)\bar Q_N^{[k]}+\theta B_{N,r}^{[k]},
\qquad 0\le\theta\le1.
\]

Duhamel differentiation gives

\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}e^{\tau_NH_{r,\theta}}
=
\int_0^{\tau_N}
 e^{(\tau_N-s)H_{r,\theta}}
 (B_{N,r}^{[k]}-\bar Q_N^{[k]})
 e^{sH_{r,\theta}}\,ds.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\left\|
 e^{\tau_NB_{N,r}^{[k]}}
 -e^{\tau_N\bar Q_N^{[k]}}
\right\|
\le
\tau_N\eta_{N,k}(T)
\exp\{2\tau_N C_{N,k}\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

So `eta` is exactly the first-order stability modulus of a local block against schedule rearrangement.

## 4. U041 — local schedule-discrepancy CTCS compiler

### Theorem U041

For every fixed `k`, suppose (3.1)--(3.2) hold and there are `W_N` and matrices `\bar Q_N^{[k]}` such that

\[
\tau_N=W_N\delta_N\to0,
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\tau_N C_{N,k}^2\to0,
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
\eta_{N,k}(T)\to0
\quad\text{for every fixed }T<\infty,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
\bar Q_N^{[k]}\longrightarrow Q_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{[k]}
\quad\text{entrywise}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then the complete partition-valued restriction converges:

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_N^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in Skorokhod }J_1.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

If these finite restrictions are consistent and define the finite `N` hierarchy, then convergence for every fixed `k` implies convergence to `CTCS(infinity)` in the frozen summable projective finite-restriction topology.

### Proof

Let

\[
K_{N,r}^{[k]}
:=
\prod_{t=rW_N}^{(r+1)W_N-1}P_{N,t}^{[k]}
\]

be the transition kernel over one local block. Expanding the product to first order and using submultiplicativity,

\[
K_{N,r}^{[k]}
=
I+\delta_N\sum_{t=rW_N}^{(r+1)W_N-1}A_{N,t}^{[k]}
+E_{N,r}^{[k]},
\tag{4.6}
\]

where uniformly for `r tau_N <= T`,

\[
\|E_{N,r}^{[k]}\|
\le
C_k\Bigl[
(\tau_N C_{N,k})^2
+\tau_N\delta_N C_{N,k}^2
\Bigr]
\exp\{C_k\tau_N C_{N,k}\}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Because `delta_N <= tau_N`, condition (4.2) implies both `tau_N C_{N,k}->0` and

\[
\frac{\|E_{N,r}^{[k]}\|}{\tau_N}\to0.
\tag{4.8}
\]

By (3.3)--(3.4),

\[
\delta_N\sum_t A_{N,t}^{[k]}
=
\tau_N\bar Q_N^{[k]}
+	au_N D_{N,r}^{[k]},
\qquad
\|D_{N,r}^{[k]}\|\le\eta_{N,k}(T).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Thus

\[
K_{N,r}^{[k]}
=
I+\tau_N\bar Q_N^{[k]}+o_T(\tau_N)
\tag{4.10}
\]

uniformly over all local blocks before time `T`. There are `O(T/tau_N)` such blocks, so a standard finite-matrix telescoping/Euler argument and (4.4) give convergence of the block-boundary skeleton to the finite-state CTMC with generator `Q_CTCS^{[k]}`.

It remains to recover the path, not only the skeleton. In one local block the probability of two or more visible jumps is bounded by

\[
C_k(\tau_N C_{N,k})^2
+C_k\tau_N\delta_N C_{N,k}^2.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Summing over `O(T/tau_N)` blocks gives

\[
O_{k,T}(\tau_N C_{N,k}^2+\delta_NC_{N,k}^2)\to0.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Therefore, with probability tending to one, every local block contains at most one visible jump. Moving that jump to a block boundary changes its time by at most `tau_N->0`, hence changes the path by `o(1)` in `J1`. This proves (4.5). The projective conclusion follows from the summable finite-restriction metric and consistency. `square`

The point of U041 is that the environment itself need not be random, periodic, Markovian, or exactly balanced over a supercycle. Only its **shrinking-window averaged generator** must converge.

## 6. Elementary discrepancy lemma for C062

The needed low-discrepancy fact can be proved directly, so U041 does not depend on importing a black-box discrepancy theorem.

For every dyadic interval

\[
I_{r,a}=[a2^{-r},(a+1)2^{-r}),
\]

membership `phi_2(t) in I_{r,a}` fixes the lowest `r` binary digits of `t`. Among `t=0,...,n-1`, every residue class modulo `2^r` occurs either `floor(n/2^r)` or `ceil(n/2^r)` times. Hence

\[
\left|
\#\{t<n:\phi_2(t)\in I_{r,a}\}
-n2^{-r}
\right|<1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let `R=ceil(log_2(max(2,n)))`. Decompose an anchored interval `[0,x)` into its first `R` binary dyadic pieces plus a remainder contained in one dyadic interval of length `2^{-R}`. The first part contributes discrepancy at most `R`; the remainder contributes at most `3` because `n2^{-R}<=1`. Therefore

\[
\sup_{0\le x\le1}
\left|
\#\{t<n:\phi_2(t)<x\}-nx
\right|
\le R+3.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Taking a difference of two anchored intervals, for every cell

\[
I_e=[e/M,(e+1)/M)
\]

we obtain

\[
\left|
\#\{t<n:j_t=e\}-\frac nM
\right|
\le2(R+3).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Taking a difference of two prefixes again, for every integer window `[a,b)`,

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\#\{a\le t<b:j_t=e\}-\frac{b-a}{M}
\right|
\le4\bigl(\lceil\log_2\max(2,b)\rceil+3\bigr).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This explicit arbitrary-window estimate is exactly what Move #20 showed was missing from a global-count argument.
