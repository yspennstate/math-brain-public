## 2. Move #1 — change category from exact independence to noise attenuation

U036 characterized what exact `d`-wise independence forces. The new route asks a different question: can a very cheap pairwise-independent source be made asymptotically sufficient by an explicit amount of independent refresh?

Fix `x in (0,1)`. Let `(Y_r)` be Bernoulli(`x`) bits satisfying exact **pairwise independence**, with arbitrary higher-order dependence. For a retention parameter `rho in [0,1]`, independently for every coordinate let

\[
C_r\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\rho),
\qquad
X_r\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(x),
\tag{2.1}
\]

where all `C`'s and `X`'s are mutually independent and independent of `Y`. Define

\[
Z_r=C_rY_r+(1-C_r)X_r.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus with probability `rho` the old pseudorandom bit is retained and otherwise it is freshly redrawn from the target Bernoulli law.

### Lemma 2.1 — exact centered-moment damping

For every finite nonempty label set `S`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\prod_{r\in S}(Z_r-x)
=\rho^{|S|}\,
\mathbb E\prod_{r\in S}(Y_r-x).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

**Proof.** Conditional on `Y_r`,

\[
\mathbb E[Z_r-x\mid Y_r]=\rho(Y_r-x).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Given the whole `Y` vector, the refresh channels are independent across coordinates, hence multiplying (2.4) over `S` and averaging over `Y` gives (2.3). QED.

Because the base law is pairwise independent, the centered moments in (2.3) vanish for `|S|=1,2`. Higher moments admit a uniform bound that has the right factor for the harmonic split measure.

### Lemma 2.2 — pairwise base-moment bound

Put `M=max{x,1-x}`. For every `S` with `r=|S|>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
\left|\mathbb E\prod_{s\in S}(Y_s-x)\right|
\le x(1-x)M^{r-2}
\le x(1-x).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

**Proof.** Choose two distinct coordinates `a,b in S`. Since every remaining factor has absolute value at most `M`,

\[
\left|\mathbb E\prod_{s\in S}(Y_s-x)\right|
\le M^{r-2}\mathbb E|Y_a-x||Y_b-x|.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Pairwise independence makes the last expectation the square of

\[
\mathbb E|Y-x|=2x(1-x),
\tag{2.7}
\]

so it equals `4x^2(1-x)^2`. Since `4x(1-x)<=1`, (2.5) follows. QED.

## 3. U037 — quantitative noise-attenuated pairwise compiler

Consider any finite family of marks `s`. Mark `s` has bias `x_s in (0,1)`, intensity `a_s>=0`, and a pairwise-independent Bernoulli(`x_s`) base vector. Assume the pair normalization

\[
\boxed{
2\sum_s a_sx_s(1-x_s)=1.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Apply the independent-refresh operation (2.2) with a common retention `rho`. For a specified unordered split `A|B` of `k=i+j` visible labels, let `Q(A|B)` be the resulting total split rate, and let

\[
G(i,j)
=\sum_s a_s
\left[x_s^i(1-x_s)^j+x_s^j(1-x_s)^i\right]
\tag{3.2}
\]

be the rate under fully iid Bernoulli marks with the same biases and intensities.

Define

\[
\Delta_k(\rho)
:=\sum_{r=3}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\rho^r.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem U037

For every specified split on `k` labels,

\[
\boxed{
|Q(A|B)-G(i,j)|\le \Delta_k(\rho)
\le 2^k\rho^3.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular, if a sequence of iid reference generators satisfies

\[
G_N(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j)
\quad\text{for every fixed }i,j,
\tag{3.5}
\]

and `rho_N -> 0`, then the refreshed pairwise-independent generators have the same CTCS beta-rate limit.

### Proof

Write `w_r=z_r-x_s`. Expand one oriented split indicator

\[
\prod_{a\in A}z_a\prod_{b\in B}(1-z_b)
\tag{3.6}
\]

as a multilinear polynomial in the centered variables `w_r`. Every coefficient has absolute value at most one, because the constants supplied by coordinates omitted from a monomial are products of numbers `x_s` and `1-x_s`. The reverse orientation has the same property, so every coefficient of the unordered split indicator has absolute value at most two.

Under the iid Bernoulli(`x_s`) law all nonempty centered monomials have expectation zero. Under the refreshed pairwise law, the terms of orders one and two still have expectation zero by exact pairwise independence. Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 therefore give, for mark `s`,

\[
\left|p_s(A|B)-
\{x_s^i(1-x_s)^j+x_s^j(1-x_s)^i\}\right|
\le
2x_s(1-x_s)
\sum_{r=3}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\rho^r.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Multiply by `a_s`, sum over `s`, and use (3.1). This gives the first inequality in (3.4). Since `0<=rho<=1`, each `rho^r<=rho^3` for `r>=3`, and the second inequality follows. QED.

### Move #13 — the monovariant

The relevant monotone quantity is now explicit. As the **refresh strength** `eta=1-rho` increases, every fixed-order defect envelope

\[
\Delta_k(\rho)=\sum_{r=3}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\rho^r
\tag{3.8}
\]

only decreases. Pairwise exactness annihilates Fourier/centered levels one and two, so the first possible error is cubic in the retention. This is the run's useful monovariant: independent refresh monotonically destroys exactly the higher-order obstruction that U036 isolated.

### Move #17 — where the `rho^r` numbers live elsewhere

The factor `rho^r` is the standard level-`r` damping pattern of a product noise operator/Fourier expansion. In pseudorandomness language, the construction takes a cheap exact pairwise source and contracts its higher-order biases toward an almost-`k`-wise source. That connection suggested looking for a quantitative generator bound rather than trying to raise the exact independence order again. ## 4. C058 — noisy affine finite-field-hash fragmentation

U037 yields a particularly explicit discrete model with only a two-parameter affine pseudorandom core.

For each `N>=2`, let `q_N` be the least prime at least `N`. Identify the leaves `0,1,...,N-1` with distinct points of `F_{q_N}`. Put

\[
L_N:=\max\{2,\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil\},
\qquad
\rho_N:=\frac1{L_N}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then `rho_N -> 0`.

For each threshold `u=1,...,q_N-1`, write

\[
x_u=\frac{u}{q_N}
\tag{4.2}
\]

and give every current nonsingleton block an independent Poisson mark process of type `u` with rate

\[
\boxed{
a_{N,u}
=\frac{1}{2(q_N-1)x_u(1-x_u)}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

At a type-`u` mark:

1. choose `A,B` independently and uniformly in `F_{q_N}` and set the affine hash `H(r)=Ar+B`;
2. independently for each ambient leaf `r`, retain `H(r)` with probability `rho_N`; otherwise redraw a fresh independent uniform element of `F_{q_N}`;
3. let `Z_r=1` iff the canonical representative of the resulting field element is `<u`;
4. split the current block according to the two colors if both colors occur in that block; otherwise the mark is invisible.

Different blocks and different marks use fresh independent randomness.

### 4.1 The affine base is exactly pairwise independent

For distinct field points `r!=s`, the map

\[
(A,B)\mapsto (Ar+B,As+B)
\tag{4.4}
\]

is a bijection from `F_q^2` to `F_q^2`, since its determinant is `r-s !=0`. Hence the two affine hash values are independent uniforms. Thresholding at `u` makes the base bits exactly pairwise-independent Bernoulli(`x_u`).

The intensities have exactly the normalization required by U037:

\[
2\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}a_{N,u}x_u(1-x_u)
=2(q-1)\frac1{2(q-1)}=1.
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular the rate at which any specified pair of distinct visible labels is separated is **exactly one for every finite `N`**.

### 4.2 The iid reference generator is the harmonic grid

For a specified `i|j` split, the iid reference rate is

\[
G_q(i,j)
=\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}
\frac{x_u^i(1-x_u)^j+x_u^j(1-x_u)^i}
{2(q-1)x_u(1-x_u)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The grid is invariant under `u <-> q-u`, so the two oriented sums are equal and

\[
\boxed{
G_q(i,j)
=\frac1{q-1}\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}
\left(\frac uq\right)^{i-1}
\left(1-\frac uq\right)^{j-1}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is a Riemann sum. Therefore

\[
G_q(i,j)\longrightarrow
\int_0^1x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\,dx
=B(i,j).
\tag{4.8}
\]

### 4.3 Uniform fixed-restriction generator convergence

Let `Q_N(A|B)` be the actual C058 rate of a specified split of `k=i+j` **named** visible labels. U037 gives the position-uniform estimate

\[
\boxed{
|Q_N(A|B)-B(i,j)|
\le
|G_{q_N}(i,j)-B(i,j)|
+\Delta_k(\rho_N)
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

No finite-`N` exchangeability assumption is needed. The affine geometry can distinguish different named four-tuples; the error bound is nevertheless uniform over all placements of the fixed labels because the proof used only pairwise independence and the universal moment bound.

### 4.4 Exact lumping despite hidden leaves

Fix a visible set `[k]`. Each current visible block lies inside exactly one current full block. The mark intensities in (4.3) depend only on global `N`, not on the hidden carrier size or composition, and each mark uses fresh ambient randomness. Consequently the rate of every visible split depends only on the visible labels in that block and is exactly `Q_N(A|B)` above.

A mark that splits only hidden labels may replace the hidden carrier by its descendant containing the visible labels, but the descendant receives fresh Poisson clocks with the same rates and the same proposal law. Exponential memorylessness therefore makes these invisible events irrelevant to the projected visible chain. This gives exact strong lumping onto a finite continuous-time Markov chain on partitions of `[k]`.

### 4.5 Finite-tree termination

Every specified pair in a nonsingleton full block is separated at rate exactly one by (4.5). Hence every nonsingleton block has a positive nontrivial-split hazard. A full `N`-leaf fragmentation has at most `N-1` nontrivial binary splits, and the waiting time to each next split is almost surely finite. Thus C058 defines a genuine finite random binary genealogy for every `N`.

### 4.6 Whole-path `J1` convergence

For fixed `k`, the state space of partitions of `[k]` is finite. Equation (4.9) gives entrywise convergence of every off-diagonal generator entry to the CTCS generator; summing the finitely many exits gives diagonal convergence as well.

Choose a common uniformization rate larger than all exit rates for sufficiently large `N` and for the limiting chain. The resulting discrete transition matrices converge entrywise. Couple the common Poisson clock and the transition choices; on every finite time interval the coupled paths agree with probability tending to one. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{N,058}^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }D([0,T],\mathcal P_k)
\text{ with Skorokhod }J_1
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

for every fixed `k,T`.

The joint law of any finite collection of restrictions is already determined by the largest restriction and has the same convergence. Standard product/projective tightness in the frozen summable metric therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{N,058}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

in R11's frozen summable projective finite-restriction path topology. Since branch times and restricted tree shapes are measurable from these finite partition paths, this is stronger than a root-split or tagged-fragment limit and supplies the required CTCS(infinity) genealogy limit.
