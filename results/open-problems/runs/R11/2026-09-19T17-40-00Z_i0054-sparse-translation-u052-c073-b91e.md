## 3. Construction C073

Reuse the arithmetic parameters from C072. Let

`d_N` = the least odd prime at least `max(3, ceil(log(N+2)))`,

and let `p_N` be the least prime satisfying

`p_N >= max(N,d_N+1)` and `p_N == 2 (mod d_N)`.

Dirichlet gives existence. Since `p_N-1 == 1 (mod d_N)`,

`gcd(d_N,p_N-1)=1`,

so

`phi_N(x)=x^(d_N)`

is a permutation of `F_{p_N}`. Also `d_N -> infinity` and `d_N=O(log N)=O(log p_N)`.

Put

`L_N = ceil(p_N^(3/4))`,

`T_N={0,...,L_N-1}`.

For every active clade, every threshold `u=1,...,p_N-1`, and every retained translation `t in T_N`, run an independent exponential proposal clock of rate

`c_N(u,t) = p_N / [2 L_N u(p_N-u)]`.

When `(u,t)` rings, color ambient leaf label `ell` red iff the standard representative of

`(t+ell)^(d_N) mod p_N`

lies in `{0,...,u-1}`. If both colors occur in the active clade, split by the two colors; otherwise the proposal is null.

The total proposal rate per active clade is finite and is exactly the same as in the full-orbit construction:

`sum_{u=1}^{p-1} sum_{t<L} p/[2 L u(p-u)] = H_{p-1}`.

Because `x -> x^d` is injective, for any retained `t` any two distinct ambient labels have distinct outputs, and some threshold lies strictly between those outputs. Hence every nonsingleton clade has positive rate to a genuine split. The finite tree therefore reaches singletons almost surely.

This model is highly non-exchangeable at finite `N`: only `L_N/p_N = p_N^(-1/4)+o(1)` of the additive translations are used.

## 4. U052 — sparse-translation discrepancy compiler

### Theorem U052

Fix a visible dimension `m`. Let `p -> infinity` through primes and let `d=d(p)<p` satisfy

1. `x -> x^d` is a permutation of `F_p`;
2. `d -> infinity` and eventually `d >= m+1`;
3. `d=O(log p)`.

Let `L=ceil(p^(3/4))`, and average only over translations `0<=t<L`, with the rate renormalization `p/L` above. Then the star discrepancy of every `m`-coordinate translated power orbit obeys

`D_{p,L}^{(m)} = O_m(p^(-1/4) (log p)^(m+2))`.

Consequently, for every specified unordered visible split `A|B`, `|A|=i`, `|B|=j`, `i+j=m`, its C073 rate satisfies

`Q_{p,L}(A|B) = B(i,j) + O_m(p^(-1/4)(log p)^(m+3))`,

where

`B(i,j)=int_0^1 x^(i-1)(1-x)^(j-1) dx = (i-1)!(j-1)!/(i+j-1)!`.

In particular all fixed visible generators converge to the CTCS generator.

### Proof, Step 1: every nonzero Fourier phase has degree at least two

Take distinct visible offsets `a_1,...,a_m` and a nonzero Fourier vector `h=(h_1,...,h_m)` with representatives `|h_j|<p/2`. The phase is

`P_h(t)=sum_{j=1}^m h_j(t+a_j)^d` over `F_p`.

Suppose `deg P_h <= 1`. The coefficients of `t^d,t^(d-1),...,t^2` vanish. Since `d>=m+1`, this includes the first `m` moment equations

`sum_j h_j a_j^r = 0`,  `r=0,...,m-1`.

The Vandermonde matrix on the distinct `a_j` is invertible in `F_p`, so every `h_j=0 (mod p)`, contradicting the chosen nonzero frequency. Hence

`2 <= deg P_h <= d < p`.

The same remains true after adding any linear completion twist `b t`: the degree-`>=2` leading part cannot be cancelled by a linear term.

### Step 2: incomplete exponential sums

For the standard additive character `e_p(z)=exp(2 pi i z/p)`, Weil's classical polynomial bound gives, uniformly in `b`,

`|sum_{t in F_p} e_p(P_h(t)+bt)| <= (d-1) sqrt(p)`.

Complete the interval indicator `1_{[0,L)}` by Fourier inversion. Its Fourier coefficients have `l^1` norm `O(p log p)`. Therefore

`|sum_{t=0}^{L-1} e_p(P_h(t))| = O(d sqrt(p) log p)`.

This is the only place where sparsifying the translation orbit costs anything. Weil bounds, interval completion, and the Erdos--Turan--Koksma inequality are classical tools; no novelty claim is made for those ingredients.

### Step 3: discrepancy

Apply the `m`-dimensional Erdos--Turan--Koksma inequality with Fourier cutoff comparable to `p`. The weighted frequency sum contributes only a product of harmonic sums, `O_m((log p)^m)`. Dividing the incomplete-sum estimate by `L` gives

`D_{p,L}^{(m)}
 <= C_m[1/p + d sqrt(p)(log p)^(m+1)/L]`.

For `d=O(log p)` and `L=ceil(p^(3/4))`,

`D_{p,L}^{(m)} = O_m(p^(-1/4)(log p)^(m+2)) -> 0`.

### Step 4: split rates

Let `C_{A,B}(u)` be the number of retained translations for which the threshold mask is red on every label of `A` and blue on every label of `B`. Star discrepancy controls this ordinary axis-aligned box (by the standard fixed-dimensional factor converting star boxes to general boxes), hence with `x=u/p`,

`C_{A,B}(u)/L = x^i(1-x)^j + O_m(D_{p,L}^{(m)})`,

and similarly with `A,B` reversed.

The specified unordered split rate is

`Q_{p,L}(A|B)
 = sum_{u=1}^{p-1} p/[2u(p-u)] * [C_{A,B}(u)/L + C_{B,A}(u)/L]`.

The volume term is the Riemann sum

`(1/(2p)) sum_u [x^(i-1)(1-x)^(j-1)+x^(j-1)(1-x)^(i-1)]`,

which converges to `B(i,j)` with fixed-`m` error `O_m(1/p)`.

For the discrepancy error use

`sum_{u=1}^{p-1} p/[u(p-u)] = 2H_{p-1}`.

Thus

`|Q_{p,L}(A|B)-B(i,j)|
 <= C_m H_{p-1} D_{p,L}^{(m)} + O_m(1/p)
 = O_m(p^(-1/4)(log p)^(m+3))`,

proving the generator claim.

## 5. Full finite-restriction process convergence

The preceding calculation is not merely a root-split calculation.

Fix `k`. In any full state, each visible block `V subset [k]` lies inside one unique active full clade. A proposal changes the visible partition precisely when its ambient mask splits `V`; the rate of that event depends only on the labels in `V`, not on the hidden labels sharing the full clade. Proposals that split hidden labels while remaining constant on `V` leave the visible state unchanged. After such an invisible split, the unique descendant containing `V` receives fresh clocks with exactly the same law. By memorylessness, the projection to `[k]` is therefore an exact finite-state CTMC (strong lumping for the visible transition rates).

For fixed `k`, eventually `d_N>=k+1`, and U052 gives entrywise convergence of that finite generator to the CTCS `[k]` generator. On a finite state space, common-clock uniformization implies that on every finite time interval `[0,T]`,

`|| Law(Pi_{N,073}^{[k]}|_[0,T]) - Law(Pi_CTCS^{[k]}|_[0,T]) ||_TV -> 0`.

Path total variation is stronger than weak convergence in Skorokhod `J_1`; hence

`Pi_{N,073}^{[k]} => Pi_CTCS^{[k]}` in `J_1` for every fixed `k`.

Under R11's frozen summable projective finite-restriction metric, convergence of every fixed restriction gives

`Pi_{N,073} => Pi_CTCS(infinity)`.

Thus C073 proves the required complete partition-valued genealogy limit, not a weaker one-dimensional or root marginal.
