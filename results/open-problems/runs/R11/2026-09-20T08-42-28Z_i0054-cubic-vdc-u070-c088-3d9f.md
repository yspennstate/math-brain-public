# 4. U070 — third-derivative compact-logit compiler through `b_N=o(N^3)`

Fix

\[
L=\log4,
\qquad
\sigma(y)=\frac{e^y}{1+e^y},
\qquad
\ell(x)=\log\frac{x}{1-x}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For each `N`, let `b_N` be a positive integer and define

\[
S_N(y)=
\begin{cases}
L\left\{\dfrac{b_Ny}{L}\right\},&0\le y<L,\\[2mm]
y,&y\notin[0,L),
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `{.}` is fractional part. Put

\[
\tau_N(x)=\sigma(S_N(\ell(x))).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Every nonsingleton clade uses fresh independent harmonic-grid clocks

\[
\lambda_{N,s}
=
\frac{N}{2s(N-s)}
=
\frac1{2N x_s(1-x_s)},
\qquad
x_s=\frac{s}{N},
\quad s=1,\dots,N-1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

When clock `s` rings, every leaf of the clade is independently colored red with probability `tau_N(x_s)` and blue otherwise. If both colors occur, split by color; otherwise the proposal is null. Descendant clocks/colors are fresh.

For a specified unordered visible split of sizes `i,j>=1`, its rate is

\[
q_N(i,j)=\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}F_{N;i,j}(s/N),
\tag{4.5}
\]

where

\[
F_{N;i,j}(x)=
\frac{
\tau_N(x)^i(1-\tau_N(x))^j
+
\tau_N(x)^j(1-\tau_N(x))^i
}{2x(1-x)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

## 4.1 Exact continuum generator for every `b_N`

Multiplication by an integer `b_N` modulo `L` preserves Lebesgue measure on `[0,L)`, and `S_N` is identity outside. Hence for every integrable `h`,

\[
\int_{\mathbb R} h(S_N(y))\,dy
=
\int_{\mathbb R} h(y)\,dy.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `dx=x(1-x)dy` under `x=sigma(y)`, (4.6) gives exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1F_{N;i,j}(x)\,dx
&=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}
\big[
\sigma(S_Ny)^i(1-\sigma(S_Ny))^j
+\sigma(S_Ny)^j(1-\sigma(S_Ny))^i
\big]\,dy\\
&=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}
\big[
\sigma(y)^i(1-\sigma(y))^j
+\sigma(y)^j(1-\sigma(y))^i
\big]\,dy\\
&=B(i,j)\\
&=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus only discrete-grid aliasing must be controlled.

## 4.2 Low complexity

As in U068/U069, on the active interval `x in [1/2,4/5)` the function `F_{N;i,j}` has `b_N` smooth branches with uniformly bounded branchwise variation, so

\[
\operatorname{Var}(F_{N;i,j})\le C_{i,j}(1+b_N).
\tag{4.9}
\]

The one-dimensional rectangle estimate gives

\[
|q_N(i,j)-B(i,j)|
\le C_{i,j}\frac{1+b_N}{N}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Therefore every subsequence with `b_N<=sqrt(N)` already has error `O(N^{-1/2})`.

## 4.3 High complexity: a third-derivative Weyl bound

Assume now

\[
b_N>\sqrt N,
\qquad
b_N=o(N^3).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Then `b_N -> infinity`. On the active interval define

\[
g(x)=\log\frac{x}{1-x}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
g'(x)=\frac1{x(1-x)},
\qquad
g''(x)=\frac{2x-1}{x^2(1-x)^2},
\tag{4.13}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 g'''(x)=
 \frac{2(3x^2-3x+1)}{x^3(1-x)^3}.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

Because

\[
3x^2-3x+1=3(x-1/2)^2+1/4,
\tag{4.15}
\]

there are constants `0<c<C<infinity` such that

\[
c\le g'''(x)\le C,
\qquad x\in[1/2,4/5].
\tag{4.16}
\]

For integers `m` and fixed `k!=0`, the Weyl phase is

\[
\phi_N(s)
=
\frac{ms}{N}
+
\frac{k b_N}{L}\,g(s/N).
\tag{4.17}
\]

Thus

\[
\phi_N'''(s)
=
\frac{k b_N}{L N^3}g'''(s/N),
\tag{4.18}
\]

and on every integer interval lying in the active region,

\[
|\phi_N'''(s)|\asymp_k
\lambda_N:=\frac{b_N}{N^3}.
\tag{4.19}
\]

The sign is constant.

### Lemma 4.1 — one differencing step

Let `S=sum_{n in I} exp(2 pi i f(n))` over an interval of `M=O(N)` consecutive integers, and suppose

\[
\lambda\le |f'''(x)|\le C\lambda
\tag{4.20}
\]

with constant sign on the corresponding real interval. If `H<=M/2`, then

\[
\frac{|S|^2}{N^2}
\le C'
\left[
H^{-1}
+\lambda^{1/2}H^{1/2}
+N^{-1}\lambda^{-1/2}H^{-1/2}
\right]
+O(H/N).
\tag{4.21}
\]

**Proof.** Van der Corput differencing gives

\[
|S|^2
\le C
\left[
\frac{N^2}{H}
+rac{N}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}|S_h|
\right]
+O(NH),
\tag{4.22}
\]

where

\[
S_h=\sum_n e^{2\pi i(f(n+h)-f(n))}
\tag{4.23}
\]

and endpoints are shortened by at most `H`. The difference phase has second derivative

\[
(f(\cdot+h)-f)''(x)
=
\int_0^h f'''(x+t)\,dt,
\tag{4.24}
\]

so its magnitude lies between constant multiples of `h lambda`, with constant sign. The classical second-derivative estimate therefore gives

\[
|S_h|
\le C
\left[N(h\lambda)^{1/2}+(h\lambda)^{-1/2}\right].
\tag{4.25}
\]

Using

\[
\sum_{h\le H}h^{1/2}=O(H^{3/2}),
\qquad
\sum_{h\le H}h^{-1/2}=O(H^{1/2}),
\tag{4.26}
\]

in (4.22) gives (4.21). `square`

Take

\[
H\asymp\lambda_N^{-1/3}
=
N b_N^{-1/3}.
\tag{4.27}
\]

Since `b_N>sqrt(N)`, we have `H/N=b_N^{-1/3}->0`; since `b_N=o(N^3)`, `H->infinity`. Equation (4.21) yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac1N
\left|
\sum_{s/N\in[1/2,4/5)}e^{2\pi i\phi_N(s)}
\right|
\le
C_{m,k}
\left[
\left(\frac{b_N}{N^3}\right)^{1/6}
+b_N^{-1/6}
+b_N^{-1/3}
\right]
=o(1).
}
\tag{4.28}
\]

The harmless endpoint term may be absorbed into the displayed bound.
