## 3. U044 — the sharp bounded circle-discrepancy criterion

### Theorem U044

Under (2.2), as `M -> infinity`, the following are equivalent:

1. for every fixed `i,j>=1`,
   \[
   q_M(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j);
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
2. the signed measures satisfy
   \[
   \mu_M\Rightarrow 0
   \quad\text{weakly on }[0,1];
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
3. the normalized prefix discrepancy satisfies
   \[
   d_M\longrightarrow0.
   \tag{3.3}
   \]

Thus, for uniformly bounded circle perturbations, U043's prefix discrepancy is not just sufficient: after quotienting out the orientation-antisymmetric invisible part, it is **necessary and sufficient** for CTCS split-rate convergence.

### Proof of `(1) <=> (2)`

Subtract (2.5) from (2.4). Put

\[
g_{M,i,j}(s/M):=
\frac{(s-1)_{i-1}}{M^{i-1}}
\frac{(M-s-1)_{j-1}}{M^{j-1}}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then exactly

\[
q_M(i,j)-B(i,j)
=
\frac{M^k}{(M)_k}
\int g_{M,i,j}(x)\,\mu_M(dx).
\tag{3.5}
\]

For fixed `i,j`, uniformly over the grid,

\[
g_{M,i,j}(x)
\longrightarrow
x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1},
\tag{3.6}
\]

and `M^k/(M)_k -> 1`. Also

\[
\|\mu_M\|_{TV}\le C.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Therefore (2) immediately implies (1).

Conversely assume (1). Taking `j=1`, `i=r+1`, equation (3.5) gives

\[
\int x^r\,\mu_M(dx)\longrightarrow0
\qquad (r=0,1,2,\ldots),
\tag{3.8}
\]

because the falling-factorial polynomial differs uniformly from `x^r` by `O_r(1/M)`. By linearity every polynomial integral tends to zero. The uniform total-variation bound (3.7), together with Weierstrass approximation of continuous functions by polynomials, yields `mu_M => 0`. Hence (1) and (2) are equivalent.

### Proof that `(3) => (1)`

This is exactly U043. If

\[
\Delta_M=\max_r\left|\sum_{s\le r}h_{M,s}\right|,
\]

then for fixed `k=i+j` and `M>=2k`,

\[
|q_M(i,j)-B(i,j)|
\le
(k-1)2^k\frac{\Delta_M}{M}
=(k-1)2^k d_M.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus `d_M->0` implies all CTCS split rates.

### Proof that `(2) => (3)`

Write the signed distribution function

\[
F_M(x)=\mu_M([0,x])
=\frac1M\sum_{1\le s\le Mx}h_{M,s}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

The jump size is at most `C/M`; its linear interpolation is `C`-Lipschitz. Suppose (3.3) fails. Then along a subsequence there are points `x_M` with `|F_M(x_M)|>=epsilon`. Passing to a further subsequence gives `x_M->x`.

Weak convergence `mu_M=>0` plus `|h_{M,s}|<=C` forces `F_M(x)->0` at each fixed `x`: approximate the indicator of `[0,x]` from above and below by continuous functions whose transition region has width `delta`; the possible signed mass of that region is at most `2C delta+O(C/M)`, and then let `delta downarrow0`. Finally the Lipschitz bound gives

\[
|F_M(x_M)-F_M(x)|
\le C|x_M-x|+O(C/M)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{3.11}
\]

contradicting `|F_M(x_M)|>=epsilon`. Thus `sup_x |F_M(x)|=d_M+O(C/M)->0`. This proves (2) => (3) and completes U044. QED.

Move #6 therefore does more than perturb a solvable model. It identifies the exact completion of the bounded circle-cut family seen by the CTCS split tests:

\boxed{
\text{CTCS rates}
\iff

}
\tag{3.12}
\]

## 4. Move #17 — the needed discrepancy sequence already exists in number theory

A persistent order-one perturbation with `d_M->0` is exactly a bounded sequence with strong cancellation of all partial sums. Character sums provide such sequences canonically.

Let `p` be an odd prime and let

\[
\chi_p(s)=\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)\in\{-1,+1\},
\qquad 1\le s<p,
\tag{4.1}
\]

be the Legendre symbol. For `p == 1 mod 4`,

\[
\chi_p(p-s)=\chi_p(-s)=\chi_p(-1)\chi_p(s)=\chi_p(s),
\tag{4.2}
\]

so the perturbation is already orientation-symmetric. The complete character sum vanishes,

\[
\sum_{s=1}^{p-1}\chi_p(s)=0,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and Pólya--Vinogradov gives

\[
\max_{r<p}
\left|\sum_{s=1}^{r}\chi_p(s)\right|
=O(\sqrt p\log p).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore the order-one perturbation

\[
h_{p,s}=\frac12\chi_p(s)
\tag{4.5}
\]

satisfies

\[
d_p=O\!\left(\frac{\log p}{\sqrt p}\right)\to0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The weights remain permanently separated from one:

\[
1+h_{p,s}\in\left\{\frac12,\frac32\right\}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

So convergence is due to arithmetic cancellation, not a small perturbation.

\[

}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At a type-`s` proposal:

1. choose a fresh uniform injection
   \[
   \phi:B\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z/p_N\mathbb Z;

   \[
   I_{R,s}=\{R,R+1,\ldots,R+s-1\}\pmod{p_N};

4. ignore a trivial proposal and evolve the two nonempty children independently by the same rule.
