## 2. Move #10 — derandomize C040's block selector completely

C040 solved the globally shared-paintbox simultaneous-jump obstruction by giving blocks fresh random modular hashes at every global mark. The random hash/channel mechanism was useful because it made multiple visible carriers simultaneously active with probability `O(1/N)`.

Here that randomness is unnecessary. On a partition of `[N]`, every block has a different minimum label. Thus the deterministic map

\[
B\longmapsto \min B
\]

is an exact collision-free channel assignment for every partition, with no random hash family at all.

This yields a simple deterministic round-robin schedule.

For `N>=3`, put

\[
\tau_N=N^{-3},
\qquad
x_{N,s}=\frac{s}{N},
\qquad
w_{N,s}=\frac{N}{2s(N-s)},
\qquad 1\le s\le N-1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

One **macrocycle** has physical duration `tau_N`. It contains `N(N-1)` microsteps, ordered lexicographically

\[
(s,c),\qquad s=1,\ldots,N-1,\quad c=1,\ldots,N,
\tag{2.2}
\]

with microduration

\[
\eta_N=\frac{\tau_N}{N(N-1)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

At microstep `(s,c)`:

1. there is at most one current block `B` with `min(B)=c`;
2. if no such nonsingleton block exists, do nothing;
3. otherwise toss one gate with probability
   \[
   a_{N,s}=\tau_N w_{N,s};
   \tag{2.4}
   \]
4. conditional on a successful gate, color the labels of `B` iid Bernoulli(`x_{N,s}`);
5. if both colors occur, replace `B` by its two nonempty color classes; otherwise do nothing.

Call the resulting finite hierarchy **C041: the minimum-label systematic-scan harmonic paintbox hierarchy**.

The gate is always legal. At the edge phases,

\[
w_{N,s}\le \frac{N}{2(N-1)},
\]

so

\[
a_{N,s}<N^{-2}.
\]

The finite mechanism has no random locus, no random channel, no random hash, no random environment order, and no independent block clocks. Its only randomness is the Bernoulli gate and the Bernoulli paintbox coloring of the unique scheduled block.

A key exact finite property is immediate:

\[
\boxed{\text{At most one block can change in one microstep.}}
\tag{2.5}
\]

## 3. The hidden-carrier minimum lemma

The delicate point is whether the deterministic channel `min(B)` survives restriction to `[k]` without remembering hidden labels.

Fix the canonical restriction `[k]`. Let `A` be a nonempty visible block and let `B` be its current full carrier. Then

\[
B\cap[k]=A.
\]

Every hidden label in `B\setminus A` is larger than `k`, while `min(A)<=k`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\min B=\min A.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

If a full split is invisible on `[k]`, all labels of `A` pass together into one child. That child still contains `min(A)`, and all of its hidden labels are still `>k`. Thus (3.1) remains true after every invisible event.

Consequently, before its next visible split, every visible block receives **exactly one opportunity at every phase `s` in every macrocycle**, at a deterministic channel determined solely by the visible block itself. Hidden carrier sizes and hidden genealogies do not alter its scan schedule.

This is stronger than a marginal lumping statement: the entire restricted process is determined by the visible partition plus the deterministic phase/channel clock.

## 4. Exact one-block harmonic generator

Fix a visible block of size

\[
b=i+j,
\]

and one specified unordered support `A | A^c` with `|A|=i`, `|A^c|=j`.

At phase `s`, conditional on the gate, that exact support is produced with probability

\[
p_{N,s}(i,j)
=x_{N,s}^i(1-x_{N,s})^j
+x_{N,s}^j(1-x_{N,s})^i.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since the block gets exactly one phase-`s` gate per macrocycle, the first-order rate contributed by that phase is `w_{N,s}p_{N,s}(i,j)`. Thus

\[
q_N(i,j)
=\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}w_{N,s}p_{N,s}(i,j).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Substituting the harmonic weight and pairing `s` with `N-s` gives the exact Riemann-sum identity

\[
\boxed{
q_N(i,j)
=
\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
\left(\frac{s}{N}\right)^{i-1}
\left(1-\frac{s}{N}\right)^{j-1}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore, for every fixed `i,j`,

\[
\boxed{
q_N(i,j)\longrightarrow
B(i,j)
=\int_0^1x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\,dx
=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

These are exactly the CTCS rates for a specified binary split.

There is also a uniform exit-rate bound. A size-`b` block changes at phase `s`, conditional on a gate, with probability

\[
1-x^b-(1-x)^b.
\]

For `x<=1/2`, this is at most `bx`; for `x>=1/2`, it is at most `b(1-x)`. Hence

\[
w_{N,s}\bigl(1-x^b-(1-x)^b\bigr)\le \frac bN
\]

up to the harmless factor `N/(N-1)` at the edge, and summing phases yields a uniform bound

\[
\boxed{r_N(b)\le b.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the total first-order visible exit rate on a partition of `[k]` is at most `k`, uniformly in `N`.

## 5. Move #19 — U020: a deterministic systematic-scan compiler

The preceding mechanism suggests a general finite-state statement.

### U020 (systematic-scan compiler)

Suppose a sequence of discrete fragmentation models has macrocycle lengths `tau_N -> 0` and, on every fixed restriction `[k]`, satisfies:

1. every current visible block receives one deterministic opportunity in each phase of every macrocycle before its next visible jump;
2. hidden evolution does not alter this visible opportunity schedule;
3. the sum of first-order visible split hazards over one macrocycle is uniformly `O_k(tau_N)`;
4. the macrocycle-averaged generator `Q_N^(k)` converges entrywise to a finite generator `Q^(k)`.

Then the entire `[k]` path converges in Skorokhod `J_1` to the continuous-time chain generated by `Q^(k)`.

### Proof

Let `C_N^(k)` be the exact one-macrocycle transition kernel. Because the total first-order visible hazard in a macrocycle is `O_k(tau_N)`, the probability of one visible jump is `O_k(tau_N)`. Conditional after one jump, the total remaining visible rate is still uniformly `O_k(1)`, because splitting preserves total visible mass `k`. Hence

\[
\Pr(\text{at least two visible jumps in one macrocycle})
=O_k(\tau_N^2).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a specified one-jump transition, the exact probability is its summed first-order macrocycle hazard plus an `O_k(tau_N^2)` correction. Therefore, rowwise,

\[
\boxed{
C_N^{(k)}
=I+\tau_N Q_N^{(k)}+O_k(\tau_N^2).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

On a bounded interval `[0,T]`, there are `O(T/tau_N)` macrocycles. A maximal coupling between the cycle-boundary chain and the Euler chain with kernel `I+tau_N Q_N^(k)` therefore fails with probability only

\[
O_{k,T}(\tau_N)\to0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `Q_N^(k)->Q^(k)`, coupling the two Euler chains over `O(T/tau_N)` steps costs

\[
O(T\|Q_N^{(k)}-Q^{(k)}\|)\to0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The Euler chain for fixed finite generator `Q^(k)` converges in `J_1` to the corresponding continuous-time chain. Finally, by (5.1), the probability that any macrocycle before time `T` contains two visible jumps is `O_{k,T}(tau_N)`. On the complementary event, move each unique intra-cycle jump to its macrocycle boundary. The state sequence is unchanged and every jump time moves by at most `tau_N`, so the `J_1` distance is at most `tau_N`.

This proves U020.
