## 3. The C029 model: deterministic finite urns, not Bernoulli paintboxes

For each integer `N>=3`, define a random hierarchy on the finite leaf set `[N]`.

Set

\[
\Delta_N=N^{-2},\qquad m_N=N-1.
\]

A deterministic rotor cycles through phases

\[
s=1,2,\ldots,N-1,1,2,\ldots .
\]

At phase `s`, take a deterministic urn/population with exactly `s` red sites and `N-s` blue sites. The identities of the red sites are irrelevant; for definiteness one may take `{1,...,s}`. Define

\[
w_{N,s}=\frac{N}{2s(N-s)},\qquad
c_{N,s}=(N-1)w_{N,s}
       =\frac{N(N-1)}{2s(N-s)},
\]

and the block activation probability

\[
a_{N,s}=\Delta_N c_{N,s}.
\]

At each raw generation and for each current nonsingleton tree block `C`, independently:

1. activate `C` with probability `a_{N,s}`;
2. if activated, choose a **uniform injection without replacement**
   \[
   \phi_C:C\hookrightarrow[N];
   \]
3. color each label of `C` by the color of its sampled urn site;
4. if both colors occur, replace `C` by the two color classes; if all sampled sites have one color, leave `C` unchanged;
5. after all current blocks have been processed, advance the global rotor phase by one.

One raw step has physical duration `Delta_N`.

The construction is genuinely finite: no infinite locus pool, no random population state, no iid Bernoulli paintbox, and no stochastic environment parameter. The environment is deterministic and the only randomization is finite uniform sampling without replacement plus block activations.

Activation is legitimate because

\[
c_{N,s}\le \frac N2
\]

at the edge phases, so

\[
0<a_{N,s}\le\frac{1}{2N}<1.
\]

The model is label exchangeable. It also has exact restriction consistency inside `[N]`: the restriction of a uniform injection to a subset of labels is again a uniform injection; a hidden split invisible on `[k]` leaves one carrier block for the visible labels; and the carrier's next independent injection has the same law. Thus for each fixed `k<=N`, the restricted visible process has the same law as the direct `k`-label rule driven by the same rotor.

## 5. Move #15 — deliberately break iid symmetry

Fix a visible block `C` of size

\[
b=i+j,\qquad i,j\ge1,
\]

and a specified **unordered** split support `A|C\setminus A` with `|A|=i`, `|C\setminus A|=j`.

At phase `s`, conditional on activation, the exact hypergeometric probability of this unordered support is

\[
\boxed{
h_{N,s}(i,j)
=
\frac{(s)_{\underline i}(N-s)_{\underline j}
      +(s)_{\underline j}(N-s)_{\underline i}}
     {(N)_{\underline{i+j}}}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The two terms are the two color orientations. When `i=j`, they remain distinct color assignments leading to the same unordered support, so the formula is still correct.

This intentionally breaks the conditional independence of the labels: at a fixed phase the colors are negatively dependent because the total number of red urn sites is fixed. That is a major finite-law difference from C028. Yet the exact without-replacement symmetry is stronger for the generator calculation.

The total probability that a size-`b` activated block splits visibly is

\[
p_{N,s}^{(b)}
=1-\frac{(s)_{\underline b}}{(N)_{\underline b}}
  -\frac{(N-s)_{\underline b}}{(N)_{\underline b}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `s<=N/2`, a union bound on the `b` sampled positions gives

\[
p_{N,s}^{(b)}\le \frac{bs}{N},
\]

and therefore

\[
c_{N,s}p_{N,s}^{(b)}
\le
\frac{N(N-1)}{2s(N-s)}\frac{bs}{N}
=
\frac{b(N-1)}{2(N-s)}
\le b.
\]

The case `s>=N/2` follows by symmetry. Hence

\[
\boxed{c_{N,s}p_{N,s}^{(b)}\le b}
\tag{5.3}
\]

uniformly in `N,s,b<=N`.

For a partition of `[k]`, summing (5.3) over its current nonsingleton blocks gives a uniform first-order visible exit rate at most `k`.

## 6. Move #21 — the hypergeometric/Vandermonde shape gives an exact CTCS generator

This is the main advance over C028.

The first-order rate of the specified support (5.1) at phase `s` is

\[
c_{N,s}h_{N,s}(i,j).
\]

Average over one deterministic rotor cycle. Since `c_{N,s}=(N-1)w_{N,s}`, the cycle-averaged rate is

\[
q_N(i,j)
=
\frac1{N-1}\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}c_{N,s}h_{N,s}(i,j)
=
\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}w_{N,s}h_{N,s}(i,j).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Consider the first orientation. The harmonic factor cancels the two boundary factors exactly:

\[
w_{N,s}
\frac{(s)_{\underline i}(N-s)_{\underline j}}
     {(N)_{\underline b}}
=
\frac{N}{2(N)_{\underline b}}
(s-1)_{\underline{i-1}}
(N-s-1)_{\underline{j-1}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The falling-factorial Vandermonde convolution is

\[
\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
(s-1)_{\underline{i-1}}
(N-s-1)_{\underline{j-1}}
=
(i-1)!(j-1)!\binom{N-1}{b-1}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Since

\[
(N)_{\underline b}
=N(N-1)_{\underline{b-1}}
=N(b-1)!\binom{N-1}{b-1},
\]

summing (6.2) over `s` gives exactly

\[
\frac12\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(b-1)!}
=
\frac12 B(i,j).
\]

The reversed orientation gives the same value. Therefore, for **every finite `N>=b`**, not merely asymptotically,

\[
\boxed{
q_N(i,j)=B(i,j).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the one-cycle averaged generator of C029 is already the exact CTCS generator on every fixed finite restriction. C028 had a Riemann-sum approximation to `B(i,j)`; C029 upgrades it to an exact finite identity through hypergeometric sampling and Vandermonde convolution.

## 7. Whole-path convergence on every fixed finite restriction

Fix `k`, and take `N>=k`. Let `S_k` be the finite state space of partitions of `[k]`. Let `K_{N,s}^{(k)}` be the exact one-raw-step transition matrix at rotor phase `s` for the restriction of the `[N]` model.

Define the generator-like matrix `A_{N,s}^{(k)}` by assigning rate

\[
c_{N,s}h_{N,s}(i,j)
\]

to every transition that splits exactly one current block along one specified unordered support, and choose the diagonal so rows sum to zero.

By (5.3), uniformly in `N,s`,

\[
\|A_{N,s}^{(k)}\|_{\mathrm{row},1}\le 2k.
\tag{7.1}
\]

In one raw step, the sum of visible split probabilities over all current blocks is at most `k Delta_N`. Hence the probability that two or more different current blocks split visibly in that raw step is `O_k(Delta_N^2)`. The same bound controls the difference between the exact probability of a specified single-block split and its first-order value. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_{N,s}^{(k)}
=I+\Delta_N A_{N,s}^{(k)}+E_{N,s}^{(k)},
\qquad
\|E_{N,s}^{(k)}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le C_k\Delta_N^2,
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

uniformly in `s,N`.

### One rotor cycle

One complete rotor cycle has duration

\[
\tau_N=(N-1)\Delta_N=\frac{N-1}{N^2}\sim N^{-1}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Let

\[
C_N^{(k)}
=K_{N,N-1}^{(k)}\cdots K_{N,1}^{(k)}.
\]

Expanding the product with (7.1)--(7.2), the sum of the raw remainder matrices has norm

\[
O_k(N\Delta_N^2)=O_k(N^{-3}),
\]

while all terms containing at least two first-order factors have total norm

\[
O_k((N\Delta_N)^2)=O_k(\tau_N^2)=O_k(N^{-2}).
\]

Thus

\[
C_N^{(k)}
=I+\Delta_N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}A_{N,s}^{(k)}+O_k(\tau_N^2).
\tag{7.4}
\]

But (6.4) holds for every specified support, so the cycle average is **exactly** the CTCS generator `Q_CTCS^(k)`:

\[
\frac1{N-1}\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}A_{N,s}^{(k)}
=Q_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{(k)}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
C_N^{(k)}
=I+\tau_NQ_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{(k)}+O_k(\tau_N^2).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This is stronger than ordinary generator convergence because the first-order cycle generator is not merely approaching the CTCS generator: it is exactly it.

At cycle boundaries, (7.6) is the standard finite-state Euler approximation to the continuous-time chain with generator `Q_CTCS^(k)`. Over `O(T/tau_N)` cycles, maximal-coupling error is `O_{k,T}(tau_N)->0`; equivalently, the geometric holding times scaled by `tau_N` converge to the CTCS exponential holding times and the jump kernels converge uniformly.

To restore raw intra-cycle jump times, note that the probability of two or more visible fragmentation jumps in one complete cycle is `O_k(tau_N^2)` by the uniform exit bound. Over `O(T/tau_N)` cycles, the probability that any cycle has two or more visible jumps is `O_{k,T}(tau_N)->0`. On the complementary event, moving a raw jump to the adjacent cycle boundary changes its time by at most `tau_N`. Therefore its Skorokhod `J_1` distance from the boundary chain tends to zero in probability.

Hence, for every fixed `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

### Full CTCS(infinity) limit

The restriction laws are exactly consistent. For each fixed `K`, the joint vector of restrictions `[2],...,[K]` is a deterministic function of the `[K]` partition path, so (7.7) for `k=K` gives their joint convergence. These consistent finite-dimensional path laws identify the unique exchangeable-partition limit CTCS(infinity) described by Aldous--Janson. In the summable projective path metric, the contribution of restrictions above `K` is at most `2^{-K+1}`, so first letting `N->infinity` at fixed `K` and then `K->infinity` gives

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Here `widehat Pi_N` denotes the growing finite-label hierarchy interpreted through its consistent finite restrictions; no claim is made that each finite `N` model is itself an infinite exchangeable partition process.
