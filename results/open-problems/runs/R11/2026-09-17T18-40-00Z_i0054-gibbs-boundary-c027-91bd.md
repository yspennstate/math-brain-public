## 3. Move #8 — discretize the beta mechanism completely

The previous C026 route reached the apparent shared-pedigree boundary `Delta_N ~ 1/N` but required another cancellation theorem to go further. Instead of adding such a lemma, this run changes the finite dynamics.

For every integer `N>=2`, put

\[
\alpha_N=\Delta_N=\frac1N,
\qquad
L_N=N^3.
\tag{3.1}
\]

There are `N` population sites and `L_N` persistent binary loci. A one-locus configuration is

\[
x=(x_1,\dots,x_N)\in\{0,1\}^N.
\]

Give each locus the exchangeable probability law

\[
\nu_{N,\alpha}(x)
=
\frac{(\alpha)_{|x|}(\alpha)_{N-|x|}}{(2\alpha)_N},
\qquad |x|=\sum_{v=1}^N x_v,
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `(a)_r=a(a+1)\cdots(a+r-1)` is the rising factorial. Initially the `L_N` loci are independent with law `nu_{N,alpha_N}`.

One raw population update is:

1. choose one **global** site `V_r` uniformly from `[N]`; this site is shared by all loci;
2. at each locus independently, erase its bit at `V_r` and redraw it from the exact Gibbs conditional under (3.2): if the other `N-1` sites contain `s` ones,
   \[
   \Pr\{X(V_r)=1\mid X([N]\setminus\{V_r\})\}
   =\frac{\alpha_N+s}{N-1+2\alpha_N};
   \tag{3.3}
   \]
3. after that population update, every current nonsingleton hierarchy block independently hashes uniformly into one of the `L_N` loci, injects its labels uniformly without replacement into the `N` population sites, and splits by their two observed colors if both colors occur;
4. one raw generation has physical duration `Delta_N`.

This is a finite, entirely discrete population/heat-bath model. No continuous beta variable is sampled inside the construction.

### Fixed-site reversibility and exact conditional independence across loci

Fix a site `v` and fix the other bits, containing `s` ones. Let `x^{(0)}` and `x^{(1)}` be the two configurations differing only at `v`. From (3.2),

\[
\frac{\nu(x^{(1)})}{\nu(x^{(0)})}
=
\frac{\alpha+s}{\alpha+N-1-s}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Therefore the conditional probability of a one at `v` is exactly (3.3), and the fixed-site heat-bath kernel `K_v` is reversible:

\[
\nu(x)K_v(x,y)=\nu(y)K_v(y,x).
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular every `K_v` separately preserves `nu`; averaging over the random site is not needed for invariance.

This matters because the same update site `V_r` is shared across the entire genome. Conditional on the entire site sequence `(V_0,V_1,...)`, each locus evolves independently through the **same deterministic sequence of invariant kernels** `K_{V_r}`. Since the loci start iid `nu`, at every raw time they remain iid `nu` conditional on the whole shared site sequence. Thus the global demographic environment can persist without generating cross-locus dependence in the one-time locus configuration array.

## 4. Exact split kernel

Consider a visible block of size `b=i+j` and a specified unordered support `A|A^c`, with `|A|=i`, `|A^c|=j`.

For an iid `nu_{N,alpha}` locus followed by a uniform injection of the `b` labels into distinct sites, exchangeability and the beta-Bernoulli predictive identity give

\[
p_{N,\alpha}(i,j)
=
\frac{2(\alpha)_i(\alpha)_j}{(2\alpha)_{i+j}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This identity is exact for every `N>=b`; the ambient `N-b` unobserved sites integrate out. It can also be obtained by summing the beta-binomial count law against the hypergeometric sampling probability.

Restriction consistency is exact. Restricting a uniform injection to visible labels remains a uniform injection. If hidden labels create a split invisible on `[k]`, there remains one visible carrier block, and its future hash and sample obey the same rule. The population/locus process is exogenous to the hierarchy, so hidden blocks cannot bias future visible locus states.

## 5. Move #29 — CTCS is the inward tangent at the compactified boundary

Adjoin the boundary point `alpha=0` to the concentration parameter. At `alpha=0`, (3.2) collapses onto the two monochromatic configurations, so no nontrivial split occurs. The first inward derivative is exactly the CTCS beta kernel.

For positive integers `i,j`, divide (4.1) by `alpha B(i,j)`. Exact cancellation gives

\[
\frac{p_{N,\alpha}(i,j)}{\alpha B(i,j)}
=
\frac{
\prod_{r=1}^{i-1}(1+\alpha/r)
\prod_{r=1}^{j-1}(1+\alpha/r)

\prod_{r=1}^{i+j-1}(1+2\alpha/r)

\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence, for each fixed `b=i+j`,

\[
p_{N,\alpha}(i,j)

\boxed{

\tag{5.3}
\]

So the CTCS split generator is literally the tangent generator of a finite Gibbs population at the `alpha=0` compactification point.

## 6. Move #13 — the collision count is the useful monovariant

The only reason finite persistent loci are not exactly fresh independent sources is that the same visible genealogy may query a locus more than once. Let `C_m` be the cumulative number of repeated-locus query pairs among the first `m` visible block queries. Then `C_m` only increases.

Fix a `[k]` restriction and a physical horizon `T`. In one raw generation the current visible block sizes sum to `k`, so there are at most `k` visible nonsingleton block queries. With

\[
M_N(T)=\left\lceil \frac{T}{\Delta_N}\right\rceil+1,
\]

there are at most

\[
Q_N\le kM_N(T)
\]

visible locus queries before time `T`. Independent uniform hashing into `L_N` loci gives

\[
\mathbb E C_{Q_N}
\le \frac{Q_N(Q_N-1)}{2L_N}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\Pr\{C_{Q_N}>0\}
\le
O_{k,T}\!\left(\frac1{L_N\Delta_N^2}\right).
\tag{6.2}

For C027,

\[
L_N\Delta_N^2=N^3\frac1{N^2}=N\to\infty,
\tag{6.3}

so the probability of any visible locus reuse on a bounded physical-time interval is `O_{k,T}(1/N)`.

On the event `C_{Q_N}=0`, every visible query uses a distinct locus. Conditional on the complete shared update-site sequence, those loci are iid with law `nu_{N,alpha_N}` by Section 3. Therefore the visible hierarchy is exactly the corresponding fresh-source chain on that event.
