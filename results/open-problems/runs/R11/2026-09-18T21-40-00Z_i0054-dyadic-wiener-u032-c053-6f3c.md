## 2. Move #20 — why bulk averaging alone is not enough

U030 and U031 showed that periodic and finite-Fourier quasiperiodic perturbations preserve the CTCS tangent. The tempting next guess was:

> if bounded positive cycle weights have Cesaro mean one, then the uniform-cycle compiler should automatically be CTCS.

The proof mechanism shows why that is not enough. Let
\[
\vartheta_m=1+a_m,\qquad a_m\ge0,
\]
and define
\[
A(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\vartheta_m}{m}z^m
=\log\frac1{1-z}+E(z),
\qquad
E(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{a_m}{m}z^m.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The weighted-permutation normalization and the law of a uniformly selected cycle depend on the **harmonic transform** `E`, not merely on the ordinary Cesaro average of `a_m`. In particular, zero density of the exceptional set does not by itself imply that `E(1)` is finite: the prime set has zero density but `sum_p 1/p` diverges. Thus the previous coefficient argument can fail even when the bulk Riemann sum sees asymptotically no exceptional indices.

This is not claimed as a counterexample to CTCS; it identifies the precise obstruction to the naive algorithm. We need a condition strong enough to control the coefficient normalization and weak enough to allow infinitely many boundary singularities.

The useful condition is a weighted Wiener condition.

## 3. U032 setup — positive sparse perturbations in a weighted Wiener algebra

Fix some
\[
\frac12<s<1.
\]
Let `(a_m)_{m>=1}` satisfy
\[
0\le a_m\le M,
\qquad
\boxed{
\sum_{m\ge1}a_m m^{s-1}<\infty.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Set `theta_m=1+a_m` and define `E` by (2.1).

For a power series `u(z)=sum_{j>=0}u_j z^j`, use the Beurling norm
\[
\|u\|_s=\sum_{j\ge0}|u_j|(1+j)^s.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Because
\[
1+j+k\le(1+j)(1+k),
\]
the weight `(1+j)^s` is submultiplicative, so this is a Banach algebra norm under Cauchy product. Equation (3.1) says exactly that `E` belongs to this weighted Wiener algebra:
\[
\|E\|_s
\le C_s\sum_{m\ge1}a_m m^{s-1}<\infty.
\tag{3.3}
\]
For `0<=t<=1`, put
\[
H_t(z)=e^{tE(z)}=\sum_{j\ge0}h_j(t)z^j.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Since `E` has nonnegative coefficients,
\[
h_j(t)\ge0.
\]
The Banach-algebra exponential estimate gives the uniform bound
\[
\sup_{0\le t\le1}\|H_t\|_s
\le e^{\|E\|_s}=:K_s<\infty.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Hence uniformly in `t`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j>J}h_j(t)\le K_s(1+J)^{-s},
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\le J}j h_j(t)
\le K_s J^{1-s}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
Also
\[
H_t(1)=e^{tE(1)},
\qquad
E(1)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{a_m}{m}<\infty.
\tag{3.8}
\]

## 5. Exact uniform-cycle pointing and the coefficient `b_n`

After sampling the weighted permutation, choose one of its cycles uniformly. Let `M_N` be its size. Define
\[
B_A(z)=\int_0^1 e^{tA(z)}dt
=\frac{F(z)-1}{A(z)}
=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nz^n.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Marking the selected cycle and using `1/K=int_0^1 t^{K-1}dt` gives the exact finite identity
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(M_N=m)=\frac{\vartheta_m}{m}\frac{b_{N-m}}{f_N}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
This exact identity is independently checked by the finite artifact described below.

Now
\[
e^{tA(z)}=(1-z)^{-t}H_t(z).
\]
Let
\[
c_r(t)=[z^r](1-z)^{-t}
=\frac{(t)_r}{r!},
\qquad c_0(t)=1.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Then
\[
b_n=\int_0^1\sum_{j=0}^{n}h_j(t)c_{n-j}(t)\,dt.
\tag{5.4}
\]

### 5.1 The range `0 <= t <= 1/2` is negligible

For `0<t<=1/2`, coefficient monotonicity in `t` gives
\[
c_r(t)\le c_r(1/2)\le C(r+1)^{-1/2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Split the convolution at `j=n/2`. For `j<=n/2`, (5.5) and `sum h_j(t)<=K_s` give `O(n^{-1/2})`. For `j>n/2`, use `c_{n-j}(t)<=1` and (3.6), giving `O(n^{-s})`. Since `s>1/2`,
\[
\int_0^{1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{n}h_j(t)c_{n-j}(t)dt
=O(n^{-1/2})=o(1/\log n).
\tag{5.6}
\]

### 5.2 Uniform convolution asymptotics for `1/2 <= t <= 1`

For `j<=n/2`, the exact ratio is
\[
\frac{c_{n-j}(t)}{c_n(t)}
=\prod_{r=n-j+1}^{n}\frac{r}{r-1+t}.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Uniformly for `1/2<=t<=1`,
\[
\left|\frac{c_{n-j}(t)}{c_n(t)}-1\right|
\le C\frac{j}{n}.
\tag{5.8}
\]
Using (3.7), the contribution of `j<=n/2` differs from
`c_n(t) sum_{j<=n/2}h_j(t)` by at most
\[
C c_n(t)n^{-1}\sum_{j\le n/2}j h_j(t)
\le C c_n(t)n^{-s}.
\]
For `j>n/2`, both the true tail and the omitted tail in `H_t(1)c_n(t)` are `O(n^{-s})` by (3.6) and `c_r(t)<=1`. Hence, uniformly on `1/2<=t<=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=0}^{n}h_j(t)c_{n-j}(t)
=H_t(1)c_n(t)+O(n^{-s}).
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Stirling's ratio estimate is uniform on this compact `t` interval:
\[
c_n(t)=\frac{n^{t-1}}{\Gamma(t)}\bigl(1+O(n^{-1})\bigr).
\tag{5.10}
\]
Combining (5.6), (5.9), and (5.10),
\[
b_n
=\int_{1/2}^{1}
\frac{e^{tE(1)}}{\Gamma(t)}n^{t-1}dt
+o(1/\log n).
\tag{5.11}
\]
Put `L=log n` and `u=(1-t)L`. The integral becomes
\[
\frac1L\int_0^{L/2}
\frac{e^{(1-u/L)E(1)}}{\Gamma(1-u/L)}e^{-u}du.
\]
Dominated convergence gives
\[
\boxed{
b_n\sim\frac{e^{E(1)}}{\log n}=\frac{C}{\log n}.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]
In particular, enlarging constants over finitely many small `n`,
\[
0\le b_n\le\frac{C_1}{\log(n+2)}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

## 6. U032 — the normalized binary tangent is CTCS

## 8. C053 — the dyadic cycle-weight hierarchy

\tag{8.1}

3. let `S` be the selected cycle support;

### Exact lumping of hidden labels
