## 3. Move #21 — the harmonic spectrum already exists in the Ewens/Feller coupling

The preceding C012 model activated channel `r` by hand with probability `delta/r`. The obvious weakness was that the harmonic spectrum had been selected because it decomposes the CTCS tagged Lévy measure.

The Ewens/Feller coupling supplies a natural deformation of exactly this spectrum. In a standard Chinese restaurant process with parameter `theta>0`, the indicator that customer `r+1` starts a new table can be realized as an independent Bernoulli variable with
\[
\boxed{p_r(\theta)=\frac{\theta}{\theta+r}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Equivalently, in the Feller coupling for Ewens(theta), these are the inhomogeneous Bernoulli successes that generate the cycle spacings.

If physical time per raw step is `delta=theta`, then
\[
\frac{r p_r(\theta)}{\delta}=\frac{r}{r+\theta}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Thus the exact Ewens table-birth mechanism is asymptotically the harmonic clock needed by C012, without imposing `delta/r` as the finite probability law.

## 4. Move #6 — deform C012 and prove a logarithmic-channel universality theorem

### Theorem U003 (logarithmic-channel robustness)

For each `N`, let `delta_N>0`, `R_N>=1`, and independent channel activations have probabilities `p_{N,r}`, `1<=r<=R_N`. Define
\[
\epsilon_N:=\max_{1\le r\le R_N}\left|\frac{r p_{N,r}}{\delta_N}-1\right|.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Assume
\[
R_N\to\infty,\qquad
\delta_N\to0,\qquad
\epsilon_N\to0,
\qquad
\delta_N(1+\epsilon_N)^2H_{R_N}^2\to0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every current nonsingleton block, independently at every raw generation:

1. choose a uniform anchor;
2. activate channels independently with probabilities `p_{N,r}`;
3. process active channels in any fixed order independent of the block labels (increasing `r` will be used for C013);
4. at active channel `r`, on the labels still sharing the anchor's survivor block, run a two-color Pólya urn with `r` initial **stay** balls and one initial **peel** ball; nonempty peel labels become a child and the stay labels remain with the anchor;
5. after all active channels, the final anchor block is the last child.

All children produced in one raw generation form one simultaneous refinement. Give a raw generation physical duration `delta_N`.

Then the entire accelerated exchangeable-partition process converges to CTCS(infinity) in the projective finite-restriction `J_1` topology.

### Proof

Fix a finite restriction `[k]`, a visible block `C` of size `b`, a particular split `A|A^c` with `|A|=i`, `|A^c|=j`, and condition on the **entire hidden history**. Suppose `C` lies inside a hidden current block of size `m>=b`.

For a single active channel `r`, the Pólya urn assigns any specified pattern of `s` stays and `d` peels probability
\[
\frac{(r)_s(1)_d}{(r+1)_{s+d}}
=rB(r+s,d+1).
\tag{4.3}
\]
If the anchor lies in `A`, the normalized ideal contribution to `A|A^c` is `B(r+i-1,j+1)`; if the anchor lies in `A^c` it is `B(r+j-1,i+1)`; if the anchor is hidden there are the two orientations `B(r+j,i+1)` and `B(r+i,j+1)`.

Let `q_R^0(m,b,i)` denote the coefficient obtained by replacing `r p_{N,r}/delta_N` by `1`. The previous C012 beta telescoping calculation is exact:
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_R^0(m,b,i)=B(i,j)
&-\frac{i}{m}B(i+R,j)
-\frac{j}{m}B(j+R,i)\\
&-\frac{m-b}{m}\{B(j+R+1,i)+B(i+R+1,j)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Hence, uniformly in the hidden size `m>=b`,
\[
0\le B(i,j)-q_R^0(m,b,i)\le\frac{2}{R+1}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For the actual channel probabilities, all beta summands are nonnegative and the multiplier `r p_{N,r}/delta_N` differs from `1` by at most `epsilon_N`. Therefore
\[
\left|q_N(m,b,i)-q_{R_N}^0(m,b,i)\right|
\le \epsilon_N q_{R_N}^0(m,b,i)
\le\epsilon_N B(i,j),
\tag{4.6}
\]
and thus
\[
\boxed{
\left|q_N(m,b,i)-B(i,j)\right|
\le \frac{2}{R_N+1}+\epsilon_N B(i,j)
\le\frac{2}{R_N+1}+\epsilon_N.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]
This is uniform in every hidden population size.

The total activation mass in one hidden block satisfies
\[
\mu_N:=\sum_{r\le R_N}p_{N,r}
\le \delta_N(1+\epsilon_N)H_{R_N}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Therefore the probability of at least two active channels in one block is at most `mu_N^2/2`. The difference between `p_{N,r}` and the probability that `r` is the **unique** active channel is bounded by the same quadratic scale. For a fixed `[k]` restriction, only finitely many visible blocks and visible split states occur, so simultaneous visible changes in different blocks are absorbed in the same `O_k(mu_N^2)` error.

Consequently, uniformly over the hidden history and visible state `rho`,
\[
\boxed{
\left\|P_N^{(k)}(\rho,\cdot)-
\bigl(I+\delta_N Q^{(k)}\bigr)(\rho,\cdot)\right\|_{TV}
\le C_k\left[
\delta_N\left(\frac{2}{R_N+1}+\epsilon_N\right)
+\delta_N^2(1+\epsilon_N)^2H_{R_N}^2
\right].
}
\tag{4.9}
\]
The diagonal/no-change term is controlled by summing the off-diagonal discrepancies.

On a fixed physical interval `[0,T]` there are at most `T/delta_N+1` raw steps. Iterated **conditional** maximal coupling (valid despite the hidden process not being visible-Markov) therefore gives
\[
\Pr(\text{coupling failure before }T)
\le C_{k,T}\left[
\frac{2}{R_N+1}+\epsilon_N+
\delta_N(1+\epsilon_N)^2H_{R_N}^2
\right]\to0.
\tag{4.10}
\]

The comparison Euler chain `I+delta_N Q^{(k)}` converges in finite-state `J_1` to the CTCS restriction: its holding time in state `rho` is geometric with parameter `delta_N lambda(rho)`, so multiplying by `delta_N` gives `Exp(lambda(rho))`, and conditional jump probabilities are exactly `Q^{(k)}(rho,rho')/lambda(rho)`. A `k`-label fragmentation makes at most `k-1` proper visible refinements. Hence
\[
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1.
\tag{4.11}
\]
For any finite `K`, the `[K]` restriction contains all coordinates `k<=K`; the tail of `d_proj` is deterministically at most `2^{-K}`. Thus (4.11) for every `K` yields
\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N\Longrightarrow\Pi_{CTCS(\infty)}.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]
This proves U003. `square`

This theorem is stronger than the previous exact-harmonic construction: the finite channel probabilities need only approximate the `1/r` profile in the precise normalized sense (4.1).

## 5. Move #30 — isolate the three errors and choose the natural Ewens scale

The proof above reduces the entire convergence problem to three scalar errors:
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{R_N},\qquad
\epsilon_N,\qquad
\delta_NH_{R_N}^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is the useful localization from move #30: truncation, clock-profile deformation, and multi-channel interaction are controlled independently.

For the Ewens/Feller clock, choose
\[
\boxed{R_N=N,\qquad \theta_N=\delta_N=\frac1N,\qquad
p_{N,r}=\frac{\theta_N}{\theta_N+r}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{r p_{N,r}}{\delta_N}=\frac{r}{r+\theta_N},
\qquad
\epsilon_N=\frac{\theta_N}{1+\theta_N}<\frac1N,
\tag{5.3}
\]
and
\[
\delta_NH_N^2=O\left(\frac{\log^2N}{N}\right)\to0.
\tag{5.4}
\]
So all hypotheses of U003 hold at the much more natural time scale `1/N`.
