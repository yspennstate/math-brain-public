## 3. C025 construction — every population generation has a common parent across the whole genome

C024 deliberately made shared-parent events sparse, with probability `rho_N=o(Delta_N)`. The next question was whether that sparsity is actually necessary.

Retain the C023 finite-genome scales

\[
m_N=\lceil N^{1/4}\rceil,\qquad
\alpha_N=\Delta_N=\frac1{m_N},\qquad
L_N=N,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and mutation probability

\[
\mu_N=\frac{\alpha_N}{N+2\alpha_N}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

There are `N` population sites and exactly `L_N=N` binary loci. Initially the loci are independent with the C023 one-locus law

\[
\nu_{N,\alpha}(x)
=
\frac{(\alpha)_{|x|}(\alpha)_{N-|x|}}{(2\alpha)_N},
\qquad x\in\{0,1\}^N.
\tag{3.3}
\]

One raw C025 generation is:

1. Choose one victim site `V_r` uniformly from `[N]`.
2. Choose one common parent site `P_r` uniformly from `[N]\setminus\{V_r\}`.
3. **At every locus** copy the allele at the same parent `P_r` into the same victim `V_r`, with independent mutation bits of probability `mu_N` across loci.
4. After that population update, every current nonsingleton tree block `B` independently hashes uniformly into the finite locus pool `{1,...,L_N}`. Inject the labels of `B` uniformly without replacement into the `N` population sites. If both allele colors appear, split `B` by those colors; if the sample is monochromatic, leave `B` unchanged.
5. One raw generation has physical duration `Delta_N`.

Thus **every** raw generation has the extremal shared-parent pedigree coupling. There is no `rho_N -> 0` mixture and no generation in which locus parents are independent. The population and finite genome persist after tree splits.

For comparison define `X_N` to be the query-after-update C023 baseline with the same scales, victims, mutations, hashes and tree-sampling maps, but with independent uniform parental sites at different loci. The ordinary C023 fixed-victim kernel preserves (3.3) exactly; conditional on the victim sequence, distinct loci remain independent with marginal law (3.3). Therefore moving the C023 query from immediately before to immediately after its ordinary update does not change its U007 proof.

The fresh-locus probability of one specified unordered `i|j` support, `i+j=b`, remains

\[
p_N(i,j)
=
\frac{2(\alpha_N)_i(\alpha_N)_j}{(2\alpha_N)_b}
=
\Delta_N B(i,j)+O_b(\Delta_N^2).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Moreover

\[
L_N\Delta_N^2
=
\frac{N}{\lceil N^{1/4}\rceil^2}
\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{3.5}
\]

so the finite-pool birthday error from C023/U007 vanishes. Consequently the comparison process `X_N` has the complete CTCS(infinity) limit.

The problem is now to show that the maximally correlated C025 population `Y_N` is nevertheless invisible to a fixed finite tree restriction at this observation scale.

## 4. Move #14 — quotient the coupled genomes by the site-permutation group

Couple `X_N` and C025 `Y_N` from exactly the same initial locus configurations. Use the same victim `V_r`, the same mutation bit at each locus, the same tree-block hashes, and the same without-replacement population samples. `X_N` uses independent per-locus parents; `Y_N` uses the common parent.

For locus `ell`, after `r` population updates define the discrepancy set

\[
D_{\ell,r}
:=\{v\in[N]:X_{N,r}^{(\ell)}(v)\ne Y_{N,r}^{(\ell)}(v)\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Only the common victim coordinate can change in either coupled genome during one population update. Hence, deterministically,

\[
\boxed{|D_{\ell,r+1}|\le |D_{\ell,r}|+1,\qquad |D_{\ell,0}|=0,}
\tag{4.2}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{|D_{\ell,r}|\le r.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is where the group action is useful. The site-permutation group `S_N` acts transitively on discrepancy sets of a fixed size. Because every tree query samples population sites uniformly without replacement, the probability that a size-`b` query sees a discrepancy depends only on the scalar orbit invariant `d=|D|`:

\[
P\{\text{sample hits }D\mid |D|=d\}
=1-\frac{\binom{N-d}{b}}{\binom Nb}
\le \frac{bd}{N}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the entire latent discrepancy geometry collapses to one integer per queried locus. No mixing theorem for the shared-pedigree population is needed.

## 5. Theorem U009 — sparse-site observation/path-coupling compiler

The preceding idea is useful beyond this one construction.

### Theorem U009

Let `X_N` and `Y_N` be two latent finite-population hierarchy models on `N` population sites, observed in raw time mesh `Delta_N`. Couple them so that:

1. their visible tree states and all query randomness agree until the first visible disagreement;
2. at every latent source/locus, after `r` raw population updates the two latent configurations differ on at most `r` population sites;
3. on a `[k]` visible restriction, one raw hierarchy generation samples in total at most `k` population sites across all current blocks, each block sample being uniform without replacement from `[N]` conditional on the past.

Then for every fixed physical horizon `T`, with

\[
M_N(T)=\left\lceil\frac{T}{\Delta_N}\right\rceil+1,
\tag{5.1}
\]

there is a coupling of the restricted hierarchy paths satisfying

\[
\boxed{
P\{X_N^{[k]}\ne Y_N^{[k]}\text{ somewhere on }[0,T]\}
\le
\frac{k\,M_N(T)(M_N(T)+1)}{2N}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular, if

\[
\boxed{N\Delta_N^2\to\infty,}
\tag{5.3}
\]

then the total-variation distance between their `[k]` path laws on every bounded interval tends to zero. Therefore any finite-restriction `J_1` limit of `X_N` transfers to `Y_N`; if the limiting restrictions are projectively consistent, the projective finite-restriction limit transfers as well.

### Proof

Work up to the first visible disagreement. At raw step `r`, the current visible blocks are identical in the two processes. Suppose block `B` has `b` visible labels and hashes to locus `ell`. By assumption the coupled latent configurations at that locus disagree on at most `r` population sites. Equation (4.4) therefore gives

\[
P\{B\text{ sees a discrepancy at step }r\mid\mathcal F_{r-1}\}
\le \frac{br}{N}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

If its sampled population sites avoid `D_{ell,r}`, then the entire sampled color vector agrees, so that block makes exactly the same split or non-split move in both hierarchies. Summing (5.4) over all current visible blocks uses

\[
\sum_B |B|=k
\]

and yields

\[
P\{\text{first visible disagreement at step }r\mid\mathcal F_{r-1}\}
\le \frac{kr}{N}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

A union bound over `r=1,...,M_N(T)` gives (5.2). Since `M_N(T)=O_T(Delta_N^{-1})`, the right side is

\[
O_{k,T}\!\left(\frac1{N\Delta_N^2}\right),
\]

which tends to zero under (5.3). This is convergence in total variation of the coupled restricted path laws on each bounded interval, hence transfers any `J_1` limit. The projective conclusion follows by first fixing a sufficiently large finite restriction so that the deterministic metric tail is small, applying the finite-restriction coupling there, and then sending that truncation size upward. `QED`.

## 6. Apply U009 to the maximally shared-parent model

For the C023/C025 coupling above, (4.2) proves hypothesis 2 of U009 exactly. Hypothesis 3 holds because a partition of `[k]` has current blocks whose sizes sum to exactly `k`; each block samples its own labels at distinct population sites, and the query maps are independent of the current latent discrepancy sets conditional on the past.

For the chosen mesh,

\[
N\Delta_N^2
=
\frac{N}{\lceil N^{1/4}\rceil^2}
\sim N^{1/2}\to\infty.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Therefore, for every fixed `k,T`,

\[
\left\|
\mathcal L\bigl(\widehat\Pi_{N,\mathrm{C025}}^{[k]}|_{[0,T]}\bigr)
-
\mathcal L\bigl(\widehat\Pi_{N,\mathrm{C023+}}^{[k]}|_{[0,T]}\bigr)
\right\|_{\rm TV}
=O_{k,T}(N^{-1/2}).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The comparison process C023+ converges by the exact stationary split law (3.4), the finite-pool condition (3.5), and the U007 Euler-chain argument. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_{N,\mathrm{C025}}^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

for every fixed `k`, and consequently

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_{N,\mathrm{C025}}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

in the retained projective finite-restriction topology.

This is a whole-genealogy convergence proof. It does not infer CTCS from a stationary split marginal, and it does not require shared-parent events to become rare: **the common-parent coupling occurs in every single population generation**.

## 7. Move #20 — the proof has a sharp observable-density obstruction

The same calculation identifies exactly where this route stops working. U009 needs

\[
N\Delta_N^2\to\infty,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\Delta_N\gg N^{-1/2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

At the critical observation mesh `Delta_Nasymp N^{-1/2}`, the deterministic path-coupling bound (5.2) is only order one. For a faster observation mesh `Delta_N=o(N^{-1/2})`, the bound becomes useless.

This is **not** a proof that the shared-parent model itself fails at or below the `N^{-1/2}` scale. It is the exact obstruction to this algorithm: after `O(Delta_N^{-1})` one-site latent perturbations, a finite query is no longer guaranteed to miss all accumulated discrepancy sites often enough for the union bound to vanish. Any faster-mesh theorem would need contraction of discrepancy sets, cancellation, recombination-specific mixing, or a different coupling rather than another application of U009.

## 8. Move #28 — the theorem is a typical-observation statement

The latent populations can be quite different without the visible tree noticing. At physical time at most `T`, equation (4.3) permits as many as

\[
O(T/\Delta_N)=O(TN^{1/4})
\]

discrepant sites at a queried locus. There is no uniform all-site closeness theorem. Instead, a fixed `b`-label tree block sees a uniformly sampled `b`-tuple, so at one query

\[
P(\text{visible discrepancy})
=O_b(N^{-3/4}).
\]

There are only `O_T(N^{1/4})` raw observations per unit physical time. Their accumulated visible discrepancy probability is therefore `O(N^{-1/2})`.

So the mechanism is genuinely “typical rather than for all”: the genomes need not couple globally; the hierarchy only requires that the *sites actually sampled by a fixed finite tree restriction* typically avoid the sparse latent disagreement cloud.
