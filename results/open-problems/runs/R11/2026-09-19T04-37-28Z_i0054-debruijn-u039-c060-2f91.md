## 2. Move #8 — discretize both the environment and physical time

The preceding harmonic-grid constructions still generated independent Bernoulli colors label by label. Here the Bernoulli paintbox is replaced by one finite deterministic cyclic word and a single random phase.

Let `q>=2`, `K>=2`, and let

\[
w=(w_0,\ldots,w_{L-1}),\qquad L=q^K,
\]

be any `q`-ary de Bruijn cycle of order `K`, with symbols in `{0,1,...,q-1}`. For threshold type `u in {1,...,q-1}`, put

\[
x_u=\frac uq,
\qquad
a_{q,u}=\frac{1}{2(q-1)x_u(1-x_u)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The total mark intensity is

\[
A_q:=\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}a_{q,u}
=\frac{q}{q-1}H_{q-1}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Choose the discrete physical mesh

\[
\delta_q=q^{-3}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For every `q>=2`, `delta_q A_q<1`, and

\[
\delta_qA_q^2
=O\!\left(\frac{(\log q)^2}{q^3}\right)\to0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

At each raw microstep of duration `delta_q`, each current nonsingleton block acts independently as follows.

1. Choose type `u` with probability `delta_q a_{q,u}`; with the remaining probability, idle.
2. Conditional on choosing `u`, choose a single uniform cyclic shift `S in Z/LZ`.
3. Give ambient leaf label `ell` the `q`-symbol
   \[
   Y_\ell=w_{S+\ell-1\pmod L}.
   \tag{2.5}
   \]
4. Give the leaf binary color `1{Y_ell<u}` and split the current block into the two colors if both are present.

Fresh shifts and type choices are used for distinct blocks and distinct microsteps. There is **no iid per-leaf random field** in one proposal; the entire coloring is read from one finite deterministic universal cycle after one random phase. Physical time is also discrete before the limit.

## 3. The universal-cycle lemma

### Lemma 3.1 — exact local product law

If `S` is uniform on the cyclic positions of a `q`-ary de Bruijn cycle of order `K`, then for every `1<=r<=K`,

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_r)
\sim \operatorname{Unif}\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^{r}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, every `r`-word occurs exactly `q^{K-r}` times among the `q^K` cyclic starting positions.

**Proof.** Each length-`K` word occurs exactly once. Fix an `r`-word. It has exactly `q^{K-r}` extensions to a length-`K` word. Those extensions occur at distinct cyclic starting positions, and precisely those positions have the chosen `r`-word as prefix. QED.

Consequently, for every threshold `u` and every `r<=K`,

\[
\left(1\{Y_1<u\},\ldots,1\{Y_r<u\}\right)
\stackrel d=
\operatorname{Bernoulli}(u/q)^{\otimes r}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is exact, not asymptotic.

## 4. Exact visible generator

Fix a visible block contained in `[k]` and a specified unordered split `A|B` of that block with

\[
|A|=i,\qquad |B|=j,\qquad i,j\ge1.
\]

Assume `K>=k`. By (3.2), at a type-`u` proposal the binary colors of all visible labels are exactly iid Bernoulli(`x_u`). Thus the probability of the specified unordered split is

\[
x_u^i(1-x_u)^j+x_u^j(1-x_u)^i.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Dividing the one-step transition probability by `delta_q`, its single-block rate is therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_q(i,j)
&=\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}a_{q,u}
  \{x_u^i(1-x_u)^j+x_u^j(1-x_u)^i\}\\
&=\frac1{q-1}\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}
\left(\frac uq\right)^{i-1}
\left(1-\frac uq\right)^{j-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The second equality pairs `u` with `q-u`. In particular,

\[
\boxed{G_q(1,1)=1}
\tag{4.3}
\]

exactly. Since (4.2) is an ordinary Riemann sum,

\[
\boxed{
G_q(i,j)\longrightarrow
\int_0^1x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\,dx
=B(i,j).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

These are exactly the CTCS rates for a specified binary split.

## 5. U039 — de Bruijn cyclic-shift CTCS compiler

### Theorem U039

Let `q_N,K_N -> infinity`. Consequently the consistent family converges in the frozen summable projective finite-restriction path topology to CTCS(infinity).

### Proof

Fix `k` and a finite physical horizon `T`. For all sufficiently large `N`, `K_N>=k`. From that point onward Lemma 3.1 makes the entire visible proposal vector on `[k]` **exactly** the iid harmonic-grid paintbox proposal, hence every one-block off-diagonal transition probability has expansion

\[
P_N(\pi,\pi')
=\delta_N Q_{q_N}^{[k]}(\pi,\pi')+O_k(\delta_N^2A_{q_N}^2),
\tag{5.1}
\]

where the first-order generator entries are sums of rates (4.2).

At most `k` visible blocks are present. Distinct blocks use independent choices in one microstep. Hence the probability that at least two visible blocks experience proposal events in the same microstep is

\[
O_k(\delta_N^2A_{q_N}^2).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Over `O(T/delta_N)` microsteps, the total probability of any such simultaneous visible event is

\[
O_k(T\delta_NA_{q_N}^2)\to0
\tag{5.3}
\]

by (2.4).

By (4.4), every off-diagonal entry of `Q_{q_N}^{[k]}` converges to the corresponding CTCS generator entry; on the finite partition state space the diagonals converge by row summation. The usual finite-state Euler-chain/uniformization coupling now gives convergence of the entire cadlag path on `[0,T]` in Skorokhod `J1`. Since `T` is arbitrary,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{N,060}^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

for every fixed `k`.

Hidden-only full-tree splits do not disturb this restriction: an invisible binary split leaves exactly one daughter carrying the visible labels, and future proposals for that daughter use the same global `q_N,K_N,delta_N` and fresh phase/type randomness. Finally, use the same frozen summable projective path metric as the prior R11 runs. Tightness of every finite restriction plus the summable product/union-bound argument gives projective tightness; any subsequential limit has every finite restriction equal to the consistent CTCS family. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{N,060}

\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}.

\tag{5.5}
\]

QED.

## 7. Move #21 and move #29 — universal cycles as a finite compactification of Bernoulli noise

The outside combinatorial language clarifies the construction. A uniformly rooted de Bruijn cycle is a finite periodic symbolic system whose rooted `r`-window law is **exactly** the uniform product law for every `r<=K`. Thus, as `K->infinity`, the rooted periodic systems converge locally to the full Bernoulli shift even though each finite environment has only `q^K` deterministic cyclic states.

Thresholding symbols at `u` transports that exact local convergence to Bernoulli(`u/q`) paintboxes. Sending `q->infinity` then turns the harmonic grid into the continuous beta integral. In this sense the two limits are separated cleanly:

\[
\text{finite universal cycle}
\xrightarrow[K\to\infty]{\text{local windows}}
\text{Bernoulli paintbox}
\xrightarrow[q\to\infty]{\text{harmonic grid}}
\text{CTCS beta generator}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

That is the useful "point at infinity" here: the Bernoulli shift is the local weak boundary of finite periodic universal cycles. No stochastic mixing estimate is needed.
