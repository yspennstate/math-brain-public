## 6. C061 really is a finite `N`-leaf tree

The condition `L>=N` in (2.1) is needed here and is not cosmetic.

A de Bruijn cycle has minimal cyclic period `L`. If two distinct positions `a,b` in `{1,...,N}` had

\[
w_{s+a-1}=w_{s+b-1}\quad\text{for every }s,
\]

then the cyclic word would be invariant under a nonzero shift and would have period smaller than `L`, contradicting the de Bruijn property.

Therefore for every pair `a!=b` there exists a phase `s` at which their symbols differ. If their two symbols are `r<r'`, any threshold

\[
r<u\le r'
\]

separates their binary colors. By the CRT property, that exact `(s,u)` pair occurs once in every supercycle. As long as `a,b` remain in the same current block, a successful gate at that microstep separates them. The gate probability is a fixed positive number `Delta c_u` for that finite `N`, independently repeated each supercycle. Hence the probability that this pair is never separated is zero.

There are only finitely many leaf pairs. Therefore with probability one every pair is eventually separated, and the fragmentation terminates in `N` singletons. C061 is a genuine random binary tree with edge lengths induced by the physical microstep clock.

\begin{aligned}
G_q(i,j)
&=\frac1M\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}c_u
\#\{s:\text{phase }s\text{ produces }A|B\}\\
&=\frac1{q-1}\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}c_u
\{x_u^i(1-x_u)^j+x_u^j(1-x_u)^i\}.

\tag{7.1}

\boxed{G_q(1,1)=1}

For one phase/type pair `(s,u)`, let `A_{s,u}^{(k)}` be the generator-like matrix containing the one-visible-block transitions at rate `c_u`; its off-diagonal entries are either `c_u` or zero depending on the deterministic coloring, with the diagonal chosen so rows sum to zero.

The exact one-microstep kernel satisfies

\[
K_{s,u}^{(k)}
=I+\Delta A_{s,u}^{(k)}+E_{s,u}^{(k)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

At most `k` visible blocks are present. Multiple visible block changes in one microstep require at least two independent gates, so

\[
\|E_{s,u}^{(k)}\|_{\rm row,1}
\le C_k\Delta^2c_u^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The crude phasewise norm is

\[
\|A_{s,u}^{(k)}\|_{\rm row,1}\le 2kc_u.
\tag{8.3}
\]

The exact threshold sums are

\[
\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}c_u=qH_{q-1},
\tag{8.4}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}c_u^2=O(q^2).
\tag{8.5}
\]

Therefore over one supercycle,

\[
S_N:=\Delta\sum_{s,u}\|A_{s,u}^{(k)}\|_{\rm row,1}
=O_k(\tau\log q).
\tag{8.6}
\]

All terms in the product expansion containing at least two first-order factors have total norm

\[
O_k(S_N^2)=O_k(\tau^2\log^2q).
\tag{8.7}
\]

The accumulated one-step error is smaller:

\[
\sum_{s,u}\|E_{s,u}^{(k)}\|
\le C_k\Delta^2L\sum_uc_u^2
=O_k(\tau^2/L).
\tag{8.8}
\]

Let `C_N^{(k)}` be the exact one-supercycle kernel and

\[
Q_N^{(k)}=\frac1M\sum_{s,u}A_{s,u}^{(k)}.
\]

Equations (8.1)--(8.8) give

\[
\boxed{
C_N^{(k)}
=I+\tau Q_N^{(k)}
+O_k(\tau^2\log^2q).
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

By the exact calculation (7.2), every specified split rate in `Q_N^{(k)}` tends to its CTCS beta rate. Because `P_k` is finite,

\[
\|Q_N^{(k)}-Q_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}\|\to0.
\tag{8.10}
\]

Over `O(T/tau)` supercycles, the coupling error between the exact supercycle chain and its Euler chain is at most

\[
O_{k,T}(\tau\log^2q)\to0,
\tag{8.11}
\]

because `tau=M^{-2}` and `M=(q-1)q^K` grows much faster than `log q`. The Euler chain for `Q_N^{(k)}` then couples to the Euler chain for `Q_CTCS^{(k)}` with total discrepancy tending to zero by (8.10), and the latter converges to the finite-state CTCS continuous-time chain.

To restore the actual within-supercycle jump times, note that the probability of two or more visible jumps in one supercycle is bounded by the same second-order scale `O_k(tau^2 log^2 q)`. Hence

\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1

\tag{8.12}
\]

for every fixed `k`.
