## 2. The idea before code

C041 removed random block selection by assigning each block the collision-free channel `min B`. The stronger question is: **how much channel collision can a deterministic shared-noise schedule tolerate?**

The answer in this natural family is sharp. Let

\[
h_N:[N]\to \mathcal C_N
\]

be an arbitrary deterministic channel map, and assign a current block `B` to channel `h_N(min B)`. Blocks in the same channel share the *same* Bernoulli gate at a phase, but their leaf colors are independent. The exact threshold is local injectivity:

\[
\tag{LI}
\forall k\ge 1\ \exists N_0(k)\ \forall N\ge N_0(k):
\quad h_N|_{[k]}\text{ is injective}.
\]

This gives both a general theorem and a genuinely new finite model. It is stronger than manufacturing another collision-free scan.

## 3. The shared-gate family `S(h_N)`

Fix

\[
\tau_N=N^{-3},\qquad x_s=\frac{s}{N},\qquad
w_{N,s}=\frac{N}{2s(N-s)},\qquad 1\le s<N.
\]

One macrocycle has physical duration `tau_N`. Within it scan phases `s=1,...,N-1` and channels `c in C_N` in any fixed deterministic order. At microstep `(s,c)`:

1. toss one gate `G_{s,c}` with
   \[
   \Pr(G_{s,c}=1)=\tau_N w_{N,s};
   \]
   gates at different microsteps are independent;
2. if the gate is zero, do nothing;
3. if it is one, then for every current block `B` with `h_N(min B)=c`, give each leaf of `B` a fresh independent Bernoulli(`x_s`) color, independently across leaves and disjoint blocks;
4. replace every such `B` by its two nonempty color classes when both colors occur; monochromatic blocks stay unchanged.

The gate is legal because `w_{N,s} <= N/[2(N-1)] < 1` and `tau_N=N^{-3}`.

The finite process almost surely terminates at singletons: every nonsingleton block has, in every macrocycle, a positive chance to be selected at at least one phase and receive a nonmonochromatic coloring; after each successful split the number of blocks strictly increases, and there can be at most `N-1` successful splits.

## 4. Hidden-label stability

Let `A` be a visible block of the restriction to `[k]`, and let `B` be its full carrier in `[N]`. Every hidden label is larger than every visible label, hence

\[
\boxed{\min B=\min A.}
\]

If a full split is invisible on `A`, all labels of `A` pass to one child, which again has minimum `min A`. Thus hidden evolution never changes the visible channel `h_N(min A)`. Future gates and colors are fresh. Therefore every fixed visible restriction is itself Markov, with no unobserved carrier-size variable.

## 5. Exact one-block rates

For a specified binary support `i|j`, a block exposed to phase `s` realizes that support with probability

\[
p_{N,s}(i,j)
=x_s^i(1-x_s)^j+x_s^j(1-x_s)^i.
\]

Each visible block receives one gate opportunity at each phase per macrocycle, regardless of the channel map. Thus its first-order macrocycle rate is

\[
q_N(i,j)=\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}w_{N,s}p_{N,s}(i,j).
\]

Using `w_{N,s}=N/[2s(N-s)]` and the symmetry `s <-> N-s`,

\[
\boxed{
q_N(i,j)
=\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
\left(\frac{s}{N}\right)^{i-1}
\left(1-\frac{s}{N}\right)^{j-1}.
}
\]

Hence, for fixed `i,j`,

\[
q_N(i,j)\longrightarrow
\int_0^1x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\,dx
=B(i,j)
=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}.
\]

These are exactly the CTCS binary split rates on a specified support.

## 6. U021 — sharp channel-collision criterion

**Theorem U021.** For the shared-gate family `S(h_N)` above, started from the one-block partition, the frozen projective `J_1` limit is CTCS(infinity) **if and only if** `(LI)` holds.

### 6.1 Sufficiency

Fix `k`. Under `(LI)`, for all sufficiently large `N`, the values `h_N(1),...,h_N(k)` are distinct. Every visible block of `[k]` has a distinct minimum, hence a distinct channel. By the hidden-label lemma, this remains true through all invisible full-tree evolution.

Consequently no single microstep can change two visible blocks. Gates governing distinct visible blocks are independent, and each block has the exact one-block phase law in Section 5. On the finite state space of partitions of `[k]`, the macrocycle kernel therefore has

\[
C_N^{(k)}=I+\tau_NQ_N^{(k)}+R_N^{(k)},
\qquad
\|R_N^{(k)}\|_{\mathrm{row},1}=O_k(\tau_N^2),
\]

because two or more visible jumps in one macrocycle require at least two successful visible gates; the total first-order visible exit rate is uniformly bounded for fixed `k`. Section 5 gives entrywise

\[
Q_N^{(k)}\longrightarrow Q_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}.
\]

Over physical time `T`, there are at most `T/tau_N+1` macrocycles, so the accumulated probability of a two-jump macrocycle is `O_{k,T}(tau_N)->0`. Couple a macrocycle with at most one jump to the continuous-time finite-state chain with generator `Q_N^{(k)}`. Move the unique intra-cycle jump to the macrocycle boundary; this changes its time by at most `tau_N`. Standard finite-state uniformization / maximal coupling then gives

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{N}^{[k]}\Longrightarrow \Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in Skorokhod }J_1.
}
\]

This holds for every fixed `k`; consistency of restriction maps gives convergence in the frozen projective finite-restriction topology:

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_N\Longrightarrow \Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}.
}
\]

### 6.2 Necessity: the exact shared-shock obstruction

Assume `(LI)` fails. Then there exist fixed `a<b` and an infinite subsequence `N_r` such that

\[
h_{N_r}(a)=h_{N_r}(b).
\]

Take `k=b+2` and the visible partition containing the two nonsingleton blocks

\[
A=\{a,b+1\},\qquad C=\{b,b+2\},
\]

with all remaining visible labels singleton. The two pair blocks have minima `a,b`, hence share a channel along the subsequence.

At phase `s`, one two-label block splits with conditional probability

\[
r_{N,s}=2x_s(1-x_s).
\]

Because the gate is shared while the colors on the two disjoint pairs are independent, the first-order **direct simultaneous two-block jump rate** is

\[
d_N=\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}w_{N,s}r_{N,s}^2.
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_N
&=\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
\frac{N}{2s(N-s)}
\frac{4s^2(N-s)^2}{N^4}\\
&=\frac{2}{N^3}\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}s(N-s)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{N^2-1}{3N^2}}\longrightarrow\frac13.
\end{aligned}
\]

CTCS has independent continuous clocks on distinct blocks, so its generator has **zero** mass on a transition in which both pairs fragment in one jump.

This is an obstruction even from the one-block initial state, not only from an artificially chosen starting partition. The displayed two-pair state is reachable in CTCS by a finite sequence of specified binary splits, each having strictly positive beta rate. Hence there is a deterministic time `t>0` at which CTCS has positive probability of occupying that state. If the root-start `S(h_{N_r})` paths converged to CTCS, their occupancy probabilities at this continuity time would converge to the same positive value. By the Markov property, conditional on that state, during a short interval of length `u` the shared-gate chain has probability `d_{N_r}u+O(u^2)` of the direct simultaneous refinement, uniformly along the subsequence, while CTCS has only `O(u^2)` probability of reaching the same refinement via two distinct jumps. Taking first `r->infinity` and then small fixed `u` contradicts path convergence. Therefore `(LI)` is necessary.

So

\[
\boxed{
S(h_N)\to\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)
\text{ in the frozen projective }J_1\text{ topology}
\iff (LI).
}
\]

This is the stronger result from move **#3**.
