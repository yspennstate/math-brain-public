## 2. Idea before code

U021 treated a phase-independent deterministic channel map `h_N` and proved CTCS convergence iff every fixed prefix is eventually collision-free. That criterion is intentionally too strong once the channel assignment may depend on the harmonic phase.

The new question is whether collisions can persist forever but be pushed into phases carrying asymptotically negligible *two-block shared-shock mass*.

The answer is yes, and in the natural phase-dependent family the exact criterion is a weighted pair-collision discrepancy.

This run's three new-method moves were:

- **#5 (add a parameter):** let the channel map depend on phase, `h_{N,s}`, so collision location along the harmonic grid becomes a tunable parameter;
- **#12 (find what is conserved):** the complete one-block split generator is invariant under every channel reassignment; all schedule dependence is confined to simultaneous multi-block shocks;
- **#30 (one scale / one direction at a time):** localize the dependence obstruction to one fixed pair of visible block minima and sum its exact shock mass over phases.

## 3. Phase-dependent shared-gate family

Fix

\[
\tau_N=N^{-3},\qquad x_s=\frac{s}{N},\qquad
w_{N,s}=\frac{N}{2s(N-s)},\qquad 1\le s<N.
\]

For each phase `s`, let

\[
h_{N,s}:[N]\to\mathcal C_{N,s}
\]

be an arbitrary deterministic channel assignment.

One macrocycle has physical duration `tau_N`. Within it scan phases `s=1,...,N-1` and, for each phase, the finitely many channels in any fixed order. At microstep `(s,c)`:

1. toss one shared gate with probability `tau_N w_{N,s}`;
2. if the gate is zero, do nothing;
3. if it is one, every current block `B` satisfying `h_{N,s}(min B)=c` receives fresh iid Bernoulli(`x_s`) colors on its leaves, independently across leaves and disjoint blocks;
4. every such block is replaced by its two nonempty color classes when both colors occur; monochromatic blocks are unchanged.

The gate probability is legal for every `N>=3` because `w_{N,s}<1` and `tau_N<1`.

Exactly as in U021, hidden labels do not destabilize visible channels. If visible block `A subseteq [k]` lies in full carrier `B`, every hidden label is `>k`, hence

\[
\boxed{\min B=\min A.}
\]

An invisible full split leaves all visible labels together, so the minimum remains `min A`. Thus the restriction to `[k]` is itself Markov, with no hidden carrier-size state.

## 4. Conserved one-block generator

For a block of size `m`, define its phase-`s` split probability

\[
r_{m,s}=1-x_s^m-(1-x_s)^m.
\]

For a specified binary support of sizes `i,j`, the phase probability is

\[
p_{N,s}(i,j)=x_s^i(1-x_s)^j+x_s^j(1-x_s)^i.
\]

The channel map does not enter this formula. Every block belongs to exactly one channel at each phase, hence its first-order specified-support rate is always

\[
q_N(i,j)=\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}w_{N,s}p_{N,s}(i,j).
\]

Using the harmonic weight and `s <-> N-s`,

\[
\boxed{
q_N(i,j)=\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
\left(\frac{s}{N}\right)^{i-1}
\left(1-\frac{s}{N}\right)^{j-1}
}
\]

and therefore

\[
q_N(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j)
=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}.
\]

So the complete one-block beta generator is **conserved under arbitrary phase-dependent channel reassignment**. Only cross-block common shocks can change the limit.

A second useful uniform estimate is

\[
\boxed{r_{m,s}\le m r_{2,s}},\qquad
r_{2,s}=2x_s(1-x_s).
\]

Indeed, for `x_s<=1/2`,

\[
r_{m,s}\le 1-(1-x_s)^m\le m x_s\le m\,2x_s(1-x_s),
\]

and the other half follows by symmetry. Consequently

\[
\sum_s w_{N,s}r_{m,s}
\le m\sum_s w_{N,s}r_{2,s}
=m\frac{N-1}{N}\le m.
\]

Thus total visible first-order activity is uniformly bounded on every fixed restriction.

## 5. U022 — sharp weighted phase-collision criterion

For fixed `k`, define

\[
\boxed{
D_N(k)=
\max_{1\le a<b\le k}
\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
 w_{N,s}r_{2,s}^{\,2}
 \mathbf 1\{h_{N,s}(a)=h_{N,s}(b)\}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since

\[
w_{N,s}r_{2,s}^{\,2}
=\boxed{\frac{2s(N-s)}{N^3}},
\tag{5.2}
\]
this is an explicit deterministic weighted collision discrepancy.

**Theorem U022.** For the phase-dependent shared-gate family above, started from the one-block partition,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_N\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}
\text{ in the frozen projective }J_1\text{ topology}
\iff
D_N(k)\to0\quad\text{for every fixed }k.
}
\]

### 5.1 Sufficiency

Fix `k` and a visible partition with nonsingleton blocks `B_1,...,B_m`, sizes `m_i`, minima `a_i`. At one phase/channel gate the colorings of different blocks are independent conditional on the common gate.

The first-order rate that at least two visible blocks split on the *same* gate is at most the pair union bound

\[
S_N\le
\sum_{i<j}\sum_s
 w_{N,s}r_{m_i,s}r_{m_j,s}
 \mathbf 1\{h_{N,s}(a_i)=h_{N,s}(a_j)\}.
\]

Using `r_{m,s}<=m r_{2,s}` and (5.1),

\[
S_N
\le
\sum_{i<j}m_i m_j D_N(k)
\le \frac{k^2}{2}D_N(k).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Compare the first-order shared-gate generator `Q_N^{(k)}` with the generator `\bar Q_N^{(k)}` obtained by giving each visible block an independent gate but retaining exactly the same phase/color laws. On an outcome where zero or one blocks split, the shared and independent first-order contributions agree. If `J>=2` blocks split on one shared gate, the shared generator contributes one simultaneous transition while the independent generator contributes the corresponding `J` single-block transitions. Since `J<=k`, (5.3) gives the row bound

\[
\boxed{
\|Q_N^{(k)}-\bar Q_N^{(k)}\|_{\mathrm{row},1}
\le C_k D_N(k)
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

for a constant depending only on `k` (for example one may take `C_k=k^2(k+1)`).

By Section 4,

\[
\bar Q_N^{(k)}\longrightarrow Q_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}.
\]

If `D_N(k)->0`, (5.4) therefore gives

\[
Q_N^{(k)}\longrightarrow Q_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

It remains to pass from the first-order generator to the actual systematic microstep chain. The total first-order visible activity in one macrocycle is at most `k`, by the bound at the end of Section 4. Hence two *distinct* successful visible gates in the same macrocycle have probability `O_k(tau_N^2)`. Same-gate multi-block changes were already included in `Q_N^{(k)}`. Therefore the macrocycle kernel satisfies

\[
C_N^{(k)}
=I+\tau_NQ_N^{(k)}+R_N^{(k)},
\qquad
\|R_N^{(k)}\|_{\mathrm{row},1}=O_k(\tau_N^2).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Over bounded physical time, the accumulated remainder is `O_{k,T}(tau_N)->0`. Uniformization/maximal coupling on the finite state space of partitions of `[k]`, together with moving a unique within-cycle jump by at most `tau_N`, yields

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_N^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in Skorokhod }J_1.
}
\]

This holds for every fixed `k`, giving the frozen projective limit.

### 5.2 Necessity

Suppose `D_N(k)` does not tend to zero for some fixed `k`. There are `epsilon>0`, an infinite subsequence and, after passing to a further subsequence, fixed labels `a<b<=k` such that

\[
\sum_s w_{N,s}r_{2,s}^2
\mathbf 1\{h_{N,s}(a)=h_{N,s}(b)\}
\ge\epsilon.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Enlarge the visible set if necessary and consider a state containing the two pair blocks

\[
A=\{a,b+1\},\qquad C=\{b,b+2\},
\]

with all other visible labels singleton. Their minima are `a,b`.

At a phase where those minima share a channel, the common gate occurs at first-order rate `w_{N,s}`; conditional on it, the two disjoint pair colorings are independent and each pair splits with probability `r_{2,s}`. Hence the **direct one-jump transition splitting both pairs simultaneously** has first-order rate exactly the left side of (5.7), at least `epsilon` along the subsequence.

CTCS has independent continuous clocks on distinct blocks, so its generator assigns zero rate to that direct two-block transition. Its probability of taking the two required pair splits within a short interval of length `u` is only `O(u^2)`.

The displayed two-pair state is reachable from the one-block state in CTCS by a finite sequence of specified binary splits, each of strictly positive beta rate. Thus there exists a deterministic continuity time `t>0` at which CTCS occupies that state with positive probability. If the root-start shared-gate paths converged to CTCS, their occupancy probability at time `t` would converge to the same positive value. By the Markov property and the uniform fixed-`k` exit-rate bound, conditional on that state the shared-gate chain reaches the doubly split state during `(t,t+u]` with probability at least `epsilon u+O(u^2)`, while CTCS has probability `O(u^2)`. Choosing fixed sufficiently small `u` contradicts convergence of the two-time distribution `(X_t,X_{t+u})`.

Therefore `D_N(k)->0` for every fixed `k` is necessary.

This proves U022.

## 6. Move #30: localize the obstruction exactly

Equation (5.2) shows the complete phase geometry:

\[
a_{N,s}:=w_{N,s}r_{2,s}^2
=\frac{2s(N-s)}{N^3}.
\]

The all-phase mass is

\[
\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}a_{N,s}
=\boxed{\frac{N^2-1}{3N^2}}\to\frac13.
\]

So collisions concentrated in the bulk can survive at order one, while collisions confined to an `o(N)` endpoint window can be harmless. U021's eventual injectivity is the special case in which every fixed-pair collision set is eventually empty; U022 identifies the exact weaker notion that matters.

## 7. C043 — endpoint-shock systematic scan

Define a phase-dependent channel schedule by

\[
h_{N,s}(a)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s\in\{1,N-1\},\\
a,&2\le s\le N-2.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus **every block shares one common gate at the two extreme harmonic phases**, while at every interior phase blocks have collision-free minimum-label channels.

For every fixed pair `a<b`, the weighted discrepancy is exactly

\[
D_N(a,b)
=a_{N,1}+a_{N,N-1}
=\boxed{\frac{4(N-1)}{N^3}}
<\frac4{N^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence U022 gives, for every fixed `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{N,043}^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1,
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{N,043}
\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}

in the frozen projective topology.

C043 strictly enlarges U021: for every `N`, the endpoint maps are maximally noninjective on every prefix of size at least two, so no phase-independent eventual-prefix-injectivity statement can certify it. The collisions persist at every `N`; what vanishes is their correctly weighted shock mass.
