## 2. Move #1 — change category: freeze a global random environment

The previous circle constructions used deterministic or freshly resampled microscopic environments. Here the entire finite tree first samples **one global random environment**, and every descendant clade reuses it. Conditional on that environment the finite restriction is Markov; after averaging over the environment it is generally not Markov because survival and observed splits update the posterior law of the hidden environment.

This changes the category from a single finite-state CTMC to a **mixture of quenched CTMCs**. The useful question is therefore not whether every environment is close to CTCS, but whether a typical environment is close in the exact split tests seen by a fixed finite restriction.

The construction below has an additional exact feature: its annealed first-order generator equals the CTCS generator for every finite `N`, even though its annealed path law is not the finite CTCS law.

## 3. Move #11 — U046: split-test discrepancy dualizes to path total variation

Let `P_k` be the finite state space of partitions of `[k]`, and let `Q_k` denote the CTCS generator on `P_k`.

For each `N`, let `E_N` be an arbitrary random environment. Suppose that conditional on `E_N=e`, the visible restriction `Pi_N^[k]` is a finite-state CTMC with generator `Q_{N,k}(e)` and the only nontrivial jumps are binary splits of one current visible block.

For a current visible block of size `b<=k` and a specified unordered nontrivial split `A|B` of that block, write `q_{N,e}(A|B)` for its conditional jump rate. Define

\[
 D_{N,k}(e)
 :=\max_{2\le b\le k}\max_{A\mid B}
 \left|q_{N,e}(A\mid B)-B(|A|,|B|)\right|.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem U046 (random-environment split-test compiler)

If, for every fixed `k`,

\[
 D_{N,k}(E_N)\xrightarrow{P}0,
\tag{3.2}
\]

then for every fixed `T<infinity`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|\mathcal L(\Pi_N^{[k]}|_{[0,T]})
 -\mathcal L(\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}|_{[0,T]})\right\|_{\rm TV}
 \longrightarrow0.
 }
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular `Pi_N^[k] => Pi_CTCS^[k]` in `J1`. If this holds for every fixed `k`, then the full projective partition process converges to CTCS(infinity).

More quantitatively, if

\[
 \delta_{N,k}(e)
 :=\max_{\pi\in P_k}\sum_{\pi'}
 |Q_{N,k}(e)(\pi,\pi')-Q_k(\pi,\pi')|,
\tag{3.4}
\]

then

\[
 \delta_{N,k}(e)
 \le 2(2^{k-1}-1)D_{N,k}(e),
\tag{3.5}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathcal L_e(\Pi_N^{[k]}|_{[0,T]})
 -\mathcal L(\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}|_{[0,T]})\|_{\rm TV}
 \le
 \min\{1,T(2^{k-1}-1)D_{N,k}(e)\}.
 }
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof

A block of size `b` has exactly

\[
 2^{b-1}-1
\tag{3.7}
\]

unordered nontrivial binary splits. Over an entire partition of `[k]`, the number of possible visible split transitions is at most `2^{k-1}-1`. Hence the off-diagonal row `l1` discrepancy is at most `(2^{k-1}-1)D`; the diagonal discrepancy, being the absolute difference of the two exit-rate sums, is bounded by the same quantity. This proves (3.5).

Fix an environment `e`. Choose any uniformization rate `lambda(e)` at least the maximum exit rate of both conditional generators. Use one Poisson clock of rate `lambda(e)` for both chains. At each clock ring, while the chains agree at state `pi`, maximally couple the discrete kernels

\[
 P_e=I+Q_{N,k}(e)/\lambda(e),
 \qquad
 P_*=I+Q_k/\lambda(e).
\tag{3.8}
\]

Their one-step total-variation distance from a common state is at most

\[
 \frac{\delta_{N,k}(e)}{2\lambda(e)}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

The expected number of clock rings by time `T` is `lambda(e)T`. A union bound over the coupled rings therefore gives

\[
 P_e(\text{the two paths disagree by time }T)
 \le \frac{T\delta_{N,k}(e)}2.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Using (3.5) proves (3.6). Averaging over `E_N` gives

\[
 \|\mathcal L(\Pi_N^{[k]})-\mathcal L(\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]})\|_{\rm TV}
 \le
 E\min\{1,T(2^{k-1}-1)D_{N,k}(E_N)\}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

The bounded random variable on the right converges to zero in probability under (3.2), hence also in `L1`. This proves (3.3).

For the infinite projective process, fix `K`. All restrictions `[j]`, `j<=K`, are deterministic images of the `[K]` path, so their joint law converges in total variation. The tail of the frozen summable projective metric beyond `K` is at most its deterministic geometric tail. First send `N->infinity`, then `K->infinity`. This gives the CTCS(infinity) limit. QED.

The important point is that U046 requires **no uniform control over all environments** and no deterministic Markov property after averaging.

## 4. Move #28 — typical random environments are enough

C067 uses a one-dimensional Rademacher environment whose discrepancy is only typically small.

For the `N`-leaf model set

\[
 m_N=\lfloor N/2\rfloor
\tag{4.1}
\]

and sample iid Rademacher signs

\[
 \xi_{N,1},\ldots,\xi_{N,m_N}\in\{-1,+1\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every arc length `1<=s<N`, define the reflected index

\[
 r(s)=\min\{s,N-s\}
\tag{4.3}
\]

and the quenched weight

\[
 \boxed{
 w_{N,s}=1+\frac12\xi_{N,r(s)}\in\{1/2,3/2\}.
 }
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus `w_{N,s}=w_{N,N-s}` exactly.

Let

\[
 \nu_N=\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}w_{N,s}\delta_{s/N}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
 D_N^*=\sup_{0\le x\le1}|\nu_N([0,x])-x|.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Write

\[
 S_j=\sum_{r=1}^j\xi_{N,r},
 \qquad
 M_N=\max_{j\le m_N}|S_j|.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The unweighted grid contributes at most `1/N` to star discrepancy. For the centered reflected contribution, every prefix of the sign word

\[
 \xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots,\xi_{m_N},\ldots,\xi_2,\xi_1
\]

(with the obvious single central term when `N` is even) is a sum of at most three ordinary partial sums. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 D_N^*\le \frac1N+\frac{3M_N}{2N}.
 }
\tag{4.8}
\]

Kolmogorov's maximal inequality gives, for fixed `epsilon>0` and all sufficiently large `N`,

\[
 P(D_N^*>\epsilon)
 \le \frac{9}{2N\epsilon^2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Hence `D_N^* -> 0` in probability. In fact Doob's `L2` maximal inequality gives

\[
 E M_N\le 2\sqrt{m_N}\le\sqrt{2N},
\tag{4.10}
\]

and therefore

\[
 E D_N^*
 \le \frac1N+\frac{3\sqrt2}{2\sqrt N}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

So the construction has an explicit `N^{-1/2}` annealed discrepancy scale.

## 5. C067 — quenched Rademacher circle-cut hierarchy

Conditional on the single environment `(xi_{N,r})`, each current nonsingleton leaf block `B` evolves as follows.

For every arc length `s=1,...,N-1`, run an independent Poisson proposal clock with rate

\[
 \boxed{
 a_{N,s}=\frac{N}{2s(N-s)}w_{N,s}.
 }
\tag{5.1}
\]

At a type-`s` proposal:

1. inject the current block uniformly into `Z/NZ`;
2. choose a uniform cyclic arc start;
3. split the block by membership in the contiguous arc of length `s`;
4. discard a trivial cut;
5. evolve nonempty children independently, but **reuse the same global environment** `(xi_{N,r})` throughout the entire tree.

Because every `w_{N,s}>=1/2`, every pair of labels has strictly positive separation rate. Thus the finite hierarchy reaches singletons almost surely.

### Exact conditional split rates

For a specified visible split of sizes `i,j>=1`, `k=i+j`, uniform-injection counting gives

\[
 q_{N,\xi}(i,j)
 =\frac{N}{(N)_k}
 \sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
 w_{N,s}(s-1)_{i-1}(N-s-1)_{j-1}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The marginal of a uniform injection of an arbitrary full block onto any fixed visible labels is again a uniform injection. Hence the rate in (5.2) is independent of how many hidden labels remain in the block. Full states with the same visible partition therefore have identical conditional rates to every visible target state: the projection is strongly lumpable conditional on the environment.

### Conditional generator convergence

For fixed `i,j`, let

\[
 p_{i,j}(x)=x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The falling-factorial grid kernel in (5.2) differs uniformly from `p_{i,j}` by `O_k(1/N)`, and `N^k/(N)_k=1+O_k(1/N)`. Since `nu_N([0,1])<=3/2`, summation by parts / one-dimensional Koksma gives

\[
 \boxed{
 |q_{N,\xi}(i,j)-B(i,j)|
 \le C_k\left(D_N^*+\frac1N\right)
 }
\tag{5.4}
\]

for a constant depending only on fixed `k`. Taking the maximum over the finitely many visible split tests gives

\[
 D_{N,k}(\xi)
 \le C_k'\left(D_N^*+\frac1N\right)
 \xrightarrow{P}0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

U046 now yields, for every fixed `k,T`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathcal L(\Pi_{N,067}^{[k]}|_{[0,T]})
 -\mathcal L(\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}|_{[0,T]})\|_{\rm TV}
 \longrightarrow0.
 }
\tag{5.6}
\]

Using (4.11) in (3.11) even gives the quantitative rate

\[
 \|\cdots\|_{\rm TV}=O_{k,T}(N^{-1/2}).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pi_{N,067}^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
 \quad\text{in }J_1
 }
\tag{5.8}
\]

for every fixed `k`, and then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pi_{N,067}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS(\infty)}
 }
\tag{5.9}
\]

in the frozen summable projective finite-restriction topology.

### Exact annealed first-order generator

Equation (5.2) is linear in the weights and

\[
 E w_{N,s}=1.
\tag{5.10}
\]

The baseline `w=1` circle model has the exact Vandermonde identity

\[
 q_N(i,j)=B(i,j)
\tag{5.11}
\]

for every finite `N>=i+j`. Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 E\,Q_{N,k}(\xi)=Q_k
 }
\tag{5.12}
\]

exactly at every admissible finite `N`. This does **not** make the annealed process CTCS at finite `N`: averaging a global quenched environment mixes semigroups rather than generators, and the observed survival/split history changes the posterior distribution of the environment. The finite witness below makes the difference exact.
