## 3. Move 8 — GO THE OTHER WAY AND DISCRETIZE: random-recursive-tree edge cuts

The preceding R11 classes were most naturally described by paintboxes/EPPFs. This run starts instead with a finite combinatorial object: a random recursive tree.

For a finite label block `B`, `|B|=m`, define an **exchangeably relabelled random recursive tree** as follows.

1. Choose a uniformly random ordering of the labels in `B`.
2. The first label is the root.
3. For rank `j=2,...,m`, attach that label to a uniformly chosen earlier rank.
4. Forget the auxiliary ranks and retain the labelled rooted tree.

For an oriented parent edge `e`, let `S_e` be its descendant fringe subtree and write

\[
s_e=|S_e|\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}.
\]

The combinatorial statistic that matters is the number `Z_{m,s}` of edges whose descendant fringe subtree has size `s`.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact fringe-size expectation

For every `1<=s<m`,

\[
\boxed{E Z_{m,s}=\frac{m}{s(s+1)}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

Build the standard recursive tree sequentially. For `s=1`, every existing leaf ceases to be a leaf with probability `1/n` when vertex `n+1` is inserted, while the new vertex contributes one new leaf. Hence

\[
E Z_{n+1,1}=(1-1/n)E Z_{n,1}+1,
\]

and `E Z_{n,1}=n/2` follows by induction.

For `s>=2`, an existing size-`s` proper fringe subtree stays size `s` unless the new vertex attaches inside it, while a size-`s-1` fringe subtree becomes size `s` exactly when the new vertex attaches inside it. Thus

\[
E Z_{n+1,s}
=\left(1-\frac{s}{n}\right)E Z_{n,s}
+\frac{s-1}{n}E Z_{n,s-1}.
\]

Substituting the induction hypotheses gives

\[
\frac{n-s}{s(s+1)}+\frac1s
=\frac{n+1}{s(s+1)}.
\]

This also covers the boundary `s=n` after setting `Z_{n,n}=0` for proper fringe subtrees. QED.

This formula is known in the random-recursive-tree fringe literature; the proof is included because the exact constant is needed below.

## 4. Move 12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: an exact beta-binomial visible-hazard identity

For a full `m`-label block, give an edge of fringe size `s` weight

\[
\boxed{w_m(s)=\frac{s+1}{2(m-s)}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now fix `b` distinguished/sample labels inside the full block and a particular nonempty proper subset `A` of them with `|A|=i`.

Because the RRT was exchangeably relabelled, conditional on `|S_e|=s`, the descendant fringe set is a uniform `s`-subset of the `m` labels. Therefore

\[
P(S_e\cap[b]=A\mid |S_e|=s)
=\frac{\binom{m-b}{s-i}}{\binom ms}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 — one orientation contributes half the CTCS beta rate

For every `m>=b>=2` and `1<=i<b`,

\[
\boxed{
E\sum_e w_m(s_e)\mathbf1\{S_e\cap[b]=A\}
=\frac12 B(i,b-i),
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

**independently of the hidden full-block size `m`.**

#### Proof

Using Lemma 3.1 and (4.2), the left side equals

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{m,b}(i)
&=\sum_s
\frac{m}{s(s+1)}
\frac{s+1}{2(m-s)}
\frac{\binom{m-b}{s-i}}{\binom ms}\\
&=\frac12\sum_s
\binom{m-b}{s-i}B(s,m-s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Set `n=m-b` and `x=s-i`. Using the beta integral,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x=0}^{n}\binom nx B(i+x,b-i+n-x)
&=\int_0^1 t^{i-1}(1-t)^{b-i-1}
\sum_{x=0}^{n}\binom nx t^x(1-t)^{n-x}\,dt\\
&=\int_0^1 t^{i-1}(1-t)^{b-i-1}\,dt\\
&=B(i,b-i).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (4.3). QED.

The unordered split `A | A^c` can occur with `A` or `A^c` as the descendant fringe set. Applying (4.3) to both orientations gives

\[
\boxed{
E\sum_e w_m(s_e)\mathbf1\{S_e\cap[b]\in\{A,A^c\}\}
=B(i,b-i),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

which is **exactly** the CTCS particular split rate (2.2), with no dependence on `m`.

That hidden-size cancellation is the conserved invariant found by Move 12. It is stronger than merely matching the full-block law: it remains true after restricting an evolving `N`-label genealogy to any fixed finite set of labels.

### Corollary 4.2 — exact total expected weight

Summing (3.1)(4.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\sum_e w_m(s_e)
&=\frac m2\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}\frac1{s(m-s)}\\
&=H_{m-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus the same construction simultaneously reproduces the CTCS total harmonic splitting rate.

## 5. Move 19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: the RRT multi-cut hierarchy

A first algorithmic attempt was **too successful and therefore unusable as a distinct model**.

### Failed one-cut version

If at each raw step one permits at most one cut in a fresh RRT and assigns each edge marginal cut probability proportional to `w_m(s)`, then (4.5) says the averaged split kernel is already the CTCS beta kernel. After geometric-to-exponential rescaling this is essentially an Eulerized sampler for the original CTCS law. Under the frozen nontriviality convention it is not counted.

This failure identifies what must change: retain the exact first-order CTCS hazard while making the finite discrete genealogy genuinely different.

### Multi-cut repair

For the concrete model set

\[
\delta_N=N^{-5}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The same proof works for any positive `delta_N` satisfying `delta_N N^4 -> 0`.

For every current nonsingleton block `B` of the full `[N]` partition, independently in each raw generation:

1. sample a fresh exchangeably relabelled RRT on `B`;
2. for every edge `e`, independently mark it with probability
   \[
   p_e=\delta_N w_{|B|}(s_e);
   \tag{5.2}
   \]
3. delete **all** marked edges simultaneously;
4. the connected components become the child blocks;
5. repeat independently on every child block at the next raw generation.

Singletons remain singletons. Give one raw generation physical length `delta_N`, and write

\[
\widehat\Pi_N(t)
=\Pi_N(\lfloor t/\delta_N\rfloor).
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is a finite discrete combinatorial tree model. Unlike CTCS and C001, it can have genuine multifurcations at finite `N`, because several RRT edges may be cut in the same generation.

The mark probabilities are valid: for `s<m<=N`,

\[
w_m(s)\le \frac m2\le\frac N2,
\qquad
p_e\le\frac1{2N^4}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

## 6. `R11-I0054-C005` — RRT-MC converges to the full CTCS(infinity) partition genealogy

**STATUS: PROVED author-side under the frozen equivalence convention; campaign remains PARTIAL pending the required independent audits and final prior-art/source-intent clearance.**

### Theorem

Let `delta_N>0` satisfy

\[
\delta_N N^4\to0,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and construct the random-recursive-tree multi-cut hierarchy above, with each raw generation assigned physical length `delta_N`.

For every fixed `k`, its restriction to labels `[k]` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi^{[k]}
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

in Skorokhod `J_1`, where `Pi` is the Aldous--Janson CTCS(infinity) exchangeable partition process. Moreover, after the standard singleton embedding of labels above `N`,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N\Longrightarrow\Pi
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

in the projective metric (2.1).

The concrete counted class uses `delta_N=N^{-5}`.

### Proof

Fix `k`. At a raw generation, condition on the complete current hidden `[N]` partition and hence on the hidden full block containing each current sampled `[k]` block.

#### Step 1: a uniform bound on multiple marks

For a realised RRT `T` on a block of size `m<=N`, let

\[
W(T)=\sum_{e\in T}w_m(s_e).
\]

Since there are `m-1` edges and each weight is at most `m/2`,

\[
W(T)\le\frac{m(m-1)}2<\frac{N^2}{2}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For independent Bernoulli marks,

\[
P(\text{at least two marks}\mid T)
\le\frac12\left(\sum_ep_e\right)^2
\le\frac18\delta_N^2N^4.
\tag{6.5}
\]

By (6.1), this is `o(delta_N)` uniformly in the hidden block size. Simultaneous visible changes in two distinct hidden blocks have the same or smaller order; for fixed `k` their total probability is `O_k(delta_N^2 N^4)=o(delta_N)`.

#### Step 2: one visible split has the exact CTCS first-order coefficient

Consider one current sampled block of size `b` contained in a hidden full block of size `m>=b`, and a particular split `A | A^c`, `|A|=i`.

If exactly one marked edge creates that visible split, then after averaging the fresh RRT its leading probability is, by (4.5),

\[
\delta_N B(i,b-i).
\]

Replacing `delta_N w_e` by the probability that `e` is the unique marked edge introduces error at most a constant multiple of

\[
\delta_N^2 W(T)^2
\le \frac14\delta_N^2N^4.
\]

Together with (6.5), uniformly over all hidden histories,

\[
\boxed{
P(\rho\to\rho'\mid\mathcal F)
=\delta_N Q^{(k)}(\rho,\rho')
+O_k(\delta_N^2N^4)
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

for every single CTCS binary jump `rho -> rho'`. All transitions not corresponding to one CTCS jump have conditional probability `O_k(delta_N^2 N^4)`. Consequently

\[
P(\rho\to\rho\mid\mathcal F)
=1-\delta_N\lambda(\rho)
+O_k(\delta_N^2N^4),
\tag{6.7}
\]

where

\[
\lambda(\rho)=\sum_{B\in\rho}H_{|B|-1}.
\]

The crucial point is that the coefficient in (6.6) is independent of every hidden full-block size; that is exactly Lemma 4.1.

#### Step 3: couple to the Euler chain

Let `Y_N` be the finite-state Euler chain on partitions of `[k]` with one-step kernel

\[
K_N=I+\delta_NQ^{(k)}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

For large `N` this is stochastic because the state space and all CTCS rates are finite.

At each raw step, maximal-couple the visible RRT-MC transition, conditional on its hidden history, to the corresponding `Y_N` transition. By (6.6)--(6.7), the mismatch probability at one step is

\[
O_k(\delta_N^2N^4).
\]

Up to any fixed physical time `T` there are at most `T/delta_N+1` raw steps, so the coupling failure probability is

\[
O_k(T\delta_NN^4)+o(1)\to0.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus the visible RRT-MC restriction and the Euler chain agree on `[0,T]` with probability tending to one.

#### Step 4: Euler chain to CTCS

At state `rho`, the Euler chain waits a geometric number of steps with success probability `delta_N lambda(rho)`. Therefore its physical holding time converges to

\[
\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda(\rho)).
\]

Conditioned on a jump, its next state is exactly distributed as

\[
\frac{Q^{(k)}(\rho,\rho')}{\lambda(\rho)}.
\]

A fragmentation of `k` labels makes at most `k-1` visible jumps before absorption. Coupling the finitely many holding times and jump choices gives

\[
Y_N\Longrightarrow\Pi^{[k]}
\]

in `J_1`. Combining with (6.9) proves (6.2).

#### Step 5: whole exchangeable genealogy

Embed the finite `[N]` path into partitions of `N` by declaring labels above `N` singleton. Given `epsilon>0`, choose `K` so that

\[
\sum_{k>K}2^{-k}<\epsilon/2.
\]

For `N>=K`, couple the `[K]` restrictions by (6.2); consistency then couples all smaller restrictions simultaneously. The deterministic tail of (2.1) is below `epsilon/2`, so the projective distance tends to zero in probability under the coupling. This proves (6.3).

Finally, for each pair `a,b`, the first time at which they lie in different partition blocks is their branchpoint/separation height. Hence the whole partition-path limit recovers the complete labelled CTCS(infinity) genealogy in the source's formalization. QED.
