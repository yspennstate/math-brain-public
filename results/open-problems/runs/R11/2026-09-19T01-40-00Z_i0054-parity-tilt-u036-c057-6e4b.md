## 2. Move #4 — prove the sharp finite-independence boundary completely

The previous run proved only the first case: pairwise independence forces every three-label binary split law but can fail on four labels. The right general statement is completely algebraic.

Let `[k]=A\sqcup B`, with `A,B` nonempty, `|A|=i`, `|B|=j`, and write `z_r in {0,1}` for color bits. The unordered split indicator is

\[
F_{A,B}(z)
=
\prod_{a\in A}z_a\prod_{b\in B}(1-z_b)
+
\prod_{a\in A}(1-z_a)\prod_{b\in B}z_b.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — exact degree

\[
\boxed{
\deg F_{A,B}=
\begin{cases}
k,&k\text{ even},\\
k-1,&k\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{2.2}
\]

**Proof.** The coefficient of the full monomial `z_1...z_k` is

\[
(-1)^j+(-1)^i.
\tag{2.3}
\]

If `k=i+j` is even, `i,j` have the same parity, so this coefficient is `2(-1)^i !=0`; hence the degree is `k`.

If `k` is odd, the parities are opposite and (2.3) vanishes. Pick `r in A`. In the coefficient of the degree-`k-1` monomial `prod_{s != r} z_s`, the first orientation contributes zero because it requires the missing factor `z_r`, while the second contributes `(-1)^{i-1}`. Hence the degree is exactly `k-1`. QED.

This immediately tells us exactly which binary split statistics a `d`-wise Bernoulli law is forced to reproduce.

## U036 — sharp split-detection theorem for `d`-wise independence

Fix `d>=1`, `k>=2`, and a specified unordered split `A|B` of `[k]`. Among all Bernoulli families with the same one-dimensional parameter `x`, the expectation of `F_{A,B}` is forced by exact `d`-wise independence whenever

\[
\deg F_{A,B}\le d.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Conversely, if `deg F_{A,B}>d`, there exists an unbiased Bernoulli(`1/2`) `d`-wise independent law for which the split probability differs from the iid value.

### Proof: forced direction

A `d`-wise independent Bernoulli(`x`) law matches the iid product expectation of every multilinear monomial involving at most `d` coordinates. If `deg F<=d`, every monomial in (2.1) lies in that span, so `E F` is forced to its iid value.

### Proof: converse

Let `r=deg F>d` and choose a size-`r` set `T` on which the maximal-degree monomial coefficient `a_T` of `F` is nonzero. On `{0,1}^k`, define

\[
\mu_\varepsilon(z)
=2^{-k}\left(1+\varepsilon\chi_T(z)\right),
\qquad
\chi_T(z)=(-1)^{\sum_{t\in T}z_t},
\qquad
0<|\varepsilon|\le1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

This is a probability law. Every marginal on fewer than `|T|` coordinates is uniform product: summing over any unobserved coordinate of `T` kills the Walsh character. In particular, because `d<|T|`, the law is `d`-wise independent Bernoulli(`1/2`).

The split expectation changes. Expand `F` in monomials. For any monomial not containing all of `T`, its product with `chi_T` has zero expectation under the uniform cube; no monomial strictly containing `T` exists because `T` has maximal degree. Therefore

\[
E_{\mu_\varepsilon}F-E_{\mu_0}F
=
\varepsilon a_T\left(-\frac12\right)^{|T|}
\ne0.
\tag{2.6}
\]

This proves the converse and U036. QED.

### Corollary 2.2 — first unavoidable detector

For a fixed independence order `d>=2`, the smallest number of visible labels on which some binary split statistic can fail is

\[
\boxed{
K_*(d)=
\begin{cases}
d+1,&d\text{ odd},\\
d+2,&d\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{2.7}
\]

This `K_*(d)` is always even. When `d` is even, every split on `d+1` labels is still forced because the odd-size indicator has degree `d`; the next even size has degree `d+2`. When `d` is odd, the very next size `d+1` is even and has degree `d+1`.

Sharpness can be witnessed by the full parity tilt

\[
\mu_\varepsilon(z)=2^{-K_*}
\left(1+\varepsilon(-1)^{\sum_rz_r}\right),
\tag{2.8}
\]

which is actually `(K_*-1)`-wise independent. For a split with `i` ones in one orientation,

\[
\Pr_{\mu_\varepsilon}(A|B)
=2^{1-K_*}\left(1+\varepsilon(-1)^i\right),
\tag{2.9}
\]

whereas the iid value is `2^{1-K_*}`.

Thus the earlier `d=2` four-label obstruction was not sporadic: it is the first member of the exact sequence

\[
K_*(2)=4,\ K_*(3)=4,\ K_*(4)=6,\ K_*(5)=6,\ldots
\tag{2.10}
\]

This is the main theorem-sized gain of the run.
