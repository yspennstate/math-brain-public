## 2. Setup: the full positive circle-cut family

The last two runs used bounded perturbations of the finite-circle model. Here I remove the upper bound completely.

Let the ambient circle size be `M`. Give oriented arc length `s in {1,...,M-1}` a nonnegative microscopic weight `w_{M,s}>=0`, and proposal intensity

\[
 a_{M,s}=\frac{M}{2s(M-s)}w_{M,s}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

At a proposal, inject the current leaf block uniformly into `Z/MZ`, choose a uniform arc start, and split according to membership in the length-`s` arc. Define the reflected symmetrization

\[
 \bar w_{M,s}=\frac{w_{M,s}+w_{M,M-s}}2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a specified unordered visible split of sizes `i,j>=1`, `k=i+j`, direct hypergeometric counting gives

\[
 q_M(i,j)=\frac{M}{(M)_k}\sum_{s=1}^{M-1}
 \bar w_{M,s}(s-1)_{i-1}(M-s-1)_{j-1}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The baseline `w=1` gives exactly

\[
 q_M(i,j)=B(i,j)=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}
\tag{2.4}
\]

by the Vandermonde identity proved in U043.

## 3. Move #12 — the conserved observable is the symmetrized positive empirical measure

Equation (2.3) shows an exact null direction: the antisymmetric component

\[
 w_{M,s}-w_{M,M-s}
\]

is invisible to every unordered binary split statistic. The complete observable carried by the circle environment is therefore the positive finite measure

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_M:=\frac1M\sum_{s=1}^{M-1}\bar w_{M,s}\,\delta_{s/M}.
 }
\tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed, if

\[
 g_{M,i,j}(s/M)=
 \frac{(s-1)_{i-1}}{M^{i-1}}
 \frac{(M-s-1)_{j-1}}{M^{j-1}},
\tag{3.2}
\]

then exactly

\[
 q_M(i,j)=\frac{M^k}{(M)_k}\int g_{M,i,j}(x)\,\nu_M(dx).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For fixed `i,j`, `g_{M,i,j}` converges uniformly on the grid to

\[
 x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the entire fixed-restriction generator depends asymptotically only on `nu_M`.

## 4. Move #20 — why the signed-discrepancy extension fails, and why positivity repairs it

A naive attempt is to remove U044's boundedness by working with arbitrary signed perturbation measures and polynomial moments. That is false as a discrepancy statement. For example

\[
 \mu_M=\delta_{1/M}-\delta_{2/M}
\tag{4.1}
\]

has

\[
 \int p\,d\mu_M\to0
\tag{4.2}
\]

for every fixed polynomial `p`, while its cumulative discrepancy is exactly one immediately after `1/M`. So moments alone do not control microscopic prefixes for signed measures with unbounded cancellation.

But (4.1) cannot be a valid circle environment: its negative atom would require a negative proposal intensity. The correct object is the **positive** measure `nu_M`, not the centered signed perturbation. Positivity restores compactness: once the pair split rate converges, the total masses `nu_M([0,1])` stay bounded, so no extra total-variation hypothesis is needed.

This is the obstruction/fix that the bounded U044 formulation was hiding.

## 5. Move #29 — U045: positive-measure compactification, including endpoint mass

### Theorem U045

For any sequence of nonnegative circle weights `w_{M,s}` (no uniform upper bound), the following are equivalent as `M->infinity`:

1. for every fixed `i,j>=1`,
   \[
   q_M(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j);
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
2. the symmetrized positive measures satisfy
   \[
   \nu_M\Rightarrow dx
   \quad\text{weakly on }[0,1];
   \tag{5.2}
   \]
3. the weighted CDF/star discrepancy satisfies
   \[
   \boxed{
   D_M^*:=\sup_{0\le x\le1}
   \left|\nu_M([0,x])-x\right|\longrightarrow0.
   }
   \tag{5.3}
   \]

Moreover, if along a subsequence `nu_M => nu` for a finite positive measure `nu` (necessarily reflection-symmetric), then for every fixed split

\[
 \boxed{
 q_M(i,j)\longrightarrow
 \int_0^1 x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\,\nu(dx).
 }
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus, after the exact reflection quotient (2.2), every subsequential limit is a **reflection-symmetric** finite positive measure on the compactified interval `[0,1]`; conversely every such symmetric finite positive measure can be approximated by symmetric grid measures of the form (3.1). Hence the weak closure of the observable positive circle-cut family is precisely the cone of reflection-symmetric finite positive measures, and CTCS is the single point `nu=dx` in that quotient.

### Proof of `(2) => (1)` and the compactification statement

From (3.3), the prefactor `M^k/(M)_k -> 1`, and `g_{M,i,j}` converges uniformly to the beta polynomial (3.4). Weak convergence of the positive finite measures gives (5.4). Taking `nu=dx` gives (5.1).

### Proof of `(1) => (2)`

First take `i=j=1`. Since `g=1`,

\[
 q_M(1,1)=\frac{M^2}{(M)_2}\nu_M([0,1]),
\tag{5.5}
\]

so (5.1) forces

\[
 \nu_M([0,1])\to1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

In particular the positive measures have uniformly bounded total mass.

Next set `j=1` and `i=r+1`. Then (3.3), (5.1), and the uniform approximation

\[
 \frac{(s-1)_r}{M^r}=\left(\frac{s}{M}\right)^r+O_r(M^{-1})
\tag{5.7}
\]

give

\[
 \int_0^1x^r\,\nu_M(dx)\to\frac1{r+1}
 =\int_0^1x^r\,dx
\qquad(r=0,1,2,\ldots).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Thus every polynomial integral converges to its Lebesgue value. Polynomials are uniformly dense in `C[0,1]`; the uniform mass bound (5.6) lets the polynomial approximation error pass through the integrals. Therefore

\[
 \nu_M\Rightarrow dx.
\tag{5.9}
\]

### Equivalence of `(2)` and `(3)`

For positive finite measures whose total masses tend to one, weak convergence to Lebesgue measure is equivalent to convergence of the corresponding distribution functions at every point. The Lebesgue CDF `x` is continuous on `[0,1]`, so the convergence is in fact uniform. This is the standard Pólya/Helly--Bray consequence for a continuous limiting CDF. Hence (5.2) iff (5.3).

This completes U045. QED.

### Endpoint classification

The points at infinity of the old open interval picture are now explicit. If a subsequential limit has

\[
 \nu=a_0\delta_0+a_1\delta_1+\nu^\circ,
\tag{5.10}
\]

then reflection symmetry of the observable circle measure forces `a_0=a_1`; it is useful to display the two endpoint contributions separately because they act on different oriented beta monomials before the symmetry is imposed.

Then the endpoint atoms contribute

\[
 a_0\mathbf 1_{\{i=1\}}+a_1\mathbf 1_{\{j=1\}}
\tag{5.11}
\]

to the limiting specified-split rate. For symmetric circle environments `a_0=a_1`. Thus nonvanishing endpoint mass is visible precisely through singleton-peeling split tests; it cannot hide inside CTCS. Interior singular mass changes higher split tests according to (5.4). This is the promised endpoint/dust-style boundary classification, stated only at the generator level and without importing fragmentation terminology beyond what is proved here.

## 6. C066 — sparse coarse arcs with unbounded microscopic weights

U045 makes it possible to build a model whose microscopic weights diverge and whose allowed arc lengths occupy a vanishing fraction of the ambient circle.

For the `n`-leaf model set

\[
 M_n=n^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Only the `n-1` coarse arc lengths

\[
 s=rn,\qquad r=1,\ldots,n-1
\tag{6.2}
\]

are active. Give each active length the unbounded weight

\[
 \boxed{w_{M_n,rn}=n+1,}
\tag{6.3}
\]

and set every other weight to zero. The weights are reflection-symmetric because `M_n-rn=(n-r)n`.

Equivalently, a current nonsingleton block has `n-1` independent Poisson proposal clocks, with type `r` rate

\[
 a_{n,r}
 =\frac{M_n}{2(rn)(M_n-rn)}(n+1)
 =\boxed{\frac{n+1}{2r(n-r)}}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

At a type-`r` proposal:

1. choose a fresh uniform injection of the current leaf block into `Z/n^2 Z`;
2. choose a fresh uniform cyclic arc start;
3. cut by the contiguous arc of length `rn`;
4. discard a trivial cut and evolve the nonempty children independently by the same rule.

This is a finite discrete-leaf binary tree model. Every pair of labels has positive separation rate under every active nontrivial arc length, so every nonsingleton block eventually splits almost surely; there are at most `n-1` successful splits.

Its positive empirical measure is

\[
 \nu_n=\frac{n+1}{n^2}
 \sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\delta_{r/n}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For `x in [r/n,(r+1)/n)`, its CDF equals `r(n+1)/n^2`, so

\[
 D_n^*\le\frac{2}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

U045 therefore gives, for every fixed `i,j`,

\[
 q_{n,066}(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j).
\tag{6.7}
\]

The pair rate is actually exact for every `n`:

\[
 q_{n,066}(1,1)
 =\frac{1}{n^2-1}(n-1)(n+1)=1.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The hidden-label restriction is exactly lumpable. If a current full block has `b` labels and only `k` are visible, the restriction of a uniform injection into the `n^2` ambient positions is a uniform injection of those `k` labels, because every visible injection has exactly `(n^2-k)_{b-k}` extensions. Hidden-only cuts leave the visible partition unchanged and future clocks/proposals are fresh. Thus the restriction to `[k]` is a finite-state CTMC whose off-diagonal generator entries are exactly the split rates in (6.7).

Finite-state generator convergence therefore gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pi_{n,066}^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
 \quad\text{in Skorokhod }J_1
 }
\tag{6.9}
\]

for every fixed `k`. Consistency and the same summable projective finite-restriction path metric used throughout the R11 package then give

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pi_{n,066}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS(infinity)}.
 }
\tag{6.10}
\]
