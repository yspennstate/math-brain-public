## 3. Why the previous `N Delta^2` barrier should be attacked

C025 uses one shared parent across all loci in every population generation and proves CTCS convergence by the deterministic discrepancy bound

\[
|D_{\ell,r}|\le r.
\]

For a finite visible sample, this gives path error of order

\[
\frac{1}{N\Delta_N^2},
\]

so U009 requires `N Delta_N^2 -> infinity`. The previous report therefore identified `Delta_N ~ N^{-1/2}` as the apparent critical scale.

The deterministic bound ignores the fact that two coupled genomes usually copy **the same allele value even when they choose different parent sites**, because the low-mutation stationary population is nearly monochromatic. The right source term is heterozygosity, not one discrepancy per generation. This observation is the core new route.

## 4. C026 construction — a genuine two-parent pedigree with free assortment

Put

\[
m_N=\lceil\sqrt N\rceil,
\qquad
\alpha_N=\Delta_N=\frac1{m_N},
\qquad
L_N=N^2,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\mu_N=\frac{\alpha_N}{N+2\alpha_N}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

There are `N` population sites and exactly `L_N=N^2` persistent binary loci. Initially the loci are independent with the exchangeable one-locus law

\[
\nu_{N,\alpha}(x)
=
\frac{(\alpha)_{|x|}(\alpha)_{N-|x|}}{(2\alpha)_N},
\qquad x\in\{0,1\}^N.
\tag{4.3}
\]

One raw C026 generation is:

1. choose one victim population site `V_r` uniformly from `[N]`;
2. choose two global parental sites `A_r,B_r` independently and uniformly from `[N]`;
3. at each locus `ell`, independently choose a fair segregation coin and use either `A_r` or `B_r` as that locus's parent; copy the pre-update parental allele into `V_r`, then independently mutate it with probability `mu_N`;
4. after the population update, every current nonsingleton tree block independently hashes uniformly into the finite locus pool `{1,...,L_N}`; inject its labels uniformly without replacement into the `N` population sites; split the block by the two sampled allele colors if both colors occur, otherwise leave it unchanged;
5. one raw generation has physical duration `Delta_N`.

The same two-parent pedigree event is shared across the whole genome in **every** population generation. It is not a sparse perturbation. Recombination/free assortment enters through the independent locus coins choosing between the two parents.

For comparison define `X_N` to be the C023-type finite-locus model with the same victims, mutation bits, hashes and tree samples, except that every locus chooses its parent independently and uniformly from `[N]`.

### Exact one-locus marginal

At a single C026 locus, the chosen parent

\[
R_\ell\in\{A,B\}
\]

is exactly uniform on `[N]`. Therefore its one-locus transition kernel, conditional on the common victim, is **identically equal** to the ordinary C023 fixed-victim kernel. In particular (4.3) is an exact one-locus stationary law in C026. The loci are not jointly independent, but every one-locus marginal remains exactly the known stationary law.

For two distinct uniformly sampled population sites under (4.3), the exact stationary heterozygosity is

\[
h_N
=\Pr\{X(U)\ne X(W)\mid U\ne W\}
=\frac{\alpha_N}{2\alpha_N+1}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For independent uniform sites `Q,R` (allowing equality), the mismatch probability is therefore

\[
\eta_N
:=\Pr\{X(Q)\ne X(R)\}
=\frac{N-1}{N}\frac{\alpha_N}{2\alpha_N+1}
\le \alpha_N.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The exact checker verifies the one-locus kernel identity and (4.4)--(4.5) in finite cases.

## 5. Move #19 — U010, a low-heterozygosity pedigree-coupling algorithm

The key is to couple C026 to the independent-parent comparison model and track the **expected** discrepancy budget rather than its worst-case size.

### Theorem U010 — low-heterozygosity shared-pedigree compiler

Consider two finite-population hierarchy models `X_N,Y_N` on `N` population sites with raw mesh `Delta_N`. Couple each locus from identical initial configurations. At every raw population update use the same victim and the same mutation bit at a given locus. Suppose `X_N` uses an independent uniform parent `Q`, while `Y_N` uses a marginally uniform parent `R`, with `Q` independent of `(R,Y_N)` conditional on the past, and suppose every one-locus marginal of `Y_N` is stationary with

\[
\eta_N=\Pr\{Y_N(Q)\ne Y_N(R)\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Assume visible block queries are uniform population samples and their total visible sample size in one raw generation is at most `k` on a `[k]` restriction. Then, for a fixed physical horizon `T`, with

\[
M_N(T)=\left\lceil\frac{T}{\Delta_N}\right\rceil+1,
\tag{5.2}
\]

there is a coupling satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{X_N^{[k]}\ne Y_N^{[k]}\text{ somewhere on }[0,T]\}
\le
\frac{k\eta_N M_N(T)(M_N(T)+1)}{2N}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Hence the sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\eta_N}{N\Delta_N^2}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

If `eta_N=O(Delta_N)`, it is enough that

\[
\boxed{N\Delta_N\to\infty.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

Fix a locus and let

\[
D_r=\{v:X_r(v)\ne Y_r(v)\},
\qquad d_r=|D_r|.
\]

Only the common victim coordinate is rewritten in one update. With the shared mutation bit cancelling from the comparison,

\[
d_{r+1}
=d_r-\mathbf 1_{\{V_r\in D_r\}}
+\mathbf 1_{\{X_r(Q_r)\ne Y_r(R_r)\}}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Use

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{X(Q)\ne Y(R)\}}
\le
\mathbf 1_{\{X(Q)\ne Y(Q)\}}
+
\mathbf 1_{\{Y(Q)\ne Y(R)\}}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Because `V_r` and `Q_r` are uniform and independent of the pre-update discrepancy set,

\[
E\mathbf 1_{\{V_r\in D_r\}}
=
E\frac{d_r}{N}
=
E\mathbf 1_{\{X_r(Q_r)\ne Y_r(Q_r)\}}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Those two terms cancel after expectation. By one-locus stationarity and (5.1),

\[
E d_{r+1}\le E d_r+\eta_N.
\]

Since `d_0=0`,

\[
\boxed{E d_r\le r\eta_N.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Now expose all visible population sites sampled by the `[k]` tree restriction through raw step `M`. If no sampled site ever belongs to the discrepancy set of its queried locus, the two visible hierarchy paths agree exactly. Conditional on the latent discrepancy array, a uniform block sample of size `b` has expected number `b d/N` of discrepant sampled sites. Hashing is uniform and independent of the latent arrays, and the current block sizes sum to `k`; by locus symmetry and (5.9), the expected number of discrepant visible samples at raw step `r` is at most

\[
\frac{k r\eta_N}{N}.
\]

Summing over `r=1,...,M` and applying Markov's inequality to the nonnegative count of sampled discrepancies gives (5.3). Equations (5.4)--(5.5) follow from `M_N(T)=O_T(Delta_N^{-1})`. `QED`.

This theorem improves U009 precisely because it uses the low-heterozygosity cancellation in (5.8), rather than the deterministic `d_r<=r` bound.

## 6. Apply U010 at the previous critical scale

For C026, (4.5) gives

\[
\eta_N\le\alpha_N=\Delta_N.
\]

Therefore U010 requires only

\[
N\Delta_N
=\frac{N}{\lceil\sqrt N\rceil}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This holds with order `sqrt(N)`. Thus, on every fixed `[k]` restriction and bounded physical interval, the C026 pedigree process and the independent-parent comparison process have path-law total-variation distance `O_{k,T}(N^{-1/2})` (up to harmless ceiling factors).

The comparison process still has the exact stationary one-block split probability for a specified unordered `i|j` support,

\[
p_N(i,j)
=
\frac{2(\alpha_N)_i(\alpha_N)_j}{(2\alpha_N)_{i+j}}
=
\Delta_N B(i,j)+O_{i+j}(\Delta_N^2).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Its finite locus pool is now much larger:

\[
L_N\Delta_N^2
=
\frac{N^2}{\lceil\sqrt N\rceil^2}
\sim N
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Hence the C023/U007 finite-pool birthday error vanishes, and the comparison one-step visible kernel satisfies

\[
\left\|
P_{N,\mathrm{cmp}}^{(k)}
-
(I+\Delta_N Q_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{(k)})
\right\|_{\rm TV}
=O_k(\Delta_N^2).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Over `O(1/Delta_N)` steps on a bounded interval the accumulated Euler error is `O_k(Delta_N)`, and the scaled geometric holding times converge to the CTCS exponential holding times. Therefore

\[
\widehat\Pi_{N,\mathrm{cmp}}^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\mathrm{CTCS}}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1.
\tag{6.5}

Combining (5.3), (6.1) and (6.5),

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_{N,\mathrm{C026}}^{[k]}

\quad\text{in }J_1

\tag{6.6}

for every fixed `k`. Exact restriction consistency and the usual projective metric tail then give

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_{N,\mathrm{C026}}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is the full exchangeable-partition genealogy limit, not a weaker marginal limit.

## 7. Move #30 — separate the two scales instead of forcing one bound to do everything

C026 exposes two independent obligations:

1. **finite-pool freshness** requires
   \[
   L_N\Delta_N^2\to\infty;
   \]
2. **shared-pedigree invisibility** under U010 requires
   \[
   \eta_N/(N\Delta_N^2)\to0.
   \]

With low mutation `eta_N=O(Delta_N)`, the second becomes `N Delta_N -> infinity`. Taking `Delta_N~N^{-1/2}` and `L_N=N^2` satisfies both strongly.

Thus the `N Delta_N^2` boundary seen by U009 was a **worst-case-discrepancy artifact**, not a genuine obstruction for low-heterozygosity populations. The refined argument pushes the natural unresolved scale down toward `Delta_N~1/N`, where `N Delta_N` is order one and a further cancellation/contraction mechanism would be needed.
