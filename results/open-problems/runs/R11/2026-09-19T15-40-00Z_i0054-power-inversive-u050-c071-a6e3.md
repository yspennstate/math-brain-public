## 3. Source specified-split generator

Fix a visible block of `m=i+j` labels and a specified **unordered** split `A|B` with `|A|=i`, `|B|=j`.

In CTCS, a size-`m` clade splits at rate `h_{m-1}` and its oriented split-size law is

\[
q(m,i)=\frac{m}{2h_{m-1}i(m-i)}.
\]

Summing the two left/right orientations of the specified split gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\rm CTCS}(A|B)
&=h_{m-1}\left[
\frac{q(m,i)}{\binom mi}+\frac{q(m,j)}{\binom mj}
\right]\\
&=\frac{m}{ij\binom mi}
=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(m-1)!}\\
&=:B(i,j)
=\int_0^1x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus convergence of every specified binary split rate to `B(i,j)` is exactly entrywise convergence of the finite-restriction off-diagonal generator.

## 4. U050 — power-inversive translation compiler

Fix an integer `r>=1`. Let `p -> infinity` range over primes satisfying

\[
p>r,\qquad \gcd(r,p-1)=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the permutation

\[
\phi_{p,r}(0)=0,\qquad
\phi_{p,r}(x)=x^{-r}\quad(x\ne0),
\tag{4.2}
\]

where values are represented by integers in `{0,...,p-1}`. Condition (4.1) makes `x -> x^{-r}` a permutation of `F_p^*`, hence (4.2) a permutation of all of `F_p`.

For every threshold `u=1,...,p-1`, let

\[
S_{p,r,u}=\{x\in F_p:\phi_{p,r}(x)<u\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The translation group acts by

\[
S_{p,r,u}-t,\qquad t\in F_p.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The key quantitative statement is the following.

### Theorem U050

Fix `m,r`. Uniformly over all distinct offsets `a_1,...,a_m in F_p`, the point set

\[
X_t=\left(
\frac{\phi_{p,r}(t+a_1)}p,\ldots,
\frac{\phi_{p,r}(t+a_m)}p
\right),\qquad t\in F_p,
\tag{4.5}
\]

has star discrepancy

\[
D_{p,r}^{(m)}
=O_{m,r}\!\left(p^{-1/2}(\log p)^m\right).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently the threshold-translation tree model of Section 6 has, for every fixed specified split `A|B`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{p,r}(A|B)
=B(|A|,|B|)
+O_{m,r}\!\left(p^{-1/2}(\log p)^{m+1}+p^{-1}\right).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

### Proof of (4.6)

Let `h=(h_1,...,h_m)` be a nonzero integer frequency vector with

\[
\|h\|_\infty\le (p-1)/2.
\]

Modulo `p`, not all coordinates vanish. After removing at most `m` exceptional translations `t=-a_j`, the additive-character phase is the rational function

\[
R_h(X)=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{h_j}{(X+a_j)^r}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Choose an index `j` with `h_j != 0 mod p`. Because the poles `-a_j` are distinct, `R_h` has a pole of **exact order `r`** at `-a_j`. Since `p>r`, this pole order is not divisible by `p`. Therefore `R_h` cannot have Artin--Schreier form `G^p-G+c`: at a pole of `G`, the term `G^p` has pole order divisible by `p` and dominates `G`.

The classical Weil bound for additive-character sums of a nondegenerate rational function therefore gives

\[
\left|
\sum_{t\in F_p}
 e_p\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^m h_j\phi_{p,r}(t+a_j)\right)
\right|
\le C_{m,r}\sqrt p+C_{m,r},
\tag{4.9}
\]

uniformly in the distinct offsets and the allowed nonzero frequency vector. The finitely many pole values contribute only the additive `O(m)` term.

Apply the `m`-dimensional Erdos--Turan--Koksma inequality with truncation `H=floor((p-1)/2)`. The standard harmonic frequency weight sum is `O_m((log p)^m)`. Dividing (4.9) by `p` and adding the `O(1/H)` truncation term gives (4.6). `square`

### Proof of (4.7)

Let `A,B` be disjoint nonempty visible label sets with `|A|=i`, `|B|=j`, `m=i+j`. For `x=u/p`, let `C_{A,B}(u)` be the number of translations for which every label of `A` lies below threshold `u` and every label of `B` lies above it. A general axis-parallel half-open box is a signed union of at most `2^m` anchored boxes, so (4.6) yields

\[
C_{A,B}(u)
=p x^i(1-x)^j
+O_{m,r}(pD_{p,r}^{(m)}),
\tag{4.10}
\]

uniformly in `u`; the same holds with `A,B` reversed.

Give every `(u,t)` proposal clock rate

\[
c_p(u,t)=\frac1{2u(p-u)}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The exact rate of the specified unordered split is

\[
Q_{p,r}(A|B)
=
\sum_{u=1}^{p-1}
\frac{C_{A,B}(u)+C_{B,A}(u)}{2u(p-u)}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

The volume part is

\[
\frac1{2p}
\sum_{u=1}^{p-1}
\left[
 x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}
+x^{j-1}(1-x)^{i-1}
\right]
=B(i,j)+O_m(p^{-1}),
\tag{4.13}
\]

by a fixed-polynomial Riemann sum. For the discrepancy part use

\[
\sum_{u=1}^{p-1}\frac1{u(p-u)}
=\frac{2H_{p-1}}p.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Combining (4.6), (4.10), and (4.14) gives

\[
O_{m,r}(D_{p,r}^{(m)}H_{p-1})
=O_{m,r}(p^{-1/2}(\log p)^{m+1}),
\]

which proves (4.7). `square`

## 5. B14: the translation action gives finite-tree validity and lumpability

Two deterministic facts are worth separating from the asymptotics.

First, because `phi_{p,r}` is injective, for any two distinct ambient labels `a != b` and any fixed translation `t`, the ranks `phi(t+a)` and `phi(t+b)` differ. A threshold between the two ranks separates the pair. Hence every nonsingleton clade has positive nontrivial split rate. The total proposal-clock rate per clade is finite and exact:

\[
\sum_{u=1}^{p-1}\sum_{t\in F_p}\frac1{2u(p-u)}
=H_{p-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore every finite `N` construction reaches singletons almost surely and defines a genuine discrete rooted binary tree after edge times are forgotten.

Second, project onto any fixed visible label set. A proposal's effect on those labels depends only on their ambient labels and on `(u,t)`, never on the hidden labels sharing the same current clade. A hidden-only split changes the hidden state but leaves every visible-changing hazard unchanged. By memorylessness of the fresh descendant clocks, the visible partition is therefore **strongly lumpable**: for each finite `N`, every visible restriction is itself a finite-state CTMC, with specified-split rates given exactly by (4.12).

This exact lumpability is the bridge from the discrete algebraic mask family to the whole partition genealogy.

## 7. B25: exact aggregate split-size identity at full ambient size

This is a useful exact theorem independent of the power map.

Let `phi` be **any permutation** of `F_p`. Use the threshold/translation masks

\[
\{\ell:\phi(t+\ell)<u\}
\]

and the rates (4.11) on the full `p`-leaf ambient set.

For each fixed threshold `u`, every mask has exactly `u` red labels because `phi` is a permutation. Thus for `1<=i<p/2`, aggregate root hazard for split size `{i,p-i}` gets contributions only from thresholds `u=i` and `u=p-i`; each threshold has `p` translations. Hence

\[
\Lambda_{p}(i,p-i)
=2p\frac1{2i(p-i)}
=\frac{p}{i(p-i)}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Count the same CTCS quantity by specified splits. There are `C(p,i)` unordered splits with smaller side `i`, and each has rate `B(i,p-i)`, so

\[
\binom pi B(i,p-i)
=\binom pi\frac{(i-1)!(p-i-1)!}{(p-1)!}
=\frac{p}{i(p-i)}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_p(i,p-i)
=\Lambda_{\rm CTCS,p}(i,p-i)
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

**exactly for every ambient permutation**, even a highly nonrandom one.

This explains why aggregate split-size checks can miss the difference. The correct finite non-equivalence invariant is the **labeled specified-split rate array**.
