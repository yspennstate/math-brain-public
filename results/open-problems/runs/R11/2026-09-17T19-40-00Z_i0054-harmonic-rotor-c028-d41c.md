## 3. Move #1 — change category: replace a random environment by a deterministic rotor

C027 still used a finite stochastic population and a random locus hash. Here there is **no population, no locus pool, and no random environment parameter**.

For every integer `N>=3`, set
\[
\Delta_N=N^{-3},\qquad m_N=N-1.
\]
A deterministic rotor has phases
\[
s=1,2,\ldots,N-1,1,2,\ldots
\]
and at phase `s` define
\[
x_{N,s}=\frac{s}{N},\qquad
w_{N,s}=\frac{N}{2s(N-s)}
       =\frac{1}{2N x_{N,s}(1-x_{N,s})},
\]
\[
c_{N,s}=(N-1)w_{N,s},\qquad
a_{N,s}=\Delta_N c_{N,s}.
\]

On a finite restriction `[k]`, at each raw step and for each current nonsingleton block `C`, independently:

1. activate `C` with probability `a_{N,s}`;
2. if activated, color the labels of `C` iid Bernoulli(`x_{N,s}`);
3. if both colors occur, replace `C` by the two color classes; if the coloring is monochromatic, leave it unchanged;
4. advance the global rotor phase by one.

One raw step has physical duration `Delta_N`.

This is a finite discrete, periodic, time-inhomogeneous fragmentation chain. The only randomness is in block activation and label colors. The environment itself is deterministic.

The activation probabilities are legitimate. Since
\[
c_{N,s}
=\frac{N(N-1)}{2s(N-s)}
\le \frac N2
\]
at the edge phases,
\[
a_{N,s}\le \frac{1}{2N^2}<1.
\]

The model is exchangeable in labels. Restriction consistency is exact: restricting an iid Bernoulli coloring to a smaller label set gives the same Bernoulli coloring law; a hidden split invisible on `[k]` leaves exactly one visible carrier block; and that carrier receives the same future phase-dependent activation/coloring law. Hence the family of `[k]` chains is projectively consistent.

## 5. Exact averaged split generator

Fix a visible block `C` of size
\[
b=i+j,\qquad 1\le i,j,
\]
and a specified **unordered** support `A|C\A` with `|A|=i`, `|C\A|=j`.

At phase `s`, conditional on activation, this exact support occurs with probability
\[
f_{N,s}(i,j)
=
x_{N,s}^{\,i}(1-x_{N,s})^j
+
x_{N,s}^{\,j}(1-x_{N,s})^i.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus its first-order raw-step rate is
\[
c_{N,s} f_{N,s}(i,j).
\]

Average over one deterministic rotor cycle. Since `c_{N,s}=(N-1)w_{N,s}`, the cycle-averaged rate is
\[
q_N(i,j)
=
\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}c_{N,s}f_{N,s}(i,j)
=
\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}w_{N,s}f_{N,s}(i,j).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Now substitute the harmonic weight:
\[
w_{N,s}x^i(1-x)^j
=
\frac1{2N}x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}.
\]
The second orientation becomes the same sum after the involution `s -> N-s`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
q_N(i,j)
=
\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
\left(\frac{s}{N}\right)^{i-1}
\left(1-\frac{s}{N}\right)^{j-1}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The integrand is a fixed polynomial for fixed `i,j`, so ordinary Riemann convergence gives
\[
\boxed{
q_N(i,j)\longrightarrow
\int_0^1x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\,dx
=
B(i,j).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

These are exactly the CTCS rates for a specified unordered binary split of a block.

There is also a useful uniform rate bound. Write
\[
p_b(x)=1-x^b-(1-x)^b.
\]
For `x<=1/2`,
\[
p_b(x)\le 1-(1-x)^b\le bx,
\]
and symmetrically for `x>=1/2`,
\[
p_b(x)\le b(1-x).
\]
Hence
\[
c_{N,s}p_b(x_{N,s})
=
\frac{N-1}{2N x(1-x)}p_b(x)
\le b.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Therefore on a `[k]` partition, the total first-order visible exit rate at every rotor phase is at most `k`, uniformly in `N,s`.

## 6. Whole-path convergence on every finite restriction

This section gives the theorem-sized convergence argument rather than only the rate heuristic.

Let `S_k` be the finite set of partitions of `[k]`. For rotor phase `s`, let `K_{N,s}^{(k)}` be the exact one-raw-step transition matrix. Define the continuous-time generator-like matrix `A_{N,s}^{(k)}` by assigning rate
\[
c_{N,s}f_{N,s}(i,j)
\]
to every transition that splits exactly one current block along one specified unordered support, and choose the diagonal so rows sum to zero.

By (5.5), uniformly in `N,s`,
\[
\|A_{N,s}^{(k)}\|_{\rm row,1}\le 2k.
\tag{6.1}
\]

In one raw step the sum of the visible split probabilities over the current blocks is at most `k Delta_N`. Hence the probability that two or more distinct current blocks split visibly in the same raw step is `O_k(Delta_N^2)`. Also, the exact probability of a specified single-block split differs from its first-order value by only the probability that another block also splits, again `O_k(Delta_N^2)`. Thus, uniformly over rows,
\[
\boxed{
K_{N,s}^{(k)}
=
I+\Delta_N A_{N,s}^{(k)}+E_{N,s}^{(k)},
\qquad
\|E_{N,s}^{(k)}\|_{\rm TV}\le C_k\Delta_N^2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### One-cycle kernel

Let
\[
\tau_N=(N-1)\Delta_N\sim N^{-2}
\]
be one rotor-cycle duration, and let
\[
C_N^{(k)}
=
K_{N,N-1}^{(k)}\cdots K_{N,1}^{(k)}.
\]

Expand the product using (6.1)--(6.2). The sum of the `E` terms is
\[
O_k(N\Delta_N^2),
\]
and all products containing at least two first-order factors have total row norm
\[
O_k\!\left((N\Delta_N)^2\right)=O_k(\tau_N^2).
\]
Because `N Delta_N^2=o(tau_N^2)` for `Delta_N=N^{-3}`, we obtain
\[
C_N^{(k)}
=
I+\Delta_N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}A_{N,s}^{(k)}
+O_k(\tau_N^2).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Define the cycle-averaged generator
\[
Q_N^{(k)}
=
\frac1{N-1}\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}A_{N,s}^{(k)}.
\]
Then (6.3) is
\[
\boxed{
C_N^{(k)}
=
I+\tau_NQ_N^{(k)}+O_k(\tau_N^2).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

By the exact calculation (5.3), every specified split rate in `Q_N^{(k)}` converges to the corresponding beta rate `B(i,j)`. Since `S_k` is finite,
\[
\boxed{
\|Q_N^{(k)}-Q_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}\|\to0.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Cycle-boundary chain

Couple the chain observed at cycle boundaries to the Euler chain with kernel
\[
I+\tau_NQ_N^{(k)}.
\]
Over at most `T/tau_N+1` cycles, (6.4) gives total coupling-failure probability
\[
O_{k,T}(\tau_N)\to0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Next couple the `Q_N` Euler chain to the `Q_CTCS` Euler chain. The per-cycle kernel discrepancy is
\[
\tau_N\|Q_N^{(k)}-Q_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}\|,
\]
so over `O(T/tau_N)` cycles the total discrepancy tends to zero by (6.5).

Finally, the Euler chain with mesh `tau_N` and fixed finite generator `Q_CTCS^{(k)}` converges in `J_1` to the CTCS finite-state continuous-time chain: a state's holding time is geometric with success probability `tau_N q+o(tau_N)`, so after multiplication by `tau_N` it converges to `Exp(q)`, and the normalized jump probabilities converge to the generator jump probabilities. A fragmentation of `[k]` has at most `k-1` proper jumps.

Thus the **cycle-boundary** process converges in `J_1`.

### Restore the within-cycle jump times

The actual rotor chain can jump inside a cycle. The probability of at least two visible jumps in one whole cycle is `O_k(tau_N^2)`: the total one-cycle visible hazard is `O_k(tau_N)` by the uniform rate bound, and two-jump terms are quadratic. Across `O(T/tau_N)` cycles,
\[
P(\text{some cycle has at least two visible jumps by }T)
=
O_{k,T}(\tau_N)\to0.
\tag{6.7}
\]

On the complementary event every cycle contains at most one visible jump. Move that jump to the cycle boundary. The partition sequence is unchanged and every jump time moves by at most `tau_N`; hence the `J_1` distance is at most `tau_N`.

Combining (6.6)--(6.7),
\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\qquad\text{in }J_1
}
\tag{6.8}
\]
for every fixed `k`.
