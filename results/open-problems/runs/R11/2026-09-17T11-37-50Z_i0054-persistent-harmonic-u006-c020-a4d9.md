## 3. Move #20 first — why a single *frozen* environment fails

The previous run's target was a one-process environment rather than another independently restarted genealogy. The first attempt is the strongest possible persistence: draw one harmonic environment once and freeze it forever.

For `N>=2`, write

\[
x_j=\frac jN,\qquad 1\le j\le N-1,
\]

\[
w_{N,j}=\frac{1}{2N x_j(1-x_j)}
=\frac{N}{2j(N-j)},
\qquad
H_N:=H_{N-1}=\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}\frac1r.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The partial-fraction identity

\[
\frac{N}{2j(N-j)}
=\frac12\left(\frac1j+\frac1{N-j}\right)
\]

gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}w_{N,j}=H_N.
\]

Hence

\[
\pi_{N,j}:=\frac{w_{N,j}}{H_N}
\tag{3.2}
\]

is a probability law.

Suppose a two-label block is repeatedly offered Bernoulli paintbox splits at physical rate `H_N` while `J` is frozen. Conditional on `J=j`, its split rate is

\[
\lambda_{N,j}=2H_Nx_j(1-x_j).
\tag{3.3}
\]

The annealed mean looks perfect:

\[
E_{\pi_N}\lambda_{N,J}
=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac1N
=\frac{N-1}{N}\longrightarrow1,
\tag{3.4}
\]

which is the CTCS two-leaf rate.

But this is a trap. For every fixed `eta>0`, if `lambda_{N,j}>eta`, then

\[
x_j>\frac{\eta}{2H_N},\qquad
1-x_j>\frac{\eta}{2H_N}.
\]

Putting `L_N=ceil(N eta/(2H_N))`, symmetry of (3.2) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_N(\lambda_{N,J}>\eta)
&\le \frac1{H_N}\sum_{j=L_N}^{N-L_N}\frac1j\\
&\le \frac{1+\log(N/L_N)}{H_N}
=O\!\left(\frac{\log H_N}{H_N}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{N,J}\to0\quad\text{in }\pi_N\text{-probability},}
\tag{3.6}
\]

while its expectation tends to one. The correct annealed first moment is carried by a vanishing set of high-rate environments.

Consequently, if the environment refresh probability `rho_N` is so slow that

\[
\rho_N/\Delta_N\to0,
\tag{3.7}
\]

then with probability tending to one there is no environmental refresh during a fixed physical interval. Conditional survival is asymptotic to `exp(-t lambda_{N,J})`, which tends to one in probability and hence in expectation. CTCS would give `exp(-t)`. So the frozen/too-slow algorithm fails already on two labels.

**Outcome of #20:** a persistent environment can work only if it genuinely self-averages on a physical time scale negligible compared with a tree split. Matching the stationary averaged generator by itself is not enough.

## 4. Move #3 — prove a stronger fast-environment compiler theorem U006

The obstruction suggests the right stronger statement. Instead of proving one model ad hoc, prove a finite-restriction averaging theorem that says exactly when persistence is harmless.

### Theorem U006 — fast refresh compiler for monotone fragmentation chains

Fix `k`. Let `S_k` be the finite set of partitions of `[k]`, ordered by refinement. Let `J_r` be a finite-state environment with stationary law `pi_N` and refresh kernel

\[
K_N=(1-\rho_N)I+\rho_N\Pi_N,
\qquad
(\Pi_Nf)(j)=\pi_N(f).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Suppose the visible partition can only refine and, conditional on environment state `j`, one raw step of duration `Delta_N` has expansion

\[
P_{N,j}^{(k)}
=I+\Delta_NA_{N,j}^{(k)}+R_{N,j}^{(k)},
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `A_{N,j}^{(k)}` is a conservative refinement generator, its total rate is at most `L_{N,k}`, and

\[
\sup_j\|R_{N,j}^{(k)}\|_{TV}
\le C_k\Delta_N^2L_{N,k}^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let

\[
\bar A_N^{(k)}:=E_{\pi_N}A_{N,J}^{(k)}.
\]

Assume entrywise

\[
\bar A_N^{(k)}\to Q^{(k)},
\tag{4.4}
\]

and for every state/test transition observable arising from `A`, its stationary second moment is bounded by `V_{N,k}`. If

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_NL_{N,k}^2\to0,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_NL_{N,k}}{\rho_N}\to0,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_NV_{N,k}}{\rho_N}\to0,
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

then the rescaled partition path converges in `J_1` to the continuous-time chain with generator `Q^{(k)}`. If the models are projectively sampling-consistent for every `k`, the compatible family converges in `d_proj` to the corresponding exchangeable-partition genealogy.

The theorem allows the environment at each post-jump epoch to have an arbitrary distribution; stationarity is not required after conditioning on a tree jump.

### Proof

The proof is finite-state and self-contained. Fix a centered environmental observable

\[
f_N(j)=a_N(j)-\pi_N(a_N)
\]

for one generator entry or total hazard. From (4.1),

\[
K_Nf_N=(1-\rho_N)f_N,
\qquad
K_N^hf_N=(1-\rho_N)^hf_N.
\tag{4.6}
\]

If the environment starts in stationarity,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(f_N(J_0),f_N(J_h))
=(1-\rho_N)^h\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_N}(f_N).
\tag{4.7}
\]

For `n<=T/Delta_N`, therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(
\Delta_N\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}f_N(J_r)
\right)
&\le
\Delta_N^2 n\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_N}(f_N)
\left(1+2\sum_{h\ge1}(1-\rho_N)^h\right)\\
&\le C T\frac{\Delta_NV_{N,k}}{\rho_N}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

For an arbitrary initial environmental state `j`, (4.6) gives the exact integrated bias

\[
\left|E_j\Delta_N\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}f_N(J_r)\right|
\le
\frac{\Delta_N\|f_N\|_\infty}{\rho_N}
\le C_k\frac{\Delta_NL_{N,k}}{\rho_N}
\to0.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Equivalently, couple after the first refresh time: the pre-refresh contribution has `L^2` size `O(Delta_N L_{N,k}/rho_N)`, and afterward the environment is exactly stationary. Thus (4.8)--(4.9) apply even after a tree jump has biased the environmental state.

The accumulated one-step remainder from (4.3) over `O(T/Delta_N)` steps is

\[
O_k(T\Delta_NL_{N,k}^2)=o(1).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Between visible fragmentation jumps the partition state is fixed, so (4.8)--(4.10) show that every integrated competing hazard is asymptotic to its stationary average. A `k`-label refinement path has at most `k-1` proper jump epochs. Inducting over those finitely many epochs identifies both each holding-time law and each conditional jump type with the chain generated by `Q^{(k)}`. This yields `J_1` convergence. The projective conclusion follows by truncating the deterministic tail of `d_proj`. QED.

The finite-jump monotonicity is important: U006 is deliberately stated only at the strength needed here rather than hiding a general averaging theorem behind a citation.

## 6. C020 — persistent harmonic-grid Markov-environment hierarchy

Set

\[
\Delta_N=N^{-3},
\qquad
\rho_N=N^{-1},
\qquad
H_N=H_{N-1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Use the grid, weights and stationary law from (3.1)--(3.2). Let a **single global environment**

\[
J_r\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
\]

run through all hierarchy generations and be shared by all current blocks. Start `J_0~pi_N`. After each raw generation, independently with probability `rho_N` redraw `J` from `pi_N`, and otherwise retain its current value. Thus

\[
K_N=(1-\rho_N)I+\rho_N\Pi_N.
\]

Given the current value `J_r=j`, put `x=x_j=j/N`. Every current nonsingleton block independently activates with probability

\[
a_N:=\Delta_NH_N.
\tag{6.2}
\]

If active, color each label in that block independently Bernoulli(`x`). If both colors occur, split the block into the two color classes; if the coloring is monochromatic, leave it unchanged. One raw generation has physical duration `Delta_N`.

This is a fully discrete-time finite model. It is binary at every finite `N`. The environment is not restarted when the tree splits and is not independently redrawn at every paintbox proposal; it persists across the entire evolving hierarchy.

### Exact sampling consistency through hidden labels

Take a visible block `C` of size `b` contained in an arbitrary hidden block. Conditional on `J_r=j`, the color of each label is iid Bernoulli(`x_j`). Restricting the coloring to `C` is therefore exactly the same Bernoulli paintbox, regardless of the number or colors of hidden labels.

Hence for a particular visible split `A|C\A`, `|A|=i`, the exact one-step probability conditional on `J_r=j` is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_NH_N
\left[x_j^i(1-x_j)^{b-i}

\tag{6.3}

## 7. The stationary averaged generator is exactly the harmonic-grid generator

Write

\[
s_{b,i}(x)=x^i(1-x)^{b-i}+x^{b-i}(1-x)^i.
\]

The physical conditional rate corresponding to (6.3) is

\[
r_{N,j}(b,i)=H_Ns_{b,i}(x_j).
\]

Averaging with respect to `pi_N` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar q_N(b,i)
&=H_N\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\pi_{N,j}s_{b,i}(x_j)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}w_{N,j}s_{b,i}(x_j)\\
&=\frac1{2N}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}
\frac{s_{b,i}(x_j)}{x_j(1-x_j)}\\
&=\boxed{
\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}
x_j^{i-1}(1-x_j)^{b-i-1}

\tag{7.1}

The last equality reflects `j <-> N-j` in the second orientation. This is exactly C001's harmonic-grid Riemann generator, but it is only the **stationary average** of the new persistent process.

For fixed `b,i`, the integrand in (7.1) is a polynomial on `[0,1]`, hence

\[

\longrightarrow
\int_0^1x^{i-1}(1-x)^{b-i-1}\,dx
=\boxed{B(i,b-i)}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For each fixed `k`, the convergence is uniform over the finitely many visible split types.
