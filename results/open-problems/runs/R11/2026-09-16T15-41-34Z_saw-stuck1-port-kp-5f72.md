## 2. Exact source formulation and dated current-status audit

### Flory 1949

Paul J. Flory, *The Configuration of Real Polymer Chains*, J. Chem. Phys. **17** (1949), 303--310, DOI `10.1063/1.1747243`. The paper is the excluded-volume polymer-scaling origin. Its abstract says that if the average linear dimension is a power of chain length then the exponent should exceed the random-flight value `1/2` and approach `0.60` for long chains in good solvent. I do **not** attribute the exact planar lattice value `3/4` directly to Flory 1949.

### Nienhuis 1982

Bernard Nienhuis, *Exact Critical Point and Critical Exponents of O(n) Models in Two Dimensions*, Phys. Rev. Lett. **49** (1982), 1062--1065, DOI `10.1103/PhysRevLett.49.1062`. The published abstract explicitly says the analytic calculation is “subject to some plausible assumptions.” Its `n -> 0` SAW specialization is the standard two-dimensional prediction

\[
\nu=\frac34,
\qquad
\gamma=\frac{43}{32}.
\]

For the original uniform nearest-neighbor `n`-step SAW `omega` on `Z^2`, the primary metric prediction is

\[
\mathbb E_n\|\omega_n\|_2^2=n^{3/2+o(1)}.
\]

The separate count prediction is

\[
c_n=\mu^n n^{11/32+o(1)}.
\]

Neither is treated as proved.

### Duminil-Copin--Hammond, arXiv:1205.0401

Current arXiv abstract checked on 2026-09-16. It uses exactly the required law: `SAW_n` is the **uniform** measure on nearest-neighbor self-avoiding walks `gamma:{0,...,n}->Z^d` with `gamma_0=0`. For every fixed `v>0`, there exists `c(v)>0` such that for every positive integer `n`,

\[
\Pr_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\!\left(
\max_{0\le k\le n}\|\omega_k\|_2>vn
\right)
\le e^{-c(v)n}.
\tag{DCH}
\]

Their result is fixed-speed sub-ballisticity. It does not give a uniform modulus for `c(v)` as `v downarrow 0`, and their own Question 1 asks for any fixed-power improvement below the trivial squared exponent `2`.

## 4. (a) Exact blocking statement

The cleanest missing uniform estimate identified by the three canonical R11 runs is:

\[
\boxed{
\exists c_0>0,\ v_0>0,\ n_0<\infty,\ \kappa<\infty
\quad\forall n\ge n_0,\ 0<v\le v_0:
\quad
\Pr_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}(R_n>vn)
\le
\exp(-c_0 n v^\kappa),
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

where

\[
R_n=\max_{0\le k\le n}\|\omega_k\|_2.

Retained `R11-GH-C001`, independently audited by R10, shows that any finite `kappa` in (BLOCK) would move the metric below `2`; `kappa<4` is enough to obtain upper exponent `3/2` by that compiler.

## 6. (c) What a successful proof must contain that the tried routes do not

A metric-moving proof needs a mechanism that stays quantitative when the observation speed tends to zero with `n`. At least one of the following genuinely new ingredients is necessary:

1. a **one-shot or bounded-depth** multivalued bridge/polygon map whose logarithmic forward-to-inverse multiplicity is at least `c n v^kappa` for some finite `kappa`, instead of composing `Theta(1/v)` shrinking thresholds;
2. a square-lattice critical bridge-height power bound together with a quantitative bridge-to-uniform-SAW transfer that replaces the hexagonal half-plane two-point summability input;
3. a direct variable-speed tail bound at one polynomial scale, bypassing the full all-`v` modulus.

Every current R11 route is missing exactly this uniform cross-scale ingredient: the DCH proof is qualitative in `v`, the quantitative unfolding compiler loses too much over `Theta(1/v)` stages, and existing square-lattice polygon insertion controls enumeration rather than displacement.

## 7. (d) Simplest concrete unproved special case

A deliberately modest source-matching special case that would already move the fleet metric is:

\[
\boxed{
\exists c,\delta>0,\ n_0<\infty:
\quad
\Pr_{\mathrm{SAW}_n}\!\left(R_n\ge n^{7/8}\right)
\le e^{-c n^\delta}
\quad\forall n\ge n_0.

\tag{SIMPLE}
\]

Indeed, since `||omega_n||<=R_n<=n`, (SIMPLE) would give

\[

for all sufficiently large `n`, hence the rigorous metric would move from `a=2` to at most `a=7/4`.

No such bound is claimed here; (SIMPLE) is the simplest fixed target selected for the recovery analysis.

## 8. (e) Nearest solved analogue and the ingredient it has that square-lattice SAW lacks

The nearest solved quantitative analogue is Krachun--Panagiotis, 2026, on the hexagonal lattice. Their main theorem gives `O(n/log n)` displacement with high probability. Their key intermediate theorem gives polynomial decay of the critical partition function `B_T` of bridges of height `T`.

Their proof has two integrable inputs not presently available for ordinary square-lattice nearest-neighbor SAW:

- the hexagonal critical parafermionic observable, satisfying a local Cauchy--Riemann-type relation, which yields nontrivial identities/comparisons among bridge, triangle and half-plane `U`-walk partition functions;
- summability of the half-plane boundary two-point function, explicitly stated in their paper to be known there via the parafermionic observable.

They then combine these inputs with bridge renewal structure and geometric endpoint redirection to obtain a recurrence and polynomial decay. This is exactly the kind of cross-scale quantitative information absent from the current DCH-only square-lattice compiler.

A second, square-lattice-adjacent solved technique is the 2020 polygon-insertion improvement of Hammersley--Welsh counts. It shows that polygon surgery can beat classical counting entropy on `Z^2`, but its established output is not the variable-speed endpoint tail required by (SIMPLE)/(BLOCK).
