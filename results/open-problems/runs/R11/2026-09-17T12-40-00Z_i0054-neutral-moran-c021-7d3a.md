## 3. Move #27 — swap the quantifiers: one population for every block

The preceding classes C015--C019 restarted a fresh genealogy inside each hierarchy block, while C020 used one persistent but deliberately engineered harmonic environment. Here I swap the quantifiers.

Instead of

> for each current tree block, generate an environment that produces the right split law,

construct

> **one global finite population whose evolving allele configuration is shared by every current tree block, at every time.**

No population is restarted when the tree fragments.

For the `N`-leaf discrete model set

\[
\alpha_N=N^{-2},
\qquad
\mu_N=\frac{\alpha_N}{N+2\alpha_N},
\qquad
\Delta_N=N^{-14},
\qquad
\varepsilon_N=\frac{\Delta_N}{\alpha_N}=N^{-12}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The global environment consists of `N` population sites, each colored red or blue. It evolves by the following neutral Moran update once per raw hierarchy generation:

1. choose a parent site uniformly;
2. copy its color and flip the copied color independently with probability `mu_N`;
3. choose a victim site uniformly, independently of the parent, and replace the victim by the offspring.

Let `J_r` be the number of red sites before the hierarchy observations at raw step `r`.

Start the population in its stationary exchangeable law. At raw step `r`, **every current nonsingleton tree block independently activates with probability `epsilon_N`**. If block `B` activates, choose a uniformly random injection

\[
\phi_B:B\hookrightarrow[N]
\]

independently of all other block injections conditional on the current population. Split `B` into the labels seeing red and the labels seeing blue if both colors occur; if the sample is monochromatic, do nothing. After all tree-block observations, perform one Moran population update. Give a raw hierarchy generation physical duration `Delta_N`.

The same population is shared by all blocks, and persists through every tree split. The independent block clocks are only observation clocks; they do not reset or resample the population.

Each successful visible split is binary. Several distinct current blocks can activate in one raw generation, but that probability will be negligible on the CTCS scale.

## 4. Move #7 — take the low-mutation boundary and recover the beta kernel

### 4.1 Exact finite Moran count chain

Write `alpha=alpha_N`. From the update rule,

\[
p_j^+
=
\Pr(J_{r+1}=j+1\mid J_r=j)
=
\frac{(N-j)(j+\alpha)}{N(N+2\alpha)},
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
p_j^-
=
\Pr(J_{r+1}=j-1\mid J_r=j)
=
\frac{j(N-j+\alpha)}{N(N+2\alpha)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The relation between the mutation and beta-binomial parameters is exactly

\[
\alpha=\frac{N\mu}{1-2\mu},
\qquad
\mu=\frac{\alpha}{N+2\alpha}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Define

\[
\pi_{N,\alpha}(j)
=
\binom Nj
\frac{(\alpha)_j(\alpha)_{N-j}}{(2\alpha)_N},
\qquad 0\le j\le N.
\tag{4.4}
\]

A one-line ratio computation gives

\[
\pi_jp_j^+
=
\pi_{j+1}p_{j+1}^-,
\tag{4.5}
\]

so (4.4) is stationary. The full population chain is permutation-equivariant; conditional on `J=j` the uniform law on configurations with `j` red sites is preserved. Thus, when started from (4.4), its stationary configuration law is exchangeable.

The conditional mean is affine:

\[
E[J_{r+1}\mid J_r=j]
=
(1-\gamma_N)j+\gamma_N\frac N2,
\qquad
\gamma_N
=
\frac{2\alpha_N}{N(N+2\alpha_N)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For the chosen `alpha_N=N^{-2}`,

\[
\boxed{\gamma_N=\frac{2}{N^4+2N}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

### 4.2 Exact stationary split law

Fix a visible tree block of size `b=i+j` and a specified unordered split `A|A^c` with `|A|=i`. Conditional on `J=s`, uniform injection without replacement gives

\[
r_{N,s}(i,j)
=
\frac{
(s)_{\underline i}(N-s)_{\underline j}
+
(s)_{\underline j}(N-s)_{\underline i}
}{
(N)_{\underline b}
}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Under the beta-binomial stationary law, sampling any `b` distinct population sites has the beta-Bernoulli predictive distribution. Equivalently, summing (4.8) against (4.4) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
E_{\pi_{N,\alpha}}r_{N,J}(i,j)
=
\frac{2(\alpha)_i(\alpha)_j}{(2\alpha)_b}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Since an activated block is offered at physical rate

\[
\varepsilon_N/\Delta_N=1/\alpha_N,
\]

the stationary averaged physical rate of this specified split is

\[
\bar q_N(i,j)
=
\frac1{\alpha_N}
\frac{2(\alpha_N)_i(\alpha_N)_j}{(2\alpha_N)_b}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Divide by the CTCS rate `B(i,j)`. Exactly,

\[
\frac{\bar q_N(i,j)}{B(i,j)}
=
\frac{
\prod_{r=1}^{i-1}(1+\alpha_N/r)
\prod_{r=1}^{j-1}(1+\alpha_N/r)
}{
\prod_{r=1}^{b-1}(1+2\alpha_N/r)
}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Therefore, for every fixed `b`,

\[
\boxed{
\bar q_N(i,j)
=
B(i,j)\{1+O_b(\alpha_N)\}
=
B(i,j)+O_b(N^{-2}).
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

This is the central limit mechanism. The critical beta kernel appears as the **low-mutation boundary of a finite neutral population's stationary predictive split law**.

### 4.3 Exact hidden-label consistency

The construction does not depend on the hidden population of tree labels. If a current hidden tree block contains a visible subset `C`, then restricting a uniform injection of the hidden block to `C` is a uniform injection of `C`. If a hidden split is invisible on `C`, exactly one hidden child continues to carry `C`, and at the next raw generation it again has one activation coin with the same law. Distinct visible blocks sit in distinct hidden carrier blocks, whose activation coins are independent conditional on the common Moran population.

Thus restriction to `[k]` is exactly the `k`-visible-label dynamics driven by the same size-`N` Moran environment. No asymptotic hidden-label cancellation is needed.

## 5. Fast self-averaging of the one persistent population

A correct stationary average is not enough: C020 showed that a frozen boundary-heavy environment can have the right mean and the wrong path law. So the new obligation is a **quenched-to-annealed averaging estimate valid after environment-biased tree jumps**.

### 5.1 Monotone coupling and exact contraction

For a birth-death kernel, stochastic monotonicity follows if adjacent kernels do not cross. Here it is enough to check

\[
p_j^+ + p_{j+1}^-\le1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Direct algebra gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&N(N+2\alpha)
-\{(N-j)(j+\alpha)+(j+1)(N-j-1+\alpha)\}\\
&\qquad
=
2\left(j-\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^2
+\frac{N^2+2N\alpha-2\alpha+1}{2}
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence there is an order-preserving coupling. Because the conditional mean is affine, the monotone coupling gives, for `s<t`,

\[
W_1(K_N(s,\cdot),K_N(t,\cdot))
=
(1-\gamma_N)(t-s).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Iterating and coupling against stationarity,

\[
W_1(K_N^r(s,\cdot),\pi_N)
\le
N(1-\gamma_N)^r.
\tag{5.4}
\]

### 5.2 Split observables are Lipschitz

Changing the red count from `s` to `s+1` can be coupled by recoloring one uniformly chosen population site. A uniform injection of `b` visible labels hits that distinguished site with probability `b/N`. Thus

\[
|r_{N,s+1}(i,j)-r_{N,s}(i,j)|
\le \frac bN.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For the physical generator entry

\[
a_N(s)=\frac{r_{N,s}(i,j)}{\alpha_N},
\tag{5.6}
\]

\[
{\rm Lip}(a_N)\le\frac{b}{\alpha_NN}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Let `f_N=a_N-pi_N(a_N)`. From (5.4),

\[
|K_N^rf_N(s)|
\le
\frac b{\alpha_N}(1-\gamma_N)^r.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Therefore the Poisson series

\[
g_N=\sum_{r\ge0}K_N^rf_N
\tag{5.9}
\]

converges and satisfies

\[
(I-K_N)g_N=f_N,
\qquad
\boxed{\|g_N\|_\infty\le\frac{b}{\alpha_N\gamma_N}.}
\tag{5.10}
\]

### 5.3 A maximal estimate from arbitrary environment states

Define martingale differences

\[
D_{r+1}=g_N(J_{r+1})-K_Ng_N(J_r).
\]

Then for every `m`,

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}f_N(J_r)
=
g_N(J_0)-g_N(J_m)
+
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}D_{r+1}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

For `n<=T/Delta_N`, Doob's inequality and (5.10) give, uniformly in the initial environment state,

\[
E\max_{m\le n}
\left|
\Delta_N\sum_{r<m}f_N(J_r)
\right|^2
\le
C_{b,T}
\left[
\frac{\Delta_N}{\alpha_N^2\gamma_N^2}
+
\frac{\Delta_N^2}{\alpha_N^2\gamma_N^2}
\right].
\tag{5.12}
\]

The deterministic Poisson boundary term is at most

\[
2\Delta_N\|g_N\|_\infty
\le
\frac{2b\Delta_N}{\alpha_N\gamma_N}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

With the chosen parameters,

\[
\frac{\Delta_N}{\alpha_N^2\gamma_N^2}
=
\frac{(N^4+2N)^2}{4N^{10}}
=O(N^{-2}),
\tag{5.14}
\]

\[
\frac{\Delta_N}{\alpha_N\gamma_N}
=
\frac{N^4+2N}{2N^{12}}
=O(N^{-8}).
\tag{5.15}
\]

Hence all integrated generator entries self-average uniformly on compact physical-time intervals, **even after conditioning on previous tree jumps has biased the current Moran state**. This is the point that a merely stationary covariance estimate would not be enough to prove.

The same argument applies simultaneously to the finitely many generator entries and total hazards of any fixed `[k]` restriction, with constants depending only on `k`.
