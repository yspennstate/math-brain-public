## 3. Move #15 — the symmetric `theta=1` permutation model is the wrong scale

Start with the most symmetric candidate: at each ancestral step take the cycle partition of a **uniform random permutation**, i.e. Ewens parameter `theta=1`.

This fails the required infinitesimal regime immediately. Two specified lineages lie in the same cycle with probability

\[
\Pr_\theta(1\sim2)=\frac1{\theta+1}.
\]

At `theta=1` this is `1/2`, while three specified lineages are all in one cycle with probability

\[
\frac{2}{(\theta+1)(\theta+2)}=\frac13.
\]

Thus binary and multiple ancestral mergers both occur with order-one probability in one discrete step. There is no vanishing mesh from which the Kingman branch-potential compiler can produce the CTCS infinitesimal split kernel.

The productive symmetry break is therefore **not** to keep the uniform-permutation measure. Weight a permutation by `theta^{#cycles}` and send `theta -> infinity`. This favors many cycles; the identity partition becomes dominant, pair mergers become first-order rare events, and all higher merger patterns become second order.

This is the actual #15 move: the maximally symmetric `theta=1` point hides the useful tangent, while the highly cycle-biased regime exposes it.

## 4. Move #5 — add the Ewens parameter and differentiate at the rare-merger boundary

For a set partition `pi` of `[r]` with blocks of sizes `n_1,...,n_k`, define the Ewens partition law

\[
\boxed{
P_\theta(\Pi_r=\pi)
=\frac{\theta^k}{\theta^{\overline r}}
\prod_{a=1}^k(n_a-1)!,
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
\theta^{\overline r}=\theta(\theta+1)\cdots(\theta+r-1).
\]

This can be realized as the cycle partition of an Ewens-distributed random permutation or by the Chinese-restaurant process. The following facts are proved directly here rather than imported as black boxes.

### 4.1 Exact sampling consistency

Let `pi` be a partition of `[r]` with `k` blocks of sizes `n_1,...,n_k`. A partition of `[r+1]` restricts to `pi` either by making `r+1` a singleton or by adjoining it to one of the `k` blocks. Summing those possibilities gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&P_{r+1}(\Pi|_{[r]}=\pi)\\
&=\frac{\theta^{k+1}\prod_a(n_a-1)!
+\theta^k\sum_{a=1}^k n_a!\prod_{b\ne a}(n_b-1)!}
{\theta^{\overline{r+1}}}\\
&=\frac{\theta^k\prod_a(n_a-1)!(\theta+\sum_an_a)}
{\theta^{\overline r}(\theta+r)}\\
&=P_r(\pi).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus deletion consistency is exact at every finite `theta`.

### 4.2 Pair collision and the first derivative

Two specified labels lie in the same Ewens block with exact probability

\[
\boxed{c_\theta=\frac1{\theta+1}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For a **specified pair** to form one two-block while all other `r-2` labels remain singleton, (4.1) gives

\[
p_{r,\theta}^{(2)}
=\frac{\theta^{r-1}}{\theta^{\overline r}}
=c_\theta\prod_{j=2}^{r-1}\frac{\theta}{\theta+j}
=c_\theta+O_r(c_\theta^2).
\tag{4.4}
\]

If a partition has deficiency

\[
d=r-\#\text{blocks}\ge2,
\]

then (4.1) is `O_r(theta^{-d})=O_r(c_theta^2)`. Since for fixed `r` there are finitely many set partitions, the total probability of all triple mergers, two simultaneous pair mergers, or more complicated mergers is `O_r(c_theta^2)`.

Consequently, on the finite partition state space,

\[
\boxed{
K_{\theta,r}=I+c_\theta G_r+O_r(c_\theta^2),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `G_r` is the Kingman generator normalized so that each specified pair merges at rate one.

Equivalently, writing

\[
q=c_\theta=\frac1{\theta+1},
\qquad \theta=\frac{1-q}{q},
\]

we have the genuine boundary derivative

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{q\downarrow0}\frac{K_{q,r}-I}{q}=G_r.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

So the Kingman generator is the first derivative of the Ewens ancestral-partition kernel at the large-`theta` boundary.

\[

\qquad

\tag{6.1}
\]

For every current nonsingleton hierarchy block `B`, independently in every raw hierarchy generation:

1. Start with one active ancestral lineage for each label of `B`.
2. Before each ancestral coarsening step, independently mark every active lineage with probability `nu_N`.
3. A mark on a lineage records the subset `S subset B` of present labels descending from that lineage.
4. Sample an independent Ewens(`theta_N`) partition of the **currently active lineages**, and merge lineages lying in the same Ewens block.

One raw hierarchy generation has physical duration `delta_N`.

The model is entirely discrete. ## 7. Whole-path convergence to CTCS(infinity)

The previous R11 report proved `R11-I0054-U005`, the **Kingman-domain ancestral compiler theorem**. I restate enough of the argument here to make the C019 application self-contained.

Suppose an exchangeable sampling-consistent ancestral chain on a fixed `m`-sample has

\[
K_{N,m}=I+c_NG_m+O_m(c_N^2),
\qquad c_N\to0,
\tag{7.1}
\]

and if `J_{N,m}` denotes the total active-lineage marking opportunities before the MRCA, assume

\[
c_N^2\,EJ_{N,m}^2=O_m(1).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Mark each opportunity with probability `nu_N=delta_N c_N/2`, where `delta_N->0`, and take the common refinement of all marked descendant bipartitions.

### 7.1 The potential gives the beta kernel

In Kingman's `m`-coalescent, the expected total branch length subtending exactly `i` leaves is

\[
E L_{m,i}=\frac2i.
\]

By exchangeability, a specified `i`-subset `A` has branch potential

\[
\frac{2}{i\binom mi}.
\]

For the unordered split `A|A^c`, `j=m-i`, either orientation can carry the mark, so the total Kingman potential is

\[
\frac{2}{i\binom mi}
+\frac{2}{j\binom mj}
=2B(i,j).
\tag{7.3}
\]

The finite-state perturbation (7.1) implies for the transient fundamental matrix

\[
c_N(I-K_{N,m})^{-1}
=(-G_m)^{-1}+O_m(c_N).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Hence the corresponding discrete ancestral opportunity mass for a specified unordered `i|j` split is

\[
\frac{2}{c_N}B(i,j)+O_m(1).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Multiplying by `nu_N=delta_N c_N/2` gives

\[
\boxed{
P_N(A\mid A^c\text{ generated by one relevant mark})
=\delta_NB(i,j)+O_m(\delta_Nc_N).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This is exactly the CTCS particular-split rate to first order.

### 7.2 Multiple marks are negligible

For C019, while at least two visible ancestral lineages remain, two selected lineages lie in the same next Ewens block with probability exactly `c_N`. Therefore the waiting time until **some** strict lineage-count decrease is stochastically dominated by a geometric(`c_N`) variable. There are at most `m-1` strict decreases before the MRCA. If `T_{N,m}` is the number of ancestral steps to absorption,

\[
T_{N,m}\preceq\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}G_a,
\qquad G_a\sim\operatorname{Geom}(c_N),
\]

and `J_{N,m}<=mT_{N,m}`. Thus

\[
\boxed{c_N^2EJ_{N,m}^2=O_m(1).}
\tag{7.7}
\]

If `M` is the number of relevant marks in one genealogy,

\[
P(M\ge2)
\le\frac{\nu_N^2}{2}EJ_{N,m}^2
=O_m(\delta_N^2).
\tag{7.8}
\]

### 7.3 Exact restriction consistency

Equation (4.2) proves that one Ewens partition step restricts exactly to an Ewens partition step on any visible subset. Iterating the construction preserves that property. Marks on hidden-only lineages disappear under restriction, while marks on visible-carrying lineages remain independent Bernoulli(`nu_N`) marks. Therefore arbitrary hidden labels do **not** change the law of the visible ancestral chain.

This is stronger than an averaged hidden-label cancellation: finite restrictions are exact pathwise projective marginals of the same ancestral mechanism.

### 7.4 Finite-restriction Euler approximation

For every fixed `[k]`, uniformly conditional on the entire hidden history,

\[
\boxed{
\left\|
P_N^{(k)}(\rho,\cdot)
-\left(I+\delta_NQ_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}\right)(\rho,\cdot)
\right\|_{\rm TV}
=O_k(\delta_Nc_N+\delta_N^2).
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

Here

\[
\delta_N=\frac1N,
\qquad c_N=\frac1{N+1},
\]

so the one-step discrepancy is `O_k(N^{-2})`. On any fixed physical interval `[0,T]` there are `O(T/delta_N)=O(N)` raw hierarchy generations. Iterated conditional maximal coupling therefore yields

\[
P(\text{visible coupling failure before }T)
=O_{k,T}(c_N+\delta_N)
=O_{k,T}(N^{-1})\to0.
\tag{7.10}
\]

The comparison Euler chain `I+delta_N Q_CTCS^{(k)}` has `delta_N`-scaled geometric holding times converging to the corresponding exponentials and exactly the CTCS jump kernel. A `k`-label fragmentation has at most `k-1` proper visible refinements. Hence the full jump-state / holding-time sequence converges, giving

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
}
\tag{7.11}
\]

in Skorokhod `J_1` for every fixed `k`.

Finally the projective metric and finite-tail truncation give

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\operatorname{CTCS}(\infty)}.
}
\tag{7.12}
\]

Thus C019 proves convergence of the **whole exchangeable-partition genealogy**, not a tagged or one-dimensional surrogate.
