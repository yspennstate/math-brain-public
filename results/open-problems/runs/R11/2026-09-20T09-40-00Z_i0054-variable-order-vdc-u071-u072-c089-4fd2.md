The preceding run left an apparent analytic boundary at `b_N ~ N^3`. Instead of adding another bespoke tree lemma, I moved the entire obstruction into classical analytic-number-theory language: a higher-derivative exponential-sum problem for the logit phase.

The key input is the standard p-th derivative form of van der Corput's estimate. If `f` is `p` times continuously differentiable, `p>=2`, and

\[
\lambda\le f^{(p)}\le\mu\lambda,
\]

then with `P=2^{p-1}`,

\[
\sum e(f(n))
\ll
M\mu^{2/P}\lambda^{1/(2P-2)}
+
M^{1-2/P}\lambda^{-1/(2P-2)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

A convenient published formulation is Lemma 2.8 of Ned Carmichael (JLMS 2026), citing the standard Theorem 8.20; importantly, that formulation states that the implied constants are independent of `p`.

# 4. Model and exact continuum generator

Retain the compact-logit hierarchy from U068--U070. Put

\[
L=\log4,
\qquad
\sigma(y)=\frac{e^y}{1+e^y},
\qquad
\ell(x)=\log\frac{x}{1-x}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For each positive integer `b_N`, define

\[
S_N(y)=
\begin{cases}
L\left\{\dfrac{b_Ny}{L}\right\},&0\le y<L,\\[2mm]
y,&y\notin[0,L),
\end{cases}
\qquad
\tau_N(x)=\sigma(S_N(\ell(x))).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every nonsingleton clade has fresh independent harmonic-grid clocks

\[
\lambda_{N,s}
=
\frac{N}{2s(N-s)}
=
\frac1{2N x_s(1-x_s)},
\quad
x_s=s/N,
\quad 1\le s\le N-1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

When clock `s` rings, each leaf is independently red with probability `tau_N(x_s)` and blue otherwise. A nontrivial coloring splits the clade; a monochromatic proposal is null. Descendant clocks and colors are fresh.

For a specified unordered visible split with sizes `i,j>=1`,

\[
q_N(i,j)
=
\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}F_{N;i,j}(s/N),
\tag{4.4}
\]

where

\[
F_{N;i,j}(x)=
\frac{
\tau_N(x)^i(1-\tau_N(x))^j
+
\tau_N(x)^j(1-\tau_N(x))^i
}{2x(1-x)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Integer multiplication modulo `L` preserves Lebesgue measure on `[0,L)`, and `S_N` is the identity outside. Since `dx=x(1-x)dy` under `x=sigma(y)`, the **continuum** rate is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^1F_{N;i,j}(x)\,dx
=B(i,j)
=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

for every integer `b_N`. Only harmonic-grid aliasing remains.

# 5. Weighted Fourier reduction

On the active interval

\[
I=[1/2,4/5),
\]

write

\[
A(x)=\frac1{2x(1-x)},
\]

and

\[
H_{i,j}(t)
=
\sigma(Lt)^i(1-\sigma(Lt))^j
+
\sigma(Lt)^j(1-\sigma(Lt))^i,
\quad 0\le t<1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then the active contribution to (4.4) is

\[
\frac1N\sum_{s/N\in I}
A(s/N)
H_{i,j}\!\left(\left\{\frac{b_N}{L}g(s/N)\right\}\right),
\qquad
g(x)=\log\frac{x}{1-x}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Define the finite positive measure on the circle

\[
\nu_N
=
\frac1N\sum_{s/N\in I}
A(s/N)
\delta_{\{b_Ng(s/N)/L\}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Its zero Fourier coefficient tends by ordinary Riemann sums to

\[
\int_I A(x)dx=L/2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus it is enough to prove that for every fixed nonzero integer `r`,

\[
\widehat\nu_N(r)
=
\frac1N\sum_{s/N\in I}
A(s/N)e^{2\pi i r b_N g(s/N)/L}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because `A` has bounded variation on `I`, Abel summation reduces (5.5) to a uniform `o(N)` bound for the corresponding **unweighted** exponential sum over every initial subinterval of the active grid.

Fourier convergence then gives

\[
\nu_N\Rightarrow (L/2)\,\mathrm{Leb}_{\mathbb T}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Although `H_{i,j}` may have one endpoint jump when regarded as a circle function, it is bounded and its discontinuity set has Lebesgue measure zero, so (5.6) still implies convergence of its integrals. Therefore the active contribution tends to its exact continuum value. Outside `I`, `tau_N(x)=x`, and ordinary Riemann sums apply.

We now prove by induction over odd `p` that `b_N=o(N^p)` suffices.

whenever `b_N=o(N^3)`. This recovers U070 directly.

\frac{b_N}{N^{p-2}}\le N^{-1}\to0,

# 7. U072 — variable derivative order and a superpolynomial compiler

The same p-th derivative estimate permits `p` to grow because its displayed implied constant is independent of `p`; we must account explicitly for the derivative-ratio factor `mu^{2/P}`.

For odd `p`, on `I=[1/2,4/5]`,

\[

=(p-1)!2^{p+1},
\tag{7.1}
\]

\[

\le2(p-1)!5^p.
\tag{7.2}
\]

\[
\mu_p\le(5/2)^p.
\tag{7.3}

\[

\tag{7.4}
\]

\[

\tag{7.5}

\[

\tag{7.6}

Then

\[

\qquad

\tag{7.7}
\]

and in particular

\tag{7.8}

For a fixed nonzero Fourier mode `r`, choose the lower derivative scale

\frac{|r|}{L}

\tag{7.9}
\]

By (7.6),

\[

\tag{7.10}

because `p_N=O(log log N)` implies

\[

Write

\[

The normalized higher-derivative bound is

\[

\right).
\tag{7.11}
\]

The explicit ratio factor is harmless:

\[
\log\mu_{p_N}^{2/P_N}
=O(p_N/2^{p_N})=o(1).
\tag{7.12}

For the first term,

\[
\log\lambda_N^{1/A_N}
=-(2+o(1))\frac{\log N}{A_N}
\longrightarrow-\infty
\tag{7.13}
\]

by (7.8). For the second,

\[
\log\left(N^{-2/P_N}\lambda_N^{-1/A_N}\right)

\left[-\frac2{P_N}+\frac{2+o(1)}{A_N}\right]\log N
\longrightarrow-\infty,
\tag{7.14}

because `A_N~2P_N` and again `log N/P_N -> infinity`.

Thus every nonzero weighted Fourier coefficient (5.5) vanishes. By Section 5,

\[
\boxed{q_N(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j)}
\tag{7.15}
\]

for every fixed specified split.

Finally,

\[
\log b_N=(p_N-2)\log N
=
\left(\frac1{2\log2}+o(1)\right)
(\log N)(\log\log N),
\tag{7.16}
\]

so C089 has **superpolynomial** branch complexity.
