## 4. Move #17 — the required harmonic numbers are the neutral Moran occupation spectrum

Consider the standard neutral discrete-time Moran allele-count chain

\[
X_t\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\},\qquad X_0=1,
\]

absorbed at `0` or `N`, with, for `1<=k<=N-1`,

\[
P(k,k+1)=P(k,k-1)=\frac{k(N-k)}{N^2},
\]

\[
P(k,k)=1-\frac{2k(N-k)}{N^2}.
\]

Let

\[
\tau=\inf\{t:X_t\in\{0,N\}\},
\qquad
L_k=\#\{0\le t<\tau:X_t=k\}.
\]

The exact Green occupation is

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_1L_k=\frac Nk.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the finite-population `1/k` neutral site-frequency spectrum appearing here as an excursion occupation measure.

### Proof of (4.1)

Ignore holding steps. The embedded chain is simple symmetric random walk on `0,...,N` absorbed at the endpoints. Its expected number of visits to state `k`, starting at `1`, is

\[
G^{\rm emb}(1,k)=\frac{2(N-k)}{N}.
\]

At `k`, the probability of leaving `k` on a Moran step is

\[
\frac{2k(N-k)}{N^2},
\]

so the expected holding length per embedded visit is

\[
\frac{N^2}{2k(N-k)}.
\]

Multiplying gives exactly `N/k`.

More generally, the exact Green function is

\[
G_N(i,k)=
\frac{N\min(i,k)(N-\max(i,k))}{k(N-k)},
\]

which the exact checker verifies directly against the discrete potential equations.

This is the decisive new way in: the harmonic spectrum used synthetically in C012/C013 occurs automatically as the occupation spectrum of a standard neutral population-genetic chain.

## 5. Move #29 — add the absorbing endpoints: one endpoint singularity is enough

The CTCS dislocation density is singular at `0` and `1`. The Moran chain supplies actual absorbing boundary points `0,N`; starting from a single mutant places the excursion one lattice step from an absorbing endpoint.

Scale an occupied state `k` to

\[
x_k=\frac{k}{N}.
\]

Equation (4.1) says the oriented occupation measure is proportional to `1/k`, i.e. in the continuum to `dx/x`. This looks one-sided, whereas CTCS is symmetric. But the **unordered** paintbox split automatically supplies the missing orientation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^1\frac{x^i(1-x)^j+x^j(1-x)^i}{x}\,dx\\
&\qquad=B(i,j+1)+B(j,i+1)\\
&\qquad=\frac{j}{i+j}B(i,j)+\frac{i}{i+j}B(i,j)\\
&\qquad=\boxed{B(i,j)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

So the absorbing endpoint is not a nuisance to truncate away: it is exactly the source of the critical harmonic beta kernel.

At the finite grid level there is an even stronger identity. Define

\[
q_N^M(i,j)=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}
\left[x_k^{i-1}(1-x_k)^j+x_k^{j-1}(1-x_k)^i\right].
\tag{5.2}
\]

By the grid symmetry `k <-> N-k`, the second sum can be reflected, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
q_N^M(i,j)
=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}x_k^{i-1}(1-x_k)^{j-1}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The right side is **exactly the C001 harmonic-grid finite generator**. Hence

\[
q_N^M(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j)
\tag{5.4}
\]

by an ordinary Riemann sum, while the finite higher-order dynamics below are different from C001.

## 7. First-order finite-restriction generator

Fix a visible block `C` of size `b=i+j` sitting inside any current hidden block `B`, and fix a particular unordered split `A|A^c` of `C`, `|A|=i`.

A gate at Moran state `k`, with `x_k=k/N`, creates this particular visible split with probability

\[
x_k^i(1-x_k)^j+x_k^j(1-x_k)^i.
\]

Using the exact occupation identity (4.1), the expected number of gated occurrences of this split in one excursion is

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_N\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\mathbb E_1L_k
&\left[x_k^i(1-x_k)^j+x_k^j(1-x_k)^i\right]\\
&=\frac{\delta_N}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac Nk
\left[x_k^i(1-x_k)^j+x_k^j(1-x_k)^i\right]\\
&=\delta_N q_N^M(i,j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The restriction to `C` is just a Bernoulli paintbox on those visible labels, so hidden labels do not alter (7.1). By (5.3)--(5.4), the first-order particular-split coefficient converges to `B(i,j)` for every fixed visible state.

Let `Q_N^{(k)}` be the finite-state generator on partitions of `[k]` built from the rates `q_N^M`; then

\tag{7.2}

entrywise, hence uniformly because the state space is finite.

## 8. Multi-gate interactions are negligible on the chosen scale

The common refinement makes C014 genuinely different at finite `N`, but it must disappear to first order.

Let `S` be the number of actual moves of the embedded simple random walk before absorption, starting at `1`. Exact gambler's-ruin recurrences give

\[
\mathbb ES=N-1,
\tag{8.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb ES^2=\frac{N^3-4N+3}{3}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

For completeness, if `m_i=i(N-i)` and `u_i=E_iS^2`, then

\[
2m_i-m_{i-1}-m_{i+1}=2,
\]

\[
2u_i-u_{i-1}-u_{i+1}=4m_i-2,

with zero endpoint values, and

\[
u_i=\frac{i^4-2Ni^3+2i^2+(N^3-2N)i}{3}.
\]

At every internal Moran state, the probability of making an actual move is

\[
\frac{2k(N-k)}{N^2}\ge\frac1N.
\]

Hence every holding period is stochastically dominated by a geometric variable of mean `N`. Conditional on `S`, dominate the absorption time `tau` by a sum of `S` iid such geometric variables. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\tau^2
\le N^2\left(\mathbb ES+\mathbb ES^2\right)
\le N^5.
}
\tag{8.3}

Conditional on `tau`, the number of gates is `Binomial(tau,a_N)`, so

\le \frac12\delta_N^2N^3.
\tag{8.4}

Also

\[
\mathbb E[\#\text{gates}]

\tag{8.5}
\]

For a fixed `[k]` restriction there are at most `k` visible blocks. Simultaneous gates in different current blocks contribute only `O_k(delta_N^2 H_{N-1}^2)`, which is dominated by the `O_k(delta_N^2 N^3)` envelope used below.

## 9. Whole-path convergence proof

By (7.2), `epsilon_{N,k}->0`.

The one-gate calculation (7.1), together with (8.4) and the corresponding cross-block bound, gives uniformly over hidden histories and visible states

\[
\boxed{
\left\|P_N^{(k)}(\rho,\cdot)
-\left(I+\delta_NQ^{(k)}\right)(\rho,\cdot)\right\|_{TV}
\le C_k\left(\delta_N\varepsilon_{N,k}
+\delta_N^2N^3\right).

\tag{9.1}

The diagonal term follows by summing the off-diagonal discrepancies. This conditional estimate is enough even though the visible projection need not itself be Markov.

\tag{9.2}
