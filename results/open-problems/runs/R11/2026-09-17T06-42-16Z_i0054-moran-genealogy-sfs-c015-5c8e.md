## 3. Move #22 — read the failures: why ordinary infinite-sites mutations are not already a tree-valued answer

The obvious population-genetic idea is “put neutral mutations on a Moran/Kingman genealogy and call the mutation families the children.” That does **not** define the required partition process.

If two mutations occur on nested branches, their descendant sets can be nested rather than disjoint. For example one mutation may affect `{1,2,3}` and a later descendant mutation `{1,2}`. These are legitimate infinite-sites mutation families, but they are not blocks of one set partition. This is exactly the structural issue highlighted in standard coalescent treatments of the infinite-sites model.

A second failure is probabilistic. Multiple mutations on a common random genealogy are not independent after one averages out the tree. The 2026 Joint Spectrum over Trees literature quantifies precisely this kind of dependence. Therefore replacing the genealogy by independent samples from its `1/i` site-frequency spectrum would not be a valid proof of a whole-tree limit.

The repair used below is deliberately minimal:

* keep the **actual common finite Moran genealogy** and the actual sequence of ancestral mutation opportunities;
* mark those opportunities independently only conditional on that genealogy;
* convert all marked bipartitions into a genuine child partition by taking their **common refinement**;
* never assume the resulting mutation-family frequencies are independent after averaging;
* instead bound every nonlinear multi-mark event by an exact second moment of the total number of ancestral opportunities.

This failure analysis is what distinguishes C015 from simply citing the classical site-frequency spectrum.

## 4. Move #3 — a stronger site-frequency-spectrum compiler theorem

The new mechanism is more general than the Moran example, so I first isolate the theorem that actually proves convergence.

### Theorem U004 — sampling-consistent SFS compiler

For each ambient size `N` and each sample size `2<=m<=N`, suppose a discrete exchangeable genealogy produces a finite sequence/multiset of **oriented mutation opportunities**, each opportunity carrying a nonempty proper descendant subset `S subset [m]`. Assume:

1. **Exchangeability.** Relabeling `[m]` relabels the opportunity process in law.
2. **Sampling consistency.** Restricting every descendant set to a fixed visible subset, discarding trivial restrictions, gives the same opportunity process in law as running the genealogy on that visible sample.
3. **Exact harmonic spectrum.** There is a constant `c_N>0`, independent of `m`, such that for every `1<=i<m`,
   \[
   \mathbb E Z_{m,i}=\frac{c_N}{i},
   \]
   where `Z_{m,i}` is the number of opportunities whose descendant set has size `i`.
4. **Second moment.** If `J_{N,m}` is the total number of opportunities, then, uniformly for `m<=N`,
   \[
   \mathbb E J_{N,m}^{2}\le c_N^2 K_N.
   \]
5. Choose `delta_N -> 0`, independently mark every opportunity with probability
   \[
   u_N=\frac{\delta_N}{c_N},
   \]
   and assume
   \[
   \delta_N K_N\longrightarrow0.
   \]

In one raw generation, refine each current block by the common refinement of all marked bipartitions `S|S^c`; give one generation physical duration `delta_N`; use fresh independent genealogies in distinct blocks and generations.

Then the full recursively generated partition path converges to CTCS(infinity) in the projective finite-restriction `J_1` topology.

### Proof

Fix a visible block `[m]` and a specified subset `A` of size `i`, with `j=m-i`. Exchangeability plus the harmonic spectrum implies

\[
\mathbb E Z(A)=\frac{c_N}{i\binom mi},
\]

because all size-i subsets have the same expected opportunity count. The unordered split `A|A^c` can be induced by an oriented opportunity carrying `A` or carrying `A^c`, hence its expected opportunity count is

\[
\frac{c_N}{\binom mi}\left(\frac1i+\frac1j\right)
=c_N\frac{m}{ij\binom mi}
=c_N B(i,j).
\tag{4.1}
\]

After Bernoulli marking with probability `u_N=delta_N/c_N`, the **linear** split mass is therefore exactly

\[
\boxed{\delta_N B(i,j)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The only discrepancy between the genuine common-refinement step and that one-mark linearization comes from two or more marked opportunities. Conditional on `J`, the mark count `M` is binomial, so

\[
\Pr(M\ge2)
\le \mathbb E\binom M2
=u_N^2\,\mathbb E\binom J2
\le \frac12u_N^2\mathbb EJ^2
\le \frac12\delta_N^2K_N.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Sampling consistency makes (4.1)--(4.3) valid conditional on arbitrary hidden labels: after restriction, the visible opportunity process is the same one. Thus for each fixed finite restriction `[k]`, uniformly over the complete hidden history,

\[
\left\|
P_N^{(k)}(\rho,\cdot)
-\bigl(I+\delta_NQ^{(k)}_{\rm CTCS}\bigr)(\rho,\cdot)
\right\|_{\rm TV}
\le C_k\delta_N^2K_N.
\tag{4.4}
\]

On a bounded physical interval `[0,T]` there are `O(T/delta_N)` raw generations, so iterative conditional maximal coupling gives

\[
\Pr(\text{coupling fails by }T)
\le C_{k,T}\delta_NK_N\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For the Euler chain `I+delta_N Q^(k)`, a state `rho` has a geometric holding time with parameter `delta_N lambda(rho)`; after multiplication by `delta_N` this converges to `Exp(lambda(rho))`, while the conditional next-state law is exactly `Q(rho,rho')/lambda(rho)`. A fragmentation of `k` labels has at most `k-1` proper refinements, so the complete finite jump-state/holding-time sequence converges, and therefore

\[
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Finally choose `K` so the deterministic tail `sum_{k>K}2^{-k}` is small and couple the first `K` restrictions. This yields

\[
\boxed{\widehat\Pi_N\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS(\infty)}}

in `d_proj`. This proves U004. The finite checker below tests the exact algebra used in (4.1)--(4.3); the theorem-sized convergence argument is written here rather than delegated to code.

## 5. C015 — intrinsic neutral-Moran-genealogy mutation refinement

Now construct the discrete genealogy supplying U004 without the external paintbox gate of C014.

1. place its `m` labels at distinct sites of a standard neutral `N`-site discrete-time Moran population and trace their ancestry **backwards** until their MRCA;
2. a Moran reproduction event chooses an ordered pair `(parent,death)` of distinct sites uniformly; if the death site carries one of the active ancestral lineages, that lineage jumps to the parent site, coalescing if the parent site already carries another active lineage;
3. call every such event whose death site carries an active lineage an **ancestral birth opportunity**; just before updating/coalescing, independently put an infinite-sites mutation mark on that ancestral lineage with probability
   \[
   u_N=\frac{\delta_N}{N-1};
   \]
4. each marked opportunity carries the set `S subset B` of present labels descending from that active lineage immediately before the event;
5. after the MRCA is reached, refine `B` by the common refinement of all marked bipartitions `S|B\setminus S`; if there is no mark, leave `B` unchanged;
6. use fresh independent Moran genealogies for different current hierarchy blocks and future raw generations.

Choose concretely

\[
\boxed{\delta_N=\frac1N,
\qquad u_N=\frac1{N(N-1)}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

One raw generation has physical duration `delta_N`.

This is a finite discrete model: finite Moran reproduction/death choices, finite Bernoulli mutation marks and finite set-partition refinement. Multiple mutations in one genealogy can create degree `3,4,...` finite vertices, so the finite hierarchy is not CTCS itself.

The marks are also intrinsic in the ordinary population-genetic sense missing from C014: they occur on actual transmitted ancestral lineages of the neutral Moran genealogy rather than at an externally sampled allele-frequency paintbox.

## 6. Exact Moran opportunity spectrum: `(N-1)/i`

Let the current sample have `m` labels. Suppose the backward genealogy currently has `b` active ancestral lineages. Condition on a Moran event hitting an active death lineage. The parent is one of the other `N-1` sites, of which exactly `b-1` carry other active lineages. Therefore the probability that this active-lineage birth event is the next coalescence is

\[
p_b=\frac{b-1}{N-1}.
\]

Hence the number `G_b` of active-lineage birth opportunities while there are `b` lineages, including the successful coalescing event, is

\[
G_b\sim\operatorname{Geom}\!\left(\frac{b-1}{N-1}\right),
\qquad
\mathbb EG_b=\frac{N-1}{b-1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

At each such event the death lineage is uniform among the `b` active lineages. The embedded topology of successive coalescences is Kingman's uniform-pair merger tree.

Let `F_{b,i}` be the number of the `b` current ancestral lineages that subtend exactly `i` of the original `m` sampled labels. The standard Kingman/Yule time reversal gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E F_{b,i}
=b\frac{\binom{m-i-1}{b-2}}
        {\binom{m-1}{b-1}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

A short proof is worth recording. Reverse the Kingman topology from `b` ancestors forward to `m` leaves. Each new leaf is born by choosing one current descendant leaf uniformly, so the descendant counts of the `b` starting ancestors evolve as a Pólya urn begun with one ball of each of `b` colors. After reaching total size `m`, the positive ordered composition of `m` into those `b` colors is uniform. For a fixed ancestral color, the chance of final size `i` is therefore

\[
\frac{\binom{m-i-1}{b-2}}{\binom{m-1}{b-1}},
\]

and summing over the `b` colors gives (6.2).

During stage `b`, the expected number of opportunities carried by size-i lineages is

\[
\mathbb EF_{b,i}\frac{\mathbb EG_b}{b}
=\mathbb EF_{b,i}\frac{N-1}{b(b-1)}.
\]

Summing over all possible `b`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E Z_{m,i}
&=(N-1)\sum_b
\frac{1}{b-1}
\frac{\binom{m-i-1}{b-2}}{\binom{m-1}{b-1}}\\
&=\frac{N-1}{i\binom{m-1}{i}}
\sum_{r=i-1}^{m-2}\binom r{i-1}\\
&=\frac{N-1}{i\binom{m-1}{i}}
\binom{m-1}{i}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{N-1}{i}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The middle equality is a factorial rearrangement with `r=m-b`, and the last is the hockey-stick identity.

Thus C015 has the exact harmonic opportunity spectrum required by U004, with

\[
\boxed{c_N=N-1.}
\]

By exchangeability, a specified subset `A` of size `i` is carried by an expected

\[
\frac{N-1}{i\binom mi}
\]

opportunities. Adding the opposite orientation `A^c`, `j=m-i`, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
(N-1)\frac{1}{\binom mi}
\left(\frac1i+\frac1j\right)
&=(N-1)\frac{m}{ij\binom mi}\\
&=\boxed{(N-1)B(i,j)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Multiplying by `u_N=delta_N/(N-1)` yields the **exact finite-N linear CTCS coefficient**

\[
\boxed{\delta_NB(i,j).}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Unlike C014, there is no Riemann-sum error at all.

## 7. Exact sampling consistency under hidden labels

The hidden-label issue is particularly clean in C015.

Take any visible sub-sample of a larger Moran genealogy. Track only the ancestral sites carrying at least one visible descendant. At an event whose death site carries a visible ancestral lineage, the parent is still uniform among the other `N-1` population sites. It contains a second visible lineage with probability exactly `(b-1)/(N-1)` when `b` visible lineages remain; a parent carrying only hidden descendants is simply an unoccupied site from the restricted visible genealogy's point of view. Therefore the restricted visible ancestral-location process is exactly the standard `b`-sample neutral Moran ancestry in the same `N`-site population.

Mutation marks are attached independently to these visible ancestral birth opportunities with the same probability `u_N`. Marks on hidden-only ancestral opportunities disappear under restriction; a marked descendant set whose visible intersection is empty or all visible labels gives a trivial restricted bipartition and is discarded. Consequently the complete marked-opportunity process is projectively sampling-consistent.

This proves assumption 2 of U004 **as a process law**, rather than only after averaging over hidden labels.

## 8. Exact second moment and negligible multi-mutation interaction

The strong Markov property at successive coalescences gives independent stage counts `G_b`, `b=2,...,m`. Write `c=N-1` and `r=b-1`. For `G_b~Geom(r/c)` on `{1,2,...}`,

\[
\mathbb EG_b=\frac cr,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_b)=\frac{c(c-r)}{r^2}.
\]

Let

\[
J_m=\sum_{b=2}^mG_b
\]

be the total number of ancestral mutation opportunities. Then

\[
\mathbb EJ_m=cH_{m-1},
\]

and exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EJ_m^2
=c^2\left(H_{m-1}^2+H_{m-1}^{(2)}\right)-cH_{m-1}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

In particular, uniformly for `m<=N`,

\[
\mathbb EJ_m^2
\le c^2K_N,
\qquad
K_N:=H_{N-1}^2+H_{N-1}^{(2)}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Conditional on `J_m`, the number `M_m` of mutations is `Bin(J_m,u_N)`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(M_m\ge2)
&\le\mathbb E\binom{M_m}{2}\\
&=u_N^2\mathbb E\binom{J_m}{2}\\
&\le\frac12\delta_N^2
\left(H_{N-1}^2+H_{N-1}^{(2)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For the chosen `delta_N=1/N`,

\[
\boxed{
\delta_NK_N
=O\!\left(\frac{\log^2N}{N}\right)\to0.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

So all genuinely nonlinear common-refinement behavior is finite-N structure that disappears at first order, exactly in the regime needed by U004.

Applying U004 now gives, for every fixed `k`, conditional on the complete hidden history,

\[
\left\|
P_N^{(k)}(\rho,\cdot)
-\left(I+\frac1NQ_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}\right)(\rho,\cdot)
\right\|_{\rm TV}
\le
\frac{C_k}{N^2}
\left(H_{N-1}^2+H_{N-1}^{(2)}\right).
\tag{8.5}
\]

Over a bounded physical interval this accumulates to

\[
O_{k,T}\!\left(\frac{\log^2N}{N}\right)\to0.
\]

Hence

\[
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1
\]

for every fixed `k`, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS(\infty)}

\]

in the full projective finite-restriction topology required by the source.

This is the complete convergence argument for C015; it is not merely the classical `1/i` marginal spectrum.

## 9. Move #20 — find why the natural unthinned algorithm fails

The new route also identifies precisely why one cannot simply take a standard non-rare population-genetic mutation scale and declare victory.

Let

\[
\theta_N=(N-1)u_N.
\]

The compiler's natural physical mesh is exactly `delta_N=theta_N`. If `theta_N -> theta>0`, then the mesh does **not** tend to zero, so the Euler-to-continuous-time argument already fails.

There is also a genuine interaction obstruction. During lineage-count stage `b`, put `r=b-1` and `c=N-1`. Since `G_b~Geom(r/c)`,

\[
\mathbb E(1-u_N)^{G_b}
=
\frac{(r/c)(1-u_N)}{1-(1-r/c)(1-u_N)}
\longrightarrow
\frac r{r+\theta}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Thus under fixed positive `theta`, each of the stages `b=3` and `b=2` has a nonvanishing probability of carrying at least one mutation. Multi-mark interactions therefore remain order one rather than becoming an `o(delta_N)` error. In particular the exact rare-event proof above cannot be salvaged at fixed theta without an additional thinning/time-splitting operation.

This is the precise obstruction to the most natural “just use the ordinary mutation rate” algorithm. It is not claimed as a theorem about every possible rescaling of every Moran mutation tree; it says exactly where the C015 CTCS compiler stops working.

A second naive algorithm also fails for a structural reason already found in Move #22: taking the mutation descendant families themselves as children does not in general produce a partition. The common-refinement step is essential.
