## 2. Source-exact target and current literature screen

The current arXiv v3 still states Open Problem 18:

> Are there superficially different discrete models whose limit is the same CTCS(infinity)?

and asks whether the characteristic subordinator approximation can arise in another discrete tree model.

The target object is not a one-leaf marginal. Aldous--Janson formalize CTCS(infinity) by the entire exchangeable partition process

\[
(\Pi(t),t\ge0)
\]

on `N`, where labels are in the same block exactly until their branchpoint separation time. As in the preceding run, the convergence theorem below therefore targets the whole consistent partition-valued path, in the same projective Skorokhod topology used for C001.

Part III, arXiv:2412.09655, identifies the particular binary split rate of an `m`-label block. For a particular unordered bipartition `A | A^c` with `|A|=i`,

\[
\boxed{
q(A|A^c)=B(i,m-i)
=\frac{(i-1)!(m-i-1)!}{(m-1)!}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The total visible split rate of an `m`-block is `H_{m-1}`.

## 3. Move 6 — DEFORM TO A SOLVABLE BOUNDARY: the Ewens tangent at `theta=0`

The standard one-parameter Chinese restaurant process with concentration `theta>0` induces the Ewens exchangeable partition of a finite `m`-set. For one **particular labelled set partition** into `r` blocks of sizes `n_1,...,n_r`, the EPPF is

\[
\boxed{
p_\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r)
=\frac{\theta^r}{(\theta)_m}
  \prod_{a=1}^r(n_a-1)!,
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
(\theta)_m=\theta(\theta+1)\cdots(\theta+m-1).
\]

Cancel the common factor `theta`:

\[
p_\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r)
=\theta^{r-1}
\frac{\prod_a(n_a-1)!}
{\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(j+\theta)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The boundary `theta=0` is degenerate: an `m`-block remains one block. But its **first derivative** has exactly the CTCS rates.

For one block,

\[
p_\theta(m)
=\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{j}{j+\theta}
=1-H_{m-1}\theta+O_m(\theta^2).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For a particular binary partition `A | A^c`, `|A|=i`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_\theta(A|A^c)
&=\theta
\frac{(i-1)!(m-i-1)!}
{\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(j+\theta)}\\
&=\theta B(i,m-i)+O_m(\theta^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Every particular `r>=3` multifurcation satisfies

\[
p_\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r)=O_m(\theta^{r-1})=O_m(\theta^2).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the CTCS binary fragmentation generator is precisely the first-order tangent of the Ewens partition kernel at the one-block boundary.

This is the key new entry point. Instead of engineering the CTCS dislocation measure directly, take a standard, independently motivated random partition model and zoom into its small-concentration boundary.

## 4. Move 21 — WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: the model is the nested Chinese restaurant process

Blei--Griffiths--Jordan's nCRP gives exactly the recursive discrete tree structure suggested by (3.1)--(3.5): a finite set of customers starts at a root restaurant; customers choose tables by a CRP; each table points to a new restaurant; at the next depth, the customers at that table choose child tables by a fresh CRP, and this repeats.

Equivalently, conditional on the current partition at one depth, **each current block is independently refined by an Ewens(`theta`) partition at the next depth**. This is not a new model invented for CTCS; it is the standard nCRP viewed as a nested sequence of label partitions.

That observation materially improves the naturality of the Open Problem 18 candidate. It comes from Bayesian nonparametrics/topic hierarchies rather than from the beta-splitting construction.

I do **not** count `recursive Ewens refinement` and `nCRP` as two classes: they are two descriptions of the same class.

## 5. Move 29 — ADD THE BOUNDARY AND BLOW UP DEPTH

For each `N>=2`, set

\[
\theta_N=\frac1N.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Run the standard nCRP with concentration `theta_N` on `N` labelled customers. Let

\[
\Pi_N(r),\qquad r=0,1,2,\ldots,
\]

be the partition of `[N]` induced by common nCRP node at depth `r`: two labels are in the same block at depth `r` iff their nCRP paths agree through that depth.

Because `theta_N>0`, every non-singleton sampled block eventually splits almost surely. For the finite observed genealogy, stop after all labels are singleton and collapse consecutive degree-one levels into edge lengths. Give one raw nCRP level length `theta_N`. This produces a finite rooted generally **multifurcating** random tree with edge lengths in `theta_N Z_{>0}`.

Define its rescaled càdlàg partition path

\[
\widehat\Pi_N(t)
:=\Pi_N(\lfloor t/\theta_N\rfloor),
\qquad t\ge0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The claim is that the degenerate `theta=0` boundary, observed on the accelerated depth scale `r\asymp t/theta`, is exactly CTCS(infinity).

## 6. `R11-I0054-C002` — small-concentration nCRP converges to CTCS(infinity)

**STATUS: PROVED under the frozen equivalence convention; campaign remains PARTIAL pending prior-art/source-intent clearance and two independent audits.**

### Theorem

Let `theta_N=1/N`, and let `widehat Pi_N` be the finite `N`-customer nCRP partition path (5.2), extended to a partition of `N` by declaring labels greater than `N` singleton blocks. Let `Pi` be the Aldous--Janson CTCS(infinity) exchangeable partition process.

For every fixed `k`, as `N->infinity`,

\[
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi^{[k]}
\tag{6.1}
\]

in Skorokhod `J_1` topology on càdlàg paths with values in the finite partition set `P_k`.

With the projective metric

\[
d_{\rm proj}(\omega,\eta)
=\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}
\bigl(1\wedge d_{J_1,k}(\omega^{[k]},\eta^{[k]})\bigr),
\tag{6.2}
\]

we furthermore have

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N\Longrightarrow\Pi.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the reduced nCRP genealogy with generation lengths rescaled by `1/N` converges to the full CTCS(infinity) object in the source's exchangeable-partition formalism, not merely in a tagged or fixed-time marginal.

### Proof

#### Step 1: finite restrictions of nCRP are Markov in depth

Fix `k`, and take `N>=k`. The ordinary CRP/Ewens partition is sampling-consistent and exchangeable. Hence if a current nCRP node contains some of the sampled labels `[k]` together with any number of unsampled labels, the partition induced on the sampled labels by its child tables is an Ewens(`theta_N`) partition of those sampled labels.

Distinct current nodes use distinct restaurants, hence independent CRP refinements. Therefore, conditional on the current restriction `rho in P_k`, the next-depth restriction is obtained by independently replacing every block `B` of `rho` by an Ewens(`theta_N`) partition of `B`.

So `Pi_N^[k](r)` is a time-homogeneous finite-state Markov chain in the depth variable `r`.

#### Step 2: one-generation transition matrix has tangent `Q^(k)`

Let `P_theta^(k)` be that one-generation transition matrix at parameter `theta`. Consider a state `rho` with blocks `B`.

For one block of size `m`, (3.3) gives no visible refinement probability

\[
1-H_{m-1}\theta+O_k(\theta^2).
\]

If `rho'` is obtained from `rho` by splitting exactly one block `B` of size `m` into the **particular** two subsets `A` and `B\A`, with `|A|=i`, then (3.4), multiplied by no-change probabilities for all other blocks, gives

\[
P_\theta^{(k)}(\rho,\rho')
=\theta B(i,m-i)+O_k(\theta^2).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Every other strict transition either contains one `>=3`-way refinement or visible refinements in at least two current blocks, and therefore has probability `O_k(theta^2)` by (3.5) and independence.

Finally,

\[
P_\theta^{(k)}(\rho,\rho)
=1-\theta\sum_{B\in\rho}H_{|B|-1}+O_k(\theta^2).
\tag{6.5}
\]

There are finitely many states for fixed `k`, so all `O_k(theta^2)` errors can be taken uniform. Thus

\[
\boxed{
P_\theta^{(k)}=I+\theta Q^{(k)}+O_k(\theta^2)
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

entrywise, where `Q^(k)` has off-diagonal particular binary split rates `B(i,m-i)` and diagonal entries minus the total rates `sum_B H_{|B|-1}`.

By the Aldous--Janson Part III rate formula, this `Q^(k)` is exactly the generator of `Pi^[k]`.

#### Step 3: explicit holding-time / jump-chain coupling

It remains to upgrade the generator tangent to path convergence without hiding a topology change.

At a nonabsorbing state `rho`, let

\[
\lambda(\rho)
:=\sum_{B\in\rho}H_{|B|-1}.
\]

From (6.5), the probability of **some** visible change in one discrete nCRP generation is

\[
s_\theta(\rho)
=\theta\lambda(\rho)+O_k(\theta^2).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Let `G_theta` be the number of generations to the next visible change. It is geometric with parameter `s_theta(rho)`. Therefore for every `t>=0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(\theta G_\theta>t)
&=(1-s_\theta(\rho))^{\lfloor t/\theta\rfloor+O(1)}\\
&\longrightarrow e^{-\lambda(\rho)t}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Hence

\[
\theta G_\theta\Longrightarrow {\rm Exp}(\lambda(\rho)).
\tag{6.9}
\]

For a particular CTCS-admissible next state `rho'`, (6.4) and (6.7) give

\[
P(\rho_{\rm next}=\rho'\mid\text{a visible change})
\longrightarrow
\frac{Q^{(k)}(\rho,\rho')}{\lambda(\rho)}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

The total probability of all non-CTCS visible transitions in one generation is `O_k(theta^2)`. Conditional on a visible change it is therefore `O_k(theta)` and tends to zero.

Couple each successive holding time by a common uniform quantile, and couple each next-state choice by a common uniform variable applied to the finite cumulative transition tables. At every currently matched state, the scaled holding times converge and the next states agree with probability tending to one.

Both the limiting binary fragmentation and the discrete refining chain can have at most `k-1` visible refinements before all `k` labels are singleton, because every visible refinement increases the block count by at least one. A union bound over at most `k-1` jumps shows that the probability of any non-CTCS transition under this coupling tends to zero. On the complementary event the complete finite state sequence agrees and all corresponding jump times converge.

For càdlàg paths in a finite discrete state space, convergence of the finite state sequence and corresponding jump times is exactly `J_1` convergence. This proves (6.1).

#### Step 4: projective whole-process convergence

Fix `epsilon>0` and choose `K` with

\[
\sum_{k>K}2^{-k}<\epsilon/2.
\]

For all `N>=K`, apply Step 3 to the `[K]` restriction. Every smaller restriction is a deterministic restriction of the same coupled `[K]` path, so the first `K` terms of (6.2) converge to zero in probability simultaneously. The remaining tail of (6.2) is deterministically below `epsilon/2`. Thus

\[
d_{\rm proj}(\widehat\Pi_N,\Pi)\to0
\]

in probability under the coupling, proving (6.3).

#### Step 5: this recovers the tree genealogy and branch heights

For labels `a,b`, define

\[
H_{a,b}^{(N)}
=\inf\{t:\ a,b\text{ lie in different blocks of }\widehat\Pi_N(t)\}.
\]

This is the rescaled nCRP depth of their last common node / separation. The nested partition path on every finite label set, together with these split times, reconstructs the reduced rooted genealogy after degree-one levels are collapsed. The limiting partition process reconstructs CTCS(infinity) in exactly the Aldous--Janson formalism.

This completes the proof. `square`

## 7. Strict non-equivalence and why this is a second model class

The frozen equivalence convention from C001 remains unchanged: two finite model families are identified only under deterministic leaf relabeling and multiplication of all times by one deterministic positive scalar (allowed to depend on `N`). No other operation is quotiented out.

The small-concentration nCRP class is strictly different from both the original finite CTCS family and C001's HGPF class.

Take `N=3`, so `theta_N=1/3`. At the **first nCRP level**, the exact Ewens probabilities are

\[
P(\text{one block})=\frac9{14},
\]

\[
P(\text{a particular }1|2\text{ split})=\frac3{28},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
P(1|1|1)=\frac1{28}>0.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus the reduced finite nCRP genealogy has a root of outdegree three with positive probability.

The original DTCS/CTCS finite tree is binary, and HGPF is binary by construction. Relabeling leaves and globally rescaling time preserve vertex outdegrees and cannot turn a positive-probability three-way split into a binary tree. Therefore this is a strict support-level non-equivalence certificate.

I also explicitly **do not** count Gnedin--Pitman's Ewens fragmentation or the `recursive Ewens refinement` description as additional classes here. The latter is just the nCRP in partition language; the former is important nearby prior art but has different dynamics and is not used as a third metric point.

Hence the fixed metric moves

\[
\boxed{1\longrightarrow2.}
\]

The two counted classes are:

1. HGPF (C001): harmonic-grid atomic paintbox, binary continuous-time finite fragmentation;
2. SC-nCRP (C002): standard nested Chinese restaurant hierarchy at concentration `1/N`, discrete generations rescaled by `1/N`, with finite multifurcations disappearing in the limit.

## 8. Why this model is materially more natural than C001, and why closure still waits

C001 directly discretized the CTCS dislocation measure; that made convergence transparent but left a serious “engineered from the answer” objection to the source's phrase “superficially different.”

C002 is different in kind. The nCRP predates the CTCS open problem and was developed in Bayesian nonparametrics/topic modeling. Its finite trees are infinitely-branching in the ambient model and can have genuine multifurcations; CTCS appears only after a **small-concentration / accelerated-depth tangent limit**. This is much closer to the type of cross-field universality Open Problem 18 appears to invite.

There is nevertheless a serious prior-art caveat. Gnedin--Pitman already developed Ewens fragmentation processes, and the first-order Ewens expansion is standard once noticed. The proof above uses standard finite-state rare-event convergence after the connection is found.
