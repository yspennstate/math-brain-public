## 2. Idea before code; what the three methods did

C048 used the Palm/size-biased incidence law: choose uniformly from **all** indexed irreducible occurrences across **all** degree-`N` polynomials. The natural model one would write down without Palm bias is different:

1. choose a monic degree-`N` polynomial uniformly;
2. factor it;
3. choose uniformly among the irreducible occurrences of that particular polynomial.

The obstruction is the denominator `1/Omega(f)`. Polynomials with few factors are overweighted relative to the C048 incidence measure, so the exact harmonic degree intensity from U027 no longer applies term-by-term.

The three actual section-B moves were:

- **#11 — dualize.** I rewrote the reciprocal factor count by pointing an occurrence and dividing the polynomial by the `r`-th copy of that irreducible. This gives an exact formula in terms of `E[1/(r+Omega)]`, and separately the standard integral identity `1/k = integral_0^1 t^{k-1} dt` connects the same quantity to the factor-count probability generating function.
- **#22 — read the failures, not the successes.** The dangerous point is precisely where a naive replacement `1/Omega(f) approx 1/log N` can fail: rare factor-poor polynomials. The 2026 function-field Selberg--Delange work and the classical random-polynomial literature identify the right control object, the distribution of `Omega(f)`. The proof below therefore establishes the lower-tail control needed for reciprocal weighting instead of assuming concentration is enough.
- **#28 — replace for-all by typical.** On the typical set `Omega(f)=(1+o(1)) log N`, the uniform-within-polynomial law differs from Palm weighting by `1/log N`. A polynomial lower-tail estimate makes the atypical set negligible even after multiplication by `log N`. Endpoint factor degrees are handled separately, so no unproved uniformity in `m` is hidden.

## 3. Factor-count singularity lemma

Fix a prime power `q`. Let `Omega(f)` count monic irreducible factors with multiplicity and let `F_n` be uniform among the `q^n` monic degree-`n` polynomials over `F_q`.

For complex `u` in a fixed compact subset of `Re u>0`, define

\[
 G_q(u,z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{\deg f=n}u^{\Omega(f)}z^n
 =\prod_{P\ {m irreducible}}(1-u z^{\deg P})^{-1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If `I_q(m)` is the number of monic irreducibles of degree `m`, then

\[
 C_q(z):=\sum_{m\ge1}I_q(m)z^m
 =\log\frac1{1-qz}+H_q(z),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `H_q` is analytic in a neighborhood of `z=1/q`. Indeed the Mobius formula

\[
 I_q(m)=\frac1m\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)q^{m/d}
\]

shows

\[
 C_q(z)=\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{\mu(d)}d\log\frac1{1-qz^d},
\]

and every `d>=2` term is analytic at `1/q`.

Taking logarithms in (3.1),

\[
 \log G_q(u,z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{u^r}{r}C_q(z^r),
\]

so all `r>=2` terms are analytic at `z=1/q`. Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 G_q(u,z)=(1-qz)^{-u}A_q(u,z),
 }
\tag{3.3}
\]

with `A_q` analytic jointly near the relevant compact `u`-set and `z=1/q`. Since `G_q(1,z)=1/(1-qz)` exactly, `A_q(1,z)=1`.

Standard algebraic-singularity transfer, uniformly on compact `u`-sets, yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E[u^{\Omega(F_n)}]
 =\frac{A_q(u,1/q)}{\Gamma(u)}n^{u-1}(1+o(1)).
 }
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is exactly the type of function-field Selberg--Delange asymptotic treated systematically in the current literature cited above; the factorization (3.3) is written here because it is all that this run needs.

For every fixed `eta in (0,1)`, Chernoff with a fixed `u_-<1` and `u_+>1` chosen near `1-eta` and `1+eta` gives constants `c_eta,C_eta>0` such that

\[
 \Pr\bigl(|\Omega(F_n)-\log n|>\eta\log n\bigr)
 \le C_\eta n^{-c_\eta}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \frac{\Omega(F_n)}{\log n}\to1
\quad\text{in probability,}
\tag{3.6}
\]

with a polynomially small lower tail.

For fixed integer `r>=1`, set

\[
 a_n(r)=\mathbb E\frac1{r+\Omega(F_n)}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

On the typical event from (3.5), `log n/(r+Omega(F_n))->1`; on the complement the contribution is at most `(log n/r) C_eta n^{-c_eta}`. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 a_n(r)\sim\frac1{\log n}
 }
\tag{3.8}
\]

for every fixed `r`, uniformly when `r` ranges over a fixed finite set. The same proof plus a finite adjustment gives

\[
 \boxed{
 a_n(r)\le a_n(1)\le\frac{C_q}{\log(n+2)}
 }
\tag{3.9}
\]

for all `n>=0`, `r>=1`, with a suitable constant `C_q`.

## 4. U028 — exact inverse-factor-count duality and beta tangent

For a monic polynomial `f`, let

\[
 C_m(f)=\sum_{\deg g=m}v_g(f)
\]

be the number of degree-`m` irreducible occurrences, counted with multiplicity. Under the **uniform-first law**, sample `F_N` uniformly and then select one of its `Omega(F_N)` occurrences uniformly. Let `M_N` be the selected factor degree. Then

\[
 p_{N,m}:=\Pr(M_N=m)
 =\mathbb E\frac{C_m(F_N)}{\Omega(F_N)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The key exact identity is obtained by indexing an occurrence by `(g,r)` with `1<=r<=v_g(f)`. Write `f=g^r h`. Multiplicity is additive even if `h` is still divisible by `g`, so

\[
 \Omega(f)=r+\Omega(h).
\]

Conversely every monic `h` of degree `N-rm` gives exactly one such indexed occurrence. Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 p_{N,m}
 =I_q(m)\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor N/m\rfloor}
 q^{-rm}a_{N-rm}(r).
 }
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is exact for every `N,m,q`; it is not an asymptotic Palm approximation.

### 4.1 Bulk selected-factor law

Fix `epsilon in (0,1/2)`. Uniformly for

\[
 \epsilon N\le m\le(1-\epsilon)N,
\]

all `r>=2` terms in (4.2) are exponentially small because

\[
 I_q(m)q^{-rm}\le \frac1m q^{-(r-1)m}.
\]

The `r=1` term, using `I_q(m)q^{-m}=m^{-1}(1+O(q^{-m/2}))` and (3.8), gives

\[
 \boxed{
 p_{N,m}=\frac{1+o(1)}{m\log N}
 }
\tag{4.3}
\]

uniformly on every fixed macroscopic bulk window.

### 4.2 Endpoint control

For `m<=epsilon N`, the `r=1` term has `N-m>=N/2`, so (3.9) gives

\[
 p_{N,m}\le \frac{C}{m\log N}+\frac{Cq^{-m}}m.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For `m>= (1-epsilon)N`, only `r=1` occurs. Writing `n=N-m`,

\[
 p_{N,N-n}\le \frac{C}{N\log(n+2)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

These two estimates are the point where the typical-set argument is needed: the crude Palm bound `p_{N,m}<=C/m` would be too weak after the eventual `log N` acceleration.

### 4.3 Visible split probabilities

Conditional on `M_N=m`, choose a uniform `m`-subset of `[N]`. For a specified unordered visible split with block sizes `i,j>=1`, `b=i+j`, its exact probability is

\[
 s_{N,m}(i,j)=
 \frac{
 (m)_{\underline i}(N-m)_{\underline j}
 +(m)_{\underline j}(N-m)_{\underline i}
 }{(N)_{\underline{i+j}}}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Set

\[
 P_N(i,j)=\sum_{m=1}^N p_{N,m}s_{N,m}(i,j).
\tag{4.7}
\]

On the bulk, multiplying (4.3) and (4.6) gives the Riemann sum

\[
 (\log N)P_N(i,j)
 \to
 \int_0^1
 \left[x^{i-1}(1-x)^j+x^{j-1}(1-x)^i\right]dx,
\]

provided the endpoints vanish. For the small-`m` endpoint, (4.4) and the falling-factorial bound give after multiplication by `log N`

\[
 O(\epsilon^i+\epsilon^j)+o(1).
\]

For the large-`m` endpoint, use (4.5), put `n=N-m`, and split the sum at `n=\sqrt N`. The range `n<=sqrt N` is `o(1)` after the `log N` factor; for `sqrt N<n<=epsilon N`, `log(n+2)>=\tfrac12\log N`, giving

\[
 O(\epsilon^{i+1}+\epsilon^{j+1}).
\]

Letting first `N->infinity` and then `epsilon->0` proves

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\log N)P_N(i,j)
 &\longrightarrow B(i,j+1)+B(j,i+1)\\
 &=\boxed{
 B(i,j)=\frac{(i-1)!(j-1)!}{(i+j-1)!}
 }.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{P_N(1,1)\sim\frac1{\log N}.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Consequently, with

\[
 \lambda_N=P_N(1,1)^{-1},
\]

we have for every fixed specified binary split

\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda_NP_N(i,j)\to B(i,j).
 }
\tag{4.10}
\]

This proves the new compiler

\[
 \boxed{\texttt{R11-I0054-U028 = PROVED\_AUTHOR\_SIDE}.}

## 5. C049 — uniform polynomial first, then uniform factor occurrence

\[
