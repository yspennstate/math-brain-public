\[
\rho=\sum_a w_a x_a(1-x_a)(\delta_{x_a}+\delta_{1-x_a}).
\]

For a specified unordered split of sizes `i,j` the exact rate is

\[
q(i,j)=\int_0^1 x^{i-1}(1-x)^{j-1}\rho(dx),
\]

while CTCS has

\[
q_{\rm CTCS}(i,j)=B(i,j)

The previous run used this only asymptotically. This run asks the stronger finite question.

## 3. Exact finite CTCS realization is a quadrature problem

Fix `k>=2`. Call a finite raw paintbox mechanism **exact through `[k]`** when its generator equals the CTCS generator on the entire finite partition state space `P_k`.

Because each block evolves independently and the split rate depends only on its size, this is equivalent to

\[
q(i,j)=B(i,j)
\quad\text{for every }i,j\ge1,\ i+j\le k.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The integrands in (3.1) are polynomials of degree at most `k-2`. Conversely, setting `j=1` and `i=m+1` gives the monomials `x^m` for every `0<=m<=k-2`. Hence:

**Lemma 3.1.** Exact CTCS agreement through `[k]` is equivalent to

\[
\int p(x)\rho(dx)=\int_0^1p(x)\,dx
\quad
\text{for every polynomial }p\text{ of degree }\le k-2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the exact finite-tree question is precisely positive quadrature for Lebesgue measure.

## 4. U024 lower bound: no positive atomic rule can use too few nodes

Let a positive atomic measure satisfying (3.2) have `M` distinct support points `z_1,...,z_M`. Consider

\[
P(x)=\prod_{r=1}^M(x-z_r)^2.
\]

If `2M<=k-2`, then exactness would give

\[
0
=
\int P\,d\rho
=
\int_0^1P(x)\,dx,
\]

which is impossible because `P>=0` and is not identically zero. Therefore

\[
M\ge \left\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rceil.
\tag{4.1}
\]

One raw Bernoulli phase contributes at most two distinct transformed support points, the complement pair `x,1-x`; at `x=1/2` these coincide. Hence if there are `r` raw phases,

\[
2r\ge M,
\]

and (4.1) yields the sharp lower bound

\[
\boxed{
r\ge
\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{4}\right\rceil .
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This lower bound uses only positivity of clock rates; it does not assume a special grid or symmetry in advance.

## 5. U024 upper bound: Gauss--Legendre attains the lower bound

Put

\[
M_k=\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rceil.
\]

Let `L_{M_k}` be the shifted Legendre polynomial of degree `M_k` on `[0,1]`, with simple roots

\[
0<z_1<\cdots<z_{M_k}<1.
\]

Let `ell_j` be the cardinal polynomial of degree `M_k-1` satisfying `ell_j(z_i)=1_{i=j}`, and define

\[
a_j=\int_0^1\ell_j(x)\,dx.
\]

The usual Gaussian-quadrature argument is short enough to record here. For any polynomial `p` of degree at most `2M_k-1`, divide

\[
p=qL_{M_k}+r,\qquad
\deg q,\deg r\le M_k-1.
\]

Orthogonality of `L_{M_k}` gives `int qL_{M_k}=0`. At every root `z_j`, `p(z_j)=r(z_j)`. Since interpolation by the `ell_j` is exact on degree at most `M_k-1`,

\[
\int_0^1p(x)\,dx
=
\int_0^1r(x)\,dx
=
\sum_{j=1}^{M_k}a_jr(z_j)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{M_k}a_jp(z_j).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The weights are positive: applying (5.1) to `ell_j^2`, whose degree is `2M_k-2`, gives

\[
a_j=\int_0^1\ell_j(x)^2\,dx>0.
\]

Reflection invariance of Lebesgue measure and
`L_m(1-x)=(-1)^mL_m(x)` imply that the nodes occur in complement pairs with equal weights, with an additional midpoint node when `M_k` is odd.

Therefore one raw phase realizes each complementary Gauss pair. If a pair `z,1-z` has Gauss weight `a` on each node, take raw bias `z<1/2` and rate

\[
w=\frac{a}{z(1-z)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Its transformed measure contributes exactly
`a delta_z+a delta_{1-z}`. If there is a midpoint node of Gauss weight `a`, take `x=1/2` and rate

\[
w=2a,
\tag{5.3}
\]

because the two transformed midpoint atoms coincide.

The number of raw phases is therefore

\[
\left\lceil\frac{M_k}{2}\right\rceil
=
\boxed{
\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{4}\right\rceil
}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since Gauss quadrature is exact through degree `2M_k-1>=k-2`, this attains the lower bound.

Among all independent finite Bernoulli-paintbox fragmentation mechanisms with positive phase rates, the minimum number of raw phases required to reproduce the **entire CTCS generator exactly on `P_k`** is

## 6. C045 — sparse Gauss-quadrature paintbox hierarchy

\[
m_N=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor,
\qquad
K_N=\min\{N,2m_N+1\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
r_N=\left\lceil\frac{m_N}{2}\right\rceil

raw Bernoulli phases using (5.2)--(5.3).

Define a fragmentation tree on `[N]` as follows. Every current nonsingleton block has independent Poisson clocks for these `r_N` raw phases. At a phase of bias `x`, color every leaf in that block iid Bernoulli(`x`). If both colors occur, replace the block by the two nonempty color classes; otherwise the mark is invisible. Child blocks receive fresh clocks with the same global `N` phase list.

This is a finite binary random hierarchy with real split times. Its restriction to visible labels is exactly Markov. ## 7. C045 is deliberately not CTCS at finite N

The quantifier swap would be uninteresting if C045 were merely the original CTCS law in disguise. It is not.

Let the `m=m_N` transformed Gauss nodes be `z_1,...,z_m`, and set

\[
P_m(x)=\prod_{j=1}^m(x-z_j)^2.
\]

This is monic of degree `2m`. The Gauss rule gives `Q_m(P_m)=0`. Every lower-degree monomial in `P_m-x^{2m}` is integrated exactly, so

\[
\int_0^1x^{2m}\,dx-Q_m(x^{2m})
=
\int_0^1P_m(x)\,dx
>0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Hence the first moment immediately beyond the Gauss exactness window is strictly wrong:

\[
\boxed{
Q_m(x^{2m})<\frac1{2m+1}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For every `N>=6`, `2m_N+2<=N`. Therefore the restriction to `2m_N+2` labels has a specified `(2m_N+1)|1` split rate different from CTCS, even though every block of size at most `2m_N+1` is exact. Moreover `q(1,1)=1`, so no positive global time rescaling can repair this mismatch. Relabeling also cannot alter block-size transition rates.
