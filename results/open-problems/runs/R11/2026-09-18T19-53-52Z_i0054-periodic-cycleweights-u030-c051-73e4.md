## 2. Move #29 — the missing boundary point is a root of unity

U029 assumed
\[
A(z)=\theta\log\frac1{1-z}+H(z)
\]
with \(H\) analytic past the unit circle. The proposed next target was to violate that assumption with a nonsummable oscillatory perturbation.

Let \((\vartheta_m)_{m\ge1}\) be a strictly positive periodic sequence of period \(d\), normalized by
\[
\frac1d\sum_{r=1}^d\vartheta_r=1.
\]
Put
\[
A(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\vartheta_m}{m}z^m,
\qquad
F(z)=e^{A(z)}=\sum_{n\ge0}f_nz^n.
\]
Writing \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/d}\) and
\[
\alpha_j=\frac1d\sum_{r=1}^d\vartheta_r\omega^{-jr},
\]
the discrete Fourier inversion gives
\[
\boxed{
A(z)=-\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_j\log(1-\omega^jz),
\qquad
F(z)=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-\omega^jz)^{-\alpha_j}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Here \(\alpha_0=1\). Because every \(\vartheta_r>0\), strictness in the triangle inequality gives
\[
\boxed{
\rho:=\max_{1\le j<d}\Re\alpha_j<1.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Thus \(z=1\) has a simple-pole exponent \(1\), while every other \(d\)-th root of unity has a strictly smaller singular exponent. This is exactly the boundary phenomenon hidden by U029's analytic-\(H\) hypothesis.

Near \(z=1\),
\[
A(z)=\log\frac1{1-z}+h_0+O(1-z),
\qquad
F(z)=\frac{C}{1-z}(1+O(1-z)),
\tag{2.3}
\]
where
\[
C=e^{h_0}>0,\qquad
h_0=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\vartheta_m-1}{m},
\]
and the last series converges by periodic zero-mean Dirichlet summation.

At any other root \(\zeta_j=\omega^{-j}\), \(j\ne0\),
\[
F(z)=C_j(1-z/\zeta_j)^{-\alpha_j}(1+O(1-z/\zeta_j)).
\tag{2.4}
\]
Standard finite-singularity transfer therefore gives
\[
\boxed{
f_n=C+O(n^{\rho-1})\longrightarrow C.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
This is the first place where adding the unit-circle boundary points matters: a direct “\(H\) analytic past \(|z|=1\)” argument is false, but the secondary singularities are still subcritical.

## 3. Exact uniform-cycle pointing identity

Sample a size-\(N\) permutation with the multiplicative cycle-weight law
\[
\Pr_N(\sigma)
=\frac1{N!f_N}\prod_{C\in{\rm Cycles}(\sigma)}\vartheta_{|C|}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Then, **after sampling the permutation**, choose one of its cycles uniformly among its \(K(\sigma)\) cycles. Let \(M_N\) be the selected cycle size.

Define
\[
B_A(z)
:=\int_0^1e^{tA(z)}\,dt
=\frac{F(z)-1}{A(z)}
=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nz^n.
\tag{3.2}
\]
All coefficients \(b_n\) are nonnegative.

The factor \(1/K\) in uniform cycle selection is exactly
\[
\frac1K=\int_0^1t^{K-1}\,dt.
\]
Marking the selected component in the labelled assembly therefore gives the **exact finite-\(N\) identity**
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(M_N=m)
=\frac{\vartheta_m}{m}\frac{b_{N-m}}{f_N}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
This identity is the uniform-component analogue of the usual labelled pointing formula; it is not the same as choosing a uniform **element**, which size-biases cycles.

Now use the root-of-unity decomposition of Section 2. At \(z=1\),
\[
B_A(z)
=
\frac{C+o(1)}
{(1-z)\{\log(1/(1-z))+h_0+o(1)\}}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
A one-logarithm transfer (equivalently the Hankel substitution \(z=1-u/n\)) gives
\[
[z^n]\frac{1}
{(1-z)\{\log(1/(1-z))+h_0\}}
\sim\frac1{\log n}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
For completeness, on the coefficient contour the substitution \(1-z=u/n\) turns the denominator into
\[
u\{\log n-\log u+h_0\},
\]
so after multiplication by \(\log n\) the local integrand tends to the standard simple-pole Hankel integrand; truncation away from the local arc is uniformly smaller. At a secondary root \(\zeta_j\), (2.4) and
\[
A(z)=\alpha_j\log\frac1{1-z/\zeta_j}+O(1)
\]
give a coefficient contribution
\[
O\!\left(\frac{n^{\Re\alpha_j-1}}{\log n}\right)
+O\!\left(\frac1{n(\log n)^2}\right)
=o(1/\log n)
\tag{3.6}
\]
because of (2.2). Summing the finitely many singularities proves
\[
\boxed{
b_n\sim\frac{C}{\log n}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
The same contour estimate, enlarged over finitely many initial \(n\), gives the bound
\[
0\le b_n\le\frac{C_1}{\log(n+2)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Combining (2.5), (3.3), and (3.7), uniformly on every fixed macroscopic window
\[
\varepsilon N\le m\le(1-\varepsilon)N,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(M_N=m)
=
\frac{\vartheta_m}{m\log N}(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

## 5. U030 — periodic mean-one cycle weights have the CTCS binary tangent

Fix visible labels forming one specified unordered split \(A\mid A^c\) with
\[
|A|=i,\qquad |A^c|=j,\qquad b=i+j.
\]
Conditional on \(M_N=m\), exchangeability makes the selected cycle support a uniform \(m\)-subset of `[N]`. Therefore the exact probability that this selected support separates the two visible sides is
\[
s_{N,m}(i,j)
=
\frac{
(m)_{\underline i}(N-m)_{\underline j}
+
(m)_{\underline j}(N-m)_{\underline i}}
{(N)_{\underline{i+j}}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let
\[
P_N(i,j)
=\sum_{m=1}^N\Pr(M_N=m)s_{N,m}(i,j).
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Bulk

For \(x=m/N\) bounded away from \(0,1\), (3.9) and (5.1) give
\[
(\log N)\Pr(M_N=m)s_{N,m}(i,j)
=
\frac1N\vartheta_m
\left[
x^{i-1}(1-x)^j+x^{j-1}(1-x)^i
\right]
+o(N^{-1})
\tag{5.3}
\]
uniformly on compact subintervals of \((0,1)\).

For every continuous \(g\),
\[
\frac1N\sum_{m=1}^N\vartheta_mg(m/N)
\longrightarrow
\int_0^1g(x)\,dx.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Indeed, split the sum into the \(d\) residue classes modulo \(d\); each residue class is a Riemann sum with asymptotic density \(1/d\), and the average of the \(d\) weights is \(1\).

### Lower endpoint

For \(m\le\varepsilon N\), (2.5), (3.3), (3.8) imply
\[
\Pr(M_N=m)\le \frac{C_2}{m\log N},
\tag{5.5}
\]
while, for fixed \(i,j\),
\[
s_{N,m}(i,j)
\le C_{i,j}\left[(m/N)^i+(m/N)^j\right].
\tag{5.6}
\]
Hence
\[
(\log N)\sum_{m\le\varepsilon N}
\Pr(M_N=m)s_{N,m}(i,j)
\le
C_{i,j}'(\varepsilon^i+\varepsilon^j).
\tag{5.7}
\]

### Upper endpoint

Write \(m=N-r\). For \(1\le r\le\varepsilon N\),
\[
\Pr(M_N=N-r)
\le \frac{C_3}{N\log(r+2)},
\tag{5.8}
\]
and if \(s=\min(i,j)\),
\[
s_{N,N-r}(i,j)\le C_{i,j}(r/N)^s.
\tag{5.9}
\]
Split \(r\le\sqrt N\) and \(\sqrt N<r\le\varepsilon N\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\log N)\sum_{r\le\varepsilon N}
\Pr(M_N=N-r)s_{N,N-r}(i,j)
&\le
C\frac{\log N}{N^{s+1}}
\sum_{r\le\sqrt N}r^s\\
&\quad+
C\frac1{N^{s+1}}
\sum_{\sqrt N<r\le\varepsilon N}r^s\\
&=o(1)+O(\varepsilon^{s+1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.10}
\]
(The term \(r=0\) cannot make a nontrivial visible split.)

Letting \(N\to\infty\) and then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), (5.3)--(5.10) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\log N)P_N(i,j)
&\longrightarrow
\int_0^1
\left[
x^{i-1}(1-x)^j+x^{j-1}(1-x)^i
\right]dx\\
&=B(i,j+1)+B(j,i+1)\\
&=\boxed{B(i,j)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.11}
\]
The last equality is exact:
\[
B(i,j+1)=\frac{j}{i+j}B(i,j),
\qquad
B(j,i+1)=\frac{i}{i+j}B(i,j).
\]

In particular
\[
P_N(1,1)\sim\frac1{\log N}.
\tag{5.12}
\]
Thus, with
\[
\lambda_N:=P_N(1,1)^{-1},
\]
we have the complete one-block generator statement
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_NP_N(i,j)\longrightarrow B(i,j)
\qquad\text{for every fixed }i,j\ge1.
}
\tag{5.13}
\]

### Theorem U030

**For every strictly positive periodic cycle-weight sequence of mean one, sampling the weighted permutation first and then one of its cycles uniformly, using that cycle support as a binary cut, and normalizing by the visible pair rate produces the CTCS beta split generator on every fixed finite restriction.**

The theorem is a genuine extension of U029: the correction to \(\log(1/(1-z))\) need not be analytic beyond the unit circle; it may have root-of-unity singularities of strictly smaller exponent.
