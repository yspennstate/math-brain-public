## 3. Move #1 — change category: from asynchronous Moran births to synchronous Wright–Fisher occupancy

C015 used an asynchronous Moran genealogy, where ancestral birth opportunities admit an exact finite harmonic spectrum `(N-1)/i`. The new route moves to the classical **generational Wright–Fisher** model, whose backward step is a random occupancy map rather than one ordered parent/death event.

Fix ambient population size `N`. A current sample with `r` active ancestral lineages chooses `r` parents independently and uniformly from `N` sites in the preceding generation. Lineages choosing the same parent merge simultaneously. The induced chain is a finite exchangeable Markov chain on set partitions.

For a fixed `r`, a particular pair of active lineages chooses the same parent with probability `1/N`. Exactly one binary merger therefore occurs with first-order probability `binom(r,2)/N`; every event involving two distinct collisions or a merger of three or more specified lineages is `O_r(N^{-2})`. Consequently its transient kernel satisfies

\[
K_{N,r}=I+\frac1N G_r+O_r(N^{-2}),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `G_r` is Kingman's finite-sample generator.

This is the decisive category change: the finite tree is no longer a Moran/Yule event tree and can have **simultaneous ancestral mergers** in one generation, but its potential kernel is still forced toward Kingman's.

## 4. Move #24 — the universal object is the Kingman potential, not one exact finite model

The tempting search for an “extremal” finite genealogy is the wrong formulation. What survives across Wright–Fisher, Moran and much of the Cannings domain is the **potential measure of the Kingman limit**.

For a Kingman genealogy on `m` leaves, let `L_{m,i}` be the total branch length subtending exactly `i` of the `m` leaves, with the conventional Kingman time scale on which each unordered pair coalesces at rate one. The classical branch-spectrum identity is

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E L_{m,i}=\frac{2}{i}},
\qquad 1\le i<m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

By exchangeability, for a fixed `i`-subset `A`,

\[
\mathbb E L(A)=\frac{2}{i\binom mi}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

If `j=m-i`, the unordered split `A|A^c` can be generated by an opportunity on the `A` orientation or on `A^c`; hence its Kingman potential is

\[
\frac{2}{i\binom mi}+\frac{2}{j\binom mj}
=2B(i,j).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus the beta split kernel is already encoded in the Kingman potential. The finite model need not have the exact harmonic SFS of C015; convergence of its ancestral potential to (4.1) is enough.

This yields the stronger theorem below.

### Theorem U005 — Kingman-domain ancestral compiler

For every fixed finite sample size `m`, let `\mathcal A_{N,m}` be an exchangeable, sampling-consistent discrete ancestral partition chain. Let one ancestral generation be one step of that chain and let `J_{N,m}` denote the total number of active-lineage opportunities before the MRCA, i.e. the sum over ancestral generations of the number of active lineages.

Assume, for each fixed `m`:

1. there is `c_N\downarrow0` such that the transient transition matrix satisfies
   \[
   K_{N,m}=I+c_NG_m+O_m(c_N^2),
   \tag{U5.1}
   \]
   entrywise, where `G_m` is the Kingman generator on nonabsorbed ancestral partitions;
2. the second moment is controlled by
   \[
   c_N^2\,\mathbb E J_{N,m}^2=O_m(1);
   \tag{U5.2}
   \]
3. choose a hierarchy time mesh `\delta_N\downarrow0` and mark every active-lineage opportunity independently with probability
   \[
   u_N=\frac{\delta_Nc_N}{2}.
   \tag{U5.3}
   \]

In one raw hierarchy generation, run a fresh ancestral chain in each current block to its MRCA, attach to each mark the descendant label set of the marked lineage, and refine the block by the common refinement of all marked bipartitions. Give one raw hierarchy generation physical duration `\delta_N`.

Then the recursively generated exchangeable-partition path converges to CTCS(infinity) in the projective finite-restriction `J_1` topology.

### Proof of U005

Fix a visible sample size `m`. Restrict the transient matrix to nonabsorbed states. From (U5.1),

\[
I-K_{N,m}=-c_NG_m+O_m(c_N^2).
\]

The state space is finite and `-G_m` is invertible on transient states. Therefore the fundamental matrix has the perturbative expansion

\[
c_N(I-K_{N,m})^{-1}=(-G_m)^{-1}+O_m(c_N).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Apply this potential kernel to the reward “number of active lineages subtending exactly the specified set `A`.” Equations (4.1)--(4.3) then imply that the expected number of ancestral opportunities producing the unordered split `A|A^c` is

\[
\frac{2}{c_N}B(i,j)+O_m(1).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Multiplying by the mark probability (U5.3), the **linear one-mark split mass** in one raw hierarchy step is

\[
\delta_NB(i,j)+O_m(\delta_Nc_N).
\tag{4.6}
\]

The only discrepancy between that linearization and the genuine common-refinement step comes from two or more marks. Conditional on `J`, the mark count is binomial, so

\[
\Pr(M\ge2)
\le \frac{u_N^2}{2}\mathbb EJ^2
=O_m(\delta_N^2),
\tag{4.7}
\]

by (U5.2).

Sampling consistency makes the same estimate valid after restriction from any larger hidden genealogy: the visible ancestral process has the same law as the process run directly on the visible sample, and the restricted marks remain Bernoulli marks on its active lineages. For every fixed hierarchy restriction `[k]`, uniformly over the complete hidden history,

\[
\left\|
P_N^{(k)}(\rho,\cdot)
-\bigl(I+\delta_NQ_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}\bigr)(\rho,\cdot)
\right\|_{\rm TV}
\le \delta_No_k(1)+O_k(\delta_N^2).
\tag{4.8}
\]

On a bounded physical interval `[0,T]` there are `O(T/\delta_N)` raw steps, so iterative conditional maximal coupling gives

\[
\Pr(\text{visible coupling fails before }T)=o_k(1).
\tag{4.9}
\]

For the comparison Euler chain `I+\delta_NQ^{(k)}`, a state `\rho` has a geometric holding time with parameter `\delta_N\lambda(\rho)`; after multiplication by `\delta_N` it converges to `Exp(\lambda(\rho))`, while the conditional next-state law is exactly `Q(\rho,\rho')/\lambda(\rho)`. A fragmentation of `k` labels has at most `k-1` proper visible refinements. Hence

\[
\widehat\Pi_N^{[k]}\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS}^{[k]}
\quad\text{in }J_1
\tag{4.10}
\]

for every fixed `k`. Truncate the deterministic tail of `d_proj` and couple finitely many restrictions to conclude

\[
\boxed{\widehat\Pi_N\Longrightarrow\Pi_{\rm CTCS(\infty)}}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

This proves U005. The finite checker below verifies representative exact Wright–Fisher transition/potential calculations; the theorem-sized argument is written here rather than delegated to code.

## 5. C016 — Wright–Fisher genealogy mutation-refinement hierarchy

Now apply U005 to the standard finite neutral haploid Wright–Fisher population.

For every global `N` and every current nonsingleton hierarchy block `B`, `|B|=m<=N`, independently in each raw hierarchy generation:

1. place the `m` labels at distinct present individuals of a neutral `N`-individual Wright–Fisher population;
2. trace their ancestry backward, generation by generation, until the MRCA; if there are `r` active lineages, each independently chooses one of the `N` parental sites uniformly, and lineages with the same parent merge;
3. **before** that generation's parent choices, independently mark every active ancestral lineage with probability
   \[
   u_N=\frac1{2N^2};
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
4. each mark carries the subset `S\subset B` of present labels descending from that lineage;
5. after the MRCA is reached, refine `B` by the common refinement of every marked bipartition `S|B\setminus S`; if no nontrivial mark occurred, leave `B` unchanged;
6. use fresh independent Wright–Fisher genealogies for different current hierarchy blocks and later raw hierarchy generations.

Give one raw hierarchy generation physical duration

\[
\boxed{\delta_N=\frac1N}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is an entirely finite discrete model. Its primitive randomness is synchronous multinomial reproduction plus rare intrinsic lineage mutation marks. Several marks can occur on one genealogy, and one Wright–Fisher generation can itself contain simultaneous mergers, so finite C016 is not the original binary CTCS transition rule.

### Verification of U005 assumptions

For each fixed visible sample size `m`, the parent-allocation calculation in Section 3 gives

\[
c_N=\frac1N,
\qquad
K_{N,m}=I+\frac1N G_m+O_m(N^{-2}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Sampling consistency is exact: ignoring untracked labels in a larger sample leaves the visible lineages choosing i.i.d. uniform parents from the same `N` sites. Collisions of a visible lineage with an untracked lineage do not alter the restricted visible partition; from the next generation the common parent is simply the visible lineage's ancestor. Marks on that lineage restrict in the same way.

For the second moment, when `r>=2` visible lineages remain, any fixed pair collides in the next generation with probability `1/N`. Thus the waiting time for a decrease is stochastically bounded by a geometric variable of mean `O_m(N)`. There are at most `m-1` decreases before absorption. Consequently the absorption time `\tau_{N,m}` satisfies

\[
\mathbb E\tau_{N,m}^2=O_m(N^2).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since `J_{N,m}\le m\tau_{N,m}`,

\[
\mathbb EJ_{N,m}^2=O_m(N^2),
\qquad
c_N^2\mathbb EJ_{N,m}^2=O_m(1).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Finally (U5.3) gives

\[
\frac{\delta_Nc_N}{2}=\frac1{2N^2}=u_N.
\]

So all hypotheses of U005 hold, and

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Pi_N^{\rm WF}
\Longrightarrow
\Pi_{\rm CTCS(\infty)}
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

for the complete exchangeable-partition genealogy in projective `J_1`.

Equivalently, for every fixed hierarchy restriction `[k]`,

\[
P_N^{(k)}
=I+\frac1NQ_{\rm CTCS}^{(k)}+O_k(N^{-2}),
\tag{5.7}
\]

and the accumulated coupling error over `O(N)` raw generations on a fixed physical-time interval is `O_k(N^{-1})`.

## 6. Move #28 — the single frozen “typical genealogy” route fails

The previous run identified the remaining artificial feature in C015: every raw hierarchy step refreshes its genealogy. I therefore tested the stronger quenched idea: draw one large Wright–Fisher genealogy once and reuse its empirical lineage hazards, hoping a “typical” genealogy self-averages to the CTCS rates.

It fails already for two labels.

Let `T_N` be their Wright–Fisher coalescence time in generations. Each generation they choose the same parent with probability `1/N`, so

\[
T_N\sim\operatorname{Geom}(1/N),
\qquad
\mathbb E\frac{T_N}{N}=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{T_N}{N}\right)=\frac{N-1}{N}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

In fact

\[
\frac{T_N}{N}\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Exp}(1).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus even the simplest empirical branch opportunity does **not** concentrate around its annealed mean. A single frozen genealogy retains order-one random hazard fluctuations. “Typical” is therefore insufficient to eliminate the refresh in C016/C015. A genuine one-forward-population answer will need additional self-averaging—many approximately independent loci, recombination, a large environmental ensemble, or another ergodic mechanism—not merely one large tree.

This is a useful obstruction rather than a new counted class.
