# 4. C082 — one fresh correlated cluster per proposal

Fix `N>=2`. For every active nonsingleton clade and every harmonic-grid mark

\[
x_s=\frac{s}{N},\qquad 1\le s\le N-1,
\]

run an independent proposal clock of rate

\[
\lambda_{N,s}
=\frac{N}{2s(N-s)}
=\frac{1}{2N x_s(1-x_s)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

At a ring, generate a **fresh** latent coloring as follows.

1. Mark every ambient label independently with probability `theta_N`.
2. Draw one common color `Z~Bernoulli(x_s)` and give it to every marked label.
3. Give every unmarked label its own independent `Bernoulli(x_s)` color.
4. Split the active clade by the two colors if both colors occur inside that clade; otherwise the proposal is null.

All marks and colors are discarded immediately after the proposal. Different proposals and different active clades use fresh independent randomness.

For C082 take

\[
\boxed{
\theta_N=rac{1}{\max\{2,\lceil\log_2N\rceil\}}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then `theta_N -> 0`, but the unique correlated block has expected ambient size

\[
N\theta_N\asymp \frac{N}{\log N}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Thus finite-`N` dependence is not obtained by keeping every dependence cell small: one cell is nearly macroscopic.

The total raw proposal rate on every active clade remains exactly

\[
\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{N,s}=H_{N-1}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For `theta_N<1`, any pair of distinct leaves has positive separation probability at every interior mark, so every nonsingleton clade has positive successful-split rate. With finitely many leaves, recursive splitting therefore gives a finite rooted binary hierarchy almost surely.

# 5. U062 — exact one-cluster deformation theorem

Fix a specified split `A|B` with

\[
|A|=i\ge1,\qquad |B|=j\ge1.
\]

At a single bias `x`, consider the oriented event `A` red and `B` blue. A direct conditioning on the common marked color gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\theta}^{i,j}(x)
={}&x\,[\theta+(1-\theta)x]^i[(1-\theta)(1-x)]^j\\
&+(1-x)[(1-\theta)x]^i[\theta+(1-\theta)(1-x)]^j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Indeed, on `Z=1`, every label of `B` must be unmarked and blue, while each label of `A` may either be marked or be unmarked-red; the `Z=0` term is symmetric.

Because the grid is invariant under `s<->N-s`, the two color orientations combine to give the exact finite-grid unordered specified-split rate

\[
\boxed{
q_{N,\theta}(i,j)
=\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}
 g_{i,j,\theta}(s/N),
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
g_{i,j,\theta}(x)
={}&\frac{P_{\theta}^{i,j}(x)}{x(1-x)}\\
={}&[\theta+(1-\theta)x]^i(1-\theta)^j(1-x)^{j-1}\\
&+(1-\theta)^i x^{i-1}[1-(1-\theta)x]^j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The apparent endpoint singularity has disappeared: `g` is a polynomial in `x` and `theta`.

Define its continuum counterpart

\[
q_{\theta}(i,j)=\int_0^1g_{i,j,\theta}(x)\,dx.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Let

\[
B_z(a,b)=\int_0^z t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\,dt
\]

be the unnormalized incomplete beta integral. In the first term of (5.3) substitute

\[
u=(1-\theta)(1-x),
\]

and in the second substitute

\[
u=(1-\theta)x.
\]

This gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
q_{\theta}(i,j)
=B_{1-\theta}(j,i+1)+B_{1-\theta}(i,j+1).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The complete beta recursion

\[
B(i,j)=B(j,i+1)+B(i,j+1)
\tag{5.6}
\]

therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
B(i,j)-q_{\theta}(i,j)
=\Delta_{\theta}(i,j),
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\theta}(i,j)
=\int_0^{\theta}
\left[(1-v)^{i-1}v^j+(1-v)^{j-1}v^i\right]dv
\ge0.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is the main U062 identity. It says that adding the one common cluster lowers every specified CTCS split rate, and it quantifies the loss exactly.

If

\[
s=\min\{i,j\},
\]

then the first nonzero power in (5.8) is `theta^{s+1}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\Delta_{\theta}(i,j)=O_{i,j}(\theta^{s+1}).}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Singleton-vs-rest splits feel the perturbation at order `theta^2`; more balanced splits are asymptotically much less sensitive.

## 5.1 Exact total-rate deficit on an `m`-block

Sum (5.8) over all unordered nontrivial splits of an `m`-set. Writing `j=m-i` and using the factor `binom(m,i)/2` for unordered bipartitions,

\[
D_m(\theta)
:=\sum_{A|A^c}\Delta_{\theta}(|A|,m-|A|).
\]

The binomial theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
D_m(\theta)
=\int_0^{\theta}
\frac{1-v^m-(1-v)^m}{1-v}\,dv.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
D_m(\theta)
=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}\frac{\theta^{a+1}}{a+1}
-\frac{1-(1-\theta)^m}{m}.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

Two immediate bounds are

\[
\boxed{
0\le D_m(\theta)
\le \min\left\{-\log(1-\theta),
\frac{m\theta^2}{2(1-\theta)}\right\}.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

For `m=2`, (5.10) reduces exactly to

\[
\boxed{D_2(\theta)=\theta^2,}
\tag{5.13}
\]

so the continuum pair-separation rate is `1-theta^2`. This also identifies the pair-collision probability of the latent dependence partition: two labels are in the unique common cell exactly when both are marked, with probability `theta^2`.

# 6. Complete convergence proof for C082

[0,1]\times[0,1/2]

its `x`-derivative is bounded by a constant depending only on `i,j`. Therefore the Riemann-sum error in (5.2) is uniform for `0<=theta<=1/2`:

\[
\sup_{0\le\theta\le1/2}
|q_{N,\theta}(i,j)-q_{\theta}(i,j)|
=O_{i,j}(N^{-1}).
\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
q_{N,\theta_N}(i,j)
=B(i,j)+o(1).
\tag{6.2}
\]

But `B(i,j)` is exactly the specified `i|j` split rate of CTCS.

### Hidden labels lump out exactly

Thus, for every fixed `k`,
