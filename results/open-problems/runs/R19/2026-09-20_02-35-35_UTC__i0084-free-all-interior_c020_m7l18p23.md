# 3. Biane's free-stable angular parametrization in a unified notation

Primary input: H. Bercovici and V. Pata, *Stable laws and domains of attraction in free probability theory*, Ann. Math. 149 (1999), with Philippe Biane's Appendix; arXiv:math/9905206. Parameter conversion is cross-checked against T. Hasebe and A. Kuznetsov, *On free stable distributions*, ECP 19 (2014), paper 56, arXiv:1404.2981.

Fix
\[
a:=\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\},\qquad b:=|a-1|\in(0,1).
\]

For every **interior** free-stable skewness, Biane's density can be parameterized by `theta in (0,pi)` as follows. There is a number
\[
A\in(0,(1-b)\pi)
\]
and
\[
B(\theta):=A+b\theta\in(0,\pi)
\]
such that
\[
q(\theta):=k(x(\theta))
=\frac1\pi(\sin\theta)^{1+1/a}(\sin B(\theta))^{-1/a}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The spatial coordinate is
\[
x(\theta)
=(\sin\theta)^{-1/a}(\sin B)^{1/a-1}
\begin{cases}
\sin(B-\theta),&0<a<1,\\
\sin(B+\theta),&1<a<2.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Biane proves this is the analytic positive density parametrization of the two-sided free stable law, with `theta` running once through the real line.

For `0<a<1`, Hasebe--Kuznetsov's positivity parameter `rho in (0,1)` gives
\[
A=a\rho\pi.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For `1<a<2`, Biane's appendix uses an auxiliary `r in (0,1)` with
\[
A=(2-a)r\pi.
\]
Comparison with Hasebe--Kuznetsov's Voiculescu transform
\[
\phi_{a,\rho}(z)=-e^{\pi i a\rho}z^{1-a}
\]
gives
\[
r=\frac{1-a\rho}{2-a},\qquad
A=(1-a\rho)\pi,
\tag{3.4}
\]
where the admissible interior is
\[
1-a^{-1}<\rho<a^{-1}.
\]
Thus (3.1)--(3.2) cover exactly the full two-sided interior in the source's standard skewness convention.

# 4. R19-I0084-C020A — every interior free stable density has a nondegenerate mode

**STATUS: PROVED INTERNALLY / AUDIT PENDING.**

This is the key new lemma. No external all-derivative bell-shape theorem is used.

Set
\[
L(\theta):=\log q(\theta).
\]
Differentiating (3.1),
\[
L'(\theta)
=\frac{a+1}{a}\cot\theta-\frac{b}{a}\cot B(\theta).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Hence a critical point satisfies
\[
(a+1)\cot\theta=b\cot B.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Put
\[
\lambda:=\frac{a+1}{b}>1
\]
and let `arccot` take values in `(0,pi)`. Since `B in (0,pi)`, (4.2) is equivalent to
\[
B(\theta)=\operatorname{arccot}(\lambda\cot\theta).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
F(\theta):=\operatorname{arccot}(\lambda\cot\theta)-b\theta=A.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The point of this translation is that the geometry of `F` is explicit. Define
\[
D(\theta):=(a+1)\cot^2\theta-(3-a).
\tag{4.4}
\]
A direct derivative calculation gives the exact identity
\[
F'(\theta)
=-\frac{aD(\theta)}{b\bigl(1+\lambda^2\cot^2\theta\bigr)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Let `theta_- in (0,pi/2)` and `theta_+=pi-theta_-` be the two roots of `D`, equivalently
\[
\cot^2\theta_-=\frac{3-a}{a+1}.
\]
Then

- `F'<0` on `(0,theta_-)`;
- `F'>0` on `(theta_-,theta_+)`;
- `F'<0` on `(theta_+,pi)`.

The endpoint expansions are equally simple:
\[
F(\theta)=-\frac{ab}{a+1}\theta+o(\theta)
\qquad(\theta\downarrow0),
\tag{4.6}
\]
and, writing `epsilon=pi-theta`,
\[
F(\pi-\epsilon)
=(1-b)\pi+\frac{ab}{a+1}\epsilon+o(\epsilon)
\qquad(\epsilon\downarrow0).
\tag{4.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
F(\theta_-)<0,
\qquad
F(\theta_+)>(1-b)\pi.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Since the allowed parameter is exactly
\[
0<A<(1-b)\pi,
\]
(4.5)--(4.8) show that equation (4.3) has **exactly one** solution `theta_m`, and it lies on the middle branch `(theta_-,theta_+)`.

We now compute curvature. At a critical point, put
\[
u:=\cot\theta_m,\qquad v:=\cot B(\theta_m)=\lambda u.
\]
From (3.1),
\[
L''(\theta_m)
=-\frac{a+1}{a}(1+u^2)+\frac{b^2}{a}(1+v^2).
\]
Using `b^2=(a-1)^2` and `v=(a+1)u/b`, this simplifies exactly to
\[
\boxed{
L''(\theta_m)
=(a+1)u^2-(3-a)=D(\theta_m)<0.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]
The final strict inequality holds because `theta_m` lies in the middle branch, where `D<0`.

Thus `q` has a unique critical point and its second derivative there is strictly negative:
\[
q''(\theta_m)=q(\theta_m)L''(\theta_m)<0.
\tag{4.10}
\]

It remains to transfer this to curvature in the physical coordinate `x`; one must not silently divide by a possibly vanishing `x'`. We verify nonvanishing exactly at the mode.

Write
\[
C=(\sin\theta)^{-1/a}(\sin B)^{1/a-1}>0.
\]
For `a>1`, equation (3.2) is `x=C sin(B+theta)`. Substituting the critical relation `v=(a+1)u/(a-1)` into its derivative gives
\[
\frac{x'(\theta_m)}{C\sin B\sin\theta_m}
=-a(1+u^2)<0.
\tag{4.11}
\]
For `0<a<1`, equation (3.2) is `x=C sin(B-theta)`, and the same substitution gives
\[
\frac{x'(\theta_m)}{C\sin B\sin\theta_m}
=\frac{a\bigl((3a+1)u^2+1-a\bigr)}{a-1}<0.
\tag{4.12}
\]
Hence `x'(theta_m) != 0` in the entire interior parameter range.

Biane's angular parametrization is one-to-one; its endpoint behavior runs from `+infinity` at `theta downarrow0` to `-infinity` at `theta uparrow pi`, so `x(theta)` is strictly decreasing. Moreover `q'=k'(x(theta))x'`. We have just shown `x'` is nonzero at the sole zero of `q'`; away from that zero, analyticity of `k` and `q' != 0` precludes `x'=0`. Thus in fact `x'<0` throughout `(0,pi)`.

At the physical mode `m=x(theta_m)`, since `q'(theta_m)=0`, the chain rule gives
\[
\boxed{
k''(m)=\frac{q''(\theta_m)}{(x'(\theta_m))^2}<0.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]
By continuity there exist `epsilon,c0>0` such that, after translating `m` to zero,
\[
k''(x)\le-c_0<0\qquad(|x|\le2\epsilon).
\tag{4.14}
\]

Finally, (4.1), the uniqueness just proved, and the endpoint signs show `q'>0` before `theta_m` and `q'<0` after it. Since `x'<0`,
\[
k'(x)<0\quad(x>m),
\qquad
k'(x)>0\quad(x<m).
\tag{4.15}
\]
Thus C008's entire central shape hypothesis is proved for **every** interior free stable density, including parameters for which an all-orders bell-shape theorem is unavailable.

# 5. R19-I0084-C020B — exact critical derivative tails from the same coordinate

**STATUS: PROVED INTERNALLY / AUDIT PENDING.**

The next requirement is a *pointwise derivative* tail of order `2+a`. It is tempting to differentiate a quoted density asymptotic, but that is not automatically legitimate. The angular formula lets us compute `k'=q'/x'` directly.

Let
\[
S_+:=\sin A>0.
\]
As `theta downarrow 0`, both versions of (3.2) give
\[
x(\theta)
=S_+^{1/a}\theta^{-1/a}(1+O(\theta)),
\tag{5.1}
\]
while (3.1) gives
\[
q(\theta)
=\frac1\pi S_+^{-1/a}\theta^{1+1/a}(1+O(\theta)).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
x'(\theta)
=-\frac1aS_+^{1/a}\theta^{-1/a-1}(1+O(\theta)),
\tag{5.3}
\]
\[
q'(\theta)
=\frac{a+1}{a\pi}S_+^{-1/a}\theta^{1/a}(1+O(\theta)).
\tag{5.4}
\]
Since (5.1) implies
\[
\theta=S_+x^{-a}(1+o(1)),
\]
we obtain directly
\[
\boxed{
k'(x)
\sim-\frac{a+1}{\pi}S_+x^{-a-2}
\qquad(x\to+\infty).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The left tail follows either by repeating the `theta -> pi` expansion or by reflection. Define
\[
S_-:=
\begin{cases}
\sin(\pi a(1-\rho)),&0<a<1,\\
\sin(\pi a(1-\rho)),&1<a<2,
\end{cases}
\]
where in the second line `rho` is in the Hasebe--Kuznetsov admissible interval. In both regimes `S_->0` for interior skewness, and similarly
\[
S_+=\sin(\pi a\rho)>0.
\tag{5.6}
\]
For `a>1`, this uses `A=(1-a rho)pi` and `sin A=sin(pi a rho)`; at the opposite endpoint `B(pi)=a(1-rho)pi`.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
k'(-x)
\sim\frac{a+1}{\pi}S_-x^{-a-2}
\qquad(x\to+\infty).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

In particular there are `R,c_+,c_->0` such that
\[
-k'(x)\ge c_+x^{-(2+a)}\quad(x\ge R),
\]
\[
k'(x)\ge c_-|x|^{-(2+a)}\quad(x\le-R).
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is exactly the frozen-source exponent. No epsilon of extra moment and no compact-support assumption has entered.

Biane's formulas also give analyticity on every compact part of the positive density, while differentiating the endpoint expansions gives `k',k'' -> 0` in both tails. Hence `k'` and `k''` are globally bounded. Together with Section 4, every C008 hypothesis is now verified with
\[
p=2+a.
\tag{5.9}
\]
