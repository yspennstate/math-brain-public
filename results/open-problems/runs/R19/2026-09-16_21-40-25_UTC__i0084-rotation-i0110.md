# 5. Three final new-method probes before rotation

These probes were used to improve the handoff and identify the minimal future package. They produce **no new theorem claim**.

## Move #8 — GO THE OTHER WAY AND DISCRETIZE

I tried to simplify the sparse source refutation to a finite-support initial law. The retained all-time proof mechanism does not survive naïve truncation. It uses an unbounded ladder of remote atoms: when the stable scale grows, a later atom supplies a new protected bump. A finite truncation supplies only finitely many such protected scales in that argument.

Therefore a finite-support witness cannot simply be obtained by truncating the established construction. I did not obtain a finite-support law that remains nonunimodal for all sufficiently large times, and I make no claim that none exists. What is proved by this route is only a **failure of the truncation method**: the present all-time certificate genuinely consumes infinitely many scales.

This is useful for an auditor because it explains why the sparse infinite law is structural rather than an unnecessarily complicated numerical choice.

## Move #14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING

The relevant symmetry is the stable dilation action. Large time corresponds to large spatial scale; after normalizing by the stable scale, every fixed finite set of translations collapses toward the origin. The sparse counterexample defeats this by placing successively smaller masses at successively larger translation scales.

Thinking of the construction modulo dilation makes the mechanism almost tautological: the proof needs a new obstruction at each scale orbit. This is why the remote-atom sequence is naturally organized as a scale ladder, and why the large-time limit can converge strongly to the stable profile while microscopic remote modes survive.

Again, this is an explanatory reformulation of retained proofs, not a new theorem.

## Move #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT

For the source-level yes/no question, the extremal skewness endpoint is the efficient object. At the endpoint the stable kernel has one-sided or super-light-tail geometry and remote bumps can be shielded. In the interior, both tails are polynomial and the separate critical-tail smoothing theorem becomes relevant.

Because Conjecture 5.5 is universal over the admissible stable laws, one endpoint counterexample is enough to refute it. Therefore the future closure package should lead with the endpoint witness and should treat the interior classification as optional strengthening rather than as a prerequisite. This materially reduces audit complexity without weakening the source-level verdict.
