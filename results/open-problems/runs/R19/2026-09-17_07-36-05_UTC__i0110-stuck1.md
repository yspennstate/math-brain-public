# 9. (e) Nearest solved problem and the ingredient it has that this route lacks

The nearest solved problem is exactly Hermon--Olesker-Taylor's random-Abelian Cayley cutoff theorem.

Their proof starts with the algebraic model itself. If `Z_1,...,Z_k` are the random generators, it introduces an auxiliary coordinate walk `W` on `Z^k` with

`S(t)=W(t) dot Z`,

and for every `gamma` passes to `Z_gamma^k` and the group quotient `G/gamma G`. The cutoff clock is the maximum of the corresponding entropic times. In symbols, the source defines

\[
\tau_*(k,G)=\max_{\gamma\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}}
\tau_0\bigl(\gamma,|G/\gamma G|\bigr)
\]

and proves cutoff at `tau_*` with high probability under the near-optimal generation hypothesis.

What that proof has and the current Salez-structural route lacks is **an externally supplied algebraic presentation and labeled generator multiset**. Those data make the quotient tower canonical. Salez's requested property should instead be visible as a property of the realized random graph/chain and deterministically trigger cutoff. The entire remaining structural problem is to replace the supplied presentation by a graph-intrinsic surrogate without collapsing back to a tautological mixing statement.

Pedrotti--Salez is the nearest solved **general analytic** theorem: a local non-equilibrium Poincare constant controls the mixing-window width, and their restricted version may ignore bad early times. But that theorem does not presently supply the missing graph-intrinsic quotient/presentation surrogate or an all-nine a.a.s. verification.
