## 3. Idea before code

The stuck obstruction was not the HOT entropy calculation. It was that HOT is handed the group and labelled random generators, whereas Salez asks for a **structural property of the realized graph**.

For `G=(Z/p^2 Z)^d`, the first genuinely nested Abelian quotient is

\[
G \longrightarrow G/pG \longrightarrow 0.
\]

The key observation is that, in a random sparse Cayley graph, **commutation squares distinguish a generator edge from its inverse edge**:

- two different generator directions always span a simple commuting 4-cycle;
- the two opposite edges `+Z_i` and `-Z_i` at one vertex do not span a simple 4-cycle unless there is an accidental relation of word length at most four.

Thus a purely graph-theoretic local rule can recover straight continuation, and then all generator directions. Once directions are recovered, taking exactly `p` straight steps recovers the cosets of `pG`. This is precisely the quotient missing from the naive one-scale cover.

No computation was written until this route was complete on paper.

## 4. New special-case theorem

### Theorem C018 — graph-intrinsic recovery of the HOT quotient tower for random `(Z/p^2 Z)^d`

Fix an odd prime `p`. Let

\[
G_d=(\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z)^d,
\qquad k=d+s_d,
\qquad s_d\to\infty,
\qquad k=O(d),
\]

and choose

\[
Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(G_d).
\]

Let `X_d` be the undirected Cayley graph with connection multiset `\{\pm Z_1,\ldots,\pm Z_k\}`, conditioned only through the random choice above (no generator labels are supplied to the reconstruction).

Then with probability `1-o(1)` as `d->infinity`, the **unlabelled simple graph `X_d` alone** canonically determines, up to base-point translation, permutation of the `k` directions, and inversion of individual directions:

1. the `k` generator-direction classes of edges;
2. a straight-continuation permutation in each direction, all of whose cycles have length `p^2`;
3. the coordinate quotient/lift architecture `Z_{p^2}^k -> V(X_d)`;
4. the partition of `V(X_d)` into the cosets of `pG_d`, equivalently the quotient `G_d/pG_d \cong (Z/pZ)^d`;
5. hence the two nonzero HOT entropic clocks `t_p` and `t_{p^2}` and the exact identity

\[
\boxed{t_* = \max\{t_p,t_{p^2}\}.}
\]

Consequently, on this source-admissible family, the model-supplied HOT coordinate presentation can be replaced by a graph-intrinsic construction. In particular, HOT's already-proved entropic/collision estimates may be applied after this reconstruction without supplying the hidden generator labels externally.

## 5. Proof of C018

### 5.1 A high-probability clean event

Write `N=|G_d|=p^{2d}`. Let `E_d` be the conjunction of:

- **generation modulo `p`**: the reductions `\bar Z_i in F_p^d` span `F_p^d`;
- **full order**: every `Z_i` has order `p^2`;
- **no nontrivial relation of word length at most four**:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^k c_i Z_i=0,
\qquad
0<\sum_i |c_i|\le4,
\]

has no solution in integers `c_i`.

The first event has exact probability

\[
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1-p^{j-k}),
\]

so

\[
\Pr(\operatorname{rank}<d)
\le \sum_{j=0}^{d-1}p^{j-k}
\le \frac{p^{-s_d}}{p-1}=o(1).
\]

An element of `(Z/p^2 Z)^d` has order smaller than `p^2` exactly when it belongs to `pG_d`; this has probability `p^{-d}`. Thus

\[
\Pr(\exists i:\operatorname{ord}(Z_i)<p^2)
\le k p^{-d}=o(1).
\]

For the short-relation event, there are `O(k^4)` coefficient vectors with `l_1` norm at most four. If `p>=5`, every nonzero coefficient among `\{-4,\ldots,4\}` is a unit modulo `p` except possible multiples of `p`, but no such nonzero multiple occurs for `p>=5` and norm at most four. For `p=3`, the only possible nonunit coefficient in that range is `\pm3`; a one-coordinate relation `3Z_i=0` is excluded by the full-order event, while any remaining relation of total `l_1` norm at most four contains another coefficient `\pm1`, hence a unit. Conditioning on all other generators, a unit-coefficient relation determines the remaining uniform generator uniquely. Therefore

\[
\Pr(\text{a nontrivial short relation}\mid\text{full order})
=O(k^4/N)=o(1).
\]

Since `k=O(d)` and `N=p^{2d}`, all three failures are `o(1)`. Hence

\[
\Pr(E_d)=1-o(1).
\]

Notice that `E_d` also excludes generator collisions `Z_i=\pm Z_j`, so on `E_d` the unlabelled graph is simple and `2k`-regular.

### 5.2 A graph-only local rule recovers straight continuation

Fix a realization in `E_d` and a vertex `x`. Let

\[
y=x+a,
\qquad a\in\{\pm Z_1,\ldots,\pm Z_k\}.
\]

Consider another neighbor `z=x+b`.

If `b\ne\pm a`, then Abelian commutation gives the simple 4-cycle

\[
x,\quad x+a,\quad x+a+b,\quad x+b,\quad x.
\]

Simplicity follows from the absence of collisions and short relations.

If `b=-a`, suppose the two incident edges `x--(x+a)` and `x--(x-a)` belonged to a simple 4-cycle. Its other two edge increments, say `u,v` from the same signed generator set, would satisfy

\[
2a+u+v=0.
\]

After combining equal generator directions this is a nonzero relation of `l_1` norm at most four unless `u=v=-a`; the latter walk immediately revisits `x` and is not a simple 4-cycle. The clean event therefore forbids such a simple 4-cycle.

Thus:

> For every incident edge `e={x,x+a}`, among all other edges incident to `x`, **exactly one** is not co-contained with `e` in any simple 4-cycle, namely `{x,x-a}`.

This is a statement purely about the unlabelled graph. Pair those two edges. If a walk arrives at `x+a` along `{x,x+a}`, the unique paired edge at `x+a` is `{x+a,x+2a}`. Iterating this graph-defined operation recovers straight continuation in direction `a`.

Because `ord(a)=p^2`, every straight cycle has graph-detectable length exactly `p^2`. In particular, even if `p` were not supplied externally, this homocyclic special case recovers `p` as the positive square root of the common straight-cycle length.

### 5.3 Global direction classes are graph-intrinsic

Define an equivalence relation on undirected edges generated by two graph-only moves:

1. pair the two locally straight edges at a vertex by the preceding non-co-4-cycle rule;
2. identify opposite sides of every simple 4-cycle.

On `E_d`, move (1) never changes the hidden generator index, and every simple 4-cycle is the standard commuting square for two distinct directions, so move (2) also preserves the hidden generator index.

Conversely, all edges with the same hidden index are connected by these moves. Local straight pairing transports an edge along its own `\pm Z_i` cycle. Opposite-side identifications in commuting squares translate that edge by every other generator `\pm Z_j`. Since the `Z_j` generate `G_d`, all translates are reached.

Hence the graph-defined equivalence classes are **exactly the `k` hidden generator directions**.

Choose a base vertex, arbitrarily order the `k` graph-recovered classes, and choose one orientation in each class. The commuting squares propagate each chosen orientation through the graph; in the hidden Cayley realization this propagation is consistent and yields commuting permutations

\[
T_1,\ldots,T_k
\]

of the vertex set, each of order `p^2`. The map

\[
\Phi:\mathbb Z^k\to V(X_d),
\qquad
(n_1,\ldots,n_k)\mapsto
T_1^{n_1}\cdots T_k^{n_k}(o)
\]

therefore factors through `Z_{p^2}^k` and reproduces the original Cayley coordinate map up to the harmless basepoint/permutation/inversion choices. Those choices leave all coordinate entropies and collision quantities invariant.

This proves graph-intrinsic recovery of the coordinate lift needed by the HOT method on this family.

### 5.4 `p` straight steps recover `G/pG`

Construct from the unlabelled graph a new graph `J_p` on the same vertex set: connect `x` to the endpoint obtained by taking exactly `p` graph-defined straight steps in any recovered direction.

In the hidden coordinates these moves are precisely

\[
x\mapsto x\pm pZ_i.
\]

Because the reductions `\bar Z_i` span `F_p^d`, the elements `pZ_i` generate

\[
pG_d\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^d.
\]

Therefore the connected components of `J_p` are exactly the cosets of `pG_d`. The component partition is thus graph-intrinsic, and its component set is canonically the quotient

\[
G_d/pG_d\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^d.
\]

In particular the graph determines

\[
|G_d/pG_d|=p^d
\]

without being handed the original group presentation.

### 5.5 The entire HOT entropic maximum collapses to the two recovered levels

HOT Definition A sets `t_gamma` by

\[
H(W_\gamma(t_\gamma))=\log|G_d/\gamma G_d|,
\]

where `W_gamma` is the rate-one auxiliary walk on `Z_gamma^k` in the relevant directed/undirected convention.

For `G_d=(Z/p^2 Z)^d`, there are only three quotient types.

**Case 1: `p` does not divide `gamma`.** Multiplication by `gamma` is an automorphism of `G_d`, so `gamma G_d=G_d`, the target entropy is zero, and

\[
t_\gamma=0.
\]

**Case 2: `v_p(gamma)=1`.** Then `gamma G_d=pG_d`, so the target is

\[
\log|G_d/pG_d|=d\log p.
\]

Reduction modulo `p` maps the walk on `Z_gamma^k` onto the walk on `Z_p^k`. Entropy cannot increase under a deterministic factor, hence

\[
H(W_\gamma(t))\ge H(W_p(t)).
\]

The entropy of these continuous-time walks is nondecreasing in `t` (their transition kernels are doubly stochastic/convolution kernels). Therefore

\[
t_\gamma\le t_p.
\]

Equality is attained at `gamma=p`.

**Case 3: `v_p(gamma)>=2`.** Then `gamma G_d=0`, so the target is

\[
\log|G_d|=2d\log p.
\]

Reduction modulo `p^2` gives

\[
H(W_\gamma(t))\ge H(W_{p^2}(t)),
\]

and therefore

\[
t_\gamma\le t_{p^2},
\]

with equality at `gamma=p^2`. The same entropy-factor comparison also dominates the `gamma=infinity` auxiliary walk if it is included in the notation.

Consequently

\[
\boxed{t_* = \max\{t_p,t_{p^2}\}.}
\]

Both entries are now graph-intrinsic:

- `k=deg(X_d)/2`;
- `p^2` is the common straight-cycle length;
- `|G_d|=|V(X_d)|`;
- `|G_d/pG_d|` is the number of components of `J_p`.

This completes C018.
