## 5. Method #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: multiresolution Haar covariance

For `f in C([0,1])` define normalized dyadic increments

\[
X_{n,k}(f)=2^{n/2}\left[f((k+1)2^{-n})-f(k2^{-n})\right]
\tag{5.1}
\]

and Haar detail

\[
H_{n,k}(f)=\frac{X_{n+1,2k}(f)-X_{n+1,2k+1}(f)}{\sqrt2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For Brownian motion the root increment and all `H_{n,k}` are iid `N(0,1)`.

For every even `n>=4`, write `M=2^{n-1}` and define

\[
\rho_n(k)=
\begin{cases}
2(k+M),&0\le k<M,\\
2(k-M)+1,&M\le k<2^n.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

It is injective; direct substitution gives reflection equivariance

\[
\boxed{\rho_n(2^n-1-k)=2^{n+1}-1-\rho_n(k)};
\tag{5.4}
\]

and if `I_{n,k}=[k2^{-n},(k+1)2^{-n}]`, then

\[
\operatorname{dist}(I_{n,k},I_{n+1,\rho_n(k)})
=\frac12-2^{-n}\ge\frac7{16}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Pairings are imposed only for even source levels, so every equivalence class used later has size at most two.

Define the cross-scale echo statistic

\[
\mathcal C_n(f)=2^{-n}\sum_{k=0}^{2^n-1}
\cos\left(H_{n,k}(f)-H_{n+1,\rho_n(k)}(f)\right),
\qquad n\ge4\text{ even}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

For Brownian motion each difference is `Z-Z'` with independent standard normals, so

\[
\mathbb E\cos(Z-Z')=e^{-1}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

## 6. Method #27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS: all Brownian phases first

### Lemma 6.1 — uniform-phase echo law

For standard Brownian motion `B` and every finite `A`, almost surely

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le\theta\le A}
\left|\mathcal C_n(B_{\theta+\cdot}-B_\theta)-e^{-1}\right|
\to0
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

along even `n`. One probability-one event works for all phases `theta>=0`.

### Proof

At deterministic phase `theta`, the level-`n` coefficients and the injected level-`n+1` coefficients are distinct shifted Haar Wiener integrals, hence independent standard normals. Injection of `rho_n` makes the `2^n` summands iid, bounded, mean `e^{-1}`. Hoeffding gives

\[
\Pr\{|\mathcal C_n(\theta)-e^{-1}|>\varepsilon\}
\le2e^{-c_\varepsilon2^n}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Mesh `[0,A]` at spacing `delta_n=2^{-8n}`. The `O_A(2^{8n})`-point union bound is summable, so Borel--Cantelli gives uniform convergence on the mesh. If `|theta-theta'|<=delta_n`, every relevant Haar detail changes by at most `C 2^{n/2} omega_B(delta_n)`. Brownian compact-interval modulus gives

\[
2^{n/2}\omega_B(2^{-8n})=O(\sqrt n\,2^{-7n/2})\to0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Cosine is 1-Lipschitz, so mesh convergence transfers to all phases. Intersect over integer `A`. QED.

The quantifier order is the point: prove the Brownian statement for **all phases before** selecting any random FBM witness index/phase.

## 7. Deterministic trace: every fixed same-scale block Brownian, cross-scale echo perfect

Start with a standard Gaussian root `G`. For Haar coefficients `xi_{n,k}`, impose

\[
\boxed{\xi_{n+1,\rho_n(k)}=\xi_{n,k}}
\tag{7.1}
\]

for every even `n>=4` and every `k`, and give distinct equivalence classes independent `N(0,1)` labels. Since only even levels are sources, classes have size one or two.

With normalized Haar wavelets `h_{n,k}` and Schauder functions `S_{n,k}(t)=\int_0^t h_{n,k}(s)ds`, define

\[
Q(t)=Gt+\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{k<2^n}\xi_{n,k}S_{n,k}(t).
\tag{7.2}
\]

At each `t` only one Schauder function per level is nonzero and `||S_{n,k}||_infty=2^{-n/2-1}`. A Gaussian union bound gives `max_k|xi_{n,k}|=O(sqrt(n))` a.s.; hence the level sup norms `O(sqrt(n)2^{-n/2})` are summable and (7.2) converges uniformly a.s. to a continuous function. By the Haar recursion,

\[
H_{n,k}(Q)=\xi_{n,k},
\tag{7.3}
\]

so (7.1) yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_n(Q)=1\quad\text{for every even }n\ge4.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### 7.1 Exact local Brownian laws

The coefficient covariance differs from the independent Brownian Haar covariance only by off-diagonal correlations between each paired `(h_{n,k},h_{n+1,rho_n(k)})`. Their supports are separated by at least `7/16`. Therefore any interval `J` with `|J|<7/16` cannot meet both supports in a correlated pair. For increment test functions supported in `J`, every new off-diagonal covariance contribution has a zero factor; diagonal variances remain one. Thus the Gaussian restriction to increments inside `J` has exactly Brownian covariance, hence exactly Brownian joint law.

Consequently, for every fixed `r`, once `r2^{-N}<7/16`, every vector of `r` consecutive level-`N` normalized increments

\[
(X_{N,k},\ldots,X_{N,k+r-1})
\tag{7.5}
\]

has ensemble law `N(0,1)^{\otimes r}`.

### 7.2 Select one deterministic realization generic for all fixed `r`

Let `M=2^N`, `L=M-r+1`, and for bounded 1-Lipschitz `F:R^r->R` put

\[
A_{N,r,F}=\frac1L\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}F(X_{N,k},\ldots,X_{N,k+r-1}).
\tag{7.6}
\]

For large `N`, exact local Brownian law gives `E A_{N,r,F}=E F(Z_1,...,Z_r)` for iid standard normals.

The coefficient covariance is a direct sum of `[1]` and paired `[[1,1],[1,1]]` blocks, so its operator norm is at most `2`. The Haar transform to the level-`N` normalized-increment vector is orthogonal in the independent case, hence the increment covariance operator norm is also at most `2`. By Cauchy--Schwarz, because each coordinate lies in at most `r` consecutive blocks, (7.6) has Euclidean Lipschitz constant at most `sqrt(r/L)`. Gaussian concentration therefore gives

\[
\Pr\{|A_{N,r,F}-EA_{N,r,F}|>\varepsilon\}
\le2\exp(-c\varepsilon^2L/r),
\tag{7.7}
\]

summable in `N`. Borel--Cantelli gives almost-sure convergence for every fixed `r,F`. Intersect over all `r` and a countable convergence-determining dense class of bounded Lipschitz `F`, together with the continuity event, and freeze one outcome. The resulting `Q` is deterministic and continuous and satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{2^N-r+1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^N-r}
\delta_{(X_{N,k}(Q),\ldots,X_{N,k+r-1}(Q))}
\Longrightarrow \gamma^{\otimes r}
\quad\text{for every fixed }r,
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

while still satisfying the perfect cross-scale echo (7.4). Thus every fixed finite same-scale microscopic block statistic is asymptotically Brownian, yet the cross-scale statistic is maximally non-Brownian.

## 9. T81 — cross-scale Haar-echo gate obstruction

### Theorem T81

Let `Q in C([0,1])` satisfy

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty,\ n\text{ even}}|\mathcal C_n(Q)-e^{-1}|>0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Suppose a deterministic space-time shape has arbitrarily far unit gate blocks: `a_m->infinity` and constants `c_m` with

\[
K(a_m+s)\subset\{c_m+Q(s)\},\qquad0\le s\le1,
\tag{9.2}
\]

and completely arbitrary fibers between gates. Then every FBM `X` obeys

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(\exists R<\infty:\ X_{-u}\in K(u)\ \forall u\ge R\right)=0.
}
\tag{T81}
\]

### Proof

For each fixed FBM witness index `j`, `B^{(j)}` from (2.1) is standard Brownian. Apply Lemma 6.1 and intersect over all integer phase windows and then over the countable witness indices. On one probability-one event `S_j->-infinity` and every witness Brownian path has echo limit `e^{-1}` at every phase.

Assume eventual confinement. Choose a gate with `a_m>=R`, then choose `j` with `S_j<=-(a_m+1)`. Set `theta=-(a_m+1)-S_j>=0`. For `0<=r<=1`, actual time `S_j+theta+r=-(a_m+1)+r` has age `a_m+1-r`, so confinement on the gate gives

\[
B^{(j)}_{\theta+r}-B^{(j)}_\theta
=Q(1-r)-Q(1)=(RQ)(r).
\tag{9.3}
\]

By (8.3), `C_n(RQ)=C_n(Q)`, contradicting the all-phase Brownian limit `e^{-1}`. The selected `j,theta` may depend on the realization, but no conditioning is used because the probability-one event was simultaneous in all fixed witnesses and all phases. QED.
