## 2. Primary source recovered again

Primary source: Krzysztof Burdzy and Michael Scheutzow, *Forward Brownian Motion*, Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 160 (2014), 95–126; arXiv:1302.6958.

Definition 2.1 says that an FBM `X=(X_t)_{t in R}` admits random witness times `S_j -> -infinity` a.s. such that, for each **fixed deterministic witness index** `j`,

\[
B^{(j)}_s:=X_{S_j+s}-X_{S_j},\qquad s\ge0,
\tag{2.1}
\]

is standard Brownian motion.

Theorem 4.3 is the source benchmark. For the parabolic strip

\[
\{(t,x):t<0,\ c_1\sqrt{|t|}<x<c_2\sqrt{|t|}\},
\]

it gives three clauses:

1. if `lambda_0(c_1,c_2)>=1`, every FBM has the universal far-past normalized obstruction;
2. if `lambda_0(c_1,c_2)<=1`, there exists a decomposable FBM with the corresponding asymptotic normalized confinement;
3. if `c_1<=0<=c_2` and `lambda_0(c_1,c_2)<1`, there exists a decomposable FBM confined pointwise to the exact parabolic strip for every `t<=0`.

The decisive analytic ingredient is classical and is not claimed new: the occupation-time formula for continuous semimartingales. If `Y=M+A` is a one-dimensional continuous semimartingale and its quadratic variation is `[Y]_s=s`, then on a pathwise probability-one event its occupation measure on `[0,T]` has a spatial density given by local time:

\[
\int_0^T f(Y_s)\,ds
=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)L_T^x(Y)\,dx
\tag{3.1}
\]

for every nonnegative Borel `f`. In particular,

\[
|A|=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Leb}\{s\in[0,T]:Y_s\in A\}=0
\tag{3.2}
\]

**simultaneously for every spatial Lebesgue-null Borel set `A` on that realized path**.

This form appears in standard local-time treatments such as Revuz–Yor and Bass. A convenient accessible semimartingale statement is also recorded in the appendix of Brody–Hughston–Macrina-style information-process literature citing Revuz–Yor, and Doney–Yor treats Brownian motion with drift. The underlying occupation-density mechanism is classical/folklore-level stochastic calculus; no priority is claimed for it.

## 5. Method #18 — say it in another language: spacetime incidence becomes occupation measure of a drifted witness

At a gate age `u`, suppose the allowed fiber is contained in a countable union

\[
\bigcup_m(v_m u+b_m+A_m),
\tag{5.1}
\]

where every `A_m` is spatially Lebesgue-null. A direct spacetime view makes the random FBM witness phase look awkward. The useful language is instead the occupation measure of the **drifted Brownian witnesses**

\[
Y^{(m)}_s:=B^{(j)}_s+v_m s.
\tag{5.2}
\]

If the witness starts at age `U=-S_j`, then the incidence

\[
X_{S_j+s}\in v_m(U-s)+b_m+A_m
\]

is exactly

\[
Y^{(m)}_s
\in
A_m+c_m,
\qquad
c_m:=v_mU+b_m-X_{S_j}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The awkward random witness phase has collapsed into a **random spatial translation** `c_m`. That is harmless, because the occupation measure of `Y^{(m)}` is pathwise absolutely continuous with respect to spatial Lebesgue measure, and therefore annihilates every null set, including a path-dependent translate.

No Cameron–Martin theorem is needed: adding the finite-variation drift `v_m s` leaves the quadratic variation equal to `s`, so the ordinary continuous-semimartingale occupation formula applies directly.

## 6. T67 — countable ballistic null-packet occupation theorem

**Theorem T67 (positive-time countable ballistic null-packet obstruction).** Let `Z subset (0,infinity)` be deterministic and measurable. Suppose there exist

\[
T>0,\qquad \eta>0,\qquad U_0<\infty
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Leb}\bigl(Z\cap[U-T,U]\bigr)\ge\eta
\qquad\text{for every }U\ge U_0.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
(v_m,b_m,A_m)_{m\ge1}
\]

be a countable deterministic family with `v_m,b_m in R` and each `A_m subset R` Borel with

\[
\operatorname{Leb}(A_m)=0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let deterministic fibers `K(u) subset R` satisfy only

\[
\boxed{
K(u)\subset
\bigcup_{m\ge1}\bigl(v_m u+b_m+A_m\bigr)
\qquad(u\in Z),
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

with **no restriction whatsoever** on `K(u)` for `u notin Z`.

Then every forward Brownian motion `X` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\exists R<\infty:\ X_{-u}\in K(u)\quad\forall u\ge R
\right)=0.
}
\tag{T67}
\]

### Proof

Assume contrariwise that the eventual-confinement event in T67 has positive probability. By the countable union over integer tail radii, choose a deterministic integer

\[
R\ge U_0
\]

such that

\[
E_R:=\{X_{-u}\in K(u)\ \forall u\ge R\}
\]

has probability

\[
p:=\mathbb P(E_R)>0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Let `(S_j)` be the FBM witness sequence. Since `S_j -> -infinity` a.s., choose one **deterministic index** `j` so far left that

\[
\mathbb P\{S_j\le-(R+T)\}>1-p/2.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
G:=E_R\cap\{S_j\le-(R+T)\}
\]

has positive probability, indeed `P(G)>=p/2`.

For this fixed deterministic index `j`, define on `[0,T]`

\[
B_s:=X_{S_j+s}-X_{S_j}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

By Definition 2.1, `B` is **unconditionally standard Brownian motion**.

For every deterministic `m`, put

\[
Y^{(m)}_s:=B_s+v_ms.
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is a continuous semimartingale with quadratic variation

\[
[Y^{(m)}]_s=s.
\]

Hence, by the occupation-time formula, with probability one its time-occupation measure

\[
\mu_m(C):=
\operatorname{Leb}\{s\in[0,T]:Y^{(m)}_s\in C\}
\tag{6.8}
\]

is absolutely continuous with respect to spatial Lebesgue measure. Equivalently, on one probability-one event,

\[
|C|=0\Longrightarrow\mu_m(C)=0
\quad\text{for every Borel }C\subset\mathbb R.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Because `m` ranges over a **countable** family, intersect these probability-one events over all `m`; call the result `Omega_occ`. Then

\[
\mathbb P(\Omega_{occ})=1.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Fix an outcome in `G cap Omega_occ` and set

\[
U:=-S_j\ge R+T.
\]

Reflect the temporal gate set into the witness window:

\[
F:=\{s\in[0,T]:U-s\in Z\}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Reflection preserves Lebesgue measure, so by (6.1)

\[
|F|
=|Z\cap[U-T,U]|
\ge\eta>0.
\tag{6.12}
\]

For each `m`, define the branch-hit set

\[
F_m:=
\left\{
 s\in F:
 X_{S_j+s}\in v_m(U-s)+b_m+A_m
\right\}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

On `E_R`, every `s in F` corresponds to an age `u=U-s>=R` lying in `Z`, so (6.3) gives

\[
F=\bigcup_{m\ge1}F_m.
\tag{6.14}
\]

For `s in F_m`,

\[
X_{S_j}+B_s
\in
v_mU-v_ms+b_m+A_m,
\]

hence

\[
Y^{(m)}_s=B_s+v_ms
\in
A_m+c_m,
\qquad
c_m:=v_mU+b_m-X_{S_j}.
\tag{6.15}
\]

The translate `A_m+c_m` is spatially Lebesgue-null, even though `c_m` may depend on the realized witness. Crucially, (6.9) is a **pathwise absolute-continuity statement simultaneous over every null Borel set**. Therefore

\[
|F_m|
\le
\mu_m(A_m+c_m)
=0
\qquad\text{for every }m.
\tag{6.16}
\]

Countable subadditivity and (6.14) imply

\[
|F|
\le\sum_{m\ge1}|F_m|
=0,
\]

contradicting (6.12). Thus the eventual-confinement event has probability zero. `QED`

### Dependence audit

No conditioning on `S_j` occurs. No independence between `S_j`, `X_{S_j}` and the Brownian future is assumed. The witness **index** `j` is fixed deterministically before invoking the Brownian occupation event. The witness phase `U`, translations `c_m`, gate set `F`, and branch-hit sets `F_m` may all be path-dependent. ### Why countability is really used

Countability enters in two irreducible places:

1. to intersect the probability-one occupation events for `Y^{(m)}` over all branches;
2. to conclude from `|F_m|=0` for all `m` that `|cup_m F_m|=0`.

### 8.1 A compact spatial null set of full Hausdorff dimension one

Let

\[
\Sigma_*:=
\{\omega=(\omega_j)_{j\ge1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}:
\omega_{k^2}=0\text{ for every }k\ge1\},
\]

and define

\[
A_*:=
\left\{
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\omega_j2^{-j}:
\omega\in\Sigma_*
\right\}\subset[0,1].
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is compact as the continuous image of the compact product set `Sigma_*`. At binary depth `n`, exactly `floor(sqrt(n))` digits are forced to zero, so `A_*` is covered by

\[
N_n=2^{n-\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor}
\]

dyadic intervals of length `2^{-n}`. Consequently

\[
|A_*|
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}N_n2^{-n}
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{-\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor}
=0.
\tag{8.2}
\]

On the other hand, put the fair product measure on the free binary digits and force square-position digits to zero. Each depth-`n` cylinder has mass

\[
2^{-(n-\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor)}.
\]

An interval of length at most `2^{-n}` meets at most two adjacent depth-`n` dyadic cylinders. Hence, for every `alpha<1`,

\[
\mu(I)
\le
2\,2^{-(n-\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor)}
\le C_\alpha |I|^\alpha
\tag{8.3}
\]

for all sufficiently small intervals `I`, because

\[
2^{-n(1-\alpha)+\sqrt n}\to0.
\]

The mass-distribution principle gives `dim_H A_*>=alpha` for every `alpha<1`; since `A_* subset R`,

\[
\boxed{|A_*|=0,\qquad \dim_HA_*=1.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Also `0 in A_*`.

\[

\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb Z}(mu+A_*).
\tag{8.7}
\]

At every fixed age this is an unbounded countable union of uncountable compact packets. It has

\[
|L_*(u)|=0,
\qquad
\dim_H L_*(u)=1.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Now let

\[
r(u):=2u\,\operatorname{dist}(u,Z_*),
\tag{8.9}
\]

and define

\[
K_*(u):=
\{x\in\mathbb R:\operatorname{dist}(x,L_*(u))\le r(u)\}.
\tag{8.10}
\]

Finally put

\[
\mathcal N_*:=
\{(-u,x):u>0,\ x\in K_*(u)\}
\cup(\{0\}\times\mathbb R).
\tag{8.11}
\]

At a gate age `u in Z_*`, `r(u)=0`, so

\[
K_*(u)=L_*(u)
=
\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb Z}(mu+A_*).
\tag{8.12}

At the midpoint

\[
u_n=n+3/4

of each complementary gap, `dist(u_n,Z_*)=1/4`, hence

\[
r(u_n)=u_n/2.
\tag{8.13}
\]

Since `0 in A_*`, the lattice `u_n Z` is contained in `L_*(u_n)`. Every real number lies within distance `u_n/2` of that lattice. Therefore

\[
\boxed{K_*(u_n)=\mathbb R\quad\text{for every }n.}
\tag{8.14}
