## 3. Primary source recovered again

### Definition 2.1

An FBM is a real-indexed continuous process `X` for which there are random times `S_n -> -infinity` a.s. such that, for every `n`,

\[
(X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n})_{t\ge0}
\]

is standard Brownian motion.

### Definition 2.2 — decomposable FBM

For each `k in Z`, take an independent Brownian motion `B^k` and an a.s.-finite stopping time `T_k` for its own filtration. If the sums of `T_k` diverge in both index directions, concatenate the stopped Brownian pieces at seams `S_{k+1}-S_k=T_k`. The resulting process is decomposable, and the strong Markov property makes it an FBM.

### Theorem 4.3 — source benchmark

For the parabolic strip

\[
c_1\sqrt{|t|}\le x\le c_2\sqrt{|t|},\qquad t\le0,
\]

with the source’s Ornstein–Uhlenbeck / Sturm–Liouville exponent `lambda_0(c_1,c_2)`:

1. `lambda_0 >= 1` gives the universal asymptotic obstruction;
2. `lambda_0 <= 1` gives a decomposable FBM asymptotically confined to the strip;
3. if `c_1 <= 0 <= c_2` and `lambda_0 < 1`, a decomposable FBM exists pointwise inside the strip for every `t<=0`.

## 6. New-method move #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: shapes as LIL gauges

A symmetric backward space-time region can be encoded by one gauge

\[
\mathcal R_{f,C}
=
\{(t,x):t\le0,\ |x|\le C f(|t|)\}.
\]

For a continuous increasing gauge `f`, the two genuinely different obstructions are now transparent:

- **seam obstruction:** a reversed Brownian piece cannot enter a boundary thinner than its local LIL modulus;
- **far-past obstruction:** the endpoint and unbounded tail of a Brownian piece must fit under the gauge.

The right sufficient conditions are therefore

\[
\tag{G0}
\frac{f(u)}{\sqrt{u\log\log(1/u)}}\longrightarrow\infty
\quad(u\downarrow0),
\]

and

\[
\tag{G∞}
\frac{f(u)}{\sqrt{u\log\log u}}\longrightarrow\infty
\quad(u\to\infty),
\]

with the logarithms interpreted only once their arguments are in the usual LIL range.

The earlier power cusp satisfies both conditions, but they also allow shapes with regular-variation index exactly `1/2`, which is where the new counted family comes from.

## 7. I0077-R07-T2 — general super-LIL gauge confinement theorem

**Status: PROVED.**

### Theorem

Let

\[
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
\]

be continuous and nondecreasing, with

\[
f(0)=0,
\qquad
f(u)>0\quad(u>0),
\]

and suppose `f` satisfies (G0) and (G∞).

Then, for every `C>0`, there exists a **decomposable forward Brownian motion** `X`, normalized by `X_0=0`, such that

\[
\boxed{
|X_t|\le C f(|t|)
\quad\text{for every }t\le0,
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{T2}
\]

Equivalently, for every pair `c_1<0<c_2`, there is a decomposable FBM satisfying

\[
c_1f(|t|)\le X_t\le c_2f(|t|)
\qquad(t\le0)
\]

by applying (T2) with `C=min(-c_1,c_2)`.

### Proof

The proof has three steps.

### Step 1. A single Brownian path has positive probability to remain in the whole gauge tube

Fix `c>0` and let `B` be standard Brownian motion. We claim

\[
\boxed{
 p_c:=
 \Pr\{|B_s|\le c f(s)\ \forall s\ge0\}>0.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

By the local Brownian law of the iterated logarithm and (G0),

\[
\frac{|B_s|}{f(s)}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(s\downarrow0)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence, for a suitable deterministic `epsilon>0`,

\[
A:=
\{|B_s|\le(c/4)f(s)\ \forall 0\le s\le\epsilon\}
\]

has positive probability. To justify the deterministic choice: the events asserting the displayed inequality on `[0,1/m]` increase to an a.s. event as `m->infinity`, so at least one has positive probability.

Choose a deterministic `R>epsilon`, to be made large below, and put

\[
d=(c/8)f(\epsilon)>0.
\]

For the independent increment process after `epsilon`, the finite-interval Brownian small-ball event

\[
E:=
\left\{
\sup_{0\le u\le R-\epsilon}
|B_{\epsilon+u}-B_\epsilon|
\le d
\right\}
\]

has positive probability. It is independent of `A`. On `A cap E`, for every `epsilon<=s<=R`,

\[
|B_s|
\le \frac c4 f(\epsilon)+\frac c8f(\epsilon)
=\frac{3c}{8}f(\epsilon)
\le c f(s).
\tag{7.3}
\]

It remains to control the entire infinite tail using only (G∞), without the stronger Gaussian-summability assumption used implicitly in the previous power proof.

Let `Z` be standard Brownian motion and define

\[
H_R
:=
\sup_{u\ge0}
\frac{|Z_u|}{f(R+u)}.
\]

We claim

\[
\boxed{H_R\to0\quad\text{a.s. as }R\to\infty.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

For `0<=u<=R`, monotonicity of `f` gives

\[
\sup_{0\le u\le R}
\frac{|Z_u|}{f(R+u)}
\le
\frac{\sup_{0\le u\le R}|Z_u|}{f(R)}.
\]

The usual Brownian LIL implies

\[
\sup_{0\le u\le R}|Z_u|
=O\bigl(\sqrt{R\log\log R}\bigr)
\quad\text{a.s.},
\]

so this part tends to zero by (G∞).

For `u>=R`, again by monotonicity,

\[
\frac{|Z_u|}{f(R+u)}
\le
\frac{|Z_u|}{f(u)}.
\]

The Brownian LIL and (G∞) give `|Z_u|/f(u)->0` a.s.; therefore the supremum over `u>=R` tends to zero. This proves (7.4).

Consequently

\[
\Pr(H_R\le c/2)\longrightarrow1.
\]

Choose `R` large enough that

\[
\Pr(H_R\le c/2)>0
\]

and also

\[
\frac{3c}{8}f(\epsilon)
\le
\frac c4 f(R).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Now set

\[
Z_u=B_{R+u}-B_R.
\]

The event

\[
F_R:=\{|Z_u|\le(c/2)f(R+u)\ \forall u\ge0\}
\]

has positive probability and is independent of the path through time `R`. On `A cap E cap F_R`, (7.5) gives, for every `u>=0`,

\[
|B_{R+u}|
\le |B_R|+|Z_u|
\le \frac c4f(R)+\frac c2f(R+u)
\le \frac{3c}{4}f(R+u)
<cf(R+u).
\]

Thus `A cap E cap F_R` is a positive-probability subset of the whole-tube event, proving (7.1).

### Step 2. An adapted integer-scan stopping rule terminates almost surely

For a one-sided Brownian motion `B`, define

\[
G_n(c)
=
\{|B_n-B_{n-s}|\le cf(s)\ \forall 0\le s\le n\}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Extend `B` to a two-sided Brownian motion `W` and set

\[
A_c
=
\{|W_0-W_{-s}|\le cf(s)\ \forall s\ge0\}.
\]

By (7.1) and time reversal,

\[
\Pr(A_c)=p_c>0.
\]

Under the integer re-rooting

\[
(\theta W)(t)=W(t+1)-W(1),
\]

the unit Brownian increment blocks form a bi-infinite iid sequence, so `theta` is ergodic. Birkhoff’s theorem gives

\[
\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N
1_{A_c}(\theta^nW)
\longrightarrow p_c>0
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Hence there are infinitely many positive integers `n` for which the **entire** reversed past from `n` obeys the gauge. In particular, every such `n` satisfies `G_n(c)` for the one-sided restriction.

For an endpoint budget, fix `delta>0`. The ordinary Brownian LIL together with (G∞) gives

\[
\frac{|B_n|}{f(n)}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
|B_n|\le\delta f(n)
\]

for all sufficiently large integers a.s.

For an integer `a>=1`, define

\[
T(a,\delta,c)
=
\inf\{n\in\mathbb N:
 n\ge a,\ G_n(c),\ |B_n|\le\delta f(n)\}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

The event tested at integer `n` is measurable with respect to the Brownian path through time `n`; continuity lets the universal condition in (7.6) be reduced to rational `s`. Hence `T(a,delta,c)` is a stopping time. Equations (7.7)–(7.8) imply

\[
\boxed{T(a,\delta,c)<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}}
\tag{7.10}
\]

for every `a,delta,c`.

### Step 3. Concatenate the good reversed pieces

Fix the target amplitude `C>0` and set

\[
c=C/2,
\qquad
\delta_m=C2^{-m-1},
\quad m\ge1.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Take independent standard Brownian motions `B^{-m}`, `m>=1`, and on the `m`-th one use the finite stopping time

\[
T_{-m}=T(m,\delta_m,c).
\]

For nonnegative indices use independent Brownian pieces of deterministic length `1`. The stopped pieces are independent; each `T_{-m}` is a stopping time in its own Brownian filtration; and

\[
\sum_{m\ge1}T_{-m}
\ge
\sum_{m\ge1}m
=\infty,
\]

while the positive-index sum also diverges. Definition 2.2 therefore yields a decomposable FBM `X`, with a seam chosen at `X_0=0`.

Fix `u>=0`, and suppose `-u` lies in the `m`-th negative piece. Let `r` be the distance in time from the right endpoint of that piece back to `-u`. Then

\[
u=\sum_{j<m}T_{-j}+r,
\]

so every completed `T_{-j}` and `r` are at most `u`. Traversing backward from zero and using the triangle inequality,

\[
|X_{-u}|
\le
\sum_{j<m}|B^{-j}_{T_{-j}}|
+
|B^{-m}_{T_{-m}}-B^{-m}_{T_{-m}-r}|.
\]

The endpoint budgets and the good reversed-piece condition give

\[
|X_{-u}|
\le
\sum_{j<m}\delta_j f(T_{-j})
+c f(r).
\]

Since `f` is nondecreasing,

\[
|X_{-u}|
\le
\left(\sum_{j<m}\delta_j+c\right)f(u)
\le
\left(\frac C2+\frac C2\right)f(u)
=Cf(u).
\]

This proves (T2). `QED`

## 8. New-method move #23 — DO WHAT THE PROPOSER DID NOT EXPECT: stay at regular-variation index 1/2 but leave the parabola

A second power law would be too close to the previous metric point. Instead, keep the **power index exactly Brownian** and perturb only by a slowly varying factor.

For

\[
0<\eta<\frac12,
\qquad
\gamma>0,
\]

define the **logarithmic cusp gauge**

\[
 f_{\eta,\gamma}(u)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \sqrt u\,[\log(e/u)]^\eta,&0<u\le1,\\[2mm]
 \sqrt u\,[\log(eu)]^\gamma,&u\ge1,\\
 0,&u=0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{8.1}
\]

All choices of `(eta,gamma)` are one counted family. A canonical representative is `eta=gamma=1/4`.

### I0077-R07-T3 — logarithmic-cusp confinement

**Status: PROVED.**

For every `C>0` and every `0<eta<1/2`, `gamma>0`, there exists a decomposable FBM `X`, `X_0=0`, such that

\[
\boxed{
|X_t|
\le
C f_{\eta,\gamma}(|t|)
\qquad\forall t\le0,
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{T3}
\]

#### Proof

The function is continuous at `u=1`, where both formulas equal `1`, and tends to zero as `u downarrow0`.

For `0<u<=1`,

\[
\frac d{du}\log f_{\eta,\gamma}(u)
=
\frac1{2u}
-
\frac{\eta}{u\log(e/u)}
\ge
\frac{1/2-\eta}{u}>0,
\]

because `log(e/u)>=1`. For `u>=1`,

\[
\frac d{du}\log f_{\eta,\gamma}(u)
=
\frac1{2u}
+
\frac{\gamma}{u\log(eu)}>0.
\]

Thus the gauge is nondecreasing.

At the seam,

\[
\frac{f_{\eta,\gamma}(u)}
{\sqrt{u\log\log(1/u)}}
=
\frac{[\log(e/u)]^\eta}
{\sqrt{\log\log(1/u)}}
\longrightarrow\infty,
\]

so (G0) holds.

At infinity,

\[
\frac{f_{\eta,\gamma}(u)}
{\sqrt{u\log\log u}}
=
\frac{[\log(eu)]^\gamma}
{\sqrt{\log\log u}}
\longrightarrow\infty,
\]

for every `gamma>0`, so (G∞) holds.

The conclusion is therefore an immediate application of Theorem T2. `QED`

### Source-appropriate amplitude classification for this family

For the symmetric logarithmic-cusp shapes

\[
\mathcal R_C
=
\{(t,x):t\le0,\ |x|\le C f_{\eta,\gamma}(|t|)\},
\]

we have the exact pointwise amplitude classification:

- every `C>0` is attainable by a decomposable FBM, by T3;
- `C=0` is impossible for any FBM.

For the second statement, `C=0` would force `X_t=0` for every `t<=0`. If `S_n->-infinity` are FBM witness times, then on the event `S_n<=-1`, the Brownian witness `X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n}` would be identically zero on `[0,1]`, a null event. Since some `S_n<=-1` eventually a.s., a countable union gives the contradiction.

At the **asymptotic** level the picture is even wider: an ordinary two-sided Brownian motion `W` satisfies

\[
\frac{|W_{-u}|}{f_{\eta,\gamma}(u)}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(u\to\infty)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

by the Brownian LIL and (G∞). Thus this family has no nonzero parabolic-style critical width: the interesting content is the pointwise decomposable-FBM enclosure, and its infimum amplitude is zero but is not attained.

This is a source-appropriate analogue, not a claim that the parabolic Sturm–Liouville exponent extends unchanged.
