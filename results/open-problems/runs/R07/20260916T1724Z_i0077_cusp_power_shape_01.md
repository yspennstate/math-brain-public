## 3. Exact source theorem and definitions recovered

### Definition 2.1 (source scope)

An FBM is a process `X=(X_t)_{t in R}` for which there are random times `S_n -> -infinity` a.s. such that for every `n`,

`(X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n})_{t>=0}`

is standard Brownian motion.

### Definition 2.2 (decomposable FBM)

For independent Brownian motions `B^k` and finite stopping times `T_k`, if the positive and negative sums of the `T_k` diverge, concatenate the stopped Brownian pieces along seams `S_{k+1}-S_k=T_k`. The resulting process is decomposable and hence FBM by the strong Markov property. If the stopped pieces are iid, it is strongly decomposable.

### Theorem 4.3 (three source clauses)

For the parabolic strip

`c1 sqrt(|t|) < x < c2 sqrt(|t|)`, `t<0`,

with Ornstein--Uhlenbeck/Sturm--Liouville exponent `lambda_0(c1,c2)`:

1. if `lambda_0>=1`, every FBM violates at least one asymptotic side almost surely;
2. if `lambda_0<=1`, a decomposable FBM exists whose backward path is asymptotically inside the strip;
3. if `c1<=0<=c2` and `lambda_0<1`, a decomposable FBM exists that is pointwise inside the strip for every `t<=0`.

The proof of the constructive clauses uses Brownian slow points, time reversal, support/scaling, and carefully chosen stopping pieces.

## 5. New-method attack #2 — attack the negation / falsify the naive extension first

A first naive idea is to replace `sqrt(s)` by a pure power `s^beta`, `beta>1/2`, and search for good **deterministic integer terminal times** `n` whose reversed Brownian piece obeys

\[
|B_n-B_{n-s}|\le C s^\beta\quad(0\le s\le n).
\]

This algorithm fails at every deterministic integer `n` almost surely. For fixed `n`, the reversed increment process near `s=0` has Brownian local law, and the local LIL gives

\[
\limsup_{s\downarrow0}
\frac{|B_n-B_{n-s}|}{\sqrt{2s\log\log(1/s)}}=1
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

For `beta>1/2`,

\[
\frac{s^\beta}{\sqrt{s\log\log(1/s)}}\to0.
\]

Hence the displayed pure-power enclosure has probability zero at each deterministic `n`; by countability there is a.s. no integer terminal time satisfying it.

This **does not refute Problem 7.8** and does not prove that no specially selected continuous Brownian time could work. It only refutes the obvious integer-terminal construction and identifies its exact obstruction: a super-diffusive large-time boundary is too narrow at the seam.

## 6. New-method attack #6 — deform to the solvable shape and track the exact obstruction

Repair only the local seam geometry while retaining a genuinely nonparabolic large-time shape. Take

\[
f(u)=u^\alpha+u^\beta,
\qquad 0<\alpha<\tfrac12<\beta.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Near zero, `u^alpha` is much larger than the Brownian local modulus `sqrt(u log log(1/u))`; at infinity, `u^beta` is much larger than the ordinary Brownian scale. This removes both ends of the deterministic-terminal obstruction while keeping asymptotic exponent `beta != 1/2`.

The key fact is that a Brownian path has a strictly positive chance to stay in the whole two-power tube.

### Lemma I0077-R07-L1 — positive whole-line tube probability

For every `c>0`,

\[
p_c:=\Pr\{|B_s|\le c f(s)\ \forall s\ge0\}>0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

**Near zero.** Brownian LIL implies

\[
\frac{|B_s|}{s^\alpha}\to0\quad(s\downarrow0)
\]

almost surely because `alpha<1/2`. Hence for some deterministic `epsilon>0`, the event

\[
A=\{|B_s|\le (c/4)f(s)\ \forall 0\le s\le\epsilon\}
\]

has positive probability.

Choose `R>epsilon` later and set `d=(c/8)f(epsilon)>0`. By independent increments, the finite-interval small-ball event

\[
E=\{\sup_{0\le u\le R-\epsilon}|B_{\epsilon+u}-B_\epsilon|\le d\}
\]

is independent of `F_epsilon` and has positive probability. On `A cap E`, for `epsilon<=s<=R`,

\[
|B_s|\le (c/4)f(\epsilon)+(c/8)f(\epsilon)
< c f(s).
\]

Take `R` so large that also `(3c/8)f(epsilon) <= (c/4)R^beta`.

**Tail.** Let `Z_u=B_{R+u}-B_R`, an independent Brownian motion, and put `q=2beta-1>0`. We claim that for `R` large,

\[
F_R=\{|Z_u|\le (c/2)(R+u)^\beta\ \forall u\ge0\}
\]

has positive probability (in fact probability tending to one). By the reflection principle and the Gaussian tail bound,

\[
\Pr\{\sup_{u\le T}|Z_u|\ge a\}\le4e^{-a^2/(2T)}.
\]

On `[0,R]` use `a=(c/2)R^beta`. On `[2^kR,2^{k+1}R]`, `k>=0`, use `a=(c/2)(2^kR)^beta` and `T=2^{k+1}R`. The total crossing probability is at most

\[
4e^{-(c^2/8)R^q}
+4\sum_{k\ge0}
\exp\{- (c^2/16)R^q 2^{kq}\},
\]

which tends to zero as `R->infinity`. Thus choose `R` with `P(F_R)>0`.

On `A cap E cap F_R`, for `s=R+u`,

\[
|B_s|\le |B_R|+|Z_u|
\le (c/4)R^\beta+(c/2)(R+u)^\beta
\le (3c/4)(R+u)^\beta
\le c f(R+u).
\]

The three pieces use successive independent increments, so their intersection has positive probability. This proves (6.2). `QED`

## 7. New-method attack #19 — make the surviving construction an algorithm

The whole construction can be made into an adapted integer-scan stopping rule.

For a one-sided Brownian motion `B`, parameters `c>0`, `delta>0`, and integer `a>=1`, define

\[
G_n(c)=\{|B_n-B_{n-s}|\le c f(s)\ \forall 0\le s\le n\}
\]

and

\[
T(a,\delta,c)
=\inf\{n\in\mathbb N:\ n\ge a,\ G_n(c),\ |B_n|\le\delta f(n)\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The event tested at integer `n` is measurable from the path up to `n`, so `T(a,delta,c)` is a stopping time (continuity lets the universal-in-`s` condition be checked on rationals).

### Lemma I0077-R07-L2 — the algorithm terminates almost surely

For every `a>=1`, `c>0`, `delta>0`,

\[
T(a,\delta,c)<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

#### Proof

Extend `B` to a two-sided Brownian motion `W`. Let

\[
A_c=\{|W_0-W_{-s}|\le c f(s)\ \forall s\ge0\}.
\]

By Lemma L1 and time reversal, `P(A_c)=p_c>0`. Under the integer re-rooting map

\[
(\theta W)(t)=W(t+1)-W(1),
\]

the sequence of unit Brownian increment blocks is iid, so the shift is ergodic. Birkhoff's theorem therefore implies that the set of positive integers `n` with `theta^n W in A_c` has asymptotic density `p_c` and in particular is infinite almost surely. Every such `n` satisfies `G_n(c)` for the one-sided restriction, since `A_c` controls even more than the interval `[0,n]`.

It remains to enforce the endpoint budget. Because `f(n)>=n^beta` and `beta>1/2`,

\[
\Pr\{|B_n|>\delta f(n)\}
\le2\exp\{-\tfrac12\delta^2 n^{2\beta-1}\},
\]

and the right-hand side is summable in `n`. Borel--Cantelli gives `|B_n|<=delta f(n)` for all sufficiently large integers almost surely. Infinitely many good `G_n(c)` therefore also satisfy the endpoint condition. Hence (7.2). `QED`

## 8. Main theorem — one genuinely nonparabolic shape family

### Theorem I0077-R07-T1 — pointwise cusp-power confinement

Fix

\[
0<\alpha<\tfrac12<\beta,
\qquad
f(u)=u^\alpha+u^\beta.
\]

For every `C>0`, there exists a **decomposable forward Brownian motion** `X`, with decomposition seam `X_0=0`, such that

\[
\boxed{
|X_t|\le C\bigl(|t|^\alpha+|t|^\beta\bigr)
\quad\text{for every }t\le0,
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\limsup_{t\to-\infty}
\frac{|X_t|}{|t|^\alpha+|t|^\beta}\le C
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The theorem includes the canonical genuinely nonparabolic representative

\[
|X_t|\le C\bigl(|t|^{1/4}+|t|^{3/4}\bigr),\qquad t\le0.
\tag{8.3}
\]

#### Proof

Set

\[
c=C/2,
\qquad
\delta_m=C\,2^{-m-1},\quad m\ge1.
\]

Thus

\[
\sum_{m\ge1}\delta_m=C/2.
\tag{8.4}
\]

For each negative piece index `-m`, take an independent standard Brownian motion `B^{-m}` and apply the stopping algorithm of Lemma L2 with

\[
T_{-m}=T(1,\delta_m,c).
\]

Then `T_{-m}` is finite a.s., `T_{-m}>=1`, and by construction

\[
|B^{-m}_{T_{-m}}-B^{-m}_{T_{-m}-s}|\le c f(s)
\quad(0\le s\le T_{-m}),
\tag{8.5}
\]

and

\[
|B^{-m}_{T_{-m}}|\le\delta_m f(T_{-m}).
\tag{8.6}
\]

For nonnegative indices choose independent Brownian pieces of deterministic duration one. All stopped pieces are independent, every duration is finite, and both time sums diverge because all negative durations are at least one and all nonnegative durations equal one. Definition 2.2 therefore produces a decomposable FBM `X` with seam `S_0=0` and `X_0=0`.

Fix `u>0`. Going backward from time zero, suppose `-u` lies in the `m`-th negative piece and write

\[
u=T_{-1}+\cdots+T_{-(m-1)}+r,
\qquad 0\le r\le T_{-m}.
\]

The completed pieces contribute their endpoint displacements and the current piece contributes one reversed partial displacement. By (8.5)--(8.6),

\[
|X_{-u}|
\le
\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\delta_j f(T_{-j})+c f(r).
\]

The function `f` is increasing and every `T_{-j}` and `r` in the last display is at most `u`. Hence

\[
|X_{-u}|
\le
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\delta_j+c\right)f(u)
\le(C/2+C/2)f(u)=Cf(u).
\]

This proves (8.1) for every `u>0`; it is trivial at `u=0`. Equation (8.2) follows immediately. `QED`

## 9. Why this is a source-appropriate nonparabolic analogue, and what it does NOT claim

Theorem 4.3(ii)--(iii) asks for asymptotic and pointwise confinement of a decomposable FBM. T1 gives both for a genuinely different shape. In fact it is stronger on construction: the same process satisfies the pointwise enclosure for all backward times.

For this super-diffusive-at-infinity family, the analogue of Theorem 4.3(i)'s nontrivial spectral obstruction disappears rather than producing a new `lambda_0`: ordinary 2BM already satisfies

\[
B_{-u}/f(u)\to0\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

because `beta>1/2` (e.g. by Gaussian Borel--Cantelli on integers plus the Brownian modulus on unit intervals). Thus no positive amplitude can lie in a universal-impossibility regime analogous to `lambda_0>=1`. The interesting part is pointwise confinement at the decomposition seam, which T1 supplies.

At amplitude `C=0`, pointwise confinement would force `X_t=0` for every `t<=0`, impossible for an FBM: since its Brownian witness origins tend to `-infinity`, some witness lies below `-1` with positive probability, while the forward Brownian increment over the next `1/2` time unit has a continuous nondegenerate normal law and cannot be identically zero there.

Therefore, for the symmetric two-power family, the existence picture is complete at the source-relevant level:

- asymptotic confinement has examples even at normalized amplitude zero (2BM, since `f` grows faster than `sqrt u` at infinity);
- pointwise confinement exists for every `C>0` by T1;
- exact pointwise amplitude `C=0` is impossible.

I do **not** claim that Problem 7.8 as an open-ended request is closed, nor that this is the strongest possible family, nor that the mechanism is novel in the literature. The bounded prior-art search found no later paper explicitly solving Problem 7.8 or this cusp-power family, but the ingredients (Brownian LIL, support/small-ball events, ergodic good times, and decomposable concatenation) are classical and may be folklore in combination.
