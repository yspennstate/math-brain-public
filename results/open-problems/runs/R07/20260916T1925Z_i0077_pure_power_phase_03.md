\[
B^{(n)}_t:=X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n},\qquad t\ge0,
\]

## 6. Section-B move #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: geometry becomes path regularity

For `beta>1/2`, the obvious concatenation algorithm fails at every seam because the tube is too narrow near `t=0`. The stronger question is whether this is merely an algorithmic failure or a universal FBM obstruction.

The geometric condition near the seam is exactly a one-sided pointwise Hölder condition on any Brownian witness. That changes the problem from FBM construction to classical Brownian sample-path regularity and makes the obstruction universal.

### Lemma R07-HOLDER-L

**Status: PROVED.** Let `B` be standard Brownian motion. For every `gamma>1/2`, almost surely there is no `s>0`, finite `K`, and `eta>0` such that

\[
|B_s-B_t|\le K|s-t|^\gamma
\qquad\forall t\in[s-\eta,s].
\]

In words: Brownian motion is a.s. nowhere **left-Hölder** of exponent greater than `1/2`.

#### Proof

Fix `gamma>1/2`, an integer horizon `M>=1`, and integer `K>=1`. Let `A_{M,K}` be the event that such an `s in (0,M]` and some `eta>0` exist with constant at most `K`.

Choose an integer `N` with

\[
N(\gamma-1/2)>1.
\]

Use only dyadic mesh sizes `n=2^m`. On `A_{M,K}`, for every sufficiently large such `n`, let `i=floor(ns)`. Then the `N` consecutive mesh increments immediately to the left of `s`,

\[
\Delta_j=B_{(j+1)/n}-B_{j/n},
\qquad j=i-N,\ldots,i-1,
\]

all have both endpoints inside the left-Hölder neighborhood. Each endpoint is within `(N+1)/n` of `s`, so

\[
|\Delta_j|
\le
2K\Bigl(\frac{N+1}{n}\Bigr)^\gamma.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a fixed block, the variables `sqrt(n) Delta_j` are independent `N(0,1)`. Since the standard normal density is bounded by `(2pi)^(-1/2)`, for all large `n`,

\[
P\left(
|\Delta_j|\le2K((N+1)/n)^\gamma
\ \forall j\text{ in the block}
\right)
\le
C_{K,N}^N n^{-N(\gamma-1/2)}.
\]

There are at most `Mn+1` possible blocks. Therefore the probability that **any** such block exists at dyadic scale `n` is at most

\[
C'_{M,K,N} n^{1-N(\gamma-1/2)}.
\]

Because `n=2^m` and `N(\gamma-1/2)>1`, these probabilities are summable in `m`. Borel–Cantelli says that almost surely only finitely many dyadic scales contain such a block. But `A_{M,K}` would force a block at every sufficiently fine dyadic scale. Hence `P(A_{M,K})=0`.

Union over countably many `M,K` proves the lemma. `QED`

The use of a one-sided version is deliberate: an FBM witness need only be inspected immediately **before** the time corresponding to the global seam `0`.

## 7. I0077-R07-T4 — complete pure-power phase portrait

**Status: PROVED, with the `beta=1/2` clause imported exactly from source Theorem 4.3.**

Fix finite constants

\[
c_1<0<c_2
\]

and `beta>0`. Consider

\[
\mathcal R_{\beta;c_1,c_2}
=
\{(t,x):t\le0,\ c_1|t|^\beta\le x\le c_2|t|^\beta\}.
\]

### (A) Subdiffusive powers: 0<beta<1/2

For **every FBM** `X`,

\[
P\bigl(
X_t\in[c_1|t|^\beta,c_2|t|^\beta]
\text{ for all sufficiently negative }t
\bigr)=0.
\tag{T4-A}
\]

In particular no FBM can be pointwise confined to this region for all `t<=0`.

#### Proof

On the displayed eventual-confinement event, with `u=-t -> infinity`,

\[
c_1u^{\beta-1/2}
\le
\frac{X_{-u}}{\sqrt u}
\le
c_2u^{\beta-1/2}.
\]

Since `beta-1/2<0`, both bounds converge to zero, so

\[
\limsup_{t\to-\infty}\frac{X_t}{\sqrt{|t|}}=0,
\qquad
\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\frac{X_t}{\sqrt{|t|}}=0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

But Burdzy–Scheutzow Remark 4.2 gives `lambda_0(-1,1)=1`; Theorem 4.3(i) therefore says that for every FBM, almost surely

\[
\limsup_{t\to-\infty}\frac{X_t}{\sqrt{|t|}}\ge1
\quad\text{or}\quad
\liminf_{t\to-\infty}\frac{X_t}{\sqrt{|t|}}\le-1.
\]

This contradicts (7.1). Thus the eventual-confinement event has probability zero. `QED`

### (B) Critical power: beta=1/2

This is exactly the parabolic family of Theorem 4.3. The full source phase is governed by `lambda_0(c_1,c_2)`:

- `lambda_0>=1`: universal asymptotic obstruction;
- `lambda_0<=1`: decomposable asymptotic existence;
- `c_1<=0<=c_2` and `lambda_0<1`: decomposable pointwise existence.

No new claim is made here; this is the benchmark that identifies `beta=1/2` as the true transition scale.

### (C) Superdiffusive powers: beta>1/2

There **does** exist an FBM asymptotically confined to `R_{beta;c1,c2}`: in fact ordinary two-sided Brownian motion suffices. But there is **no FBM at all** that is pointwise confined for every `t<=0`.

More precisely:

1. If `W` is 2BM(0), then a.s.
   \[
   \frac{W_{-u}}{u^\beta}\longrightarrow0,
   \qquad u\to\infty,
   \]
   so eventually
   \[
   c_1u^\beta\le W_{-u}\le c_2u^\beta.
   \tag{T4-C1}
   \]
2. For every FBM `X`,
   \[
   P\bigl(
   c_1|t|^\beta\le X_t\le c_2|t|^\beta
   \ \forall t\le0
   \bigr)=0.
   \tag{T4-C2}
   \]

#### Proof of asymptotic existence

For the negative half of 2BM(0), the Brownian law of the iterated logarithm gives

\[
|W_{-u}|=O(\sqrt{u\log\log u})
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\]

Since `beta>1/2`,

\[
\frac{\sqrt{u\log\log u}}{u^\beta}\to0.
\]

Thus `W_{-u}/u^beta ->0`; because `c_1<0<c_2`, eventual containment follows. `QED`

#### Proof of pointwise impossibility

Suppose an FBM `X` were confined pointwise. At `t=0` the tube collapses to `{0}`, so `X_0=0`. Put

\[
C=\max\{-c_1,c_2\}.
\]

Then

\[
|X_{-h}|\le Ch^\beta
\qquad(h\ge0).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Let `{S_n}` be FBM witness times. For each `n`,

\[
B^{(n)}_t=X_{S_n+t}-X_{S_n},\qquad t\ge0,
\]

is standard Brownian motion. By R07-HOLDER-L, for each fixed `n` its path is a.s. nowhere left-Hölder `beta`; taking the countable intersection over `n` preserves probability one.

Also `S_n -> -infinity`, so on a probability-one event some witness satisfies `S_n<0`. Fix such an `n` and put

\[
T=-S_n>0.
\]

For every `0<h<T`,

\[
|B^{(n)}_T-B^{(n)}_{T-h}|
=
|X_0-X_{-h}|
=
|X_{-h}|
\le Ch^\beta.
\]

Thus `B^{(n)}` is left-Hölder of exponent `beta>1/2` at the positive time `T`, contradicting R07-HOLDER-L. Therefore no such FBM exists. `QED`

### Interpretation

The pure-power family has a sharp qualitative transition at the Brownian exponent `1/2`:

| exponent | far-past asymptotic confinement | pointwise confinement through `t=0` |
|---|---|---|
| `0<beta<1/2` | impossible for every FBM | impossible |
| `beta=1/2` | source spectral `lambda_0` phase | source spectral `lambda_0` phase |
| `beta>1/2` | possible already for 2BM | impossible for every FBM |

This is a complete source-appropriate theorem for one genuinely nonparabolic structural family. It is **not** a claim that Problem 7.8 as an open-ended classification problem is closed.

## 8. Section-B move #29 — logarithmic-time compactification / OU phase portrait

The preceding theorem becomes geometrically transparent under the same log-time transform underlying the source Sturm–Liouville analysis.

Set

\[
u=e^s,
\qquad
z=e^{-s/2}x.
\]

For ordinary Brownian motion, the normalized process

\[
Y_s=e^{-s/2}B_{e^s}
\]

is stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck with generator

\[
\frac12\left(\frac{d^2}{dx^2}-x\frac d{dx}\right),
\]

which is exactly the operator appearing in source Remark 4.1.

The pure-power boundaries become

\[
c_i e^{(\beta-1/2)s},\qquad i=1,2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Now `s->-infinity` is the seam `u downarrow0`, while `s->+infinity` is the far past `u->infinity`.

- `beta<1/2`: the OU window expands near the seam but shrinks to zero in the far past — the asymptotic obstruction.
- `beta>1/2`: the OU window shrinks to zero at the seam but expands in the far past — the local Hölder obstruction.
- `beta=1/2`: the OU window is constant in logarithmic time — exactly the stationary spectral problem with principal exponent `lambda_0(c_1,c_2)`.

This explains why the parabolic case is not just one parameter value in a smooth quantitative formula: it is the unique power for which neither endpoint of logarithmic time automatically dominates. The source spectral eigenvalue appears precisely at this balance point.
