## 6. Move #17 — the right language is realized quarticity

The number `3` is not mysterious: it is the fourth moment of a standard Gaussian. For standard Brownian motion, on a unit interval with mesh `h=1/n`,

\[
\frac1h\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(B_{(k+1)h}-B_{kh})^4
=\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}Z_k^4
\longrightarrow \mathbb E Z^4=3.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The high-frequency statistics literature calls this normalization realized quarticity. The only issue for the FBM application is the random witness phase: the Brownian unit interval is not anchored at a deterministic time. Therefore the fixed-phase strong law is not enough. The needed statement is simultaneous over all phases.

## 7. Uniform-in-phase Brownian quarticity

For standard Brownian motion `B`, define

\[
\mathcal K_N(B;\theta)
=2^N\sum_{k=0}^{2^N-1}
\bigl(B_{\theta+(k+1)2^{-N}}-B_{\theta+k2^{-N}}\bigr)^4.
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Lemma 7.1 — uniform realized quarticity over compact phase ranges

For every finite `A`, almost surely,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le\theta\le A}
|\mathcal K_N(B;\theta)-3|\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Moreover one probability-one event can be chosen so that (7.2) holds for every integer `A>=1` simultaneously.

### Proof

Set `n=2^N`, `h=1/n`, and use the phase mesh

\[
D_N=\{j\delta_N:0\le j\delta_N\le A+\delta_N\},
\qquad \delta_N=n^{-8}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

For a fixed `theta`, the `n` increments of length `h` are independent `N(0,h)`, so

\[
\mathcal K_N(B;\theta)=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n Z_k^4.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For any fixed integer `p>=10`, Rosenthal's inequality (or the elementary multinomial moment bound for centered iid variables with finite `2p` moment) gives

\[
\mathbb E\left|
\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n(Z_k^4-3)
\right|^{2p}
\le C_p n^{-p}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Hence for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(
\max_{\theta\in D_N}|\mathcal K_N(B;\theta)-3|>\epsilon
\right)
\le C_{p,\epsilon}(A+1)n^{8-p}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Choose `p=10`. Since `n=2^N`, the right side is summable in `N`. Borel--Cantelli gives convergence uniformly on the phase mesh.

Now take arbitrary `theta in [0,A]` and a nearest `theta' in D_N`. Standard Brownian modulus of continuity on `[0,A+2]` gives, eventually almost surely,

\[
\omega_B(r)\le C\sqrt{r\log(1/r)}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

If `x_k` and `y_k` are the length-`h` increments started at `theta` and `theta'`, then

\[
|x_k-y_k|\le 2\omega_B(\delta_N),
\qquad
\max(|x_k|,|y_k|)\le C\sqrt{h\log n}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Using `|x^4-y^4| <= 4 max(|x|,|y|)^3 |x-y|`, the difference of quarticities is at most

\[
C n^2(h\log n)^{3/2}(\delta_N\log n)^{1/2}
= C n^{-7/2}(\log n)^2\to0.
\tag{7.9}
\]

This upgrades the phase-mesh convergence to all `theta in [0,A]`. Intersect over integer `A`. QED.

The key dependence point is that (7.2) is a pathwise statement simultaneously over every phase **before** the random FBM witness time is introduced.

## 8. Roughify the trace without changing either quadratic clock or quartic signature

The raw Takagi trace `F` is `1/2`-Hölder, so the earlier critical-Hölder-web theorem could potentially subsume a gate graph built directly from `F`. To force a genuinely different family, add a finite-variation perturbation exactly at Brownian logarithmic modulus scale.

Define

\[
S(0)=0,\qquad
S(t)=\sqrt{t(1-\log t)}=\sqrt{t\log(e/t)},\quad 0<t\le1,
\tag{8.1}
\]

and

\[
Q(t)=F(t)+S(t).
\tag{8.2}
\]

The function `S` is increasing and concave because

\[
S''(t)=
-\frac{(1-\log t)^2+1}
{4t^{3/2}(1-\log t)^{3/2}}<0.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Thus `TV(S)=1`, and for dyadic mesh `h`,

\[
0\le\Delta S\le S(h)=\sqrt{h\log(e/h)}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

### 8.1 The complete local quadratic clock is still Brownian

Fix a dyadic interval

\[
I=[j2^{-r},(j+1)2^{-r}].
\tag{8.5}
\]

On `I`, the levels `m<r` of `F` form one affine function with slope `lambda_I`, while the higher levels are exactly a rescaled copy of `F`:

\[
F(t)=\text{affine}(t)+2^{-r/2}F(2^r t-j).
\tag{8.6}
\]

The high-frequency copy vanishes at both endpoints of `I`, so its cross term with the affine increment sums to zero. Therefore, for `N>=r`,

\[
V_N(F;I)
=2^{-r}(1-2^{-(N-r)})
+\lambda_I^2 2^{-N-r}
\longrightarrow2^{-r}=|I|.
\tag{8.7}
\]

A continuous finite-variation perturbation has zero dyadic quadratic variation, and

\[
\left|\sum_I \Delta F\,\Delta S\right|
\le \max_I|\Delta F|\,TV(S;I)\to0.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{V_N(Q;I)\longrightarrow |I|\quad\text{for every dyadic }I.}
\tag{8.9}
\]

Thus both run 53's total-QV test and run 54's localized quadratic-clock test are silent on `Q`.

### 8.2 The quarticity stays `7/3`

For `h=2^{-N}`, let `a_k=Delta F`, `b_k=Delta S`. Uniform `1/2`-Hölder control of `F` gives `max|a_k| <= C sqrt(h)`, while (8.4) gives `max b_k <= sqrt(h L)` with `L=log(e/h)` and `sum b_k=1`. Expanding `(a+b)^4-a^4` and using these bounds gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
h^{-1}\sum |a|^3b &\le C\sqrt h,\\
h^{-1}\sum a^2b^2 &\le C\sqrt{hL},\\
h^{-1}\sum |a|b^3 &\le C\sqrt h\,L,\\
h^{-1}\sum b^4 &\le C\sqrt h\,L^{3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.10}
\]

All four terms vanish. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal K_N(Q)\longrightarrow\frac73\ne3.}
\tag{8.11}
\]

### 8.3 This is not a disguised critical-Hölder or BV trace

At `h=2^{-N}`,

\[
\frac{S(h)}{\sqrt h}=\sqrt{1+N\log2}\to\infty,
\tag{8.12}
\]

and `F(h)>=0`, so `Q` is **not** `1/2`-Hölder at the left endpoint. On the other hand, its dyadic quadratic variation on every nondegenerate dyadic interval is positive, so it cannot be of bounded variation there. More generally, for every `beta<2`, the positive quadratic variation plus `max |Delta Q|->0` forces the dyadic `beta`-variation sums to diverge. The earlier critical-Hölder and BV-trace gate theorems are therefore genuinely silent.

## 9. New general theorem T75 — non-Gaussian quartic-signature gate obstruction

### Theorem T75

Let `Q in C([0,1])` and suppose

\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}|\mathcal K_N(Q)-3|>0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Let `a_m -> infinity` be deterministic and `c_m in R`. Let `K(u)` be any deterministic family of spatial fibers such that on every unit gate block

\[
K(a_m+s)\subset\{c_m+Q(s)\},
\qquad 0\le s\le1,
\tag{9.2}
\]

with **no restriction whatsoever** on `K(u)` between gate blocks.

Then every forward Brownian motion `X` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\exists R<\infty:\ X_{-u}\in K(u)\ \forall u\ge R
\right)=0.
}
\tag{T75}
\]

### Proof

For integer `R>=1`, let

\[
E_R=\{X_{-u}\in K(u)\ \forall u\ge R\}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Assume `P(E_R)>0`. Choose a deterministic gate index `m` with `a_m>=R`. Since the FBM witness times satisfy `S_j->-infinity` almost surely, for

\[
C_j=\{S_j\le-(a_m+1)\}
\tag{9.4}
\]

we have `1_{C_j}->1` almost surely and hence `P(E_R cap C_j)->P(E_R)`. Fix a deterministic `j` with

\[
P(E_R\cap C_j)>0.
\tag{9.5}
\]

For this fixed witness, `B_t=X_{S_j+t}-X_{S_j}` is unconditional standard Brownian motion. On `C_j`, put

\[
\theta=-a_m-1-S_j\ge0.
\tag{9.6}
\]

For `0<=q<=1`, the physical time `-a_m-1+q` has age `a_m+1-q`. On `E_R cap C_j`, gate confinement gives

\[
B_{\theta+q}
=c_m-X_{S_j}+Q(1-q).
\tag{9.7}
\]

Vertical translation does not affect realized quarticity, and time reversal only reverses the list of increments and changes their signs. Therefore

\[
\mathcal K_N(B;\theta)=\mathcal K_N(Q)
\quad\text{for every }N
\tag{9.8}
\]

on `E_R cap C_j`.

But Lemma 7.1 holds on one probability-one Brownian event simultaneously for every finite phase `theta`, including this random phase. Hence the left side of (9.8) tends to `3`, contradicting (9.1). Thus `P(E_R)=0` for every integer `R`, and T75 follows. QED.

No conditioning on `S_j` and no independence between `S_j`, `X_{S_j}`, and the Brownian future is used.
