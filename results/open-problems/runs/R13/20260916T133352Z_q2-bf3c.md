## R13-X01 — exact rational obstruction to multiplicative rank-two/complex → Boolean rounding

**Status: PROVED.**

Take the rational Komlós matrix

A = [1  1  1] ∈ Q^{1×3}.

Every column has Euclidean norm 1. For Boolean signs,

min_{ε∈{±1}^3} |ε_1+ε_2+ε_3| = 1,

because a sum of three signs is an odd integer.

For rank-two/vector discrepancy choose unit vectors

x_1=(1,0),

x_2=(-1/2, √3/2),

x_3=(-1/2, -√3/2).

They satisfy x_1+x_2+x_3=0 exactly. Hence

Vdisc_2(A)=0,

while disc(A)=1. Equivalently, the complex phases 1,ω,ω² make the complex discrepancy zero.

Therefore there is **no finite universal C** for which

    disc(A) ≤ C Vdisc_2(A)

or

    disc(A) ≤ C cdisc(A)

holds for all real Komlós matrices. This refutes a homogeneous multiplicative transfer from the Guillen–Kobzar theorem to Boolean discrepancy. It does **not** refute Komlós.

## R13-C01 — exact Fourier identity for random-hyperplane rounding of a rank-two solution

**Status: PROVED.**

Represent rank-two unit vectors as z_j=e^{iθ_j}. For a row a=(a_j), random-hyperplane rounding uses

ε_j(φ)=sgn cos(θ_j−φ),   φ∼Unif[0,2π),

and D_a(φ)=Σ_j a_j ε_j(φ). The Fourier series

sgn cos t = (4/π) Σ_{r≥0} (-1)^r cos((2r+1)t)/(2r+1)

and orthogonality give the exact identity

E_φ D_a(φ)^2
 = (8/π²) Σ_{r≥0} 1/(2r+1)² · |Σ_j a_j e^{i(2r+1)θ_j}|².      (C01)

Thus rank-two vector discrepancy controls only the first odd harmonic. Boolean hyperplane rounding depends on **all** odd harmonics.

For X01 with equilateral phases, the first harmonic vanishes. More generally S_k=1+ω^k+ω^{2k} vanishes unless 3|k, while S_k=3 when 3|k. Among positive odd k, these are k=3(2ℓ+1). Therefore

E D(φ)^2
 = (8/π²) Σ_{ℓ≥0} 9/[3(2ℓ+1)]²
 = (8/π²) Σ_{ℓ≥0} 1/(2ℓ+1)²
 = 1.

Since D is an odd integer almost everywhere, |D|≥1, so |D|=1 almost everywhere. Hyperplane rounding happens to attain the Boolean optimum on this example, but only because the third and higher odd harmonics carry the missing energy. Any attempt to exploit the rank-two theorem toward Boolean signs therefore needs simultaneous higher-harmonic control, not just the first-harmonic norm supplied by vector discrepancy.

This gives a falsifiable target for any complex-to-Boolean route:

Σ_{r≥0} (2r+1)^{-2}|Σ_j a_{ij} z_j^{2r+1}|² = O(1)

uniformly over rows i, together with a single-angle selection argument. Guillen–Kobzar alone gives no such estimate.

## R13-C02 — explicit pre-GFL all-n Boolean baseline

**Status: PROVED from Banaszczyk's theorem.**

This is retained as an independently checkable fallback while the new constant-bound preprint is audited.

Let E=span(v_1,…,v_n), d=dim E≤n, and let G be standard Gaussian in E. For coordinate i, define leverage

σ_i² = ||P_E e_i||².

Then 0≤σ_i²≤1 and

Σ_i σ_i² = tr(P_E)=d≤n.

For t≥√2 and 0<s≤1,

\barΦ(t/√s) ≤ s \barΦ(t).                                      (C02.1)

Proof: h(u)=u²\barΦ(u) is decreasing for u≥√2, because Mills' inequality \barΦ(u)<φ(u)/u gives

h'(u)=u[2\barΦ(u)−uφ(u)] < uφ(u)(2/u−u)≤0.

Hence for t≥√2,

P(||G||∞>t)
 ≤ 2Σ_i \barΦ(t/σ_i)
 ≤ 2\barΦ(t)Σ_iσ_i²
 ≤ 2n\barΦ(t).

For n≥4 set

t_n = Φ^{-1}(1−1/(4n)).

Then t_n≥√2 and the cube section K=E∩[−t_n,t_n]^m has Gaussian measure at least 1/2. Apply Banaszczyk's theorem to the vectors v_j/5 in E: there are signs with Σ ε_j(v_j/5)∈K. Therefore

||Σ ε_j v_j||∞ ≤ 5 Φ^{-1}(1−1/(4n)),      n≥4.               (C02.2)

Independently, random signs satisfy

E||Σ ε_jv_j||₂²=Σ_j||v_j||₂²≤n,

so at least one signing has infinity norm at most √n. Combining this with Ercan gives the explicit pre-GFL bound

B_pre(n)=√n,                                                  1≤n≤3,

B_pre(n)=min{√n, 5Φ^{-1}(1−1/(4n)),
             2395(1+log_+(n/9))^{1/4}+2√2},                 n≥4.

This bound is full Boolean and uniform in m. It is not the current best theorem claim if Guo–Fang–Lu is correct; it is the independently transparent fallback.

## Audit of the new Guo–Fang–Lu full-scope theorem

**Status: PARTIAL AUDIT; closure not declared.**

The primary arXiv HTML was read, including Theorem 1.1 and the statements of Lemma 2.1, Proposition 2.2, Corollary 2.3, and Lemma 2.4.

Two local pieces were independently checked:

### A. Translate containment / backward sign recovery — PASS

Their open transform is

T_v K = ((K−v)∩(K+v)) + {tv: −2<t<2}.

If y=z+tv with z±v∈K, then for t≥0, y−v=z+(t−1)v∈K by convexity, hence y∈K+v; for t≤0, y+v=z+(t+1)v∈K, hence y∈K−v. Thus

T_vK ⊂ (K−v)∪(K+v).

If K is symmetric and convex, iterating nonempty symmetric transforms leaves 0 in the terminal body, and the containment recovers one sign at each step backwards. This part of the Boolean sign reduction is valid.

### B. Initial cosine-squared cube density — PASS

For

ρ_C(x)=C^{-d}∏_i cos²(πx_i/(2C)) 1_{(-C,C)^d}(x),

differentiation gives

V_u(ρ_C)=(π/C) E|Σ_i u_i T_i|,

where the T_i are iid with density 2/[π(1+t²)²]. This distribution has representation

T_i = G_i/√S_i,

with G_i∼N(0,1), S_i∼χ²_3 independent. Conditional on the S_i,

E_G|Σ_i u_iG_i/√S_i|
 = √(2/π)(Σ_i u_i²/S_i)^{1/2}.

Jensen and E[S_i^{-1}]=1 yield

E|Σ_i u_iT_i| ≤ √(2/π)||u||₂,

so

V_u(ρ_C)≤√(2π)C^{-1}||u||₂.

At C=3√(2π), the variation parameter is exactly 1/3, matching Proposition 2.2's step threshold for ||v||₂≤1. The exact one-dimensional density normalization, E[S^{-1}]=1, and constant arithmetic are archived in the checker.
