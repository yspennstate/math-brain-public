## 4. R13-C21 — arbitrary-row ten-column bound `149/50`

### Theorem C21

For every real `m x 10` matrix `A` satisfying

\[
\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2\le1\qquad(1\le j\le10),
\]

there exists `epsilon in {-1,+1}^10` such that

\[
\boxed{\|A\epsilon\|_\infty\le\frac{149}{50}.}
\]

### Proof

Fix one row

\[
r=(a_1,\ldots,a_{10}),\qquad |a_j|\le1,
\]

and let

\[
X=\sum_{j=1}^{10}a_j\varepsilon_j,
\qquad
S=\sum_{j=1}^{10}a_j^2.
\]

Set

\[
t=\frac{149}{50},
\qquad
S_0=\frac{55}{16}.
\]

Let `b(r)` be the number of antipodal Boolean sign classes `{epsilon,-epsilon}` for which

\[
|r\cdot\varepsilon|>t.
\]

By symmetry, exactly one representative of every bad antipodal class has positive dot product exceeding `t`, hence

\[
b(r)=2^{10}\Pr(X>t).
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Case 1: `S>S_0`

For independent symmetric summands `a_j epsilon_j` with absolute values at most one, the exact random-walk comparison gives at the nonintegral threshold `t in (2,3)`

\[
\Pr(X>t)\le\Pr(W_{10}>t)
=\Pr(W_{10}\ge4).
\]

Here

\[
\Pr(W_{10}\ge4)
=2^{-10}\left[\binom{10}{7}+\binom{10}{8}+\binom{10}{9}+\binom{10}{10}\right]
=\frac{176}{1024}
=\frac{11}{64}.
\]

Thus

\[
b(r)\le176.
\]

Because

\[
\frac{256}{5}S_0
=\frac{256}{5}\frac{55}{16}
=176,
\]

strict `S>S_0` yields

\[
\boxed{b(r)<\frac{256}{5}S.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Case 2: `0<S<=S_0`

Normalize

\[
Y=\frac{X}{\sqrt S},
\qquad
x=\frac{t}{\sqrt S}.
\]

The normalized coefficients of `Y` have squared sum one. Define

\[
x_0=\frac{t}{\sqrt{S_0}},
\qquad
x_0^2
=\frac{(149/50)^2}{55/16}
=\frac{88804}{34375}>2.
\]

Hence `x>=x_0>sqrt(2)`. Bentkus--Dzindzalieta give

\[
\Pr(Y>x)\le c_*\bar\Phi(x),
\qquad
c_*=[4\bar\Phi(\sqrt2)]^{-1}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For

\[
f(x)=x^2\bar\Phi(x),
\]

Mills' inequality `barPhi(x)<phi(x)/x` gives

\[
f'(x)
=2x\bar\Phi(x)-x^2\phi(x)
<(2-x^2)x\phi(x)/x
=(2-x^2)\phi(x)<0
\]

for `x>sqrt(2)`. Therefore

\[
x^2\bar\Phi(x)\le x_0^2\bar\Phi(x_0).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The exact-arithmetic checker certifies

\[
\boxed{c_*\bar\Phi(x_0)<\frac{11}{64}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

It does this without floating-point acceptance: Machin alternating-series bounds bracket `pi`; rational squares bracket `(2pi)^(-1/2)` and `sqrt(2)`; a rational `x_L=4018231/2500000<x_0` is certified with `x_L^2>2`; and an alternating-series lower bound for `int_0^{x_L} exp(-u^2/2)du` gives a rational upper bound on `barPhi(x_L)`. The exact positive margin in the archived output is approximately `2.4286932947795254e-4`.

Combining (4.3)--(4.5),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(X>t)
&\le c_*\bar\Phi(x)\\
&\le c_*\frac{x_0^2\bar\Phi(x_0)}{x^2}\\
&<\frac{11}{64}\frac{x_0^2}{x^2}
=\frac{11}{64}\frac{S}{S_0}
=\frac{S}{20}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using (4.1),

\[
\boxed{b(r)<2^{10}\frac S{20}=\frac{256}{5}S.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The zero-energy case is trivial, so (4.6) holds for every row.

Now sum over the rows `r_i` of `A`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i b(r_i)
&<\frac{256}{5}\sum_i\|r_i\|_2^2\\
&=\frac{256}{5}\sum_{j=1}^{10}\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2^2\\
&\le\frac{256}{5}\,10
=512.
\end{aligned}
\]

There are exactly `2^(10-1)=512` antipodal Boolean sign classes. If every class were spoiled by at least one row, the incidence sum would be at least `512`, contradiction. Hence one antipodal class is good for every row, proving

\[
\boxed{\|A\varepsilon\|_\infty\le149/50.}
\]

`QED`

This theorem is deterministic and arbitrary-row. Random signs are only a finite counting device; no random-input matrix or average-case assumption is introduced.
