## 4. R13-C03 — finite-direction directional-TV preservation is sufficient

**Status: PROVED.**  This is a proof simplification/audit lemma; no literature novelty is claimed.

The Guo--Fang--Lu paper proves Proposition 2.2 with one density controlling **all** directions. For a finite Komlós input `v_1,...,v_n`, that infinite-direction conclusion is stronger than necessary.

### Statement

Let `K` be a nonempty bounded open convex subset of `R^d`. Let
\[
\mathcal U=\{u_1,\dots,u_N\}
\]
be a finite list containing the coordinate basis, and let `b_i>0`. Suppose `K` supports a probability density `ρ∈BV` with
\[
V_{u_i}(\rho)\le b_i\qquad(1\le i\le N).
\]
Let `v` be one of the listed directions and assume
\[
V_v(\rho)\le\frac13.
\]
Then the open Banaszczyk transform
\[
\mathcal T_vK=((K-v)\cap(K+v))+\{tv:-2<t<2\}
\]
is nonempty and supports a probability density `\tilde ρ` satisfying the same finite list of bounds
\[
V_{u_i}(\widetilde\rho)\le b_i\qquad(1\le i\le N).
\]

### Proof

Use the Guo--Fang--Lu lift
\[
R(y,s)=\frac16\rho(y+sv)\mathbf1_{\{|s|<3\}}.
\]
Fiberwise symmetric decreasing rearrangement in `s` gives `R^*`. Their layer-cake contraction proof is direction-by-direction, so for every `u_i` in the finite list,
\[
V_{(u_i,0)}(R^*)\le V_{u_i}(\rho)\le b_i. \tag{4.1}
\]
Their exact height identity is
\[
\int |s|R^*
=\frac32-\frac1{24}\int_0^6(6-h)\|\rho-\rho(\cdot+hv)\|_1\,dh.
\]
The BV translation inequality gives
\[
\|\rho-\rho(\cdot+hv)\|_1\le hV_v(\rho),
\]
and therefore, since `∫_0^6h(6-h)dh=36`,
\[
\int|s|R^*\ge\frac32-\frac32V_v(\rho)\ge1. \tag{4.2}
\]
The symmetrized lift is a bounded open convex set `B^*`, symmetric in the vertical coordinate, whose height-one section is exactly `\mathcal T_vK`.

It remains only to prove the **finite-budget prescribed-section statement**. For arbitrary coefficients `β_i≥0`, set
\[
H_\beta(\xi)=\sum_i\beta_i|u_i\cdot\xi|.
\]
For the horizontal section `D_t` of `B^*`, let
\[
g_\beta(t)=h_{H_\beta}(D_t).
\]
Minkowski convexity of the anisotropic Cheeger value makes `g_β` convex; nesting of the symmetric convex sections makes it nondecreasing. For almost every `s`, let `R_s=R^*(\cdot,s)` and `q(s)=\int R_s`. Slicing, Fatou, and (4.1) give
\[
\int E_{H_\beta}(R_s)\,ds
\le\sum_i\beta_i b_i. \tag{4.3}
\]
Whenever `q(s)>0`, the normalized slice is supported in `D_{|s|}`, so
\[
q(s)g_\beta(|s|)\le E_{H_\beta}(R_s).
\]
Let `M=\int|s|q(s)ds`. By (4.2), `M≥1`. Jensen and monotonicity give
\[
h_{H_\beta}(D_1)=g_\beta(1)
\le g_\beta(M)
\le\int q(s)g_\beta(|s|)ds
\le\sum_i\beta_i b_i. \tag{4.4}
\]
This holds for every nonnegative `β`.

Now apply finite-dimensional separation. Define the upward-closed convex set
\[
S=\{z\in\mathbb R^N:\exists f\in\mathcal P(D_1),\ z_i\ge V_{u_i}(f)\},
\]
and the open coordinate box
\[
Q=\{z:z_i<b_i\ \forall i\}.
\]
If no common density obeys all `b_i`, `S` and `Q` are disjoint. Separation gives a nonzero `β`; upward closure forces every `β_i≥0`. Normalizing is optional. The separation inequality then contradicts (4.4). A minimizing sequence has uniformly bounded coordinate variations because the coordinate basis is in the finite list; the BV compactness lemma gives an `L^1` limit, and lower semicontinuity preserves all finite bounds exactly. Hence a common density exists on `D_1=\mathcal T_vK`. ∎

### Consequence for finite Komlós inputs

At stage `j` of the signing recursion, it is enough to retain the finite list
\[
\{e_1,\ldots,e_m,v_j,v_{j+1},\ldots,v_n\}.
\]
After transforming by `v_j`, discard that direction and retain the remaining future list. Thus the countable-dense/all-directions final passage in Lemma 3.3 is unnecessary for the finite-dimensional signing theorem. This does not improve `3√(2π)`; it reduces the audit surface.

## 5. R13-AUDIT-GFL-P2.2 — analytic-core self-audit

**Status: PASS as an R13 self-audit; NOT an independent closure audit.**

I rederived the complete chain used in Proposition 2.2 rather than relying on its statement.

### 5.1 Directional BV and compactness

The source's Lemma 3.1 identifies directional variation with the `L^1` translation derivative, proves convexity/lower semicontinuity, and derives compactness from coordinate variation bounds. The argument is standard mollification plus finite-mesh approximation. I found no gap relevant to the finite-direction form above.

### 5.2 Anisotropic Cheeger convexity

For
\[
H(\xi)=\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell|u_\ell\cdot\xi|,
\]
the energy is exactly
\[
E_H(\rho)=\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell V_{u_\ell}(\rho).
\]
The source first treats smooth strongly convex norms and defines the first Dirichlet Finsler `p`-eigenvalue `λ_{p,H}`. Wang--Xia's stronger inverse-power Brunn--Minkowski inequality implies the linear upper bound used by the paper: if
\[
\lambda_t^{-1/p}\ge(1-t)\lambda_0^{-1/p}+t\lambda_1^{-1/p},
\]
then monotonicity of weighted power means gives
\[
\lambda_t\le(1-t)\lambda_0+t\lambda_1.
\]
The direction is correct.

The paper proves
\[
\lim_{p\downarrow1}\lambda_{p,H}(K)=h_H(K)
\]
by compactly supported smoothing for the upper bound and BV compactness/lower semicontinuity for the lower bound. For the degenerate finite mixture it regularizes by
\[
H_\varepsilon(\xi)=\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell
\sqrt{(u_\ell\cdot\xi)^2+\varepsilon^2\|\xi\|_2^2}.
\]
Here `H≤H_ε≤H+ε||·||`, each `H_ε` is a smooth strongly convex norm away from zero, and fixing a near-minimizer before sending `ε↓0` avoids any need for uniform BV control of the degenerating minimizers. This proves the required Minkowski convexity of `h_H`.

### 5.3 Eigenfunction input / Appendix A

Appendix A supplies the needed eigenvalue comparison for the regularized norms. The mapped Mosconi--Riey--Squassina hypotheses are explicit: kinetic integrand `F^p`, reaction `λs^{p-1}`, primitive `λs^p/p`; the resulting first eigenfunction is log-concave and has the quoted boundary regularity. The infimal-convolution proof uses the common-gradient stationarity equations and the harmonic-mean Hessian relation. The matrix arithmetic has the correct sign: Loewner arithmetic--harmonic mean followed by trace against the positive matrix `A(q)` yields the PDE subsolution inequality.

The appendix separately handles critical gradients using a cutoff around the compact critical set and removes smooth-boundary assumptions by inner exhaustion. I checked that these are not silently discarded in the main Lemma 3.2 invocation. No normalization or sign inconsistency was found.

### 5.4 Common density and prescribed section

For a finite direction list, the separation proof in Lemma 3.3 is exactly the one written in R13-C03. The source then takes a countable dense list only to recover all directions; that extra step is not needed for finite signing.

For the prescribed-section lemma, vertical symmetry and convexity give nested sections `D_t` and Minkowski inclusion
\[
(1-\theta)D_s+\theta D_t\subset D_{(1-\theta)s+\theta D_t.
\]
(Equivalently, with the obvious corrected notation on the right, `D_{(1-θ)s+θt}`.) Domain monotonicity plus Cheeger convexity makes `g(t)=h_H(D_t)` convex and nondecreasing. Slicing yields the averaged energy bound, and Jensen at the mean absolute height gives the target section. The mean lies inside the vertical support because the density is supported in the open bounded lift. The logic is sound.

### 5.5 Lift, rearrangement, and the exact constant

The geometric lift has height-one section exactly `\mathcal T_vK`; both inclusions follow by a one-dimensional fiber argument. Fiberwise symmetric decreasing rearrangement preserves mass and support in the Steiner symmetral. Its horizontal `L^1` contraction follows from the maximal intersection property of centered intervals at every level set, hence does not assume differentiability.

The exact retained-height identity is
\[
\int|s|R^*
=\frac32-rac1{24}\int_0^6(6-h)
\|\rho-\rho(\cdot+hv)\|_1dh.
\]
Using the translation bound gives loss at most
\[
\frac1{24}V_v(\rho)\int_0^6h(6-h)dh
=\frac32V_v(\rho).
\]
Thus `V_v(ρ)≤1/3` leaves mean absolute height at least `1`, exactly what the prescribed-section lemma requires.

The initial product cosine-squared cube density has
\[
V_u(\rho_C)\le \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{C}\|u\|_2.
\]
Taking
\[
C=3\sqrt{2\pi}
\]
gives `κ=1/3`. Iterating the transform and using translate containment recovers Boolean signs backwards. Therefore the proof chain does establish the source's Theorem 1.1, conditional only on the cited established analytic inputs whose hypotheses were checked above.

\[
\varphi_T(s)=(1+|s|)e^{-|s|}.

\[
E|S_t|=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty

\[
E|S_t|=\frac{2t}{\pi}\int_0^\infty(1+s)^{t-1}e^{-ts}ds.

\[
E|S_t|
=\frac2\pi\frac{t!}{t^t}\sum_{r=0}^{t-1}\frac{t^r}{r!}.

The cube half-width required by the transform is `C=3πE|S_t|`, hence `C=R_t`.

For strict comparison with the Gaussian, for every `s>0`,
\[
\log(1+s)-s+\frac{s^2}{2}>0,

because its derivative is `s^2/(1+s)>0`. Therefore
\[
(1+s)^te^{-ts}>e^{-ts^2/2}.
\]
Substitution in the absolute-moment integral gives
\[
E|S_t|<E|N(0,t)|=\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\pi}},

\[
