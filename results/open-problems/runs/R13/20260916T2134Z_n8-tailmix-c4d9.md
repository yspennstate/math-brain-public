## 4. Method #21 — ask who else has this shape

Run 9's direct diameter-downset compiler suggests the conjectural `n=8` threshold `sqrt(6)`, but proving the required diameter-five convex-mean theorem appears substantially harder.

Instead I translated a *single row* into a weighted Rademacher tail problem and searched the sharp one-dimensional literature.

Two exact ingredients fit together:

### 4.1 Tight Gaussian comparison

Bentkus--Dzindzalieta prove that if

\[
Y=\sum_{j=1}^n b_j\varepsilon_j,\qquad \sum_j b_j^2\le1,
\]

then for every real `x`

\[
\Pr(Y\ge x)\le c_*\bar\Phi(x),
\qquad
c_*=\frac1{4\bar\Phi(\sqrt2)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### 4.2 Exact bounded-random-walk tail

Dzindzalieta--Sileikis--Juskevicius prove that for independent symmetric `X_j` with `|X_j|\le1`,

\[
\Pr\Big(\sum_{j=1}^n X_j\ge x\Big)
\le
\begin{cases}
\Pr(W_n\ge x),&\lceil x\rceil+n\text{ even},\\
\Pr(W_{n-1}\ge x),&\lceil x\rceil+n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

At `n=8` and any `2<x<3`, the second case gives

\[
\Pr(S_8\ge x)\le\Pr(W_7\ge3)
=\frac{\binom75+\binom76+\binom77}{2^7}
=\frac{29}{128}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

These two theorems have complementary strengths: the Gaussian comparison sees row energy, while the exact random-walk comparison gives a hard finite cap independent of energy.

## 5. Method #15 — break symmetry by row energy

Fix one row

\[
r=(a_1,\dots,a_8),
\qquad
S:=\|r\|_2^2=\sum_{j=1}^8a_j^2.
\]

Because every column of the ambient matrix has Euclidean norm at most one,

\[
|a_j|\le1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
X=\sum_{j=1}^8a_j\varepsilon_j
\]

for uniform independent signs. Set

\[
t:=\frac{349}{125},
\qquad
S_0:=\frac{29}{8}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For this row let `b(r)` be the number of antipodal Boolean sign classes with `|X|>t`. There are `2^7=128` antipodal classes, and every bad class has exactly one orientation with `X>t`. Therefore

\[
b(r)=256\,\Pr(X>t).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The proof splits at the exact energy `S0`.

## 6. Method #4 — prove the weaker rational threshold completely

### 6.1 Low-energy rows: `0<S<=29/8`

Normalize

\[
Y=X/\sqrt S.
\]

Its coefficient squares sum to one, so (4.1) gives

\[
\Pr(X>t)\le c_*\bar\Phi(t/\sqrt S).
\tag{6.1}

Put

\[

We need

\[

\tag{6.2}

Since `S=t^2/x^2`, (6.2) is equivalent to

\[
16c_*x^2\bar\Phi(x)<t^2.
\tag{6.3}

For

\[

\[

\[
f'(x)=2x\bar\Phi(x)-x^2\phi(x)
<(2-x^2)\phi(x)<0
\qquad(x>\sqrt2).
\tag{6.4}

### 6.2 High-energy rows: `S>29/8`

Apply the exact bounded-random-walk theorem (4.2) directly to the eight variables `a_j epsilon_j`, which are symmetric and satisfy `|a_j epsilon_j|\le1`.

\[
\boxed{

\tag{R13-C15}

## 7. Global incidence argument

Let `r_i` be the rows of an arbitrary admissible `m x 8` matrix `A`.

Summing C15 gives

\[
\sum_i b(r_i)
<
16\sum_i\|r_i\|_2^2
=
16\sum_{j=1}^8\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2^2
\le128.
\tag{7.1}
\]

There are exactly

\[
2^{8-1}=128
\]

antipodal Boolean sign classes. If every class were bad in at least one row, the total row-class incidence count would be at least `128`, contradicting (7.1).

Therefore some class is bad in no row. Any representative `epsilon` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\|A\varepsilon\|_\infty\le\frac{349}{125}.
}
\]

This proves R13-C14.
