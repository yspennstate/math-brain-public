## 3. Main theorem

### R13-C39

For every real matrix with five columns of Euclidean norm at most one,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{disc}(A)\le\sqrt{\frac{300}{89}}}.
\]

\[
\boxed{K_5^*\le\sqrt{\frac{300}{89}}=1.835970184086\ldots.}
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
T=\sqrt{\frac{300}{89}},
\qquad
c=T^{-2}=\frac{89}{300}.
\]

Assume for contradiction that an admissible five-column matrix `A` satisfies `disc(A)>T`. Scale every row `a_\alpha` by `T`:

\[
r_\alpha=a_\alpha/T.
\]

For every coordinate `j`, the column-norm hypothesis gives the energy cap

\[
E_j:=\sum_\alpha r_{\alpha j}^2\le c=\frac{89}{300},
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^5E_j\le\frac{89}{60}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

There are 16 antipodal/projective Boolean sign classes. For every class choose a row witnessing badness, orient the class so the row dot product is positive and `>1`, assign the class to that row, and discard unused rows. Let `q` be the number of active rows. If row `i` receives `k_i` classes with oriented representatives `t`, write

\[
v_i=\sum_{t\in B_i}t.
\]

Then

\[
r_i\cdot v_i>k_i.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The cases `q<=3`, `q=4`, and `q>=5` exhaust all possibilities.

### Case 1: `q<=3`

Use the retained partial-rounding lemma. If vectors `w_1,\dots,w_n` lie in the Euclidean unit ball of `\mathbb R^q`, choose an extreme point of

\[
\{x\in[-1,1]^n:\sum_jx_jw_j=0\}.
\]

At most `q` coordinates are fractional. Independently round those coordinates with their prescribed expectations. The expected squared Euclidean rounding error is at most `q`, so some Boolean rounding has norm at most `\sqrt q`.

Apply this to the active `q`-row submatrix of `A`. Some signing has

\[
\|A\varepsilon\|_\infty\le\|A\varepsilon\|_2\le\sqrt q\le\sqrt3<T,
\]

contradicting `disc(A)>T`.

### Case 2: `q>=5`

For `k` distinct projective five-cube classes, define

\[
M_k=\max\left\|\sum_{j=1}^k t_j\right\|_2^2,
\]

where each class may be oriented independently. The retained exact table is

\[
(M_1,\ldots,M_{16})
=(5,16,29,48,61,80,101,128,133,144,157,176,189,208,229,256).
\tag{3.3}
\]

By (3.2) and Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\|r_i\|_2^2>\frac{k_i^2}{\|v_i\|_2^2}
\ge\frac{k_i^2}{M_{k_i}}.
\]

The exact rational partition scan gives

\[
\min_{q\ge5,\ \sum k_i=16}\sum_i\frac{k_i^2}{M_{k_i}}
=\frac{89}{60},
\tag{3.4}
\]

with extremal profile `(1,1,2,4,8)`. Therefore

\[
\sum_i\|r_i\|_2^2>\frac{89}{60},
\]

while (3.1) gives the opposite weak inequality. Contradiction. The strictness comes from the strict witness inequality in (3.2), so the endpoint `c=89/300` itself is allowed.

### Case 3: `q=4`

Let the four block sizes be `k_i`. Before any diagonal duality, the same argument implies that a profile can survive only if

\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\frac{k_i^2}{M_{k_i}}<\frac{89}{60}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The exact scan leaves 33 sorted profiles: 30 have maximum block size at most 10, and precisely three have larger blocks:

\[
(1,1,2,12),\quad(1,2,2,11),\quad(1,1,3,11).
\tag{3.6}
\]

For a positive diagonal matrix

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_5),
\qquad \lambda_j>0,\quad\sum_j\lambda_j=1,
\]

weighted Cauchy and (3.2) give

\[
r_i^T\Lambda r_i>
\frac{k_i^2}{v_i^T\Lambda^{-1}v_i}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

On the other hand the coordinate caps give

\[
\sum_i r_i^T\Lambda r_i
=\sum_j\lambda_jE_j\le c=\frac{89}{300}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

So it suffices to show, for every surviving oriented four-row configuration, that some positive diagonal `\Lambda` satisfies

\[
\sum_{i=1}^4
\frac{k_i^2}{v_i^T\Lambda^{-1}v_i}
\ge\frac{89}{300}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
