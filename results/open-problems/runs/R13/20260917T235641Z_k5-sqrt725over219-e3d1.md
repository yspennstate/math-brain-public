## 3. Main theorem

### R13-C40

For every real matrix `A` with five columns, each of Euclidean norm at most one,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{disc}(A)\le \sqrt{\frac{725}{219}}}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{K_5^*\le \sqrt{\frac{725}{219}}
=1.819478574511\ldots.}
\]

### Proof

Set

\[
T=\sqrt{\frac{725}{219}},\qquad c=T^{-2}=\frac{219}{725}.
\]

Assume for contradiction that an admissible five-column matrix `A` has `disc(A)>T`. Scale its rows by `T`:

\[
r_\alpha=a_\alpha/T.
\]

For each coordinate `j`, the unit-column condition gives

\[
E_j:=\sum_\alpha r_{\alpha j}^2\le c,
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^5E_j\le 5c=\frac{219}{145}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

There are 16 antipodal/projective Boolean sign classes. For every class choose one row witnessing badness, orient the sign representative `t` so `r_i\cdot t>1`, assign the class to that row, and discard unused rows. Let `q` be the number of active rows. If row `i` receives block `B_i`, let `k_i=|B_i|` and `v_i=\sum_{t\in B_i}t`. Then

\[
r_i\cdot v_i>k_i.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For any positive diagonal matrix

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_5),
\qquad \lambda_j>0,\quad \sum_j\lambda_j=1,
\]

weighted Cauchy gives

\[
r_i^T\Lambda r_i>
\frac{k_i^2}{v_i^T\Lambda^{-1}v_i},
\tag{3.3}
\]

while (3.1) gives

\[
\sum_i r_i^T\Lambda r_i
=\sum_j\lambda_jE_j\le c.
\tag{3.4}
\]

We exclude all possible values of `q`.

### Case 1: `q<=3`

Apply the retained partial-rounding lemma to the active `q`-row submatrix. Its five column vectors lie in the Euclidean unit ball of `R^q`. Choose an extreme point of

\[
\{x\in[-1,1]^5:\sum_jx_jw_j=0\}.
\]

At most `q` coordinates are fractional. Independently round only those coordinates with expectations `x_j`. Cross terms vanish, so the expected squared Euclidean rounding error is at most `q`; hence some Boolean signing satisfies

\[
\|A\varepsilon\|_\infty
\le \|A\varepsilon\|_2
\le \sqrt q\le\sqrt3<T,
\]

contradicting the chosen active witnesses. The exact comparison is `3 < 725/219`.

### Case 2: `q>=6`

For `k` distinct projective five-cube classes define

\[
M_k=\max\left\|\sum_{j=1}^k t_j\right\|_2^2,
\]

with independent orientations. The retained exact table is

\[
(M_1,\ldots,M_{16})
=(5,16,29,48,61,80,101,128,133,144,157,176,189,208,229,256).
\tag{3.5}
\]

By (3.2) and ordinary Cauchy,

\[
\|r_i\|_2^2>\frac{k_i^2}{M_{k_i}}.
\]

The exact partition scan now gives

\[
\min_{q\ge6,\ \sum k_i=16}
\sum_i\frac{k_i^2}{M_{k_i}}
=\frac{49}{30},
\tag{3.6}
\]

attained at `(1,1,1,1,4,8)`. But

\[
\frac{49}{30}-\frac{219}{145}
=\frac{107}{870}>0.
\]

Thus `q>=6` contradicts (3.1).

### Case 3: `q=5`

The same exact total-energy pruning shows that only four sorted block-size profiles can satisfy the strict baseline inequality

\[

\[

(1,1,2,4,8),\quad
(2,2,2,2,8),\quad
(2,2,4,4,4).
\tag{3.7}

Their baseline energies are respectively `921/610`, `89/60`, `3/2`, `3/2`. The next two profiles `(1,2,2,3,8)` and `(1,3,4,4,4)` equal `219/145` exactly and are excluded because every block inequality is strict.

For the four surviving profiles, exact profile-wise norm pruning and oriented-state enumeration leave **2,020** genuine five-row configurations: 480, 480, 180, and 880 by the order in (3.7). For each, the exact checker finds a positive rational diagonal weight with denominator 10 such that
