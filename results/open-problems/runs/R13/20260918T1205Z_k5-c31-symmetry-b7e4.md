## 5. The block subset dual

For a block `B` of oriented projective cube classes and a positive diagonal matrix

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_5),
\qquad \sum_j\lambda_j=1,
\]

every nonempty `U subseteq B` obeys

\[
r\cdot u_U>|U|,
\qquad
u_U=\sum_{t\in U}t.
\]

Weighted Cauchy therefore gives

\[
r^T\Lambda r>
\frac{|U|^2}{u_U^T\Lambda^{-1}u_U}.
\]

Define

\[
\psi_\Lambda(B)
=
\max_{\varnothing\ne U\subseteq B}
\frac{|U|^2}{u_U^T\Lambda^{-1}u_U}.
\]

If an oriented subblock has zero sum, its strict witness inequalities already contradict `r\cdot0=0`, and the checker treats that case as an immediate certificate. Otherwise, for active blocks `B_i`,

\[
\sum_i r_i^T\Lambda r_i
>
\sum_i\psi_\Lambda(B_i).
\]

On the other hand, if `E_j` is scaled energy in column `j`,

\[
\sum_i r_i^T\Lambda r_i
=\sum_j\lambda_j E_j\le c.
\]

Thus any rational diagonal weight satisfying

\[
\sum_i\psi_\Lambda(B_i)\ge c
\tag{5.1}
\]

excludes the configuration. The old full-block dual is the special choice `U=B` in every row.
