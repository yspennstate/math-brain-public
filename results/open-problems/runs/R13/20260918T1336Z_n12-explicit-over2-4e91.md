### #6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE

The old six-column obstruction has a signed support whose saturation sets partition the Boolean cube. I stopped optimizing its five real magnitudes and instead deformed the **support itself** by a simple refinement operation.

If a signed template row `s` on `n` coordinates is replaced on `n+1` coordinates by the two rows

\[
(s,+1),\qquad(s,-1),
\tag{2.1}
\]

while every other row receives zero in the new coordinate, then the old saturation set of `s` is partitioned into two sets according to the new Boolean sign. Thus a unique sign-cover remains a unique sign-cover. Repeating this operation grows a finite obstruction without any probabilistic loss.

Starting from the exact six-column support, I used six such refinements, choosing at each discovery step the row whose split gave the best convexly rescaled child template. At twelve columns the resulting support admits a rational scaling with discrepancy strictly greater than `2`.

### #11 — DUALIZE

For a fixed unsigned support `S_i`, optimizing positive magnitudes is the SOCP

\[
\max t
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_{j\in S_i}x_{ij}\ge t\ \forall i,
\qquad
\sum_{i:j\in S_i}x_{ij}^2\le1\ \forall j,
\qquad x_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Its standard dual is

\[
\min_{\alpha_i\ge0,\ \sum_i\alpha_i=1}
\sum_j\sqrt{\sum_{i:j\in S_i}\alpha_i^2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Numerically solving (2.3) was used only for **discovery**. It suggested a twelve-row/twelve-column template value near

\[
2.013131721239\ldots .
\]

I then rounded the primal magnitudes downward with a large safety margin and proved the resulting rational matrix exactly. No numerical optimizer is used in the theorem.
