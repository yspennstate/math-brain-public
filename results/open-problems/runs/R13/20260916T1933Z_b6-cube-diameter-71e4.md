## #18 SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE — one-row bad signs are a Boolean-cube downset

Fix a row `r=(r_1,...,r_6)` and put `a_j=|r_j|`. Each `a_j<=1`, since every matrix column has Euclidean norm at most one. Absorb coordinate signs into the Boolean coloring. For `T subset [6]`, let `s_T` be `-1` on `T` and `+1` off `T`, and put
\[
t_0=3/\sqrt2,\qquad
\mathcal F(r)=\{T\subset[6]:a\cdot s_T>t_0\}.\tag{1}
\]
Every antipodal Boolean sign class with `|r dot epsilon|>t_0` has exactly one positive orientation in `F(r)`, so if `b(r)` is the number of bad antipodal classes then
\[
b(r)=|\mathcal F(r)|.\tag{2}
\]
The family is a downset. If `T,U in F(r)`, then
\[
a\cdot(s_T+s_U)>2t_0.
\]
Coordinates in `T triangle U` cancel, and each matching coordinate contributes at most `2`; hence
\[
a\cdot(s_T+s_U)\le2(6-|T\triangle U|).
\]
Since `t_0>2`,
\[
\boxed{|T\triangle U|\le3\quad\forall T,U\in\mathcal F(r).}\tag{3}
\]
Thus every one-row bad family is a downset of Hamming diameter at most three.

## #17 LOOK FOR YOUR NUMBERS SOMEWHERE ELSE — exact structure at diameter three

Let `F` be a nonempty downset in `2^[6]` with diameter at most three. Since `emptyset in F`, all members have size at most three.

* If a three-set `T` occurs, downset closure gives `2^T`. Any member using a coordinate outside `T` would force an outside singleton at distance four from `T`; therefore `F=2^T`, `b=8`.
* If no two-set occurs, `F` is the empty set plus `k` singleton sets, `0<=k<=6` (rank-one type).
* Otherwise regard the two-sets as graph edges. Two disjoint edges have distance four, so the edge family is pairwise intersecting. Such a simple graph is either a star, or, if it has no common vertex, exactly a triangle. Thus:
  - star type: `q>=1` star edges plus `l` isolated singleton vertices, `q+l<=5`, and `b=2+2q+l`;
  - triangle type: the three triangle edges/vertices plus `l` isolated singleton vertices, `0<=l<=3`, and `b=7+l`.

This classification is complete. It also gives `b<=12`, consistent with Kleitman's classical diameter theorem.

## R13-C11 — convex-mean certificate

**PROVED.** For every such nonempty `F`, with `b=|F|`, there is a probability distribution `p` on `F` such that
\[
c=\mathbb E_p s_T,\qquad \boxed{\|c\|_2^2\le24/b}.\tag{4}
\]

**Three-cube.** Uniform measure on `2^T` gives three zero means and three `+1` means, so `||c||^2=3=24/8`.

**Rank-one.** Uniform measure on the empty set and `k` singletons gives active-coordinate mean `(k-1)/(k+1)` and inactive mean `1`, hence
\[
\|c\|^2=\frac{k(k-1)^2}{(k+1)^2}+6-k,
\]
and exactly
\[
\frac{24}{k+1}-\|c\|^2=-\frac{2(k^2-6k-9)}{(k+1)^2}>0
\quad(0\le k\le6).\tag{5}
\]

**Triangle.** Uniform measure on all `b=7+l` members gives triangle-coordinate mean `(b-6)/b`, isolated-singleton mean `(b-2)/b`, and `3-l` inactive means `1`. Direct simplification gives
\[
\frac{24}{b}-\|c\|^2=\frac{2(9-l^2)}{(l+7)^2}\ge0.\tag{6}
\]

**Star.** Put `k=q+l`. If `q>=l`, give every edge mass `1/(2q)`, every leaf singleton mass `1/k-1/(2q)>=0`, and every isolated singleton mass `1/k`; all other sets receive mass zero. These masses total one. The center mean is zero, every other active mean is `1-2/k`, and there are `5-k` inactive coordinates, so
\[
\|c\|^2=1+4/k.
\]
Since `b=2+q+k<=2+2k`,
\[
24/b\ge12/(k+1)\ge1+4/k
\]
for `1<=k<=5`, the last inequality being `k^2-7k+4<=0`.

If `q<l`, only `(q,l)=(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,3)` occur. Uniform measure on the whole downset gives respectively
\[
( b,\|c\|^2,24/b-\|c\|^2)
=(6,28/9,8/9),
(7,142/49,26/49),
(8,11/4,1/4),
(9,206/81,10/81).
\]
Thus (4) holds in every case.

## #23 DO WHAT THE PROPOSER DID NOT EXPECT — arbitrary rows, not a square extremizer

Apply C11 to the bad family of a row `r`. Every support point satisfies `a dot s_T>t_0`, so its convex mean satisfies `a dot c>t_0`. Cauchy and (4) give
\[
t_0^2<\|a\|_2^2\|c\|_2^2\le\|r\|_2^2\frac{24}{b(r)}.
\]
Since `t_0^2=9/2`, every row with a bad class satisfies the strict charge inequality
\[
\boxed{b(r)<\frac{16}{3}\|r\|_2^2.}\tag{7}
\]

Now let `A` be an arbitrary real `m x 6` matrix with column norms at most one. There are `32` antipodal sign classes. Let `b_i` count the classes bad in row `i`. Counting bad incidences and applying (7) only to rows with `b_i>0`,
\[
I=\sum_i b_i
<\frac{16}{3}\sum_i\|r_i\|_2^2
=\frac{16}{3}\sum_{j=1}^6\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2^2
\le32.\tag{8}
\]
As `I` is an integer, `I<=31`. Therefore the 32 sign classes cannot all be bad in some row. One class has no bad row, and any representative satisfies
\[
\boxed{\|A\varepsilon\|_\infty\le3/\sqrt2}.\tag{9}
\]
This proves R13-C10 for every row dimension `m`.
