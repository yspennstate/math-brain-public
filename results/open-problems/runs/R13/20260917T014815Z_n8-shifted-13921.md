## 3. Method #13 — compress any counterexample to a shifted downset

The previous run left the following desirable `n=8` statement as an unproved structural route. This run proves it.

For `T subseteq [8]`, write

\[
s_T\in\{-1,+1\}^8,
\qquad
(s_T)_j=
\begin{cases}
-1,&j\in T,\\
+1,&j\notin T.
\end{cases}
\]

### R13-C17 — diameter-five convex-mean lemma

Let `F` be a nonempty downset in `2^[8]` of Hamming diameter at most five. Then

\[
\boxed{
|F|\,\operatorname{dist}\!\left(0,\operatorname{conv}\{s_T:T\in F\}\right)^2\le96.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equality is attained by a principal five-dimensional subcube `F=2^D`, `|D|=5`.

The first reduction is exact and conceptual rather than computational.

For a downset, Hamming diameter at most five is equivalent to

\[
|A\cup B|\le5\qquad(A,B\in F).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The forward implication is slightly stronger than just testing `A Delta B`: since `B\A subseteq B` belongs to `F`,

\[
|A\cup B|=|A\triangle(B\setminus A)|\le5.
\]

The reverse implication follows from `|A Delta B|<=|A union B|`.

Now suppose `0` is outside `K_F=conv{s_T:T in F}`. Its distance from zero has the separating representation

\[
d(F)=\max_{\|u\|_2=1}\min_{T\in F}u\cdot s_T>0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

An optimizing separator may be chosen coordinatewise nonnegative. Indeed, if `u_j<0` and `T` contains `j`, downclosure gives `T\{j} in F`, and

\[
u\cdot s_{T\setminus\{j\}}
=u\cdot s_T+2u_j
<u\cdot s_T.
\]

Hence the minimum is already attained among sets omitting `j`. Replacing `u_j` by zero raises that minimum while decreasing `||u||_2`; after renormalization the separating margin increases, contradicting optimality. Thus `u_j>=0`.

Permute coordinates so

\[
0\le u_1\le u_2\le\cdots\le u_8.
\]

Apply the standard `(i,j)` compression (`i<j`) to `F`: if `j in T`, `i notin T`, and `T-j+i` is absent, replace `T` by `T-j+i`. Standard compression preserves cardinality, downclosure and Hamming diameter. For completeness, the diameter claim can be checked directly: if only one of a pair is changed and the other set contains `j` but not `i`, then either the other set is changed too, or its shifted mate already belonged to the old family; in the latter case the new pair's symmetric-difference size equals that of an old admissible pair. All other cases preserve or reduce distance.

Moreover, because `u_i<=u_j`, a replacement decreases `u(T)` and therefore **increases**

\[
u\cdot s_T=\sum_k u_k-2u(T).
\]

So the separator's minimum cannot decrease under compression. Iterating all compressions produces a shifted downset `F^*` with

\[
|F^*|=|F|,\qquad \operatorname{diam}(F^*)\le5,
\qquad d(F^*)\ge d(F).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Therefore it is enough to prove (3.1) for shifted downsets. This is the main use of method #13: the distance-to-convex-hull objective becomes a monovariant under a finite compression process.

## 4. Method #21 — identify the finite Hamming-anticode object

The shifted objects are finite hereditary Hamming anticodes. The exact finite core can be enumerated without enumerating all `2^256` set families.

For a subset `S subseteq [8]`, let `P(S)` be the smallest family containing `S` and closed under deletions and all left shifts `j -> i`, `i<j`. Call it the principal shifted ideal generated by `S`.

Every shifted downset `F` is exactly the union of `P(S)` over `S in F`. If `F` satisfies (3.2), every one of those principal ideals also satisfies (3.2). Conversely, pairwise-union validity is monotone under deleting family members, so incompatible unions can be pruned permanently.

The exact checker therefore:

1. constructs all 256 principal shifted closures;
2. deduplicates them;
3. retains exactly the principal ideals satisfying `|A union B|<=5` internally;
4. branches include/exclude over those ideals, pruning a branch as soon as a cross-pair has union size `>5`;
5. independently re-verifies that every resulting family is shifted, a downset, and satisfies (3.2).

There are exactly

\[
\boxed{74}
\]

valid distinct principal shifted ideals and exactly

\[
\boxed{9612}
\]

shifted downsets satisfying the diameter-five condition.

This uses the classical compression/anticode shape, but the target is not merely Kleitman's cardinality theorem: we need a quantitative **convex-hull distance** bound coupled to family size.

## 5. Method #9 — randomize over facets to build the convex-hull witness

Fix one of the 9612 shifted downsets `F`, let `b=|F|`, and let its inclusion-maximal members (facets) be

\[
M_1,\ldots,M_h.
\]

Write

\[
d_j=|\{r:j\in M_r\}|.
\]

Choose a facet uniformly. Conditional on that facet, retain each coordinate `j` in it with probability

\[
\theta_j=\min\!\left(1,\frac{h}{2d_j}\right)
\]

(with the zero-degree case irrelevant). Because `F` is downward closed, the retained subset is always still in `F`. Its coordinate-inclusion marginal is

\[
p_j=\min\!\left(\frac{d_j}{h},\frac12\right).
\]

Hence the mean sign vector of this distribution is

\[
c_j=1-2p_j
=\left(1-\frac{2d_j}{h}\right)_+.
\tag{5.1}
\]

So this distribution proves (3.1) whenever

\[
Q(F):=b\sum_{j=1}^8\left(1-\frac{2d_j}{h}\right)_+^2\le96.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The exact enumeration finds that (5.2) succeeds for **9611 of the 9612** shifted families. The sole exception has `b=47` and facets, using zero-based coordinates,

\[
\{0,1,2,3\},\{0,1,2,4\},\{0,1,2,5\},\{0,1,2,6\},
\{0,1,7\},\{0,2,7\},\{1,2,7\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Uniform facet sampling gives degree vector

\[
(6,6,6,1,1,1,1,3)
\]

and

\[
Q(F)=\frac{4747}{49}>96.
\]

This is repaired by a different exact facet distribution: weight `1/5` on each of the four four-element facets and `1/15` on each of the three three-element facets. The coordinate coverages become

\[
(14/15,14/15,14/15,1/5,1/5,1/5,1/5,1/5).
\]

Thin every coordinate whose coverage exceeds `1/2` down to marginal `1/2`. The resulting mean sign vector is zero on the first three coordinates and `3/5` on the last five, so

\[
\|c\|_2^2=5\left(\frac35\right)^2=\frac95,
\]

and therefore

\[
|F|\|c\|_2^2
=47\cdot\frac95
=\frac{423}{5}<96.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The principal five-cube has `b=32` and the standard construction gives

\[
\|c\|_2^2=3,
\qquad
b\|c\|_2^2=96,
\]

so the constant in C17 is sharp for this lemma.

Combining the compression step with the exact shifted-family certificates proves C17 for **every** diameter-five downset, not just the 9612 compressed representatives.

## 6. Application to arbitrary-row Boolean Komlos at n=8

Let `A` be an arbitrary real `m x 8` matrix with

\[
\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2\le1
\qquad(j=1,\ldots,8).
\]

Fix one row `r`. Absorb the signs of its coordinates, so write

\[
a_j=|r_j|\in[0,1],
\qquad
S=\|r\|_2^2=\sum_{j=1}^8a_j^2.
\]

At threshold

\[
t=\sqrt6,
\]

define the positively oriented bad family

\[
F_r=\{T\subseteq[8]:a\cdot s_T>\sqrt6\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

It is a downset. If `T,U in F_r`, then

\[
2\sqrt6<a\cdot(s_T+s_U).
\]

Coordinates in `T Delta U` cancel; coordinates in `T cap U` contribute nonpositively; coordinates outside `T union U` contribute at most two each. Consequently

\[
2\sqrt6
<a\cdot(s_T+s_U)
\le2(8-|T\cup U|).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since `sqrt6>2`, (6.2) forces

\[
|T\cup U|\le5.
\]

Thus C17 applies. Let `b(r)=|F_r|`. If `b(r)>0`, C17 gives a probability distribution on `F_r` with mean sign vector `c` satisfying

\[
\|c\|_2^2\le\frac{96}{b(r)}.
\]

Every support point obeys `a dot s_T>sqrt6`, so averaging and Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
6<(a\cdot c)^2
\le S\|c\|_2^2
\le\frac{96S}{b(r)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{b(r)<16\|r\|_2^2.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

After absorbing coordinate signs, `b(r)` is exactly the number of antipodal Boolean signing classes `{epsilon,-epsilon}` spoiled by this row: every bad antipodal class has exactly one orientation with positive row dot product exceeding `sqrt6`.

Sum (6.3) over all rows:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i b(r_i)
&<16\sum_i\|r_i\|_2^2\\
&=16\sum_{j=1}^8\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2^2\\
&\le16\cdot8=128.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

There are exactly `2^(8-1)=128` antipodal Boolean sign classes. The left side of (6.4) is an integer, hence at most 127. Therefore all 128 classes cannot be spoiled, so some signing `epsilon` is good in every row:

\[
\boxed{\|A\epsilon\|_\infty\le\sqrt6.}
\tag{R13-C18}
\]

This is a deterministic, arbitrary-row, full real Boolean theorem for eight columns.

\[

\[

\[
\frac{b_t(r)}{\|r\|_2^2}
