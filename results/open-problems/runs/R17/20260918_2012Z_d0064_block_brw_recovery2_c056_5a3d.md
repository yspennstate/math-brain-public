1. a continuous version on compact sets; and
2. finite-range covariance in sup norm: for some finite `R`,
   \[
   \operatorname{Cov}(\omega(x),\omega(y))=0
   \quad\text{whenever }\|x-y\|_\infty>R.
   \tag{3.1}
   \]

This is a genuine source-valid subclass (stronger than the review's covariance-decay hypothesis), and it is exactly the kind of finite-range specialization selected in C055. No moment of `M` is assumed. In fact, the proof below uses only stationarity and nontriviality of `M`, not ergodicity.

### Proposition 3.1 — moment-free one-scale Malthusian root

Under the two extra finite-range/continuous-version assumptions above, if a nontrivial stationary Radon measure `M` satisfies the lognormal star equation at `epsilon=1/b`, then there exists

\[
\boxed{\alpha_b\in(0,1]}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E e^{\alpha_b\omega_{1/b}(0)}=b^{-d}=\varepsilon^d.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

No positive moment of `M` appears in the statement or proof.

### Proof

#### Step 1. Iterate the **same** star equation exactly

Take independent copies `omega_0,omega_1,...` of `omega_{1/b}`. Repeated use of

\[
M^\varepsilon(A)\stackrel d=M(A/\varepsilon)
\]

constructs, for every `n`, a coupling in which

\[
M(A)\stackrel d=
\int_A \exp\{S_n(x)\}\,M^{(n)}(dx),
\qquad
S_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_k(b^k x),
\tag{3.3}
\]

where `M^{(n)}` is independent of all multiplier fields and

\[
M^{(n)}(A)\stackrel d=M(b^nA).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus a `b^{-n}`-cube in physical coordinates has terminal mass distributed as the mass of a unit cube of an independent stationary copy of `M`.

#### Step 2. Group `L` original scales into one super-step

Put `B=b^L`. On a unit parent cube define the `L`-scale block field

\[
\Xi_L(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\omega_k(b^k y).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Choose an integer

\[
G>R+1
\]

and a digit set `D_L` consisting of those vectors in `{0,...,B-1}^d` whose coordinates are multiples of `G`. Hence

\[
K_L:=|D_L|=B^d G^{-d}\,e^{o(L)},
\qquad
\frac1L\log K_L\longrightarrow d\log b.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For `j in D_L`, let

\[
Q_{L,j}=\frac{j+[0,1]^d}{B}
\]

and define the lower-envelope edge displacement

\[
Z_{L,j}:=\inf_{y\in Q_{L,j}}\Xi_L(y).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Because selected parent indices in the base-`B` tree remain at sup-norm separation at least `G`, restrictions of every Gaussian layer belonging to different selected parents have zero cross-covariance. Gaussianity plus (3.1) therefore makes the reproduction vectors

\[
(Z_{L,j})_{j\in D_L}
\]

**iid from parent to parent**. The coordinates inside one reproduction vector may be dependent; classical BRW/smoothing-transform theory allows that.

So the selected subcubes carry an honest `K_L`-ary branching random walk with edge point process `{Z_{L,j}:j in D_L}`.

#### Step 3. The continuous star integral dominates that BRW with the true terminal masses

After `r` super-steps, every selected leaf `v` corresponds to a disjoint physical cube of side `B^{-r}`. Let

\[
S_v=\sum_{e\prec v} Z_e
\]

be its BRW displacement. By the definition through infima, the full multiplier field on that leaf is everywhere at least `S_v`. Therefore (3.3) gives the exact lower bound in the constructed coupling

\[
M([0,1]^d)
\ \ge\
\sum_{|v|=r} e^{S_v}X_v,
\tag{3.8}
\]

where `X_v` is the terminal mass of the corresponding unit cube after applying (3.4).

Crucially, the family `(X_v)` need **not** be independent. It is only a sampled stationary field of unit-cube masses from an independent copy of `M`, and it is independent of the BRW weights.

Since `M` is stationary and nontrivial, there exist `a>0` and `p>0` such that

\[
\mathbb P(X_v\ge a)=p
\tag{3.9}
\]

for every leaf location.

#### Step 4. Arbitrary dependence of the terminal masses does not kill an exponentially growing BRW partition function

Let

\[
W_r=\sum_{|v|=r}e^{S_v}.
\]

For the iid reproduction point process above, the standard BRW free-energy formula is

\[
F_L=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac1r\log W_r
=
\inf_{0<q\le1}\frac1q\log m_L(q),
\tag{3.10}
\]

with

\[
m_L(q)=\mathbb E\sum_{j\in D_L}e^{qZ_{L,j}}
=K_L\,\mathbb E e^{qZ_L},
\tag{3.11}
\]

where `Z_L` denotes one marginal edge displacement. All needed exponential moments are finite for the continuous Gaussian field on a compact set.

I now prove that `F_L` cannot be positive. Suppose `F_L>0`. Choose a small `eta>0`. Standard exponential-tail bounds for the finite-offspring BRW imply that, with probability tending to one, all leaf displacements lie in `[-Cr,Cr]` for some deterministic `C`, while

\[
W_r\ge e^{(F_L-\eta)r}.
\]

Bin the leaves by `S_v` into intervals of width `eta r`. Only a bounded number of bins occur on the preceding event, so one weights-measurable bin `A_r` contributes at least a fixed fraction of `W_r`. All weights in that bin differ by at most `e^{eta r}`.

Conditional on the multiplier tree, let `N=|A_r|` and

\[
C_r=\sum_{v\in A_r}{\bf1}_{\{X_v\ge a\}}.
\]

No independence among the indicators is used. Stationarity and independence from the multiplier tree give

\[
\mathbb E[C_r\mid\text{multipliers}]=pN,
\qquad 0\le C_r\le N.
\]

Hence the elementary bound

\[
\mathbb P\!\left(C_r\ge \frac p2N\mid\text{multipliers}\right)\ge \frac p2.
\tag{3.12}
\]

On this event, because the weights in the chosen bin are within `e^{eta r}`, the terminal-good contribution to (3.8) is at least

\[
\text{const}\cdot e^{-\eta r}\sum_{v\in A_r}e^{S_v}
\ge
\exp\{(F_L-3\eta)r\}
\]

for all large `r`. Taking `eta<F_L/4` makes the right-hand side diverge. Thus with a fixed positive probability, the right side of (3.8) escapes every finite bound as `r` grows.

But the left side has the same fixed finite Radon-mass law for every `r`, hence is tight. Contradiction. Therefore

\[
\boxed{F_L\le0.}
\tag{3.13}
\]

This is the key place where the discrete smoothing-transform method survives **without iid child masses**: the iid object needed is the multiplier BRW, while the true terminal masses enter only through the one-point positive probability `p` and the deterministic bound (3.12).

#### Step 5. Every block length therefore has a BRW Malthusian root

From (3.10)--(3.13),

\[
\inf_{0<q\le1}\frac1q\log m_L(q)\le0.
\]

Since `m_L(0)=K_L>1` and `q -> m_L(q)` is continuous, there exists

\[
\alpha_L\in(0,1]
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{K_L\,\mathbb E e^{\alpha_L Z_L}=1.}
\tag{3.14}
\]

#### Step 6. The block infimum has the same exponential-scale Laplace transform as one point

Let `y_L` be the center of one selected `B^{-1}`-cube. Then

\[
\Xi_L(y_L)=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\omega_k(b^k y_L)
\]

is a sum of `L` iid copies of `omega(0)`, so for `0<=q<=1`,

\[
\mathbb E e^{q\Xi_L(y_L)}
=\left(\mathbb E e^{q\omega(0)}\right)^L.
\tag{3.15}
\]

Set

\[
\Omega_L=\sup_{y\in Q_{L,j}}|\Xi_L(y)-\Xi_L(y_L)|.
\]

The `k`th Gaussian layer is sampled over diameter `O(b^{k-L})`. Under the exponential tilt by `e^{q\Xi_L(y_L)}`, the layers remain independent Gaussians with the same covariance and only a Cameron--Martin mean shift. Continuity on compacts implies that the expected oscillation over diameter `b^{-s}` tends to zero as `s->infinity`, uniformly for `q in [0,1]`. Therefore a Cesaro sum over `s=1,...,L` gives

\[
\sup_{0\le q\le1}
\mathbb P_q(\Omega_L>\delta L)\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{for every }\delta>0.
\tag{3.16}
\]

Since

\[
\Xi_L(y_L)-\Omega_L\le Z_L\le\Xi_L(y_L),
\]

(3.15)--(3.16) yield the uniform exponential-rate identity

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le q\le1}
\left|
\frac1L\log\mathbb E e^{qZ_L}
-
\log\mathbb E e^{q\omega(0)}
\right|\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{3.17}
\]

The upper bound is immediate. For the lower bound, on `Omega_L<=delta L`, factor the point Laplace transform and use the tilted probability; then let `L->infinity` and `delta->0`.

#### Step 7. Let the block length tend to infinity

Choose a subsequence on which `alpha_L -> alpha_b in [0,1]`. Taking `L^{-1} log` in (3.14), and using (3.6) and (3.17), gives

\[
0=d\log b+\log\mathbb E e^{\alpha_b\omega(0)}.
\]

The limit cannot be `alpha_b=0`, because the right side would be `d log b>0`. Hence `alpha_b in (0,1]`, and

\[
\mathbb E e^{\alpha_b\omega(0)}=b^{-d}.
\]

This proves Proposition 3.1. `square`

### What this does and does not prove

This is a genuine moment-free Malthusian necessity theorem for the continuous star equation in a finite-range Gaussian subclass. It does **not** classify a source regime and it does **not** prove that the exponent is the same for different scales.

I found no checked source stating this exact block-BRW lower-envelope argument. That is **not** a novelty certificate; the mechanism uses standard BRW free energy plus a new-to-this-track embedding, and may be folklore or subsumed by a stronger result I did not locate.

## 4. Method #27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS

The source asks

\[
\exists\alpha\in(0,1]\ \forall\varepsilon:
\quad \mathbb E e^{\alpha\omega_\varepsilon(0)}=\varepsilon^d.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Proposition 3.1 proves, for the finite-range continuous subclass at every reciprocal-integer scale to which it is applied,

\[
\forall b\ge2\ \exists\alpha_b\in(0,1]:
\quad \mathbb E e^{\alpha_b\omega_{1/b}(0)}=b^{-d}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This exposes a clean quantifier gap rather than the previous vague `dependent children` obstruction:

\[
\boxed{\forall b\ \exists\alpha_b\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad\exists\alpha\ \forall b.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The simplest still-unproved special case is now:

> **Two-scale common-root problem.** In `d=1`, assume the same nontrivial stationary star fixed point has continuous finite-range Gaussian multipliers at scales `1/2` and `1/3`. Proposition 3.1 gives roots `alpha_2` and `alpha_3`. Prove `alpha_2=alpha_3`.

That is much smaller than classifying a critical or atomic measure.

There is an immediate conditional closure of this quantifier gap. Suppose, in addition, the one-point multiplier laws form a continuous convolution semigroup in logarithmic scale:

\[
\omega_{\varepsilon\varepsilon'}(0)
\stackrel d=
\omega_\varepsilon(0)+\widetilde\omega_{\varepsilon'}(0),
\qquad\widetilde\omega_{\varepsilon'}\perp\omega_\varepsilon.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then for each `q`,

\[
\kappa_q(t):=\log\mathbb E e^{q\omega_{e^{-t}}(0)}
\]

is continuous and additive, hence `kappa_q(t)=c(q)t`. Applying Proposition 3.1 at one reciprocal-integer scale gives some `alpha` with `c(alpha)=-d`, and therefore

\[
\mathbb E e^{\alpha\omega_\varepsilon(0)}=\varepsilon^d
\]

for **every** `epsilon`.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite-range block-BRW necessity}
+
\text{one-point scale semigroup}
\Longrightarrow
\text{the full exponent gateway (1.1).}
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The catch is exactly source-relevant: Rhodes--Sohier--Vargas derive multiplier composition / infinite-divisibility properties under positive-moment assumptions. In the no-moment regime, obtaining the semigroup directly from equality of random weighted measures is itself nontrivial. I do not assume that step away.

## 5. Method #30 — ONE SCALE / ONE DIRECTION AT A TIME

The decisive device is to group `L` scales before extracting independent spatial branches.

If I selected separated children **at every original scale**, finite-range independence would cost a fixed fraction of children per generation, replacing `b^d` by roughly `b^d/G^d`; that entropy loss is too large to recover the exact source normalization.

Grouping `L` scales first leaves roughly

\[
K_L\asymp \frac{b^{dL}}{G^d}
\]

independent superchildren, so the independence cost is only a **constant once per block**. Dividing logarithms by `L` sends that cost to zero:

\[
\frac1L\log K_L\to d\log b.
\]

At the same time, the infimum over a `b^{-L}` child cube differs from the point block sum by only `o(L)` under the exponentially tilted Gaussian law. These two vanishing errors are what let the exact factor `b^d` reappear in the limit.

This is the main new route from this run. It avoids both of the C055 obstructions:

1. it never declares spatial terminal masses iid; and
2. it does not need a pre-existing positive moment of `M`.
