# 6. Move 18 — translate the problem to weighted automata / Perron supersolutions

## Alternate language

The bounded-span formulation is a random offline packing problem. Lueker’s upper route is better expressed as a **weighted regular-language growth problem**:

- canonical/minimal match-pair lists encode LCS decompositions;
- a finite automaton accepts a cover of all canonical lists;
- each transition contributes a nonnegative `z`-dependent weight;
- the total accepted generating-function mass is controlled by powers of a sparse nonnegative transition operator `A_M(z)`;
- a positive vector `v` with `A_M(z)v <= lambda v` is a Collatz-Wielandt/Perron supersolution, hence an immediately checkable finite upper certificate.

This language preserves the true LCS combinatorics: it is not an independent-cell matching model and does not postulate a product stationary measure.

## Why this is a better fit now

Lueker already proved the two structural pieces R17 had been trying to rebuild indirectly:

1. validity: the accepted canonical language yields a rigorous upper bound;
2. convergence: increasing history can approach the true `gamma_2`.

The modern opportunity is computational/certification rather than another probabilistic lemma: quotient symmetries, sparse state minimization, and a rational or directed-rounding positive supersolution can turn a large floating-point computation into a short verifier.

**Outcome of move 18:** successful route translation. It localizes the missing proof object to a finite sparse weighted automaton plus a positive supersolution certificate.

# 7. Move 20 — identify why the previous algorithmic route fails to be metric-relevant

The `B=1` renewal algorithm succeeded because every source block has reward capacity one; greedily finding the next compatible target bit asymptotically saturates that trivial capacity dual.

For `B>=2`, a source block can trade target span for multiple reward units. Even at `m=2,B=2`, the optimizer can face the choice “take reward 1 now” versus “consume/wait for two target positions and obtain reward 2.” The globally optimal decision depends on future source blocks and future target bits. A single-block renewal decomposition therefore stops being exact.

A natural Lagrange idea is to price target span and optimize each block separately. But pricing only the chosen interval length misses **skipped target gaps**, while pricing total elapsed target time introduces a state/cocycle. For an offline optimum, independent per-block suprema may also use overlapping or anticipatively chosen target regions. Without a telescoping Bellman potential, the would-be dual does not bound the global optimum.

This is the precise reason not to spend recovery episode 2 on `q_{2,2}`. Even a beautiful exact value there would not by itself move the certified interval, and the existing `m/(B+1)` error requires large `B` before the route can be competitive.

Lueker’s weighted automaton supplies exactly the missing telescoping/global upper structure. It already prices all canonical alternatives collectively rather than pretending the offline blocks decouple.

**Outcome of move 20:** the bounded-span route is retained as a valid primal hierarchy but is deprioritized for the next metric-moving upper attack.
