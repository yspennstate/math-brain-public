## 2. #22 — read the failure, not the successful upper-bound proof

The promising side route was the older Dancik--Paterson domination construction. Lueker's 2008 ANALCO paper formulated the binary uniqueness statement as Lemma 3.2 and used it to support convergence of a natural domination-based sequence. The published corrigenda is crucial: it says that the proof of Lemma 3.2 is incorrect and explicitly leaves both that lemma and its dependent theorem open.

The later JACM paper states the surviving open statement cleanly as Conjecture 3.19:

> Over a binary alphabet, no string pair is generated by two distinct undominated lists of minimal match pairs.

In Lueker's terminology, if a collation of order `ell` has cumulative total sizes `s_1,...,s_ell`, its key is the reverse tuple

\[
(s_\ell,s_{\ell-1},\ldots,s_1),
\]

and among collations of the same order generating the same pair, lexicographically smaller key dominates larger key. A list of minimal match pairs is undominated when the corresponding collation obtained by appending the empty pair is undominated.

This was not a theorem to reuse. The corrigenda made attacking its negation the correct move.

## 3. #2 — explicit binary counterexample

Consider the binary string pair

\[
\boxed{P=\binom{110110}{0010110}.}
\tag{C029.1}
\]

It is generated by the following two distinct order-four lists of minimal match pairs:

\[
\boxed{
L=
\binom{1}{001}
\binom{1}{01}
\binom{01}{1}
\binom{10}{0},
}
\tag{C029.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
L'=
\binom{110}{0}
\binom{1}{01}
\binom{1}{01}
\binom{0}{10}.
}
\tag{C029.3}
\]

Concatenating top and bottom strings in either list gives exactly (C029.1).

Every displayed factor is a minimal match pair. Directly, each factor has LCS length one, its two final characters agree, and deleting either final character destroys all common matches. Equivalently, each has one of the binary hook forms

\[
\binom{c}{\bar c^{\,r}c}
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
\binom{\bar c^{\,r}c}{c}.
\]

The four factor sizes in **both** lists are

\[
4,3,3,3.
\]

Therefore their cumulative sizes are

\[
4,7,10,13
\]

and their reverse collation keys are identical:

\[
\boxed{K(L)=K(L')=(13,10,7,4).}
\tag{C029.4}
\]

The exact checker below enumerates, from the original LCS/essential-match definition rather than the hook shortcut, **all** order-four minimal-match decompositions of `P`. There are exactly two: (C029.2) and (C029.3). Thus there is no same-order collation generating `P` with a lexicographically smaller key. Both lists are undominated.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\text{Lueker Conjecture 3.19 is false.}}
\tag{C029.5}
\]

The counterexample is stronger than needed for the JACM conjecture. The full pair satisfies

\[
\operatorname{LCS}(110110,0010110)=5,
\]

while deleting the final top or final bottom symbol gives LCS length `4` in either case. Thus the pair ends on an essential match in Lueker's sense. Consequently the same witness also refutes the stronger binary Lemma 3.2 from the 2008 ANALCO version, the statement whose proof the corrigenda had withdrawn.

## 5. Consequence for the natural domination machine

The JACM construction forms `D_h` from minimally dominated lists of total size at most `h` and makes the domination machine reject whenever such a list appears as a contiguous sublist. An undominated list cannot contain a dominated contiguous sublist: replacing such a sublist by its dominator preserves the generated full pair and makes the corresponding block of the full reverse key lexicographically smaller, which would dominate the full list.

Hence both `L` and `L'` are accepted by the natural domination machine for every `h>=13`. They generate the same string pair `P` of size 13. Definition 3.12(2) of Lueker's convergence criterion requires that no pair of size at most `h` be generated by two distinct accepted lists. Thus the explicit witness shows

\[
\boxed{\text{the proposed domination sequence fails Definition 3.12(2) for every }h\ge13.}
\tag{C029.6}
\]

This does **not** prove that every domination-based upper-bound scheme fails to converge, nor does it affect the proved convergence of Lueker's canonicity-based machines. It kills this particular conjectured injectivity route and prevents R17 from spending more runs trying to prove a false statement.

## 6. Why the collision occurs and what to try next

The two lists have exactly the same scalar cumulative-size history while moving their cuts through different top/bottom coordinates:

\[
(1,3),(2,5),(4,6),(6,7)
\]

versus

\[
(3,1),(4,3),(5,5),(6,7).
\]

The scalar domination key remembers only `i+j`; it forgets the imbalance `i-j`. C029 therefore identifies a concrete information-loss mechanism, not just an isolated numerical failure.
