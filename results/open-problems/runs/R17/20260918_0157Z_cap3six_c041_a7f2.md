## 4. Why the new forbidden words preserve covering

Let a saturated canonical collation for a physical pair `P` have order

\[
\ell=\operatorname{LCS}(P).
\]

If it contained a contiguous block of `r` designated minimal-match factors whose generated physical substring pair had LCS at least `r+1`, replace those `r` designated matches by the longer local common subsequence, retaining all designated matches before and after the block. The factor boundaries preserve the order of the surrounding matches, so this produces a common subsequence of `P` of length at least `\ell+1`, a contradiction.

Hence no saturated LCS collation contains a locally deficient block. Deleting any collection of such physical blocks, including the 147,576 exact six-factor words above, therefore preserves at least one covering saturated collation for every pair.
