## 1. (a) Exact statement that blocks

The cleanest exact blocker remains the C071 threshold/downshadow formulation. For a finite percolation instance, let

\[
C=\{o\leftrightarrow B\},\qquad C_b=\{o\leftrightarrow b\}.
\]

For a decreasing set `F` containing `C^c`, define

\[
\operatorname{Sh}_b(F):=\downarrow(F\cap C_b).
\]

The unresolved full-source statement is

\[
\boxed{
T_*(\delta):=
\sup_{G,p,o,B}
\sup_{\substack{F\downarrow,\ C^c\subseteq F\\
\mu_p(\operatorname{Sh}_b(F))\le\delta\ \forall b\in B}}
\frac{\mu_p(F\cap C)}{\mu_p(C)}
\xrightarrow[\delta\downarrow0]{}0.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

C071 already established that controlling this threshold problem is equivalent, at the qualitative level needed here, to the original failure-kernel problem. Nothing in the later recovery work controls (1.1) uniformly over arbitrary nonplanar finite graphs.

The same obstruction appears in C074's contact language. If

\[
S=\{b:o\leftrightarrow b\},\qquad N=|S|,\qquad
m=\min_{b\in S}q_b,
\]

then the all-factorial Harris inequalities constrain failure-weighted contact moments, and the exact lower-tail reduction gives

\[
R\le \Pr(N\le a\,\mathbb E[N\mid C]\mid C)+\frac{\varepsilon}{a}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

But no dimension-free connectivity theorem currently forces the first term in (1.2) to vanish in the full source class. C075 shows that recursive branching can in fact put much more failure mass on thin-contact configurations than stars do, so simply strengthening a concentration estimate for `N` without using more geometry is not a credible universal route.

## 2. (b) Routes tried and the precise reason each stopped

This is the consolidated obstruction map; it is intentionally not another lemma list.

### R7-02: exact failure-kernel reduction and bounded contact

Auxiliary randomness was eliminated exactly by decreasing kernels `q_b` with `E q_b<=epsilon`, yielding

\[
R=\frac{\mathbb E[1_C\min_{b\in S}q_b]}{\Pr(C)}.
\]

Harris--FKG gives `R<=epsilon E[N|C]`. This solves bounded-contact classes but fails when conditional contact count diverges. The natural monotone partition-of-unity selector was then shown impossible already on a star: support plus monotonicity forces total mass to grow with the number of arms.

### C069: symmetry and complete star optimization

Automorphism averaging legitimately reduces symmetric candidate families. The full variational problem on stars was solved and has sharp scale `epsilon log(1/epsilon)`. This is useful counterexample information but stars have completely separated arms; they contain no shared interior edge capable of changing many boundary labels at once. The source-wide blocker is precisely shared connectivity.

### C070: LP/fractional-selector dual and independent arms

The failure problem was dualized to a fractional-selector discrepancy problem. Heterogeneous independent boundary membership, root-neighborhood decorations, and related separated classes are controlled. The dual does not solve its own construction problem: on a general graph, a single edge can merge components or create many boundary contacts, so local selector cost can reappear multiplied by fanout.

### C071: downshadows, union-closed contact support, and Boolean-boundary charging

The threshold problem (1.1) isolated the exact combinatorial core. Connectivity contact sets are union-closed, excluding the most obvious arbitrary-increasing-event counterexample support. Every Boolean exit from a bad downset can be charged either to a rare-kernel boundary or to a connectivity pivotal. The route stops because neither charge is dimension-free: a tiny downset may have large total influence, while one pivotal edge can create many boundary labels. Serial-stem / articulation-separated modules avoid this by construction but do not model unrestricted cycles.

### C072--C073: conditioned-slack port and cluster-local envelope

The nearest mechanically checked finite-graph gluing machinery suggested a relay-peeling hierarchy. For observables determined by the boundary vertex's open cluster, C073 could collapse all labels into one increasing max-envelope and obtain a strong restricted result. The attempted extension by conditioning an arbitrary increasing observable on the cluster is invalid: even on a two-edge path, that projection can reverse monotonicity. Hence the remaining information is genuinely non-cluster-local edge information.

### C074: contact-size interpolation and factorial Harris hierarchy

The exact change of measure between connection-Palm and uniform-contact laws is `M/N`, and all factorial contact moments contract by `epsilon` under failure weighting. This sharply identifies inverse-contact-size bias. It does not supply a universal lower-tail inequality for `N`, and the intermediate `Q_theta` laws are not product measures, so ordinary Harris--FKG gives no signed covariance control for `Cov(q_b,log N)`.

### C075: deterministic priorities and recursive branching

Random-priority contact selection derandomizes exactly to a uniform connected label, but applying Harris to a deterministic priority order loses exactly the same `E N` factor as the elementary bound. The recursive `d`-ary tree-spine construction then showed that branching can make the quantitative modulus substantially worse than the star scale.

## 3. (c) What a full proof must contain that no tried route contains

Every successful partial route avoids the same phenomenon: **adaptive label switching through shared nonplanar connectivity while the label's failure kernel may inspect unrevealed global edges**.

A full proof needs a dimension-free mechanism with all three properties below simultaneously.

1. **Adaptive ownership without multiplicity.** On each connected configuration it must assign, fractionally or procedurally, enough mass to one or more connected boundary labels without paying `|B|`, `E N`, edge fanout, or total influence.

2. **Conditional monotonicity survives revelation.** After the exploration chooses or changes a boundary label, the relevant failure observable must remain in a class to which an FKG-type inequality applies under the revealed stopping sigma-field. Ordinary cluster projection fails this property; ordinary fixed priorities preserve monotonicity but pay contact multiplicity.

3. **Closure under shared-component mergers.** When one newly opened edge merges components and simultaneously makes many boundary labels connected, the error term created at that step must stay in the same inductive class instead of spawning one term per new label. This is the precise feature supplied by planar left/right ordering in the source's solved case and absent from all current nonplanar attempts.

Equivalently in the downshadow language, a proof must turn the local hypotheses

\[
\mu_p(\downarrow(F\cap C_b))\le\delta\quad\forall b
\]

into a global estimate on `mu_p(F cap C)` **without summing those local bounds over `b` or over pivotal edges**. None of C069--C075 contains such a mechanism.

What the unrestricted problem lacks is exactly the planar order that makes “first relevant boundary contact” stable. There is no canonical left/right separator preventing later information from changing the ownership decision.

### van den Berg--Haggstrom--Kahn conditional cluster inequalities

Their 2006 Random Structures & Algorithms paper proves strong conditional association statements for percolation clusters, including positive self-association of the open cluster of `s` under certain no-connection conditioning and negative correlation with another cluster. This confirms that nontrivial conditional FKG structures survive some connectivity conditioning.

What it does not provide here is an inequality for an **adaptively selected member of a family of arbitrary global decreasing kernels** `q_b`. The label itself depends on the random connectivity pattern, and C073 already showed that projecting a global monotone observable down to the selected cluster can destroy monotonicity. So the available cluster-correlation theorem does not close the label-switching step.

## 8. Method #17 — LOOK FOR YOUR NUMBERS SOMEWHERE ELSE

The `K_{3,n}` percolation state has a standard neighboring language: a **bipartite / random-intersection representation**. Each `b_i` independently chooses a random subset of the three left hubs via its open incident edges; left hubs become connected when these random subsets overlap them. Random-intersection-graph literature explicitly uses such bipartite incidence representations to study connectivity and phase transitions. For example, Blackburn--Gerke (2008/2009) study connectivity of uniform random intersection graphs, and van der Hofstad--Komjathy--Vadon (2021/2022) analyze phase transitions through an underlying bipartite representation.

The useful import is conceptual: **condition on the finite left-core connectivity state and treat the boundary columns as iid incidence objects**, rather than explore the nonplanar graph edge-by-edge.
