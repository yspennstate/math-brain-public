## 3. #5 — add log-fugacity parameters and differentiate

Write

\[
p=\log x,
\qquad q=\log y,
\qquad
\Phi(p,q)=\log\rho\!\left(A_{e^p,e^q}\right),
\]

where `A_{x,y}` is exactly the true-dependent-string h=8 covering matrix from C027. The C027 upper functional is

\[
B(p,q)=\frac{\log4+p+q}{\Phi(p,q)}.
\tag{C028.1}
\]

The useful new coordinates are

\[
s=p+q=\log(xy),
\qquad
d=\frac{p-q}{2}.
\tag{C028.2}
\]

The numerator of (C028.1) depends only on `s`; the imbalance `d` acts only through the spectral radius. This separates the two-dimensional minimax into a scale variable and an imbalance variable.

### Theorem R17-C028-A — fixed-product convexity

For the finite-history matrix `A_{x,y}`, the function

\[
(p,q)\longmapsto \Phi(p,q)=\log\rho(A_{e^p,e^q})
\]

is convex on the admissible domain `p,q<log(1/2)`. Consequently, for every fixed `s`,

\[
d\longmapsto
\Phi\!\left(\frac s2+d,\frac s2-d\right)
\tag{C028.3}
\]

is convex. Because `log 4+s<0` and `Phi<0` on a valid upper-bound point, minimizing the C027 upper functional at fixed product `xy=e^s` is **equivalent** to minimizing the spectral radius along the one-dimensional convex function (C028.3).

### Proof

Every non-boundary transition weight is a monomial in `x,y`; in `(p,q)` coordinates it is `exp(ap+bq)`, hence log-affine. The two clipped infinite-tail weights are

\[
\frac{e^{8p+q}}{1-e^p},
\qquad
\frac{e^{p+8q}}{1-e^q}.
\]

For the first,

\[
\log\frac{e^{8p+q}}{1-e^p}
=8p+q-\log(1-e^p),
\]

and

\[
\frac{d^2}{dp^2}\big[-\log(1-e^p)\big]
=\frac{e^p}{(1-e^p)^2}>0.
\]

Thus every individual transition weight is log-convex in `(p,q)`. A matrix entry is a finite sum of such weights; Hölder's inequality shows that a finite sum of log-convex functions is log-convex. Kingman's 1961 log-convexity theorem for nonnegative matrices then gives log-convexity of the spectral radius, i.e. convexity of `Phi`.

Fixing `s` restricts a convex function to an affine line, proving (C028.3). Finally, for fixed `s`, put `N=log4+s<0`. Since

\[
\frac{d}{d\Phi}\frac{N}{\Phi}
=-\frac{N}{\Phi^2}>0,
\]

minimizing `B=N/Phi` is exactly minimizing `Phi`. QED.

This use of matrix log-convexity is **not claimed as a new general theorem**. The standard source is J. F. C. Kingman, *A Convexity Property of Positive Matrices*, Quarterly Journal of Mathematics 12 (1961), 283–284. The R17 contribution here is the way it interacts with C027's two-fugacity LCS covering matrix.

## 4. #12 — the conserved quantity is zero Perron imbalance drift

At a point where the dominant Perron root is simple, let `v,w>0` be right and left Perron vectors, normalized arbitrarily. Decompose the matrix into individual transition contributions `a_e(p,q)` from state `i(e)` to state `j(e)` and define

\[
\mu(e)
=
\frac{w_{i(e)}a_e(p,q)v_{j(e)}}
{\rho(A)\,w^Tv}.
\tag{C028.4}
\]

Since `w^TAv=rho w^Tv`, `mu` is a probability distribution over Perron-weighted transition contributions. Standard eigenvalue differentiation gives

\[
\partial_p\Phi
=
\mathbf E_\mu[\partial_p\log a_e],
\qquad
\partial_q\Phi
=
\mathbf E_\mu[\partial_q\log a_e].
\tag{C028.5}
\]

For a finite offset transition, these logarithmic derivatives are exactly the two consumed string lengths. For the positive clipped tail

\[
a_e=\frac{x^8y}{1-x},
\]

one has

\[
\partial_p\log a_e
=8+\frac{x}{1-x},
\qquad
\partial_q\log a_e=1,
\tag{C028.6}
\]

which is precisely the geometrically weighted mean consumed top length in that infinite tail; the negative tail is analogous.

Along the fixed-product coordinate `d`,

\[
\frac{d}{dd}\Phi\!\left(\frac s2+d,\frac s2-d\right)
=\partial_p\Phi-\partial_q\Phi.
\tag{C028.7}
\]

Hence every differentiable interior minimizer satisfies the balance law

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf E_\mu[L_X^{\rm eff}]
=
\mathbf E_\mu[L_Y^{\rm eff}].
}
\tag{C028.8}
\]

Without differentiability, convexity gives the correct global replacement `0 in partial Phi_s(d)`. Thus the right imbalance parameter is not found by blind two-dimensional gridding: it is the zero-drift point of a one-dimensional convex Perron problem.

At a differentiable interior optimum in both `p` and `q`, differentiating (C028.1) further gives

\[
\partial_p\Phi=\partial_q\Phi

\frac{\Phi}{\log4+p+q}

\frac1B.
\tag{C028.9}
\]

So the full stationary condition says the effective Perron consumption in each coordinate is `1/B`, and the total is `2/B`. This recovers the scalar elasticity equation from the preceding R17 work as the sum of two coordinate balance equations.
