## 3. Required stuck report (a): the exact statement that blocks

The sharpest first blocker is no longer “remove the `1+delta` moment assumption.” It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Given an arbitrary nontrivial ergodic good lognormal star fixed point }M,
\text{ prove that a common }\alpha\in(0,1]\text{ satisfying (2.1) exists.}
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

C054 supplied a positive-Laplace observable that would detect such an exponent if regular variation were already known. For a nonzero compactly supported `f>=0`,

\[
\Phi_f(q):=-\log\mathbb E e^{-qM(f)},
\qquad
 a_f(q):=\frac{q\Phi_f'(q)}{\Phi_f(q)}\in[0,1].
\]

A sufficient route to (3.1) would be to prove a common small-`q` regular-variation law

\[
\Phi_f(q)=c(f)\,q^\alpha L(1/q)(1+o(1)),\qquad q\downarrow0,
\tag{3.2}
\]

with the same exponent `alpha` for all admissible nonzero tests `f`, and then transfer the star equation to the multiplier balance (2.1). C054 did **not** prove (3.2), and I do not claim it here.

### This run, method #21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE

The closest solved architecture is the discrete smoothing transform

\[
X\ \stackrel d=\ \sum_i T_iX_i,
\tag{4.2}
\]

with independent branch copies `X_i`. In the one-dimensional theory, the Malthusian function

\[
m(s)=\mathbb E\sum_i T_i^s
\]

controls existence; under the standard hypotheses, a nontrivial fixed point forces an `alpha in (0,1]` with `m(alpha)=1`, and fixed-point Laplace transforms are regularly varying at zero. The critical multivariate theory likewise uses `m(alpha)=1`, a Markov-renewal/branching representation, and a derivative martingale.

This is almost exactly the shape of Conjecture 23. If a scale step of the continuous star equation could be replaced by `epsilon^{-d}` independent branches with weights `e^{omega_epsilon}`, the balance `m(alpha)=1` would read

\[
\varepsilon^{-d}\,\mathbb E e^{\alpha\omega_\varepsilon(0)}=1,
\]

which is precisely (2.1).

But the replacement is **not legitimate as stated**. The star equation is a continuous weighted integral. Even if the Gaussian multiplier has finite-range or rapidly decaying dependence, the rescaled random measure contributes correlated masses to different spatial blocks. Independent Bernoulli or independent-branch substitutions would change the model, just as the old LCS track could not replace its dependent match array by Bernoulli matching. The missing theorem is an approximate-branching/renewal result whose error is controlled under the source's actual decorrelation assumptions.

## 5. Required stuck report (c): what a proof must contain that no route yet contains

## 7. Required stuck report (e): nearest solved problem and the ingredient D0064 lacks

The nearest solved problem is the fixed-point theory of the one-dimensional smoothing transformation, together with its multivariate critical extension. Kolesko--Mentemeier summarize the one-dimensional necessity theorem: under the standard nonlattice/moment hypotheses, a nontrivial fixed point forces `m(alpha)=1`, `m'(alpha)<=0` for some `alpha in (0,1]`; its Laplace transform has a regularly varying small-argument asymptotic. Their critical multivariate theorem then derives uniqueness/tails using a weighted branching process, many-to-one transform, Markov renewal theory, and a derivative martingale.

What that proof has and D0064 lacks is not “more moments” in the abstract. It has an **actual independent branching tree**:

\[
X=\sum_iT_iX_i,\qquad X_i\text{ iid and independent of the weights}.
\]

The continuous star equation gives independence of the fresh multiplier field from the rescaled measure, but not iid independence of the masses of all spatial subblocks inside that rescaled measure. The source's decay assumptions must be converted into a substitute for the branching renewal theorem. That is the structural gap.
