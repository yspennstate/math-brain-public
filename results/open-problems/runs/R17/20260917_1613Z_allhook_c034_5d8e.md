## 2. #16 — exact classification of every deficient two-factor word

Write `bar(c)=1-c`. Lueker's minimal-match-pair label `(c,l)` generates

\[
M(c,l)=
\begin{cases}
(\bar c^{\,l}c,\ c),&l\ge0,\\
(c,\ \bar c^{-l}c),&l<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{C034.1}
\]

A two-factor word is **deficient** when the pair it generates has LCS strictly larger than its factor order two.

### Lemma C034.1 — complete length-two deficiency classification

For two labels `(c_1,l_1),(c_2,l_2)`, their concatenation is deficient if and only if

\[
\boxed{c_1=c_2,\qquad l_1l_2<0,\qquad |l_1|\ge2,\ |l_2|\ge2.}
\tag{C034.2}
\]

More precisely, in the deficient case the local LCS is

\[
\operatorname{LCS}=1+\min(|l_1|,|l_2|),
\]
so the deficiency is `min(|l_1|,|l_2|)-1`.

**Proof.** First suppose `c_1=c_2=c` and the signs agree. Then one of the two generated strings has length exactly two, hence the LCS is at most two; the two designated terminal matches show it is exactly two.

Now take opposite signs, say `l_1=a>0`, `l_2=-b<0`. The generated pair is

\[
(\bar c^a c c,\ c\bar c^b c).
\tag{C034.3}
\]

Put `m=min(a,b)`. The subsequence `bar(c)^m c` is common, so the LCS is at least `m+1`. Conversely, a common subsequence using both `c`'s cannot use any `bar(c)`: all `bar(c)` symbols occur before both `c`'s in the first string but between the two `c`'s in the second. A common subsequence using at most one `c` contains at most `m` copies of `bar(c)`, so its length is at most `m+1`. Hence the LCS is `max(2,m+1)`, which exceeds two exactly when `m>=2`. The reversed sign order is symmetric.

Finally suppose `c_1!=c_2`. If the signs agree, one generated side again has length two. If the signs are opposite, after renaming symbols the pair has the form

\[
(\bar c^a c\bar c,\ c^{b+1}\bar c)
\]

or its transpose. One string has only one `c` and the other only one `bar(c)`, in the relevant order, so no common subsequence can have length three; the designated matches give length two. This proves (C034.2). QED.

The exact finite regression enumerates all `30^2=900` h=8 representative label pairs (`c in {0,1}`, `-7<=l<=7`) and independently recomputes each LCS by integer dynamic programming. It finds exactly

The h=8 tail labels `l=+7` and `l=-7` aggregate actual lengths at least seven. Criterion (C034.2) depends only on the sign and on whether the length is at least two, so every constituent of either tail receives the same safe forbidden-factor classification.
