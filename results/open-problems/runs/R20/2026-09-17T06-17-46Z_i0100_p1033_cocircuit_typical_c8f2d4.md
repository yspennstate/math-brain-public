# 3. Method #8 — discretize the continuous zonotope obstruction exactly

The polar problem (2.2) looks continuous. It is in fact a finite oriented-matroid problem.

## Theorem R20-DISC1 — cocircuit discretization of the zonotope radius

Let `v_0,...,v_{m-1}` lie in a real vector space, and after quotienting by the common nullspace of the positive-weight generators suppose their span has dimension `r` and `v_0` is nonzero. For nonnegative weights `a_j`, define

\[
\rho(a)=
\inf_{\langle y,v_0\rangle=1}
\sum_{j\ne0}a_j|\langle y,v_j\rangle|.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then an optimizer may be chosen at a vertex of the hyperplane arrangement

\[
\langle y,v_j\rangle=0,
\qquad j\ne0,\ a_j>0.
\]

Equivalently, there is a set `J` of `r-1` indices whose corresponding restrictions are independent together with `v_0`, such that the chosen optimizer `y_J` is uniquely determined by

\[
\langle y_J,v_0\rangle=1,
\qquad
\langle y_J,v_j\rangle=0\quad(j\in J),
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\rho(a)=
\min_{J\in\mathfrak C}
\sum_{j\ne0}a_j|\langle y_J,v_j\rangle|,
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

where `C` is the finite set of arrangement bases that occur after quotienting the zero-cost recession space.

### Proof

Restrict to the affine hyperplane

\[
P=\{y:\langle y,v_0\rangle=1\}.
\]

The objective in (3.1) is a proper closed convex piecewise-linear function. Quotienting the directions annihilated by every positive-weight functional removes its zero-cost recession space. In the quotient, every sublevel set is a bounded polyhedron, hence the minimum is attained.

On each cell of the arrangement cut out by the signs of `\langle y,v_j\rangle`, the objective is linear. If an optimum lies in a positive-dimensional cell, the linear objective is constant in some feasible direction or has a descent direction. A descent direction contradicts optimality; in the constant case move to the boundary without changing the objective. Iterating reaches a zero-dimensional cell of the quotient arrangement. Since `P` has dimension `r-1`, at least `r-1` independent coordinate hyperplanes are active there. Choosing an independent subfamily gives (3.2), and minimizing over the finite set of such vertices gives (3.3). QED.

### Consequence for I0100

The odd gluing obstruction no longer requires minimizing over a continuum. It is enough to control the **fundamental cocircuits** of the Paley odd Fourier evaluation configuration. Each candidate is a trigonometric polynomial with `f_0=1` and many forced coordinate zeros; by Cramer's rule its nonzero values are ratios of Fourier minors. This unifies the preceding zonotope route with the earlier determinant/oriented-matroid route: they are the same obstruction seen from the primal and polar sides.

For the especially clean subfamily in which `m` is prime, Tao's prime-cyclic uncertainty theorem / Chebotarev full-spark theorem says that any nonzero function with Fourier support `K` obeys

\[
|\operatorname{supp}f|+|K|\ge m+1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus a cocircuit at the dimension threshold has physical support of order `m-|K|+1\sim m/2`. This is a useful complete weaker reduction, but support cardinality alone does not control its weighted `l1` mass.

The 2025--2026 extensions of Chebotarev-type nonsingularity to some composite cyclic orders (Loukaki; Emmrich--Kunis; related principal-minor work) are relevant to selected arithmetic subfamilies, but they do not give a quantitative lower bound on the Paley Fourier minors strong enough to settle (1.1).

# 5. Method #28 — replace worst-case Fourier coefficients by their exact typical scale

The previous Jacobi argument bounded every nontrivial projection-kernel coefficient by the Weil scale. That is a worst-case statement. The exact average scale is smaller by a factor of two.

Let `K subset Z_m` be the Paley odd-frequency set from the preceding report, let

\[
d=|K|/m,
\]

and let `P_K` be the orthogonal projection onto the corresponding `m`-point Fourier subspace. Write its circulant kernel as

\[
(P_K)_{xy}=c_{x-y},
\qquad
c_t=\frac1m\sum_{k\in K}e^{2\pi i kt/m}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Theorem R20-TYP1 — exact off-diagonal energy and exceptional-set bound

One has

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t\ne0}|c_t|^2=d(1-d).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently, for every `L>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\left\{t\ne0:|c_t|>\frac{L}{\sqrt m}\right\}
\le
\frac{m\,d(1-d)}{L^2}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Since `P_K` is an orthogonal projection,

\[
(P_K^2)_{00}=(P_K)_{00}=d.
\]

But

\[
(P_K^2)_{00}=\sum_t |(P_K)_{0t}|^2=\sum_t|c_t|^2,
\]

and `c_0=d`. Subtracting `d^2` proves (5.2). Markov's inequality applied to the nonnegative numbers `|c_t|^2` gives (5.3). QED.

For the Paley `K`, the preceding Jacobi calculation gives

\[
d=\frac12+O(m^{-1/2}).
\]

Therefore the **RMS** nontrivial kernel coefficient is

\[
\left(\frac{d(1-d)}{m-1}\right)^{1/2}
=
\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)m^{-1/2},
\tag{5.4}
\]

whereas the uniform Jacobi/Weil estimate used in R20-ODD1 is roughly

\[
\max_{t\ne0}|c_t|\le(1+o(1))m^{-1/2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

So the worst-case kernel coefficient is allowed to be twice the RMS scale. This precisely explains the factor lost by the previous one-point proof.

At the source-relevant intermediate threshold

\[
|c_t|>\frac1{\sqrt{2m}},
\]

(5.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
|B_p|\le 2m\,d(1-d)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)m.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus at least half of the coordinates lie below the `1/sqrt(2m)` scale needed for the source constant.

## Why “typical” does not yet close the problem

For `f in V_K` with `f_0=1`, the projection identity gives

\[
(1-d)
\le
\sum_{t\ne0}|c_t|\,|f_t|.
\tag{5.7}
\]

A typical vector in the half-dimensional Fourier space spreads its mass across coordinates where `|c_t|` is near the RMS scale, and then there is ample constant-factor room. But R20-DISC1 says the polar infimum in (2.2) is attained by an **extreme cocircuit**, not by a typical vector. The obstruction may deliberately concentrate on the exceptional high-`|c_t|` coordinates.

The prime-`m` uncertainty theorem shows why pure support counting is exactly borderline. A nonzero `f` with Fourier support `K` has

\[
|\operatorname{supp} f|
\ge m-|K|+1
=
\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)m,
\tag{5.8}
\]

while the exceptional set in (5.6) may also have size `(1/2+o(1))m`. Therefore Chebotarev/Tao support uncertainty by itself cannot force quantitative `l1` mass onto the good half. One needs a **quantitative cocircuit anti-concentration theorem**, i.e. control of Fourier-minor ratios or of the mass of every fundamental cocircuit on the low-kernel half.

This is substantially narrower than full Paley ETF/RIP. It asks only about the finite family of extremal separators selected by the one-localization theta cone.

The three methods therefore meet at one precise new target:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Prove that every Paley odd fundamental cocircuit with }f_0=1
\text{ places enough weighted }\ell_1\text{ mass outside }B_p.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

A theorem of this form, coupled to an even-feasible outer measure `a`, would upgrade the typical/RMS constant in (5.4) to the sharp radial-depth constant needed in (2.1).

### Precise obstruction

The source-level upper half now reduces to a quantitative statement about **extreme Paley Fourier cocircuits**, not generic vectors:

- cardinality uncertainty says a cocircuit has support about `m/2`;
