# 3. Method #1, second pass — change the category to a scattered zero-autocorrelation extremal problem

Write

\[
H=G_{p,1}=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z_n,S),
\qquad n=(p-1)/2,
\]

where `S=-S` is the localized Paley edge-difference set. Let

\[
R_z(t)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_n}z_j\overline{z_{j+t}}
\]

be periodic autocorrelation. Then the exact circulant theta dual from the previous R20 runs has the following spectral-factor form.

## Theorem R20-ZCZ1 — exact coherent-gain/autocorrelation formulation

Let

\[
T_p:=\vartheta(\overline{G_{p,1}}).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
T_p=
\min_{\substack{z\in\mathbb C^n\\ \sum_jz_j\ne0\\ R_z(s)=0\ \forall s\in S}}
\frac{n\|z\|_2^2}{\left|\sum_jz_j\right|^2}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, the maximum coherent processing gain among periodic sequences whose autocorrelation vanishes at every Paley edge lag is

\[
\boxed{
\max \frac{|\sum_jz_j|^2}{\|z\|_2^2}=\frac n{T_p}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Use the nonunitary DFT

\[
Z_m=\sum_jz_j e^{-2\pi imj/n}.
\]

The exact circulant theta dual can be written

\[
T_p=\min\left\{\sum_mq_m:
q_m\ge0,\ q_0=1,\ \check q(s)=0\ (s\in S)\right\},
\tag{3.3}
\]

where `check q` is the inverse DFT. Given a feasible `q`, choose arbitrary phases and set `|Z_m|^2=q_m`; inverse Fourier transform gives `z`. Wiener--Khinchin gives

\[
R_z(s)=\frac1n\sum_m|Z_m|^2e^{2\pi ims/n}=\check q(s),
\]

while Parseval and the zero-frequency identity give

\[
\sum_mq_m=n\|z\|_2^2,
\qquad
q_0=|Z_0|^2=\left|\sum_jz_j\right|^2.
\]

After homogeneous rescaling, (3.1) follows. Conversely any `z` satisfying the autocorrelation zero conditions yields a feasible nonnegative power spectrum `q_m=|Z_m|^2` after normalizing `q_0` to one. QED.

This is not a claim that spectral factorization or zero-autocorrelation sequences are new. Finite cyclic positive-definite support problems belong to classical Caratheodory--Fejer/Turan theory; Krenedits--Revesz explicitly treat finite cyclic Caratheodory--Fejer-type problems. The signal-processing ZCZ literature likewise studies periodic zero-correlation constraints. What is special here is the **scattered arithmetic half-density Paley zero set together with maximization of DC/coherent gain**. The ZCZ constructions located in the bounded search use contiguous zero zones and/or families of sequences and therefore do not directly solve (3.1).

A useful route diagnosis falls out immediately. If `A` is a clique of `H`, then the shifts `{tau_a z:a in A}` are pairwise orthogonal for every feasible `z`, because all nonzero differences from `A-A` lie in `S`. Bessel's inequality against the constant vector gives

\[
|A|\,\left|\sum_jz_j\right|^2
\le n\|z\|_2^2,
\]

so (3.1) returns only

\[
T_p\ge |A|.
\]

Thus generic ZCZ orthogonal-shift cardinality bounds merely recover the ordinary clique obstruction. The sharp `sqrt(n)` theta scale needs the **weighted coherent-gain geometry**, not just a count of zero-correlation shifts.

# 4. Method #23, second pass — attack theta and its complement simultaneously

The preceding runs repeatedly showed that a one-sided certificate tends to lose the sharp constant. The vertex-transitive product

\[
\vartheta(H)\vartheta(\overline H)=n
\]

suggests doing the opposite of the usual one-sided attack: combine the two complementary moment cones before estimating either one.

Let

\[
N=\mathbb Z_n\setminus(\{0\}\cup S).
\]

Define

\[
\mathcal M_N=
\left\{\mu\ge0:\sum_x\mu_x=1,\ \widehat\mu(r)=0\ (r\in N)\right\},
\]

\[
\mathcal M_S=
\left\{\nu\ge0:\sum_x\nu_x=1,\ \widehat\nu(r)=0\ (r\in S)\right\}.
\]

The exact theta LPs give

\[
\max_{\mu\in\mathcal M_N}\mu_0=\frac{T_p}{n},
\qquad
\max_{\nu\in\mathcal M_S}\nu_0=\frac1{T_p}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now define the real self-adjoint Fourier multiplier `L_p` by

\[
\widehat{L_pf}(r)=
\begin{cases}
+\widehat f(r),&r\in S,\\
-\widehat f(r),&r\in N,\\
0,&r=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus `L_p^2=I` on the mean-zero subspace.

## Theorem R20-SYM1 — one symmetric LP for both theta sides

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T_p}{n}+\frac1{T_p}
=
\max_{\substack{u\in\mathbb R^n\\ \sum_xu_x=0\\
2/n+(L_pu)_x\ge |u_x|\ \forall x}}
\left(\frac2n+(L_pu)_0\right).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
T_p+\frac n{T_p}
=n\max_{u\ \mathrm{feasible}}
\left(\frac2n+(L_pu)_0\right).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Take arbitrary `mu in M_N` and `nu in M_S`, and put

\[
u=\mu-\nu,
\qquad h=\mu+\nu.
\]

Both probabilities have total mass one, hence `u` is mean-zero and `h` has Fourier coefficient two at zero. At a nonzero frequency in `S`, only `mu` can have a Fourier coefficient, so `hat h=hat u`; at a nonzero frequency in `N`, only `nu` can have a Fourier coefficient, so `hat h=-hat u`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{h=2U+L_pu,}
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `U` is uniform measure. Nonnegativity of `mu=(h+u)/2` and `nu=(h-u)/2` is exactly

\[
h\ge|u|
\]

coordinatewise. This proves that every pair of complementary feasible probability measures yields a feasible `u` in (4.3).

Conversely, given a mean-zero `u` satisfying the displayed inequality, define `h=2U+L_pu` and then `mu=(h+u)/2`, `nu=(h-u)/2`. They are nonnegative probabilities, and (4.2) shows exactly that `mu` has Fourier zeros on `N` while `nu` has Fourier zeros on `S`. Hence the parametrization is bijective.

The objective is `h_0=mu_0+nu_0`. Maximizing over the product of the two independent feasible sets gives the sum of their individual maxima, which is (4.1). QED.

The source conjecture is therefore equivalent to the single balanced-involution statement

\[
\boxed{
\max_{u\ \mathrm{feasible}}
\left(\frac2n+(L_pu)_0\right)
=\frac{2+o(1)}{\sqrt n}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is not weaker or stronger than Conjecture 26; it is an exact reformulation. Its value is that it exposes a different object: an `L^1`-type majorant problem for one involutive Paley Fourier multiplier, instead of two separate theta programs.

# 5. Method #11, second pass — dualize the symmetric multiplier problem

Let

\[
K_p=L_p\delta_0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since `L_p` is self-adjoint, `(L_pu)_0=<K_p,u>`. Linearize the absolute-value constraint in (4.3) as

\[
u-L_pu\le \frac2n\mathbf1,
\qquad
-u-L_pu\le \frac2n\mathbf1.
\]

Dual variables `a,b>=0` give

\[
K_p=(a-b)-L_p(a+b)+\lambda\mathbf1.
\]

Writing

\[
\phi=a+b\ge0,
\qquad \psi=a-b,
\]

is equivalent to `|psi|<=phi`. Eliminating `psi` gives the exact dual.

## Theorem R20-SYM2 — a one-function majorant certificate

If `H_p` denotes the optimum on the right side of (4.3), then

\[
\boxed{
H_p=
\frac2n+rac2n
\min_{\substack{\phi\ge0,\ \lambda\in\mathbb R\\
|K_p+L_p\phi-\lambda\mathbf1|\le\phi}}
\sum_x\phi_x.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently Conjecture 26 is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\min_{\substack{\phi\ge0,\lambda\\
|K_p+L_p\phi-\lambda\mathbf1|\le\phi}}
\|\phi\|_1
=(1+o(1))\sqrt n.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The proof is the LP dual calculation above; finite-dimensional strong duality applies because the original product moment polytopes are nonempty and bounded.

This is the most concrete new analytic target of the run. A candidate `phi` satisfying the pointwise majorant in (5.2) is simultaneously a certificate controlling theta and co-theta. In particular, the earlier factor-`sqrt(2)` loss from a one-sided projected-delta/Hoffman certificate is no longer baked into the formulation.

A natural first try is to build `phi` from `|K_p|` and its `L_p` iterates. Floating discovery experiments at the newly certified `p=1049` show that the naive two-parameter family `phi=c|K_p|+r*1` remains far from the exact symmetric optimum, so this is not promoted as a theorem or certificate. The useful lesson is that one application of the absolute-value envelope is insufficient; any proof through (5.3) must exploit higher arithmetic structure of the Paley multiplier, not merely its pointwise kernel envelope.

For orientation, the exact p=1049 theta value implies

\[
T_{1049}+\frac{524}{T_{1049}}
=45.7982972379\ldots,
\]

while `2 sqrt(524)=45.7820925690...`. Thus the symmetric objective is already much closer to its conjectural extremum than either one-sided ratio. This numerical observation is not used as proof.
