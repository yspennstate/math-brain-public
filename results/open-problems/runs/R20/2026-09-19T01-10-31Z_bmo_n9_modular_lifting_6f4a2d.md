## 1. #11 + #18: the signless-Laplacian lifting formulation

Let

\[
Q=D+A,
\]

and let `d` be the degree vector. Then the pushing sums satisfy the exact vector identity

\[
\boxed{\sigma_\rho=d+Q\rho.}
\]

This suggests replacing the integer problem by a modular image-coloring problem.

### Theorem 1 — modular unit criterion

Let `G` have maximum degree `Delta`. Suppose there is an integer `q` with

\[
\chi(G)\le q\le \Delta+1
\]

such that

\[
\gcd(\det Q,q)=1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{P^l(G)\le \Delta.}
\]

#### Proof

Because `chi(G)<=q`, choose a proper vertex coloring

\[
c:V(G)\to \mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z.
\]

The determinant condition says that `Q` is invertible over `Z/qZ`. Hence there is a unique residue vector `rho` satisfying

\[
Q\rho=c-d\pmod q.
\]

Choose for every coordinate its standard representative in `{0,1,...,q-1}`. Since `q-1<=Delta`, this is an admissible integer pushing scheme with `max rho<=Delta`. Moreover

\[
\sigma_\rho=d+Q\rho\equiv c\pmod q.
\]

Adjacent vertices receive distinct residues under `c`, hence their integer `sigma` values cannot be equal. Thus `rho` is proper. QED.

This criterion is elementary; I make **no novelty claim** for the linear-algebra mechanism. The bounded source search did not find it stated for pushing schemes, but the source itself already uses modular coloring ideas, so any priority claim requires a dedicated literature review.

### Theorem 2 — one-dimensional kernel translation criterion

Let `p` be an odd prime with

\[
\chi(G)\le p\le \Delta+1.
\]

Suppose `Q` over `F_p` has nullity one, with kernel generated by a nonzero vector `z`, and suppose

\[
\langle z,\mathbf 1\rangle\ne0\pmod p.
\]

Then again

\[
\boxed{P^l(G)\le\Delta.}
\]

#### Proof

Because `Q` is symmetric of rank `n-1`,

\[
\operatorname{im}Q=z^\perp.
\]

Also `Q 1=2d`. Since `z^TQ=0` and `p` is odd,

\[
0=z^TQ\mathbf1=2z^Td,
\]

so `z^Td=0` in `F_p`.

Choose any proper `p`-coloring `c`. Adding the same constant to every color preserves properness. Since `z^T1` is nonzero, choose

\[
b=-\frac{z^Tc}{z^T\mathbf1}\in\mathbb F_p
\]

and put `c'=c+b 1`. Then `c'` remains proper and

\[
z^T(c'-d)=0.
\]

Therefore `c'-d` lies in `z^perp=im Q`, so there is `rho in F_p^V` with

\[
Q\rho=c'-d.
\]

Take representatives `rho(v) in {0,...,p-1}`. Since `p-1<=Delta`, this is admissible, and

\[
\sigma_\rho=d+Q\rho\equiv c'\pmod p
\]

is proper. QED.

This identifies a concrete algebraic obstruction for the unresolved part of the conjecture: for a useful small modulus, the difficult graphs are those where the signless-Laplacian cokernel has dimension at least two, or has a one-dimensional kernel orthogonal to the all-ones vector, or where no admissible modulus supports a proper coloring.

## 2. #25: the polynomial dual and the exact cancellation obstruction

For an edge `uv`, write

\[
L_{uv}(\rho)=\sigma_\rho(u)-\sigma_\rho(v).
\]

Expanding gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{uv}={}&d(u)-d(v)+(d(u)-1)\rho(u)-(d(v)-1)\rho(v)\\
&+\sum_{x\in N(u)\setminus(N(v)\cup\{v\})}\rho(x)
-\sum_{y\in N(v)\setminus(N(u)\cup\{u\})}\rho(y).
\end{aligned}
\]

Set

\[
F_G(\rho)=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}L_{uv}(\rho).
\]

A Combinatorial-Nullstellensatz proof of Conjecture 2.6 would follow from a nonzero coefficient of a monomial

\[
\prod_v \rho(v)^{t_v},\qquad 0\le t_v\le\Delta.
\]

There is an important easy half: in a nice graph, every edge has at least one endpoint of degree at least two, so every factor `L_uv` has a nonzero endpoint coefficient. Choosing one such endpoint term from every factor assigns each edge to an endpoint, and the resulting raw term has

\[
t_v\le d(v)\le\Delta.
\]

Thus **exponent feasibility is never the obstruction**. The real issue is that distinct selections can land on the same exponent vector and cancel in the coefficient of `F_G`. A universal polynomial proof must therefore supply a noncancellation mechanism (for example a sign-reversing involution with an unmatched term, a genuine global term order, or a finite-field parity argument). The naive claim that the endpoint selection alone proves a nonzero coefficient would be false; I do not make it.
