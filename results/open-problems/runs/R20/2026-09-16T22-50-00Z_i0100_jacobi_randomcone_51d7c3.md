## 4. Method #25 — count the same thing twice: an exact multiplicative-sequence identity

This is the cleanest new derivation of the run.

Let

\[
p\equiv1\pmod4,\qquad L=p-1=2n,
\]

let `g` be a primitive root modulo `p`, and let `chi` be the quadratic character, extended by `chi(0)=0`. Define the length-`L` real sequence

\[
a_j=\chi(1-g^j),\qquad j\in\mathbb Z_L.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proposition 4.1 — exact autocorrelation

For

\[
C(r)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_L}a_j a_{j+r},
\]

we have exactly

\[
\boxed{
C(0)=p-2,\qquad
C(r)=\begin{cases}
0,&r\text{ odd},\\
-2,&r\ne0\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

At `r=0`, exactly one term, `j=0`, is zero and all others have square `1`, so `C(0)=L-1=p-2`.

Fix `r != 0` and put `c=g^r != 1`. As `x=g^j` ranges over `F_p^*`,

\[
C(r)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p^*}
\chi((1-x)(1-cx)).
\]

Add the missing `x=0` term, which equals `1`. The polynomial

\[
(1-x)(1-cx)=cx^2-(1+c)x+1
\]

has nonzero discriminant `(1-c)^2`. The standard quadratic-character sum for a nondegenerate quadratic therefore gives

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}
\chi(cx^2-(1+c)x+1)=-\chi(c).
\]

Consequently

\[
C(r)=-\chi(c)-1.
\]

Because `g` is primitive, it is a nonsquare, hence `chi(g^r)=(-1)^r`. This gives exactly (4.2). QED.

This identity was independently sanity-checked by exact integer arithmetic for **every prime `p = 1 mod 4` up to 421**, not merely the eight theta-certified primes. The checker output is archived below. That finite self-check is not the proof; the proof is the character-sum argument above.

### Link to the localized graph

Put `alpha=g^2`, so the nonzero quadratic residues are `alpha^d`, `d in Z_n`. The one-localized Cayley connection set is

\[
S_p=\{d\ne0:\chi(1-\alpha^d)=1\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because `p=1 mod 4`, `chi(-1)=1`, so the sign convention agrees with the source adjacency condition. Equation (4.1) says precisely that

\[
d\in S_p\iff a_{2d}=1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the full multiplicative sequence has a striking exact property: **all nonzero even-shift autocorrelations are the same `-2`, while the local Paley graph is cut out by the signs of the even samples themselves.**

This looked at first like a possible route to an exact all-prime spectral factor with the conjectured constant. Method #5 tests that possibility exactly.

## 5. Method #5 — add a parameter and differentiate: the natural Jacobi/SLCE factor stops exactly at the old `sqrt(p)` scale

Perturb the unique zero of `a` by a real parameter `t`. Define its even/odd decimations

\[
y_d^{(t)}=a_{2d}+t\,1_{\{d=0\}},
\qquad
z_d=a_{2d+1},
\qquad d\in\mathbb Z_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `Y_m(t),Z_m` be their length-`n` discrete Fourier transforms and set

\[
R_m(t)=|Y_m(t)|^2+|Z_m|^2\ge0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

By Wiener--Khinchin and Proposition 4.1, for every local edge lag `d in S_p`,

\[
\frac1n\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}R_m(t)e^{2\pi i md/n}
=C(2d)+t(a_{2d}+a_{-2d})
=-2+2t.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The right-hand side is **constant over all local edge lags**. Therefore it can be killed by changing only the zero Fourier coordinate. Define, for `m != 0`,

\[
q_m(t)=R_m(t),
\]

and

\[
q_0(t)=R_0(t)+2n(1-t).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then the inverse Fourier transform of `q(t)` vanishes on every `d in S_p`, exactly the standard circulant theta-dual equality condition. All nonzero coordinates are nonnegative by construction.

It remains to compute the objective exactly. First,

\[
\sum_d a_{2d}=-1,\qquad
\sum_d a_{2d+1}=0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For example, the first identity follows by splitting the standard Jacobi sum `J(chi,chi)=-1` into the square/nonsquare cosets; the second follows from `sum_j a_j=-1`.

Put

\[
u=1-t.
\]

Then

\[
R_0(t)=u^2,
\qquad
q_0(t)=u(2n+u).
\tag{5.6}
\]

For `u>0` this is positive. Parseval and `sum_j a_j^2=p-2=2n-1` give

\[
\sum_mR_m(t)
=n(2n-1+t^2).
\]

After the zero-coordinate correction (5.4),

\[
\sum_mq_m(t)=n(2n+u^2).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Scaling by `q_0(t)` produces a valid standard-theta dual with objective

\[
B_n(u)
=
\frac{n(2n+u^2)}{u(2n+u)},
\qquad u>0.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Differentiate:

\[
B_n'(u)
\text{ has the sign of }
 u^2-2u-2n.
\]

The unique positive minimizer is

\[
u_*=1+\sqrt{2n+1}=1+\sqrt p.
\]

Substitution gives the exact minimum

\[
\boxed{\min_{u>0}B_n(u)=\sqrt p-1.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

So this natural **all-prime nonnegative spectral-factor family is completely explicit, but it misses Conjecture 26 by the asymptotic factor `sqrt(2)`**:

\[
\sqrt p-1
\sim
\sqrt2\,\sqrt{p/2}.
\]

This is a useful negative result about a route, not a refutation of the source conjecture.

There is an additional diagnostic. For `m != 0`, aliasing the full length-`p-1` Fourier transform shows that `R_m(t)` is affine in the local adjacency eigenvalue `lambda_m`. Thus the entire one-parameter construction, despite arising from the full multiplicative/Jacobi sequence, collapses back to a Hoffman/extreme-eigenvalue type certificate. It never uses the **relative phase arrangement** of different Jacobi frequencies.

**Conclusion from #5:** the missing certificate cannot be obtained merely by exploiting the constant-modulus / two-level autocorrelation structure of the classical Paley multiplicative sequence. It must use higher joint phase information.

## 6. Method #9 — randomize: two random analogues land numerically on the desired constant, but the closest Fourier analogue is itself open

The preceding run recast Conjecture 26 as radial depth of a half-dimensional projected regular simplex. I therefore tested two randomizations of exactly that geometry rather than another Paley prime.

The artifact `artifacts/R20/2026-09-16T22-50-00Z_i0100_jacobi_randomcone_probe.py` contains a `DISCOVERY ONLY` experiment with fixed seed `20260917`. It compares:

1. a **random symmetric Fourier support** of size `n/2-1` (the fixed-edge-count analogue of the dense random circulant graph); and
2. a **Haar/Gaussian projected simplex** of dimension `n/2-1`.

For each `n in {40,80,120,210,400}`, 12 independent instances were solved by the moment LP. This is floating-point discovery, **not an exact certificate and not metric movement**.

The most informative endpoint was `n=400`:

- random symmetric Fourier model: mean `theta/sqrt(n) = 0.9967528080`, standard deviation `0.0441739678`, median `0.9943469150`;
- Haar/Gaussian projected simplex: mean `1.0270929743`, standard deviation `0.0520148805`, median `1.0319826837`.

At `n=210`, the respective means were `0.9959986132` and `0.9960981748`.

Across this small probe both randomizations sit strikingly near the constant `1` in the normalization `theta/sqrt(n)`. This agrees with the Randomstrasse random-circulant Conjecture 18 and with the projected-simplex heuristic behind I0100.

But this does **not** furnish a shortcut:

- the sharp `sqrt(n)` constant for random dense circulant graphs is itself Randomstrasse Conjecture 18;
- the 2025 random-circulant work still loses a logarithmic factor in its upper bound;
- its RIP route would require a deterministic Paley Fourier statement of exactly the kind R20 is instructed not to silently absorb from the distinct Paley-ETF/RIP track;
- Donoho--Tanner random projected-simplex theory proves deep neighborliness results, but the specific `1/sqrt(n)` radial-depth constant used here is not one of the results I verified from that source.

Therefore randomization is useful as a **mechanism detector**, not a proof transfer.

### A narrower derandomization target than Paley RIP

The polar form from the preceding R20 report can be written as a one-sided Fourier inequality. If `f` is a real trigonometric polynomial whose nonzero Fourier support is contained in `S_p`, then the required upper half of Conjecture 26 is equivalent to the sharp asymptotic inequality

\[
-f(0)
\le
(1+o(1))\sqrt n\,\max_{m\ne0}f(m)
\tag{6.1}
\]

in the relevant normalized mean-zero class.

This is much more targeted than full restricted isometry: it is a **one-sided extremal inequality in one distinguished direction**. A promising future route is to prove (6.1) directly from higher Jacobi/SLCE phase correlations, avoiding a wholesale attack on C28's Paley RIP conjecture.

## 7. What #25 + #5 say together: magnitude is exhausted; phase is the missing resource

So the run isolates a sharper remaining obligation than the previous generic phrase “find an inverse-positive Fourier minor”:

> Construct a feasible theta dual whose positivity uses **joint phase information across Jacobi frequencies**, not only their individual magnitudes or the two-level autocorrelation of the underlying multiplicative sequence.

Equivalently, in projected-simplex language, prove that the arithmetic half-dimensional projection has the same critical radial-depth constant as the random Fourier / Haar benchmarks, but by a one-sided phase-sensitive argument rather than full RIP.

A concrete next object is the family of higher cyclic correlations

\[
\sum_m
\lambda_m^k e^{2\pi i md/n},
\]

which counts length-`k` additive configurations in the local connection set. Degree `k=1` is exactly the spectral/Hoffman family and loses `sqrt(2)`. Degree `k=2` already sees the nonconstant local common-neighbor / elliptic-trace fluctuations. The first genuinely new certificate must therefore either compensate those fluctuations or exploit them.
