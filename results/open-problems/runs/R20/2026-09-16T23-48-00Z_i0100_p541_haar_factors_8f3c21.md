## 4. Method #12 — the conserved object is Haar measure under complementary theta factors

Write

\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

identify the one-localized graph as

\[
G_{p,1}=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z_n,S),
\]

where `S=-S` is the localized **edge** difference set, and let

\[
N=\mathbb Z_n\setminus(\{0\}\cup S)
\]

be the localized nonedge differences.

Use the Fourier convention

\[
\widehat\mu(r)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_n}\mu(x)e^{2\pi i rx/n}.
\]

Define two probability-moment polytopes

\[
\mathcal M_S=
\left\{
\mu\ge0:\ \sum_x\mu(x)=1,\ 
\widehat\mu(s)=0\ \forall s\in S
\right\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal M_N=
\left\{
\nu\ge0:\ \sum_x\nu(x)=1,\ 
\widehat\nu(t)=0\ \forall t\in N
\right\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Put

\[
T_p=\vartheta(\overline{G_{p,1}}).
\]

### Proposition R20-HF1 — exact primal/dual atom formulas

\[
\boxed{
\max_{\mu\in\mathcal M_S}\mu(0)=\frac1{T_p}
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\max_{\nu\in\mathcal M_N}\nu(0)=\frac{T_p}{n}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

The symmetry-reduced primal used by the exact finite certificates is

\[
T_p=n\max\{\nu(0):\nu\ge0,\ \sum_x\nu(x)=1,\ \widehat\nu(t)=0\ (t\in N)\},
\]

which is exactly (4.4).

For the dual, the standard circulant theta LP asks for a nonnegative trigonometric vector `q` with `q(0)>=1` and whose inverse Fourier transform is supported in `{0} union N`, minimizing `sum_x q(x)`. The support statement is equivalent to

\[
\widehat q(s)=0\qquad(s\in S).
\]

At optimum `q(0)=1`: if `q(0)>1`, positive rescaling downward preserves nonnegativity and all Fourier equalities while decreasing the objective. Let

\[
\mu=\frac{q}{\sum_xq(x)}.
\]

Then `mu` belongs to `M_S` and

\[
\mu(0)=\frac1{\sum_xq(x)}=\frac1{T_p}.
\]

Conversely, if `mu in M_S` and `mu(0)>0`, then `q=mu/mu(0)` is dual-feasible with objective `1/mu(0)`. Hence the best dual objective is the reciprocal of the largest possible zero atom, proving (4.3). QED.

### Theorem R20-HF2 — every complementary feasible pair convolves exactly to Haar measure

For **every**

\[
\mu\in\mathcal M_S,
\qquad
\nu\in\mathcal M_N,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
\mu*\nu=U_n,
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `U_n(x)=1/n` is Haar/uniform probability on `Z_n`.

#### Proof

Fourier transform turns convolution into multiplication:

\[
\widehat{\mu*\nu}(r)=\widehat\mu(r)\widehat\nu(r).
\]

At `r=0` both factors equal 1. Every nonzero `r` lies in exactly one of `S` or `N`. If `r in S`, then `hat mu(r)=0`; if `r in N`, then `hat nu(r)=0`. Therefore

\[
\widehat{\mu*\nu}(0)=1,
\qquad
\widehat{\mu*\nu}(r)=0\quad(r\ne0).
\]

Fourier inversion on the finite cyclic group gives (4.5). QED.

This is the conservation law: **once the two theta feasibility systems use complementary nonzero Fourier zero sets, their convolution is forced to be Haar measure, independently of optimality.**

### Corollary R20-HF3 — optimal primal and dual supports are complementary away from zero

Let `mu_*` and `nu_*` attain (4.3) and (4.4). Evaluating (4.5) at zero,

\[
\frac1n
=(\mu_* *\nu_*)(0)
=\sum_x\mu_*(x)\nu_*(-x).
\tag{4.6}
\]

But the zero summand already equals

\[
\mu_*(0)\nu_*(0)
=\frac1{T_p}\frac{T_p}{n}
=\frac1n.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Every summand in (4.6) is nonnegative. Hence all remaining summands vanish:

\[
\boxed{
\mu_*(x)\nu_*(-x)=0
\qquad\forall x\ne0.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Because the feasible sets and objective are invariant under reflection, symmetric optimizers can be chosen. For such a pair,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{supp}\mu_*\cap\operatorname{supp}\nu_*=\{0\}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Moreover `(mu_* * nu_*)(z)=1/n>0` for every `z`, so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{supp}\mu_*+\operatorname{supp}\nu_*=\mathbb Z_n.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus a matching theta primal/dual pair is a **weighted additive factorization of Haar measure** with complementary physical supports away from zero and prescribed complementary Paley Fourier zeros.

### Corollary R20-HF4 — converse optimality certificate

Suppose `mu in M_S` and `nu in M_N` satisfy

\[
\mu(x)\nu(-x)=0\qquad(x\ne0).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Then the Haar convolution identity gives

\[
\mu(0)\nu(0)=1/n.
\tag{4.12}
\]

On the other hand, by (4.3)–(4.4),

\[
\mu(0)\le1/T_p,
\qquad
\nu(0)\le T_p/n,
\]

whose upper-bound product is exactly `1/n`. Equality in (4.12) therefore forces equality in both bounds. So **any complementary-support Haar-factor pair is automatically a matching optimal primal/dual theta certificate.**

This gives an infinite-prime certificate architecture that is more symmetric than the previous one-sided dual search.

## 5. Conjecture 26 becomes a balance theorem for two complementary Haar factors

For an optimal pair define

\[
a_p=\mu_*(0)=1/T_p,
\qquad
b_p=\nu_*(0)=T_p/n.
\]

The conservation theorem gives the exact invariant

\[
\boxed{a_pb_p=1/n.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
T_p\sim\sqrt n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{a_p}{b_p}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `n=(p-1)/2`, this is precisely the source asymptotic `T_p~sqrt(p/2)`.

So the remaining analytic obligation can be stated sharply:

> **Paley Haar-factor balance problem.** Among probability measures on `Z_n` whose Fourier zeros are the localized Paley edge set `S` and its complementary nonedge set `N`, prove that the maximal possible zero atoms of the two complementary factor classes are asymptotically equal.

Equivalently, construct feasible complementary-support factors `mu_p,nu_p` with

\[
\mu_p(0)=(1+o(1))/\sqrt n,
\qquad
\nu_p(0)=(1+o(1))/\sqrt n.
\tag{5.3}
\]

By Corollary R20-HF4, such a pair would already be a matching primal/dual asymptotic certificate family.

This formulation explains why the previous SLCE/Jacobi one-parameter spectral factor stalled at the `sqrt(p)` rather than `sqrt(p/2)` scale: it built a one-sided nonnegative Fourier certificate but did not solve the **balance** problem for the complementary Haar factor.

## 6. Method #21 — who else has this shape? finite-group uniform-sum decompositions

Sherstnev's 1998/1999 theorem is directly relevant: it gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a sum of independent random variables on a finite Abelian group to be uniform. His 2002/2003 follow-up studies decompositions of the uniform distribution on a finite group. Therefore the convolution-to-Haar mechanism itself belongs to an established finite-group factorization literature.

What is special here is the extremal Fourier partition:

- one factor must vanish on the Paley localized edge characters;
- the other must vanish on the complementary nonedge characters;
- both must be nonnegative probability measures;
- their zero atoms are extremized;
- theta duality forces the atom product exactly `1/n` and optimal physical-support complementarity.

The next literature-port task is therefore not to rediscover the Fourier criterion for Haar convolution. It is to inspect Sherstnev's parameterization/classification and classical finite-group factorization methods for a theorem that controls **the relative sizes of the distinguished atom** under a prescribed complementary character partition. I did not find such a Paley-specific balance theorem in the bounded search.

The nearest random analogue remains random-circulant theta. Its known upper/lower results do not currently provide the exact constant one here, so a direct random-to-Paley transfer would still require new derandomization of this factor balance.
