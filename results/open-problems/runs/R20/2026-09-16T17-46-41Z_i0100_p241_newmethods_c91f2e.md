## 6. Move #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: exact autocorrelation factorization of the dual

This move produced the most useful analytic reformulation of the run. It is an exact equivalence, but the mechanism is standard finite-abelian Fourier/Delsarte theory; **novelty is not claimed**.

Let `S_p` be the localized **edge** differences in `Z_n`, so `S_p` and `N_p` partition the nonzero differences up to sign.

Define the dual trigonometric polynomial values

\[
r_m=a+\sum_{d\in N_p}\lambda_d\cos(2\pi md/n).
\]

Then `(D_p)` is exactly

\[
r_m\ge0\quad(m\ne0),\qquad r_0\ge1,
\]

with objective

\[
na=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z_n}r_m,
\]

because every nonconstant character sums to zero. At an optimum one may scale to `r_0=1`; if `r_0>1`, dividing every `r_m` by `r_0` preserves the Fourier-support constraints and lowers the objective.

Because `r` is real, even and nonnegative, choose

\[
z_m=\sqrt{r_m}=z_{-m}\ge0,
\]

and define the inverse Fourier transform

\[
x_j=\frac1n\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z_n}z_m e^{2\pi i mj/n}.
\]

Then `x_j` is real. Moreover,

\[
\sum_jx_j=z_0=1,
\]

and Parseval gives

\[
\sum_mr_m=n\sum_jx_j^2.
\]

Its cyclic autocorrelation is

\[
C_x(d):=\sum_jx_jx_{j+d}
=\frac1n\sum_m r_m e^{2\pi i md/n}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

But the Fourier coefficients of `r` are supported only on `{0} union N_p`; hence

\[
C_x(d)=0\qquad(d\in S_p).
\]

Conversely, if a real cyclic sequence `x` satisfies

\[
\sum_jx_j=1,
\qquad
C_x(d)=0\quad(d\in S_p),
\]

then with `z_m` its Fourier transform and `r_m=|z_m|^2`, one has `r_m>=0`, `r_0=1`, and the inverse Fourier coefficients of `r` vanish on every `d in S_p`. Thus `r` has exactly the Fourier support allowed by `(D_p)` and is dual-feasible. Parseval gives the same objective.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T_p
=
n\min\left\{
\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_n}x_j^2:
 x_j\in\mathbb R,
 \sum_jx_j=1,
 \sum_jx_jx_{j+d}=0\ \forall d\in S_p
\right\}.
}
\tag{AC_p}
\]

This changes the problem from graph SDP language into a **zero-autocorrelation-zone sequence-design / finite Delsarte--Turan extremal problem**. Conjecture 26 becomes

\[
\min \|x\|_2^2\sim n^{-1/2}
\qquad(n=(p-1)/2),
\]

for a highly arithmetic prescribed half-set of zero autocorrelations.

Why this may matter: generic spectral/Hoffman arguments collapse all constraints to one weighted sum and lose the target constant. `(AC_p)` preserves all roughly `n/2` exact orthogonality constraints simultaneously. The recent general Delsarte/Turan positive-definite-function literature supplies a natural toolbox, though the bounded search found no theorem specialized enough to settle this arithmetic zero set.

## 7. Move #17 — LOOK FOR YOUR NUMBERS SOMEWHERE ELSE: an exact SLCE/Sidelnikov identity

The local connection sequence itself is a simple half-shift product of a classical pseudorandom binary sequence.

Let `g` be a primitive root modulo `p`, let

\[
n=(p-1)/2,
\qquad
\alpha=g^2,
\]

and let `chi` be the quadratic character, with `chi(0)=0`. Define the length `2n` character sequence

\[
u_j=\chi(g^j+1),
\qquad j\in\mathbb Z_{2n}.
\]

This is the standard character form underlying the binary Sidelnikov--Lempel--Cohn--Eastman (SLCE) sequence, up to the conventional conversion from signs to bits.

For a localized multiplicative difference `d in Z_n`, set

\[
s_d=\chi(\alpha^d-1).
\]

Since `g^n=-1` and `chi(-1)=1` for `p=1 mod4`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_du_{d+n}
&=\chi(g^d+1)\chi(g^{d+n}+1)\\
&=\chi(g^d+1)\chi(1-g^d)\\
&=\chi((g^d+1)(g^d-1))\\
&=\chi(g^{2d}-1)\\
&=s_d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{s_d=u_du_{d+n}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For `d != 0`, the localized edge condition is exactly `s_d=+1`. Consequently the zero-autocorrelation set in `(AC_p)` is the `+1` sign class of the half-period product of an SLCE sequence.

For every nontrivial multiplicative frequency `m`, if `lambda_m` is the localized adjacency eigenvalue, then

\[
\boxed{
2\lambda_m+1
=
\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z_n}s_d e^{2\pi i md/n}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

So the same object appears in two languages:

- localized-Paley spectral/Jacobi-sum language;
- SLCE sequence character-value/correlation language.

The SLCE literature located in this run provides Jacobi-sum formulas, autocorrelation distributions, and square-root-scale cross-correlation bounds for various decimations. That is relevant but not yet enough: Conjecture 26 requires an extremal sequence `x` whose autocorrelation is **exactly zero on every `+1` location of (7.2)**. Bounds on the magnitude of the Fourier spectrum of `s` recover only spectral-scale information and do not by themselves manufacture the required nonnegative dual `r`.

This is nevertheless a genuinely different literature/technique route. The most promising next search is for SLCE character-polynomial divisibility or exact decimation identities that can produce a factor `x` in `(AC_p)`, not merely a Weil-type `O(sqrt p)` correlation estimate.
