## 4. Symmetry-reduced exact program

Let

\[
n=(p-1)/2,
\]

and identify the multiplicative group of nonzero quadratic residues with `Z_n` via a generator.  The one-localized graph is circulant.  Let `N` denote representatives, modulo `d ~ -d`, of the nonzero differences that are **nonedges of the localized graph** (equivalently, edges of its complement).

The standard-theta program reduces to the Fourier-mass LP already frozen in the previous run:

### Primal

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max &\quad n w_0,\\
\text{s.t.}&\quad \sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}w_m=1,\\
&\quad \sum_m w_m\cos(2\pi md/n)=0\qquad(d\in N),\\
&\quad w_m\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{P_p}
\]

Here `w_m` is the combined Fourier mass of the conjugate pair `{m,-m}`, with the singleton frequencies treated in the obvious way.

### Dual

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min &\quad na,\\
\text{s.t.}&\quad
 a+\sum_{d\in N}\lambda_d\cos(2\pi md/n)
 \ge 1_{\{m=0\}}
 \qquad(m=0,\ldots,n-1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{D_p}
\]

with the `lambda_d` free.

For any feasible primal and dual pair, weak duality gives `n w_0 <= n a`.  Therefore exact feasibility together with `w_0=a` certifies the exact standard Lovasz theta value.

Magsino--Mixon--Parshall prove the circulant reduction of standard Lovasz theta in Proposition 2 of `arXiv:1907.05971`.  The program above is the real/even Fourier-mass rewriting used by the continuing R20 computation.

## 7. Analytic infinite-prime attack — exact obstruction to importing the Schrijver magic-function certificates

The finite metric is secondary to the asymptotic target, so I also re-examined the analytic dual literature.

Magsino--Mixon--Parshall distinguish:

- standard Lovasz theta `theta_L`, whose circulant primal is Proposition 2;
- Schrijver's strengthened `theta_LS`, whose primal adds entrywise nonnegativity (Proposition 3).

Their dual/magic-function formulation for the Schrijver bound permits functions `f,g` satisfying

\[
f\ge g+1,
\]

together with the Paley localization vanishing constraints and Fourier nonnegativity.  Crucially, they state that the comparable **standard Lovasz theta** dual is obtained by adding the stronger equality

\[
\boxed{f=g+1.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

This precisely identifies why the successful Hanson--Petridis / Schrijver-style magic-function route does not automatically certify Conjecture 26.  Any positive slack

\[
f-g-1>0
\]

that is useful in the strengthened Schrijver program is unavailable in the standard-theta dual tracked by I0100.

I therefore attempted the natural infinite-family port and found the exact obstruction: it must solve the **zero-slack Fourier interpolation problem** (7.1), not merely the inequality used for `theta_LS`.

This is a sharper analytic target than the previous extreme-eigenvalue baseline, but it is not a solution.  The previous all-prime Jacobi/spectral certificate family remains part of the inherited state and is not re-derived here; it only gives an asymptotic normalized constant window too wide to reach `1/sqrt(2)`.

Kunisky (`arXiv:2303.16475`, later Experimental Mathematics) proves weak spectral convergence for the `a=1` localization after rescaling and conjectures convergence of the minimum eigenvalue to the corresponding spectral edge.  Weak empirical spectral convergence does not control the extremal/active Fourier inequalities needed for `(P_p)/(D_p)`, and the needed extreme-eigenvalue statement is itself conjectural.  Thus it does not close Conjecture 26.
