T=T_p:=\vartheta(\overline{G_{p,1}}),\qquad n=(p-1)/2,

M_p:=

\[
\frac{T}{n}+\frac1T

## 4. Method #12 — the conserved Haar factor forces an exact saturated dual

Define the two complementary probability-moment polytopes

\[
\mathcal M_N=\{\mu\ge0:\sum_x\mu_x=1,\ \widehat\mu|_N=0\},
\]

\[
\mathcal M_S=\{\nu\ge0:\sum_x\nu_x=1,\ \widehat\nu|_S=0\}.
\]

The exact theta LPs give

\[
\max_{\mu\in\mathcal M_N}\mu_0=\frac{T}{n},
\qquad
\max_{\nu\in\mathcal M_S}\nu_0=\frac1T.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Choose optimal `mu,nu`, and average each with its reflection if necessary. The sets `S,N` are reflection-invariant, so this preserves feasibility, optimality and the atom at zero. Hence we may take `mu,nu` reflection-symmetric.

Their complementary Fourier zero sets imply the exact conservation law

\[
\boxed{\mu*\nu=U_n,}
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `U_n` is uniform probability on `Z_n`: at every nonzero character one of the two Fourier factors vanishes, while the zero-frequency coefficient is one.

At zero,

\[
\frac1n=(\mu*\nu)(0)=\sum_x\mu(x)\nu(-x).
\]

But the `x=0` term alone is already

\[
\mu(0)\nu(0)=\frac{T}{n}\frac1T=\frac1n.
\]

All summands are nonnegative. Reflection symmetry therefore gives the exact complementary-support relation

\[
\boxed{\mu(x)\nu(x)=0\qquad(x\ne0).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This conserved Haar factor is the key input.

### Theorem R20-SYM3 — canonical pointwise-saturated optimal majorant

Set

\[
a=\frac{n}{2T},\qquad b=\frac T2,
\]

and define

\[
\phi=a\mu+b\nu-\delta_0,
\qquad
 g=a\mu-b\nu,
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{b-a}{n}=\frac{T-n/T}{2n}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\phi\ge0,\qquad \phi_0=0,}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and `(phi,lambda)` is an optimal solution of (3.1) satisfying the stronger exact equality

\[
\boxed{|K+L\phi-\lambda\mathbf1|=\phi\quad\text{coordinatewise}.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{\|\phi\|_1=a+b-1
=\frac12\left(T+\frac nT-2\right)=M_p.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

At zero, (4.1) gives

\[
a\mu_0=b\nu_0=\frac12,
\]

so `phi_0=0`. Away from zero, (4.3) says `mu` and `nu` have disjoint supports, hence

\[
\phi=a\mu+b\nu\ge0,
\qquad
|g|=|a\mu-b\nu|=a\mu+b\nu=\phi.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Because `mu` has no nonzero Fourier support on `N`, while `nu` has no nonzero Fourier support on `S`, the multiplier satisfies

\[
L\mu=\mu-U_n,
\qquad
L\nu=U_n-\nu.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Also `L delta_0=K`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
K+L\phi
&=K+aL\mu+bL\nu-L\delta_0\\
&=a(\mu-U_n)+b(U_n-\nu)\\
&=g+(b-a)U_n\\
&=g+\lambda\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus `g=K+L phi-lambda 1`, and (4.8) gives (4.6). Finally, since `phi>=0`,

\[
\|\phi\|_1=\sum_x\phi_x=a+b-1,
\]

which equals the exact dual optimum (3.3). QED.

The important consequence is methodological: the true optimizer is **not** a loose majorant. Any strategy that tries to win by merely reducing generic pointwise slack is attacking a feature absent from the canonical optimum.

## 5. Method #18 — exact defect identity and linear-complementarity language

The symmetric majorant LP has a nonnegative defect decomposition valid for every feasible point, not just the canonical optimizer.

### Theorem R20-DEF1 — the entire majorant excess splits into three nonnegative defects

Let `(phi,lambda)` be any feasible point of (3.1), write

\[
m=\|\phi\|_1,
\qquad
g=K+L\phi-\lambda\mathbf1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
(1+m)^2-n
=2n\phi_0+n^2\lambda^2+n\bigl(\|\phi\|_2^2-\|g\|_2^2\bigr).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every term on the right is nonnegative. In particular,

\[
\boxed{m\ge\sqrt n-1.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

Since `L` annihilates constants and is an isometry on the mean-zero subspace,

\[
K+L\phi=L(\delta_0+\phi),
\]

and therefore

\[
\|K+L\phi\|_2^2
=
\left\|\delta_0+\phi-\frac{1+m}{n}\mathbf1\right\|_2^2
=1+2\phi_0+\|\phi\|_2^2-\frac{(1+m)^2}{n}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The vector `K+L phi` has mean zero, hence is orthogonal to `lambda 1`, so

\[
\|g\|_2^2
=1+2\phi_0+\|\phi\|_2^2-\frac{(1+m)^2}{n}+n\lambda^2.
\]

Rearranging gives (5.1). Feasibility says `|g|<=phi` coordinatewise and `phi>=0`, hence `||g||_2<=||phi||_2`; also `phi_0>=0`. Thus the right side is nonnegative, proving (5.2). QED.

For the canonical optimizer of Theorem R20-SYM3, both non-scalar defects vanish:

\[
\phi_0=0,
\qquad
|g|=\phi\Longrightarrow \|g\|_2=\|\phi\|_2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{(1+M_p)^2-n=n^2\lambda^2,}
\tag{5.4}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{T-n/T}{2n}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{M_p-(\sqrt n-1)=\frac{(T-\sqrt n)^2}{2T}.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus the source conjecture is equivalent to the **single scalar balance condition**

\[
\boxed{\lambda=o(n^{-1/2}).}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Indeed `n=(p-1)/2`, so `sqrt(p/2)/sqrt(n)->1`, and (5.5) gives `sqrt(n) lambda -> 0` if and only if `T/sqrt(n)->1`.

There is also a useful finite-dimensional free-boundary / linear-complementarity form. At a saturated optimum choose a sign vector `s_x` with

\[
g_x=s_x\phi_x,
\qquad s_x\in\{-1,1\}\ \text{whenever }\phi_x>0,
\]

and write `D=diag(s)`. Then

\[
\boxed{(D-L)\phi=K-\lambda\mathbf1,\qquad \phi\ge0,\qquad\phi_0=0.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Zeros of `phi` are boundary/free coordinates. The sign regions are exactly the two complementary Haar-factor supports in the canonical construction. This turns the problem from a generic Fourier majorant search into an oriented-sign-cell / linear-complementarity problem with one imbalance scalar.

I do **not** claim that linear complementarity or finite-group extremal duality is new. The Paley-specific reduction (5.4)--(5.8) has not been located in the bounded search and remains unaudited prior to any novelty claim.

## 6. Method #20 — why the previous majorant algorithm was aimed at the wrong obstruction

The previous run suggested constructing an explicit `phi` satisfying

\[
|K+L\phi-\lambda1|\le\phi
\]

with small `l1` norm. Theorem R20-SYM3 shows that this framing contains an avoidable degree of freedom: at the actual optimum, the inequality is saturated everywhere and `phi_0=0`. The defect identity shows that the only surviving obstruction is `lambda`.

So a route that repeatedly improves a generic envelope without controlling `lambda` can converge to the wrong constant even if it makes pointwise slack tiny. The exact asymptotic problem is now narrower:

\[
\boxed{
\text{control the imbalance between the two complementary optimal Haar factors, i.e. prove }
\frac{T-n/T}{2n}=o(n^{-1/2}).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
a=\frac{n}{2T},\qquad b=\frac T2,
\]

one must prove `a/b -> 1`. This is the same balance phenomenon previously seen at the level of the two optimal zero atoms, but now it is embedded in an exact saturated KKT/LCP system rather than an informal heuristic.

A useful next mathematical object is therefore the sign operator `D` in (5.8). For each admissible sign cell, `lambda` is a Schur-complement/Cramer functional of `D-L`. The Paley problem asks why the **optimal orientation-positive sign cell** has that scalar asymptotically zero. This is much narrower than full Paley ETF/RIP and does not reassign C28's Conjecture-29 scope.
