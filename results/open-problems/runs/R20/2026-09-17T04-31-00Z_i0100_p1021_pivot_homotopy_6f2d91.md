# 4. Setup for the new route

Put

\[
n=(p-1)/2=2m,
\qquad
G_{p,1}=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z_n,S),
\qquad |S|=m-1,
\]

and let

\[
N=\mathbb Z_n\setminus(\{0\}\cup S),\qquad |N|=m.
\]

Use the retained Haar-factor LP in the following real form. Let `M_S` be any realification of the Fourier moment equations consisting of the normalization row and a real basis of the conjugation-invariant character rows indexed by `S`; normalize the first row to be all ones. Let

\[
b=(1,0,\ldots,0)^\top,
\qquad
P_S=\{\mu\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^n:M_S\mu=b\}.
\]

Then `rank M_S=m`, the uniform probability vector is feasible, and the already established source-normalized identity is

\[
\max_{\mu\in P_S}\mu_0=\frac1{T_p}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For determinant formulas it is convenient to use the unitary DFT `U`. Put `R_S={0} union S`. For a basis `A` of `m` columns containing 0, write

\[
D_1(A)=|\det U[R_S,A]|^2,
\qquad
D_0(A)=|\det U[S,A\setminus\{0\}]|^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Realification is an invertible row transformation, so the notions of basic feasibility and column-basis exchange are unchanged.

# 5. Method #3 — stronger theorem: exact determinant variational principle

## Theorem R20-DET2

Let `B_+` be the set of all `m`-column bases `A` containing 0 for which the unique basic solution `mu_A` of the moment equations is nonnegative. Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T_p^2}{n}
=
\min_{A\in\mathcal B_+}
\frac{D_1(A)}{D_0(A)}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Moreover, there is a minimizing basis for which one **fundamental cocircuit sign test** supplies the entire complementary theta certificate.

### Proof of (5.1)

The polytope `P_S` is a closed subset of the probability simplex, hence compact. The linear functional `mu -> mu_0` therefore has an optimal vertex. Because the uniform probability is feasible, the optimum has positive zero atom, so 0 lies in the support of every maximizing vertex chosen with positive objective.

`M_S` has real rank `m`. By the basic-feasible-solution theorem, any vertex can be represented by an `m`-column nonsingular basis `A`; in the degenerate case one extends the positive support by zero basic variables. We may choose such a basis containing column 0. Thus

\[
\max_{\mu\in P_S}\mu_0
=
\max_{A\in\mathcal B_+}\mu_A(0).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Cramer's rule in the unitary DFT representation gives, exactly,

\[
\mu_A(0)^2
=
\frac{D_0(A)}{nD_1(A)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed, replacing the column indexed by 0 in `U[R_S,A]` by `e_0/sqrt(n)` leaves only the cofactor `U[S,A\{0}]`; taking absolute squares gives (5.3). Combining (4.1), (5.2), and (5.3) yields

\[
\frac1{T_p^2}
=
\max_{A\in\mathcal B_+}\frac{D_0(A)}{nD_1(A)},
\]

which is (5.1). QED.

This is strictly stronger than the preceding one-basis sufficient criterion: it identifies the exact theta value as the minimum of a Plucker-coordinate ratio over the positive bases.

# 6. A new one-basis primal/dual certificate formula

The stronger useful part is not merely the variational equality. Arrange a basic-feasible basis `A` with column 0 first and set

\[
y_A=M_{S,A}^{-\top}e_0,
\qquad
q_A=M_S^\top y_A.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then on the basis columns,

\[
q_A(0)=1,
\qquad
q_A(a)=0\quad(a\in A\setminus\{0\}).
\tag{6.2}
\]

For a nonbasic column `j`, `q_A(j)` is exactly the coefficient of the distinguished column in the expansion of column `j` in basis `A`; equivalently it is the oriented replacement-minor ratio

\[
q_A(j)
=
\frac{\det M_{S,A(0\leftarrow j)}}{\det M_{S,A}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The LP dual of (4.1) is

\[
\min b^\top y
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
M_S^\top y\ge e_0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Therefore a primal-feasible basis `A` satisfying

\[
\boxed{q_A(j)\ge0\quad\forall j\notin A}
\tag{6.5}
\]

is automatically globally optimal: `mu_A` and `y_A` are feasible primal/dual solutions with the same objective, because

\[
b^\top y_A=e_0^\top\mu_A=\mu_A(0).
\]

Conversely, standard LP basis optimality guarantees that at least one optimal basis can be chosen with (6.5), even if the optimal vertex is degenerate.

Now use the Fourier structure. Since all nonconstant rows of `M_S` sum to zero,

\[
\sum_j q_A(j)
=y_A^\top M_S\mathbf1
=n\,b^\top y_A
=n\mu_A(0).
\tag{6.6}
\]

At a basis satisfying (6.5), define

\[
\boxed{
\nu_A(j)=\frac{q_A(j)}{n\mu_A(0)}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Then `nu_A` is a probability measure. Its Fourier support is contained in `{0} union S`, because `q_A` lies in the row space of `M_S`; hence

\[
\widehat\nu_A(t)=0\qquad(t\in N).
\]

Also

\[
\nu_A(0)=\frac1{n\mu_A(0)}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

If `A` is optimal, `mu_A(0)=1/T_p`, so

\[
\boxed{
\nu_A(0)=\frac{T_p}{n},
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

which is exactly the optimum of the complementary Haar-factor LP. Thus **one optimal primal basis already contains the matching complementary dual certificate**; paired positivity does not have to be searched independently.

Furthermore (6.2) shows

\[
\operatorname{supp}\nu_A
\subseteq
\{0\}\cup A^c.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Because `mu_A(0)>0`, (5.3) gives `D_0(A)>0`; Jacobi's complementary-minor identity then makes the complementary DFT square minor on `B={0} union A^c` nonsingular. Hence (6.7) is also the unique complementary Cramer solution on `B`. This removes the extra `bi-positive` hypothesis from the previous route: at an optimal dual-feasible basis, it is automatic.

This is standard LP duality/oriented-matroid machinery specialized to the Paley Fourier moment system; I do not claim generic simplex duality as new. The useful new reduction for this track is the explicit certificate formula (6.3)--(6.9).

# 7. Method #13 — determinant descent has no nonoptimal sink

Equation (6.5) immediately gives a monotone algorithmic interpretation.

Let `v` be any nonoptimal vertex of `P_S`. The tangent cone of a polytope at a vertex is generated by its incident edge rays. If `v_*` is an optimal vertex, then `v_*-v` belongs to that tangent cone and

\[
e_0^\top(v_*-v)>0.
\]

Therefore at least one incident edge ray has positive `mu_0` derivative. Following that edge reaches an adjacent vertex with strictly larger zero atom. Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No nonoptimal vertex is a local maximum of }\mu_0.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Using (5.3), the same statement is

\[
\boxed{
\text{No nonoptimal vertex is a local minimum of }
R(A):=D_1(A)/D_0(A).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

At a nondegenerate basic feasible solution, this is the ordinary simplex pivot statement: if some nonbasic `q_A(j)<0`, then the reduced cost `-q_A(j)` is positive, and the ratio test produces an improving adjacent basis. At degenerate vertices one may need zero-length anti-cycling pivots before an edge with strictly larger objective; the vertex-level claim (7.1) is unaffected.

Thus there is always a finite monotone edge path to an optimal orientation-positive basis, and along every genuine improving step

\[
\mu_0\nearrow,
\qquad
R(A)=\frac1{n\mu_0^2}\searrow.
\tag{7.3}
\]

# 8. Method #6 — the obvious cyclic/q radial deformation is an exact gauge and therefore fails

Karp's cyclic-Grassmannian work makes deformations of cyclic Fourier fixed points a natural candidate. The first deformation to test is the radial one: replace every selected unit root `omega_s` by `r omega_s`, `r>0`. If the corresponding Fourier/evaluation row matrix has columns indexed by `j=0,...,n-1`, then exactly

\[
M(r)=M(1)D_r,
\qquad
D_r=\operatorname{diag}(1,r,r^2,\ldots,r^{n-1}).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Consider the positive LP

\[
v(r)=\max\{x_0:x\ge0,\ M(r)x=b\}.
\]

The change of variables

\[
y=D_rx
\]

is a bijection of the nonnegative orthant, leaves the distinguished coordinate unchanged (`y_0=x_0`), and converts the constraints to

\[
M(1)y=b.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{v(r)=v(1)\quad\text{for every }r>0.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

So the most obvious cyclic/radial deformation is **completely useless for changing theta**: it is only positive diagonal gauge. It preserves the oriented-matroid signs and the distinguished LP objective exactly. In particular it cannot carry the Paley row space to an easier balanced point with a different determinant ratio.

This is a precise failure of route #6, not merely a lack of progress. Any useful deformation must change relative phases / the selected Fourier eigenspaces, not merely apply a common radial scaling.

There is still a narrower non-gauge homotopy target. For a real-analytic family `M(t)` with fixed rank, normalization row, and a fixed unique nondegenerate optimal basis `A` on an interval, put

\[
x_A(t)=M_A(t)^{-1}b,
\qquad
R_A(t)=\frac1{n x_A(t)_0^2}.
\]

Differentiating gives

\[
x_A'=-M_A^{-1}M_A'x_A
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\log R_A(t)
=
2\,
\frac{e_0^\top M_A(t)^{-1}M_A'(t)x_A(t)}{x_A(t)_0}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

At a generic basis wall the optimal value is continuous and one pivots to the neighboring branch. Consequently a non-gauge deformation would only require controlling (8.3) **along the optimal basis path**, not bounding all half-size Fourier minors. This is materially narrower than the Paley-ETF/RIP problem assigned elsewhere.

The blocker is now precise: no natural same-rank solvable endpoint/path is known for which the total logarithmic drift in (8.3) can be proved `o(1)`. The radial/cyclic candidate fails identically by (8.2).

\[
\boxed{
R(A_*)=\frac{D_1(A_*)}{D_0(A_*)}=1+o(1)

for a terminating optimal basis `A_*`.
