## 4. Method #7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: an all-prime translated-sign Bernoulli theorem

Let

\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

let `alpha` generate the subgroup of nonzero quadratic residues, let `chi` be the Legendre symbol, and define the cyclic localized sign sequence

\[
\sigma(d)=\chi(\alpha^d-1),\qquad d\in\mathbb Z_n,
\]

so `sigma(0)=0`. Since `chi(-1)=1`, for `d!=0`, `sigma(d)=+1` exactly on localized Paley edge differences and `-1` on localized nonedge differences.

### Proposition R20-LIM1 — uniform k-shift character-correlation bound

For every set of distinct shifts

\[
H=\{h_1,\ldots,h_k\}\subset\mathbb Z_n,
\]

put

\[
C_H=\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z_n}\prod_{h\in H}\sigma(d+h).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
|C_H|\le \frac{(2k-1)\sqrt p+1}{2}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** Put

\[
F_H(x)=\prod_{h\in H}(\alpha^h x-1).
\]

As `x=alpha^d` ranges over the nonzero squares, the square indicator on `F_p^*` is `(1+chi(x))/2`. Therefore

\[
C_H
=
\frac12\left(
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(F_H(x))-1
+
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(xF_H(x))
\right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The `h_i` are distinct, so `F_H` is squarefree of degree `k`; its roots are nonzero, so `xF_H(x)` is squarefree of degree `k+1`. The standard quadratic-character Weil bounds give

\[
\left|\sum_x\chi(F_H(x))\right|\le(k-1)\sqrt p,
\qquad
\left|\sum_x\chi(xF_H(x))\right|\le k\sqrt p.
\]

Taking absolute values in (4.2) proves (4.1). QED.

The recovered exact-integer self-check verifies the corresponding identities and inequalities for `k<=3` on primes `13,17,29,37,41,53,61,73,89,97,101`; its output ends `PASS: exact integer self-checks`. The checker is a self-check of examples, not the proof of (4.1).

### Corollary R20-LIM2 — uniform translated-pattern counts

For distinct shifts `H={h_1,...,h_k}` and a sign pattern `epsilon in {+1,-1}^k`, let

\[
N(H,\epsilon)
=
\#\{d:\ d+h_i\ne0,\ \sigma(d+h_i)=\epsilon_i\ \forall i\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\left|N(H,\epsilon)-\frac{n}{2^k}\right|
\le
k+
\frac{\sqrt p}{2}\left(k-1+2^{-k}\right)
+
\frac12(1-2^{-k}).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

**Proof.** Away from the at most `k` shifts hitting the unique zero of `sigma`, expand the pattern indicator:

\[
2^{-k}\prod_{i=1}^k(1+\epsilon_i\sigma(d+h_i)).
\]

Summing and expanding over nonempty subsets `J` gives an error bounded by

\[
2^{-k}
\sum_{r=1}^k {k\choose r}
\frac{(2r-1)\sqrt p+1}{2},
\]

plus the at-most-`k` exceptional shifts. Use

\[
\sum_{r=1}^k {k\choose r}(2r-1)
=(k-1)2^k+1,
\]

and

\[
\sum_{r=1}^k {k\choose r}=2^k-1
\]

to obtain (4.3). QED.

Consequently, uniformly over every choice of `H` and every sign pattern,

\[
2^k k=o(\sqrt p)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N(H,\epsilon)=\frac n{2^k}(1+o(1)).
\tag{4.4}
\]

In particular, for every fixed `delta>0`, for all sufficiently large `p`, every shift-set of size

\[
k\le(1/2-\delta)\log_2 p
\]

is shattered by cyclic translations of the Paley local sign sequence: every one of the `2^k` sign patterns occurs.

This gives a rigorous local finite-dimensional Bernoulli limit, uniform through logarithmic-size cylinders. It is stronger than merely checking fixed moments.

### Why this does not close theta

Lovasz theta is a global convex-geometric functional, not a local-cylinder-continuous statistic. A sequence may have all `O(log p)` translated sign patterns asymptotically random while the positive Fourier cone governing theta still has global arithmetic structure. Therefore R20-LIM1/LIM2 is a genuine deterministic pseudorandomness theorem but not a proof of Conjecture 26.

## 5. Exact global-local conference identity

There is also a useful exact all-prime block identity. Partition the nonzero Paley vertices into squares `Q` and nonsquares `NQ`. Let `H=G_{p,1}` on `Q`, let `A_H` be its adjacency matrix, let

\[
S_H=J-I-2A_H
\]

be the local Seidel matrix, and let `B` be the `Q x NQ` Paley cross-adjacency block. Then

\[
\boxed{
S_H^2+4BB^T=pI+(n-2)J.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

**Proof.** The global Paley character matrix `C=(chi(x-y))` satisfies `C^2=pI-J`. On the `Q x Q` block, `C_QQ=-S_H`; on the `Q x NQ` block, `C_QN=2B-J`; and the `Q`-to-zero column is `1`. The local graph degree is `n/2-1`, so every row and column of `B` sums to `n/2`. Hence

\[
(2B-J)(2B^T-J)=4BB^T-nJ.
\]

Taking the `Q x Q` block of `C^2=pI-J` gives (5.1). QED.

On `1^perp`, this becomes `S_H^2+4BB^T=pI`. It is an exact conservation law, but by itself it contains principally singular-value/energy information and collapses toward spectral bounds. It does not determine the positive theta cone.

## 7. Method #27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS: from explicit optimizer to one positive Fourier basis

The p=541 report converted theta to complementary nonnegative Haar factors. A more algebraic way to use that structure is to stop trying to guess the optimizer coordinate-by-coordinate and instead ask only for **one** positive basic Fourier solution with the right determinant ratio.

Write `n=2m`. For the localized edge set `S` and nonedge set `N`,

\[
|S|=m-1,
\qquad |N|=m.
\]

Let `U` be the unitary DFT matrix on `Z_n`, and put

\[
R=\{0\}\cup S.
\]

For `A' subset Z_n\setminus\{0\}` with `|A'|=m-1`, set `A={0}\cup A'` and

\[
D_1(A')=|\det U[R,A]|^2,
\qquad
D_0(A')=|\det U[S,A']|^2.
\]

Cauchy--Binet gives two exact aggregate identities:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{A'}D_1(A')=\frac mn=\frac12,
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{A'}D_0(A')
=1-\frac{m-1}{n}
=\frac{m+1}{2m}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

One way to see (7.1) is to view the squared minors of the row-orthonormal matrix `U[R,*]` as the projection-DPP basis weights: the total mass is one and the probability that column 0 is included is the diagonal projection entry `m/n`. Formula (7.2) is the same statement for the `(m-1)`-row matrix `U[S,*]`, restricted to bases excluding column 0.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sum D_1}{\sum D_0}=\frac m{m+1}=1-O(n^{-1}).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Now suppose the Fourier moment system on support `A` has a **positive** basic probability solution `mu`. Cramer's rule gives

\[
\boxed{
\mu(0)^2=\frac{D_0(A')}{nD_1(A')}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Since `max mu(0)=1/T_p`, any such positive basis supplies the upper certificate

\[
\boxed{
T_p\le\sqrt{n\,D_1(A')/D_0(A')}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The exact p=17 symbolic self-check verifies (7.1)--(7.5), Jacobi complementary-minor equalities, a complementary positive physical-support pair, and the known exact value

\[
T_{17}=8-4\sqrt2.
\]

At p=17 it records

\[
\sum D_1=1/2,
\qquad
\sum D_0=5/8,
\qquad
\frac{\sum D_1}{\sum D_0}=4/5.
\]

### What this changes

The asymptotic upper half would follow from the following much weaker existential statement:

> for every sufficiently large admissible prime, there exists **one positive Fourier basis** `A'` for which `D_1(A')/D_0(A') <= 1+o(1)`.

An analogous complementary statement, together with vertex-transitive theta product duality, would give the lower half.

This swaps the impossible-looking quantifier “write the entire optimizer explicitly for every prime” for “find one positivity-compatible near-average basis.”

### Exact obstruction: average minors do not control positive bases

The Cauchy--Binet average (7.3) is tantalizing but not enough. Positivity of the Cramer solution is an oriented-matroid/sign constraint, and the determinant mass could in principle be concentrated on bases with the wrong sign pattern.

This cannot be repaired by dimension/density alone. On `Z_{2m}`, take the connection set consisting of all nonzero even residues. The Cayley graph is two disjoint copies of `K_m`; therefore

\[
\vartheta(\overline G)=m
\]

by the Lovasz sandwich `omega(G)<=theta(bar G)<=chi(G)`, since both `omega(G)` and `chi(G)` equal `m`. This graph has the same rough half-density but theta is linear, not square-root. Any proof from (7.1)--(7.3) alone would falsely apply here.

So the missing Paley-specific theorem is now precise:

\[
\boxed{
\text{show that the Paley Fourier oriented matroid has a positive basis with near-average minor ratio.}
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

R20-LIM2 is a plausible input because it says every logarithmic signed local pattern occurs, but it is not yet strong enough to control the signs of these half-dimensional Fourier bases.
