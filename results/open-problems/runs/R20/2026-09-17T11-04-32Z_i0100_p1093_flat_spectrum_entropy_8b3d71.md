## 4. Method #21 — ask who else has this shape: exact dual = a flat negative Fourier plateau

The most useful new result is the exact LP dual of (3.1).

Use the unnormalized DFT

\[
\widehat y(k)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_n}y(x)e^{-2\pi i kx/n}.
\]

### Theorem R20-FLAT1 — flat-spectrum dual

The Chebyshev constant has the exact dual representation

\[
\boxed{
 a_p=
 \max\left\{a:\begin{array}{l}
 y(x)\ge0,\quad \sum_x y(x)=1,\quad y(0)=0,\\[2mm]
 \widehat y(k)=-a\quad\text{for every }k\in N
 \end{array}\right\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Moreover optimal dual `y` and optimal theta probability `mu` are in exact bijection:

\[
\boxed{
\mu=\frac{y+a\delta_0}{1+a},
\qquad
y=(1+a)\mu-a\delta_0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the optimizer, `mu(0)=T_p/n` and `a=a_p=T_p/(n-T_p)`.

### Proof

Write the primal as

\[
\min t
\]

subject to `r in E_N`, `r(0)=1`, and `r(x)+t>=0` for every `x`, where

\[
E_N=\{r:\widehat r(0)=0,\ \operatorname{supp}\widehat r\subseteq N\}.
\]

Attach nonnegative multipliers `y(x)` to `-(r(x)+t)<=0` and a scalar multiplier `lambda` to `r(0)=1`. The Lagrangian is

\[
 t\left(1-\sum_x y(x)\right)
 +\langle -y+\lambda\delta_0,r\rangle-\lambda.
\]

The infimum over `t` is finite exactly when `sum y=1`. The infimum over unrestricted `r in E_N` is finite exactly when

\[
P_{E_N}y=\lambda P_{E_N}\delta_0.
\]

Setting `a=-lambda`, this is precisely

\[
\widehat y(k)=-a\qquad(k\in N).
\]

The dual objective is `a`. Finite-dimensional LP strong duality applies. Complementary slackness at `x=0` gives `y(0)=0`, because at an optimum

\[
r(0)+a=1+a>0.
\]

This proves (4.1). For (4.2), if `y` is dual-feasible then `y+a delta_0` has Fourier transform zero on `N`; dividing by `1+a` gives a probability in the original theta moment cone with zero atom `a/(1+a)`. At the optimum, (3.2) gives

\[
\frac{a_p}{1+a_p}=\frac{T_p}{n}.
\]

Conversely, an optimal theta probability `mu` gives the displayed `y`, which is nonnegative off zero and has `y(0)=0`; its `N`-Fourier coefficients equal `-a`. QED.

### Interpretation

After reflection-averaging `y`, convolution by `y` is a reversible circulant Markov operator with

* no self-loop (`y(0)=0`),
* eigenvalue `1` on constants,
* one **exact flat negative eigenvalue** `-a_p` on every frequency in `N`, a subspace of dimension `m=n/2`,
* unrestricted remaining eigenvalues on `S`.

Thus the source conjecture is equivalent to the extremal flat negative plateau having depth

\[
\boxed{a_p\sim n^{-1/2}}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This places the problem naturally next to finite-group Caratheodory-Fejer point-value extremals, Delsarte/Turan duality, trigonometric moment polytopes/orbitopes, and inverse spectral questions for circulant Markov chains. Krenedits-Revesz treat point-value extremals on finite/LCA groups; Kolountzakis-Lev-Matolcsi emphasize that finite-group Turan/Delsarte strong linear duality is automatic; Sanyal-Sottile-Sturmfels identify convex hulls of trigonometric curves as Caratheodory orbitopes/projected spectrahedra. None of the bounded searches produced the Paley-specific ray-depth asymptotic (4.3).

## 5. Method #5 — add a parameter and differentiate it: a canonical moment ray

The flat-spectrum dual has a universal interior starting point that does not use Paley arithmetic. Let

\[
y_{\mathrm{base}}(0)=0,
\qquad
y_{\mathrm{base}}(x)=\frac1{n-1}\quad(x\ne0).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every nonzero frequency,

\[
\widehat y_{\mathrm{base}}(k)=-\frac1{n-1}.
\]

Hence (4.1) is feasible at

\[
a_0=\frac1{n-1}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Convexity now gives an exact scalar homotopy: if `y_*` is an optimizer at `a_p`, then for every

\[
a\in[a_0,a_p]
\]

a convex combination of `y_base` and `y_*` is a probability on `Z_n\{0}` satisfying

\[
\widehat y(k)=-a\qquad(k\in N).
\tag{5.3}
\]

So `a_p` is literally the **radial exit time** of one prescribed ray from a finite trigonometric moment polytope.

Choose a minimal independent real feature map

\[
\Phi:\mathbb Z_n\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb R^d
\]

whose moments encode all real and imaginary parts of (5.3), and a fixed vector `b` so that the target moment is `-a b`. For every interior `a<a_p`, the unique maximum-entropy distribution on the fiber has exponential-family form

\[
y_a(x)=\frac{\exp(\langle\lambda(a),\Phi(x)\rangle)}{Z(\lambda(a))},
\tag{5.4}
\]

with

\[
\mathbb E_{y_a}\Phi=-a b.
\tag{5.5}
\]

At `a=a_0`, `y_a=y_base` and `lambda(a_0)=0`.

Differentiating (5.5) gives the exact continuation ODE

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}_{y_a}(\Phi)\,\lambda'(a)=-b.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

In minimal coordinates the covariance is positive definite as long as the target remains in the relative interior. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\lambda'(a)=-\operatorname{Cov}_{y_a}(\Phi)^{-1}b.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

If `H(a)` is the Shannon entropy of `y_a`, then

\[
\boxed{H'(a)=\langle\lambda(a),b\rangle},
\qquad
\boxed{H''(a)=-b^T\operatorname{Cov}_{y_a}(\Phi)^{-1}b\le0.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is an actual differentiable mechanism rather than a rephrasing: to prove the lower side `a_p >= (1-o(1))/sqrt(n)`, it is enough to control the directional inverse covariance/Fisher metric along this single Paley moment ray strongly enough that `lambda(a)` cannot escape to infinity before `a=(1-o(1))/sqrt(n)`. The previous formulations required controlling an optimizer whose support jumps; the entropy path stays smooth until the precise boundary one needs to reach.

No such covariance estimate is proved here. R20-HOM1 is therefore an exact reduction plus a concrete analytic target, not the asymptotic solution.

## 6. Method #10 — derandomize, and identify why the straight derandomization stalls

A natural deterministic continuation is to move from `y_base` in the minimum-`l2` tangent direction satisfying the prescribed `N`-moment derivative. This can be solved exactly and explains a recurring factor-sqrt(2) barrier.

Let `q` satisfy

\[
q(0)=0,\qquad \sum_xq(x)=0,
\qquad \widehat q(k)=-1\quad(k\in N),
\tag{6.1}
\]

and minimize `||q||_2`. Since `|N|=m` and `|S|=m-1`, Parseval plus `q(0)=0` says the free `S`-Fourier coefficients must sum to `m`; their squared norm is minimized when they are all equal. Thus

\[
\widehat q(s)=\frac{m}{m-1}\qquad(s\in S).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let `L` be the Paley Fourier involution with multiplier `+1` on `S`, `-1` on `N`, and `0` at frequency zero, and let

\[
K=L\delta_0.
\]

Then the minimum-energy tangent is exactly

\[
\boxed{
q=\frac{n-1}{n-2}K+\frac1{n-2}(\delta_0-U_n).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed its Fourier multiplier is `-1` on `N`, `m/(m-1)` on `S`, zero at frequency zero, and its physical value at zero is zero.

The straight path

\[
y_a=y_{\mathrm{base}}+(a-a_0)q
\tag{6.4}
\]

therefore satisfies the flat-spectrum equations **exactly** for every scalar `a`; only nonnegativity can fail. Its exact positivity threshold is

\[
\boxed{
 a_{\mathrm{lin}}
 =a_0+
 \min_{\substack{x\ne0\\q(x)<0}}
 \frac1{(n-1)(-q(x))}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

For `x ne 0`,

\[
q(x)=\frac{n-1}{n-2}K(x)-\frac1{n(n-2)}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

So this least-energy derandomization is controlled by the single worst negative coefficient of the Paley kernel `K`. The earlier Jacobi/Weil control gives only

\[
\|K\|_\infty\le \frac{\sqrt p+O(1)}{n}
=\frac{\sqrt2+o(1)}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Using only that uniform bound in (6.5) can therefore guarantee at best the same factor-sqrt(2)-scale that appeared in the raw-projection Chebyshev certificate. This does **not** prove that the straight path itself always stops there; it proves that the generic pointwise Jacobi bound cannot certify the desired constant-one depth of that path.

That failure is useful. It says the missing mechanism must exploit redistribution among the free `S` Fourier coordinates as `a` grows. The maximum-entropy path in Section 5 does exactly that nonlinearly; the fixed `l2` tangent does not.

I label (6.3)--(6.5) **R20-LIN1**. It is a self-check theorem, not externally audited.
