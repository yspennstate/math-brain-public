## 3. D0054 — the intake status is materially stale, but the source is not honestly closed

### Exact source

D0054 is Ewain Gwynne, *Random Surfaces and Liouville Quantum Gravity*, Notices AMS (2020), Problem 3. The literal question is:

> Is it possible to construct random metrics associated with log-correlated Gaussian fields on R^n or, more generally, on n-dimensional manifolds, for n >= 3?

The surrounding source text says regularized random measures exist in every dimension but the associated metric had only been constructed in dimension two.

The intake currently treats D0054 as open/unverified and its historical attempt says the higher-dimensional existence half is merely plausible. That description is now obsolete.

### Current literature

Jian Ding, Ewain Gwynne and Zijie Zhuang, *Tightness of exponential metrics for log-correlated Gaussian fields in arbitrary dimension*, arXiv:2310.03996, prove for every `d >= 2` and every subcritical parameter `xi` that appropriately normalized exponential metrics are tight and **every subsequential limit is a genuine metric on R^d inducing the Euclidean topology**. This gives actual higher-dimensional continuum random metrics as subsequential limits of natural approximations.

The later Andres A. Contreras Hip--Zijie Zhuang preprint *Weak exponential metrics for high-dimensional log-correlated Gaussian fields*, arXiv:2512.06292, goes further: for `gamma in (0,sqrt(2d))` it proves that each such subsequential limit can be realized as a measurable map `h -> D_h` satisfying a canonical list of metric axioms (length-space property, locality, Weyl scaling, translation invariance and tightness across scales), and derives sharp metric properties, Hausdorff dimension statements and a KPZ relation.

However, the later authors explicitly keep **uniqueness and full convergence** open in dimensions `d >= 3`. Current 2026 descriptions of the program still say that only subsequential limits are known, not a unique canonical full scaling limit. Hence it would be too strong to mark the whole historical problem simply RESOLVED if its intended meaning is a canonical metric theory analogous to two-dimensional LQG.

### Honest status correction

The correct present classification is therefore:

`D0054 = MAJOR_PARTIAL / EXISTENCE_OF_SUBSEQUENTIAL_R^d_METRICS_PROVED / CANONICAL_UNIQUENESS_FULL_CONVERGENCE_AND_GENERAL_MANIFOLD_SCOPE_OPEN`.

## 7. Neighboring D0046 check — eigenvalue-process limits do not close the operator question

I also rechecked Virag's Question 8 / D0046, since it is a superficially attractive RMT successor. The source asks:

`Are there operator limits of matrix-valued (say Hermitian) Brownian motion?`

Recent work has moved this substantially but does not supply a clean source-level operator-process theorem:

- Gorin--Xu--Zhang construct the Airy_beta line ensemble and prove convergence of the largest Dyson-Brownian eigenvalue trajectories for every beta.
- Huang--Zhang give a convergence framework and universality of the Airy_beta line ensemble for several continuous-time matrix processes.
- Edelman--Jeong--Nissim study the tridiagonal beta-Dyson Brownian process; they prove an explicit fixed-`k` process limit for beta=1, give numerical evidence for beta=1,2,4, and **conjecture** a dynamical beta-stochastic Airy operator.

This is exactly the quantifier distinction relevant to the source: convergence of finitely many eigenvalue trajectories, or even every fixed finite tridiagonal corner, is not yet an operator-valued scaling limit in a topology strong enough to identify the full spectrum/process. D0046 is therefore `OPEN/PARTIAL / HARD`, not a clean CLOSABLE successor. No claim is created.
