## 3. Method #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: from graph theta to Turan / Radau moment geometry

Put
\[
n=\frac{p-1}{2}=2m,
\qquad
G_{p,1}=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z_n,S),
\]
where `S=-S` is the local edge-difference set,
\[
|S|=m-1,
\qquad
N=\mathbb Z_n\setminus(\{0\}\cup S),
\qquad
|N|=m.
\]
Write
\[
T_p=\vartheta(\overline{G_{p,1}}).
\]

Retain the exact Haar-factor LP formulation from the preceding runs:
\[
\max\{\mu(0):\mu\ge0,\ \sum_x\mu(x)=1,\ \widehat\mu(s)=0\ (s\in S)\}
=\frac1{T_p},
\tag{3.1}
\]
and
\[
\max\{\nu(0):\nu\ge0,\ \sum_x\nu(x)=1,\ \widehat\nu(t)=0\ (t\in N)\}
=\frac{T_p}{n}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### R20-TUR1 — exact complementary Turan formulation

For a symmetric set `Omega` containing `0`, define the finite-group Turan constant
\[
\tau(\Omega)=
\max\left\{
\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_n} f(x):
f(0)=1,\ \operatorname{supp}f\subseteq\Omega,\ f\text{ positive definite}
\right\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
With the unnormalized Fourier transform, positive definiteness is equivalent to
\(\widehat f\ge0\).

Then
\[
\boxed{
\tau(\{0\}\cup S)=T_p,
\qquad
\tau(\{0\}\cup N)=\frac n{T_p},
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\tau(\{0\}\cup S)\tau(\{0\}\cup N)=n.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

**Proof.**
If `f(0)=1` is positive definite, put
\[
\rho(k)=\frac{\widehat f(k)}n.
\]
Then \(\rho\) is a probability measure because \(\widehat f(k)\ge0\) and
\[
\sum_k\widehat f(k)=n f(0)=n.
\]
Fourier inversion gives
\[
\widehat\rho(r)=f(-r).
\]
Therefore `supp f subset {0} union S` is exactly the statement that
\(\widehat\rho\) vanishes on `N`, and
\[
\rho(0)=\frac1n\sum_xf(x).
\]
Maximizing and using (3.2) gives the first identity in (3.4). Interchanging
`S,N` and using (3.1) gives the second. Multiplication gives (3.5). QED.

This is a classical Turan/Delsarte-type reformulation; I do **not** claim that finite-group positive-definite duality is new. Kolountzakis--Revész and, more recently, Kolountzakis--Lev--Matolcsi are directly relevant prior art on Turan/Delsarte extremal functions and duality.

The source conjecture is now exactly the **balance** statement
\[
\boxed{
\tau(\{0\}\cup S)
\sim
\tau(\{0\}\cup N)
\sim
\sqrt n.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

There is also a quadrature interpretation. A measure in (3.1) is a positive quadrature formula for uniform measure on \(\mathbb Z_n\), exact on the sparse trigonometric space
\[
\operatorname{span}\{1,e_s:s\in S\},
\]
and \(1/T_p\) is the largest possible weight of the prescribed node `0`.
Thus I0100 is a sparse cyclic **Gauss--Radau / maximal prescribed-node weight** problem.

This category is useful because it tells us exactly what classical positive-quadrature theory would need to supply, and exactly why its standard hypotheses fail below.

## 4. A stronger exact certificate mechanism: bi-positive complementary Fourier bases

The previous run reduced the upper bound to one positive Fourier basis. There is a stronger paired statement that removes the remaining objective optimization once the signs are right.

Let \(U\) be the unitary DFT,
\[
U_{r,x}=n^{-1/2}e^{2\pi i rx/n}.
\]
Set
\[
R_S=\{0\}\cup S,\qquad R_N=\{0\}\cup N.
\]

Take any
\[
A\subset\mathbb Z_n,\qquad 0\in A,\qquad |A|=m,
\]
and set
\[
B=\{0\}\cup A^c,
\qquad |B|=m+1.
\]
Assume the square matrices
\[
U[R_S,A],\qquad U[R_N,B]
\]
are nonsingular. Define the unique signed moment solutions by
\[
U[R_S,A]\mu_A=\frac1{\sqrt n}e_0,
\qquad
U[R_N,B]\nu_B=\frac1{\sqrt n}e_0.
\tag{4.1}
\]
The first row in each system forces the weights to sum to one; the remaining rows impose the required Fourier zeros.

Define
\[
D_1(A)=|\det U[R_S,A]|^2,
\qquad
D_0(A)=|\det U[S,A\setminus\{0\}]|^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### R20-BIPOS1 — paired positivity is automatically exact theta optimality

If every coordinate of both \(\mu_A\) and \(\nu_B\) is nonnegative, then
\[
\boxed{
\mu_A(0)=\frac1{T_p},
\qquad
\nu_B(0)=\frac{T_p}{n},
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
T_p^2=n\,\frac{D_1(A)}{D_0(A)}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

**Proof.**

Cramer's rule in the first system gives, after taking absolute squares,
\[
\mu_A(0)^2
=
\frac{D_0(A)}{nD_1(A)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For the second system, Cramer's rule gives
\[
\nu_B(0)^2
=
\frac{|\det U[N,B\setminus\{0\}]|^2}
     {n|\det U[R_N,B]|^2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Since `B\{0}=A^c`, Jacobi's complementary-minor identity for a unitary matrix yields
\[
|\det U[N,A^c]|
=
|\det U[R_S,A]|.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Also `R_N^c=S` and `B^c=A\setminus\{0\}`, so
\[
|\det U[R_N,B]|
=
|\det U[S,A\setminus\{0\}]|.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Substituting (4.7)--(4.8) into (4.6),
\[
\nu_B(0)^2=\frac{D_1(A)}{nD_0(A)}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Nonnegativity therefore gives the exact product
\[
\mu_A(0)\nu_B(0)=\frac1n.
\tag{4.10}
\]

But feasibility and (3.1)--(3.2) imply
\[
\mu_A(0)\le\frac1{T_p},
\qquad
\nu_B(0)\le\frac{T_p}{n},
\]
whose upper bounds also multiply to \(1/n\). Equality in (4.10) forces equality in both individual bounds, proving (4.3). Equations (4.5) and (4.3) then give (4.4). QED.

The existing exact `p=17` symbolic self-check already verifies the Jacobi minor identities, a complementary positive support pair, the two Cramer formulas, \(\mu_0\nu_0=1/8\), and
\[
T_{17}=8-4\sqrt2.
\]

### Why this is stronger than the preceding route

A one-sided positive basis only supplies an upper certificate. A **bi-positive complementary basis** supplies a matching primal/dual pair and is automatically exact; the objective no longer needs to be guessed or optimized.

Consequently, an analytic infinite-prime certificate family can be obtained by finding \(A_p\) such that

1. the two complementary Cramer solutions in (4.1) are nonnegative; and
2.
\[
\frac{D_1(A_p)}{D_0(A_p)}=1+o(1).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Then (4.4) gives
\[
T_p=(1+o(1))\sqrt n=(1+o(1))\sqrt{p/2}.
\]

This is now my cleanest source-matching target.

## 5. Method #22 — READ THE FAILURES: why three classical shortcuts do not supply the missing signs

### 5.1 Classical Gauss--Radau works for Chebyshev systems; Paley modes are not in that regime

Classical moment-space / generalized Gaussian quadrature theory gives positive principal representations under a Chebyshev-system hypothesis. That machinery would be almost perfect for (3.1): it is designed to control positive quadrature formulas and prescribed-node variants.

The obstruction is the function system. The Paley Fourier rows are a sparse, arithmetic set of high trigonometric frequencies, not a consecutive low-degree Chebyshev system. Already at `p=1009`, the self-inverse frequency `n/2=252` belongs to the Paley edge set, so the continuous trigonometric extension contains a frequency with far more oscillation/zeros than an `m`-function T-system permits. Thus the standard Markov--Krein / generalized Gauss--Radau positivity theorem does not apply.

This is not merely a technicality. Karp's work on cyclic symmetry and positive Grassmannians shows how exceptional global total nonnegativity is for cyclic-shift fixed Fourier-type planes: the unique totally nonnegative cyclic fixed point is the moment-curve one. I use this as a route diagnosis, not as a claim that Karp's signed-cyclic convention directly proves a Paley non-total-positivity theorem without further conjugation bookkeeping.

So “prove all relevant Fourier minors have one sign” is much stronger than what I0100 needs and is the wrong target. R20-BIPOS1 asks for only **one complementary pair of positive circuits**.

### 5.2 Tchakaloff/Caratheodory gives support size, not the distinguished atom

General positive moment representation theorems can guarantee a positive quadrature with at most the dimension number of nodes. They do not force the prescribed node `0` to receive mass \(\asymp n^{-1/2}\). The source constant is entirely in that exceptional atom.

This explains why support-count arguments have repeatedly looked promising without controlling theta.

### 5.3 Turan packing/spectral bounds ask for the wrong combinatorial object

Kolountzakis--Revész-type packing bounds can upper-bound a Turan constant if one has a large set \(\Lambda\) with
\[
(\Lambda-\Lambda)\cap\Omega=\{0\}.
\]
For \(\Omega=\{0\}\cup S\), obtaining a \(\sqrt n\)-scale Turan bound this way would require a packing set of order \(\sqrt n\). In graph language that is already a \(\sqrt n\)-scale independent/clique-type object, precisely the sort of object the Paley problem is not expected to have.

So packing is not the mechanism behind the conjectured theta constant.

### 5.4 Random-circulant RIP remains too global and still loses logarithms

The 2025 random-circulant theta paper reduces the problem to a Fourier LP but needs sparse-Fourier/RIP input and obtains the square-root scale only up to a `log log` factor. The deterministic Paley translated-pattern theorem R20-LIM2 only controls `O(log p)` local cylinders and cannot be black-box substituted into that argument.

Thus the useful failure lesson is consistent across quadrature, Turan packing, total positivity, and random RIP:

> **The missing theorem is a positivity-selection theorem for a half-dimensional arithmetic Fourier basis, not a support-size theorem and not an extreme-eigenvalue theorem.**

## 6. Method #30 — one prime / one scale: p=1009 exposes a mod-8 endpoint effect

The exact `p=1009` run also rules out treating the symmetry-reduced support count as a universal formula.

Let
\[
d_*=\frac n2.
\]
Since the generator \(\alpha\) of nonzero squares has order `n`,
\[
\alpha^{d_*}=-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
d_*\in S
\iff
\chi(-2)=1.
\]
For \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), \(\chi(-1)=1\), so
\[
\chi(-2)=\chi(2)=(-1)^{(p^2-1)/8}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
d_*\in S\iff p\equiv1\pmod8,
\qquad
d_*\in N\iff p\equiv5\pmod8.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

At `p=1009 ≡ 1 mod 8`, the self-inverse half-frequency belongs to `S`; at `p=661 ≡ 5 mod 8` it belongs to `N`. This changes which side of the reflection-reduced LP has a singleton endpoint and changes the apparent basic-support counts.
