## 2. Source conventions recovered exactly

Let `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)` be prime and let `Q` be the nonzero quadratic residues in `F_p`. The Paley graph `G_p` has vertex set `F_p` and

`x ~ y  <=>  x-y ∈ Q`.

The source's one-localization is

`G_{p,1} = G_p[Q]`,

the induced graph on the neighbors of 0. It has

`n = |Q| = (p-1)/2`

vertices and degree `(p-5)/4`.

The source's theta quantity is the standard Lovasz theta of the **complement**:

`T_p := vartheta(complement G_{p,1})`.

I use the standard primal normalization

`vartheta(H) = max <J,X>`

subject to

`X >= 0 (PSD), Tr X = 1, X_uv = 0 for distinct uv ∈ E(H)`.

Thus, for `H = complement G_{p,1}`, the zero constraints are exactly on the **nonedges** of the localized Paley graph. This is the clique-bounding side:

`omega(G_{p,1}) <= vartheta(complement G_{p,1})`.

## 5. Exact symmetry-reduced theta program

### R20-C01 — Delsarte LP for the source-normalized quantity

**STATUS: PROVED.**

Choose a generator `alpha` of the cyclic quadratic-residue group `Q`, which has order

`n=(p-1)/2`,

and identify `alpha^j` with `j ∈ Z_n`. The localized graph becomes a real undirected circulant graph. Its connection set is

`S_p = { d ≠ 0 : alpha^d - 1 is a quadratic residue mod p }`.

Let

`N_p = Z_n \ ({0} ∪ S_p)`

be the connection set of the complement. Both are invariant under `d -> -d`.

Average any theta-primal feasible matrix for `H_p := complement G_{p,1}` over cyclic translations. The averaged matrix is circulant and no worse in objective. Write its Fourier eigenvalues as nonnegative numbers `mu_m`, summing to one. Reality and symmetry give `mu_m=mu_{n-m}`. For `0 <= m <= floor(n/2)`, let `w_m` be the combined mass of the frequency pair `{m,-m}` (singletons at `m=0`, and `m=n/2` when applicable).

Then the exact theta problem is

`maximize  n w_0`

subject to

`sum_m w_m = 1`,

`sum_m w_m cos(2 pi m d/n) = 0` for every representative `d ∈ N_p/{±1}`,

`w_m >= 0`.

Indeed the inverse Fourier formula says that the circulant entry at difference `d` is `(1/n)` times the displayed cosine sum, and the theta zero constraints are exactly the complement-edge differences `N_p`. The objective is `1^T X 1 = n mu_0 = n w_0`.

Scaling the usual LP dual by `n` gives the matching exact dual

`minimize  n a`

subject to

`a + sum_{d∈N_p/{±1}} lambda_d cos(2 pi m d/n) >= 1_{m=0}`

for every Fourier frequency `m=0,...,n-1`.

Therefore any matching exact primal/dual pair is a complete finite certificate for `T_p` in the source normalization.

## 8. Analytic infinite-prime certificate attempt

### R20-C03 — explicit all-prime spectral certificate family

**STATUS: PROVED / CLASSICAL-STYLE BOUND; DOES NOT CLOSE CONJECTURE 26. No novelty claim.**

Let `L_p=G_{p,1}` and put `s=sqrt(p)`. A nontrivial character `psi` of the multiplicative group `Q` extends to a multiplicative character `phi` of `F_p^*`. The localized adjacency eigenvalue is

`lambda_psi = -1/2 + (1/4)(J(phi,chi)+J(phi chi,chi))`,

where `chi` is the quadratic character and `J` denotes the Jacobi sum.

Proof: under the multiplicative identification of `Q`, the connection indicator for a ratio `t ∈ Q` is

`(1 + chi(1-t))/2 - (1/2) 1_{t=1}`.

The nontrivial character sum of the constant term vanishes. Replacing the restriction to `Q` by `(1+chi(t))/2` on `F_p^*` gives exactly the two Jacobi sums above.

Because `psi` is nontrivial, `phi`, `phi chi` and the relevant products with `chi` are all nontrivial. Therefore both Jacobi sums have magnitude `sqrt(p)`, and

`|lambda_psi + 1/2| <= sqrt(p)/2`.

Hence

`lambda_min(L_p) >= -(s+1)/2`,

`lambda_max(L_p) <= (s-1)/2`.

The complement has nontrivial eigenvalues `-1-lambda_psi`, so

`lambda_min(complement L_p) >= -(s+1)/2`.

For any `d`-regular graph `G` on `n` vertices whose smallest eigenvalue is at least `-A`, the Lovasz dual matrix

`M = J - [n/(d+A)] A_G`

has the required value `1` on the diagonal and every nonedge, while its largest eigenvalue is at most

`n A/(d+A)`.

Thus

`vartheta(G) <= n A/(d+A)`.

Applying this to the complement of `L_p`, whose degree is `(p-1)/4`, gives the explicit all-prime upper certificate

`T_p <= sqrt(p)-1`.

Applying it to `L_p`, whose degree is `(p-5)/4`, gives

`vartheta(L_p) <= (sqrt(p)+1)^2/(sqrt(p)+3)`.

Multiplication by `Q` acts transitively on the vertices of `L_p`, so Lovasz's vertex-transitive product theorem gives

`vartheta(L_p) T_p = (p-1)/2`.

Therefore

`T_p >= (sqrt(p)-1)(sqrt(p)+3)/(2(sqrt(p)+1))`

or equivalently

`T_p >= (sqrt(p)+1)/2 - 2/(sqrt(p)+1)`.

So for every admissible prime,

`(sqrt(p)+1)/2 - 2/(sqrt(p)+1) <= T_p <= sqrt(p)-1`.

This reproduces the familiar spectral-scale window `T_p/sqrt(p) ∈ [1/2+o(1),1+o(1)]`; it does **not** identify the conjectured constant `1/sqrt(2)`. Current public formal work records equivalent non-closing spectral-scale information, so I do not claim this as new prior art.

### Method ceiling exposed

Any proof using only the extreme-eigenvalue/Jacobi-sum modulus bounds above loses exactly the phase information needed to move the constant from the interval `[1/2,1]` to `1/sqrt(2)`. The exact finite certificates show that the true LP uses many Fourier constraints simultaneously. The next analytic attack must exploit more of the full Jacobi-sum phase pattern, or an equivalent spectral-completion statistic, rather than one extreme eigenvalue.
