## 5. Every route tried so far and the precise failure

This section is deliberately a diagnosis, not another lemma list.

### Route R1 — ordinary theta / vertex-only dual

The initial exact square-root certificate gives `U4(p)<=sqrt(p)`. Restricting the degree-four dual to the vertex block is exactly the ordinary degree-two theta mechanism. It cannot produce the requested polynomial saving by itself.

**Failure:** it discards every degree-four triangle/4-clique compatibility variable.

### Route R2 — condition on an edge and recurse to degree two

A tempting degree-four-to-degree-two recursion was tested exactly and failed even on `K_3`: degree-four positivity does not survive conditioning on a degree-two monomial because positivity of the conditioned square would require degree six.

**Failure:** wrong SOS degree bookkeeping, not merely a weak estimate.

### Route R3 — one-vertex localization compiler

Vertex conditioning is valid at degree four and yields

`SOS4(G) <= 1 + max_v theta(overline(G[N(v)]))`.

For Paley, however, fixed-level localization lower bounds stay on square-root scale.

**Failure:** scalar localization throws away simultaneous compatibility between different conditioned witnesses; the local theta objective itself remains too large.

### Route R4 — exact low-clique polynomial identity

The degree-four polynomial identity proves `U4(G)=3` for `K4`-free graphs containing a triangle, giving exact Paley values at small primes such as 13 and 17.

**Failure:** asymptotically Paley graphs contain many `K4`s, and the residual term is precisely a positive weighted sum of degree-four clique moments. This is a finite/sparse structural theorem, not a uniform Paley exponent mechanism.

### Route R5 — edge-compatibility theta compiler

Using the edge-edge principal block gives

`U4(G) <= (1+sqrt(1+8 theta(overline(H2(G)))))/2`.

This is the cleanest surviving reduction because it retains genuinely degree-four edge-edge information.

**Failure so far:** no sublinear upper bound for the unrestricted auxiliary theta value has been proved.

### Route R6 — adjacency/Hoffman dual on `H2(G_p)`

The star test vector produces a nonconstant eigenvalue of order `p`, so every dual restricted to `span{I,A_H,J}` has objective `Omega(p)`.

**Failure:** the adjacency algebra is too small; it cannot distinguish the finer finite-field orbit structure.

### Route R7 — global scalar triangle/K4 moment compression

Local-square Gram inequalities depending only on the four aggregate scalar moments `T,P2,P3,P4` were tested against the fixed-size aggregate pattern and remain PSD through the square-root regime.

**Failure:** compressing all orbit information to four global scalars loses exactly the compatibility that degree four adds.

### Route R8 — full affine symmetry reduction without within-block analysis

Averaging reduces the full edge-indexed PSD condition to one zero-frequency block and two nonzero-frequency block types (square and nonsquare additive frequencies), each still of dimension `Theta(p)`.

**Failure:** symmetry removes the `Theta(p^2)` ambient redundancy but leaves a genuinely large operator problem. Block classification alone is not a bound.

### Route R9 — low-degree character-polynomial theta duals

The previous run built two independent valid entrywise character-polynomial directions `K1,K2` and optimized the full family `J+tK1+sK2`. Exact representation-sector analysis shows every member has objective at least `p/3+O(1)`.

**Failure:** low-degree character polynomials live in a tiny commutative shadow of the orbit algebra; they cannot independently tune the internal modes of the `Theta(p)` Fourier blocks.

### Route R10 — random-graph pseudocalibration technology

Kunisky--Yu explicitly exhibit a graph matrix whose Paley norm is asymptotically `p^2`, versus the random-graph bound `O~(p^(3/2))` used in the random SOS lower-bound machinery. For Paley, the identity `S^2=pI-11^T` creates an algebraic spike.

**Failure:** the random graph-matrix norm theory cannot simply be transplanted. Importantly, this is a failure of a *lower-bound* technology; it does not automatically provide the upper dual required by I0102.

## 6. What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

Every failed route above either compresses away the internal orbit modes or controls only bulk/fixed-moment behavior. A successful proof now appears to require all three of the following simultaneously:

1. **Spike extraction.** Algebraic low-rank sectors forced by Paley identities such as `Q^2=pI-J` must be isolated explicitly rather than bounded by a generic norm estimate.
2. **Uniform nonzero-frequency operator control.** After removing those algebraic sectors, one needs an operator-norm or extremal-eigenvalue estimate for the remaining character-sum kernel, uniform over the two nonzero additive-frequency classes and all sufficiently large admissible primes.
3. **PSD gluing with objective accounting.** The per-frequency estimates must assemble into an actual Kunisky--Yu degree-four dual satisfying every union-moment constraint, with total objective `C p^(1/2-epsilon)`. Entrywise Weil bounds or empirical spectral convergence alone do not supply this gluing.

The missing ingredient is therefore not another scalar character sum. It is a **matrix-valued high-moment/character-sum estimate after exact algebraic spike removal**, together with a dual factorization that uses it.

## 8. Nearest solved problem and the ingredient it has that R12 lacks

The nearest solved analytic result I found is Kunisky's `arXiv:2303.16475`: for one localization (`a=1`), a family of finite-field character-sum estimates is proved strongly enough to identify the **empirical spectral distribution** after rescaling with the corresponding Kesten--McKay limit. The paper explicitly leaves the minimum-eigenvalue edge behavior conjectural.

That distinction is exactly the gap here. Fixed-moment trace convergence can identify the bulk while allowing a small number of algebraic spikes of much larger operator norm. Kunisky--Yu's Section 4.3 supplies an explicit warning: a Paley graph matrix can have norm order `p^2` where the random counterpart has `O~(p^(3/2))` norm.

What the solved `a=1` argument has is a tractable character-sum/moment expansion. What R12 additionally needs is **high enough moment control to reach an extremal eigenvalue after deterministic spike subtraction**, uniformly in the relevant Fourier blocks, and then a conversion of that operator estimate into a feasible degree-four dual.

**Outcome:** do not search for an asymmetric final optimum; use asymmetry as a proof coordinate system for a symmetric certificate.

### #27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS

The previous dual searches implicitly asked for one fixed low-degree polynomial formula whose coefficients work uniformly for every prime. That is stronger than the source requires. The source permits a different feasible dual `Z_p` for every prime, provided one proves a uniform objective envelope.

**Outcome:** allow prime-dependent orbit/Fourier coefficients obtained from the actual finite-field character table, and prove only the uniform norm/objective estimate. This avoids forcing the certificate into a fixed finite-dimensional polynomial family like `J+tK1+sK2`.
