## C07 — exact independent min-plus decomposition

\[
\boxed{
T_N^{\rm ord}
=
(M_0\otimes M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{k-1})(0,0),
}
\tag{C07}
\]

where

\[
(A\otimes B)(a,c)=\min_b\{A(a,b)+B(b,c)\}.
\]

Moreover the random transfer matrices `M_0,...,M_{k-1}` are independent and identically distributed up to the deterministic endpoint labels.

### Proof

Any ordered path has a unique interface sequence

\[
(0,y_0),(n,y_1),\dots,(kn,y_k),\qquad y_0=y_k=0,
\]

and its segment between successive interfaces lies entirely in the corresponding slab and touches each slab boundary only at the prescribed endpoint. Therefore its cost is at least

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}M_j(y_j,y_{j+1}).
\]

Conversely, concatenating slab minimizers for any interface sequence produces an ordered path of exactly that cost. Minimizing over the intermediate heights gives C07.

Each `M_j` is a measurable function only of the edge weights in the disjoint slab edge set described above. Since the FPP edge weights are iid, the transfer matrices are independent; translation invariance gives identical laws. QED.

## C07b — exact comparison error is only confinement plus recrossing

There is no hidden finite computation in these identities.

# 6. Method #24: the extremal obstruction is vertical-facet geometry

Let `mu` be the ordinary FPP time-constant norm for the B2 law. Reflection symmetry and convexity imply that

\[
f(t):=\mu(e_1+t e_2)
\]

is even and convex, hence minimized at `t=0`.

For a macroscopic midpoint detour through

\[
z=(N/2,\rho N),
\]

the deterministic shape-level cost is

\[
N\mu(\tfrac12 e_1+\rho e_2)
+
N\mu(\tfrac12 e_1-\rho e_2)
= N\mu(e_1+2\rho e_2).
\]

Thus the shape penalty relative to the axial route is

\[
\boxed{
N g(\rho),\qquad
g(\rho):=\mu(e_1+2\rho e_2)-\mu(e_1)\ge0.
}
\tag{C08}
\]

The qualitative positivity of this penalty is exactly the vertical-facet issue.

### Proof of the equivalence

Suppose `g(rho_0)=0` for some `rho_0>0`. Put `t_0=2rho_0`. Then `f(t_0)=f(0)`. Convexity gives for `0<=t<=t_0`

\[
f(t)\le (1-t/t_0)f(0)+(t/t_0)f(t_0)=f(0),
\]

while even convexity gives `f(t)>=f(0)`. Hence `f(t)=f(0)` on `[0,t_0]`, and by evenness on `[-t_0,t_0]`. Therefore

\[
\frac{e_1+t e_2}{\mu(e_1)},\qquad |t|\le t_0,
\]

is a nontrivial segment of the limit-shape boundary parallel to the `e_2` axis through the axial boundary point.

Conversely, such a vertical boundary segment forces `f(t)=f(0)` for some nonzero `t`, hence `g(rho)=0` for the corresponding `rho`. QED.

The ADH survey's Question 8 asks precisely for the absence of such a segment in the `e_1` direction for arbitrary passage-time laws. Therefore even the **qualitative** fixed-fraction shape gap needed by this natural lattice confinement argument meets another unresolved source question. Sublinear corridors `W=o(N)` would require more: a quantitative modulus for `g(rho)` as `rho downarrow0`, i.e. curvature-type information. The survey's stronger uniform-curvature Question 11 is stated for continuous passage-time laws and does not directly cover the atomic B2 law.

This does **not** show that Q17 implies Question 8, and it does not refute Q17.
