## 3. Set-up and normalization

Work in the planar Kahn/Kendall road model with parameter `beta>2`. Write an unoriented line as

\[
\ell(\theta,r)=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:z\cdot n_\theta=r\},\qquad
0\le \theta<\pi,
\]

where `n_theta=(cos theta,sin theta)`. Normalize the invariant line measure by

\[
\mu(d\ell)=\frac1\pi\,d\theta\,dr,
\qquad
\mu\{\ell:\ell\cap B(z,R)\ne\varnothing\}=2R.
\]

The marked road PPP has intensity

\[
\frac{\beta-1}{2}\,\mu(d\ell)\,v^{-\beta}\,dv.
\]

Thus for any measurable line set `A` of finite `mu`-measure,

\[
\Lambda(A\times(v,\infty))
=\frac{\mu(A)}2v^{-(\beta-1)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `0<alpha<1/2` and two distinct points `x,y`, let

\[
\mathcal A_\alpha(x,y)
:=\{\ell:\operatorname{dist}(x,\ell)\le \alpha |x-y|,
\ \operatorname{dist}(y,\ell)\le \alpha |x-y|\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is exactly the line set from which Kendall's recursive `V`-path chooses its fastest road at the pair `(x,y)`.

Method #14 removes every irrelevant parameter from a single transition: by translation, rotation and scaling it is enough to take `(x,y)=(0,e_1)`. Spatial scaling by `D=|x-y|` multiplies line measure by `D`, while the speed scale is `D^{1/(beta-1)}`.

## 4. #4 — a complete exact one-scale transition kernel

### Proposition 4.1 (two-ball line measure)

For `0<alpha<1/2`, define

\[
g(\alpha)
=\frac{2}{\pi}
\left[
2\alpha\arcsin(2\alpha)-1+\sqrt{1-4\alpha^2}
\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then for `D=|x-y|`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu(\mathcal A_\alpha(x,y))=D\,g(\alpha).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

Normalize to `(0,e_1)`. For fixed normal angle `theta`, a line hits `B(0,alpha)` iff

\[
r\in[-\alpha,\alpha],
\]

and hits `B(e_1,alpha)` iff

\[
r\in[\cos\theta-\alpha,\cos\theta+\alpha].
\]

The overlap length in the `r` coordinate is

\[
(2\alpha-|\cos\theta|)_+.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(\mathcal A_\alpha(0,e_1))
&=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi(2\alpha-|\cos\theta|)_+\,d\theta\\
&=\frac2\pi\int_{\arccos(2\alpha)}^{\pi/2}
(2\alpha-\cos\theta)\,d\theta\\
&=g(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
\]

Scaling gives (4.2). `□`

As a small-scale check,

\[
g(\alpha)=\frac{4}{\pi}\alpha^2+O(\alpha^4).
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proposition 4.2 (fastest-road kernel)

Let `(L,V)` be the fastest marked road whose line lies in `A_alpha(x,y)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(V\le v)
=
\exp\left[-\frac{Dg(\alpha)}2v^{-(\beta-1)}\right],
\qquad v>0.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equivalently, if `E~Exp(1)`,

\[
\boxed{
V\ \stackrel d=
\left(\frac{Dg(\alpha)}{2E}\right)^{1/(\beta-1)}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Moreover, `L` and `V` are independent, and

\[
L\sim \frac{\mu(\cdot\cap\mathcal A_\alpha(x,y))}
{\mu(\mathcal A_\alpha(x,y))}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

In the normalized pair `(0,e_1)`, the angle density is explicitly

\[
\boxed{
f_\Theta(\theta)
=\frac{(2\alpha-|\cos\theta|)_+}{\pi g(\alpha)},
\quad 0\le\theta<\pi,
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

and, conditional on `Theta=theta`, `r` is uniform on

\[
[-\alpha,\alpha]
\cap[\cos\theta-\alpha,\cos\theta+\alpha].
\tag{4.8}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (4.4) is the void probability using (3.1) and (4.2). For the joint top atom, the PPP density is

\[
\frac{\beta-1}{2}\mu(d\ell)v^{-\beta}dv
\exp\left[-\frac{Dg(\alpha)}2v^{-(\beta-1)}\right].
\]

It factors into the normalized line measure (4.6) and the marginal top-speed density. Equations (4.7)–(4.8) are the disintegration already computed in Proposition 4.1. `□`

## 5. #30 — the first gluing question has a surprisingly clean positive answer

The immediate concern was that conditioning on the parent fastest road would truncate the same Poisson roads needed by a child recursion. For sufficiently small `alpha`, this does **not** happen at one generation.

Set

\[
\alpha_*:=\frac{\sqrt{37}-1}{18}
=0.2823756961\ldots.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proposition 5.1 (parent/child candidate sets are disjoint)

Assume `0<alpha<alpha_*`. Let `x\ne y`, let `ell` be **any** line in `A_alpha(x,y)`, and let `x'` be the orthogonal projection of `x` onto `ell`. If `x'\ne x`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_\alpha(x,y)
\cap
\mathcal A_\alpha(x,x')
=\varnothing.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The analogous statement holds for the child `(y',y)` at the other endpoint. In particular, every `alpha<=1/4` is safely in this regime.

#### Proof

Let `D=|x-y|`, `d=|x-x'|`, and orient the unit normal `n` of `ell` so that

\[
x'-x=dn.
\]

Since `ell in A_alpha(x,y)`,

\[
d\le\alpha D,
\qquad
\operatorname{dist}(y,\ell)\le\alpha D,
\]

hence for `u=(y-x)/D`,

\[
|u\cdot n|\le2\alpha.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that a line `h` belongs to both candidate sets, and let `m` be a unit normal to `h`. Since `h` is within `alpha d` of both `x` and `x'`,

\[
|(x'-x)\cdot m|\le2\alpha d,
\]

so

\[
|n\cdot m|\le2\alpha.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Also `h` is within `alpha d` of `x` and within `alpha D` of `y`, giving

\[
|u\cdot m|
\le \alpha\left(1+\frac dD\right)
\le\alpha(1+\alpha).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Now use only two-dimensional geometry. Write `u=a n+b n^perp` and `m=c n+d_0 n^perp`. From (5.3)–(5.4), `|a|,|c|<=2alpha`, so

\[
|b|,|d_0|\ge\sqrt{1-4\alpha^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
|u\cdot m|
\ge |bd_0|-|ac|
\ge 1-8\alpha^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Equations (5.5)–(5.6) are incompatible whenever

\[
1-8\alpha^2>\alpha(1+\alpha),
\]

i.e.

\[
9\alpha^2+\alpha-1<0,
\]

which is exactly `alpha<alpha_*`. `□`

### Corollary 5.2 (one-generation renewal for Kendall's maximum-road choice)

For `alpha<alpha_*`, after revealing the parent maximum road `(L_0,V_0)`, the marked PPP restricted to either immediate child candidate set is untouched. Hence conditional on the child endpoints, its fastest candidate road has exactly the same kernel (4.4)–(4.8) as a fresh problem at that child pair.

This looked at first like the desired regeneration mechanism.

## 6. The second scale breaks that renewal: exact two-generation memory

The positive result above does **not** iterate.

### Proposition 6.1 (grandchild/ancestor overlap occurs on an open set)

Fix any `0<alpha<min(alpha_*,1/2)`. There is an open set of legal first-two-generation Kendall line geometries for which a grandchild candidate set has strictly positive `mu`-overlap with the **grandparent** candidate set.

#### Explicit witness

Normalize the root pair to

\[
x_0=(0,0),\qquad y_0=(1,0).
\]

Choose numbers

\[
0<\delta<\alpha,
\qquad
0<\varepsilon<\alpha\delta.
\]

Take a legal root line

\[
\ell_0:\ y=\delta.
\]

It lies in `A_alpha(x_0,y_0)`, and the projection of `x_0` is

\[
x_0'=(0,\delta).
\]

For the left child pair `(x_0,x_0')`, take the legal child line

\[
\ell_1:\ x=\varepsilon.
\]

Its distance from each endpoint is `epsilon<alpha delta`, and the projection of `x_0` onto `ell_1` is

\[
x_{00}'=(\varepsilon,0).
\]

The corresponding left-left grandchild pair is therefore

\[
(0,0),\quad(\varepsilon,0).
\]

The horizontal line `h_0:y=0` passes through both grandchild endpoints **and** both root endpoints. Since all four relevant ball radii are strictly positive, an open neighborhood of `h_0` in line space lies simultaneously in

\[
\mathcal A_\alpha((0,0),(\varepsilon,0))
\cap
\mathcal A_\alpha((0,0),(1,0)).
\]

Hence that intersection has strictly positive `mu`-measure. All inequalities used to define `ell_0` and `ell_1` are strict, so the same remains true on an open neighborhood of these first-two-generation states. The line-density part of Proposition 4.2 therefore gives this configuration positive probability. `□`

So the orientation alternation created by orthogonal projection gives a real phenomenon:

- generation 1 is automatically separated from its parent for small `alpha`;
- generation 2 can point back in the grandparent direction and reuse a positive-measure set of the grandparent's candidate lines.

This is the first exact place where the scales fail to glue as an independent recursion.

## 7. Exact conditional law after an ancestor maximum: the memory term

The obstruction can be written explicitly rather than described qualitatively.

Let `b=beta-1`. Suppose a candidate set `A_0` has already had its maximum speed revealed as `V_0=v_0`. The PPP on `A_0` then has no remaining road of speed above `v_0`, while outside `A_0` it is unchanged.

Let `B` be a later candidate line set, put

\[
m=\mu(B),\qquad q=\mu(B\cap A_0).
\]

Then the maximum remaining road speed `W_B` in `B` has conditional CDF

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(W_B\le w\mid V_0=v_0,\text{ ancestor state})
=
\exp\left\{
-\frac12(m-q)w^{-b}
-\frac12 q\bigl(w^{-b}-v_0^{-b}\bigr)_+
\right\}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

#### Proof

On `B\setminus A_0`, roads above `w` remain a PPP with mean `(m-q)w^{-b}/2`. On `B\cap A_0`, roads were truncated at `v_0`; if `w<v_0`, the forbidden interval `(w,v_0]` has mean

\[
\frac q2\left(w^{-b}-v_0^{-b}\right),
\]

and if `w>=v_0` there is nothing further to forbid. Independence on disjoint PPP regions gives (7.1). `□`

For the grandchild states from Proposition 6.1, `q>0`, so (7.1) differs genuinely from the fresh kernel

\[
\exp[-mw^{-b}/2].
\]

Proposition 5.1 says the immediate child's own maximum does not cause this correction, but the grandparent does. Thus the obvious endpoint-only recursive kernel is **exact at depth one and already false at depth two**.

### General cap-field form

After ancestor maxima `(A_i,v_i)` have been exposed, define on line space

\[
\kappa(\ell)
:=\min\{v_i:\ell\in A_i\},
\]

with `kappa(ell)=infinity` when no ancestor set contains `ell`. Conditional on this history, the unrevealed marked process is still Poisson, but with the deterministic line-dependent speed cap `kappa`. Therefore for a new candidate set `B`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(W_B\le w\mid\mathcal H)
=
\exp\left[
-\frac12\int_B
\bigl(w^{-b}-\kappa(\ell)^{-b}\bigr)_+
\,\mu(d\ell)
\right].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is an exact recursion, but its state is an inherited **function on line space**, not just the new endpoint pair. After finitely many levels the cap field is the minimum of finitely many ancestor thresholds on overlapping candidate sets; the state complexity grows with the explored tree.

That is the precise gluing obstruction produced by this run.
