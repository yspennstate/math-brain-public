## 3. (b) Every substantive route tried, with the precise failure

### R1 — Green bound + Nash bound + smoothing

The source's finite Green bound, `O(t^{-1})` Nash estimate, and first-derivative control are insufficient by themselves. The first I0002 run produced a smooth nonnegative scalar countermodel satisfying the analogous inequalities while retaining `t^{-1}`-scale spikes along an infinite sequence. Extra Manhattan structure is indispensable.

### R2 — line-polymer / loop counting

Environment averaging gives an exact hard line-consistency constraint and a reward for sharing already used lines. The representation is exact but the interaction is attractive on compatible reuses and zero on conflicts. Line count alone has no sign-definite mechanism yielding a local-limit power.

### R3 — opposite-environment collision / RWRS language

Time reversal converts return to a collision of a walk in `omega` with one in `-omega`, and relative displacement becomes a row/column scenery sum weighted by occupation counts. The partially oriented proof factors after conditioning because the skeleton is independent of the scenery. In the full Manhattan model the occupation weights depend on the same static sceneries; the characteristic-function product disappears.

### R4 — Walsh spectral parity

The endpoint kernel yields an exact correlated-environment generating function and writes return as collision norm times a parity bias. But large spectral degree/influence does not force parity mixing: a degree distribution can be supported on even integers and have parity bias one. The missing mod-two mixing estimate was not obtained.

### R5 — `q` deformation from the solved partial endpoint

For `G_q=S+A_H+qA_V`, Duhamel differentiation is exact, but no derivative sign emerges because the existing horizontal skew already couples parity sectors. A generic statement that extra skew lowers return is false, even in finite Eulerian examples.

### R6 — D4 symmetry / Bernstein positivity

Exact arithmetic through embedded length eight supports a useful positivity pattern, but the single axis word `HVVHVV` has conditional return `(1+q^2)^2/16`, increasing with `q`. Thus the desired positivity is not wordwise; any proof would require nonlocal cancellation among axis-word families.

### R7 — parity-sector resolvent

The source vector sees a Schur complement with direct dissipative and feedback terms of opposite sign,

\[
D_{00}(q)-q^2ZD_{11}(q)^{-1}Z^*.
\]

The formulation identifies the sign battle but does not produce a strict Loewner comparison.

### R8 — positive full/partial path comparison and global change of measure

On a fully line-consistent path, the full/static versus fresh-vertical likelihood ratio is `2^{V-v}`, where `V` is the number of vertical moves and `v` the number of vertically used columns. Repeated rectangular loops make this ratio exponential. The associated Renyi cost is linear in repeated-line count. Hence Hölder, Cauchy--Schwarz, positive Radon--Nikodym domination, or any pointwise path comparison pays exactly where the model gains its anomalous behavior.

### R9 — adaptive likelihood martingale

Freshening vertical signs gives a mean-one density

\[
L_n=2^{R_n}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_n},
\]

and freshening both coordinates gives `Lambda_n` in B1. This correctly converts the exponential path ratio into a fair sequential bet, but expectation one alone does not control its correlation with the return event. The quantity that must be small is a **conditional** expectation under an SRW bridge.

### R10 — low-reuse / high-reuse decomposition

The previous run rigorously showed that the contribution with at most `(1/8)log_2 n` line reuses in either coordinate is `O(n^{-9/8})`. The unresolved mass is therefore the high-reuse sector

\[
T_3(n):=
\mathbb P_{\rm full}\!\left(
X_n=0,
R_n^{H}>\tfrac18\log_2 n,
R_n^{V}>\tfrac18\log_2 n
\right).
\tag{B3}
\]

High reuse is not known to be rare under the full measure and may carry the dominant return mass.

### R11 — generic adaptive anti-concentration

A fresh literature search was aimed specifically at the missing step. The closest robust tools do not yet fit:

- Peres--Schapira--Sousi's martingale-defocusing theorem handles genuinely adapted martingales with nondegenerate quadratic variation and controlled jumps. With jumps allowed to grow like `(log n)^a`, `a<1`, its small-ball gain is stretched-exponential in a power of `log n`; for `a>0` this is not a fixed `n^{-epsilon}` power. More importantly, after a Manhattan line sign has been revealed, repeated uses of that line contribute predictable drift rather than fresh quadratic variation.
- Rao's Littlewood--Offord theorem for Markov chains allows dependence among the signs, but the coefficient vectors are fixed rather than generated adaptively from those same signs. It therefore does not resolve the Manhattan feedback.

The conclusion of this search is not that adaptive anti-concentration is impossible. It is that **the missing theorem must first manufacture enough genuinely deferred randomness with coefficients fixed before revelation**. None of R1--R10 does that.

## 5. Method #13 — find what only increases: reuse count is the wrong monovariant, deferred randomness is the right candidate

The obvious nondecreasing statistic is total line-reuse count. It is useful for the exact likelihood formula but it moves in the wrong direction analytically: every compatible reuse doubles the surviving likelihood. A large reuse count by itself gives no anti-concentration.

The useful quantity would instead count **unrevealed line-orientation bits that still have certified endpoint leverage** after conditioning on the rest of the history. Such a count could feed a Littlewood--Offord or martingale-defocusing estimate. The difficulty is that final line multiplicities and even future line choices generally depend on the orientation being withheld, so one cannot simply declare all first-use signs independent coefficients after the fact.

This run therefore does not claim a new monotonic invariant. It identifies the exact design requirement for one: a revealment filtration in which, on a source-relevant portion of the high-reuse return event,

1. many line signs remain conditionally fair until late;
2. the effect of flipping each deferred sign on a suitable endpoint/block statistic is measurable before that sign is revealed;
3. the corresponding conditional variance accumulates on polynomially many scales or blocks;
4. the jump sizes stay bounded enough to convert defocusing into a fixed power rather than a merely stretched-log gain.

No previous route simultaneously supplies all four points.

## 6. Method #26 — hunt the sporadic case: repeated rectangles are the stress test

The earlier exact repeated-rectangle family is the right adversarial test for any proposed high-reuse theorem. A path can reuse only a tiny collection of lines many times while remaining perfectly line-consistent, producing a very large likelihood reward. Therefore a theorem that sees only `R_n^H+R_n^V` cannot prove W1.

Any source-closing statistic must distinguish two qualitatively different high-reuse mechanisms:

- **concentrated reuse:** many traversals concentrated on very few lines, as in repeated directed rectangles;
- **spread reuse:** substantial reuse distributed over many rows and columns, creating many potentially useful independent orientation bits.

The likely route is not to prove both have the same anti-concentration mechanism. It is to kill concentrated reuse by path/bridge entropy or a geometric cost, and use deferred-sign anti-concentration only on spread reuse. This is a diagnostic split, not a theorem claim in this thinking-only report.

A proof following the strongest current route must contain a **two-scale deferred-line decomposition** with all of the following features.

### C1. A predictable revealment order

Expose axis choices, geometry, and some line signs in an order that leaves a set of line orientations conditionally fair. The coefficient or endpoint effect attached to a deferred sign must be determined before that sign is exposed; otherwise classical or martingale Littlewood--Offord machinery is circular.

### C2. A quantitative dichotomy on the high-reuse sector

For every high-reuse return history, or after summing over histories, one must show either:

- reuse is concentrated on sufficiently few lines that the total bridge contribution is already `O(n^{-1-epsilon})` by an entropy/geometric estimate; or
- there are polynomially many spread/deferred line bits with nontrivial endpoint leverage.

The repeated-rectangle example shows why this dichotomy is necessary.

### C3. Fixed-power conditional anti-concentration

On the spread branch, the deferred bits must yield a conditional small-ball bound with a genuine `n^{-epsilon}` gain. A stretched-log improvement is not enough for B0. This likely requires bounded-size blocks or a uniform lower bound on usable conditional quadratic variation across `Theta(log n)` scales.

### C4. Two-coordinate compatibility

Return imposes both horizontal and vertical closure. Conditioning one coordinate family must not destroy the randomness needed in the other. The natural asymmetric attack is to freeze/reveal one family first, derive a one-coordinate gain, then swap roles or combine the two bounds.

### C5. Explicit continuous-time transfer

After the embedded estimate is obtained, the proof must state explicit finite constants and threshold under the source's continuous-time convention. No metric movement occurs before this bookkeeping is written.

## 9. (e) Nearest solved problems and the missing ingredients

### E1. Partially oriented random lattice: the `n^{-5/4}` local limit

Castell--Guillotin-Plantard--Pene--Schapira obtain the `n^{-5/4}` return scale in the relevant finite-variance partially oriented class. Their key structural advantage is an orientation-independent one-dimensional skeleton: after conditioning on its local times, the scenery variables remain independent and the Fourier characteristic function factors over visited levels.

The full Manhattan model lacks exactly this separation. The occupation profile is generated using the same orientation fields being averaged.

### E2. Martingale defocusing

Peres--Schapira--Sousi show that adapted martingales can be forced away from a point when conditional variance accumulates and jumps are controlled, using a multiscale sequence of spacetime rectangles. This is conceptually closer to what I0002 now needs than a fixed-coefficient Littlewood--Offord theorem because it tolerates adaptation.

What it has that Manhattan currently lacks is a **genuine fresh martingale increment** on enough scales. Reusing a revealed static line supplies deterministic repetition, not new conditional variance. The missing source-specific step is therefore to build a deferred-line filtration that converts spread line reuse into fresh conditional variance without changing the model.

### E3. Littlewood--Offord for Markov chains

Rao's theorem shows that dependent signs can still admit Littlewood--Offord bounds, at a spectral-gap cost, when the coefficient vectors are fixed. The Manhattan coefficients are chosen adaptively through the same signs. This confirms that sign dependence alone is not the central obstruction; coefficient/scenery feedback is.

These are bounded literature comparisons, not novelty certificates.
