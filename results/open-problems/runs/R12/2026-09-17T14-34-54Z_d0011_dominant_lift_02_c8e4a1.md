## 4. Method #3 — prove something stronger: a dominant-coordinate lifting theorem

The four-variable question suggested by the preceding run has a stronger recursive form.

### Theorem R12-D0011-C02A — dominant-coordinate lifting

Fix positive weights `w_1,...,w_n`, `n>=2`, and put

\[
W=\sum_{j=2}^n w_j.
\]

Assume

\[
\boxed{w_1\ge W.}\tag{4.1}
\]

Suppose the following tail property holds for the weights `(w_2,...,w_n)`:

> for **every** Bernoulli marginal vector `(q_2,...,q_n) in [0,1]^{n-1}`, the tail sum `sum_{j=2}^n w_j C_j` admits a convex-order least law among all couplings `C_j~Bernoulli(q_j)`.

Then for **every** `(p_1,...,p_n) in [0,1]^n`, the full weighted Bernoulli sum

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n w_iB_i
\]

admits a convex-order least law.

Moreover, a least coupling can be constructed in two stages:

1. simultaneously force every overlap `P(B_1=B_j=1)` to its Fréchet lower bound;
2. inside each slice `B_1=0` and `B_1=1`, use a convex-order least tail coupling for the induced Bernoulli margins.

### Proof

Fix arbitrary Bernoulli marginals `p_1,...,p_n` and an arbitrary initial coupling `q` on `{0,1}^n`. For `j>=2`, write

\[
u_j=\mathbb P_q(B_1=B_j=1),
\qquad
\ell_j=(p_1+p_j-1)_+.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every coupling satisfies `u_j>=ell_j` by the Fréchet bound.

#### Step 1: an overlap-reducing swap never increases any convex objective

Assume `u_j>ell_j`. Then both events

\[
A_j=\{B_1=1,B_j=1\},
\qquad
C_j=\{B_1=0,B_j=0\}
\]

have positive mass. Indeed,

\[
\mathbb P(C_j)=1-p_1-p_j+u_j>0
\]

precisely because `u_j>(p_1+p_j-1)_+`.

Choose atoms `a in A_j` and `c in C_j` of positive mass. Move an amount `epsilon>0`, not exceeding the smaller atom mass, from `a,c` to two new atoms obtained by swapping **only coordinate `j`** between them. This preserves every one-dimensional Bernoulli marginal.

Let

\[
r_a=\sum_{k\ne 1,j}w_k a_k,
\qquad
r_c=\sum_{k\ne 1,j}w_k c_k.
\]

Before the swap, the two weighted sums are

\[
H=w_1+w_j+r_a,
\qquad
L=r_c.
\]

After the swap, they are

\[
H'=w_1+r_a=H-w_j,
\qquad
L'=w_j+r_c=L+w_j.
\]

They have the same total:

\[
H+L=H'+L'.
\]

Also

\[
H'-L'=w_1+r_a-w_j-r_c
\ge w_1-w_j-(W-w_j)=w_1-W\ge0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
L\le L'\le H'\le H.
\]

The new pair is obtained from the old pair by a mean-preserving contraction. Hence, for every convex function `phi`,

\[
\boxed{\phi(L')+\phi(H')\le\phi(L)+\phi(H).}\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the swap weakly decreases **every convex objective simultaneously**.

The swap reduces `u_j` by exactly `epsilon`. It does not change `u_k` for any `k\ne j`, because it changes only coordinate `j` and leaves `B_1` and all other coordinates fixed. Repeating finitely many such mass transfers exhausts either an `A_j` or `C_j` source atom at each step and reaches

\[
u_j=\ell_j.
\]

Apply this successively for `j=2,...,n`. Earlier minimized overlaps are preserved. Hence from every coupling we can reach, without increasing any convex objective, a coupling satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(B_1=B_j=1)=\ell_j
\quad(j=2,...,n).
}\tag{4.5}
\]

#### Step 2: the conditional tail marginals are then forced

Assume first `0<p_1<1`. Under (4.5), for each `j>=2`,

\[
\mathbb P(B_j=1\mid B_1=1)=\frac{\ell_j}{p_1},
\tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb P(B_j=1\mid B_1=0)=\frac{p_j-\ell_j}{1-p_1}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus once all overlaps with the dominant coordinate are pushed to their lower Fréchet bounds, the one-dimensional tail marginals inside each `B_1` slice are no longer free.

By the tail hypothesis, there exists a convex-order least weighted tail sum in each slice for exactly those induced Bernoulli margins. Choose those two least tail couplings, one conditional on `B_1=0` and one conditional on `B_1=1`.

For an arbitrary convex `phi`, the `B_1=0` slice minimizes

\[
\mathbb E\phi\!\left(\sum_{j=2}^n w_jB_j\right),
\]

while the `B_1=1` slice minimizes

\[
\mathbb E\phi\!\left(w_1+\sum_{j=2}^n w_jB_j\right),
\]

because `x -> phi(w_1+x)` is again convex. Combined with Step 1, the resulting full coupling has no larger `E phi(S)` than **any** starting coupling, for **every** convex `phi`.

All full couplings have the same mean `sum_i w_i p_i`, so this is exactly

\[
S_*\le_{cx}S
\]

for every competing coupling.

If `p_1=0` or `p_1=1`, only one slice is present and the conclusion reduces immediately to the tail hypothesis. This proves the theorem. `square`

## 5. Infinite-class corollary — a dominant-gap chain terminating in three arbitrary gaps

The preceding R12 theorem supplies the three-coordinate base case with **no restriction on the final three gaps**. Iterating Theorem C02A therefore gives a much larger class.

### Corollary R12-D0011-C02B

Let `F_1,...,F_n` be arbitrary at-most-two-point laws. Let `w_i>0` be their nonzero atom gaps. Suppose the coordinates can be permuted so that

\[
\boxed{
 w_k\ge\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}w_j
 \qquad\text{for every }k=1,...,n-3.
}\tag{5.1}
\]

There is **no restriction** on the final three gaps `w_{n-2},w_{n-1},w_n`, and no restriction on any Bernoulli probability or atom location.

Then the aggregation set contains a convex-order smallest element.

### Proof

For `n<=3`, this is the previous R12 three-two-point theorem. For `n>=4`, the first gap dominates the total remaining gap by (5.1), and the tail `(w_2,...,w_n)` again satisfies the same hypothesis with one fewer coordinate. Inductively the tail admits a least aggregate for every induced probability vector. Apply Theorem C02A. `square`

For `n=4`, the statement is already a nontrivial new four-margin class:

\[
\boxed{
\max_i w_i\ge\sum_{j\ne i}w_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{every choice of four two-point margins has a convex-order least aggregate.}
}\tag{5.2}
\]

The construction is explicit and recursive: anti-align the dominant Bernoulli coordinate with each other coordinate down to the pairwise Fréchet floor, then solve the two conditional tail problems using the three-variable formula from the preceding run; repeat recursively in longer dominant-gap chains.

## 7. Method #20 — find why the lifting algorithm fails outside dominance

The dominance condition in Theorem C02A is not mere slack in the proof: the exact overlap-reducing swap can reverse direction for a convex objective once the dominant gap no longer controls the entire tail.

Take weights

\[
(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)=(4,3,2,2).
\]

For `j=3`, compare two source atoms

\[
a=(1,0,1,0),\qquad c=(0,1,0,1).
\]

Their weighted sums are

\[
S(a)=6,\qquad S(c)=5.
\]

Swapping only coordinate `3` to reduce the `(B_1,B_3)=(1,1)` overlap produces

\[
a'=(1,0,0,0),\qquad c'=(0,1,1,1),
\]

with

\[
S(a')=4,\qquad S(c')=7.
\]

The total is preserved, `6+5=4+7=11`, but for the convex objective `phi(x)=x^2`,

\[
6^2+5^2=61<65=4^2+7^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

So the overlap-reducing swap **increases** a convex objective. This is an exact obstruction to extending the dominant-lifting proof mechanically to all four weights. It is **not** a counterexample to the existence of a convex-order least aggregate for those margins; it only kills this proof algorithm outside its dominance chamber.
