## 3. Method #5 — correlated-environment interpolation and an exact parity formula

The previous run tried to decouple the scenery from its occupation field. A cleaner interpolation is available before conditioning on any occupation field: correlate **two complete environments** and let Boolean noise do the bookkeeping exactly.

Let `L` be the countable set of all horizontal rows and vertical columns, and identify an environment with

\[
\omega=(\omega_\ell)_{\ell\in L}\in\{-1,+1\}^{L}
\]

under product fair measure. For `s>=0` and `z in Z^2` set

\[
h_{s,z}(\omega):=p_s^\omega(0,z).
\]

Every `h_{s,z}` lies in `L^2` of the product space. Write its Walsh expansion

\[
h_{s,z}(\omega)=\sum_{S\Subset L}\widehat h_{s,z}(S)\chi_S(\omega),
\qquad
\chi_S(\omega)=\prod_{\ell\in S}\omega_\ell.
\]

Define the nonnegative chaos energies

\[
E_k(s):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}\sum_{|S|=k}\widehat h_{s,z}(S)^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

These sums are finite in total because

\[
\sum_z\|h_{s,z}\|_2^2
=\mathbb E\sum_z p_s^\omega(0,z)^2
\le \mathbb E\sum_z p_s^\omega(0,z)=1.
\]

For `r in [-1,1]`, couple two environments `(omega,eta)` independently line by line with uniform marginals and

\[
\mathbb E[\omega_\ell\eta_\ell]=r.
\]

Put

\[
K_s(r):=\mathbb E_r\sum_z h_{s,z}(\omega)h_{s,z}(\eta).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### C06 — exact noise polynomial and the source return as spectral parity bias

For every `r in [-1,1]`,

\[
\boxed{K_s(r)=\sum_{k\ge0}E_k(s)r^k.}
\tag{C06}
\]

At the three distinguished correlations,

\[
K_s(1)=\mathbb E\sum_z p_s^\omega(0,z)^2,
\tag{3.3}
\]

\[
K_s(0)=\sum_z\overline p_s(0,z)^2,
\tag{3.4}
\]

and, using `eta=-omega` at `r=-1` together with the preceding run's adjoint/collision identity,

\[
\boxed{K_s(-1)=\overline p_{2s}(0,0).}
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

For correlated Rademacher copies,

\[
\mathbb E_r[\chi_S(\omega)\chi_T(\eta)]
=\mathbf 1_{S=T}r^{|S|}.
\]

Insert the Walsh expansions, use Parseval and Tonelli (the total squared energy is finite), and sum over `z`; this gives C06. The cases `r=1` and `r=0` are immediate. At `r=-1`, the coupling is deterministically `eta=-omega`, so

\[
K_s(-1)=\mathbb E\sum_z p_s^\omega(0,z)p_s^{-\omega}(0,z),
\]

which equals `bar p_{2s}(0,0)` by C03. QED.

Now normalize the nonnegative energies at `r=1`. If `K_s(1)>0`, define a random integer `D_s` by

\[
\mathbb P(D_s=k)=\frac{E_k(s)}{K_s(1)}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then C06 becomes the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\overline p_{2s}(0,0)
=K_s(1)\,\mathbb E[(-1)^{D_s}].
}
\tag{C06a}
\]

Because the left side is nonnegative and `|K_s(-1)|<=K_s(1)`, the parity bias satisfies

\[
0\le \mathbb E[(-1)^{D_s}]\le1.
\]

The source Nash bound (2.1) gives, pathwise in `omega`,

\[
\sum_z p_s^\omega(0,z)^2
\le \sup_z p_s^\omega(0,z)\sum_zp_s^\omega(0,z)
\le\frac2{s+2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{K_s(1)\le\frac2{s+2}.}
\tag{C06b}
\]

Combining C06a and C06b isolates a new exact sufficient target:

> If for some `epsilon>0` one can prove
> \[
> \mathbb E[(-1)^{D_s}]\le C s^{-\epsilon}\qquad(s\ge s_0),
> \tag{3.7}
> \]
> then the exact source problem closes with `beta=1+epsilon`; indeed for `t=2s>=2s0`,
> \[
> \overline p_t(0,0)\le 2^{2+\epsilon}C\,t^{-(1+\epsilon)}.
> \]

This is sharper than a generic influence target: **Problem 2 is a parity-mixing problem for the line-environment spectral degree.** A large mean degree alone is not enough, because a degree distribution supported on even integers can have arbitrarily large mean and parity bias one.

### C07 — exact derivative / influence identities, and why they stop short

Since C06 has nonnegative coefficients,

\[
K_s'(r)=\sum_{k\ge1}kE_k(s)r^{k-1},
\tag{C07}
\]

and every derivative of `K_s` is nonnegative on `[0,1]`. In particular

\[
\boxed{
\frac{K_s'(1)}{K_s(1)}=\mathbb E D_s.
}
\tag{C07a}
\]

More generally, for `0<r<=1`, the logarithmic derivative is the mean degree under the positive tilted law proportional to `E_k(s)r^k`:

\[
r\frac{d}{dr}\log K_s(r)
=\frac{\sum_k kE_k(s)r^k}{\sum_kE_k(s)r^k}.
\tag{C07b}
\]

Thus the interpolation gives a clean influence observable. The obstruction is equally clean: all positive-correlation derivatives see the size of `D_s`, whereas the source return at `r=-1` sees `D_s mod 2`. The next theorem must create **aperiodicity in spectral degree**, not merely spectral growth.

This is the main outcome of method #5: the coupled-scenery replacement problem from C05 is converted into a one-dimensional Boolean-noise endpoint problem with an exact derivative calculus.

## 4. Method #21 — port the solved partially oriented endpoint through a vertical-persistence parameter

The old local-limit theorem suggests a second interpolation whose endpoint is already solved.

Keep horizontal line orientations `H_y` static as in the source. Let vertical line signs `V_x` also be iid fair, and for `q in [0,1]` define the continuous-time generator

\[
\begin{split}
(\mathcal G_q f)(x,y)
&=f(x+H_y,y)-f(x,y)\\
&\quad+\frac{1+qV_x}{2}[f(x,y+1)-f(x,y)]
+\frac{1-qV_x}{2}[f(x,y-1)-f(x,y)].
\end{split}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathcal G_q=S+A_H+qA_V,
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `S` is the rate-two simple-random-walk symmetric part and `A_H,A_V` are the horizontal and vertical skew parts.

At `q=1`, (4.1) is exactly the full Manhattan source model. At `q=0`, vertical jumps are fresh symmetric `+/- e2` jumps while horizontal rows retain iid fixed orientations. The embedded chain chooses the oriented horizontal step with probability `1/2` and a symmetric vertical step with total probability `1/2`, so it lies in the general partially oriented class covered by the Castell--Guillotin-Plantard--Pene--Schapira local limit theorem. In the Rademacher/Rademacher case that theorem gives return probability of order `n^{-5/4}` (the Campanino--Petritis `p=1/3` walk is one named member; their theorem allows the horizontal/vertical mixing parameter to vary).

Poissonization preserves the upper exponent: if the `q=0` embedded return satisfies `p_n^{(0)}(0,0)<=C(n+1)^{-5/4}`, then with the rate-two clock `N_t`, split on `N_t>=t` and use a Poisson lower-tail bound on `N_t<t` to obtain

\[
\boxed{\overline p_t^{(0)}(0,0)=O(t^{-5/4}).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore the following is a **source-closing bridge**:

\[
\boxed{
\overline p_t^{(1)}(0,0)
\le C_0\,\overline p_t^{(0)}(0,0)
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }t
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

would immediately prove the original source target with

\[
\boxed{\beta_{cert}\ge5/4.}
\]

I have **not** proved (4.4), so the metric does not move. But this is substantially narrower than the previous generic decoupling request: it asks for a comparison along the one-parameter skew interpolation `S+A_H+qA_V` between one endpoint with a complete local limit theorem and the source endpoint.

There is an exact Duhamel derivative. For fixed environment and any `q` at which the bounded finite-time semigroup differentiation is justified,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dq}\,p_t^{(q),\omega}(0,0)
=
\int_0^t
\langle \delta_0,
 e^{(t-u)\mathcal G_q}A_Ve^{u\mathcal G_q}\delta_0

\tag{C08}
\]

Averaging gives the corresponding derivative for `bar p_t^(q)`. Because `V\mapsto -V` has the same law, the annealed return is even in `q`; in particular its first derivative at zero vanishes. What is missing is a sign or an integrable upper control on C08 for `0<q<1`. ## 5. Method #25 — count the same pair kernel as Walsh energy and as line-polymer overlap

C06 has a second exact derivation from the path representation of the preceding run. The equality reveals a non-obvious positivity statement on signed line overlaps.

Work first in discrete time. For a length-`n` path `alpha` from `0` to `z`, let `L(alpha)` be the number of used horizontal rows plus vertical columns, and let `sigma_alpha(ell) in {+1,-1}` be the required orientation on a used line `ell`; paths inconsistent on a line have zero weight and are omitted.

Take two line-consistent paths `alpha,beta` from `0` to the same `z`, one in `omega` and one in an `r`-correlated environment `eta`. On a line used by exactly one path the environment cost is `1/2`. On a shared line, the exact joint probability of the two requested signs is

\[
\mathbb P(\omega_\ell=\sigma_\alpha(\ell),
          \eta_\ell=\sigma_\beta(\ell))
=
\frac14\left(1+r\,\sigma_\alpha(\ell)\sigma_\beta(\ell)\right).
\]

Let

\[
\tau_\ell=\sigma_\alpha(\ell)\sigma_\beta(\ell).
\]

Including the two independent axis-choice probabilities gives

### C09 — exact correlated path-pair formula

\[
\boxed{
K_n(r)=4^{-n}
\sum_z\sum_{\alpha,\beta:0\to z}
2^{-L(\alpha)-L(\beta)}
\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal L(\alpha)\cap\mathcal L(\beta)}
(1+r\tau_\ell).
}
\tag{C09}
\]

The sums are over individually line-consistent paths.

Expanding the product in `r` and comparing with the Walsh derivation gives, for every `k>=0`, the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
4^{-n}\sum_z\sum_{\alpha,\beta:0\to z}
2^{-L(\alpha)-L(\beta)}
 e_k\!\left((\tau_\ell)_{\ell\in\mathcal L(\alpha)\cap\mathcal L(\beta)}\right)
=
\sum_z\sum_{|S|=k}\widehat h_{n,z}(S)^2
\ge0.
}
\tag{C09a}
\]

Here `e_k` is the elementary symmetric polynomial. The left side is a **signed** path-pair sum and is not termwise nonnegative; its positivity is hidden on the polymer side and becomes automatic on the Walsh side. This is a genuine count-twice identity.

At `r=-1`, every shared line with `tau_ell=+1` kills the pair and every shared line with `tau_ell=-1` contributes a factor two. If `beta` is reversed in time, these surviving opposite-sign requirements become exactly the compatibility condition for the two halves to form one source-consistent closed loop of length `2n`. Consequently C09 at `r=-1` is exactly the C01 closed-loop count of the preceding run, while C06 says the same number is the even-minus-odd Walsh energy.

Thus the source return has three exactly equivalent descriptions:

\[
\boxed{
\text{closed line-polymer mass}
=
\text{opposite-environment collision mass}
=
\text{even chaos energy}-\text{odd chaos energy}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The new leverage is conceptual but precise: to gain a power over `t^{-1}`, it is enough to exhibit cancellation between the even and odd chaos energies. On the path side the same cancellation is encoded by signed elementary overlap sums. A future argument may choose whichever side exposes the needed pairing/involution.

## 6. What failed, and what a successful proof must add

Three tempting shortcuts were tested conceptually and rejected.

1. **Large total influence is insufficient.** C07 can show large mean spectral degree if one controls influences, but parity bias can remain one on a purely even degree distribution. The source target needs mod-two spectral mixing.

2. **The partially oriented theorem cannot simply be conditioned into the full model.** Its decisive product characteristic function comes from independence of local times and scenery. In C05 the local-time/occupation field already contains feedback from the same signs one wants to average.

3. **Generic skew drift does not give the needed monotonicity.** In the interpolation `S+A_H+qA_V`, the quadratic resolvent penalty contains horizontal/vertical cross terms. A comparison between `q=0` and `q=1` therefore requires Manhattan symmetries or a special path pairing, not the slogan that antisymmetric drift always lowers returns.

The strongest source-closing subtargets now are either

\[
\mathbb E[(-1)^{D_s}]\le Cs^{-\epsilon}
\tag{6.1}
\]

for some `epsilon>0`, or the endpoint comparison (4.4), which would in fact give the explicit exponent `5/4`.
