3. **Fast conditional mixing or an equally strong substitute.** Prove a weighted-norm estimate of the schematic form

\[
\left\|
\frac{\alpha K_t}{\alpha K_t\mathbf 1}-\nu
\right\|_V
\le C e^{-\gamma t}
\tag{I.2 target}

uniformly over the family of first-contact laws generated by the source front, for some quasi-stationary profile `nu` and gap `gamma>0`; or prove another estimate strong enough to replace (I.2). This report does **not** assert (I.2) for the source.

# 9. Method #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: target a reservoir state-space-collapse theorem

The previous route tried to prove only the scalar predictable-characteristics statement (I.1). This method deliberately strengthens the intermediate target so that (I.1) becomes a corollary rather than a sequence of ad hoc error estimates.

## 9.1 Proposed exact state

In the one-type random-regular dissipative special case, let `W_N(t)` be inactive vertices that have experienced exactly one failed-neighbor contact and have not crossed threshold. Give each such vertex a mark `z` sufficient to restart its source SDE from the current time until its next neighbor failure. Because the underlying diffusion is Markov after the current environment is specified, the mark should be chosen from the source dynamics themselves; at minimum it contains the present diffusion state and the relevant one-contact environment/time information.

Define the empirical reservoir measure

\[
\mu_N(t)=\sum_{v\in W_N(t)}\delta_{z_v(t)}.
\]

This is a proposed bookkeeping object, not a claim that a particular reduced coordinate is already sufficient.

## 9.2 Stronger target

Rather than estimate each second-contact error separately, seek a state-space-collapse statement of the schematic form

\[
\boxed{
\mu_N(t)
=
W_N(t)\,\nu_t
+\mathcal E_N(t),
\qquad
\|\mathcal E_N(t)\|_{V}
=O_{\mathbb P}(N^{1/3})
}
\tag{S.1 target}
\]

uniformly through the critical time interval, where `W_N(t)=Theta(N^{2/3})` on that interval and `nu_t` is either a fixed one-contact QSD or a low-dimensional slowly varying family slaved to the active frontier. Again, (S.1) is a **target**.

Why this is the right strengthening: a uniformly sampled reservoir half-edge then sees a deviation of relative order

\[
N^{1/3}/N^{2/3}=N^{-1/3}.
\]

Since the probability that the next exploration pairing hits the reservoir is itself of order

\[
W_N/N=N^{-1/3},
\]

the induced one-step frontier-drift error is heuristically of order `N^{-2/3}`, safely below the `N^{-1/3}` leading drift in (I.1a). This arithmetic is a **route calculation**, not a theorem about the source.

## 9.3 Why QSD mixing is a plausible mechanism rather than a decorative analogy

At any bounded physical-time horizon, newly first-contacted survivors form only a front-width layer if the rate of new contacts is proportional to the active frontier. Older one-contact survivors have had time to relax under the killed one-contact semigroup. If that semigroup has a uniform spectral/QSD gap, then the reservoir naturally splits into:

- a “young” non-equilibrated layer of order front width;
- an “old” bulk of order explored volume whose conditional mark law is close to a common profile.

That is exactly the source's qualitative desideratum that critical error accumulation should be controlled by **front width rather than cluster volume**. The point of method #3 is to prove the stronger reservoir collapse that would make this slogan quantitative.

# 10. Method #13 — FIND WHAT ONLY INCREASES / LYAPUNOV MONOVARIANT: response-weighted deviation from the collapsed reservoir

There is no obvious scalar quantity that literally increases monotonically in physical time, because an OU survivor both receives new forcing and relaxes. The useful “monovariant” is therefore a dissipative Lyapunov quantity whose deterministic drift has one sign between injections.

## 10.1 Candidate deviation functional

For a probability law `alpha` of a one-contact survivor, define a distance `D(alpha,nu)` in a weighted norm compatible with the killed semigroup. For the finite reservoir, consider

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_N(t)
=
\sum_{v\in W_N(t)}
D\big(\alpha_{v,t},\nu\big),
}
\tag{L.1 target}
\]

where `alpha_{v,t}` is the conditional law/state needed for that vertex's next-contact response. This is a proposed Lyapunov functional, not an asserted source identity.

If the one-contact killed semigroup has gap `gamma>0`, the desired estimate would have the forced-dissipative form

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathbb E[\mathcal L_N(t)]
\le
-\gamma\,\mathbb E[\mathcal L_N(t)]
+C\,\mathbb E[A_N(t)]
+\text{controlled echo forcing}.
}
\tag{L.2 target}
\]

In the source dissipative regime, the echo forcing is exactly the place where the existing geometric decay should be used; it should not be discarded as if it were the main finite-size effect.

If `A_N=O_P(N^{1/3})` and (L.2) can be proved uniformly, then the stable forced system suggests `L_N=O_P(N^{1/3})`, precisely the scale required in (S.1). This would convert the source's qualitative front-width intuition into a single energy estimate.

## 10.2 Why this is better than another per-vertex coupling

A per-vertex worst-case bound accumulates over `Theta(N^{2/3})` explored vertices and reproduces the bad cluster-volume factor. The proposed Lyapunov sums deviations **after** allowing old marks to contract. It asks only that the non-equilibrated mass be replenished at frontier rate and dissipated at a positive spectral rate.

**Outcome of method #13:** a concrete candidate energy estimate now explains how front-width accumulation could emerge. It remains unproved for the exact source semigroup and therefore does not move the metric.

# 11. Method #19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: exact event-driven marked exploration before taking limits

The reduced two-hit model hid the hard step by replacing a survivor's full state by one number `q`. The correct algorithmic reformulation should make that omission impossible.

## 11.1 Event-driven source-valid skeleton to build

For the homogeneous one-type random-regular special case, the finite cascade should be represented as a marked exploration with the following state components:

1. unmatched graph half-edges and the active failed frontier;
2. current failed/healthy status of exposed vertices;
3. for every exposed healthy vertex adjacent to at least one failed vertex, the current source-SDE state needed to continue its threshold evolution;
4. first-contact time / current failed-neighbor environment when this information is not already encoded in the Markov state;
5. a priority/event mechanism for threshold crossings and graph contacts, using the source killed transition law rather than a memoryless Bernoulli substitute.

The algorithm should expose randomness only when needed. In the one-contact sector, all current marks are summarized by `mu_N`; a second graph contact samples a vertex from that reservoir according to its number of still-unmatched half-edges, updates the SDE environment, and then follows the exact source law.

## 11.2 Generator decomposition to seek

Once the exact marked process is written, its generator should decompose conceptually into

\[
\mathcal G_N
=
\mathcal G_{\rm fast}^{\rm killed\;OU}
+
\mathcal G_{\rm inject}^{\rm first\;contact}
+
\mathcal G_{\rm sample}^{\rm second\;contact}
+
\mathcal G_{\rm graph}^{\rm depletion/collision}
+
\mathcal G_{\rm echo}.
\tag{A.1 target}
\]

This is a roadmap decomposition, not an asserted exact formula. Its value is that each previously disconnected technique gets one precise job:

- killed-OU/QSD theory controls `G_fast`;
- front-width bookkeeping controls `G_inject`;
- the reservoir law produces the true second-contact response in `G_sample`;
- configuration-model exploration supplies the parabolic depletion in `G_graph`;
- the source's `kappa<1` argument bounds `G_echo`.

## 11.3 The Poisson-equation / averaging formulation

If `G_fast` has a spectral gap, the natural fast-slow method is to solve a Poisson equation for the centered second-contact response `r(z)-nu(r)`, schematically

\[
-\mathcal G_{\rm fast}u
=r-\nu(r).
\tag{A.2 target}
\]

Then the cumulative error from using the averaged response `nu(r)` can be expressed as a martingale plus boundary/injection terms rather than bounded independently at every contact. This is the same structural move that often converts a volume error into a fluctuation/front error in averaging theory.

The resulting effective scalar `q_eff=nu(r)` would replace the ad hoc `q` of the two-hit surrogate only **after** (A.2) and the reservoir estimate are proved. In finite type, `q_eff` becomes a response tensor contracted against Perron left/right vectors.

**Outcome of method #19:** the remaining layer now has an explicit algorithmic object and a fast-slow decomposition. No source theorem is claimed.
