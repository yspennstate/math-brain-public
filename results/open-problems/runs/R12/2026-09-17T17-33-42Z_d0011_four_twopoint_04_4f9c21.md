## 5. Method #24 — find the extremal object: the simplex containing the marginal vector

Lemma 4.1 turns the full optimization problem into a one-line construction.

Fix arbitrary probabilities

\[
p=(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)\in[0,1]^4.
\]

Choose `J=A` or `B` according to the weight chamber. Because `T_J` triangulates `[0,1]^4`, there is a simplex

\[
T=\operatorname{conv}\{v^{(0)},\ldots,v^{(4)}\}\in T_J
\]

containing `p`. Let

\[
p=\sum_{r=0}^4\lambda_r v^{(r)},
\qquad
\lambda_r\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_r\lambda_r=1
\tag{5.1}
\]

be its barycentric representation.

Define a Bernoulli vector `B_*` by

\[
P(B_*=v^{(r)})=\lambda_r.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equation (5.1) gives

\[
E B_*=p,
\]

so each coordinate has exactly the prescribed Bernoulli marginal.

Now fix **any** threshold `t`. Let `ell=ell_{T,w,t}` be the affine interpolant from Lemma 4.1. For an arbitrary competing coupling `B` with the same marginal vector `p`, (4.3) gives pointwise on the cube

\[
h_t(w\cdot B)\ge \ell(B).
\]

Taking expectations and using affinity,

\[
E h_t(w\cdot B)
\ge E\ell(B)
=\ell(EB)
=\ell(p).
\tag{5.3}
\]

For `B_*`, all mass lies on the five interpolation vertices, so equality holds pointwise there and

\[
E h_t(w\cdot B_*)
=\sum_r\lambda_r h_t(w\cdot v^{(r)})
=\ell(p).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Combining (5.3)--(5.4),

\[
\boxed{
E(w\cdot B_*-t)_+
\le E(w\cdot B-t)_+
\quad\text{for every }t\in\mathbb R
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

for every competing coupling `B`.

All couplings have the same mean

\[
E(w\cdot B)=w\cdot p.
\]

By the standard stop-loss characterization of convex order, equal means plus (5.5) imply

\[
\boxed{w\cdot B_*\le_{cx}w\cdot B}
\]

for every admissible coupling. Restoring the translation `A` proves Theorem R12-D0011-C05.

The stop-loss equivalence itself is standard/folklore and was already the recovered intake reduction `D0011-A001`; no novelty is claimed for that criterion. The new content is the chamber-uniform common triangulation/certificate and its consequence for arbitrary four two-point margins.
