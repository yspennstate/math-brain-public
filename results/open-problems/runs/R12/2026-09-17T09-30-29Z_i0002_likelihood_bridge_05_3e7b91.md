## 3. Method #1 — change category: from a disordered walk to a sequential likelihood-betting problem

The preceding run showed that a positive pathwise comparison to the partially oriented model fails exponentially: if a vertical line is reused many times, the full/static model can assign an exponentially larger likelihood than the fresh-vertical endpoint. That is the wrong category for the comparison.

The right object is a **likelihood-ratio martingale**.

### 3.1 The one-coordinate baseline

Work first with the embedded discrete chain and compare only the vertical mechanism.

Under the baseline law `P_0`:

- each step first chooses horizontal or vertical with probability `1/2`;
- horizontal row orientations are iid static fair signs, exactly as in the full Manhattan model;
- whenever a vertical step is selected, its vertical direction is a fresh fair Rademacher sign.

Under the full law `P_1`, the only change is that every vertical column carries one iid static fair sign, reused at every vertical traversal of that column.

Index total steps by `j`. Under `P_0`, when step `j` is vertical let `C_j` be the current column immediately before the fresh vertical sign `eps_j` is drawn. The current column and the fact that it is new or old are known from the past. On the first vertical use of a column `c`, store the observed sign as `sigma_c`.

Define the one-step likelihood multiplier

\[
Z_j=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{if step }j\text{ is horizontal, or if }C_j\text{ is a new vertical column},\\[2mm]
2\,\mathbf 1_{\{\varepsilon_j=\sigma_{C_j}\}},&\text{if step }j\text{ is a vertical revisit}.
\end{cases}
\tag{C12a}
\]

and

\[
L_n:=\prod_{j=1}^n Z_j.
\tag{C12b}
\]

### Proposition C12 — adaptive static-line likelihood martingale

With respect to the natural baseline filtration,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_0[L_n\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]=L_{n-1},\qquad \mathbb E_0L_n=1.}
\tag{C12c}
\]

Moreover, for every event `A` determined by the trajectory and revealed line information through step `n`,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb P_1(A)=\mathbb E_0[L_n\mathbf 1_A].}
\tag{C12d}
\]

If `V_n` is the number of vertical steps through time `n`, `D_n` is the number of distinct columns used vertically, and

\[
R_n:=V_n-D_n
\]

is the number of vertical revisits, then

\[
\boxed{L_n=2^{R_n}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_n},}
\tag{C12e}
\]

where `C_n` is the event that all baseline vertical directions assigned to the same column agree. Under the full law, `C_n` is automatic and the realized density equals `2^{R_n}`.

#### Proof

If the current vertical column is new, the full model and baseline both draw a fair first sign, so the likelihood multiplier is one. If the column is old, its stored sign is measurable from the past while the baseline's current vertical sign is still fresh and fair. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E_0[Z_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
=\tfrac12\cdot 2+\tfrac12\cdot0=1
\]

on a revisit, and it is trivially one otherwise. Hence `L_n` is a nonnegative mean-one martingale.

Sequentially, `Z_j` is exactly the ratio of the full/static conditional law of the next vertical sign to the fresh baseline law: on a revisit the full law forces the previously stored sign, whereas the baseline gives each sign probability `1/2`. Multiplying these conditional ratios proves C12d. Every revisit contributes a factor two if it is consistent and kills the likelihood otherwise, which gives C12e. ∎

**Why this is a real change of category.** The exponential factor `2^{R_n}` found last run is not an uncontrolled error term. It is one side of an exact fair sequential bet: under the baseline, the equally important zero branch cancels it. The task is therefore not to dominate `2^{R_n}` pointwise; it is to understand how the terminal return event correlates with this mean-one betting martingale.

## 4. Method #12 — find what is conserved: adaptive Walsh/Krawtchouk cancellation survives the feedback

The preceding run identified the one-column interpolation kernel

\[
A_{r,k}(q)=\frac12\Big[(1+q)^k(1-q)^{r-k}+(1-q)^k(1+q)^{r-k}\Big].
\]

The martingale viewpoint reveals its exact Boolean structure.

Suppose, for the moment, a single column receives `r` baseline fresh signs `eps_1,...,eps_r`. The likelihood of the `q`-persistent latent-sign model relative to fresh signs is

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_c(q)
&=\frac12\prod_{i=1}^r(1+q\varepsilon_i)
 +\frac12\prod_{i=1}^r(1-q\varepsilon_i)\\
&=\boxed{\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[r]\\ |S|\ \mathrm{even}}}
q^{|S|}\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{C13a}
\]

Thus every nonconstant term is an even Walsh character and has zero expectation under fresh signs. At `q=1`,

\[
\boxed{
L_c(1)=\prod_{i=2}^r(1+\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_i)
=2^{r-1}\mathbf1_{\{\varepsilon_1=\cdots=\varepsilon_r\}}.
}
\tag{C13b}
\]

This is exactly the Krawtchouk cancellation from the last report, now written as a Walsh likelihood.

For a **fixed nonadaptive** partition of sign indices into columns, orthogonality immediately gives expectation one. In the Manhattan walk, however, the partition is adaptive: the column used at the next vertical time depends on the prior trajectory, and hence on prior signs. Ordinary tensor-product orthogonality cannot simply be applied after freezing the final partition.

Proposition C12 is the important conservation law: because the column assignment is **predictable before the current fresh sign is drawn**, the cancellation survives adaptivity one step at a time. The precise conserved quantity is

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_0L_n=1\quad\text{for every }n,}
\tag{C13c}
\]

not a small deterministic likelihood ratio.

### 4.1 Exact stopping identity at a reuse budget

Let

\[
\tau_m:=\inf\{n:R_n=m\}
\]

be the time of the `m`-th vertical revisit. For every deterministic horizon `N`, the stopped change-of-measure identity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_1(\tau_m\le N)
&=\mathbb E_0[L_{\tau_m}\mathbf1_{\{\tau_m\le N\}}]\\
&=2^m\,\mathbb P_0(\tau_m\le N,\mathcal C_{\tau_m}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_0(\tau_m\le N,\mathcal C_{\tau_m})
=2^{-m}\mathbb P_1(\tau_m\le N)
\le2^{-m}.
}
\tag{C14}
\]

This is stronger and more informative than merely applying Markov's inequality to `L_n`: the rare `2^{-m}` consistency event in the fresh model and the `2^m` static-line reward are exact duals.

It also explains why the previous positive-comparison idea was doomed. Under the fresh law, surviving many line-reuse constraints is exponentially rare; under the full law, those constraints are built into the environment and can be typical. The exponential density is not a loose estimate that can be sharpened away.

The likelihood-ratio martingale and bounded-stopping change-of-measure step are standard probability machinery. The report's claim is only the source-specific identification C12--C14 and its use to locate the Manhattan obstruction. Publication novelty of this reduction is **UNVERIFIED**.

## 5. A second category change: the full source return probability is an SRW-bridge likelihood expectation

The same construction can freshen **both** horizontal and vertical directions. Take ordinary planar simple random walk as baseline: at every step choose an axis and then a fresh fair sign, so each of the four neighboring steps has probability `1/4`.

Whenever the walk uses a horizontal row or vertical column for the first time in that direction class, the static Manhattan model has the same first fair sign and the likelihood multiplier is one. Whenever it reuses a line, multiply by two if the new fresh sign matches the first sign previously stored for that line and by zero otherwise.

Exactly the same predictable-sign argument proves that the resulting full two-axis density `Lambda_n` is a mean-one martingale and

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_n
=2^{n-\mathcal L_n}\mathbf1_{\{\gamma_n\text{ is line-consistent}\}},
}
\tag{C15a}
\]

where `L_n` is the number of distinct horizontal rows and vertical columns on which the length-`n` baseline path actually moves. This recovers the line-polymer weight from the first I0002 report, but now as a sequential martingale rather than only a path sum.

Let `S_n` be planar simple random walk and `X_n` the annealed embedded full Manhattan walk. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(X_n=0)
=\mathbb E_{\rm SRW}[\Lambda_n\mathbf1_{\{S_n=0\}}]
=\mathbb P_{\rm SRW}(S_n=0)\,
\mathbb E_{\rm SRW}[\Lambda_n\mid S_n=0].
}
\tag{C15b}
\]

For even times `n=2m`,

\[
\mathbb P_{\rm SRW}(S_{2m}=0)
=\frac{\binom{2m}{m}^2}{16^m}
\asymp \frac1m.
\tag{C15c}
\]

Therefore the discrete-time analogue of the source target is reduced exactly to a **bridge-likelihood decay problem**:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\rm SRW}[\Lambda_{2m}\mid S_{2m}=0]
=O(m^{-\varepsilon})
\quad\text{for some }\varepsilon>0.
}
\tag{T2}
\]

Any T2 estimate would give a discrete return exponent `1+eps`; standard Poissonization/de-Poissonization bookkeeping would then have to be supplied carefully before moving the frozen continuous-time metric.

This is, in my view, the cleanest formulation found so far. It removes the misleading exponential path ratio and asks directly for a polynomial **negative correlation between the line-consistency likelihood martingale and an SRW bridge**.

Transience alone does not give T2. It implies summability of the full return probabilities, hence only a weighted summability constraint on the bridge factors; as the scalar no-go from the first I0002 run showed, integrability plus smoothing need not imply a uniform polynomial pointwise improvement.

## 6. Method #28 — replace “all histories” by a reuse-budget regime: one part already has exponent >1

The likelihood martingale gives a rigorous restricted contribution with the desired power, without claiming anything about the unresolved complement.

Return to the one-coordinate partial baseline of Section 3. Let

\[
A_n:=\{X_n=0\}
\]

and keep `R_n=V_n-D_n`, the number of vertical line revisits. On the event `R_n<=m`, C12e gives `L_n<=2^m`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_1(A_n,R_n\le m)
\le 2^m\mathbb P_0(A_n).
}
\tag{C16a}
\]

Using the established `q=0` partially oriented local-limit input

\[
\mathbb P_0(A_n)=O(n^{-5/4})
\]

on the admissible parity subsequence, put

\[
m=\left\lfloor\theta\log_2 n\right\rfloor,
\qquad 0<\theta<\frac14.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_1(A_n,R_n\le\theta\log_2 n)
=O(n^{-5/4+\theta}).
}
\tag{C16b}
\]

In particular, with `theta=1/8`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_1(A_n,R_n\le\tfrac18\log_2 n)
=O(n^{-9/8}).
}
\tag{C16c}
\]

By symmetry the same estimate holds if horizontal line reuse is at most `(1/8) log_2 n`, using the horizontally refreshed partial endpoint. Thus all return histories with **low reuse in at least one direction** already satisfy an exponent strictly larger than one. The remaining discrete obstruction can be isolated as

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_1\!\left(
X_n=0,\;
R_n^{\rm vert}>\tfrac18\log_2 n,\;
R_n^{\rm horiz}>\tfrac18\log_2 n
\right).
}
\tag{T3}
\]

A bound `T3=O(n^{-1-eps})` for any `eps>0` would close the discrete source analogue.

### Important limitation

C16 is **PARTIAL**, not metric movement. The high-reuse event is not known to be rare under the full model and may well contain the dominant return mass. Formula C14 shows why naive typicality under the fresh baseline is misleading: the full measure exactly reweights the exponentially rare consistency histories back up. Thus `T3` is a sharper obligation, not a solved exceptional-set estimate.

Still, this is the first decomposition in the I0002 work where one entire source-relevant regime has a rigorously source-quality exponent `9/8` and the unresolved mass is specified by an intrinsic geometric statistic rather than by a failed proof technique.

## 7. What a successful proof now has to exploit

The likelihood representation also explains where a power can come from.

If the final partition of vertical steps into columns were fixed independently of the signs, a column used `r` times would contribute the Walsh factor C13. Mean one would be exact and there would be no gain merely from having many repeated uses. The bridge event, however, enforces two global signed-balance conditions. In an idealized frozen-profile model these become weighted Rademacher equations of the form

\[
\sum_y h_y\sigma_y=0,
\qquad
\sum_x v_x\tau_x=0,
\]

for row/column traversal counts `h_y,v_x`; ordinary Littlewood--Offord anti-concentration would then produce a power whenever many nonzero weights are present.

The actual Manhattan difficulty is that the weights and even the set of used lines are chosen adaptively using the same orientation signs. The source-level missing theorem can therefore be stated much more concretely than before:

> Recover any polynomial fraction of the frozen-profile two-equation anti-concentration gain when the line local-time profile is generated adaptively by the Manhattan trajectory.

This is the point where martingale small-ball technology, sequential Rademacher complexity, or an inverse Littlewood--Offord theorem adapted to predictable line assignments could conceivably enter. A bounded search this run did not locate a theorem that directly supplies such an adaptive two-equation estimate.

The general likelihood-martingale mechanism is folklore; the adaptive Littlewood--Offord step is the genuinely missing source-specific content.
