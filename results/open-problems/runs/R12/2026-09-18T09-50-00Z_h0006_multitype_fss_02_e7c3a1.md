## 2. The finite-type theorem proved in this run

I state the theorem in a form that makes every regularity assumption explicit.

Let the type set be `{1,...,d}`. For `epsilon>=0` near zero, let a particle of type `i` have offspring vector `K=(K_1,...,K_d)` with probability generating function

\[
f_i^{(\varepsilon)}(z)
=\mathbb E_i^{(\varepsilon)}\prod_{j=1}^d z_j^{K_j}.
\]

Assume:

1. the pgfs are analytic on one common polydisc strictly larger than the closed unit polydisc and are `C^3` in `epsilon` there;
2. the mean matrices
   \[
   M_\varepsilon=Df^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathbf1)
   \]
   are primitive with a uniform Perron spectral gap near `epsilon=0`;
3. the parameter is chosen so that
   \[
   \rho(M_\varepsilon)=1-\varepsilon;
   \]
4. the critical process is genuinely branching, so the Perron variance defined below is positive;
5. scalar total progeny is span-one (the standard aperiodicity condition; for span `q>1`, the same theorem holds on the admissible residue class with the usual span factor).

Let `pi` and `h` be the left and right Perron vectors of the critical matrix `M=M_0`, normalized by

\[
\pi\mathbf1=1,
\qquad
\pi h=1,
\qquad
Mh=h,
\qquad
\pi M=\pi.
\]

Write

\[
Q_i(a,b)
:=D^2f_i^{(0)}(\mathbf1)[a,b]
\]

and define the intrinsic Perron offspring variance

\[
\boxed{
\beta:=\pi Q(h,h)>0.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
S_h:=\sum_{u\text{ child of }v}h_{\operatorname{type}(u)},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\beta=\sum_i\pi_i\operatorname{Var}_i(S_h).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Indeed, `E_i S_h=h_i` and

\[
E_iS_h^2=Q_i(h,h)+(Mh^{\odot2})_i,
\]

so averaging the variance with `pi` cancels the last two terms because `pi M=pi`.

Let `T_\varepsilon` denote total progeny, started from type `i`. Then the following hold.

### Theorem A — quadratic Perron large-deviation rate

There exists an analytic singularity `r_\varepsilon>1` with

\[
\boxed{
\log r_\varepsilon
=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2\beta}+O(\varepsilon^3).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently the exponential total-progeny cutoff satisfies

\[
\boxed{
(\log r_\varepsilon)^{-1}
\sim 2\beta\,(1-\rho(M_\varepsilon))^{-2}
=2\beta\,\xi^2,
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

where the source parameter is `xi=(1-rho(M_epsilon))^{-1}`.

### Theorem B — explicit finite-size crossover

Let `epsilon_N ->0` satisfy

\[
\sqrt N\,\varepsilon_N\to\lambda\in[0,\infty),
\]

and let `m_N=floor(xN)` for fixed `x>0`. Then, uniformly for `x` in compact subsets of `(0,infinity)`,

\[
\boxed{
N^{3/2}\,
\mathbb P_i^{(\varepsilon_N)}(T_{\varepsilon_N}=m_N)
\longrightarrow
\frac{h_i}{\sqrt{2\pi\beta}}
 x^{-3/2}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\lambda^2x}{2\beta}\right].
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

For a fixed initial type distribution `alpha`, replace `h_i` by `alpha h`.

Thus the finite-type branching skeleton competes with finite size `N` when

\[
\boxed{
1-\rho(M_N)\asymp N^{-1/2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\xi_N\asymp\sqrt N.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

The source parameter `xi` is a susceptibility / mean-growth scale; the tail cutoff for total progeny is quadratic in it.

## 3. #11 DUALIZE — the critical Perron exponential tilt

The useful dual object is not a scalar Chernoff transform. It is a vector exponential tilt chosen so that the tilted multitype process is exactly critical.

For small `epsilon`, solve for a positive vector `tau_epsilon` and a scalar `r_epsilon` near `(1,1)`:

\[
\boxed{
\tau_\varepsilon
=r_\varepsilon f^{(\varepsilon)}(\tau_\varepsilon),
\qquad
\rho\!\left(r_\varepsilon Df^{(\varepsilon)}(\tau_\varepsilon)\right)=1.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

These equations have a unique analytic local solution. Here is the nondegeneracy calculation rather than an appeal to a black box.

At `(epsilon,tau,r)=(0,1,1)`, linearizing the first equation in `(x,a)=(delta tau,delta r)` gives

\[
(I-M)x-a\mathbf1=0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Linearizing the Perron equation in the same variables gives

\[
\pi Q(x,h)+a=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Left-multiplying (3.2) by `pi` yields `a=0`; hence `x=ch`. Then (3.3) gives `c beta=0`, so `c=0`. The Jacobian is therefore invertible because `beta>0`, proving the local implicit-function claim.

Differentiate (3.1) with respect to `epsilon` at zero. Since `f^(epsilon)(1)=1` for every `epsilon`, the first equation gives

\[
(I-M)\tau'_0=r'_0\mathbf1.
\]

Left multiplication by `pi` shows `r'_0=0`, so `tau'_0=ch`. The derivative of the Perron condition has one contribution `-1` from

\[
\frac d{d\varepsilon}\rho(M_\varepsilon)\bigg|_0=-1
\]

and contribution `c beta` from moving `tau` in the Perron direction. Therefore

\[
0=-1+c\beta,
\qquad
\boxed{
\tau_\varepsilon
=\mathbf1+\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta}h+O(\varepsilon^2).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

To obtain the first nonzero term of `r_epsilon`, put

\[
x=\tau_\varepsilon-\mathbf1,
\qquad
u=r_\varepsilon-1.
\]

Taylor expansion of the first equation in (3.1) gives

\[
(I-M_\varepsilon)x
=\nu\mathbf1+\frac12Q_\varepsilon(x,x)+O(\varepsilon^3),
\tag{3.5}
\]

because `x=O(epsilon)` and, as the equation itself now implies, `nu=O(epsilon^2)`. Let `pi_epsilon` be the left Perron probability for `M_epsilon`. Since

\[
\pi_\varepsilon(I-M_\varepsilon)
=\varepsilon\pi_\varepsilon,
\]

projection of (3.5) yields

\[
\varepsilon\pi_\varepsilon x
=\nu+rac12\pi_\varepsilon Q_\varepsilon(x,x)+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]

Using (3.4), continuity of the Perron vectors and `pi h=1`, `pi Q(h,h)=beta`,

\[
\varepsilon\pi_\varepsilon x
=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\beta}+O(\varepsilon^3),
\]

while

\[
\frac12\pi_\varepsilon Q_\varepsilon(x,x)
=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2\beta}+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
r_\varepsilon
=1+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2\beta}+O(\varepsilon^3),
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

which is equivalent to (2.3).

Now define the exactly tilted offspring law

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde p_i^{(\varepsilon)}(k)
=
\frac{p_i^{(\varepsilon)}(k)\,\tau_\varepsilon^k}
{f_i^{(\varepsilon)}(\tau_\varepsilon)}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Its mean matrix is

\[
\widetilde M_\varepsilon
=r_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(\tau_\varepsilon)^{-1}
Df^{(\varepsilon)}(\tau_\varepsilon)
\operatorname{diag}(\tau_\varepsilon),
\tag{3.8}
\]

which is similar to `r_epsilon Df^(epsilon)(tau_epsilon)` and therefore has spectral radius exactly one. The subcritical process has been dualized to an exactly critical process.

## 4. #25 COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE — exact tree-level cancellation

The key identity is exact for every finite typed tree, not merely asymptotic.

Let `t` be any finite rooted typed family tree of total size `n`, rooted at type `i`. Under the original law,

\[
P_\varepsilon(t)=\prod_{v\in t}p_{i_v}^{(\varepsilon)}(k_v).
\]

Using (3.7),

\[
p_{i_v}^{(\varepsilon)}(k_v)
=
\widetilde p_{i_v}^{(\varepsilon)}(k_v)
\frac{f_{i_v}^{(\varepsilon)}(\tau_\varepsilon)}
{\tau_\varepsilon^{k_v}}
=
\widetilde p_{i_v}^{(\varepsilon)}(k_v)
\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon,i_v}}
{r_\varepsilon\tau_\varepsilon^{k_v}}.
\]

Across all vertices, every non-root factor `tau_{type}` in the numerators is cancelled exactly once by the denominator factor contributed by its parent. Only the root survives. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_\varepsilon(t)
=\tau_{\varepsilon,i}\,r_\varepsilon^{-n}
\widetilde P_\varepsilon(t).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Summing (4.1) over **all** typed trees of size `n` yields the exact total-progeny identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_i^{(\varepsilon)}(T=n)
=	au_{\varepsilon,i}\,r_\varepsilon^{-n}
\widetilde{\mathbb P}_i^{(\varepsilon)}(T=n).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the multitype replacement for the one-type Dwass/binomial computation used in the previous R12 run.

### Critical factor in (4.2)

For completeness, I derive the critical `n^{-3/2}` constant rather than merely cite it.

For any fixed critical member of the tilted family, write `F_i(s)=E_i s^T`. Then

\[
F(s)=s\widetilde f(F(s)).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let

\[
z=\mathbf1-F(s),
\qquad
t=1-s.
\]

Taylor expansion of (4.3) at `(s,F)=(1,1)` gives

\[
(I-\widetilde M)z
=t\mathbf1-rac12\widetilde Q(z,z)
+O(t\|z\|+\|z\|^3).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Perron decomposition and the uniform spectral gap imply that the non-Perron component of `z` is `O(t)`, so

\[
z=a\widetilde h\sqrt t+O(t).
\]

Projecting (4.4) by the left Perron probability `widetilde pi` gives

\[
0=t-\frac{a^2}{2}\widetilde\beta t+o(t),
\]

where

\[
\widetilde\beta
=\widetilde\pi\widetilde Q(\widetilde h,\widetilde h).
\]

Thus

\[
a=\sqrt{2/\widetilde\beta}
\]

and

\[
F_i(s)
=1-\widetilde h_i\sqrt{\frac2{\widetilde\beta}}
\sqrt{1-s}+O(1-s).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The span-one positivity/aperiodicity assumption excludes other singularities of the same modulus. Standard square-root transfer therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde{\mathbb P}_i^{(\varepsilon)}(T=n)
=
\left(
\frac{\widetilde h_{\varepsilon,i}}
{\sqrt{2\pi\widetilde\beta_\varepsilon}}
+o(1)
\right)n^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The common analytic neighborhood, uniform primitive gap and aperiodicity make this estimate uniform as `epsilon ->0`; equivalently, one may invoke the standard positive-system square-root theorem of Drmota. Continuity gives

\[
\widetilde h_\varepsilon\to h,
\qquad
\widetilde\beta_\varepsilon\to\beta.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Combining (4.2), (3.4), (3.6), (4.6) and (4.7) proves Theorems A and B. Indeed, for `m_N=floor(xN)` and `sqrt(N)epsilon_N -> lambda`,

\[
m_N\log r_{\varepsilon_N}
\to\frac{\lambda^2x}{2\beta},
\]

while `tau_{epsilon_N,i}->1`; multiplying by `N^(3/2)m_N^(-3/2)->x^(-3/2)` gives exactly (2.5).

## 5. #18 SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE — Perron-mass exploration

The same constant has a probabilistic interpretation that makes the `sqrt(N)` window almost inevitable and clarifies what must survive at the finite-graph layer.

At criticality, explore one particle at a time and measure population in Perron mass `h`. A type-`i` explored particle removes mass `h_i` and replaces it by

\[
S_h=\sum_{u\text{ child}}h_{\operatorname{type}(u)}.
\]

Criticality says

\[
E_i(S_h-h_i)=0.
\]

Under the left Perron type-frequency `pi`, its one-step variance is exactly

\[
E_\pi\operatorname{Var}(S_h)=\beta.
\]

A subcritical gap `epsilon` introduces a drift of order `epsilon` per explored particle, whereas `n` critical exploration steps fluctuate on scale `sqrt(beta n)`. The drift and fluctuation compete when

\[
n\varepsilon\asymp\sqrt{\beta n},
\]

or

\[
\varepsilon\sqrt n=O(1).
\]

The exponential-tilt proof above turns this Markov-additive heuristic into the exact crossover law (2.5). It also identifies the correct variance as the Perron-weighted offspring-pair variance, agreeing with the neighboring H0011 track's independently derived CRT variance algebra; a scalar type-blind child-count variance is not invariant under the multitype reduction.
