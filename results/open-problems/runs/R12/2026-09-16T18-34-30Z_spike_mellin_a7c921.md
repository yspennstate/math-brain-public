## 4. R12-C09 — nonzero Fourier blocks are rank-two spike plus O(p)

**STATUS: PROVED.**  
**Scope:** all primes `p=1 mod 4`, every nonzero additive frequency.  
**Novelty:** no literature-novelty claim is made; the statement is recorded as a route theorem for I0102.

Let `chi` be the quadratic character on `F_p`, extended by `chi(0)=0`, and let

\[
\psi_\xi(t)=e^{2\pi i\xi t/p},\qquad \xi\ne0.
\]

Represent an undirected Paley edge as

\[
e_{x,[h]}=\{x-h,x+h\},
\]

where `chi(2h)=1` and `[h]` identifies `h` with `-h`. Fix any representative set `H`; its size is

\[
r=\frac{p-1}{4}.
\]

For two edges `e_{0,[h]}` and `e_{t,[k]}`, all four cross differences are

\[
t\pm h\pm k.
\]

Put

\[
q(z)=1_{\{z=0\text{ or }\chi(z)=1\}}
=\frac{1+\chi(z)+1_{z=0}}2.
\]

The exact edge-compatibility kernel, before the harmless loop subtraction, is

\[
\prod_{\epsilon,\delta=\pm1}q(t+\epsilon h+\delta k).
\]

For a nonzero additive frequency define its translation Fourier block

\[
A_\xi(h,k)
=
\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}
\psi_\xi(-t)
\left[
\prod_{\epsilon,\delta=\pm1}q(t+\epsilon h+\delta k)
-1_{\{t=0,h=k\}}
\right].
\tag{C09.1}
\]

### 4.1 Replace exact edge indicators by quadratic-character bulk

Let

\[
b(z)=\frac{1+\chi(z)}2.
\]

For fixed `h,k`, the products using `q` and `b` differ only when one of the four cross differences is zero, hence for at most four values of `t`. Both products lie in `[0,1]`; therefore every Fourier-block entry of their difference has absolute value at most `4`.

Since the block dimension is `r=(p-1)/4`, the row-sum bound gives

\[
\|E_\xi\|\le4r+1\le p+1,
\tag{C09.2}
\]

where the extra `1` includes the loop subtraction.

It remains to study the bulk block

\[
B_\xi(h,k)
=
\frac1{16}
\sum_t\psi_\xi(-t)
\prod_{\epsilon,\delta=\pm1}
(1+\chi(t+\epsilon h+\delta k)).
\tag{C09.3}
\]

The empty-subset term vanishes because `xi!=0`.

### 4.2 The exact rank-two spike

Write

\[
g_\xi=\sum_t\psi_\xi(-t)\chi(t),
\qquad |g_\xi|=\sqrt p,
\]

\[
c_\xi(h)=\psi_\xi(h)+\psi_\xi(-h),
\]

and

\[
F_\xi(a)=
\sum_t\psi_\xi(-t)\chi(t^2-a^2).
\]

The four singleton terms in (C09.3) sum to

\[
g_\xi c_\xi(h)c_\xi(k).
\]

Among the six quadratic terms, the four pairs sharing one sign coordinate sum to

\[
c_\xi(h)F_\xi(k)+F_\xi(h)c_\xi(k).
\]

Consequently

\[
S_\xi(h,k)
=
\frac1{16}
\left[
 g_\xi c_\xi(h)c_\xi(k)
+c_\xi(h)F_\xi(k)
+F_\xi(h)c_\xi(k)
\right]
\tag{C09.4}
\]

has

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}S_\xi\le2.}
\tag{C09.5}
\]

This is the deterministic spike predicted by the stuck-recovery diagnosis.

### 4.3 The two remaining quadratic terms cost at most 2p

The remaining two quadratic terms are

\[
F_\xi(h-k),\qquad F_\xi(h+k).
\]

On the full additive group `F_p`, the Fourier transform in `a` is

\[
\widehat F_\xi(\eta)
=p\,\chi(\xi^2-\eta^2).
\tag{C09.6}
\]

Indeed set `x=t-a`, `y=t+a`; the map `(t,a)<->(x,y)` is bijective because `p` is odd, and the two character factors separate. Hence convolution by `F_xi` has norm at most `p`, while the Hankel kernel `F_xi(h+k)` is convolution followed by reflection and has the same norm. Restriction to `H` can only reduce operator norm. Thus the two terms together cost at most

\[
2p.
\tag{C09.7}
\]

### 4.4 Each cubic term costs at most p

Consider one representative cubic term

\[
M(h,k)=
\sum_t\psi_\xi(-t)
\chi(t+h+k)\chi(t+h-k)\chi(t-h+k).
\]

Apply the unitary two-dimensional additive Fourier transform in `(h,k)`. With

\[
x=t+h+k,\quad y=t+h-k,\quad z=t-h+k,
\]

the transform factors into three Gauss sums and has the form

\[
\widehat M(\eta,\zeta)
=\sqrt p\,D_1(\eta)\,Q_{\eta\zeta}\,D_2(\zeta),
\tag{C09.8}
\]

where the diagonal multipliers have modulus at most one and

\[
Q_{\eta\zeta}=\chi(\zeta-\eta).
\]

The Paley character identity

\[
Q^2=pI-J
\]

gives `||Q||=sqrt(p)`. Therefore

\[
\|M\|\le p.
\tag{C09.9}
\]

There are four cubic terms, for a total cost at most `4p` before the global factor `1/16`.

### 4.5 Quartic term: projective reduction to a Legendre convolution

The quartic term is

\[
M_4(h,k)=
\sum_t\psi_\xi(-t)
\prod_{\epsilon,\delta=\pm1}
\chi(t+\epsilon h+\delta k).
\]

After the unitary two-dimensional Fourier transform, one obtains the real integer character sum

\[
T_\xi(\eta,\zeta)
=
\sum_{s\in\mathbb F_p}
\prod_{j=1}^4\chi(s-r_j),
\tag{C09.10}
\]

with

\[
r_1=\frac{\xi+\eta-\zeta}{2},\quad
r_2=-\frac\zeta2,\quad
r_3=\frac\eta2,\quad
r_4=\frac\xi2.
\]

Put

\[
A_u=\xi^2-u^2,
\qquad
E=\mathbb F_p\setminus\{\xi,-\xi\}.
\]

For `eta,zeta in E`, except `(eta,zeta)=(0,0)`, a projective change of variable sending three of the four roots to `0,1,infinity` gives

\[
\boxed{
T_\xi(\eta,\zeta)
=
\chi(A_\zeta)
L\!\left(\frac{A_\eta}{A_\zeta}\right)-1,
}
\tag{C09.11}
\]

where

\[
L(\lambda)=
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}
\chi(x(x-1)(x-\lambda)).
\tag{C09.12}
\]

The exceptional doubly-degenerate value is

\[
T_\xi(0,0)=p-2.
\tag{C09.13}
\]

If `eta=+-xi` or `zeta=+-xi`, direct reduction to a quadratic character sum gives

\[
|T_\xi(\eta,\zeta)|\le2.
\tag{C09.14}
\]

The finite exact checker archived with this run verifies (C09.11)--(C09.14) for every nonzero `xi` at `p=5,13,17,29,37`.

### 4.6 Mellin diagonalization gives exact p-scale ambient norm

For a multiplicative character `rho` on `F_p^*`, define

\[
\widehat L(\rho)
=
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}\rho(\lambda)L(\lambda).
\]

Interchanging the `x,lambda` sums and writing `lambda=xu` gives the exact Jacobi-sum identity

\[
\boxed{
\widehat L(\rho)=J(\rho,\chi)^2.
}
\tag{C09.15}
\]

For `rho` different from the trivial character and `chi`, the standard Jacobi-sum magnitude is

\[
|J(\rho,\chi)|=\sqrt p;
\]

for `rho=1` or `rho=chi`, its magnitude is `1`. Hence the multiplicative convolution matrix

\[
C_{a,b}=\chi(b)L(a/b),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb F_p^*,
\tag{C09.16}
\]

has exact operator norm

\[
\boxed{\|C\|=p.}
\tag{C09.17}
\]

Equivalently, and in a form checked by exact integer arithmetic,

\[
\boxed{
CC^T
=p^2I-(p+1)(J+\chi\chi^T).
}
\tag{C09.18}
\]

Let `P` pull functions on `F_p^*` back by `eta -> A_eta`. Every fiber has size at most two, so `||P||<=sqrt(2)`. Thus the generic part in (C09.11) has norm at most `2p`; the `-1` matrix contributes at most `p`; (C09.13) contributes at most `p`; and the two exceptional rows/columns in each direction contribute `O(sqrt(p))`, which is at most `2p` for `p>=5`. A safe uniform bound is therefore

\[
\boxed{\|M_4\|\le6p.}
\tag{C09.19}
\]

No attempt is made to optimize the constant.

Combining (C09.7), the four bounds (C09.9), and (C09.19), the part of the bulk block outside the spike satisfies

\|B_\xi-S_\xi\|
\le

Adding the exact-indicator correction (C09.2) gives

## 5. R12-C10 — exact K4 triality gluing identity

**STATUS: PROVED.**  
**Scope:** every affine-symmetrized feasible degree-four Paley pseudoexpectation.  
**Role:** identifies a full-SOS4 compatibility constraint discarded by the auxiliary edge-theta relaxation.

Let the affine-symmetrized edge-edge moment kernel be written

\[
F(u;h,k)
=
M(y)_{e_{0,[h]},e_{u,[k]}}.
\]

Whenever the two edges together form a clique, this entry is the pseudomoment of their union.

Parameterize four distinct vertices by

\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=m+r+s+t,\\
b&=m+r-s-t,\\
c&=m-r+s-t,\\
d&=m-r-s+t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{C10.1}
\]

Assume `{a,b,c,d}` is a `K4` in the Paley graph. Pairing the same four vertices into opposite edges in the three possible ways gives:

- `(ab)|(cd)`: centers `m+r,m-r`, half-lengths `s+t,s-t`;
- `(ac)|(bd)`: centers `m+s,m-s`, half-lengths `r+t,r-t`;
- `(ad)|(bc)`: centers `m+t,m-t`, half-lengths `r+s,r-s`.

Every corresponding edge-edge moment equals the same union moment

\[
y_{\{a,b,c,d\}}.
\]

Translation invariance therefore yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
F(-2r;s+t,s-t)
=
F(-2s;r+t,r-t)
=
F(-2t;r+s,r-s)
}
\tag{C10.2}
\]

for every admissible Paley `K4` quadruple (with half-lengths interpreted modulo sign).

Equivalently, if

\[
G(r,s,t)=F(-2r;s+t,s-t),
\]

then on the Paley-`K4` admissible locus,

\[
\boxed{G(r,s,t)\text{ is invariant under every permutation of }r,s,t.}
\tag{C10.3}
\]

This is the precise **gluing condition** absent from C05. The auxiliary theta matrix treats compatible edge pairs as independent entries subject only to PSD and zeros; a true degree-four moment matrix forces all three opposite-edge descriptions of a `K4` to agree.
