## 4. R12-C07 — a linear nonconstant eigenvalue in the edge-compatibility graph

**STATUS: PROVED.** No literature-novelty claim is made.

Recall C05's edge-compatibility graph `H_2(G)`: its vertices are the edges of `G`, and distinct graph-edges `e,f` are adjacent exactly when `e union f` is a clique of `G`.

Let `G=G_p` be the Paley graph, and write `H=H_2(G_p)`. Put

\[
n_H=|V(H)|=|E(G_p)|=\frac{p(p-1)}4.
\]

The Paley affine group is transitive on graph-edges, so `H` is regular; write its degree as `d_H`.

Fix a Paley vertex `v` and let

\[
\mathcal S_v=\{\{v,u\}:u\in N_G(v)\}.
\]

Then

\[
s:=|\mathcal S_v|=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

The induced compatibility graph on this star is exactly the one-localization `G_p[N(v)]`: two star edges `{v,u}` and `{v,w}` are compatible iff `{v,u,w}` is a triangle, equivalently iff `u~w`. Since Paley is strongly regular, this induced graph is

\[
\lambda=\frac{p-5}{4}
\]

regular.

For a fixed Paley edge `e={u,v}`, every `H`-neighbor `f` must have both of its endpoints in

\[
\{u,v\}\cup(N(u)\cap N(v)),
\]

which has size `lambda+2=(p+3)/4`. Therefore

\[
d_H\le \binom{(p+3)/4}{2}-1
=\frac{p^2+2p-35}{32}.
\tag{C07.1}
\]

Now center the star indicator:

\[
x=1_{\mathcal S_v}-\frac{s}{n_H}{\bf1}.
\]

Then `x perpendicular 1`, and, since `1_S^T A_H 1_S=s lambda`, direct expansion gives

\[
\frac{x^TA_Hx}{\|x\|_2^2}
=
\frac{\lambda-d_Hs/n_H}{1-s/n_H}.
\tag{C07.2}
\]

Because

\[
\frac{s}{n_H}=\frac2p,
\]

C07.1 yields

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_2(H_2(G_p))
\ge
\frac{(3p-7)(p-5)}{16(p-2)}
=\frac{3}{16}p+O(1).
}
\tag{C07.3}
\]

Here `lambda_2` means the largest adjacency eigenvalue on the orthogonal complement of the constants.

This is a deterministic all-prime statement for admissible `p>=13`; it is not a finite numerical extrapolation.

## 5. R12-X04 — adjacency/Hoffman theta duals cannot make C05 sublinear

**STATUS: PROVED RESTRICTED-FAMILY OBSTRUCTION.** This does **not** lower-bound the unrestricted auxiliary theta number.

C05 showed

\[
U_4(G)
\le
\frac{1+\sqrt{1+8\vartheta(\overline{H_2(G)})}}2.
\]

A tempting next step is to upper-bound `theta(overline H)` by a spectral dual depending only on `I`, `A_H`, and `J`. C07 proves that this whole certificate family is asymptotically too expensive.

For a `d_H`-regular `H` on `n_H` vertices, the standard theta dual for `theta(overline H)` requires a symmetric matrix equal to one on the diagonal and on every `H`-edge. Every matrix in `span{I,A_H,J}` satisfying those equalities is

\[
B_t=t(I+A_H)+(1-t)J.
\tag{X04.1}
\]

Let `L=lambda_2(H)` be the largest nonconstant adjacency eigenvalue. For `t>=0`,

\[
\lambda_{\max}(B_t)
\ge
\max\bigl\{
 n_H-t(n_H-d_H-1),\ t(1+L)
\bigr\}.
\]

The exact minimum of the right side is attained at

\[
t_*=rac{n_H}{n_H-d_H+L}
\]

and equals

\[
V_{\rm spec}
=
\frac{n_H(1+L)}{n_H-d_H+L}.
\tag{X04.2}
\]

For `t<0`, the constant eigenvalue is already greater than `n_H`, so it cannot improve this value. Since `L<=d_H`, the denominator in X04.2 is at most `n_H`, and hence

\[
V_{\rm spec}\ge1+L.
\]

Combining with C07 gives

\[
\boxed{
V_{\rm spec}
\ge
1+\frac{(3p-7)(p-5)}{16(p-2)}
=\frac{3}{16}p+O(1).
}
\tag{X04.3}
\]

Thus **no theta dual using only the adjacency algebra `span{I,A_H,J}` can prove `theta(overline H_2(G_p))=o(p)`**. If the C05 compiler is to break square root, its theta certificate must see finer Paley affine/character orbits, not only the adjacency spectrum.

This is a route obstruction only. It does not say the actual `theta(overline H_2)` is linear.

## 6. R12-X05 — aggregate local-square PSD does not beat the square-root point

**STATUS: PROVED RESTRICTED-FAMILY OBSTRUCTION.** The scalar aggregate ansatz below is not asserted to be a full degree-four pseudoexpectation.

This tests a second tempting certificate family before investing in it.

For an affine-symmetrized feasible degree-four pseudoexpectation put

\[
T=\sum_vy_v,
\quad
P_2=\sum_{e\in E}y_e,
\quad
P_3=\sum_{\triangle}y_{\triangle},
\quad
P_4=\sum_{K_4}y_{K_4}.
\]

For real `c`, define the degree-two polynomial

\[
q_v=x_v\Big(\sum_{u\in N(v)}x_u-c\Big).
\]

The Gram matrix

\[
K_{vw}=\widetilde{\mathbb E}[q_vq_w]
\]

is PSD. Nonedge entries vanish in the clique quotient, and affine edge-transitivity gives

\[
K=k_0I+k_1A_{G_p},
\]

where exact counting yields

\[
k_0
=
\frac{(2-4c)P_2+6P_3+c^2T}{p},
\tag{X05.1}
\]

and

\[
k_1
=
\frac{(1-c)^2P_2+3(3-2c)P_3+12P_4}{|E(G_p)|}.
\tag{X05.2}
\]

To test whether this aggregate family could even exclude the square-root scale, impose only the fixed-size scalar moment pattern

\[
T=t,\qquad P_j=\binom tj\quad(j=2,3,4).
\tag{X05.3}
\]

This is only a necessary aggregate pattern motivated by a deterministic clique size; it is **not** claimed to satisfy the omitted full moment constraints.

Using the Paley adjacency eigenvalues

\[
\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad
\frac{-1+\sqrt p}{2},\qquad
\frac{-1-\sqrt p}{2},
\]

the three eigenvalues of `K` factor exactly as

\[
\frac{t^2(t-1-c)^2}{p},
\]

\[
\frac{t(\sqrt p+t)(t-1-c)^2}{p(\sqrt p+1)},
\]

and

\[
\frac{t(\sqrt p-t)(t-1-c)^2}{p(\sqrt p-1)}.
\tag{X05.4}
\]

Hence all are nonnegative for every real `c` whenever

\[
0\le t\le\sqrt p.
\]

So this entire **aggregate one-vertex local-square Gram test**, considered without the omitted full degree-four constraints, has no chance to exclude the square-root point. A successful certificate must retain more than the four scalar clique-moment totals.

Again, X05 is not a lower bound on `U4`; it kills only this reduced certificate route.
