## 5. Method #14 — use the symmetry group: reduce arbitrary two-point margins to one weighted Bernoulli chamber

Consider three at-most-two-point margins. In the nondegenerate case write

\[
F_i=(1-p_i)\,\delta_{\alpha_i}+p_i\,\delta_{\beta_i},
\qquad
0\le p_i\le1,
\qquad
\alpha_i<\beta_i.
\]

Set

\[
w_i=\beta_i-\alpha_i>0.
\]

Adding the deterministic constant `alpha_1+alpha_2+alpha_3` does not affect convex-order comparisons, so it is enough to minimize

\[
T=w_1B_1+w_2B_2+w_3B_3,
\qquad B_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i).
\]

Permuting the three margins also leaves the aggregate problem unchanged. Hence choose the chamber

\[
\boxed{w_1\ge w_2,\qquad w_1\ge w_3.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Ties can be broken arbitrarily. Degenerate margins are obtained by deleting zero-gap or deterministic coordinates and are easier.

The crucial point is that no ordering between `w_2` and `w_3` is needed.

## 6. Method #30 — one support pattern: exact theorem for the three-cube

### Theorem D0011-R12-C01 — every triple of two-point margins has a convex-order least aggregate

Let `F_1,F_2,F_3` be arbitrary probability laws on `R`, each supported on at most two points. Then the aggregation set

\[
D_3(F_1,F_2,F_3)
\]

contains a smallest element in convex order.

More explicitly, in the normalized Bernoulli representation above, relabel so that (5.1) holds and put

\[
\ell_2=(p_1+p_2-1)_+,
\qquad
\ell_3=(p_1+p_3-1)_+,
\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
r_1=(\ell_2+\ell_3-p_1)_+,
\qquad
r_0=(p_1+p_2+p_3-1-\ell_2-\ell_3)_+.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Define a law `q_*` on `{0,1}^3` by

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
(b_1,b_2,b_3)&q_*(b_1,b_2,b_3)\\ \hline
111&r_1\\
110&\ell_2-r_1\\
101&\ell_3-r_1\\
100&p_1-\ell_2-\ell_3+r_1\\
011&r_0\\
010&p_2-\ell_2-r_0\\
001&p_3-\ell_3-r_0\\
000&1-p_1-p_2-p_3+\ell_2+\ell_3+r_0.
\end{array}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Then `q_*` has Bernoulli marginals `p_1,p_2,p_3`, and if

\[
T_*=w_1B_1^*+w_2B_2^*+w_3B_3^*,
\qquad (B_1^*,B_2^*,B_3^*)\sim q_*,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
T_*\le_{cx}w_1B_1+w_2B_2+w_3B_3
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

for **every** other coupling of the same Bernoulli margins.

Consequently the corresponding translated sum of the original two-point variables is a convex-order smallest element of `D_3(F_1,F_2,F_3)`.

### Proof

Fix an arbitrary convex function `phi:R->R`. All random variables below are finitely supported, so no integrability issue arises.

#### Step 1: the four mixed-difference coefficients

Define

\[
A_{12}=\phi(w_1+w_2)-\phi(w_1)-\phi(w_2)+\phi(0),
\tag{6.5}
\]

\[
A_{13}=\phi(w_1+w_3)-\phi(w_1)-\phi(w_3)+\phi(0),
\tag{6.6}
\]

\[
A_{23}^{(0)}=\phi(w_2+w_3)-\phi(w_2)-\phi(w_3)+\phi(0),
\tag{6.7}
\]

and

\[
A_{23}^{(1)}=
\phi(w_1+w_2+w_3)-\phi(w_1+w_2)-\phi(w_1+w_3)+\phi(w_1).
\tag{6.8}
\]

For convex `phi`, the increment

\[
x\mapsto \phi(x+h)-\phi(x)
\]

is nondecreasing for every `h>=0`. Therefore

\[
A_{12},A_{13},A_{23}^{(0)},A_{23}^{(1)}\ge0.
\tag{6.9}
\]

The largest-gap choice (5.1) gives the two stronger comparisons needed later. Using `h=w_2`,

\[
A_{12}
=[\phi(w_1+w_2)-\phi(w_1)]-[\phi(w_2)-\phi(0)],
\]

while

\[
A_{23}^{(0)}
=[\phi(w_3+w_2)-\phi(w_3)]-[\phi(w_2)-\phi(0)].
\]

Since `w_1>=w_3`,

\[
\boxed{A_{12}\ge A_{23}^{(0)}.}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Similarly, using `h=w_3` and `w_1>=w_2`,

\[
\boxed{A_{13}\ge A_{23}^{(0)}.}
\tag{6.11}
\]

#### Step 2: exact expectation decomposition for an arbitrary coupling

Let `q` be any coupling of the Bernoulli margins and define

\[
u=\mathbb P_q(B_1=B_2=1),
\qquad
v=\mathbb P_q(B_1=B_3=1),
\tag{6.12}
\]

\[
z_1=\mathbb P_q(B_1=B_2=B_3=1),
\qquad
z_0=\mathbb P_q(B_1=0,B_2=B_3=1).
\tag{6.13}
\]

The Boolean-cube Moebius expansion of

\[
(b_1,b_2,b_3)\mapsto \phi(w_1b_1+w_2b_2+w_3b_3)
\]

gives the **exact identity**

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_q\phi(T)
=K_\phi+A_{12}u+A_{13}v+A_{23}^{(0)}z_0+A_{23}^{(1)}z_1,
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

where the part fixed by the one-dimensional marginals is

\[
K_\phi
=\phi(0)+\sum_{i=1}^3p_i[\phi(w_i)-\phi(0)].
\tag{6.15}
\]

For completeness: the coefficient of `b_1b_2b_3` in the Moebius expansion is `A_{23}^{(1)}-A_{23}^{(0)}` and

\[
\mathbb E(B_2B_3)=z_0+z_1,
\]

which produces exactly (6.14).

#### Step 3: conditional Fréchet lower bounds

Inside the slice `B_1=1`, whose total mass is `p_1`, the masses of `B_2=1` and `B_3=1` are respectively `u` and `v`. Hence

\[
\boxed{z_1\ge (u+v-p_1)_+.}
\tag{6.16}
\]

Inside the slice `B_1=0`, whose total mass is `1-p_1`, those masses are `p_2-u` and `p_3-v`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
z_0\ge
\bigl[p_1+p_2+p_3-1-u-v\bigr]_+.
}
\tag{6.17}
\]

Because both coefficients in (6.14) are nonnegative, every coupling satisfies

\[
\mathbb E_q\phi(T)\ge F_\phi(u,v),
\tag{6.18}
\]

where, with `A=p_1+p_2+p_3-1`,

\[
F_\phi(u,v)=K_\phi+A_{12}u+A_{13}v
+A_{23}^{(0)}(A-u-v)_+
+A_{23}^{(1)}(u+v-p_1)_+.
\tag{6.19}
\]

#### Step 4: dual monotonicity certificate

Every Bernoulli coupling obeys the pairwise Fréchet lower bounds

\[
u\ge\ell_2,
\qquad
v\ge\ell_3.
\tag{6.20}
\]

We now show that `F_phi` is coordinatewise nondecreasing on the whole plane.

If `u' >= u` and `Delta=u'-u`, then positive-part 1-Lipschitzness gives

\[
(A-u'-v)_+-(A-u-v)_+\ge-\Delta,
\tag{6.21}
\]

while

\[
(u'+v-p_1)_+-(u+v-p_1)_+\ge0.
\tag{6.22}
\]

Therefore, using (6.10),

\[
F_\phi(u',v)-F_\phi(u,v)
\ge
(A_{12}-A_{23}^{(0)})\Delta
\ge0.
\tag{6.23}
\]

The same argument in `v`, now using (6.11), gives

\[
F_\phi(u,v')-F_\phi(u,v)\ge0
\qquad(v'\ge v).
\tag{6.24}
\]

Hence every coupling satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_q\phi(T)
\ge F_\phi(u,v)
\ge F_\phi(\ell_2,\ell_3).
}
\tag{6.25}
\]

This is the desired objective-independent dual-cone certificate: the same lower corner `(ell_2,ell_3)` works for every convex objective because convexity forces (6.10)--(6.11).

#### Step 5: one coupling attains every inequality simultaneously

It remains to show that (6.3) is a legitimate coupling that attains the lower bound.

On the slice `B_1=1`, of total mass `p_1`, prescribe

\[
\mathbb P(B_2=1,B_1=1)=\ell_2,
\qquad
\mathbb P(B_3=1,B_1=1)=\ell_3,
\]

and couple the two Bernoulli indicators countermonotonically within that slice. Their smallest possible overlap is exactly

\[
r_1=(\ell_2+\ell_3-p_1)_+.
\]

This produces the four `B_1=1` cells in (6.3), all nonnegative.

On the slice `B_1=0`, of total mass `1-p_1`, the residual masses are

\[
p_2-\ell_2,
\qquad
p_3-\ell_3.
\]

By definition of the pairwise Fréchet lower bounds, each lies in `[0,1-p_1]`. Couple these two residual Bernoulli indicators countermonotonically in the slice. Their smallest overlap is

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_0
&=\bigl[(p_2-\ell_2)+(p_3-\ell_3)-(1-p_1)\bigr]_+\\
&=(p_1+p_2+p_3-1-\ell_2-\ell_3)_+.
\end{aligned}
\]

This gives the four `B_1=0` cells in (6.3), again all nonnegative.

Thus `q_*` is a probability distribution with the desired Bernoulli marginals, with

\[
u=\ell_2,
\qquad
v=\ell_3,
\]

and with equality in both conditional Fréchet bounds (6.16)--(6.17). Consequently

\[
\mathbb E_{q_*}\phi(T_*)=F_\phi(\ell_2,\ell_3).
\tag{6.26}
\]

Combining (6.25) and (6.26),

\[
\mathbb E_{q_*}\phi(T_*)\le\mathbb E_q\phi(T)
\]

for every convex `phi` and every coupling `q` of the same margins. All feasible sums have the same mean because the margins are fixed, so this is exactly

\[
T_*\le_{cx}T.
\]

Undoing the deterministic translation proves the theorem. `square`

## 7. Method #11 — what the dualization actually bought

\[

A_{13}-A_{23}^{(0)}\ge0.

Therefore the universal optimizer is obtained by taking both pair overlaps with the largest-weight coordinate to their Fréchet minima and then taking the two residual overlaps to their conditional Fréchet minima.

This is stronger than checking the finitely many stop-loss thresholds one by one: the certificate holds for the entire convex cone simultaneously.
