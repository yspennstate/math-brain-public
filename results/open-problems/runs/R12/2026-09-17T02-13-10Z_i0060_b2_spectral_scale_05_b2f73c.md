# 4. Method #25 — count the same thing twice: exact spectral/pivotal identity in the B2 model

Write

\[
T_n:=T(0,ne_1),\qquad f_n:=T_n-ET_n,\qquad V_n:=\operatorname{Var}(T_n).
\]

Encode each edge weight by an unbiased sign

\[
\tau_e=\frac{3+\sigma_e}{2},\qquad \sigma_e\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

Since every edge has weight at least 1 and the straight axis path has cost at most `2n`, every geodesic has at most `2n` edges. Hence every geodesic is contained in the `l^1` ball of radius `2n`, so `T_n` is exactly a function of finitely many Boolean coordinates. Expand

\[
f_n(\sigma)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S}\widehat f_n(S)\chi_S(\sigma),
\qquad
\chi_S(\sigma)=\prod_{e\in S}\sigma_e.
\]

Define the spectral sample `\mathscr S_n` by

\[
\Pr(\mathscr S_n=S)=\frac{\widehat f_n(S)^2}{V_n}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For an edge `e`, freeze all other coordinates and define the one-coordinate passage-time gap

\[
\Delta_e:=T_n(\sigma_e=+1)-T_n(\sigma_e=-1).
\]

Monotonicity, the fact that changing one edge changes its weight by exactly one, and integer-valued passage times give the exact dichotomy

\[
\boxed{\Delta_e\in\{0,1\}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

On the other hand, Fourier expansion gives

\[
\Delta_e
=2\sum_{S\ni e}\widehat f_n(S)\chi_{S\setminus\{e\}}.
\]

Parseval on the remaining coordinates and (4.2) imply

\[
\Pr(\Delta_e=1)
=E\Delta_e^2
=4\sum_{S\ni e}\widehat f_n(S)^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Summing (4.3) over edges yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
E|\mathscr S_n|
=\frac{1}{4V_n}\sum_e \Pr(\Delta_e=1).
}
\tag{C07}
\]

This is the first count: mean spectral size equals normalized total passage-time sensitivity.

For the second count, choose any deterministic tie-breaking rule and let `\gamma_n(\sigma)` be one selected geodesic. If `\Delta_e=1` and the actual value of `e` is the low weight 1, then **every** geodesic must use `e`: otherwise a low-weight geodesic avoiding `e` would have exactly the same cost after changing `e` from 1 to 2, contradicting `\Delta_e=1`. Because `\Delta_e` depends only on the other coordinates,

\[
\Pr(\Delta_e=1)
=2\Pr(\Delta_e=1,\tau_e=1)
\le 2\Pr(e\in\gamma_n).
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_e\Pr(\Delta_e=1)
\le2E|\gamma_n|
\le4n,
\]

and C07 gives

\[
\boxed{
1\le E|\mathscr S_n|\le \frac{n}{V_n},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad V_n\le n.
}
\tag{C07'}
\]

The lower bound `E|\mathscr S_n|>=1` is simply because the centered Fourier expansion has no empty-set mass.

Ahlberg--Deijfen--Sfragara prove, in the source-valid integer-valued setting, that the relevant co-influence is comparable to the expected intersection of all geodesics, whose expected size is linear in `n`. Combining their published comparison with the Boolean identity above recovers the stronger scale relation

\[
E|\mathscr S_n|\asymp\frac{n}{V_n}
\tag{4.4}
\]

for this B2 model, up to constants depending only on the weight law/dimension. I record (4.4) as a **literature-assisted reformulation**, not as a new theorem of R12.

Thus the variance scale has an exact Boolean meaning: its inverse natural chaos scale is, up to constants, the mean spectral-sample size.

## 5. Method #13 — find what only increases: a genuine monovariant exists, but in noise time rather than spatial scale

Refresh each Boolean edge independently with probability `t`, obtaining `\sigma(t)`, and define

\[
C_n(t):=E[f_n(\sigma(0))f_n(\sigma(t))].
\]

The coordinate correlation is `1-t`, so Fourier orthogonality gives

\[
\frac{C_n(t)}{V_n}
=E(1-t)^{|\mathscr S_n|}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Set

\[
s=-\log(1-t),\qquad
\Phi_n(s):=\frac{C_n(1-e^{-s})}{V_n}
=E e^{-sK_n},\qquad K_n:=|\mathscr S_n|.
\]

Define the effective spectral degree

\[
m_n(s):=-\frac{d}{ds}\log\Phi_n(s).
\]

Differentiating the Laplace transform gives

\[
m_n(s)=E_s K_n,
\]

where `E_s` denotes exponential tilting by `e^{-sK_n}`, and a second derivative gives

\[
\boxed{
m_n'(s)=-\operatorname{Var}_s(K_n)\le0.
}
\tag{C08}
\]

Equivalently, `-log Phi_n(s)` is increasing and concave. This is an exact monovariant: as noise grows, the spectral degree seen by the surviving covariance can only decrease.

Jensen and C07' also give a useful exact stability lower bound:

\[
\frac{C_n(t)}{V_n}
=E e^{-sK_n}
\ge e^{-sE K_n}
\ge e^{-s n/V_n}
=(1-t)^{n/V_n}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since C07' gives `V_n/n<=1`, for every `0<=theta<=1/2`, taking

\[
t=\theta\frac{V_n}{n}
\]

and using `log(1-x)>=-2x` for `0<=x<=1/2` yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_n(\theta V_n/n)}{V_n}\ge e^{-2\theta}.
}
\tag{C08'}
\]

Thus the covariance is rigorously stable at a constant fraction of the natural scale `V_n/n`. This matches the *location* of the Ahlberg--Deijfen--Sfragara transition from an elementary Boolean-spectral direction.

### Why #13 did not solve the recovery target

C08 is monotonicity in **noise parameter `s` at fixed spatial scale `n`**. Q17 needs regularity as `n` changes. I found no valid comparison such as

\quad\text{or}\quad
V_{2n}\asymp V_n

at the precision needed to rule out polynomial oscillation. The monovariant exists in the wrong variable.

This is a useful negative diagnosis: the dynamical covariance technology is already strong enough to canonicalize the fluctuation scale at each `n`; what is absent is a theorem gluing those canonical scales across `n`.

# 6. Method #23 — assume the opposite: what polynomial oscillation would force

Suppose the B2 model really has distinct `L^2` liminf/limsup exponents

\[
\alpha:=\underline\chi_2<\overline\chi_2=: \beta.
\]

Because `\|f_n\|_2=\sqrt{V_n}`, this means that along subsequences

\[
V_{n_j}=n_j^{2\alpha+o(1)},
\qquad
V_{m_j}=m_j^{2\beta+o(1)}.
\]

Define the canonical stability/chaos scale

\[
t_n^*:=\frac{V_n}{n}.
\]

Then automatically

\[
\liminf_n\frac{\log t_n^*}{\log n}=2\alpha-1,
\qquad
\limsup_n\frac{\log t_n^*}{\log n}=2\beta-1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

So failure of a single `L^2` fluctuation exponent is **equivalent to polynomial oscillation of the natural noise scale**. Using the literature-assisted relation (4.4), it is likewise equivalent, up to constants invisible to logarithmic exponents, to polynomial oscillation of the inverse mean spectral size:

E|\mathscr S_n|\asymp \frac{n}{V_n}=\frac1{t_n^*}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This gives a concrete contradiction target. A proof of any one of the following would rule out the scale defect in B2:

\[

\qquad

\text{or another comparable regular-variation estimate.}
\tag{6.3}

But neither C08 nor the published chaos theorem compares different `n`. The hypothetical oscillation therefore survives all current one-scale noise information.

## 7. The moment-order half in exact Fourier language

Method #25 also gives a clean description of the other half of the B2 target. Normalize Fourier coefficients by

\[
b_S:=\frac{\widehat f_n(S)}{\sqrt{V_n}}.
\]

Since multiplication of Walsh characters is symmetric difference, Parseval applied to `f_n^2` gives

\[
\frac{E f_n^4}{V_n^2}
=
\sum_A
\left(
\sum_S b_S b_{S\triangle A}
\right)^2.
\tag{C09}
\]

Define the signed Fourier additive energy

\[
\mathcal E_n:=
\sum_A
\left(
\sum_S b_S b_{S\triangle A}
\right)^2
=\frac{\|f_n\|_4^4}{\|f_n\|_2^4}\ge1.
\]

Then exactly

\[
\frac{\log\|f_n\|_4}{\log n}
=
\frac12\frac{\log V_n}{\log n}
+
\frac14\frac{\log\mathcal E_n}{\log n}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus the special-case equality (B2-target) would follow from the conjunction

\[
\frac{\log V_n}{\log n}\ \text{has a limit}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal E_n=n^{o(1)}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

This sharpens the earlier abstract two-defect decomposition: in the Boolean laboratory, the **scale defect** is regularity of `V_n` / mean spectral size, while the **moment defect** is subpolynomial signed Fourier additive energy.

Importantly, the noise-stability curve (5.1) only sees the probability weights `b_S^2` and the cardinality `|S|`; C09 depends on the signed autocorrelations `b_S b_{S triangle A}`. Therefore even perfect control of the distribution of `|\mathscr S_n|` would not by itself prove the `L^4/L^2` part. This is another precise reason the chaos theorem alone cannot settle B2-target.

# 8. Method #25 continued — the exact dyadic gluing identity and where independence is lost

For a midpoint hyperplane

\[
H_n:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:z_1=n\},
\]

the metric property and the fact that every geodesic from `0` to `2ne_1` meets `H_n` give the exact, standard min-plus identity

\[
\boxed{
T(0,2ne_1)
=
\min_{z\in H_n}
\bigl(T(0,z)+T(z,2ne_1)\bigr).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Only finitely many `z` can matter in B2 because every geodesic has at most `4n` edges. Identity (8.1) is standard dynamic-programming/metric folklore; it is **not claimed as novel**.

This looked like the missing dyadic recurrence: if the two half-passage fields were independent copies with controlled transverse dependence, competition over many `z` might force regularity of the minimum and suppress variance spikes.

The attempt fails at exactly the important point. The two unrestricted random fields

\[
z\mapsto T(0,z),\qquad
z\mapsto T(z,2ne_1)
\]

are functions of heavily overlapping edge environments. A geodesic to a midpoint may wander across the central hyperplane and back; positive weights do not force a single crossing. Thus (8.1) is an exact gluing identity but **not an independent block decomposition**.

One can force independence by restricting left and right passage times to disjoint half-spaces/corridors, but then one changes the random metric. Recovering the unrestricted metric requires controlling the cost of midpoint-slab backtracking and transverse wandering. Any deterministic slab of width `w` introduces an error on at least the `w` scale. To use such a construction for Q17 one would need that error to be negligible relative to the *unknown* fluctuation scale. When the putative exponent may be below `1/2` or even have liminf zero, choosing such a width non-circularly is exactly the missing geometry.

The simpler one-midpoint inequality

\[
T_{2n}\le T(0,ne_1)+T(ne_1,2ne_1)
\tag{8.2}
\]

has a second defect: after centering it incurs the nonrandom subadditivity gap

\[
\Delta_n:=2ET_n-ET_{2n}\ge0.
\]

Classical nonrandom-fluctuation estimates are on roughly square-root scales (up to logarithms) under bounded/exponential-moment hypotheses. That is too coarse to propagate a hypothetical good fluctuation scale below `n^{1/2}`. So neither (8.1) nor (8.2), with presently known controls, gives (6.3).

This is the concrete **gluing obstruction** requested by the recovery protocol: the obstruction is not lack of a dyadic identity; it is lack of a fluctuation-scale-accurate way to decouple the two random metric fields across the midpoint.
