## 4. R12-I0060-C01 — Q17 is exactly the sum of two nonnegative defects

**STATUS: PROVED.** This is an exact reformulation, not a solution.

Fix a nonzero direction and write

\[
Y_n:=T(0,nx)-ET(0,nx),
\qquad
s_p(n):=\|Y_n\|_p.
\]

For `q>2` for which the relevant moments are finite, define

\[
\mathcal O_2:=\overline\chi_2-\underline\chi_2\ge0
\tag{4.1}
\]

(the **scale-oscillation defect**) and

\[
\mathcal M_q:=\overline\chi_q-\overline\chi_2\ge0
\tag{4.2}
\]

(the **upper-moment defect**). Jensen/Lyapunov monotonicity gives the second inequality.

Then identically

\[
\boxed{
\overline\chi_q-\underline\chi_2
=
\mathcal O_2+\mathcal M_q.
}
\tag{C01}
\]

Because both summands are nonnegative,

\[
\boxed{
\underline\chi_2=\overline\chi_q
\iff
\mathcal O_2=0\ \text{and}\ \mathcal M_q=0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular Q17 is not merely a higher-moment comparison statement. It simultaneously requires:

1. existence of the `L^2` polynomial fluctuation exponent (`liminf=limsup`), and
2. stability of the upper polynomial exponent when `p` is increased from `2` to some `2+delta`.

### Proof

For every `n`, `s_2(n)<=s_q(n)`. Hence

\[
\underline\chi_2\le\overline\chi_2\le\overline\chi_q.
\]

Subtract `underline chi_2` and insert/subtract `overline chi_2`; this is C01. Equality of the endpoints holds iff both nonnegative differences vanish. QED.

A slightly stronger endpoint-collapse observation is useful: if Q17 holds for `q`, then

\[
\underline\chi_2
=\overline\chi_2
=\underline\chi_q
=\overline\chi_q.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed `s_2(n)<=s_q(n)` also gives `underline chi_2<=underline chi_q<=overline chi_q`.

## 5. Method #5: an exact entropy derivative for the moment defect

The useful parameter is the moment order `p`, not a geometric interpolation parameter.

### R12-I0060-C02 — entropy integral identity

**STATUS: PROVED.**

Let `Y` be a nonzero random variable with a finite `r`-th moment for some `r>q>2`. For `2<=p<=q`, put

\[
M(p):=E|Y|^p.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dp}\log\|Y\|_p
=
\frac{1}{p^2}
\frac{\operatorname{Ent}(|Y|^p)}{E|Y|^p},
}
\tag{C02.1}
\]

where

\[
\operatorname{Ent}(Z):=E[Z\log Z]-EZ\log EZ.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\|Y\|_q}{\|Y\|_2}
=
\int_2^q
\frac{1}{p^2}
\frac{\operatorname{Ent}(|Y|^p)}{E|Y|^p}\,dp.
}
\tag{C02.2}
\]

### Proof

Since `Y in L^r` with `r>q`, differentiation under the expectation is justified on `[2,q]`. Writing

\[
f(p)=\log\|Y\|_p=\frac1p\log M(p),
\]

we obtain

\[
f'(p)
=-\frac{1}{p^2}\log M(p)
+\frac1p\frac{E(|Y|^p\log|Y|)}{M(p)}.
\]

But

\[
\operatorname{Ent}(|Y|^p)
=pE(|Y|^p\log|Y|)-M(p)\log M(p).
\]

Substitution gives C02.1; integration gives C02.2. QED.

### FPP consequence

For `Y=Y_n`, if for some fixed `q>2`

\[
\boxed{
\int_2^q
\frac{\operatorname{Ent}(|Y_n|^p)}{p^2E|Y_n|^p}\,dp
=o(\log n),
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

then

\[
\log\frac{s_q(n)}{s_2(n)}=o(\log n),
\]

so

\[
\underline\chi_q=\underline\chi_2,
\qquad
\overline\chi_q=\overline\chi_2,
\qquad
\mathcal M_q=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus (5.1) is an exact analytic target for the **moment-stability half** of Q17.

At `p=2`, the infinitesimal form is particularly transparent:

\[
\left.\frac d{dp}\log\|Y_n\|_p\right|_{p=2}
=
\frac14\frac{\operatorname{Ent}(Y_n^2)}{EY_n^2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

This identifies normalized entropy of the squared centered passage time as the local obstruction to moving away from `p=2`.

## 6. R12-I0060-C03 — standard-deviation-scale concentration kills the moment defect

**STATUS: PROVED conditional criterion.** It does not prove Q17 because C01 leaves the scale-oscillation defect.

Suppose for some constants `A,B,beta>0`, uniformly in `n`,

\[
P(|Y_n|\ge t\,s_2(n))\le A e^{-Bt^\beta}
\qquad(t\ge0).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then for every fixed `q>2`,

\[
\frac{s_q(n)}{s_2(n)}
\le
\left[
\frac{Aq}{\beta}
B^{-q/\beta}\Gamma(q/\beta)
\right]^{1/q}
\tag{6.2}
\]

uniformly in `n`, and hence `M_q=0`.

### Proof

For `Z_n=|Y_n|/s_2(n)`, the layer-cake formula gives

\[
EZ_n^q
=q\int_0^\infty t^{q-1}P(Z_n\ge t)\,dt
\le
Aq\int_0^\infty t^{q-1}e^{-Bt^\beta}\,dt.
\]

The change of variables `u=Bt^beta` yields C03. QED.

This explains why standard-deviation-scale concentration is highly relevant to Q17. But it addresses only `M_q`, not `O_2`.

## 7. Method #2: exact counterexample to “tight concentration alone solves Q17”

**R12-I0060-X01 — ROUTE OBSTRUCTION, not an FPP counterexample.**

Let `Z~N(0,1)` and choose constants `0<a<b`. Define

\[
r_n=\begin{cases}a,&n\text{ even},\\b,&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad
Y_n=n^{r_n}Z.
\]

Then for every fixed `p>0`,

\[
\|Y_n\|_p=n^{r_n}\|Z\|_p,
\]

so the standardized variables are *exactly the same Gaussian law at every scale* and all fixed moment ratios are bounded constants. Nevertheless

\[
\underline\chi_2=a,
\qquad
\overline\chi_{2+\delta}=b
\]

for every `delta>0`.

Therefore even perfect scale-by-scale Gaussian concentration cannot prove a liminf-to-limsup statement like Q17 without an independent argument controlling **cross-scale variance oscillation**. This example is deliberately abstract; it does not satisfy the FPP/subadditive geometry and is not a refutation of the source question.

## 8. Method #18: the problem in a more useful language

C01 changes the attack from one opaque exponent equality into two separate modules:

### Module A — scale regularity

Prove

\[
\boxed{\mathcal O_2=\overline\chi_2-\underline\chi_2=0.}
\tag{A}
\]

This is exactly the existence of the variance fluctuation exponent in the source sense. The fact that Lam--Nakajima 2026 still make existence of the logarithmic variance limit an explicit assumption is strong evidence that this is a genuine current bottleneck even for bounded weights.

### Module B — moment regularity

For some `q=2+delta>2`, prove

\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_q=\overline\chi_q-\overline\chi_2=0.}
\tag{B}
\]

C02 turns one sufficient route to B into the entropy estimate (5.1). C03 gives another sufficient route: standard-deviation-scale stretched-exponential concentration.

The decomposition also prevents a false closure: proving B alone, even with a constant `L^q/L^2` ratio, does **not** imply Q17 unless A is also proved.

## 9. Why current ordinary-lattice concentration does not close Module B

Damron--Hanson--Sosoe prove, under exponential moments, concentration of `T(0,x)` around its mean at scale approximately

\[
\sqrt{|x|/\log|x|}.
\]

Q17 needs comparison with the unknown actual `s_2(n)`. If the true KPZ scale in two dimensions is `n^{1/3}`, then `sqrt(n/log n)` is polynomially larger. Thus integrating the known tail bound yields fixed-`p` moment upper bounds on the known concentration scale, not a source-uniform `s_q(n)<=n^{o(1)}s_2(n)` comparison.

This is exactly the gap highlighted by Basu--Sidoravicius--Sly in a different, rotationally invariant class: their standard-deviation-scale concentration is new precisely because the ordinary lattice theory does not provide it.

## 11. Failed routes / what a source proof now has to contain

1. **Tight concentration alone:** killed abstractly by X01 because Q17 compares a liminf to a limsup.
2. **Known ordinary-lattice concentration:** scale is `sqrt(n/log n)`, not the unknown standard deviation, so it does not force `M_q=0` at the target scale.
3. **Exactly solvable LPP / thin cylinders / rotationally invariant FPP:** useful analogies, wrong source model.
4. **Assume the fluctuation exponent exists:** circular for Module A; Lam--Nakajima 2026 explicitly package this as an assumption.
5. **Interpolation only:** log-convexity of `L^p` norms can make `overline chi_p` right-continuous under a higher-moment bound, but Q17 requires exact equality for one fixed positive increment and also Module A. Approximate equality is not the source statement.

A full Q17 proof must contain mechanisms for **both** A and B.
