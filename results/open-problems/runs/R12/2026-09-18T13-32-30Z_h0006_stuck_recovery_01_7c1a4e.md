# 6. Section-I(c): what a proof must contain that no route currently contains

A source-valid layer-3 proof needs five ingredients simultaneously.

### (i) A front state that is rich enough for the SDE but small enough for an FCLT

At minimum it must separate untouched vertices from once-contacted surviving vertices and record whatever mark controls their conditional second-contact failure law: residual position, elapsed time, inherited failure-strength type, or a proved low-dimensional sufficient statistic. If no finite statistic exists, the proof needs a state-space-collapse theorem for the empirical mark measure.

### (ii) A source-SDE one-step expansion, not a proxy probability

For first and second contacts, one needs a uniform expansion along typical critical histories of the actual conditional failure probabilities. That expansion must produce the linear Perron term and the finite-population quadratic response `Gamma_2` (or prove its cancellation) with an error small enough for (I.1).

### (iii) Separation of genuine second-contact signal from echo approximation error

The source's dissipative/front-tracking machinery may be used to show that residual back-reaction errors vanish. But order-`N^{1/3}` second-contact responses cannot all be pushed into an error term: they are the finite-population drift. A successful proof has to retain them while bounding only the remaining echo discrepancy.

### (iv) Fast-mode / transverse collapse

In a multitype process the Perron direction is critical while other type modes should contract. In the source SDE, the once-contacted mark reservoir adds additional transverse coordinates. The proof needs a quantitative collapse result on the `N^{2/3}` exploration clock, not merely an infinite-tree spectral statement.

### (v) Martingale tightness and excursion/QSD extraction

Once the characteristics converge, one still needs Lindeberg, compact-containment, reflection/restart or stopping arguments, and then the mapping from the limiting frontier process to finite-cascade size and the quasi-stationary observables the source asks about.

# 8. Section-I(e): nearest solved problem and what its proof has that H0006 lacks

The nearest solved family is the classical **near-critical SIR / critical random-graph exploration** literature.

Martin-Löf's nearly critical epidemic has the characteristic scaling

\[
\lambda_N=1+aN^{-1/3},\qquad I_0\asymp N^{1/3},\qquad K_N\asymp N^{2/3},
\]

with the final size described by first passage of a Wiener process to a parabolic barrier. Van der Hofstad–Janssen–van Leeuwaarden make the connection between critical epidemics, random graphs, and Brownian motion with parabolic drift explicit. Critical configuration-model proofs likewise derive Brownian/parabolic exploration limits by tracking finite-population depletion and surplus terms directly rather than demanding vanishing whole-component TV distance to a GW tree. Multistage epidemic work such as Simatos adds the second key ingredient: **state-space collapse** can reduce a higher-dimensional critical epidemic to a lower-dimensional diffusion.

What those solved proofs have that H0006 currently lacks is not a stronger branching theorem. They have an **exact finite-population Markov exploration/state description with computable one-step predictable characteristics**. Susceptible depletion, repeated contacts, and stage counts are explicit state variables. That lets the proof identify the `N^{-1/3}` drift and `N^{1/3}` noise accumulation directly.

The threshold-diffusion cascade instead carries continuous path history at a contacted-but-not-yet-failed vertex. The source gives a powerful one-parent branching reduction and dissipative echo control, but it does not yet give the finite-population marked-state closure or state-space-collapse theorem needed to compute the actual critical drift. That is the missing analogue of the epidemic machinery.
