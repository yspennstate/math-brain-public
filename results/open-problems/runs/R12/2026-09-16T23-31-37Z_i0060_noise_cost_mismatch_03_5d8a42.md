## 4. Method #20 — exact noisy-geodesic regret identity

The Ahlberg--Deijfen--Sfragara perturbation refreshes each edge independently with probability `t`. I first asked whether their multiple-valleys mechanism could be strengthened from `o(n)` near-optimality to near-optimality on the **standard-deviation scale**. The first calculation shows a precise obstruction.

Fix `d>=2`, direction `e1`, and a non-degenerate edge law `F` with finite mean

\[
m=E\tau_e<\infty.
\]

For the lower bound below we additionally retain the source-relevant finite `2+delta` moment and `F(0)<p_c(d)` so finite geodesics exist with linear expected length. Let

\[
T_n=T(0,ne_1).
\]

Use the standard refresh coupling

\[
\omega_t(e)=
\begin{cases}
\omega(e),&U_e>t,\\
\omega'(e),&U_e\le t,
\end{cases}
\]

where `omega,omega'` are iid environments and the `U_e` are iid uniform variables. Let `pi_t` be any fixed measurable geodesic selection for `omega_t` (lexicographic tie-breaking is enough when ties occur).

### R12-I0060-C04 — exact mean regret of a refreshed geodesic

For every `t in [0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
E\big[T_0(\pi_t)-T_0\big]
=t\Big(m\,E|\pi_0|-E T_n\Big).
}
\tag{C04}
\]

Here `T_0(pi_t)` means the original-environment cost of the geodesic selected in the refreshed environment.

### Proof

Write `I_e=1_{U_e<=t}`. The vector `I=(I_e)` is independent of the refreshed environment `omega_t`: conditional on any fixed `I`, each coordinate of `omega_t` is still an independent `F`-sample. Hence `I` is also independent of `pi_t`, since `pi_t` is a measurable function of `omega_t`.

Condition on `(omega_t,I)`. If `I_e=0`, then `omega(e)=omega_t(e)`. If `I_e=1`, then `omega(e)` is an independent fresh `F`-sample even after `omega_t(e)=omega'(e)` is revealed. Therefore

\[
E[\omega(e)-\omega_t(e)\mid\omega_t,I]
=I_e(m-\omega_t(e)).
\]

Since `pi_t` is fixed under this conditioning,

\[
E[T_0(\pi_t)-T_t(\pi_t)\mid\omega_t,I]
=\sum_{e\in\pi_t}I_e(m-\omega_t(e)).
\]

Average first over `I`, using independence from `(omega_t,pi_t)`:

\[
E[T_0(\pi_t)-T_t(\pi_t)\mid\omega_t]
=t\sum_{e\in\pi_t}(m-\omega_t(e))
=t(m|\pi_t|-T_t).
\]

Now average over `omega_t`. By stationarity in `t`, `(T_t,|pi_t|)` has the same law as `(T_0,|pi_0|)`. Moreover `E T_t=E T_0`. Hence

\[
E[T_0(\pi_t)-T_0]
=E[T_0(\pi_t)-T_t]+E[T_t-T_0]
=t(mE|\pi_0|-ET_n).
\]

This proves C04. Since `T_0<=T_0(pi_t)` pointwise, it also confirms that the coefficient in parentheses is nonnegative. QED.

This identity is stronger than the absolute-value upper bound used in the published multiple-valleys proof. I did not find C04 stated explicitly in the bounded prior-art search; **novelty is UNVERIFIED** and the mechanism is elementary coupling, not claimed as a literature-first result.

## 5. Method #30 — the coefficient is genuinely linear in distance

The exact identity would be less informative if

\[
mE|\pi_0|-ET_n
\]

could be `o(n)`. For ordinary non-degenerate iid FPP it cannot.

### R12-I0060-C05 — strict time-constant gap and the noise-cost mismatch

Assume `d>=2`, `F` is non-degenerate, and `E tau_e^2<infinity`. Then

\[
\boxed{\mu(e_1)<m:=E\tau_e.}
\tag{C05.1}
\]

Consequently, if also `F(0)<p_c(d)` so that `E|pi_0|<=C n`, then there exist constants `0<c<C<infinity` such that for every sufficiently large `n` and every `t in [0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
ctn\le E[T_0(\pi_t)-T_0]\le Ctn.
}
\tag{C05.2}
\]

In particular, writing `V_n=Var(T_n)` and choosing any fixed `lambda>0` with

\[
t_n=\lambda V_n/n\le1,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
E[T_0(\pi_{t_n})-T_0]\asymp V_n.
}
\tag{C05.3}
\]

Thus whenever `V_n->infinity`, the expected cost of a geodesic at the natural stability/chaos noise scale is much larger than the standard deviation:

\[
\frac{E[T_0(\pi_{t_n})-T_0]}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\asymp \sqrt{V_n}\to\infty.
\tag{C05.4}
\]

### Proof of the strict gap `mu(e1)<m`

Let `sigma^2=Var(tau_e)>0`. For an integer `L`, consider two edge-disjoint paths from `0` to `Le1`:

- the straight path, with cost `A_L=sum_{i=1}^L X_i`;
- the path that first steps once in the `e2` direction, makes `L` horizontal steps, and steps back, with cost `B_L=sum_{i=1}^{L+2}Y_i`.

The `X_i` and `Y_i` can be chosen mutually independent because these two paths are edge-disjoint. We have

\[
EA_L=Lm,\qquad EB_L=(L+2)m.
\]

Moreover

\[
B_L-A_L
=\sum_{i=1}^L(Y_i-X_i)+Y_{L+1}+Y_{L+2}.
\]

By the central limit theorem,

\[
\frac{B_L-A_L}{\sqrt{2L\sigma^2}}\Longrightarrow N(0,1),
\]

because the deterministic mean offset `2m` and the final two summands are negligible on the `sqrt(L)` scale. Hence for all sufficiently large `L`,

\[
P(B_L<A_L)>0.
\]

Therefore

\[
E\min(A_L,B_L)
=EA_L-E(A_L-B_L)_+
<Lm.
\]

Translate this two-route gadget block by block along the `e1` axis. Concatenating in each block whichever of the two routes is cheaper produces a valid walk; if a boundary edge is retraced, deleting the resulting loop can only lower the cost because weights are nonnegative. Thus, for every `k`,

\[
ET(0,kLe_1)\le k\,E\min(A_L,B_L).
\]

Divide by `kL` and let `k->infinity` to obtain

\[
\mu(e_1)\le\frac1L E\min(A_L,B_L)<m.
\]

This proves C05.1.

Now every nearest-neighbour path from `0` to `ne1` has at least `n` edges, so

\[
mE|\pi_0|-ET_n\ge mn-ET_n.
\]

Since `ET_n/n->mu(e1)<m`, the right-hand side is at least `c n` for all large `n`. On the other hand, under `F(0)<p_c(d)` and finite second moment the standard linear geodesic-length estimate gives `E|pi_0|<=C_0n`, so the same coefficient is at most `mC_0n`. Insert these two bounds into C04 to obtain C05.2 and C05.3. C05.4 is immediate. QED.

### Interpretation

Ahlberg--Deijfen--Sfragara locate the geodesic stability/chaos transition at

\[
t\asymp V_n/n.
\]

C05 shows that the **same standard refresh perturbation** produces paths whose mean excess cost in the original environment is of order `V_n`, not order `sqrt(V_n)`. Therefore the published noise/multiple-valleys mechanism cannot simply be tightened by replacing an `o(n)` estimate with a variance-scale estimate: there is a genuine first-order selection drift.

This isolates a concrete reason the most obvious FPP-specific algorithm fails. To use chaos to prove the moment-order half of Q17, one needs a mechanism that either removes this drift without destroying the invariant edge law, or obtains moment comparison without requiring standard-deviation-scale near-optimal paths.

## 6. A no-go theorem for the most obvious drift cancellation

One tempting repair is to seek a nontrivial stationary edge coupling `(X,Y)` with the same marginal law `F` and zero conditional drift,

\[
E[X\mid Y]=Y,
\]

so that a path chosen from the `Y`-environment would see no first-order regression toward the mean. This is impossible under finite second moment.

### R12-I0060-X03 — no nontrivial same-marginal martingale perturbation

If `X` and `Y` have the same law, `EX^2<infinity`, and

\[
E[X\mid Y]=Y,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{X=Y\quad\text{a.s.}}
\tag{X03}
\]

Indeed,

\[
E(X-Y)^2
=EX^2+EY^2-2E[XY]
=2EY^2-2E[Y\,E(X\mid Y)]
=0.
\]

So no nontrivial one-edge stationary perturbation can eliminate conditional regression **exactly** while retaining the same finite-second-moment marginal. A future route must therefore use a weaker collective cancellation, a non-product conservative perturbation, or a proof that never requires zero drift at the path level.

This is an elementary coupling obstruction, not a source refutation.

## 7. Method #11 — dualize the scale defect into a noise-spectral degree

The preceding exact drift obstruction concerns the path geometry. A separate use of the same noise coupling gives a much cleaner algebraic description of the **cross-scale L2 defect**.

For

\[
f_n=T(0,ne_1)-ET(0,ne_1),\qquad V_n=\|f_n\|_2^2,
\]

let

\[
K_n(t)=\sum_e \operatorname{Inf}_e(T_0,T_t).
\]

Ahlberg--Deijfen--Sfragara Proposition 10 gives, exactly,

\[
\boxed{
V_n=\int_0^1 K_n(t)\,dt.
}
\tag{C06.1}
\]

At `t=0`,

\[
K_n(0)=\sum_e E[(T_n-E_eT_n)^2].
\]

In the integer-valued setting of their Proposition 15, with `F(0)<p_c(d)` and finite second moment,

\[
\operatorname{Inf}_e(T_0,T_t)\asymp P(e\in\pi_0\cap\pi_t)
\]

uniformly. Summing at `t=0` and using `E|pi_0|\asymp n` gives

\[
\boxed{K_n(0)\asymp n.}
\tag{C06.2}
\]

Define the **variance-weighted mean noise degree**

\[
D_n:=\frac{K_n(0)}{V_n}.
\tag{C06.3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{D_n\asymp\frac{n}{V_n}.}
\tag{C06.4}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
r_n:=\frac{\log\|f_n\|_2}{\log n},\qquad
d_n:=\frac{\log D_n}{\log n},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{d_n=1-2r_n+o(1).}
\tag{C06.5}
\]

Consequently, writing

\[
\underline d=\liminf d_n,\qquad\overline d=\limsup d_n,
\]

we obtain the exact exponent conversion

\[
\boxed{
\underline\chi_2=\frac{1-\overline d}{2},\qquad
\overline\chi_2=\frac{1-\underline d}{2}.
}
\tag{C06.6}
\]

Thus in this source-valid integer-weight subclass,

\[
\boxed{
\underline\chi_2=\overline\chi_2
\iff
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log D_n}{\log n}\text{ exists}.
}
\tag{C06.7}
\]

There is also a direct ANOVA interpretation. For a finite product approximation, write the Hoeffding decomposition

\[
f_n=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S}(f_n)_S.
\]

Orthogonality gives

\[
V_n=\sum_S\|(f_n)_S\|_2^2,
\qquad
K_n(0)=\sum_S|S|\,\|(f_n)_S\|_2^2.
\]

Hence `D_n` is literally the mean set-size under the probability measure assigning mass

\[
\|(f_n)_S\|_2^2/V_n
\]

to the ANOVA component `S`. Infinite-volume FPP follows by the standard finite-coordinate `L2` approximation because C06.2 makes the total influence finite.

Finally define the initial-slope noise time

\[
\tau_n:=\frac{V_n}{K_n(0)}=D_n^{-1}.
\]

Ahlberg--Deijfen--Sfragara's geometric chaos transition occurs at `V_n/n`, and C06.2 shows

\[
\tau_n\asymp V_n/n.
\]

So the existence of the L2 fluctuation exponent is exactly the existence of a polynomial exponent for the **noise-spectral mean degree / chaos time**. This is the FPP-specific replacement for the generic one-dimensional mixing language ruled out in the previous run.
