## 4. R12-I0060-X02 — bounded positive strongly-mixing exact-additive processes can have oscillating polynomial fluctuation scales

**STATUS: PROVED ROUTE OBSTRUCTION. This is NOT an FPP counterexample.**

### Theorem

Fix any

\[
\frac12<a<b<1.
\]

There exists a strictly positive bounded stationary strongly-mixing process `(Z_k)_{k in Z}` with

\[
1\le Z_k\le 3\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

such that the exactly additive process

\[
X_{m,n}:=\sum_{k=m+1}^n Z_k
\qquad(m<n)
\]

has, for every fixed `delta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\underline\chi_2(X)\le a<b\le\overline\chi_{2+\delta}(X).
}
\tag{X02.1}
\]

Thus the source-type endpoint equality can fail even with:

1. exact additivity (stronger than subadditivity),
2. bounded strictly positive increments,
3. stationarity and ergodicity,
4. strong mixing,
5. moments of every order.

Therefore any proof of the actual FPP Question 17 must exploit genuinely spatial metric/geodesic structure; no argument using only one-dimensional subadditive/stationary/mixing input can suffice.

### Construction

Let

\[
h_j=\begin{cases}a,&j\text{ even},\\ b,&j\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

We construct integers `N_j -> infinity` recursively, and put

\[
v_j:=N_j^{2h_j-2},\qquad L_j:=N_j^2,\qquad \rho_j:=e^{-1/L_j}.
\]

Choose `N_j` so rapidly increasing that, for every `j>=2`,

\[
v_j\le \frac14 v_{j-1},
\tag{4.1}
\]

and, with

\[
A_{j-1}:=\sum_{k<j}v_kL_k,
\]

\[
20N_j A_{j-1}\le N_j^{2h_j}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is possible because `h_j>1/2`, so `N^{2h_j-1}->infinity`, while `A_{j-1}` is fixed when `N_j` is chosen. The same choice can make `sum_j v_j<infinity`.

For each `j`, let `(G_k^(j))_{k in Z}` be an independent stationary Gaussian AR(1) process with

\[
E G_0^{(j)}G_m^{(j)}=v_j\rho_j^{|m|}.
\]

Set

\[
G_k:=\sum_{j\ge1}G_k^{(j)}.
\]

The sum converges in `L^2` since `sum v_j<infinity`. It is stationary Gaussian with nonnegative covariance

\[
r(m)=\sum_jv_j\rho_j^{|m|}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Each AR(1) component is mixing. The countable product shift is mixing (first for cylinder events, then by approximation), and `(G_k)` is a measurable factor of that product. Hence `(G_k)` is strongly mixing.

Let

\[
\sigma^2=r(0)=\sum_jv_j,
\qquad W_k=G_k/\sigma,
\qquad \eta_k=\tanh(W_k),
\]

and finally

\[
Z_k:=2+\eta_k.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then `1<Z_k<3` almost surely. Since `tanh` is odd and `W_k` is centered Gaussian, `E eta_k=0`, so `EZ_k=2`. Stationarity and strong mixing pass to measurable factors.

### Step 1. The Gaussian partial-sum variance oscillates between powers `2a` and `2b`

Write

\[
V_G(n)=\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{r=1}^nG_r\right)
=\sum_j V_j(n),
\]

where

\[
V_j(n)=v_j\left[n+2\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}(n-r)\rho_j^r\right].
\tag{4.5}
\]

At `n=N_j`, since `L_j=N_j^2`, for `0<=r<N_j`,

\[
\rho_j^r=e^{-r/N_j^2}\ge e^{-1/N_j}\ge e^{-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
V_j(N_j)\ge e^{-1}v_jN_j^2=e^{-1}N_j^{2h_j}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For a past scale `k<j`, use

\[
\sum_{r\ge1}\rho_k^r=\frac{\rho_k}{1-\rho_k}\le 2L_k
\]

for `L_k>=1`. Hence

\[
V_k(N_j)\le 5v_kN_jL_k.
\]

Summing and using (4.2),

\[
\sum_{k<j}V_k(N_j)\le5N_jA_{j-1}\le\frac14N_j^{2h_j}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

For future scales, simply use `V_k(N_j)<=v_kN_j^2`. From (4.1),

\[
\sum_{k>j}v_k\le\frac13v_j,
\]

so

\[
\sum_{k>j}V_k(N_j)\le\frac13N_j^{2h_j}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Combining (4.6)--(4.8), there are absolute constants `0<c<C<infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
cN_j^{2h_j}\le V_G(N_j)\le CN_j^{2h_j}
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

for all `j`. Hence

\[
\frac{\log\|\sum_{r=1}^{N_j}G_r\|_2}{\log N_j}=h_j+o(1).
\tag{4.10}
\]

### Step 2. The bounded transform preserves the partial-sum variance exponent

Let `(U,V)` be standard jointly Gaussian with correlation `q in [0,1]`, and let

\[
\phi(x)=\tanh x.
\]

Expand `phi` in probabilists' Hermite polynomials,

\[
\phi(x)=\sum_{m\ge1}a_mH_m(x),
\]

with `a_1=E[Z tanh Z]>0` for `Z~N(0,1)`. Mehler's identity gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(U),\phi(V))
=\sum_{m\ge1}m!a_m^2q^m.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Since `0<=q<=1`,

\[
a_1^2q
\le
\operatorname{Cov}(\phi(U),\phi(V))
\le
q\sum_{m\ge1}m!a_m^2
=q\operatorname{Var}(\tanh Z).
\tag{4.12}
\]

For `(W_k)`, the correlation at lag `m` is `q_m=r(m)/sigma^2`, and (4.3) gives `q_m>=0`. Summing (4.12) over all pairs `1<=r,s<=n` therefore yields constants `0<c_phi<C_phi<infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{c_\phi}{\sigma^2}V_G(n)
\le
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{r=1}^n\eta_r\right)
\le
\frac{C_\phi}{\sigma^2}V_G(n)
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

for every `n`.

Since

\[
X_{0,n}-EX_{0,n}=\sum_{r=1}^n\eta_r,
\]

(4.9) and (4.13) imply

\[
\frac{\log\|X_{0,N_j}-EX_{0,N_j}\|_2}{\log N_j}=h_j+o(1).
\tag{4.14}
\]

Along even `j` the right side tends to `a`, and along odd `j` it tends to `b`. Thus

\[
\underline\chi_2\le a<b\le\overline\chi_2.
\tag{4.15}
\]

Finally, Lyapunov monotonicity gives

\[
\|Y\|_{2+\delta}\ge\|Y\|_2
\]

for every `delta>0`, hence

\[
\overline\chi_{2+\delta}\ge\overline\chi_2\ge b.
\]

Together with (4.15) this proves X02.1. QED.

## 5. Why X02 matters for the actual FPP problem

X02 is much closer to the source hypotheses than the previous toy Gaussian scaling example: it has positive bounded increments, exact additivity, stationarity, ergodicity, strong mixing and all moments. Yet the fluctuation exponent still need not exist.

What it still lacks is precisely the genuinely spatial FPP structure:

- passage times are minima over many competing lattice paths;
- the underlying randomness is iid **in space**, not merely a one-dimensional mixing increment process;
- triangle inequalities hold simultaneously for every intermediate lattice point;
- rerouting gives many alternative midpoint decompositions, not just one additive decomposition.

Therefore the next attack should not spend another run on generic subadditivity, one-dimensional mixing, or same-scale concentration. Those categories are now rigorously ruled out as sufficient mechanisms.

## 6. The next FPP-specific route

For `x=e_1`, every deterministic midpoint `z` gives

\[
T(0,2ne_1)\le T(0,z)+T(z,2ne_1).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The useful structure is the **simultaneous family** of inequalities (6.1) over many well-separated `z` in a transverse section. The proposed next target is to quantify a variance/moment smoothing effect from competition among many alternate decompositions.

A concrete first lemma to seek is a source-valid corridor statement of the form:

> there exists a polynomial-size family of transverse midpoint corridors whose restricted two-half passage sums have enough conditional decorrelation that the minimum over the family cannot sustain the kind of long-scale variance oscillation constructed in X02.

The obstruction is clear: unrestricted half-passage times reuse large portions of the same iid edge environment, so simply calling the candidates independent is false. Any proof must either isolate disjoint corridor variables and control the cost of restriction, or use geodesic-overlap/chaos estimates to quantify the dependence. R07's cavity/overlap identities may become relevant exactly at this point.

This is a different mechanism from the prior entropy route: it targets the **scale-oscillation defect** rather than only moment-order stability.
