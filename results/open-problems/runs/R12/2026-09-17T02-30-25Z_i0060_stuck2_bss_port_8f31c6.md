# 4. Required STUCK RECOVERY 2 report

## (a) Exact statement that blocks

Put

\[
Y_n:=T(0,nx)-ET(0,nx),\qquad s_p(n):=\|Y_n\|_p.
\]

The exact missing inequality is

\[
\boxed{
\exists\delta>0:\quad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log s_{2+\delta}(n)}{\log n}
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log s_2(n)}{\log n}.
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

The reverse inequality is automatic from monotonicity of `L^p` norms and liminf/limsup ordering. Thus a proof has to destroy **both** sources of slack: cross-scale `L^2` oscillation and the same-scale moment-order gap above `p=2`.

The B2 recovery attempt sharpened the scale half. In the planar equiprobable `{1,2}` model, the natural noise/chaos scale is tied to `V_n/n`, where `V_n=Var T_n`, and the Boolean spectral sample gives a clean one-scale description. The remaining problem is that all those identities live at a fixed `n`.

## (b) Every route tried and the precise failure

### 1. Differentiate in the moment order

The exact entropy identity for `d/dp log ||Y_n||_p` gives a route to moment stability. **Failure:** even perfect `L^(2+delta)/L^2` comparison at every fixed scale does not force `log s_2(n)/log n` to have a limit.

### 2. Standard-deviation-scale concentration

A sequence can have uniformly well-controlled standardized laws while its standard deviation changes polynomial exponent along subsequences. **Failure:** same-scale concentration alone cannot propagate a good scale to later scales.

### 3. Generic subadditivity / additivity / stationarity / mixing

The prior R12 countermodel showed that bounded positive stationary strongly-mixing exactly additive processes can still have oscillating fluctuation powers. **Failure:** Q17 needs spatial FPP geometry, not abstract process structure alone.

### 4. Independent-refresh geodesic chaos

The exact refresh-regret calculation showed that at the natural chaos scale the noisy geodesic pays old-environment regret of order `Var T_n`, not one standard deviation. **Failure:** the obvious multiple-valleys route loses too much in selection drift.

### 5. Same-marginal zero-drift one-edge coupling

The prior no-go calculation showed a square-integrable same-law martingale coupling is trivial. **Failure:** one cannot remove the refresh drift by a nontrivial product perturbation with zero conditional regression.

### 6. Noise-spectral interaction degree

For integer-valued FPP, `n/Var T_n` is comparable to the relevant one-scale spectral/co-influence degree. **Failure:** this reformulates scale oscillation but supplies no regularity in `n`.

### 7. Boolean spectral sample in the B2 model

The last run identified the exact pivotal/spectral first moment and a genuine monovariant in noise time; it also recovered the canonical stability scale `V_n/n`. **Failure:** the monotonicity variable is the noise parameter, not spatial scale. No valid comparison between the spectral sample at `n` and at a larger macroscopic scale followed.

### 8. Fourier fourth-moment energy

The B2 `L^4/L^2` ratio can be written as a signed Fourier additive-energy quantity. **Failure:** noise stability sees squared Fourier mass and spectral-set size, whereas the fourth moment depends on signed autocorrelations. Controlling the size distribution alone is not enough.

### 9. Midpoint / corridor gluing

The exact min-plus identity through a separating hyperplane gives the right algorithmic shape. **Failure:** candidate midpoint passage fields heavily reuse the same edge environment. Restricting to disjoint slabs/corridors restores independence only by changing the metric, and bounding the confinement/backtracking error at the true fluctuation scale is itself missing.

These failures now point to one common gap: **cross-scale geometric confinement/gluing at the true fluctuation scale**.

## (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

The missing ingredient is stronger than a local or dyadic continuity estimate. A statement of the schematic form

\[
\frac{s_2(2n)}{s_2(n)}=n^{o(1)}
\]

would prevent large jumps between adjacent dyadic scales but, by itself, need not force a unique logarithmic exponent: very slow exponent drift can survive adjacent-scale control. Likewise, control for a positive density of scales does not settle a liminf/limsup equality unless the uncovered gaps are quantitatively small enough to be filled at the fluctuation scale.

A successful route therefore needs a **macroscopic/polynomial-scale semigroup or confinement principle**, not merely one-step smoothness. One useful schematic target would compare a good scale `n` directly to `n^c` for a fixed `c>1`, with an error that is subpolynomial relative to the candidate fluctuation scale. Equivalently, one needs a mechanism strong enough that iterating across a bounded number of polynomial scale jumps cannot permit slow drift of the logarithmic exponent.

Separately, one still needs moment stability for one `q=2+delta>2`. The entropy-in-`p` route remains a plausible module for that half once the scale module exists.

The proof component absent from every route so far is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a source-valid cross-scale confinement/gluing estimate at the true fluctuation scale.}
}
\]

This is exactly what the closest rotationally invariant program has and the ordinary lattice problem currently lacks.

## #19 — make it an algorithm

The natural algorithm is a slab/corridor min-plus dynamic program:

1. divide a long point-to-point problem into longitudinal slabs;
2. record crossing locations on each interface;
3. use restricted passage times inside each slab as transfer costs;
4. compose them by min-plus convolution;
5. compare the restricted optimum with unrestricted FPP.

The algorithm fails at exactly step 5. Unrestricted undirected geodesics may leave/re-enter slabs or use paths whose environments overlap across candidate interface states. Forcing disjoint corridors repairs independence but changes the metric. To prove that the algorithm tracks the true passage time to within the fluctuation scale requires a confinement/transversal estimate that is currently unavailable in ordinary lattice FPP. Increasing corridor width reduces confinement error but simultaneously destroys the clean finite-state/weak-dependence advantage. This is a precise algorithmic obstruction, not a vague appeal to “dependence.”

## #28 — replace for all with for typical

Typical-scale control is not enough for Q17 unless it comes with a quantitative maximum-gap statement. A positive density, density-one, or random-scale theorem can still leave a sparse subsequence carrying the limsup exponent. To upgrade typical good scales to all scales, the good scales must be sufficiently **syndetic at the relevant fluctuation resolution** that deterministic endpoint/metric continuity can fill every gap without paying a polynomially larger error. No such max-gap result is available from the one-scale spectral identities.

Thus the useful “typical” target is not “most scales are good.” It is “good scales have no macroscopic polynomial gaps after normalization by the candidate fluctuation scale,” which is nearly a cross-scale theorem again.
