## 4. Method #28 — the typical quartic modes are exactly the saturated modes

### R12-C19 — exact signed spectrum of the quartic Legendre operator

**Status: PROVED for every admissible prime.** This is a route theorem, not a source exponent improvement.

The operator `mathcal K` in (3.5) is real symmetric and has the exact spectrum

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}(\mathcal K)
=\{+1,-1,
\underbrace{+p,\ldots,+p}_{(p-3)/2},
\underbrace{-p,\ldots,-p}_{(p-3)/2}\}.
}
\tag{C19.1}
\]

Equivalently, its singular values are

\[
\boxed{
1,1,
\underbrace{p,\ldots,p}_{p-3\text{ times}}.
}
\tag{C19.2}
\]

Thus the raw quartic channel has **no large typical sub-`p` sector at all**: all but two multiplicative modes are exactly at the ambient `p` scale. The two exceptional modes are the benign ones.

### Proof

Equation (3.5) gives

\[
\mathcal K=Q'DQ',
\]

so `mathcal K` is symmetric. From (3.3), `M=B^2`, while `D1=d` and `Dd=1`; using (3.2),

\[
M\mathbf1=\mathbf1,
\qquad
Md=d.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal K\mathbf1=Md=d,
\qquad
\mathcal K d=M\mathbf1=\mathbf1.
\tag{C19.3}
\]

Hence on

\[
E=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,d\}
\]

the eigenvectors `1+d` and `1-d` have eigenvalues `+1` and `-1`.

Because `mathcal K` is symmetric, `W=E^perp` is invariant. On `W`, (3.6) becomes

\[
\mathcal K^2=p^2I.
\]

So every eigenvalue on `W` is `+p` or `-p`.

It remains only to determine their multiplicities. Since

\[
L(1)=\sum_x\chi(x(x-1)^2)=-1,
\]

the diagonal entries satisfy

\[
\mathcal K_{a,a}=-\chi(a).
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal K
=-\sum_{a\ne0}\chi(a)=0.
\]

The trace on `E` is already `1+(-1)=0`, so the trace on `W` is zero. Since `dim W=p-3` is even for `p=1 mod 4`, the `+p` and `-p` multiplicities are both `(p-3)/2`. This proves (C19.1)--(C19.2). QED.

## 6. Method #6 — deform to a commuting endpoint, and quantify where the deformation becomes nonperturbative

### R12-C21 — constant distance from the Mellin commutant

**Status: PROVED for every admissible prime `p>=13`.**

Let

\[

Then

\[
\boxed{

\tag{C21.1}

\[
\boxed{

\ge

\longrightarrow t\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
}
\tag{C21.2}

Thus for every fixed `t>0`, the deformed cell operator is already noncommutative at a fixed positive fraction of the full `sqrt(p)` scale. The actual endpoint cannot be treated as an `o(1)` operator-norm perturbation of the commuting Mellin problem as `p -> infinity`.

### Proof

With the same block decomposition as above,

\[
[B,P]
=
\begin{pmatrix}

Z&0
\end{pmatrix}.

In particular

\[
\|[B,P]\|_{op}\ge\|Y\|_{op}.

\[
\|Y\|_{op}^2
\ge\frac{\|Y\|_F^2}{k}

\tag{C21.3}

\[
