## 3. Exact source and prior attempt: D0012

Source: Ruodu Wang, *Current Open Questions in Complete Mixability*, arXiv:1411.6190 / Probability Surveys 12 (2015), Open Problem 12, section 2.10.

For prescribed margins `F_1,...,F_n`, let `D_n` denote the aggregation set, i.e. the set of all laws of `X_1+...+X_n` over couplings with `X_i ~ F_i`. The source asks:

> **Open Problem 12.** How can one characterize `D_n` (maybe for some simple marginal distributions)? Equivalently, for a proposed distribution `G`, determine whether `G` is the law of a sum in `D_n`.

The source itself emphasizes that this summarizes the general complete/joint-mixability difficulty and is generally open.

The attempt's real obstruction is the absence, for continuous margins, of a finite normal form for these dual certificates. I preserve that obstruction rather than restarting the old derivation.

## 4. Current primary/later literature check, dated 2026-09-17

I read the source page directly and refreshed the current author/later-work chain.

### Uniform margins are already a substantial solved special class

Tiantian Mao, Bin Wang and Ruodu Wang, *Sums of Standard Uniform Random Variables*, arXiv:1811.11981 / Journal of Applied Probability 56 (2019), gives a **full characterization of the aggregation set for standard uniform marginals in dimension `n >= 3`** (with only partial characterization in dimension two). Ruodu Wang's current Waterloo working-paper page explicitly describes this as a **small partial answer to Open Problem 12**, not a closure of the general question.

Thus the most tempting “simple marginal” narrowing—standard uniforms with `n>=3`—is already KNOWN. Re-proving it would violate the current-status rule.

### Later work shows strong margin-specific variability, not a general characterization

Andrzej Komisarski and Jacques Labuschagne, *The Distributions of the Mean of Random Vectors with Fixed Marginal Distribution*, Journal of Theoretical Probability 37 (2024), proves that for every bounded Borel set `B` (in any finite dimension) there exists a fixed marginal law `mu` such that, for every `n>=3`, **every** probability law on `B` can occur as the distribution of the sample mean of `n` variables with marginal `mu`. This is a striking exact aggregation-set theorem for specially constructed margins, but it does not characterize `D_n` for arbitrary prescribed margins.

The current author page for the complete-mixability series still says Open Problem 12 is **partially solved** by the uniform-margin work; it does not list a full solution. Fresh bounded searches for `aggregation set`, prescribed marginals, Open Problem 12, and 2025–2026 follow-up work did not surface a later theorem giving a general source-matching characterization. This is bounded screening evidence, not a novelty certificate.
