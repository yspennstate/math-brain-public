## 5. R09-PASSAGE-L1 — geometric reduction of the passage constant

**Status: PROVED compiler; this refines the retained PASS.4 theorem without changing its exponent.**

Let

\[
R_L^{(1)}=\{0\leftrightarrow S_L\},
\qquad
\rho_p^{(1)}(L)=\mathbb P_p(R_L^{(1)}).
\]

Every nearest-neighbor path from 0 to the l-infinity boundary of [-L,L]^3 must hit l1 norm exactly L before reaching a point of l-infinity norm L. Hence

\[
\boxed{A_L\subset R_L^{(1)}}.
\tag{PL1.1}
\]

Unlike A_L represented on the entire cube, the event R_L^{(1)} depends only on the l1 ball

\[
B_L^{(1)}=\{x:\|x\|_1\le L\}.
\]

Its exact number of SITE coordinates is

\[
\boxed{
V_L=|B_L^{(1)}|
=1+\sum_{r=1}^L(4r^2+2)
=\frac{4L^3+6L^2+8L+3}{3}.
}
\tag{PL1.2}
\]

The previous report proved for every increasing N-coordinate SITE event E, with

\[
\Psi(x)=\arcsin\sqrt{x},
\]

that

\[
\Psi(\mathbb P_p(E))-\Psi(\mathbb P_q(E))
\le\sqrt N\,[\Psi(p)-\Psi(q)].
\tag{PL1.3}
\]

Apply PL1.3 to E=R_L^{(1)}, p=pc and q=pc-delta. The elementary self-avoiding-path bound gives pc>=1/5, and Campanino--Russo gives pc<1/2. Thus for 0<delta<=1/10 the entire parameter interval lies inside [1/10,1/2], where

\[
\Psi'(s)=\frac1{2\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\le\frac53.
\]

Using PL1.1 gives the refined uniform passage inequality

\[
\boxed{
\arcsin\sqrt{\alpha_{p_c}(L)}
\le
\arcsin\sqrt{\rho_{p_c-\delta}^{(1)}(L)}
+\frac53\sqrt{V_L}\,\delta.
}
\tag{PL1.4}
\]

Compared with the previous direct cube-coordinate bound, the asymptotic coordinate coefficient improves by

\[
\frac{\sqrt{(4/3)L^3}}{\sqrt{8L^3}}
\longrightarrow\frac1{\sqrt6}.
\tag{PL1.5}
\]

The exponent does not change: a sufficient passage-to-criticality condition remains

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{p_c-\delta_L}^{(1)}(L)\to0
\quad\text{and}\quad
L^{3/2}\delta_L\to0
\Longrightarrow
\alpha_{p_c}(L)\to0.
}
\tag{PL1.6}
\]

This is a real constant improvement and a cleaner geometric support, but **not metric movement by itself** under the frozen b(L) metric. The metric movement in this run is FV12.

The exact finite checker verifies PL1.2 for every 0<=L<=100; PL1.1 and PL1.3--PL1.6 are proved on the page.
