## 5. Smooth setup and the identities inherited from the `1/9` theorem

Let `Q=(0,1)^2`, let `(X,Y)` be uniform on `Q`, and for the moment let `pi` have a positive smooth density. Let

\[
T=\nabla\phi=(u,v)
\]

be the Brenier map carrying `nu` to `pi`. Since `pi` is a copula, both `u(X,Y)` and `v(X,Y)` are uniform on `[0,1]`. Write

\[
D^2\phi=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ b&c\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
\qquad w(s)=s(1-s).
\]

For `t,s in [0,1]`, define the horizontal and vertical conditional sublevel lengths

\[
q_t(y):=\lambda\{x:u(x,y)\le t\},
\qquad
r_s(x):=\lambda\{y:v(x,y)\le s\}.
\]

Because `x -> u(x,y)` and `y -> v(x,y)` are nondecreasing, these are threshold lengths. Uniformity of `u` and `v` gives

\[
\int_0^1q_t(y)\,dy=t,
\qquad
\int_0^1r_s(x)\,dx=s.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Set

\[
I_1:=\int_0^1\operatorname{Var}_y(q_t(Y))\,dt,
\qquad
I_2:=\int_0^1\operatorname{Var}_x(r_s(X))\,ds.
\]

The level-set change of variables from the previous theorem gives

\[
J_1:=\int_Qw(x)a(x,y)\,dxdy=\frac16-I_1,
\qquad
J_2:=\int_Qw(y)c(x,y)\,dxdy=\frac16-I_2,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and the exact cost identity

\[
W_2^2(\pi,\nu)=I_1+I_2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For completeness, (5.2) follows from `dt=a dx` on a horizontal slice and

\[
\int_0^1w(x)u_x(x,y)\,dx
 =\int_0^1w(q_t(y))\,dt,
\]

then `w(q)=q-q^2` and (5.1). The same argument gives the second identity. The usual quadratic-cost expansion then yields (5.3).

Where `0<q_t(y)<1`, implicit differentiation of

\[
u(q_t(y),y)=t
\]

gives

\[
q_t'(y)=-\frac{b}{a}
\]

at `(q_t(y),y)`; clipped portions are locally constant a.e. Hence, defining

\[
E_1:=\int_Qw(y)\frac{b^2}{a}\,dxdy,
\qquad
E_2:=\int_Qw(x)\frac{b^2}{c}\,dxdy,
\tag{5.4}
\]

we have the exact coarea/change-of-variables forms

\[
E_1=\int_0^1\int_0^1w(y)\,q_t'(y)^2\,dy\,dt,
\qquad
E_2=\int_0^1\int_0^1w(x)\,r_s'(x)^2\,dx\,ds.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Positive semidefiniteness gives `b^2<=ac`, so

\[
E_1\le J_2=\frac16-I_2,
\qquad
E_2\le J_1=\frac16-I_1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Using only the spectral gap `2` in (5.5) gives the old `1/9` theorem. We now retain the next Jacobi modes instead of discarding them.

## 6. B27 — swap the quantifiers: one integrated higher-mode quantity controls every level set

Fix `t`. Put

\[
A_t:=12\int_0^1\Bigl(y-\frac12\Bigr)f_t(y)\,dy,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(q_t(Y))=\frac{A_t^2}{12}+h_t.

## 7. The CDF box lemma: large first Jacobi mode forces higher modes

This is the key new estimate.

### Lemma 7.1

With `f_t,A_t,g_t,h_t` as above, let

\[
a_t:=|A_t|,
\qquad m_t:=\min(t,1-t).
\]

Then

\[
a_t\le6t(1-t)\le\frac32,
\tag{7.1}
\]

and, with

\[
C_0:=\frac7{24}\,36^{1/3}<\frac{97}{100},
\]

we have

\[
\frac{A_t^2}{12}
\le\frac{m_t^2}{3}+C_0h_t^{1/3}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

#### Proof

Because `0<=q_t<=1` and `int q_t=t`,

\[
A_t=12\int_0^1\Bigl(y-\frac12\Bigr)q_t(y)\,dy.
\]

Among all measurable `q:[0,1]->[0,1]` with integral `t`, the increasing linear functional on the right is maximized by `1_[1-t,1]` and minimized by `1_[0,t]` (the elementary bathtub/rearrangement principle). Therefore

\[
|A_t|\le12\int_{1-t}^1\Bigl(y-\frac12\Bigr)dy
=6t(1-t),
\]

which proves (7.1).

Reflecting `y -> 1-y` if necessary, it is enough to consider the affine part

\[
q_t^{(1)}(y)=t+a_t\Bigl(y-\frac12\Bigr).
\]

Since

\[
q_t(y)=q_t^{(1)}(y)+g_t(y)\in[0,1],
\]

the residual `g_t` must at least cancel every excursion of `q_t^(1)` outside `[0,1]`. Direct integration of the squared excursions gives

\[
h_t\ge
\frac{(a_t-2t)_+^3+(a_t-2(1-t))_+^3}{24a_t}
\tag{7.3}
\]

for `a_t>0`; for `a_t=0` the statement is trivial.

Indeed, for example, if `a_t>2t`, the lower excursion occupies

\[
0\le y<\frac12-\frac{t}{a_t},
\]

and its squared integral is exactly `(a_t-2t)^3/(24a_t)`; the upper endpoint is identical after reflection.

Let

\[
\delta_t:=(a_t-2m_t)_+.
\]

One of the two terms in (7.3) is `delta_t^3/(24a_t)`. By (7.1), `24a_t<=36`, hence

\[
h_t\ge\frac{\delta_t^3}{36},
\qquad
\delta_t\le(36h_t)^{1/3}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

If `a_t<=2m_t`, then `a_t^2<=4m_t^2`. Otherwise `a_t=2m_t+delta_t`; using `4m_t<=2` and `delta_t<=a_t<=3/2`,

\[
a_t^2
=4m_t^2+\delta_t(4m_t+\delta_t)
\le4m_t^2+\frac72\delta_t.
\]

Together with (7.4),

\[
\frac{a_t^2}{12}
\le\frac{m_t^2}{3}
+\frac7{24}36^{1/3}h_t^{1/3},
\]

which is (7.2). Finally, since all quantities are positive,

\[
\left(\frac7{24}36^{1/3}\right)^3
=\frac{343}{384}
<\left(\frac{97}{100}\right)^3
=\frac{912673}{1000000},
\]

so `C_0<97/100`. QED.

### Passage to an arbitrary copula

Let `K_epsilon` be a strictly positive smooth heat kernel on the circle. If `(U,V)~pi`, add independent circular noises with density `K_epsilon` to the two coordinates modulo one. The resulting law `pi_epsilon` is a positive smooth copula: circular convolution preserves each uniform marginal exactly.

Moreover `pi_epsilon -> pi` in Euclidean `W_2`: away from the vanishing wrap strips the displacement is the small circular noise, the wrap probability tends to zero because each marginal is uniform, and the square has bounded diameter. Hence

\[

\[
W_2^2(\pi,\nu)
=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}W_2^2(\pi_\varepsilon,\nu)
\le s_0.
\]

This proves Theorem 8.1. QED.
