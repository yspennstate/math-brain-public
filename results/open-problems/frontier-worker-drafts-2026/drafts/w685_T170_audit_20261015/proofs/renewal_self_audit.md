## All-length cut and boundary derivation

For a unit-step height word starting at 0 and ending at H, a uniquely visited height 0<k<H separates the entire earlier word below k from the entire later word above k. Otherwise a unit-step crossing would visit k again. Thus the eligible cuts 4<k<H are ordered, their incoming and outgoing steps are positive, and consecutive cuts enclose a strict positive bridge. Every intermediate height has all its visits inside that bridge; a single visit would itself be an eligible cut. Hence these bridges are irreducible in exactly the sense used in the scalar enumeration.

After four seed steps let the height be h0 in [-4,4]. A prefix of n remaining steps to its first cut k has n-1>=2(k-5), since each height 5,...,k-1 is visited at least twice before that cut and not during the seed. Writing k=h0+n-2m gives 4m>=n+2h0-9>=n-17. A no-cut word ending at H>4 obeys the same count. For H<=4 its displacement is at most 8, so 4m>=2n-16>=n-17. A suffix from its last cut to H satisfies n-1>=2(H-k-1)=2(n-2m-1), giving 4m>=n-1. These inequalities cover all lengths, including small n; no finite enumeration is used for the conclusion.

For any quota 4m>=n-C and t>1, the scalar weight (d/a)^n z^(n-2m) is bounded by itself times t^(4m-n+C). Summing all sign words gives t^C [d(z+t^4/z)/(a t)]^n. Thus the prefix/no-cut and suffix sums converge whenever r<1. The constants are C=17 and C=1 respectively. Restriction to boundary states only decreases these positive sums. With Bv<=kappa v, kappa<1, and positive finite-dimensional v,w, their comparability makes the prefix/B^j/suffix sum finite.

## Joint shortcut law

Condition on the sigma-field generated by the actual edges of a fixed vertex-simple path. Each selected alternative uses disjoint edges outside this sigma-field. The alternate-edge sets are mutually disjoint by the geometric argument below. Their passage times are therefore jointly conditionally independent, giving the product of their conditional survival functions. The actual edge times remain shared arguments of those survival functions and must subsequently be integrated jointly. No product of separately integrated marginal survival probabilities is used.

An even square's alternative edge sharing an actual edge must share the same even actual endpoint. Simplicity makes its odd endpoint the adjacent actual predecessor or successor. The different-axes test excludes the original middle vertex; the remaining possibility supplies a three-step chord incident to the square's other endpoint. Sharing a three-step chord itself also touches an even endpoint and is excluded. Two even squares sharing an edge share an even actual endpoint, so are adjacent blocks; their common alternative midpoint puts the outer endpoints within distance two, excluded by the four-step test. An odd square selected at a uniquely visited even-index height has an alternative even midpoint outside the entire actual path. This rules out actual/chord overlap and overlap with every even square, whose even endpoint is actual. Two selected odd squares sharing an edge must share their alternative even midpoint, hence its height and the original uniquely visited middle vertex, so are the same square.

## Transfer scaling and terminal factors

Use zero-based edge indices. Finalising the pair b,b+1 with b even requires the newly appended edge b+4, i.e. the (b+5)-th edge. At this point the last possible chord incident to endpoint b+2, namely the chord from b+2 to b+5, has been seen. The left and right four-step tests have also been seen. The number of unintegrated edges is four at even length, three at odd length. If q is the number of integrated edges after n>=4 edges, q=2 floor((n-3)/2).

Let T be the raw transition. Define D_even,p=a^(-1-p), D_odd,p=a^(-p), and M=D_source^(-1) T D_destination. A finalising transition has even source and odd destination, hence entry a^(1+p-q) B_pq. A nonfinalising transition has odd source and even destination, keeps p, and has entry 1/a. For incoming flag I and moment p the raw terminal integral is g=(a+I)^(-p-1) a^(-m+1). In either parity D^(-1)g=a^(-3)(a/(a+I))^(p+1). The initial seed has I=p=0 and D=a^(-1), yielding the total seed factor a^(-4). This explicitly resolves the source/destination direction of the similarity.

For the pair integral the polynomial is t0^p(1+t0+t1)^E(1+t1+t2)^O and the rates are a+c0+I+E and a+c1+E+O. Its coefficient of t2^q integrates to the sum of factorial moments term by term. The incoming exponential and moment are retained; crossing a cut does not reset either. Dropping unfinished competition factors at termination increases the integral since each original survival factor is at most one; the incoming odd factor already expanded is retained.

## Exhaustive finite encoding audit

The separate checker imports no producer. For each encoded state it constructs a full coordinate-word witness, then reconstructs every chord, its edge incidences, dirty endpoint and four-step exclusion, pending even/odd flag, incoming flag and moment from that entire history. It compares the multiset of all legal old-axis/fresh-axis, sign, mark and moment transitions with the saved row. All 641 states are reached, both mark rows at every state are checked (1,282 rows, 7,952 transition records), and no extra state or transition is left unreconciled. The maximum witness length is six. This finite closure check supports the following all-length induction, rather than replacing it: every new chord depends on the last three directions; the four-step exclusion depends on those directions plus the appended direction; all older information affecting an unfinished block is retained in its count/flag queues. The full-coordinate formula therefore gives the same update for any longer history with the same encoded state. An axis absent from those last three directions has exactly d-r possible global labels and the same local tests; an old axis has one. Longer returns are deliberately retained as positive formal overcounts. Actual simple paths are included.

The graph also includes formal seeds with an initial odd flag true. Every actual four-edge seed uses the false choice. The extra choices enlarge the formal state space and do not remove any actual path. Unique-height eligibility is a deterministic test on the complete fixed path; no adaptedness or stopping-time assumption is used.

Decomposition at the first uniquely visited interior height gives the formal coefficient identity A=B+BA: the first factor is an irreducible strict bridge and the remainder is an arbitrary strict bridge. If no such interior height exists, the entire bridge is irreducible. Length and displacement weights multiply. The recurrence is coefficientwise, uses no analytic continuation, and matches every saved coefficient enclosure. All omitted irreducible profiles satisfy the already derived C=1 quota. The dimension-specific scalar ceilings are 0.9 for d21 and 0.91 for d19/d18; their exact length>400 tails are below 10^-15.

## Passage to FPP and audit disposition

For every fixed time t choose a Laplace parameter q>2d-1. The expected number of vertex-simple paths from 0 with total time at most t is bounded by exp(qt) sum_{n>=1} 2d(2d-1)^(n-1)/(1+q)^n, which is finite. Simultaneously for rational t, only finitely many such paths exist almost surely. A fixed finite path reaches H_N at finite time; choose a rational time above that value. Minimisation among the finite candidates proves existence of a fastest simple path. Every selected alternative must lose to its corresponding subpath: replacing it would give a cheaper walk to H_N, whose loop erasure (or earlier hit of H_N) costs no more. Thus the competition event is necessary.

The finite tilted sum K consequently bounds P(T(0,H_N)<=cN) by K exp((sc-log(z))N). Borel-Cantelli for a countable increasing sequence of c<log(z)/s gives the stated liminf. Since T(0,H_(dk))<=T(0,k(1,...,1)), division by k sqrt(d) gives the Euclidean diagonal bound sqrt(d) log(z)/s. Existence of that time constant follows from the standard subadditive FPP limit for independent Exp(1) edges.
