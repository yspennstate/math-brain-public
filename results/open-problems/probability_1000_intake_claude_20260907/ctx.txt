A C0116 result: DLP show t_cov ≍ |E| (E max GFF)^2; the later result of Ding (2012, 'Asymptotics of cover times via Gaussian free fields: bounded-degree graphs and general trees') gives asymptotic equality t_cov ~ |E| (E max eta)^2 for bounded-degree graphs when t_cov/t_hit -> infinity, and Zhai (2018) proved the asymptotic formula for general graphs under the same condition. Given the asymptotic formula, a (1+eps) approximation of t_cov reduces to a (1+eps) approximation of E max of a Gaussian vector with given covariance (the Green function), which is a deterministic computational problem: computing E max of a Gaussian vector to within 1+eps is not known to be in deterministic polynomial time in general (the majorizing-measure/generic-chaining bounds are only within constants; exact computation of Gaussian maxima expectations is #P-hard in some formulations). So the question reduces, for graphs with t_cov/t_hit -> infinity, to the deterministic approximability of E max of a Gaussian vector with a graph-Laplacian covariance; a positive answer would follow from a deterministic FPTAS for Gaussian expected maxima on such covariances, which is open; graphs with t_cov ≍ t_hit (e.g. cycles, where the cover time is dominated by hitting times) need a separate treatment and are the easier case since hitting times are computable exactly. Reduction recorded.

A C0117 result: Upper tail: for any graph, P(tau_cov > t_cov + s t_hit) <= e^{-cs}? The standard argument: after time 2 t_hit... using the Markov property at multiples of the 'maximal hitting time' scale, the probability that a fixed uncovered vertex remains uncovered for another 2 t_hit is at most 1/2 by Markov's inequality on hitting times, but the union over uncovered vertices costs the number of uncovered vertices, so the naive bound gives only P(tau_cov > t_cov + s t_hit) <= n 2^{-s}, i.e. exponential decay after a log n shift, not at scale t_hit from the mean unless t_cov - t_cov... For the complete graph, tau_cov is a coupon-collector time with standard deviation of order n ~ t_hit, and the centred cover time divided by t_hit converges to a Gumbel, which has exponential tails: consistent. For the cycle Z_n, t_hit ≍ n^2 ≍ t_cov and tau_cov/n^2 converges to a non-degenerate law with exponential tail (the cover time of the cycle is the time for the range to reach n, a functional of Brownian motion with Gaussian-type tails): consistent, and the standard deviation is of order n^2 = t_hit: consistent. For the two-dimensional torus, t_hit ≍ n^2 log n... t_cov ≍ n^2 (log n)^2, and the cover time fluctuations are known to be of order n^2 log n (Belius-Kistler / Abe: the cover time of the 2D torus has fluctuations of order t_hit... the second-order term is of order n^2 log n times a random variable), consistent with the question. So the conjecture passes all the standard test cases; the general lower-tail statement (tau_cov not much smaller than t_cov) is the hard side because early coverage requires all vertices to be hit quickly, which involves correlations of hitting times; via the GFF isomorphism (Ding-Lee-Peres) the cover time corresponds to the level at which the GFF maximum exceeds a threshold, and concentration of the cover time at scale t_hit corresponds to tightness of the GFF maximum at scale sqrt(t_hit/|E|), which is tightness of the maximum of the Gaussian free field on the graph in the correct units; tightness of the GFF maximum is known for the 2D torus and trees but not for general graphs. Reduction: the question is essentially equivalent to tightness of the graph GFF maximum in units of the maximal hitting time.

A C0132 result: In M(1,1) the first coordinate moves only at first visits and the second at later visits; the set of visited vertices is the range, and the first-coordinate process is a simple random walk on Z run at the rate of new visits. Recurrence of M(1,1) would follow if the walk returns to the origin infinitely often; since the second coordinate performs a simple random walk on the column above the current first coordinate whenever the vertex was visited before, the walk behaves in each column like a 1D random walk (recurrent) until it hits an unvisited vertex, at which time the first coordinate steps. The number of new visits up to time n is the range R_n; the first coordinate after n steps is a simple random walk at time R_n, so |X^1_n| is of order sqrt(R_n), and the range of a planar walk-like process is at most n, giving |X^1_n| <= sqrt n; a return to the origin requires both coordinates to vanish. A heuristic: if R_n ≍ n/log n as for planar simple random walk then the first coordinate is diffusive at the slower clock, and the walk is 'more recurrent' than planar simple random walk, supporting the conjecture; but the range of M(1,1) is not that of simple random walk because the exploration is column-wise. A rigorous partial fact: M(1,1) is recurrent if and only if the first coordinate returns to 0 infinitely often at times when the second coordinate is 0; since within a column the second coordinate is a recurrent 1D walk that keeps returning to 0 until it finds a new vertex, and new vertices in a column are found at the ends of the visited interval, the question is whether the first-coordinate walk (which is a simple random walk in the clock of new visits) visits 0 infinitely often, which it does a.s. (1D recurrence), and whether at those visits the second coordinate is 0 - not automatic. So recurrence of M(1,1) reduces to: at the (infinitely many) times the first coordinate is 0, the second coordinate, which then performs a 1D walk in the column of 0 with the visited interval of that column, returns to height 0 before finding a new vertex - which has probability bounded below by a constant each time if the visited interval around height 0 in column 0 has length bounded... the visited interval in column 0 grows, and the probability of returning to 0 from the boundary of an interval of length L before exiting is of order 1/L... summing over the visits to column 0 gives a divergent series only if the interval lengths grow slowly enough. This is the crux and I did not settle it; the reduction is recorded.

A C0134 result: Under (BD) the branching process with migration has bounded migration per generation, and the criterion delta > 2 comes from the tail of the total progeny of a critical branching process with migration delta: P(total progeny > n) ≍ n^{-delta/2}... the speed is positive iff the expected total progeny (expected excursion time) is finite iff delta > 2. With unbounded stacks under (POS1) the migration is unbounded but positive; the total progeny tail is then governed by the same exponent if the migration has finite mean, and the natural conjecture is that the criterion persists as long as the expected total drift delta = E sum_i (2 omega(0,i) - 1) is finite, with additional conditions on the tail of the stack size (as in the source's assumption (6.2) with the exponential-moment-type condition on the number of cookies far from 1/2). A partial result: under (POS1), (IID), (UEL) and E[sup{i : omega(0,i) not 1/2}] < infinity (the condition in (6.2)), the branching process with migration has migration with finite mean, and the Kesten-Kozlov-Spitzer-type tail analysis for branching processes with migration of finite mean gives the same exponent delta/2 for the total progeny, hence the same criterion delta > 2 for positive speed; the source proves the recurrence/transience part under (6.2) for the functional CLT in the recurrent regime, so the ballisticity part appears to follow the same route. I did not verify the tail analysis under unbounded migration with finite mean; recorded as the expected answer with the condition from (6.2).
