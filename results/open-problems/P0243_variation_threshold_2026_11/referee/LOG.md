### 6(d) Optimal stationary stopping by LP — completed

Derivation independent of the paper's cited formula: define x_j=E_i integral_0^U 1_(X_s=j) ds (continuous time). Dynkin's identity for state indicators gives xQ=pi-delta_i and E_i U=sum_j x_j. Thus the program is

    minimize 1^T x, subject to Q^T x = pi - e_i, x >= 0.

With exit rates q_j=-Q_jj and the embedded jump kernel R, v_j=q_j x_j gives the alternative program v(I-R)=delta_i-pi and objective sum_j v_j/q_j. Let Z=(Pi-Q)^(-1)-Pi. Since ZQ=Pi-I, every solution is x=Z_i+t*pi, and Z1=0 makes its total mass t. Therefore the exact optimum is max_j(-Z_ij/pi_j).

This is NOT merely a lower bound: for a feasible x, on entering j stop with probability pi_j/(pi_j+q_j x_j); otherwise wait an Exp(q_j) holding time and jump with R. The arrival-balance equation shows expected arrivals pi_j+q_j x_j, terminal mass pi_j, and occupation x_j. Every stop probability is positive on this finite irreducible chain, so termination has finite mean. This realizes the feasible occupation measure. Equivalently one can construct via positive stopping hazards; immediate-stopping states handle x_j=0. In discrete time, occupation counts before U obey x(P-I)=pi-delta_i, objective sum x_j, and stopping on each visit with probability pi_j/(pi_j+x_j) realizes the solution (including self transitions).

LP “exact” means the full optimum problem, solved numerically in double precision, not a rational certificate.
