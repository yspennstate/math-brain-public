\begin{abstract}
For a tall random matrix $A\in\F^{N\times m}$ ($\F\in\{\R,\C,\HH\}$, $d_\F=\dim_\R\F$) we study the
smallest least singular value over all of its square row submatrices,
$M_m=\min_{|T|=m}\smin(A_T)$, the quantity whose Gaussian exponential base
$M_m=c_\gamma^{-m/d_\F+o_\PP(m)}$ ($N/m\to\gamma>1$) governs the Balan--Wang stability of phase
retrieval.  We isolate the mechanism --- the competition between the entropy $h(\gamma)$ of the
submatrix family and the small-ball exponent $d_\F$ of one least singular value --- as an abstract
first/second-moment principle, and use it to push the Gaussian theorem far beyond its original
form.  We prove: a distribution-free \emph{lower} side for essentially every continuous ensemble
(bounded or subexponential density, entrywise profiles, directional anti-concentration, isotropic
log-concave rows, smoothed deterministic frames, normalized and diagonally preconditioned Gaussian
frames), with almost-sure envelopes; the matching exact base for all rotationally invariant
radially-concentrated ensembles, including a new \emph{exact} quaternionic law $M_m=c_\gamma^{-m/4+o_\PP(m)}$
that completes the picture across the three division algebras; the exact rectangular and
two-sided geometric bases on the lower side; an upper-tail large-deviation rate; and a deterministic
finite-alphabet obstruction showing that critical random sign frames fail phase-retrieval injectivity
with probability one.  For independent non-rotationally-invariant entries the matching upper side
is reduced, via a proved two-projection local central limit theorem and a proved high-overlap
negligibility estimate, to a single explicit overlap moment, which we pose as the central open
problem; the same estimate governs the fluctuation law, for which we prove the point-process
intensity and the one-sided Gumbel bound and conjecture the explicit law $1-e^{-e^{d_\F x}}$.
All bases and the Gumbel shape are confirmed numerically.  We close with a list of open problems on
universality, fluctuations, atomic full spark, complex phase retrieval, and concentration of the
extremal stability constant.
\end{abstract}
