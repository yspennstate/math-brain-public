\subsection*{A master finite-budget lower bound}

All of the distribution-free lower-side results have the same two-line proof: a per-submatrix
small-ball bound feeds the finite-budget principle.  We isolate this once.

\begin{theorem}[Master finite-budget lower bound]\label{thm:master-budget}
Let $\mathcal T_m$ be a finite family of selectors, and for each $T\in\mathcal T_m$ let $Y_T\ge0$
satisfy the per-selector small-ball bound
\[
  \PP\{Y_T\le t\}\le C_T\,t^{a_m},\qquad 0<t\le1,
\]
with a common exponent $a_m>0$ and deterministic constants $C_T\ge0$.  Put the
\emph{budget} $B_m=\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_m}C_T$.  Then for every $u_m>0$,
\[
  \PP\Bigl\{\min_{T\in\mathcal T_m}Y_T\le \exp\bigl[-(\log B_m+u_m)/a_m\bigr]\Bigr\}\le e^{-u_m},
\]
and on any common coupling with $\sum_m e^{-u_m}<\infty$ the event fails only finitely often a.s.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $t=\exp[-(\log B_m+u_m)/a_m]$, so $B_m t^{a_m}=e^{-u_m}$.  A union bound gives
$\PP\{\min_T Y_T\le t\}\le\sum_T C_T t^{a_m}=B_m t^{a_m}=e^{-u_m}$.  Summability and the first
Borel--Cantelli lemma give the almost-sure statement.  No independence is used.
\end{proof}

Every finite-budget lower side in the paper is Theorem~\ref{thm:master-budget} with the selector
small-ball constant $C_T$ supplied by the negative-second-moment distance estimate (the row
distance, resp.\ column distance and Cauchy--Binet block for tall and two-sided cases).  The
table records the data; in each row $C_F$ absorbs the universal $d_\F$-dimensional ball constant
and the $m^{O(1)}$ polynomial loss from the $\sqrt m$ in the distance bound.

\begin{remark}
Taking $u_m=a_m\varepsilon m$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:master-budget} recovers each
convergence-in-probability lower side $\liminf_m\tfrac1m\log\min_T Y_T\ge-\lim\tfrac{\log B_m}{a_m m}$,
and a summable $u_m$ (e.g.\ $u_m=2\log m$) gives the almost-sure lower envelope.  Thus the
density-rate ($K_m=e^{(\kappa+o(1))m}$), smoothed-frame ($K_m=C\sigma_m^{-d_\F}$),
moving-aspect (finite-entropy), and conditioning-transfer statements are all read off from the
single $\log B_m/a_m$ formula by substituting the corresponding budget.
\end{remark}
