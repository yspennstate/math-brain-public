## 1. First decisive correction: pointwise one-dimensional Fourier decay is false

For completeness, B17's source-valid obstruction is retained because it changes the proof architecture. For each fixed `s != 0` and every `delta in (0,pi/2)`, the phase-resonant sets

`I_k={x>0: |s x^2-2 pi k|<=delta}`

have lengths asymptotic to a positive constant times `k^{-1/2}`. Their total measure diverges. Choose a measurable union/subset `E` of total measure exactly `1` and put `f=1_E`. Then `f` is a probability density with `||f||_infty<=1`, while

`|int exp(i s x^2) f(x) dx| >= cos(delta)`.

Letting `delta` tend to zero shows

`sup_{f>=0, int f=1, ||f||_infty<=1} |int exp(i s x^2)f(x)dx| = 1`.

Hence no law-uniform pointwise `|s|^{-1/2}` decay can be a valid premise under the owner's hypotheses. This refutes only that proof step, not the conjecture.

## 2. New theorem: any definite 2x2 coordinate principal block closes with an explicit constant

### Theorem (conditional two-coordinate definite block)

Fix distinct original independent coordinates `i,j`, and let

`B=[[A_ii,A_ij],[A_ij,A_jj]]`.

If `B` is positive definite or negative definite, then the density of the full quadratic form `Q=X^TAX`, whenever it exists, satisfies the stronger small-ball estimate

`P(Q in I) <= (pi/sqrt(|det B|)) |I|`

for every interval `I`. Consequently `Q` is absolutely continuous with

`||p_Q||_infty <= pi/sqrt(|det B|)`.

In particular, if `|det B|>=1`, then `||p_Q||_infty<=pi`.

### Proof

Condition on all coordinates except `X_i,X_j`. Independence means that, for every fixed value of the other coordinates, `(X_i,X_j)` still has joint density

`g(x_i,x_j)=f_i(x_i)f_j(x_j) <= 1`.

The conditional quadratic is

`q_z(x)=x^T B x + ell_z^T x + c_z`,

where `x=(x_i,x_j)`. Complete the square. If `B` is positive definite,

`q_z(x)=(x-x_0)^T B (x-x_0)+c'_z`.

For an interval `I` of length `eta`, the planar Lebesgue area of the shell `{x:q_z(x) in I}` is at most

`pi eta/sqrt(det B)`.

Indeed, after `u=B^{1/2}(x-x_0)`, the Jacobian is `1/sqrt(det B)` and the area between squared-radius levels differing by `eta` is exactly `pi eta/sqrt(det B)` whenever both levels are above the minimum, and no larger when the interval crosses the minimum. Since `g<=1`,

`P(q_z(X_i,X_j) in I | X_{[n]\{i,j}}=z) <= pi eta/sqrt(det B)`.

For negative definite `B`, apply the same argument to `-q_z` and `-B`. Averaging the conditional inequality over the remaining coordinates preserves the same constant. The interval small-ball bound implies absolute continuity and the displayed `L^infty` density bound. QED.

### How this consumes B17

B17's diagonal same-sign pair is the special case `A_ij=0`. The argument above removes the coordinate-diagonality requirement **inside the selected pair**: arbitrary cross term `2A_ij X_iX_j` and arbitrary linear terms generated by conditioning the other coordinates are harmless as long as the selected 2x2 principal block is definite. Thus the same `pi` mechanism extends from two independent shifted squares to a full planar ellipse shell.

A useful corollary is that if three original coordinates have pairwise-zero mutual off-diagonal entries and diagonal magnitudes at least one, then two of their diagonal signs agree and the full arbitrary ambient matrix has density at most `pi` after conditioning the other coordinates. More generally, a single definite principal pair with determinant at least one suffices.

## 3. The tempting spectral-to-principal-pair bridge is false

The theorem above does **not** solve the owner's spectral hypothesis, because three large eigenvalues need not produce a coordinate principal pair with determinant bounded below by a universal constant.

For `n=2^m`, take three orthonormal Walsh vectors `u_1,u_2,u_3` whose coordinates are all `+/- 1/sqrt(n)`, and let

`A = u_1 u_1^T + u_2 u_2^T + u_3 u_3^T`.

Then `A` is an orthogonal projection of rank three. Its eigenvalues are exactly `1,1,1,0,...,0`, so it satisfies the target spectral threshold sharply. Yet

`A_ii=3/n`

for every coordinate, and for every pair `i != j`,

`0 <= det A[{i,j}] = 9/n^2 - A_ij^2 <= 9/n^2`.

Indeed `A_ij` is a sum of three signs divided by `n`, so the nonzero pair determinants are of order `1/n^2` (in the Walsh construction they are `8/n^2` when nonzero). Thus no argument of the form

`three eigenvalues >=1 => some definite coordinate 2x2 principal block has determinant >= c>0`

can be valid with dimension-independent `c`.

This is a genuine source-valid falsifier of the first obvious bridge from the new theorem to the full conjecture.

## 4. A second naive averaging bridge also fails

Let `E` be a two-dimensional subspace and `F(x)=prod_i f_i(x_i)`. A crude attempt after splitting off a same-sign two-eigenvector subspace is to bound each elliptic fiber by its amplitude supremum and hope for

`int_{E^perp} sup_{y in E} F(y+z) dz <= C`.

That mixed norm is not dimension-free even for cube densities. Take `F=1_[0,1]^n` and let `E` be spanned by two orthonormal flat Walsh sign vectors `u,v`. Then the left side is exactly the `(n-2)`-dimensional volume of the orthogonal projection of the unit cube onto `E^perp`. By the zonotope projection formula this equals

`sum_{i<j} |u_i v_j-u_j v_i|`.

Half the indices have sign ratio `u_i/v_i=+1` and half `-1`; exactly `(n/2)^2` cross-ratio pairs contribute `2/n`, giving projection volume `n/2`. Therefore the fiberwise-supremum coarea route loses a factor growing linearly with dimension and cannot establish the universal target.

This does not refute a properly averaged coarea/Brascamp-Lieb estimate; it identifies why the amplitude must remain inside the level-set integral.

## 5. Compact conditional end-to-end route and first unpaid implication

Among three eigenvalues of magnitude at least one, two have the same sign. Let `E` be their two-dimensional invariant eigenspace. On `E`, the quadratic form is definite with determinant at least one in spectral coordinates. If one could prove the nonlinear product-measure slice estimate

`sup_t int_{E^perp} int_{q_E(y)=t-q_{E^perp}(z)} F(y+z)/|grad q_E(y)| d sigma(y) dz <= C`

with `C` universal for every product density `F=prod f_i`, `||f_i||_infty<=1`, then the full conjecture would follow immediately (the displayed expression is exactly the density of `Q` after the orthogonal spectral splitting).

The new principal-block theorem proves this estimate when `E` is spanned by two original coordinate axes. The Walsh projection family shows that one cannot reduce the general case to one coordinate pair with uniformly nondegenerate determinant. The cube projection calculation shows that one cannot take a fiberwise supremum before integrating over `E^perp`.

**First essential missing implication:** prove a dimension-free, genuinely averaged nonlinear Brascamp-Lieb/coarea inequality for a two-dimensional same-sign spectral subspace while retaining the product amplitude inside the elliptic level integral, and use the third large eigenvalue to prevent the opposite-sign/logarithmic degeneration in the complementary geometry. A valid proof must survive the Walsh rank-three projection family above.
