# Round 440 truncated annular SAW boundary energy

Round 440 defines the boundary-energy quantity that matches the corrected
\(Q\)-weighted annular transfer kernel.

Fix a root \(x\), level \(u\ge1\), and truncation \(L\ge2\).  Let
\[
  X_u=V\setminus B_{u-1}(x)
\]
and let
\[
  C_{Q,\le L}^{X_u}(y,z)
  =
  \sum_{\substack{\eta:y\to z,\ \eta\subset X_u\ {\rm SAW}\\|\eta|\le L}}
  Q^{|\eta|}.
\]
For the boundary multiplicity vector
\[
  \nu_u(y)=\#\{a\in S_{u-1}(x):a\sim y\},
  \qquad y\in S_u(x),
\]
the truncated annular return mass satisfies
\[
  \sum_{a,b\in S_{u-1}(x)} A_{Q,u,\le L}^x(a,b)
  \le
  Q^2 \langle \nu_u,C_{Q,\le L-2}^{X_u}\nu_u\rangle.
\]
Here \(A_{Q,u,\le L}^x\) is the same exterior-return transfer kernel as in
Round 438, restricted to return intervals of length at most \(L\).

This is the exact deterministic counterpart of the killed percolation
boundary energy from Round 436.  The normalized scalar diagnostic is
\[
  \mathcal A_{Q,u,L}^x
  =
  \frac{Q^2}{|S_{u-1}(x)|}
  \langle \nu_u,C_{Q,\le L-2}^{X_u}\nu_u\rangle.
\]
If \(\mathcal A_{Q,u,L}^x\) were uniformly small at the slab scales, then
diffuse endpoint weights would give an annular return saving.  But Round 439
shows that scalar smallness is not enough for arbitrary skeleton endpoints.

The useful strengthened target is a weighted version.  For nonnegative
boundary weights \(f,g\) on \(S_{u-1}(x)\), define their pushed-out weights on
\(S_u(x)\) by
\[
  \widehat f(y)=\sum_{a\sim y,\ a\in S_{u-1}(x)} f(a),
  \qquad
  \widehat g(y)=\sum_{b\sim y,\ b\in S_{u-1}(x)} g(b).
\]
Then
\[
  \langle f,A_{Q,u,\le L}^x g\rangle
  \le
  Q^2
  \langle \widehat f,C_{Q,\le L-2}^{X_u}\widehat g\rangle.
\]

Thus the next analytic target can be stated cleanly:
prove a boundary-vector estimate for
\[
  \langle \widehat f,C_{Q,\le L}^{X_u}\widehat g\rangle
\]
for the particular endpoint weights \(f,g\) generated by radial-profile
skeletons.  A full \(L^2\) bound on \(C_{Q,\le L}^{X_u}\) would be sufficient
but likely too strong.  The more plausible route is an endpoint-spread
dichotomy: concentrated \(f,g\) should force a separate congestion profile,
while diffuse \(f,g\) can be controlled by the scalar boundary energy
\(\mathcal A_{Q,u,L}^x\).
