# T197: the free group factor isomorphism problem

Checked 12 Nov 2026. Status of the row: **reported solved, and the report stands up to a full reading.** The
23-page proof was read from the first line to the last and every step was checked by hand; no error was found. The
combinatorial and spectral core was also tested by computer. A specialist has since published a self-contained
proof of a stronger statement by the same strategy.

## What is claimed

1. OpenAI, "An isomorphism of the free group factors", 23 September 2026, 23 pages (repository openai/math at commit
   1581d4cb, `preprints/An-isomorphism-of-the-free-group-factors-September-23-2026/`; not on arXiv).
   - Theorem 1.1. For every integer n ≥ 3, L(F_n) ≅ L(F_{n+1}) by a unital normal trace-preserving ∗-isomorphism.
   - Theorem 1.2. L(F_2) ≅ L(F_3).
   - Corollary 6.1. All interpolated free group factors L(F_r), 1 < r ≤ ∞, are isomorphic, and each has fundamental
     group R_{>0}.
   - Corollary 7.1. None of the four free entropy dimensions δ, δ_0, δ^∗, δ^⋆ is an invariant of the von Neumann
     algebra generated: each takes every integer value n ≥ 2 on generating tuples of L(F_2).
2. D. Shlyakhtenko, "On the isomorphism of semicircular algebras", arXiv 2610.19590 (14 October 2026), 28 pages.
   "Building on OpenAI's proof of isomorphism of free group factors, we give a self-contained proof of a
   corresponding result for C∗-algebras generated by free semicircular systems": C∗(S_1, …, S_n) ≅ C∗(S_1, S_2) for
   every n in {2, 3, …, ∞}, which implies L(F_n) ≅ L(F_2) directly, rank two included. "This is the same strategy as
   OpenAI's paper, although implemented differently." Its acknowledgements say that the work is based on the
   OpenAI paper and on AI-generated proofs, and that a language model was used in analysing that proof and in
   preparing the manuscript.

Before 2026: Murray–von Neumann (1943) introduce the factors; Voiculescu (1990) and Rădulescu (1992) show that the
fundamental group of L(F_∞) is R_{>0}; Dykema and Rădulescu (1994) construct the interpolated factors, prove
L(F_s)^t ≅ L(F_{1+(s−1)/t²}), and deduce the alternative: the L(F_r) are all isomorphic or pairwise non-isomorphic.

## The argument

Fix freely independent Haar unitaries A_1, …, A_n, C generating M = L(F_{n+1}), put N = W∗(A_1, …, A_n) ≅ L(F_n),
Γ = F_n, and write A_g for the group word g evaluated at the A_j.

- S = arg(C) is self-adjoint, ‖S‖ ≤ π, τ(S) = 0, e^{iS} = C. The conjugates S_g = A_g S A_g∗, g in Γ, are free
  (Lemma 2.3), and ‖Σ k(g) S_g‖ ≤ 3π ‖k‖_{ℓ²} for every finitely supported real k (Lemma 2.5).
- For real finitely supported h_1, …, h_n on Γ, the differential equation X_j′ = i (Σ_g h_j(g) X_g S X_g∗) X_j
  with X(0) = A has a global unitary solution A(t) (Lemma 3.3), and A_j ↦ A_j(t), S ↦ S extends to a
  trace-preserving automorphism β_t of M (Proposition 3.1). The trace is preserved because the first variation of
  every moment vanishes at time 0 for every polynomial (Lemma 3.2: conjugating one S_g by a unitary from the algebra
  generated by the others does not change the joint law), hence all time derivatives vanish at 0, and the moments
  are real-analytic in t (Lemma 3.4).
- With the cocycle D on Γ defined by D x_j = h_j, D(gb) = Dg + λ(g) Db, one gets ‖β_t(A_j) − A_j‖ ≤ 3π|t| ‖h_j‖ and
  ‖β_t(A_w) − e^{itS} A_w‖ ≤ 3π|t| ‖Dw − δ_e‖. So if the h_j are small and Dw is close to δ_e for some word w, the
  automorphism β_1 hardly moves the generators and sends A_w close to C A_w.
- Lemma 4.1 produces such data. Take a = x_1, b_j = x_2^j x_3 x_2^{−j}, p_j = (a b_1)⋯(a b_j), w_m = p_m a,
  h_1 = h, h_j = 0 for j ≥ 2. Then Dw_m = T_m h with T_m = 1 + Σ_{j ≤ m} λ(p_j) (Lemma 4.2). The p_j are free, the
  spectral measure of T_m T_m∗/m at δ_e converges to (1/2π)√((4 − x)/x) dx on (0, 4), which has no atom at 0
  (Lemma 4.3), and h = T_m∗ R δ_e, with R the inverse of T_m T_m∗ cut off below dm, satisfies ‖h‖² ≤ 1/(dm) and
  ‖T_m h − δ_e‖² = μ_m([0, d)). Both are small for d small and then m large. This is where n ≥ 3 is used.
- Proposition 5.1. Let γ be the automorphism fixing the A_j with γ(C) = C A_w, and α = γ^{−1} ∘ β_1. Then α moves
  each A_j by little and α(A_w) is close to C: an automorphism of M that almost fixes a finite part of N and carries
  an element of N almost onto the missing generator. Iterating with error budgets chosen step by step gives a limit
  n-tuple that is Haar, free, and generates M, which is Theorem 1.1.
- Theorem 1.2: L(F_3) ≅ L(F_4) ≅ L(F_5); amplify by √2 and use L(F_3)^{√2} ≅ L(F_2), L(F_5)^{√2} ≅ L(F_3).

## What was verified by hand

Every section, each step.

- Section 2: the identification of M with L(F_d) from a generating Haar tuple (Lemma 2.2), freeness of the
  conjugates (2.3), the label rule for traces (2.4), and the norm bound (2.5) through the creation, annihilation and
  diagonal parts, 2π‖k‖ + π max|k| ≤ 3π‖k‖.
- Section 3: the first-variation identity and its extension to all orders (Lemma 3.2), global solvability and
  unitarity (3.3; H_j is self-adjoint because the coefficients are real), the uniform Taylor bound and analytic
  continuation (3.4), and Proposition 3.1 (isometry from the moments, surjectivity by running the flow backwards,
  the two displacement bounds).
- Section 4: freeness of a, b_1, …, b_m and of the prefixes p_j, the value Dw_m = T_m h, the count
  τ(Q_m^r) = Cat_r (m)_r + O_r(m^{r−1}) by noncrossing matchings, the passage from moments to weak convergence, the
  moments of the limit density, and the cutoff inversion. The vector h is real because T_m has integer coefficients
  and the cutoff is a real function of T_m T_m∗; replacing it by a finitely supported real vector costs an arbitrarily
  small ℓ² error since T_m is bounded.
- Section 5: the identities of Proposition 5.1, the budget arithmetic of the iteration, norm convergence of the
  generators, the Haar property of the limit tuple, and generation of M through L² density and the conditional
  expectation.
- Section 6: 1 + (3 − 1)/2 = 2 and 1 + (5 − 1)/2 = 3 in the amplification formula; the dichotomy then gives
  Corollary 6.1.
- Section 7: transport of joint distributions by the isomorphisms; δ = δ_0 = n on a free semicircular n-tuple and
  δ^∗ = δ^⋆ = β_1^{(2)}(F_n) − β_0^{(2)}(F_n) + 1 = n on the real and imaginary parts of the group generators.

Taken from the literature without re-proving: Dykema's amplification formula and the Dykema–Rădulescu alternative
(including Rădulescu's version with r = ∞), the bounded Borel functional calculus, the values of the free entropy
dimensions on semicircular systems, and the Mineyev–Shlyakhtenko formula. Theorem 1.1 itself uses none of these.

## A closed form the paper does not need

Multiplying T_m by a Haar unitary free from the p_j turns it into a sum of N = m + 1 free Haar unitaries, and the
Hermitian dilation of such a sum is the adjacency operator of the N-regular tree. So for every finite m the law of
T_m T_m∗ at δ_e is the image under s ↦ s² of the Kesten–McKay law:

  density N √(4(N − 1)t − t²) / (2π t (N² − t)) on 0 < t < 4(N − 1), N = m + 1.

There is no atom at 0 for any m ≥ 1, the mass below dm is at most ν([0, d]) ≈ (2/π)√d, and the two errors of the
cutoff inverse are explicit integrals. Lemma 4.1 therefore holds with explicit constants: for a target η take
d ≈ (πη²/2)² and m ≥ 1/(dη²).

## Reception

arXiv searches of 12 Nov 2026 (`lit/search/s1.xml`, `s2.xml`, `s3.xml`; papers submitted since 20 Sep 2026): the
Shlyakhtenko paper is the only follow-up on the isomorphism itself, and no paper disputes it. Shlyakhtenko derives
further consequences: a continuum of groups with isomorphic von Neumann algebras that are pairwise not measure
equivalent, Z ∗ Z ≅ C∗(S_1, S_2) for the Jiang–Su algebra, and free absorption for reduced free group C∗-algebras.
No formalization of the theorem is listed in `lean/formalization.yaml` of openai/math.

## For the list

Take T197 off the problems to attack: L(F_n) ≅ L(F_m) for all n, m ≥ 2 (OpenAI, 23 Sep 2026; Shlyakhtenko,
arXiv 2610.19590). Level of the check: full reading of the original, by one reader; the specialist's paper was read
in its introduction, Proposition 1.1 and Section 5, not line by line.

Open next to it:
- an explicit isomorphism, or an explicit pair of generators of L(F_3) with the law of two free Haar unitaries; the
  proof gives a limit of automorphisms with no closed form;
- whether L(Γ) ≅ L(Λ) forces the first L²-Betti numbers to vanish together (stated as open by Shlyakhtenko);
- which inclusions N ⊂ M have the automorphism approximation property; the reduced C∗-algebras of free groups of
  different ranks are not isomorphic (Pimsner–Voiculescu), so the property fails there;
- an invariant replacing free entropy dimension that does see the algebra and not the generators (for instance
  1-boundedness-type properties), now that the dimension itself is ruled out.

Reusable: a derivation built from a group cocycle whose value on one long word is nearly δ_e while its values on the
generators are small; the cutoff inverse of a sum of free unitaries, whose spectrum is of size m with no mass near 0;
the Baire-category form of the conclusion (an automorphism almost fixing a finite set of the subalgebra and carrying
one of its elements almost onto a given element of the big algebra gives an isomorphism).
