### A useful 2026 separation fact

Wu–Yang prove that the set \(U\) of counterexamples to the **uniform** Littlewood statement has full Hausdorff dimension \(2\). Einsiedler–Katok–Lindenstrauss prove that the possible counterexample set \(E\) to the **classical** conjecture has Hausdorff dimension \(0\). Therefore

\[
\dim_H(U\setminus E)=2.
\]

So there is a full-dimensional set of pairs that fail the uniform conjecture while satisfying the classical conjecture. This makes it mathematically unsafe to mine the 2026 uniform counterexamples as candidate refutations of the present target without an additional argument establishing a positive classical liminf.

## 3. Elementary reductions that should be permanently removed from the hard core

### Proposition 3.1 — rational coordinates

If either \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\) is rational, the conjecture is immediate: if \(\alpha=a/b\), then every positive multiple \(Q\) of \(b\) has \(\|Q\alpha\|=0\).

This includes the user's requested elementary cases rather than silently discarding them.

### Proposition 3.2 — rational affine dependence of \(1,\alpha,\beta\)

If \(1,\alpha,\beta\) are linearly dependent over \(\mathbb Q\), then the classical Littlewood conjecture holds for \((\alpha,\beta)\).

#### Proof

The rational-coordinate case was handled above, so assume \(\alpha\) irrational. After clearing denominators we may write

\[
\beta=\frac{r\alpha+s}{t}
\]

with integers \(r,s,t\), \(t\ne0\). Let \(p_k/q_k\) be convergents of \(\alpha\), so

\[
|q_k\alpha-p_k|<\frac1{q_{k+1}}.
\]

Put \(Q_k=|t|q_k\). Then, using the integers \(|t|p_k\) and the appropriate integer obtained from \(r p_k+s q_k\),

\[
\|Q_k\alpha\|
\le |t|\,|q_k\alpha-p_k|
<\frac{|t|}{q_{k+1}},
\]

and

\[
\|Q_k\beta\|
=\left\|\operatorname{sgn}(t)(r q_k\alpha+s q_k)\right\|
\le |r|\,|q_k\alpha-p_k|
<\frac{|r|}{q_{k+1}}.
\]

Hence

\[
Q_k\|Q_k\alpha\|\|Q_k\beta\|
< |t|^2|r|\frac{q_k}{q_{k+1}^2}
\le \frac{|t|^2|r|}{q_k}\to0.
\]

Therefore every putative counterexample must satisfy

\[
1,\alpha,\beta\quad\text{linearly independent over }\mathbb Q.
\]

In particular, if \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) lie in the same quadratic field, then \(1,\alpha,\beta\) are automatically \(\mathbb Q\)-dependent and the pair is not in the hard core. Thus pairs such as \((\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\), which lie in distinct quadratic fields, remain legitimate stress tests.

### Proposition 3.3 — both coordinates must be badly approximable

If either coordinate is not badly approximable, then the conjecture holds by taking a sequence \(Q_j\) with \(Q_j\|Q_j\alpha\|\to0\) (or the analogous sequence for \(\beta\)) and bounding the other nearest-integer factor by \(1/2\).

Thus the genuine hard core is

- \(1,\alpha,\beta\) rationally independent, and
- both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) badly approximable.

This is the scope assumed below.

## 4. Exact fixed-lattice theorem for every adjacent-convergent frame

Let \(p_j/q_j\) be the convergents of \(\alpha\), and \(r_j/s_j\) those of \(\beta\). Fix indices \(m,n\), and set

\[
\theta=[a_{m+2};a_{m+3},\ldots],
\qquad
\eta=[b_{n+2};b_{n+3},\ldots].
\]

Write

\[
D_\alpha=q_{m+1}\theta+q_m,
\qquad
D_\beta=s_{n+1}\eta+s_n.
\]

Define the rank-three integer kernel

\[
L_{m,n}:=
\{(x,y,z,v)\in\mathbb Z^4:
 xq_m+yq_{m+1}=zs_n+vs_{n+1}\}.
\]

For \(u=(x,y,z,v)\in L_{m,n}\), define

\[
Q=xq_m+yq_{m+1}=zs_n+vs_{n+1},
\]

\[
P=xp_m+yp_{m+1},
\qquad
R=zr_n+vr_{n+1}.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — all frames normalize to one fixed unimodular Littlewood lattice

The map

\[
\Phi_{m,n}:L_{m,n}\to\mathbb R^3,
\qquad
u\mapsto(Q,Q\alpha-P,Q\beta-R)
\]

is a bijection onto the lattice

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}
=
\{(Q,Q\alpha-P,Q\beta-R):P,R,Q\in\mathbb Z\}.
}
\]

The lattice \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\) is unimodular and, crucially, is **independent of \(m,n\)**.

#### Proof

Consecutive convergent matrices

\[
U_\alpha=
\begin{pmatrix}
p_m&p_{m+1}\\
q_m&q_{m+1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U_\beta=
\begin{pmatrix}
r_n&r_{n+1}\\
s_n&s_{n+1}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

have determinant \(\pm1\). Thus every integer pair \((P,Q)\) has a unique integer coordinate pair \((x,y)\) in the columns of \(U_\alpha\), and every integer pair \((R,Q)\) has a unique integer coordinate pair \((z,v)\) in the columns of \(U_\beta\). Therefore \((P,R,Q)\in\mathbb Z^3\) is in bijection with \(L_{m,n}\).

Under that bijection, \(\Phi_{m,n}\) is simply

\[
(P,R,Q)\mapsto(Q,Q\alpha-P,Q\beta-R).
\]

The corresponding real matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&1\\
-1&0&\alpha\\
0&-1&\beta
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is \(1\). Hence the image of \(\mathbb Z^3\) has covolume one. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — exact covolume in unnormalized tail coordinates

Let

\[
A=y-x\theta,
\qquad
B=v-z\eta.
\]

The prior exact formulas give

\[
Q\alpha-P=\frac{(-1)^{m+1}A}{D_\alpha},
\qquad
Q\beta-R=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}B}{D_\beta}.
\]

Therefore the image lattice

\[
\{(Q,A,B):u\in L_{m,n}\}
\]

has covolume exactly

\[
\boxed{D_\alpha D_\beta.}
\]

This is not merely an asymptotic determinant estimate. It is exact.

### Interpretation

The adjacent-convergent kernel method does **not** generate a changing family of new Diophantine lattices. After the natural tail normalization, every frame is exactly the same lattice \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\), presented in a different anisotropic coordinate gauge.

This explains why a universal theorem about the critical kernel vectors is essentially a theorem about the classical Littlewood lattice itself rather than a separate four-variable problem.

## 5. Exact inverse formulas: coefficient boxes are anisotropic boxes in the fixed lattice

Let

\[
\varepsilon_m=(-1)^{m+1}.
\]

Since

\[
p_mq_{m+1}-p_{m+1}q_m=\varepsilon_m,
\]

solving for \((x,y)\) from \((P,Q)\) gives

\[
x=\frac{q_{m+1}P-p_{m+1}Q}{\varepsilon_m},
\qquad
 y=\frac{-q_mP+p_mQ}{\varepsilon_m}.
\]

Put

\[
E_\alpha=Q\alpha-P.
\]

Using

\[
q_{m+1}\alpha-p_{m+1}=\frac{\varepsilon_m}{D_\alpha},
\qquad
q_m\alpha-p_m=-\frac{\varepsilon_m\theta}{D_\alpha},
\]

we obtain the exact inverse-coordinate identities

\[
\boxed{
x=\frac{Q}{D_\alpha}-\varepsilon_m q_{m+1}E_\alpha,
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
y=\frac{\theta Q}{D_\alpha}+\varepsilon_m q_mE_\alpha.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Likewise, with \(\varepsilon_n=(-1)^{n+1}\) and \(E_\beta=Q\beta-R\),

\[
\boxed{
z=\frac{Q}{D_\beta}-\varepsilon_n s_{n+1}E_\beta,
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
v=\frac{\eta Q}{D_\beta}+\varepsilon_n s_nE_\beta.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

These formulas invert the tail identities exactly and are useful because the coefficient norm can now be read geometrically in the fixed lattice.

### Proposition 5.1 — uniform norm equivalence in the badly approximable hard core

Assume all partial quotients of \(\alpha\) are at most \(A\). Then

\[
1<\theta<A+1,
\qquad
\frac1{A+1}\le \frac{q_m}{q_{m+1}}<1,
\]

and hence

\[
D_\alpha\asymp_A q_{m+1}.
\]

Equations (5.1)–(5.2) imply, with constants depending only on \(A\),

\[
\boxed{
\max(|x|,|y|)
\asymp_A
\max\left(\frac{|Q|}{D_\alpha},\ q_{m+1}|E_\alpha|\right).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

If \(T=\max(|Q|/D_\alpha,q_{m+1}|E_\alpha|)\), then (5.1) gives \(|x|\le2T\), while (5.2) gives

\[
|y|\le (A+1)T+T=(A+2)T.
\]

Conversely, if \(H=\max(|x|,|y|)\), then

\[
|Q|\le H(q_m+q_{m+1})\le2Hq_{m+1}\le2H D_\alpha,
\]

and from the exact tail form

\[
D_\alpha|E_\alpha|=|y-x\theta|\le H(1+\theta)\le H(A+2).
\]

Since \(q_{m+1}/D_\alpha<1\),

\[
q_{m+1}|E_\alpha|\le H(A+2).
\]

This proves (5.5). QED.

The identical statement holds for \(\beta\), with its partial-quotient bound.

### Corollary 5.2 — the \(1/3\) exponent is the true volume threshold, without a comparability assumption

Let

\[
q_*:=q_{m+1},
\qquad s_*:=s_{n+1},
\qquad Q_0:=\max(q_*,s_*).
\]

In the badly approximable hard core, the coefficient cube

\[
|x|,|y|,|z|,|v|\le H
\]

intersected with the kernel is, up to constants depending only on the bounded-partial-quotient constants, the same as asking the fixed unimodular lattice \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\) for points in an anisotropic box of scales

\[
|Q|\lesssim H\min(q_*,s_*),
\]

\[
|E_\alpha|\lesssim \frac{H}{q_*},
\qquad
|E_\beta|\lesssim \frac{H}{s_*}.
\]

The volume scale of this box is therefore

\[
\asymp
H^3\frac{\min(q_*,s_*)}{q_*s_*}
=
\boxed{\frac{H^3}{Q_0}}.
\]

Thus the critical threshold \(H\asymp Q_0^{1/3}\) is not an artifact of assuming comparable convergent denominators. It is the intrinsic constant-volume threshold of the fixed rank-three Littlewood lattice under these continued-fraction gauges.

This strengthens the previous checkpoint's geometric diagnosis: highly unbalanced frames change the shape of the box but not the critical exponent.

## 6. The many-convergent-variable route collapses exactly to rank three

A tempting way to improve the Thue–Siegel exponent is to use many consecutive convergent denominators on each side, producing many formal variables. The following exact lemma shows why raw variable count by itself cannot create a higher-dimensional approximation lattice.

### Theorem 6.1 — finite block collapse

Fix two consecutive convergent vectors

\[
c_m=(p_m,q_m),\qquad c_{m+1}=(p_{m+1},q_{m+1}).
\]

Because \(\det(c_m,c_{m+1})=\pm1\), they form a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of \(\mathbb Z^2\). Therefore every convergent vector \(c_j=(p_j,q_j)\) has unique integer coordinates

\[
c_j=A_jc_m+B_jc_{m+1},
\qquad A_j,B_j\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Consequently, for any finite integer coefficient family \((\xi_j)\),

\[
\sum_j\xi_j c_j
=Xc_m+Yc_{m+1},
\]

where

\[
X=\sum_j\xi_jA_j,
\qquad
Y=\sum_j\xi_jB_j.
\]

The same holds on the \(\beta\) side. Hence every common-denominator equation formed from arbitrary finite blocks of convergents collapses **exactly** to one adjacent-pair equation

\[
Xq_m+Yq_{m+1}=Zs_n+Vs_{n+1},
\]

and its associated numerator sums collapse at the same time:

\[
\sum_j\xi_jp_j=Xp_m+Yp_{m+1}.
\]

Therefore its approximation errors are exactly those of the effective four coefficients \((X,Y,Z,V)\).

#### Consequence

A high-dimensional Siegel-lemma bound on the *raw* coefficients \(\xi_j\) does not improve Littlewood unless one also proves that the induced effective adjacent coefficients \(X,Y,Z,V\) remain small or acquire extra tail cancellation. The presentation dimension is larger, but the true arithmetic lattice remains rank three.

### Example 6.2 — exponential collapse amplification for the all-ones continued fraction

For a continued fraction segment with recurrence

\[
c_{j+1}=c_j+c_{j-1},
\]

we have for \(k\ge1\)

\[
\boxed{
c_{m+k}=F_{k-1}c_m+F_kc_{m+1},
}
\]

where \(F_0=0,F_1=1\).

Thus a raw block coefficient vector having a single coefficient \(1\) on a distant convergent \(c_{m+k}\) induces adjacent coefficients of size \(F_k\), exponential in block length. This is a concrete obstruction to arguing that more formal variables automatically improve the critical exponent.

## 7. Reinterpretation of the previous tail-cancellation criterion

The prior checkpoint defined

\[
A=y-x\theta,
\qquad
B=v-z\eta
\]

and obtained

\[
Q\|Q\alpha\|\|Q\beta\|
\le
\frac{|Q|\,|A|\,|B|}{D_\alpha D_\beta}.
\]

Theorem 4.1 now shows that

\[
\frac{A}{D_\alpha}=\pm E_\alpha,
\qquad
\frac{B}{D_\beta}=\pm E_\beta,
\]

so the normalized product is simply

\[
|Q E_\alpha E_\beta|.
\]

This is the classical multiplicative height on the fixed unimodular lattice \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\).

The useful conclusion is not that the previous criterion was wrong; it is that its missing cancellation cannot be supplied by coordinate changes alone. A proof must establish a genuinely arithmetic/dynamical recurrence phenomenon for the fixed lattice, or exploit a special cone/sign structure not captured by symmetric geometry of numbers.

## 8. Relation to Matala-aho's 2026 `++`-solution obstruction

Matala-aho's recent paper identifies sign-restricted small solutions of

\[
xB_m+yB_{m+1}=zD_n+vD_{n+1}
\]

as a sufficient mechanism in one of its criteria; the paper explicitly leaves the needed infinite sequence of suitably bounded `++` solutions open.

The fixed-lattice theorem clarifies why symmetric Thue–Siegel alone cannot guarantee this. A symmetric short-vector theorem sees a centrally symmetric rank-three lattice and can return coefficients with cancellation signs. The `++` condition asks for a lattice point in a specific cone after applying a frame-dependent unimodular coordinate map. Cone occupancy is extra information, not a consequence of covolume alone.

A promising precise subproblem is therefore:

> In the badly approximable hard core, can one prove that infinitely many of the frame-dependent critical boxes contain a point of \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\) whose inverse coordinates satisfy the sign pattern required by the `++` criterion?

Equations (5.1)–(5.4) turn that sign question into explicit inequalities in \((Q,E_\alpha,E_\beta)\), making it possible to compare directly with positive-diagonal dynamics rather than treating \((x,y,z,v)\) as opaque coefficients.

## 10. Failed routes / obstructions sharpened in this run

1. **More consecutive convergents do not create more arithmetic dimension.** They collapse exactly to two adjacent coordinates on each side.
2. **Unbalanced convergent scales do not beat the \(1/3\) threshold by volume alone.** In the badly approximable hard core, the continued-fraction coefficient box always has fixed-lattice volume scale \(H^3/Q_0\), where \(Q_0\) is the larger denominator scale.
3. **Uniform-Littlewood counterexamples are not classical candidates by default.** In fact a full-dimensional set of them satisfy the classical conjecture, by combining Wu–Yang with the dimension-zero classical exceptional-set theorem.
4. **Changing frames is not changing the normalized lattice.** Every frame is the same \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\) in a different anisotropic gauge. Any proof based only on finding a symmetric short vector at each frame is therefore confronting the original Littlewood obstruction in disguise.

These are negative/structural results, not a resolution.
