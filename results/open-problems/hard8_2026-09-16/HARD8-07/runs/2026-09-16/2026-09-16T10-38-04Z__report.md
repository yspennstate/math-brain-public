## 3. Elementary hard-core reduction and notation

If either coordinate is rational, the conjecture is immediate along multiples of its denominator. If an irrational coordinate has unbounded continued-fraction partial quotients, its convergent denominators already give a sequence on which the Littlewood product tends to zero. Thus a genuine counterexample must have both coordinates badly approximable.

Assume from now on that the partial quotients of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are bounded by
\[
A_\alpha,\qquad A_\beta.
\]
Write convergents
\[
\frac{A_m}{B_m}\to\alpha,
\qquad
\frac{C_n}{D_n}\to\beta.
\]
For the \(\beta\)-tail use
\[
\eta_n=[d_{n+2};d_{n+3},\ldots]>1,
\qquad
\mathcal D_n=D_{n+1}\eta_n+D_n.
\]
Because all \(\beta\)-digits are at most \(A_\beta\),
\[
1<\eta_n<A_\beta+1,
\qquad
D_{n+1}\le(A_\beta+1)D_n.
\]
Put
\[
C_\beta=A_\beta+2.
\]

For integers \((z,v)\ne(0,0)\), define the signed portal
\[
q_0=zD_n+vD_{n+1},
\qquad
q=|q_0|.
\]
The exact continued-fraction identity from the earlier checkpoint is
\[
q_0\beta-(zC_n+vC_{n+1})
=
\frac{\varepsilon_n(v-z\eta_n)}{\mathcal D_n},
\qquad
\varepsilon_n\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
Define the **tail defect**
\[
s_n(z,v)=|v-z\eta_n|.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\|q\beta\|
\le
\frac{s_n(z,v)}{\mathcal D_n}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For an \(\alpha\)-frame put
\[
M=B_{m+1}
\]
and define the exact modular coordinate
\[
\rho_{m,n}(z,v)
=
\left\|
\frac{qA_{m+1}}{M}
\right\|.
\]
The preceding checkpoint proved, by the centered representation \(q=xB_m+yM\),
\[
\boxed{
\|q\alpha\|
\le
2\rho_{m,n}(z,v)+\frac{q}{M^2}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
No numerical approximation to \(\alpha\) is used in \(\rho\); it is a rational modular quantity.

## 4. Main new inequality: retain the exact beta-tail defect

The previous run bounded \(s_n(z,v)\) by \(O(R)\). Keeping it produces a genuinely stronger two-parameter estimate.

### Theorem 4.1 — anisotropic signed-portal inequality

Suppose
\[
|z|,|v|\le R.
\]
Then, for every pair of indices \(m,n\),
\[
\boxed{
q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|
\le
2C_\beta R\,s_n(z,v)\,\rho_{m,n}(z,v)
+
C_\beta^2R^2D_n\,\frac{s_n(z,v)}{M^2}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

First,
\[
q
\le
R(D_n+D_{n+1})
\le
C_\beta RD_n.
\]
Also \(\mathcal D_n\ge D_n\), so by (3.1)
\[
q\|q\beta\|
\le
\frac q{\mathcal D_n}s_n(z,v)
\le
C_\beta R s_n(z,v).
\]
Multiplying this by (3.2) gives (4.1). QED.

The old isotropic estimate is recovered by throwing away the new information and using
\[
s_n(z,v)
\le |v|+\eta_n|z|
< C_\beta R.
\]
The point of (4.1) is that many useful portals have \(s_n\ll R\).

## 5. Matched scale and an anisotropic Littlewood criterion

Choose \(m=m_*(n)\) by
\[
B_{m+1}\le D_n<B_{m+2}.
\]
Since \(B_{m+2}\le(A_\alpha+1)B_{m+1}\),
\[
\frac{D_n}{A_\alpha+1}<M_n^*:=B_{m_*(n)+1}\le D_n.
\]
Write
\[
\rho_n^*(z,v)
=
\left\|
\frac{qA_{m_*(n)+1}}{M_n^*}
\right\|.
\]
Then (4.1) becomes
\[
\boxed{
q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|
\le
2C_\beta R s_n(z,v)\rho_n^*(z,v)
+
C_2\frac{R^2s_n(z,v)}{D_n},
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
where
\[
C_2=C_\beta^2(A_\alpha+1)^2.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — anisotropic growing-strip criterion

Let \(R_n,S_n>0\) satisfy
\[
R_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
\frac{R_n^2S_n}{D_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Suppose that for infinitely many \(n\) there are nonzero integer pairs \((z_n,v_n)\) with
\[
|z_n|,|v_n|\le R_n,
\qquad
s_n(z_n,v_n)\le S_n,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
R_nS_n\rho_n^*(z_n,v_n)\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Then Littlewood holds for \((\alpha,\beta)\).

#### Proof

The bounded-type denominator lemma proved in the preceding report gives, for \(R_n<D_n\),
\[
q_n\ge\frac{D_n}{C_\beta R_n}\to\infty.
\]
Apply (5.1), then use \(s_n\le S_n\). Its first term tends to zero by (5.3), while its second tends to zero by (5.2). QED.

This strictly strengthens the prior isotropic criterion. If one only knows \(S_n\asymp R_n\), then (5.2) gives \(R_n=o(D_n^{1/3})\) and (5.3) becomes the former \(R_n^2\rho\to0\). But if
\[
S_n\asymp R_n^{-1},
\]
which is the natural size of a continued-fraction-quality approximation to \(\eta_n\), the criterion becomes merely
\[
R_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
\rho_n^*(z_n,v_n)\to0.
\]
So the coefficient range can move from the old cubic-root scale to almost linear scale when the portal is also well aligned with the \(\beta\)-tail.

More generally, choosing
\[
S_n=R_n^{-1+\epsilon}
\]
requires only
\[
R_n^{1+\epsilon}=o(D_n),
\]
so \(R_n\) may be as large as \(D_n^{1/(1+\epsilon)-o(1)}\).

## 6. Exact identification of tail-convergent portals

The smallest-tail-defect portals are not mysterious. Let
\[
\frac{P_j^{(n)}}{Q_j^{(n)}}
\]
be the \(j\)-th convergent of
\[
\eta_n=[d_{n+2};d_{n+3},\ldots],
\]
indexed so \(P_0^{(n)}/Q_0^{(n)}=d_{n+2}/1\). Then the continuant recurrences give exactly
\[
\boxed{
Q_j^{(n)}D_n+P_j^{(n)}D_{n+1}=D_{n+j+2}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Thus taking
\[
z=Q_j^{(n)},\qquad v=P_j^{(n)}
\]
simply reproduces a later \(\beta\)-convergent denominator. Correspondingly,
\[
|v-z\eta_n|
<\frac1{Q_{j+1}^{(n)}}.
\]
For bounded type, consecutive \(Q_j^{(n)}\)'s are comparable, so at coefficient scale \(R\asymp Q_j^{(n)}\) this has precisely the favorable size \(s_n\asymp1/R\).

This observation both clarifies and limits the most naive use of Theorem 5.1: restricting only to exact tail convergents reduces to asking whether \(\|D_N\alpha\|\to0\) along some subsequence of \(\beta\)-convergent denominators, which need not be forced by a generic lacunary argument. The real opportunity is therefore to exploit the **whole thin strip** of near-tail approximants, not only one convergent ray.

## 7. New counterexample obstruction: near-tail portals must form a near-optimal circle packing

Theorem 5.1 gives a stronger contrapositive when one uses differences of two near-tail portals.

Assume, for contradiction purposes, that
\[
\Lambda
:=
\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|
>0.
\tag{7.1}
\]
For fixed \(n,R,S\), define the strip of signed portals
\[
\mathcal C_n(R,S)
=
\left\{
(z,v)\in\mathbb Z^2:
|z|,|v|\le R,
\ |v-z\eta_n|\le S
\right\}.
\]
Associate to each \(u=(z,v)\) its rational phase
\[
\theta_n(u)
=
\frac{(zD_n+vD_{n+1})A_{m_*+1}}{M_n^*}
\pmod1.
\tag{7.2}
\]
For two vectors \(u,u'\), the circular phase distance is exactly the modular coordinate of the difference:
\[
\|\theta_n(u)-\theta_n(u')\|
=
\rho_n^*(u-u').
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — pairwise separation under a Littlewood counterexample

Let \(R_n,S_n\) satisfy
\[
R_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
\frac{R_n^2S_n}{D_n}\to0.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Then, under (7.1), for all sufficiently large \(n\), every two distinct points
\[
u,u'\in\mathcal C_n(R_n,S_n)
\]
satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\|\theta_n(u)-\theta_n(u')\|
\ge
\frac{\Lambda}{32C_\beta R_nS_n}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

#### Proof

For \(w=u-u'=(z,v)\),
\[
|z|,|v|\le2R_n,
\qquad
s_n(w)\le2S_n.
\]
Since \(R_n=o(D_n)\), eventually \(2R_n<D_n\). A nonzero difference cannot give the exact zero denominator relation \((D_{n+1},-D_n)\), and the earlier bounded-type denominator lemma gives
\[
q(w)
\ge
\frac{D_n}{2C_\beta R_n}
\to\infty.
\]
Hence, by (7.1), eventually
\[
q(w)\|q(w)\alpha\|\|q(w)\beta\|\ge\frac\Lambda2.
\]
Apply (5.1) to \(w\), with coefficient radius \(2R_n\) and tail bound \(2S_n\):
\[
\frac\Lambda2
\le
8C_\beta R_nS_n\rho_n^*(w)
+
8C_2\frac{R_n^2S_n}{D_n}.
\]
By (7.4), the final term is eventually at most \(\Lambda/4\). Thus
\[
\rho_n^*(w)
\ge
\frac{\Lambda}{32C_\beta R_nS_n},
\]
and (7.3) gives (7.5). QED.

This is much stronger than saying one short character must miss zero. A genuine counterexample forces **all** modular phases coming from an entire thin \(\beta\)-resonant strip to repel one another at the reciprocal cardinality scale.

## 8. There are linearly many near-tail portals in strip area \(RS\)

The previous theorem is useful only if the strip contains many lattice points. Bounded continued fractions supply that deterministically.

### Lemma 8.1 — bounded-type strip population

Let \(\eta>1\) be irrational with all continued-fraction digits at most \(A\). Let \(Z\ge2\), choose a convergent denominator \(Q\) of \(\eta\) with
\[
Q\le Z<Q',
\]
where \(Q'\) is the next denominator. Then
\[
Q'\le(A+1)Q,
\qquad
Q\ge\frac{Z}{A+1}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
If
\[
0<S\le\frac14,
\qquad
S\ge\frac2Z,
\]
then the number of integers \(1\le z<Q\) for which
\[
\|z\eta\|\le S
\]
is at least
\[
\boxed{
2QS-7.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(P/Q\) be the chosen convergent. Then
\[
\left|\eta-\frac PQ\right|<\frac1{QQ'}.
\]
For \(0\le z<Q\),
\[
\left|z\eta-\frac{zP}{Q}\right|<\frac1{Q'}=:\delta.
\]
Because \(P\) is invertible modulo \(Q\), the points \(zP/Q\pmod1\) are exactly the uniform grid \(j/Q\). Also \(Q'>Z\), hence \(\delta<1/Z\le S/2\).

Every grid point in
\[
[\delta,S-\delta]\cup[1-S+\delta,1-\delta]
\]
has its corresponding \(z\eta\pmod1\) inside
\([
0,S]\cup[1-S,1)
\), so \(\|z\eta\|\le S\). The total length of the two shrunken intervals is \(2S-4\delta\). Counting grid points loses at most one point per interval, hence gives at least
\[
Q(2S-4\delta)-2
>
2QS-6.
\]
Removing \(z=0\) costs at most one more point, proving (8.2). QED.

Apply this to \(\eta_n\), whose digits are bounded by \(A_\beta\). To ensure both coefficients stay inside the radius \(R\), put
\[
Z=\left\lfloor\frac{R}{A_\beta+2}\right\rfloor.
\]
For \(1\le z\le Z\), choose \(v\) to be the nearest integer to \(z\eta_n\). Since \(\eta_n<A_\beta+1\), for large \(R\),
\[
|v|<R.
\]
Therefore, whenever \(2/Z\le S\le1/4\), the strip \(\mathcal C_n(R,S)\) contains at least
\[
\boxed{
\frac{2ZS}{A_\beta+1}-7
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
explicit one-sided candidates with \(z>0\).

In particular, if \(RS\to\infty\), the strip contains \(\gg_{A_\beta}RS\) candidates.

## 9. Combined rigidity statement

Combining Theorem 7.1 and Lemma 8.1 gives the strongest structural conclusion of this iteration.

### Corollary 9.1 — persistent near-critical packing

If \((\alpha,\beta)\) is a classical Littlewood counterexample, then for every scale choice satisfying
\[
R_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
R_n^2S_n=o(D_n),
\qquad
R_nS_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{1}{R_n}\ll_{A_\beta} S_n\le\frac14,
\]
the \(\gg R_nS_n\) explicit near-tail portals from Lemma 8.1 have \(\alpha\)-modular phases that are pairwise separated by
\[
\gg_{\alpha,\beta}\frac1{R_nS_n}.
\]

Thus the phases constitute a **constant-density, constant-factor optimal packing of the circle** at every such admissible scale.

A particularly revealing choice is
\[
S_n=R_n^{-1+\epsilon},
\qquad 0<\epsilon<1.
\]
Then there are \(\gg R_n^\epsilon\) beta-resonant candidates, while the deterministic error condition is only
\[
R_n^{1+\epsilon}=o(D_n).
\]
Hence \(R_n\) may approach the almost-linear range
\[
R_n=D_n^{1/(1+\epsilon)-o(1)},
\]
yet a counterexample would still have to arrange the corresponding growing collection of phases almost as efficiently as the best possible circle packing.

This is a materially stronger necessary condition than the preceding isotropic result, which only forced constant-factor saturation of a two-dimensional Dirichlet bound in boxes \(R=o(D_n^{1/3})\).

## 10. Why a bare hyperbolic pigeonhole still does not close the proof

The natural hope is that the two small quantities
\[
s_n(z,v)=|v-z\eta_n|
\quad\text{and}\quad
\rho_n^*(z,v)
\]
should have a product better than the rectangular Dirichlet threshold. The strip population shows why simple counting remains critical.

At coefficient scale \(R\), a strip of width \(S\) contains on the order of \(RS\) integer pairs. Mapping those pairs to the circle through \(\theta_n\), a generic pigeonhole principle only guarantees a pairwise phase collision of order
\[
\frac1{RS}.
\]
For the difference vector, this gives exactly
\[
RS\,\rho=O(1),
\]
whereas Theorem 5.1 needs \(o(1)\). So simply partitioning the hyperbola into dyadic rectangles, or replacing one isotropic box by many thin strips, does not by itself provide the missing vanishing factor.

Theorem 7.1 identifies what a counterexample must do instead: it must realize this critical packing essentially simultaneously for every admissible strip scale. Any successful continuation of this route has to exploit incompatibility of these packings under the continued-fraction recurrence
\[
\eta_n=d_{n+2}+\frac1{\eta_{n+1}}
\]
and the corresponding recurrence of \((D_n,D_{n+1})\), rather than a one-scale volume argument.

## 11. Exact modular-zero lattice: a secondary obstruction to a tempting shortcut

Another tempting idea is to force
\[
\rho_{m,n}(z,v)=0
\]
exactly by solving a congruence. Because \(\gcd(A_{m+1},M)=1\), this is equivalent to
\[
M\mid zD_n+vD_{n+1}.
\]
Define
\[
L_{n,M}
=
\{(z,v)\in\mathbb Z^2:
 zD_n+vD_{n+1}\equiv0\pmod M\}.
\]
Since \(\gcd(D_n,D_{n+1})=1\), the congruence map onto \(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\) is surjective, so
\[
\boxed{[\mathbb Z^2:L_{n,M}]=M.}
\tag{11.1}
\]
The primitive exact-zero-denominator vector
\[
w_0=(D_{n+1},-D_n)
\]
belongs to \(L_{n,M}\). For any \(u=(z,v)\in L_{n,M}\),
\[
\boxed{
\det(w_0,u)=D_nz+D_{n+1}v=q_0.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]
Therefore, if \(q_0\ne0\), then \(q_0\) is a nonzero multiple of \(M\), and
\[
M
\le|q_0|
\le(D_n+D_{n+1})\|u\|_\infty.
\]
Hence every nonzero-denominator exact modular resonance obeys
\[
\boxed{
\|u\|_\infty
\ge
\frac{M}{D_n+D_{n+1}}
\ge
\frac{M}{C_\beta D_n}.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

This shows why merely enlarging the \(\alpha\)-frame denominator and invoking an exact congruence does not automatically beat the earlier estimates: once \(M\) is large, a nontrivial exact modular resonance must pay at least the corresponding \(M/D_n\) coefficient scale unless additional arithmetic structure creates a special small solution. Such special structure would itself be a useful Littlewood resonance; generic lattice-index information does not provide it.

## 13. Failed routes / concrete obstruction found

1. **Pure isotropic Dirichlet:** remains critical at \(R^2\rho=O(1)\).
2. **Dyadic hyperbolic decomposition alone:** a strip of population \(\asymp RS\) only gives \(\rho=O((RS)^{-1})\), again exactly critical.
3. **Use only exact tail convergents:** these are simply later \(D_N\)'s; this reduces to a lacunary subsequence problem and does not exploit the full two-variable portal family.
4. **Force \(\rho=0\) only through congruence index:** the kernel lattice has index \(M\), and any nonzero-denominator solution must pay the determinant bound (11.3). Index alone gives no subcritical gain.

These failures narrow the useful next move to **cross-scale recurrence incompatibility**, not a larger one-scale search body.

## 14. Next attack

The most concrete next target is to compare two adjacent strip phase sets under
\[
\eta_n=d_{n+2}+\eta_{n+1}^{-1},
\qquad
(D_{n+1},D_{n+2})=(D_{n+1},d_{n+2}D_{n+1}+D_n).
\]
A near-tail vector \((z,v)\) at stage \(n\) transforms by an integral unimodular change of coordinates into a near-tail vector at stage \(n+1\). The alpha phase is also an exact rational linear form modulo the matched denominator. The next iteration should derive this coordinate conjugacy explicitly and ask whether two consecutive constant-factor-optimal circle packings can coexist when their moduli \(M_n^*\) and \(M_{n+1}^*\) are constrained by the \(\alpha\)-continued fraction. A quantitative incompatibility, even on a sparse subsequence, would turn Corollary 9.1 into a proof of Littlewood for the bounded-type hard core.

The full conjecture remains **OPEN**.
