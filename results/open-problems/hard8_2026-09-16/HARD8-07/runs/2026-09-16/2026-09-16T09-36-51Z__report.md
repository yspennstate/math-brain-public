## 3. Elementary reductions and notation

If either \(\alpha\) or \(\beta\) is rational, the conjecture is immediate: along multiples of its denominator one norm is exactly zero. Hence only irrational pairs matter.

If one irrational coordinate has unbounded continued-fraction partial quotients, Littlewood is also immediate. For example, if \(A_m/B_m\) are convergents of \(\alpha\), then
\[
B_m\|B_m\alpha\|\le \frac{1}{a_{m+1}},
\]
so an unbounded subsequence \(a_{m+1}\to\infty\), together with \(\|B_m\beta\|\le 1/2\), forces the Littlewood product to zero. Thus a genuine counterexample must have both coordinates badly approximable.

Accordingly, in the hard core assume all partial quotients of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are bounded by constants
\[
A_\alpha\ge1,\qquad A_\beta\ge1.
\]
Write convergents
\[
\frac{A_m}{B_m}\to\alpha,
\qquad
\frac{C_n}{D_n}\to\beta.
\]
Then
\[
B_{m+2}\le (A_\alpha+1)B_{m+1},
\qquad
D_{n+1}\le (A_\beta+1)D_n.
\]

For each sufficiently large \(n\), choose the canonical index \(m=m(n)\) uniquely by
\[
\boxed{B_{m+1}^{\,3}\le D_n^2<B_{m+2}^{\,3}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Set
\[
M_n=B_{m+1}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{D_n^{2/3}}{A_\alpha+1}<M_n\le D_n^{2/3}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 4. Signed portals

The preceding checkpoint used positive combinations
\[
q=zD_n+vD_{n+1},\qquad z,v\ge0.
\]
For the classical conjecture there is no reason to discard signed combinations. In this run let
\[
Q_n(z,v)=|zD_n+vD_{n+1}|,
\qquad (z,v)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Call these **signed portals**.

For \(R\ge1\) and \(|z|,|v|\le R\), the standard convergent estimates give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|Q_n(z,v)\beta\|
&\le |z|\,|D_n\beta-C_n|
   +|v|\,|D_{n+1}\beta-C_{n+1}|\\
&<\frac{|z|}{D_{n+1}}+\frac{|v|}{D_{n+2}}
\le \frac{2R}{D_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Also
\[
Q_n(z,v)\le(A_\beta+2)RD_n.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
Q_n(z,v)\,\|Q_n(z,v)\beta\|
\le2(A_\beta+2)R^2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
This is the only estimate on the \(\beta\)-side needed below.

## 5. A finite bounded-type denominator lemma

The signed portal must actually tend to infinity if the coefficient box grows much more slowly than \(D_n\). The following standard bounded-CF estimate makes this explicit.

### Lemma 5.1

Let \(P/Q\in(0,1)\) be reduced and suppose every partial quotient in its finite simple continued fraction is at most \(A\). Then for every integer \(1\le b<Q\),
\[
\boxed{
\|bP/Q\|\ge \frac{1}{(A+2)b}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(p_j/q_j\) be the convergents to \(P/Q\), including the final denominator \(Q\). Choose \(j\) with
\[
q_j\le b<q_{j+1}.
\]
The best-approximation property of convergents, valid up to the final rational convergent, gives
\[
\|bP/Q\|\ge\|q_jP/Q\|.
\]
For a convergent,
\[
\|q_jP/Q\|\ge\frac{1}{q_j+q_{j+1}}.
\]
Since \(q_{j+1}\le(A+1)q_j\),
\[
\|bP/Q\|\ge\frac1{(A+2)q_j}\ge\frac1{(A+2)b}.
\]
QED.

Apply the lemma to
\[
\frac{D_{n-1}}{D_n}
=[0;d_n,d_{n-1},\ldots,d_1],
\]
whose digits are bounded by \(A_\beta\). Because
\[
D_{n+1}=d_{n+1}D_n+D_{n-1},
\]
for \(v\ne0\),
\[
Q_n(z,v)
=
\left|(z+d_{n+1}v)D_n+vD_{n-1}\right|
\ge
\frac{D_n}{(A_\beta+2)|v|}.
\]
If \(v=0\), trivially \(Q_n(z,0)=|z|D_n\). Hence, whenever \(1\le R<D_n\),
\[
\boxed{
Q_n(z,v)\ge\frac{D_n}{(A_\beta+2)R}
\qquad
(0<\max(|z|,|v|)\le R).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
In particular every coefficient scale \(R_n=o(D_n)\) yields \(Q_n(z,v)\to\infty\) uniformly over nonzero pairs in the box.

## 6. Exact modular coordinate in the \(\alpha\)-frame

Fix \(n\), put \(m=m(n)\) and \(M=M_n=B_{m+1}\), and let
\[
q=Q_n(z,v)>0.
\]
Because \(\gcd(B_m,M)=1\), there is a unique centered integer \(x=x_n(z,v)\) with
\[
-\frac M2 < x\le\frac M2,
\qquad
xB_m\equiv q\pmod M.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Write
\[
q=xB_m+yM,
\qquad y\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{6.2}
\]
The convergent determinant identity
\[
A_{m+1}B_m-A_mB_{m+1}=\pm1
\]
shows that \(B_m^{-1}\equiv\pm A_{m+1}\pmod M\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\frac{|x_n(z,v)|}{M_n}
=
\left\|
\frac{Q_n(z,v)A_{m+1}}{M_n}
\right\|.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
This is an exact integer certificate; no floating-point approximation to \(\alpha\) is involved.

Now use
\[
|B_m\alpha-A_m|<\frac1M,
\qquad
|M\alpha-A_{m+1}|<\frac1{B_{m+2}}\le\frac1M.
\]
Since \(B_m<M\),
\[
|y|
\le\frac qM+|x|.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|q\alpha\|
&\le |x|\,|B_m\alpha-A_m|
    +|y|\,|M\alpha-A_{m+1}|\\
&\le\frac{2|x|}{M}+\frac q{M^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining (3.2) and (4.3),
\[
\boxed{
\|Q_n(z,v)\alpha\|
\le
2\rho_n(z,v)
+C_0 R D_n^{-1/3},
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
where
\[
\rho_n(z,v)
:=
\left\|
\frac{Q_n(z,v)A_{m+1}}{M_n}
\right\|,
\tag{6.5}
\]
and
\[
C_0=(A_\beta+2)(A_\alpha+1)^2.
\tag{6.6}
\]

## 7. Main new theorem: a signed-portal modular criterion

Multiply (6.4) by the \(\beta\)-factor (4.4).

### Theorem 7.1 — quantitative signed-portal bound

For every sufficiently large \(n\), every \(R\ge1\), and every nonzero integer pair \((z,v)\) with \(|z|,|v|\le R<D_n\),
\[
\boxed{
Q_n(z,v)\,\|Q_n(z,v)\alpha\|\,\|Q_n(z,v)\beta\|
\le
4(A_\beta+2)R^2\rho_n(z,v)
+C_1R^3D_n^{-1/3},
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
with
\[
C_1=2(A_\beta+2)^2(A_\alpha+1)^2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Everything on the right except the fixed bounded-type constants is an exact integer/modular quantity.

### Corollary 7.2 — growing-box sufficient criterion

Let \(R_n\to\infty\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{R_n=o(D_n^{1/9}).}
\tag{7.3}
\]
If there are infinitely many \(n\) and nonzero pairs
\[
|z_n|,|v_n|\le R_n
\]
for which
\[
\boxed{
R_n^2\rho_n(z_n,v_n)\longrightarrow0,
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
then the classical Littlewood conjecture holds for \((\alpha,\beta)\).

#### Proof

By (5.2), the corresponding \(Q_n(z_n,v_n)\to\infty\). The first term in (7.1) tends to zero by (7.4); the second tends to zero because
\[
R_n^3D_n^{-1/3}
=
\left(\frac{R_n}{D_n^{1/9}}\right)^3\to0.
\]
QED.

This strictly extends the previous fixed positive-portal criterion: the coefficients may now have either sign and may grow slowly with \(n\).

## 8. Main obstruction: a counterexample must asymptotically saturate two-dimensional Dirichlet

Define the canonical rational torus point
\[
\Theta_n
=
\left(
\frac{D_nA_{m+1}}{M_n},
\frac{D_{n+1}A_{m+1}}{M_n}
\right)
\pmod1.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Then
\[
\rho_n(z,v)=\|z\Theta_{n,1}+v\Theta_{n,2}\|
\tag{8.2}
\]
(the absolute value in \(Q_n\) changes only an irrelevant sign).

For \(R\ge1\), define the short-character constant
\[
\mu_n(R)
=
R^2
\min_{\substack{(z,v)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\\
|z|,|v|\le R}}
\|z\Theta_{n,1}+v\Theta_{n,2}\|.
\tag{8.3}
\]
A two-dimensional pigeonhole/Dirichlet argument gives, for every torus point,
\[
\boxed{\mu_n(R)\le1.}
\tag{8.4}
\]
Indeed, place the \((R+1)^2\) numbers
\[
z\Theta_{n,1}+v\Theta_{n,2}\pmod1,
\qquad0\le z,v\le R,
\]
into \(R^2\) equal subintervals of the circle. Two lie in one interval; their difference gives a nonzero pair with coefficients bounded by \(R\) and norm at most \(1/R^2\).

The new reduction says that merely matching the generic Dirichlet scale is not enough; one needs a little-o improvement along the structured sequence \(\Theta_n\).

### Theorem 8.1 — Dirichlet-saturation obstruction for every counterexample

Assume, for contradiction-testing purposes, that
\[
\Lambda
:=
\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|>0.
\tag{8.5}
\]
Let \(R_n\to\infty\) be any integer sequence with
\[
R_n=o(D_n^{1/9}).
\tag{8.6}
\]
Then for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\mu_n(R_n)
\ge
\frac{\Lambda}{16(A_\beta+2)}.
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

#### Proof

For all sufficiently large integers \(q\), the Littlewood product is at least \(\Lambda/2\). By (5.2), every nonzero signed portal in the \(R_n\)-box tends uniformly to infinity, so this lower bound applies to all of them for large \(n\).

For any such pair, (7.1) yields
\[
\frac\Lambda2
\le
4(A_\beta+2)R_n^2\rho_n(z,v)
+C_1R_n^3D_n^{-1/3}.
\]
Because of (8.6), the second term tends to zero uniformly. Eventually it is at most \(\Lambda/4\), so
\[
R_n^2\rho_n(z,v)
\ge
\frac{\Lambda}{16(A_\beta+2)}.
\]
Taking the minimum over all nonzero pairs in the box proves (8.7). QED.

Thus a classical counterexample would force
\[
\boxed{
c(\alpha,\beta)
\le\mu_n(R_n)\le1
}
\tag{8.8}
\]
for **every** slowly growing scale \(R_n=o(D_n^{1/9})\), eventually, with a fixed positive constant \(c(\alpha,\beta)\).

In words:

> At the canonical Matala scale, a counterexample must make the rational torus points \(\Theta_n\) asymptotically **constant-factor extremizers of two-dimensional Dirichlet approximation** throughout every coefficient range below \(D_n^{1/9-o(1)}\).

This is substantially stronger than the previous statement that each fixed portal must miss a shrinking target.

## 9. Why this is not already a proof

There exist genuinely badly approximable linear forms in two variables for which
\[
\inf_{(z,v)\ne0}
\max(|z|,|v|)^2\|z\theta_1+v\theta_2\|>0.
\]
Therefore one cannot replace the structured points \(\Theta_n\) by arbitrary torus points and hope to beat (8.4). A bare pigeonhole argument reaches exactly \(1/R^2\), while Corollary 7.2 needs little-o of that scale.

This is the concrete obstruction found in this run: the remaining gain must use arithmetic coupling between

1. the \(\beta\)-denominators \((D_n,D_{n+1})\),
2. the canonical \(\alpha\)-denominator \(M_n=B_{m(n)+1}\), and
3. the multiplier \(A_{m(n)+1}\pmod{M_n}\).

The unrestricted two-dimensional Dirichlet theorem alone is exactly critical and cannot close the route.

## 10. Relation to the preceding positive-portal theorem

The previous report's fixed positive portal
\[
q_n=zD_n+vD_{n+1},\qquad z,v\ge0
\]
is the special case \(R=O(1)\) of (7.1). The present theorem adds two genuinely new features:

- **signed** combinations such as \(D_{n+1}-D_n\) are allowed;
- the coefficient box may grow to infinity, provided \(R_n=o(D_n^{1/9})\).

A particularly transparent signed candidate is
\[
q_n=D_{n+1}-D_n.
\]
Its \(\beta\)-factor is uniformly bounded in the bounded-type hard core. Therefore if
\[
\left\|
\frac{(D_{n+1}-D_n)A_{m+1}}{B_{m+1}}
\right\|\to0
\]
along any subsequence, Littlewood follows immediately. Theorem 8.1 says a counterexample must keep this quantity, and every other fixed signed character, uniformly away from zero; the growing-box form says much more.

## 11. Claim ledger

### Known / reused

- Rational coordinate \(\Rightarrow\) classical Littlewood trivially.
- Unbounded partial quotients in either coordinate \(\Rightarrow\) classical Littlewood.
- Hence any counterexample pair is badly approximable in both coordinates.
- Standard continued-fraction determinant, best-approximation and error bounds.
- Two-dimensional Dirichlet/pigeonhole bound \(\min\|z\theta_1+v\theta_2\|\le R^{-2}\).
- Classical conjecture remains open; uniform-variant counterexamples do not settle it.

### Newly proved in this checkpoint

1. Uniform signed-portal \(\beta\)-bound (4.4).
2. Uniform lower growth of all signed portals in a bounded coefficient box, (5.2), using the reversed bounded continued fraction of \(D_{n-1}/D_n\).
3. Exact modular certificate (6.3) for the centered \(\alpha\)-frame coefficient of an arbitrary signed portal.
4. Quantitative product bound (7.1).
5. Growing signed-box sufficient criterion (Corollary 7.2): a little-o improvement over \(R^{-2}\) at any scale \(R=o(D_n^{1/9})\) proves Littlewood.
6. Dirichlet-saturation obstruction (Theorem 8.1): every genuine counterexample must keep the normalized short-character constant \(\mu_n(R_n)\) bounded below by a positive constant for every such slowly growing scale.

### Conditional / conjectural / open

- No proof is given that the structured \(\Theta_n\) must satisfy \(\mu_n(R_n)\to0\).
- No claim is made that the signed-portal reduction is new in the literature without a dedicated historical comparison.
- No finite numerical experiment can establish or refute the universal statement.
- The full classical Littlewood conjecture remains open.

## 12. Failed / closed routes in this iteration

1. **Pure two-dimensional pigeonhole:** gives exactly \(O(R^{-2})\), while (7.1) needs \(o(R^{-2})\) to force the product to zero at growing \(R\). This is a real constant-factor barrier, not merely a weak implementation.
2. **Treating the portal coefficients as necessarily positive:** unnecessary. Signed portals have equally good \(\beta\)-control and enlarge the arithmetic search space.
3. **Ignoring portal growth under cancellation:** unsafe in principle. Lemma 5.1 repairs this in the bounded-type hard core and gives the uniform lower bound (5.2).

## 13. Next attack

The next useful target is now precise and finite-scale:

> Prove that the structured canonical points \(\Theta_n\) cannot remain constant-factor badly approximable as linear forms on every scale \(R_n=o(D_n^{1/9})\).

Three concrete avenues should be tested next:

1. **Successive-minima / lattice reformulation.** Encode
   \[
   zD_nA_{m+1}+vD_{n+1}A_{m+1}-wM_n
   \]
   as a rank-three integer lattice and translate \(\mu_n(R)\) into an anisotropic first minimum. The determinant is explicit, so a transference inequality exploiting the simultaneous continued-fraction origin of all three coefficients may beat arbitrary-torus Dirichlet.
2. **Canonical-index jumps.** Track \(\Theta_n\) when \(m(n)\) stays fixed versus when it increases. The recurrences for \(D_n\) and \((A_{m+1},B_{m+1})\) may force a short character to improve when the canonical scale crosses a denominator threshold.
3. **Exact search for a structural identity.** For bounded digit alphabets, compute \(\mu_n(R)\) exactly from modular integers, not floating point, and classify near-extremal states. The goal is not numerical evidence but an invariant or exchange identity proving that a putative sequence with \(\mu_n(R)\ge c\) must eventually violate one of the continued-fraction recurrences.

The full conjecture is not resolved in this checkpoint.
