# HARD8-07 — Classical Littlewood conjecture

**SOLUTION STATUS: OPEN / PARTIAL STRUCTURAL REDUCTION**

Target: for all real `alpha,beta`, prove or refute

\[
\liminf_{q\to\infty} q\,\|q\alpha\|\,\|q\beta\|=0.
\]

This report is about the classical non-uniform conjecture only. No counterexample or complete proof was obtained in this run.

## 2. Standard hard-core reduction, recorded explicitly

If either coordinate is rational, the target is immediate because one factor is zero on infinitely many denominators.

If, say, `alpha` is irrational but not badly approximable, then

\[
\inf_{q\ge1} q\|q\alpha\|=0.
\]

Choose `q_j -> infinity` with `q_j||q_j alpha|| -> 0`. Since `||q_j beta|| <= 1/2`,

\[
q_j\|q_j\alpha\|\|q_j\beta\|\le \frac12 q_j\|q_j\alpha\|\to0.
\]

Hence a putative counterexample must have **both alpha and beta badly approximable**, equivalently both simple continued fractions have bounded partial quotients.

This is known, but it is the correct scope for every subsequent attack.

## 3. Exact local-coordinate theorem for adjacent convergents

Let

\[
\alpha=[a_0;a_1,a_2,\ldots],\qquad \frac{p_j}{q_j}
\]

be its convergents, and let

\[
\theta=\alpha_{m+2}=[a_{m+2};a_{m+3},\ldots].
\]

Likewise let `r_j/s_j` be convergents of `beta` and

\[
\eta=\beta_{n+2}.
\]

### Proposition 3.1 — unimodular coordinate equivalence

For every integer triple `(P,R,Q) in Z^3`, there is a unique integer quadruple `(x,y,z,v)` satisfying

\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=x p_m+y p_{m+1},\\
Q&=x q_m+y q_{m+1},\\
R&=z r_n+v r_{n+1},\\
Q&=z s_n+v s_{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Conversely every integer quadruple satisfying the common-denominator relation

\[
xq_m+yq_{m+1}=zs_n+vs_{n+1}
\]

comes from exactly one `(P,R,Q)`.

#### Proof

The consecutive-convergent matrices

\[
U_\alpha=
\begin{pmatrix}p_m&p_{m+1}\\q_m&q_{m+1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U_\beta=
\begin{pmatrix}r_n&r_{n+1}\\s_n&s_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}
\]

have determinant `+1` or `-1`. Therefore each is an element of `GL_2(Z)`, so every integer pair `(P,Q)` has unique integer coordinates `(x,y)` in the columns of `U_alpha`, and every `(R,Q)` has unique coordinates `(z,v)` in `U_beta`. The common second coordinate is exactly the displayed denominator relation. QED.

### Consequence

The four-coefficient equation used in recent continued-fraction approaches is not a smaller hidden arithmetic set: after choosing adjacent convergent bases it is simply an **integer change of coordinates on the original lattice `Z^3` of `(P,R,Q)`**.

Therefore a proof based only on existence of a small nonzero solution of the one linear equation cannot by itself evade the classical critical exponent. Extra information must enter through the *orientation* of the coefficient vector relative to the continued-fraction tails, or through additional arithmetic structure.

## 4. Exact tail-renormalization identity

The standard complete-quotient formula is

\[
\alpha=\frac{p_{m+1}\theta+p_m}{q_{m+1}\theta+q_m}.
\]

Using

\[
p_mq_{m+1}-p_{m+1}q_m=(-1)^{m+1},
\]

one obtains exactly

\[
q_m\alpha-p_m
 =\frac{(-1)^m\theta}{q_{m+1}\theta+q_m},
\]

and

\[
q_{m+1}\alpha-p_{m+1}
 =\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{q_{m+1}\theta+q_m}.
\]

Hence for

\[
Q=xq_m+yq_{m+1},\qquad P=xp_m+yp_{m+1},
\]

we have the exact signed identity

\[
\boxed{
Q\alpha-P
=\frac{(-1)^{m+1}(y-x\theta)}{q_{m+1}\theta+q_m}.
}
\]

Similarly, if

\[
Q=zs_n+vs_{n+1},\qquad R=zr_n+vr_{n+1},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
Q\beta-R
=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}(v-z\eta)}{s_{n+1}\eta+s_n}.
}
\]

Therefore every common-denominator coefficient relation gives the rigorous bound

\[
\boxed{
Q\|Q\alpha\|\|Q\beta\|
\le
\frac{Q\,|y-x\theta|\,|v-z\eta|}
{(q_{m+1}\theta+q_m)(s_{n+1}\eta+s_n)}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This formula identifies the missing resource in coefficient-size arguments: **signed cancellation against the forward continued-fraction tails**.

## 5. A precise “Minkowski-threshold plus cancellation” sufficient criterion

Let

\[
Q_0=\max(q_{m+1},s_{n+1})
\]

and assume the two adjacent denominator scales are comparable:

\[
\min(q_{m+1},s_{n+1})\ge cQ_0
\]

for a fixed `c>0` along an infinite sequence of blocks.

For a common-denominator relation define

\[
H=\max(|x|,|y|,|z|,|v|)
\]

and the normalized tail-cancellation factor

\[
C=\frac{|y-x\theta|\,|v-z\eta|}{H^2}.
\]

Since `theta,eta>1`, both denominators in (4.1) are at least `cQ_0`; and

\[
Q\le |x|q_m+|y|q_{m+1}\le2HQ_0.
\]

Thus

\[
Q\|Q\alpha\|\|Q\beta\|
\le \frac{2}{c^2}\frac{H^3}{Q_0}C.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Corollary 5.1

If along infinitely many comparable blocks there are positive common denominators with

\[
\frac{H^3}{Q_0}=O(1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
C\to0,
\]

then the classical Littlewood conjecture holds for `(alpha,beta)`.

This isolates an exact attack target compatible with the `H=O(Q_0^{1/3})` four-variable Thue–Siegel/Minkowski scale: **one does not need a better exponent on coefficient size if one can prove any asymptotically nontrivial multiplicative tail cancellation.**

Conversely, if `(alpha,beta)` were a counterexample with

\[
Q\|Q\alpha\|\|Q\beta\|\ge\delta>0
\]

for all sufficiently large `Q`, then every comparable-block relation at the critical `H^3=O(Q_0)` scale would have its `C` bounded away from zero quantitatively. A counterexample therefore forces persistent avoidance of *both* local tail slopes inside every critical-size kernel vector.

This is stronger information than “both numbers are badly approximable”: it is a joint arithmetic rigidity condition on the coefficient kernel in every pair of nearby continued-fraction frames.

## 6. Why the raw coefficient-size route stalls exactly at the critical exponent

If signed cancellation is discarded, then trivially

\[
|y-x\theta|\le H(1+\theta),\qquad
|v-z\eta|\le H(1+\eta).
\]

For bounded partial quotients and comparable denominators, (4.1) therefore has scale

\[
O\!\left(\frac{H^3}{Q_0}\right).
\]

The generic one-equation lattice bound has `H=O(Q_0^{1/3})`, producing only `O(1)`, not `o(1)`. This explains algebraically why the recent four-coefficient framework needs an additional mechanism beyond the bare Thue–Siegel existence estimate.

The natural next question is therefore not merely “can the coefficients be made small?” but:

> Among critical-size kernel vectors, must there exist infinitely many for which at least one of the two coefficient ratios aligns unusually well with the corresponding forward continued-fraction tail, with enough *joint* gain to force `C -> 0`?

A universal positive answer proves Littlewood by Corollary 5.1.

## 7. Exact experiment on the concrete open pair `(sqrt(2),sqrt(3))`

This pair is a useful stress test because it is a classical explicit hard example: even this concrete pair has historically been singled out as unresolved.

Let `p_k/q_k` be the convergents of `sqrt(2)`. They satisfy

\[
p_k^2-2q_k^2=(-1)^{k+1},
\]

so

\[
|q_k\sqrt2-p_k|=\frac1{p_k+q_k\sqrt2}.
\]

For each such `q_k`, let `r_k` be the nearest integer to `q_k sqrt(3)` and define the exact integer

\[
\Delta_k=|3q_k^2-r_k^2|.
\]

Then

\[
|q_k\sqrt3-r_k|=\frac{\Delta_k}{r_k+q_k\sqrt3}.
\]

Since `p_k>=q_k` and `r_k>=q_k`,

\[
q_k\|q_k\sqrt2\|\|q_k\sqrt3\|
<
\frac{\Delta_k/q_k}{(1+\sqrt2)(1+\sqrt3)}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

An exact-integer scan of the first **10,000** `sqrt(2)` convergent denominators found 13 successive records for `Delta_k/q_k`; the last record occurs at

\[
k=1585,
\]

where `q_k` has 607 decimal digits and

\[
\Delta_k/q_k\approx 4.2350728671642779\times10^{-5}.
\]

Equation (7.1) therefore gives the rigorous numerical upper bound

\[
q_k\|q_k\sqrt2\|\|q_k\sqrt3\|
< 6.421\times10^{-6}
\]

for that exactly certified denominator.

This is **finite evidence only** and does not prove a liminf of zero. Notably, no new record occurred between `k=1585` and `k=9999`, illustrating how weak record searches can be even for a natural quadratic test pair.

## 9. Failed / blocked routes this run

- Bare four-variable pigeonhole/Thue–Siegel coefficient bounds stop at `H ~ Q^(1/3)`, which only gives an `O(1)` product bound if cancellation is ignored.
- Searching natural convergent denominators for `(sqrt(2),sqrt(3))` gives striking finite records but no asymptotic theorem; a 10,000-term scan cannot certify a liminf.
- The 2026 disproofs of the uniform Littlewood conjecture cannot be imported as classical counterexamples because the uniform quantifier is strictly stronger.

## 10. Next attack

The highest-value next step is to study the **critical kernel vectors with their signs retained** rather than only their `l_infinity` size. Two concrete directions:

1. For comparable convergent frames, analyze the rank-three kernel lattice under the two tail linear forms
   `y-theta*x` and `v-eta*z`. Seek a geometry-of-numbers statement forcing the normalized product `C` to have arbitrarily small values unless the sequence of frames lies in a rigid low-entropy exceptional set.
2. Specialize first to bounded alphabets of continued-fraction digits. Because the tail parameters then range in a compact Cantor set and the denominator matrices lie in a finitely generated positive semigroup, attempt a finite-state/renormalization dichotomy: either recurring words create tail-aligned critical vectors (giving Littlewood by Corollary 5.1), or the symbolic pair has enough entropy to invoke a quantitative recurrence mechanism.

The second direction may interface with the known measure-rigidity picture without duplicating the separate Furstenberg x2/x3 task.
