## 3. Notation

Write the convergents of \(\alpha\) as
\[
\frac{A_m}{B_m}
\]
and those of \(\beta\) as
\[
\frac{C_n}{D_n}.
\]
For the \(\beta\)-tail put
\[
\eta_n=[d_{n+2};d_{n+3},\ldots]>1,
\qquad
\mathcal D_n=D_{n+1}\eta_n+D_n.
\]
Use
\[
\varepsilon_n=C_nD_{n+1}-C_{n+1}D_n=(-1)^{n+1}.
\]
Then
\[
\beta=\frac{C_{n+1}\eta_n+C_n}{D_{n+1}\eta_n+D_n}.
\]
For integers \(z,v\), define
\[
q=zD_n+vD_{n+1},
\qquad
r=zC_n+vC_{n+1},
\qquad
E_\beta=q\beta-r.
\]

## 4. Newly proved: exact `++` cone in the fixed Littlewood lattice

### Theorem 4.1 — exact coefficient/error inversion

For every \(z,v\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
\boxed{
E_\beta
=\frac{\varepsilon_n(v-z\eta_n)}{\mathcal D_n}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Conversely,
\[
\boxed{
z=\frac{q}{\mathcal D_n}-\varepsilon_nD_{n+1}E_\beta,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
\[
\boxed{
v=\frac{\eta_nq}{\mathcal D_n}+\varepsilon_nD_nE_\beta.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

From the continued-fraction expression for \(\beta\),
\[
D_n\beta-C_n=-\frac{\varepsilon_n\eta_n}{\mathcal D_n},
\qquad
D_{n+1}\beta-C_{n+1}=\frac{\varepsilon_n}{\mathcal D_n}.
\]
Therefore
\[
E_\beta
=z(D_n\beta-C_n)+v(D_{n+1}\beta-C_{n+1})
=\frac{\varepsilon_n(v-z\eta_n)}{\mathcal D_n}.
\]
Equations (4.2) and (4.3) follow by solving the two linear equations
\[
q=zD_n+vD_{n+1},
\qquad
\mathcal D_n\varepsilon_nE_\beta=v-z\eta_n.
\]
QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — exact `++` wedge

Assume \(q>0\). Then \(z,v\ge0\) if and only if
\[
\boxed{
-\frac{\eta_nq}{\mathcal D_nD_n}
\le
\varepsilon_nE_\beta
\le
\frac{q}{\mathcal D_nD_{n+1}}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
For integer \(z,v\), \(q>0\) already rules out \(z=v=0\), so Matala-aho's additional condition \(z+v\ge1\) is automatic.

Equivalently, \(z,v\ge0\) if and only if the rational slope \(r/q\) lies in the closed interval between the adjacent convergents
\[
\frac{C_n}{D_n}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\frac{C_{n+1}}{D_{n+1}}.
\]

### Corollary 4.3 — exact aperture

At a fixed positive \(q\), the allowed interval in the \(E_\beta\)-coordinate has width
\[
\boxed{
\frac{q}{D_nD_{n+1}}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\frac{q}{\mathcal D_nD_{n+1}}
+
\frac{\eta_nq}{\mathcal D_nD_n}
=
\frac{q(D_n+\eta_nD_{n+1})}
{\mathcal D_nD_nD_{n+1}}
=
\frac{q}{D_nD_{n+1}}.
\]
Thus the sign obstruction is an exact shrinking wedge in the fixed lattice, not an opaque property of the four coefficients.

## 5. Newly proved: the adjacent `++` cones are nested, not sweeping

Let
\[
c_n=(C_n,D_n)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Since consecutive convergents satisfy
\[
c_{n+2}=d_{n+2}c_{n+1}+c_n,
\qquad d_{n+2}\ge1,
\]
the real positive cones satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cone}_{\ge0}(c_{n+1},c_{n+2})
\subset
\operatorname{cone}_{\ge0}(c_n,c_{n+1}).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Because \(|\det(c_n,c_{n+1})|=1\), every integer point in the older real cone has a unique representation
\[
z c_n+v c_{n+1},\qquad z,v\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
Furthermore, a deeper positive combination
\[
z c_{n+1}+v c_{n+2}
\]
becomes
\[
v c_n+(z+d_{n+2}v)c_{n+1},
\]
so the integer semigroups are nested as well.

The intersection of the real cones is the ray of slope \(\beta\):
\[
\bigcap_n \operatorname{cone}_{\ge0}(c_n,c_{n+1})
=\mathbb R_{\ge0}(\beta,1).
\]
For irrational \(\beta\), that ray has no nonzero integer point. Hence:

> **Every fixed integer pair \((r,q)\ne(0,0)\) is a `++` point for only finitely many adjacent frames.**

This corrects a tempting but false picture from the prior checkpoint: the Matala sign cones do not rotate around the lattice and sweep many directions. They form a nested family converging onto the irrational \(\beta\)-ray. Any infinite `++` argument must produce genuinely new lattice vectors at increasing depths.

## 6. Newly proved: exact cone-volume / Littlewood-product identity

The prior report proved that
\[
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}
=
\{(q,E_\alpha,E_\beta):
E_\alpha=q\alpha-p,
E_\beta=q\beta-r,
\ p,r,q\in\mathbb Z\}
\]
has covolume one.

For \(T,A>0\), define the truncated `++` body
\[
\mathcal W_n(T,A)
=
\left\{
(q,E_\alpha,E_\beta):
0<q\le T,
\ |E_\alpha|\le A,
\ -\frac{\eta_nq}{\mathcal D_nD_n}
\le\varepsilon_nE_\beta
\le
\frac{q}{\mathcal D_nD_{n+1}}
\right\}.
\]
It is a convex one-sided truncated cone.

At height \(q\), the \(E_\alpha\)-width is \(2A\), and by (4.5) the \(E_\beta\)-width is \(q/(D_nD_{n+1})\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{vol}\mathcal W_n(T,A)
=
\frac{AT^2}{D_nD_{n+1}}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every lattice point of \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\cap\mathcal W_n(T,A)\) satisfies
\[
\|q\alpha\|\le |E_\alpha|\le A,
\]
and, because the wedge contains zero and has total width \(q/(D_nD_{n+1})\),
\[
\|q\beta\|\le |E_\beta|
\le \frac{q}{D_nD_{n+1}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|
\le
\frac{AT^2}{D_nD_{n+1}}
=\operatorname{vol}\mathcal W_n(T,A).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is an exact explanation of the critical-volume obstruction from the previous run: within the `++` cone architecture, the dimensionless volume of the search body is already the natural upper bound for the Littlewood product.

Therefore a proof that forces Littlewood by finding points in such bodies with product tending to zero must find nonzero lattice points in bodies whose volumes themselves tend to zero. Generic covolume/volume arguments such as a bare Minkowski or Blichfeldt application cannot do this uniformly. Extra arithmetic recurrence, distribution, or rigidity is required.

This is a methodological obstruction, not a proof that all geometry-of-numbers approaches fail.

## 7. Newly proved: nonadjacent bracketing convergents do not improve the volume threshold

One possible escape from the narrowing adjacent cones is to replace \(c_n,c_{n+1}\) by farther convergents on opposite sides of \(\beta\). The wider real cone looks attractive. The determinant shows that this apparent gain is exactly paid for by lattice sparsity.

Fix \(i<j\) with \(C_i/D_i\) and \(C_j/D_j\) on opposite sides of \(\beta\), and put
\[
\Delta_{ij}=|C_iD_j-C_jD_i|.
\]
Let
\[
L_{ij}=\mathbb Zc_i+\mathbb Zc_j\subset\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Then
\[
[\mathbb Z^2:L_{ij}]=\Delta_{ij}.
\]
Define the corresponding sublattice of the fixed Littlewood lattice by
\[
\Gamma_{ij}
=
\{(q,q\alpha-p,q\beta-r):
 p\in\mathbb Z,\ (r,q)\in L_{ij}\}.
\]
The underlying triples \((p,r,q)\) have basis determinant \(\Delta_{ij}\), while the real map
\[
(p,r,q)\mapsto(q,q\alpha-p,q\beta-r)
\]
has determinant \(1\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{ij})=\Delta_{ij}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The slope interval between the two endpoints has exact width
\[
\left|\frac{C_i}{D_i}-\frac{C_j}{D_j}\right|
=
\frac{\Delta_{ij}}{D_iD_j}.
\]
Therefore the analogous positive-cone body with \(0<q\le T\) and \(|E_\alpha|\le A\) has Euclidean volume
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{vol}\mathcal W_{ij}(T,A)
=
\frac{AT^2\Delta_{ij}}{D_iD_j}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
After dividing by its lattice covolume,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{vol}\mathcal W_{ij}(T,A)}
{\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma_{ij})}
=
\frac{AT^2}{D_iD_j}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
The determinant gain from the wider angle cancels exactly.

For a fixed lower index \(i\), every farther opposite-side convergent has \(D_j>D_{i+1}\), so the normalized volume in (7.3) is strictly smaller than for the adjacent frame \((i,i+1)\). Thus:

> **Among bracketing convergent frames with a fixed first endpoint and fixed \((T,A)\), the adjacent frame is optimal for any existence argument depending only on volume relative to lattice covolume.**

Farther convergents can still contain special arithmetic information, but there is no free geometric gain from their wider angle.

## 8. Newly proved: the critical \(D_n^{2/3}\) scale turns the sign problem into finitely many portals

This section sharpens Matala-aho's Remark 17 in the bounded-type hard core.

Assume both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are badly approximable. Equivalently, their continued-fraction partial quotients are bounded. Let
\[
1\le b_k\le A_\alpha,
\qquad
1\le d_k\le A_\beta
\]
for all sufficiently large \(k\).

For each large \(n\), choose \(m=m(n)\) exactly by
\[
\boxed{
B_{m+1}^3\le D_n^2<B_{m+2}^3.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
This is the integer form of the critical relation
\[
B_{m+1}\asymp D_n^{2/3}.
\]
Since
\[
B_{m+2}\le(A_\alpha+1)B_{m+1},
\]
(8.1) implies
\[
\boxed{
\frac{D_n^{2/3}}{A_\alpha+1}
< B_{m+1}
\le D_n^{2/3}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
Also
\[
B_m\ge\frac{B_{m+1}}{A_\alpha+1}
>
\frac{D_n^{2/3}}{(A_\alpha+1)^2}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Let
\[
J(m,n)=ig(B_m+B_{m+1}+D_n+D_{n+1}\big)^{1/3}.
\]
Because
\[
D_{n+1}\le(A_\beta+1)D_n
\]
and \(B_m+B_{m+1}\le2D_n^{2/3}\le2D_n\) for large \(n\),
\[
D_n^{1/3}
\le J(m,n)
\le C_\beta D_n^{1/3},
\qquad
C_\beta=(A_\beta+4)^{1/3}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

### Theorem 8.1 — bounded-H `++` solutions have bounded portal type

Suppose for infinitely many \(n\) there is a `++` solution
\[
xB_m+yB_{m+1}=zD_n+vD_{n+1}=q>0
\]
with
\[
\max(|x|,|y|,z,v)\le HJ(m,n)
\]
for one constant \(H\).
Then every such solution satisfies
\[
\boxed{
1\le z+v\le 2HC_\beta.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]
Hence only finitely many pairs \((z,v)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\) can occur. In particular, one fixed pair \((z,v)\) occurs for infinitely many \(n\).

#### Proof

The \(\alpha\)-side gives
\[
q
\le |x|B_m+|y|B_{m+1}
\le HJ(B_m+B_{m+1})
\le 2HC_\beta D_n.
\]
The positivity of \(z,v\) gives
\[
q=zD_n+vD_{n+1}\ge(z+v)D_n.
\]
Divide by \(D_n\). QED.

The number of possible portal pairs is at most
\[
\sum_{s=1}^{K}(s+1)=\frac{K(K+3)}2,
\qquad
K=\lfloor2HC_\beta\rfloor.
\]

This is substantially stronger than merely saying that a positive solution is needed: at the critical \(2/3\) scale, a bounded-H proof can only use finitely many fixed positive combinations of consecutive \(\beta\)-denominators.

## 9. Newly proved: fixed-portal shrinking-target criterion with an explicit \(D_n^{-1/3}\) rate

### Theorem 9.1 — fixed portal implies Littlewood

Under the bounded-type assumptions above, choose \(m(n)\) by (8.1). Suppose there exist fixed integers
\[
z,v\ge0,
\qquad
z+v\ge1,
\]
and a constant \(C\) such that for infinitely many \(n\), with
\[
q_n=zD_n+vD_{n+1},
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\|q_n\alpha\|\le C D_n^{-1/3}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
q_n\|q_n\alpha\|\|q_n\beta\|
\ll_{A_\beta,z,v,C} D_n^{-1/3}\to0,
}
\]
so the classical Littlewood conjecture holds for \((\alpha,\beta)\).

#### Proof

The portal denominator satisfies
\[
q_n
\le(z+v)(A_\beta+1)D_n.
\]
For convergents,
\[
\|D_n\beta\|<\frac1{D_{n+1}},
\qquad
\|D_{n+1}\beta\|<\frac1{D_{n+2}},
\]
so
\[
\|q_n\beta\|
\le z\|D_n\beta\|+v\|D_{n+1}\beta\|
\le\frac{z+v}{D_n}.
\]
Together with (9.1),
\[
q_n\|q_n\alpha\|\|q_n\beta\|
\le
C(A_\beta+1)(z+v)^2D_n^{-1/3}.
\]
QED.

This criterion only asks for an exponent \(1/3\) shrinking target along one fixed positive combination of two consecutive \(\beta\)-denominators. The \(\beta\)-factor is automatically of order \(1/D_n\).

## 10. Newly proved: critical-scale equivalence with the Matala `++` mechanism

The previous theorem can be tied exactly to the short-coefficient formulation.

### Theorem 10.1 — finite-portal equivalence, up to constants

For a fixed bounded-type pair \((\alpha,\beta)\), with \(m(n)\) chosen by (8.1), the following are equivalent in the sense that each statement holds for infinitely many \(n\) with some constant if and only if the other does:

**(A)** There is a constant \(H\) and infinitely many Matala `++` solutions
\[
xB_m+yB_{m+1}=zD_n+vD_{n+1}
\]
with
\[
\max(|x|,|y|,z,v)\le HJ(m,n).
\]

**(B)** There is one fixed portal \((z,v)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\) and a constant \(C\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\|(zD_n+vD_{n+1})\alpha\|\le C D_n^{-1/3}
}
\tag{10.1}
\]
for infinitely many \(n\).

Either condition implies Littlewood for the pair.

#### Proof that (A) implies (B)

By Theorem 8.1 only finitely many portals can occur, so one fixed \((z,v)\) repeats infinitely often. For those \(n\),
\[
q=xB_m+yB_{m+1}.
\]
The convergent error estimate gives
\[
\|q\alpha\|
\le |x|\|B_m\alpha\|+|y|\|B_{m+1}\alpha\|
<\frac{|x|}{B_{m+1}}+\frac{|y|}{B_{m+2}}.
\]
Using \(|x|,|y|\le HJ\), (8.2), and (8.4), the right side is
\[
O_{A_\alpha,A_\beta,H}(D_n^{-1/3}).
\]
Thus (B) holds.

#### Proof that (B) implies (A)

For a successful \(n\), choose an integer \(p\) with
\[
E_\alpha=q\alpha-p,
\qquad
|E_\alpha|=\|q\alpha\|.
\]
Because consecutive convergent vectors form a unimodular basis, there are unique integers \(x,y\) with
\[
(p,q)=x(A_m,B_m)+y(A_{m+1},B_{m+1}).
\]
The exact inverse formulas from the prior checkpoint give
\[
x=\frac{q}{\mathcal B_m}-\varepsilon_mB_{m+1}E_\alpha,
\]
\[
y=\frac{\theta_mq}{\mathcal B_m}+\varepsilon_mB_mE_\alpha,
\]
where
\[
\mathcal B_m=B_{m+1}\theta_m+B_m,
\qquad
1<\theta_m\le A_\alpha+1.
\]
Since \(q=O(D_n)\), \(B_{m+1}\asymp D_n^{2/3}\), and \(|E_\alpha|=O(D_n^{-1/3})\), both \(|x|\) and \(|y|\) are \(O(D_n^{1/3})\). The fixed \(z,v\) are eventually smaller than the same scale. Since \(J(m,n)\ge D_n^{1/3}\), one obtains
\[
\max(|x|,|y|,z,v)\le HJ(m,n)
\]
for some constant \(H\). Positivity of the fixed portal gives a `++` solution. QED.

Thus, in the bounded-type hard core, the unknown canonical-scale sign problem is not an amorphous four-variable problem. It is equivalent, up to constants, to a one-dimensional shrinking-target question along one fixed portal sequence.

## 11. Newly proved: exact modular certificate for a portal hit

The fixed-portal criterion has a fully integer modular form.

Let
\[
M=B_{m+1},
\qquad b=B_m,
\qquad q=zD_n+vD_{n+1}.
\]
Since \(\gcd(b,M)=1\), any representation
\[
q=xb+yM
\]
must satisfy
\[
\boxed{
x\equiv q b^{-1}\pmod M.}
\tag{11.1}
\]
The convergent determinant identity
\[
A_{m+1}B_m-A_mB_{m+1}=(-1)^m
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
B_m^{-1}\equiv(-1)^mA_{m+1}\pmod{B_{m+1}}.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]
Therefore the least possible absolute value of the coefficient \(x\) is exactly
\[
\boxed{
|x|_{\min}
=B_{m+1}
\left\|
\frac{qA_{m+1}}{B_{m+1}}
\right\|.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]
At the canonical scale \(B_{m+1}\asymp D_n^{2/3}\), the desired coefficient bound \(|x|=O(D_n^{1/3})\) is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
\left\|
\frac{(zD_n+vD_{n+1})A_{m+1}}
{B_{m+1}}
\right\|
=O(D_n^{-1/3}).
}
\tag{11.4}
\]
Because
\[
\left|\alpha-\frac{A_{m+1}}{B_{m+1}}\right|
<\frac1{B_{m+1}B_{m+2}}
\]
and \(q=O(D_n)\), replacing the convergent fraction in (11.4) by \(\alpha\) changes the phase by only \(O(D_n^{-1/3})\). Thus (11.4) is equivalent up to constants to (10.1).

This provides an exact, reproducible certificate for each candidate portal hit: no floating-point nearest-integer decision is needed. One computes \(m(n)\) from the integer inequality \(B_{m+1}^3\le D_n^2<B_{m+2}^3\), computes the modular inverse of \(B_m\) modulo \(B_{m+1}\), and checks the least absolute residue of \(qB_m^{-1}\).

## 12. Portal-budget tradeoff beyond the exact critical scale

The same calculation gives a useful continuum between a fixed finite portal set and the full four-variable search.

Suppose instead that along a subsequence
\[
B_{m+1}=\rho_n D_n^{2/3},
\]
up to bounded-type constant factors, with \(1\lesssim\rho_n\ll D_n^{1/3}\). For a bounded-H `++` solution, the same argument as in Theorem 8.1 yields
\[
\boxed{
z+v=O(\rho_n).}
\tag{12.1}
\]
so only \(O(\rho_n^2)\) portal pairs are available at that scale.

Meanwhile the bound in Matala-aho's proof is
\[
q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|
\ll_H \frac{B_{m+1}}{D_n}
\asymp
\boxed{\rho_nD_n^{-1/3}.}
\tag{12.2}
\]
Thus any regime with
\[
\rho_n=o(D_n^{1/3})
\]
still has enough asymptotic room to prove Littlewood if the corresponding `++` solutions can be forced. The parameter \(\rho_n\) simultaneously measures:

- portal complexity: \(O(\rho_n^2)\) possible positive pairs; and
- the resulting Littlewood upper bound: \(O(\rho_nD_n^{-1/3})\).

This is a concrete tradeoff for future attacks. A slowly growing portal budget, for example polylogarithmic \(\rho_n\), would still give a strong decay rate while avoiding the rigidity of a fixed finite portal set.

## 13. Known obstruction that remains relevant

Matala-aho quotes Pollington–Velani's theorem that, for a fixed badly approximable number with convergent denominators \(B_n\), there are badly approximable numbers \(\gamma\) for which
\[
\|B_n\gamma\|\ge\delta>0
\]
for every \(n\). Therefore the single portal \((z,v)=(1,0)\) cannot be universal, even if one only asks for recurrence to a fixed neighborhood of zero rather than the shrinking target \(D_n^{-1/3}\).

The present finite-portal equivalence should therefore not be misread as a proof that one predetermined portal works for all pairs. The unresolved question is whether, for each fixed hard pair, some portal from a bounded or slowly growing family must recur at the required rate.

## 14. Failed routes closed in this run

### 14.1 “The `++` cones sweep enough directions as \(n\) varies” — false

They are nested by (5.1) and shrink to the irrational \(\beta\)-ray.

### 14.2 “Use farther bracketing convergents to widen the cone for free” — false at the covolume level

The width gain \(\Delta_{ij}\) is exactly cancelled by the sublattice index \(\Delta_{ij}\); equation (7.3) is the exact accounting identity.

### 14.3 “A pure volume argument should force the needed sign-safe point” — insufficient

For adjacent cones, the normalized search volume itself equals the natural Littlewood-product upper bound, equation (6.2). To prove a limit of zero one needs lattice points in vanishing-volume bodies, so generic volume threshold arguments cannot supply the missing asymptotic gain.

- Any counterexample must have both coordinates badly approximable.
- The classical exceptional set has Hausdorff dimension zero (Einsiedler–Katok–Lindenstrauss).
- Matala-aho's Theorem 16 gives a sufficient criterion from infinitely many small `++` solutions, and the paper explicitly leaves their general existence open.
- Uniform-Littlewood counterexamples do not refute the classical statement.

### Proved in prior HARD8-07 checkpoints

- Rational coordinates and rational affine dependence of \(1,\alpha,\beta\) satisfy Littlewood.
- Every adjacent-convergent kernel frame is exactly a coordinate presentation of the same covolume-one lattice \(\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}\).
- Exact inverse formulas convert between frame coefficients and \((q,E_\alpha,E_\beta)\).
- Adding more consecutive convergents does not increase the true rank-three arithmetic dimension.

### Newly proved in this report

1. Exact `++` wedge inequalities (4.4).
2. Exact wedge aperture \(q/(D_nD_{n+1})\).
3. Adjacent `++` cones and integer semigroups are nested.
4. Exact `++` cone-body volume \(AT^2/(D_nD_{n+1})\).
5. Every point in that body has Littlewood product at most the same quantity.
6. For nonadjacent bracketing convergents, angular widening is exactly cancelled by sublattice index in normalized volume.
7. The canonical scale \(B_{m+1}\asymp D_n^{2/3}\) makes any bounded-H `++` solution use one of finitely many portal pairs \((z,v)\).
8. At that scale, existence of infinitely many bounded-H `++` solutions is equivalent up to constants to one fixed portal sequence hitting \(\alpha\) at rate \(O(D_n^{-1/3})\).
9. Any such fixed portal proves Littlewood with an explicit \(O(D_n^{-1/3})\) product bound.
10. Portal success has an exact modular certificate using \(B_m^{-1}\pmod{B_{m+1}}\), with \(B_m^{-1}\equiv(-1)^mA_{m+1}\).
11. A general portal-budget tradeoff is controlled by \(\rho_n=B_{m+1}/D_n^{2/3}\): portal count \(O(\rho_n^2)\), product bound \(O(\rho_nD_n^{-1/3})\).

### Not proved / still open

- No argument forces any fixed portal to hit the shrinking target for every bounded-type pair.
- No argument yet forces a slowly growing portal family to contain a successful portal infinitely often.
- No entropy-zero/dynamical rigidity theorem currently supplies the required nested-cone recurrence.
- No universal proof or counterexample has been obtained.

## 17. Next attack

The next useful target is now sharply defined.

1. **Adaptive portal recurrence.** Work at the exact integer critical scale \(B_{m+1}^3\asymp D_n^2\). Study the residue family
   \[
   (zD_n+vD_{n+1})B_m^{-1}\pmod{B_{m+1}}
   \]
   for \(z,v\ge0\) with \(z+v\le R_n\), where \(R_n\) grows as slowly as possible. A hit of size \(O(D_n^{1/3})\) yields Littlewood; any \(R_n=o(D_n^{1/3})\) remains asymptotically useful by Section 12.
2. **Do not rely on volume alone.** Section 6 shows that generic volume/covolume arguments stop exactly at constant Littlewood product. The needed gain must be arithmetic: discrepancy, recurrence, additive structure, or rigidity of the residue sequence.
3. **Connect to dynamics only after preserving the cone geometry.** The cones are nested, so any entropy-zero argument must prove recurrence into increasingly thin neighborhoods of the \(\beta\)-ray rather than invoke a vague “moving cone” heuristic.
4. **Test finite-portal obstruction rigorously.** A relevant next literature check is whether winning-set results for lacunary denominator sequences can be applied simultaneously to finitely many fixed portal sequences. If so, that would prove that a uniformly bounded portal family cannot be the universal mechanism and would justify moving directly to slowly growing \(R_n\).
