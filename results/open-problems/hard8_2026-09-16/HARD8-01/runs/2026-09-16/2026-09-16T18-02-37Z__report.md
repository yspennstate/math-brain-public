## Mathematical idea before code

The previous report isolated the difficult family

\[
\mathcal I_{8,4}=\{A=\{a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3\}: A\text{ synchronizing, all }a_i\text{ distinct, and every }A\setminus\{a_i\}\text{ nonsynchronizing}\}.
\]

Any automaton in this family has, for every deletion i, at least one unordered pair of states p_i that the remaining three letters cannot merge. The key change of language is to record only one such witness pair per deletion. Its reachable set in the 28-state unordered-pair automaton is automatically closed under the remaining letters and diagonal-free. Thus every irreducible automaton admits a four-pair witness front-end even though the full closed witness sets can be much larger.

This does **not** enumerate all automata. It gives a complete front-end sharding by possible witness quartets.

## B18 — pair-automaton translation

After forgetting the names of the four letters via S_4, a witness quartet is simply a multiset of four unordered state pairs. Modulo state relabeling S_8, these are exactly unlabeled loopless multigraphs with four edges on at most eight vertices, isolated vertices included.

There are exactly

\[
\binom{28+4-1}{4}=\binom{31}{4}=31,465
\]

labeled four-edge multisets. The exact checker canonicalized every one under S_8 and found exactly

\[
\boxed{23}
\]

S_8 x S_4 witness-pattern orbits.

## B25 — independent coverage count

The checker did not trust canonicalization counts alone. For each of the 23 representatives it explicitly enumerated the automorphism group and verified

\[
|\operatorname{Orb}(G)|=\frac{8!}{|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|}
\]

against the number of labeled multisets assigned to that canonical class. The 23 orbit sizes sum back to exactly 31,465. This is a second exact count of the same finite search front-end.

The claim certified here is therefore:

> Every four-deletion witness quartet for an 8-state four-letter irreducibly synchronizing automaton belongs to one of 23 explicitly enumerated S_8 x S_4 witness-pattern classes.

This is only a search decomposition, not a Černý theorem and not movement of the frozen metric.

## B2 — attack the negation with a stronger counterexample architecture

To make the front-end immediately executable, I deliberately strengthened the witness condition. For each deletion i require its chosen pair p_i to be preserved **setwise** by every remaining letter. Restrict all four letters to rank 7, and require the omitted letter a_i to escape its own pair p_i; otherwise the full automaton could never synchronize that pair.

This `singleton-witness + rank-7 + escape` architecture is strictly narrower than the full irreducible family, so a complete result in it remains PARTIAL.

The exact enumeration of all 23 witness-pattern orbits gives a sharp finite classification inside this architecture:

- exactly 8 of 23 witness-pattern orbits admit at least one rank-7 escaping letter for every color/deletion;
- those 8 are exactly the simple-forest four-edge patterns;
- the other 15 patterns have an empty letter pool for at least one required color.

The eight feasible representative patterns and exact per-color rank-7 pool sizes are archived in the checker output. The classification is finite and executable; no asymptotic inference is made from it.
