## 3. (a) Exact blocker

After the exact reductions already recovered by this track and P16, a missing four-letter counterexample can be sought among synchronizing 8-state automata with four distinct induced transformations and every three-letter deletion nonsynchronizing. Let

\[
\mathcal I_{8,4}
=
\left\{
A:\ |Q|=8,\ |\Sigma|=4,\ A\text{ synchronizing},\ 
A\setminus\{a\}\text{ nonsynchronizing for each }a\in\Sigma,
\text{ and the four letter maps are distinct}
\right\}.
\]

The blocking statement is therefore

\[
\boxed{\max_{A\in\mathcal I_{8,4}}\operatorname{rt}(A)\le 49.}
\]

This is the exact obligation that the previous simple-idempotent, common-cyclic, selected-witness, and singleton-trap calculations do not cover.

A useful literature correction narrows the search **without claiming anything new**: by Berlinkov–Szykuła, any hypothetical `n=8` counterexample must have

\[
\boxed{\min_{a\in\Sigma}\operatorname{rank}(a)\ge 4.}
\]

Thus the rank-3 shard that looked attractive in the previous planning notes is already settled externally and must not consume another run.

## 4. (b) Routes tried and why each failed to close the blocker

### Simple-idempotent / restricted transformation families

Exact subset-BFS censuses established the square bound for substantial simple-idempotent and common-cyclic-permutation subclasses, including larger `n`, but these are proper subclasses of `\mathcal I_{8,4}`. Their coverage theorem stops at the family definition; it does not classify arbitrary 8-state four-letter supports.

### Selected deletion witnesses

The previous B2/B18/B25 run represented each nonsynchronizing three-letter deletion by one chosen unmergeable pair and reduced the four selected pairs to 23 `S_8 x S_4` witness-pattern orbits. This is a useful front end, but witness choice is noncanonical and discards almost all information in a deletion's pair obstruction. The restricted rank-7 singleton-trap shard produced an irreducibly synchronizing example of threshold 31, not a counterexample, and it does not cover larger closed pair regions or other letter ranks.

### P16 parent-safe extension sieve

The Kisielewicz–Szykuła extension-safe inequalities give rigorous pruning once a complete nonsynchronizing ternary parent stream is available. P16 validated the machinery on a complete small case, but the full canonical `n=8` ternary-parent stream plus durable fourth-letter shards was not completed. A theorem-backed pruning inequality is not itself a complete enumeration certificate.

### Greedy rank reduction / subset potentials

Kernel partitions and rank are useful only when rank actually falls; they are blind along long equal-rank plateaus in the subset automaton. A potential depending only on current rank cannot by itself certify a 49-step global bound.

### Raw exhaustive enumeration

A verifier without a provably complete canonical stream does not move the frozen metric. Sampling, timeout, or enumerating a convenient support family is explicitly insufficient.

## 5. (c) What a successful proof/computation must contain that these routes lack

It must contain a **coverage mechanism for every member of `\mathcal I_{8,4}`**, not just a faster reset-word verifier. Concretely, at least one of the following is required:

## 6. B3 — prove something stronger: use the full canonical deletion traps

The prior four selected witness pairs should be replaced by the complete pair obstruction of each deletion.

For each letter `a_i`, define the canonical candidate object

\[
U_i
=
\left\{

\{p,q\}\text{ is not mergeable using }\Sigma\setminus\{a_i\}

The next route will explicitly prove and then exploit the closure facts that follow directly from this definition, rather than treating them as an unverified shortcut. The intended encoding assigns each of the 28 unordered state pairs a four-bit incidence word

\[

The stronger target is not merely “there exists one witness per deletion”, but “the four *entire* deletion traps admit only finitely many canonical incidence/lift patterns compatible with a slowly synchronizing automaton.” If that stronger finite classification can be proved complete, the chosen-witness ambiguity disappears and the search is forced onto the real obstruction rather than an arbitrary representative.

## 7. B11 — dualize: four labelled cuts in the 28-state pair automaton

The unordered-pair automaton has 28 non-diagonal pair states. The full-trap viewpoint suggests dualizing each `U_i` from a set of bad pairs into a labelled cut: under the three letters of the deletion, the cut has no outgoing edge to a mergeable pair; only the omitted letter can be responsible for escaping that deletion obstruction.

This suggests an exact finite CSP whose primary variables are:

- four actual state transformations `f_i:Q->Q` (so liftability is built in rather than guessed after the fact);
- the 28 x 4 trap-incidence bits;
- closure constraints for each deletion trap;
- full four-letter pair-compressibility constraints;
- the known lower-rank exclusion `rank(f_i)>=4` from Berlinkov–Szykuła;
- a canonical `S_8 x S_4` symmetry break.

The crucial point is that an arbitrary transition system on the 28 pairs is **not** automatically induced by eight-state maps. Therefore the finite abstraction must retain the eight vertex maps or separately prove an integrability theorem. That liftability issue is exactly what the previous witness-only quotient lacked.

Again, this is a proposed finite encoding to be proved complete before use; it is not a new mathematical claim from this thinking run.

## 10. (e) Nearest solved problem and the ingredient missing here

The nearest structural solved class is Berlinkov–Szykuła's theorem for a synchronizing automaton having a sufficiently low-rank letter. Their linear-algebraic argument proves the Černý bound whenever a letter has rank at most `cuberoot(6n-6)`, hence through rank 3 at `n=8`.

What the present blocker lacks is precisely that amount of collapse: rank 4 is just beyond the theorem's `n=8` threshold, and irreducibility/deletion traps must supply the additional shortening if this route is to work.

The nearest *enumerative* solved problem is the complete ternary `n=8` verification reported by Kisielewicz–Kowalski–Szykuła. Its proof architecture has a complete generation/pruning pipeline. The missing four-letter cell lacks the corresponding complete retained-parent stream / coverage certificate at tractable size; P16's handoff records this gap explicitly.
