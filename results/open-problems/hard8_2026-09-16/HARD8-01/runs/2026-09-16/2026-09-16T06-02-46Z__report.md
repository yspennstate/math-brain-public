# 1. Exact reduction to the strongly connected core, with quantitative entry bound

The qualitative fact that one may reduce to strongly connected automata is standard and is used explicitly in Rodaro–Venturi. The following self-contained quantitative version is recorded because it gives a clean budget that can be reused in later arguments. **No novelty claim is made for the qualitative reduction.**

## Proposition 1 (sink-core entrance bound)

Let \(\mathcal A=(Q,\Sigma,\delta)\) be a synchronizing DFA on \(n\) states. Let \(C\subseteq Q\) be its unique sink strongly connected component, let \(|C|=m\), and put \(N=n-m\). Then there exists a word \(u\) such that

\[
\delta(Q,u)\subseteq C
\]

and

\[
|u|\le \frac{N(N+1)}2.
\]

### Proof

Because \(\mathcal A\) is synchronizing, it has a reset word \(w\) with image \(\{q\}\). Any sink strongly connected component is closed under every letter, hence also under every word. Therefore, if \(D\) is a sink strongly connected component, \(\delta(D,w)=\{q\}\subseteq D\), so \(q\in D\). Two distinct sink SCCs are disjoint, so there can be only one. Denote it by \(C\).

For \(x\in Q\), let \(d(x)\) be the length of a shortest directed path in the underlying labeled transition digraph from \(x\) to \(C\). Thus \(d(x)=0\) for \(x\in C\), and every \(x\notin C\) has \(1\le d(x)\le N\).

We need a sharper elementary fact. Let \(T\subseteq Q\setminus C\) be nonempty with \(|T|=k\). Then

\[
\min_{x\in T} d(x)\le N-k+1. \tag{1}
\]

Indeed, choose \(x\in T\) minimizing \(d(x)=d\), and choose a shortest path

\[
x=x_d\to x_{d-1}\to\cdots\to x_1\to C,
\]

where necessarily \(d(x_i)=i\). The \(d-1\) intermediate outside states \(x_{d-1},\ldots,x_1\) are not in \(T\), because their distance is smaller than the minimum distance of a state in \(T\). Hence the \(N\) outside states contain the disjoint collection consisting of the \(k\) states of \(T\) and these \(d-1\) intermediate states. Therefore \(N\ge k+d-1\), proving (1).

Now begin with the image \(S_0=Q\). Suppose after some word \(u_j\) the current image is \(S_j=\delta(Q,u_j)\), and let

\[
T_j=S_j\setminus C,
\qquad k_j=|T_j|>0.
\]

By (1), choose \(x\in T_j\) and a word \(v_j\) of length at most \(N-k_j+1\) that maps \(x\) into \(C\). Since \(C\) is a sink SCC, every state already in \(C\) stays in \(C\) under \(v_j\). At the end of \(v_j\), the selected outside image state \(x\) lies in \(C\), and only the other \(k_j-1\) outside image states can contribute outside images. Thus

\[
|\delta(S_j,v_j)\setminus C|\le k_j-1.
\]

Repeat until no outside image state remains. If some step decreases the number of outside images by more than one, the bound only improves. In the worst case the successive outside-image counts are \(N,N-1,\ldots,1\), so

\[
|u|\le \sum_{k=1}^{N}(N-k+1)=1+2+\cdots+N=\frac{N(N+1)}2.
\]

This proves the proposition. \(\square\)

## Corollary 2 (strongly connected reduction with slack)

If the Černý bound is true for every strongly connected synchronizing DFA with fewer than or equal to \(n\) states, then it is true for every synchronizing DFA with \(n\) states.

### Proof

Use Proposition 1 to map \(Q\) into the sink SCC \(C\) in at most \(N(N+1)/2\) letters. The restriction of every letter to \(C\) defines a strongly connected synchronizing DFA on \(m\) states: synchronizing follows because any reset word for the whole automaton also resets the closed set \(C\). By hypothesis, \(C\) can then be reset in at most \((m-1)^2\) further letters.

Thus

\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le \frac{(n-m)(n-m+1)}2+(m-1)^2.
\]

The Černý budget dominates this quantity because

\[
(n-1)^2-
\left[
\frac{(n-m)(n-m+1)}2+(m-1)^2
\right]
=
\frac{(n-m)(3m+n-5)}2\ge0.
\]

Hence \(\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)\le(n-1)^2\). \(\square\)

### Consequence for a minimal counterexample

Any counterexample of minimum state cardinality must be strongly connected. More generally, an induction proof is free to spend the explicit triangular entrance cost above before invoking a sharp bound on the sink SCC.

This is consistent with, but quantitatively more explicit than, the standard strongly-connected reduction used in the 2026 hereditariness work. It does **not** resolve any of the three remaining hard classes (simple, quasi-simple, radical).

# 2. A natural odd-budget compression program fails on the Černý family itself

A tempting way to make \((n-1)^2\) appear is to assign to a rank-\(k\) image the one-step compression budget

\[
b_n(k)=2(n-k)+1.
\]

Indeed,

\[
\sum_{k=2}^{n} b_n(k)
=1+3+\cdots+(2n-3)
=(n-1)^2.
\]

If every reachable image \(S\) of size \(k\) could always be compressed to smaller cardinality in at most \(b_n(k)\) letters, the conjecture would follow immediately.

That local statement is false. The failure is not exotic: it already occurs inside the extremal Černý automata. For example, in the standard \(4\)-state Černý automaton with cyclic letter \(a:i\mapsto i+1\pmod4\) and defect-one letter \(b:0\mapsto1\) fixing the other states, the reachable pair \(\{1,3\}\) requires 6 letters to synchronize, while \(b_4(2)=5\). This agrees with the known hard-subset behavior behind Cardoso’s subset-synchronization conjecture.

So independent per-rank odd budgets cannot be the sought proof invariant.

# 3. Two-rank amortization looked promising at n=4, but an exact C5 obstruction kills it

I next tested a weaker amortized idea: instead of demanding the odd budget at every single rank, demand it only in pairs of rank decreases.

For a reachable image \(S\) of size \(k\ge3\), let \(d_2(S)\) be the shortest length of a word \(w\) with

\[
|\delta(S,w)|\le k-2.
\]

The paired odd budget would be

\[
b_n(k)+b_n(k-1)=4(n-k)+4. \tag{2}
\]

## Exact exhaustive n=4 binary check

An exact enumeration over all ordered pairs of transformations on four states contains

\[
4^4\cdot4^4=65{,}536
\]

labeled binary DFAs. Filtering to strongly connected synchronizing automata leaves **18,648**. For every reachable subset \(S\) with \(|S|\ge3\), exact BFS in the subset automaton verifies

\[
d_2(S)\le 4(4-|S|)+4.
\]

There are **50,232** such `(automaton, reachable subset)` checks. The maximum gap `d_2(S)-budget` is 0, so the bound is attained. One tight labeled witness has

- `a = (0,0,2,3)`,
- `b = (2,0,3,1)`,
- `S = {0,2,3}`,
- `d_2(S)=8`,
- paired budget `=8`,
- reset threshold `=9`.

The supporting exact verifier is saved separately in this task’s `artifacts/` directory.

This finite result is **not** evidence of a universal theorem by itself.

## Exact n=5 counterexample to (2)

The paired inequality (2) is false already for the standard five-state Černý automaton. Use states \(0,1,2,3,4\) and letters

\[
a(i)=i+1\pmod5,
\]

and

\[
b(0)=1,\qquad b(i)=i\quad(i=1,2,3,4).
\]

The subset

\[
S=\{1,3,4\}
\]

is reachable from \(Q\); for example

\[
Q\cdot baab=S.
\]

Exact BFS in the 31-state subset automaton gives

\[
\min\{|w|:|S\cdot w|=1\}=13.
\]

One shortest synchronizing word for \(S\) is

`aabaaaabaaaab`

of length 13. But for \(n=5,k=3\), the paired budget is only

\[
4(5-3)+4=12.
\]

Thus the two-rank amortization hypothesis fails by one.

This is not an accidental random obstruction. The same subset attains the Cardoso conjectural subset-synchronization value

\[
f(5,3)
=(5-1)^2-
\left\lceil\frac{5-3}{3}\right\rceil
\left(2\cdot5-3\left\lceil\frac53\right\rceil-1\right)
=16-3=13.
\]

The full automaton has reset threshold 16, with shortest reset word

`baaaabaaaabaaaab`.

Therefore a proof strategy calibrated only to rank, even with two-step averaging, is too optimistic: it underprices the **terminal synchronization debt** of certain reachable subsets in the extremal family itself.

# 4. Quantifying the terminal-debt obstruction

The sum of the naive odd budgets from rank \(m\) down to rank 1 is

\[
T(n,m)=\sum_{k=2}^{m}[2(n-k)+1]
=(m-1)(2n-m-1). \tag{3}
\]

Cardoso’s conjectured sharp bound for synchronizing an arbitrary \(m\)-subset in an \(n\)-state synchronizing automaton is

\[
f(n,m)
=(n-1)^2-
\left\lceil\frac{n-m}{m}\right\rceil
\left(
2n-m\left\lceil\frac nm\right\rceil-1
\right). \tag{4}
\]

The Černý series is known to have subsets attaining the Cardoso expression for every subset size, so (4) is the correct calibration for that extremal family if the conjecture is true.

For representative values, the gap \(f(n,m)-T(n,m)\) is already substantial:

| n | m | Cardoso value f(n,m) | odd terminal budget T(n,m) | gap |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 2 | 6 | 5 | 1 |
| 5 | 3 | 13 | 12 | 1 |
| 6 | 3 | 20 | 16 | 4 |
| 8 | 4 | 42 | 33 | 9 |
| 10 | 3 | 60 | 32 | 28 |
| 10 | 5 | 72 | 56 | 16 |

So the gap is not a small-boundary artifact. A successful non-greedy potential must allow early rank compression to build enough **credit** to pay for expensive low-rank subset synchronization later. Any proof that assigns each rank a fixed local budget independently of the geometry of the image subset is structurally mismatched to the known extremal family.

This gives a sharper design criterion for subsequent work:

> **Required shape of a viable potential.** It should jointly track (i) how cheaply the current image was reached and (ii) how expensive its internal synchronization geometry is. Rank alone is insufficient, and even bounded two-rank averaging is insufficient.

The recent Rodaro–Venturi reduction suggests doing this only inside the strongly connected simple/quasi-simple/radical classes. The Zhu one-cluster proof suggests that a useful potential may need an additional structured operator (there, powers of a distinguished one-cluster letter and the associated linear-algebraic/spectral structure) rather than just cardinality.

# 5. Relation to current published open subproblems

Szykuła’s August 2026 open-problems survey makes the same general bottleneck visible from a different angle:

- the best general reset threshold is still cubic, about \(0.1654n^3+o(n^3)\);
- a linear bound on avoiding one state would improve the cubic coefficient but still not by itself prove Černý;
- a subquadratic bound for avoiding logarithmically many states would already yield a subcubic reset threshold;
- the strongly connected “compress a specified state with another state in \(O(n)\)” conjecture remains open;
- subset synchronization bounds stronger than trivial pairwise compression would improve the general reset threshold.

The exact C5 obstruction above says that a direct attempt to force the whole problem into a sequence of locally cheap rank drops is unlikely to be sufficient. The next attack should instead seek a **credit/debt potential** compatible with hard Černý subsets and then try to prove monotonicity or bounded drift for the three Rodaro–Venturi hard classes.

# 7. Next concrete attack

1. Work inside **strongly connected simple/quasi-simple/radical automata**, using the Rodaro–Venturi reduction rather than spending effort on reducible automata.
2. Define a candidate structural debt \(D(S)\) that upper-bounds or predicts subset synchronization difficulty and that equals the known hard-subset profile on \(C_n\). Rank-only functions are ruled out by the C5 obstruction.
3. Test whether linear-algebraic spans of preimage indicators, transition-monoid ideal depth, or shortest avoiding-set costs can serve as the missing state variable in \(D(S)\).
4. Require a telescoping inequality of the form

\[
|v| + D(Sv)\le D(S)
\]

for at least one admissible extension/compression word \(v\), with \(D(Q)=(n-1)^2\) and \(D(\{q\})=0\). The potential must reproduce, not contradict, the hard-subset costs in \(C_n\).
5. Use exact small automata only to falsify candidate potentials quickly; any surviving candidate must then be proved symbolically. In particular, the `C5` subset above should be an immediate unit test for every future proposed local budget.
