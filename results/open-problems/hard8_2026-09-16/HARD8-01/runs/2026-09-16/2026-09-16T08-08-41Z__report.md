# 1. Current literature status

The unrestricted conjecture remains open in the latest bounded primary-source check.

* Marek Szykuła, **“Synchronizing Automata: Open Problems,”** arXiv:2608.24245, submitted 2026-08-25, states the Černý conjecture as an open problem and emphasizes avoiding words, pair compression, subset synchronization and linear-algebraic methods as the main active directions: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24245
* Mikhail Volkov, **“List of Results on the Černý Conjecture and Reset Thresholds for Synchronizing Automata,”** arXiv:2508.15655, is a living annotated list. The accessible text explicitly states the conjecture is neither proved nor refuted and records the classical exact \((n-1)^2\) threshold of the Černý series and the \((n-1)^2\) upper bound for circular automata: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15655
* Yinfeng Zhu, **“The Černý Conjecture for One-Cluster Automata via Annular Spectral Descent,”** arXiv:2607.19675, proves the conjecture for the one-cluster class, not for arbitrary automata: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19675
* Igor Rystsov, **“Cerny type automata and rank conjecture,”** arXiv:2501.19166, treats automata generated by a simple idempotent and a regular permutation group and proves the square bound for that restricted class: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19166

The family analyzed below is circular and is also within the simple-idempotent / regular-group setting, so this checkpoint does **not** claim a new unrestricted class. The useful new item is an exact elementary lower-bound mechanism that handles arbitrary noncompressing uses of the defect letter and yields a closed reset-threshold formula for the two-letter cyclic family. I did not locate this exact formula/proof in the bounded search; that search miss is not a novelty claim.

# 2. The normalized-hole dynamics

Fix \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le d\le n-1\). Let
\[
\mathcal A_{n,d}=(\mathbb Z_n,\{a,b\})
\]
with
\[
a(i)=i+1\pmod n,
\qquad
b(0)=d,
\qquad
b(i)=i\quad(i\ne0).
\tag{1}
\]

For a current image \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z_n\), let its physical complement be
\[
C=\mathbb Z_n\setminus S.
\]
Let \(c\) be the total number of occurrences of \(a\) read so far. When \(\gcd(n,d)=1\), let \(d^{-1}\) denote the inverse of \(d\) modulo \(n\), and define the **normalized hole set**
\[
H=d^{-1}(C-c)
=\{d^{-1}(x-c):x\in C\}\subseteq\mathbb Z_n.
\tag{2}
\]

An occurrence of \(a\) sends \(C\) to \(C+1\) and \(c\) to \(c+1\), so it leaves \(H\) unchanged.

Immediately before an occurrence of \(b\), define its **site**
\[
j=-c\,d^{-1}\pmod n.
\tag{3}
\]
Physical states \(0\) and \(d\) then correspond in normalized coordinates to \(j\) and \(j+1\). Directly from the definition of \(b\), its action on \(H\) is
\[
B_j(H)=
\begin{cases}
H,&j\in H,\\
(H\setminus\{j+1\})\cup\{j\},&j\notin H.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]
The second formula includes both possibilities:

* if \(j+1\notin H\), then \(b\) increases \(|H|\) by one;
* if \(j+1\in H\), then \(b\) merely moves a hole from \(j+1\) to \(j\), preserving rank.

Thus all “wasted” or noncompressing uses of \(b\) are represented exactly; no normal-form assumption is made.

# 3. Last-establishment lemma

The key observation is about the **last time a final hole is established**.

## Lemma 1 (ordered last establishment)

Let a sequence of site operations \(B_{j_1},\dots,B_{j_m}\) act on hole sets in \(\mathbb Z_n\). Suppose the final hole set is
\[
H_m=\mathbb Z_n\setminus\{f\}.
\]
For a final hole \(r\in H_m\) that is not permanently inherited from the initial set, let \(\tau(r)\) be the last operation index at which \(r\) enters the hole set.

If both \(r\) and \(r+1\) are final holes and \(\tau(r)>0\), then
\[
\boxed{\tau(r)<\tau(r+1).}
\tag{5}
\]

### Proof

At time \(\tau(r)\), the site operation is necessarily \(B_r\) and \(r\notin H\) immediately before it. By (4), immediately after this operation the site \(r+1\) is **not** a hole: if \(r+1\) was a hole, it is removed; if it was not a hole, it stays absent. Since \(r+1\) is a final hole, it must enter the hole set again at some later operation. Its last entrance therefore occurs after \(\tau(r)\). \(\square\)

Two cases will be used.

## Corollary 2 (empty initial complement)

If \(H_0=\varnothing\) and \(|H_m|=n-1\), then the site word \(j_1\cdots j_m\) contains, as a subsequence,
\[
\boxed{f+1,f+2,\dots,f-1}
\tag{6}
\]
with \(n-1\) consecutive residues modulo \(n\).

### Proof

Every final hole must have a positive last-establishment time because there were no initial holes. Applying Lemma 1 successively along the final consecutive run
\[
f+1,f+2,\dots,f-1
\]
gives strictly increasing establishment times, and the operation at each such time has the corresponding site label. \(\square\)

## Corollary 3 (one initial hole)

If \(|H_0|=1\) and \(|H_m|=n-1\), then the site word contains a cyclically consecutive subsequence of length at least \(n-2\).

### Proof

If the unique initial hole is not a final hole, or if it is removed and later re-established, then all final holes have positive last-establishment times and Corollary 2's argument actually gives \(n-1\) consecutive sites.

Otherwise, the unique initial hole persists to the end without re-establishment. Assign it time \(0\). Along the final run \(f+1,\dots,f-1\), Lemma 1 implies that the last-establishment times are strictly increasing after this single zero. Hence the persistent initial hole must be the first element of that ordered run, and the remaining \(n-2\) final holes occur at strictly increasing positive establishment times. Their site labels are consecutive. \(\square\)

The importance of these corollaries is that they survive **arbitrarily many** rank-preserving \(b\)-moves. Such moves can reorder holes locally, but they cannot destroy the ordered chain of last establishments that must exist in any successful synchronization.

# 4. Converting consecutive sites into a rotation-cost lower bound

Let \(j_s\) and \(j_t=j_s+1\) be two consecutive site labels occurring in this order in the site sequence of a word. Let \(c_s,c_t\) be the cumulative numbers of \(a\)'s before those occurrences of \(b\). By (3),
\[
j_t-j_s\equiv -(c_t-c_s)d^{-1}\pmod n.
\]
Since \(j_t-j_s\equiv1\pmod n\),
\[
c_t-c_s\equiv-d\equiv n-d\pmod n.
\tag{7}
\]
Because \(c_t-c_s\) is a nonnegative integer counting actual letters \(a\),
\[
\boxed{c_t-c_s\ge n-d.}
\tag{8}
\]

If a site word contains \(L\) consecutive residues as a subsequence, the intervals between the chosen occurrences are disjoint. Therefore the word contains at least
\[
\boxed{(L-1)(n-d)}
\tag{9}
\]
occurrences of \(a\).

This is the missing amortization from the previous checkpoint: a shortcut between some effective \(b\)'s can exist, but the final-hole last-establishment chain forces compensating rotation cost elsewhere.

# 5. Exact reset threshold of the cyclic defect-one family

## Theorem 4

For \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le d\le n-1\), the automaton \(\mathcal A_{n,d}\) is synchronizing if and only if
\[
\gcd(n,d)=1.
\]
When it is synchronizing,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A_{n,d})
=(n-2)(n-d)+n-1.
}
\tag{10}
\]

### Proof: non-coprime case

Let \(g=\gcd(n,d)>1\). The letter \(a\) adds \(1\) modulo \(g\), while \(b\) preserves residues modulo \(g\), because \(b(0)=d\equiv0\pmod g\) and every other state is fixed. Hence for every word \(w\),
\[
q\cdot w\equiv q+|w|_a\pmod g.
\tag{11}
\]
Two states initially in different residue classes modulo \(g\) can therefore never merge. The automaton is not synchronizing.

### Proof: coprime lower bound

Now assume \(\gcd(n,d)=1\), and let \(w\) be any reset word. Initially the complement is empty, so \(H_0=\varnothing\). At the end the image is a singleton, hence \(|H_m|=n-1\). By Corollary 2, the site sequence of the occurrences of \(b\) contains \(n-1\) consecutive residues. By (9), \(w\) contains at least
\[
(n-2)(n-d)
\]
letters \(a\).

The permutation letter \(a\) never changes image size, while one occurrence of \(b\) can decrease image size by at most one. Going from rank \(n\) to rank \(1\) therefore requires at least \(n-1\) occurrences of \(b\). Consequently
\[
|w|\ge(n-2)(n-d)+n-1.
\tag{12}
\]
This lower bound allows arbitrary extra rank-preserving or no-op uses of \(b\).

### Proof: coprime upper bound

The preceding checkpoint gave the explicit word
\[
\boxed{b(a^{n-d}b)^{n-2}}
\tag{13}
\]
and proved by complement growth that it resets \(\mathcal A_{n,d}\). Its length is exactly the right-hand side of (10). Combining with (12) proves equality. \(\square\)

## Corollary 5 (exact slack below the Černý square)

For \(\gcd(n,d)=1\),
\[
\boxed{
(n-1)^2-\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A_{n,d})=(n-2)(d-1).
}
\tag{14}
\]
Thus \(d=1\) is exactly the classical Černý series and saturates the conjectured bound; every \(d>1\) in this family has explicit positive slack.

# 6. The previous “wasted-\(b\)” gap is fully closed

The prior report conjectured, based on exact search through \(n\le16\), that the word
\[
w_{n,d}=b(a^{n-d}b)^{n-3}
\]
was shortest among all words synchronizing **some** \((n-1)\)-subset, but only proved this for words with exactly \(n-2\) occurrences of \(b\).

Corollary 3 removes that hypothesis.

## Theorem 6 (exact easiest \((n-1)\)-subset synchronization threshold)

Let \(n\ge3\), \(1\le d\le n-1\), and \(\gcd(n,d)=1\). Among all \((n-1)\)-subsets \(S\subset\mathbb Z_n\), the minimum length of a word synchronizing \(S\) is
\[
\boxed{
L_{n,d}=(n-3)(n-d)+(n-2).
}
\tag{15}
\]

### Proof

Start from an arbitrary \((n-1)\)-subset. Its complement has one element, so \(|H_0|=1\). If a word synchronizes it, the final complement has size \(n-1\). Corollary 3 gives a consecutive site subsequence of length at least \(n-2\), and therefore at least
\[
(n-3)(n-d)
\]
letters \(a\). Rank must drop by \(n-2\), so at least \(n-2\) letters \(b\) are required. Thus every such word has length at least the right-hand side of (15), regardless of how many noncompressing occurrences of \(b\) it contains.

The prior checkpoint explicitly constructed a subset and a word of exactly this length. Hence equality holds. \(\square\)

This is stronger than the previous normal-form proposition and exactly resolves its stated gap.

## Still open

The central obstruction remains extending this kind of ordered last-event certificate beyond a single defect-one letter conjugated by a cyclic permutation. For a general synchronizing automaton, rank-preserving singular actions need not behave as one-dimensional hole transfers, and there is no canonical cyclic order forcing a long subsequence of last establishments.

# 9. Failed / superseded route

The previous run attempted to prove optimality in \(\mathcal A_{n,d}\) by showing that extra noncompressing \(b\)'s cannot save enough rotations. Direct local exchange analysis is awkward because a noncompressing \(b\) can indeed enable a temporary rotational shortcut. The new last-establishment proof shows why that route was unnecessarily local: shortcuts are possible, but the final set of holes globally forces an ordered consecutive subsequence, and that subsequence alone pays the full rotation budget.

Thus the exact gap is not solved by forbidding wasted letters; it is solved by finding a global event ordering invariant insensitive to them.

# 10. Next attack

The useful generalization target is now precise.

Seek an analogue of **last establishment** for automata generated by several defect-one letters and a transitive permutation group. Each defect-one letter replaces one missing state by another, so complements evolve by directed hole transfers on a graph rather than on one cycle. A successful reset establishes \(n-1\) final holes. If their last-establishment events can be forced to contain a long path in a suitable Schreier/Rystsov graph, then graph distance between successive forced events can supply a non-greedy lower/upper accounting similar to (9).

Concrete next bounded tasks:

1. formulate the hole-transfer dynamics for an arbitrary simple-idempotent letter \(e_{u\to v}\) conjugated by a permutation group;
2. identify when final-hole last-establishment times orient edges acyclically toward the unique surviving image state;
3. test whether a spanning arborescence/path certificate can replace the cyclic consecutive-site chain;
4. compare the resulting path-cost functional with the Ferens–Szykuła large-fiber/reaching certificate from the prior run;
5. search exact small strongly connected simple/quasi-simple automata for cases where the last-establishment DAG has small longest path, to learn what additional potential is necessary.

A successful general theorem here would not immediately prove Černý, but it would export the precise mechanism that defeated rank-preserving detours in the cyclic family into one of the structural classes emphasized by current literature.
