\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)\le (n-1)^2,
\]
or exhibit an exact synchronizing counterexample with a certified shortest reset word exceeding \((n-1)^2\). Arbitrary finite alphabets remain in scope. Restricted SI/permutation subclasses below are research reductions only, not the full target.

That report proved an exact metric/arborescence formula for automata whose letters are permutations or simple idempotents (SI automata). If \(P\) is the set of permutation letters, \(G=\langle P\rangle\), and \(e=e_{s\to t}\) is a simple idempotent, then the conjugated defect arc associated with a cumulative permutation prefix \(g\in G\) is
\[
\alpha(g,e)=(s g^{-1},t g^{-1}).
\]
For rank \(k\), the report showed that the shortest word from \(Q\) to rank \(k\) equals \(n-k\) singular letters plus the minimum directed Cayley travel through witnesses of a child-before-parent \(k\)-rooted spanning forest. It also showed that rank-preserving singular uses are globally dispensable in this class.

The open task left there was to quotient the witness-prefix travel by stabilizers and seek an amortized exchange/transport bound. This run carries out the first quotient step exactly and identifies a sharper one-coordinate transport law for consecutive defect arcs.

# 2. SI notation recalled

Let the alphabet split as
\[
\Sigma=P\sqcup E,
\]
where every \(p\in P\) acts as a permutation and every \(e\in E\) is a simple idempotent
\[
e=e_{s_e\to t_e},\qquad e(s_e)=t_e,\qquad e(q)=q\ (q\ne s_e).
\]
Let
\[
G=\langle P\rangle\le\operatorname{Sym}(Q)
\]
under the right-action convention \(q(fg)=(qf)g\).

For \(e=e_{s\to t}\) define
\[
H_e=\operatorname{Stab}_G(s,t)=\{h\in G: sh=s,\ th=t\}.
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal O_e=\{(s g^{-1},t g^{-1}):g\in G\}
\]
be the orbit of directed defect arcs generated by conjugates of \(e\), and put
\[
m_e:=|\mathcal O_e|=[G:H_e].
\tag{1}
\]
The full defect digraph \(\Gamma\) is the union of the \(\mathcal O_e\).

For a fixed arc \(a\in\mathcal O_e\), define its witness set
\[
W_e(a)=\{g\in G:\alpha(g,e)=a\}.
\]

# 3. Exact witness-coset lemma

## Lemma 1

If \(a=\alpha(g,e)\), then
\[
\boxed{W_e(a)=gH_e.}
\tag{2}
\]

### Proof

If \(h\in H_e\), then
\[
\alpha(gh,e)
=(s(gh)^{-1},t(gh)^{-1})
=(s h^{-1}g^{-1},t h^{-1}g^{-1})
=(s g^{-1},t g^{-1}),
\]
so \(gH_e\subseteq W_e(a)\).

Conversely, if \(\alpha(g',e)=\alpha(g,e)\), then
\[
s(g')^{-1}=sg^{-1},\qquad t(g')^{-1}=tg^{-1}.
\]
Right-multiplying these state equalities by \(g'\) gives
\[
s g^{-1}g'=s,\qquad t g^{-1}g'=t.
\]
Hence \(g^{-1}g'\in H_e\), so \(g'\in gH_e\). \(\square\)

Thus an arc witness is a **right coset** of the ordered-pair stabilizer. The subtle point is that right multiplication by the permutation generators does not act well on right cosets. The correct quotient for a travel bound is obtained by inversion.

# 4. Schreier entrance bound to an arbitrary prescribed defect arc

Let \(d_P(x,y)\) be the shortest positive-generator word length taking \(x\) to \(y\):
\[
d_P(x,y)=\min\{|u|:u\in P^*,\ xu=y\}.
\]

## Theorem 2 (orbit-size entrance bound)

Fix \(e\in E\), any target arc \(a\in\mathcal O_e\), and any current permutation prefix \(f\in G\). Then there exists a witness \(g'\in W_e(a)\) such that
\[
\boxed{d_P(f,g')\le m_e-1.}
\tag{3}
\]

Equivalently, from **any** current prefix, one can steer to some witness of any prescribed conjugate arc of \(e\) using at most \(|\mathcal O_e|-1\) permutation letters.

### Proof

Choose one witness \(g\) of \(a\), so by Lemma 1 the target witness set is \(gH_e\).

Consider the left-coset Schreier graph
\[
H_e\backslash G
\]
with generator set \(P^{-1}\). It has exactly
\[
[G:H_e]=m_e
\]
vertices and is strongly connected because \(P\) generates the finite group \(G\). Therefore there is a simple directed path of length at most \(m_e-1\) from \(H_e\) to the left coset
\[
H_e g^{-1}f.
\]
Let \(v\in(P^{-1})^*\) be its label. Then
\[
|v|\le m_e-1,
\qquad
H_e v=H_e g^{-1}f.
\]
Hence for some \(h\in H_e\),
\[
v=h g^{-1}f.
\]
Set
\[
u=v^{-1}\in P^*.
\]
The inverse reverses the word and replaces each inverse generator by the corresponding original generator, so \(|u|=|v|\). Moreover
\[
f u
=fv^{-1}
=f(f^{-1}g h^{-1})
=g h^{-1}\in gH_e=W_e(a).
\]
Thus appending \(u\) to the current prefix reaches a witness of \(a\) in at most \(m_e-1\) permutation letters. \(\square\)

This theorem is independent of \(|G|\): only the ordered-pair orbit size matters.

# 5. Orbit-weighted spanning-forest upper bound

Give every occurrence of an arc \(a\in\Gamma\) a **label** \(e\in E\) such that \(a\in\mathcal O_e\). Its orbit weight is
\[
w(a,e)=m_e.
\tag{4}
\]
If an arc belongs to several letter orbits, the label is part of the certificate and may be chosen to minimize weight.

A labeled \(k\)-rooted forest means a directed spanning forest on \(Q\), oriented toward \(k\) roots, with every nonroot vertex having one outgoing arc, each selected arc belonging to \(\Gamma\) and carrying a witnessing letter label.

## Theorem 3 (static orbit-weight forest bound)

For every SI automaton and every \(1\le k<n\),
\[
\boxed{
\rho_k(\mathcal A)
\le
\min_{F}
\sum_{(a,e)\in F}m_e,
}
\tag{5}
\]
where the minimum is over all labeled \(k\)-rooted spanning forests of \(\Gamma\), and \(\rho_k\) is the shortest length of a word taking \(Q\) to rank at most \(k\).

In particular, if the automaton is synchronizing,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le
\min_T\sum_{(a,e)\in T}m_e,
}
\tag{6}
\]
where \(T\) ranges over labeled spanning in-arborescences of \(\Gamma\).

### Proof

Fix such a forest \(F\). Order its \(n-k\) arcs in any child-before-parent order. Start with current permutation prefix \(f=1\).

Suppose the next prescribed forest arc is \(a\), labeled by \(e\). By Theorem 2, append at most \(m_e-1\) permutation letters so that the cumulative prefix becomes a witness of \(a\), then append the singular letter \(e\). This block has length at most
\[
(m_e-1)+1=m_e.
\]

Because the forest is processed child-before-parent, immediately before its arc \(r\to p\) is applied, both \(r\) and \(p\) are still live: descendants of \(r\) may already have been deleted, but the operation deleting \(r\) is the current one, while the operation deleting \(p\), if any, occurs later. Hence this singular letter lowers the rank by exactly one.

After all \(n-k\) forest arcs, exactly the \(k\) roots remain. Summing the block lengths gives (5), and \(k=1\) gives (6). \(\square\)

### Significance

The previous checkpoint’s exact formula involved a precedence-constrained path through the full group \(G\). Theorem 3 gives a coarser but purely **static graph certificate**: choose a labeled rooted forest and add ordered-pair orbit sizes. The certificate can be checked from orbit/stabilizer data plus acyclicity; no subset BFS or reset-word search is required for the upper bound.

A direct corollary is:

## Corollary 4

If \(\Gamma\) has a spanning in-arborescence whose selected labels all satisfy
\[
m_e\le n-1,
\]
then
\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)\le(n-1)^2.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus any SI counterexample to Černý must defeat **every** such low-orbit arborescence.

# 6. Exact first-leaf discount

The generic weight \(m_e\) charges up to \(m_e-1\) permutation letters **before** every selected singular arc. For the first arc this can be sharpened exactly.

For \(a\in\mathcal O_e\), define its entrance distance
\[
\eta_e(a)=\min\{|u|:u\in P^*,\ \alpha(\bar u,e)=a\}.
\tag{8}
\]
By Theorem 2,
\[
0\le\eta_e(a)\le m_e-1.
\]

If \(a\) is a leaf arc of a rooted forest, it may be placed first in a child-before-parent order.

## Theorem 5 (leaf-discounted forest bound)

For a labeled rooted forest \(F\) and any leaf arc \((a,e)\in F\),
\[
\boxed{
\rho_k(\mathcal A)
\le
1+\eta_e(a)
+
\sum_{(b,f)\in F\setminus\{(a,e)\}}m_f.
}
\tag{9}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\rho_k(\mathcal A)
\le
\sum_{(b,f)\in F}m_f
-
\bigl(m_e-1-\eta_e(a)\bigr).
\tag{10}
\]

### Proof

Put \(a\) first. Reach its witness in \(\eta_e(a)\) permutation letters and append \(e\), costing \(1+\eta_e(a)\). For every later forest arc use Theorem 2, costing at most its orbit weight. The same child-before-parent rank-drop argument as in Theorem 3 applies. \(\square\)

If the leaf arc is literally an original alphabet arc \((s_e,t_e)\), then the identity prefix witnesses it, so
\[
\eta_e(a)=0.
\tag{11}
\]
The discount is then the full \(m_e-1\).

This yields an exact necessary obstruction for any SI counterexample: for every labeled spanning arborescence \(T\) and every leaf arc \((a,e)\) of \(T\),
\[
\sum_{(b,f)\in T}m_f-igl(m_e-1-\eta_e(a)\bigr)>(n-1)^2.
\tag{12}
\]
If even one certificate violates (12), the automaton satisfies the square bound.

# 7. Recovery of the regular one-idempotent square

This gives a useful consistency check against the previous checkpoint and Rystsov’s regular-group class.

Suppose \(G\) acts regularly on \(Q\), \(|G|=n\), and there is one simple idempotent \(e\). Then the ordered-pair stabilizer is trivial, so
\[
m_e=n.
\tag{13}
\]
If the automaton synchronizes, the previous checkpoint showed that its defect orbit is one directed \(n\)-cycle. Delete one edge to obtain a spanning in-arborescence and choose the root so that the original alphabet arc of \(e\) is a leaf edge. Then Theorem 5 gives
\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le
1+(n-2)n
=(n-1)^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the orbit-weight method recovers the square in the regular one-idempotent case without using the stronger generator-sensitive exact formula from checkpoint 4.

This is only an upper-bound recovery; checkpoint 4 contains the sharper exact threshold
\[
(n-1)+(n-2)\ell
\]
with \(\ell\) the relevant directed generator distance.

# 8. Transitive actions: exact subdegree interpretation

Assume now that \(G\) acts transitively on \(Q\). For \(e=e_{s\to t}\), let
\[
d_e:=|tG_s|
\tag{15}
\]
be the subdegree of \(t\) under the stabilizer \(G_s\). Orbit-stabilizer gives
\[
\boxed{m_e=n d_e.}
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed,
\[
[G:H_e]=[G:G_s][G_s:G_s\cap G_t]=n|tG_s|.
\]

This exposes both the strength and the limitation of Theorem 3. A generic transitive ordered-pair orbit already costs at least \(n\), and a 2-transitive action has \(d_e=n-1\), so the independent orbit-entry bound is far too expensive there.

However the leaf discount gives a nontrivial exact criterion.

## Corollary 6 (one exceptional suborbit)

Assume \(G\) is transitive. Suppose \(\Gamma\) has a labeled spanning in-arborescence \(T\) with a leaf edge \((a_0,e_0)\) that is literally the original alphabet arc of \(e_0\), and suppose **every other selected edge** is labeled by a letter \(e\) with subdegree
\[
d_e=1.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)\le(n-1)^2,}
\tag{17}
\]
regardless of the subdegree of the exceptional first leaf \(e_0\).

### Proof

The exceptional leaf has \(\eta=0\). Its weight is \(nd_{e_0}\), but Theorem 5 discounts \(nd_{e_0}-1\), leaving cost \(1\). Each of the remaining \(n-2\) edges has weight \(n\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le1+n(n-2)
=(n-1)^2.
\]
\(\square\)

Regular actions have all subdegrees equal to one, so this contains the preceding recovery. It also identifies a broader structural possibility: a transitive nonregular action may still satisfy the square certificate when one arbitrary-subdegree original leaf is enough to connect a tree whose remaining arcs lie in subdegree-one orbitals.

Conversely, (16) shows why a proof based only on repeatedly entering arbitrary prescribed pair orbits cannot handle highly transitive groups: it pays \(\Theta(n^2)\) for a single defect arc in a 2-transitive action. A successful general argument must exploit dependence between consecutive arcs rather than re-enter each pair orbit independently.

# 9. A sharper consecutive-arc identity: only one state must be transported

The preceding limitation suggests looking at **consecutive** forest arcs before quotienting them independently.

Let
\[
e=e_{s\to t},\qquad f=e_{s'\to t'}.
\]
Suppose the current cumulative permutation prefix is \(g\), so the current defect arc is
\[
\alpha(g,e)=(r,p).
\]
Append a permutation word inducing \(u\in G\), and then use \(f\). The next defect arc is
\[
\alpha(gu,f).
\]

## Lemma 7 (one-coordinate transporter law)

The next defect arc starts exactly at the current target \(p\), i.e.
\[
\alpha(gu,f)=(p,q)\quad\text{for some }q,
\]
if and only if
\[
\boxed{t u=s'.}
\tag{18}
\]

### Proof

The current target is
\[
p=tg^{-1}.
\]
The next source is
\[
s'(gu)^{-1}=s'u^{-1}g^{-1}.
\]
These are equal iff
\[
s'u^{-1}g^{-1}=tg^{-1},
\]
which, after cancelling \(g^{-1}\), is equivalent to
\[
s'u^{-1}=t,
\]
or \(tu=s'\). \(\square\)

### Consequence

If \(t\) and \(s'\) lie in the same \(G\)-orbit, there is a word \(u\) satisfying (18) of length at most
\[
|tG|-1\le n-1,
\tag{19}
\]
by a shortest path in the state Schreier graph.

Thus, **if the target of the next defect arc is allowed to be chosen adaptively**, extending a defect chain by one edge costs at most \(n-1\) permutation letters. The expensive ordered-pair stabilizer disappears; only a one-state transporter remains.

The new endpoint is
\[
q=t'u^{-1}g^{-1}.
\tag{20}
\]
The obstruction is no longer travel length but **collision avoidance**: can one choose successive letter types and short transporters so that the newly generated vertices remain distinct until all states are covered?

This is the key structural narrowing obtained in this run.

# 10. Short-transporter Hamiltonian defect-chain certificate

The previous lemma gives a direct square-length certificate.

Choose simple-idempotent letter types
\[
e_i=e_{s_i\to t_i}\qquad(1\le i\le n-1)
\]
and permutation words \(u_i\in P^*\) for \(1\le i\le n-2\). Define
\[
g_1=1,\qquad g_{i+1}=g_i\bar u_i.
\tag{21}
\]
Assume
\[
t_i\bar u_i=s_{i+1}
\qquad(1\le i\le n-2),
\tag{22}
\]
so Lemma 7 makes the defect arcs concatenate. Put
\[
r_i=s_i g_i^{-1}
\quad(1\le i\le n-1),
\qquad
r_n=t_{n-1}g_{n-1}^{-1}.
\tag{23}
\]
Then the conjugated defect arcs are exactly
\[
r_1\to r_2\to\cdots\to r_n.
\tag{24}
\]

## Theorem 8 (short defect-chain certificate)

If the \(n\) states in (23) are pairwise distinct and
\[
|u_i|\le n-1
\qquad(1\le i\le n-2),
\tag{25}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)\le(n-1)^2.}
\tag{26}
\]

### Proof

Use the word
\[
e_1u_1e_2u_2\cdots u_{n-2}e_{n-1}.
\tag{27}
\]
The first singular letter costs one and needs no permutation prefix. By (22)–(24), its conjugated singular arcs form a directed Hamiltonian path in child-before-parent order. Hence each of the \(n-1\) singular letters deletes one new state and the word resets the automaton.

Its length is at most
\[
(n-1)+(n-2)(n-1)=(n-1)^2.
\]
\(\square\)

This certificate is stronger than the independent orbit-weight bound precisely in high-subdegree actions: it pays at most \(n-1\) per **adjacent chain step** instead of up to \(n(n-1)-1\) to enter a prescribed ordered-pair orbit.

For the classical cyclic defect-one family from checkpoint 3, one may take all \(e_i=e\) and all \(u_i\) equal to the same shortest transporter from \(t\) back to \(s\). The resulting chain is the previously proved consecutive-site chain.

# 11. What remains open after this checkpoint

No unrestricted reset-threshold bound is improved here. The full Černý conjecture remains open.

The new results divide the SI subproblem into two exact mechanisms:

1. **Static orbit-weight mechanism.** A cheap labeled arborescence proves the square via Theorems 3 and 5. This is strongest when ordered-pair orbits are small.
2. **Adaptive chain mechanism.** Lemma 7 reduces each chain extension to a one-state transporter of length at most \(n-1\); the hard issue is avoiding repeated endpoints in (20). This is potentially strongest when pair orbits are large but point orbits are small (especially transitive/highly transitive actions).

A naive attempt to prove the square by replacing every pair-witness transition with a state-Schreier distance is **invalid** for a preassigned arborescence: the state condition \(tu=s'\) fixes only the next source, while the next target in (20) is then determined by the chosen transporter. Prescribing both source and target returns to an ordered-pair coset problem. The adaptive choice of the next target is therefore essential.

# 13. Next attack

The next bounded iteration should target the collision-avoidance problem in (20), rather than return to full ordered-pair travel immediately.

For fixed letter types \(e=(s\to t)\), \(f=(s'\to t')\) and current prefix \(g\), analyze the endpoint set
\[
\mathcal N_{e,f}(g;L)
=
\{t'u^{-1}g^{-1}:u\in G,\ tu=s',\ |u|_P\le L\}.
\tag{28}
\]
The desired square would follow in the SI setting from a Hall/expansion statement strong enough to choose, for \(L=n-1\), a sequence of \(n-2\) chain extensions whose endpoints are all new.

Two concrete subproblems are now sharply formulated:

1. **Short-transporter expansion.** Lower-bound \(|\mathcal N_{e,f}(g;n-1)|\) in terms of subdegrees/stabilizer orbits. Determine exactly when it collapses to one endpoint (the regular cyclic situation) and when high transitivity forces many choices.
2. **Branch-jump amortization.** If a Hamiltonian chain is impossible, decompose a rooted arborescence into as few adaptive chains as possible. Pay \(\le n-1\) for within-chain transitions via Lemma 7 and reserve the heavier ordered-pair Schreier bound only for jumps between chains. Derive a numerical condition on the number and orbit sizes of those jumps that still fits the square budget.

An exact small-order verifier should enumerate SI automata, compare subset-BFS reset thresholds with the orbit-weight/leaf-discount certificates, and search for short-transporter Hamiltonian chains. Any failure is a failure of the sufficient certificate only, not a Černý counterexample.
