# 3. Permutation-orbit transfer digraph

Let
\[
\mathcal A=(Q,P\cup E)
\]
be an SI automaton, and let
\[
G=\langle P\rangle
\]
be the permutation group generated by the permutation letters. Let
\[
\mathcal O_1,\ldots,\mathcal O_r
\]
be the \(G\)-orbits on \(Q\).

Define the **orbit-transfer digraph** \(D(\mathcal A)\) as follows.

- Its vertices are the permutation orbits \(\mathcal O_i\).
- For distinct orbits \(\mathcal O_i,\mathcal O_j\), put an arc
  \[
  \mathcal O_i\to\mathcal O_j
  \]
  whenever the alphabet contains a simple idempotent \(e_{s\to t}\) with
  \[
  s\in\mathcal O_i,
  \qquad
  t\in\mathcal O_j.
  \]
- Singular letters whose source and target are in the same permutation orbit do not create cross-orbit arcs.

Permutation letters preserve every \(\mathcal O_i\) setwise. A simple idempotent can move a state between permutation orbits only along one of the arcs of \(D(\mathcal A)\).

# 4. Unique terminal SCC theorem

## Theorem 1

If an SI automaton \(\mathcal A\) is synchronizing, then its orbit-transfer digraph \(D(\mathcal A)\) has a **unique sink strongly connected component** \(C\).

Let
\[
U_C=\bigcup_{\mathcal O_i\in C}\mathcal O_i.
\]
Then \(U_C\) is invariant under every letter of \(\mathcal A\), and the restriction
\[
\mathcal A_C:=\mathcal A\restriction U_C
\]
is itself a synchronizing SI automaton.

### Proof

Let \(C\) be any sink SCC of \(D(\mathcal A)\). A permutation letter preserves each permutation orbit, hence preserves \(U_C\). Consider a simple idempotent \(e_{s\to t}\).

- If \(s\notin U_C\), then \(e_{s\to t}\) fixes every point of \(U_C\).
- If \(s\in U_C\), then the orbit of \(s\) lies in the sink SCC \(C\). Since \(C\) has no outgoing arc to another SCC, the orbit of \(t\) also lies in \(C\). Thus \(t\in U_C\).

Hence every letter maps \(U_C\) into itself.

If there were two distinct sink SCCs \(C_1,C_2\), their unions \(U_{C_1},U_{C_2}\) would be nonempty, disjoint, and invariant under every letter. For every word \(w\),
\[
U_{C_1}w\subseteq U_{C_1},
\qquad
U_{C_2}w\subseteq U_{C_2},
\]
so no word could map a state from each set to one common state. This contradicts synchronization. Therefore the sink SCC is unique.

Finally, let \(w\) reset \(\mathcal A\). Since \(U_C\) is invariant,
\[
U_Cw
\]
is contained in \(U_C\); because \(w\) maps all of \(Q\) to one state, \(U_Cw\) is a singleton. Thus \(\mathcal A_C\) is synchronizing. Restricting permutation letters to \(U_C\) keeps them permutations, and restricting simple idempotents either gives a simple idempotent on \(U_C\) or the identity there. Hence the restricted automaton is again SI after redundant identities are discarded. \(\square\)

# 5. Exact triangular cleanup of all nonterminal permutation orbits

The point of Theorem 1 is not merely that \(U_C\) is a synchronizing invariant subautomaton. The SI structure gives a cheap constructive way to collapse **every orbit outside \(C\)** into the terminal core before running a core reset word.

For every permutation orbit \(\mathcal O\notin C\), choose one outgoing cross-orbit singular letter
\[
e_{\mathcal O}=e_{s_{\mathcal O}\to t_{\mathcal O}}
\]
lying on a directed arborescence of the orbit-transfer condensation toward \(C\). Thus the source lies in \(\mathcal O\) and the target lies in the chosen parent orbit. Process these nonterminal orbits in child-before-parent order.

The following elementary directed-Schreier estimate is the key local step.

## Lemma 2 (full-orbit evacuation)

Let \(\mathcal O\) be a \(G\)-orbit of size \(m\), and suppose a chosen singular letter
\[
e=e_{s\to t}
\]
has \(s\in\mathcal O\) and \(t\) in a parent permutation orbit that is currently **full** in the image set. If the current image contains all of \(\mathcal O\), then all \(m\) states of \(\mathcal O\) can be removed from the image using at most
\[
\boxed{\frac{m(m+1)}2}
\]
letters, while keeping the parent orbit full.

### Proof

The positive-generator Schreier digraph of \(P\) on \(\mathcal O\) is strongly connected because \(\mathcal O\) is a \(G\)-orbit and \(G\) is finite.

Suppose \(k\) states of \(\mathcal O\) are currently live. Let
\[
B_d=\{q\in\mathcal O:\text{some positive permutation word of length at most }d\text{ maps }q\text{ to }s\}.
\]
Reverse breadth-first search in a strongly connected digraph gives
\[
|B_d|\ge \min(m,d+1).
\]
There are \(m-k\) dead states in \(\mathcal O\). Taking \(d=m-k\), the set \(B_d\) has at least \(m-k+1\) states, so it cannot be contained in the dead set. Hence some live state can be moved to \(s\) by at most \(m-k\) permutation letters.

The parent orbit is full, and permutation letters preserve it as a set, so after this permutation word the target state \(t\) is still live. Applying \(e_{s\to t}\) therefore lowers the image size by one and removes one live state from \(\mathcal O\). The parent orbit remains full because \(e\) fixes every parent-orbit state and maps the source into an already-live target.

For \(k=m,m-1,\ldots,1\), the permutation costs are bounded by
\[
0,1,\ldots,m-1,
\]
and there are \(m\) singular occurrences. Hence the total is at most
\[
(0+1+\cdots+(m-1))+m
=\frac{m(m+1)}2.
\]
\(\square\)

## Theorem 3 (terminal-SCC reduction bound)

Let \(C\) be the unique sink SCC of \(D(\mathcal A)\), let
\[
m=|U_C|,
\]
and let the permutation orbits outside \(C\) have sizes
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_h.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A_C)
+
\sum_{i=1}^h\frac{a_i(a_i+1)}2.
}
\tag{1}
\]

### Proof

Every orbit outside the unique sink SCC reaches \(C\). Choose one orbit-transfer arc from each nonterminal orbit toward \(C\), producing a directed forest rooted in \(C\). Process the outside orbits in child-before-parent order.

Before a nonterminal orbit \(\mathcal O\) is processed, its chosen parent orbit is still full. Indeed, descendants may have transferred states into it, but it was already full, so this does not remove any parent state; permutation letters preserve the parent orbit as a full set; and no earlier evacuation uses a singular letter whose source lies in that parent orbit. Therefore Lemma 2 applies to every outside orbit.

After all outside orbits have been evacuated, the image set is exactly \(U_C\): no core state has ever been removed, and every noncore state has been removed. Append a shortest reset word for \(\mathcal A_C\). Summing the evacuation costs proves (1). \(\square\)

# 6. Counterexample-core reduction

Put
\[
S=n-m=\sum_{i=1}^h a_i.
\]
Since
\[
\sum_i\frac{a_i(a_i+1)}2
\le
\frac{S(S+1)}2,
\]
Theorem 3 gives
\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A_C)+\frac{S(S+1)}2.
\tag{2}
\]

If the terminal core satisfies the Černý square,
\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A_C)\le(m-1)^2,
\]
then
\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le
(m-1)^2+\frac{S(S+1)}2.
\]
But
\[
\begin{aligned}
(n-1)^2-\left[(m-1)^2+\frac{S(S+1)}2\right]
&=(m+S-1)^2-(m-1)^2-\frac{S(S+1)}2\\
&=\frac{S(4m+S-5)}2\\
&\ge0
\end{aligned}
\]
for integers \(m\ge1\), \(S\ge0\) arising here (the \(S=0\) case is equality; for \(S\ge1\), the expression is nonnegative).

Therefore:

## Corollary 4 (counterexamples reduce to orbit-strongly-connected cores)

If an SI automaton \(\mathcal A\) violates the Černý bound, then its terminal-core restriction \(\mathcal A_C\) also violates the Černý bound.

Equivalently, every SI counterexample contains an invariant SI counterexample on the union of the permutation orbits in the unique terminal SCC of its orbit-transfer digraph.

In particular, a **smallest-state SI counterexample** must satisfy
\[
\boxed{D(\mathcal A)\text{ is strongly connected}.}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus all reducible feed-in orbit structure can be stripped away. The genuinely hard SI case has either one permutation orbit (the transitive Bellman core already studied in prior checkpoints) or several permutation orbits joined by a strongly connected cycle of cross-orbit simple idempotents.

This is a useful narrowing because previous HARD8-01 work treated the transitive one-idempotent branch in detail, but did not isolate the exact multi-orbit obstruction for arbitrary SI alphabets.

# 7. Strong singleton-core corollary

If the unique terminal SCC consists of one singleton permutation orbit \(\{t\}\), then \(m=1\) and
\[
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A_C)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le
\sum_i\frac{a_i(a_i+1)}2
\le
\frac{n(n-1)}2.
}
\tag{4}
\]

This strictly generalizes the previous checkpoint’s one-idempotent nontransitive branch: here there may be arbitrarily many permutation orbits and arbitrarily many simple idempotent letters, provided all nonterminal orbit flow ultimately feeds a singleton terminal core.

For \(n\ge2\),
\[
\frac{n(n-1)}2\le(n-1)^2
\]
except at \(n=2\), where both are \(1\). So this entire class satisfies Černý.

# 8. Exact finite census

A deterministic standard-library Python verifier was written for this checkpoint. It exhaustively enumerates all automata on \(n=4\) with:

- one permutation letter \(p\) (all \(24\) permutations are used), and
- an arbitrary nonempty subset of the \(12\) simple idempotents \(e_{s\to t}\), \(s\ne t\).

Thus the census contains
\[
24(2^{12}-1)=98,280
\]
exact automata.

For each automaton the verifier:

1. computes the exact reset threshold by breadth-first search on the 16 subset states;
2. computes the permutation orbits;
3. constructs the orbit-transfer digraph and its SCCs;
4. checks that every synchronizing instance has exactly one sink SCC;
5. constructs the terminal invariant core \(U_C\);
6. checks directly that the core is synchronizing;
7. computes the exact core reset threshold from the full core subset;
8. verifies the inequality (1).

# 9. Claim ledger

## Known from literature / prior checkpoints

- The unrestricted Černý conjecture remains open.
- General reset-threshold bounds remain cubic in the unrestricted setting.
- Numerous special classes satisfy the square; one-cluster automata are among the classes proved by 2026.
- Prior HARD8-01 checkpoints established exact SI hole dynamics, canonical rank-drop forms, forest/transport formulations, the one-idempotent Bellman recurrence, a first-gap shortening theorem, and finite prefix-credit evidence.

## Newly proved in this checkpoint

1. A synchronizing SI automaton has a unique sink SCC in the orbit-transfer digraph of its permutation-group orbits.
2. The union of that SCC is an invariant synchronizing SI subautomaton.
3. Every nonterminal permutation orbit of size \(m\) can be evacuated toward a full parent orbit at cost at most \(m(m+1)/2\).
4. Exact reduction bound
   \[
   \operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
   \le
   \operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A_C)+\sum_i a_i(a_i+1)/2.
   \]
5. Any SI counterexample has a terminal SCC core that is itself an SI counterexample.
6. Therefore a smallest-state SI counterexample must have a strongly connected orbit-transfer digraph.
7. If the terminal core is one fixed state, then \(\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)\le n(n-1)/2\).

## Computationally checked, not elevated beyond its scope

- The terminal-SCC theorem and bound were checked on all 98,280 four-state SI automata with one arbitrary permutation letter and an arbitrary nonempty subset of simple idempotents.

## Still open

- The full Černý conjecture.
- Even within SI automata, the orbit-strongly-connected core remains unresolved in general.
- In the transitive one-idempotent core, the previous viability-aware prefix-credit problem remains unresolved.
- In the genuinely multi-orbit core, strong connectivity of the transfer digraph introduces cyclic dependence: no orbit can be permanently treated as a full sink while all others are removed.

# 10. Failed / insufficient routes identified this run

A naive attempt to extend the one-idempotent nontransitive proof to arbitrary multi-idempotent SI automata by selecting one root permutation orbit fails if the root orbit has more than one state and cross-orbit singular letters form a directed cycle. After all other orbits are emptied, a singular letter from the root to an empty orbit need not lower rank on its first use; worse, subsequent permutation words move the seeded target state in the empty orbit in a coupled way. The “parent stays full” invariant is exactly what makes Lemma 2 work, and it disappears inside a terminal strongly connected orbit component.

The terminal-SCC reduction isolates this obstruction cleanly instead of pretending the same elimination argument works inside the strongly connected core.

# 11. Next attack

The next target should be the smallest genuinely new core excluded by all previous reductions:

- at least two permutation orbits;
- orbit-transfer digraph strongly connected;
- no proper terminal invariant union of permutation orbits;
- simple idempotents provide cross-orbit cycles.

A promising exact subproblem is the **two-orbit strongly connected core**. Let the two permutation orbits have sizes \(a,b\), with at least one singular letter in each direction. The goal is to find a two-reservoir potential that replaces the “full parent” invariant: one should quantify how much rank can be lost while alternately seeding and draining the two orbits, and prove an amortized cost bounded by the global square budget. Any theorem for the two-orbit core would combine with Theorem 3 automatically for arbitrary feed-in trees.

In parallel, the transitive one-idempotent Bellman prefix-credit program remains the other irreducible branch.

No full solution is claimed, and self-review is not independent verification.
