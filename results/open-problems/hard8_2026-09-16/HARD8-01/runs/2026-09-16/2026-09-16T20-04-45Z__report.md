## 1. Mathematical idea before code

The previous HARD8-01 report selected the minimum-letter-rank-4 layer as the simplest still-unproved subcase and proposed a full 28-pair deletion-trap CSP. Before building that CSP, B30 says to do one rank layer at a time and B8 says to discretize the obstruction. The highest-value check is therefore whether the whole rank-4 layer actually needs enumeration.

It does not.

P16 had already source-ported the following rank-reduction theorem from A. Kisielewicz and M. Szykuła, *Generating Synchronizing Automata with Large Reset Lengths*, arXiv:1404.3311v4:

> If a word `u` has rank `r` and the automaton admits some word of rank at most `r-1`, then it admits a word of rank at most `r-1` of length at most `2|u| + n-r+1`.

For a synchronizing automaton, the lower-rank existence hypothesis can be iterated until rank one. Applying the theorem directly to a **letter** of rank four on eight states eliminates the entire rank-4 layer.

## 2. Exact corollary: every 8-state counterexample has all letter ranks at least 5

### Proposition
Let `A` be a synchronizing complete deterministic automaton on eight states. If `A` has a letter of rank at most four, then

`rt(A) <= 47 < 49 = (8-1)^2`.

Hence any 8-state counterexample to the Černý bound must have every letter of rank at least five.

### Proof
Ranks one, two and three are already included in the same recurrence, so it is enough to start with a rank-four letter `a`. Put `u_4=a`; then `|u_4|=1` and `rank(u_4)=4`.

Because `A` is synchronizing, a rank-three-or-less word exists. Applying the source theorem with `n=8,r=4` gives a word `u_3` with

`|u_3| <= 2*1 + 8-4+1 = 7`.

Again a rank-two-or-less word exists. Apply the theorem with `r=3`:

`|u_2| <= 2*7 + 8-3+1 = 20`.

Finally a reset word exists, so apply the theorem with `r=2`:

`|u_1| <= 2*20 + 8-2+1 = 47`.

Thus `rt(A) <= 47 < 49`. QED.

This is a specialization/corollary of the published/source-ported theorem; I do **not** claim literature novelty for it.

## 3. Stronger short-rank necessary profile for any hypothetical n=8 counterexample

The same recurrence gives exact front-end exclusions which should be built into any future exhaustive four-letter search:

- any word of rank at most 2 and length `L <= 21` forces a reset word of length at most `2L+7 <= 49`;
- any word of rank at most 3 and length `L <= 7` forces a reset word of length at most `4L+19 <= 47`;
- any rank-4 **letter** (`L=1`) forces a reset word of length at most `47`.

Therefore a hypothetical 8-state counterexample must satisfy all three:

1. every letter has rank at least 5;
2. there is no rank-3-or-less word of length at most 7;
3. there is no rank-2-or-less word of length at most 21.

For the irreducible four-letter cell retained by this track, the exact remaining obstruction is sharpened from

`max_{A in I_{8,4}} rt(A) <= 49`

to

`max_{A in I_{8,4}^{>=5}} rt(A) <= 49`,

where `I_{8,4}^{>=5}` consists of synchronizing 8-state automata with four distinct letter transformations, every three-letter deletion nonsynchronizing, and every letter rank at least five; a source-level counterexample inside it would additionally have to avoid all rank-3 words through length 7 and all rank-2 words through length 21.

This is a genuine reduction of the remaining finite search, but it is PARTIAL and does not move the frozen `n` metric.
