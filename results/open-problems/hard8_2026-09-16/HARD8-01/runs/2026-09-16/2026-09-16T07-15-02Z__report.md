# 2. New exact “large-fiber certificate” for completely reachable automata

The previous checkpoint showed that trying to pay for synchronization only as a sequence of rank drops is structurally wrong: hard low-rank subsets in the Černý family can carry much more terminal debt than their rank suggests. The Ferens–Szykuła reaching theorem gives a natural way to couple **prefix credit** and **terminal debt**: first manufacture a word with a large fiber, then use complete reachability to reach exactly that fiber.

Let
\[
H_k=1+\frac12+\cdots+\frac1k.
\]

## Theorem 1 (fiber certificate)

Let \(\mathcal A\) be an \(n\)-state completely reachable automaton, and let \(\ell\) be the maximum size of a group orbit in the sense of Ferens–Szykuła. Suppose there is a word \(w\) of length \(L\) and a state \(q\) such that the fiber
\[
S=w^{-1}(q)=\{p\in Q: p w=q\}
\]
has size \(r\), where \(2\le r<n\). Put \(x=n-r\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)
\le
L+x(n+\ell)-nH_x.
}
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, the Černý bound holds whenever
\[
\boxed{
x\ell+L\le n(H_x+r-2)+1.}
\tag{2}
\]

### Proof

Because \(\mathcal A\) is completely reachable, \(S\) is the image of \(Q\) under some word \(u\). Ferens–Szykuła Theorem 5.7 supplies such a reaching word with
\[
|u|\le x(n+\ell)-nH_x.
\]
By the definition of \(S=w^{-1}(q)\), every state of \(S\) is sent to the same state \(q\) by \(w\). Hence
\[
Q u w=S w=\{q\},
\]
so \(uw\) is a reset word. This gives (1).

For (2), require the right-hand side of (1) to be at most \((n-1)^2\). Since \(r=n-x\),
\[
(n-1)^2-xn
=n^2-2n+1-xn
=n(r-2)+1.
\]
Therefore
\[
L+x(n+\ell)-nH_x\le(n-1)^2
\]
is exactly equivalent to
\[
x\ell+L\le n(H_x+r-2)+1.
\]
\(\square\)

### Two useful specializations

### Corollary 2 (a two-fiber certificate)

If a one-letter word has a fiber of size two, so \(r=2\) and \(L=1\), the square bound follows whenever
\[
\boxed{
(n-2)\ell\le nH_{n-2}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This is the exact inequality obtained by applying the refined Ferens–Szykuła reaching estimate, and is a sharper explicit cutoff than replacing \(\ell\) by the simpler sufficient condition \(\ell\le\log n\). No novelty claim is made for consequences obtainable directly by algebra from their theorem.

For orientation, (3) allows integer orbit size \(\ell=3\) through \(n=23\), \(\ell=4\) from \(n=24\), \(\ell=5\) from \(n=75\), \(\ell=6\) from \(n=216\), \(\ell=7\) from \(n=604\), and \(\ell=8\) from \(n=1660\). Asymptotically the cutoff is \(H_{n-2}n/(n-2)=\log n+\gamma+o(1)\).

### Corollary 3 (near-reset fiber)

If some word \(w\) of length \(L\) has a fiber of size \(n-1\), then \(x=1\) and \(H_1=1\), so
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rt}(\mathcal A)\le L+\ell.
}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, the full Černý bound follows as soon as
\[
\boxed{L\le(n-1)^2-\ell.}
\tag{5}
\]

This is an especially clean realization of the credit/debt idea: \(L\) is the cost of creating an almost-reset fiber and \(\ell\) is an upper bound on the cost of reaching that fiber from \(Q\).

# 3. Exact calibration on a cyclic defect-one family

The preceding certificate suggests that the right object to seek is not merely a low-rank image but a **large preimage fiber reached by a short terminal word**. The standard Černý automaton sits inside a simple two-parameter cyclic family where this can be worked out exactly.

For integers \(n\ge3\) and \(1\le d\le n-1\) with \(\gcd(n,d)=1\), define \(\mathcal A_{n,d}\) on \(Q=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) by
\[
a(i)=i+1\pmod n,
\qquad
b(0)=d,
\qquad
b(i)=i\quad(i\ne0).
\tag{6}
\]
The case \(d=1\) is the standard Černý family in this convention. Put
\[
h=n-d\equiv-d\pmod n.
\]

## Proposition 4 (explicit rank-two word with an \((n-1)\)-fiber)

The word
\[
\boxed{w_{n,d}=b(a^{n-d}b)^{n-3}}
\tag{7}
\]
has length
\[
\boxed{L_{n,d}=(n-3)(n-d)+(n-2)}
\tag{8}
\]
and has exactly two image states. More precisely,
\[
(Q\setminus\{n-d\})w_{n,d}=\{d\},
\qquad
(n-d)w_{n,d}=2d\pmod n.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus one fiber has size \(n-1\) and the other has size one.

### Proof by complement growth

Let
\[
S_0=Q\setminus\{-d\}.
\]
After applying the first \(b\), the image complement is
\[
Q\setminus S_0b=\{0,-d\}.
\]
Now \(a^{n-d}=a^{-d}\) rotates every point by \(-d\). Suppose inductively that after \(j\) blocks the image complement is
\[
M_j=\{0,-d,-2d,\ldots,-(j+1)d\}.
\]
After the next \(a^{-d}\), the complement is
\[
\{-d,-2d,\ldots,-(j+2)d\}.
\]
For \(j\le n-4\), neither \(0\) nor \(d\) belongs to this set: the residues \(kd\) are distinct because \(\gcd(n,d)=1\), and the displayed indices have not wrapped to the forbidden residues. Therefore both \(0\) and \(d\) are present in the image. Applying \(b\) merges \(0\) into \(d\), reducing image size by one and adding \(0\) to the complement. Hence
\[
M_{j+1}=\{0,-d,-2d,\ldots,-(j+2)d\}.
\]
After \(n-3\) such blocks, the complement contains \(n-1\) distinct residues
\[
\{0,-d,-2d,\ldots,-(n-2)d\},
\]
whose unique missing residue is
\[
-(n-1)d\equiv d.
\]
Therefore \(S_0w_{n,d}=\{d\}\).

The omitted state \(-d\) is fixed by the first \(b\). During the subsequent blocks it successively occupies
\[
-2d,-3d,\ldots,-(n-2)d\equiv2d,
\]
never hitting \(0\) before the last stage, so every intervening \(b\) fixes it. Thus its final image is \(2d\ne d\). This proves (9), and counting the letters gives (8). \(\square\)

## Corollary 5 (explicit reset word and exact slack from the Černý square)

The subset \(S_0=Q\setminus\{n-d\}\) itself is reached from \(Q\) by
\[
 u=ba^{n-d},
\]
because \(Qb=Q\setminus\{0\}\). Consequently
\[
uw_{n,d}=b(a^{n-d}b)^{n-2}
\tag{10}
\]
is a reset word of length
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}=(n-2)(n-d)+n-1.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover
\[
\boxed{
(n-1)^2-R_{n,d}=(n-2)(d-1).
}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(d=1\) exactly saturates the Černý square, while every \(d>1\) in this family has explicit linear slack \((n-2)(d-1)\).

This is a calibration result for the potential architecture, not a new proof of the full conjecture; circular/one-cluster families are already known subclasses. I did not find a direct match to the exact formula (7)–(12) in the bounded literature search, but a search miss is not evidence of novelty.

# 4. An exact lower bound in the no-wasted-\(b\) normal form

Proposition 4 would be substantially more informative if the \((n-1)\)-fiber word were shortest among all such fibers. A full proof of that unrestricted statement is not yet obtained, because an extra **noncompressing** occurrence of \(b\) can rearrange a subset without changing its cardinality. However, the minimal normal form with no wasted \(b\)'s is exactly solvable.

## Proposition 6 (normal-form lower bound)

In \(\mathcal A_{n,d}\), let \(S\) be any subset of size \(n-1\), and let \(w\) synchronize \(S\). Assume \(w\) contains exactly \(n-2\) occurrences of \(b\). Then
\[
\boxed{|w|\ge(n-3)(n-d)+(n-2)=L_{n,d}.}
\tag{13}
\]
Equality is achieved by the subset and word in Proposition 4.

### Proof

Starting with \(n-1\) states and ending at one state requires \(n-2\) rank decreases. The letter \(a\) is a permutation and each occurrence of \(b\) can reduce the current subset size by at most one. Since the word contains exactly \(n-2\) copies of \(b\), every one of them must reduce the size by exactly one.

Track the complement of the current image. Between occurrences of \(b\), only powers of \(a\) occur. Let \(c_j\) be the cumulative number of \(a\)'s before the \(j\)-th occurrence of \(b\), and normalize the current complement by subtracting \(c_j\) modulo \(n\). In this moving frame, powers of \(a\) do not change the normalized complement.

At the \(j\)-th \(b\), physical state \(0\) corresponds to
\[
y_j=-c_j\pmod n,
\]
and physical state \(d\) corresponds to \(y_j+d\). For \(b\) to reduce rank, both \(0\) and \(d\) must be present in the current image. Equivalently, neither \(y_j\) nor \(y_j+d\) may already lie in the normalized complement. The action of this effective \(b\) inserts \(y_j\) into the normalized complement. Thus the normalized process is:

> start with one occupied point \(m\in\mathbb Z_n\); at each step insert a new point \(y\) only if both \(y\) and its successor \(y+d\) are unoccupied.

After \(n-2\) insertions, exactly one point of the \(d\)-cycle remains unoccupied. The predecessor \(m-d\) of the original point \(m\) can never be inserted, because its successor \(m\) was occupied from the beginning. Hence the final hole is necessarily \(m-d\).

Every other point must eventually be inserted. In particular, \(m+d\) must be inserted before \(m+2d\), because once \(m+2d\) is present the insertion rule permanently forbids insertion of \(m+d\). Inductively, the only order compatible with filling all points except \(m-d\) is
\[
m+d,\ m+2d,\ldots,\ m+(n-2)d.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore
\[
y_j=m+jd\pmod n,
\qquad j=1,\ldots,n-2.
\]
Since \(y_j=-c_j\), consecutive cumulative \(a\)-counts satisfy
\[
c_{j+1}-c_j\equiv-d\pmod n.
\]
They are nonnegative ordinary integers, so each of the \(n-3\) gaps between consecutive \(b\)'s contains at least the least nonnegative representative of \(-d\), namely \(n-d\), copies of \(a\). Hence
\[
\#a\ge(n-3)(n-d).
\]
Adding the exactly \(n-2\) occurrences of \(b\) proves (13).

Equality occurs with initial missing point \(m=-d\), \(c_1=0\), and every inter-\(b\) gap equal to \(n-d\), precisely giving Proposition 4. \(\square\)

### Exact remaining obstruction

To upgrade Proposition 6 to an unrestricted shortest-\((n-1)\)-fiber theorem, one must rule out a word with **more than \(n-2\) copies of \(b\)** whose noncompressing uses rearrange the subset enough to save more \(a\)-letters than the extra \(b\)'s cost. This is now a sharply isolated finite-combinatorial question rather than a vague subset-synchronization problem.

# 6. What this changes relative to the first checkpoint

The first checkpoint concluded that a viable proof cannot assign a fixed local cost to each image rank. The new results sharpen that design constraint:

* The relevant terminal object can be a **fiber**, not merely an image subset.
* In a completely reachable automaton, any large fiber can be turned into a reset certificate because the fiber itself can be reached from \(Q\).
* The cost separates cleanly into
  \[
  \text{terminal word length }L
  +
  \text{fiber-reaching debt}.
  \]
* Ferens–Szykuła’s harmonic reaching theorem supplies the second term exactly enough to obtain criterion (2).
* The cyclic defect-one family shows how this architecture captures the extremal Černý example: for \(d=1\), the near-reset-fiber construction spends exactly the entire square budget; for \(d>1\), it creates explicit slack.

This suggests a more specific global program than “find a mysterious potential”:

> **Find a short word with a sufficiently large fiber, and prove that its fiber has sufficiently low reaching debt.**

Complete reachability is one setting where reaching debt is already quantitatively controlled. For the unrestricted conjecture, the missing analogue is a way to reach, extend, or otherwise exploit a large fiber without assuming every subset is reachable.

# 8. Next concrete attack

The next iteration should pursue two tightly scoped obligations rather than restart broad proof search.

### A. Finish the cyclic-family shortest-fiber theorem

Model the normalized complement process when a noncompressing \(b\) is allowed. A noncompressing \(b\) can only replace one of \(0,d\) by the other or act trivially on the current set. Translate this into the moving-frame occupancy process and seek an exchange argument showing that every such detour can be deleted or transformed without increasing the number of \(a\)'s. If successful, this proves the unrestricted exact formula
\[
\min_{|S|=n-1}\operatorname{syn}(S)=L_{n,d}
\]
for the whole coprime cyclic family.

### B. Generalize “reach a large fiber” beyond complete reachability

For a word \(w\) with large fiber \(F=w^{-1}(q)\), seek a short word \(u\) with \(Qu\subseteq F\) rather than equality \(Qu=F\). Equality is what complete reachability gives, but containment is sufficient for reset. The relevant target is therefore an **image-into-fiber** bound:
\[
|u|+|w|\le(n-1)^2,
\qquad Qu\subseteq w^{-1}(q).
\]
This formulation is strictly closer to the unrestricted conjecture and interfaces naturally with avoiding-word and extending-word methods from the 2026 open-problems survey.
