## 2. Source recheck and dated current/later-literature check — 2026-09-17

The source PDF was re-opened at printed pp. 135–136. Bradley states that his 1994 construction gives, for `d>=2`, a nondegenerate strictly stationary real-valued random field that is (a) lattice-halfspace one-dependent and (b) measurable modulo null sets with respect to its own tail sigma-field, and then asks in Question 10 whether the same can be achieved with finitely many states, even two states.

The 1994 paper was also re-opened at the stationarization step. Its Section 3 takes independent copies `X^(j)` of the nonstationary binary field, one copy for every `j in Z^d`, and defines the stationary real coordinate by a countable weighted sum. Its equation (3.2) identifies the sigma-field of one real coordinate with the sigma-field of **all** copy-layer bits appearing there. This countably infinite copy overlap is exactly the feature targeted by B20 below.

Fresh bounded searches dated 2026-09-17 for the exact phrases `lattice-halfspace one-dependent`, `tail deterministic`, `finite-state`, `two-state`, and Bradley `Question 10` again recovered Bradley's source and adjacent literature but no later theorem matching all source requirements. This is bounded negative evidence only, not a novelty certificate.

Two later/neighboring results matter positively:

1. Robert M. Burton, Manfred Denker and Meir Smorodinsky, *Finite state bilaterally deterministic strongly mixing processes*, Israel J. Math. 95 (1996), 115–133, constructs a finite-state one-dimensional process that is both strongly mixing and bilaterally deterministic. Thus finite alphabet + stationarity + tail reconstruction + ordinary strong mixing is possible.

2. Robert Burton and Kyewon K. Park, *Spatial determinism for a free Z^2-action*, Ergodic Theory Dynam. Systems 32 (2012), 479–489, DOI `10.1017/S0143385711000770`, extends bilateral determinism to `Z^2` actions. Its abstract states that a complete `Z^2` name can be determined from an orbit subset of arbitrarily small density and, in the zero-entropy case, from an arbitrarily small one-sided cone for a suitable partition.

The Burton–Park paper is the most relevant later constructive lead found in this run. It does **not** supply Bradley's exact lattice-halfspace independence, and its zero-entropy cone theorem cannot directly be the desired field because the previous D0038 run proved that every nondegenerate finite-state Bradley-halfspace candidate has positive entropy density. Still, it shows that stationarity plus genuinely spatial deterministic coding need not require Bradley's literal real-valued digit packing.

Adjacent erasure-entropy literature (Verdu–Weissman and van Enter–Verbitskiy) supplies useful language for the condition `H(X_F | X_{F^c})=0`, but no source-matching cut-independent construction or impossibility theorem was found there.

## 3. B21 — transfer neighboring bilateral/spatial determinism: what survives and what fails

### Outcome

**Useful transfer, not closure.** The later literature removes one false strategic assumption: a finite alphabet does not force tail reconstruction to be local or low-entropy. The genuine extra rigidity in D0038 is the **exact independence of entire separated coordinate halfspaces**.

The previous run already used Burton–Denker–Smorodinsky to kill the naive zero-entropy contradiction. Burton–Park now adds a higher-dimensional lesson: deterministic information can be spatially distributed over a very sparse subset of a `Z^2` orbit. Therefore a viable positive solution should not be sought only as a finite replacement for the real number at each site in Bradley's formula. It may instead encode the information needed for reconstruction across space.

However, the transfer stops at a precise wall. Bradley requires, for every coordinate `i` and every integer `m`, independence of the two sigma-fields generated by

`{k : k_i < m}`

and

`{k : k_i > m+2}`.

Ordinary strong mixing, bilateral determinism, sparse determining sets, and one-sided-cone determination do not imply this exact factorization. Any marker/tower/spatial-determinism construction imported from ergodic theory must therefore be rebuilt so that its marker information is **not simultaneously visible in two separated halfspaces**.

That is the main B21 diagnosis:

> the missing ingredient is not finite-state tail coding; finite-state tail coding exists. The missing ingredient is finite-state tail coding whose stationarization has no shared latent information across any separated coordinate cut.

A useful negative corollary for the Burton–Park zero-entropy subtheorem follows immediately from the previous run's iid-sublattice lemma. If `X` is a nondegenerate finite-state D0038 candidate, then

`{X_{4z}: z in Z^d}`

is iid and hence its box entropy has positive density. Thus no zero-entropy spatial-determinism theorem can by itself instantiate D0038.

## 4. B18 — say it as a stationary perfect-secret-sharing / zero-erasure code problem

This section makes the information structure exact rather than metaphorical.

### 4.1 Directional shares reveal exactly zero information

Let `X` be a hypothetical finite-state D0038 solution. Let `F` be any finite set of sites, and write

`a_i = min{k_i : k in F}`, `b_i = max{k_i : k in F}`.

Bradley's exact halfspace definition implies

\[
\sigma(X_F) \perp \sigma(X_k:k_i>b_i+3)
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\sigma(X_F) \perp \sigma(X_k:k_i<a_i-3)
\tag{4.2}
\]

for every coordinate `i`. The constants are deliberately kept with slack to match the printed `<m` / `>m+2` convention.

**Proof.** For (4.1), choose `m=b_i+1`. Then `F` lies in `G_{i,m}={k:k_i<m}`, while `{k:k_i>b_i+3}` is `H_{i,m+2}`. The defining halfspace one-dependence gives independence. The negative direction is symmetric. ∎

Thus any one remote directional halfspace is a perfect privacy share for the finite secret `X_F`: it contains **zero** mutual information about the secret.

### 4.2 The union of the directional shares determines the whole secret

Now put `F` inside a centered box `Q_n=[-n,n]^d`. For `R>n+3`, the complement of `Q_R` is the union of `2d` remote coordinate halfspaces

\[
Q_R^c
=
\bigcup_{i=1}^d \{k:k_i>R\}
\cup
\bigcup_{i=1}^d \{k:k_i<-R\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Tail determinism says `X_F` is measurable with respect to `sigma(X_k:k notin Q_R)` for every finite `Q_R`. Therefore

\[
H\!\left(X_F\,\middle|\,X_{Q_R^c}\right)=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equations (4.1)–(4.4) give an exact secret-sharing access structure:

- every single directional share has zero information about `X_F`;
- the join of all directional shares determines `X_F` exactly.

This is stronger and more useful than the rejected claim that tail determinism forces zero entropy rate.

### 4.3 Finite remote approximate decoders and an extensive synergy lower bound

Because the alphabet is finite, (4.4) can be approximated by finite subsets of the exterior. Enumerate `Q_R^c` by increasing finite sets. Martingale convergence of the conditional distribution of the finite random vector `X_F` implies: for every `epsilon>0`, there exists a finite `D subset Q_R^c` with

\[
H(X_F\mid X_D)<\epsilon.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Assign every site of `D` to one coordinate/sign witnessing that it lies outside `Q_R`. This partitions `D` into at most `2d` finite directional shares `D_1,...,D_{2d}`. By (4.1)–(4.2),

\[
I(X_F;X_{D_r})=0
\qquad (1\le r\le 2d),
\tag{4.6}
\]

while their union has conditional entropy below `epsilon`.

Now take the secret to be the sparse-grid block

\[
S_n:=X_{F_n},
\qquad
F_n:=4\mathbb Z^d\cap[-n,n]^d.
\]

The previous run proved these coordinates iid, so

\[
H(S_n)=|F_n|H(X_0)=\Theta(n^d)
\tag{4.7}
\]

for every nondegenerate finite-state candidate.

Order the directional shares arbitrarily and apply the mutual-information chain rule:

\[
I(S_n;X_{D_1},\ldots,X_{D_{2d}})
=
\sum_{r=1}^{2d}
I(S_n;X_{D_r}\mid X_{D_1},\ldots,X_{D_{r-1}}).
\tag{4.8}
\]

The left side is at least `H(S_n)-epsilon` by (4.5), while the first summand is zero by (4.6). Therefore for at least one `r>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
I(S_n;X_{D_r}\mid X_{D_1},\ldots,X_{D_{r-1}})
\ge
\frac{H(S_n)-\epsilon}{2d-1}
=
\Omega(n^d).
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

So a D0038 solution must realize **extensive purely synergistic information** at arbitrarily large scales: every single remote directional share is perfectly private, yet after conditioning on other shares one direction must release order-volume information.

This is a clean necessary condition, not an impossibility proof. The exterior decoder may live at an arbitrarily large radius and contain arbitrarily many sites, so a naive `surface area versus volume` entropy argument is invalid without a quantitative decoding-radius theorem.

### 4.4 A binary stationary tail-deterministic near miss that exposes the exact failure

Let `(A_i)_{i in Z}` and `(B_j)_{j in Z}` be independent iid fair bits and define on `Z^2`

\[
X_{i,j}=A_i\oplus B_j.
\tag{4.10}
\]

This field is binary, nondegenerate and strictly stationary. It is also tail deterministic. Indeed, for any finite deleted set and any target `(i_0,j_0)`, choose distant `i,j` so that the three sites `(i_0,j)`, `(i,j)`, `(i,j_0)` avoid the deletion. Then

\[
X_{i_0,j}\oplus X_{i,j}\oplus X_{i,j_0}=X_{i_0,j_0}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

But it fails Bradley halfspace independence in the clearest possible way. For any two columns `j,j'` and any row `i`,

\[
X_{i,j}\oplus X_{i,j'}=B_j\oplus B_{j'}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

A row chosen in the far left halfspace and a row chosen in the far right halfspace therefore recover the **same nonconstant bit** `B_j xor B_j'`. Hence the two halfspace sigma-fields have a nontrivial common random variable and cannot be independent.

This example is useful because it shows why the secret-sharing reformulation alone does not solve D0038. Finite-state stationary tail coding is easy; what must be eliminated is reusable transverse latent information visible on both sides of a cut.

## 5. B20 — find why the obvious stationarization fails: finite independent-copy footprint theorem

The previous report proved only that one cannot replace Bradley's real coordinate by a finite-state coordinate while **still decoding every individual independent layer bit at that site**. That left open the possibility that a much cleverer many-to-one nonlinear code of finitely many layers might preserve tail determinism without preserving Bradley equation (3.2).

This run rules out that entire finite-layer idea.

### Theorem 5.1 — finite independent-copy footprint is incompatible with nondegenerate tail determinism

Let `(U^h)_{h in Z^d}` be independent random elements, with no restriction on their individual state spaces. Fix a finite set `C subset Z^d`. Suppose a random field `Y=(Y_k)` satisfies

\[
Y_k\in\sigma(U^h:h\in k+C)
\qquad\text{for every }k\in\mathbb Z^d.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `Y` is tail deterministic, then `Y_0` is almost surely constant. In particular, no nondegenerate tail-deterministic field can be produced by a translation-equivariant rule with a fixed finite footprint in the independent-copy index.

#### Proof

`Y_0` is measurable with respect to

\[
\mathcal A:=\sigma(U^h:h\in C).
\]

Let

\[
B:=C-C=\{c-c':c,c'\in C\},
\]

a finite subset of `Z^d`.

If `k notin B`, then `(k+C) cap C` is empty. Indeed, if `k+c=c'` with `c,c' in C`, then `k=c'-c in C-C`, contradiction. Therefore every `Y_k` with `k notin B` is measurable with respect to source variables whose copy indices are disjoint from `C`.

Because the family `(U^h)` is independent,

\[
\mathcal A
\perp
\sigma(Y_k:k\notin B).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence `Y_0` is independent of `sigma(Y_k:k notin B)`.

But tail determinism says `Y_0` is measurable with respect to the tail sigma-field, and the tail sigma-field is contained in `sigma(Y_k:k notin B)` for this particular finite `B`. Consequently `Y_0` is both measurable with respect to and independent of the same sigma-field. Thus `Y_0` is independent of itself, so every event in `sigma(Y_0)` has probability zero or one. `Y_0` is almost surely constant. ∎

### Corollary 5.2 — no finite-layer Bradley/Olshen stationarization, even with arbitrary nonlinear compression

In Bradley's 1994 notation, let `X^(h)` be the independent translated copy fields. Replace his countable sum by any rule using only a finite set `A subset Z^d` of diagonal layers:

\[
Y_k
=
F\bigl((X^{(k-a)}_a)_{a\in A}\bigr),
\tag{5.3}
\]

where `F` may be arbitrary and its output may be finite, countable, or real valued.

Treat each whole copy `X^(h)` as the independent random element `U^h`. Then `Y_k` uses only copy indices `k-A`, a fixed finite translate. Theorem 5.1 applies with `C=-A`. Therefore (5.3) cannot be both nondegenerate and tail deterministic.

This is strictly stronger than the previous run's entropy argument. No invertibility, no recovery of the individual layer bits, and no finite-alphabet assumption is needed.

The structural conclusion is:

> In the Bradley/Olshen independent-copy architecture, **infinitely many independent copy layers must influence every output site**. Countably infinite overlap is not merely a convenient way to pack digits into a real number; finite copy footprint is itself incompatible with tail determinism.

### What this theorem does not prove

It does **not** rule out a finite-state output that is an infinite-range, many-to-one function of countably many independent layers. Ornstein–Weiss and Burton–Denker–Smorodinsky are warnings that infinite-range finite-generator codings can create bilateral determinism. Thus the next obstruction must use Bradley's exact halfspace independence to constrain such infinite-range codings; Theorem 5.1 alone cannot be promoted to a full finite-alphabet impossibility theorem.

## 6. Failed routes and precise lessons

### 6.1 Directly port Burton–Park / Ornstein–Weiss

Failed as a source proof. Their determinism machinery shows that information can be distributed spatially, but the retrieved statements do not give exact independence of every pair of separated coordinate halfspace sigma-fields. D0038 needs equality-level independence, not decay of dependence.

### 6.2 Surface-area versus volume entropy contradiction

Failed without an additional quantitative theorem. A block of `Theta(n^d)` independent sparse-grid symbols has `Theta(n^d)` entropy, and its exterior determines it, but tail measurability gives no useful upper bound on the radius or cardinality of a finite approximate decoder. It is therefore invalid to assume the information must cross an `O(n^{d-1})` boundary budget.

### 6.3 Product row/column latent secret sharing

The explicit binary field (4.10) proves that strict stationarity and full tail determinism can coexist in a very simple finite alphabet. It fails because both separated vertical halfspaces recover the same transverse differences, and both horizontal halfspaces recover the same row differences. Any replacement based on reusable global row/column latent variables faces the same exact-independence test.

### 6.4 Finite number of Bradley layers per site

Ruled out completely by Theorem 5.1, even for arbitrary nonlinear coding and arbitrary output state space. Do not spend another run trying finite-layer truncations or finite-window compression of Bradley's translated independent copies.

## 7. New constructive design constraint

Combining B21, B18 and B20 gives a sharper design specification than the previous run had.

A positive finite-state construction must simultaneously:

1. have positive entropy density (previous sparse-iid theorem);
2. have zero finite-erasure entropy / full spatial-tail reconstruction;
3. realize order-volume **synergistic** information between multiple directional exterior shares while every single directional halfspace has exactly zero information about a central block;
4. avoid any nonconstant latent variable recoverable from both sides of a separated cut;
5. avoid finite independent-copy footprint at every site;
6. distribute the infinite-range coding spatially rather than storing countably many independently decodable layer bits in one symbol.

This points away from linear shifts, row/column latent models and finite-layer Bradley recodings. The most plausible surviving class is a **nonlinear stationary hierarchical shell/tiling code** in which every reconstruction relation geometrically surrounds what it reconstructs and every random choice capable of correlating two opposite halfspaces is localized to the separating slab rather than shared globally.

That is only a design target, not a construction.
