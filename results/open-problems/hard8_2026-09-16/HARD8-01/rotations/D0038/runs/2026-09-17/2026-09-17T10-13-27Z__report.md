## 3. Section-I stuck trigger and exact blocking statement

This is the **third consecutive complete current-root D0038 run with `E_finite_tail=0`**. Therefore this run follows the mandatory thinking-only stuck protocol rather than adding another incremental lemma.

The exact obstruction can be written in one line. For a finite alphabet `A`, one must either construct or rule out a nondegenerate stationary law on `A^{Z^d}` satisfying simultaneously, for every coordinate `i`, integer `m`, and finite `B subset Z^d`,

\[
\boxed{
I\!\left(X_{\{k:k_i<m\}};X_{\{k:k_i>m+2\}}\right)=0
\quad\text{and}\quad
H\!\left(X_0\mid X_{B^c}\right)=0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The first equality is an **exact sigma-field factorization across every coordinate cut**. The second says every finite erasure, in particular the origin, is exactly reconstructible from arbitrarily remote data. None of the existing routes supplies a stationary finite-alphabet mechanism realizing both, and none proves an information inequality that makes (3.1) impossible.

## 4. Routes already tried and the precise failure of each

This list is intentionally cumulative so that the next researcher does not restart old routes.

### 4.1 Historical entropy-rate contradiction

Rejected. `D0038-A001` asserted that tail determinism should force zero entropy rate. Burton-Denker-Smorodinsky already show finite-state positive-entropy bilateral determinism is possible. The valid entropy statement recovered by this track is instead zero finite-erasure entropy together with positive entropy density on a sparse iid sublattice.

### 4.2 Sparse iid sublattice / positive entropy density

Useful necessary condition, not a contradiction. Bradley halfspace independence gives an iid sufficiently sparse lattice, hence every nondegenerate finite-state candidate has positive box-entropy density. Tail determinism can coexist with positive entropy through genuinely nonlocal synergistic coding, so this alone does not close the problem.

### 4.3 Scalar finite-field/algebraic shifts

Ruled out as a proper subclass in the first current-root report. Minimal finite-support relations can be dilated to produce a common nonconstant character in two supposedly independent separated halfspaces. This kills scalar parity/Ledrappier-type candidates, but does not cover arbitrary nonlinear or nonalgebraic finite-state laws.

### 4.4 Finite-state sitewise invertible replacement of Bradley's real digit packing

Ruled out. One finite symbol cannot sitewise and invertibly carry the countably many independent translated-copy bits used in Bradley equation (3.2). This did not rule out a many-to-one infinite-range finite factor.

### 4.5 Burton-Denker-Smorodinsky / Ornstein-Weiss / Burton-Park transfer

Useful conceptually, insufficient as stated. Those results show finite-state and spatial tail coding is possible, but the retrieved constructions do not preserve exact independence of **every** pair of separated coordinate halfspace sigma-fields. Ordinary strong mixing or sparse determining sets are much weaker than equality in (3.1).

### 4.6 Exact secret-sharing reduction

Necessary but not impossible. Every remote directional halfspace is a zero-information share for a central block, while the union of all `2d` directional exteriors determines it. Finite approximate decoders therefore exhibit `Omega(n^d)` conditional synergy. Classical parity secret sharing shows such synergy is information-theoretically possible, so no contradiction follows without a spatial-consistency theorem.

### 4.7 Row/column XOR latent model

Near miss only. `X_{i,j}=A_i xor B_j` is binary, stationary and tail deterministic, but opposite halfspaces recover the same nonconstant transverse latent difference. Exact cut independence fails.

### 4.8 Finite independent-copy footprint

Ruled out completely in the previous report. Any translation-equivariant output depending on a fixed finite set of independent copy indices is independent of its own sufficiently remote exterior; tail determinism then forces degeneracy. Hence finite-layer Bradley truncations, even nonlinear and even with arbitrary output state space, cannot work.

### 4.9 Surface-area versus volume entropy

Insufficient. Tail measurability gives finite approximate decoders but gives no bound on decoder radius/cardinality. One cannot assume `Theta(n^d)` central information must pass through only `O(n^{d-1})` boundary symbols.

## 5. B9 — RANDOMIZE: stationary hierarchical shells/tiles

### Why this route was tried

Bradley's nonstationary binary field already has the desired finite alphabet and both source properties. The only operation that leaves the finite alphabet is the stationarization step. Therefore the most direct new attack is to stationarize **the hierarchy**, rather than stationarize by packing every translated copy into one real coordinate.

A natural stationary candidate is a random nested dyadic hierarchy generated by a Haar-random `2`-adic shift. Unlike a single finite random shift, the `2`-adic hierarchy has a translation-invariant law on all scales. One can then try to replace Bradley's deterministic concentric shells by random nested boxes/shells and propagate a bit outward through parity/secret-sharing constraints.

### What survived the stress test

The geometry is promising for exactly the right reason. A reconstruction constraint whose support is a connected shell crossing from the left side of a coordinate cut to the right side necessarily visits the intervening slab. A fresh mask on that slab can make the two remote pieces independent after the slab is marginalized. This is the geometric analogue of the masking mechanism implicit in Bradley's shell proof.

Also, direct copying is unnecessary: a central secret can be represented by several perfect shares, each individual directional share carrying zero information. This matches the exact secret-sharing normal form from the previous run.

### Precise failure / missing step

The random hierarchy itself is a new latent variable. A global dyadic shift or marker structure is seen, directly or indirectly, on arbitrarily large portions of both sides of a cut unless the construction is engineered so that **conditional halfspace laws are the same for every hierarchy realization**. Mixing over conditionally independent laws is not generally independent if the conditional marginals depend on the shared hierarchy.

Even if cut independence is repaired, the tail decoder must work from the finite-state field `X` **without being handed the hidden hierarchy**. No current construction proves both of these at once:

1. every cross-cut hierarchy choice is perfectly masked so the two halfspace sigma-fields factor exactly; and
2. the hierarchy/reconstruction relation needed to recover `X_0` is itself inferable from `X_{B^c}` for every finite `B`.

Therefore B9 did not produce a source-valid construction.

## 6. B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: small vector alphabets and nonlinear local gadgets

The previous scalar algebraic no-go suggested moving first to `d=2` and allowing a small vector alphabet or a quasigroup/Latin-square gadget. The diagnostic baseline was a four-state local gradient field built from iid fair bits `A_{i,j}`:

\[
X_{i,j}=\bigl(A_{i,j}\oplus A_{i+1,j},\;A_{i,j}\oplus A_{i,j+1}\bigr).
\tag{6.1}
\]

This was used only as a **stress test**, not promoted as a new theorem or metric-bearing result.

It demonstrates why finite local gadgets are deceptive. The output has exact local consistency identities around plaquettes and its dependence neighborhood is thin enough that separated coordinate halfspaces can be sourced from disjoint iid variables. Some individual erased edge-differences can be recovered from neighboring plaquette identities. It therefore looks much closer to the desired secret-sharing geometry than the row/column XOR near miss.

But it is still a finite-radius factor of iid. A finite perturbation of the underlying vertex bits changes only finitely many output symbols, so a sufficiently large finite erasure is not uniquely reconstructible. This is exactly the failure predicted by the finite-copy-footprint obstruction from the preceding report.

The important conclusion for B15 is methodological rather than theorem-sized:

> Replacing XOR by a nonlinear quasigroup or Latin-square rule does not address the present wall if the rule still has fixed finite source footprint. The next small-alphabet construction must be genuinely infinite-range/hierarchical, not merely nonlinear and local.

Thus B15 failed as a source construction but sharply narrows what “break symmetry” must mean next.

## 7. B30 — ONE SCALE AT A TIME: finite secret-sharing tiles and projective gluing

This run deliberately asked whether the obstruction already appears on one finite scale.

A finite parity-code toy shows that it does **not**. On a finite box/torus, a uniform binary vector conditioned on one global parity relation has the following elementary behavior: if two observed regions leave at least one unobserved mask coordinate between them, their joint projection can still be uniform, while a single erased coordinate is determined by all the others. This is only a finite-scale diagnostic, not a source result.

The implication is important: exact cut independence and exact erasure reconstruction are not mutually contradictory at one scale. Any impossibility proof must use **stationary consistency across arbitrarily many scales**, not only Shannon inequalities for one finite secret-sharing instance.

Trying to extend the finite toy exposes the gluing wall:

- To reconstruct arbitrary fixed finite erasures as scale grows, the number/strength of parity checks must grow.
- If those checks drift to larger and larger scales without leaving any fixed-scale footprint, local weak/projective limits tend to forget them; the limiting field becomes locally iid and loses tail reconstruction.
- If the checks are made translation-invariant through fixed local algebraic constraints, they fall back into the already excluded local/algebraic mechanisms and tend to create cross-cut relations.
- If a random global codebook or hierarchy chooses the checks, that hidden codebook can become a shared latent variable visible from both sides of a cut, breaking exact independence unless its effect is perfectly masked.

So B30 identifies the genuine compatibility problem as a **projective stationary gluing problem**: retain infinitely many reconstruction relations in a finite alphabet at positive entropy density, while every relation that geometrically crosses a cut remains masked by the separating slab and while no global codebook is jointly visible from both sides.

No finite enumeration was run because the one-scale toy is not the source target and the stuck protocol explicitly calls for thinking, not checker production.

## 8. What a successful proof must contain that no route yet contains

### Positive answer

A positive proof must exhibit an explicit finite-alphabet stationary law and prove, at the full sigma-field level, both clauses below:

1. **Cut factorization:** after integrating over every marker/hierarchy/codebook variable, the law on `G_{i,m}` times the law on `H_{i,m+2}` is exactly the product law for every `i,m`. It is not enough that correlations decay or that the conditional laws factor given a hidden hierarchy.
2. **Hierarchy-free tail decoder:** for every finite deleted set `B` and every target site, the target symbol is a measurable function of `X_{B^c}` alone. The decoder may not be given a hidden random origin, codebook or layer labels unless those are themselves recoverable from the same exterior.

No present route proves these two properties for the same finite-state construction.

### Negative answer

A negative proof must establish a genuinely finite-alphabet obstruction to extensive multi-directional synergy under stationary cut factorization. In practical terms it needs a theorem of the form

\[
I(\text{opposite halfspaces})=0\ \forall\text{ cuts}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H(X_0\mid X_{B^c})>0
\text{ for some finite }B
\tag{8.1}
\]

for every nondegenerate finite-alphabet stationary field.

The present entropy arguments do not yield (8.1): finite parity secret-sharing models show that zero-information shares can determine a secret jointly, and tail measurability supplies no quantitative radius bound on the decoder.

## 10. Nearest solved problems and the ingredient they have that D0038 lacks

There are two complementary nearest solved results.

### Bradley 1994

Has exactly the required multidimensional cut independence and tail determinism, but stationarity is obtained by encoding countably many independent translated-copy bits into one nonatomic real coordinate. What it lacks is a finite alphabet.

### Burton-Denker-Smorodinsky 1996 / Burton-Park 2012

Show that finite-state or spatial deterministic coding can be distributed nonlocally without forcing zero entropy. What the retrieved statements lack is Bradley's equality-level one-dependence of **every** separated coordinate halfspace pair.

Therefore the missing bridge is now very specific:

> a finite generator/coding technique that is nonlocal enough to retain tail determinism but **cut-respecting enough that it preserves exact halfspace factorization**.

That bridge, rather than another entropy inequality or another local parity gadget, is what the next recovery should target.
