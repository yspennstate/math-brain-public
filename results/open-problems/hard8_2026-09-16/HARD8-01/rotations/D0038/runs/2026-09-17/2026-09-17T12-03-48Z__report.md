## 4. Method B22 — read the failures: efficient finitary coding is the wrong construction class

### 4.1 Literature recovered

Two neighboring results are decisive for route selection.

1. **Yinon Spinka (2020), “Finitely dependent processes are finitary,” Annals of Probability 48(4), 2088–2177.** It proves that every ordinarily finitely dependent invariant process on a transitive amenable graph is a finitary factor of IID. This does **not** solve D0038 because Bradley's hypothesis is only *lattice-halfspace* one-dependence, a weaker condition than ordinary finite dependence. Treating the two notions as identical would erase the very loophole exploited by Bradley's 1994 construction.

2. **Pál Galicza and Gábor Pete (published online 21 June 2025; PTRF volume 195 (2026)), “Sparse reconstruction in spin systems II: Ising and other factor of IID measures,” Proposition 4.3.** They prove that on an infinite transitive graph, an FFIID field with coding radius `R(o)` and
   \[
   \mathbb E\,|B_{2R(o)}|<\infty
   \]
   has **trivial full tail**. On `\mathbb Z^d`, this condition is equivalent up to fixed geometric constants to a finite `d`-th coding-radius moment.

Consequently, a nondegenerate D0038 solution cannot belong to this robust finite-coding-volume class, because D0038 tail determinism makes `X_0` a nonconstant full-tail measurable variable.

This is a literature import, not a new theorem of this track. It is nevertheless a useful route-killer: many standard symbolic constructions — coupling-from-the-past codings, high-noise Markov/Gibbs codings, Mallows-style finite-dependence constructions with exponential coding tails, or any local algorithm with summable reverse influence — are structurally incapable of producing D0038.

Galicza–Pete explicitly discuss FIID systems with nontrivial/full tails, and one-dimensional bilateral-determinism constructions already warn that Bernoulli/factor structure does not preclude a large double tail. What fails is **efficient locality of the coding**, not factor-of-IID status alone.

Likewise Spinka's theorem cannot be applied by silently upgrading Bradley's halfspace condition to ordinary finite dependence. The source deliberately asks about the weaker halfspace notion.

## 5. Method B25 — count the same thing twice: reverse influence of one IID source symbol

The literature theorem above suggests an elementary way to see exactly what must go wrong in any surviving finitary coding. I record the derivation as an internal route diagnostic, not as a novelty claim.

Assume for this section only that a candidate field `X` is represented as a translation-equivariant finitary factor of an iid source `Y=(Y_u)_{u\in\mathbb Z^d}`. Let `R_v(Y)<\infty` be a coding radius for `X_v`, so that the source variables in the ball

\[
B(v,R_v)
\]

certify the value of `X_v`.

For a fixed source site `u`, define its **reverse coding footprint**

\[
\mathcal I_u(Y):=\{v\in\mathbb Z^d:\ u\in B(v,R_v(Y))\},
\qquad
N_u:=|\mathcal I_u|\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}.
\]

This counts output coordinates whose chosen finite certificate reaches the same source coordinate `u`.

### 5.1 Double count

By translation equivariance,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E N_0
&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^d}
   \mathbb P\bigl(0\in B(v,R_v)\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^d}
   \mathbb P\bigl(\|v\|\le R_0\bigr)\\
&=\mathbb E\,|B(0,R_0)|.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus finite expected coding volume is exactly finite expected reverse footprint. On `\mathbb Z^d`,

\[
\mathbb E|B(0,R_0)|<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbb E(1+R_0)^d<\infty
\]

up to norm-dependent constants.

### 5.2 Tail determinism forces a pivotal source symbol to have infinite reverse reach

Because the output is nondegenerate, there exists an output event depending on `X_0` with probability strictly between zero and one. For an iid source representation, a standard martingale/Efron–Stein argument then gives at least one source coordinate `u` whose independent resampling changes that local output event with positive probability. Equivalently, for an independent resample `Y'_u`, if `\widetilde Y` differs from `Y` only at `u`, and `\widetilde X=F(\widetilde Y)`, then for some such `u`

\[
p_*:=\mathbb P(X_0\ne \widetilde X_0)>0
\]

after passing to a suitable nonconstant finite-valued coordinate/event.

Now suppose on an outcome with `X_0\ne\widetilde X_0` that `\mathcal I_u(Y)` were finite. If `v\notin\mathcal I_u(Y)`, the original certificate ball for `X_v` does not contain `u`; `Y` and `\widetilde Y` agree on that certificate, so

\[
X_v=\widetilde X_v.
\]

Hence `X` and `\widetilde X` would differ at only finitely many output sites.

Let `\Lambda_n\uparrow\mathbb Z^d` be deterministic boxes. Tail measurability supplies measurable decoders `\phi_n` such that

\[
X_0=\phi_n(X_{\Lambda_n^c})
\quad\text{a.s. for every }n.
\]

The resampled source still has the iid source law, so the same identities hold for `\widetilde X` simultaneously on a probability-one event. If the difference set is finite, choose `n` containing it. Then

\[
X_{\Lambda_n^c}=\widetilde X_{\Lambda_n^c}
\]

forces

\[
X_0=\phi_n(X_{\Lambda_n^c})
=\phi_n(\widetilde X_{\Lambda_n^c})
=\widetilde X_0,
\]

a contradiction.

Therefore the pivotal-resampling event can occur only when the source symbol has an infinite reverse footprint:

\[
\boxed{
\{X_0\ne\widetilde X_0\}
\subseteq
\{N_u=\infty\}
\quad(\bmod 0).
}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(N_u=\infty)\ge p_*>0.
}
\]

By translating the relative pivotal offset, every site of the iid source has the same positive probability of possessing an infinite reverse coding footprint.

This is stronger as a route diagnostic than merely saying an expected moment diverges: **some positive density of source sites (by ergodicity of the iid source and the equivariant indicator) must have certificates reaching infinitely many outputs.** Consequently

\[
\mathbb E N_0=\infty,
\qquad
\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^d}\mathbb P(R_0\ge\|v\|)=\infty,
\qquad
\mathbb E(1+R_0)^d=\infty.
\]

This agrees with, and is consistent with, the Galicza–Pete tail-triviality obstruction. I do **not** claim publication novelty for this coupling formulation; the literature itself notes that related tail-triviality arguments may have appeared earlier.

### 5.3 What the count teaches constructively

A surviving FFIID candidate cannot merely have a large but finite random radius. Its coding has to be globally nonlocal in the reverse direction: with positive probability, changing one iid source symbol must force changes at infinitely many output sites. That is precisely the kind of global information transport absent from all fixed-radius and finite-layer constructions already eliminated in earlier D0038 runs.

## 7. Why this does not collapse into Spinka's finitely-dependent theorem

This distinction is important enough to record explicitly.

Ordinary `k`-dependence says sigma-fields generated by **arbitrary** sets at graph distance greater than `k` are independent. Bradley's condition only gives independence of pairs of **coordinate halfspaces** separated by the prescribed gap. A surrounding exterior can interlace the center in all coordinate directions and can carry multi-party secret-sharing information even though every opposite cut is independent.

Therefore:

\[
\text{Bradley halfspace one-dependence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{ordinary finite dependence}
\]

by any argument recovered here. Spinka's 2020 theorem cannot simply be invoked to make a hypothetical D0038 field FFIID. Doing so would be a scope error.

This is also why finite-dependent coloring papers, including recent 2026 constructions on `\mathbb Z^d`, do not answer Question 10: they supply finite-state finite dependence/finitary coding but not the required self-tail determinism.
