## 2. Exact source statement and the 1994 motivating construction

### 2.1 Bradley's definitions

Bradley (1994), *On regularity conditions for random fields*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 121 (1994), 593–598, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9939-1994-1219721-3`, fixes `d>=2` and, for coordinate `i` and integer `m`, defines coordinate half-spaces of the form

`G_{i,m} = {k in Z^d : k_i <=/< m}` and `H_{i,m} = {k in Z^d : k_i >=/> m}`

(the PDF uses a one-lattice-layer gap; the exact endpoint convention is immaterial below because every construction/proof here uses a gap at least four).

The field is **lattice-halfspace one-dependent** when the sigma-fields generated by two such coordinate half-spaces separated by the prescribed one-layer gap are independent, for every coordinate direction and every translate.

Its spatial tail is

\[
\mathcal T_X
 =
\bigcap_{S\subset\mathbb Z^d\ \mathrm{finite}}
\sigma(X_k:k\in\mathbb Z^d\setminus S).
\]

Bradley's second property is that the full field is measurable, modulo null sets, with respect to this tail: equivalently every coordinate `X_k` is `T_X`-measurable.

### 2.2 What the 1994 paper actually achieves

The 1994 paper first builds a **binary-valued but nonstationary** field. It partitions `Z^d` into sup-norm shells

\[
S_n=\{k:\|k\|_\infty=n\},
\]

leaves all but one site of each shell as fresh fair signs, and defines the distinguished shell site recursively so that an earlier sign is recovered as the product of the signs on a later shell. In the notation of the paper, equation (2.3) has the form

\[
X_{\phi(n)}=\prod_{k\in S_n}X_k.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus every finite coordinate is reconstructible from arbitrarily distant shells. This gives the tail-deterministic property while the geometry of the shell construction gives lattice-halfspace one-dependence.

The difficulty is strict stationarity.

Bradley's Section 3 stationarizes by taking **countably many independent copies** of the nonstationary binary field and encoding, at each lattice site, one sign from every copy as digits of a single real number. With a bijection

\[
\theta:\mathbb Z^d\to\{3^{-1},3^{-2},3^{-3},\ldots\},
\]

the stationary coordinate has the form

\[
Y_k=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z^d}\theta(j)X^{(k-j)}_j.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The crucial identity is Bradley's equation (3.2):

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(Y_k)
=
\sigma\bigl(X^{(k-j)}_j:j\in\mathbb Z^d\bigr).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The rapidly decreasing ternary weights make every layer sign individually decodable from the single real coordinate. Shift of the copy index proves strict stationarity; copywise half-space independence proves lattice-halfspace one-dependence; and (2.3) lets the nonstationary tail reconstruction be performed layer by layer.

This identifies precisely where the infinite information capacity of a real-valued coordinate enters the proof.

### 2.3 Bradley's 2005 Question 10

The 2005 survey states that the 1994 example has both desired properties but is real-valued and non-discrete, and asks whether for a given `d>=2` there exists such a strictly stationary field taking finitely many values, or even only two values.

That exact finite-state question is the present target.

## 4. B2 — attack the negation: correct the entropy premise and extract the valid information constraint

### 4.1 Correction to D0038-A001

The historical attempt asserted, heuristically, that

> tail-measurability forces zero entropy rate.

That assertion is false in the relevant two-sided sense.

For a stationary process, entropy rate is not obtained by conditioning a coordinate on the full two-sided tail used in bilateral determinism. A process can have positive Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy and nevertheless allow every finite central block to be reconstructed from the two remote sides. Burton–Denker–Smorodinsky provide a finite-state strongly mixing example of exactly this phenomenon in one dimension, and Ornstein–Weiss put it in a general ergodic-theoretic framework.

Therefore there is **no valid entropy contradiction of the form**

\[
\text{finite alphabet} + \text{tail determinism}\implies h(X)=0.
\]

This route must not be used again without an additional hypothesis.

### 4.2 What half-space one-dependence really does force

It does force a different, rigorous information statement.

#### Lemma 4.1 — a sparse iid sublattice

Let `X=(X_k,k in Z^d)` be strictly stationary and lattice-halfspace one-dependent in Bradley's sense. Then the family

\[
\{X_{4z}:z\in\mathbb Z^d\}
\]

is iid.

The spacing `4` is deliberately conservative; it avoids all endpoint-convention ambiguity in the printed definition. A sharper spacing can be used after fixing whether Bradley writes `<`/`>` or `<=`/`>=` at the cut.

**Proof.** Take a finite set `F subset 4 Z^d`. Group its sites by their first coordinate. Distinct occupied first-coordinate levels differ by at least four. By lattice-halfspace one-dependence, the sigma-field generated by all sites of `F` in the leftmost occupied first-coordinate layer is independent of the sigma-field generated by all sites of `F` in every later occupied first-coordinate layer. Iterating from left to right factors the joint law over the first-coordinate layers.

Now work inside any fixed first-coordinate layer. Group the remaining sites by their second coordinate. The same half-space independence statement remains valid after restricting both sigma-fields to this layer, so the joint law factors over second-coordinate layers. Continue through coordinates `3,...,d`. At the end, the joint law factors over individual sites. Strict stationarity gives identical one-site marginals. Hence every finite subset of `4Z^d` is an independent identically distributed family. ∎

#### Corollary 4.2 — positive entropy density for every nondegenerate finite-state candidate

Suppose the alphabet `A` is finite and the one-site law is nondegenerate. Then `H(X_0)>0`. For the box

\[
B_N=[-N,N]^d\cap\mathbb Z^d,
\]

entropy monotonicity and Lemma 4.1 give

\[
H(X_{B_N})
\ge
H\bigl(X_{B_N\cap 4\mathbb Z^d}\bigr)
=
|B_N\cap4\mathbb Z^d|\,H(X_0).
\]

Consequently

\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\frac{H(X_{B_N})}{|B_N|}
\ge
4^{-d}H(X_0)>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus every nondegenerate finite-state solution of D0038 would necessarily have **strictly positive entropy density**.

### 4.3 Tail determinism gives zero finite-erasure entropy, not zero rate

If the same field is measurable with respect to its own tail, then for every finite `F subset Z^d`,

\[
\mathcal T_X
\subseteq
\sigma(X_k:k\notin F),
\]

and every `X_j`, hence every finite vector `X_F`, is `T_X`-measurable. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H(X_F\mid X_{F^c})=0
\qquad\text{for every finite }F.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equations (4.1) and (4.2) are compatible. The correct information-theoretic target is therefore not a zero-entropy contradiction. It is a much more structured object:

> a positive-entropy-density finite-alphabet field with zero finite-erasure entropy and exact directional independence across every one-layer-separated coordinate cut.

This is naturally a stationary infinite error-correcting / secret-sharing code problem.

That reformulation is the useful B2 output.

## 5. B1 — change category: an exact no-go theorem for finite-field linear symbolic dynamics

The most obvious finite-state attempts after seeing Bradley's shell parity identity are linear/parity constructions: Ledrappier-type algebraic shifts, linear cellular automata, plaquette constraints, or a Haar measure on a finite-field subshift. This run rules out that entire scalar finite-field class.

### Theorem 5.1 — no nontrivial proper linear algebraic shift can have Bradley half-space one-dependence

Let `p` be prime and let

\[
V\subseteq\mathbb F_p^{\mathbb Z^d}
\]

be a closed shift-invariant linear subspace. Let `mu_V` be Haar measure on `V`, and let `X` be the coordinate process under `mu_V`.

If `V` is nontrivial and proper, then `X` is **not** lattice-halfspace one-dependent in Bradley's sense.

Equivalently, among scalar `F_p` Haar linear shifts, a lattice-halfspace-one-dependent field is either the full iid Bernoulli shift or a degenerate field. In particular no nondegenerate two-state linear/parity construction can solve D0038.

#### Proof

Write

\[
R=\mathbb F_p[u_1^{\pm1},\ldots,u_d^{\pm1}].
\]

A Laurent polynomial

\[
f=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z^d}c_a u^a
\]

with finite support defines the character

\[
\chi_f(x)
=
\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{p}
\sum_a c_a x_a\right),
\qquad x\in V.
\]

The annihilator

\[
I=\{f\in R:\chi_f\equiv1\text{ on }V\}
\]

is a shift-invariant ideal, and the character group of `V` is `R/I`.

Because `V` is proper and closed, Pontryagin duality separates `V` from the full shift, so `I` contains a nonzero polynomial. Because `V` is nontrivial/nondegenerate, `I` contains no monomial: if `c u^a in I` with `c != 0`, then the coordinate character at `a` is identically one, so `x_a=0` for all `x in V`; shift invariance then forces every coordinate to vanish and `V={0}`.

Choose a nonzero

\[
f\in I
\]

having the **minimum possible number of monomials** among all nonzero elements of `I`. Since `I` has no monomial, `f` has at least two terms.

Hence there is some coordinate direction `i` in which two monomials of `f` have distinct `u_i`-exponents. Choose a Frobenius power

\[
q=p^r\ge4.
\]

In characteristic `p`,

\[
f^q
=
\sum_{a\in\operatorname{supp}f}c_a^q u^{qa}
=
\sum_{a\in\operatorname{supp}f}c_a u^{qa}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are no cross terms. Thus `f^q in I`, it has exactly the same number of monomials as `f`, and its distinct `i`-coordinate exponent levels are separated by at least `q>=4`.

Choose a cut between two adjacent occupied `i`-levels of `f^q`. Split

\[
f^q=L+R,
\tag{5.2}
\]

where `L` contains all monomials on the left of the cut and `R` all monomials on the right. Both are nonzero, and their coordinate supports lie in two Bradley half-spaces separated by more than the required one-layer gap.

Since `f^q in I`, in `R/I` we have

\[
[L]=-[R].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Moreover `[L] != 0`. If `L in I`, then `L` would be a nonzero annihilating polynomial with strictly fewer monomials than the chosen minimal-support `f`, contradiction. The same argument gives `[R] != 0`.

Therefore the nontrivial Haar character

\[
Z:=\chi_L(X)=\chi_{-R}(X)
\tag{5.4}
\]

is measurable with respect to the sigma-field generated by the left half-space coordinates, because `L` is supported there, and also measurable with respect to the sigma-field generated by the separated right half-space coordinates, because `R` is supported there.

If those two half-space sigma-fields were independent, every random variable measurable with respect to both would be almost surely constant. More explicitly, because `[L] !=0`, the character `chi_L` is a nontrivial character of the compact group `V`; Haar orthogonality gives

\[
E_{\mu_V}Z=0,
\qquad
E_{\mu_V}|Z|^2=1.
\]

But independence of the two sigma-fields and the two representations in (5.4) would imply

\[
E|Z|^2
=E[Z\overline Z]
=EZ\,E\overline Z
=0,
\]

a contradiction.

Thus `X` cannot be lattice-halfspace one-dependent. ∎

### Consequences and scope

This theorem rules out, in one stroke:

- every nontrivial two-state linear parity subshift;
- Ledrappier/three-dot-type Haar systems;
- finite-field plaquette parity constructions;
- scalar linear cellular-automaton spacetime measures that can be represented as proper Haar subshifts.

The obstruction uses **Frobenius dilation** to turn any finite local linear relation into the same nontrivial character visible on both sides of an arbitrarily wide coordinate gap. This is stronger than merely saying a particular parity rule fails.

It is nevertheless only a proper subclass theorem. A nonlinear finite-state field is not covered, so `E_finite_tail` remains `0`.

## 6. B19 — make Bradley's construction algorithmic and identify the exact finite-state bottleneck

B19 asks for the constructive procedure and the precise step at which global tail information must be carried.

Bradley's 1994 proof can be decomposed as follows.

### Stage A — a binary tail-deterministic half-space-one-dependent field

Use independent fair signs on all but one site of each expanding shell; impose one parity/product equation per shell so that an earlier sign is exactly decoded from the complete later shell. Every finite set is eventually surrounded by shells carrying enough parity equations to reconstruct the set. This stage is already finite-state.

What it lacks is translation invariance.

### Stage B — stationarize by an independent copy at every translation

For every translation/copy index `r in Z^d`, take an independent copy `X^(r)` of Stage A. At physical site `k`, collect one bit from every copy so that translating `k` merely permutes the copy labels.

This creates exact stationarity without destroying copywise half-space independence.

### Stage C — put all countably many copy bits into one coordinate

The real number (2.2) stores all copy bits as ternary digits. Bradley's equation (3.2) says no layer information is lost:

\[
\sigma(Y_k)
=
\sigma(B_{k,1},B_{k,2},\ldots),
\tag{6.1}
\]

where the `B_{k,j}` are countably many independent fair signs coming from distinct independent copies.

This is exactly what lets the tail reconstruction of every individual copy survive after stationarization.

### Proposition 6.1 — the Bradley copy-stacking stationarization cannot be made finite-state by a sitewise invertible recoding

Let `B_1,B_2,...` be independent fair bits. There is no finite-valued random variable `Y`, taking at most `M<infinity` values, such that

\[
\sigma(B_1,B_2,\ldots)\subseteq\sigma(Y).
\tag{6.2}
\]

In particular there is no finite-state replacement for Bradley's single-site ternary encoding that preserves equation (3.2) for all countably many independent copy layers.

**Proof.** If (6.2) held, then for every `N`, the vector `(B_1,...,B_N)` would be a function of `Y`. Hence

\[
N\log2
=H(B_1,\ldots,B_N)
\le H(Y)
\le\log M.
\]

Taking `N>\log_2M` is impossible. ∎

The same conclusion follows atomically: `sigma(Y)` has at most `M` atoms, whereas the iid bit sequence generates arbitrarily fine partitions.

### Interpretation

This proposition does **not** prove D0038 impossible. It proves something narrower and more actionable:

> the exact 1994 stationarization cannot be finite-ized merely by replacing Bradley's real base-3 coordinate with a clever finite code while retaining sitewise recovery of every independent translated copy.

A successful positive construction must change the stationarization mechanism itself. It must distribute or recycle the copy/layer information spatially so that no single site has to decode countably many independent bits, while preserving exact strict stationarity and exact independence across every separated coordinate half-space.

That is the constructive bottleneck.

## 7. Additional failed routes and obstruction tests

### 7.1 Shared row/column latent bits fail immediately

A tempting two-dimensional model is

\[
X_{i,j}=A_i\oplus B_j
\]

with iid row and column bits. It is stationary and has many deterministic rectangle identities. But a coordinate half-space contains infinitely many sites from which it can recover nontrivial differences of the same transverse latent sequence. Opposite half-spaces therefore share nontrivial latent information; their sigma-fields are not independent. This is the same structural failure exposed abstractly by Theorem 5.1 for linear systems.

### 7.2 Reversible cellular-automaton spacetime

If one coordinate is interpreted as time and successive rows are related by an invertible finite cellular automaton, then distant rows can reconstruct the present and tail determinism is natural. But a future row is a deterministic function of a past row, so the two time half-spaces share their entire state. Exact half-space independence fails maximally.

### 7.3 Pure entropy contradiction

As explained in Section 4, this route is invalid. Positive entropy and bilateral tail determinism coexist in finite-state strongly mixing processes. The extra Bradley half-space independence, not entropy alone, must do the real work.

### 7.4 Linear parity codes

Theorem 5.1 shows this route is exhausted in the scalar finite-field/Haar setting. Any new positive attempt should be genuinely nonlinear rather than another finite local parity constraint in disguise.

## 8. What remains and the next attack

The source question is now compressed into a sharper design problem.

A positive D0038 solution must simultaneously realize:

1. **finite alphabet** and nondegenerate one-site law;
2. **positive entropy density**, forced by sparse iid sublattices;
3. **zero finite-erasure entropy**, forced by full spatial-tail measurability;
4. **exact independence across every one-layer-separated coordinate half-space**;
5. **strict translation invariance**;
6. a **nonlinear, non-sitewise-copy-stacking** reconstruction mechanism, because Theorem 5.1 rules out scalar finite-field linear shifts and Proposition 6.1 rules out direct finite recoding of Bradley's stationarization.

The nearest solved mechanism in the literature is Burton–Denker–Smorodinsky's finite-state strongly mixing bilaterally deterministic process, together with the Ornstein–Weiss bilateral-generator method. Those constructions show that finite-state positive-entropy tail reconstruction is possible; what they do **not** supply is exact half-space one-dependence in multiple lattice directions.

The next run should therefore use a completely different triple:

- **B21 — ask who else has this shape:** recover the Burton–Denker–Smorodinsky / Ornstein–Weiss coding architecture in enough detail to identify the exact operation that creates bilateral determinism, then test whether it can be localized to independent coordinate slabs;
- **B18 — say it in another language:** formulate D0038 as a stationary positive-rate zero-erasure code / secret-sharing system with exact cut independence; identify an entropy or matroid inequality that distinguishes this from ordinary bilateral determinism;
- **B20 — find why the algorithm fails:** prove the broadest possible no-go theorem for `Bradley translated copies + finite coordinate factor`, allowing noninvertible finite factors and spatially shifted decoding, and determine whether any finite-radius or finitary stationarization is impossible.

Counterexample/impossibility remains first priority. If those attacks fail, the most promising positive direction is an **equivariant hierarchical marker allocation** that spreads the countably many Bradley copy layers over space instead of over the digits of one site.
