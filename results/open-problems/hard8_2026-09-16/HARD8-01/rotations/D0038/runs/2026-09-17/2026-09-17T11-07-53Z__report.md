## 3. Exact blocker carried into this run

The cleanest form of the source conflict is still:

\[
\boxed{
I\!\left(
X_{\{k:k_i<m\}};
X_{\{k:k_i>m+2\}}
\right)=0
\quad\text{for every coordinate cut,}
\qquad
H(X_0\mid X_{B^c})=0
\quad\text{for every finite }B.
}
\]

The first condition says separated opposite coordinate halfspaces carry **exactly zero shared information**. The second says deleting *any finite set* leaves enough information at infinity to reconstruct a central symbol exactly.

The hard point is no longer to exhibit finite-scale secret sharing. Finite parity examples already show that it is easy to hide a secret from each individual share while letting several shares recover it jointly. The hard point is to make that synergy simultaneously:

- translation invariant,
- compatible across all scales,
- finite-alphabet,
- exact across every separated coordinate cut,
- and strong enough that the full infinite exterior reconstructs each erased finite block.

This run sought a global obstruction rather than another local coding gadget.

## 4. Method B23 — do what the proposer did not expect: try to force full-tail triviality

### 4.1 Literature bridge recovered this run

A much sharper neighboring theorem is available than the generic mixing comparisons used in the first three D0038 rotations.

Frank den Hollander and Jeffrey E. Steif, *On K-automorphisms, Bernoulli shifts and Markov random fields*, ETDS 17 (1997), 405–415, prove among other things:

- for general translation-invariant random fields, **Følner independence implies trivial full tail**;
- stronger implications such as Bernoulli `=>` Følner independence need a Markov/Gibbs-type assumption, and bilaterally deterministic Bernoulli shifts show why one cannot drop such structure blindly.

Scot Adams, *Følner Independence and the amenable Ising model*, ETDS 12 (1992), 633–657, introduced the relevant Følner-independence criterion and proved it implies finite determination/Bernoullicity in the amenable setting.

This is useful because a D0038 solution is the exact opposite of full-tail trivial: by tail determinism,

\[
\sigma(X_0)\subseteq \mathcal T \quad (\bmod 0),
\]

and nondegeneracy forces `\mathcal T` to be nontrivial. Thus any D0038 solution must live **outside** the Følner-independent class.

That is not a new theorem of this track; it is a direct use of the cited tail-triviality theorem to identify the right obstruction.

### 4.2 New route selected

The central candidate implication is now:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite alphabet}
+\text{ strict stationarity}
+\text{ Bradley lattice-halfspace one-dependence}
\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}
\text{Følner independence (or enough exterior decoupling for trivial full tail).}
}
\]

If this implication were proved, den Hollander–Steif would give an impossibility theorem for D0038 immediately.

This formulation is much more precise than the historical `D0038-A001` entropy heuristic. It does **not** say tail determinism forces entropy zero; that route was false. Instead it asks whether a finite alphabet upgrades exact independence across every separated coordinate cut to a global Følner-decoupling property.

### 4.3 Why the implication is genuinely nontrivial

There are two decisive warnings.

First, Bradley's 1994 construction already has strict stationarity, exact lattice-halfspace one-dependence and self-tail determinism. Therefore no implication of the displayed form can hold without using the **finite alphabet** in an essential way.

Second, den Hollander–Steif explicitly point to bilaterally deterministic Bernoulli shifts as counterexamples to stronger tail implications absent a Markov assumption. Thus ordinary strong mixing, Bernoullicity, or even very strong one-dimensional stochastic regularity is not enough by itself.

The only plausible new leverage is finite per-site information capacity combined with *multidirectional exact* cut independence.

## 5. Method B27 — swap the quantifiers in tail reconstruction

The tail condition contains a useful finite-alphabet compactness consequence, but the quantifiers have to be written correctly.

Fix a finite erasure set `B` containing the origin. Tail determinism gives

\[
H(X_0\mid X_{B^c})=0.
\]

Choose finite sets `D_R\uparrow B^c`. Because `X_0` has finite entropy and conditional entropy is continuous downward along increasing finite sigma-fields,

\[
H(X_0\mid X_{D_R})\downarrow H(X_0\mid X_{B^c})=0.
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\forall B\Subset\mathbb Z^d,\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\ \exists\text{ finite }D\subset B^c:
H(X_0\mid X_D)<\varepsilon.
}
\]

By strict stationarity, translating the same geometric decoder preserves its error/entropy law at every site.

This is the correct finite-decoder normal form. It is stronger operationally than simply saying “the tail knows the origin”: every target accuracy can be achieved using finitely many remote observed coordinates.

### 5.1 Why this still does not close the problem

The quantifier order is exactly where the proof fails. Tail determinism provides

\[
\forall B\ \forall\varepsilon\ \exists D(B,\varepsilon),
\]

but gives no source-visible bound on:

- the diameter of `D(B,\varepsilon)`,
- its cardinality,
- how far it reaches in each coordinate direction,
- how `D` changes as `B` grows,
- or whether the family of decoders can be chosen projectively/temperately across scales.

A decoder may therefore send its information through ever farther transverse regions, precisely evading a naive boundary-versus-volume entropy estimate.

A useful missing lemma would be a **tempered finite-decoder theorem** of the form: for a finite-state stationary field satisfying Bradley cut independence, an erased box `\Lambda_n` can be decoded to fixed accuracy using an exterior set whose relevant complexity grows subvolumetrically or whose information can be charged to a controlled boundary. Nothing recovered in the source or literature supplies this automatically.

### 5.2 Outcome of B27

**Outcome: exact quantifier normalization; no source-complete theorem.**

This tells the next run what uniformity must be proved and prevents the common mistake of replacing tail measurability by a uniform local decoder that the hypothesis does not actually give.

## 6. Method B11 — dualize the problem into an exterior-information cut bound

The prior D0038 reports already established two ingredients for a hypothetical nondegenerate finite-state solution:

1. a sparse sublattice can be chosen whose sampled variables are iid, giving positive entropy density; and
2. tail determinism makes every finite erasure exactly reconstructible from the full exterior.

For a box `\Lambda_n`, the second property implies for every larger finite box `\Lambda_R\supset\Lambda_n`:

\[
H(X_{\Lambda_n}\mid X_{\Lambda_R^c})=0,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
I(X_{\Lambda_n};X_{\Lambda_R^c})=H(X_{\Lambda_n}).
}
\]

The sparse-iid argument says that the right side is volumetric along boxes:

\[
H(X_{\Lambda_n})\ge c|\Lambda_n|
\]

for some `c>0` depending on the nondegenerate finite marginal and the chosen sparse residue class.

On the other hand, Bradley's hypothesis says that any *single sufficiently separated directional halfspace* has zero mutual information with an opposite halfspace containing the central box. Thus all of the volumetric information reconstructing `X_{\Lambda_n}` from infinity must be stored in genuinely **multi-directional synergistic correlations**.

This suggests a dual target inequality:

\[
\boxed{
I(X_{\Lambda_n};X_{\Lambda_R^c})
\le C|\partial\Lambda_n|\log|\mathcal A|+o(|\Lambda_n|),
}
\]

uniformly along a suitable `R=R(n)` or Følner exhaustion. Such an inequality would contradict the volumetric lower bound and solve D0038 negatively.

### 6.1 Exact obstruction to this inequality

There is no Markov separator in the source hypotheses. Information can be absent from each individual far side and nevertheless become visible when several sides are observed jointly. The finite-scale parity/secret-sharing examples from the previous run demonstrate that no plain Shannon subadditivity argument can turn individual-side independence into the displayed cut-set bound.

Therefore the missing ingredient must use **stationarity plus consistency across infinitely many translated scales**, not merely one finite box.

This is precisely where the Følner formulation appears natural: it asks whether enough approximate independence from complements emerges on increasingly invariant sets. The problem is thus now phrased as an asymptotic consistency question rather than a local entropy inequality.

### 6.2 Outcome of B11

**Outcome: a concrete dual inequality whose proof would settle the negative direction, but no proof.**

The route is useful because it specifies what “finite alphabet must matter” means quantitatively: a finite alphabet supplies only `O(1)` bits per site, so an impossibility proof needs to show that multi-directional synergy cannot transport `\Theta(n^d)` bits through a boundary of order `n^{d-1}` while preserving all Bradley cuts at every translated scale.
