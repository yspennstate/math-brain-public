## 1. Source target and current-status check

Primary source: Ruodu Wang, *Current Open Questions in Complete Mixability*, Probability Surveys 12 (2015), DOI `10.1214/14-PS250`, arXiv `1411.6190v3`, Section 2.5, Open Problem 5.

The vector-valued definitions are essential. For a semigroup `G` (in particular `R^d`), an `n`-tuple of laws is jointly mixable if a coupling exists on `G^n` supported on a constant-sum hyperplane. A law `F` is `n`-completely mixable when all `n` marginals are `F`. For `G=R^d`, if the common sum is `K`, the complete-mix center is `K/n`.

Open Problem 5 asks whether, for every convex set/body `C subset R^d`, the uniform law on `C` is `n`-CM for every `n >= d+1`; the source explicitly says it is equivalent to the `(d+1)`-CM case. The source records `d=0,1` as trivial/known and warns that in `d=2`, complete mixability of every one-dimensional projection is not by itself enough for vector-valued complete mixability.

Source readback used the current arXiv record (v3, last revised 2015-07-24) and the PDF pages containing Definitions 1–3 and Open Problem 5. Current bounded status check on 2026-09-16/17: Wang's current working-paper page still describes the 2015 paper as the open-question survey and specifically flags Open Problem 1 as solved and Open Problem 12 as partially solved, but does not flag Open Problem 5 as solved. Searches for `"uniform distribution on a convex set" complete mixability`, `convex body complete mixability`, and later vector-valued mixability papers did not retrieve a theorem covering every convex body. This is bounded negative evidence only, not a proof of openness or novelty.

Later literature checked includes Wang–Wang (2016) on joint mixability, Yin–Zhu arXiv:1706.05499v4 (2023 revision) on elliptical and multivariate elliptical families, Mao–Wang–Wang (2019) on sums of scalar uniform laws, and Koike–Lin–Wang (2024/2025) on joint mixability/negative dependence. ## 3. Counterexample/obstruction first: zero mean is far from enough

Before trying to prove the planar case, isolate what any barycentric argument must use beyond the centroid condition.

Let

`nu = (delta_{e1}+delta_{-e1}+delta_{e2}+delta_{-e2})/4`

on `R^2`. Its mean is zero. Nevertheless `nu` is **not** 3-CM with center zero. Indeed, a 3-complete mix supported on these four points would require every support triple `(x,y,z)` to satisfy `x+y+z=0`. But a zero sum of vectors chosen from `{+/-e1,+/-e2}` requires equal positive and negative counts on each coordinate axis, hence an even total number of nonzero vectors. Three is odd, so no such triple exists. Thus no coupling exists.

Conclusion: any proof of D0005 cannot merely say “the uniform law has centroid `g`, hence decompose it into `d+1` barycentric points.” Equal weights are the nontrivial constraint.

## 4. B18 — exact vector barycentric-decomposition formulation

### Proposition (vector empirical-measure formulation)

Let `mu` be a Borel probability measure on `R^d` with finite first moment and barycenter `m`. For any integer `n>=2`, the following are equivalent.

1. `mu` is `n`-CM with center `m`.
2. There exists a probability measure `Lambda` on ordered tuples `(x_1,...,x_n)` satisfying `sum_i x_i = n m` such that the average of the coordinate marginals is `mu`; after symmetrization, every coordinate marginal is `mu`.
3. `mu` is a barycentric mixture of equal-weight `n`-point empirical measures having barycenter `m`:

   `mu = integral [ (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n delta_{x_i} ] dQ(x_1,...,x_n)`,

   with `Q` supported on `sum_i x_i = n m`.

This is the source's scalar representation theorem rewritten in the vector-valued language of Definitions 1–3; the proof below does not rely on an unproved extension.

### Proof

`(1) => (3)`: take an `n`-complete mix `(X_1,...,X_n)` with each `X_i~mu` and `sum_i X_i = n m` almost surely. Given the realized tuple, form the empirical law `E_X=(1/n)sum_i delta_{X_i}`. For every bounded measurable `f`,

`E[ integral f dE_X ] = (1/n) sum_i E f(X_i) = integral f dmu`.

Thus `mu=E[E_X]`, a mixture of equal-weight empirical measures with barycenter `m`.

`(3) => (1)`: first sample a tuple from `Q`, then independently choose a uniformly random permutation `pi` of `{1,...,n}` and output `(x_{pi(1)},...,x_{pi(n)})`. The sum remains `nm`. By the assumed mixture identity, every coordinate has marginal `mu`. Hence this is an `n`-complete mix.

This proves the equivalence. `square`

For D0005 in dimension `d`, after translating the centroid of `C` to zero, the source target at `n=d+1` becomes exactly:

> Is `Unif(C)` a mixture of equal-weight `(d+1)`-atomic measures supported on tuples in `C^{d+1}` whose vector sum is zero?

For `d=2`, the atoms are centered triangles (degenerate triangles allowed):

`(delta_x+delta_y+delta_z)/3`, with `x,y,z in C` and `x+y+z=0`.

This formulation cleanly separates the easy Carathéodory fact (arbitrary barycentric weights) from the hard requirement here (all three weights exactly `1/3`).

## 5. B18/B4 — exact dual criterion for the complete planar case

Let `C subset R^2` be a compact convex body, translated so its centroid is zero, and let `mu_C` be normalized area measure. Define

`T_C = { (delta_x+delta_y+delta_z)/3 : x,y,z in C, x+y+z=0 }`.

By the proposition, `mu_C` is 3-CM iff it belongs to the closed convex hull of `T_C` in the weak topology. Since `C` is compact, the centered-triple set is compact and the map from a triple to its empirical measure is continuous, so `T_C` is compact.

Hahn–Banach separation therefore gives an exact functional form:

### Planar dual criterion

`Unif(C)` is 3-CM iff for every continuous `f:C->R` such that

`f(x)+f(y)+f(z) >= 0` whenever `x+y+z=0`,

one has

`integral_C f(x) dx >= 0`.

(The area normalization is positive and irrelevant to the sign.)

Proof: if `mu_C` is a mixture of centered equal-weight triples, integrate the pointwise triple inequality over the mixing law. Conversely, if `mu_C` is outside the closed convex hull, a continuous affine functional on probability measures separates it; such functionals are exactly integration against continuous `f`, yielding a separating `f` that violates the displayed implication after shifting the separating threshold to zero. `square`

This is the cleanest current all-planar target: prove one deterministic integral inequality for every continuous separator `f`, or produce one separator and thereby refute the conjecture in `d=2`.

## 6. B18 — cone-measure lifting lemma in the plane

This is the strongest new construction reduction in this run.

Translate a planar convex body `C` so its area centroid is the origin. Because the centroid lies in the interior, write `C` in polar form

`C = { r u(theta) : 0 <= r <= rho(theta) }`,

where `u(theta)=(cos theta,sin theta)` and `rho(theta)>0` is the radial function.

Define the boundary-cone probability measure `eta_C` as the law of

`V = rho(Theta) u(Theta)`,

where `Theta` has density proportional to `rho(theta)^2`.

### Lemma (cone lift)

If `eta_C` is 3-CM with center zero, then the uniform area law on `C` is 3-CM with center zero.

### Proof

Let `(V_1,V_2,V_3)` be a complete mix for `eta_C`, so each `V_i~eta_C` and `V_1+V_2+V_3=0` almost surely. Let `U~Unif[0,1]` be independent and common to all three coordinates, and set

`X_i = sqrt(U) V_i`.

Then `sum_i X_i = sqrt(U) sum_i V_i = 0` almost surely.

It remains to identify each marginal. In polar coordinates, normalized area on `C` has element proportional to `r dr dtheta`. Put `r=sqrt(U) rho(theta)`. Conditional on `theta`, `U` uniform gives radial density `2r/rho(theta)^2`; multiplying by the angular weight proportional to `rho(theta)^2` produces density proportional to `2r dr dtheta`, exactly normalized area measure. Thus every `X_i` is uniform on `C`. `square`

The centroid condition implies `eta_C` itself has mean zero:

`integral_C x dx = (1/3) integral rho(theta)^3 u(theta) dtheta = 0`,

while

`E[V]` is the same vector integral divided by `integral rho(theta)^2 dtheta`.

So the full planar problem has been reduced to a one-dimensional boundary law **provided** one can prove the special cone measure `eta_C` is 3-CM. The four-axis counterexample above shows that mean zero alone is insufficient; the missing ingredient must exploit the fact that `eta_C` comes from the radial boundary of a convex body with precisely the `rho^2` angular weighting.

This is a genuine narrowing of the geometric mechanism, but it is only a sufficient route, not an equivalence: an area complete mix need not use a common radial factor.

## 7. B15/B4 — a symmetry-breaking subclass beyond simplices: every parallelotope

The old successful simplex proof is an orbit proof. To break that symmetry mechanism, use product structure instead.

### Theorem (parallelotope subclass)

For every `d>=1`, the uniform law on every `d`-dimensional parallelotope is `n`-CM for every `n>=2`. In particular, every parallelogram in `R^2` is 3-CM.

### Proof

It is enough by affine invariance to treat the cube `[0,1]^d`. For each coordinate `j=1,...,d`, use the known one-dimensional fact that `Unif[0,1]` is `n`-CM: choose `(U_{1j},...,U_{nj})` with each marginal uniform on `[0,1]` and

`sum_{i=1}^n U_{ij} = n/2`

almost surely. Choose these complete-mix couplings independently across the coordinate index `j`. Define row vectors

`X_i=(U_{i1},...,U_{id})`.

For each fixed `i`, independence across `j` makes `X_i` uniform on the cube. Coordinatewise,

`sum_i X_i = (n/2,...,n/2)`

almost surely. Hence the cube law is `n`-CM. Any parallelotope is an invertible affine image of the cube; applying the same affine map to every coordinate preserves identical uniform marginals and makes the sum constant. `square`

This family is genuinely outside the order-3 simplex orbit mechanism: a generic parallelogram has no affine automorphism cyclically permuting three sectors/vertices in the required way. It is nevertheless a proper subclass, so it does **not** move `d_all`.

More generally, the same coordinatewise argument proves: if probability laws `mu_1,...,mu_k` are all `n`-CM, then their product law is `n`-CM. This may be useful for product convex bodies when the factors have source-valid `n`-CM constructions.
