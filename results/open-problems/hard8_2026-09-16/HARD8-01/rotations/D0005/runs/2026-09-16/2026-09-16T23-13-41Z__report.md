## 4. B21 — exact two-marginal aggregation / midpoint equivalence

### Proposition 4.1 (3-CM iff a reflected law is a two-copy sum law)

Let `mu` be a probability law on `R^d` with finite mean `m`. Then the following are equivalent.

1. `mu` is 3-completely mixable with center `m`.
2. There exists a coupling `(Y,Z)` with `Y ~ mu` and `Z ~ mu` such that

   `Y + Z ~ (x -> 3m - x)_# mu`.

After translating `m` to zero, this becomes simply

`Y + Z ~ (-Id)_# mu`.

Equivalently, in aggregation-set notation,

`(-Id)_# mu in D_2(mu,mu)`

for the centered law.

#### Proof

If `(X_1,X_2,X_3)` is a 3-complete mix, then each `X_i ~ mu` and

`X_1+X_2+X_3 = 3m`

almost surely. Put `Y=X_1`, `Z=X_2`. Then

`Y+Z = 3m-X_3`,

so the sum law is the image of `mu` under `x -> 3m-x`.

Conversely, suppose `(Y,Z)` is a coupling of two copies of `mu` whose sum has that image law. Define

`X_1=Y`, `X_2=Z`, `X_3=3m-Y-Z`.

By the assumed sum law, `X_3 ~ mu`, and the three variables sum identically to `3m`. Hence they form a 3-complete mix. `square`

### Corollary 4.2 (random-chord formulation for the planar convex-body problem)

Let `C subset R^2` be a convex body with centroid translated to zero, and let `mu_C=Unif(C)`. Then `mu_C` is 3-CM iff there exists a self-coupling `(Y,Z)` of `mu_C` such that

`Y+Z ~ Unif(-C)`.

Equivalently, its midpoint

`M=(Y+Z)/2`

must be uniform on `-C/2` while each endpoint is uniform on `C`.

Thus the entire planar source problem can be phrased as:

> Does every centered planar convex body admit a random chord whose two endpoints are each area-uniform on the body and whose midpoint is area-uniform on the reflected half-body?

This is not just a change of notation. It removes one marginal and places the problem inside the source's aggregation-set framework. Open Problem 12 in the same source asks generally which sum laws lie in an aggregation set. D0005 in dimension two is therefore a very special self-marginal `D_2` membership problem, not an arbitrary `D_3` problem.

The 2024 Komisarski–Labuschagne theorem is the nearest recovered result of the same broad shape: it can make a sample mean follow any bounded target distribution after choosing a specially designed common marginal. D0005 is harder in exactly the missing place: the common marginal is prescribed in advance and must itself be the uniform law on `C`.

## 5. B11 — weak closure: the planar problem reduces to rational polygons

The next step is topological rather than combinatorial.

### Lemma 5.1 (weak closure of fixed-`n` complete mixability on a common compact set)

Fix `n`. Let `mu_k` be probability laws supported in one compact set `K subset R^d`, with `mu_k => mu` weakly. Assume every `mu_k` is `n`-CM. Then `mu` is `n`-CM.

#### Proof

Let `m_k` be the mean of `mu_k`. Compact support makes the coordinate functions bounded on `K`, hence weak convergence gives `m_k -> m`, the mean of `mu`.

For each `k`, choose an `n`-complete-mix coupling `Pi_k` on `K^n`. The space of probability measures on compact `K^n` is compact in the weak topology, so along a subsequence `Pi_k => Pi`.

Every coordinate projection is continuous, hence every coordinate marginal of `Pi` is `mu`. Let

`S(x_1,...,x_n)=x_1+...+x_n`.

The pushforward `S_# Pi_k` is the point mass `delta_{n m_k}`. Continuity of `S` and weak convergence imply

`S_# Pi = lim S_# Pi_k = delta_{n m}`.

Therefore `Pi` is an `n`-complete mix for `mu`. `square`

The common-compact-set assumption can be weakened to the usual tightness/uniform-integrability hypotheses, but the compact version is exactly what is needed here.

### Corollary 5.2 (rational polygon reduction for `d=2`)

The following statements are equivalent.

1. Every planar convex body has a 3-CM uniform law.
2. Every convex polygon in `R^2` has a 3-CM uniform law.
3. Every convex polygon with rational vertices has a 3-CM uniform law.

Lower-dimensional convex sets reduce to the already-known interval/singleton cases, so it is enough to discuss bodies with positive area.

#### Proof

`1 => 2 => 3` is immediate.

For `3 => 1`, let `C` be any planar convex body. Choose rational convex polygons `P_k` converging to `C` in Hausdorff distance while all lie in a fixed large compact ball. For convex bodies, Hausdorff convergence implies convergence of the indicator functions almost everywhere away from the boundary; convex-body boundaries have planar Lebesgue measure zero. Hence normalized area measures satisfy

`Unif(P_k) => Unif(C)`.

By assumption every `Unif(P_k)` is 3-CM. Lemma 5.1 gives that `Unif(C)` is 3-CM. `square`

A useful contrapositive is exact:

> **If the planar D0005 conjecture is false, then some rational convex polygon is already a counterexample.**

This converts a continuum search over all planar convex bodies into a countable exact search without changing the source statement.

## 6. B11/B2 — a complete exact rational-LP hierarchy for finding a planar counterexample

The prior report proved the exact dual criterion: for a centered compact convex `C`, `Unif(C)` is 3-CM iff every continuous `f:C->R` satisfying

`f(x)+f(y)+f(z) >= 0` whenever `x+y+z=0`

also satisfies

`int_C f >= 0`.

For a rational polygon, that dual can be made finite at every resolution.

### Theorem 6.1 (finite rational piecewise-linear separator hierarchy)

Let `P subset R^2` be a rational convex polygon translated so its centroid is zero. Let `T_k` be any sequence of rational triangulations of `P` with mesh tending to zero, and let `V_k` be the continuous functions that are affine on every triangle of `T_k`.

For each `k`, consider the normalized feasibility/optimization problem

`min (1/area(P)) int_P f`

over `f in V_k`, subject to

1. `f(x)+f(y)+f(z) >= 0` for all `x,y,z in P` with `x+y+z=0`, and
2. `|f(v)| <= 1` at every triangulation vertex `v`.

Then:

(a) each level is a **finite rational linear program**;

(b) if some level has negative optimum, its rational nodal optimizer is an exact continuous separator and proves `Unif(P)` is not 3-CM;

(c) if `Unif(P)` is not 3-CM, then some finite level has negative optimum.

Hence failure of 3-complete mixability for a fixed rational polygon has a finite exact rational LP certificate.

#### Proof of (a): finite reduction of all centered triples

Fix three triangles `A,B,D` of `T_k`. Parameterize a centered triple by `x in A`, `y in B`, and `z=-x-y in D`. The feasible set

`Q_{A,B,D} = {(x,y): x in A, y in B, -x-y in D}`

is a compact rational polytope (possibly empty). Because `f` is affine on each of `A,B,D`,

`L_f(x,y)=f(x)+f(y)+f(-x-y)`

is affine on `Q_{A,B,D}`. Therefore `L_f >= 0` throughout this polytope iff it is nonnegative at all vertices of the polytope. There are finitely many triangle triples and finitely many vertices for each. Rational input triangles give rational polytope vertices, and evaluating a piecewise-affine function there is rational-linear in its nodal values.

The area integral of `f` is also a rational-linear functional of the nodal values, since on each rational triangle it is the triangle area times the mean of its three vertex values. The nodal normalization constraints are rational. Thus the level is a finite rational LP.

#### Proof of (b)

A negative feasible objective is exactly a continuous function satisfying every centered-triple inequality but having negative uniform integral. The dual criterion from the previous report then excludes 3-CM. Rational LP data allow the separator to be archived with exact rational coefficients; no floating-point distance or approximate feasibility is needed.

#### Proof of (c): completeness of the hierarchy for refutation

Assume `Unif(P)` is not 3-CM. By the continuous dual criterion, there is a continuous separator `f` with

`f(x)+f(y)+f(z) >= 0` on every centered triple,

but

`I(f):=(1/area(P)) int_P f < 0`.

Continuous piecewise-affine functions on triangulations with mesh tending to zero are uniformly dense in `C(P)`. Choose `g in V_k` with

`||g-f||_infinity <= epsilon`.

Then on every centered triple,

`g(x)+g(y)+g(z) >= -3 epsilon`.

Set `h=g+epsilon`. The constant shift adds `3 epsilon` to every triple sum, so `h` satisfies the exact nonnegative triple constraint. Also

`I(h) <= I(f)+2 epsilon`.

Choose `epsilon < -I(f)/2`. Then `I(h)<0`. Finally scale `h` by a small positive constant so that all nodal values have absolute value at most one; feasibility and the sign of the integral are unchanged. Therefore the finite LP at that refinement has a negative optimum. `square`

### Corollary 6.2 (finite-certificate consequence for the full planar source question)

Combining Corollary 5.2 with Theorem 6.1:

> If Wang's Open Problem 5 is false already in dimension two, then there exists a rational convex polygon and a finite rational piecewise-linear function whose finitely many rational centered-triple inequalities constitute an exact certificate of failure.

Equivalently, there is an exhaustive exact-arithmetic semidecision procedure for **refuting** the planar conjecture: enumerate rational convex polygons, rational triangulation refinements, and solve the finite rational LPs. If the planar conjecture is false, this search eventually terminates with a checkable separator.

`reflected Unif(C) in D_2(Unif(C),Unif(C))`.

> construct, for every centered rational polygon `P`, a doubly-uniform coupling on `P x P` whose sum is uniform on `-P`; or produce one exact PL separator proving that such a self-coupling cannot exist.

## 9. Failed routes / honest limitations

1. **Zero mean is still insufficient.** The previous four-axis discrete example remains a valid warning; no argument may replace the equal-weight coupling condition by barycenter zero.
2. **Finite affine symmetry is not the general answer.** Generic convex bodies can have trivial affine automorphism groups.
3. **Simplex partitioning does not glue automatically.** Different piece centroids give different complete-mix centers.
4. **The rational-LP hierarchy is complete only for finding a negative separator.** A finite collection of nonnegative levels does not prove 3-CM; a proof of the positive planar theorem needs a construction, a uniform dual argument, or a proof covering all refinement levels.
5. **The rational-polygon reduction is dimension-two only in this report.** The weak-closure lemma is general, but the specific 3-variable-to-2-variable aggregation equivalence and centered-triangle PL hierarchy are tied to `(d,n)=(2,3)`.
6. **No novelty claim.** The two-copy sum equivalence is elementary and may be folklore. The LP/refinement consequence was not found in the bounded literature search, but a search miss is not a novelty certificate.
