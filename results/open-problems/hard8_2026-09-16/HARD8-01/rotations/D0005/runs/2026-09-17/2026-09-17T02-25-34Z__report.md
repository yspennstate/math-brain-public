## 3. Mathematical idea before any computation

The previous positive theorem works for every planar trapezoid because, in a slicing direction parallel to its bases, both the slice width `w(y)` and slice midpoint `c(y)` are affine in the slice coordinate. A scalar complete mix with constant `sum Y_i` therefore automatically makes both `sum w(Y_i)` and `sum c(Y_i)` deterministic, after which the exact uniform-interval joint-mixability criterion closes the horizontal fibers.

The simplest generic quadrilateral introduces exactly one new geometric feature: a **hinge**. After affine normalization, one endpoint of a horizontal slice changes supporting edge at a vertex. Consequently `c(y)` is affine plus a nonzero multiple of `(y-t)_+`.

B5 asks whether the trapezoid coupling can be continuously deformed through this hinge. B12 asks what additional statistic would have to be conserved. The answer is unexpectedly rigid: if horizontal conditional means remain local to their own slice, then both `sum Y_i` and `sum (Y_i-t)_+` would have to be constant. An atomless marginal with mass on both sides of `t` admits no such three-coupling. Thus the trapezoid two-stage mechanism fails *immediately* when a genuine hinge appears.

B27 then says what a successful proof must do instead: it must let the conditional horizontal mean of coordinate `i` depend genuinely on the **other two slice coordinates**. This produces an exact bounded martingale-defect equation rather than another vague statement that the fiber lift is difficult.

No code was written: the claims are theorem-sized and the complete proofs are on the page.

## 4. B5 — deform a trapezoid through a one-parameter generic quadrilateral family

Any ordered convex quadrilateral is affinely equivalent to

`Q(p,q) = conv{ A=(0,0), B=(1,0), C=(p,q), D=(0,1) }`,

with

`p>0`, `q>0`, `p+q>1`.

The opposite pair `AB,CD` is parallel exactly when `q=1`, while `AD,BC` is parallel exactly when `p=1`. Hence `p!=1` and `q!=1` is the generic no-parallel-side case.

To see the first deformation away from a covered trapezoid, fix `p>0`, `p!=1`, and put

`q = 1 + epsilon`, `epsilon > 0`.

At `epsilon=0` the top and bottom bases are parallel and the previous trapezoid theorem applies. For every `epsilon>0`, neither pair of opposite sides is parallel.

For `q>1`, horizontal slices have right and left endpoints

`r(y) = 1 + ((p-1)/q) y`,

and

`l(y) = 0` for `0<=y<=1`,

`l(y) = (p/(q-1))(y-1)` for `1<=y<=q`.

Write

`alpha=(p-1)/q`, `beta=p/(q-1)`, `h(y)=(y-1)_+`.

Then on the whole vertical range

`l(y)=beta h(y)`,

`w(y)=r(y)-l(y)=1+alpha y-beta h(y)`,

`c(y)=(r(y)+l(y))/2 = 1/2 + (alpha/2)y + (beta/2)h(y)`.        (4.1)

The area is `(p+q)/2`. The vertical marginal of a uniform point in `Q(p,q)` has density proportional to `w(y)`. This density is atomless, is strictly positive on the interiors of both `(0,1)` and `(1,q)`, and vanishes only at the top endpoint. Thus it gives positive probability to both sides of the hinge `t=1`.

For `q<1`, the same phenomenon occurs with the hinge at `t=q`: below `q` the right endpoint lies on `BC`, above `q` it lies on `CD`, and the midpoint is an affine function of `y` plus a nonzero multiple of `(y-q)_+`. The argument below is therefore not tied to the sign of the deformation.

The key point is already visible in (4.1): the perturbation is not a small additional affine term. The coefficient `beta=p/(q-1)` is singular as `q downarrow 1`, while the cap on which the hinge is active shrinks. The *body* varies continuously to a trapezoid, but the local slice formula changes category.

## 5. B12 — single-hinge rigidity lemma

### Lemma 5.1 — two pathwise conserved statistics force an atom

Let `nu` be an atomless probability law on an interval, and let `t` lie strictly inside its essential support in the sense that

`nu((−infinity,t))>0` and `nu((t,infinity))>0`.

There do not exist identically distributed random variables

`Y_1,Y_2,Y_3 ~ nu`

and constants `A,B` such that simultaneously

`Y_1+Y_2+Y_3=A` a.s.                                         (5.1)

and

`(Y_1-t)_+ + (Y_2-t)_+ + (Y_3-t)_+ = B` a.s.                 (5.2)

#### Proof

Because the constraints and marginals are invariant under coordinate permutations, randomly permute the coordinates and assume without loss that the coupling is exchangeable.

Let

`K = #{ i : Y_i>t }`.

Atomlessness gives `P(Y_i=t)=0`. Taking expectations in (5.2),

`B = 3 E[(Y-t)_+] > 0`,

so `K=0` is impossible.

If `K=1`, the unique variable above `t` must satisfy

`Y_i-t=B`,

hence it equals the deterministic value `t+B`. By exchangeability each coordinate has probability `P(K=1)/3` of being that unique upper variable. Since `nu` is atomless, this forces

`P(K=1)=0`.

If `K=2`, write the two upper coordinates as `t+u` and `t+v`. Equation (5.2) gives `u+v=B`. Equation (5.1) then forces the unique lower coordinate to equal

`A - 2t - B`,

again a deterministic value. Exchangeability and atomlessness force

`P(K=2)=0`.

The only remaining possibility is `K=3` almost surely. But then every marginal lies above `t` almost surely, contradicting `nu((−infinity,t))>0`.

Therefore no such coupling exists. QED.

### Corollary 5.2 — no locally barycentric slice lift for a genuine quadrilateral hinge

Let `(X_i,Y_i)`, `i=1,2,3`, be a putative 3-complete mix of `Unif(Q(p,q))`, and suppose the vertical coordinates form a complete mix,

`sum_i Y_i = 3 mu_Y` a.s.

Assume additionally that the conditional horizontal mean remains local to its own slice:

`E[X_i | Y_1,Y_2,Y_3] = c(Y_i)` a.s. for every `i`.            (5.3)

Then such a coupling cannot exist whenever the chosen slicing direction has a genuine one-hinge midpoint function as in (4.1).

Indeed, the complete-mix condition also requires

`sum_i X_i = 3 mu_X` a.s.

Taking conditional expectation given the whole vertical tuple and using (5.3),

`sum_i c(Y_i) = 3 mu_X` a.s.                                  (5.4)

For `q>1`, substitute (4.1). Since `sum_i Y_i` is already constant and `beta!=0`, equation (5.4) forces

`sum_i (Y_i-1)_+ = constant` a.s.

Lemma 5.1 gives a contradiction because the vertical marginal is atomless with positive mass on both sides of 1. The `q<1` branch is identical with hinge `t=q`.

QED.

### What this rules out

The trapezoid proof from the preceding positive run uses a stronger property than (5.3): conditional on the entire vertical tuple, the `i`th horizontal marginal is the original uniform law on the full slice `I(Y_i)`. Its conditional mean is therefore exactly `c(Y_i)`. Corollary 5.2 proves that this successful trapezoid architecture cannot survive a genuine quadrilateral hinge.

More generally, suppose only that `E[X_i | Y_1,Y_2,Y_3]` is a function of `Y_i` alone. By the tower property and the fact that a uniform point in the body has

`E[X_i | Y_i=y]=c(y)`,

that one-variable function must equal `c(Y_i)`. Hence **any successful 3-mix of a generic quadrilateral must have genuinely nonlocal conditional horizontal means**: the mean horizontal placement of one point must react to the other two slice coordinates.

This is the precise obstruction that the previous phrase "the slice midpoint is only piecewise affine" was missing.

## 6. B27 — swap the construction order: the exact nonlocal defect equation

Instead of first choosing a scalar complete mix and then demanding a local uniform interval mix on every realized slice tuple, define the horizontal conditional-mean correction

`delta_i(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3) := E[X_i | Y_1,Y_2,Y_3] - c(Y_i)`.

Every genuine vector complete mix must satisfy three exact requirements.

### 6.1 Marginal preservation

Because `E[X_i|Y_i]=c(Y_i)`, the tower property gives

`E[delta_i | Y_i] = 0` a.s.                                  (6.1)

Thus the correction may depend on the other two coordinates, but it must average away on every fixed own slice.

### 6.2 Fiber support

Since `X_i` always lies in the slice interval

`[l(Y_i),r(Y_i)]`

whose midpoint is `c(Y_i)` and width is `w(Y_i)`, conditional expectation cannot leave that interval. Hence

`|delta_i| <= w(Y_i)/2` a.s.                                  (6.2)

### 6.3 Exact defect conservation

The horizontal complete-mix identity gives

`sum_i delta_i = 3 mu_X - sum_i c(Y_i)`.                      (6.3)

For `q>1`, use (4.1) and the vertical identity `sum_i Y_i=3mu_Y`. Since

`mu_X = E[c(Y)] = 1/2 + (alpha/2)mu_Y + (beta/2)E[h(Y)]`,

we obtain the exact formula

`sum_i delta_i`
` = (beta/2) * ( 3 E[h(Y)] - sum_i h(Y_i) )`,                 (6.4)

with `h(y)=(y-1)_+`.

Equation (6.4) is the first clean positive target beyond trapezoids. The hinge fluctuation is *not* to be conserved; it has to be redistributed among the three horizontal conditional means through functions satisfying the martingale constraints (6.1) and the sharp fiber bounds (6.2).

This makes the missing ingredient testable. A future positive proof for generic quadrilaterals must first produce a vertical 3-mix and bounded nonlocal corrections solving (6.1)-(6.4), and then lift those conditional means to full conditional slice distributions whose `Y_i`-averages are the exact uniform slice laws.

### 6.4 Hilbert-space version of the mean-level obstruction

Fix any chosen vertical 3-mix and work in `L^2` of its coupling. Put

`H_i=L^2(sigma(Y_i))`,

`K_i={g: E[g|Y_i]=0}=H_i^perp`.

Then

`closure(K_1+K_2+K_3) = (H_1 intersect H_2 intersect H_3)^perp`. (6.5)

This is the elementary orthogonal-complement identity obtained from

`(K_1+K_2+K_3)^perp = K_1^perp intersect K_2^perp intersect K_3^perp`.

Consequently, if one can choose a vertical complete mix whose three coordinate sigma-fields have only constants in common, then every mean-zero defect — including the right side of (6.4) — lies in the **L2 closure** of sums of three marginal-preserving corrections. This is only an approximation statement at the conditional-mean level. It does not give exact decomposition, the pointwise width bounds (6.2), or uniform conditional laws. I record it because it identifies a concrete structural property of the scalar mix that the next positive construction should seek, rather than asking for an arbitrary concave-density complete mix.

## 7. Counterexample-first consequence of B27: Delta-free sets give an exact source refutation test

The quantifier swap also yields a clean route in the opposite direction.

Center the body at its centroid, and call a measurable set `A subset C` **Delta-free** if there are no `x,y,z in A` with

`x+y+z=0`.

If `Unif(C)` were 3-CM and `(X_1,X_2,X_3)` were a centered complete mix, then every realized triple contains at most two points of `A`. Therefore

`3 mu_C(A) = E[ sum_i 1_A(X_i) ] <= 2`,

so necessarily

`mu_C(A) <= 2/3`.                                             (7.1)

Hence **any planar convex body with a Delta-free measurable subset of area proportion greater than `2/3` is an immediate source-level counterexample to Wang Open Problem 5 in `d=2`**.

Equivalently, if `mu_C(A)>2/3`, the bounded measurable dual function

`f = 2 - 3 1_A`

has negative integral, while on every centered triple at least one point is outside `A`, so

`f(x)+f(y)+f(z) >= 0`.

This is an explicit separating certificate.

This does not prove that such an `A` exists. It gives a sharply normalized counterexample target. Ghosal-Tsarev (arXiv:2607.07991, July 2026) use exactly the `Delta`-free terminology for `a+b+c=0` and apply Brunn-Minkowski ideas in symmetric regions. Their retrieved results do not produce the `>2/3` asymmetric convex-body witness required here, but the overlap is sufficiently exact that their failure/success mechanisms are now a high-value neighboring technique rather than a vague analogy.

## 8. Failed routes and what was learned

1. **Naive deformation of the trapezoid coupling fails.** It is not enough to perturb the vertical scalar mix continuously. As soon as a hinge appears, a local conditional-mean lift would force two pathwise conserved scalar statistics, which Lemma 5.1 forbids.
2. **Adding the hinge statistic as a second conserved quantity also fails.** B12 shows that this is not merely hard; for atomless slice marginals it is impossible. A positive proof must *redistribute* hinge fluctuation, not conserve it.
3. **The conditional-mean equation is not yet a complete coupling.** The Hilbert identity (6.5) only addresses approximate mean corrections and says nothing by itself about pointwise interval support or exact uniform conditional marginals.
4. **The Delta-free criterion is one-sided.** Absence of a `>2/3` Delta-free set is necessary for 3-CM but is not known here to be sufficient; general dual separators can be weighted rather than indicator-valued.
5. **No finite negative certificate was obtained.** I therefore did not manufacture a discretization artifact or archive a numerical LP result. The useful output of this run is the exact analytic obstruction and the new nonlocal target.
