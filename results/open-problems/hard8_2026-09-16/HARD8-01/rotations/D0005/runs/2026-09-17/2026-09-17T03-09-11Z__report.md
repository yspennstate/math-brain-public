## 3. (a) Exact statement that now blocks

Center a planar convex body `C` at its area centroid, let `mu = Unif(C)`, and define the zero-sum triple set

`E_C := { (x,y,z) in C^3 : x+y+z=0 }`.

The full `d=2` source target is exactly the existence, for every such `C`, of a probability measure `pi` supported on `E_C` whose three one-coordinate marginals are all `mu`.

The earlier dual-separator result can be normalized into the following equivalent blocker. Define the continuous fractional-cover value

`tau_f(C) := inf { int_C g dmu : g:C->[0,infinity) continuous, g(x)+g(y)+g(z) >= 1 for every (x,y,z) in E_C }`.

The constant function `g=1/3` always gives `tau_f(C) <= 1/3`. The already-proved dual criterion says that the desired 3-complete mix exists exactly when no continuous separator has negative mean. Algebraically repackaging that criterion gives the exact obstruction

`boxed: prove tau_f(C) = 1/3 for every centered planar convex body C, or exhibit one C with tau_f(C) < 1/3.`

This is not promoted as a new theorem in this thinking-only run; it is a normalization of the existing dual criterion that makes the remaining obligation harder to evade.

For completeness, the normalization is elementary. If `g` is a fractional cover with `int g dmu < 1/3`, then `f=g-1/3` has nonnegative sum on every zero-sum triple and negative integral, so it is an existing-type dual separator. Conversely, suppose the existing dual criterion fails through a continuous `f` with `f(x)+f(y)+f(z)>=0` on every zero-sum triple and `int f dmu<0`. Let `m=min_C f<0` and define

`g=(f-m)/(-3m)`.

Then `g>=0`, every edge has `g(x)+g(y)+g(z)>=1`, and `int g dmu<1/3`. Thus the positive and negative routes meet at the same fractional-cover threshold.

## 5. B23 — do what the proposer did not expect: assume the planar conjecture is false

I spent this route trying to understand what a counterexample fractional cover would have to survive rather than adding another positive subclass.

### 5.1 Repeated-point zero-sum triples are too weak

For a planar convex body centered at its centroid, the classical centroid asymmetry inclusion implies `-C/2 subset C`. Hence each `x in C` participates in the zero-sum triple

`(x,-x/2,-x/2)`.

For an **integral** vertex cover `B` (so `A=C\B` is `Delta`-free), every `x in A` forces `-x/2 in B`. Therefore `-A/2 subset B`, and planar area scaling gives only

`mu(B) >= mu(A)/4`, hence `mu(B)>=1/5` and `mu(A)<=4/5`.

This is much weaker than the required `mu(B)>=1/3`. So the most obvious centroid geometry cannot by itself prove the `2/3` integral threshold, much less the fractional-cover threshold.

### 5.2 The full zero-sum fiber gives a sharper self-consistency condition

For `x in C`, define the zero-sum partner fiber

`D_x := C intersect (-x-C)`

and its normalized area

`q(x) := area(D_x)/area(C)`.

If `B` is an integral cover and `x notin B`, then for every `y in D_x`, the zero-sum triple `(x,y,-x-y)` must hit `B` in one of its last two coordinates. The involution `y -> -x-y` preserves area and preserves `D_x`, so integrating the cover inequality over `D_x` gives

`q(x) <= 2 mu(B)`.

Equivalently,

`{x in C : q(x) > 2 mu(B)} subset B`.

Thus any hypothetical integral counterexample with `mu(B)<1/3` must be self-consistent with the distribution of the covariogram-like function `q`. This is a stronger diagnostic than the repeated-point edge, but it still does not solve the problem: `q(x)` can become small near maximally asymmetric boundary points, so no uniform lower bound of the needed strength is available even in triangle-like geometries.

The integral-cover diagnostics above do not produce `mu(B)<1/3`, and the true dual obstruction may be fractional rather than integral. ## 6. B29 — add the boundary points: why the exact LP hierarchy has no known finite stopping level

`(Tg)(x) := sup { 1-g(y)-g(z) : y,z in C, y+z=-x }`.

Then cover feasibility is exactly

`g(x) >= max(0,(Tg)(x))` for every `x in C`.

For a rational polygon and a fixed piecewise-affine representation of `g`, each fixed cell-pair of the constraint is a parametric linear program; its value is piecewise affine on a finite polyhedral subdivision.
