## 3. Exact replay target: Haugland's nested graphs G1, G2, G3

Haugland's paper is unusually structured: a 21-point sevenfold unit-distance graph supplies 84 oriented unit vectors, 231 listed paths produce `G1`, two rotations of `G1` produce `G2`, and two copies of `G2` related by one further rotation produce the final `G3`.

The paper reports:

\[
|V(G_1)|=740,\quad |E(G_1)|=3985,
\]
\[
|V(G_2)|=1066,\quad |E(G_2)|=6264,
\]
\[
|V(G_3)|=2131,\quad |E(G_3)|=12530.
\]

The source's construction contains numerical separation checks. Because this project requires exact geometry, I rebuilt the listed construction inside an exact number field rather than using floating-point distance tolerances as acceptance criteria.

### 3.1 The exact cyclotomic field

Let

\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/84},\qquad K=\mathbb Q(\zeta).
\]

The checker reduces every element to the basis `1,zeta,...,zeta^23` using

\[
\Phi_{84}(x)=x^{24}+x^{22}-x^{18}-x^{16}+x^{12}-x^8-x^6+x^2+1.
\]

Thus equality in `K` is coefficientwise rational equality after reduction modulo `Phi_84`; no tolerance is used.

Following Haugland's notation, the exact radii may be written

\[
\frac{\alpha}{2}=\frac{i}{\zeta^{12}-\zeta^{-12}},\qquad
\frac{\beta}{2}=\frac{i}{\zeta^{24}-\zeta^{-24}},\qquad
\frac{\gamma}{2}=\frac{i}{\zeta^{48}-\zeta^{-48}},
\]

with `i=zeta^21`, and the 21 base points are

\[
P_j=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\zeta^{12j+21},\quad
Q_j=\frac{\alpha}{2}\zeta^{12j+7},\quad
R_j=\frac{\beta}{2}\zeta^{12j+35}\qquad(j\in\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z).
\]

Haugland's Table 1 gives 84 oriented differences among these points. The checker reconstructs all 84 exactly and verifies

\[
u\overline u=1
\]

in `K` for every one of them. Hence every edge generated by one of these differences is rigorously a Euclidean unit edge.

### 3.2 Exact `G1`

The checker contains all 231 paths from Appendix A of arXiv:2608.04542v4. There are exactly

\[
33\text{ paths of length }5\quad\text{and}\quad198\text{ paths of length }6.
\]

Starting at `A=0`, every listed path is accumulated in `K`. Every path ends exactly at

\[
B=-(u_{56}+u_{70})=i\sqrt3.
\]

The union of all path vertices contains exactly

\[
\boxed{740}
\]

distinct points. Defining an edge whenever the difference of two listed vertices is one of the 84 exactly verified unit directions gives exactly

\[
\boxed{3985}
\]

edges. Therefore the replayed `G1` is an exact 740-vertex, 3985-edge planar unit-distance graph.

### 3.3 Exact `G2`

Put

\[
c_-={-1+i\sqrt3\over2},\qquad c_+={1+i\sqrt3\over2}.
\]

The two copies are the exact rotations

\[
R_-(z)=c_-+\zeta^{-14}(z-c_-),\qquad
R_+(z)=c_++\zeta^{14}(z-c_+).
\]

Because `|zeta^14|=1`, these maps are isometries. The exact unit-direction set is invariant under multiplication by `zeta^{14}` and `zeta^{-14}`. The two 740-point copies have exact intersection size

\[
414,
\]

so their union has

\[
740+740-414=\boxed{1066}
\]

vertices. Exact difference lookup against the 84 unit vectors yields

\[
\boxed{6264}
\]

unit edges, matching the source count.

### 3.4 Exact `G3`

Extend to

\[
L=K(\sqrt{15}).
\]

The final affine isometry is

\[
F(z)=-1+\left({7\over8}+{i\sqrt{15}\over8}\right)(z+1).
\]

Its linear coefficient has exact squared modulus

\[
{49\over64}+{15\over64}=1.
\]

The original `G2` and its image under `F` intersect in exactly the fixed point `-1`, so the vertex union has

\[
1066+1066-1=\boxed{2131}
\]

points. The inherited copies contribute `2*6264=12528` edges. The checker verifies exactly, in the quadratic extension, that the two additional bridge pairs

\[
(B,F(B)),\qquad (1,F(1))
\]

have squared Euclidean distance one. Concretely, if a difference is written `A+B_1 sqrt(15)` with `A,B_1 in K`, the checker expands its squared norm as a `K`-part plus a `sqrt(15)`-part and checks coefficientwise that the latter is zero and the former is exactly one.

Thus the reconstructed graph has exactly

\[
\boxed{2131\text{ vertices and }12530\text{ explicitly certified unit edges}.}
\]

This is a new project-level exact replay of the latest Haugland construction. It does **not** improve its known five-chromatic status and therefore does not change the source lower bound. The checker need not assert that the chosen edge list is the complete induced unit-distance relation: for a valid finite unit-distance graph, it is enough that every listed edge has exact length one. The reported Haugland counts are nevertheless reproduced exactly by this construction.

## 4. Actual finite five-colour relation search

The previous HARD8-08 route needs nontrivial terminal relations that survive **five** colours. Haugland's construction is built from elegant relations that are strong for four colours, so the first disjoint finite search block was to test whether those same designated relations persist with five colours.

The bounded search used a fixed-seed TabuCol-style graph-colouring constraint search. It is used only to locate SAT witnesses. Every returned colouring is then checked independently against every edge and the requested terminal equality/inequality. Failure of the heuristic would **not** be called UNSAT, and no timeout is used as evidence.

### 4.1 `G1`: the source's four-colour forced-different pair becomes five-colour flexible

Haugland's first relation uses

\[
A=0,\qquad B=i\sqrt3.
\]

The new search tested both five-colour constraints

\[
c(A)=c(B)
\]

and

\[
c(A)\ne c(B).
\]

Both instances are SAT. Explicit 740-entry colour vectors are archived. The exact verifier checks each vector uses colours `0,...,4`, checks all 3985 unit edges, and then checks the requested terminal relation.

Search effort in the recorded fixed-seed run:

- `G1, A=B`: SAT witness after 1023 Tabu iterations;
- `G1, A!=B`: SAT witness after 675 Tabu iterations.

Therefore the pair `(A,B)` carries **no nontrivial binary colour relation in the five-colour solution space of G1**.

### 4.2 `G2`: the source's four-colour forced-equal pair also becomes five-colour flexible

Haugland's second relation uses the distinguished points

\[
m=(-1,0),\qquad p=(1,0),
\]

which are forced to be monochromatic in every four-colouring of `G2` in the source construction.

The new search again tested both five-colour constraints

\[
c(m)=c(p)
\]

and

\[
c(m)\ne c(p).
\]

Both instances are SAT, with explicit 1066-entry colour vectors archived and exactly checked on all 6264 edges.

Search effort:

- `G2, m=p`: SAT witness after 34271 iterations;
- `G2, m!=p`: SAT witness after 17997 iterations.

So this key four-colour equality gadget also has **no nontrivial binary relation at five colours**.

### 4.3 Consequence for the search program

This does not prove that no useful five-colour relation exists anywhere in Haugland's graphs. It does prove, by finite certificates, that the two designated terminal relations on which the published `G1 -> G2 -> G3` escalation is built do not simply lift one colour higher.

That is a concrete obstruction to the most immediate attempt to turn the 2026 structured construction into a six-chromatic graph. A five-colour attack must mine different terminal pairs, multi-terminal relations, or a larger coupled construction.

No UNSAT claim was produced in this run, so no UNSAT certificate is asserted. A certificate-producing external SAT solver (CaDiCaL/DRAT or equivalent) was not exposed in this runtime; accordingly I did not promote any heuristic non-solution to an impossibility statement.
