## 2. Exact two-terminal relation classification

The previous checkpoint proposed mining rigid 5-color terminal relations.  There is a useful exact simplification for **two** terminals.

### Lemma 2.1 — binary color-relation trichotomy

Let `G` be any finite graph with distinguished terminals `u,v`, and fix `k>=2`.  Define
\[
R_G(u,v)=\{(c(u),c(v)):c\text{ is a proper }k\text{-coloring of }G\}\subseteq [k]^2.
\]
The symmetric group `S_k` acts on colorings by relabeling colors.  Under the induced diagonal action on `[k]^2`, there are exactly two orbits:

- the equality orbit `Eq={(i,i)}`;
- the inequality orbit `Neq={(i,j):i!=j}`.

Hence `R_G(u,v)` is a union of these two orbits.  Therefore it is exactly one of
\[
\varnothing,\qquad Eq,\qquad Neq,\qquad Eq\cup Neq=[k]^2.
\]
If `G` is `k`-colorable, the empty case is excluded.  Thus every two-terminal `k`-color gadget either forces equality, forces inequality, or imposes no relation at all.  There is no more exotic binary color-symmetric relation to search for.

This is elementary but strategically important: for the 5-to-6 Hadwiger–Nelson step, a two-terminal search should explicitly classify candidates as **5-color equality gadgets** or **5-color inequality gadgets**.

## 3. Equality gadgets are equivalent to the six-color lower-bound target

A stronger reduction comes from the equality case.

### Theorem 3.1 — any nontrivial unit-distance 5-color equality gadget implies `chi(R^2)>=6`

Assume there exists a finite unit-distance graph `U` embedded in the plane, with two **distinct geometric terminals** `a,b`, such that:

1. `U` is properly 5-colorable; and
2. every proper 5-coloring of `U` satisfies `c(a)=c(b)`.

Write `r=|a-b|>0`.

Take `m` isometric copies of `U` and identify the second terminal of copy `j` with the first terminal of copy `j+1`, placing all terminal displacement vectors in the same direction.  The resulting chain is still a finite unit-distance graph.  In every proper 5-coloring its two endpoints have the same color, while their Euclidean separation is exactly `mr`.

Choose `m` so that `R=mr>=1/2`.  Take two copies of this chain, identify their initial endpoints, and rotate one chain relative to the other through an angle `theta` satisfying
\[
2R\sin(\theta/2)=1.
\]
Such an angle exists because `R>=1/2`.  The two final endpoints are now at unit distance.  Add their unit edge.  Each chain forces its final endpoint to have the color of the common initial endpoint, whereas the added edge requires the two final colors to differ.  Thus the resulting finite unit-distance graph is not 5-colorable, proving
\[
\chi(\mathbb R^2)\ge6.
\]
Possible overlaps between different copies do not invalidate the implication: a coloring of the geometric union restricts to a valid coloring of each copy, so the equality constraints remain valid (and an accidental unit loop/extra identification would only strengthen impossibility).

### Converse

If a finite planar unit-distance graph `H` is 6-chromatic, choose an edge-minimal 6-chromatic subgraph and delete one edge `xy`.  The resulting graph is 5-colorable, and in every proper 5-coloring its now-nonadjacent terminals `x,y` must have the same color; otherwise the deleted edge could be restored and `H` would be 5-colored.  Hence a two-terminal equality gadget exists.

Therefore the lower-bound statement `chi(R^2)>=6` is equivalent to existence of a finite 5-colorable unit-distance graph with a distinct terminal pair forced equal in every 5-coloring.

This gives a concrete computational reformulation: **mine existing 5-chromatic unit-distance drawings for a pair of vertices that is equal in every proper 5-coloring.**  Finding even one such pair would settle the lower-bound side immediately.

## 4. A second exact reduction through Parts's golden-ratio graph

The inequality case does not automatically raise the chromatic number, but Parts's two-distance graph turns one particular inequality gadget into a complete sufficient target.

Let
\[
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — a `phi`-separated 5-color inequality gadget would prove `chi(R^2)>=6`

Assume there is a finite unit-distance graph `U` with terminals `a,b` satisfying `|a-b|=phi`, such that every proper 5-coloring of `U` has `c(a)\ne c(b)`.  (If `U` itself is not 5-colorable, the lower bound 6 is already proved.)

Take Parts's 31-vertex 6-chromatic two-distance graph `G31`, whose edges all have lengths 1 or `phi`.  Retain every length-1 edge.  For each length-`phi` edge `xy`, attach an isometric copy of `U` whose terminals map to `x,y`.  The resulting finite geometric graph uses unit edges only.  If it had a proper 5-coloring, each copy of `U` would force the endpoints of every former `phi`-edge to have different colors, so the 31 original vertices would form a proper 5-coloring of `G31`, contradicting `chi(G31)=6`.

Thus existence of a 5-color `Neq` gadget at terminal separation `phi` also implies
\[
\chi(\mathbb R^2)\ge6.
\]

Parts's construction contains 56 `phi`-edges (28 in each `G16` copy).  Therefore, if a candidate gadget `U` has `s` vertices, the naive replacement construction uses at most
\[
31+56(s-2)=56s-81
\]
vertices before geometric overlap identifications, and at most 57 retained skeleton unit edges plus the unit edges contributed by the 56 copies.

This is a substantially narrower target than unconstrained 6-chromatic graph search: find **one** finite unit-distance graph whose two terminals are exactly golden-ratio distance apart and are forced different in every 5-coloring.

## 5. Exact reanalysis of Parts's 16-vertex terminal gadget

Parts publishes the exact edge lists of `G16`:

- 28 length-1 edges;
- 28 length-`phi` edges.

The paper proves that `G16` is 5-colorable and that terminals 1 and 16 have the same color in every proper 5-coloring.  I independently encoded exactly those published edge lists and ran deterministic exhaustive DSATUR backtracking.

### New checked property in this checkpoint — relation-edge-criticality

For **every one of the 56 edges** `e` of `G16`, deleting only `e` produces a proper 5-coloring satisfying
\[
c(1)\ne c(16).
\]
Moreover, in each returned witness the endpoints of the omitted edge receive the same color, as they must: if they differed, the witness would extend to the full graph and contradict Parts's equality relation.

So `G16` is edge-minimal not merely for ordinary chromatic number, but for its exact two-terminal equality relation:

> every single unit edge and every single golden-ratio edge is logically necessary to force `c(1)=c(16)` under five colors.

This was checked with exact integer graph data only; no floating-point coordinate test enters the coloring certificate.

### Corollary — the 31-vertex Parts graph is edge-critical 6-chromatic

Abstractly, `G31` is formed from two copies of `G16` sharing vertex 1, plus one extra edge joining their two copies of vertex 16.  It has 31 vertices and 113 listed edges (`56+56+1`).

- Full graph: each `G16` copy forces its terminal 16 to have the common color of vertex 1, while the cross edge requires those two terminal colors to differ, so no 5-coloring exists.
- Delete the cross edge: use a full 5-coloring of each `G16` copy with common color labels aligned.
- Delete any internal edge in one copy: use that edge's terminal-different witness in the damaged copy.  Use an ordinary full `G16` coloring in the other copy and relabel its colors so the shared vertex matches.  The intact copy's terminal 16 equals the shared color, while the damaged copy's terminal 16 differs from it; hence the cross edge is automatically proper.

Thus deleting **any one** of the 113 edges yields a proper 5-coloring.  Restoring the deleted edge can always be handled by a fresh sixth color at one endpoint, so the full graph is exactly 6-chromatic.  This gives an explicit edge-criticality certificate for the Parts skeleton.

A particularly important consequence for Theorem 4.1 is that **none of the 56 golden-ratio constraints can simply be omitted in this skeleton**: each is individually necessary for the known 5-color contradiction.  Any pure unit-distance conversion based on this exact `G31` therefore needs to simulate all 56 golden-ratio inequality constraints, unless additional unit-distance structure is added that changes the logic.
