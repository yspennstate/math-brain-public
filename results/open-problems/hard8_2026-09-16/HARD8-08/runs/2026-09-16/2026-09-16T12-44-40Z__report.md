## 2. General exact completion-as-hitting-set lemma

This run uses a simple but useful exact finite reduction.

Let `U=(V,E_1)` be a fixed graph consisting of edges already certified to be unit distance in a geometric point set. Let `C` be a finite collection of additional geometric pairs that one might wish to enforce as colour inequalities by attaching relation gadgets later.

For every proper five-colouring `c` of `U`, define its candidate-violation set
\[
V(c)=\{e=xy\in C:c(x)=c(y)\}.
\]
For `S\subseteq C`, form the abstract completion
\[
U+S=(V,E_1\cup S).
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — exact hitting-set criterion

`U+S` is not five-colourable if and only if
\[
S\cap V(c)\ne\varnothing
\]
for every proper five-colouring `c` of `U`.

#### Proof

A colouring `c` of `U` extends to a proper colouring of `U+S` precisely when no edge of `S` is monochromatic, i.e. precisely when `S\cap V(c)=\varnothing`. Negating this statement over all proper five-colourings gives the criterion. QED.

Consequently the inclusion-minimal sets among the family `{V(c)}` form a complete exact certificate for which candidate relations must be enforced. In particular, a singleton minimal violation mask `{e}` proves that **every** non-five-colourable completion must contain `e`: there exists a five-colouring of the unit base for which `e` is the only violated candidate relation.

This is stronger than edge-criticality after one particular completion has already been chosen. It characterizes all completions inside the fixed candidate family at once.

## 4. Exact theorem: all five-colour-killing completions are characterized

Let `U` be the 27-edge unit-distance graph obtained after scaling. Let `C` be all 23 remaining source edges, classified by geometric separation `sqrt(3)` or `2sqrt(3)`.

Define the following 18 candidate relations:

### Mandatory `sqrt(3)` relations (13)

\[
\begin{gathered}
i_0i_2,\ i_0i_4,\ i_0a_2,\ i_0a_4,\\
i_1i_3,\ i_1a_1,\ i_1a_3,\ i_2i_4,\\
i_3i_5,\ i_5a_1,\ i_5a_5,\ a_1a_3,\ a_1a_5.
\end{gathered}
\]

### Mandatory `2sqrt(3)` relations (5)

\[
i_2a_4,\qquad i_3a_3,\qquad i_3a_5,\qquad i_4a_2,\qquad a_3a_5.
\]

Call this 18-edge set `M`. Finally set
\[
p=i_1i_5,\qquad q=a_2a_4,
\]
both at separation `sqrt(3)`.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact completion characterization

For every subset `S\subseteq C`,
\[
\boxed{
\chi(U+S)\ge6
\iff
M\subseteq S\ \text{and}\ S\cap\{p,q\}\ne\varnothing.
}
\]

Therefore:

1. the minimum number of non-unit relation edges required in this exact 14-point geometry is
   \[
   \boxed{19};
   \]
2. there are exactly two minimum completions,
   \[
   M\cup\{i_1i_5\},\qquad M\cup\{a_2a_4\};
   \]
3. each minimum completion uses exactly
   \[
   \boxed{14\text{ relations at }\sqrt3\quad+\quad5\text{ relations at }2\sqrt3};
   \]
4. the three original length-1 triangle edges `Oh_0,Oh_1,h_0h_1` (which become `sqrt(3)` after scaling) are irrelevant to five-colour infeasibility inside this completion family.

### Proof / exact certificate

I exhaustively enumerated the proper five-colourings of the 27-edge unit base **modulo colour permutation** using a restricted-growth representation. The exact count is
\[
\boxed{50,261}.
\]

For each colouring the program computed its 23-bit violation mask `V(c)`. There are
\[
\boxed{21,177}
\]
distinct masks. Their inclusion-minimal members are exactly
\[
\boxed{19},
\]
with size distribution
\[
18\text{ singletons}+1\text{ two-element set}.
\]

The 18 singleton masks are exactly the edges of `M`. The unique two-element minimal mask is
\[
\{i_1i_5,a_2a_4\}.
\]

By Lemma 2.1, these minimal masks are a complete hitting-set characterization. Every non-five-colourable completion must hit every singleton, hence must contain all 18 edges of `M`, and must hit the final two-element mask, hence must contain at least one of `p,q`. Conversely either `M+{p}` or `M+{q}` hits every inclusion-minimal violation set, hence every violation set, so it is not five-colourable. QED.

The archived output includes an explicit normalized five-colouring witnessing **each** of the 18 singleton minimal masks and an explicit five-colouring whose only violated candidate relations are `{i_1i_5,a_2a_4}`. Thus the necessity direction is witnessed constructively rather than inferred from a solver timeout.

## 5. A 46-edge six-chromatic three-distance graph with a short counting proof

Choose
\[
S=M\cup\{a_2a_4\}.
\]
Then `U+S` has
\[
27+19=46
\]
edges on 14 vertices. It uses only the distances
\[
\{1,\sqrt3,2\sqrt3\}.
\]

The verifier exhaustively checked
\[
\alpha(U+S)=3.
\]
It also checked that neither `i_0` nor `a_1` belongs to any independent triple, while `i_0a_1` is a **unit edge** after scaling.

This gives a compact independent proof of non-five-colourability. If a proper five-colouring existed, every colour class would have size at most three. Fourteen vertices distributed among five classes therefore have class-size multiset
\[
(3,3,3,3,2).
\]
Since `i_0` lies in no independent triple, its colour class must be the unique class of size at most two. The same holds for `a_1`. Hence `i_0` and `a_1` would have to lie in that same small class, contradicting the unit edge `i_0a_1`.

An explicit proper six-colouring is

\[
\{i_0,i_3\},\quad
\{a_1,O\},\quad
\{i_1,h_0,a_2\},\quad
\{i_5,a_3,i_2\},\quad
\{a_5,h_1,i_4\},\quad
\{a_4\}.
\]

Each class was checked exactly against the 46-edge set. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\chi(U+S)=6.}
\]

This is **not** a new Hadwiger–Nelson lower bound: 19 edges still have non-unit lengths. Its value is that the relation burden of this tiny exact geometry is now completely understood.

## 6. Conditional pure-unit consequence

Let `F_{sqrt(3)}^(5)` denote a pure unit-distance graph with terminals at separation `sqrt(3)` forced different in every five-colouring, and define `F_{2sqrt(3)}^(5)` analogously.

Applying the already-proved substitution theorem to either minimum completion gives:

### Corollary 6.1

If both gadget species `F_{sqrt(3)}^(5)` and `F_{2sqrt(3)}^(5)` exist, then replacing the 14 `sqrt(3)` relations and 5 `2sqrt(3)` relations of a minimum completion yields a finite pure unit-distance graph that is not five-colourable. Consequently
\[
\chi(\mathbb R^2)\ge6.
\]

This uses 19 black-box gadget copies.

This is **not numerically better** than the earlier HARD8-08 six-terminal scaffold, which requires only six black-box relation gadgets if suitable gadgets are available at its two separations. The present result has a different advantage: the full completion polytope of a very small algebraic point configuration is known exactly, together with explicit necessity witnesses for every required relation. It is therefore a useful target for **coupled gadgets** that enforce several terminal inequalities at once rather than one independent gadget per edge.

## 9. Failed/limited route and next attack

The key limitation is now explicit: treating candidate relations as independent black boxes still leaves 19 non-unit inequalities in this small skeleton. The earlier six-terminal construction is cheaper if one can realize two independent gadget species, so merely substituting 19 separate gadgets is not the best use of this object.

The next attack should exploit the **joint structure** exposed by the minimal-mask certificate. In particular:

1. search for a single pure-unit-distance multi-terminal gadget that enforces several of the 18 mandatory inequalities simultaneously;
2. prioritize the five mandatory `2sqrt(3)` pairs, because every non-five-colourable completion in this geometry requires all five — a coupled construction that enforces this five-edge pattern would remove the rarest distance species in one step;
3. test whether Haugland/Parts five-chromatic cores admit terminal tuples whose five-colour relation is strong enough to realize one of these multi-edge masks, rather than mining pairs independently;
4. retain the Parts-509 pair-SAT route for the parallel assignment and avoid duplicating it unless exact certificate tooling becomes available here;
5. if a candidate coupled gadget is found, require exact algebraic unit-distance verification and a complete finite five-colour UNSAT certificate before claiming any lower-bound advance.
