## 5. B15 — break the historical relative-rotation symmetry on purpose

### Idea before code

Parts identifies the strongest small family then known as Type M:

\[
G=L\cup \rho S,
\]

with a large exact subgraph `L`, a smaller exact subgraph `S`, a shared rotation center, and the particular historical rotation `rho`. The paper says working Type-M constructions with other rotations were not known in that text. Rather than perturb `rho` numerically, the next route should enumerate **exact alternate rotations forced by cross-unit-edge equations**.

For fixed exact points

\[
\ell=(\ell_x,\ell_y)\in L,
\qquad
s=(s_x,s_y)\in S,
\]

write an unknown rotation as

\[
R(c,u)=\begin{pmatrix}c&-u\\u&c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad c^2+u^2=1.
\]

The demand that `ell` and `R(c,u)s` be one unit apart is

\[
\|\ell-R(c,u)s\|^2=1,
\]

which rearranges to the exact **linear** equation

\[
A_e c+B_e u=C_e,
\]

where

\[
A_e=2(\ell_xs_x+\ell_ys_y),\qquad
B_e=2(-\ell_xs_y+\ell_ys_x),\qquad
C_e=\|\ell\|^2+\|s\|^2-1.
\]

Thus two nonparallel proposed cross edges give a 2-by-2 exact linear system for `(c,u)`. The candidate is retained only if the resulting exact pair also satisfies `c^2+u^2=1`. After that, no proposed edge list is trusted: the complete strict unit-distance graph on `L union R S` is reconstructed from the exact coordinates.

This is a **search design, not a theorem claim** in this thinking run. In particular, the atlas generated by two independent edge equations is complete only for the finite class of rotations determined by at least two nonparallel selected cross-edge constraints. Parallel/degenerate systems form a separate branch and cannot be silently discarded.

### Why this is genuinely different

- It abandons the G14 lattice entirely.
- It does not stay at `rho` and merely delete vertices.
- It adds an entire rotated exact point set/batch, directly answering Parts's warning that single-vertex replacement tends to remain trapped in a local minimum.
