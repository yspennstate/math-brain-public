## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The previous checkpoint expected squared-distance 3 to be the first recursive G14 alignment class capable of escaping the earlier mod-3 coloring obstruction, because the pair-frame formula divides by 3. That expectation is wrong for a structural reason.

The right category is not just a graph family but a lattice over the ramified prime above 3 in `Q(sqrt(3))`.

Let

\[
R=\mathbf Z_{(3)}[\pi],\qquad \pi=\sqrt3,\qquad \pi^2=3,
\]

where `Z_(3)` consists of rationals whose reduced denominators are not divisible by 3. The scaled G14 seed lies in `R^2`. Reduction modulo `(pi)` gives

\[
R/(\pi)\cong\mathbf F_3.
\]

The new observation is that equal-length target vectors automatically contain exactly enough powers of `pi` to cancel the powers of 3 introduced by the pair-frame denominator. Thus the complete recursive pair-alignment mechanism preserves `R^2` not only for distance 13, but for **every integer squared-distance class of the seed**, including 3, 9, and 12.

That turns the old distance-13 obstruction into an all-distance obstruction for this entire G14 self-gluing architecture.

## 4. New theorem: all recursive equal-distance G14 pair-gluing remains 3-colorable

### 4.1 Valuation lemma

**Lemma.** Let `x,y in R` and suppose

\[
x^2+y^2=3^t s,
\]

where `t>=0` and `s in Z_(3)` is a 3-adic unit. Then

\[
x,y\in \pi^t R.
\]

**Proof.** Induct on `t`. The case `t=0` is immediate. Suppose `t>=1`. Reduce the identity modulo `(pi)`. Because `pi^2=3`, the right side is zero modulo `(pi)`, so in `F_3`

\[
\bar x^2+\bar y^2=0.
\]

The only squares in `F_3` are 0 and 1, and `-1=2` is not a square. Hence `\bar x=\bar y=0`. Therefore `x=\pi x_1` and `y=\pi y_1` for some `x_1,y_1 in R`. Substituting and using `pi^2=3` gives

\[
3(x_1^2+y_1^2)=3^t s,
\]

and cancellation in the domain yields

\[
x_1^2+y_1^2=3^{t-1}s.
\]

The induction hypothesis gives `x_1,y_1 in pi^(t-1)R`, hence `x,y in pi^t R`. ∎

### 4.2 Pair-frame closure

For a source ordered pair `A,B` in the G14 seed, put

\[
u=B-A,
\qquad r=\|u\|^2.
\]

For a seed point `X`, put `w=X-A`. An isometry sending `A,B` to a target pair `C,D` of the same squared distance has the exact pair-frame form

\[
T(X)=C+\alpha v+\varepsilon\beta Jv,
\qquad v=D-C,
\]

where `epsilon in {+1,-1}`, `J` is quarter-turn rotation, and

\[
\alpha=\frac{w\cdot u}{r},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{u\times w}{r}.
\]

**Lemma.** Assume `A,B,X,C,D in R^2` and `||u||^2=||v||^2=r`, where `r in Z_(3)` is nonzero. Then `T(X) in R^2`.

**Proof.** Write `r=3^t s=\pi^{2t}s`, with `s` a 3-adic unit. Applying the valuation lemma to the two coordinates of `u` and separately to those of `v` gives

\[
u,v\in \pi^tR^2.
\]

Since `w in R^2`, both `w dot u` and `u times w` lie in `pi^t R`. Consequently

\[
\alpha,\beta\in \pi^{-t}R,
\]

because division by `s` is allowed in `R`. But `v,Jv in pi^t R^2`, so

\[
\alpha v,\ \beta Jv\in R^2.
\]

Finally `C in R^2`, hence `T(X) in R^2`. ∎

### 4.3 Application to every G14 seed distance class

The exact scaled G14 seed used by this track has squared-distance histogram

\[
1:27,\quad 3:18,\quad 4:14,\quad 7:18,\quad
9:5,\quad 12:5,\quad 13:4.
\]

In particular every seed squared distance is an integer, hence belongs to `Z_(3)`. All seed coordinates have denominator at most 2 and therefore lie in `R^2`.

Starting from this seed, recursively perform any finite sequence of operations of the following kind:

- choose **any** seed pair `(A,B)`;
- choose **any** pair `(C,D)` already generated with exactly the same squared distance;
- add either orientation of the isometric image of the whole G14 seed determined by those pairs.

By the pair-frame closure lemma, every newly generated point stays in `R^2`. Induction therefore proves that **every point at every finite recursive depth lies in `R^2`**, regardless of whether the chosen seed squared distance is 1, 3, 4, 7, 9, 12, or 13.

### 4.4 Universal 3-coloring of the retained lattice

Define the residue map

\[
\rho:R\to\mathbf F_3,
\qquad
\rho(a+b\pi)=a\pmod 3,
\]

and color a point `(x,y) in R^2` by

\[
\kappa(x,y)=\rho(x)+\rho(y)\in\mathbf F_3.
\]

Suppose two points of `R^2` are at unit distance, and write their difference as `(x,y)`. Then

\[
x^2+y^2=1.
\]

Reducing modulo `(pi)` gives

\[
\rho(x)^2+\rho(y)^2=1
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_3.
\]

The squares in `F_3` are only 0 and 1. Therefore exactly one of `rho(x),rho(y)` is nonzero, so

\[
\rho(x)+\rho(y)\ne0.
\]

Hence the two endpoints receive different `kappa` colors.

**Theorem (new project result).** Every finite unit-distance graph contained in the recursively generated equal-distance pair-alignment closure of this scaled G14 seed is 3-colorable. This remains true if **all** exact unit-distance cross-copy edges are added.

Equivalently, this entire recursive G14 pair-gluing architecture can never produce even a 4-chromatic unit-distance graph, and therefore cannot produce the required non-5-colorable graph or improve the 509-vertex 5-chromatic benchmark.

This is a route-elimination theorem, not a statement about arbitrary unit-distance graphs and not a resolution of Hadwiger–Nelson.
