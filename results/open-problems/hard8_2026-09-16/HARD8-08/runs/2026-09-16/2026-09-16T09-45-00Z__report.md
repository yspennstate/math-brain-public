## 2. A geometric obstruction to the most direct six-colour lift

Haugland's four-colour construction suggests a natural higher-colour template: choose marked points whose pairwise constraints form \(K_{k+1}\) minus one edge, realize the constrained pairs by a small number of geometric distances, and attach forced-inequality gadgets for the non-unit distances. For \(k=5\), the first question is whether a \(K_6-e\) scaffold can use only two distances.

### Theorem 2.1 — no planar six-point \(K_6-e\) scaffold supported by only two distances

Let
\[
X=\{p,q,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}\subset\mathbb R^2
\]
be six distinct points. Suppose there are two positive numbers \(a<b\) such that every pair of distinct points except possibly the pair \(\{p,q\}\) has distance in \(\{a,b\}\). Then no such configuration exists.

#### Proof

Delete \(p\). The remaining five points
\[
X_p=\{q,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}
\]
have every pairwise distance in \(\{a,b\}\). They cannot form a one-distance set, because an equidistant set in \(\mathbb R^2\) has cardinality at most three. Hence \(X_p\) is a planar two-distance set of cardinality five.

Likewise
\[
X_q=\{p,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}
\]
is a planar two-distance set of cardinality five.

The classical classification of optimal planar two-distance sets says that every five-point planar two-distance set is similar to a regular pentagon. Therefore both \(X_p\) and \(X_q\) are regular pentagons (up to similarity).

They share the four distinct points \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\). Any three non-collinear vertices of a regular pentagon determine its circumcircle. Since a regular pentagon has no three collinear vertices, the two pentagons lie on the same circumcircle. On a fixed circle, four common vertices of a regular pentagon determine the fifth vertex uniquely: equivalently, the five vertices are a coset of the fifth roots of unity after choosing an orientation, and two such cosets sharing four elements are equal. Hence \(p=q\), contradicting distinctness.

Therefore the assumed six-point configuration does not exist. QED.

### Consequence

A direct five-to-six analogue of the Haugland marked-point scaffold cannot realize the fourteen constrained pairs of \(K_6-e\) using only unit distance plus **one** additional geometric distance. If this architecture is used, at least two non-unit distance classes (or some more elaborate non-pairwise propagation) are necessary.

This is a geometric obstruction, not a chromatic lower bound.

## 3. A positive three-distance scaffold: the regular hexagon

The preceding theorem suggests moving from two geometric distance classes to three. The regular unit hexagon gives the smallest obvious exact candidate.

### Definition 3.1 — five-colour inequality gadget at a prescribed separation

For \(d>0\), call a finite planar unit-distance graph \(F_d\) a **5-inequality gadget at distance \(d\)** if it has distinguished vertices \(s_d,t_d\) satisfying
\[
|s_d-t_d|=d,
\]
the graph is 5-colourable, and every proper 5-colouring satisfies
\[
c(s_d)\ne c(t_d).
\]

The distinguished vertices need not themselves be adjacent unless \(d=1\).

### Theorem 3.2 — two prescribed inequality gadgets would prove \(\chi(\mathbb R^2)\ge6\)

If 5-inequality gadgets exist for both
\[
d=\sqrt3\quad\text{and}\quad d=2,
\]
then there exists a finite planar unit-distance graph of chromatic number at least six. Consequently
\[
\chi(\mathbb R^2)\ge6.
\]

#### Proof

Take six marked vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_5\) at the vertices of a regular hexagon of side length one. Their pairwise distances are:

- \(1\) for the six adjacent pairs \(v_i,v_{i+1}\);
- \(\sqrt3\) for the six next-nearest pairs \(v_i,v_{i+2}\);
- \(2\) for the three opposite pairs \(v_i,v_{i+3}\),

with indices modulo six.

Keep the six unit edges. For every next-nearest pair place an isometric copy of \(F_{\sqrt3}\) with its distinguished terminals identified with that pair. For every opposite pair place an isometric copy of \(F_2\) with its distinguished terminals identified with that pair. The finite union is still a planar unit-distance graph: every edge came from a unit-distance graph under an isometry.

In any proper 5-colouring, each adjacent marked pair differs by its unit edge, each next-nearest marked pair differs by the \(F_{\sqrt3}\) property, and each opposite marked pair differs by the \(F_2\) property. Thus all fifteen pairs among the six marked vertices have different colours. Six pairwise different colours are impossible with only five colours. Hence the union is not 5-colourable. QED.

### Interpretation

This reduces one concrete lower-bound route to two sharply specified finite gadget searches. Haugland's current paper already provides a **4-colour** forced-inequality gadget at separation \(\sqrt3\), which is a useful structural precedent, but it does not provide either of the required **5-colour** gadgets in Theorem 3.2.

The exact verifier in Section 7 checks the regular-hexagon distance multiplicities over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\):
\[
1^2:6,\qquad (\sqrt3)^2:6,\qquad 2^2:3.
\]

## 4. Universal-rainbow sets force a rooted strong clique immersion

The previous checkpoint's saturation condition can be strengthened without enumerating colourings by extracting a necessary connectivity/flow property.

### Definition 4.1

Let \(H\) be \(k\)-colourable. A set
\[
S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_k\}\subseteq V(H)
\]
is **universally rainbow** if every proper \(k\)-colouring of \(H\) assigns pairwise distinct colours to the \(k\) vertices of \(S\).

### Lemma 4.2 — Kempe connectivity for a universally different pair

Let \(H\) be \(k\)-colourable, let \(x,y\in V(H)\), and suppose every proper \(k\)-colouring has \(c(x)\ne c(y)\). Fix a proper \(k\)-colouring \(c\), with
\[
c(x)=i,\qquad c(y)=j.
\]
Then \(x\) and \(y\) lie in the same connected component of the subgraph induced by the two colour classes \(i,j\).

#### Proof

Suppose not. In the bichromatic subgraph \(H[c^{-1}(\{i,j\})]\), interchange colours \(i\) and \(j\) on the connected component containing \(x\), leaving all other colours unchanged. This is the standard Kempe swap and preserves properness. The vertex \(x\) changes from colour \(i\) to \(j\), while \(y\), lying in a different component, remains colour \(j\). The resulting proper \(k\)-colouring has \(x\) and \(y\) equal, contradiction. QED.

### Theorem 4.3 — universal rainbow implies a rooted strong immersion of \(K_k\)

Let \(S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_k\}\) be universally rainbow in a \(k\)-colourable graph \(H\). Then \(H\) contains a strong immersion of \(K_k\) whose branch vertices are exactly \(S\).

#### Proof

Fix a proper \(k\)-colouring and relabel the colours so that
\[
c(s_i)=i.
\]
For every unordered pair \(\{i,j\}\), Lemma 4.2 gives a path
\[
P_{ij}:s_i\leadsto s_j
\]
using only vertices of colours \(i\) and \(j\).

If \(\{i,j\}\ne\{r,s\}\), the paths \(P_{ij}\) and \(P_{rs}\) are edge-disjoint. Indeed every edge of a proper colouring has endpoints carrying a unique unordered pair of distinct colours; an edge appearing on \(P_{ij}\) has endpoint-colour pair \(\{i,j\}\), so it cannot appear on a path associated with a different colour pair.

Also no branch vertex \(s_\ell\) with \(\ell\notin\{i,j\}\) can be an internal vertex of \(P_{ij}\), because \(P_{ij}\) uses only colours \(i,j\).

Thus the \(\binom{k}{2}\) paths are pairwise edge-disjoint and no path has another branch vertex internally. This is precisely a rooted strong immersion of \(K_k\). QED.

### Corollary 4.4 — exact cut lower bounds

For every \(U\subseteq V(H)\), put
\[
t=|S\cap U|.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{|\delta_H(U)|\ge t(k-t).}
\]

#### Proof

Exactly \(t(k-t)\) pairs of branch vertices are separated by the cut. Their immersion paths are edge-disjoint, and every such path must use at least one edge of \(\delta_H(U)\). QED.

In particular each root \(s\in S\) has degree at least \(k-1\).

## 5. Consequence for a minimum-degree forced-equality terminal

Suppose \(G\) is \(k\)-colourable and \(u,v\) are forced equal in every proper \(k\)-colouring. The previous checkpoint proved that if
\[
\deg_G(u)=k-1,
\]
then in every \(k\)-colouring of \(G-u\), the \(k-1\) neighbours of \(u\) use all colours except the colour of \(v\). Therefore
\[
S=N_G(u)\cup\{v\}
\]
is universally rainbow in \(H=G-u\).

By Theorem 4.3:

\[
\boxed{\text{\(G-u\) contains a rooted strong \(K_k\)-immersion on }N(u)\cup\{v\}.}
\]

For \(k=5\), every minimum-degree equality terminal would therefore impose a rooted strong \(K_5\)-immersion.

There is also a small geometric edge-count consequence. Let \(G\) be a planar unit-distance graph, \(k=5\), and suppose \(\deg(u)=4\). The four neighbours \(A=N(u)\) lie on the unit circle centred at \(u\). An induced unit edge between two members of \(A\) is a chord subtending \(60^\circ\), so the unit-distance graph on four distinct points of that circle has at most three edges: the full unit-chord graph of a circle decomposes into six-cycles in angular coordinates, and four selected points cannot complete such a cycle. Also a distinct point \(v\) has at most two common unit neighbours with \(u\), hence at most two edges from \(v\) to \(A\). Therefore
\[
e_{G-u}(S)\le 5.
\]
A rooted strong \(K_5\)-immersion has ten edge-disjoint root-pair paths. A path whose endpoints are nonadjacent in \(G-u\) uses at least two edges, while an adjacent pair may use one. Hence the immersion uses at least
\[
e(S)+2(10-e(S))=20-e(S)\ge15
\]
distinct edges in \(G-u\). Adding the four edges incident with \(u\) gives the crude necessary bound
\[
|E(G)|\ge19.
\]
This is only a local sanity bound, not a route to the global six-colour lower bound.

## 6. New partner-independent filter for the edge-critical-core search

Return to the one-layer architecture of checkpoint 4. Let \(C\) be an edge-critical 5-chromatic core. Let a proposed terminal \(u\) have a degree-five core-neighbourhood
\[
A=N_C(u),\qquad |A|=5.
\]
If \(u\) is to be paired with a distinct terminal \(v\), the Euclidean common-neighbour bound gives
\[
|A\cap N_C(v)|\le2,
\]
so at least three vertices \(a\in A\) are exclusive to \(u\).

Checkpoint 4 proved the following edge-deletion saturation necessity for every exclusive \(a\): for every incident core edge \(e=az\), every proper 4-colouring of \(C-e\) assigns all four colours to
\[
S_a=A\setminus\{a\}.
\]
Thus \(S_a\) is universally rainbow in \(C-e\).

Applying Theorem 4.3 with \(k=4\) gives a strictly cheaper necessary test:

### Corollary 6.1 — rooted-\(K_4\) immersion/min-cut prefilter

For every exclusive \(a\in A\), for every incident edge \(e=az\), the graph \(C-e\) must contain a rooted strong \(K_4\)-immersion with branch set
\[
A\setminus\{a\}.
\]
Equivalently, for every \(U\subseteq V(C)\), if
\[
t=|(A\setminus\{a\})\cap U|,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{|\delta_{C-e}(U)|\ge t(4-t).}
\]
In particular:

- every \(1\)-versus-\(3\) root separation has edge cut at least \(3\);
- every \(2\)-versus-\(2\) root separation has edge cut at least \(4\).

A degree-five geometric neighbourhood \(A\) can participate in a one-layer equality pair only if at least three choices \(a\in A\) pass this cut condition for **every** incident edge \(az\).

This produces a partner-independent max-flow/min-cut prefilter before any 4-colouring SAT query. It is necessary, not sufficient: surviving candidates still require the exact saturation test and eventually a certified forced-equality test.
