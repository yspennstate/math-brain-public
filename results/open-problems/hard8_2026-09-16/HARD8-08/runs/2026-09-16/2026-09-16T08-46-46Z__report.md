## 2. Correction: exact attachment criterion must ignore nonextendible core colourings

Let `C` be a graph and add two distinct nonadjacent terminals `u,v`, with every terminal neighbour lying in `C`. Write
\[
A=N(u)\cap V(C),\qquad B=N(v)\cap V(C).
\]
For a proper `k`-colouring `c` of `C`, define the available-colour sets
\[
L_A(c)=[k]\setminus c(A),\qquad L_B(c)=[k]\setminus c(B).
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — corrected two-terminal core-attachment criterion

Assume the enlarged graph `G` is `k`-colourable. Then `u,v` are forced equal in every proper `k`-colouring of `G` **if and only if** for every proper `k`-colouring `c` of `C`, one of the following holds:

1. `L_A(c)=emptyset` or `L_B(c)=emptyset`, so `c` has no joint extension to both terminals; or
2. there is a colour `gamma(c)` such that
   \[
   L_A(c)=L_B(c)=\{\gamma(c)\}.
   \]

Because `G` is `k`-colourable, case 2 occurs for at least one core colouring.

#### Proof

Fix `c`. Since `u,v` are nonadjacent and have no neighbours outside `C`, their terminal colours can be chosen independently. Hence the set of terminal-colour pairs extending `c` is exactly
\[
L_A(c)\times L_B(c).
\]
If either factor is empty there is no extension and forced equality imposes no condition on this `c`. If both factors are nonempty, every extending pair lies on the equality diagonal if and only if the two factors are the same singleton. Applying this to every core colouring proves the claim. QED.

### Why the prior statement was too strong

Checkpoint 3 required the singleton condition for **every** `k`-colouring of `C`. That incorrectly excluded core colourings for which one terminal has no available colour. Such a colouring is irrelevant to the colourings of `G` because it does not extend. The correction matters exactly when terminal degree is at least `k`, since a neighbourhood of size at most `k-1` cannot itself exhibit all `k` colours.

## 3. Edge-critical inclusion theorem at minimum terminal degree

The corrected criterion combines with edge-criticality in a useful way.

### Theorem 3.1 — minimum-size terminal neighbourhood is contained in its partner neighbourhood

Let `C` be a connected edge-critical `k`-chromatic graph: `chi(C)=k` and for every edge `e`, `C-e` is `(k-1)`-colourable. Add distinct nonadjacent terminals `u,v`, adjacent only to `C`, and suppose the enlarged graph is `k`-colourable and forces `c(u)=c(v)` in every `k`-colouring. Let
\[
A=N_C(u),\qquad B=N_C(v).
\]
If
\[
|A|\le k-1,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{A\subseteq B.}
\]
Symmetrically, if `|B|<=k-1`, then `B subseteq A`.

#### Proof

Suppose toward a contradiction that `a in A\B`. Because `C` is connected and nontrivial, `a` has an incident edge `e=az`. Since `C` is edge-critical, `C-e` has a proper `(k-1)`-colouring `phi`. Necessarily `phi(a)=phi(z)`: if they were different, restoring `e` would leave a proper `(k-1)`-colouring of `C`, contradicting `chi(C)=k`.

Now recolour only `a` with a fresh colour `k`, leaving every other vertex as in `phi`. This gives a proper `k`-colouring `c` of `C`.

Because `a notin B`, no vertex of `B` has the fresh colour `k`, so
\[
k\in L_B(c),
\]
and in particular `L_B(c)` is nonempty.

Because `a in A`, colour `k` occurs on `A`, so `k notin L_A(c)`. On the other hand `|A|<=k-1`, hence `A` cannot use all `k` colours; therefore `L_A(c)` is also nonempty.

Thus this core colouring is jointly extendible to the two terminals, but `L_A(c)` and `L_B(c)` cannot be the same singleton: the latter contains `k` while the former does not. This contradicts Theorem 2.1. Therefore no such `a` exists and `A subseteq B`. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — equal minimum-degree terminals have identical core neighbourhoods

The terminal minimum-degree theorem from checkpoint 3 remains valid: any terminal of a forced-equal pair under `k` colours has degree at least `k-1`. In the present one-layer architecture the terminal degree is exactly the size of its core neighbourhood. Consequently, if both terminals have degree `k-1`, then Theorem 3.1 in both directions gives
\[
\boxed{A=B.}
\]

This is weaker than the incorrectly stated checkpoint-3 criterion for arbitrary larger neighbourhoods, but it is exact at the minimum possible degree.

## 4. Planar consequence: one-layer equality terminals around an edge-critical 5-core must both have degree at least five

Let `C` now be a finite planar unit-distance embedding of a connected edge-critical 5-chromatic graph. Add two distinct geometric terminals `u,v`, assume they are nonadjacent, and assume every neighbour of either terminal lies in `C`.

Two distinct points in the Euclidean plane have at most two common unit-distance neighbours: a common neighbour lies in the intersection of the two unit circles centred at `u` and `v`, and two distinct circles meet in at most two points.

Suppose `u,v` were forced equal in every proper 5-colouring of the enlarged graph. By terminal minimum degree,
\[
\deg(u),\deg(v)\ge4.
\]
If, say, `deg(u)=4`, Theorem 3.1 gives
\[
N_C(u)\subseteq N_C(v).
\]
The two distinct geometric terminals would then have at least four common unit neighbours, impossible. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\deg(u)\ge5\quad\text{and}\quad\deg(v)\ge5.}
\]

This corrects and sharpens the search prescription from checkpoint 3. For an edge-critical 5-chromatic core such as the reported 509-vertex/2259-edge Parts reduction, **external candidate points having exactly four core incidences can be discarded without any SAT query.** A two-terminal one-layer equality gadget, if it exists at all, must start with at least five unit incidences at each terminal.

The statement is intentionally limited to the one-layer architecture: if the new terminals have neighbours among additional newly added vertices, this theorem does not apply directly.

## 5. Stronger necessary condition for every exclusive core neighbour

The same recolouring argument yields a useful test even when terminal degrees exceed `k-1`.

### Theorem 5.1 — edge-deletion saturation

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.1, let `a in A\B`. Then for **every** core edge `e=az` incident with `a` and for **every** proper `(k-1)`-colouring `phi` of `C-e`,
\[
\boxed{\phi(A\setminus\{a\})=[k-1].}
\]
That is, after deleting any incident edge of an exclusive neighbour, the rest of the terminal neighbourhood must contain all `k-1` old colours in every `(k-1)`-colouring.

#### Proof

As above, `phi(a)=phi(z)` and recolouring only `a` with the fresh colour `k` yields a proper `k`-colouring `c` of `C`. Since `a notin B`, the fresh colour `k` is available to `v`, so `L_B(c)` is nonempty and contains `k`.

If `c(A)` failed to contain all `k` colours, then `L_A(c)` would also be nonempty. But `k notin L_A(c)` because `a in A` is coloured `k`. Therefore the two nonempty available sets could not be the same singleton, contradicting forced equality via Theorem 2.1. Hence `c(A)=[k]`. Only `a` received the fresh colour, so the remaining set `A\{a}` must use every one of the old colours `1,...,k-1`. QED.

### Geometric single-neighbourhood filter for k=5

For distinct planar terminals,
\[
|A\cap B|\le2.
\]
Therefore at least `|A|-2` vertices of `A` lie in `A\B`, and every one of those vertices must satisfy Theorem 5.1. This yields a partner-independent filter:

> A candidate core-neighbourhood `A` of size `d` can participate in a one-layer 5-colour equality pair only if at least `d-2` of its vertices are edge-deletion-saturated.

For the first possible degree `d=5`, at least **three** vertices `a in A` must have the following property:

> for every core edge `e=az` and every proper 4-colouring of `C-e`, the other four vertices `A\{a}` receive all four colours, hence are rainbow.

This is much stronger than merely requiring `A` to see four colours in ordinary 5-colourings of the core.

### Exact SAT/DRAT refutation query

For a proposed degree-5 neighbourhood `A` and a chosen `a in A`, saturation can be disproved by finding just one incident edge `e=az` and one 4-colouring of `C-e` in which `A\{a}` omits a colour. Conversely, certification that `a` is saturated amounts to UNSAT certificates for those edge-deletion queries. With full colour symmetry, one may pin a missing colour after an appropriate symmetry break; without relying on that simplification, four missing-colour cases can be checked explicitly. A degree-5 geometric candidate can be discarded as soon as fewer than three of its five vertices survive this saturation test.

This is the next computational target for the 509-vertex edge-critical core. It uses the edge-critical 4-colourable deletions directly rather than enumerating all 5-colourings of the core.

## 7. Consequences for the lower-bound search

The lower-bound reformulation from checkpoint 2 remains useful: a finite unit-distance graph with a distinct pair forced equal under five colours would imply `chi(R^2)>=6`. However the search architecture should now be adjusted.

### Route A — edge-critical 509 core plus two direct terminals

For the reported edge-critical 509-vertex/2259-edge core:

1. enumerate exact geometric points at unit distance from at least **five** core vertices, not merely four;
2. retain the exact core-neighbourhood `A` of each point;
3. before any pairwise terminal comparison, run the partner-independent saturation filter of Section 5;
4. only neighbourhoods with at least `|A|-2` saturated vertices can survive;
5. only then test pairs, remembering that any pair can share at most two core neighbours geometrically.

A degree-4 candidate is now mathematically impossible in this architecture.

### Route B — additional noncritical layer

If Route A dies, the theorem identifies the escape hatch precisely: add auxiliary noncritical vertices between the terminals and the critical core. Then some terminal constraints are mediated by new-new edges rather than by a single core-neighbourhood list. The next structural question is the minimum depth/number of auxiliary vertices needed to transmit a 5-colour equality relation through planar unit-distance geometry.

### Route C — Haugland-style path-sum geometry

Haugland's v4 construction demonstrates that a large structured unit-distance lattice can force a two-terminal inequality under four colours. A 5-colour analogue remains a plausible alternative, but it must be tested at the correct colour level; the existing 4-colour relation does not itself imply a six-colour lower bound.
