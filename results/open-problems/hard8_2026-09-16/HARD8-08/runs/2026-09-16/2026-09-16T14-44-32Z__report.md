## 5. Algebraic coloring theorem for all 3-integral points

### Theorem 5.1

Let

\[
R=A[\sqrt3],\qquad A=\mathbb Z_{(3)}.
\]

Define a three-coloring of `R^2` by

\[
\kappa(a+b\sqrt3,\ c+d\sqrt3)=\bar a+\bar c\in\mathbb F_3.
\]

Then every pair of points in `R^2` at Euclidean distance exactly one receives different colors.

### Proof

Take two points and subtract them. Write their coordinate difference as

\[
\Delta x=a+b\sqrt3,\qquad
\Delta y=c+d\sqrt3,
\]

with `a,b,c,d in A`. Unit Euclidean distance means

\[
(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta y)^2=1.
\]

Expanding in the basis `1,sqrt(3)` gives the rational-part identity

\[
a^2+c^2+3(b^2+d^2)=1
\]

and the `sqrt(3)`-coefficient identity

\[
2(ab+cd)=0.
\]

Reduce the first identity modulo 3. Since all denominators are invertible modulo 3,

\[
\bar a^2+\bar c^2=1\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_3.
\]

The only squares in `F_3` are 0 and 1. Hence exactly one of `bar a`, `bar c` is nonzero: if both vanished the sum would be 0, while if both were nonzero the sum would be `1+1=2`, not 1. Therefore

\[
\bar a+\bar c\ne0.
\]

But this is exactly the color difference between the two points. Hence the colors are distinct. QED.

This theorem is used here as a project-level route obstruction. No claim of literature novelty is made.

## 6. Closure of the recursive squared-distance-13 alignment family

The finite second-generation result can be strengthened to the **entire recursive d^2=13 closure**.

### Theorem 6.1

Start with the verified 505-point first-generation set `W`. Repeatedly perform the following operation any finite number of times:

- choose one of the four squared-distance-13 source pairs `(A,B)` of the base G14 seed;
- choose any squared-distance-13 target pair `(C,D)` already present in the current point set;
- choose either target order and either orientation;
- add the isometric image of all 14 seed points determined by that ordered pair match.

Then every generated point lies in `R^2=A[sqrt(3)]^2`. Consequently the complete planar unit-distance graph on **every finite stage**, including every cross-copy unit-distance edge, is 3-colorable by `kappa` from Theorem 5.1.

### Proof

The finite checker establishes the base case: every point of `W` lies in `R^2`.

Assume the current target points lie in `R^2`. Put

\[
u=B-A,\qquad v=D-C,
\]

so both have squared norm 13. For a seed point `X`, write `w=X-A`. The exact pair-alignment formula is

\[
T(X)=C+\alpha v+\beta Jv,
\]

where

\[
\alpha=\frac{w\cdot u}{13},\qquad
\beta=\pm\frac{u\times w}{13},
\]

and `J` is the quarter-turn `(x,y)->(-y,x)`. The seed coordinates, hence `u` and `w`, lie in `R^2`; the target displacement `v` lies in `R^2`; and `R` is closed under addition and multiplication. Since `13` is invertible in `A` modulo 3, division by 13 preserves membership in `A`, hence in `R`. Therefore `alpha,beta in R` and `T(X) in R^2`.

Induction proves that every recursively generated point remains in `R^2`. Theorem 5.1 then colors the complete unit-distance graph at every finite stage with three colors. QED.

### Consequence

The exact next route proposed in the previous HARD8-08 report is not merely unsuccessful at generation two. It is impossible at **all recursive depths**:

\[
\boxed{\text{recursive G14 pair alignment using only source distance }\sqrt{13}\text{ can never yield }\chi\ge4.}
\]

In particular it cannot yield the required graph that is not 5-colorable.
