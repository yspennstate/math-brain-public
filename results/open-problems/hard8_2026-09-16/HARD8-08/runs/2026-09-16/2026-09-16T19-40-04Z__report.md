## 3. Idea before code: replace the two-edge rotation atlas by a complete single-edge atlas

The preceding recovery report proposed determining an alternate relative rotation from two independent prospective cross-unit edges. That was unnecessarily expensive.

Let an exact large-side point be

\[
\ell=(\ell_x,\ell_y),
\]

an exact small-side point be

\[
s=(s_x,s_y),
\]

and write the unknown relative rotation as

\[
R(c,u)=
\begin{pmatrix}
c&-u\\
u&c
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad c^2+u^2=1.
\]

The demand

\[
\|\ell-R(c,u)s\|^2=1
\]

is equivalent to one exact linear equation

\[
2(\ell_xs_x+\ell_ys_y)c
+2(-\ell_xs_y+\ell_ys_x)u
=\|\ell\|^2+\|s\|^2-1.
\]

For a genuinely new non-center cross edge, the coefficient pair is nonzero. Its intersection with the unit circle has at most two points. Therefore **one** prospective new cross edge determines at most two relative rotations; two edges are not needed to generate candidates.

For Parts's published `L_374` and `S_136`, there are

\[
374\cdot136=50,864
\]

raw large/small vertex pairs, hence at most

\[
2\cdot50,864=101,728
\]

single-edge rotation roots before exact deduplication. The earlier two-edge plan would begin from

\[
\binom{50,864}{2}=1,293,547,816
\]

unordered raw pair systems. The single-edge atlas is complete for every new relative rotation that creates at least one genuinely new non-center cross-unit edge. Shared-center incidences do not determine the relative angle and are not the intended coupling mechanism.

This is the B18 change of language: the geometric search becomes an exact line-circle algebra problem before any SAT instance is generated. B25 supplies two independent coverage counts: raw vertex-pair roots and, below, orbit-level exact roots.

## 4. Exact finite theorem: the Parts reference orbit forces the historical angle class if the auxiliary orbit also connects

Define

\[
\rho=\frac{7+i\sqrt{15}}8,
\qquad
\zeta_6=\frac{1+i\sqrt3}{2}.
\]

Using Parts's exact coordinate map

\[
(a,b,c,d)\mapsto
\frac{a+b\sqrt{33}+i c\sqrt3+i d\sqrt{11}}{12}
\]

for the `h=1` orbit data, together with the five exact orbit generators printed in the paper, let

\[
O=\operatorname{Orb}(0,4,4,0)
\]

be the reference connection orbit and

\[
A=\operatorname{Orb}(0,0,2,6)
\]

be the auxiliary connection orbit.

### Proposition

The following statements hold exactly.

1. `O` has 12 points, each with squared norm 4.
2. Every relative rotation `q` that makes at least one ordered pair `(\ell,s)\in O\times O` a unit-distance cross edge belongs to a finite set of exactly **36** rotations.
3. Among those 36 rotations, 24 create exactly 6 `O`-to-`O` unit edges and 12 create exactly 12 such edges.
4. The 12 maximizers are exactly

\[
\boxed{\{\zeta_6^k\rho^{\pm1}:0\le k<6\}}.
\]

5. `A` has 12 points and splits into two six-cycles of squared radii

\[
\boxed{n_\pm=\frac{17\pm\sqrt{33}}6}.
\]

6. For the same 36 reference-induced rotations, the complete exact connection-count tuple

\[
(E_{OO},E_{AA},E_{OA},E_{AO})
\]

is

\[
(12,12,0,0)
\]

for precisely the 12 rotations in the displayed historical class, and

\[
(6,0,0,0)
\]

for the other 24.

Consequently, **within these exact two Parts connection orbits, every relative rotation that creates a reference-orbit cross edge and also an auxiliary-orbit cross edge is symmetry-equivalent to the historical angle `\rho^{\pm1}`.** Thus the route “retain the same reference/auxiliary connection mechanism but change the relative angle” is exhausted at this two-orbit level.

### Proof

The orbit-generation part is finite exact arithmetic. Applying Parts's five rational linear transformations to `(0,4,4,0)` closes after 12 vectors; substituting them into the coordinate map gives squared norm 4 for all 12. The same procedure on `(0,0,2,6)` closes after 12 vectors.

Fix `\ell,s\in O`. Since both have norm 2 and `q` is a unit complex number,

\[
1=|\ell-qs|^2
=|\ell|^2+|s|^2-2\operatorname{Re}(\ell\overline{qs})
=8-2\operatorname{Re}(\ell\overline{qs}).
\]

Equivalently the reduced angle between `\ell` and `qs` has cosine `7/8`. Therefore its two unit-circle possibilities are exactly `\rho` and `\overline\rho`, and every candidate has the form

\[
q=\frac{\ell\overline{s}}4\rho
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
q=\frac{\ell\overline{s}}4\overline\rho.
\]

This formula proves coverage: every rotation producing even one reference-orbit edge appears among the `12\cdot12\cdot2=288` exact roots. Exact deduplication in the multiquadratic field leaves 36 distinct rotations. Re-evaluating every `O\times O` distance for each of those 36 gives the exact multiplicity distribution `24 x 6 + 12 x 12 = 288`, so no root is omitted or counted heuristically. The 12 full-reference rotations are exactly the six hexagonal rotations of `\rho` and of `\overline\rho`, namely `\zeta_6^k\rho^{\pm1}`.

For `A`, direct exact norm calculation gives six points of each squared radius `n_+` and `n_-`; each six-point class is exactly one `\zeta_6`-orbit. Reconstructing all `A\times A`, `O\times A`, and `A\times O` distances under all 36 reference-induced rotations yields the two exact connection-count classes stated above. Because the initial 288-root derivation covered every possible rotation with an `O`-edge, the final classification is exhaustive for the proposition. QED.

This proposition is a **restricted construction-family theorem**, not a claim about arbitrary unit-distance graphs and not a Hadwiger–Nelson solution.

## 5. B2: attack the negation by forcing a genuinely nonhistorical connection mechanism

The exact classification above says that merely perturbing the historical Type-M angle while keeping both old connection orbits active cannot work: as soon as a reference-orbit edge and an auxiliary-orbit edge coexist, the angle falls back into `\zeta_6^k\rho^{\pm1}`.

The negation-first response is therefore to **drop the reference-edge mechanism on purpose** and ask whether the auxiliary orbit can create a different exact coupling angle.

Take two auxiliary points on the same squared-radius class `n_+` or `n_-`. If their reduced relative angle is `\delta`, the unit-distance condition is

\[
1=2n_\pm(1-\cos\delta),
\]

hence

\[
\cos\delta_\pm
=1-\frac1{2n_\pm}
=\frac{205\pm3\sqrt{33}}{256}.
\]

Indeed,

\[
\frac1{2n_\pm}
=\frac3{17\pm\sqrt{33}}
=\frac{51\mp3\sqrt{33}}{256}.
\]

These values are exactly different from the historical

\[
\cos\alpha=\frac78=\frac{224}{256},
\]

because `\sqrt{33}` is irrational. Since each radius class is a six-cycle under `\zeta_6`, this gives the exact 24-candidate family

\[
\boxed{
q_{\pm,k,\epsilon}
=\zeta_6^k
\left(c_\pm+i\epsilon\sqrt{1-c_\pm^2}\right),
\quad
c_\pm=\frac{205\pm3\sqrt{33}}{256},
\quad
0\le k<6,
\quad
\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}.
}
\]

At the connection-orbit level, every member supplies six same-radius auxiliary unit edges. None supplies a reference-orbit edge: if it did, it would belong to the exhaustive 36-member reference-induced list; because it also has an auxiliary edge, Proposition 4 would force it into the historical angle class, contradicting `c_\pm\ne7/8`.

This is the first exact candidate family in the current route that **genuinely breaks** the historical reference-plus-auxiliary mechanism instead of reparametrizing it. It changes the remaining obligation in a useful way even though it does not move the vertex-count metric.
