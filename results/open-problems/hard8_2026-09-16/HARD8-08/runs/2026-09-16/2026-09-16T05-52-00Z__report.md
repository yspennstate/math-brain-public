## 2. Two strategic no-go deductions

### Lemma A — fractional/independence-ratio methods alone cannot prove the lower bound 6

Croft gives a measurable 1-avoiding set in the plane of upper density
\[
\delta_{\rm Croft}=0.22936\ldots.
\]
Averaging Euclidean motions of this set over any finite planar unit-distance point configuration \(G\) gives
\[
\alpha(G)\ge \delta_{\rm Croft}|V(G)|.
\]
Equivalently, the fractional chromatic number of every finite planar unit-distance graph satisfies
\[
\chi_f(G)\le \chi_f(\mathbb R^2)\le \delta_{\rm Croft}^{-1}<4.36<5.
\]
Therefore no route whose certificate is merely \(\chi_f(G)\ge5\), or merely \(\alpha(G)/|V(G)|<1/5\), can possibly produce a planar unit-distance lower bound 6.  A hypothetical 6-chromatic planar unit-distance graph must exhibit a genuine integrality gap:
\[
\frac{\chi(G)}{\chi_f(G)}>\frac{6}{4.36}>1.37.
\]
This sharply changes the useful search target after the 2026 Dúcz–Varga result: pushing fractional chromatic number from just above 4 toward 5 cannot close the Hadwiger–Nelson lower-bound gap.  The next lower-bound attack should instead optimize **integer 5-color obstructions / forced-color gadgets**.

### Lemma B — polygonal/map-type six-coloring constructions are already ruled out

Sokolov–Voronov (arXiv:2502.01958) prove a seven-color lower bound for their locally finite map-type class and state, as a corollary, the same lower bound when the regions are arbitrary polygons.  Consequently a proof of \(\chi(\mathbb R^2)\le6\), if one exists, cannot be a standard periodic polygonal tiling with six polygonal color regions.  It must escape those hypotheses, e.g. through sufficiently non-map-type / non-polygonal boundary structure (or another mechanism outside their framework).  This makes routine searches over periodic polygon tiles strategically low-value for the unrestricted problem.

Together Lemmas A and B squeeze the two remaining directions:

- **To prove \(\chi\ge6\):** search for an integer-coloring gadget with a large integrality gap, not for a stronger fractional/independence-ratio witness.
- **To prove \(\chi\le6\):** search outside ordinary polygonal/map-type tilings; boundary topology/regularity is not a cosmetic issue but a proved obstruction.

## 3. New finite obstruction inside the regular-hexagon ansatz

Although polygonal six-colorings are already ruled out in general by Sokolov–Voronov, I derived a small, completely explicit local certificate for one natural regular-hexagon ansatz.  It is useful as a sanity-check/seed for automated construction searches and is much smaller than invoking the full map-type theorem.

Tile the plane by regular hexagons of side
\[
s=\frac14,
\]
with center lattice
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z u+\mathbb Z v,\qquad
u=(\sqrt3/4,0),\quad v=(\sqrt3/8,3/8).
\]
Index a hexagon by \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\).  For displacement \((r,t)\), write
\[
Q(r,t)=r^2+rt+t^2.
\]
The center-center squared distance is exactly
\[
\|ru+tv\|^2=\frac{3}{16}Q(r,t).
\]

For each shell \(Q\in\{3,4,7,9\}\), two such hexagon interiors contain a pair of points at distance exactly 1.  It is enough to exhibit one pair with distance below 1 and another pair with distance above 1, then connect the two pairs continuously through the product of the two convex interiors.

Exact representative checks (computed first at unit hexagon side and then scaled by 1/4):

- \(Q=3\), representative displacement \((1,1)\): center distance \(3/4<1\); a far vertex pair has distance \(5/4>1\).
- \(Q=4\), representative \((2,0)\): center distance \(\sqrt3/2<1\); a far vertex pair has squared distance \(28/16=7/4>1\).
- \(Q=7\), representative \((2,1)\): center distance \(\sqrt{21}/4>1\); an explicit near vertex pair has squared distance \(7/16<1\).
- \(Q=9\), representative \((3,0)\): center distance \(3\sqrt3/4>1\); an explicit near vertex pair has squared distance \(12/16=3/4<1\).

The dihedral symmetry of the regular hexagonal tiling covers all lattice vectors in these shells.

Now take the following 23 cell indices:

```text
(-2,0), (-2,2),
(-1,0), (-1,1), (-1,2), (-1,3),
(0,-2), (0,-1), (0,0), (0,1), (0,2),
(1,-2), (1,-1), (1,0), (1,1), (1,2),
(2,-3), (2,-2), (2,-1), (2,0), (2,1),
(3,-2), (3,-1).
```

Join two indices whenever their displacement has \(Q\in\{3,4,7,9\}\).  This gives a finite **cell-conflict graph** with 23 vertices and 143 edges.  Exhaustive DSATUR-style exact-integer backtracking found:

- no proper 6-coloring (142357 recursive search calls with the fixed deterministic ordering in the archived verifier);
- a proper 7-coloring immediately (24 calls).

Hence any plane coloring in which every interior of these side-1/4 regular hexagon cells is monochromatic must use at least seven colors already on this 23-cell patch.  This is **not** a new unrestricted Hadwiger–Nelson lower bound and is not itself a unit-distance graph: each conflict edge certifies the existence of some unit-distance pair between two colored cells.  It is an ansatz obstruction, consistent with and subsumed by the broader Sokolov–Voronov polygonal theorem.

## 4. What this rules out / what remains useful

### Ruled out or strategically weak

1. Trying to turn the Dúcz–Varga \(\chi_f>4\) result into \(\chi\ge6\) solely by driving \(\chi_f\) to 5: Croft's upper bound forbids this.
2. Searching ordinary six-color periodic polygonal tilings: Sokolov–Voronov already imply at least seven colors in that class.
3. Treating Mundinger–Pokutta–Spiegel–Zimmer's off-diagonal sixth color as an ordinary six-coloring: their sixth color avoids another distance \(d\), not necessarily unit distance.
4. Treating Haugland's Moser-spindle-free graph as a lower-bound improvement: it remains 5-chromatic, but its independent construction technology is potentially valuable.

### Promising lower-bound route

The best next route is **integer color-forcing gadget synthesis**.  The 2026 Haugland construction is especially interesting because it supplies a structurally different 5-chromatic family not relying on the Moser spindle.  A useful next checkpoint should recover exact coordinates / colorings for Haugland's intermediate graphs \(G_1,G_2,G_3\), enumerate the equivalence classes of proper 5-colorings of a manageable rigid seed, and search for distinguished terminal tuples with forced equality/inequality patterns.  The objective is not to minimize a 5-chromatic graph but to find a gadget that becomes non-5-colorable under an exact rotation/translation/gluing operation while preserving unit distances.

Concretely, for a rigid seed \(H\), record the **5-color relation**
\[
\mathcal R_H(T)=\{\text{color partitions induced on terminal tuple }T\text{ by proper 5-colorings of }H\}/S_5.
\]
Then search exact geometric gluings of copies for which the conjunction of the terminal relations is unsatisfiable.  This targets the required integer integrality gap directly and can emit independently checkable SAT/DRAT certificates if a candidate is found.
