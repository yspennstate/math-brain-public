## 5. B20 — isolate the first broken implication

The previous report simplified the near-point part of the source heat-kernel chain using

\[
p_n^\omega(x,y)\le
\sqrt{p_n^\omega(x,x)p_n^\omega(y,y)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Taken literally at the *same time* `n`, (5.1) is not valid for a general discrete-time bipartite chain at odd times. The two-state alternating chain is the elementary warning: its odd-time off-diagonal transition is positive while both odd-time diagonals vanish.

The comb graph is bipartite, so a proof of the endpoint must not silently use (5.1) at odd times. The source proof of its off-diagonal estimate contains a same-time Cauchy--Schwarz-looking step as well; regardless of whether the source intended an implicit parity convention, the internal I0012 proof should be independent of that ambiguity.

This is the first concrete mathematical defect found in the same-track stress tests. It does **not** refute the endpoint theorem, because the correct semigroup version loses only one unit of time.

**B20 outcome:** SUCCESS. The exact vulnerable implication is identified: same-time PSD/Cauchy--Schwarz at odd times. No other part of the mass-transport or collision second-moment identity is implicated.

## 6. B8 — parity-safe discretization of the finite demands

Let the symmetric transition density be

\[
p_n^\omega(x,y)=\frac{P_x^\omega(X_n=y)}{\mu_\omega(y)},
\qquad \mu_\omega(y)=\deg_\omega(y).
\]

For any reversible chain and integers `a,b>=0`, the density semigroup gives

\[
p_{a+b}^\omega(x,y)
=\sum_z p_a^\omega(x,z)p_b^\omega(z,y)\mu_\omega(z).
\tag{6.1}
\]

By symmetry and Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{a+b}^\omega(x,y)^2
&\le
\left(\sum_z p_a^\omega(x,z)^2\mu_\omega(z)\right)
\left(\sum_z p_b^\omega(y,z)^2\mu_\omega(z)\right)\\
&=p_{2a}^\omega(x,x)p_{2b}^\omega(y,y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Set

\[
a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\qquad b=\lceil n/2\rceil.
\]

Then for every integer `n`, with no parity restriction,

\[
\boxed{
p_n^\omega(x,y)
\le
\sqrt{
p_{2\lfloor n/2\rfloor}^\omega(x,x)
 p_{2\lceil n/2\rceil}^\omega(y,y)}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For even `n`, this reduces to the familiar same-time inequality. For odd `n`, the two on-diagonal times are `n-1` and `n+1`, both even.

The finite-demand compiler from the previous report is therefore repaired by adding only the neighboring even-time demands

\[
q_-(n)=2\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\qquad
q_+(n)=2\lceil n/2\rceil.
\tag{6.4}
\]

At the critical exponent, the source on-diagonal estimate is `p_t(z,z) <= C t^{-2/3}` on the corresponding finite-scale good event. Since `q_\pm(n)\asymp n` for `n>=2`, (6.3) gives, for every demanded near point,

\[
p_n^\omega(x,y)\le Cn^{-2/3}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Only a constant-factor enlargement of the set of physical times is needed. Thus the polynomial demand count in the `07-46-30Z` compiler remains polynomial, and the union of the source pre-Borel--Cantelli bounds (equations 4.8, 4.44, 4.49) still has the form

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P((G_n^{\dagger})^c)
\le Cn^{A'}e^{-cn^\eta}}
\tag{6.6}
\]

for deterministic `A',c,eta>0`.

The same repair applies wherever the proof of the far off-diagonal estimate uses a Cauchy--Schwarz step at a remaining time `r`: demand the on-diagonal estimates at `q_-(r),q_+(r)`. In the relevant range those times are within one of `r`, hence comparable to the original physical scale. There are still only polynomially many center/radius/time demands. The exponent and stretched-exponential failure budget are unchanged.

Small times `n<2` are absorbed into the deterministic constant and never affect summability.

**B8 outcome:** SUCCESS. The exact parity-sensitive demand set is finite and polynomially sized; the endpoint finite-scale probability budget survives unchanged.

## 7. B25 — count the overlap both ways and verify that no factor was lost

On `G_n^{\dagger}`, the repaired near estimate gives

\[
|y|\le n^{1/3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p_n^\omega(0,y)\le Cn^{-2/3}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Hence the near part of the backbone square overlap is

\[
\sum_{|y|\le n^{1/3}}p_n^\omega(0,y)^2
\le C n^{1/3}n^{-4/3}
=Cn^{-1}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For the far part, the parity-repaired source off-diagonal chain retains

\[
p_n^\omega(0,y)\le C|y|^{-2},
\qquad |y|>n^{1/3},
\tag{7.3}
\]

so

\[
\sum_{|y|>n^{1/3}}p_n^\omega(0,y)^2
\le C\sum_{k>n^{1/3}}k^{-4}
\le Cn^{-1}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{K_n^\omega(0)\le Cn^{-1}\quad\text{on }G_n^{\dagger}.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The profile-independent fallback from the earlier report is also parity-safe. Indeed,

\[
K_n^\omega(0)
=\sum_{y\in\text{backbone}}p_n^\omega(0,y)^2
\le
\sum_{v\in G_\omega}p_n^\omega(0,v)^2\mu_\omega(v)
=p_{2n}^\omega(0,0)
\le Cn^{-1/2}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Thus

\[
\mathbf E[K_n^\omega(0)^2]
\le Cn^{-2}+Cn^{-1}\mathbf P((G_n^{\dagger})^c).
\tag{7.7}
\]

Because the second term has a stretched-exponential factor, its square root is summable. Minkowski's inequality in `L^2(\mathbf P)` now makes the logarithmic Green-overlap estimate completely explicit:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|m_T^\omega(0)\|_{L^2(\mathbf P)}
&\le \sum_{n\le T}\|K_n^\omega(0)\|_{L^2(\mathbf P)}\\
&\le C + C\sum_{n=2}^T n^{-1}
 + C\sum_{n=2}^T n^{-1/2}\mathbf P((G_n^{\dagger})^c)^{1/2}\\
&\le C\log(T+2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2]\le C(\log(T+2))^2.}
\tag{7.9}
\]

This is exactly the upper moment needed in the weighted-collision Paley--Zygmund argument, now with no same-time odd-parity shortcut.

The stationary shift identity from the earlier B14 report remains unchanged:

\[
\mathbf E\sum_{n\le T,x}p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)
=\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2].
\tag{7.10}
\]

The source lower first-moment mechanism is also unaffected. Its lower heat-kernel argument already tracks graph parity, and the relevant common start `v=w=0` has even distance. The disjoint cubic scale windows therefore still give

\[
\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)]\ge c\log T
\tag{7.11}
\]

along the chosen geometric horizons. Combining (7.9), (7.11), the exact weighted-collision first/second moment identities, Paley--Zygmund, the monotone `T\to\infty` passage, the fixed-environment collision zero-one law, and horizontal environment ergodicity yields the same source endpoint theorem.

**B25 outcome:** SUCCESS. Recounting the overlap after the parity repair gives exactly the required `n^{-1}` good-environment cost and `O((log T)^2)` annealed second moment; no missing degree, parity, or scale factor appears.
