## 4. Mathematical idea before code

The source's equation (4.120) first bounds each pointwise term and then sums over both time and backbone location. That discards a semigroup identity that is tailor-made for the sum over locations.

The idea is to reverse the order:

> **sum over all backbone locations first, identify the resulting `L^2` mass with an on-diagonal heat kernel, and bound that on-diagonal term by an elementary resistance/exit argument that is uniform over every possible tooth profile.**

This gives a profile-independent `t^{-1/2}` bound on the entire backbone `L^2` mass at time `t`, hence only a `sqrt(L)` cost up to lag `L`. It is strictly stronger than the source's short-lag estimate for the quantity actually needed in Proposition 4.14.

## 5. B11 — dualize the short-lag sum through resistance and the semigroup

### Proposition 5.1 (universal short-lag backbone overlap on any finite-tooth comb)

Let `G=Comb(Z,H)` for an arbitrary deterministic profile with every tooth finite. Let

\[
p_t(x,y)=\frac{\mathbb P_x(X_t=y)}{\deg(y)}
\]

be the degree-normalized symmetric transition density of discrete-time simple random walk. For every backbone point `b=(z,0)` and every integer `t>=1`,

\[
\boxed{p_t(b,b)\le C t^{-1/2}}
\tag{5.1}
\]

with a numerical constant `C` independent of the profile `H`, of `z`, and of `t`. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
K_t(b):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z}p_t\bigl(b,(y,0)\bigr)^2
\le C t^{-1/2}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and hence, for every integer `L>=1`,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{t=1}^L K_t(b)\le C'\sqrt L.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The same bounds hold with a killed kernel in place of the full kernel whenever killing only removes paths.

#### Proof

Fix `t>=1` and let

\[
r=\lceil 2\sqrt t\rceil.
\]

Let `B_r` be the finite subgraph containing the entire teeth above the horizontal sites

\[
\{z-r,z-r+1,\ldots,z+r\}.
\]

Because every tooth over this finite interval is included in full, the walk can leave `B_r` only by making a horizontal step from one of the two endpoints of the backbone interval to the outside.

Record only the successive horizontal moves of the comb walk. By symmetry and the strong Markov property, their directions form an ordinary simple symmetric random walk `(S_j)` on `Z`; vertical excursions merely insert holding time between horizontal moves. By physical time `t` the comb walk has made at most `t` horizontal moves. Therefore

\[
\mathbb P_b(\tau_{B_r}\ge t)
\ge
\mathbb P_0\left(\max_{0\le j\le t}|S_j|\le r\right).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since `S_j` is a martingale and `S_j^2` is a submartingale, Doob's maximal inequality gives

\[
\mathbb P_0\left(\max_{0\le j\le t}|S_j|\ge r+1\right)
\le \frac{\mathbb E S_t^2}{(r+1)^2}
=\frac{t}{(r+1)^2}
\le\frac14.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus

\[
\mathbb P_b(\tau_{B_r}\ge t)\ge\frac34.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Electrically, all vertical teeth inside `B_r` are pendant branches. They carry no current in the minimizing unit-voltage flow from `b` to `B_r^c`. In particular, using just one horizontal path to the boundary gives

\[
R_{\mathrm{eff}}(b,B_r^c)\le r+1.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Apply Barlow–Peres–Sousi Lemma 5.3:

\[
p_t(b,b)
\le
\frac{2R_{\mathrm{eff}}(b,B_r^c)}{t\,\mathbb P_b(\tau_{B_r}\ge t)}
\le
\frac{8(r+1)}{3t}
\le C t^{-1/2},
\]

which proves (5.1).

For the normalized symmetric kernel, the semigroup identity gives

\[
p_{2t}(b,b)
=\sum_{v\in G}\deg(v)\,p_t(b,v)^2.
\tag{5.8}
\]

All degrees are at least one, so restricting (5.8) to backbone vertices yields

\[
K_t(b)
\le p_{2t}(b,b)
\le C t^{-1/2}.
\]

This is (5.2), and summing `t^{-1/2}` proves (5.3). `square`

### Why this matters for the source endpoint

The source's equation (4.120) controls precisely a spatially truncated version of `K_t(b)` but does so pointwise before summing. Proposition 5.1 gives the stronger estimate, uniformly in the environment,

\[
\sum_{t\le L}\sum_{|y|\le N}
 p_t^\omega\bigl((z,0),(y,0)\bigr)^2
\le C\sqrt L.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus a short-lag cutoff `L_N=O((log N)^2)` costs only `O(log N)`, exactly the scale tolerable at `gamma=1/3`.

The exponent `1/2` cannot be improved uniformly over all tooth profiles: the zero-tooth profile reduces to ordinary `Z`, whose full backbone `L^2` mass has order `t^{-1/2}`. Therefore any `t^{-1}` critical gain must genuinely use the heavy-tailed random environment at longer times.

## 6. B15 — break the worst-center supremum in the second moment

The source passes from its Markov decomposition to a supremum over every possible collision center in equation (4.115). That is sufficient, but not necessary.

### Lemma 6.1 (collision-weighted second moment)

Let

\[
H=\sum_{n=1}^T I_n
\]

count collisions of two independent copies of a Markov chain in a designated set (with any stopping rule that can only remove future collisions). Let

\[
a_n(z)=\mathbb P(I_n=1,\ \text{collision location at time }n\text{ is }z),
\]

and define

\[
A(z)=\sum_{n=1}^T a_n(z),
\qquad
\nu(z)=\frac{A(z)}{\mathbb E H}
\quad(\mathbb E H>0).
\]

For a common starting point `z`, let

\[
J_T(z)=\sum_{r=1}^T
\mathbb P_{z,z}(\text{a permitted collision occurs at lag }r).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E H^2
\le
\mathbb E H\left(1+2\,\mathbb E_{Z\sim\nu}J_T(Z)\right).}
\tag{6.1}
\]

#### Proof

Expanding the square,

\[
\mathbb E H^2
=\mathbb E H+2\sum_{1\le n<k\le T}\mathbb P(I_n=1,I_k=1).
\]

Condition on the collision location `z` at time `n` and use the strong Markov property. The remaining permitted future collision probability is at most the corresponding common-start probability from `z`. Hence

\[
\sum_{k=n+1}^T\mathbb P(I_n=1,I_k=1)
\le
\sum_z a_n(z)J_{T-n}(z)
\le
\sum_z a_n(z)J_T(z).
\]

Summing in `n` gives

\[
\mathbb E H^2
\le
\mathbb E H+2\sum_z A(z)J_T(z)
=
\mathbb E H\left(1+2\sum_z\nu(z)J_T(z)\right).
\]

This is (6.1). `square`

### Application to the comb backbone

For a backbone point `z`,

\[
\mathbb P_z(X_r=(y,0))
=\deg(y,0)\,p_r(z,(y,0)),
\]

and comb degrees are at most three. Therefore

\[
J_L(z)
\le
9\sum_{r\le L}\sum_y p_r(z,(y,0))^2
\le C\sqrt L
\tag{6.2}
\]

by Proposition 5.1, uniformly in every environment.

More importantly, (6.1) shows that the endpoint does **not** require a heat-kernel estimate uniform over every center if one can instead prove that centers with anomalously large long-lag overlap receive negligible mass under the actual collision-location law `nu`. This directly attacks the uniformity obstruction identified by the source authors.

This is an exact reformulation, not yet the missing estimate: this run does **not** prove the required collision-weighted long-lag bound.

## 7. B30 — one scale at a time: audit where the endpoint really breaks

A closer rereading of the source sharpens the previous diagnosis.

### 7.1 Proposition 4.6 and Lemma 4.7 themselves already include `gamma=1/3`

Subsections 4.1–4.6 work under the standing assumption `gamma in (0,1)` until the separate `gamma=1` section. Proposition 4.6 is stated in that regime, and its key Case-1 exponent comparison is

\[
3-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}
<
\frac{4}{\gamma+1},
\tag{7.1}
\]

explicitly valid for all `gamma in (0,1)`. At `gamma=1/3`, the two sides are `5/2` and `3`, leaving a positive gap `1/2`. Thus the local-time concentration step does not algebraically fail at the endpoint. Lemma 4.7 then inherits the same regime and gives the fast-exit estimate for spatial scales above the source's uniformity cutoff.

Likewise, Lemmas 4.8–4.12 are stated before the strict second-moment conclusion and provide the lower/on-diagonal/off-diagonal heat-kernel estimates for arbitrary small positive power cutoffs. Their endpoint exponents become

\[
p_t(x,x)\lesssim t^{-2/3},
\qquad
p_t(x,y)\lesssim |x-y|^{-2}\quad\text{in the far regime}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The strict inequality `gamma>1/3` first becomes visibly indispensable in the displayed Proposition-4.14 calculation at (4.120), where the source needs to absorb a positive power of `N` coming from short lags.

### 7.2 Exact endpoint long-lag arithmetic

Assume for the moment that the source upper heat-kernel bounds are available uniformly for all lags `t>=L_N`. At `gamma=1/3`, the near region at lag `t` contains `O(t^{1/3})` backbone sites and Lemma 4.12 gives `p_t^2=O(t^{-4/3})`; hence

\[
\sum_{y\text{ near}}p_t(z,y)^2=O(t^{-1}).
\tag{7.3}
\]

For the far region, Proposition 4.11 gives `p_t(z,y)=O(|y-z|^{-2})`, so

\[
\sum_{|y-z|\ge t^{1/3}}p_t(z,y)^2
\le C\sum_{\ell\ge t^{1/3}}\ell^{-4}
=O(t^{-1}).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{t=L_N}^{T_N}\sum_y p_t(z,y)^2
\le C\log(T_N/L_N)
\le C\log N,
\tag{7.5}
\]

because `T_N` is of order `N^3` at the endpoint.

Together with Proposition 5.1, this proves the following clean reduction.

### Proposition 7.1 (a `log^2` uniform heat-kernel cutoff closes the endpoint)

Suppose the source endpoint first-moment lower bound of Lemma 4.13 is used at `gamma=1/3`, and suppose the source upper bounds of Lemma 4.12 and Proposition 4.11 can be made uniform over the required centers for every lag

\[
t\ge L_N,
\qquad
L_N\le C(\log N)^2.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Then the exact source boundary has the infinite collision property.

#### Proof

Lemma 4.13 becomes

\[
\mathbb E^\omega H_N\ge c>0.
\tag{7.7}
\]

For lags up to `L_N`, Proposition 5.1 gives a total backbone-overlap cost `O(sqrt(L_N))=O(log N)`. For lags above `L_N`, (7.3)–(7.5) give another `O(log N)`. Repeating the source Markov decomposition therefore yields

\[
\mathbb E^\omega H_N^2\le C\log N.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Paley–Zygmund gives a collision probability at scale `N` bounded below by `c/log N`, uniformly over the source-admissible restart points. On geometric scales `N_m=b^m`, this becomes `c/m`. The conditional probabilities have divergent sum, so the same strong-Markov/conditional-Borel–Cantelli argument as the source yields collisions on infinitely many scales almost surely. `square`

### 7.3 Why this is not yet closure

The source's heat-kernel lemmas are currently uniform only after a positive power cutoff `N^delta`. The previous run lowered the *basic good-disk geometry* to a logarithmic spatial cutoff, but that does not automatically lower every later quenched heat-kernel estimate to the physical-time cutoff `(log N)^2` required by Proposition 7.1.

Indeed, a generic statement of the form `L_N=(log N)^A` is not enough: Proposition 5.1 would cost `(log N)^{A/2}`. On geometric scales, a power strictly larger than one in `log N` can make the Paley–Zygmund lower bounds summable. The exponent of the polylogarithmic cutoff matters.

This identifies the remaining source-level obstruction much more precisely than “endpoint uniformity”:

> **Either push the relevant quenched long-lag estimates uniformly down to time `O((log N)^2)`, or use Lemma 6.1 to avoid worst-center uniformity and prove the same `O(log N)` budget only under the collision-location measure.**

## 8. Counterexample/obstruction-first checks and failed routes

1. **Can Proposition 5.1 itself be sharpened to `t^{-1}` uniformly? No.** The comb with no teeth is `Z`, and its backbone `L^2` mass is order `t^{-1/2}`. A critical `t^{-1}` estimate must use the heavy-tailed environment, not graph topology alone.

2. **Can an arbitrary polylogarithmic cutoff close the argument? No.** If the only uniform critical estimate begins at `L_N=(log N)^A`, the universal short-lag contribution is `O((log N)^{A/2})`. For `A>2` this is beyond the harmonic-series budget on geometric scales.

3. **Can the full-graph semigroup replace backbone localization? No.** The critical full on-diagonal scale is `t^{-2/3}`; summing it to time `N^3` is polynomial. The backbone codimension is essential.

4. **Can the older maximum-tooth sufficient criterion decide the endpoint? No.** For a `z^{-1/3}` tail, maxima over `O(n)` sites are naturally of cubic scale, so the maximum-tooth reciprocal criterion is far too small to force divergence. This is a heuristic diagnosis of that criterion, not a converse theorem.

5. **Can the collision-weighted reformulation be declared sufficient without further work? No.** Lemma 6.1 removes the supremum algebraically, but no bound has yet been proved showing that collision mass avoids the exceptional centers where the environment has poor medium-scale regularity.

6. **Does the source local-time machinery itself exclude the endpoint? No.** Proposition 4.6's key exponent inequality is valid at `gamma=1/3`; the obstacle is the quantitative scale of *simultaneous* uniformity needed later, not a sign failure in the local-time estimate.
