## 5. B11 — dual/operator form of the second-moment core

The central same-track argument can be decomposed into a reversible-kernel identity plus an environment-shift identity. For the normalized symmetric density

\[
p_n^\omega(x,y)=\frac{P_x^\omega(X_n=y)}{\mu_\omega(y)},
\]

define the backbone overlap

\[
K_n^\omega(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z}p_n^\omega(x,y)^2,
\qquad
m_T^\omega(x)=\sum_{n\le T}K_n^\omega(x),
\]

and the degree-normalized collision count

\[
W_T=\sum_{n\le T}\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_n^1=X_n^2=x\}}{d_\omega(x)^2}.
\]

The proof-producing report gives, for each fixed environment,

\[
E_{0,0}^\omega W_T=m_T^\omega(0)
\]

and

\[
E_{0,0}^\omega W_T^2
\le m_T^\omega(0)
+2\sum_{n\le T}\sum_xp_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x).
\]

The object that an independent auditor must verify is therefore not a hidden probabilistic coupling but the bilinear form

\[
\mathcal B_{n,T}(\omega)
:=\sum_xp_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x).
\]

Translation covariance and symmetry reduce its **annealed** expectation to

\[
\mathbf E\,\mathcal B_{n,T}
=\mathbf E\bigl[m_T^\omega(0)K_n^\omega(0)\bigr],
\]

and summing in `n` gives

\[
\mathbf E\sum_{n\le T}\mathcal B_{n,T}
=\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2].
\]

This operator rewrite adds no theorem claim. Its value is audit localization: an auditor can reject or confirm the proof by checking (i) degree cancellation in the quenched two-walk expansion, (ii) covariance under horizontal shifts, and (iii) symmetry of the normalized kernel.
