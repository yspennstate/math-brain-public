## 4. The new idea before any code

No checker or search program was written in this run. The decisive change is conceptual.

The source proves a quenched second-moment theorem by taking a supremum over all possible collision centers. At the critical exponent, enforcing one environment event that is simultaneously good at every center and every relevant scale costs too much. Instead:

1. **weight backbone collisions by inverse squared degree**, so their quenched expectation is exactly the square of the symmetric heat kernel;
2. **average the second moment over the stationary i.i.d. environment before taking a supremum**;
3. use the **mass-transport principle on the backbone** to move the future-overlap factor from the random collision center back to the fixed origin;
4. use the source proof's **finite-scale stretched-exponential failure probabilities**, rather than its final almost-sure all-large-scales statement, to show that the one-root Green overlap has a square-integrable `O(log T)` envelope;
5. retain the source's lower heat-kernel estimate on disjoint cubic time windows to get a matching logarithmic first moment.

This is B12 (a conserved stationary mass), B28 (typical finite-scale environments instead of every center), and B3 (a stronger annealed weighted-collision theorem that implies the desired quenched source statement through a 0-1/ergodicity argument).

## 5. Normalized kernel and the weighted collision count

Fix an environment `omega` and write `G_omega=Comb(Z,H^omega)`. For a backbone vertex `x` abbreviate `(x,0)` by `x`, and let

\[
d_\omega(x)=\deg_{G_\omega}(x)\in\{2,3\}.
\]

The source and Barlow--Peres--Sousi use the symmetric transition density

\[
p_n^\omega(u,v):=\frac{P_u^\omega(X_n=v)}{\deg_\omega(v)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Reversibility gives

\[
p_n^\omega(u,v)=p_n^\omega(v,u).
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `T>=0` define the one-root backbone overlap

\[
K_n^\omega(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z}p_n^\omega(x,y)^2,
\qquad
m_T^\omega(x):=\sum_{n=0}^T K_n^\omega(x).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Let `X^1,X^2` be independent walks in the same environment, both started at backbone origin `0`. Define

\[
W_T:=\sum_{n=0}^T\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_n^1=X_n^2=x\}}{d_\omega(x)^2}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because `d_omega(x)` is either 2 or 3, `W_infinity=infinity` if and only if there are infinitely many backbone collisions. In particular `W_infinity=infinity` implies the source infinite-collision property.

### Lemma 5.1 — exact quenched first moment

For every environment,

\[
E_{0,0}^\omega W_T=m_T^\omega(0).
\tag{5.5}
\]

**Proof.** For a fixed time and backbone vertex,

\[
P_0^\omega(X_n=x)^2
=d_\omega(x)^2p_n^\omega(0,x)^2.
\]

Multiplication by the weight `d_omega(x)^(-2)` cancels the degree exactly. Summing in `x,n` gives (5.5). `square`

### Lemma 5.2 — exact quenched second-moment reduction

For every environment,

\[
E_{0,0}^\omega W_T^2
\le m_T^\omega(0)
 +2\sum_{n=0}^T\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z}
 p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x).
\tag{5.6}
\]

**Proof.** The diagonal contribution is bounded by the first moment because every weight is at most one. For `n<k`, condition on a common location `x` at time `n` and a common location `y` at time `k`. The two-walk probability is

\[
\bigl[d_\omega(x)p_n^\omega(0,x)\;d_\omega(y)p_{k-n}^\omega(x,y)\bigr]^2.
\]

Multiplying by the two weights `d_omega(x)^(-2)d_omega(y)^(-2)` again cancels both degree factors, leaving exactly

\[
p_n^\omega(0,x)^2p_{k-n}^\omega(x,y)^2.
\]

Summing first in `y` and then in future lags bounds the future factor by `m_T^omega(x)`, proving (5.6). `square`

The degree weights are important. Without them the environment shift below carries extra random degree factors and the exact conservation law is obscured.

## 6. B12 — the stationary mass-transport identity

Let `theta_j` shift the i.i.d. tooth environment by `j`. The law of `omega` is stationary and ergodic under `(theta_j)_{j in Z}`, and the kernel and `m_T` are translation-covariant.

For fixed `n,T` define the nonnegative covariant mass transport

\[
F_{n,T}(\omega,x,y)
:=p_n^\omega(x,y)^2m_T^\omega(y).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Stationarity on `Z` gives expected mass sent from the origin equals expected mass received at the origin:

\[
\mathbf E\sum_yF_{n,T}(\omega,0,y)
=
\mathbf E\sum_xF_{n,T}(\omega,x,0).
\tag{6.2}
\]

By symmetry (5.2), the right side is

\[
\mathbf E\left[m_T^\omega(0)
\sum_xp_n^\omega(0,x)^2\right]
=
\mathbf E\left[m_T^\omega(0)K_n^\omega(0)\right].
\tag{6.3}
\]

Summing (6.2) over `n<=T` therefore yields the exact identity

\[
\mathbf E\sum_{n\le T}\sum_x
p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)
=
\mathbf E\bigl[m_T^\omega(0)^2\bigr].
\tag{6.4}
\]

Combining with Lemma 5.2 gives the annealed second-moment inequality

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E W_T^2
\le
\mathbf E m_T^\omega(0)+2\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2].}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Here `mathbb E` denotes the environment-and-walk annealed law.

This is the central conservation law of the run. The source's worst-center supremum has disappeared completely: the random future collision center is returned to the origin by stationarity.

## 7. B28 — finite-scale source events give a square-integrable logarithmic envelope

The remaining task is to prove

\[
\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2]\le C(\log(T+2))^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The source proves its heat-kernel statements by first deriving finite-scale environment estimates and only then invoking Borel--Cantelli. We retain the finite-scale probabilities instead of discarding them.

### Lemma 7.1 — finite-scale critical heat-kernel event

At `gamma=1/3`, there exist deterministic constants `A,c,C,eta>0` and events `G_N`, depending only on the environment in the source scale-`N` construction, such that

\[
\mathbf P(G_N^c)\le C N^A e^{-cN^\eta},                         \tag{7.2}
\]

and on `G_N`, for time `n=N`, uniformly over every backbone site `y` reachable from the origin by time `n`,

\[
p_n^\omega(0,y)\le
\begin{cases}
C n^{-2/3},&|y|\le n^{1/3},\\
C|y|^{-2},&n^{1/3}<|y|\le n.
\end{cases}                                                     \tag{7.3}
\]

**Proof.** Choose the source parameters `delta_1,delta,delta_3,epsilon,...` strictly positive and sufficiently small, with

\[
\delta_1<1/3,
\qquad
3\delta_1\le\delta_3<1,
\qquad
\delta<1/3.
\tag{7.4}
\]

These choices are compatible with the source hierarchy: in Lemma 4.10 the requirement is `delta_3 >= 4 delta_1/(gamma+1)`, which becomes `delta_3>=3 delta_1`, and Proposition 4.6's key inequality (4.39) is strict for every `gamma in (0,1)`, hence at `gamma=1/3`.

Now inspect the source proof *before* Borel--Cantelli.

- Lemma 4.2, equation (4.8), gives for all required disks and dyadic tooth levels a failure bound
  \[
  C N^{2-\delta_1}(u(N)+1)
  \exp\{-cN^{\delta_1(1-\gamma)/(1+\gamma)}\}.
  \]
  At `gamma=1/3` the stretched-exponential exponent is `delta_1/2>0`.

- In Proposition 4.6, Case 1, equation (4.44) gives an environment failure at a fixed interval/scale at most `exp(-c n^{epsilon_1/2})`; the source then explicitly notes that the number of intervals and scales in the union is only polynomial. Since the local scales obey `n>=N^delta`, this is bounded by a polynomial times `exp(-cN^{delta epsilon_1/2})`.

- In Proposition 4.6, Case 2, the simultaneous deep-tooth event is equation (4.49), whose failure probability is
  \[
  \exp\{-cN^{\delta\epsilon\gamma/12}\};
  \]
  at `gamma=1/3` this has positive exponent `delta epsilon/36`.

The remaining estimates in Proposition 4.6 are random-walk estimates conditional on those environment events. Lemma 4.7 is then a deterministic quenched consequence of Proposition 4.6; Lemma 4.10 is a deterministic consequence of Lemma 4.7 and the resistance identity; Proposition 4.11 and Lemma 4.12 are deterministic consequences of that same chain. Taking the intersection of the finite-scale source events above therefore gives (7.2) for some `eta>0`.

On `G_N`, set physical time `n=N`. Since `delta_3<1`, the source requirement `n>=N^{delta_3}` is satisfied. Its Lemma 4.12 at `gamma=1/3` gives the first line of (7.3). Its Proposition 4.11 gives exponent

\[
\frac{3-\gamma}{1+\gamma}=2
\]

and hence the second line. A walk can move horizontally by at most `n` in `n` steps, so `|y|<=n` contains every backbone endpoint with nonzero transition probability. `square`

### Corollary 7.2 — one-root overlap is `1/n` outside a stretched-exponentially rare event

On `G_n`,

\[
K_n^\omega(0)\le \frac Cn.                                      \tag{7.5}
\]

Indeed, the near set has `O(n^{1/3})` sites, each square bounded by `Cn^{-4/3}`, and

\[
\sum_{|y|>n^{1/3}}|y|^{-4}\le Cn^{-1}.
\]

On `G_n^c`, use the profile-independent theorem (2.1):

\[
K_n^\omega(0)\le Cn^{-1/2}.                                    \tag{7.6}
\]

Thus, pathwise,

\[
K_n^\omega(0)
\le Cn^{-1}+Cn^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{G_n^c}.                         \tag{7.7}
\]

Define

\[
R(\omega):=\sum_{n\ge n_0} n^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{G_n^c}.           \tag{7.8}
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz for indicators and (7.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf E[R^2]
&=\sum_{m,n}(mn)^{-1/2}
\mathbf P(G_m^c\cap G_n^c)\\
&\le
\left(\sum_n n^{-1/2}\mathbf P(G_n^c)^{1/2}\right)^2
<\infty.                                                        \tag{7.9}
\end{aligned}
\]

Summing (7.7) gives the pathwise envelope

\[
m_T^\omega(0)\le C\log(T+2)+CR(\omega),                       \tag{7.10}
\]

and hence exactly the desired

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2]\le C(\log(T+2))^2.}                 \tag{7.11}
\]

This is the point at which replacing “all centers in one quenched environment” by “the stationary origin in environment average” pays for the critical endpoint. The exceptional scales may exist, but their total contribution is square integrable.

## 8. B3 — logarithmic first moment from disjoint source windows

We next prove a matching lower bound along a deterministic sequence.

At `gamma=1/3`, source Lemma 4.13 gives

\[
E_{v,w}^\omega H_N^1\ge c_{14}N^{(3\gamma-1)/(1+\gamma)}=c_{14},
\tag{8.1}
\]

for all sufficiently large `N`, uniformly over the source-admissible starts; in particular for `v=w=0`. More specifically, its proof obtains this lower bound by summing only over the time window

\[
I_N=[\lfloor c_lN^3\rfloor,\lfloor c_uN^3\rfloor].             \tag{8.2}
\]

Choose `b>1` so large that

\[
c_lb^3>c_u,                                                     \tag{8.3}
\]

and set `N_j=floor(b^j)`. The windows `I_{N_j}` are disjoint for large `j`.

The source count is an unweighted stopped backbone-collision count. Every stopped collision counted there is also an unrestricted backbone collision, and for every backbone vertex `d_omega(x)<=3`, so our weight in (5.4) is at least `1/9`. Therefore the contribution of our `W` from `I_{N_j}` has quenched expectation at least `c_{14}/9` for every sufficiently large `j`, for `P`-a.e. environment.

Let

\[
T_M=\lfloor c_uN_M^3\rfloor.
\]

Then, for `P`-a.e. `omega`,

\[
\liminf_{M\to\infty}\frac{m_{T_M}^\omega(0)}{M}\ge c_*>0.     \tag{8.4}
\]

Since the variables are nonnegative, Fatou's lemma gives

\[
\liminf_{M\to\infty}
\frac{\mathbf E[m_{T_M}^\omega(0)]}{M}
\ge c_*.                                                        \tag{8.5}
\]

As `log T_M` is asymptotic to `3M log b`, there is `c>0` such that for all large `M`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf E[m_{T_M}^\omega(0)]\ge c\log T_M.}                   \tag{8.6}
\]

By Lemma 5.1 this is the annealed first moment of `W_{T_M}`.

## 9. Full proof of the source endpoint

We now combine the preceding lemmas.

From (5.5) and (8.6),

\[
\mathbb E W_{T_M}\ge c\log T_M.                                \tag{9.1}
\]

From (6.5) and (7.11),

\[
\mathbb E W_{T_M}^2\le C(\log T_M)^2.                          \tag{9.2}
\]

Paley--Zygmund therefore yields a deterministic `rho>0` such that for every sufficiently large `M`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(
W_{T_M}\ge\frac12\mathbb E W_{T_M}
\right)\ge\rho.                                                \tag{9.3}
\]

The threshold on the left tends to infinity. Since `W_T` is nondecreasing in `T`, for every fixed `L` and all sufficiently large `M`, event (9.3) is contained in `{W_infinity>=L}`. Hence

\[
\mathbb P(W_\infty\ge L)\ge\rho\qquad\text{for every }L.
\]

Letting `L->infinity`,

\[
\mathbb P(W_\infty=\infty)\ge\rho>0.                           \tag{9.4}
\]

This is an annealed statement so far.

For `P`-a.e. environment every tooth is finite. Such a comb is a recurrent connected graph: all teeth are finite pendant branches, while either horizontal ray has infinite unit resistance to infinity (equivalently one may use Nash--Williams cutsets along the backbone).

For a fixed recurrent environment, Barlow--Peres--Sousi Proposition 2.1 implies that the quenched probability of infinitely many collisions is in `{0,1}` and that the alternative is independent of the common starting vertex. Let

\[
q(\omega)=P_{0,0}^\omega(\text{infinitely many collisions}).
\]

Then `q(omega) in {0,1}` a.s. Translation of the environment by `j` converts a start at the shifted origin into a start at `(j,0)` in the original comb. Since the 0-1 alternative is independent of starting vertex,

\[
q(\theta_j\omega)=q(\omega),\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.              \tag{9.5}
\]

The i.i.d. environment is ergodic under these shifts, so `q` is almost surely constant. Equation (9.4) implies

\[
\mathbf E q(\omega)>0,
\]

and therefore the only possible ergodic value is

\[
q(\omega)=1\quad\text{for P-a.e. }\omega.                     \tag{9.6}
\]

Because `W_infinity=infinity` already means infinitely many **backbone** collisions, (9.6) proves the source's unrestricted two-walk infinite-collision property a fortiori.

This proves the theorem. `square`

## 11. Obstruction-first checks and failed routes

1. **Uniform `K_n<=Cn^{-1}` for every tooth profile is false.** The zero-tooth comb is just `Z`, whose backbone `L^2` mass is order `n^{-1/2}`. The critical improvement genuinely comes from the heavy-tailed random environment.

2. **The previous `O((log N)^2)` all-center cutoff route is unnecessarily strong.** The source itself explains that simultaneous uniform estimates are what make the endpoint difficult. This run avoids that obligation rather than forcing it.

The finite-scale failure bounds are essential for the annealed second moment.

4. **Unweighted collision counting obscures stationarity.** The factors `deg(x)^2` in collision probabilities move with the collision center. The inverse-degree-squared weighting makes the symmetric kernel appear exactly and is what makes (6.4) clean.

5. **Positive annealed probability alone is not the source conclusion.** The final recurrent-graph 0-1 law and i.i.d.-shift ergodicity are both required to upgrade positive environment measure to `P`-a.e. environments.

6. **No claim is made for three-walk collisions or another tail exponent.** The theorem is exactly the source boundary for two walks.
