## 5. B6 — explicit endpoint parameter gate for the source upper heat-kernel input

The prior proof used the source's pre-Borel--Cantelli estimates to construct finite-scale good events `G_N` with stretched-exponential failure. A possible hidden defect would be an incompatible parameter requirement at `gamma=1/3`. The source permits `delta_1` and `delta` arbitrarily small in the relevant environment/exit estimates, and Lemma 4.10 requires

\[
\delta_3\ge \frac{4\delta_1}{1+\gamma}.
\]

At `gamma=1/3`, this is `delta_3 >= 3 delta_1`. Take, for example,

\[
\boxed{\delta_1=\frac1{24},\qquad \delta=\frac1{24},\qquad \delta_3=\frac16.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
3\delta_1=\frac18<\frac16=\delta_3<\frac12.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The first inequality supplies Lemma 4.10. The second gives the inequality used inside Lemma 4.12 after setting the outer scale equal to the time, namely

\[
N^{\delta_3}\le n^{1/2}\quad\text{when }N=n.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The exit scale used by Lemma 4.7 is `k \asymp n^{(1+gamma)/4}=n^{1/3}`. With `N=n`,

\[
k\asymp N^{1/3}\ge N^{\delta_1},\qquad k\ge N^{\delta},
\tag{5.4}
\]

so both the environment-disk lower cutoff and the local-time/exit lower cutoff are satisfied.

Now inspect the source finite-scale probabilities before Borel--Cantelli. At `gamma=1/3`:

1. Equation (4.8) has stretched-exponential exponent
   \[
   \delta_1\frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma}=\frac{\delta_1}{2}=\frac1{48}>0.

2. Equation (4.44), after taking the smallest allowed local scale `N^delta`, retains exponent `delta epsilon_1/2>0` for the source's small positive `epsilon_1`.

The only additional unions described in the source are polynomial in the outer scale. Hence for this explicit parameter hierarchy the required finite-scale event can be chosen with

## 6. B13 — monotone finite-horizon-to-infinite-horizon lemma

Let

\[
W_T=\sum_{n\le T}\sum_x
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_n^1=X_n^2=(x,0)\}}{\deg(x,0)^2}.
\]

The previous proof gives an unbounded deterministic sequence `T_j` and constants `a,c_*>0` such that

\[
\mathbb P\left(W_{T_j}\ge a\log T_j\right)\ge c_*.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The passage from (6.1) to `P(W_infinity=infinity)>0` needs no independence. The key is simply that `W_T` is nondecreasing in `T`.

Fix any integer `M`. Choose `j(M)` with `a log T_{j(M)} >= M`. Then

\[
\{W_{T_{j(M)}}\ge a\log T_{j(M)}\}\subseteq\{W_\infty\ge M\},
\]

so

\[
\mathbb P(W_\infty\ge M)\ge c_*.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The events `{W_infinity >= M}` decrease as `M` increases. By continuity from above,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(W_\infty=\infty)
=\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathbb P(W_\infty\ge M)
\ge c_*>0.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus the annealed second-moment argument needs neither independent scale blocks nor conditional Borel--Cantelli. Because every backbone degree is in `{2,3}`, every backbone collision contributes at least `1/9`; hence `W_infinity=infinity` implies infinitely many actual collisions.

**B13 outcome:** closes an implicit logical passage in the proof and removes a possible but unnecessary cross-scale-independence obligation.

## 7. B27 — exact annealed-to-quenched quantifier swap

The source target is quenched almost sure, whereas the weighted-collision argument first proves a positive **annealed** probability. The quantifier swap is valid because of two zero-one mechanisms at two different levels.

For an environment `omega`, let

\[
q(\omega)=P_{0,0}^{\omega}(Z=\infty),
\]

where `Z` is the number of all collisions. Every realized random comb has finite teeth attached to the recurrent backbone `Z`; electrically, the pendant teeth create no additional route to infinity, so the graph is recurrent.

By Barlow--Peres--Sousi Proposition 2.1, for every fixed recurrent environment,

\[
q(\omega)\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Moreover their proposition gives the common-start independence needed here: if one common starting vertex has positive infinite-collision probability, all common starting vertices in the relevant communicating/parity class have probability one. Thus the graph property

\[
A=\{\omega:q(\omega)=1\}
\tag{7.2}
\]

is invariant under horizontal shifts of the tooth environment.

The weighted proof and (6.3) give

\[
\mathbb P_{\mathrm{annealed}}(Z=\infty)
=\mathbf E[q(\omega)]>0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Because of (7.1), (7.3) says `P(A)>0`. Since the tooth sequence is i.i.d., horizontal shifts are ergodic, and since `A` is shift-invariant,

\[
\boxed{\mathbf P(A)=1.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore for almost every source environment,

\[
P_{0,0}^{\omega}(Z=\infty)=1.
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is exactly the source quantifier order.

**B27 outcome:** the annealed-first strategy is source-valid; the quantifier swap is not an illicit exchange of `for almost every environment` and `with positive walk probability`.

## 8. Failed routes / checks that did not improve the theorem

1. **Reversible-random-rooted-graph shortcut.** Hutchcroft--Peres prove infinite collisions for recurrent reversible random rooted graphs, but the source comb rooted deterministically at `(0,0)` is not a reversible random rooted graph under one walk step: a vertical first step changes the root type. The natural vertex-size-biased rooting is also obstructed here by the infinite mean tooth height at `gamma=1/3`. So this theorem is useful context, not a shortcut to the source boundary.

2. **Monotonicity in tooth heights.** The phase orientation is opposite the naive useful direction: heavier/longer teeth suppress collisions. A proof at a lighter-tail exponent `gamma>1/3` therefore does not transfer monotonically to the critical heavier-tail environment.

3. **Direct use of the source Proposition 4.14.** Its short-lag estimate (4.120) is precisely where the authors need `gamma>1/3`; at the endpoint that proof cannot simply be quoted. The weighted stationary one-root argument remains essential.
