## 5. B5 — exponent deformation is diagnostic, but not a valid endpoint proof

A natural idea is to take `gamma_j downarrow 1/3`, use Theorem 4.1-(1) for each `gamma_j>1/3`, and pass to the critical law. This does **not** prove the source endpoint under the admitted hypothesis.

The issue is not merely technical. The hypothesis

\[
\mathbf P(H_0>z)\sim Cz^{-1/3}
\]

specifies only the tail asymptotic. It does not place the critical distribution in a canonical monotone coupling with arbitrary laws having tails `z^{-gamma_j}`. Even if one modifies the far tail to manufacture stochastic order, the law on every finite range of tooth heights is unrestricted by the asymptotic and may be changed independently. Moreover, Theorem 4.1 supplies almost-sure conclusions under different environment measures, with random thresholds and constants that are not asserted uniform as `gamma downarrow 1/3`. Weak convergence of the one-site laws alone does not transport an infinite-time collision event.

So the deformation cannot legitimately exchange

\[
\gamma\downarrow 1/3
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{\text{infinitely many collisions}\}.
\]

What the deformation *does* reveal is useful: the preliminary source inequalities used in the upper heat-kernel chain are nonsingular at the endpoint. In particular, equation (4.39)

\[
3-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}<\frac{4}{\gamma+1}
\]

is strict for every `gamma in (0,1)`, including `1/3`; Lemma 4.10 then has exponent `(3-gamma)/4=2/3`, and Proposition 4.11 has far exponent `(3-gamma)/(gamma+1)=2`. The genuinely singular published step occurs later in Proposition 4.14, equation (4.120), where a positive-power short-lag loss is absorbed using `3 gamma - 1>0`.

**B5 outcome:** endpoint-by-law-deformation is rejected as non-source-valid. The exercise confirms that the obstruction is the final all-center second-moment architecture, not a degeneration of the preliminary heat-kernel exponents.

## 6. B14 — horizontal-shift quotient gives the mass-transport identity without Palm bias

Fix the critical i.i.d. environment and let `theta_j` be horizontal translation by `j`. For backbone points write

\[
p_n^\omega(x,y)=\frac{P_x^\omega(X_n=y)}{d_\omega(y)},
\qquad
K_n^\omega(x)=\sum_y p_n^\omega(x,y)^2,
\qquad
m_T^\omega(x)=\sum_{n\le T}K_n^\omega(x).
\]

The i.i.d. law is invariant under every `theta_j`, and covariance gives, with the obvious convention for the shift direction,

\[
p_n^{\theta_j\omega}(a,b)=p_n^\omega(a+j,b+j),
\qquad
m_T^{\theta_j\omega}(a)=m_T^\omega(a+j).
\tag{6.1}
\]

For each fixed backbone site `x`, stationarity therefore gives

\[
\mathbf E\left[p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)\right]
=
\mathbf E\left[p_n^\omega(-x,0)^2m_T^\omega(0)\right].
\tag{6.2}
\]

The normalized kernel is symmetric by reversibility, so

\[
p_n^\omega(-x,0)=p_n^\omega(0,-x).
\]

Summing (6.2) in `x` and renaming `-x` yields

\[
\mathbf E\sum_x p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)
=
\mathbf E\left[m_T^\omega(0)K_n^\omega(0)\right].
\tag{6.3}
\]

Summing in `n<=T` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf E\sum_{n\le T}\sum_xp_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)
=
\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2].}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is the earlier mass-transport identity, now derived term-by-term solely from the `Z` action. No random-root/Palm theorem is being invoked. The deterministic root at zero is harmless because the *environment law* is stationary under backbone shifts. The inverse-square degree weight in the collision count is what removes the random degree factors before (6.4); without that normalization the quotient identity would contain an extra local weight.

**B14 outcome:** successful independent derivation of the central conservation law; no hidden root-size bias is present.

## 7. B19 — finite-demand critical heat-kernel compiler

### 7.1 The concern being removed

The proof-producing report extracted from the source a finite-scale good event `G_N` of stretched-exponentially high probability, then used the source's Lemma 4.10 / Proposition 4.11 / Lemma 4.12 chain at physical time `n=N`.

A subtle point is worth making completely explicit. Proposition 4.6 is stated for **all** `t in (0,1]`, while its Case-1 proof first obtains equation (4.44) for a fixed `t` and then performs a polynomial union over intervals and integer spatial scales. For the present theorem we do not need to justify a union over uncountably many `t`. At one physical time there are only finitely many downstream exit estimates that are ever queried.

This run replaces the blanket event by a finite-demand event and, additionally, observes that the present one-root overlap estimate does not need Lemma 4.12 at all.

### 7.2 Explicit critical parameters

Work at `gamma=1/3`. Fix, for instance,

\[
\delta_1=\delta=\frac1{24},
\qquad
\delta_3=\frac16.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The Lemma 4.10 compatibility condition is

\[
\delta_3\ge \frac{4\delta_1}{1+\gamma}=3\delta_1=\frac18,
\]

which holds. Also `delta,delta_1<1/3`, so the natural spatial scale

\[
\ell_N\asymp N^{(1+\gamma)/4}=N^{1/3}
\]

lies above both source lower cutoffs `N^delta` and `N^{delta_1}`.

### 7.3 What must be demanded at physical time N

To bound

\[
K_N^\omega(0)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z}p_N^\omega(0,y)^2,
\]

we need only the following finite family of source inputs.

**Demand A: geometry.** The Lemma 4.2 / Corollaries 4.3--4.4 good-disk conclusions for the centers and integer radii used below, all of which lie inside `[-N,N]` and have radius at least `N^{delta_1}`. The source's pre-Borel--Cantelli equation (4.8) already controls the simultaneous failure of the relevant disk family by

\[
N^{O(1)}\exp\{-cN^{\delta_1(1-\gamma)/(1+\gamma)}\}
=
N^{O(1)}e^{-cN^{1/48}}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

**Demand B: the on-diagonal exit scale.** Lemma 4.10 uses Lemma 4.7 at radius `ell_N asy N^{1/3}` and at a constant-order normalized time. This is one normalized-time demand for each backbone center at which an on-diagonal estimate will be used. There are only `O(N)` such centers.

**Demand C: the far off-diagonal exit scales.** For each integer distance

\[
k\in[\lceil N^{1/3}\rceil,N],
\]

Proposition 4.11 invokes Lemma 4.7 at radius comparable with `k`, with normalized time of order

\[
t_k\asymp \frac{N}{k^{4/(1+\gamma)}}=\frac{N}{k^3}\in(0,O(1)].
\tag{7.3}
\]

For the one-root bound there are at most polynomially many triples `(center,k,t_k)` even if we include the reversed term in Proposition 4.11. Thus Case 1 of Proposition 4.6 requires equation (4.44) only at a finite polynomial-size set of `t_k` values. When `t_k` falls into Case 2, the source's event (4.49) is already simultaneous over all integer radii and intervals, and the subsequent random-walk estimates are deterministic conditional on that environment event.

There is therefore no uncountable-union issue in this endpoint application.

### 7.4 Probability of the finite-demand event

For Case 1, equation (4.44) says that a fixed demanded quenched estimate fails as an environment statement with probability at most

\[
e^{-c r^{\varepsilon_1/2}},
\]

where the local integer radius `r` is at least `N^delta`. A polynomial union over all demanded centers/radii/times therefore costs at most

\[
N^{A_1}\exp\{-cN^{\delta\varepsilon_1/2}\}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For Case 2, equation (4.49) gives a simultaneous environment failure bound

\[
\exp\{-cN^{\delta\varepsilon\gamma/12}\}
=
\exp\{-cN^{\delta\varepsilon/36}\}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Intersecting (7.2), (7.4), (7.5) gives a finite-demand event `G_N^*` with

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P((G_N^*)^c)
\le C N^A e^{-cN^\eta},
\qquad
\eta=\min\left\{\frac1{48},\frac{\delta\varepsilon_1}{2},\frac{\delta\varepsilon}{36}\right\}>0.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

No final Borel--Cantelli statement from the source is used to obtain (7.6); it is assembled from the source's displayed pre-Borel--Cantelli finite-scale probabilities.

### 7.5 Deterministic compilation on G_N^*

Conditional on `G_N^*`, the source proof of Proposition 4.6 yields the demanded instances of Lemma 4.7. Corollaries 4.3--4.4 and Lemma 4.5 are deterministic consequences of Demand A. Hence Lemma 4.10 can be executed at time `N` for every needed backbone center, giving

\[
p_N^\omega(z,z)\le C N^{-2/3}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

For **near** backbone sites `|y|<=N^{1/3}`, no Lemma 4.12 is necessary. Symmetry and Cauchy--Schwarz for the normalized kernel give directly

\[
p_N^\omega(0,y)
\le \sqrt{p_N^\omega(0,0)p_N^\omega(y,y)}
\le C N^{-2/3}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

For **far** backbone sites `N^{1/3}<|y|<=N`, the finite family of demanded exit estimates allows the source proof of Proposition 4.11 to run, yielding

\[
p_N^\omega(0,y)\le C|y|^{-2}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

A walk cannot have horizontal displacement exceeding `N` in `N` steps, so these two ranges cover every backbone endpoint with nonzero transition probability. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_N^\omega(0)
&\le C N^{1/3}N^{-4/3}
 +C\sum_{|y|>N^{1/3}}|y|^{-4}\\
&\le \frac{C}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.10}
\]

This is exactly the critical one-root estimate needed by the proof-producing report.

On `(G_N^*)^c`, retain the already proved profile-independent bound

\[
K_N^\omega(0)\le C N^{-1/2}.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Thus

\[
K_N^\omega(0)
\le CN^{-1}+CN^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{(G_N^*)^c}.
\tag{7.12}
\]

The stretched-exponential bound (7.6) implies

\[
\mathbf E\left[\left(\sum_{N\ge N_0}N^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{(G_N^*)^c}\right)^2\right]<\infty,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2]\le C(\log(T+2))^2.}
\tag{7.13}
\]

This recovers the only delicate annealed upper-moment input of the internal endpoint proof while demanding only finitely many source estimates at each scale.

### 7.6 Why this is a real strengthening of the internal proof

The earlier report cited the full Lemma 4.12 near estimate and summarized the source finite-scale extraction globally. The present compiler removes two avoidable dependencies:

1. it never needs uniformity over an uncountable family of normalized times; only polynomially many `(center,radius,t)` demands occur at a fixed physical time;
2. it never needs Lemma 4.12 for the backbone one-root overlap; Lemma 4.10 plus Cauchy--Schwarz supplies the near range, and Proposition 4.11 supplies the far range.

So the endpoint proof depends on a strictly smaller portion of the source argument than previously recorded.

**B19 outcome:** SUCCESS. The finite-scale upper-moment extraction is now an explicit finite-demand deterministic compiler with stretched-exponential environment failure.
