## 5. B2 — attempt to break the proof

Fix an environment `omega`. Write the source-normalized symmetric kernel

\[
p_n^\omega(u,v)=\frac{P_u^\omega(X_n=v)}{\deg_\omega(v)}.
\]

For a backbone root `x`, define

\[
K_n^\omega(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z}p_n^\omega(x,y)^2,
\qquad
m_T^\omega(x)=\sum_{n=0}^T K_n^\omega(x).
\]

For two independent walks from the origin define

\[
W_T=\sum_{n=0}^T\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z}
\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_n^1=X_n^2=(x,0)\}}{\deg_\omega(x,0)^2}.
\]

### Attack 1: degree normalization could invalidate the first/second moment algebra

It does not. Since

\[
P_0^\omega(X_n=(x,0))=\deg_\omega(x,0)p_n^\omega(0,x),
\]

the weight cancels exactly and

\[
E_{0,0}^\omega W_T=m_T^\omega(0).
\]

For `n<k`, conditioning on common backbone locations `x` and `y`, both degree factors cancel again, leaving exactly

\[
p_n^\omega(0,x)^2p_{k-n}^\omega(x,y)^2.
\]

Thus

\[
E_{0,0}^\omega W_T^2
\le m_T^\omega(0)+2\sum_{n\le T}\sum_xp_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The diagonal is even smaller because the squared collision weight is at most the collision weight.

### Attack 2: the stationary mass transport could fail because the graph measure is degree-biased

The environment shift acts on the **backbone index**, whose law is ordinary i.i.d. and translation-stationary; the degree normalization has already put the transition density in symmetric form. Hence for

\[
F_{n,T}(\omega,x,y)=p_n^\omega(x,y)^2m_T^\omega(y)
\]

one has the ordinary `Z` mass-transport identity. Therefore

\[
\mathbf E\sum_xp_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)
=\mathbf E\big[m_T^\omega(0)K_n^\omega(0)\big].
\]

Summing `n` gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbf E\sum_{n,x}p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)
=\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2].}
\tag{5.2}
\]

No Palm or degree-biased environment is being substituted.

### Attack 3: the endpoint upper heat-kernel event might only be almost-sure, with no usable probability tail

This is the main vulnerability identified in the previous proof. Section 6 below resolves it directly from the source before its Borel–Cantelli step.

### Attack 4: the logarithmic lower moment might secretly require one environment to be good on all selected scales

It does not for the annealed lower bound. On each selected scale `N_j`, a finite-scale lower-heat-kernel event of probability tending to one contributes a deterministic positive constant to the scale block. Linearity of expectation then sums these constants; independence across scales is unnecessary. Section 7 makes this explicit.

### Attack 5: parity could kill the lower block

The comb is bipartite, so the source imposes the usual parity restriction. In every cubic time window, a fixed positive fraction of time/site pairs have compatible parity. This changes only the absolute constant and not the scale power.

### Attack 6: the tail assumption is only asymptotic, not an exact power law

The source's environment arguments already start from the asymptotic hypothesis and replace it at large scales by two-sided constant bounds. At `gamma=1/3`, all exponents below remain strictly positive. No exact Pareto law is inserted.

### Attack 7: positive annealed probability need not mean almost every environment

Let `A(omega)` be the event that the recurrent graph `Comb(Z,H^omega)` has the infinite collision property. The previous proof gives positive annealed probability of infinitely many **backbone** collisions, hence positive annealed probability of infinitely many collisions. For each fixed recurrent comb, Barlow–Peres–Sousi Proposition 2.1 gives the collision event quenched probability `0` or `1` (and start-independence in the stated sense). The property `A(omega)` is invariant under shifting the i.i.d. tooth environment. Ergodicity therefore upgrades positive environment measure to full environment measure. This step uses the 0-1 law for the full collision event, not a nonexistent 0-1 law specifically for backbone collisions.

No counterexample to the proof survived these attacks.

## 6. B22 — recover the finite-scale source probabilities, not only their final a.s. form

This is the key audit of the previous run's most vulnerable step.

Up through Subsection 4.6, the source explicitly works under `gamma in (0,1)`, so `gamma=1/3` is inside the standing range of the environment, local-time, exit-time and heat-kernel lemmas. The later strict `gamma>1/3` occurs in the collision second-moment summation, not in the signs below.

Choose the source parameters `delta_1, delta, epsilon, epsilon_1` strictly positive and sufficiently small. At `gamma=1/3`:

1. **Lemma 4.2 / equation (4.8).** Before Borel–Cantelli, the simultaneous disk/level environment failure is bounded by a polynomial factor times
   \[
   \exp\{-cN^{\delta_1(1-\gamma)/(1+\gamma)}\}
   =\exp\{-cN^{\delta_1/2}\}.
   \tag{6.1}
   \]

2. **Proposition 4.6, Case 1 / equation (4.44).** The source obtains a fixed interval/scale environment failure at most
   \[
   \exp\{-c n^{\epsilon_1/2}\}.
   \]
   It then explicitly says that the number of intervals and scales in the union is only polynomial. Since the local scale obeys `n >= N^delta`, the full outer-scale failure is bounded by
   \[
   N^A\exp\{-cN^{\delta\epsilon_1/2}\}.
   \tag{6.2}
   \]

3. **Proposition 4.6, Case 2 / equation (4.49).** The simultaneous deep-tooth event fails with probability at most
   \[
   \exp\{-cN^{\delta\epsilon\gamma/12}\}
   =\exp\{-cN^{\delta\epsilon/36}\}.
   \tag{6.3}
   \]

4. The exponent comparison used to obtain the Case-1 Bernstein gain is source equation (4.39),
   \[
   3-\frac{2\gamma}{1+\gamma}<\frac{4}{1+\gamma},
   \]
   which the source itself states for every `gamma in (0,1)`. It is strict at `gamma=1/3`.

Thus, for some deterministic `A,c,eta>0`, one may define a finite-scale event `G_N` on which the deterministic implications used in Corollary 4.4, Proposition 4.6, Lemma 4.7, Lemma 4.10, Proposition 4.11 and Lemma 4.12 all hold at the required scales, with

\[
\boxed{\mathbf P(G_N^c)\le C N^A e^{-cN^\eta}.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is not an inference from the final random threshold `N_2(omega)`: it is recovered from the source's explicit pre-Borel–Cantelli estimates.

Now set the outer spatial scale equal to the time, `N=n`. The source condition `n >= N^{delta_3}` is automatic for any chosen `delta_3<1`, and every backbone point reachable from the origin by time `n` lies in `[-N,N]`. On `G_n`, Lemma 4.12 gives for `|y| <= n^{1/3}`

\[
p_n^\omega(0,y)\le Cn^{-2/3},
\]

while Proposition 4.11 gives for `|y|>n^{1/3}`

\[
p_n^\omega(0,y)\le C|y|^{-2}.
\]

Consequently

\[
K_n^\omega(0)
\le C\left(n^{1/3}n^{-4/3}+\sum_{k>n^{1/3}}k^{-4}\right)
\le \frac Cn.
\tag{6.5}
\]

On `G_n^c`, the previously proved profile-independent bound remains

\[
K_n^\omega(0)\le Cn^{-1/2}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Let `q_n=P(G_n^c)` and

\[
R_T=\sum_{n\le T}n^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{G_n^c}.
\]

Then

\[
m_T^\omega(0)\le C\log(T+2)+CR_T.
\]

Without assuming any independence between the bad-scale events,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf E R_T^2
&=\sum_{n,m\le T}(nm)^{-1/2}\mathbf P(G_n^c\cap G_m^c)\\
&\le\sum_{n,m}(nm)^{-1/2}\sqrt{q_nq_m}\\
&=\left(\sum_n n^{-1/2}\sqrt{q_n}\right)^2<\infty,
\end{aligned}
\]

because of (6.4). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)^2]\le C(\log(T+2))^2.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This closes the explicit finite-scale probability gap that was highlighted for audit in the previous report.

## 7. B25 — independent double counts for both moments

### 7.1 Direct translation proof of the mass identity

For each fixed `n,T`, stationarity gives termwise

\[
\mathbf E[p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)]
=\mathbf E[p_n^\omega(-x,0)^2m_T^\omega(0)].
\]

Using kernel symmetry and summing over `x`,

\[
\sum_x\mathbf E[p_n^\omega(0,x)^2m_T^\omega(x)]
=\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)K_n^\omega(0)],
\]

which is (5.2) without invoking mass-transport terminology. This independent derivation removes a possible ambiguity about the reference measure.

### 7.2 Rebuild the logarithmic lower first moment from disjoint source windows

At `gamma=1/3`, source Lemma 4.9 has heat-kernel exponent

\[
\frac{3-\gamma}{4}=\frac23
\]

and natural time scale

\[
N^{4/(1+\gamma)}=N^3.
\]

Use the finite-scale good event underlying Lemma 4.9. For constants `0<a<b<infinity`, on that event and for a fixed positive fraction of parity-compatible pairs,

\[
p_n^\omega(0,y)\ge c n^{-2/3}\asymp cN^{-2}
\]

for `n in [aN^3,bN^3]` and a fixed positive fraction of the `Theta(N)` backbone roots in the central rectangle. Hence for each such time

\[
K_n^\omega(0)\ge cN\cdot N^{-4}=cN^{-3}.
\]

Summing over the `Theta(N^3)` compatible times in that block gives

\[
\sum_{n\in[aN^3,bN^3]}K_n^\omega(0)\ge c_0
\tag{7.1}
\]

on the good event. The finite-scale environment probability tends to one by the same pre-Borel–Cantelli source estimates, so for all sufficiently large `N`,

\[
\mathbf E\sum_{n\in[aN^3,bN^3]}K_n^\omega(0)\ge c_0/2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Choose a geometric sequence `N_j=r^j` with `r` large enough that the time blocks `[aN_j^3,bN_j^3]` are disjoint. If `T` contains `J` such blocks, then `J=Theta(log T)`, and linearity of expectation gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbf E[m_T^\omega(0)]\ge c\log(T+2).}
\tag{7.3}
\]

No cross-scale independence and no simultaneous all-scale environment event is required.

### 7.3 Paley–Zygmund and the quenched conclusion

Combining (5.1), (5.2), (6.7), and (7.3),

\[
\mathbb E W_T\ge c\log T,
\qquad
\mathbb E W_T^2\le C(\log T)^2.
\]

Paley–Zygmund therefore gives constants `a,c_*>0` and an unbounded sequence `T_j` such that

\[
\mathbb P(W_{T_j}\ge a\log T_j)\ge c_*.
\]

Since `W_T` is increasing,

\[
\mathbb P(W_\infty=\infty)\ge c_*>0.
\]

Backbone degrees lie in `{2,3}`, so each backbone collision has weight at least `1/9`; thus `W_infty=infinity` implies infinitely many actual collisions. Every realized finite-tooth comb is recurrent (finite pendant teeth attached to the recurrent line do not create a finite-resistance route to infinity). Barlow–Peres–Sousi Proposition 2.1 then gives the quenched collision 0-1 law. Finally, the property that the shifted environment graph has the infinite-collision property is shift-invariant, so i.i.d. ergodicity upgrades positive environment measure to one.

This independently reconstructs the endpoint theorem.
