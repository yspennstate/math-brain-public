## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The source's strict-regime proof asks for a scale-uniform positive collision probability because it drives the second moment to the same polynomial order as the square of the first moment. At the boundary this is unnecessarily strong.

The key observation of this run is:

> **At geometric scales, a collision probability of order `1/(m log m)` is already enough.**

Thus an endpoint proof may tolerate a logarithmic or `log N log log N` loss in the second moment. This changes the target from “remove every endpoint logarithm” to “keep the backbone self-overlap no worse than the borderline divergent-series budget.”

No code was written because there is no finite candidate worth certifying yet.

## 4. B20 — locate exactly where the strict proof loses the endpoint

Let `H_N` denote the source Section-4 collision count on the backbone, stopped at its outer scale, with time horizon

\[
T_N=\lfloor c_{13}N^{4/(1+\gamma)}\rfloor.
\]

At `gamma=1/3`, `T_N` is of order `N^3`.

The source first-moment formula in Lemma 4.13 has scale

\[
\mathbb E H_N \gtrsim N^{(3\gamma-1)/(\gamma+1)}.
\]

Algebraically this becomes order one at `gamma=1/3`. This report does **not** silently assume Lemma 4.13 has already been proved at the endpoint: every preceding environment and heat-kernel estimate must be audited there. Instead, the endpoint first-moment lower bound is isolated as an explicit hypothesis of the sufficient criterion in Section 6 below.

The second moment in Proposition 4.14 is driven by the quenched backbone self-overlap

\[
G_N(\omega)
:=
\sup_{|z|\le N}
\sum_{r=1}^{T_N}
\sum_{|y|\le N}
 p_r^\omega((z,0),(y,0))^2,
\]

where the source uses its symmetric degree-normalized heat kernel.

The source splits the time sum into short and long lags. The decisive endpoint arithmetic is:

- in the long-lag near/far estimates, the time exponent is
  \[
  -\frac{5-3\gamma}{4};
  \]
  at `gamma=1/3` this is exactly `-1`, hence the corresponding sum is logarithmic;
- the explicitly strict use `gamma>1/3` in the displayed short-lag step (source equation (4.120)) is the choice of a fixed positive power cutoff `N^{4 delta_4}` whose crude contribution can be swallowed by the positive polynomial first-moment exponent. That exponent vanishes at the boundary.

So the visible endpoint obstruction is not that the long-lag calculation explodes polynomially. It is that the proof uses a **power-scale short-lag cutoff and a worst-center supremum**, while the boundary budget is only logarithmic.

This suggests that the correct endpoint object is the backbone overlap itself, not another global heat-kernel exponent.

## 5. B20/B21 — a critical logarithmic-uniformity geometry lemma

The following is a genuine new lemma obtained by rerunning the concentration argument of source Lemma 4.2 at the critical exponent. It is a geometric/environment statement, not yet the complete heat-kernel endpoint theorem.

### Lemma 5.1 (polylogarithmic uniform good-disk threshold at `gamma=1/3`)

Use the source's definition of an `ell`-good disk and its dyadic tooth-height bins. Assume

\[
\mathbb P(H_0>z)\sim C z^{-1/3}.
\]

There is a deterministic finite constant `A` such that, almost surely, for every sufficiently large `N`, every source disk `D_k^z` contained in `D_N` with

\[
k\ge A(\log N)^2
\]

is `ell`-good simultaneously for every source-admissible dyadic level `0<=ell<=u(k)`.

#### Proof

The source proof of Lemma 4.2 applies a Chernoff estimate to the number of teeth in each dyadic height bin. At `gamma=1/3`, uniformly over the source-admissible levels `ell<=u(k)`, the smallest expected bin population occurs at the top scale and is bounded below by

\[
c\, k^{(1-\gamma)/(1+\gamma)}=c\sqrt{k}
\]

for all sufficiently large `k`; the tail asymptotic supplies the needed two-sided constant comparison once the dyadic height is large, while finitely many lower bins are absorbed into the constants. Therefore the same Chernoff argument gives

\[
\mathbb P(D_k^z\text{ fails to be `ell`-good for some admissible }\ell)
\le C\log k\,e^{-c\sqrt{k}}.
\]

For all disks `D_k^z subset D_N`, an intentionally crude count gives at most `C N^2` choices of `(z,k)`, and at most `C log N` relevant dyadic levels. Restricting to `k>=A(log N)^2`, a union bound therefore gives

\[
\mathbb P(\exists\text{ such bad disk})
\le
C N^2\log N\,e^{-c\sqrt{A}\log N}
= C\log N\,N^{2-c\sqrt A}.
\]

Choose `A` so large that `2-c sqrt(A)<-2`. The right side is summable in `N`. Borel–Cantelli proves the assertion. `square`

### What this immediately gives, and what it does not

The source's volume and effective-resistance corollaries following Lemma 4.2 are deterministic consequences of disk goodness, so their geometric conclusions inherit the same polylogarithmic lower cutoff.

However, the later local-time/exit-time machinery (notably the source's Proposition 4.6 and Lemma 4.7) also uses separate environment events involving deep teeth. Lemma 5.1 alone does **not** prove that every subsequent quenched heat-kernel estimate is valid down to `(log N)^2` times. That propagation is a remaining obligation, not hidden inside this lemma.

## 6. B4 — complete critical self-overlap sufficient criterion

This is the cleanest full endpoint lemma I could prove in this run. It reduces the source conjecture to two quantitative quenched inputs and explicitly permits the critical logarithmic loss.

### Proposition 6.1 (a `log N log log N` self-overlap bound suffices)

Fix `gamma=1/3` and the exact source i.i.d. comb law. Suppose that on an almost-sure environment event, for all sufficiently large `N` and uniformly over the starting states allowed by the source scale-to-scale construction, the source collision count `H_N` satisfies

\[
\mathbb E^\omega H_N\ge c_0>0, \tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
G_N(\omega)\le C_0\log N\,\log\log N. \tag{6.2}
\]

Then two independent simple random walks on the source comb collide infinitely often almost surely.

#### Proof

Write `C_n` for the collision event counted at time `n`. The exact Markov decomposition used in source Proposition 4.14 yields

\[
\mathbb E^\omega H_N^2
=
\mathbb E^\omega H_N
+2\sum_{n<k}\mathbb P^\omega(C_n\cap C_k).
\]

After a collision at a backbone point `(z,0)`, the strong Markov property bounds the future collision probability by the two-walk return overlap from that same point. Keeping the same bounded-degree normalizations as the source gives a deterministic constant `C_1<infinity` for which

\[
\mathbb E^\omega H_N^2
\le
\mathbb E^\omega H_N\,[1+C_1G_N(\omega)]. \tag{6.3}
\]

Paley–Zygmund at threshold zero and (6.1) therefore give

\[
\mathbb P^\omega(H_N>0)
\ge
\frac{(\mathbb E^\omega H_N)^2}{\mathbb E^\omega H_N^2}
\ge
\frac{c_0}{1+C_1G_N(\omega)}. \tag{6.4}
\]

Using (6.2),

\[
\mathbb P^\omega(H_N>0)
\ge
\frac{c_2}{\log N\,\log\log N} \tag{6.5}
\]

for all sufficiently large `N`.

Now use the same geometric scale architecture as the source: `N_m=b^m` with fixed `b>1`, and restart the pair of walks by the strong Markov property at the successive scale boundaries. The assumed uniformity over allowed starting states turns (6.5) into the conditional bound

\[
\mathbb P^\omega(\mathrm{Col}_m\mid\mathcal F_{m-1})
\ge
\frac{c_3}{m\log m} \tag{6.6}
\]

for all sufficiently large `m`, because `log N_m` is comparable to `m` and `log log N_m` to `log m`. Therefore

\[
\sum_m
\mathbb P^\omega(\mathrm{Col}_m\mid\mathcal F_{m-1})
=\infty. \tag{6.7}
\]

The conditional Borel–Cantelli lemma implies `Col_m` occurs infinitely often almost surely. Each such event contains a genuine collision of the two walks, so there are infinitely many collisions. `square`

### Consequence

A stronger estimate `G_N=O(log N)` is more natural and would give a `1/m` per-scale lower bound, but it is **not necessary**. The endpoint can close even with an extra `log log N` loss.

This matters because it removes a false goal: the critical proof does not have to recover a scale-independent positive collision probability.

## 7. B21 — transfer from critical trapping/renewal structure

The random-trap/fractional-kinetics literature suggests the critical spatial scale

\[
|X_t^{\rm hor}|\asymp t^{(1+\gamma)/4}=t^{1/3}
\]

at `gamma=1/3`. Consistently, the source heat-kernel exponent becomes

\[
p_t(x,x)\asymp t^{-(3-\gamma)/4}=t^{-2/3}.
\]

If backbone mass is spread across `t^{1/3}` horizontal sites at size about `t^{-2/3}` per site, then its squared `L^2` mass has critical order

\[
t^{1/3}\,(t^{-2/3})^2=t^{-1},
\]

whose time sum is logarithmic. This exactly matches the endpoint exponent obtained by substituting `gamma=1/3` into the source's long-lag calculation.

This paragraph is a **route heuristic**, not a theorem: the fractional-kinetics scaling result does not automatically supply the quenched, uniformly localized backbone `L^2` estimate required in Proposition 6.1.

## 8. Failed routes and precise reasons

1. **Plain Green-kernel recurrence criterion.** The Barlow–Peres–Sousi collision criterion is structurally relevant, but applying it directly requires the correct uniform comparison of killed Green kernels on this random comb. The source's boundary geometry does not provide that comparison for free.

2. **Semigroup over all vertices.** Symmetry gives a generic bound of the form
   \[
   \sum_{y\text{ on backbone}}p_r(z,y)^2
   \le \sum_{y\text{ all vertices}}p_r(z,y)^2
   \asymp p_{2r}(z,z).
   \]
   At the critical heat-kernel exponent this is only of order `r^{-2/3}`, whose time sum is polynomial. It destroys the codimension gain of restricting collisions to the backbone and is far too weak.

3. **Scaling limit alone.** Fractional-kinetics convergence predicts the correct harmonic self-overlap but is not a quenched local-limit theorem and does not by itself control the supremum over collision centers.

4. **Blindly lowering the source's global cutoff.** Lemma 5.1 improves the good-disk geometry to a polylogarithmic scale, but Proposition 4.6/Lemma 4.7 have additional local-time/deep-tooth environment inputs. Treating the geometry lemma as if it automatically upgraded every heat-kernel lemma would be a gap.

5. **Uniform worst-center control may be unnecessarily expensive.** In the raw second moment, before the source takes a supremum, one has schematically
   \[
   \sum_n\sum_z
   \mathbb P(C_n\text{ occurs at }z)\,R_z,
   \]
   with `R_z` the future backbone-overlap from `z`. Replacing this immediately by `P(C_n) sup_z R_z` pays for rare anomalous teeth/centers that may carry negligible collision mass. This is the strongest reason to change route next run.

## 9. Source-internal precedent for a vanishing scale probability

Section 3 of the same paper proves deterministic-comb per-scale lower bounds of order `c/log N` (and at its own boundary, `c/(log N log log N)`). On geometric scales `N=b^m`, these become `c/m` and `c/(m log m)`, both nonsummable. Thus the conditional-Borel–Cantelli mechanism used in Proposition 6.1 is not foreign to the paper's architecture: a scale-independent positive probability is stronger than necessary.

I record this only as a proof-design observation. It does not transfer the deterministic-comb theorem to the random-comb endpoint.

## 10. Remaining exact obstruction

A source-level positive solution can now be organized around the following two endpoint inputs:

1. prove the source first-moment lower bound uniformly at `gamma=1/3`, auditing every prerequisite rather than substituting the algebraic endpoint exponent; and
2. prove, either uniformly or in a correctly weighted/good-center version of the second moment,
   \[
   G_N(\omega)=O(\log N\log\log N)
   \]
   almost surely at all sufficiently large scales.

The source long-lag algebra is already compatible with a logarithm. The unresolved issue is the short-lag/worst-center part and the endpoint propagation of local-time/exit-time estimates.

## 11. Next attack — new methods, not B20/B21/B4

- **B11 (dualize):** express backbone self-overlap as a trace/Dirichlet/renewal quantity that retains the projection onto the backbone instead of applying the all-vertex semigroup bound.

The most promising concrete route is B15: keep the collision-location weights in the raw second moment rather than paying for the worst tooth in `[-N,N]`.
