## 3. Hard core and notation

As established in the first checkpoint, any hypothetical failure of Lehmer yields a sequence of monic irreducible noncyclotomic reciprocal polynomials
\[
Q(x)=x^D R(x+x^{-1})\in\mathbf Z[x]
\]
with

- \(\deg Q=2D\to\infty\),
- \(Q(0)=1\),
- \(R\in\mathbf Z[y]\) monic irreducible of degree \(D\),
- \(1<M(Q)\to1\).

Let \(\alpha\) be one root of \(Q\) and
\[
\beta=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}.
\]
Put
\[
L=\mathbf Q(\alpha),\qquad F=\mathbf Q(\beta).
\]
Then \([L:\mathbf Q]=2D\), \([F:\mathbf Q]=D\), and \([L:F]=2\). The involution over \(F\) is \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\), so
\[
N_{L/F}(\alpha)=1.
\]
All roots \(\beta_i\) of \(R\) are algebraic integers because each is \(\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1}\) with \(\alpha_i\) an algebraic unit.

# NEW RESULT I — an explicit trace-local p-adic energy inequality

## 4. Local collision energy of trace conjugates

Fix a rational prime \(p\). Let \(K/\mathbf Q_p\) be a finite extension, with ramification index
\[
e=e(K/\mathbf Q_p)
\]
and residue field
\[
\kappa_K\cong\mathbf F_q.
\]
Fix once and for all an embedding of a splitting field of \(R\) into \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_p\). Define
\[
S_K(R)=\{\beta_i:\beta_i\in K\},\qquad s=|S_K(R)|.
\]
Because every \(\beta_i\) is integral, every member of \(S_K(R)\) lies in \(\mathcal O_K\) and therefore has a well-defined residue in \(\mathbf F_q\).

For \(a\in\mathbf F_q\), let
\[
N_a=|\{\beta_i\in S_K(R):\bar\beta_i=a\}|.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_q}N_a=s.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — trace-local discriminant energy

With the above notation,
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\operatorname{Disc}R)
 \ge
 \frac1e\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_q}N_a(N_a-1)
 \ge
 \frac1e\left(\frac{s^2}{q}-s\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Equivalently, since the left side is nonnegative,
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\operatorname{Disc}R)
 \ge
 \frac1e\left(\frac{s^2}{q}-s\right)_+.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Write the discriminant in a fixed algebraic closure of \(\mathbf Q_p\):
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(R)=\prod_{i<j}(\beta_i-\beta_j)^2.
\]
Use the extension of the normalized rational valuation with \(v_p(p)=1\). If two elements of \(\mathcal O_K\) have the same residue modulo the maximal ideal of \(K\), then their difference has integral \(K\)-valuation at least one. Since \(v_p=v_K/e\) on \(K\),
\[
\bar\beta_i=\bar\beta_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_p(\beta_i-\beta_j)\ge\frac1e.
\]
All differences of algebraic integers have nonnegative \(p\)-adic valuation, so discarding pairs outside \(S_K(R)\) cannot lower-bound the total incorrectly. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_p(\operatorname{Disc}R)
&=2\sum_{i<j}v_p(\beta_i-\beta_j)\\
&\ge \frac2e\sum_{a\in\mathbf F_q}\binom{N_a}{2}\\
&=\frac1e\sum_a N_a(N_a-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\sum_aN_a^2\ge\frac{(\sum_aN_a)^2}{q}=\frac{s^2}{q},
\]
so
\[
\sum_aN_a(N_a-1)=\sum_aN_a^2-s\ge\frac{s^2}{q}-s.
\]
This proves (4.1) and (4.2). ∎

### Interpretation

The inequality becomes nontrivial only once \(s>q\): merely having a few trace conjugates in a local field does not force a discriminant contribution. Once the number exceeds the residue-field capacity, however, residue collisions are unavoidable and the forced energy is quadratic in \(s\).

This is the precise local mechanism behind the familiar \(\sqrt{D\log D}\)-scale in p-adic Lehmer criteria: to overcome the archimedean discriminant term \(D\log D\), a fixed local field must contribute \(s^2\) energy.

## 5. Several rational primes: additive trace energy

Choose a finite set \(\mathcal P\) of **distinct rational primes**. For each \(p\in\mathcal P\), choose one finite extension
\[
K_p/\mathbf Q_p
\]
with ramification index \(e_p\), residue-field size \(q_p\), and
\[
s_p=|S_{K_p}(R)|.
\]
Define the trace-local energy
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathcal P}(R)
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\frac{\log p}{e_p}
\left(\frac{s_p^2}{q_p}-s_p\right)_+.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because distinct rational primes contribute independently to the integer \(\operatorname{Disc}R\), Theorem 4.1 immediately gives
\[
\boxed{
\log|\operatorname{Disc}R|\ge E_{\mathcal P}(R).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
No splitting assumption is being made: the local field \(K_p\) may be ramified and may have arbitrary fixed residue degree.

# NEW RESULT II — explicit Mahler lower bound from trace-local energy

## 6. Combining local energy with the reciprocal discriminant identity

We use two exact/standard ingredients.

First, the archived reciprocal identity (1.1):
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}Q|
=
|Q(1)Q(-1)|\,|\operatorname{Disc}R|^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Second, Mahler's discriminant inequality for a monic degree-\(n\) polynomial:
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}Q|
\le n^nM(Q)^{2n-2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
For \(n=2D\),
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}Q|
\le (2D)^{2D}M(Q)^{4D-2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 — trace-local Mahler inequality

For every finite set \(\mathcal P\) and local fields \(K_p\) as above,
\[
\boxed{
\log M(Q)
\ge
\frac{
E_{\mathcal P}(R)
+\tfrac12\log|Q(1)Q(-1)|
-D\log(2D)
}{2D-1}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
In particular, because \(Q(\pm1)\ne0\) for a noncyclotomic irreducible hard-core polynomial and hence \(|Q(1)Q(-1)|\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\log M(Q)
\ge
\frac{E_{\mathcal P}(R)-D\log(2D)}{2D-1}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Proof

Take logarithms in (6.1) and combine with (5.2):
\[
\log|\operatorname{Disc}Q|
\ge
\log|Q(1)Q(-1)|+2E_{\mathcal P}(R).
\]
On the other hand, (6.3) gives
\[
\log|\operatorname{Disc}Q|
\le
2D\log(2D)+(4D-2)\log M(Q).
\]
Therefore
\[
(4D-2)\log M(Q)
\ge
2E_{\mathcal P}(R)
+\log|Q(1)Q(-1)|
-2D\log(2D).
\]
Divide by \(2\), then by \(2D-1\), obtaining (6.4). Dropping the nonnegative endpoint logarithm gives (6.5). ∎

### Corollary 6.2 — an explicit sufficient criterion for a uniform gap

If for some \(c>0\),
\[
E_{\mathcal P}(R)
\ge
D\log(2D)+c(2D-1),
\tag{6.6}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{M(Q)\ge e^c.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Thus a full Lehmer proof would follow from any theorem forcing a fixed positive excess of trace-local p-adic collision energy above the \(D\log D\) discriminant threshold for every sufficiently large reciprocal hard-core polynomial.

### Corollary 6.3 — necessary local-energy budget for a hypothetical counterexample sequence

If \(Q_D\) is a hypothetical hard-core sequence with
\[
M(Q_D)\longrightarrow1,
\]
then for **every** finite choice of one local field \(K_p/\mathbf Q_p\) at each of finitely many distinct rational primes,
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathcal P}(R_D)
\le
D\log(2D)+o(D).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

So a counterexample cannot place too many trace conjugates simultaneously into small fixed local residue fields. This is a concrete adelic restriction on any putative near-one sequence.

### One-field scale

For one fixed \(K/\mathbf Q_p\), (6.5) reads
\[
\log M(Q)
\ge
\frac{
\frac{\log p}{e}(s^2/q-s)_+-D\log(2D)
}{2D-1}.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Ignoring the lower-order linear \(s\)-term, the local occupancy needed to force a constant gap has scale
\[
s\asymp
\sqrt{\frac{e q}{\log p}\,D\log D}.
\]
This matches the qualitative \(\sqrt{d\log d}\) threshold of Dixit–Kala, but (6.9) is written directly for **trace conjugates** \(\beta_i=\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1}\) and gives an explicit elementary constant in terms of \(e,q,p\).

I do **not** claim literature novelty for this inequality without a broader comparison to p-adic height literature; it is a new exact campaign checkpoint and a useful interface with the reciprocal trace machinery.

# NEW RESULT III — the norm-one quadratic extension ramifies only at endpoint primes

## 7. Relative discriminant of `L/F`

Recall
\[
L=\mathbf Q(\alpha),\qquad F=\mathbf Q(\beta),\qquad
\beta=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}.
\]
Over \(F\), \(\alpha\) satisfies
\[
T^2-\beta T+1=0.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The \(\mathcal O_F\)-order \(\mathcal O_F[\alpha]\) therefore has basis \(1,\alpha\) and relative discriminant
\[
\beta^2-4.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak D_{L/F}\) denote the relative field discriminant ideal.

### Theorem 7.1 — endpoint control of finite ramification

One has the ideal divisibility
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D_{L/F}\mid (\beta^2-4)\mathcal O_F.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
N_{F/\mathbf Q}(\mathfrak D_{L/F})
\le |Q(1)Q(-1)|.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Every finite prime of \(F\) ramified in \(L/F\) lies above a rational prime dividing \(Q(1)Q(-1)\).

In the extremal endpoint-unit case
\[
|Q(1)Q(-1)|=1,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D_{L/F}=\mathcal O_F,
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
so the quadratic norm-one extension \(L/F\) is unramified at every finite prime.

### Proof

For any integral order \(\mathcal O_F[\alpha]\subseteq\mathcal O_L\), the relative field discriminant divides the order discriminant. The discriminant of the basis \(1,\alpha\) for the quadratic polynomial (7.1) is
\[
\beta^2-4,
\]
which proves (7.3).

Now take absolute norms. Since \(R\) is the minimal polynomial of \(\beta\),
\[
|N_{F/\mathbf Q}(\beta^2-4)|
=
|R(2)R(-2)|.
\]
The trace relation gives
\[
Q(1)=R(2),\qquad
Q(-1)=(-1)^D R(-2),
\]
so
\[
|N_{F/\mathbf Q}(\beta^2-4)|=|Q(1)Q(-1)|.
\]
Norming (7.3) proves (7.4). A ramified prime divides the relative discriminant, giving the support assertion. Finally, if the positive integer on the right of (7.4) is \(1\), then the relative discriminant ideal has norm \(1\), hence is the unit ideal. ∎

### Relation to known unramified Salem structure

For Lehmer's degree-ten polynomial,
\[
Q_L(1)=-1,\qquad Q_L(-1)=1.
\]
Thus its quadratic extension over the trace field is finite-unramified. This agrees with the established notion of an **unramified Salem polynomial** in Gross–McMullen; the point here is not to claim that phenomenon as new, but to place it directly beside the p-adic trace-energy inequality and the campaign's trace-resultant framework.

## 8. Local-field bridge from traces back to reciprocal roots

The trace-local formulation is genuinely useful because a trace conjugate can lie in a fixed local field even when its reciprocal roots do not.

Fix \(K/\mathbf Q_p\). For every \(\beta_i\in K\), the corresponding reciprocal roots satisfy
\[
T^2-\beta_iT+1=0
\]
and hence belong to
\[
K(\sqrt{\beta_i^2-4}),
\]
a field of degree at most two over \(K\). A p-adic local field has only finitely many quadratic extensions (equivalently, \(K^\times/(K^\times)^2\) is finite). Therefore, if \(s\) trace conjugates lie in one fixed \(K\), then a positive fraction depending only on \(K\) of the corresponding \(2s\) reciprocal conjugates lie in one fixed finite extension of \(K\).

This shows qualitatively why the Dixit–Kala criterion can also be activated by sufficiently many trace conjugates in a fixed local field. The direct inequality (6.9), however, avoids a square-class pigeonhole and exposes exactly where the residue-field size \(q\) and ramification index \(e\) enter.

For
\[
Q_L(x)=x^{10}+x^9-x^7-x^6-x^5-x^4-x^3+x+1,
\]
the trace polynomial is
\[
R_L(y)=y^5+y^4-5y^3-5y^2+4y+3.
\]
Exact arithmetic gives
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(R_L)=36497,
\]
\[
Q_L(1)=-1,\qquad Q_L(-1)=1,
\]
and therefore, by (1.1),
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(Q_L)=36497^2=1332031009.
\]
The square absolute discriminant is consistent with the finite-unramified quadratic extension of Theorem 7.1.

These identities themselves were already checked in an earlier campaign artifact; their **relative-field interpretation** is what is new in this checkpoint.

## 10. Concrete obstruction encountered: residue-degree abundance is not enough

The preceding theorem also clarifies why the previous finite-field clean-prime result does not immediately close the problem.

Suppose \(p\) is unramified for \(R\) and one only knows that many reduction roots of \(R\) have bounded residue degree. A fixed residue field \(\mathbf F_q\) has only \(q\) residue values. For distinct unramified roots in one local field, reduction is separable, so there is no forced multiplicity beyond that capacity. The trace-energy term
\[
\left(\frac{s^2}{q}-s\right)_+
\]
is zero unless \(s>q\), precisely the point at which residue collisions force **ramification/discriminant energy**.

Thus merely upgrading the Breuillard–Varju clean-prime theorem from degree-one roots to bounded residue-degree roots does not by itself trigger (6.9). The missing input must either:

1. force genuinely p-adically close trace conjugates (hence discriminant valuation), or
2. extend the finite-field sum-product argument to extension fields strongly enough that no degree-one reduction is needed.

This is a concrete reason to keep the p-adic energy route and the extension-field counting route as complementary rather than interchangeable.

## 12. Next attack

The next useful target is no longer a generic search for small resultants. It is the following more precise dichotomy for a reciprocal hard-core polynomial of small Mahler measure:

> **Either** some fixed-size p-adic trace field contains enough mutually close conjugates to make
> \[
> E_{\mathcal P}(R)>D\log(2D)+cD,
> \]
> **or** the trace roots remain sufficiently dispersed in every small local field that an extension-field Breuillard–Varju sum-product/counting argument can be run without degree-one splitting.

Three concrete subproblems follow.

1. **Ramified-trace concentration.** Use the exact factorization
   \[
   \operatorname{Disc}Q=Q(1)Q(-1)\operatorname{Disc}(R)^2
   \]
   together with the norm-one quadratic extension to determine whether small endpoint factors force a large share of ramification into \(F=\mathbf Q(\beta)\) itself, and whether that ramification must concentrate in a bounded-residue-degree local field.

2. **Extension-field sum-product.** Rework the Breuillard–Varju finite-field iteration in \(\mathbf F_{p^f}\), keeping all proper-subfield alternatives explicit. The trace cube contains \(1\) and the generating trace root, so any subfield obstruction should be tied to the actual residue degree of that root rather than to an arbitrary ambient subfield.

3. **Energy-spreading extremals.** Search exactly, in trace-polynomial data, for configurations that minimize
   \[
   \sum_p\frac{\log p}{e_p}(s_p^2/q_p-s_p)_+
   \]
   subject to the archived resultant and discriminant identities. A structural extremal pattern could suggest the missing global inequality; a family evading all such energy would identify why discriminant methods alone hit the classical \(D\log D\) barrier.

The full Lehmer conjecture remains unresolved after this iteration.
