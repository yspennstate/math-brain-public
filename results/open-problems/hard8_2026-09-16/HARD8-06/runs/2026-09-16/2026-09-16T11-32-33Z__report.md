**Run date:** 2026-09-16 UTC  
**SOLUTION STATUS:** **OPEN.** This checkpoint does not prove a degree-uniform Mahler gap and does not produce a sequence with Mahler measure tending to one. It extends the previous affine all-level p-adic collision method to arbitrary algebraic numbers by replacing the affine residue tree with the full projective residue tree `P^1(O_K/p_K^ell)`. This is a project-derived refinement; no claim is made that the projective-energy formalism is absent from the broader Berkovich/local-height literature.

# NEW RESULT I — exact projective discriminant identity

## 3. Primitive polynomial and local chordal valuation

Let
\[
f(x)=a\prod_{i=1}^d(x-\alpha_i)\in\mathbf Z[x]
\]
be primitive and irreducible, of degree `d>=2`. Fix a rational prime `p` and a finite extension `L/Q_p` containing all roots. Write `v_p` for the normalized valuation with `v_p(p)=1`.

For a finite point `u\in L`, define
\[
h_p(u)=\max(0,-v_p(u)).
\]
For two distinct finite points define the projective/chordal valuation
\[
\boxed{
\delta_p(u,v)=v_p(u-v)+h_p(u)+h_p(v).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
This is always nonnegative. Equivalently, choose primitive homogeneous coordinates
\[
[u:1]\quad(v_p(u)\ge0),\qquad [1:u^{-1}]\quad(v_p(u)<0).
\]
Then `delta_p(u,v)` is the valuation of the determinant of the two primitive homogeneous coordinate vectors.

### Lemma 3.1 — p-adic Gauss normalization

Because `f` is primitive, its p-adic Gauss norm is one. Hence
\[
\boxed{
v_p(a)=\sum_{i=1}^d h_p(\alpha_i).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

The multiplicativity of the nonarchimedean Gauss norm gives
\[
1=\|f\|_{G,p}=|a|_p\prod_i\max(1,|\alpha_i|_p).
\]
Taking `-log_p` yields (3.2). ∎

### Theorem 3.2 — exact projective discriminant decomposition

\[
\boxed{
v_p(\operatorname{Disc}f)
=2\sum_{1\le i<j\le d}\delta_p(\alpha_i,\alpha_j).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

By definition,
\[
v_p(\operatorname{Disc}f)
=(2d-2)v_p(a)+2\sum_{i<j}v_p(\alpha_i-\alpha_j).
\]
Using (3.2), each `h_p(alpha_i)` occurs exactly `d-1` times in the pair sum, so
\[
(2d-2)v_p(a)
=2\sum_{i<j}\bigl(h_p(\alpha_i)+h_p(\alpha_j)\bigr).
\]
Substitution gives exactly (3.3). ∎

This identity is the projective analogue of the usual integral-root discriminant pair energy. It is especially useful because every pair contributes a nonnegative amount, so any selected set of local conjugates may be retained and all other pairs discarded.

# NEW RESULT II — all levels of the projective residue tree

## 4. Projective residue classes

Fix a finite extension
\[
K/\mathbf Q_p
\]
with ramification index `e`, valuation ring `O_K`, maximal ideal `p_K`, and residue-field size
\[
q=|O_K/p_K|.
\]
Normalize `v_K` by `v_K(pi_K)=1`, so `v_p=v_K/e` on `K`.

For `ell>=1`, the finite projective line over the local principal ring has cardinality
\[
\boxed{
 m_\ell=|\mathbf P^1(O_K/p_K^\ell)|=(q+1)q^{\ell-1}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Indeed, the number of primitive pairs modulo `p_K^ell` is
\[
q^{2\ell}-q^{2\ell-2},
\]
and quotienting by the `q^\ell-q^{\ell-1}` units gives `(q+1)q^{ell-1}`.

For primitive homogeneous representatives `P,Q`, one has
\[
\boxed{
P\equiv Q\text{ in }\mathbf P^1(O_K/p_K^\ell)
\iff
v_K(\det(P,Q))\ge \ell.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
To see this, one coordinate of a primitive vector is a unit. If the determinant vanishes modulo `p_K^ell`, solve for the unique unit scalar that makes the corresponding unit coordinate agree; the other coordinate then agrees because the determinant is zero modulo `p_K^ell`.

Now suppose exactly `s` distinct conjugates of `alpha` lie in `K`. At level `ell`, let `N_{ell,a}` denote their occupancy of projective class
\[
a\in\mathbf P^1(O_K/p_K^\ell).
\]
For a pair of selected conjugates, its integer `v_K` chordal valuation equals the number of levels on which their projective reductions agree. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
2\sum_{i<j\text{ selected}}\delta_K(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)
=
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\sum_a N_{\ell,a}(N_{\ell,a}-1).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 5. Balanced projective energy

For `m,s>=1`, write `s=am+r`, `0<=r<m`, and define the exact balanced-box minimum
\[
C_m(s)=ma(a-1)+2ra.
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is the minimum of `sum_j n_j(n_j-1)` over all distributions of `s` objects into `m` boxes; the exchange proof was archived in the previous checkpoint.

Define the **projective hierarchical collision energy**
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_q(s)
=\sum_{\ell\ge1}C_{(q+1)q^{\ell-1}}(s).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
The sum is finite because `C_m(s)=0` once `m>=s`.

### Theorem 5.1 — projective all-level local discriminant bound

If `s` conjugates of `alpha` lie in `K`, then
\[
\boxed{
v_p(\operatorname{Disc}f)
\ge
\frac{\mathscr P_q(s)}{e}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

By Theorem 3.2, discard every nonnegative chordal pair contribution except pairs among the selected `K`-conjugates. Convert `v_p` to `v_K/e`. Equation (4.3) gives the exact selected contribution. At level `ell`, the `s` points occupy `m_ell=(q+1)q^{ell-1}` projective residue classes, so the level contribution is at least `C_{m_ell}(s)`. Summing over all levels proves (5.3). ∎

### Relation to the one-level p-adic criterion

The first level of (5.2) is exactly `C_{q+1}(s)`, corresponding to the `q` finite residue classes plus the point at infinity. Every deeper level contributes nonnegatively. Thus (5.3) is an exact all-depth strengthening of the one-level projective collision step behind the `q+1` coefficient in the arbitrary-algebraic-number p-adic criterion.

# NEW RESULT III — exact formula and improved threshold

## 6. Closed form

Let
\[
L=L_q^{\mathrm{proj}}(s)
=\max\{\ell\ge1:(q+1)q^{\ell-1}<s\},
\]
with `L=0` if `s<=q+1`. For `1<=ell<=L`, put
\[
m_\ell=(q+1)q^{\ell-1},\qquad
s=a_\ell m_\ell+r_\ell,
\quad0\le r_\ell<m_\ell.
\]
As in the affine calculation,
\[
C_{m_\ell}(s)
=\frac{s^2}{m_\ell}-s
+r_\ell\left(1-\frac{r_\ell}{m_\ell}\right).
\]
The geometric sum is
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac1{m_\ell}
=
\frac{q(1-q^{-L})}{q^2-1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_q(s)
=
\frac{q(1-q^{-L})}{q^2-1}s^2
-Ls
+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}
 r_\ell\left(1-\frac{r_\ell}{m_\ell}\right).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_q(s)
=
\frac{q}{q^2-1}s^2
-s\log_q s
+O_q(s).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
The proof of the error term is the same finite-geometric-tail estimate as in the affine case: `L=log_q s+O_q(1)`, the omitted geometric tail contributes `O_q(s)`, and the residue corrections have total `O_q(s)`.

The leading coefficient improves from the one-level projective coefficient
\[
\frac1{q+1}
\]
to
\[
\boxed{
\frac{q}{q^2-1}
=
\frac1{q+1}\frac{q}{q-1}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
For `q=2` this doubles the leading local collision energy.

## 7. Mahler consequence for one field

Mahler's discriminant inequality for primitive `f` is
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}f|
\le d^d M(f)^{2d-2}.
\]
Combining it with (5.3) yields
\[
\boxed{
\log M(f)
\ge
\frac{
(\log p/e)\,\mathscr P_q(s)-d\log d
}{2d-2}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

If
\[
s=C\sqrt{d\log d},
\]
then (6.2) gives
\[
\frac{\log p}{e}\mathscr P_q(s)
=
\left(
\frac{C^2q\log p}{e(q^2-1)}+o(1)
\right)d\log d.
\]
Therefore any
\[
\boxed{
C>\sqrt{\frac{e(q^2-1)}{q\log p}}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
forces `log M(f)` to be bounded away from zero (indeed to grow like a positive multiple of `log d`) for all sufficiently large `d` within this class.

The corresponding one-level `q+1` threshold is
\[
C>\sqrt{\frac{e(q+1)}{\log p}},
\]
so the all-level projective tree improves the threshold constant by
\[
\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{q}}.
\]

# NEW RESULT IV — affine specialization and multi-prime sum

## 8. Algebraic integers recover the stronger affine energy

If `alpha` is an algebraic integer, every p-adic conjugate is integral. Thus all selected projective points lie in the affine chart and, at level `ell`, can occupy only `q^ell` residue classes rather than `(q+1)q^{ell-1}` projective classes. Consequently the previous affine energy
\[
\mathscr E_q(s)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}C_{q^\ell}(s)
\]
applies directly to the minimal polynomial of `alpha`, giving
\[
\boxed{
v_p(\operatorname{Disc}f)
\ge \frac{\mathscr E_q(s)}e,
\qquad
\mathscr E_q(s)
=\frac{s^2}{q-1}-s\log_qs+O_q(s).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Thus the threshold becomes
\[
\boxed{
C>\sqrt{\frac{e(q-1)}{\log p}}
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
for `s=C sqrt(d log d)`.

This identifies the prior trace theorem as an instance of a more general fact: its extra strength came from integrality, not from reciprocity itself.

## 9. Multi-prime projective energy

For a finite set of **distinct rational primes** `p in P`, choose one finite extension `K_p/Q_p` for each prime and let `s_p` be the number of conjugates lying in `K_p`. Distinct rational-prime valuations of the integer discriminant add, so
\[
\boxed{
\log|\operatorname{Disc}f|
\ge
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\frac{\log p}{e_p}\mathscr P_{q_p}(s_p).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\log M(f)
\ge
\frac{
\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
(\log p/e_p)\mathscr P_{q_p}(s_p)
-d\log d
}{2d-2}.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
For algebraic integers, replace every `P_{q_p}` by the stronger affine `E_{q_p}`.

In asymptotic square-root scale, if
\[
s_p=C_p\sqrt{d\log d},
\]
then a sufficient condition for a growing Mahler gap is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\frac{C_p^2q_p\log p}{e_p(q_p^2-1)}>1
}
\tag{9.3}
\]
for arbitrary algebraic numbers, or
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\frac{C_p^2\log p}{e_p(q_p-1)}>1
}
\tag{9.4}
\]
for algebraic integers.

This makes the "many individually subcritical local occupancies can add" principle exact at leading order.

## 10. Why this does not solve Lehmer

The missing global step remains severe. A hypothetical small-Mahler sequence is not presently known to force `sqrt(d log d)`-scale populations into any fixed small local field, nor enough aggregate population over distinct rational primes to make (9.3)/(9.4) exceed one. Breuillard–Varju's finite-field reformulation concerns additive growth of geometric progressions modulo primes; it does not by itself force the local-field occupancy required here.

The all-level tree also has a built-in limitation: balanced projective `q`-ary configurations attain the occupancy minimum at every level. Therefore no argument using only the number `s` of points in `P^1(K)` can improve the leading coefficient `q/(q^2-1)` without exploiting additional arithmetic structure of the conjugates.

## 11. Failed/blocked routes recorded this run

1. **Cyclotomic-resultant square route.** For reciprocal even-degree polynomials `A=x^a f(x+x^{-1})` and `B=x^b g(x+x^{-1})`, the resultant is a square `Res_x(A,B)=Res_y(f,g)^2` (up to the conventional resultant sign). This is useful bookkeeping and is related to the known theory of reciprocants, but by itself the integrality jump from `1` to at least `4` is too weak after normalization by large cyclotomic degree to produce a degree-uniform Mahler gap.

2. **More p-adic pigeonhole levels alone.** The new projective energy is asymptotically optimal for pure residue-tree packing. Any further local improvement must use restrictions on which balanced configurations can arise as Galois conjugates, ramification/square-class constraints, or coupling across primes.

3. **Finite-field factor degree alone.** Squarefree reduction modulo `p` can have many low-degree factors while contributing zero p-adic discriminant valuation. Therefore low factor degree is not interchangeable with the repeated-residue collisions used here; a bridge must involve resultants, repeated factors, or additive-growth data, not merely factor-degree counts.

## 14. Next attack

The best next attack is to combine this projective energy with arithmetic restrictions on **which projective residue-tree leaves can actually contain conjugates of one primitive polynomial**. Two concrete directions are:

1. derive a Galois/Frobenius obstruction to perfectly balanced occupancy simultaneously at many levels and many small primes, ideally producing a positive excess over `P_q(s)` that sums globally;
2. connect Breuillard–Varjú's small additive-growth residue classes to repeated-resultant or projective-clustering certificates, rather than to factor degree alone.

A successful bridge of either kind would turn the current local-energy inequalities from sufficient criteria into a genuine universal mechanism. The full Lehmer conjecture remains open.
