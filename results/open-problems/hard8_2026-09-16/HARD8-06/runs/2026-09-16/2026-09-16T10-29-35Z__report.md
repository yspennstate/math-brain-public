## 3. Carry-forward hard core

As established in earlier checkpoints, a hypothetical failure may be reduced to a sequence of monic irreducible noncyclotomic reciprocal polynomials
\[
Q(x)=x^D R(x+x^{-1})\in\mathbf Z[x]
\]
with

- `deg Q = 2D -> infinity`,
- `Q(0)=1`,
- `R in Z[y]` monic irreducible of degree `D`,
- `1<M(Q)->1`.

Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(Q\), let
\[
\beta=\alpha+\alpha^{-1},
\]
and put
\[
L=\mathbf Q(\alpha),\qquad F=\mathbf Q(\beta).
\]
Then `[L:F]=2` and
\[
\alpha^2-\beta\alpha+1=0.
\]
All roots \(\beta_i\) of \(R\) are algebraic integers.

The previous one-level local theorem fixed a finite extension `K/Q_p`, counted trace conjugates in `K`, reduced them only modulo the maximal ideal, and obtained a lower bound proportional to `(s^2/q-s)_+/e`. The next theorem uses every residue level simultaneously.

# NEW RESULT I — exact hierarchical p-adic trace energy

## 4. Residue-tree notation

Fix a rational prime \(p\) and a finite extension
\[
K/\mathbf Q_p.
\]
Write
\[
e=e(K/\mathbf Q_p),\qquad
\mathcal O_K\supset\mathfrak p_K,
\qquad
|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p_K|=q.
\]
Normalize \(v_K\) by `v_K(pi_K)=1`; then on \(K\),
\[
v_p=\frac1e v_K.
\]

Fix an embedding of a splitting field of \(R\) into \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_p\), and let
\[
S_K(R)=\{\beta_i:\beta_i\in K\},\qquad s=|S_K(R)|.
\]
Every member of this set lies in \(\mathcal O_K\).

For every level \(\ell\ge1\) and residue class
\[
a\in\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p_K^\ell,
\]
define
\[
N_{\ell,a}=\#\{\beta_i\in S_K(R):\beta_i\equiv a\pmod{\mathfrak p_K^\ell}\}.
\]
There are exactly \(q^\ell\) residue classes at level \(\ell\).

## 5. Exact pair-energy identity inside the selected local roots

For two distinct integral elements \(u,v\in K\),
\[
v_K(u-v)
=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathbf 1_{u\equiv v\pmod{\mathfrak p_K^\ell}}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
2\sum_{i<j\,;\,\beta_i,\beta_j\in K}v_K(\beta_i-\beta_j)
=
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\sum_a N_{\ell,a}(N_{\ell,a}-1).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is an identity, not an inequality: an ordered pair is counted once at every common residue depth.

Because all conjugate differences are differences of algebraic integers, every omitted pair contributes nonnegative p-adic valuation. Since
\[
\operatorname{Disc}R=\prod_{i<j}(\beta_i-\beta_j)^2,
\]
(5.1) gives
\[
\boxed{
v_p(\operatorname{Disc}R)
\ge
\frac1e
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\sum_a N_{\ell,a}(N_{\ell,a}-1).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

## 6. Exact balanced occupancy minimum

For positive integers \(m,s\), distribute \(s\) objects among \(m\) boxes with occupancies \(n_1,\ldots,n_m\). The minimum of
\[
\sum_{j=1}^m n_j(n_j-1)
\]
occurs when the occupancies differ by at most one.

Write
\[
s=am+r,\qquad 0\le r<m.
\]
Then the exact minimum is
\[
\boxed{
C_m(s)=ma(a-1)+2ra.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

If some two occupancies satisfy `n_i >= n_j+2`, moving one object from box `i` to box `j` changes the objective by
\[
(n_i-1)(n_i-2)+(n_j+1)n_j-n_i(n_i-1)-n_j(n_j-1)
=2(n_j-n_i+1)<0.
\]
Thus every minimizer is balanced. In a balanced distribution, `r` boxes have size `a+1` and `m-r` have size `a`, yielding (6.1). ∎

Define the **hierarchical q-adic collision energy**
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s)=\sum_{\ell\ge1} C_{q^\ell}(s).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
The sum is finite because `C_m(s)=0` once `m>=s`.

### Theorem 6.1 — hierarchical trace discriminant bound

With the notation above,
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\operatorname{Disc}R)
 \ge
 \frac{\mathscr E_q(s)}{e}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof

At residue level \(\ell\), the `s` selected roots occupy `q^ell` boxes, so by (6.1)
\[
\sum_aN_{\ell,a}(N_{\ell,a}-1)
\ge C_{q^\ell}(s).
\]
Sum this over all \(\ell\ge1\) and apply (5.2). ∎

This strictly strengthens the previous checkpoint's one-level Cauchy estimate whenever higher residue levels are forced to collide.

## 7. Closed form and asymptotics of the hierarchical energy

Let
\[
L_q(s)=\max\{\ell\ge1:q^\ell<s\},
\]
with `L_q(s)=0` if `s<=q`. For each `1<=ell<=L_q(s)`, write
\[
s=a_\ell q^\ell+r_\ell,
\qquad0\le r_\ell<q^\ell.
\]
A direct expansion of (6.1) gives the exact identity
\[
C_{q^\ell}(s)
=
\frac{s^2}{q^\ell}-s
+r_\ell\left(1-\frac{r_\ell}{q^\ell}\right).
\tag{7.1}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s)
=
\frac{s^2(1-q^{-L_q(s)})}{q-1}
-L_q(s)s
+
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_q(s)}
 r_\ell\left(1-\frac{r_\ell}{q^\ell}\right).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s)
\ge
\frac{s^2(1-q^{-L_q(s)})}{q-1}-L_q(s)s.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Since every correction term is at most `q^ell/4`, while `q^{L_q(s)}<s<=q^{L_q(s)+1}`, one gets
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s)
=
\frac{s^2}{q-1}-s\log_q s+O_q(s).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The leading coefficient is therefore `1/(q-1)`, whereas the previous single-residue-level Cauchy bound had leading coefficient `1/q`.

This leading constant is also optimal for an argument that knows only how many integral points occupy the q-adic residue tree: balanced q-ary digit sets attain the occupancy minimum simultaneously at every level. Thus further improvement must exploit arithmetic restrictions on the conjugates beyond bare p-adic packing.

# NEW RESULT II — strengthened multi-prime Mahler inequality

## 8. Hierarchical adelic energy

For finitely many distinct rational primes \(p\in\mathcal P\), choose finite extensions
\[
K_p/\mathbf Q_p
\]
with ramification index \(e_p\), residue-field size \(q_p\), and
\[
s_p=|S_{K_p}(R)|.
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_{\mathcal P}(R)
=
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
\frac{\log p}{e_p}\,\mathscr E_{q_p}(s_p).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Theorem 6.1 and additivity of rational-prime valuations give
\[
\boxed{
\log|\operatorname{Disc}R|
\ge \mathcal H_{\mathcal P}(R).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Use the archived exact reciprocal identity
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}Q|
=|Q(1)Q(-1)|\,|\operatorname{Disc}R|^2
\tag{8.3}
\]
and Mahler's discriminant inequality
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}Q|
\le(2D)^{2D}M(Q)^{4D-2}.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Exactly as in the previous checkpoint, but with the stronger energy (8.1), this yields:

### Theorem 8.1 — hierarchical trace-local Mahler bound

\[
\boxed{
\log M(Q)
\ge
\frac{
\mathcal H_{\mathcal P}(R)
+\tfrac12\log|Q(1)Q(-1)|
-D\log(2D)
}{2D-1}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\log M(Q)
\ge
\frac{\mathcal H_{\mathcal P}(R)-D\log(2D)}{2D-1}.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

Thus any fixed `c>0` with
\[
\mathcal H_{\mathcal P}(R)
\ge D\log(2D)+c(2D-1)
\]
forces
\[
M(Q)\ge e^c.
\]

### One-field threshold constant

For fixed `K/Q_p`, if
\[
s=C\sqrt{D\log D},
\]
then (7.4) gives
\[
\frac{\log p}{e}\mathscr E_q(s)
=
\left(\frac{C^2\log p}{e(q-1)}+o(1)\right)D\log D.
\]
Therefore every constant
\[
\boxed{
C>\sqrt{\frac{e(q-1)}{\log p}}
}
\tag{8.7}
\]
forces a positive Mahler gap for all sufficiently large `D` via this elementary discriminant route.

The previous one-level coefficient required the larger threshold
\[
C>\sqrt{\frac{eq}{\log p}}.
\]
For `K=Q_2`, the leading occupancy threshold improves by the factor `1/sqrt(2)`.

This remains a restricted sufficient condition, not a proof of Lehmer: a hypothetical counterexample may keep every fixed-local-field occupancy below the square-root scale, or distribute local information across fields whose residue parameters grow with `D`.

# NEW RESULT III — trace-to-root transfer through finitely many p-adic square classes

## 9. A fixed trace field can lift only into finitely many quadratic local fields

The preceding energy works directly with trace conjugates \(\beta_i\). Dixit–Kala formulate their criterion using conjugates of the original algebraic number \(\alpha\). For reciprocal hard cores these two viewpoints are linked by a simple but useful finite-square-class theorem.

Let
\[
h_K=|K^\times/K^{\times2}|.
\]
For every finite extension of \(\mathbf Q_p\), this number is finite. If `p` is odd, in fact
\[
\boxed{h_K=4.}
\tag{9.1}
\]
Indeed, valuation parity contributes two classes, the residue-field unit group contributes two square classes, and the principal-unit group is uniquely 2-divisible by Hensel's lemma because `2` is a unit.

### Theorem 9.1 — finite quadratic lift pigeonhole

Let `s=s_K(R)` trace conjugates lie in a fixed finite extension `K/Q_p`. Then there exists an extension
\[
E/K,\qquad [E:K]\le2,
\]
which contains at least
\[
\boxed{
\frac{2s}{h_K}
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
conjugates of \(\alpha\).

For odd `p`, some `E/K` of degree at most two therefore contains at least
\[
\boxed{s/2}
\]
conjugates of \(\alpha\).

### Proof

For each selected root \(\beta_i\in K\), put
\[
\delta_i=\beta_i^2-4.
\]
Because the hard-core polynomial is irreducible and noncyclotomic, `beta_i` cannot equal `+2` or `-2`; hence `delta_i` is nonzero.

Partition the `s` traces according to the square class
\[
[\delta_i]\in K^\times/K^{\times2}.
\]
One class contains at least `s/h_K` traces. Choose a representative `d` of that class and set
\[
E=K(\sqrt d).
\]
For every trace in this class there is `u_i in K^×` with
\[
\delta_i=d u_i^2.
\]
The two reciprocal roots over \(\beta_i\) are
\[
\alpha_i^{\pm}
=\frac{\beta_i\pm\sqrt{\beta_i^2-4}}2
=\frac{\beta_i\pm u_i\sqrt d}{2},
\]
so both lie in `E`. They are distinct because `delta_i != 0`. Therefore at least twice the number of traces in the selected square class are conjugates of `alpha` lying in `E`, proving (9.2). ∎

### Consequence for the Dixit–Kala criterion

Dixit–Kala prove a Lehmer gap for classes having `>> sqrt(d log d)` conjugates in one fixed finite extension of `Q_p`. Since a fixed `K` has only finitely many quadratic extensions and `deg(alpha)=2D`, Theorem 9.1 implies:

> For every fixed local field `K/Q_p`, sufficiently many trace conjugates in `K` at the scale `sqrt(D log D)` already put sufficiently many ordinary `alpha`-conjugates into one fixed field from a finite list of degree-at-most-two extensions of `K`.

Thus the trace-local square-root threshold is not an artifact of changing from `alpha` to `beta=alpha+alpha^{-1}`. Any hypothetical Lehmer counterexample sequence must evade both the elementary hierarchical-energy criterion above and the fixed-local-field Dixit–Kala mechanism.

This bridge does **not** aggregate occupancies at different rational primes into one local field; the hierarchical discriminant sum in Theorem 8.1 remains useful precisely because it can add subcritical contributions from several primes.

# NEW RESULT IV — ideal-level refinement of the relative discriminant square class

## 10. The square factorization before taking norms

The previous addendum proved only after taking absolute norms that
\[
\frac{|Q(1)Q(-1)|}{N(\mathfrak D_{L/F})}
\]
is a square integer. There is a cleaner prime-ideal statement.

Set
\[
A=\mathcal O_F[\alpha]\subseteq\mathcal O_L.
\]
As an \(\mathcal O_F\)-order, `A` has basis `1,alpha`; the discriminant of this basis is
\[
(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})^2=\beta^2-4.
\]
Let \(\mathfrak i(A)\) be the relative index ideal of `A` in `O_L`, characterized by the standard order/field discriminant relation
\[
\operatorname{disc}(A/\mathcal O_F)
=\mathfrak D_{L/F}\,\mathfrak i(A)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
(\beta^2-4)\mathcal O_F
=
\mathfrak D_{L/F}\,\mathfrak i(A)^2.
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

This proves prime-ideal square-class rigidity, not merely norm square-class rigidity.

For every non-dyadic prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\nmid2\), quadratic ramification is tame, so
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak D_{L/F})\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Taking valuations in (10.1) gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak p\text{ ramifies in }L/F
\iff
v_{\mathfrak p}(\beta^2-4)\text{ is odd}.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]
Moreover, because
\[
(\beta-2,\beta+2)\mid(4),
\]
a non-dyadic prime ideal cannot divide both factors. Hence away from `2`, the two endpoint branches `beta-2` and `beta+2` separately encode the odd-prime ramification parity.

Taking norms in (10.1) recovers the global square quotient archived in the preceding run. In fact
\[
N_{F/\mathbf Q}((\beta^2-4))
=|R(2)R(-2)|
=|Q(1)Q(-1)|,
\]
and
\[
N(\mathfrak i(A))=I_L/I_F^2,
\]
so (10.1) gives exactly
\[
N(\mathfrak D_{L/F})I_L^2
=|Q(1)Q(-1)|I_F^4.
\]

This ideal identity also explains Theorem 9.1 conceptually: locally, adjoining a root of the reciprocal quadratic is exactly adjoining a square root of the Kummer class `beta^2-4`.

## 12. Failed/limited routes and what this checkpoint rules out

### 12.1 Merely reducing modulo the first residue field loses a real constant

The previous bound retained only level `ell=1`. The all-level identity shows that for large fixed-field occupancy the correct residue-tree leading coefficient is `1/(q-1)`, not `1/q`. Any continuation of the local-discriminant route should therefore use hierarchical residue data rather than repeat the one-level estimate.

### 12.2 The p-adic packing argument is asymptotically saturated

Balanced q-ary digit sets attain the occupancy minima level by level. Therefore no universal improvement beyond `mathscr E_q(s)` can come from pigeonhole occupancy alone. A stronger theorem must use algebraic restrictions linking different conjugates, different primes, Frobenius data, or the reciprocal norm-one structure.

### 12.3 Trace occupancy is not a loophole around alpha-occupancy

Theorem 9.1 shows that a large set of traces in one fixed local field automatically yields a comparably large set of ordinary roots in one of finitely many quadratic extensions. Thus the next useful local theorem should not merely try to convert beta-conjugates to alpha-conjugates; that conversion costs only a fixed factor.

### 12.4 Still no universal gap

A near-one sequence could in principle have every fixed local occupancy below the square-root threshold and still avoid enough cumulative hierarchical energy. Neither the present argument nor Dixit–Kala forces the required local concentration for every reciprocal hard core.

## 14. Next attack

The strongest next target is no longer the first-residue collision count. The residue-tree packing problem is now solved exactly and is asymptotically saturated.

The next useful attack should couple **different primes or different residue depths through algebraic constraints**. Two concrete directions are:

1. **Frobenius/factor-degree versus hierarchical energy dichotomy.** At a prime where the trace subset-sum construction has no low-depth collision, all factors of `R mod p` have large residue degree; at a prime with many local trace roots, Theorem 8.1 extracts p-adic discriminant energy. A theorem forcing one of these alternatives at enough small primes could connect the Breuillard–Varju finite-field side to the new local-energy side.

2. **Kummer-class rigidity across embeddings.** The local quadratic fields needed to lift `beta_i` to `alpha_i` are determined by the square classes of `beta_i^2-4`, while globally `(beta^2-4)=D_{L/F} * square`. Exploit this to show that too much dispersion among local square classes forces large relative ramification/endpoints, while too little dispersion forces many alpha-conjugates into one fixed local field and triggers Dixit–Kala.

Either would be a genuine advance over pure occupancy pigeonhole arguments.
