## 3. Carry-forward hard core and notation

As proved in the first checkpoint, a hypothetical failure may be reduced to monic irreducible noncyclotomic reciprocal polynomials
\[
Q(x)=x^D R(x+x^{-1})\in\mathbf Z[x]
\]
of even degree \(2D\to\infty\), with \(R\in\mathbf Z[y]\) monic irreducible of degree \(D\), constant term of \(Q\) equal to one, and \(M(Q)\downarrow1\). Thus it is harmless in the near-one regime to impose \(M(Q)\le2\).

Define
\[
C_0(y)=2,\qquad C_1(y)=y,\qquad C_{j+1}(y)=yC_j(y)-C_{j-1}(y).
\]
Then
\[
C_j(x+x^{-1})=x^j+x^{-j}.
\]
For a root \(t\) of \(R\), put
\[
T_m(t)=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^m\varepsilon_j C_j(t):\varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\right\}.
\]

For \(m<D\), the previous checkpoint defined
\[
\mathcal E_m(R)
=
\left\{p:\ p\mid \mathcal R(R,B_c)
\text{ for some }0\ne c\in\{-1,0,1\}^{m+1}\right\},
\]
where
\[
B_c(y)=\sum_{j=0}^m c_jC_j(y),
\]
and proved
\[
|\mathcal E_m(R)|
\le
(3^{m+1}-1)
\left[D\log_2(2(m+1))+m\log_2M(Q)\right]. \tag{3.1}
\]
If \(p\notin\mathcal E_m(R)\), then for **every** reduction root \(\bar t\) of \(R\bmod p\), in whatever finite extension it lies,
\[
|T_m(\bar t)|=2^{m+1}. \tag{3.2}
\]

# NEW RESULT I — exact square factorization of every torsion resultant

## 4. Cyclotomic trace polynomials

For each integer \(k\ge1\), define \(A_k(y)\in\mathbf Z[y]\) as follows.

If \(k=2r+1\) is odd, define it by the symmetric Laurent identity
\[
A_k(x+x^{-1})=x^r+x^{r-1}+\cdots+x^{-r}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
A_{2r+1}(y)=1+\sum_{j=1}^r C_j(y).
\]
If \(k=2r\) is even, define it by
\[
A_k(x+x^{-1})
=\frac{x^r-x^{-r}}{x-x^{-1}}
=x^{r-1}+x^{r-3}+\cdots+x^{-(r-1)}.
\]
This is the integral polynomial \(U_{r-1}(y/2)\), where \(U_j\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.

### Lemma 4.1 — exact Chebyshev square identities

For all \(k\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
C_k(y)-2=
\begin{cases}
(y-2)A_k(y)^2,&k\text{ odd},\\
(y^2-4)A_k(y)^2,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

For \(k=2r+1\),
\[
x^{k/2}-x^{-k/2}
=(x^{1/2}-x^{-1/2})
(x^r+x^{r-1}+\cdots+x^{-r}).
\]
Squaring and using
\[
(x^{k/2}-x^{-k/2})^2=C_k(y)-2,
\qquad
(x^{1/2}-x^{-1/2})^2=y-2
\]
gives the odd identity.

For \(k=2r\),
\[
x^r-x^{-r}
=(x-x^{-1})A_k(y).
\]
Squaring and using
\[
(x-x^{-1})^2=y^2-4
\]
gives the even identity. ∎

The first examples are
\[
A_1=A_2=1,\quad
A_3=y+1,\quad
A_4=y,\quad
A_5=y^2+y-1,\quad
A_6=y^2-1.
\]

## 5. Exact trace-square factorization of \(\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)\)

### Theorem 5.1

Let
\[
Q(x)=x^DR(x+x^{-1})
\]
be monic reciprocal of degree \(2D\). Then for every \(k\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)
=\mathcal R(R,2-C_k).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)=
\begin{cases}
Q(1)\,\mathcal R(R,A_k)^2,& k\text{ odd},\\
Q(1)Q(-1)\,\mathcal R(R,A_k)^2,& k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Pair the roots of \(Q\) as \(\alpha_i,\alpha_i^{-1}\), with
\[
\beta_i=\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1}
\]
a root of \(R\). Then
\[
(\alpha_i^k-1)(\alpha_i^{-k}-1)
=2-(\alpha_i^k+\alpha_i^{-k})
=2-C_k(\beta_i).
\]
Multiplying over \(i=1,\ldots,D\) gives (5.1).

For odd \(k\), (4.1) gives
\[
2-C_k(y)=-(y-2)A_k(y)^2.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathcal R(R,2-C_k)
=(-1)^D\prod_i(\beta_i-2)\,\mathcal R(R,A_k)^2.
\]
Since
\[
\prod_i(\beta_i-2)=(-1)^DR(2)
\]
and \(Q(1)=R(2)\), the signs cancel and the odd case of (5.2) follows.

For even \(k\),
\[
2-C_k(y)=-(y-2)(y+2)A_k(y)^2.
\]
Using \(Q(1)=R(2)\) and
\[
Q(-1)=(-1)^DR(-2)
\]
gives exactly the even formula. ∎

### Corollary 5.2 — parity law away from endpoint factors

For every prime \(p\),

- if \(k\) is odd and \(p\nmid Q(1)\), then
  \[
  v_p\!\left(\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)\right)
  \text{ is even};
  \]
- if \(k\) is even and \(p\nmid Q(1)Q(-1)\), then the same valuation is even.

Thus, except for fixed endpoint factors, every torsion resultant of a reciprocal polynomial is an exact square in the trace coordinate.

This is useful because low multiplicative order modulo \(p\) is now certified by a **half-degree trace resultant**, rather than by the full reciprocal resultant.

## 6. Improved exact bound for low-order exceptional primes

For a noncyclotomic irreducible \(Q\), all integers
\[
\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)
\]
are nonzero. For \(L\ge1\), define
\[
\mathcal O_L(Q)
=
\left\{p:\ p\mid\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)
\text{ for some }1\le k\le L\right\}.
\]
If \(p\notin\mathcal O_L(Q)\), then **every** root \(\bar\alpha\) of \(Q\bmod p\) has multiplicative order strictly larger than \(L\). Indeed a root of order \(k\le L\) would be a common root of \(Q\) and \(x^k-1\) modulo \(p\).

For every root \(\rho\) of \(Q\),
\[
|\rho^k-1|\le2\max(1,|\rho|)^k,
\]
so
\[
|\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)|
\le2^{2D}M(Q)^k. \tag{6.1}
\]
The square factorization improves the distinct-prime count because the endpoint factors occur only once globally. Since
\[
|Q(\pm1)|\le2^{2D}M(Q),
\]
we have
\[
\omega(Q(1)Q(-1))
\le4D+2\log_2M(Q). \tag{6.2}
\]
Also (5.2), (6.1), and the fact that the endpoint factors are nonzero integers imply
\[
|\mathcal R(R,A_k)|
\le2^D M(Q)^{k/2},
\]
so
\[
\omega(\mathcal R(R,A_k))
\le D+\frac{k}{2}\log_2M(Q). \tag{6.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal O_L(Q)|
\le
4D+2\log_2M(Q)
+LD
+\frac{L(L+1)}4\log_2M(Q).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
For the near-one regime \(M(Q)\le2\), this is
\[
|\mathcal O_L(Q)|
\le4D+2+LD+\frac{L(L+1)}4.
\tag{6.5}
\]
The previous direct full-resultant union bound has leading term \(2DL\); the reciprocal trace square structure cuts that leading term in half and isolates all order-one/order-two endpoint effects into a fixed set of prime divisors of \(Q(1)Q(-1)\).

# NEW RESULT II — simultaneous additive-collision / multiplicative-order sieve

## 7. Exact simultaneous clean-prime theorem

Define
\[
\mathcal B_{m,L}(Q)
=\mathcal E_m(R)\cup\mathcal O_L(Q),
\qquad m<D.
\]
Combining (3.1) and (6.4) gives the explicit bound
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal B_{m,L}(Q)|
\le{}&(3^{m+1}-1)
\left[D\log_2(2(m+1))+m\log_2M(Q)\right]\\
&+4D+2\log_2M(Q)+LD
+\frac{L(L+1)}4\log_2M(Q).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Theorem 7.1

If \(p\notin\mathcal B_{m,L}(Q)\), then simultaneously:

1. for **every** root \(\bar t\) of \(R\bmod p\),
   \[
   \boxed{|T_m(\bar t)|=2^{m+1};}
   \tag{7.2}
   \]
2. for **every** root \(\bar\alpha\) of \(Q\bmod p\),
   \[
   \boxed{\operatorname{ord}(\bar\alpha)>L.}
   \tag{7.3}
   \]

No splitting hypothesis is used. The statements hold in the actual finite residue extensions containing the roots.

### Proof

(7.2) is precisely the collision-resultant theorem of the previous checkpoint. For (7.3), if a reduction root had order \(k\le L\), then it would be a common root modulo \(p\) of \(Q\) and \(x^k-1\), forcing
\[
p\mid\mathcal R(Q,x^k-1),
\]
contrary to \(p\notin\mathcal O_L(Q)\). ∎

This is the first point in the campaign where the additive and multiplicative finite-field requirements are simultaneously certified for the **same rational primes**.

## 8. Polynomial-interval abundance and the exponent \(\log_3 2\)

The finite exceptional-set estimate yields more than existence of one clean prime.

### Theorem 8.1 — many polynomially located clean primes

Fix any
\[
0<\delta<\log_3 2=\frac{\log2}{\log3}=0.630929\ldots.
\]
Choose a constant \(a>0\) and then \(\eta>0\) so that
\[
a\log2
>\delta\bigl(1+a\log3+\eta\bigr). \tag{8.1}
\]
This is possible exactly because \(\delta<\log_3 2\).

For sufficiently large \(D\), set
\[
m=\lfloor a\log D\rfloor,
\qquad
L=\lceil(\log D)^3\rceil,
\qquad
X=D^{1+a\log3+\eta}.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Assume \(Q\) is in the reciprocal hard core and \(M(Q)\le2\). Then
\[
|\mathcal B_{m,L}(Q)|
=O\!\left(D^{1+a\log3}\log\log D
+D(\log D)^3\right)
=o\!\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right). \tag{8.3}
\]
Consequently, by the prime number theorem, **all but \(o(1)\) of the primes in the interval \([X,2X]\), as a fraction of the primes in that interval, lie outside \(\mathcal B_{m,L}(Q)\)**.

For every such clean prime \(p\), every reduction trace root \(\bar t\) and every reciprocal root \(\bar\alpha\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{|T_m(\bar t)|=2^{m+1}>p^\delta,}
\tag{8.4}
\]
\[
\boxed{m<\log_2p,}
\tag{8.5}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(\bar\alpha)>(\log_2p)^2.}
\tag{8.6}
\]

### Proof

Since \(m=a\log D+O(1)\),
\[
3^{m+1}=D^{a\log3+o(1)}.
\]
Substituting \(M(Q)\le2\) into (7.1) gives (8.3). The PNT gives
\[
\pi(2X)-\pi(X)\sim\frac{X}{\log X},
\]
which dominates the total number of exceptional primes.

For a clean prime, (7.2) gives
\[
|T_m(\bar t)|=2^{m+1}=D^{a\log2+o(1)}.
\]
Since \(p\le2X\), condition (8.1) implies (8.4) for large \(D\).

Also
\[
\log_2 p
=\frac{1+a\log3+\eta+o(1)}{\log2}\log D,
\]
while \(m=a\log D+O(1)\). Because
\[
\frac{a\log2}{1+a\log3+\eta}<\frac{\log2}{\log3}<1,
\]
we obtain (8.5).

Finally \(L=(\log D)^3+O(1)\), whereas \((\log_2p)^2=O((\log D)^2)\), so for large \(D\),
\[
L>(\log_2p)^2.
\]
Then (7.3) gives (8.6). ∎

### Interpretation and exact remaining obstruction

This theorem is deliberately phrased over the actual residue extensions. It says that, for every sufficiently large reciprocal hard-core polynomial, there are polynomially sized intervals containing asymptotically many rational primes at which **all reduction roots simultaneously have**:

- multiplicative order larger than the Breuillard–Varju scale \((\log p)^2\); and
- a binary trace subset set of size exceeding \(p^\delta\) at a depth \(m<\log_2p\), for any preassigned \(\delta<\log_3 2\).

What it does **not** give is residue degree one. The root \(\bar t\) may lie in \(\mathbf F_{p^f}\) with \(f>1\), and \(\bar\alpha\) may lie in a further quadratic extension. Breuillard–Varju's `bad prime` formulation is specifically about elements of \(\mathbf F_p\). Therefore this theorem is not a proof of Lehmer. It isolates the missing arithmetic input much more sharply: one needs a uniform way to obtain enough degree-one reductions among these personalized clean primes, or an extension-field version of the finite-field counting implication strong enough to replace degree one.

The constant \(\log_3 2\) is not asserted to be a fundamental Lehmer barrier. It is the efficiency limit of this particular union bound: the additive collision family has \(3^{m+1}\) possible signed differences while the collision-free binary cube has size \(2^{m+1}\).

# NEW RESULT III — characteristic 3 is exactly controlled by the least factor degree

## 9. Exact \(p=3\) criterion

The alphabet \(\{-1,0,1\}\) becomes **all of \(\mathbf F_3\)** modulo 3. This makes the previous exceptional-prime construction completely transparent at \(p=3\).

Let
\[
f_3(R)=\min\{\deg F:F\text{ is a nonconstant irreducible factor of }R\bmod3\}.
\]

### Theorem 9.1

For every \(m<D\),
\[
\boxed{3\in\mathcal E_m(R)\iff f_3(R)\le m.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

### Proof

Over \(\mathbf F_3\), the polynomials
\[
C_0,C_1,\ldots,C_m
\]
form a basis for all polynomials of degree at most \(m\): \(C_0=2\) is a nonzero constant and, for \(j\ge1\), \(C_j\) is monic of degree \(j\), so the change-of-basis matrix from monomials is triangular with nonzero diagonal.

Moreover every coefficient of \(\mathbf F_3\) has a unique representative in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Hence the reductions of the polynomials
\[
B_c=\sum_{j=0}^m c_jC_j,
\qquad c_j\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
run through **every** polynomial over \(\mathbf F_3\) of degree at most \(m\), exactly once.

Thus \(3\in\mathcal E_m(R)\) iff \(R\bmod3\) has a nonconstant common factor with some nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(m\), which is equivalent to having an irreducible factor of degree at most \(m\). ∎

### Corollary 9.2 — exact two-cube saturation in the residue trace field

If \(\bar t\) has degree \(f\) over \(\mathbf F_3\), then
\[
C_0(\bar t),\ldots,C_{f-1}(\bar t)
\]
is an \(\mathbf F_3\)-basis of \(\mathbf F_{3^f}\). Consequently
\[
T_{f-1}(\bar t)
\]
is exactly the binary coordinate cube \(\{0,1\}^f\) in this basis, and
\[
\boxed{T_{f-1}(\bar t)+T_{f-1}(\bar t)=\mathbf F_{3^f}.}
\tag{9.2}
\]
Indeed every ternary coordinate \(0,1,2\) is a sum of two binary coordinates.

This supplies a completely exact finite-field model at characteristic 3. It is still a fixed-characteristic statement and does not itself imply a degree-uniform Mahler gap.

## 10. Exact benchmark: Lehmer's polynomial

For
\[
Q_L(x)=x^{10}+x^9-x^7-x^6-x^5-x^4-x^3+x+1
\]
and
\[
R_L(y)=y^5+y^4-5y^3-5y^2+4y+3,
\]
one has
\[
Q_L(1)=-1,
\qquad
Q_L(-1)=1.
\]
Therefore Theorem 5.1 says that **every torsion resultant is a signed perfect square**:
\[
\mathcal R(Q_L,x^k-1)
=\begin{cases}
-\mathcal R(R_L,A_k)^2,&k\text{ odd},\\
-\mathcal R(R_L,A_k)^2,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
(The two lines coincide here because the endpoint values are \(-1\) and \(+1\).)

Exact computation for \(1\le k\le12\) gives

| \(k\) | \(\mathcal R(R_L,A_k)\) | \(\mathcal R(Q_L,x^k-1)\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
|1|1|-1|
|2|1|-1|
|3|1|-1|
|4|-3|-9|
|5|1|-1|
|6|1|-1|
|7|1|-1|
|8|-3|-9|
|9|1|-1|
|10|1|-1|
|11|1|-1|
|12|9|-81|

Thus through order 12 the only rational low-order prime is \(3\).

The characteristic-3 factorization is
\[
R_L(y)\equiv y(y^4+y^3+y^2+y+1)\pmod3.
\]
Hence \(f_3(R_L)=1\), and Theorem 9.1 says exactly that \(3\) enters the signed-collision exceptional set at depth \(m=1\). The linear root is \(t=0\); the corresponding reciprocal roots satisfy
\[
x^2+1=0
\]
over \(\mathbf F_9\), so they have order 4, matching the first nontrivial torsion resultant at \(k=4\).

## 11. Failed routes and what this run rules out

1. **The Frobenius-resultant divisibility from the previous run does not by itself give a gap.** Its lower divisibility \(p^D\) is too weak against naive upper bounds on the full resultant, which carry dimension-squared losses. No universal bound was inferred from it here.

2. **Having many clean rational primes is not the same as having many clean degree-one primes.** Theorem 8.1 avoids Chebotarev entirely precisely because it makes no splitting demand. The missing residue-degree-one step is real, not a bookkeeping issue.

3. **Finite initial maximal growth is still not asymptotic growth.** Even though \(T_m\) is maximally large for every \(m<D\), and now for many finite-field reductions at logarithmic depth, this does not by itself force the asymptotic Bernoulli entropy used by Breuillard–Varju. A proof must control repeated long-scale collisions, not only the first collision window.

4. **No numerical search is interpreted as a proof of a gap.** The Lehmer-polynomial computations above are exact certificates illustrating the algebraic theorems only.

## 13. Next attack

The next high-value attack is now more specific than a generic Chebotarev search.

1. **Extension-field BV step.** Re-run the sum-product part of Breuillard–Varju over \(\mathbf F_{p^f}\), explicitly tracking proper-subfield obstructions. Here the reduction element generates its actual residue field, and Theorem 8.1 supplies high multiplicative order plus a large trace cube at logarithmic depth for many primes. Determine whether field-generation is enough to exclude concentration in proper subfields after bounded sum-product iteration.

2. **Degree-one alternative.** Seek a theorem giving a degree-one prime inside a polynomial interval after deletion of a polynomial-size, polynomially specified resultant set. A plain qualitative Chebotarev theorem is insufficient; the dependence on growing degree/discriminant is the entire issue.

3. **Dobrowolski integration.** Use the square identity
   \[
   \mathcal R(Q,x^k-1)=\text{endpoint}\times\text{square}
   \]
   inside a many-prime auxiliary-product argument. Because the non-endpoint contribution lives in the trace field and has half degree, test whether the standard determinant/resultant estimates lose a factor that reciprocity can genuinely recover.

A verifier for all exact polynomial/resultant identities and the characteristic-3 criterion is to be archived separately under `artifacts/` after this mathematical report.
