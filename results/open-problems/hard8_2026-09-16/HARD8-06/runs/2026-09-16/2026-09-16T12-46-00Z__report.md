# NEW RESULT I — exact digit formulas and sharpness of the local packing energies

## 3. Balanced-box increments

For integers `m>=1`, `s>=0`, let
\[
C_m(s)=\min_{n_1+\cdots+n_m=s}\sum_{j=1}^m n_j(n_j-1).
\]
The preceding checkpoint proved that if `s=am+r`, `0<=r<m`, then
\[
C_m(s)=ma(a-1)+2ra.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The increment has the particularly simple exact form
\[
\boxed{C_m(s+1)-C_m(s)=2\left\lfloor\frac{s}{m}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Indeed, if `s=am+r`, increasing `s` by one increases one balanced occupancy from `a` to `a+1`, and the objective increases by `2a`; this remains true when `r=m-1` and the quotient changes.

Consequently each `C_m` is discretely convex, hence superadditive:
\[
\boxed{C_m(a+b)\ge C_m(a)+C_m(b).}
\tag{3.3}
\]
To see this directly from increments,
\[
C_m(a+b)-C_m(a)
=\sum_{t=0}^{b-1}\bigl(C_m(a+t+1)-C_m(a+t)\bigr)
\]
and every increment on the right is at least the corresponding increment from `0` to `b`.

## 4. Affine energy as a base-q digit-sum functional

Recall
\[
\mathscr E_q(s)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}C_{q^\ell}(s).
\tag{4.1}
\]
By (3.2),
\[
\mathscr E_q(s+1)-\mathscr E_q(s)
=2\sum_{\ell\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac{s}{q^\ell}\right\rfloor.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Let `sigma_q(s)` be the sum of the base-`q` digits of `s`. The elementary base-expansion identity
\[
s-\sigma_q(s)
=(q-1)\sum_{\ell\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac{s}{q^\ell}\right\rfloor
\tag{4.3}
\]
gives the exact increment
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s+1)-\mathscr E_q(s)
=\frac{2(s-\sigma_q(s))}{q-1}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Summing from `0` to `s-1` yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s)
=\frac{s(s-1)-2\sum_{t=0}^{s-1}\sigma_q(t)}{q-1}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
This is equivalent to the residue-remainder formula in the previous checkpoint but exposes the exact loss from the formal quadratic main term as a cumulative digit sum.

For a complete block `s=q^L`, every digit position takes every value `0,...,q-1` equally often, so
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{q^L-1}\sigma_q(t)
=\frac{L(q-1)q^L}{2}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(q^L)
=\frac{q^L(q^L-1)}{q-1}-Lq^L.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Because the increments (4.4) are nondecreasing by the floor-sum representation (4.2),
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(a+b)\ge\mathscr E_q(a)+\mathscr E_q(b).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]
This is useful below: if two disjoint groups of local conjugates actually lie in the same local field, merging them can only strengthen the collision lower bound.

## 5. Projective energy recursion

Recall
\[
\mathscr P_q(s)
=\sum_{\ell\ge1}C_{(q+1)q^{\ell-1}}(s).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Its increment is
\[
\mathscr P_q(s+1)-\mathscr P_q(s)
=2\sum_{j\ge0}\left\lfloor\frac{s}{(q+1)q^j}\right\rfloor.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Put
\[
a=\left\lfloor\frac{s}{q+1}\right\rfloor.
\]
Then
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{s}{(q+1)q^j}\right\rfloor
=\left\lfloor\frac{a}{q^j}\right\rfloor,
\]
so (4.3), now including the `j=0` term, gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_q(s+1)-\mathscr P_q(s)
=\frac{2(qa-\sigma_q(a))}{q-1}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Again the increment is nondecreasing, hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_q(a+b)\ge\mathscr P_q(a)+\mathscr P_q(b).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

There is a more structural exact recursion. Write
\[
s=(q+1)a+r,\qquad0\le r<q+1.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr P_q(s)
=C_{q+1}(s)
+(q+1-r)\mathscr E_q(a)
+r\mathscr E_q(a+1).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Proof

At projective level one there are `q+1` branches. A balanced distribution places `a` points in `q+1-r` branches and `a+1` points in `r` branches, contributing `C_{q+1}(s)`. Below any fixed first-level branch the projective residue tree is an ordinary `q`-ary affine residue tree. Hence the minimum possible deeper contribution is `E_q(a)` or `E_q(a+1)` according to the branch size. Conversely, choosing each branch internally balanced realizes those minima simultaneously. This proves (5.5). ∎

## 6. Simultaneous sharpness: there is no hidden cross-level packing gain

The prior bounds minimized each residue level separately. One possible concern was that the levelwise balanced minima might be mutually incompatible because residue classes are nested. They are compatible.

### Theorem 6.1 — exact local-tree lower envelopes

For every integer `q>=2` and every `s>=1`:

1. among all sets of `s` distinct boundary points of a rooted regular `q`-ary residue tree, the minimum total ordered collision depth is exactly `E_q(s)`;
2. among all sets of `s` distinct boundary points of a projective residue tree with `q+1` first branches and `q` children thereafter, the minimum is exactly `P_q(s)`.

Moreover these abstract trees are precisely the residue trees of `O_K` and `P^1(K)` for a nonarchimedean local field with residue size `q`, so the minima are attained by actual distinct local points.

### Proof

The lower bound is already established level by level by (3.1). For attainability, choose a depth `L` at which the number of residue classes is at least `s`. Label the level-`L` leaves so that reduction to every earlier level is the natural quotient into equal child blocks, and choose `s` leaves by the recursive balanced rule: at every occupied node distribute its descendants among its children with sizes differing by at most one. Then at every level the global occupancies differ by at most one, so that level contributes exactly `C_m(s)`. Since the chosen leaves are distinct, deeper levels contribute zero.

For `O_K`, each residue-tree node has exactly `q` children and every finite residue class contains a `K`-point. For `P^1(K)`, level one has `q+1` classes and every later node has `q` children; again every finite-depth class contains a `K`-point. Choosing one actual point in each selected terminal class realizes the abstract configuration. ∎

### Consequence

The coefficients `1/(q-1)` (affine) and `q/(q^2-1)` (projective) are not improvable by a pure argument that knows only `(q,s)` and the residue-tree incidence relation. Any further local gain must use arithmetic structure of *conjugates of a common algebraic number* (Galois constraints, resultants, field-generation constraints, etc.), not just p-adic packing.

# NEW RESULT II — all-depth local-field spectrum inequality

## 7. Partition conjugates by their exact generated p-adic field

Let
\[
f(x)=a\prod_{i=1}^d(x-\alpha_i)\in\mathbf Z[x]
\]
be primitive and irreducible of degree `d>=2`. Fix once and for all an embedding of its splitting field into `\overline{Q}_p`.

For each conjugate define its exact local field
\[
K_i=\mathbf Q_p(\alpha_i)\subset\overline{\mathbf Q}_p.
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal K_p(f)=\{K_i:1\le i\le d\}
\]
be the set of distinct actual subfields that occur. For `K in K_p(f)`, put
\[
\Omega_K=\{i:K_i=K\},\qquad s_K=|\Omega_K|,
\]
and write
\[
e_K=e(K/\mathbf Q_p),\qquad q_K=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p_K|.
\]
The sets `Omega_K` form a genuine partition of the conjugates; this avoids overlap even when one local field is contained in another.

### Theorem 7.1 — projective spectrum bound

For an arbitrary primitive irreducible `f`,
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\operatorname{Disc}f)
 \ge
 \sum_{K\in\mathcal K_p(f)}
 \frac{\mathscr P_{q_K}(s_K)}{e_K}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof

By the exact projective discriminant identity archived in the preceding checkpoint,
\[
v_p(\operatorname{Disc}f)
=2\sum_{i<j}\delta_p(\alpha_i,\alpha_j),
\]
and every summand is nonnegative. Retain only pairs `i<j` belonging to the same exact class `Omega_K`. Different classes use disjoint sets of pairs. For a fixed `K`, all selected points lie in `K`; converting the normalized valuation from `K` to `Q_p` contributes the factor `1/e_K`, and Theorem 6.1 / the prior projective collision theorem gives at least `P_{q_K}(s_K)`. Summing over `K` proves (7.1). ∎

### Theorem 7.2 — affine spectrum bound for algebraic integers

If `f` is monic, so all conjugates are algebraic integers, then
\[
\boxed{
 v_p(\operatorname{Disc}f)
 \ge
 \sum_{K\in\mathcal K_p(f)}
 \frac{\mathscr E_{q_K}(s_K)}{e_K}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The proof is identical, except every selected point is integral and therefore lies in the affine residue tree, whose exact lower envelope is `E_q`.

### Relation to Dixit–Kala

Corollary 1.6 of Dixit–Kala sums one-level quantities of the shape
\[
\frac{1}{e_K}\left(\frac{s_K^2}{q_K}-s_K\right)
\]
(over the local fields generated by the conjugates of an algebraic integer, after taking a positive part at the prime level). Formula (7.2) replaces each one-level collision term by the exact all-depth energy `E_{q_K}(s_K)`, whose leading quadratic coefficient is `1/(q_K-1)` rather than `1/q_K` and whose full finite-size correction is given by (4.5). This is a project refinement of the same discriminant mechanism, not a universal Lehmer proof.

## 8. Global Mahler inequalities from the entire local spectrum

Define the projective spectrum energy
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{proj}}(f)
=
\sum_p(\log p)
\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_p(f)}
\frac{\mathscr P_{q_K}(s_K)}{e_K}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Only finitely many primes can contribute positively because (7.1) is bounded by the valuation of the fixed nonzero discriminant.

Then
\[
\log|\operatorname{Disc}f|\ge\mathcal A_{\mathrm{proj}}(f).
\]
Mahler's discriminant inequality
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}f|\le d^dM(f)^{2d-2}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\log M(f)
\ge
\frac{\mathcal A_{\mathrm{proj}}(f)-d\log d}{2d-2}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

If `f` is monic, define
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{aff}}(f)
=
\sum_p(\log p)
\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_p(f)}
\frac{\mathscr E_{q_K}(s_K)}{e_K}.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Then the stronger exact inequality is
\[
\boxed{
\log M(f)
\ge
\frac{\mathcal A_{\mathrm{aff}}(f)-d\log d}{2d-2}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This uses every exact generated local-field class at every rational prime. Choosing one class recovers the one-field criterion; selecting finitely many primes/classes gives immediately checkable partial criteria.

# NEW RESULT III — finite-family aggregation and the reciprocal escape ellipsoid

## 9. A uniform lower bound for `E_q(s)`

The previous closed form implies, for every `q>=2` and `s>=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s)
\ge
\frac{s^2}{q-1}
-s\log_q s
-\frac{q}{q-1}s.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

### Proof

Let `L=max{ell>=1:q^ell<s}`, with `L=0` if `s<=q`. For `L>=1`, the archived formula gives
\[
\mathscr E_q(s)
\ge
\frac{s^2(1-q^{-L})}{q-1}-Ls.
\]
Since `s<=q^{L+1}`, one has `q^{-L}<=q/s`, and `L<=log_q s`, giving (9.1). When `L=0`, the right side of (9.1) is nonpositive, so the inequality follows from `E_q(s)>=0`. ∎

A purely integer version, convenient for certificates, is
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr E_q(s)
\ge
\frac{s^2}{q-1}
-s\lceil\log_2 s\rceil-2s.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
because `log_q s<=log_2 s` and `q/(q-1)<=2`.

## 10. Finite-family aggregation at one prime

Fix a rational prime `p`. Let `F` be any finite subcollection of exact generated local fields at `p`, of size
\[
H=|\mathcal F|,
\]
and suppose uniformly
\[
e_K\le E,\qquad q_K\le Q
\qquad(K\in\mathcal F).
\]
Let
\[
S=\sum_{K\in\mathcal F}s_K.
\]
Then (9.2), Cauchy, and `sum s_K ceil(log_2 s_K) <= S ceil(log_2 S)` give the exact aggregate lower bound
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{K\in\mathcal F}
\frac{\log p}{e_K}\mathscr E_{q_K}(s_K)
\ge
\frac{\log p}{E}
\left(
\frac{S^2}{H(Q-1)}
-S\lceil\log_2 S\rceil
-2S
\right).
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

This is stronger than merely passing all `S` conjugates to a compositum and applying a one-field theorem when that compositum has a much larger residue field or ramification index. It also shows quantitatively that splitting the local population among finitely many bounded-complexity fields only costs a factor `H` in the leading quadratic energy.

## 11. Reciprocal trace hard core

As established in earlier checkpoints, any hypothetical failure of the full classical conjecture can be reduced to a sequence of monic irreducible noncyclotomic reciprocal polynomials
\[
Q_D(x)=x^D R_D(x+x^{-1}),
\qquad \deg R_D=D,
\]
with
\[
D\to\infty,\qquad M(Q_D)\to1.
\]
Here `R_D` is monic irreducible and its roots `beta_i` are algebraic integers.

For each prime `p`, partition the trace roots by their exact local fields
\[
K=\mathbf Q_p(\beta_i)
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal H_D
=
\sum_p(\log p)
\sum_K\frac{\mathscr E_{q_K}(s_{p,K})}{e_K}.
\tag{11.1}
\]
The reciprocal discriminant identity archived earlier,
\[
|\operatorname{Disc}Q_D|
=|Q_D(1)Q_D(-1)|\,|\operatorname{Disc}R_D|^2,
\]
and Mahler's inequality give
\[
\boxed{
\log M(Q_D)
\ge
\frac{
\mathcal H_D+	frac12\log|Q_D(1)Q_D(-1)|
-D\log(2D)
}{2D-1}.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]
The endpoint product is a nonzero integer and hence contributes nonnegatively. Therefore a hypothetical counterexample sequence with `M(Q_D)->1` must satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_D
\le
D\log(2D)+(2D-1)\log M(Q_D)
= D\log D+O(D)+o(D).
}
\tag{11.3}
\]
This is the total **local collision-energy budget** available to any near-one reciprocal sequence.

## 12. One-prime escape law

Fix once and for all a prime `p` and a finite family `F_p` of actual finite extensions of `Q_p`. Count only those trace roots whose exact generated field belongs to `F_p`, and call this number `S_p(D)`. Put
\[
H_p=|\mathcal F_p|,
\qquad
E_p=\max_{K\in\mathcal F_p}e_K,
\qquad
Q_p=\max_{K\in\mathcal F_p}q_K.
\]
Then (10.1) and (11.3) imply
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{D\to\infty}
\frac{S_p(D)}{\sqrt{D\log D}}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{E_pH_p(Q_p-1)}{\log p}}.
}
\tag{12.1}
\]

### Proof

If the limsup exceeded the displayed constant, then along a subsequence the leading quadratic term in (10.1) would exceed `D log D` by a fixed positive proportion. The negative term
\[
S_p(D)\lceil\log_2 S_p(D)\rceil+2S_p(D)
\]
is `o(D log D)` at the square-root scale, contradicting (11.3). ∎

Thus a hypothetical Lehmer counterexample must make its trace conjugates **escape every fixed finite collection of p-adic fields** at at least the square-root scale, with the explicit constant (12.1).

## 13. Simultaneous multi-prime escape ellipsoid

The same argument combines subcritical local populations at several primes.

Fix a finite set of rational primes `P`. For each `p in P`, fix a finite local-field family `F_p` and constants `H_p,E_p,Q_p` as above. Let `S_p(D)` be the number of trace roots whose exact generated p-adic field lies in `F_p`. Define
\[
c_p=\frac{\log p}{E_pH_p(Q_p-1)}.
\tag{13.1}
\]
Then every reciprocal near-one sequence satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{D\to\infty}
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
 c_p\frac{S_p(D)^2}{D\log D}
\le1.
}
\tag{13.2}
\]

### Proof

Apply (10.1) at every `p in P` and add. By the one-prime conclusion (12.1), each `S_p(D)=O(sqrt(D log D))`. Since `P` is finite,
\[
\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}
S_p(D)\lceil\log_2S_p(D)\rceil
=o(D\log D).
\]
The quadratic terms in (10.1) are exactly `c_p S_p(D)^2`. Divide the resulting lower bound for `H_D` by `D log D` and combine with (11.3). ∎

Equation (13.2) is stronger than checking each prime independently: several individually subcritical local populations can jointly exhaust the entire discriminant budget.

7. For the reciprocal trace hard core, the local-energy budget (11.3), one-prime escape law (12.1), and finite-prime ellipsoidal budget (13.2).

These are project-derived statements; no external novelty priority is claimed.

### Known inputs reused with hypotheses checked

- Mahler's discriminant inequality for a primitive irreducible polynomial.
- The exact projective discriminant identity and all-level affine/projective collision bounds proved and archived in the preceding HARD8-06 checkpoint.
- The reciprocal trace identity
  `Disc(Q)=Q(1)Q(-1) Disc(R)^2` in absolute value, archived and exactly checked earlier.
- The standard reduction of a near-one failure to monic irreducible reciprocal noncyclotomic units: nonmonic/content contributions already have measure at least `2`, Mahler measure is multiplicative under factorization, cyclotomic/monomial factors have measure `1`, and the nonreciprocal irreducible case has a known absolute gap.

### What remains open

None of the new inequalities forces a universal positive gap. A hypothetical counterexample can, in principle, arrange that for every fixed finite family of small p-adic fields the number of trace conjugates in that family remains below the square-root budget, while the local degrees/residue fields needed to contain most conjugates grow with `D`.

The sharpness theorem shows that **bare residue-tree packing cannot remove this escape mechanism**: the balanced configurations really exist locally. The next useful step must therefore inject arithmetic coherence between different conjugates/places, not merely sharpen convexity in one tree.

## 15. Failed/closed routes this run

1. **Search for a better universal coefficient from nesting alone.** Closed negatively. The levelwise minima are simultaneously attainable, so there is no extra “incompatibility bonus” between residue depths. The affine `1/(q-1)` and projective `q/(q^2-1)` leading coefficients are sharp for pure local packing.

2. **Treat every local field separately without exact-field partitioning.** This double-counts roots whenever fields are nested. The exact generated-field classes `Q_p(alpha_i)` give a genuine partition and make the spectrum sum rigorous.

3. **Use good unramified primes to force energy automatically.** At a prime not dividing the discriminant, distinct local roots have distinct terminal residue data, and the local collision energy is zero. Thus the method is inherently a discriminant-ramification mechanism; Chebotarev/factorization data at good primes do not by themselves create the needed energy.

4. **Claim literature novelty for the digit identities.** Not justified. They are elementary consequences of base-`q` digit sums and are recorded only as exact project tools.

## 16. Next attack

The strongest next target is an **arithmetic excess over the sharp packing baseline**. Three concrete options are now isolated:

1. **Galois-orbit excess:** prove that conjugates of one irreducible global polynomial cannot realize balanced residue-tree occupancies independently in too many exact local-field classes/primes. Any positive quadratic excess over `E_q` or `P_q`, summed over enough places, would strengthen (13.2).

2. **Resultant coupling:** combine the already archived collision-free trace subset-sum resultants with the spectrum budget. The finite-field resultants constrain reductions of the *same* trace roots at many primes; a theorem showing that balanced local packing forces many small trace-resultant divisors could convert that cross-prime coherence into extra discriminant energy.

3. **Growing-field escape control:** quantify how fast the ramification index/residue degree of `Q_p(beta_i)` must grow if the fixed-family counts obey (12.1), then compare that forced local-degree growth at several small primes with the global degree `D` and with reciprocal/Galois structure. This directly attacks the only escape mechanism left by the present method.

A full proof still requires a universal argument covering every reciprocal hard-core sequence, or a certified sequence with `M -> 1` for refutation. Neither has been obtained here.
