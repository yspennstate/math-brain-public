## 1. Exact target

For a nonzero polynomial
\[
P(x)=a\prod_{j=1}^n(x-\alpha_j)\in\mathbb Z[x],
\qquad
M(P)=|a|\prod_{j=1}^n\max(1,|\alpha_j|),
\]
prove or refute the existence of an absolute constant \(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
M(P)>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M(P)\ge 1+\varepsilon
\]
for every degree and every integer polynomial.  The stronger assertion that Lehmer's degree-ten polynomial realizes the minimum is **not** part of the required target.

## 2. Literature/status check

The full conjecture is still treated as open in current accepted literature.

Primary/background sources checked:

1. Emmanuel Breuillard and Péter P. Varjú, **On the Lehmer conjecture and counting in finite fields**, Discrete Analysis 2019:5, arXiv:1810.13363, DOI 10.19086/da.8306.  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.13363>

   For
   \[
   S_d(\alpha)=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_j\alpha^j:\varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\right\},
   \]
   they prove, with an absolute exponent that may be taken as \(c=0.44\),
   \[
   \min\{2,M(\alpha)\}^c
   \le
   \lim_{d\to\infty}|S_d(\alpha)|^{1/d}
   \le
   \min\{2,M(\alpha)\}.
   \]
   In particular, Lehmer is equivalent to a uniform exponential-growth gap for these Bernoulli subset-sum sets for algebraic \(\alpha\) that are not roots of unity.

2. C. J. Smyth, **The Mahler measure of algebraic numbers** (survey).  The classical nonreciprocal theorem gives
   \[
   M(\alpha)\ge M(x^3-x-1)=1.324717\ldots
   \]
   for nonreciprocal algebraic integers outside the cyclotomic exceptions.  This means the unresolved near-one regime is reciprocal.

3. Gunther Cornelissen, David Hokken and Berend Ringeling, **The asymptotic Mahler measure of Gaussian periods**, Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society 133 (2026), e70198, first published 25 August 2026, DOI 10.1112/plms.70198.  
   <https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1112/plms.70198>

   This is a recent restricted-family result for cyclotomic/Gaussian-period algebraic integers.  It does not supply a sequence contradicting the classical Lehmer gap, nor an all-algebraic-integer proof.

4. Matthew Baker, **Reciprocity via Reciprocants**, arXiv:2309.02512 (revised 2026).  
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02512>

   This gives a clean trace-polynomial formalism for reciprocal polynomials and, in particular, the square-resultant identity reused below.

I found no accepted 2026 source establishing either a full proof or a counterexample sequence \(1<M(P_k)\to1\).  Claimed proof manuscripts are therefore not treated as closure without independent acceptance/checking.

## 3. First reduction: the only unresolved hard core is primitive monic irreducible reciprocal, of even degree tending to infinity

This section records the reductions explicitly because they matter for all subsequent attacks.

### Proposition 3.1 — reduction of a hypothetical counterexample sequence

If the Lehmer gap is false, then there is a sequence \(Q_k\in\mathbb Z[x]\) such that:

- \(Q_k\) is primitive, monic and irreducible;
- \(Q_k\) is noncyclotomic and reciprocal;
- \(Q_k(0)=1\);
- \(\deg Q_k=2d_k\to\infty\);
- \(1<M(Q_k)\to1\).

#### Proof

Mahler measure is multiplicative:
\[
M(PQ)=M(P)M(Q).
\]
If an integer polynomial has content of absolute value at least \(2\), or primitive leading coefficient of absolute value at least \(2\), then its Mahler measure is at least \(2\).  Consequently any sequence with measure tending to \(1\) can, after discarding finitely many terms and removing harmless unit factors, be taken monic and primitive.

Factor a monic \(P\) into monic irreducibles.  Kronecker's theorem says the irreducible factors of Mahler measure \(1\) are cyclotomic (or the monomial factor \(x\), which is also measure one).  If \(M(P)>1\), at least one irreducible noncyclotomic factor \(Q\) satisfies
\[
1<M(Q)\le M(P).
\]
Thus a sequence tending to \(1\) yields an irreducible sequence tending to \(1\).

Smyth's theorem gives a fixed lower bound \(1.324717\ldots\) for the nonreciprocal irreducible case, so all sufficiently late members of a hypothetical near-one sequence must be reciprocal.

For a monic irreducible reciprocal polynomial \(Q\), reciprocity gives constant term \(\pm1\).  The anti-reciprocal sign is impossible for a noncyclotomic irreducible: if
\[
x^nQ(1/x)=-Q(x),
\]
then setting \(x=1\) gives \(Q(1)=0\), hence irreducibility forces \(Q=x-1\), which is cyclotomic.  Therefore the hard core is genuinely reciprocal with constant term \(1\).

A reciprocal polynomial of odd degree satisfies \(Q(-1)=0\), hence an irreducible reciprocal odd-degree polynomial is cyclotomic.  The noncyclotomic irreducible hard core therefore has even degree \(2d\).

Finally, in every fixed degree, bounded Mahler measure bounds the coefficients (e.g. by the elementary symmetric functions of the roots), hence only finitely many monic integer polynomials occur.  A sequence of distinct noncyclotomic irreducibles with \(M\to1\) must therefore have degree tending to infinity.  ∎

This reduction is standard in spirit, but it is useful operationally: a full attack may concentrate entirely on reciprocal algebraic units of growing even degree.

## 4. Trace polynomial and an exact Green-energy restatement

Let \(Q\in\mathbb Z[x]\) be monic reciprocal of degree \(2d\).  There is a unique monic \(R\in\mathbb Z[y]\), degree \(d\), such that
\[
\boxed{Q(x)=x^dR(x+x^{-1}).}
\]
Write the roots of \(R\) as \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_d\).  Each \(\beta_i\) corresponds to a reciprocal root pair \(\alpha_i,\alpha_i^{-1}\) of \(Q\) through
\[
\beta_i=\alpha_i+\alpha_i^{-1}.
\]

Define the Green function of the complement of \([-2,2]\) by
\[
g(z)=\log\left|\frac{z+\sqrt{z^2-4}}{2}\right|,
\]
where the inverse-Joukowski branch is chosen with modulus at least one; equivalently \(g=0\) on \([-2,2]\) and \(g(z)=\log|w|\) when \(z=w+w^{-1}\), \(|w|\ge1\).

For one reciprocal pair,
\[
\log\max(1,|\alpha|)+\log\max(1,|\alpha|^{-1})
=|\log|\alpha||
=g(\alpha+\alpha^{-1}).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\log M(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^d g(\beta_i).}
\]

Thus the reciprocal hard core of Lehmer can be stated as follows:

> Is there an absolute \(\eta>0\) such that every monic irreducible \(R\in\mathbb Z[y]\) arising from a noncyclotomic reciprocal polynomial has
> \[
> \sum_{R(\beta)=0}g(\beta)\ge\eta?
> \]

The value can tend to zero only if an increasing-degree collection of algebraic conjugates accumulates extremely tightly near the interval \([-2,2]\).

## 5. New exact reduction: two traces compress the reciprocal Bernoulli problem to one-dimensional Chebyshev subset sums

This is the main new lemma of this run.  I do **not** claim literature novelty; it is a campaign-new deduction from reciprocity plus the Breuillard–Varjú subset-sum framework.

Let \(\alpha\ne\pm1\) be a reciprocal algebraic number and set
\[
t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}.
\]
Define the Chebyshev-type polynomials
\[
C_0(t)=2,\qquad C_1(t)=t,\qquad
C_{j+1}(t)=tC_j(t)-C_{j-1}(t).
\]
Then
\[
C_j(t)=\alpha^j+\alpha^{-j}.
\]
For \(d\ge0\), define
\[
S_d(\alpha)=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_j\alpha^j:\varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\right\},
\]
\[
T_d(t)=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_j C_j(t):\varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\right\}.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — two-trace compression

For every \(d\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{|T_d(t)|\le |S_d(\alpha)|\le |T_d(t)|\,|T_{d+1}(t)|\le |T_{d+1}(t)|^2.}
\]

#### Proof

Because the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) is reciprocal and \(\alpha^{-1}\in\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), the assignment
\[
\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}
\]
extends to a field automorphism of \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), of order two.

For
\[
Y=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_j\alpha^j\in S_d(\alpha),
\]
we have
\[
Y+\sigma(Y)=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_j(\alpha^j+\alpha^{-j})
=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_jC_j(t).
\]
As \((\varepsilon_j)\) ranges over all binary choices, the image is exactly \(T_d(t)\).  Therefore
\[
|T_d(t)|\le |S_d(\alpha)|.
\]

Now consider the map
\[
\Phi:K\to K^2,
\qquad
\Phi(Y)=\bigl(Y+\sigma(Y),\ \alpha Y+\alpha^{-1}\sigma(Y)\bigr).
\]
It is injective.  Indeed, if \(\Phi(Y)=(0,0)\), then \(\sigma(Y)=-Y\), and the second coordinate becomes
\[
(\alpha-\alpha^{-1})Y=0.
\]
Since \(\alpha\ne\pm1\), this gives \(Y=0\).

For \(Y\in S_d(\alpha)\), the first coordinate belongs to \(T_d(t)\), while
\[
\alpha Y+\alpha^{-1}\sigma(Y)
=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_j(\alpha^{j+1}+\alpha^{-(j+1)})
=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_jC_{j+1}(t),
\]
which is a subset of \(T_{d+1}(t)\) (the coefficient of \(C_0\) is simply zero).  Hence injectivity gives
\[
|S_d(\alpha)|\le |T_d(t)|\,|T_{d+1}(t)|.
\]
Finally \(T_d(t)\subseteq T_{d+1}(t)\), by choosing the last coefficient to be zero, so the square bound follows. ∎

### Corollary 5.2 — exponential growth is equivalent up to a square root

Let
\[
G(\alpha)=\lim_{d\to\infty}|S_d(\alpha)|^{1/d},
\]
whose existence in the relevant setting is part of the Breuillard–Varjú framework.  Then
\[
\boxed{
G(\alpha)^{1/2}
\le
\liminf_{d\to\infty}|T_d(t)|^{1/d}
\le
\limsup_{d\to\infty}|T_d(t)|^{1/d}
\le
G(\alpha).
}
\]

The upper bound comes from \(|T_d|\le|S_d|\).  The lower bound follows from
\(|S_d|\le|T_{d+1}|^2\) after taking \(d\)-th roots and shifting the index.

Combining this with Breuillard–Varjú gives a particularly concrete reciprocal reformulation:

> **Reciprocal trace-growth formulation.**  Modulo the already-gapped nonreciprocal case, Lehmer is equivalent to the existence of an absolute \(\delta>0\) such that for every non-root-of-unity reciprocal algebraic \(\alpha\ne\pm1\), with \(t=\alpha+\alpha^{-1}\),
> \[
> \liminf_{d\to\infty}|T_d(t)|^{1/d}\ge 1+\delta.
> \]

This reduces a complex-valued Bernoulli sum problem to a one-dimensional recurrence in the trace field.

## 6. Exact finite-field analogue on the norm-one torus

The previous lemma has a useful finite-field version that matches the Breuillard–Varjú philosophy very closely.

Let \(p\) be odd and let
\[
x\in\mathbb F_{p^2}^{\times},\qquad x^{p+1}=1,\qquad x\ne\pm1.
\]
Then
\[
x^p=x^{-1}
\]
and therefore
\[
t=x+x^{-1}\in\mathbb F_p.
\]
Define \(C_j(t)\), \(S_d(x)\), and \(T_d(t)\) exactly as above, now over the finite fields.

### Theorem 6.1 — norm-one trace sandwich

For every \(d\),
\[
\boxed{|T_d(t)|\le|S_d(x)|\le|T_{d+1}(t)|^2.}
\]

#### Proof

Frobenius is the involution \(Y\mapsto Y^p\).  For
\[
Y=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_jx^j,
\]
its field trace is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_{p^2}/\mathbb F_p}(Y)
=Y+Y^p
=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_j(x^j+x^{-j})
=\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_jC_j(t),
\]
so the trace image of \(S_d(x)\) is exactly \(T_d(t)\), giving the lower cardinality bound.

For the upper bound, use the \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear map
\[
Y\longmapsto\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}(Y),\operatorname{Tr}(xY)\bigr).
\]
If both coordinates vanish, then \(Y^p=-Y\), and
\[
0=\operatorname{Tr}(xY)=xY+x^pY^p=(x-x^{-1})Y.
\]
Since \(x\ne\pm1\), this forces \(Y=0\), so the map is injective.  For \(Y\in S_d(x)\), the two coordinates lie in \(T_d(t)\) and \(T_{d+1}(t)\), respectively.  Hence
\[
|S_d(x)|\le |T_d(t)|\,|T_{d+1}(t)|\le |T_{d+1}(t)|^2.
\]
∎

## 7. Proposed bridge: inversion Frobenius + Chebotarev + trace growth

This is the most promising next route, but it is **not yet a proof**.

Let \(\alpha\) be in the reciprocal hard core and let \(\sigma\) be the automorphism \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}\).  Extend \(\sigma\) to an element of the Galois group of the normal closure.  Chebotarev provides a positive-density set of unramified primes whose Frobenius lies in the conjugacy class of such an extension.  At a corresponding residue place and for a suitable conjugate \(\beta\), reduction gives
\[
\bar\beta^p=\bar\beta^{-1}.
\]
Thus \(x=\bar\beta\) lies on the norm-one torus in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\), and
\[
t=x+x^{-1}\in\mathbb F_p.
\]

This is exactly the setting of Theorem 6.1.  It suggests translating the Breuillard–Varjú finite-field counting criterion into a one-dimensional problem for the recurrence
\[
C_{j+1}=tC_j-C_{j-1}.
\]

For a **fixed** algebraic \(\alpha\), primes at which the reduced \(x\) has unusually small multiplicative order can be attacked, as in Breuillard–Varjú, by noting that order \(n\) implies the prime divides a norm of \(\alpha^n-1\).  The required quantitative uniformity in the degree/height, however, is precisely where a full Lehmer proof becomes difficult.

A concrete finite-field target emerging from this run is:

> Find absolute \(c,\delta>0\) such that, outside an exceptional prime set quantitatively small enough for the Breuillard–Varjú argument, every
> \[
> x\in\mathbb F_{p^2},\quad x^{p+1}=1,\quad x\ne\pm1,
> \]
> with sufficiently large multiplicative order and \(t=x+x^{-1}\) has some \(d\le c\log p\) for which
> \[
> |T_d(t)|\ge p^\delta.
> \]

The trace recurrence has the product identity
\[
\boxed{C_j(t)C_k(t)=C_{j+k}(t)+C_{|j-k|}(t),}
\]
which follows directly from \(C_j= x^j+x^{-j}\).  Thus coefficient-bounded trace sets have a structured sum-product calculus.  For example, if
\[
T_d^{(K)}(t)=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^d a_jC_j(t): |a_j|\le K\right\},
\]
then addition stays in \(T_d^{(2K)}\), while products lie in a coefficient-bounded set of degree \(2d\).  This provides an entry point for a finite-field sum-product growth argument.  The missing point is a **uniform initial-expansion lemma** that rules out a long sequence of tiny Chebyshev subset-sum sets when \(x\) has high order.

## 8. Reciprocal resultants and discriminants: exact arithmetic package

Let
\[
Q(x)=x^dR(x+x^{-1}),\qquad
B(x)=x^eS(x+x^{-1})
\]
be monic reciprocal polynomials of even degrees \(2d\) and \(2e\).

Baker's reciprocant identity gives
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_x(Q,B)=\operatorname{Res}_y(R,S)^2.}
\]
This immediately implies strong square constraints on cyclotomic resultants.

A direct root calculation also gives the discriminant identity
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Disc}_x(Q)
=R(2)R(-2)\operatorname{Disc}_y(R)^2
=(-1)^dQ(1)Q(-1)\operatorname{Disc}(R)^2.
}
\]

Indeed, if \(R(y)=\prod_i(y-\beta_i)\), then
\[
Q(x)=\prod_i(x^2-\beta_i x+1).
\]
The discriminant of one quadratic factor is \(\beta_i^2-4\), and the resultant of two distinct quadratic factors is \((\beta_i-\beta_j)^2\).  Multiplying the factor discriminants and squared pairwise resultants gives
\[
\prod_i(\beta_i^2-4)\prod_{i<j}(\beta_i-\beta_j)^4
=R(2)R(-2)\operatorname{Disc}(R)^2.
\]

For every \(n\ge3\), \(\Phi_n\) is monic reciprocal of even degree.  If \(\Psi_n\) is its trace polynomial, then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}(Q,\Phi_n)=\operatorname{Res}(R,\Psi_n)^2.}
\]
Thus every nonzero cyclotomic resultant in the reciprocal hard core is a nonnegative perfect square.

For the cyclic resultant
\[
\Delta_m(Q)=\operatorname{Res}(Q,x^m-1)
=\prod_{n\mid m}\operatorname{Res}(Q,\Phi_n),
\]
it follows that

- if \(m\) is odd,
  \[
  \Delta_m(Q)=Q(1)\times(\text{integer square});
  \]
- if \(m\) is even,
  \[
  \Delta_m(Q)=Q(1)Q(-1)\times(\text{integer square}).
  \]

This packages all higher cyclotomic interactions into square arithmetic; only the \(\Phi_1\) and \(\Phi_2\) factors control the square class.

## 9. Exact Lehmer-polynomial checks and an obstruction to a naive resultant route

Take Lehmer's polynomial
\[
L(x)=x^{10}+x^9-x^7-x^6-x^5-x^4-x^3+x+1.
\]
Its trace polynomial is exactly
\[
\boxed{R_L(y)=y^5+y^4-5y^3-5y^2+4y+3.}
\]
Exact integer arithmetic gives
\[
R_L(2)=R_L(-2)=-1,
\]
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(R_L)=36497,
\]
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(L)=36497^2=1,332,031,009.
\]

For \(3\le n\le30\), direct exact resultant computation verifies that every
\(\operatorname{Res}(L,\Phi_n)\) is a perfect square, as the theorem predicts.  More importantly, **20 of these 28 resultants are already equal to \(1\)**.  The nonunit values in this range are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
n&\operatorname{Res}(L,\Phi_n)\\\hline
4&9\\
12&9\\
14&169\\
15&841\\
19&1369\\
22&529\\
25&2401\\
26&625
\end{array}
\]

Therefore the tempting idea

> “small Mahler measure should force every small cyclotomic resultant to be nontrivially large”

is false even at the extremal benchmark.  Any resultant proof must use a more global primitive-divisor/density statement, not pointwise nonunitness at every small order.

## 10. Claim ledger

### Known and rechecked

- Mahler measure is multiplicative.
- Kronecker reduces measure-one irreducibles to cyclotomic/monomial factors.
- Smyth supplies an absolute gap for the nonreciprocal irreducible case.
- The unresolved near-one regime can therefore be taken monic, irreducible, reciprocal, even-degree, and of degree tending to infinity.
- Breuillard–Varjú converts Lehmer to an exponential-growth gap for Bernoulli subset sums.
- Baker's reciprocant identity makes reciprocal resultants perfect squares in trace coordinates.

### Newly proved in this campaign run

1. **Two-trace compression theorem**
   \[
   |T_d(t)|\le|S_d(\alpha)|\le|T_{d+1}(t)|^2.
   \]

2. **Growth equivalence up to square root**
   \[
   G(\alpha)^{1/2}\le\liminf|T_d|^{1/d}\le\limsup|T_d|^{1/d}\le G(\alpha).
   \]

3. **Finite-field norm-one trace sandwich** with the same inequality in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\).

4. Direct discriminant identity
   \[
   \operatorname{Disc}(Q)=R(2)R(-2)\operatorname{Disc}(R)^2.
   \]

5. Cyclic-resultant square-class consequences.

These statements have been algebraically checked and, where finite, exhaustively verified by the archived scripts.  Literature novelty for the two-trace formulation has **not** been established.

### Conditional/proposed

- Chebotarev should provide a positive-density inversion-Frobenius prime family for each fixed reciprocal algebraic number.
- A sufficiently uniform finite-field expansion theorem for \(T_d(t)\) on the norm-one torus would combine with Breuillard–Varjú to prove Lehmer.
- A primitive-divisor theorem exploiting the square structure of reciprocal cyclotomic resultants may provide an alternative route.

### Still open

- No uniform initial-expansion theorem for \(T_d(t)\) is proved.
- No degree-uniform Chebotarev/counting constants adequate for the full conjecture are obtained.
- No absolute positive Mahler gap has been proved.
- No certified sequence with Mahler measure tending to one has been constructed.

## 11. Failed/blocked approaches worth retaining

1. **Pointwise cyclotomic-resultant lower bounds.**  Lehmer's own polynomial has \(\operatorname{Res}(L,\Phi_n)=1\) for most \(3\le n\le30\), so a proof demanding nonunit resultants at all small \(n\) cannot work.

2. **Treating recent restricted families as a solution.**  The August 2026 Gaussian-period paper gives sophisticated asymptotics for a particular Galois family but does not settle the unrestricted gap.

3. **Trace polynomial alone without counting structure.**  Replacing \(Q\) by \(R\) halves the degree, but plain coefficient/discriminant information does not visibly force positive Green energy.  The new two-trace counting lemma is more promising because it preserves the exponential-growth criterion.

## 12. Next attack

The highest-value next step is to prove or sharply refute a **norm-one Chebyshev expansion lemma**.

For odd \(p\), high-order
\[
x\in\mathbb F_{p^2}^{\times},\qquad x^{p+1}=1,
\]
and \(t=x+x^{-1}\in\mathbb F_p\), study
\[
T_d(t)=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^d\varepsilon_jC_j(t):\varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\right\}.
\]
The concrete tasks are:

1. derive a quantitative small-doubling consequence if \(|T_d|\) stays subexponential;
2. use
   \[
   C_jC_k=C_{j+k}+C_{|j-k|}
   \]
   to feed that structure into a finite-field sum-product theorem;
3. show the resulting structural alternative forces \(x\) to have small multiplicative order;
4. quantify the exceptional small-order primes via norms of \(\alpha^n-1\) in the Breuillard–Varjú/Chebotarev setup;
5. track all constants uniformly in the varying degree, since that is the essential Lehmer difficulty.

A parallel arithmetic route is to study primitive prime divisors of the trace resultants \(\operatorname{Res}(R,\Psi_n)\), now knowing that the original reciprocal resultants are exact squares.
