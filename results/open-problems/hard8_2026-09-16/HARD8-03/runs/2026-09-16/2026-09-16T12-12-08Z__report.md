## 0. Exact target and convention

For every finite total Boolean communication matrix `M in {0,1}^{X x Y}` of real rank `r`, prove universal constants `C,c>0` such that

`D(M) <= C [log_2(r+1)]^c`,

or exhibit an asymptotic family violating every fixed power of `log r`.

Alice receives a row index, Bob a column index, and a deterministic protocol is a binary transcript tree whose leaf is labelled by the output bit. `D(M)` is minimum worst-case transcript length. The target is total Boolean matrices and real rank; approximate rank, sign rank, randomized complexity, and the false log-approximate-rank analogue are different problems.

The project route considers a finite set `V` of distinct nonzero Boolean columns. A subset `S subset V` is **dissociated** if no nonzero coefficient vector in `{-1,0,1}^S` sums to zero. For maximal dissociated `S`, every omitted `v` has a signed representation

`v = sum_{a in A} a - sum_{b in B} b`,

and

`nu_S(v) = min |B|`.

Define

`H_k(S)={v in V: nu_S(v)>k}`

and

`rho_V(k)=min_{S maximal dissociated} rank_R H_k(S)`.

The exploratory profile inequality is

`(k+1) rho_V(k) <= r := rank_R(V)`.                    `(P_k)`

It is a sufficient structural route toward log-rank, not an established equivalent formulation.

The preceding checkpoint proved `(P_k)` for real nullity `|V|-r<=2`. Its addendum defined the **signed-relation rank**

`sigma(V)=dim_R span{c in {-1,0,1}^V : sum_v c_v v=0}`

and proved that every maximal dissociated `S` satisfies

`|V\S| <= sigma(V) <= |V|-r`.                           `(2.1)`

This run attacks the first unresolved case `|V|-r=3`.

## 3. A minimum-support signed relation cannot use every column when sigma>=2

### Lemma 3.1

Assume `sigma(V)>=2`. Then a minimum-support nonzero signed relation has support strictly smaller than `V`.

#### Proof

Let `c in {-1,0,1}^V` be a nonzero signed relation of minimum support. Suppose `supp(c)=V`. Then every coordinate of `c` is `+1` or `-1`.

Because `sigma(V)>=2`, choose a signed relation `d` linearly independent of `c`. If `d` has a zero coordinate, then `supp(d)` is a proper subset of `V`, contradicting minimality of `c`. Thus every coordinate of `d` is also `+1` or `-1`.

Since `d` is not `c` or `-c`, both

`(c+d)/2` and `(c-d)/2`

are nonzero. Every coordinate of each vector lies in `{-1,0,1}`; both are real kernel vectors because `c,d` are. Each has at least one zero coordinate, hence strictly smaller support than `c`, contradiction. QED.

For nullity three, `|V|=r+3`; therefore whenever `sigma>=2`, a support-minimal signed relation has size at most `r+2`.

## 4. Nullity three is completely safe when signed-relation rank is at most two

### Theorem 4.1

Let `V` be a finite set of distinct nonzero Boolean columns with

`|V|-rank(V)=3`

and `sigma(V)<=2`. Then `(P_k)` holds for every integer `k>=0`.

#### Proof

Write `r=rank(V)` and `N=r+3`.

If `sigma=0`, there is no nonzero signed relation at all, so `V` itself is dissociated and `rho_V(k)=0` for all `k`.

Assume first `sigma=1`. Take a support-minimal signed relation

`sum_{p in P}p = sum_{n in N_-}n`.

Choose `t` on its smaller side. As in the prior one-light omission lemma, the support with `t` removed is dissociated; extend it greedily, never adding `t`, to a maximal dissociated `S`. The original relation guarantees `t` remains non-addable and gives

`nu_S(t) <= min(|P|,|N_-|)-1 <= floor((N-2)/2)=floor((r+1)/2)`.

By `(2.1)`, a maximal dissociated set omits at most one column. Since `t` is omitted, it is the only omission. Thus `rho(k)=0` once `k>=floor((r+1)/2)`, while below that threshold `rho(k)<=1`; hence `(k+1)rho(k)<=r` throughout.

Now assume `sigma=2`. By Lemma 3.1 a minimum-support signed relation has size `s<=r+2`. Choose `t` on its smaller side and perform the same extension. Put

`w=floor((s-2)/2) <= floor(r/2)`.

Then `nu_S(t)<=w`, and `(2.1)` says `S` omits at most two columns.

- If `k<w`, then `rho(k)<=2`, so

  `(k+1)rho(k) <= 2w <= r`.

- If `w<=k<=r-1`, the omission `t` is light, leaving at most one heavy omission; hence `rho(k)<=1`, and

  `(k+1)rho(k)<=k+1<=r`.

- If `k>=r`, then there are no heavy omissions for this same `S`. If `S` omits only `t`, this is immediate. If it omits two columns, then `|S|=N-2=r+1`; the universal nonnegative-column width estimate from the prior checkpoint gives every omitted column width at most `|S|-1=r`. Thus `rho(k)=0`.

This proves `(P_k)` whenever `sigma<=2`. QED.

### Corollary 4.2

Any nullity-three counterexample to `(P_k)` must have

`sigma(V)=3`.

Thus the signed relations must span the **entire** three-dimensional real kernel; real-kernel directions invisible to `{-1,0,1}` cancellation cannot be responsible for the first obstruction.

## 5. Improved high-threshold vanishing for all nullity-three configurations

### Proposition 5.1

For every distinct nonzero Boolean configuration with `|V|-r=3`,

`rho_V(k)=0` for all `k>=r`.                              `(5.1)`

This improves the generic prior bound, which only gave vanishing from `k>=r+1` when specialized to nullity three.

#### Proof

If `V` is dissociated, trivial. Otherwise take a support-minimal signed relation, choose `t` on its smaller side, and extend the relation support minus `t` to a maximal dissociated `S` omitting `t`.

The displayed relation gives

`nu_S(t)<=floor((r+1)/2)<=r`.

Write `q=|V\S|`. By the real-nullity omission bound, `q<=3`.

- If `q=1`, `t` is the only omission.
- If `q>=2`, then `|S|=r+3-q<=r+1`. Every other omitted column has width at most `|S|-1<=r` by nonnegativity of the Boolean columns.

Thus every omitted column is light at threshold `k=r`, and therefore at all larger thresholds. QED.

## 6. Rank-three heavy tails cannot violate the profile inequality

The next theorem is the main new structural result of this run.

### Theorem 6.1

Assume `|V|-r=3`. If for some `k`

`rho_V(k)=3`,

then in fact

`3(k+1) <= r-1`.                                        `(6.1)`

In particular a rank-three heavy tail can never violate `(P_k)`.

#### Proof

Because every maximal dissociated set omits at most three columns, `rho(k)=3` forces a very rigid situation: **every** maximal dissociated set omits exactly three columns, all three are heavy, and those three heavy columns are linearly independent. Consequently every maximal dissociated set has size `r` and is a real basis.

### Step 1: signed girth is at least `2k+4`

Take any support-minimal signed relation

`sum_{p in P}p = sum_{n in N_-}n`.

If, say, `|P|<=k+1`, choose `t in P`. The relation with `t` removed is dissociated by support-minimality. Extend it to a maximal dissociated set `S` that still omits `t`. The relation represents `t` with negative width at most `|P|-1<=k`, contradicting the just-established fact that every omission of every maximal set is heavy.

The same applies to the other side. Hence

`|P|,|N_-| >= k+2`,

so every nonzero signed relation has support at least

`g_pm(V) >= 2k+4`.                                      `(6.2)`

### Step 2: each omitted basis coordinate vector has support at least `2k+3`

Fix a maximal dissociated basis `S` and an omitted column `u`. Because `S` is a real basis, the signed representation is unique:

`u = sum_{s in S} a_s s`, `a_s in {-1,0,1}`.

Let

`p=|{s:a_s=+1}|`, `n=|{s:a_s=-1}|`.

Since `u` is heavy, `n>=k+1`.

Also `p>=1`: if all coefficients were nonpositive, a nonzero nonnegative Boolean vector `u` would be a nonpositive linear combination of nonnegative Boolean columns, impossible.

Choose a basis element `s` with `a_s=+1`. By the exact exchange criterion from the preceding checkpoint, replacing `s` by `u` yields a dissociated set `S'`. If `S'` were not maximal, it could be enlarged to a maximal dissociated set of size at least `r+1`, contradicting the fact that all maximal sets have size `r`. Hence `S'` is itself maximal.

In the new basis,

`s = u - sum_{x != s} a_x x`.

Its negative width is exactly `p-1`. Since every omission of every maximal basis is heavy,

`p-1>k`, so `p>=k+2`.

Therefore the support `A={s:a_s!=0}` obeys

`|A|=p+n >= 2k+3`.                                      `(6.3)`

### Step 3: two omitted coordinate vectors are sign-consistent on their overlap

Take two omitted columns `u,v` with unique coefficient vectors `a,b` and supports `A,B`.

Suppose there are coordinates `i,j in A cap B` with opposite relative signs: for example `b_i=a_i` but `b_j=-a_j`. Exchange `i` with `u`. The new set is a maximal real basis by Step 2. In that basis the unique real coefficient of `v` at the old basis element `j` is

`b_j - (b_i/a_i) a_j = -a_j-a_j = -2a_j`,

which is not in `{-1,0,1}`. Then `v` has no signed representation over the new basis, contradicting maximality. Therefore on `A cap B` the ratio `b_s/a_s` is constant whenever the overlap is nonempty; it is either always `+1` or always `-1`.

Let this constant be `lambda in {+1,-1}` (choose either sign if `A cap B` is empty). Then

`u - lambda v = sum_{s in S} (a_s-lambda b_s)s`

is a signed relation: overlap coordinates cancel, and outside the overlap each coefficient is `0,+1`, or `-1`. Its support is exactly

`2 + |A triangle B|`.

By `(6.2)`,

`|A triangle B| >= 2k+2`.                               `(6.4)`

### Step 4: counting contradiction at the would-be violating scale

Because `A,B subset S` and `|S|=r`,

`|A triangle B| <= 2r-|A|-|B|`.

Using `(6.3)` twice,

`|A triangle B| <= 2r-4k-6`.

Together with `(6.4)`,

`2k+2 <= 2r-4k-6`,

so

`6k+8 <= 2r`, equivalently `3k+4<=r`.

Therefore

`3(k+1)=3k+3 <= r-1`,

which is `(6.1)`. QED.

### Interpretation

A putative nullity-three obstruction cannot live in the regime where three independent heavy directions survive. The exchange closure of maximal bases forces large positive and negative sides in each fundamental signed relation, while pairwise pivot compatibility creates a much smaller signed relation from the symmetric difference of two supports. Those two requirements are numerically incompatible at any scale that could violate `(P_k)`.

## 7. Exact normal form for any remaining nullity-three counterexample

Combining Theorems 4.1 and 6.1 with Proposition 5.1 gives a sharp reduction.

### Corollary 7.1

If a nullity-three configuration violates `(P_k)`, then necessarily

1. `sigma(V)=3`;
2. `rho_V(k)=2`;
3. `2(k+1)>r`, hence `k>=floor(r/2)`;
4. `k<=r-1`.

Moreover there exists a maximal dissociated **real basis** `S` with exactly three omitted columns `{t,u,v}` such that

- `nu_S(t)<=k`;
- `nu_S(u)>k` and `nu_S(v)>k`;
- `u,v` are linearly independent.

#### Proof

Only `rho=0,1,2,3` are possible by nullity three. `rho=3` is safe by Theorem 6.1. `rho=1` is safe for `k<=r-1`, and Proposition 5.1 kills `k>=r`. Thus a violation must have `rho=2`; Theorem 4.1 forces `sigma=3`, and violation gives `2(k+1)>r`.

Since `sigma>=2`, Lemma 3.1 says a minimum-support signed relation has size at most `r+2`. Choose `t` from its smaller side and extend the other support elements to a maximal dissociated set. Then

`nu_S(t)<=floor(r/2)<=k`.

If this maximal set omitted only one or two columns, after `t` is light there could be at most one heavy omitted direction, contradicting `rho=2`. Therefore it omits exactly three columns and has size `r`; the two remaining omissions must be heavy and independent. QED.

This is the exact unresolved nullity-three core.

## 8. Common-negative pivot lemma inside the unresolved core

The two heavy omitted columns in Corollary 7.1 have unique basis-coordinate vectors

`u=sum a_i s_i`, `v=sum b_i s_i`.

Their negative supports each have size at least `k+1>r/2`, so they intersect. Choose `s` with

`a_s=b_s=-1`.

Exchange `s` with `u`; the resulting real basis is dissociated. The unique transformed coefficients of `v` are

`b'_u=1`,

`b'_i=b_i-a_i` for `i!=s`.                              `(8.1)`

There are two possibilities.

1. If all coefficients in `(8.1)` lie in `{-1,0,1}`, then opposite-sign overlaps of `a,b` are absent. The negative coefficients of `v` after the pivot form

   `(N_b\supp(a)) union (P_a\supp(b))`,

   a disjoint subset of the complement of `N_a`. Hence

   `nu(v) <= r-|N_a| <= r-(k+1) <= k`.

   Thus the pivot makes the *other* heavy omission light.

2. If some coefficient in `(8.1)` is `+2` or `-2`, then `v` has no signed representation over the exchanged basis. Therefore adjoining `v` preserves dissociation, producing a dissociated set of size `r+1`.

So every common-negative pivot either **lightens the second heavy target immediately** or **increases the dissociated-set size**. A genuine `rho=2` obstruction must use the third omitted column `t` to restore two heavy directions after every such move. This is a much narrower finite exchange problem than the original nullity-three question.

## 10. Failed route and lesson

A stronger potential conjecture considered during this iteration was that one might always find a maximal dissociated `S` with

`sum_{v notin S} nu_S(v) <= r`.

This is **false in general**, even for small Boolean configurations: a rank-four example in the four-dimensional Boolean cube has minimum such sum `5`. Therefore a proof of `(P_k)` cannot simply charge every negative coefficient independently to rank. The rank filtration in `H_k(S)` genuinely matters.

This failed route was not used in any theorem above.
