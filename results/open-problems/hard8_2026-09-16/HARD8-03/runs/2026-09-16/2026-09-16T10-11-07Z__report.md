## 0. Exact target and protocol convention

For every finite total Boolean communication matrix `M in {0,1}^{X x Y}` of real rank `r`, prove universal constants `C,c>0` such that

`D(M) <= C [log_2(r+1)]^c`,

or exhibit an asymptotic family of total Boolean matrices violating every fixed power of `log r`.

Protocol convention: Alice receives `x in X`, Bob receives `y in Y`; a deterministic protocol is a binary transcript tree and the reached leaf carries the common output bit `M[x,y]`. `D(M)` is the minimum worst-case transcript length. The `+1` in the conjectural bound handles rank zero/small rank. Nothing below substitutes approximate rank, sign rank, randomized complexity, or the false log-approximate-rank analogue.

## 3. Exact single-exchange criterion

Let `S` be dissociated and let `v notin S` have at least one signed representation over `S`. Let

`R_S(v) = {(A,B): A,B subset S, A cap B = empty, v = sum A - sum B}`.

Define the **essential support**

`E_S(v) = intersection_{(A,B) in R_S(v)} (A union B)`.

### Theorem 3.1 (single-exchange criterion)

For `s in S`,

`(S \ {s}) union {v}` is dissociated **iff** `s in E_S(v)`.

#### Proof

Put `S'=(S\{s}) union {v}`.

If `S'` is not dissociated, there is a nonzero relation with coefficients in `{-1,0,1}` among its elements. Since `S\{s}` is a subset of the dissociated set `S`, the coefficient of `v` in this relation cannot be zero. Multiplying the relation by `-1` if needed and rearranging gives

`v = sum C - sum D`

with disjoint `C,D subset S\{s}`. Thus there is a representation of `v` avoiding `s`, so `s notin E_S(v)`.

Conversely, if `s notin E_S(v)`, some representation `v=sum C-sum D` avoids `s`. Moving the two sides together gives a nontrivial `{-1,0,1}` relation inside `S'`, so `S'` is not dissociated. QED.

### Interpretation

A basis element is exchangeable with `v` exactly when it is used by **every** signed representation of `v`; uniqueness of the representation makes every support element exchangeable. This is the correct local exchange object. Merely belonging to one minimum-negative representation is not enough.

## 4. Subspace-extension inequality

The optimized profile has a useful hereditary inequality that was not isolated in the previous checkpoint.

Let `W <= span_R(V)` be a subspace spanned by `U := V cap W`, and write

`d = rank(U)`, `r = rank(V)`.

### Theorem 4.1

For every `k>=0`,

`rho_V(k) <= (r-d) + rho_U(k)`.

#### Proof

Choose a maximal dissociated `S_W subset U` attaining `rho_U(k)`. Extend it, by greedily adjoining columns from `V\U` whenever dissociation is preserved, to a maximal dissociated set `S subset V`.

Every signed representation of a column `u in U` over `S_W` is still available over the larger set `S`. Hence

`H_k(S) cap U subset H_k(S_W)`.

The part of `H_k(S)` outside `W` contributes at most `r-d` new dimensions modulo `W`. Therefore

`rank H_k(S) <= rank(H_k(S) cap W) + (r-d)`

`<= rho_U(k) + (r-d)`.

Minimizing over maximal `S` proves the claim. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 (large tame subspace criterion)

If `U=V cap W` has `rho_U(k)=0`, then

`rho_V(k) <= r-d`.

Thus the candidate bound `rho_V(k) <= r/(k+1)` would follow whenever one can find a `k`-tame column-spanned subspace of dimension at least `rk/(k+1)`. This recasts the route as a search for a large internally tame subspace rather than a globally perfect basis.

## 5. Abstract equal-sum blocks: the previous formula depends only on relation geometry

The coordinate-partition family from the previous checkpoint was more general than its presentation suggested. Its proof used only the real relation space, not the Cartesian-product realization.

Let `V` be partitioned into disjoint groups

`G_1,...,G_g`, `|G_j|=h_j>=2`,

with total `N=sum_j h_j`. Assume:

1. all group sums are equal:
   `sum_{v in G_1}v = ... = sum_{v in G_g}v`;
2. `rank_R(V)=N-g+1`.

The `g-1` obvious equal-sum relations then span the entire real relation space. Equivalently, every real relation has coefficients constant on each group,

`a_v=lambda_j` for `v in G_j`, with `sum_j lambda_j=0`.

Set `t_j=h_j-1` and `r=1+sum_j t_j`.

### Theorem 5.1 (maximal dissociated sets)

A subset `S subset V` is dissociated iff it contains at most one complete group. It is maximal dissociated iff exactly one group `G_*` is complete and every other group is missing exactly one column.

#### Proof

If two groups are complete, equality of their sums gives a nontrivial `{-1,0,1}` relation. Conversely, any signed relation supported on `S` has coefficients constant within ambient groups. Every incomplete group contains an absent vector whose coefficient is zero, forcing that group's constant to be zero. If at most one group is complete, the condition `sum lambda_j=0` forces the final constant to vanish as well.

Maximality follows exactly as in the prior coordinate proof: if no group is complete one may add a missing vector, and if a non-complete group misses at least two vectors one may add one without creating a second complete group. QED.

### Theorem 5.2 (unique omitted-column representation)

If `G_*` is the complete group and `v` is the unique omitted vector of `G_j`, `j != *`, then its signed representation is unique and equals

`v = sum_{u in G_*}u - sum_{u in G_j\{v}}u`.

Hence

`nu_S(v)=h_j-1=t_j`.

The uniqueness follows because moving any representation to one side yields a real relation; the relation-space hypothesis forces constant coefficients groupwise.

### Corollary 5.3 (exact optimized profile)

Let

`N_k = |{j : t_j>k}|`.

Then

`rho_V(k)=max(0,N_k-1)`.

A largest group may be chosen as the complete group and simultaneously optimizes every `k`. Furthermore

`(k+1) rho_V(k) <= r-1 < r`.

#### Proof

For a fixed complete group, the heavy columns are precisely the omitted columns from groups with `t_j>k`; these omitted columns are linearly independent by the same relation-space argument. If at least one group is heavy, making one heavy group complete removes exactly one heavy omitted vector, giving `N_k-1`. Choosing a largest group is heavy whenever any group is heavy.

Each remaining heavy group contributes `t_j>=k+1`, so

`(k+1)rho_V(k) <= sum_{heavy j != *} t_j <= r-1`. QED.

### Consequence

The sharp near-equality examples from the prior coordinate-partition construction are not artifacts of coordinate indicator columns. They are a property of the entire equal-sum relation geometry. Any counterexample to the constant-1 profile route must therefore use more complicated overlapping relations than a collection of groups sharing one common sum.

## 6. Complete profile for real nullity one

The second broad relation geometry is a configuration with exactly one real relation.

Let `V` have real rank `r` and `|V|=r+1`. Let `c=(c_v)_{v in V}` span the one-dimensional real kernel.

### Theorem 6.1

If the kernel contains no nonzero vector with entries in `{-1,0,1}`, then `V` itself is dissociated and

`rho_V(k)=0`

for all `k`.

Otherwise scale the unique signed relation so that

`sum_{v in P} v = sum_{v in N} v`,

with disjoint nonempty `P,N`; columns outside `P union N` have coefficient zero in the unique real relation. Put `a=|P|`, `b=|N|`.

Then the maximal dissociated sets are exactly

`S_t = V\{t}`, `t in P union N`.

For `t in P`,

`nu_{S_t}(t)=a-1`,

and for `t in N`,

`nu_{S_t}(t)=b-1`.

Consequently

`rho_V(k) = 1` if `k < min(a-1,b-1)`,

and

`rho_V(k) = 0` otherwise.

#### Proof

If there is no signed kernel vector, the definition of dissociation gives the first statement.

In the signed-relation case, deleting any one element of the support destroys the only real relation, so the remaining set is linearly independent on that support (with any zero-coefficient coloops still included) and hence dissociated. Omitting two support elements is not maximal because one can be reinserted. Omitting a zero-coefficient column is never maximal because that column is independent of the relation support. Thus the displayed `S_t` are exactly the maximal dissociated sets.

The unique relation solves for `t`. If `t in P`,

`t = sum N - sum(P\{t})`,

so the negative side has size `a-1`; uniqueness of the real relation makes this the unique signed representation. The `t in N` case is symmetric. Optimizing over the omitted side gives the formula. QED.

### Corollary 6.2

Every real-nullity-one Boolean configuration satisfies the candidate constant-1 profile inequality

`(k+1) rho_V(k) <= r`.

Indeed, if `rho=1`, then `k+1 <= min(a-1,b-1) <= (a+b-2)/2 <= (r-1)/2`.

Thus a finite obstruction to the constant-1 route must have real relation nullity at least two.

## 7. Linear direct-sum additivity (generalization of the prior row-block statement)

The previous checkpoint proved additivity when configurations occupy disjoint row blocks. The row-block realization is unnecessary.

Suppose `V=V_1 union V_2` and

`span(V_1) cap span(V_2) = {0}`.

Then a signed relation splits uniquely into relations in the two summands. Therefore:

- `S subset V` is dissociated iff `S cap V_i` is dissociated in each `V_i`;
- maximality is componentwise;
- signed representations and negative widths of `v in V_i` use only the `i`th component;
- hence

`rho_V(k)=rho_{V_1}(k)+rho_{V_2}(k)`

and ranks add.

The same holds for any finite linear direct sum.

## 8. Minimal-counterexample rigidity for the candidate constant-1 profile inequality

The inequality

`(k+1) rho_V(k) <= rank(V)`                                            (P_k)

is still conjectural. It is only a sufficient route toward log-rank, not known to be necessary. The following theorem sharply restricts any finite counterexample to `(P_k)`.

### Theorem 8.1

Fix `k`. Let `V` be a counterexample to `(P_k)` chosen first with minimum rank and then, at that rank, with minimum number of columns. Put

`r=rank(V)`, `t=floor(r/(k+1))`.

Then:

1. `V` has no real coloop: for every `v in V`, `v in span(V\{v})`.
2. `V` is indecomposable as a nontrivial linear direct sum.
3. `|V|-r >= 2`; in particular it is not a real-nullity-one configuration.
4. For every `v in V`, let `U=V\{v}`. Every maximal dissociated `S subset U` satisfying `rank H_k^U(S) <= t` has all of the following properties when viewed inside `V`:
   - `S union {v}` is **not** dissociated;
   - hence `S` is already maximal in `V`;
   - `nu_S(v)>k`;
   - `rank H_k^U(S)=t`;
   - `v notin span(H_k^U(S))`.

Thus every deletion-good certificate must reintroduce the deleted column as a genuinely new heavy rank direction.

#### Proof

**No coloop.** If `v` is outside `span(U)`, every maximal dissociated set of `V` contains `v`: adjoining it to a dissociated subset of `U` can never create a linear relation. Removing `v` gives a bijection between maximal dissociated sets of `V` and of `U`. Representations of columns of `U` cannot use `v`, again by linear independence from `span(U)`, so `rho_V(k)=rho_U(k)`. Since `rank(U)=r-1`, minimality in rank and `(P_k)` for `U` give

`rho_V(k)=rho_U(k) <= floor((r-1)/(k+1)) <= t`,

contradiction.

**Direct-sum indecomposability.** If `V=V_1 direct_sum V_2`, both lower-rank pieces satisfy `(P_k)` by minimality. Additivity gives

`rho_V(k) <= floor(r_1/(k+1))+floor(r_2/(k+1)) <= floor(r/(k+1))=t`,

contradiction.

**Nullity.** By item 1 every deletion preserves rank `r`. If `|V|-r=1`, Theorem 6.1/Corollary 6.2 applies, contradiction. Hence nullity at least two.

**Deletion criticality.** Fix `v`. By item 1, `U` has rank `r`; by column-minimality it satisfies `(P_k)`, so choose maximal dissociated `S subset U` with `rank H_k^U(S)<=t`.

If `S union {v}` were dissociated, it would be maximal in `V`: every other omitted `u in U\S` already creates a signed relation with `S`, and that relation persists after adding `v`. Adding `v` can only create additional representations for old columns, so their negative widths cannot increase; `v` itself has width zero. Hence `V` would have a maximal dissociated set with heavy rank at most `t`, contradiction.

Therefore `S union {v}` is not dissociated. Since `S` was maximal in `U`, it is now maximal in `V` as well. Old widths are unchanged because the generating set is unchanged. For `V` to violate `(P_k)`, `v` must itself be heavy, the old heavy rank must already be exactly `t`, and `v` must lie outside its span; otherwise adjoining one vector could not raise the heavy rank above `t`. QED.

### Search consequence

Any finite disproof of the constant-1 route must simultaneously have:

- at least two independent real relations;
- no coloops;
- no nontrivial linear direct-sum decomposition;
- relation geometry more complicated than the equal-sum-block class;
- the deletion-critical heavy-direction property above for **every** column.

This is a much smaller target than arbitrary rank-`r` Boolean configurations.

## 10. What failed and what changed

### Failed route closed this run

A naive one-exchange descent lemma is false. Even in the already verified 14-column rank-5 configuration, `S_loc` has no heavy-rank-improving single-swap neighbor. Therefore a proof of the optimized profile bound cannot simply choose any nonoptimal maximal dissociated set and demand a strictly improving one-element exchange.

### Surviving route

The exact exchange criterion shows what a successful local move must exploit: essential supports of signed representations. The plateau path shows that one may have to preserve the objective for several exchanges before a large drop becomes available. This points to either:

1. a **plateau-connectivity theorem** for an appropriate lexicographically refined potential; or
2. a genuine **multi-exchange lemma** that replaces several basis columns simultaneously.

The minimal-counterexample theorem in Section 8 supplies strong hypotheses under which such a lemma only needs to be proved: no coloops, direct-sum indecomposable, nullity at least two, and every deletion-good certificate reintroducing a new independent heavy direction.
