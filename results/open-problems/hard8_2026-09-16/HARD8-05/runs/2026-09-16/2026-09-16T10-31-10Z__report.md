# HARD8-05 — Erdős–Straus conjecture: saturation-subgroup checkpoint

**Run status:** PARTIAL / FULL CONJECTURE OPEN  
**Exact target:** prove or refute that for every integer `n >= 2` there exist positive integers `x,y,z` with

\[
\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z.
\]

This report does **not** claim a proof of the full conjecture. The main new result is a general structural theorem for failed divisor portals: after enough multiplicity accumulates in a residue class, that class can be treated as fully available in the unit group, and every remaining failure must concentrate near a maximal subgroup avoiding `-1`. The quotient of such a maximal subgroup is necessarily cyclic of 2-power order. This replaces ad hoc exponent-four casework by a general mechanism valid for every fixed portal modulus.

## 3. Universal prime reduction retained

The case `n=2` is elementary:
\[
\frac42=1+\frac12+\frac12.
\]

If `d` has a solution and `n=md`, multiplying all three denominators by `m` gives a solution for `n`. Therefore a counterexample has a prime divisor that is itself a counterexample. Standard elementary congruence identities reduce the genuinely hard prime case to

\[
\boxed{n\equiv1\pmod{24}.}
\]

The current work is a necessary-condition analysis for such a prime counterexample.

## 4. Saturated residue classes

Fix a modulus `m` and put

\[
G=(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times,\qquad \tau=-1\in G.
\]

Let

\[
N=\prod_p p^{e_p}.
\]

A divisor congruent to `-1 mod m` must be coprime to `m`, so primes dividing `m` can be ignored. For every residue `r in G`, define the total available multiplicity

\[
E_r(N)=\sum_{\substack{p\equiv r\pmod m\\(p,m)=1}}e_p.
\]

Let `o(r)` be the order of `r` in `G`. Call `r` **saturated** when

\[
E_r(N)\ge o(r)-1.
\]

Let

\[
H_{\rm sat}(N;m)=
\left\langle
r\in G:\ E_r(N)\ge o(r)-1
\right\rangle .
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — saturation obstruction

If `N` has no divisor `s` with

\[
s\equiv-1\pmod m,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\tau\notin H_{\rm sat}(N;m).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Assume instead that `tau` lies in the subgroup generated by the saturated residues. Then

\[
\tau=\prod_{r\in S}r^{c_r}
\]

for integers `c_r`, where every `r in S` is saturated. Reduce `c_r` modulo `o(r)` to obtain

\[
0\le c_r\le o(r)-1.
\]

Saturation gives `E_r(N) >= o(r)-1 >= c_r`. Hence among the prime factors of `N` lying in residue class `r`, counted with multiplicity, we can choose exactly `c_r` of them. Taking the product over all `r` gives an actual divisor of `N` whose residue is `tau=-1`, contradiction. ∎

This theorem is stronger than a uniform Davenport-constant threshold: each residue class saturates at its own exact order minus one.

## 5. Maximal escape subgroup and cyclic 2-power quotient

### Theorem 5.1 — bounded-exception structure of every failed portal

Assume `N` has no divisor `-1 mod m`. Then there exists a subgroup

\[
K\le G
\]

such that

1. `H_sat <= K`;
2. `-1 notin K`;
3. `K` is maximal among subgroups avoiding `-1`;
4. the quotient is cyclic of 2-power order:
   \[
   \boxed{G/K\cong C_{2^j}}
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
   for some `j>=1`;
5. the image of `-1` is the unique element of order two in `G/K`;
6. for every residue `r notin K`,
   \[
   \boxed{E_r(N)\le o(r)-2.}
   \tag{5.2}
   \]

Consequently the number of prime factors outside `K`, counted with multiplicity, is bounded by the modulus-dependent constant

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_{G\setminus K}(N)
\le
B_m(K):=
\sum_{r\in G\setminus K}(o(r)-2).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

By Theorem 4.1, `H_sat` avoids `tau`. Since `G` is finite, extend it to a subgroup `K` maximal under the condition `tau notin K`.

Let `A=G/K` and let `t=tau K`. If `A` had a nontrivial subgroup `B` not containing `t`, the preimage of `B` would strictly contain `K` and still avoid `tau`, contradicting maximality. Thus every nontrivial subgroup of `A` contains `t`.

If an odd prime divided `|A|`, Cauchy's theorem would give a subgroup of odd prime order, which cannot contain the order-two element `t`. Hence `A` is a 2-group.

If the finite abelian 2-group `A` were noncyclic, its 2-torsion would contain at least three nonidentity elements. Choosing an order-two element different from `t` would give a nontrivial subgroup not containing `t`, again impossible. Therefore `A` is cyclic of order `2^j`, and `t` is its unique order-two element.

Finally, if `r notin K`, then `r notin H_sat`; therefore it is not saturated and
\[
E_r(N)<o(r)-1,
\]
which is exactly (5.2). Summing gives (5.3). ∎

### Interpretation

A failed portal cannot have arbitrary prime-factor residue structure. Apart from a bounded multiset of exceptional factors, its unit prime factors are trapped in a subgroup `K` whose quotient sees `-1` as the unique order-two element.

For index-two `K`, this is exactly a real-character obstruction of the type appearing in modern Type-I/Type-II work. The theorem also explains the genuinely exponent-four branches from the previous checkpoint: maximal avoiding subgroups can have index four, giving a cyclic quartic quotient instead of a quadratic character.

This is a structural theorem for the portal method. It does not prove the Erdős–Straus conjecture because the bounded exceptional factors can still obstruct the target divisor.

## 6. Explicit modulus-40 classification

The next residual certificates repeatedly use the reciprocal pair

Here

\[
\begin{aligned}

## 7. A new exact hard-class elimination from `(2,5)`

\[

## 8. Reciprocal `(2,5)/(5,2)` coupling

Let

\[
N_1=2n+5,\qquad N_2=5n+2.
\]

Then

\[
5N_1-2N_2=21.
\]

For every hard prime `n == 1 mod 24`,

\[
3\nmid N_1,N_2,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(N_1,N_2)=
\begin{cases}
7,&n\equiv1\pmod7,\\
1,&n\not\equiv1\pmod7.
\end{cases}}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Let this gcd be `g` and put

\[
A=N_1/g,\qquad B=N_2/g.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\gcd(A,B)=1,\qquad
5A-2B=\frac{21}{g}\in\{21,3\}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This is the exact affine coupling to be used in the next run.

For the two remaining classes in (7.2), the total residues are:

- `n == 1 mod120`: `N_1 == 7 mod40`, `N_2 == 7 mod40`;
- `n == 49 mod120`: `N_1 == 23 mod40`, `N_2 == 7 mod40`.

Among the six groups in (6.1), the residues `7` and `23` lie in exactly `K_3` and `K_5`. Therefore, in the **zero-exception** branch of Theorem 5.1, simultaneous failure of the reciprocal portals forces each linear form to have all its unit prime factors in one of only

\[
\boxed{
\ker\left(\frac{-5}{\cdot}\right)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\ker\left(\frac{-10}{\cdot}\right).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The two forms can choose these kernels independently. General failures may additionally contain the bounded exceptional factors allowed by Theorem 5.1.

## 14. Failed / insufficient routes

1. **Finite depth is not a proof.** Even exact coverage through `4.3e6` does not imply a universal bound on `J(n)`.
2. **Saturation is one-way.** `H_sat` avoiding `-1` is necessary for failure, not sufficient; unsaturated exceptional factors can still either create or fail to create the target divisor.
3. **Quadratic kernels alone do not contradict the affine relation.** There are many finite examples in which both reciprocal linear forms lie entirely in the `(-5)` or `(-10)` splitting kernels.
4. **A claimed 2026 covering proof cannot be imported.** The missing covering-system step is exactly the central universal obligation.
