# HARD8-05 — Erdős–Straus conjecture: power-amplified saturation and fixed-shift square portals

**Run status:** PARTIAL / FULL CONJECTURE OPEN  
**Exact target:** for every integer `n >= 2`, find positive integers `x,y,z` such that

\[
\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z.
\]

This checkpoint does **not** claim a proof of the full conjecture. It develops a second exact divisor mechanism, based on divisors of a square, and proves a general `h`-fold saturation theorem that quantitatively strengthens the preceding single-power subgroup obstruction. The main structural consequence is that failure of a square-divisor portal has at most roughly half as many exceptional prime factors as failure of the corresponding ordinary divisor portal. For the mod-40 groups used in the previous checkpoint the worst exact exceptional budget drops from `8` to `4`; for a cube portal it drops to `0`.

## 3. Exact fixed-shift square-divisor portal

The following elementary equivalence is the starting point for this run. It is closely aligned with the divisor-square formulations used in recent Type-I/Type-II work, but is derived here directly so that all integrality hypotheses are explicit.

### Theorem 3.1 — fixed-shift square portal

Let `n,c` be positive integers satisfying

\[
n+c\equiv0\pmod4,
\qquad
\gcd(n,c)=1,
\]

and put

\[
X=\frac{n+c}{4}.
\]

Then `gcd(X,c)=1`. If there is a positive divisor `d | X^2` such that

\[
\boxed{d\equiv -X\pmod c,}
\tag{3.1}
\]

then, defining

\[
Y=\frac{X+d}{c},
\qquad
Z=\frac{X+X^2/d}{c},
\tag{3.2}
\]

we have positive integers `Y,Z` and the exact Erdős–Straus decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\frac4n=\frac1X+\frac1{nY}+\frac1{nZ}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Conversely, every decomposition of the form (3.3) with this fixed first denominator `X=(n+c)/4` produces a positive divisor `d | X^2` satisfying (3.1), by setting

\[
d=cY-X.
\]

#### Proof

Since `4X=n+c`,

\[
\frac4n-\frac1X
=\frac{4X-n}{nX}
=\frac{c}{nX}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus (3.3) is equivalent to

\[
\frac1Y+\frac1Z=\frac cX.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Rearranging (3.5) gives

\[
cYZ=X(Y+Z),
\]

hence

\[
(cY-X)(cZ-X)=X^2.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Now assume `d | X^2` and (3.1). Because `gcd(X,c)=1`, `d` is also a unit modulo `c`, and

\[
\frac{X^2}{d}
\equiv
X^2(-X)^{-1}
\equiv -X
\pmod c.
\]

Therefore both quantities in (3.2) are positive integers. Equation (3.6) holds with

\[
cY-X=d,
\qquad
cZ-X=X^2/d,
\]

so (3.5) and then (3.3) follow.

Conversely, from a positive solution of (3.5), equation (3.6) shows that `d=cY-X` is positive (indeed `cY-X=XY/Z>0`), divides `X^2`, and is congruent to `-X mod c`. ∎

The key point for the present project is that the factor supply is `X^2`, not merely `X`. Every prime factor of `X` is therefore available twice, which amplifies the saturation mechanism.

## 4. New general theorem: `h`-fold power saturation

The preceding checkpoint treated divisors of an integer `N` and target residue `-1`. The next theorem works for divisors of `N^h` and for an **arbitrary target residue**.

Let `G` be a finite abelian group written multiplicatively, let `tau in G`, and let `N` be an integer all of whose relevant prime residues lie in `G` (for the application, `G=(Z/mZ)^×` and `gcd(N,m)=1`). For `r in G`, write `E_r(N)` for the total multiplicity of prime factors of `N` having residue `r`. Let `o(r)` be the order of `r`.

Define the `h`-saturated subgroup

\[
H_h(N)=
\left\langle
r:\;hE_r(N)\ge o(r)-1
\right\rangle.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — power-saturation obstruction

Assume `N^h` has **no divisor** whose residue is `tau`. Then

\[
\boxed{\tau\notin H_h(N).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently `H_h(N)` is contained in a subgroup `K <= G` maximal by inclusion subject to `tau notin K`, and for every `r notin K`,

\[
\boxed{
E_r(N)
\le
\left\lfloor\frac{o(r)-2}{h}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

For each saturated residue `r`, the integer `N^h` contains at least `o(r)-1` prime-factor copies having residue `r`. Therefore every power

\[
1,r,r^2,\ldots,r^{o(r)-1}
\]
can be realized by a divisor contribution using only those copies.

Any element of the subgroup generated by the saturated residues can be written as a product of such reduced powers, one for each generator. Hence **every element of `H_h(N)` occurs as the residue of some divisor of `N^h`**. If `tau` belonged to `H_h(N)`, `N^h` would have a divisor of residue `tau`, contradiction. This proves (4.2).

Choose a maximal subgroup `K` avoiding `tau` and containing `H_h(N)`; it exists because `G` is finite. If `r notin K`, then `r` is not `h`-saturated, so

\[
hE_r(N)<o(r)-1.
\]

Since both sides are integral,

\[
hE_r(N)\le o(r)-2,
\]

which is exactly (4.3). ∎

### Corollary 4.2 — zero-exception regime

If `lambda(G)` denotes the exponent of `G` and

\[
h\ge\lambda(G)-1,
\]

then every prime residue actually occurring in `N` is saturated. Therefore failure of the target divisor implies that **all prime residues of `N` lie in one subgroup avoiding `tau`**.

This is particularly sharp for unit groups of exponent four: a cube-divisor portal has no exceptional prime factors at all.

## 5. Exact power-budget refinement

As in the preceding checkpoint, the coordinate caps (4.3) are not independent: the exceptional residues themselves, when replicated `h` times, cannot contain a subproduct equal to `tau`.

For a maximal target-avoiding subgroup `K`, define

\[
c_r^{(h)}=
\left\lfloor\frac{o(r)-2}{h}\right\rfloor
\qquad(r\notin K).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `F_h(G,tau,K)` be the integer vectors `(e_r)_{r notin K}` with

\[
0\le e_r\le c_r^{(h)}
\]

such that the multiset containing **`h e_r` copies** of each residue `r` has no submultiset product equal to `tau`. Define

\[
\boxed{
B_h^*(G,\tau,K)
=
\max_{e\in F_h(G,\tau,K)}
\sum_{r\notin K}e_r.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then every failed `N^h` target portal obeys

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r\notin K}E_r(N)
\le B_h^*(G,\tau,K).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The proof is identical in logic to the single-power exact budget theorem: the actual multiplicity vector satisfies (5.1), and any forbidden subproduct of the replicated exceptional sequence would be an actual divisor of `N^h` with target residue.

## 7. Exact hard-prime consequence at shift `c=3`

Now restrict to the only prime residue class requiring consideration in the classical prime reduction,

\[
n\equiv1\pmod{24}.
\]

Take `c=3`. Then

\[
X_3=\frac{n+3}{4}=6m+1\equiv1\pmod3.
\]

The square portal requires a divisor

\[
d\mid X_3^2,
\qquad
d\equiv-1\equiv2\pmod3.
\]

The unit group modulo 3 has exponent two, so the condition has an exact elementary characterization.

### Corollary 7.1

For every prime `n ≡ 1 mod 24`, the fixed-shift `c=3` portal succeeds **if and only if** `X_3=(n+3)/4` has a prime factor congruent to `2 mod 3`.

Equivalently, failure of this portal is exactly

\[
\boxed{
p\equiv1\pmod3
\quad\text{for every prime }p\mid X_3.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

#### Proof

If `p | X_3` and `p ≡2 mod3`, choose `d=p`. Conversely, if every prime factor is `1 mod3`, every divisor of `X_3^2` is `1 mod3`, so no target divisor exists. ∎

Whenever `p | X_3`, `p ≡2 mod3`, one obtains the explicit certificate

\[
Y=\frac{X_3+p}{3},
\qquad
Z=\frac{X_3+X_3^2/p}{3},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\frac4n=
\frac1{X_3}+
\frac1{nY}+
\frac1{nZ}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus any hypothetical counterexample must pass a very rigid first square-shift test: `(n+3)/4` is composed entirely of primes split in the nontrivial unit class `1 mod 3`.

This condition is not claimed as a literature-first theorem; it is recorded because it is an exact base case of the project’s new power-saturation hierarchy and because it meshes directly with the fixed-shift sieve perspective in Dahan.

## 8. Shift `c=7`: at most two exceptional factors on the Mordell hard fibers

Take

\[
X_7=\frac{n+7}{4}.
\]

For the six classical Mordell residue classes modulo 840,

\[
1,121,169,289,361,529,
\]

the residues of `n mod 7` are only

\[
1,2,4.
\]

Since `4^{-1}=2 mod7`,

\[
X_7\equiv2n\pmod7,
\]

so the target

\[
\tau=-X_7
\]

is respectively `5,3,6 mod7`.

The group `(Z/7Z)^×` is cyclic of order six. Exact application of Theorem 4.1 and the target-free power budget gives:

\[
\boxed{
\text{if the shift-7 square portal fails on a Mordell hard class,}
\text{ then all but at most two prime factors of }X_7
\text{ lie in one maximal subgroup avoiding }-X_7.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

More precisely:

* for `n ≡1 mod7` (`X_7 ≡2`, target `5`), the maximal target-avoiding subgroup is either `{1,6}` or `{1,2,4}`; exact total-residue compatibility forces exactly two exceptions in the `{1,6}` branch and `0` or `2` in the quadratic-residue branch;
* for `n ≡2 mod7` (`X_7 ≡4`, target `3`), the same statement holds;
* for `n ≡4 mod7` (`X_7 ≡1`, target `6=-1`), the only maximal branch is `{1,2,4}`, with `0` or `2` exceptional factors.

This is the square-shift analogue of the preceding mod-40 finite-exception theorem, but with only two exceptional factors.

Dahan’s August 2026 paper proves that a fixed shift (notably the shift `7` analysis) cannot by itself leave only finitely many hard primes, so (8.1) should **not** be interpreted as a route to finish the conjecture using `c=7` alone. Its value is that simultaneous failure of several shifts can now be formulated as simultaneous bounded-exception subgroup factorizations of several nearby linear forms.

## 9. Strategic synthesis with the previous portal-transfer program

The previous checkpoint showed that ordinary divisor portals such as

\[
2n+5,\qquad5n+2,\qquad4n+1
\]
can all exhibit genuine subgroup trapping; the example `n=1201` proved that the reciprocal mod-40 pair is not universal.

The present theorem suggests a stronger transfer architecture:

1. ordinary divisor portals give bounded-exception factorizations of selected linear forms, with the mod-40 budget at most `8`;
2. square-shift portals give **smaller** bounded-exception factorizations of nearby forms `(n+c)/4`; the base shifts `3` and `7` have respectively `0` and at most `2` exceptional factors;
3. because the linear forms are affinely related, a future proof can try to show that their simultaneous subgroup traps are incompatible, rather than demanding that any one fixed shift succeed universally.

This avoids the dead end identified by Dahan: a fixed shift can have infinitely many failures, but simultaneous failure across a growing, algebraically coupled family may still be impossible.

The immediate next mathematical target is therefore a **two-shift transfer lemma**. The cleanest first case is to combine

\[
X_3=\frac{n+3}{4}
\]
with all prime factors `1 mod3` and

\[
X_7=\frac{n+7}{4}=X_3+1
\]
with at most two exceptional prime factors relative to one of the mod-7 escape subgroups. The consecutive relation

\[
\boxed{X_7-X_3=1}
\tag{9.1}
\]

is substantially tighter than the earlier affine relation between `2n+5` and `5n+2`. Any theorem ruling out the simultaneous subgroup patterns for consecutive integers would immediately force one of the two square portals to succeed.

At present no such universal consecutive-factor theorem has been proved; (9.1) is the precise remaining gap, not an established implication.
