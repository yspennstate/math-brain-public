# HARD8-05 — Erdős–Straus conjecture research checkpoint

**Run status:** PARTIAL / FULL CONJECTURE OPEN  
**Target:** For every integer `n >= 2`, prove or refute the existence of positive integers `x,y,z` with

\[
\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z.
\]

This checkpoint does **not** claim a proof of the Erdős–Straus conjecture. It records an exact divisor normal form, a finite completeness bound for a fixed modulator, and a certified analysis of the nine small `n ≡ 1 (mod 24)` prime exceptions to the `a=1` slice appearing in recent work.

## 1. Current source status

The full conjecture remains open in the current primary literature checked for this run.

* X. Xu, *Congruence Classes of Supporting the Erdős–Straus Conjecture I: Tame Solutions*, arXiv:2605.23601v1 (22 May 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23601. Xu reports nine “wild” primes among the 7,185 primes `n=24m+1` with `m<=30000` that escape the tame / `a=1` mechanism used there.
* B. Dahan, *Sieve dimension and search depth for the Erdős–Straus conjecture, n ≡ 1 mod 24*, arXiv:2608.24035 (submitted 25 Aug 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24035. Dahan still states the full conjecture as open and develops a two-parameter Type-II divisor criterion. In particular, the paper notes that restoring the second parameter captures the finite “hyperbolic” exceptional list investigated there.
* J. Bello-Hernández, M. Benito, and J. L. Fernández, arXiv:2606.10922 (9 June 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10922, gives another contemporary divisor-parametrization perspective.

A February 2026 preprint advertised as a full proof was not treated as established resolution because later August 2026 primary literature continues to state the conjecture as open.

## 2. Exact Type-II factor portal

The useful algebra can be stated directly in terms of the actual unit-fraction denominators.

### Lemma 2.1 — factor portal

Let `n,a,u` be positive integers with `gcd(a,u)=1`. Suppose a positive divisor `s` of `a n + u` satisfies

\[
s\equiv -1\pmod{4au}.
\]

Define

\[
q=\frac{an+u}{s},\qquad t=\frac{s+1}{4au}.
\]

Then `q,t` are positive integers, `a | (u+q)`, and

\[
\boxed{\frac4n
 =\frac1{uqt}+\frac1{anqt}+\frac1{anut}.}
\]

#### Proof

From `qs=an+u` and `s ≡ -1 (mod a)`, reduction modulo `a` gives

\[
-q\equiv u\pmod a,
\]

hence `a | (u+q)`.  Since `s=4aut-1`,

\[
an+u=q(4aut-1),
\]

so

\[
an=4auqt-u-q.
\]

Therefore

\[
an+u+q=4auqt.
\]

Using the common denominator `anuqt`,

\[
\frac1{uqt}+\frac1{anqt}+\frac1{anut}
=\frac{an+u+q}{anuqt}
=\frac{4auqt}{anuqt}
=\frac4n.
\]

This proves the identity. ∎

This is the two-parameter Type-II condition used by Dahan, rewritten as a concrete denominator certificate. The point of keeping this form is that every certificate is trivial to verify with exact integer arithmetic.

## 3. A finite completeness bound at fixed `a`

The portal has a symmetry between `u` and `q`: the identity

\[
an=4auqt-u-q
\]

is symmetric in those two variables. Thus, whenever a witness exists, we may rename the pair so that `u <= q`.

### Lemma 3.1 — finite `u` search for a fixed modulator

For fixed positive integers `n,a`, if a Type-II witness exists for modulator `a`, then one exists with

\[
4a u^2-2u\le an.
\]

Equivalently it is enough to test

\[
1\le u\le U_a(n),\qquad
U_a(n):=\max\{r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:4ar^2-2r\le an\}.
\]

#### Proof

Choose the symmetric representative with `u<=q`. Since `t>=1`,

\[
an=4auqt-u-q
   \ge 4au^2-u-u
   =4au^2-2u.
\]

That gives the bound. ∎

This converts “no witness for a given `a`” from an unbounded search claim into a finite exact certificate. In particular it was used below to exhaust the entire `a=2` slice for `n=102001`.

## 4. A very small factor portal: `(a,u)=(2,1)`

Set `a=2,u=1`. The condition becomes

\[
s\mid 2n+1,\qquad s\equiv7\pmod8.
\]

Write `s=8t-1` and `q=(2n+1)/s`. Then Lemma 2.1 gives the especially simple decomposition

\[
\boxed{\frac4n
=\frac1{qt}+\frac1{2nqt}+\frac1{2nt}.}
\]

For the difficult prime residue class `n ≡ 1 (mod 24)`, in particular `n≡1 (mod4)`, so

\[
2n+1\equiv3\pmod8.
\]

There is an elementary exact characterization of when this portal fires.

### Proposition 4.1 — factorization characterization of Portal A

If `n≡1 (mod4)` and `M=2n+1`, then `M` has a divisor congruent to `7 (mod8)` **iff** `M` has at least one prime divisor congruent to `5` or `7 (mod8)`.

#### Proof

If a prime factor is `7 mod8`, that prime itself is a suitable divisor.

Suppose instead a prime factor `p` is `5 mod8` and there is no `7 mod8` prime factor. Because the total product is `M≡3 mod8`, the remaining prime factors cannot all have residues in `{1,5}`: products of `1` and `5` lie in `{1,5}`. Hence some prime factor `r≡3 mod8` occurs. Then `pr≡5·3≡7 mod8`, giving the required divisor.

Conversely, if every prime factor lies in `{1,3} mod8`, every divisor has residue in the multiplicative subgroup `{1,3}` modulo 8, so no divisor is `7 mod8`. ∎

Thus this portal fails for `n≡1 (mod4)` precisely when every prime factor of `2n+1` lies in the residue classes `1` or `3 mod8`. This converts one portion of the unit-fraction problem into a very explicit multiplicative sieve condition.

## 5. Exact reconstruction of the nine small wild primes

A pure-integer checker was written for this run and archived at

`hard8_2026-09-16/HARD8-05/artifacts/factor_portal_wild_prime_checker.py`.

It performs these checks without floating point:

1. sieve all primes through `720001`;
2. select primes `n≡1 (mod24)`;
3. for every such `n`, exhaust the complete `a=1` search using Lemma 3.1;
4. compare the exceptions to Xu's nine-prime list;
5. for each exception, exhaust complete fixed-`a` searches for `a=1,2,3` until the minimum such `a` is found;
6. verify every displayed solution by the cross-multiplied identity
   `4xyz = n(xy+xz+yz)`.

The exact count is 7,185 primes in this range, and the `a=1` exceptions are exactly

\[
409,577,5569,9601,23929,83449,102001,329617,712321.

This independently reconstructs the nine small exceptions reported in the contemporary literature.

## 6. Three tiny portals cover all nine exceptions

Seven of the nine are captured by the single portal `(a,u)=(2,1)`:

\[
409,577,5569,9601,23929,329617,712321.
\]

Their `2n+1` factorizations are

* `409`: `819 = 3^2·7·13`;
* `577`: `1155 = 3·5·7·11`;
* `5569`: `11139 = 3·47·79`;
* `9601`: `19203 = 3·37·173`, with `s=111=3·37`;
* `23929`: `47859 = 3·7·43·53`;
* `329617`: `659235 = 3·5·71·619`;
* `712321`: `1424643 = 3·11·23·1877`.

The two failures of Portal A have the exact factorizations

\[
2(83449)+1=166899=3\cdot55633,
\]

and

\[
2(102001)+1=204003=3^2\cdot19\cdot1193.
\]

All displayed prime residues are `1` or `3 mod8`, exactly as Proposition 4.1 predicts.

They are repaired by two nearby portals:

* `n=83449`: `(a,u)=(2,3)`, because
  `2n+3=166901=7·113·211`, and choose `s=791=7·113 ≡ -1 (mod24)`;
* `n=102001`: `(a,u)=(3,5)`, because
  `3n+5=306008=2^3·29·1319`, and choose `s=1319 ≡ -1 (mod60)`.

So the three portals

\[
(2,1),\quad(2,3),\quad(3,5)
\]

cover all nine of the small Xu exceptions. This is a useful compact structural description of the sample, but it is **not** evidence that these three portals cover all integers or all future exceptional primes.

## 7. What this does and does not prove

### Proved / certified in this run

* Lemma 2.1 gives an exact two-parameter divisor-to-unit-fraction certificate.
* Lemma 3.1 gives a complete finite search bound for fixed `a`.
* Portal A `(2,1)` has the exact prime-factor residue characterization in Proposition 4.1.
* The `a=1` exhaustive checker reproduces exactly the nine small `n≡1 mod24` prime exceptions through `720001`.
* Eight of those nine have minimum modulator `a=2` among `a=1,2,3`; `102001` has no `a=2` witness and has an `a=3` witness.
* Three small portals cover all nine examples.

### Not proved

* No universal finite bound on `a` has been shown.
* No fixed finite family of portals has been shown to cover every prime `n≡1 mod24`.
* No density or sieve argument here turns the finite calculation into a proof for all primes.
* Nothing here rules out numbers outside these Type-II parameterizations; therefore failure of a bounded portal search is never a counterexample to Erdős–Straus.
* The full conjecture remains OPEN.

## 8. Failed / obstructed routes

1. **“Maybe `a=2` repairs every `a=1` exception.”** Exact exhaustive search disproves this already at `n=102001`.
2. **“Maybe the single shifted factorization `2n+1` is enough.”** It misses `83449` and `102001` in the nine-prime sample, and Dahan's fixed-depth/fixed-shift analysis gives broader reasons that one fixed shift leaves blind sets.
3. **“Finite success suggests a finite covering.”** The sample is too small for this inference. A universal finite portal cover would itself require a rigorous multiplicative congruence/sieve theorem.

## 9. Next attack

The next useful step is to turn the three successful portals into explicit **failure-set descriptions** and study their intersection.

* Portal A `(2,1)` is now completely characterized: every prime divisor of `2n+1` must lie in `{1,3} mod8` for failure.
* For Portal B `(2,3)`, failure means `2n+3` has no divisor `-1 mod24`. Classify this condition in terms of the subgroup/semigroup generated by prime-factor residues modulo 24, while handling nonunits and powers exactly.
* For Portal C `(3,5)`, analogously classify absence of a divisor `-1 mod60` from `3n+5`.
* Intersect these three multiplicative restrictions under `n≡1 mod24` and ask whether the intersection has a provably stronger sieve dimension or forces a new algebraic portal.
* Extend the exact checker from the nine small Xu exceptions to the full finite list of fourteen hyperbolic exceptions below `5·10^9` reported by Dahan, and classify the minimum modulator / smallest `(a,u)` certificate for each. This is still finite evidence, but it may reveal the correct next portal or invariant.

The strategic objective is not merely to accumulate residue classes. It is to find a structural descent: prove that simultaneous failure of a controlled collection of divisor portals forces a factorization pattern that automatically activates a different portal, with the process terminating uniformly. No such universal descent is proved yet.
