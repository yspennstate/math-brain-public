## 3. Saturation theorem retained

Fix a modulus `m`, write

\[
G=(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times,\qquad \tau=-1\in G,
\]

and for an integer `N` let `E_r(N)` be the total multiplicity of prime factors of `N` in the unit residue class `r mod m`. If `o(r)` denotes the order of `r` in `G`, call `r` saturated when

\[
E_r(N)\ge o(r)-1.
\]

Let

\[
H_{\rm sat}=\langle r:E_r(N)\ge o(r)-1\rangle.
\]

The prior checkpoint proved:

* if `N` has no divisor congruent to `-1 mod m`, then `-1 notin H_sat`;
* hence `H_sat` is contained in a maximal subgroup `K <= G` avoiding `-1`;
* `G/K` is cyclic of 2-power order;
* for every `r notin K`,
  \[
  E_r(N)\le o(r)-2.
  \tag{3.1}
  \]

The previous coarse bound simply summed the right side of (3.1). The present checkpoint uses the fact that the exceptional prime factors are **not independent**: their residues themselves cannot contain a subproduct equal to `-1`.

## 4. New exact subset-product exception bound

For a fixed maximal subgroup `K` avoiding `tau`, define the class capacities

\[
c_r=o(r)-2\qquad(r\in G\setminus K).
\]

Let `F_m(K)` be the set of all integer vectors

\[
e=(e_r)_{r\in G\setminus K}
\]

such that

\[
0\le e_r\le c_r
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the multiset containing `e_r` copies of each residue `r` has **no submultiset whose product is `tau`**. Define the exact finite obstruction constant

\[
\boxed{
B_m^*(K)=\max_{e\in F_m(K)}\sum_{r\notin K}e_r.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact exceptional-factor budget

If `N` has no divisor congruent to `-1 mod m`, and `K` is any maximal `-1`-avoiding subgroup containing `H_sat(N;m)`, then

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_{G\setminus K}(N)\le B_m^*(K).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Here the left side counts prime factors with multiplicity whose unit residue lies outside `K`.

### Proof

Let `e_r=E_r(N)` for `r outside K`. By the saturation theorem, (3.1) gives exactly the coordinate bounds (4.1).

If the outside-residue multiset had a submultiset with product `tau`, choosing the corresponding actual prime factors of `N` would produce a divisor of `N` congruent to `-1 mod m`, contradicting the failed-portal hypothesis. Therefore the actual count vector belongs to `F_m(K)`. Its total multiplicity is bounded by the maximum (4.2). ∎

This theorem is strictly stronger than the previous bound

\[
B_m(K)=\sum_{r\notin K}(o(r)-2),
\]

because `B_m(K)` ignores all interactions between exceptional residue classes.

## 5. New closed-form complement-pair refinement

There is a useful symbolic bound between the old sum and the exact finite optimization. Define

\[
\iota(r)=\tau r^{-1}=-r^{-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is an involution on `G`.

### Lemma 5.1 — complement exclusion

For a failed portal:

1. if `r` and `iota(r)` are distinct residue classes and both occur among the prime factors of `N`, then their product gives a divisor congruent to `-1`, impossible;
2. if `r=iota(r)`, equivalently `r^2=-1`, then `E_r(N)<=1`.

### Proof

In the first case choose one prime factor from each class. Their product is congruent to

\[
r\,(-r^{-1})=-1\pmod m.
\]

In the fixed-point case, two prime factors from the same class have product `r^2=-1 mod m`. ∎

Consequently, when both members of a two-cycle `{r,iota(r)}` lie outside `K`, their combined contribution is at most

\[
\max(c_r,c_{\iota(r)}),
\]

rather than `c_r+c_iota(r)`. A fixed point outside `K` contributes at most `min(c_r,1)`. Classes whose complement lies inside `K` retain their individual capacity. Summing these orbit contributions gives a completely explicit bound

\[
\boxed{B_m^*(K)\le \widehat B_m(K)\le B_m(K).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The exact DP below shows that higher-order subset products can improve even further beyond this pairwise bound for some moduli.

## 8. Fixed-quotient portal family and recovery of the classical mod-840 hard set

This subsection is a validation/reformulation of classical structure, **not a novelty claim**.

Take coprime positive `a,u` and a positive integer `q` satisfying

\[
a\mid q+u.
\]

Put

\[
c=\frac{q+u}{a}.
\]

If

\[
\boxed{n\equiv-c\pmod{4uq},}
\tag{8.1}
\]

write `n=4uqT-c`. Then

\[
an+u=q(4auT-1).
\]

Thus

\[
s=\frac{an+u}{q}=4auT-1\equiv-1\pmod{4au},
\]

and (1.1) gives the explicit solution

\[
\boxed{
\frac4n=
\frac1{uqT}+\frac1{anqT}+\frac1{anuT}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Restricting to the 24 unit residue classes `n == 1 mod 24` modulo 840, only six such fixed-quotient portal classes are needed to cover 18 classes:

| `(a,u,q)` | congruence modulus | covered hard residues mod 840 |
|---|---:|---|
| `(1,2,5)` | 40 | `73,193,313,433,673,793` |
| `(2,1,5)` | 20 | `97,337,457,577,697,817` |
| `(1,1,14)` | 56 | `601,769` |
| `(1,2,21)` | 168 | `481,649` |
| `(2,1,21)` | 84 | `241` |
| `(2,7,15)` | 420 | `409` |

The uncovered set is exactly

\[
\boxed{1,121,169,289,361,529\pmod{840},}
\tag{8.3}
\]

which is exactly the classical Mordell six-class residual set and equals the unit square classes modulo 840. This is a useful sanity check that the portal formalism naturally recovers the standard hard congruence fibers before the newer saturation analysis is applied.

## 11. Failed/closed routes and next attack

The previous sum-of-capacities constant should no longer be used for higher-index escape branches: it loses strong subset-product interactions and can overcount by a factor two or more.

The next attack should use the new bounded-exception statement in two stages:

1. **Zero-exception affine classification.** Treat the four combinations of `K_3/K_5` support for `A` and `B` in `5A-2B=3` or `21`, using the class-number-two binary quadratic form structure of discriminants `-20` and `-40` only after verifying exactly which prime-splitting hypothesis is available.
2. **One/two-exception lift.** Because every other modulus-40 branch has odd exception count and the observed finite data overwhelmingly sits at one or three exceptions, derive explicit congruence/character conditions for a single exceptional residue and test whether the affine equation forces a portal in the reciprocal form. The exact universal cap is now 8, so this is a finite combinatorial hierarchy rather than an unbounded residue-support problem.
3. In parallel, apply `B_m^*` rather than the old `B_m` to the modulus-48 and modulus-60 portals `(3,4)` and `(3,5)`, where the worst bound also drops from 16 to 8.

A proof of one of these affine incompatibilities would be a genuine new infinite-class reduction. At present no such universal incompatibility is claimed.
