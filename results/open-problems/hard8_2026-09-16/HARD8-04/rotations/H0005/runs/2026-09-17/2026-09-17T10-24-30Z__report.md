## 3. B18 — say it in another language: factorial moments under UGW size bias

The cleanest language is not “root variance versus offspring variance” but **factorial-moment transport under the Palm/size-biased law**.

Let `D` have pgf

\[
g(s)=E[s^D],\qquad \mu=g'(1)=ED\in(0,\infty).
\]

Let `D^*` be the size-biased law

\[
P(D^*=j)=\frac{jP(D=j)}{\mu},
\]

and let

\[
\nu=D^*-1
\]

be the source non-root forward count. For the falling factorial `(x)_k=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)`, for every integer `k>=1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[(\nu)_k]
&=\sum_{j\ge1}(j-1)_k\frac{jP(D=j)}{\mu}\\
&=\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{j\ge1}(j)_{k+1}P(D=j)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{g^{(k+1)}(1)}{g'(1)}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular,

\[
E\nu=\frac{g''(1)}{g'(1)},
\qquad
E[\nu(\nu-1)]=\frac{g'''(1)}{g'(1)}.
\]

Now take the source one-type saturated branching reduction with transmission probability `r∈(0,1)` and

\[
Z\mid \nu\sim\operatorname{Bin}(\nu,r).
\]

Conditional binomial factorial moments give

\[
E[(Z)_k\mid\nu]=r^k(\nu)_k,
\]

hence exactly

\[
\boxed{
E[(Z)_k]=r^k\frac{g^{(k+1)}(1)}{g'(1)}.
}
\]

For `k=1,2`,

\[
EZ=r\frac{g''(1)}{g'(1)},
\qquad
E[Z(Z-1)]=r^2\frac{g'''(1)}{g'(1)}.
\]

Therefore, for every positive transmission probability `r`, finite root `g''(1)` **does not imply finite effective offspring variance**. In fact, once `EZ<∞`,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z)<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
g'''(1)<\infty
\]

within this one-type thinning family.

This is the exact structural point that the source's Lemma 6.1 and Appendix D miss. The UGW transform consumes one moment.

**B18 outcome: success.** The source endpoint is translated into the right invariant: the Brownian finite-variance class is controlled by the second factorial moment of the *size-biased forward law*, hence the third factorial moment of the root law, not by `g''` alone.

## 5. B30 — one scale at a time: a source-compatible heavy-tail law and the wrong universality class

### 5.1 Strengthen the previous example to remove an avoidable connectedness concern

Define the root degree/offspring law `D` by

\[
P(D=3)=1-\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}k^{-7/2},
\qquad
P(D=k)=k^{-7/2}\quad(k\ge4),
\]

and `P(D=0)=P(D=1)=P(D=2)=0`.

Since

\[
\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}k^{-7/2}<1,
\]

this is a probability law, supported entirely on degrees at least three. In particular the previous report's mass-at-zero artefact is gone.

Its mean is finite and strictly greater than three:

\[
\mu=g'(1)
=3P(D=3)+\sum_{k\ge4}k^{-5/2}
\approx 3.04967400295.
\]

Its second factorial moment is finite:

\[
\begin{aligned}
g''(1)
&=6P(D=3)+\sum_{k\ge4}k(k-1)k^{-7/2}\\
&=6P(D=3)+\sum_{k\ge4}(k-1)k^{-5/2}\\
&\approx 6.86403827813<\infty,
\end{aligned}
\]

because the tail summand is asymptotic to `k^{-3/2}`.

But

\[
\begin{aligned}
g'''(1)
&=6P(D=3)+\sum_{k\ge4}k(k-1)(k-2)k^{-7/2}\\
&=\infty,
\end{aligned}
\]

because the tail summand is asymptotic to `k^{-1/2}`.

Thus the law satisfies exactly the requested replacement moment condition `g''(1)<∞`, with finite mean and finite root variance, while failing the moment actually needed after size bias.

### 5.2 The source non-root law has a `3/2`-stable tail

For `j>=3`,

\[
P(\nu=j)
=P(D^*=j+1)
=\frac{(j+1)P(D=j+1)}{\mu}
=\frac{1}{\mu}(j+1)^{-5/2}.
\]

Hence, by the integral test / Karamata summation,

\[
\boxed{
P(\nu>x)\sim \frac{2}{3\mu}x^{-3/2}.
}
\]

The mean is

\[
m_*=E\nu=\frac{g''(1)}{g'(1)}
\approx 2.25074492273.
\]

### 5.3 Tune exactly to source criticality

Take the one-type reduction and choose

\[
r=\frac{1}{m_*}
=\frac{g'(1)}{g''(1)}
\approx 0.444297347914.
\]

This lies strictly between zero and one. The source's OU two-boundary hitting probability ranges continuously in `(0,1)` as the boundary geometry/forcing parameters are varied, so this scalar transmission probability is admissible at the branching-reduction level.

Let

\[
Z\mid\nu\sim\operatorname{Bin}(\nu,r).
\]

Then

\[
EZ=rE\nu=1.
\]

Thus in the one-type model `M=(1)` and `rho(M)=1` exactly. Irreducibility is automatic. Aperiodicity is also automatic because with `0<r<1` the binomial thinning assigns positive probability to both `Z=0` and `Z=1` (already from any `nu>=2`).

### 5.4 Bernoulli thinning preserves the heavy-tail index

Fix `epsilon∈(0,1)`. Conditional Chernoff bounds imply, for large `x`,

\[
P\!\left(\nu>\frac{(1+\epsilon)x}{r}\right)-e^{-c_\epsilon x}
\le P(Z>x)
\le
P\!\left(\nu>\frac{(1-\epsilon)x}{r}\right)+e^{-c_\epsilon x}.
\]

Since `P(ν>x)` is regularly varying with index `-3/2`, divide by `x^{-3/2}`, let `x→∞`, and then `epsilon↓0`. This yields

\[
\boxed{
P(Z>x)\sim \frac{2}{3\mu}r^{3/2}x^{-3/2}.
}
\]

Therefore the critical offspring variable `Z` belongs to the domain of attraction of a spectrally positive stable law with index

\[
\alpha=\frac32,
\]

and has infinite variance.

### 5.5 Exact-total-progeny conditioning gives a stable tree, not Brownian CRT

Duquesne's conditioned-GW theorem applies to a critical aperiodic offspring law in the domain of attraction of an `alpha`-stable law. Let `B_n` normalize the centered offspring random walk. For a regularly varying `3/2` tail,

\[
B_n\asymp n^{2/3}
\]

(up to the usual constant/slowly-varying normalization; here the tail has an asymptotically constant slowly varying factor). The theorem says that, conditionally on total progeny exactly `n`, the height/contour process becomes nondegenerate after multiplying graph heights by

\[
\frac{B_n}{n}\asymp n^{-1/3},
\]

and the limiting real tree is the `3/2`-stable Lévy tree.

Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{height}(S_n)=\Theta_P(n^{1/3}),
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(S_n)=\Theta_P(n^{1/3})
\]

at the scale level relevant here.

The source endpoint demands the Brownian scaling `Σ/sqrt(n)` with a finite positive `Σ`. Under that normalization,

\[
\frac{\Sigma}{\sqrt n}\operatorname{diam}(S_n)
=O_P(n^{-1/6})\longrightarrow0.
\]

Hence the rescaled compact metric space converges in Gromov–Hausdorff distance to a one-point space: indeed

\[
d_{GH}\big((S_n,\Sigma n^{-1/2}d_{gr}),\{*\}\big)
\le \frac{\Sigma}{2\sqrt n}\operatorname{diam}(S_n)
\xrightarrow{P}0.
\]

With `unif_{S_n}` as probability measure, the Prokhorov component is automatic after collapsing the whole support to diameter zero, so the GHP limit is likewise the one-point measured space, not Aldous' nondegenerate CRT.

The source's own scale constant also exposes the failure: Appendix D builds `Σ` from covariance matrices containing `E[ν(ν-1)]`; in this example that quantity is infinite. Thus there is no finite positive source `Σ` to preserve.

### 5.6 Root exception does not repair the limit

The UGW root has the unsized law `D`, while non-root vertices use `D^*-1`. The root is one exceptional generation. Its law has finite variance and its contribution is negligible under conditioning on total progeny tending to infinity; the bulk genealogy is governed by the critical heavy-tailed non-root offspring law above. Adding or altering finitely many initial vertices cannot turn the `3/2`-stable height scale into `sqrt(n)` or a Brownian CRT.

**B30 outcome: decisive success.** The proposed endpoint does not merely lack a proof: it permits a different stable universality class with an incompatible metric exponent and incompatible limiting tree.
