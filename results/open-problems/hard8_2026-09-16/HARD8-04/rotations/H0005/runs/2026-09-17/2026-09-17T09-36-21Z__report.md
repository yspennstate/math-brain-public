## 3. B2 — attack the negation first: a finite-`g''`, infinite-effective-variance law

### 3.1 An explicit source UGW law

Let the root offspring/degree variable `D` have

\[
P(D=k)=4k^{-7/2},\qquad k\ge 2,
\]

and

\[
P(D=0)=1-4\sum_{k\ge2}k^{-7/2},
\qquad P(D=1)=0.
\]

This is a probability law because

\[
4\sum_{k\ge2} k^{-7/2}
=4(\zeta(7/2)-1)
\approx0.506935<1.
\]

Its mean is

\[
\mu=g'(1)=4\sum_{k\ge2}k^{-5/2}
=4(\zeta(5/2)-1)
\approx1.365949>1,
\]

so it obeys the source's supercritical-UGW standing mean assumption.

Its second factorial moment is finite:

\[
\begin{aligned}
g''(1)
&=4\sum_{k\ge2}k(k-1)k^{-7/2}\\
&=4\sum_{k\ge2}(k-1)k^{-5/2}\\
&=4\big[(\zeta(3/2)-1)-(\zeta(5/2)-1)\big]\\
&\approx5.083552<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]

But its third factorial moment diverges:

\[
g'''(1)
=4\sum_{k\ge2}k(k-1)(k-2)k^{-7/2}
=\infty,
\]

because the summand is asymptotic to `4 k^{-1/2}`.

Thus this law satisfies exactly the proposed replacement hypothesis `g''(1)<∞` but not the next moment.

### 3.2 What size bias does

For `j>=1`, the source non-root forward count `nu=D^*-1` has

\[
P(\nu=j)
=\frac{(j+1)P(D=j+1)}{\mu}
=\frac{4}{\mu}(j+1)^{-5/2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
P(\nu>x)\sim c_\nu x^{-3/2}
\]

for an explicit positive constant `c_nu`. In particular

\[
E\nu=m_* = \frac{g''(1)}{g'(1)}\approx3.721627<\infty,
\qquad
E\nu^2=\infty.
\]

The finite-second-moment hypothesis on the **root law** has become only a finite-first-moment statement for the **non-root reproduction law**.

### 3.4 Bernoulli thinning preserves the stable tail

For any fixed `epsilon in (0,1)`, Chernoff concentration conditional on `nu` gives exponentially small probability that `Bin(nu,r)` differs from `r nu` by a fixed relative fraction once `nu` is large. Hence

\[
P\!\left(\nu>\frac{(1+\epsilon)x}{r}\right)-e^{-c_\epsilon x}
\le P(Z>x)
\le
P\!\left(\nu>\frac{(1-\epsilon)x}{r}\right)+e^{-c_\epsilon x}.
\]

Regular variation of `nu` and then `epsilon downarrow 0` imply

\[
\boxed{
P(Z>x)\sim c_\nu r^{3/2}x^{-3/2}.
}
\]

Thus the **critical** effective offspring `Z` lies in the domain of attraction of a spectrally-positive `alpha=3/2` stable law and has infinite variance.

### 3.5 Consequence under exact total-progeny conditioning

Duquesne's conditioned-Galton-Watson invariance principle applies to a critical aperiodic offspring law in the domain of attraction of a stable law of index `alpha in (1,2]`. If `B_n` is the random-walk norming sequence, `B_n ~ n^{1/alpha}L(n)`, the conditioned height/contour scale is `n/B_n`. For `alpha=3/2`, graph distances are therefore of order

\[

\[

At the **conditional branching-process level**, the proposed endpoint `g''(1)<∞` is therefore false, and false in the strongest possible way: it does not merely fail to provide a technical uniform-integrability estimate; it allows a different universality class.

## 4. B4 — prove the clean obstruction completely: the UGW moment-shift lemma

### Proposition 4.1 (exact factorial-moment transfer)

Let `D` be a nonnegative integer root offspring variable with `0<mu=ED<∞`, let `D^*` be its size bias,

\[
P(D^*=k)=\frac{kP(D=k)}{\mu},
\]

and let `nu=D^*-1`. Conditional on `nu`, let `Z~Bin(nu,r)` for a fixed `r in (0,1]`. If `g(s)=E[s^D]`, then

\[
\boxed{E\nu=\frac{g''(1)}{g'(1)}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{E[\nu(\nu-1)]=\frac{g'''(1)}{g'(1)}}.
\]

Moreover

\[
E Z=r\frac{g''(1)}{g'(1)},
\qquad
E[Z(Z-1)] = r^2\frac{g'''(1)}{g'(1)}.
\]

In particular, when `r>0`, `g''(1)<∞` alone does not imply finite variance of the effective failed-child count `Z`; if `g'''(1)=∞`, then `Var(Z)=∞`.

### Proof

By size bias and the change of variable `j=k-1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\nu
&=\sum_{k\ge1}(k-1)\frac{kP(D=k)}{\mu}\\
&=\frac{E[D(D-1)]}{ED}
=\frac{g''(1)}{g'(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Similarly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[\nu(\nu-1)]
&=\sum_{k\ge1}(k-1)(k-2)\frac{kP(D=k)}{\mu}\\
&=\frac{E[D(D-1)(D-2)]}{ED}
=\frac{g'''(1)}{g'(1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For binomial thinning, conditional factorial moments are

\[
E[Z\mid\nu]=r\nu,
\qquad
E[Z(Z-1)\mid\nu]=r^2\nu(\nu-1).
\]

Taking expectations proves the displayed formulas. Finally

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z)
=E[Z(Z-1)]+EZ-(EZ)^2,
\]

so if `r>0` and `g'''(1)=∞`, then `Var(Z)=∞`. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 (correction to the source endpoint bookkeeping)

In the source Appendix D covariance matrix, the term `E[nu(nu-1)]` is not controlled by `g''(1)<∞`; under the source's own UGW definition it is `g'''(1)/g'(1)`. Likewise the degree-fluctuation term in Lemma 6.1 is finite only when the **size-biased forward count** has finite second moment. In root-`g` notation a sufficient direct moment condition is `g'''(1)<∞`, not merely `g''(1)<∞`.

This does not say `g'''(1)<∞` is the sharp hypothesis for every multitype CRT theorem; it says the requested `g''` endpoint includes infinite-effective-variance examples and therefore cannot support universal Brownian-CRT scaling without some additional structural restriction.

**B4 outcome:** complete. The exact moment shift and the infinite-variance obstruction are proved on the page, with no finite checker needed.

## 5. B21 — transfer the neighboring CRT literature, and why it does not rescue `g''`

The literature check changes the route in an important way.

### 5.1 Finite-second-moment multitype CRT machinery exists

Haas–Stephenson's work on scaling limits of multitype branching trees includes critical multitype Galton–Watson applications with finite second moments and Brownian CRT limits. This is precisely the kind of theorem one would want to replace Miermont's exponential-moment input.

But after Proposition 4.1, this does **not** prove H0005: source `g''(1)<∞` need not imply finite second moments of the effective multitype offspring law at all.

### 5.2 Exact total-size conditioning is also available under stronger moments

Addario-Berry, Beltran, Stufler and Thévenin, arXiv:2507.23241, treat critical multitype Bienaymé trees under conditioning by linear combinations of type counts, which includes the total-size shape required here, and obtain Brownian CRT scaling under a finite exponential-moment condition. This confirms that the conditioning itself is not the fundamental obstacle once the offspring geometry has suitable tails.

Again, however, it does not bridge the source's proposed `g''` endpoint: the heavy-tailed `Z` above is not finite variance, let alone exponential.

### 5.3 Stable conditioned GW is the correct neighboring theory for the counterexample

Duquesne's conditioned-GW theorem explicitly covers critical aperiodic offspring laws in the domain of attraction of an `alpha`-stable law with `alpha in (1,2]`; for `alpha<2` the normalized height process converges to the stable height excursion, not Brownian excursion. The explicit `7/2` root tail above yields `alpha=3/2` after UGW size bias and Bernoulli thinning.

Thus the literature transfer points in the *opposite* direction from the source's expectation: it predicts a stable-tree phase inside the set `g''(1)<∞` unless an additional hypothesis rules out the size-biased infinite variance.
