## 3. From an `E=32` pair to an exact near-TCC Gram matrix

Let `v` be odd. Let `a,b in {+1,-1}^v` be normalized binary sequences with

\[
\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i=1,
\]

and let their defect be

\[
d_t=\operatorname{PAF}_a(t)+\operatorname{PAF}_b(t)+2.
\]

At energy `E=32`, the previous checkpoint proved that there are two distinct nonzero inversion classes `±r, ±s` with

\[
d_{\pm r}=+4,\qquad d_{\pm s}=-4,
\]

and all other nonzero `d_t=0`.

Negate `a,b` if necessary so their sums are `-1`; PAFs are unchanged. Let `A,B` be the corresponding `v x v` circulant matrices. Put

\[
N=2v+2.
\]

Form the standard TCC-shaped matrix

\[
\mathcal H=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&1&\mathbf 1&\mathbf 1\\
1&-1&\mathbf 1&-\mathbf 1\\
\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&A&B\\
\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&-\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}&B^{\mathsf T}&-A^{\mathsf T}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Let `D` be the symmetric circulant matrix whose first row is `(d_0,d_1,...,d_{v-1})`, with `d_0=0`.

### Proposition 3.1 — exact Gram defect

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H\mathcal H^{\mathsf T}-NI_N
=
\operatorname{diag}(0_2,D,D).
}
\]

**Proof.** Since the row sums of `A,B` are `-1`, the first two rows are orthogonal to the lower row blocks. The lower diagonal block is

\[
2J+AA^{\mathsf T}+BB^{\mathsf T}=NI+D.
\]

The lower off-diagonal block is

\[
AB-BA=0
\]

because circulant matrices commute. The same calculation applies to the second lower diagonal block. `□`

Thus an `E=32` pair is not merely a low scalar energy state: it produces a completely explicit high-rank Gram error.

## 4. Factorization and exact rank of the defect matrix

Let `P` be the cyclic permutation matrix of order `v`. Then

\[
D=4(P^r+P^{-r}-P^s-P^{-s}).
\]

Because `v` is odd, `2` is invertible modulo `v`. Choose

\[
a\equiv (r+s)/2,\qquad b\equiv(r-s)/2\pmod v.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
D=4(P^a-P^{-a})(P^b-P^{-b}).
}
\]

Indeed,

\[
(P^a-P^{-a})(P^b-P^{-b})
=P^r-P^s-P^{-s}+P^{-r}.
\]

This factorization makes the nullspace exact.

Let `z` be the nullity of `D`. At a Fourier eigenvector indexed by `j mod v`, the eigenvalue is

\[
\lambda_j
=4(\zeta^{jr}+\zeta^{-jr}-\zeta^{js}-\zeta^{-js})
=8\left(\cos\frac{2\pi jr}{v}-\cos\frac{2\pi js}{v}\right).
\]

The equality `lambda_j=0` is equivalent to

\[
\zeta^{j(r-s)}=1
\quad\text{or}\quad
\zeta^{j(r+s)}=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
z=\gcd(v,r-s)+\gcd(v,r+s)-\gcd(v,r-s,r+s),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}D=v-z.
}
\]

In particular, if `v` is prime then `z=1` and

\[
\operatorname{rank}D=v-1.
\]

The full Gram defect therefore has rank

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal H\mathcal H^{\mathsf T}-NI)=2(v-z).
}
\]

## 5. Same-order repair requires changing many columns

Suppose `H'` is any `N x N` Hadamard matrix obtained from `mathcal H` by changing entries only in `t` columns. No structural assumption on those changes is needed.

Write the columns of `mathcal H` as `h_j` and the changed columns as `h'_j`. Then

\[
H'H'^{\mathsf T}-\mathcal H\mathcal H^{\mathsf T}
=\sum_{j\in S}(h'_jh_j'^{\mathsf T}-h_jh_j^{\mathsf T}),
\]

where `|S|=t`. Each summand has rank at most `2`. Since `H'H'^T=NI`,

\[
2(v-z)
=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal H\mathcal H^{\mathsf T}-NI)
\le 2t.
\]

Hence:

### Theorem 5.1 — column-support lower bound

\[
\boxed{
t\ge v-z.}
\]

For prime `v`, every same-order exact repair must touch at least

\[
\boxed{v-1}
\]

of the `2v+2` columns.

So the `E=32` TCC defect cannot be fixed by a constant number of column edits. A combinatorially tiny support trade is necessarily a matrix-global move.

## 6. Why a balanced support exchange is matrix-global

A one-point balanced support trade in one sequence replaces a `+1` at `x` by `-1` and a `-1` at `y` by `+1`. At the circulant-matrix level,

\[
\Delta A=\pm 2(P^y-P^x).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}(\Delta A)=v-\gcd(v,y-x).
}
\]

For prime `v`, one support exchange already has rank `v-1`. Thus the balanced-exchange dynamics from the previous run are exactly of the correct matrix scale to overcome the high-rank Gram obstruction. What looks local in the support set is global after circulant lifting.

## 7. A no-small-border theorem

A natural alternative to editing the TCC core is to keep `mathcal H` unchanged as a principal block and add a small border. The `E=32` defect forbids this completely once `v>=11`.

Suppose a Hadamard matrix `K` of order `N+c` contains `mathcal H` as its upper-left `N x N` principal block. Write the first `N` rows of `K` as

\[
[\mathcal H\;X],\qquad X\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times c}.
\]

Orthogonality of these first `N` rows gives

\[
XX^{\mathsf T}=cI_N-G,
\qquad
G:=\operatorname{diag}(0_2,D,D).
\]

Hence `cI-G` is positive semidefinite and has rank at most `c`.

Let `m_c` be the multiplicity of the eigenvalue `c` of `D`. Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}(cI-G)=2+2(v-m_c),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
m_c\ge v-\frac{c-2}{2}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For an `E=32` defect matrix,

\[
\operatorname{tr}D=0,
\qquad
\lambda_j\ge -16,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(D^2)=64v.
\]

The first two facts imply

\[
0=\sum_j\lambda_j
\ge m_c c-16(v-m_c),
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{m_c\le \frac{16v}{c+16}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Combining (7.1) and (7.2) yields the necessary inequality

\[
\boxed{c^2+(14-2v)c-32\ge0.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

For `v>=11`, the left side at `c=8` is negative, so because `N=2v+2` and every larger Hadamard order is again divisible by four, any proper border must satisfy

\[
c\ge12.
\]

The square moment gives a second bound:

\[
m_c c^2\le64v.
\]

Together with (7.1),

\[
\boxed{c^2(2v-c+2)\le128v.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

If the border is proper, then `0<c<N`; since `c` is divisible by four,

\[
12\le c\le N-4=2v-2.
\]

Define

\[
g(c)=c^2(2v+2-c).
\]

Its derivative has only one positive interior critical point, and that critical point is a maximum. Hence the minimum on `[12,2v-2]` is attained at an endpoint. But for `v>=11`,

\[
g(12)=144(2v-10)>128v,
\]

and

\[
g(2v-2)=16(v-1)^2>128v.
\]

Therefore (7.4) is impossible.

### Theorem 7.1 — no proper principal bordering for `v>=11`

Let `v>=11` be odd and let `mathcal H` be the TCC-shaped `E=32` near-Hadamard matrix above. There is **no** Hadamard matrix of order `N+c` with

\[
0<c<N=2v+2
\]

that contains `mathcal H` unchanged as an `N x N` principal block.

Equivalently, an `E=32` near-TCC matrix cannot be converted to a nearby Hadamard order by merely appending fewer than `N` rows and columns while preserving the whole old block.

This rules out a broad and tempting constant-border repair strategy.

B = {0,1,2,3,5,9,10,12,15,16}

Its half-defect is

\[

so `r=6`, `s=8` and `N=40`.

Because `19` is prime,

\[
\operatorname{rank}D=18,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}G=36.
\]

Thus any order-40 Hadamard repair of this explicit near-TCC matrix must change at least **18 distinct columns**.

The principal-border theorem rules out every Hadamard supermatrix of orders

\[
44,48,52,56,60,64,68,72,76
\]

that keeps this particular `40 x 40` near-TCC matrix unchanged as a principal block. (This is a statement about preserving this block, not a nonexistence statement for Hadamard matrices at those orders.)

## 11. Failed / closed route and next attack

The idea “take an `E=32` near-TCC matrix and repair it by a constant-size border” is now closed for all odd `v>=11` if the old block is preserved. Likewise, same-order repair cannot be confined to a constant number of columns for generic/prime defect pairs.

This shifts the useful effort back toward operations that are combinatorially local but **circulantly global**:

1. continue the balanced-trade plateau program and seek an invariant forcing eventual `E=0`;
2. analyze the factorized defect
   \[
   (P^a-P^{-a})(P^b-P^{-b})
   \]
   inside four-block Goethals-Seidel / orthogonal-design constructions, where a second structured block pair might cancel it without preserving the original TCC block;
3. on composite lengths, use the exact nullity formula as a secondary statistic during plateau motion, while recognizing that prime lengths have constant rank `v-1` and therefore require a different progress measure.

The next bounded attack should therefore search for an **exact defect-cancellation identity across two circulant block pairs**, not a small border.
