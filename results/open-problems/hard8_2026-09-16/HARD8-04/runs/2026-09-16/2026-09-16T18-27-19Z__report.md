### Proof

Ignore signs temporarily and encode the four support components by two bits over `F_2`:

\[
e_0\leftrightarrow(0,0),\quad
 e_1\leftrightarrow(0,1),\quad
 e_2\leftrightarrow(1,0),\quad
 e_3\leftrightarrow(1,1).
\]

Write the support label of `q(x)` as

\[
c(x)=(u(x),v(x))\in\mathbb F_2^2.
\]

The support permutation underlying `R` is

\[
0\mapsto0,\quad1\leftrightarrow3,\quad2\mapsto2.
\]

In the chosen two-bit coordinates this is exactly

\[
(u,v)\longmapsto(u+v,v).
\]

Hence (4.2) implies

\[
v(hx)=v(x),\qquad u(hx)=u(x)+v(x).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Now reduce the correlation condition modulo two. Since signs `+1` and `-1` both become `1`, `C(s) mod 2` is simply the parity of the number of `x` for which `c(x)=c(x+s)`. For two bit-pairs,

\[
\mathbf 1_{(u,v)=(u',v')}
=(1+u+u')(1+v+v')
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2.
\]

Summing this identity over all `x` and using translation invariance, all linear and same-point quadratic terms cancel in pairs. Because 523 is odd, the constant term contributes one. Therefore for every shift `s`,

\[
C(s)\equiv
1+
\sum_x\bigl(u(x)v(x+s)+v(x)u(x+s)\bigr)\pmod2.
\]

Define

\[
B(s)=\sum_x\bigl(u(x)v(x+s)+v(x)u(x+s)\bigr)\in\mathbb F_2.
\]

By (4.1), `C(s)=0` for `s ne 0`, while `C(0)=523` is odd. Thus

\[
B(0)=0,
\qquad
B(s)=1\quad(s\ne0).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Pass to the group algebra. Put

\[
U(z)=\sum_xu(x)z^x,
\qquad
V(z)=\sum_xv(x)z^x
\]

in `F_2[z]/(z^523-1)`, and let `*` be the involution `z -> z^{-1}`. Equation (5.2) is exactly

\[
U V^*+V U^*=J+1,
\qquad
J=1+z+\cdots+z^{522}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The exact arithmetic check gives

\[
2^{522}\equiv1\pmod{523},
\]

while, for the prime divisors `2,3,29` of 522,

\[
2^{261}\equiv522,\qquad
2^{174}\equiv462,\qquad
2^{18}\equiv121
\pmod{523}.
\]

None is 1, so

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{523}(2)=522.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently the cyclotomic polynomial

\[
\Phi_{523}(z)=1+z+\cdots+z^{522}
\]

is irreducible over `F_2`. Let `zeta` be a primitive 523rd root in `F_{2^{522}}`. Evaluating (5.3) at `zeta` kills `J` and gives

\[
U V^*+V U^*=1,
\tag{5.5}
\]

where from now on `U=U(zeta)` and `V=V(zeta)`.

Because `h=2^{29}`, the equivariance relations (5.1) become Frobenius identities. Reindexing the sums gives

\[
V^{2^{29}}=V,
\qquad
U^{2^{29}}=U+V.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
V\in\mathbb F_{2^{29}},
\qquad
U^{2^{58}}=U,
\quad\text{so}\quad
U\in\mathbb F_{2^{58}}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The involution `*` is evaluation at `zeta^{-1}`. By (5.4),

\[
zeta^{-1}=zeta^{2^{261}},
\]

so `*` is the 261st Frobenius iterate. Since

\[
261\equiv0\pmod{29},
\qquad
261\equiv29\pmod{58},
\]

(5.7) and (5.6) imply

\[
V^*=V,
\qquad
U^*=U^{2^{29}}=U+V.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Substitute (5.8) into (5.5). In characteristic two,

\[
UV+V(U+V)=V^2=1.
\]

A field has no nonzero nilpotents, so `(V+1)^2=0` forces

\[
V=1.
\tag{5.9}
\]

We now return from the field value to the binary support function. The polynomial

\[
V(z)+1
\]

has degree at most 522 and vanishes at `zeta`. By irreducibility and degree 522, it is either zero or `Phi_523(z)`. But `q(0)=e_0` gives `v(0)=0`, so `V(z)` cannot be the constant polynomial 1. Hence

\[
V(z)+1=\Phi_{523}(z),
\]

and therefore

\[
V(z)=z+z^2+\cdots+z^{522}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Thus

\[
v(x)=1\qquad\text{for every }x\ne0.
\]

The bit `v=1` occurs exactly on support components `e_1` and `e_3`. Hence no nonzero field element belongs to component 0 or component 2. In particular component 0 occurs only at `x=0`, where its sign is `+1`, so its row sum must be

\[
r_0=1.
\]

But the exact necessary orbit/row-sum sieve (4.3) forces `r_0=19`. Contradiction.

This proves the canonical family empty. The preceding pinned signed-permutation conjugacy sieve proved that every other `d=18` action either fails the necessary row/orbit-sum conditions already or is conjugate to this canonical survivor. Therefore the whole `d=18` signed-equivariant tier is empty. QED.
