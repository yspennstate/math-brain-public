## 3. B10 — choose a split prime that makes modular feasibility exact

The previous `q=19` character equations were only a necessary sieve because an integer correlation residual could vanish modulo `19` without vanishing over the integers. The key new move is to choose a split prime larger than the residual scale while preserving the order-nine Frobenius collapse.

Set

\[
p=523,\qquad q=617.
\]

Exact arithmetic gives

\[
617\text{ is prime},\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{523}(617)=9.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Moreover

\[
617\equiv94\pmod{523},
\]

and `94` belongs to the same order-nine subgroup

\[
K=\langle19\rangle
 =\{1,19,60,94,217,361,410,462,468\}
 \le F_{523}^{\times}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

As before, `3` has order `58` modulo `523` and

\[
3^{29}\equiv-1\pmod{523}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence the nonzero multiplier cosets are

\[
C_j=3^jK,\qquad j\in Z/58Z,
\]

with

\[
-C_j=C_{j+29}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Let `zeta` be a primitive 523rd root in `F_{617^9}`. If `f:F_523 -> F_617` is `K`-invariant, then

\[
\widehat f(t)^{617}
 =\sum_x f(x)\zeta^{617tx}.
\]

Since multiplication by `617 mod 523 =94` permutes each `K`-orbit, `f(94^{-1}x)=f(x)`, and therefore

\[
\widehat f(t)^{617}=\widehat f(t).
\]

Thus every `K`-invariant Fourier value lies in the base field:

\[
\boxed{\widehat f(t)\in F_{617}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This gives the same 29 negation-paired character equations as before, now over `F_617`.

Let `X_i` be the four negative sets and write

\[
A_i=1-2\,1_{X_i}\in\{\pm1\}^{523}.
\]

At every nonzero frequency `t in C_c`, periodic complementarity requires

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3\widehat A_i(t)\widehat A_i(-t)=4p.
\]

Since `\widehat A_i(t)=-2\widehat{1_{X_i}}(t)` for nonzero `t`, dividing by four gives the 29 equations

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^3
 \widehat{1_{X_i}}(C_c)
 \widehat{1_{X_i}}(C_{c+29})
 =523\pmod{617},
\qquad c=0,\ldots,28.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The important point is that, at `q=617`, these equations are no longer merely a cheap necessary sieve.

## 4. Exact lifting theorem for the d=9 GS search

### Theorem

Consider four `K`-invariant sign sequences `A_0,...,A_3` of length `523`, with either of the two surviving `d=9` row-sum profiles

\[
(37,19,19,1)
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
(17,17,17,35),
\tag{4.1}
\]

up to permutation/sign normalization. If their 29 character equations (3.6) hold in `F_617`, then the four sequences are **exactly periodic complementary over the integers**:

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3 \operatorname{PAF}_{A_i}(s)=0
\qquad(s\ne0).
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Define the integer residual

\[
R(s)=\sum_{i=0}^3\operatorname{PAF}_{A_i}(s).
\]

First, both row-sum profiles satisfy

\[
37^2+19^2+19^2+1^2=2092=4\cdot523,
\]

and

\[
17^2+17^2+17^2+35^2=2092=4\cdot523.
\tag{4.3}
\]

So the zero-frequency Fourier identity already has the target value `4p` exactly.

For the 58 nonzero frequency cosets, the 29 equations (3.6), together with the negation pairing `C_c <-> C_{c+29}`, imply

\[
\sum_i\widehat A_i(t)\widehat A_i(-t)
 \equiv4p\pmod{617}
\tag{4.4}
\]

at every nonzero 523rd-root frequency. Since `523` is invertible in `F_617`, inverse discrete Fourier transform yields

\[
R(s)\equiv0\pmod{617}
\qquad(s\ne0).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Now use an integer parity fact that does **not** come from the finite field. For a single sign sequence `A` and a shift `s`, let `D_A(s)` be the Hamming distance between `A(x)` and `A(x+s)`. Then

\[
\operatorname{PAF}_{A}(s)=523-2D_A(s).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Also

\[
(-1)^{D_A(s)}
 =\prod_x A(x)A(x+s)
 =(\prod_xA(x))^2
 =1,
\]

so `D_A(s)` is even. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{PAF}_{A}(s)\equiv523\equiv3\pmod4,
\]

and the sum of four autocorrelations obeys

\[
R(s)\equiv0\pmod4.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Finally, each autocorrelation lies in `[-523,523]`, hence

\[
|R(s)|\le4\cdot523=2092.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Equations (4.5) and (4.7) imply that `R(s)` is divisible by

\[
4\cdot617=2468.
\]

But `2468>2092`, so the only possible such integer in the allowed interval is zero. Therefore `R(s)=0` for every nonzero shift, proving (4.2). `QED`.

### Consequence

Within the `d=9`, common-order-nine-multiplier GS search, solving the 29 equations over `F_617` under either fixed row-sum/weight profile is already an **exact construction certificate for periodic complementarity**. There is no longer a separate “modular survivor -> maybe integer correlations” gap.

This is a restricted-family theorem. It does **not** prove that `H(2092)` exists, and failure of this `d=9` family would not prove that `H(2092)` does not exist.
