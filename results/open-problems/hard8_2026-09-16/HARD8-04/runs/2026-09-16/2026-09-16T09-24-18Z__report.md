## 3. Normalized Legendre defect and its exact Gram form

Let `v` be odd, and let `a,b in {+1,-1}^v` satisfy

\[
\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i=1.
\]

Let `A,B` be their circulant matrices. For nonzero cyclic shifts define

\[
d_t=\operatorname{PAF}_a(t)+\operatorname{PAF}_b(t)+2,
\]

and put `d_0=0`. Let `\Delta` be the symmetric circulant matrix with first row `(d_0,...,d_{v-1})`.

Because

\[
\sum_{t=0}^{v-1}\operatorname{PAF}_a(t)=\left(\sum_i a_i\right)^2=1
\]

and likewise for `b`, one has the exact zero-sum identity

\[
\boxed{\sum_{t=1}^{v-1}d_t=0.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The pair Gram matrix is

\[
\boxed{
AA^{\mathsf T}+BB^{\mathsf T}
=(2v+2)I_v-2J_v+\Delta.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Indeed the diagonal is `2v`; every nonzero circulant diagonal is `-2+d_t`.

For a true binary Legendre pair, `\Delta=0`.

## 4. Exact defect-transfer theorem into a Goethals–Seidel quadruple

We now ask for two further binary circulant rows `c,e in {+1,-1}^v`, with circulant matrices `C,E`, such that the four rows satisfy the GS Gram equation.

### Theorem 4.1 — Legendre/D-optimal defect transfer

The quadruple `(A,B,C,E)` satisfies

\[
AA^{\mathsf T}+BB^{\mathsf T}+CC^{\mathsf T}+EE^{\mathsf T}=4vI_v
\]

if and only if

\[
\boxed{
CC^{\mathsf T}+EE^{\mathsf T}
=(2v-2)I_v+2J_v-\Delta.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently, for every nonzero shift,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{PAF}_c(t)+\operatorname{PAF}_e(t)=2-d_t.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** Subtract (3.2) from `4vI_v`. The diagonal of the right side of (4.1) is `2v`; for a nonzero cyclic displacement its entry is `2-d_t`, proving the PAF form. `□`

When `\Delta=0`, equation (4.1) is exactly the standard two-circulant D-optimal equation. Thus a near-Legendre pair does not ask for an unrelated second pair: it asks for a **D-optimal pair carrying precisely the opposite Gram defect**.

This is the central structural result of the run.

## 5. Zero-frequency arithmetic obstruction: a sum of two squares

Let

\[
\sigma_c=\sum_i c_i,\qquad \sigma_e=\sum_i e_i.
\]

Summing (4.2) over all nonzero shifts and using (3.1) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_c^2+\sigma_e^2
&=\sum_t\operatorname{PAF}_c(t)+\sum_t\operatorname{PAF}_e(t)\\
&=2v+\sum_{t\ne0}(2-d_t)\\
&=2v+2(v-1)\\
&=4v-2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence every two-row GS completion of a normalized pair must satisfy

\[
\boxed{\sigma_c^2+\sigma_e^2=4v-2.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because `v` is odd, both row sums are odd. Put

\[
u=\frac{\sigma_c+\sigma_e}{2},\qquad
w=\frac{\sigma_c-\sigma_e}{2}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{u^2+w^2=2v-1.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Conversely, from any representation `2v-1=u^2+w^2`, the numbers `u+w` and `u-w` are odd and satisfy (5.1); for odd `v>=5` their absolute values are automatically at most `v` because

\[
(u\pm w)^2\le2(u^2+w^2)=4v-2<v^2.
\]

Therefore:

### Corollary 5.2 — exact arithmetic gate for this architecture

A necessary zero-frequency condition for a two-row GS completion is that

\[
\boxed{2v-1\text{ is a sum of two integer squares}.}
\]

Equivalently, every prime `p=3 mod 4` must occur to an even exponent in the factorization of `2v-1`.

This obstruction is **independent of the detailed Legendre defect**. For example,

* `v=11`: `2v-1=21=3*7`, so no normalized near-Legendre pair of length 11 can be completed by only two extra binary circulant rows in this way.
* `v=17`: `2v-1=33=3*11`, so the same architecture is impossible at length 17.
* `v=19`: `2v-1=37=1^2+6^2`, so row sums `(7,5)` are arithmetically admissible.

This does **not** obstruct Hadamard matrices of orders `44` or `68`; it only obstructs this specific two-row circulant completion architecture anchored at a normalized pair.

## 6. Spectral target: no positivity obstruction for `E=32` once `v>=9`

Let `zeta=exp(2*pi*i/v)` and

\[
\widehat d(j)=\sum_{t=1}^{v-1}d_t\zeta^{jt}.
\]

For every nonzero frequency `j`, (3.2) yields

\[
|\widehat a(j)|^2+|\widehat b(j)|^2
=2v+2+\widehat d(j),
\]

while the desired compensator has

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat c(j)|^2+|\widehat e(j)|^2
=2v-2-\widehat d(j).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

At the minimum positive energy `E=32`, the prior checkpoint proved that for two distinct inversion classes `+-r,+-s`,

\[
d_{+-r}=4,\qquad d_{+-s}=-4,
\]

and all other nonzero `d_t` vanish. Hence

\[
\widehat d(j)=8\left(
\cos\frac{2\pi jr}{v}-\cos\frac{2\pi js}{v}
\right),
\]

so

\[
-16\le\widehat d(j)\le16.
\]

Therefore the compensator target (6.1) obeys

\[
2v-18\le
|\widehat c(j)|^2+|\widehat e(j)|^2
\le2v+14.
\]

Thus for `v>=9` there is no coarse PSD-sign obstruction. The matrix target in (4.1) is spectrally compatible at every nonzero Fourier mode; the difficult part is the **binary circulant factorization**, not positive semidefiniteness.

This contrasts sharply with the previous run's principal-border route, which was killed by rank and spectral-moment constraints.

## 7. Support-set form: opposite almost-SDS defects

Let `X,Y` be the plus supports of `c,e`, with sizes `k_c,k_e`, and put

\[
K=k_c+k_e,
\qquad
\lambda=K-\frac{v-1}{2}.
\]

For a support `X`, let `nu_X(t)` count ordered differences equal to `t`. Since

\[
\operatorname{PAF}_c(t)=v-4k_c+4\nu_X(t),
\]

condition (4.2) is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\nu_X(t)+\nu_Y(t)=\lambda-\frac{d_t}{4}
\qquad(t\ne0).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

So an `E=32` compensator is itself an almost supplementary difference pair with exactly the **opposite** surplus/deficit:

* at the near-Legendre surplus class `+-r`, the compensator is deficient by one;
* at the near-Legendre deficit class `+-s`, the compensator is surplus by one;
* all other difference classes have the D-optimal baseline multiplicity `lambda`.

Combining the two pairs gives a genuine four-block cyclic supplementary difference family and hence a Goethals–Seidel Hadamard matrix.

This makes the next combinatorial target precise: **repair/transport a two-block D-optimal SDS by a bounded balanced trade so that its defect is the negative of the Legendre near-pair defect.** The balanced-exchange group-ring identity from the previous checkpoint applies verbatim to these compensator supports.

## 8. Exact `v=19` compensation certificate and Hadamard matrix of order 76

Take the explicit hardest `E=32` state from the prior plateau checkpoint:

```text
A = {0,1,2,3,4,5,8,10,14,16}
B = {0,1,2,3,5,9,10,12,15,16}
```

where a listed position carries `+1` and every omitted position carries `-1`.

Its half-defect is

```text
(0,0,0,0,0,4,0,-4,0),
```

so the desired compensator PAF sum at shifts `1,...,9` is

```text
(2,2,2,2,2,-2,2,6,2).
```

The arithmetic gate gives `2v-1=37=1^2+6^2`, hence allowable compensator row sums `7` and `5`.

An exact compensator is:

```text
C = {0,1,2,3,5,7,8,10,11,12,14,16}
E = {0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,11,12,14,15}
```

with row sums `5` and `7`. Their half-PAF vectors are

```text
PAF(C) = (-5, 3,-1,-1, 3,-1,-1, 3, 3)
PAF(E) = ( 7,-1, 3, 3,-1,-1, 3, 3,-1)
```

and therefore

```text
PAF(C)+PAF(E) = (2,2,2,2,2,-2,2,6,2),
```

exactly as required.

Consequently the four circulant matrices satisfy

\[
AA^{\mathsf T}+BB^{\mathsf T}+CC^{\mathsf T}+EE^{\mathsf T}=76I_{19}.
\]

Putting them into the standard Goethals–Seidel array gives an exact `76 x 76` Hadamard matrix. The new verifier constructs the full array and checks

\[
\boxed{HH^{\mathsf T}=76I_{76}}
\]

with integer arithmetic.

Order 76 was known long before this project (for example from classical Paley-type constructions), so this is **not a new Hadamard order**. Its value here is structural: an `E=32` state that was provably resistant to local TCC/border repair is exactly completable by an opposite D-optimal-type defect in a different block architecture.

## 10. Why this is stronger than the previous repair attempts

The preceding checkpoint established that an `E=32` TCC matrix carries a Gram defect of rank nearly `2v`, so preserving that matrix and correcting only a few columns or appending a proper border is impossible in the prime-length case.

The present construction does not preserve the TCC matrix. Instead it uses the exact decomposition

\[
\underbrace{(2v+2)I-2J+\Delta}_{\text{near-Legendre pair}}
+
\underbrace{(2v-2)I+2J-\Delta}_{\text{D-optimal-type compensator}}
=4vI.
\]

Thus the high-rank defect is cancelled by another high-rank structured object, not by a low-rank correction. This is precisely the kind of architecture the previous rank obstruction left open.

## 12. Failed/closed routes and next attack

Closed from the previous run: small same-order column repair and proper principal bordering of `E=32` TCC matrices.

Newly closed here: the naive belief that zero-frequency sum-of-two-squares compatibility alone guarantees an opposite two-row binary factorization; `v=5,7` are exact counterexamples for some abstract `E=32` patterns.

The next useful attack is now specific.

1. Start from a genuine two-circulant D-optimal SDS `(X,Y)` at an admissible odd `v`.
2. Write its exact balanced-exchange trade vectors in the same group-ring coordinates used for the Legendre `E=32` plateau.
3. Determine when a bounded sequence of equal-energy trades can create exactly one `-1` error at `+-r` and one `+1` error at `+-s` in the difference multiplicities.
4. Quotient by affine/decimation symmetry and test whether the reachable defect graph is connected for known infinite D-optimal families.
5. If such a transport theorem can be made uniform in `(r,s)`, combine it with a route that brings arbitrary near-Legendre defects to `E=32`; otherwise search for a different four-block completion not constrained by the two-row sum-of-squares gate.

A particularly important distinction is that the arithmetic obstruction depends on `2v-1`, whereas the Hadamard conjecture asks for every order `4v`. Therefore this GS-completion architecture cannot by itself be universal unless it is paired with a different construction at the excluded `v`.
