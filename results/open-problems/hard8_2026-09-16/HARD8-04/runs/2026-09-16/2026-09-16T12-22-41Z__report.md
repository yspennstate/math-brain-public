## 3. Quotient-order-five reduction

Assume `5|v`; since the bordered target requires even `v`, write

\[
v=10s,\qquad s\ge1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a putative four-block cyclic difference family of parameters (1.1), project each block to `Z_5`. Let

\[
n_i=(n_i(0),\ldots,n_i(4))\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^5
\]

be the occupancies of the five quotient fibers. Each fiber in `Z_v -> Z_5` has size `2s`, so a genuine quotient certificate must obey

\[
0\le n_i(r)\le2s.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The row sums are

\[
\sum_r n_1(r)=5s-1,
\qquad
\sum_r n_i(r)=5s\quad(i=2,3,4).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For `a=1,2`, define the cyclic quadratic energies

\[
Q_a(n)=\frac12\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z_5}(n(r)-n(r+a))^2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If

\[
C_n(a)=\sum_r n(r)n(r+a),
\]

then

\[
Q_a(n)=C_n(0)-C_n(a).
\tag{3.5}
\]

The general quotient identity proved in the preceding checkpoint specializes to

\[
\sum_i C_{n_i}(0)=20s^2+6s+1,
\tag{3.6}
\]

and, for each nonzero quotient shift,

\[
\sum_i C_{n_i}(a)=20s^2-4s.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Hence every valid quotient certificate must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^4Q_1(n_i)=\sum_{i=1}^4Q_2(n_i)=10s+1.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Conversely, (3.3) and (3.8) imply the complete quotient autocorrelation system (3.6)--(3.7). Indeed, write

\[
A_j=\sum_iC_{n_i}(j),\qquad j=0,1,2.
\]

Equation (3.8) says `A_0-A_1=A_0-A_2=10s+1`, so `A_1=A_2`. On the other hand

\[
A_0+2A_1+2A_2
=(5s-1)^2+3(5s)^2
=100s^2-10s+1.
\]

Solving gives exactly (3.6)--(3.7).

Thus order five reduces to constructing four bounded integer occupancy vectors with the two equal energy totals in (3.8).

## 4. A universal `A_4` norm lemma

Define the positive-definite integral quaternary quadratic form

\[
\mathcal Q(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
=
\sum_{i=1}^4x_i^2+
\sum_{1\le i<j\le4}x_ix_j.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\mathcal Q(x)
=
\frac12\left(
\sum_{i=1}^4x_i^2+
\left(\sum_{i=1}^4x_i\right)^2
\right),
\tag{4.2}
\]

so it is positive definite.

### Lemma 4.1 — `A_4` norm universality

For every positive integer `N`, there exist integers

\[
u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4
\]

with

\[
\sum_{r=0}^4u_r=0,
\qquad
\frac12\sum_{r=0}^4u_r^2=N.
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

By the Bhargava--Hanke 290-Theorem, it suffices to show that the positive-definite integral form `Q` in (4.1) represents the 29 test integers (2.1). Explicit representations are listed below. Each row gives a zero-sum five-vector `u`; deleting its final coordinate gives a representation of the displayed `N` by `Q`, because

\[
\mathcal Q(u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3)
=\frac12\sum_{r=0}^4u_r^2.
\]

|1|`(-1,0,0,0,1)`|
|2|`(-1,-1,0,1,1)`|
|3|`(-2,0,0,1,1)`|

## 5. Universal feasibility of the complete `d=5` quotient system

### Theorem 5.1

For every integer `s>=1`, hence for every even `v` divisible by `5`, the complete quotient occupancy equations modulo `5` for the bordered cyclic parameter family

\[
(v;v/2-1,v/2,v/2,v/2;v-2)
\]

have a feasible integer solution satisfying the genuine fiber-capacity bounds `0<=n_i(r)<=v/5`.

Equivalently, quotient order five can never by itself refute this stronger bordered cyclic target.

#### Proof

First choose a zero-sum integer five-vector `u` satisfying

\[
\frac12\sum_ru_r^2=2s.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `s>=4`, Lemma 4.1 gives such a vector, and automatically

\[
|u_r|\le\sqrt{4s}=2\sqrt s\le s.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For the three small values, use the explicit bounded choices

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
s&u\\ \hline
1&(-1,-1,0,1,1)\\
2&(-2,-1,1,1,1)\\
3&(-3,0,1,1,1).
\end{array}
\tag{5.3}
\]

They satisfy (5.1) and `|u_r|<=s`. Thus for every `s>=1` we may assume

\[
\sum_ru_r=0,
\qquad
\frac12\sum_ru_r^2=2s,
\qquad
|u_r|\le s.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Let `tau` be multiplication by two on the five coordinates:

\[
(\tau u)_r=u_{2r\bmod5}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
n_1&=(s-1,s,s,s,s),\\
n_2&=(s,s,s,s,s)+u,\\
n_3&=(s,s,s,s,s)+\tau u,\\
n_4&=(s,s,s,s,s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The row sums are exactly (3.3). By `|u_r|<=s`, every coordinate of `n_2,n_3` lies in `[0,2s]`; the same is obvious for `n_1,n_4`. Hence the quotient-fiber capacity condition (3.2) is respected.

The first block satisfies

\[
Q_1(n_1)=Q_2(n_1)=1,
\tag{5.7}
\]

and the constant block has both energies zero. Multiplication of indices by two swaps distance-one and distance-two edges of the 5-cycle, so

\[
Q_1(\tau u)=Q_2(u),
\qquad
Q_2(\tau u)=Q_1(u).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Finally, if

\[
C_j(u)=\sum_ru_ru_{r+j},
\]

then the zero-sum identity gives

\[
0=\left(\sum_ru_r\right)^2=C_0(u)+2C_1(u)+2C_2(u).
\tag{5.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1(u)+Q_2(u)
&=2C_0(u)-C_1(u)-C_2(u)\\
&=\frac52C_0(u)\\
&=5\left(\frac12\sum_ru_r^2\right)\\
&=10s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Combining (5.7)--(5.10),

\[
\sum_{i=1}^4Q_1(n_i)
=1+Q_1(u)+Q_2(u)
=10s+1,
\]

and identically

\[
\sum_{i=1}^4Q_2(n_i)=10s+1.
\]

By the converse reduction in Section 3, all five quotient autocorrelation equations hold exactly. `□`

## 6. What this rules out — and what it does not

Combining this theorem with the preceding checkpoint gives

\[
\boxed{
\text{The complete cyclic quotient occupancy systems of orders }2,3,4,5
\text{ are universally feasible for the bordered target.}
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is stronger than a finite search statement. In particular, the first real cyclotomic field `Q(sqrt(5))` does not create a quotient obstruction; its two real Fourier-energy conditions collapse to the universal `A_4` norm identity above.

However, quotient feasibility is only a necessary condition for a binary cyclic difference family. It forgets the `0/1` arrangement of elements inside quotient fibers. Therefore (6.1) does **not** construct the four subsets of `Z_v`, does not construct Hadamard matrices for all orders, and does not prove the Hadamard conjecture.

It also does not show that every quotient order is feasible. The next genuinely new obstruction levels are `d=6` and `d=7`. Order six mixes characters of orders `2,3,6`; order seven introduces a real cubic cyclotomic field. An obstruction may also require simultaneous quotient constraints or within-fiber binary data rather than a single occupancy projection.

## 8. Failed routes / corrected expectations

This run shows that expectation is false at the occupancy level: the pentagonal Fourier constraints are universally soluble. Therefore continuing to search for a bare order-five quotient contradiction is not useful.

1. derive and test the **complete `d=6` quotient system**, where several character orders must be satisfied simultaneously;
2. move directly to **`d=7`**, where the real cubic cyclotomic field provides the first higher-degree spectral compatibility problem;
3. keep `d=5` but restore **within-fiber binary information**, asking whether the universal occupancy certificates can always be refined to actual subsets, or whether exact lifting obstructions appear there;
4. combine two quotient maps simultaneously, because individually feasible marginals need not admit a common binary refinement.

The companion exact verifier for this run is intended only to check the finite 290-certificate and many explicit quotient certificates. It is a consistency check, not an independent proof of the 290-Theorem or of the Hadamard conjecture.
