## 3. B11 — why the characteristic-two single-character obstruction is intrinsically too weak

This subsection is a proved diagnostic, not a Hadamard construction and not a literature-novelty claim.

Let `K` be the unique order-nine subgroup of `F_523^*`. The earlier exact arithmetic gives

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{523}(2)=522,
\]

so `K=<2^58>`. Let `zeta` be a primitive 523rd root over `F_2`. If `S_i` is the Fourier value at `zeta` of the support indicator of the `i`th T-component, K-invariance gives

\[
S_i\in F_{2^{58}}.
\]

The involution corresponding to frequency negation is

\[
S\mapsto S^*=S^{2^{261}}=S^{2^{29}}
\]

on `F_{2^{58}}`, since `261=4*58+29`. Thus `*` is the nontrivial automorphism of the quadratic extension

\[
F_{2^{58}}/F_{2^{29}}.
\]

Modulo two, all signs disappear. The exact T-correlation equations reduce at a nonzero character to

\[
\sum_{i=0}^3 S_iS_i^*=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^3S_i=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Put

\[
x=S_0+S_1,\qquad y=S_0+S_2.
\]

Expanding (3.1) in characteristic two gives exactly

\[
xy^*+yx^*=1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equation (3.2) forces `y!=0`. Writing `z=x/y` and `N(y)=yy^*` gives

\[
z+z^*=N(y)^{-1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

But `z -> z+z^*` is the relative trace

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{F_{2^{58}}/F_{2^{29}}}(z).
\]

Its kernel is exactly `F_{2^{29}}`, so it is surjective and every right-hand side in `F_{2^{29}}` has exactly `2^{29}` preimages. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{for every nonzero }y,\text{ the single-character mod-2 equation has exactly }2^{29}\text{ solutions }x.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

So the characteristic-two one-character route is far too permissive to be a useful obstruction. It may still participate in a global coupled system, but it should not receive another run as a stand-alone local obstruction. That negative methodological result is why B11 is redirected to an odd split prime below.

## 4. B30 — the split-prime `19` Fourier sieve

The useful arithmetic accident is

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{523}(19)=9.
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[

\[

\qquad

\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the 58 nonzero K-cosets can be indexed

\[

\]

\[
-C_j=C_{j+29}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `zeta` be a primitive 523rd root in `F_{19^9}`. For a K-invariant indicator `f:F_523 -> F_19`, write

\[
\widehat f(t)=\sum_{x\in F_{523}}f(x)\zeta^{tx}.
\]

Then

\[
\widehat f(t)^{19}
 =\sum_x f(x)\zeta^{19tx}
 =\sum_y f(19^{-1}y)\zeta^{ty}
 =\widehat f(t),
\]

because multiplication by 19 lies in K. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\widehat f(t)\in F_{19}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the key localization: the Fourier values of every K-invariant block are not elements of a degree-nine extension after all; they are ordinary scalars modulo 19.

\[
\eta_j=\sum_{x\in C_j}\zeta^x\in F_{19}.
\tag{4.5}

\[
\widehat{1_X}(t)

\tag{4.6}

\sum_{i=0}^3
