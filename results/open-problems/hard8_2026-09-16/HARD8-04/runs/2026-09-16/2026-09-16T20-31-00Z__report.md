## 3. B18 — exact 3-bit code-balance identity

Retain the previous run's pointwise Hadamard transform. At every coordinate, the sparse T-state is one of

`+e0,-e0,+e1,-e1,+e2,-e2,+e3,-e3`,

and its transformed `(A,B,C)` signs can be encoded by a word

\[
g(x)\in\mathbf F_2^3,
\]

where bit `1` means the corresponding sign is negative. The eight T states map bijectively to

\[
0,7,4,3,2,5,6,1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The fourth sign satisfies `D=ABC`, so it is already determined by the parity of the three bits.

For a shift `s`, define

\[
\Delta_s(x)=g(x)+g(x+s)\in\mathbf F_2^3.
\]

Let

\[
N_0(s)=\#\{x:\Delta_s(x)=000\},\qquad
N_7(s)=\#\{x:\Delta_s(x)=111\}.
\]

Let `C(s)` be the aggregate T-correlation

\[
C(s)=\sum_{i=0}^3\operatorname{PAF}_{T_i}(s).
\]

### Proposition 3.1

For every shift `s`,

\[
\boxed{C(s)=N_0(s)-N_7(s).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

For a fixed pair of positions `x,x+s`, put

\[
p=A(x)A(x+s),\quad q=B(x)B(x+s),\quad r=C(x)C(x+s).
\]

Because `D=ABC`, the fourth sign product is `pqr`. The H4 orthogonality identity from the preceding report gives

\[
T(x)\cdot T(x+s)=\frac{p+q+r+pqr}{4}.
\]

The three-bit difference `Delta_s(x)` records which of `p,q,r` equal `-1`. If its Hamming weight is zero, the numerator is `4`; if its weight is one or two, the numerator is `0`; if its weight is three, the numerator is `-4`. Thus the summand is respectively `+1,0,-1`, proving (3.2) after summing over `x`. QED.

Hence the periodic-T condition on a nonzero shift is not an opaque four-sequence autocorrelation equation. It is exactly the combinatorial balance

\[
\boxed{N_0(s)=N_7(s):
\text{ equally many equal-code and bitwise-complement code pairs}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This gives a materially simpler exact target for SAT/PB, meet-in-the-middle, or branch-and-bound search.
