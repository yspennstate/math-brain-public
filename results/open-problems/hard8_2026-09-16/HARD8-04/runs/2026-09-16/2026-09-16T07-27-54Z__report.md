## 3. Exact support-set reformulation of the `E=32` layer

Let `v` be odd and let `a,b in {+1,-1}^v` be normalized by

\[
\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i=1.
\]

Define the plus supports

\[
A=\{i:a_i=+1\},\qquad B=\{i:b_i=+1\}
\]

inside the cyclic group `G=Z/vZ`. Then

\[
|A|=|B|=k:=\frac{v+1}{2}.
\]

For a subset `C subset G`, write

\[
\nu_C(t)=|C\cap(C-t)|,
\]

the number of ordered pairs `(x,y) in C^2` with `x-y=t`.

### Proposition 3.1 — PAF as a difference multiplicity

For every nonzero shift `t`,

\[
\operatorname{PAF}_a(t)=4\nu_A(t)-v-2,
\]

and similarly for `b`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{d_t=4\left(\nu_A(t)+\nu_B(t)-k\right).}
\]

**Proof.** The Hamming distance between `A` and `A-t` is

\[
D_t=2(k-\nu_A(t)).
\]

For a `\{\pm1\}` sequence, `PAF_a(t)=v-2D_t`; substituting `2k=v+1` gives

\[
\operatorname{PAF}_a(t)
=v-4(k-\nu_A(t))
=4\nu_A(t)-v-2.
\]

Adding the two PAFs and `2` yields the displayed defect formula. `□`

This makes the minimum positive energy completely combinatorial.

### Corollary 3.2 — exact almost-SDS characterization

The pair has `E=32` if and only if there are two distinct inversion classes of nonzero shifts, represented by `r,s in {1,...,(v-1)/2}`, such that, after choosing the signs appropriately,

\[
\nu_A(t)+\nu_B(t)=
\begin{cases}
k+1,&t=\pm r,\\
k-1,&t=\pm s,\\
k,&\text{every other }t\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus an `E=32` pair is an **almost supplementary difference pair with exactly one surplus inverse-pair and one deficit inverse-pair**.

This is stronger structurally than merely saying that the defect vector has one `+4` and one `-4`: it identifies the defect with an exact error in the cyclic difference multiset.

## 4. Group-ring normal form and exact repair equation

Let `g` denote a generator of the integral group ring `Z[G]`, and identify a subset with its indicator sum, e.g.

\[
A=\sum_{x\in A}g^x.
\]

Write `A^{(-1)}=\sum_{x\in A}g^{-x}` and similarly for `B`. For a true Legendre pair the support sets satisfy

\[
AA^{(-1)}+BB^{(-1)}=k(G+e),
\]

where `G=sum_{x in Z/vZ}g^x` and `e=g^0`.

For an `E=32` state, Corollary 3.2 gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
AA^{(-1)}+BB^{(-1)}
=k(G+e)
+g^r+g^{-r}-g^s-g^{-s}
}
\]

after orienting `r` as the surplus and `s` as the deficit.

This is the algebraic state that a repair move must annihilate.

### Proposition 4.1 — exact one-exchange trade identity

Suppose `x in A`, `y notin A`, put `C=A\setminus\{x\}`, and replace `x` by `y`:

\[
A'=C\cup\{y\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
A'A'^{(-1)}-AA^{(-1)}
=(g^y-g^x)C^{(-1)}+C(g^{-y}-g^{-x}).
}
\]

**Proof.** Expand

\[
A'A'^{(-1)}=(C+g^y)(C^{(-1)}+g^{-y}),
\]

\[
AA^{(-1)}=(C+g^x)(C^{(-1)}+g^{-x}).
\]

The `CC^{(-1)}` and identity terms cancel, leaving exactly the stated expression. `□`

If `u in B` is replaced by `w notin B`, the total correction is the sum of the two analogous trade expressions. Hence an `E=32` pair repairs to a Legendre pair in one simultaneous balanced-exchange round **if and only if** those two local trade corrections sum to

\[
-g^r-g^{-r}+g^s+g^{-s}.
\]

This is a finite, exact multiset equation; it contains no numerical tolerance and no optimization heuristic.

### Coordinate form

For a single trade `x -> y` in `A`, the change in the ordered difference multiplicity at nonzero `t` is

\[
\Delta\nu_A(t)
=1_C(y-t)+1_C(y+t)-1_C(x-t)-1_C(x+t).
\]

The simultaneous two-sequence repair condition is therefore an explicit system of `v-1` equations with right side supported only at `±r,±s`.

This gives a natural route to a theorem: prove that every cyclic almost-SDS of the special Legendre parameters admits a bounded-volume trade satisfying this equation.

## 5. Exact plateau classification through odd length 19

The previous checkpoint showed one length-15 minimum-energy state with no strict decrease in its immediate balanced-exchange neighborhood but with a path `32 -> 32 -> 0`. This run asks a stronger question:

> Starting from **any** normalized binary pair with `E=32`, can one remain on the minimum-positive plateau `E=32` for a bounded number of balanced-exchange rounds and then reach `E=0`?

Here one round allows each sequence independently either to stay fixed or exchange one `+1` position with one `-1` position.

The exact enumerator developed in this run works only with integer bitmasks, exact popcounts, and exact difference multiplicities.

### 5.1 State quotient used for exhaustive checking

For a balanced support `A`, define its half-difference vector

\[
N(A)=(\nu_A(1),\ldots,\nu_A((v-1)/2)).
\]

and its one-round **reach signature**

\[
R(A)=\{N(A'): A' \text{ is obtained from }A\text{ by at most one balanced exchange}\}.
\]

For each of `v=15,17,19`, exhaustive enumeration verified two finite facts:

1. equal reach signatures imply equal current `N(A)`;
2. every two supports with the same reach signature have exactly the same set of reach-signature types among their one-round neighbors.

These are *verified finite facts at these lengths*, not claimed as a theorem for arbitrary `v`. They allow exact quotienting of the finite search without losing any state transition.

### Still open

The universal statement “every odd length has a binary Legendre pair” remains open and is stronger than the Hadamard conjecture. Even a proof that every `E=32` state can be repaired would not by itself solve it: one would also need a theorem taking an arbitrary balanced pair of energy `E>32` down to the minimum-positive layer without getting trapped, or an independent way to produce an `E=32` state for every odd length.

Likewise, binary Legendre pairs are only one structured sufficient construction for Hadamard matrices. Failure of this route at any length would not refute Hadamard.

## 8. Concrete obstruction learned this run

The previous checkpoint suggested using the separation of the two defective shifts as a secondary plateau potential. The length-15/17/19 classification shows this is too naive. There are hard `E=32` states whose surplus and deficit shifts are already adjacent in the inversion quotient, yet they still require plateau motion. Thus **defect separation alone cannot be a universal strictly decreasing secondary potential**.

The repair identity instead points to a richer invariant: the *trade availability profile* of the two supports, i.e. which difference-vector corrections can be generated by one balanced exchange in each block.

## 9. Next attack

The most concrete next theorem is now:

> **Bounded trade conjecture for minimum defect.** There exists an absolute `R` such that every normalized binary `E=32` pair at every odd length can be transformed to a Legendre pair by at most `R` rounds, each round exchanging at most one `+` and one `-` in each sequence, while never increasing `E` above 32.

The exact data give `R<=1` through `v=13`, `R<=2` at `v=15,17`, and `R<=3` at `v=19` when the final `32->0` repair round is counted.

A proof should use the group-ring trade identity, not merely an energy argument. The likely combinatorial object is an alternating trade graph whose vertices are support positions and whose edges record the surplus/deficit difference incidences. The `E=32` group-ring equation says every difference is balanced except four directed classes; this extreme regularity may force a short alternating cycle that realizes the needed trade.

In parallel, the same trade formalism should be derived for four-block Goethals–Seidel difference families. That route is less restrictive than binary Legendre pairs and may survive even if a universal binary-Legendre theorem fails.
