# Setup recalled from the previous checkpoint

Let `T` have `m` edges and let

`f:V(T)->{0,...,m}`

be graceful. Fix a root `r` and an insertion threshold

`L in {0,...,m+1}`.

For each difference `d in {1,...,m}`, let `e_d` be the unique edge of difference `d`, and define `c_d(L)=1` iff the endpoints of `e_d` lie on opposite sides of the cut

`{f<L} | {f>=L}`.

The previous checkpoint proved that gap insertion at `L` keeps the old edge-differences pairwise distinct iff

`c_1<=c_2<=...<=c_m`.

Thus there is a cutoff `k in {0,...,m}` with

`c_d=0` for `d<=k`, `c_d=1` for `d>k`.

The unique missing difference after inserting one gap is `k+1`. Attaching a new leaf at `r` with label `L` completes the graceful labeling iff either

* **low-root branch:** `f(r)=L-k-1<L`, or
* **high-root branch:** `f(r)=L+k>=L`.

I will call `(f,L,k,r)` satisfying these conditions a **one-gap certificate**.

# New result 1: pendant-star amplification theorem

## Theorem

Let `(f,L,k,r)` be a one-gap certificate on a tree `T` with `m` edges. For every integer `t>=0`, form `T_t` from `T` by adjoining `t` new leaves

`z_1,...,z_t`

all adjacent to the same root `r`.

Then `T_t` is graceful. More strongly, there is an explicit labeling requiring no search, and the resulting rooted tree `(T_t,r)` again carries a one-gap certificate at the **same threshold `L`**.

### Common labeling rule

For every old vertex `v`, put

`F_t(v)=f(v)` if `f(v)<L`,

`F_t(v)=f(v)+t` if `f(v)>=L`.

Give the new leaves the consecutive labels

`F_t(z_j)=L+j-1`, `1<=j<=t`.

This is visibly a bijection onto `{0,...,m+t}`: the old low labels remain below `L`, the new leaves fill `L,...,L+t-1`, and the old high labels shift to `L+t,...,m+t`.

## Low-root branch

Assume

`f(r)=L-k-1`.

Then the root is below the cut and is not shifted. The old edge-differences split as follows.

* Old noncrossing edges have differences `1,...,k` and remain unchanged.
* Old crossing edges have original differences `k+1,...,m`; shifting their high endpoints by `t` changes these to

  `k+t+1,...,m+t`.
* The `t` new leaf edges have differences

  `|F_t(r)-F_t(z_j)| = |(L-k-1)-(L+j-1)| = k+j`,

  namely exactly

  `k+1,...,k+t`.

Hence the complete edge-difference set is

`{1,...,k} union {k+1,...,k+t} union {k+t+1,...,m+t}`

`={1,...,m+t}`.

So `F_t` is graceful.

Moreover, with the same threshold `L`, every new leaf edge crosses the cut, as do the shifted old crossing edges, while old differences `1,...,k` remain noncrossing. Thus the new crossing vector is still

`0^k 1^(m+t-k)`.

The root still has label

`F_t(r)=L-k-1`,

so `(F_t,L,k,r)` is again a one-gap certificate.

### Consequence

In the low-root branch, the cutoff `k` is invariant under an arbitrary number of same-root leaf attachments.

## High-root branch

Assume instead

`f(r)=L+k`.

The root shifts to

`F_t(r)=L+k+t`.

Again old differences `1,...,k` remain unchanged and old crossing differences become

`k+t+1,...,m+t`.

But now the new leaves and the root are all on the high side of the threshold. Their edge-differences are

`F_t(r)-F_t(z_j)`

`=(L+k+t)-(L+j-1)`

`=k+t-j+1`,

so as `j=1,...,t` they fill exactly

`k+1,...,k+t`.

Therefore `F_t` is graceful for the same reason as above.

Relative to threshold `L`, the new leaf edges are **noncrossing**. Thus the noncrossing difference block has grown from `1,...,k` to

`1,...,k+t`,

while the old crossing edges begin at `k+t+1`. The new cutoff is therefore

`k_t=k+t`.

Finally

`F_t(r)=L+k+t=L+k_t`,

so `(F_t,L,k+t,r)` is again a one-gap certificate.

### Consequence

In the high-root branch, each same-root leaf attachment increments the cutoff by one.

# Why this matters for the one-gap program

The external order-22 computation only checks that *some* one-step certificate exists for every rooted tree of those sizes. The theorem above gives an exact infinite closure operation: once one certificate is available at a root, no re-search is needed to attach an arbitrary pendant star there.

It also clarifies the MathWorld warning about repeated extension. A labeling obtained from one gap extension need not support an arbitrary **new prescribed root** on the next step. But it always supports another extension at the **same old root**, by the theorem above.

This is the first exact composition rule obtained in this campaign from the suffix-cut criterion. It is still far too narrow to prove the universal conjecture, because a general leaf-pruning induction changes the distinguished root from step to step.

# New result 2: `k=0` boundary certificates propagate along paths

The opposite useful behavior occurs when the root is allowed to move to the newly attached leaf.

## Proposition

Suppose `(f,L,0,r)` is a one-gap certificate. Attach one new leaf `z` at `r` by the usual gap insertion. Then the resulting graceful labeling admits a `k=0` one-gap certificate **rooted at the new leaf `z`**.

Consequently one may attach an arbitrarily long path at `r`, one vertex at a time, always moving the designated root to the newest endpoint and maintaining a `k=0` certificate.

## Proof

When `k=0`, every old edge crosses the threshold `L`. Equivalently, `f` is an alpha-labeling with alpha boundary `L-1`, and the distinguished root has one of the two boundary labels:

* low branch: `f(r)=L-1`, or
* high branch: `f(r)=L`.

### Low branch

Gap insertion at `L` shifts the high side by one, so every old difference `d` becomes `d+1`. The new leaf receives label `L`, while the old root remains `L-1`; hence the new edge has difference `1`.

Relative to the **same threshold `L`**, every old edge still crosses and the new edge also crosses. Therefore the new crossing vector is again all ones, i.e. cutoff zero. The new root `z` has label `L`, exactly the high boundary label. So `(new labeling, L, 0, z)` is a certificate.

### High branch

The old root shifts from `L` to `L+1` and the new leaf has label `L`, again giving new difference `1`; every old difference increases by one.

Now use threshold `L+1`. The old low side remains below `L+1`, every old high vertex was shifted to at least `L+1`, and the new edge joins labels `L` and `L+1`. Hence every edge crosses the new threshold. The new root `z` has label `L=(L+1)-1`, the low boundary label. Thus `(new labeling, L+1, 0, z)` is a certificate.

The two cases alternate under iteration, proving arbitrary path extension. QED.

## Interpretation

The suffix-cut calculus therefore has two complementary exact closure modes:

* **arbitrary `k`:** grow a pendant star while keeping the same root;
* **`k=0`:** grow a path while moving the root along the new path.

The `k=0` case is exactly the alpha-boundary situation, providing a direct bridge between the new gap language and classical alpha-labeling composition results.

# New exact example: an interior-gap root amplifies to an infinite non-caterpillar family

Consider the 9-vertex tree `B` with edges

`(0,1), (0,4), (0,7), (1,2), (2,3), (4,5), (4,6), (7,8)`

and designated root

`r=3`.

The displayed labeling is

* `f(0)=0`,
* `f(4)=1`,
* `f(8)=2`,
* `f(2)=3`,
* `f(5)=4`,
* `f(3)=5`,
* `f(6)=6`,
* `f(1)=7`,
* `f(7)=8`.

Its edge-differences are exactly `1,...,8`.

Take

`L=4`, `k=1`.

By increasing difference, the crossing vector is

`(0,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)`.

The root has

`f(3)=5=L+k`,

so this is the **high-root interior-gap branch**.

## Exact endpoint obstruction

The artifact for this run exhaustively enumerates **all 248 graceful labelings** of this fixed labeled tree. Across all of them, the designated root `3` attains exactly the values

`{1,2,3,4,5,6,7}`

and never `0` or `8`.

Thus standard endpoint/0-rotatable leaf extension is unavailable at this root, while the interior-gap certificate works.

## Closed-form infinite family

For each `t>=0`, attach `t` new leaves `w_0,...,w_(t-1)` to root `3`. By the high-branch amplification theorem, a graceful labeling is obtained by

* keeping the old labels `0,1,2,3` fixed on vertices `0,4,8,2`;
* assigning the new leaves labels `4,5,...,t+3`;
* shifting the old high labels by `t`:
  * `f_t(5)=t+4`,
  * `f_t(3)=t+5`,
  * `f_t(6)=t+6`,
  * `f_t(1)=t+7`,
  * `f_t(7)=t+8`.

The differences split exactly as

* old noncrossing difference: `{1}`;
* new root-leaf differences: `{2,...,t+1}`;
* shifted old crossing differences: `{t+2,...,t+8}`.

Hence every member is graceful.

For `t>=1` the family is non-caterpillar, and its diameter is 6. Therefore this example is not explained merely by the blanket facts that caterpillars are graceful or that all diameter-at-most-5 trees are graceful. I make **no literature-novelty claim** for the family itself; its role here is to demonstrate that interior-gap amplification operates beyond the endpoint mechanism on a structurally non-caterpillar family.

# Interaction with the LPW exactness bottleneck

Letzter–Pokrovskiy–Williams already split a general tree into controlled pieces and embed most of it rainbowly. The missing exact theorem is not merely “find a gap”; the previous checkpoint showed it is a very rigid suffix-cut requirement.

The present amplification theorem identifies one circumstance where the composition problem is completely solved: a whole pendant star may be absorbed into the gap interval with no new global search. In the high-root branch the new leaves enlarge the internal/noncrossing initial block of colors; in the low-root branch they enlarge the crossing suffix.

This suggests refining the LPW decomposition so that a large proportion of the discarded/absorber vertices appear as **leaf bundles attached at a small set of separator roots**. A suffix-cut certificate on the core would then absorb those bundles exactly via the theorem above. What remains difficult is handling vertices that form paths or deeper rooted subtrees. The `k=0` path-propagation lemma handles one extreme of that problem, but arbitrary `k` does not yet permit the root to move.

# Failed / limited directions clarified this run

1. **Repeated one-gap extension is not universally self-propagating to arbitrary next roots.** MathWorld explicitly records failure of the stronger prescribed-second-root property at order 6. The new closure theorem is deliberately same-root only.
2. **The pendant-star theorem alone cannot prove the conjecture.** A general tree cannot be reconstructed from a one-gap-certified core using only leaves at one fixed root.
3. **`k=0` path propagation is too restrictive by itself.** It requires an alpha-type cut in which every old edge crosses the threshold. The small exact examples from the previous run needing interior `k>0` show that the general one-gap phenomenon is strictly broader.
4. **No claim is made that the explicit amplified family is a new graceful class in the literature.** The useful result is the general certificate-preserving operation and its exact formulas.

# Next concrete attack

The highest-value next step is to extend the composition calculus from depth 1 to depth 2:

> **Depth-two suffix-cut composition problem.** Given a one-gap certificate `(f,L,k,r)`, characterize rooted trees `S` of depth two that can be attached at `r` while preserving a suffix-cut certificate after relabeling by finitely many contiguous label blocks.

A first target is a **broom lemma**: attach a path edge `r-u` and then an arbitrary leaf bundle at `u`. The `k=0` path-propagation theorem handles this completely; the open task is to determine the exact extra arithmetic/cut condition for general `k`.

If such a depth-two operation can be iterated, it would begin to cover the rooted components produced by tree-splitting arguments rather than only pendant stars. Computationally, the next useful search is therefore not another raw order sweep but a classification of certificate transitions under one root move: from a certificate `(L,k,r)`, record which thresholds/cutoffs are available when the newly added leaf becomes the next root. The goal is to discover a finite transition rule generalizing the clean `k=0` alternation above.
