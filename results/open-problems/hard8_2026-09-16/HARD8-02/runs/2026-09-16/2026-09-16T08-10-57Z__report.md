**OPEN.** No universal proof or certified counterexample is obtained here.

This checkpoint advances the exact one-gap program from the prior report by proving four related facts.

1. **Multi-gap-block formula.** If a one-gap cut has cutoff `k` and we insert `s` consecutive new labels at that cut, then the old edges use exactly
   `1,...,k` and `k+s+1,...,m+s`; the missing colours are the *entire interval* `k+1,...,k+s`.
2. **Sharp component obstruction.** If those `s` labels are occupied by new vertices, and their induced forest has `c` components, then any graceful block insertion must satisfy
   `c >= min(s,k+1)`. In particular, for `s>=2`, a connected pendant subtree can be absorbed by a single consecutive gap only when `k=0`.
3. **Balanced-broom root-transfer theorem.** The obstruction above is sharp. From any one-gap certificate of cutoff `k`, and for any `K>=k`, one can explicitly add a new neighbour `u`, exactly `k` compensating leaves at the old root, and `K-k` leaves at `u`, obtaining a graceful tree carrying a new one-gap certificate of cutoff `K` rooted at `u`. 4. **Exact connected-graft classification at `k=0`.** A connected `s`-vertex pendant tree can be inserted through one consecutive gap exactly when its attachment root has an endpoint graceful labeling. If the inserted tree has a suitable alpha-labeling, the `k=0` certificate propagates to a chosen boundary vertex of the inserted block.

These are exact algebraic statements about the one-gap mechanism. I make no claim here that the composition principles are new in the graceful-labeling literature; the value for this campaign is that they are proved directly in the suffix-cut language and identify both a sharp obstruction and an optimal root-moving construction.

# 1. Recalled one-gap certificate

Let `T` be a tree with `m` edges and let

`f:V(T)->{0,...,m}`

be graceful. Fix a threshold

`L in {0,...,m+1}`.

For the unique old edge of difference `d`, write `c_d(L)=1` if its endpoints lie on opposite sides of

`{f<L} | {f>=L}`,

and `c_d(L)=0` otherwise.

The first checkpoint proved that inserting one empty label at `L` preserves pairwise distinct old edge differences if and only if

`c_1<=c_2<=...<=c_m`.

Thus there is a cutoff `k in {0,...,m}` for which

`c_d=0` for `d<=k`,

`c_d=1` for `d>k`.

The root `r` permits attachment of the new leaf exactly when either

* **low branch:** `f(r)=L-k-1`, or
* **high branch:** `f(r)=L+k`.

Such a quadruple `(f,L,k,r)` is called a **one-gap certificate** in these reports.

# 2. Multi-label block insertion

The one-label calculation has a useful exact `s`-label version.

## Lemma 2.1 — block-insertion spectrum

Assume the cut at `L` has crossing vector `0^k 1^(m-k)`. Fix `s>=1`, and shift every old label on the high side upward by `s`:

`F_s(v)=f(v)` if `f(v)<L`,

`F_s(v)=f(v)+s` if `f(v)>=L`.

The unused labels are the consecutive block

`I={L,L+1,...,L+s-1}`.

Then the old edge differences are exactly

`{1,...,k} union {k+s+1,...,m+s}`.

Equivalently, the missing edge-colour block is exactly

`B_{k,s}={k+1,k+2,...,k+s}`.

### Proof

The unique old edge of difference `d` has both endpoints shifted together or neither shifted when `d<=k`, so its difference stays `d`. For `d>k` the edge crosses the threshold, exactly one endpoint is shifted by `s`, and its difference becomes `d+s`. Hence the old differences are

`1,...,k,k+s+1,...,m+s`,

which leaves precisely `k+1,...,k+s`. QED.

This is the basic accounting identity used below.

# 3. A sharp component obstruction for any single-block absorber

Suppose the `s` empty labels in `I` are filled by a set `U` of `s` new vertices. Keep all old vertices and all old edges of `T`, and add edges involving `U` so that the final graph `T'` is again a tree.

Let

`F=T'[U]`

be the forest induced by the new vertices, and let `c` be its number of connected components.

Because `T` itself is connected and `T'` is a tree, **each component of `F` has exactly one edge to the old tree**. Thus there are exactly `c` old-new attachment edges and exactly `s-c` new-new edges.

## Theorem 3.1 — component lower bound

If the resulting block-inserted labeling is graceful, then

`c >= min(s,k+1)`.

### Proof

Every new-new edge has both labels in the interval `I`, which has span `s-1`. Therefore every new-new edge has difference at most `s-1`.

By Lemma 2.1, all new edges together must realize the missing block

`B_{k,s}={k+1,...,k+s}`.

Every member of `B_{k,s}` which is at least `s` cannot be realized by a new-new edge, and so must be realized by an old-new attachment edge.

The number of integers in `B_{k,s}` that are at least `s` is

`min(s,k+1)`:

* if `k+1<s`, they are `s,s+1,...,s+k`, exactly `k+1` values;
* if `k+1>=s`, all `s` members of `B_{k,s}` are at least `s`.

There are exactly `c` attachment edges, hence

`c >= min(s,k+1)`.

QED.

## Corollary 3.2 — connected pendant graft obstruction

For `s>=2`, if the new vertices induce a **connected** subtree (`c=1`), then a single consecutive-gap block insertion can work only when

`k=0`.

Thus the `k=0` special behavior in the previous report is not an accident of the one-leaf proof: positive cutoff creates a genuine metric obstruction to inserting a deeper connected branch into one consecutive gap.

## Corollary 3.3 — large-cutoff rigidity

If `k>=s-1`, then

`c>=s`.

Since always `c<=s`, one has `c=s`: the induced forest on the new vertices has no edges. In other words, in this regime a single-gap block absorber must consist entirely of separate attachment vertices; no edge can join two newly inserted labels.

This explains why the arbitrary-`k` pendant-star theorem is natural at large cutoff.

# 4. Sharpness: a balanced-broom root-transfer theorem

The component lower bound is exact. More importantly, the extremal construction moves the distinguished root.

## Theorem 4.1 — monotone-cutoff root transfer

Let `(f,L,k,r)` be a one-gap certificate on a tree `T` with `m` edges. Let `K>=k` be arbitrary.

Construct a larger tree by adding:

* one new vertex `u` adjacent to the old root `r`;
* exactly `k` additional new leaves `p_1,...,p_k` adjacent to `r`;
* exactly `K-k` new leaves `q_1,...,q_{K-k}` adjacent to `u`.

Thus exactly

`s=K+1`

new vertices and edges are added.

Then the enlarged tree is graceful. More strongly, it has a one-gap certificate of cutoff `K` rooted at the **new vertex `u`**.

The construction is explicit in both branches.

---

## 4.1 Low-branch proof

Assume the old certificate is low:

`f(r)=L-k-1`.

Insert a block of `s=K+1` labels at `L`: every old label at least `L` is shifted upward by `K+1`.

Assign the new labels as follows:

* `u` gets `L+K`;
* for `j=1,...,K-k`, give `q_j` the label `L+j-1`, so the `q`-labels are
  `L,...,L+K-k-1`;
* for `i=1,...,k`, give `p_i` the label `L+K-k+i-1`, so the `p`-labels are
  `L+K-k,...,L+K-1`.

These labels use the entire inserted block exactly once.

By Lemma 2.1, the old edges use

`1,...,k`

and

`k+K+2,...,m+K+1`.

The new edge differences are:

### Edges from `u` to its `K-k` leaves

For `j=1,...,K-k`,

`|(L+K)-(L+j-1)|=K-j+1`.

These are exactly

`k+1,k+2,...,K`.

### The `k` compensating leaves at `r`

Since `r` still has label `L-k-1`, for `i=1,...,k`,

`|(L+K-k+i-1)-(L-k-1)|=K+i`.

These are exactly

`K+1,...,K+k`.

### The bridge `ru`

Its difference is

`(L+K)-(L-k-1)=K+k+1`.

Therefore the new edges fill exactly

`k+1,...,K+k+1`.

But because `s=K+1`, this is precisely the missing block

`k+1,...,k+s`.

Hence the complete edge-difference set is

`1,...,m+K+1`,

so the new labeling is graceful.

### The new certificate

Use the **same threshold `L`**. The old differences `1,...,k` remain noncrossing. The `u-q_j` edges are entirely on the high side and contribute `k+1,...,K`, also noncrossing. The edges `r-p_i` and `ru` cross the threshold and contribute `K+1,...,K+k+1`. The old crossing edges contribute every larger difference.

Thus the new crossing vector is

`0^K 1^(m+K+1-K)`.

The new root `u` has label

`L+K`,

which is exactly the high-branch root formula for cutoff `K`. Hence

`(F,L,K,u)`

is a one-gap certificate.

---

## 4.2 High-branch proof

Assume instead that the old certificate is high:

`f(r)=L+k`.

After inserting `s=K+1` labels at `L`, the old root shifts to

`L+k+K+1`.

Assign:

* `u` the label `L`;
* `p_i` the labels `L+i`, `i=1,...,k`;
* `q_j` the labels `L+k+j`, `j=1,...,K-k`.

Again the whole inserted block `L,...,L+K` is used exactly once.

The `u-q_j` differences are exactly

`k+1,...,K`.

The `r-p_i` differences are

`(L+k+K+1)-(L+i)=k+K+1-i`,

namely exactly

`K+1,...,K+k`.

The bridge `ru` has difference

`K+k+1`.

Together these fill the missing block `k+1,...,k+K+1`, while the old edges occupy the complement, so the enlarged labeling is graceful.

For the new certificate, use threshold

`L'=L+K+1`.

All inserted labels lie below `L'`; all old labels that were at least `L` were shifted to at least `L'`. Therefore the old crossing/noncrossing pattern is preserved relative to `L'`. The `u-q_j` edges are noncrossing and fill `k+1,...,K`; the edges from the shifted old root to `u,p_i` cross and fill `K+1,...,K+k+1`.

Hence the new crossing vector is again `0^K1^*`.

Finally

`F(u)=L=(L+K+1)-K-1=L'-K-1`,

so `u` is the low-branch boundary root. Thus

`(F,L+K+1,K,u)`

is a one-gap certificate.

QED.

# 5. Optimality and useful corollaries of root transfer

The new vertices in Theorem 4.1 induce exactly `k+1` components:

* one component consists of `u` together with its `K-k` leaves;
* the `k` vertices `p_i` are isolated in the induced new-vertex forest.

Since `s=K+1>=k+1`, Theorem 3.1 requires at least `k+1` components. Therefore the balanced-broom construction is **optimal in the number of new components / old-tree attachment edges** among all single-block insertions with these parameters.

The `k` compensating attachments are not a defect of the proof; the metric lower bound shows that some `k` additional attachment components are unavoidable once one insists on moving through a positive-cutoff single gap.

## Corollary 5.1 — root transfer without changing cutoff

Take `K=k`.

Then there are no `q`-leaves. The construction simply adds exactly `k+1` new leaves at the old root `r`; one of them is designated `u` and the other `k` are compensating siblings.

The enlarged tree has a one-gap certificate with the **same cutoff `k` rooted at `u`**.

Thus every one-gap certificate can be transferred to a newly added leaf. The exact price is `k` additional sibling leaves.

For `k=0`, this reduces to the previous checkpoint's one-leaf path-propagation rule.

## Corollary 5.2 — arbitrary monotone cutoff increase

For any prescribed `K>=k`, the root can be moved to a new neighbour while raising the cutoff from `k` to `K`. The price is

* `k` leaves at the old root, and
* `K-k` leaves at the new root.

This gives an exact state transition

`(root r, cutoff k) -> (root u, cutoff K)`

with `K>=k`.

The branch flips: low becomes high, and high becomes low (with the threshold shifted across the inserted block).

## Lemma 5.3 — complement swaps branches

If `(f,L,k,r)` is a certificate on an `m`-edge tree, define

`f^c(v)=m-f(v)`,

`L^c=m+1-L`.

Then `(f^c,L^c,k,r)` is again a one-gap certificate with the same cutoff and the opposite branch.

Indeed complementing reverses the two sides of the cut without changing edge differences; and

`f(r)=L-k-1`

becomes

`f^c(r)=m-L+k+1=L^c+k`,

while the converse calculation reverses high to low.

This means branch orientation is not an essential obstruction in recursive uses of Theorem 4.1.

# 6. Exact classification of connected single-gap grafts

The component lower bound completely settles the consecutive-gap mechanism when the inserted vertices form one connected pendant subtree.

Let `H` be a tree on `s` new vertices, attached to the old tree by a single bridge from the old root `r` to a designated attachment vertex `a in V(H)`. Put all vertices of `H` into the consecutive inserted label block `I`.

Assume `s>=2`.

## Theorem 6.1 — connected-graft classification

Such a block insertion is graceful **if and only if** all of the following hold:

1. the base certificate has `k=0`;
2. `H` has a graceful labeling
   `h:V(H)->{0,...,s-1}`;
3. the attachment vertex `a` receives the endpoint label opposite the old root:
   * in the low branch, `h(a)=s-1`;
   * in the high branch, `h(a)=0`.

### Necessity

By Corollary 3.2, connectedness forces `k=0`.

Then the missing block is exactly

`{1,...,s}`.

All `s-1` internal edges of `H` have both labels inside an interval of span `s-1`, so none can have difference `s`. Therefore the bridge must have difference `s`, and the internal edges must realize exactly `1,...,s-1`.

After subtracting `L` from the inserted labels, the labeling of `H` is therefore graceful.

In the low branch the old root has label `L-1`, and the unique inserted label at distance `s` is `L+s-1`, forcing `h(a)=s-1`.

In the high branch the shifted old root has label `L+s`, and the unique inserted label at distance `s` is `L`, forcing `h(a)=0`.

### Sufficiency

Conversely, use the given graceful labeling of `H`, translated by `L`. Its internal edges realize `1,...,s-1`, the bridge realizes `s`, and every old edge (all of which crossed the original `k=0` threshold) has its difference increased by `s`, giving `s+1,...,m+s`. Thus every difference `1,...,m+s` occurs exactly once.

QED.

This theorem precisely characterizes why `k=0` is the connected-composition regime for a single consecutive gap.

# 7. Reusable `k=0` blocks via alpha-labelings

The preceding classification gives gracefulness but not automatically a reusable certificate at a new vertex. An alpha-labeling supplies exactly that extra structure.

## Proposition 7.1 — two-terminal alpha-block propagation

Start from a `k=0` one-gap certificate `(f,L,0,r)`.

Let `H` be an `s`-vertex tree with distinguished **input** vertex `a` and **output** vertex `b`. Suppose `H` has an alpha-labeling

`h:V(H)->{0,...,s-1}`

with alpha threshold `A`, meaning every edge of `H` joins a label at most `A` to a label at least `A+1`.

Assume:

* the input is at an endpoint, `h(a)=0` or `s-1`, chosen to be opposite the old root branch as in Theorem 6.1;
* the output lies on the alpha boundary,
  `h(b) in {A,A+1}`.

Then grafting `H` into the consecutive gap produces a graceful tree which again carries a `k=0` certificate, now rooted at `b`.

### Proof

Translate the labels of `H` by `L` and use Theorem 6.1 to obtain gracefulness.

Use the combined threshold

`L+A+1`.

Every old base edge crosses this threshold because, before insertion, every old edge crossed `L`; after insertion all old low endpoints are below `L`, while all old high endpoints are at least `L+s`.

Every internal edge of `H` crosses `L+A+1` by the alpha property. The bridge crosses because its old endpoint lies outside the inserted block and its new endpoint is the extreme inserted label on the opposite side.

Hence **every edge of the enlarged tree crosses one common threshold**: the new cutoff is again zero.

Finally the output label is either

`L+A`

or

`L+A+1`,

which is exactly one of the two `k=0` boundary-root positions. Therefore the certificate propagates to `b`.

QED.

### Relation to the previous path result

A path with its standard alpha-labeling and suitable endpoint/boundary choices is one such two-terminal block, so the earlier `k=0` path-propagation proposition is a special case.

A star also supplies a simple nontrivial block: label its centre by an extreme and choose a leaf on the alpha boundary as the output. More complicated alpha-labeled rooted trees can therefore be chained in the same way whenever the two terminal conditions are met.

This is close in spirit to classical alpha-labeling composition results (for example, Huang–Kotzig–Rosa style constructions); no literature-novelty claim is made here. The point is that the one-gap calculus makes the exact interface conditions transparent.

# 8. Why the new obstruction and construction matter for the LPW route

Letzter–Pokrovskiy–Williams split a large tree so that, after removing a small exceptional set, many copies of a rooted forest remain; their Lemma 4.1 arranges that each rooted component has at most one neighbour in the high-degree core.

Theorem 3.1 shows that **many components are not merely tolerable but necessary** for a positive-cutoff consecutive-gap absorber. At cutoff `k`, a large enough gap block requires at least `k+1` components to supply the colours which are too large to occur between two labels inside the block.

This suggests a sharper exact bridge problem than the earlier vague phrase “absorb a forest”:

> Given a one-gap core certificate of cutoff `k`, place a rooted forest into the missing interval `k+1,...,k+s` so that at least `k+1` of its components supply the large attachment colours, while its internal edges fill the remaining colours exactly once.

The balanced broom of Theorem 4.1 solves this interval-packing problem in the extremal simplest case and proves that the component count lower bound is attainable.

For a forest whose components all attach to the same low-branch root, an exact formulation is especially clean. If the inserted labels are represented by offsets `0,...,s-1` and component roots have offsets `a_1,...,a_c`, then the attachment colours are

`k+1+a_i`,

while internal forest colours are ordinary offset differences. The absorber condition is the disjoint partition

`D_internal  disjoint_union  {k+1+a_i : 1<=i<=c}
 = {k+1,...,k+s}`.

In the high branch the attachment term is reflected to

`k+s-a_i`.

A useful next theorem would be an interval-packing result for many isomorphic rooted components of the kind produced by the LPW splitting lemma.

# 9. Failed route converted into an exact theorem

The previous checkpoint proposed a “depth-two broom” as the next test: add a new vertex `u` at the root and then attach leaves at `u`, hoping to move the certificate through `u` for general `k`.

Theorem 3.1 now shows why the naive version cannot work inside one consecutive gap. If `u` and all of its new descendants form one connected inserted component and at least two new vertices are inserted, then `c=1`; positive `k` is impossible.

Theorem 4.1 gives the sharp repair: add exactly `k` additional attachment components. Taking them to be direct leaves at the old root yields a balanced broom, and then root transfer works in closed form.

Thus this run replaces an open subquestion with a precise obstruction-plus-optimal-construction pair.

# 11. Next attack

The most promising immediate target is no longer a connected broom. That case is now understood within the single-gap mechanism.

The next exact target should be a **rooted-forest interval-packing theorem**. Start with `k+1` or more rooted components, ideally many identical copies of a small rooted tree as in LPW Lemma 4.1, and seek label placements in `0,...,s-1` satisfying

`internal differences + attachment differences = {k+1,...,k+s}`

without overlap.

Two concrete subtargets are:

1. **Star-of-blocks theorem.** Replace the `k` isolated compensating leaves in Theorem 4.1 by small endpoint-graceful or alpha-labeled rooted blocks while retaining a closed-form interval partition.
2. **Repeated-component packing.** For a fixed rooted tree `H`, determine whether sufficiently many disjoint copies of `H` admit a translated/reflected packing whose internal difference sets tile an interval and whose roots occupy the complementary attachment offsets.

A theorem of the second form would line up directly with the repeated-forest structure in LPW. Any failure should be recorded as an exact arithmetic obstruction, not a search timeout.
