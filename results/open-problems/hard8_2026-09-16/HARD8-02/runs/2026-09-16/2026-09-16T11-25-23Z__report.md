## 2. Exact `P_3` absorber setup

Let `F_c` be the disjoint union of `c` copies of an endpoint-rooted path

`r_i -- m_i -- l_i`,

with root `r_i`.

A suffix-root cutoff-`k` absorber is a bijection

`lambda:V(F_c)->{0,...,3c-1}`

such that

1. the roots receive exactly the suffix block

   `{2c,...,3c-1}`;

2. the `2c` internal edge differences are exactly

   `I_{c,k}={k+1,...,k+2c}`.

The second condition is equivalent to the full absorber equation because the attachment differences are then automatically

`{k+1+lambda(r_i)}={k+2c+1,...,k+3c}`.

Since every root label is at least `2c` and every non-root label is at most `2c-1`, every root-middle difference is simply

`lambda(r_i)-lambda(m_i)`

without an absolute-value ambiguity.

# 3. Exact deficit identity

For each copy write

`a_i=min(lambda(m_i),lambda(l_i))`,

`b_i=max(lambda(m_i),lambda(l_i))`.

Let

`epsilon_i=1`

if the middle `m_i` carries the larger value `b_i`, and `epsilon_i=0` otherwise. Define

`E = sum_i a_i - c(c-1)/2`.

Because the `a_i` are `c` distinct nonnegative integers,

`E>=0`.

## Theorem 3.1 — exact `P_3` absorber defect budget

Every suffix-root cutoff-`k` absorber on `c` endpoint-rooted copies of `P_3` satisfies

`c(c-2k-1) = 3E + sum_i epsilon_i (b_i-a_i).`      (3.1)

### Proof

The target sum of all `2c` internal edge differences is

`sum_{d=k+1}^{k+2c} d = c(2k+2c+1).`              (3.2)

The sum of the root labels is fixed:

`sum_i lambda(r_i) = sum_{j=2c}^{3c-1} j = c(5c-1)/2.`   (3.3)

For one path, the contribution of its two internal edges is

`lambda(r_i)-lambda(m_i)+|lambda(m_i)-lambda(l_i)|`.

After removing the root term, define

`f_i=-lambda(m_i)+|lambda(m_i)-lambda(l_i)|`.

If the middle is the smaller endpoint (`epsilon_i=0`), then

`f_i=-a_i+(b_i-a_i)=b_i-2a_i`.

If the middle is the larger endpoint (`epsilon_i=1`), then

`f_i=-b_i+(b_i-a_i)=-a_i`

`=(b_i-2a_i)-(b_i-a_i)`.

Thus in both cases

`f_i=b_i-2a_i-epsilon_i(b_i-a_i).`                 (3.4)

All non-root labels are exactly `0,...,2c-1`, whose sum is

`T=c(2c-1)`.

Therefore `sum_i b_i=T-sum_i a_i`, and summing (3.4) gives

`sum_i f_i = T-3 sum_i a_i - sum_i epsilon_i(b_i-a_i).`  (3.5)

Substitute

`sum_i a_i = c(c-1)/2 + E`

into (3.3)+(3.5). The total internal-difference sum becomes

`3c^2 - 3E - sum_i epsilon_i(b_i-a_i).`             (3.6)

Equating (3.2) and (3.6) yields exactly (3.1). QED.

# 4. Minimum multiplicity is exactly `2k+1`

The right side of (3.1) is nonnegative. Hence

`c(c-2k-1)>=0`.

Since `c>0`, every suffix-root cutoff-`k` absorber of repeated endpoint-rooted `P_3` satisfies

`boxed: c>=2k+1.`                                    (4.1)

The preceding checkpoint already gave an explicit absorber at equality `c=2k+1` by the tensor construction. Therefore:

## Corollary 4.1 — exact minimum multiplicity for `P_3`

For every `k>=0`, the minimum number of endpoint-rooted `P_3` copies that can form a suffix-root cutoff-`k` absorber is exactly

`boxed: 2k+1.`

This is stronger than the previous tensor-ansatz obstruction: the lower bound holds for **every possible labeling**, with arbitrary interleaving of the two path-edge types.

# 5. Rigidity at the minimum

Set `c=2k+1`. Then the left side of (3.1) vanishes. Every term on the right is nonnegative, so necessarily

`E=0`

and

`epsilon_i=0` for every `i`.

Since the `a_i` are distinct nonnegative integers and their sum is the minimum possible

`0+1+...+(c-1)`,

we have

`{a_i}={0,...,c-1}`.

Every one of these low labels is already used as a minimum, so none can occur as a maximum in another pair. Consequently

`{b_i}={c,...,2c-1}`.

And because every `epsilon_i=0`, the middle is always the low endpoint.

Thus:

## Corollary 5.1 — forced low/high geometry at equality

Every minimum-multiplicity absorber (`c=2k+1`) has

* middle labels exactly `{0,...,c-1}`;
* leaf labels exactly `{c,...,2c-1}`;
* `lambda(m_i)<lambda(l_i)` on every path.

This is not an ansatz; it is forced by the difference sum.

### Two-permutation normal form

Index paths by their middle label `i in {0,...,c-1}`. There are permutations `pi,rho` of `{0,...,c-1}` such that

`lambda(l_i)=c+pi(i)`,

`lambda(r_i)=2c+rho(i)`.

The internal differences are therefore

`c+pi(i)-i`

and

`2c+rho(i)-i`.

Hence a minimum absorber is exactly a pair of permutations satisfying

`{c+pi(i)-i : 0<=i<c} union {2c+rho(i)-i : 0<=i<c}`

`={k+1,...,k+2c}`

as a disjoint multiset union.

Conversely any such pair of permutations gives a minimum absorber.

The tensor construction is the diagonal special case `pi=rho=mu`, where for odd `c=2k+1`, `mu(i)` is multiplication by `2^{-1}` modulo `c` (represented in `0,...,c-1`). Its integer displacements are exactly `{-k,...,k}` and recover the two consecutive edge blocks from the preceding report.

So the previous tensor labeling is now located inside a complete normal form for all equality cases.

# 6. Larger multiplicities really do occur

The equality rigidity does **not** extend to an iff theorem `c=2k+1` for `P_3`.

## Example 6.1 — cutoff `k=0`, three copies

Take roots `6,7,8` and triples `(root,middle,leaf)`

`(6,0,4)`,

`(7,2,1)`,

`(8,5,3)`.

The six internal differences are

`6,4,5,1,3,2`,

hence exactly `{1,2,3,4,5,6}`.

The roots are the required suffix `{6,7,8}`. Thus `c=3` is a suffix-root cutoff-zero absorber even though the tensor minimum is `2k+1=1`.

## Example 6.2 — positive cutoff, one copy above the minimum

For `(c,k)=(4,1)`, take

`(8,2,4)`,

`(9,0,7)`,

`(10,5,1)`,

`(11,3,6)`.

The internal differences are

`6,2,9,7,5,4,8,3`,

exactly `{2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9}`.

So `c=4` works although `2k+1=3`.

These examples are important for the LPW-style repeated-forest program: the odd tensor count is a **minimum**, not a hard equality constraint once the motif has depth two.

# 7. The exceptional pair `(c,k)=(2,0)` is impossible

The lower bound `c>=2k+1` permits `(2,0)`, but no absorber exists.

From (3.1),

`2 = 3E + sum_i epsilon_i(b_i-a_i)`.

Hence `E=0` and the total reversed-pair difference equals `2`.

As in the equality argument, `E=0` forces the two non-root pairs to pair the low labels `{0,1}` with the high labels `{2,3}`.

A cross-half pair of difference `1` can only be `{1,2}`, so two distinct reversed pairs of difference `1` are impossible. Therefore there must be exactly one reversed pair of difference `2`.

The only cross-half difference-`2` pairs are `{0,2}` and `{1,3}`. Whichever one is used, the remaining two labels form the other difference-`2` pair. Consequently the two leaf edges have the same difference `2`, contradicting the required distinct spectrum `{1,2,3,4}`.

Thus `(c,k)=(2,0)` is rigorously impossible, without relying on a search timeout.

# 8. Exact relation to Langford pairings for the naive split spectrum

A particularly natural stronger architecture would force

* all leaf-edge differences to be `{k+1,...,k+c}`;
* all root-middle differences to be `{k+c+1,...,k+2c}`.

If the first condition holds, then the `2c` non-root labels `0,...,2c-1` are partitioned into `c` pairs whose differences are exactly

`k+1,k+2,...,k+c`.

After shifting positions by one, this is precisely a classical Langford sequence of order

`t=c`

and defect

`d=k+1`.

Therefore the exact Langford existence theorem gives a **necessary condition for this split-by-edge-type architecture**:

`c>=2k+1`, and

* if `k` is even (`d=k+1` odd), then `c≡0 or 1 (mod 4)`;
* if `k` is odd (`d` even), then `c≡0 or 3 (mod 4)`.

The full absorber problem is less rigid because root and leaf edge colors may interleave. Example 6.1 has `(c,k)=(3,0)`: here `d=1` is odd but `c=3 (mod 4)`, so a split leaf spectrum is impossible by Langford parity, yet the full absorber exists because the leaf edges use differences `{1,2,4}` and the root edges use `{3,5,6}`.

This gives a concrete reason to avoid forcing the two local edge types into separate consecutive global blocks. The interleaving that the previous checkpoint identified as a possible escape is genuinely necessary already in the smallest nontrivial example.

`q=c-(2k+1)`.

It rewrites exactly as

`cq = 3E + sum_{reversed leaf pairs} (b_i-a_i).`

A proof of the `P_3` threshold spectrum would not solve the graceful tree conjecture, but it would remove the first exact multiplicity obstruction encountered by the absorber program.
