# Drifts with linear growth in the running path maximum

Let \(H=L^2(\mathbb T;\mathbb R)\), with the torus of mass one,
\(S_t=e^{t\Delta}\), \(T>0\), and \(h\in H\) deterministic. Suppose
\[
0<\beta<1,\qquad |g(z)-g(y)|\le L|z-y|^\beta,
\qquad |g(z)|\ge c>0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
No upper bound on \(g\) is assumed. Put
\(\mathcal X=C([0,T];H)\) and
\[
m_t(\omega)=\sup_{0\le r\le t}\|\omega_r\|_2.
\]
Let \(B:[0,T]\times\mathcal X\to H\) be jointly Borel and nonanticipating:
\[
B(t,\omega)=B(t,\omega^{t}),\qquad
\|B(t,\omega)\|_2\le K(1+m_t(\omega)),                       \tag{2}
\]
where \(\omega^t_s=\omega_{s\wedge t}\) and \(K<\infty\).

The uniqueness input is explicitly conditional:

**(D)** The equation with zero drift, coefficient \(g\), and initial
state \(h\) has a weak mild solution with continuous \(H\) paths and a
unique state-path law \(\mu_0\) on \(\mathcal X\).

The round-3 proof derives (D) from bounded-start uniqueness. Its external
input is Yi Han, *Weak uniqueness for the stochastic heat equation with
nondegenerate diffusion: the full Hölder regime*,
[arXiv:2611.07310v1, Proposition 8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07310v1).
Han's Section 8.2 transfers drift-free uniqueness to pointwise measurable
linear-growth drifts. Here (D) is assumed; its main uniqueness argument
is not proved anew.

**Theorem.** Under (1), (2), and (D),
\[
U_t=S_th+\int_0^t S_{t-s}B(s,U)\,ds
              +\int_0^t S_{t-s}M_{g(U_s)}\,dW_s             \tag{3}
\]
has a weak mild solution and a unique state-path law \(\mu_B\).
The two laws \(\mu_B,\mu_0\) are equivalent. For a Borel function
\(J_B:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\),
\[
\frac{d\mu_B}{d\mu_0}=\exp(J_B-A_B/2),\qquad
A_B(\omega)=\int_0^T
 \left\|\frac{B(s,\omega)}{g(\omega_s)}\right\|_2^2ds.        \tag{4}
\]
Both relative entropies are finite, with
\[
\operatorname{Ent}(\mu_B\mid\mu_0)=\tfrac12 E_{\mu_B}A_B,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ent}(\mu_0\mid\mu_B)=\tfrac12 E_{\mu_0}A_B.     \tag{5}
\]
In particular, each is at most
\[
c^{-2}K^2T C_2(T)(1+\|h\|_2^2),                             \tag{6}
\]
where a finite explicit \(C_2(T)\) is given in Section 3. The assertion
concerns state laws; no Markov or pathwise uniqueness assertion is made.

## 1. Predictability and pathwise energy

The canonical filtration is the raw filtration generated by evaluations.
For every fixed path, (2) makes \(B(\cdot,\omega)\) a bounded strongly
measurable \(H\)-valued function. Extend it by zero to negative times and set
\[
B_n(t,\omega)=n\int_{(t-1/n)^+}^{t}B(s,\omega)\,ds.           \tag{7}
\]
Measurable integration of its countably many Hilbert coordinates shows
that \(B_n\) is jointly Borel. Its paths are continuous, since indefinite
Bochner integrals are continuous. It is adapted: for \(s\le t\),
nonanticipation gives \(B(s,\omega)=B(s,\omega^t)\), and the stopped-path
map is measurable for the canonical past sigma field. Thus \(B_n\) is
raw predictable.

Define \(\widetilde B\) to be the \(H\)-limit of \(B_n\), and zero where
there is no limit. The Cauchy set is predictable, so this defines a
predictable jointly Borel function. Bochner Lebesgue differentiation gives
\(\widetilde B(t,\omega)=B(t,\omega)\) for almost every time on every
individual path. Moreover, for every \(n,t,\omega\),
\[
\|B_n(t,\omega)\|_2\le K(1+m_t(\omega)).                    \tag{8}
\]
Thus the limit retains (2) everywhere, including the zero values assigned
at nonconvergence points. At time zero it is zero. The modification
changes neither the deterministic convolution nor the energy in (4).
Pulling back raw predictable rectangles along any adapted continuous
process shows that \(\widetilde B(t,U)\) is predictable in its ambient
filtration. Below use this representative and omit the tilde.

There is no dependence here on a null set chosen for a particular law.
The differentiation theorem applies path by path to a bounded Bochner
integrable function. It follows, for example, by continuous-function
approximation and the scalar one-sided maximal inequality applied to
the norm of the approximation error.

Spatial equivalence classes cause no ambiguity. A jointly Borel version
of \(v\in H\) is the finite limit of \(S_{1/n}v(x)\), with zero assigned
where it does not exist. Heat approximation at Lebesgue points agrees
with \(v\) almost everywhere. Applying this map to the predictable
\(B(t,\omega)\) and \(\omega_t\) gives predictable spatial representatives.
In particular
\[
\theta(t,\omega)=B(t,\omega)/g(\omega_t),\quad
q(t,\omega)=B(t,\omega)/g(\omega_t)^2                         \tag{9}
\]
are predictable \(H\)-valued maps. Their Hilbert coordinates are
measurable integrals; ellipticity bounds their norms. Also
\[
g(\omega_t)\theta(t,\omega)=B(t,\omega),\qquad
\|\theta(t,\omega)\|_2^2\le 2c^{-2}K^2(1+m_t(\omega)^2).    \tag{10}
\]
Every continuous \(H\) path has finite \(m_T\), so \(A_B\) is finite
on all of \(\mathcal X\). It is Borel by measurable integration.

## 2. A maximal stochastic convolution estimate in H

For \(k\in H\) and \(r>0\), heat smoothing gives
\[
\|S_rM_k\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=p_{2r}(0)\|k\|_2^2,
\qquad p_{2r}(0)\le 1+d r^{-1/2},\quad d=(8\pi)^{-1/2}.     \tag{11}
\]
The identity follows by integrating \(p_r(x-y)^2\) first in \(x\).
The bound follows from
\(p_{2r}(0)=1+2\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-8\pi^2j^2r}\) and domination of
the decreasing summand by its integral on \([0,\infty)\).
Thus multiplication by \(k\) need not be a bounded operator on \(H\).

One valid terminal Hilbert martingale bound is
\[
E\left\|\int_0^s\Phi_r\,dW_r\right\|_2^8
 \le b_8 E\left(\int_0^s\|\Phi_r\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2dr\right)^4,
\quad b_8=28^4(8/7)^{24}.                                  \tag{12}
\]
Here is a derivation of a permissible constant. Put \(M=\int\Phi dW\)
and \(V=\int\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\). Itô's formula for
\(\|M\|^8\) bounds its drift by \(28\|M\|^6dV\): the two
trace contributions are bounded by \(4+24=28\).
After localization, Holder's inequality and Doob's inequality give
\[
E\|M_s\|^8\le 28(E\sup_{r\le s}\|M_r\|^8)^{3/4}(EV_s^4)^{1/4},
\quad E\sup_{r\le s}\|M_r\|^8\le(8/7)^8E\|M_s\|^8.
\]
Solving the two inequalities gives (12). They first apply to stopped
martingales, for which all moments are finite; Fatou removes the stops.
Finite-dimensional projection followed by the Hilbert isometry gives
the same formulas for Hilbert martingales.

Let \(a_s\) be predictable and \(H\)-valued, with
\(E\int_0^T\|a_s\|_2^8ds<\infty\). Define
\(Z_t=\int_0^tS_{t-r}M_{a_r}dW_r\). Then \(Z\) has a continuous
\(H\)-valued version and, for \(t\le T\),
\[
E\sup_{r\le t}\|Z_r\|_2^8
 \le F(t)\int_0^t E\|a_r\|_2^8dr,                         \tag{13}
\]
where
\[
\eta=3/16,\quad c_\eta=\sin(\pi\eta)/\pi,\qquad
F(t)=b_8 c_\eta^8 14^7\,t\big((8/5)\sqrt t+8d\big)^4.       \tag{14}
\]

To prove this, first take \(a\) bounded in \(H\). Set
\[
Y_s=\int_0^s(s-r)^{-\eta}S_{s-r}M_{a_r}\,dW_r,\qquad
k(v)=v^{-3/8}+d v^{-7/8}.
\]
By (11), (12), and Holder's inequality for integration against
\(k(v)dv\),
\[
E\|Y_s\|_2^8
 \le b_8\left(\int_0^s k(v)dv\right)^3
       \int_0^s k(s-r)E\|a_r\|_2^8dr.                      \tag{15}
\]
In fact \((\int k f^2)^4\le(\int k)^3\int k f^8\) pathwise.
Both exponents of \(k\) are integrable. Write
\[
Q(t)=\int_0^t k(v)dv=(8/5)t^{5/8}+8d t^{1/8}.
\]
Integration of (15) in \(s\), followed by Tonelli, yields
\[
\int_0^tE\|Y_s\|_2^8ds
 \le b_8 Q(t)^4\int_0^tE\|a_r\|_2^8dr.                    \tag{16}
\]

Factorization gives
\[
Z_t=c_\eta\int_0^t(t-s)^{\eta-1}S_{t-s}Y_s\,ds.             \tag{17}
\]
Indeed the inner scalar integral after stochastic Fubini is
\[
\int_r^t(t-s)^{\eta-1}(s-r)^{-\eta}ds
 =\pi/\sin(\pi\eta),
\]
and the heat semigroup factors combine to \(S_{t-r}\).
For bounded \(a\), stochastic Fubini is justified by (11): the
\(L^2\) norm of its inner stochastic convolution is bounded by a
constant times \((s^{1-2\eta}+s^{1/2-2\eta})^{1/2}\), and
integration against \((t-s)^{\eta-1}\) is finite. One can first truncate
the singularities, apply Fubini, and then use these bounds to remove
the truncations.

The conjugate exponent to eight is \(8/7\). Heat contraction and Holder
give
\[
\begin{split}
\sup_{r\le t}\left\|\int_0^r(r-s)^{\eta-1}S_{r-s}Y_sds\right\|_2^8
&\le \left(\int_0^t v^{(\eta-1)8/7}dv\right)^7
          \int_0^t\|Y_s\|_2^8ds\\
&=14^7t^{1/2}\int_0^t\|Y_s\|_2^8ds.                       \tag{18}
\end{split}
The exponent is \(-13/14\), so the integral before its seventh power
is \(14t^{1/14}\). The deterministic operator in (18) maps
\(L^8([0,T];H)\) into \(C([0,T];H)\). For continuous inputs this follows
from strong continuity of \(S\) and the integrable fractional kernel;
continuous inputs are dense in \(L^8\), and (18) gives uniform convergence
of their outputs and value zero at time zero.

Combine (16)--(18). Since
\[
t^{1/2}Q(t)^4=t\big((8/5)\sqrt t+8d\big)^4,
\]
this proves (13)--(14). For general \(a\) as above, truncate its \(H\)
norm. The same estimate applied to differences makes the truncated
convolutions Cauchy in \(L^8(\Omega;C([0,T];H))\). At each fixed time
the stochastic isometry identifies the limit with the original convolution;
alternatively Holder in time applies since
\((1+v^{-1/2})^{4/3}\) is integrable. The truncated versions therefore
converge to the desired continuous version, and (13) passes to the limit.

## 3. The stopped maximal estimate

Set \(G=|g(0)|+L\). On a space of mass one, (1) and
\(|z|^\beta\le1+|z|\) imply
\[
\|g(v)\|_2\le G(1+\|v\|_2),\qquad
\|g(v)\|_2^8\le128G^8(1+\|v\|_2^8).                       \tag{19}
\]
The scalar inequality used in the last step is
\((1+x)^8\le2^7(1+x^8)\).

Consider any continuous-\(H\) mild solution whose drift is
\(\kappa_s B(s,U)\), for an arbitrary predictable scalar
\(0\le\kappa_s\le1\). The probability space and Brownian motion are
arbitrary. Stop at
\(\sigma_R=\inf\{t:m_t(U)\ge R\}\), \(R>\|h\|_2\), and define
\[
V_t^R=S_th+\int_0^t1_{s\le\sigma_R}S_{t-s}\kappa_sB(s,U)ds
       +\int_0^t1_{s\le\sigma_R}S_{t-s}M_{g(U_s)}dW_s.       \tag{20}
\]
The integrands are stopped; \(V^R\) is not asserted to equal
\(U_{t\wedge\sigma_R}\). Its integrands have bounded \(H\) norm.
Equation (13) gives continuous stochastic paths and finite eighth moments
of their maximum. Thus
\(N_R(t)=E\sup_{r\le t}\|V_r^R\|_2^8\) is finite and bounded on
\([0,T]\) for each \(R\), before any Gronwall argument.

On \(s\le\sigma_R\), the histories of \(U\) and \(V^R\) through \(s\)
agree: equality first holds at all deterministic times before the stop,
and continuity extends it simultaneously to all times and to the hit.
Consequently
\[
\begin{split}
1_{s\le\sigma_R}\|B(s,U)\|_2^8
 &\le128K^8(1+\sup_{r\le s}\|V_r^R\|_2^8),\\
1_{s\le\sigma_R}\|g(U_s)\|_2^8
 &\le128G^8(1+\sup_{r\le s}\|V_r^R\|_2^8).                 \tag{21}
\end{split}
Only the growth bound is needed; no value of \(B\) on the altered future
of \(V^R\) is used.

The maximum norm of the drift convolution is at most the time integral
of the integrand norm. Its eighth moment is bounded by \(t^7\) times
the integral of the eighth moment of that norm. Using
\(\|a+b+c\|^8\le3^7(\|a\|^8+\|b\|^8+\|c\|^8)\), (13), (21),
and monotonicity of \(F\), gives
\[
1+N_R(t)\le3^7(1+\|h\|_2^8)
 +\Lambda_T\int_0^t(1+N_R(s))ds,                           \tag{22}
\]
where
\[
\Lambda_T=128\,3^7\big(K^8T^7+G^8F(T)\big),\quad
C_8(T)=3^7e^{\Lambda_T T},\quad C_2(T)=1+C_8(T)^{1/4}.        \tag{23}
\]
Ordinary integral Gronwall now gives
\(1+N_R(T)\le C_8(T)(1+\|h\|_2^8)\), uniformly in \(R,\kappa\).
Every continuous \(H\) path is bounded, so \(\sigma_R>T\) eventually
on each path. Fatou proves
\[
E\sup_{t\le T}\|U_t\|_2^8\le C_8(T)(1+\|h\|_2^8),\qquad
E(1+m_T(U)^2)\le C_2(T)(1+\|h\|_2^2).                     \tag{24}
\]
For the second bound use Holder and
\((1+x^8)^{1/4}\le1+x^2\), \(x\ge0\).
Thus, uniformly over all the multipliers and probability measures above,
\[
EA_B(U)\le 2c^{-2}K^2T C_2(T)(1+\|h\|_2^2).                \tag{25}
\]
This is the maximal estimate needed for growth in the running norm.

## 4. Both changes of measure are normalized

For any predictable direction \(\theta\), put
\[
A_t=\int_0^t\|\theta_s\|_2^2ds,\quad
\tau_n=\inf\{t:A_t\ge n\},\quad
R_t=\exp\{\int_0^t\langle\theta_s,dW_s\rangle-A_t/2\}.
\]
Assume \(A_T<\infty\) almost surely. The stopped exponential is a
martingale, since its bracket is bounded by \(n\). Let
\(dQ_n=R_{T\wedge\tau_n}dP\). If \(E_{Q_n}A_T\le C\) for all \(n\),
then
\[
1\ge E_PR_T\ge E_P[R_T1_{A_T<n}]
  =Q_n(A_T<n)\ge1-C/n.                                     \tag{26}
\]
The full exponential is a nonnegative local martingale; the equality
uses equality of the stopped and full densities on \(\{A_T<n\}\).
Letting \(n\) increase proves normalization. The supermartingale property
then gives constant expectation at every time and the martingale property.
The terminal value is finite and positive, so the new measure is equivalent.

Start from a drift-free realization \((X,W)\) and use \(\theta(s,X)\)
from (9). Under \(Q_n\), the Brownian motion is
\(W^n=W-\int_0^{\cdot\wedge\tau_n}\theta_sds\), and \(X\)
has drift \(1_{s\le\tau_n}B(s,X)\). Formula (25) applies, since its
multiplier lies in \([0,1]\). Hence (26) normalizes the full density.
Under \(dQ=R_TdP\), \(W^B=W-\int_0^\cdot\theta_sds\) is Brownian
and \(X\) solves (3). This proves weak existence.

For any drifted solution \((U,W)\), use instead
\[
\widehat R_t=\exp\{-\int_0^t\langle\theta(s,U),dW_s\rangle-A_t(U)/2\}.
                                                                    \tag{27}
\]
Its stopped changes of measure leave drift
\(1_{s>\tau_n}B(s,U)\), another multiplier in \([0,1]\).
The same (25)--(26) normalize \(\widehat R\). Under
\(d\widehat Q=\widehat R_TdP\),
\(\widehat W=W+\int_0^\cdot\theta(s,U)ds\) is Brownian and \(U\)
is drift-free, with state law \(\mu_0\) by (D).

The changed mild identities used here can be justified before claiming
any changed-measure moments. For smooth tests the semimartingale
change-of-integrator formula and \(g\theta=B\) give the weak identities.
Localize at \(\sigma_R\). The drift and diffusion have bounded \(H\)
norm there; (11) makes heat stochastic integrals and stochastic Fubini
legitimate. Finite Fourier tests and variation of constants give the
localized mild equation. Let \(R\) increase, first at rational times;
the original continuous state, continuous deterministic convolution, and
the localized stochastic convolution give the simultaneous identity.
Only then is (24) applied. This avoids using a moment estimate to justify
the equation to which that estimate is applied.

## 5. The density is determined by the state

Under \(\mu_0\), define the Borel path functions, for smooth periodic
real \(\varphi\),
\[
M_t(\varphi)=\langle X_t-h,\varphi\rangle
             -\int_0^t\langle X_s,\Delta\varphi\rangle ds.
\]
They are square-integrable continuous martingales in the canonical
filtration, by conditioning the identities of a drift-free realization.
Their cross brackets are
\[
[M(\varphi),M(\psi)]_t
 =\int_0^t\!\int g(X_s(x))^2\varphi(x)\psi(x)dxds.           \tag{28}
\]
For bounded Borel \(\mathcal F_r^X\)-measurable \(\xi\), set
\[
I(\xi1_{(r,t]}\varphi)=\xi[M_t(\varphi)-M_r(\varphi)]
\]
and extend by finite sums. These are Borel state-path functions and
\[
E_{\mu_0}|I(v)-I(w)|^2
 =E_{\mu_0}\int_0^T\!\int g(X_s)^2|v-w|^2dxds.              \tag{29}
\]
The weighted measure on the right is finite, by (19), (24). Elementary
predictable rectangles times spatial Borel sets generate its sigma field
apart from the time-zero null set. Their simple functions are dense.
For each such rectangle the induced spatial measure is a finite Borel
measure on the compact torus. Continuous spatial functions are dense in
its \(L^2\), and smooth functions are uniformly dense in continuous
ones. Thus the elementary functions above are dense in the predictable
subspace for the norm in (29), even though \(g\) is unbounded.

The predictable target \(q=B/g^2\) has weighted norm squared
\(E_{\mu_0}A_B<\infty\). Choose elementary \(v_j\) converging to it
in that norm. The integrals \(I(v_j)\) converge in \(L^2(\mu_0)\);
choose a subsequence also converging almost surely. Define \(J_B\) to
be its finite limit, and zero off its Borel convergence set. On every
drift-free realization with state law \(\mu_0\), the same subsequence
and the stochastic isometry give
\[
J_B(X)=\int_0^T\langle\theta(s,X),dW_s\rangle.               \tag{30}
\]
Consequently the forward density is precisely (4), and it integrates to one.

For an arbitrary drifted solution, use \(\widehat Q,\widehat W\) from
Section 4. The state law under \(\widehat Q\) is \(\mu_0\), so (30)
applies there. The same elementary functions give, under \(P\),
\[
I(v_j)(U)=\int_0^T\langle g(U_s)v_j(s,U),dW_s\rangle
                   +\int_0^T\langle v_j(s,U),B(s,U)\rangle ds.
\]
The weighted squared errors
\(E_j(U)=\int_0^T\|g(U_s)v_j(s,U)-\theta(s,U)\|_2^2ds\)
converge to zero in probability under \(\widehat Q\), hence under \(P\)
by equivalence. Martingale errors against \(W\) converge in probability
by stopping their brackets. The drift error is bounded by
\(A_B(U)^{1/2}E_j(U)^{1/2}\), which also converges to zero in probability.
The fixed subsequence defining \(J_B\) converges almost surely under both
equivalent measures. Therefore
\[
J_B(U)=\int_0^T\langle\theta(s,U),dW_s\rangle+A_B(U),\qquad
\widehat R_T^{-1}=\exp\{J_B(U)-A_B(U)/2\}.                  \tag{31}
\]
This argument uses convergence in probability, not an unproved transfer
of \(L^2\) convergence across equivalent measures.

For every bounded Borel \(f\) on \(\mathcal X\),
\[
E_Pf(U)=E_{\widehat Q}[f(U)\widehat R_T^{-1}]
       =\int f\exp(J_B-A_B/2)d\mu_0.                        \tag{32}
\]
This identifies every drifted state-path law, proving uniqueness.
Positivity and finiteness of the density give equivalence.
Under each law the stochastic integral in (30) or (31) is square
integrable by (25), and has mean zero. Taking logarithms and expectations
gives (5); halving (25) gives (6).

## 6. A strict extension and independent checks

Fix \(0<\delta<T/2\) and \(a\ne0\). The delay drift
\[
B(t,\omega)=a\omega_{(t-\delta)^+}                          \tag{33}
\]
is jointly continuous, nonanticipating, and satisfies (2) with \(K=|a|\).
It is not bounded by one fixed multiple of \(1+\|\omega_t\|_2\), even
on paths starting from zero: a continuous path can reach \(Ne\) at
\(\delta\), then return to zero at \(2\delta\), with \(\|e\|_2=1\).
At the latter time (33) has norm \(|a|N\), and the current state is zero.
Thus the theorem extends the round-5 growth hypothesis strictly.
Discontinuous Borel history gates multiplying such drifts are also allowed
when their absolute value is at most one.

For an exact check, take \(g\equiv\sigma\ne0\), \(h=0\), and the
rank-one delay drift
\[
B_*(t,\omega)=a\langle\omega_{(t-\delta)^+},1\rangle\,1.
\]
It satisfies (2) and also fails any uniform current-norm growth bound.
Project onto the normalized constant spatial function and divide by
\(\sigma\). Its coordinate \(Z\) solves
\(dZ_t=aZ_{(t-\delta)^+}dt+dW_t\). Until \(\delta\), \(Z=W\);
on \([\delta,2\delta]\),
\[
Z_t=W_t+a\int_0^{t-\delta}W_rdr.
\]
In particular
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_{2\delta})
 =2\delta+a\delta^2+a^2\delta^3/3
 =\int_0^\delta[1+a(\delta-r)]^2dr+\delta
 \ge5\delta/4.                                            \tag{34}
\]
The first expression follows by integrating
\(\operatorname{Cov}(W_s,W_t)=\min(s,t)\):
\(\operatorname{Cov}(W_{2\delta},\int_0^\delta W_rdr)=\delta^2/2\)
and \(\operatorname{Var}(\int_0^\delta W_rdr)=\delta^3/3\).
The second expression follows independently by stochastic Fubini and
isometry. Completing the square in \(a\) gives the lower bound, with
equality at \(a=-3/(2\delta)\). The same mass equation holds for (33),
but only the rank-one example has a density depending on this mode alone.

For the rank-one example on the horizon \(2\delta\), the drift-free
mass is a Brownian motion \(W\), and its state-path density is
\[
D=\exp\{J-A/2\},\quad
J=a\int_\delta^{2\delta}W_{s-\delta}dW_s,\qquad
A=a^2\int_0^\delta W_r^2dr.                                \tag{35}
\]
Conditionally on the Brownian history through \(\delta\), \(J\) is
centered Gaussian with variance \(A\), since its integrand is already
known and future Brownian increments are independent. Thus
\(E[D\mid\mathcal F_\delta]=1\) directly, for every \(a\). The tilted
law preserves this first-interval history, so both laws have expected
energy \(a^2\delta^2/2\). Under the tilt, the conditional mean of \(J\)
is \(A\); without the tilt it is zero. Each entropy is therefore
\(a^2\delta^2/4\), checking (5) for an unbounded past-dependent drift.

Full Novikov can fail even in this example. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(A\ge (a^2/\delta)(\int_0^\delta W_rdr)^2\). The latter Gaussian
integral has variance \(\delta^3/3\), by either derivation used in (34).
A centered Gaussian of variance \(v\) has infinite quadratic exponential
moment \(Ee^{bY^2}\) when \(2bv\ge1\), by direct Gaussian integration.
Hence \(Ee^{A/2}=\infty\) if \(a^2\delta^2\ge3\). This sufficient
condition is not claimed sharp. Normalization in (35) still holds.

The exponents in (14) also admit an independent scaling check. For general
\(p>4\) and \(1/p<\eta<1/4\), the singular heat contribution to the
inner integrated moment has power \((1/2-2\eta)p/2\), while the outer
Holder estimate has power \(p\eta-1\). Their sum is \(p/4-1\), equal
to one for \(p=8\). Direct substitution of \(\eta=3/16\) gives the
same one: \(1/2+4(1/8)=1\). The nonsingular heat part contributes an
additional polynomial factor in \(\sqrt t\), already retained in (14).

For the exact inner integral one can also substitute \(v=x^8\):
\(k(v)dv=(8x^4+8d)dx\), giving \(Q(t)\) without fractional integration.
The outer integral in (18) becomes constant after \(v=x^{14}\).
These give separate checks on \(8/5\), \(8\), \(14\), and the time power.

The inequality \(\sup_tE\|U_t\|^2<\infty\) alone would not justify
(25) with (2). For example, take a uniform center \(C\) on \([1/4,3/4]\),
\(n\ge8\), and \(X_t=\sqrt n(1-n|t-C|)^+e\) on \([0,1]\),
where \(\|e\|_2=1\). Its paths are continuous with \(X_0=0\).
Integration of the squared triangle gives
\(\sup_tE\|X_t\|_2^2\le4/3\), but
\(E\sup_t\|X_t\|_2^2=n\). Mixing \(n=2^{j+2}\) with probabilities
\(2^{-j}\), \(j\ge1\), gives one continuous process with bounded
fixed-time second moments and infinite expected squared maximum.
These processes are examples about moments, not claimed SPDE solutions.

Nor is it valid to write a mild equation for the stopped
state \(U_{t\wedge\sigma_R}\) using the original heat kernel. The proof
uses (20) and proves the running maximum estimate before applying Girsanov.

No assertion for \(\beta=0\), nonelliptic diffusion, or pathwise uniqueness
follows. The maximal estimate uses only linear diffusion growth, so it
also applies to globally Lipschitz diffusion; when using it for uniqueness,
the appropriate drift-free input must still be supplied. The constants
in (23) are explicit finite upper bounds and are not claimed to be sharp.
