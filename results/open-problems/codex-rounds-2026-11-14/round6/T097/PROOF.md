# Boundary scores and sharpness of the general comparison method

The Gaussian free field on Z^3 has covariance

    g(x,y) = sum_(n>=0) P_x(X_n=y),

where P is simple random walk. Put B_R=[-R,R]^3 intersect Z^3 for
integers R>=1, and let F_R be the sigma field generated by phi on B_R.
All paths in this note are nearest-neighbor lattice paths. All logarithms
are natural. Write Phi for the standard normal distribution function,
Q=1-Phi, and varphi for its density.

Use the round 5 capacity constant

    kappa = inf { (1/6) integral |grad F|^2 :
                  F compactly supported and Lipschitz,
                  F=1 on [-1,1]^3 }.

Its independently checked sandwich is

    kappa R <= a_R := cap(B_R) <= kappa(R+1/2),          (1)

so a_R/R tends to kappa>0. This is a deterministic input. The new
results below do not determine a lattice percolation exponent.

## 1. Statements

Let e_R be the equilibrium measure of B_R and define

    M_R = sum_(x in B_R) e_R(x) phi_x.

Then M_R is supported on the inner vertex boundary of B_R and is a
centered Gaussian martingale for F_R. For S>=R,

    Var(M_R)=a_R,    Cov(M_R,M_S)=a_R,
    M_S-M_R is independent of F_R.                      (2)

In particular, its joint law is that of Brownian motion sampled at the
deterministic times a_R.

For the arm event A_R(h)={0 connects to boundary B_R in {phi>=h}}, put
p_R(h)=P(A_R(h)) and m_R(h)=-d/dh log p_R(h). The exact identities are

    m_R(h) = E[M_R | A_R(h)],
    m_R'(h) = a_R-Var(M_R | A_R(h)) <= a_R.             (3)

If

    M := limsup_(R->infinity) m_R(h_*)/sqrt(a_R) < infinity, (4)

then the relative radius from the earlier reports satisfies

    liminf_(t downarrow 0) t^2 L_rel(t)
      >= (sqrt(M^2+2 log 2)-M)^2/kappa > 0.             (5)

Condition (4) is not proved here. Section 4 expresses it as an explicit
ratio of Gaussian averages involving a residual connection threshold.

There also exist events D_R, measurable on B_R, increasing in every field
coordinate and decreasing as R grows, such that for every epsilon>0,

    P(D_R) >= R^(-2-epsilon) for all sufficiently large R, (6)

but their translated relative radius

    L_D(t) = min{R>=1: P(phi-t1 belongs to D_R)<=P(D_R)/2}

satisfies the exact equality

    liminf_(t downarrow 0) t^2 log(1/t) L_D(t)
      = (log 2)^2/(8 kappa),                            (7)
    liminf_(t downarrow 0) t^2 L_D(t) = 0.              (8)

Here phi-t1 in D_R means subtracting t from all field coordinates used
by D_R. These are artificial events, not connection events. Equations
(6)--(8) establish sharpness of the preceding comparison bound within
this broader class, even when monotonicity and nesting are imposed.

## 2. The boundary Gaussian martingale

For a finite nonempty set K, let G_K=(g(x,y))_(x,y in K). It is positive
definite. Indeed, in a finite box U the killed Green matrix is
(I-P_U)^(-1). The symmetric substochastic matrix P_U has spectrum in
(-1,1), so its Green matrix is bounded below by (1/2)I. The same lower
bound holds for its principal submatrices and their increasing-box
limits, including G_K.

For x in K set

    e_K(x)=P_x(X_n not in K for all integers n>=1).

A transient walk has a last visit to finite K almost surely. Decomposing
according to the time and position of that visit gives

    G_K e_K=1,    cap(K)=sum_x e_K(x)=1^T G_K^(-1)1.    (9)

In particular e_K is nonnegative. For K=B_R, a vertex outside the inner
boundary has every neighbor in B_R, so its first step is a return to B_R.
Thus e_R vanishes at all such vertices.

Equation (9) gives, for every x in B_R,

    Cov(M_R,phi_x)=sum_y e_R(y)g(y,x)=1.               (10)

Multiplying (10) by e_R(x) and summing proves Var(M_R)=a_R. If S>=R,
both M_S and M_R have covariance one with each phi_x, x in B_R.
Their difference is jointly Gaussian with this finite vector and has
zero covariance with it. Hence M_S-M_R is independent of F_R, is
centered, and

    E[M_S | F_R]=M_R.

Also Cov(M_R,M_S)=sum_x e_R(x)=a_R. Consequently
Var(M_S-M_R)=a_S-a_R>=0. This proves (2), including monotonicity of the
times a_R. Increments over disjoint radius intervals are independent:
the earlier increment is F_R measurable and the later one is independent
of F_R. A centered Gaussian process with these covariances has precisely
the finite-dimensional distributions of Brownian motion at times a_R.

## 3. Exact translation, derivatives, and the endpoint criterion

Fix R and abbreviate a=a_R, G=G_(B_R), e=G^(-1)1, and M_R=e^T phi.
For any Borel event D on this finite vector, its centered Gaussian
density f satisfies

    f(x+t1)/f(x) = exp(-t e^T x-a t^2/2).              (11)

Therefore, when p=P(D)>0,

    P(phi-t1 in D)/p
      = exp(-a t^2/2) E[exp(-t M_R) | D].              (12)

This holds for every real t, without monotonicity of D.

For arms, shifting phi by -t changes level h to h+t. The event is
measurable on B_R, since a path can be stopped at its first boundary
visit. Thus

    p_R(h+t)/p_R(h)
      = exp(-a_R t^2/2) E[exp(-t M_R) | A_R(h)].       (13)

There is a path of finite length to the boundary, so an open set of
field configurations realizes A_R(h). Another open set has phi_0<h.
Finite Gaussian nondegeneracy gives 0<p_R(h)<1.

For completeness, differentiation is legitimate despite the event's
corners. Write p_R(h)=integral_(A_R(0)) f(y+h1)dy. On any bounded
interval of h, derivatives of this density are polynomials times shifted
Gaussian densities, dominated by an integrable Gaussian envelope times
a polynomial. Differentiation under the integral gives

    p_R'(h) = -E[M_R 1_(A_R(h))],
    p_R''(h) = E[(M_R^2-a_R)1_(A_R(h))].              (14)

Taking the logarithmic derivative twice proves (3). Monotonicity of
p_R in h gives m_R(h)>=0. Integrating the second identity of (3) gives

    m_R(h+u) <= m_R(h)+a_R u,    u>=0.                (15)

In particular an endpoint bound of order sqrt(a_R) gives a bound of
the same order throughout h in [h_*,h_*+c/sqrt(R)], for each fixed c.
Indeed u sqrt(a_R)<=c sqrt(kappa(1+1/(2R))) by (1).

Jensen's inequality in (13) gives the sharper useful form

    p_R(h+t)/p_R(h)
      >= exp(-t m_R(h)-a_R t^2/2),    t>=0.           (16)

Suppose now that (4) holds. Fix

    0<c<(sqrt(M^2+2 log 2)-M)^2/kappa.

The positive root of M y+y^2/2=log 2 is
y=sqrt(M^2+2 log 2)-M. Hence

    M sqrt(kappa c)+kappa c/2 < log 2.

Choose M'>M sufficiently close that this remains true with M'. For
all sufficiently large R, m_R(h_*)<=M' sqrt(a_R). If R<=c/t^2, (1)
and (16) bound the negative logarithm of the lower ratio by

    M' sqrt(kappa(c+t^2/2))+kappa(c+t^2/2)/2.

It is strictly less than log 2 for all sufficiently small t, uniformly
over these R. At each remaining finitely many radii the ratio tends to
one by (13) or density continuity. Consequently
L_rel(t)>floor(c/t^2). Letting t decrease to zero and then c increase to
its endpoint proves (5). No monotonicity of the ratio in R is needed.

The finiteness of L_rel(t) uses only the earlier external input: Stephen
Muirhead, *Percolation of strongly correlated Gaussian fields II.
Sharpness of the phase transition*,
[arXiv:2206.10724v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.10724v2), Theorems 1.2 and
1.10. In d=3, these give theta(h_*-v)>=c_q v^q for every q>4 and small
v>0, and p_R(h_*+t)<=exp(-c_t sqrt(R)) for each fixed t>0. To obtain
the needed critical lower bound, take v=R^(-1/2). Then
p_R(h_*-v)>=c_q R^(-q/2) and v sqrt(a_R) is bounded by (1).
Apply (20), proved below, at this lower level. For fixed s>=0 the
function p exp(-s sqrt(2 log(1/p))-s^2/2) increases with p. It follows
that log(1/p_R(h_*))<=(q/2)log R+O_q(sqrt(log R)). Choosing q>4
sufficiently close to 4 gives p_R(h_*)>=R^(-2-epsilon) eventually.
The subcritical bound then makes the relative ratio tend to zero. No endpoint
density assertion at q=4 is used. These are the only external
probabilistic inputs for the statement about actual arms in (5).

## 4. Independent common mode and the residual path threshold

Define on B_R

    H_R=M_R/a_R,    psi_(R,x)=phi_x-H_R.

By (10), Var(H_R)=1/a_R and Cov(H_R,phi_x)=1/a_R. Hence H_R is
independent of the whole residual vector psi_R. Its covariance is

    Cov(psi_(R,x),psi_(R,y))=g(x,y)-1/a_R,
    sum_x e_R(x)psi_(R,x)=0.                           (17)

The residual is allowed to be degenerate; the independence assertion is
the elementary zero-covariance criterion for jointly Gaussian vectors.

Let P_R be the finite nonempty collection of simple paths from 0 to
boundary B_R, stopped at their first boundary visit, and put

    T_R(psi_R)=max_(pi in P_R) min_(x in pi) psi_(R,x).

Every connection has such a path after erasing loops. A common additive
shift changes every path minimum by the same amount. Therefore

    A_R(h) = {H_R+T_R(psi_R)>=h}.

Conditional on psi_R, sqrt(a_R) H_R is a standard normal variable.
Writing u_R(h)=sqrt(a_R)(h-T_R(psi_R)) gives

    p_R(h)=E[Q(u_R(h))],
    m_R(h)/sqrt(a_R)
      = E[varphi(u_R(h))]/E[Q(u_R(h))].                (18)

For the second formula use integral_u^infinity z varphi(z)dz=varphi(u)
and the first formula of (3), or differentiate the first formula of
(18). Formula (18) isolates the endpoint quantity in (4). No estimate
of the distribution of T_R at the positive lattice level h_* is supplied.

## 5. A universal lower bound for translated events

This section records exactly which information suffices for the old
coefficient, so its sharpness in section 6 has a precise meaning.
Let E_R be any B_R-measurable events, with probabilities p_R>0, and
q_R(t)=P(phi-t1 in E_R). Suppose that for each epsilon>0,

    log(1/p_R)<=(2+epsilon)log R eventually.            (19)

No nesting assumption is needed in this section. Define L_E(t) as the
first integer R with q_R(t)<=p_R/2, taking infinity if there is none.

Under the law with mean -t1, the likelihood of the centered law has
moments exp(r(r-1)a_R t^2/2), r>1. To check this, (11) shows that its
logarithm has the form sZ-s^2/2 for Z standard normal and
s=t sqrt(a_R). Holder's inequality gives

    p_R <= q_R(t)^((r-1)/r) exp((r-1)s^2/2).

Solving for the ratio and setting r-1=sqrt(2 log(1/p_R))/s gives

    q_R(t)/p_R
      >= exp(-t sqrt(2 a_R log(1/p_R))-a_R t^2/2).     (20)

If p_R=1 or t=0 the same bound follows directly, or by a limit.

Put delta=log 2 and c<delta^2/(8 kappa). Choose epsilon>0 with
sqrt(4 kappa c(2+epsilon))<delta. For
R<=c/(t^2 log(1/t)), (1) and (19) give, uniformly over all sufficiently
large such radii,

    a_R t^2<=kappa c/log(1/t)+kappa t^2/2,
    log(1/p_R)<=2(2+epsilon)log(1/t).

The loss in (20) is at most

    sqrt((kappa c/log(1/t)+kappa t^2/2)
            *4(2+epsilon)log(1/t))
       +(kappa c/log(1/t)+kappa t^2/2)/2.

It tends to sqrt(4 kappa c(2+epsilon))<delta. Every remaining finite
radius has q_R(t)/p_R tending to one by Gaussian density continuity.
Thus L_E(t)>floor(c/(t^2 log(1/t))) for sufficiently small t, and

    liminf_(t downarrow 0) t^2 log(1/t) L_E(t)
      >= delta^2/(8 kappa).                            (21)

This proof allows the value infinity and uses no monotonicity in R of
the probability ratio.

## 6. Nested increasing events attaining the coefficient

Set, for n>=1,

    R_n=2^(2^(2^n)),    ell_n=log R_n,
    b_n=2 sqrt(ell_n),    a_n=a_(R_n).

These specify integers without requiring their numerical construction.
Their separation gives

    ell_n=(log 2)2^(2^n),
    ell_n/ell_(n-1)=2^(2^(n-1)) tends to infinity,
    sum_(j=1)^n ell_j=(1+o(1))ell_n,
    n log ell_n=o(ell_n).                              (22)

For the third assertion, the earlier sum is at most (n-1)ell_(n-1),
whose ratio to ell_n tends to zero. The last assertion follows directly
from the displayed formula for ell_n.

Define

    D_R = intersection_(j: R_j<=R) {M_(R_j)>=b_j sqrt(a_j)}, (23)

with the empty intersection equal to the whole space. Each D_R is
F_R measurable. Since every e_(R_j) is nonnegative, it is increasing
in every field coordinate. The events decrease with R.

We first prove their probability lower bound. By (2) the increments

    W_1=M_(R_1),    W_j=M_(R_j)-M_(R_(j-1)), j>=2,

are independent centered Gaussian variables. Every partial sum M_(R_j)
is coordinatewise increasing in W_1,...,W_n. For independent real
variables, bounded increasing functions are positively associated.
Here is a direct proof. In one dimension, with an independent copy X',

    Cov(f(X),g(X))
      = (1/2)E[(f(X)-f(X'))(g(X)-g(X'))]>=0.

For several coordinates, condition on the last coordinate. Apply the
inductive assertion to the others and then the one-dimensional assertion
to the two conditional means, which remain increasing. This proves
association by induction. Applying it repeatedly to the increasing
partial-sum events in (23) gives

    P(D_(R_n)) >= product_(j=1)^n Q(b_j).              (24)

For b>=0, integrate the normal density only from b to b+1/(b+1).
The density decreases there, and

    (b+1/(b+1))^2/2-b^2/2 <= 3/2.

Consequently, with c0=e^(-3/2)/sqrt(2 pi)>0,

    Q(b)>=c0 exp(-b^2/2)/(b+1).                       (25)

Equations (24)--(25) imply

    log(1/P(D_(R_n)))
      <= 2 sum_(j=1)^n ell_j
            +sum_(j=1)^n log(b_j+1)+n log(1/c0)
      <= (2+o(1))ell_n.                               (26)

Indeed log(b_j+1)<=log 3+(1/2)log ell_n, since ell_j>=1 and
ell_j<=ell_n. The last two terms are o(ell_n) by (22).
For R_n<=R<R_(n+1), D_R=D_(R_n). Given epsilon>0, (26) thus gives
log(1/P(D_R))<=(2+epsilon)log R for every sufficiently large R.
This proves (6), including radii between the selected ones.

These probabilities also tend to zero: D_(R_n) is contained in
{M_(R_n)>=b_n sqrt(a_n)}, whose probability is Q(b_n) tending to zero.
Thus the obstruction does not rely on positive limiting probability.

At R_n, membership in D_(R_n) forces

    M_(R_n)>=b_n sqrt(a_n).

Using the exact identity (12) and t>0 gives the upper bound

    P(phi-t1 in D_(R_n))/P(D_(R_n))
      <= exp(-t b_n sqrt(a_n)-a_n t^2/2).              (27)

For every fixed t>0 this tends to zero as n grows. Thus L_D(t) is
finite. Set

    t_n=delta/(b_n sqrt(a_n)),    delta=log 2.

Then t_n tends to zero, and (27) is strictly less than
exp(-delta)=1/2. Hence L_D(t_n)<=R_n. By (1), a_n/R_n tends to kappa,
and

    log(1/t_n)
      = (1/2)log a_n+log b_n-log delta
      = (1/2)ell_n+o(ell_n).

Therefore

    t_n^2 log(1/t_n) R_n
      = delta^2 R_n log(1/t_n)/(4 a_n ell_n)
      tends to delta^2/(8 kappa),                     (28)
    t_n^2 R_n=delta^2 R_n/(4 a_n ell_n) tends to zero. (29)

Equation (21) applies to D_R by (6), while (28) provides the matching
upper bound on its liminf. This proves (7). Nonnegativity and (29)
prove (8).

The endpoint obstruction itself is explicit:

    E[M_(R_n) | D_(R_n)]/sqrt(a_n)>=b_n
      =2 sqrt(log R_n) tends to infinity.             (30)

In fact this is its asymptotic size. For any event D of probability p,
the Gaussian exponential moment and Jensen give, for lambda>0,

    lambda E[M_R/sqrt(a_R) | D]
      <= lambda^2/2+log(1/p).

Minimizing in lambda proves the upper bound sqrt(2 log(1/p)). At R_n,
(26) and (30) give

    E[M_(R_n) | D_(R_n)]/sqrt(a_n)
      =(2+o(1))sqrt(log R_n).

Thus the old square-root logarithmic score loss is attained in this
nested class, and so is its relative-radius coefficient.

## 7. What remains for actual connections

The exact statistic in (18) concerns the residual bottleneck over lattice
paths at the positive level h_*. Bounding it uniformly would imply (5)
and the uniform near-critical score estimate through (15). The
construction in section 6 proves that such a bound cannot follow solely
from increasing nested events, the boundary Gaussian martingale, and
the critical probability lower bound (19).

No claim is made that actual connection events attain the artificial
construction's sensitivity. No critical nonpercolation, existence or
value of lattice critical exponents, identification with a conventional
correlation length, or agreement with cable exponents follows. The
sharpness result is about the information used by the preceding method.
