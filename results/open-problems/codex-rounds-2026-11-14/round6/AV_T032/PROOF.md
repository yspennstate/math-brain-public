# Verification of the skeleton encounter estimates

The source is OpenAI, *Bose–Einstein condensation at positive temperature in the dilute hard-sphere gas* (October 5, 2026), Section 8, Lemma 8.2, `lem:skeleton`, in [lattice-current.tex](sources/lattice-current.tex). This result concerns the explicitly defined random skeletons before their adaptation to bad cells. It does not establish the later detour estimate, the thermal certificate bound, or condensation.

## Source statement

```tex
\begin{lemma}[Skeleton encounters]\label{lem:skeleton}
For two independent skeletons just constructed, there are absolute
constants $c_{\rm hit}>0$ and $C_{\rm hit}<\infty$ such that, for every
integer $R_s\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{lattice:imported-hit}
 \E e^{\alpha_sJ_{\rm occ}^{(R_s)}}\le C_{\rm hit},
 \qquad \alpha_s=\frac{c_{\rm hit}}{(1+R_s)^2}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $B>0$, there are $\xi(B)>0$ and $C(B)<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{lattice:imported-soft}
 \E e^{\xi(B)S_B(\Gamma,\Gamma')}\le C(B).
\end{equation}
Both estimates are uniform in $K$ and the admissible endpoint pair.
\end{lemma}
```

## 1. Independent recheck of the preceding conditional-law result

Fix the endpoint configurations and purification variable. Write the displayed slab density relative to the product of free torus bridges as a constant times the mutual-avoidance indicator. Fix the entire eligibility vector, allocation, and paths outside the retained group (I). The conditional density on a retained fiber is then

\[
 c(b)\,\mathbf1_{A_{II}}\prod_{i\in I}\mathbf1_{C_i(F)}\mathsf T_i(d\omega_i).
\tag{1}
\]

Here (b) denotes the exposed datum. This follows directly by factoring the joint Radon–Nikodym derivative: the allocation kernel depends only on the now fixed eligibility vector; every retained indicator is one; and all restrictions on frozen paths are fixed. No certificate event is conditioned on. Thus (c(b)) is independent of retained paths. This is the point at which an allocation using further information from a retained path would invalidate (1).

The torus bridge is the projection of a mixture of Euclidean bridges ending at (z_i+Km), with weights

\[
 w_i(m)=p^{\mathbb R^3}_{2t}(y_i,z_i+Km)/p^{\rm tor}_{2t}(Y_i,Z_i).
\]

For (K>20), the inverse image of the radius-ten confinement ball is a disjoint union of Euclidean balls. A continuous lifted path starting in the near ball cannot enter another one while remaining confined. All (m\ne0) terms therefore vanish on (C_i(F)). Replacing (mathsf T_i) by its near-endpoint Euclidean bridge (B_i) in (1) costs exactly the positive endpoint constant (w_i(0)). These constants cancel on normalization.

Let (H(b)) be the integral of (mathbf1_{A_{II}}prod_imathbf1_{C_i(F)}B_i). The exposed marginal has the factor (H(b)), so (H(b)>0) almost everywhere under that marginal. Since (H(b)\le a_i:=B_i(C_i(F))\le1), every (a_i) is positive there. Dividing by (prod_i a_i) gives

\[
 \lambda_I=Z_I^{-1}\mathbf1_{A_{II}}\prod_{i\in I}P_i,
 \quad P_i=\mathbf1_{C_i(F)}B_i/a_i,
 \quad Z_I=H(b)/\prod_i a_i>0.
\tag{2}
\]

Empty groups use empty products equal to one. This independently recovers round 5's main identity and its almost-everywhere, rather than uniform, positivity. A separate finite-state audit enumerates retained fibers with four path states and nonuniform rational weights; it is an additional check of the algebra, not a replacement for this path-space argument.

## 2. Skeleton law and filtration

Translate the chosen lift of the initial vertex to zero. The terminal displacement is ((n,d_2,d_3)), where (n\le K/8), (n\ge1), and (|d_j|\le n). Put

\[
 c=(1-2^{-1/4})/2,
 \qquad
 v_i^{(j)}=c\sum_{\ell\ge0}2^{-\ell/4}
 U^{(j)}_{\ell,\lfloor(i-1)/2^\ell\rfloor},
 \quad Z_t^{(j)}=\sum_{i=1}^t v_i^{(j)}.
\]

All variables (U) are independent and uniform on ([-1,1]); the two skeleton arrays are independent. Absolute convergence gives (|v_i^{(j)}|\le1/2), hence (|Z_t^{(j)}|\le t/2). With (h=\lfloor n/2\rfloor) and (f(t)=\min(t,n-t)), the base vertices are

\[
 u_t=(t,\lfloor td_2/n+Z_{f(t)}^{(2)}\rfloor,
             \lfloor td_3/n+Z_{f(t)}^{(3)}\rfloor).
\tag{3}
\]

Because (|f(t+1)-f(t)|\le1), each real transverse increment is at most (3/2) in magnitude. A difference of its two floors has magnitude at most two. The prescribed refinement takes the first-coordinate step, then the coordinate-two steps, then the coordinate-three steps. Each refinement has at most five steps. Every occurrence at first-coordinate level (t) is within maximum distance two of (u_t); level zero has one occurrence and every other level has at most five. No vertex at the join is added twice.

Fix the entire second array. Let (mathcal F_t) be generated by it and by all first-array variables whose index blocks meet ({1,\ldots,t}). This includes blocks of arbitrarily large length, some continuing into the future. It is nevertheless an increasing filtration, and (Z_t), (Z'_t), and (D_t:=|Z_t-Z'_t|_\infty) are (mathcal F_t)-measurable.

## 3. A fresh block and a quantitative conditional density bound

For (k\ge2), let (L) be the largest power of two at most (k/2). Then (k/4<L\le k/2). Set (q=L\lceil t/L\rceil). The aligned block ({q+1,\ldots,q+L}) starts strictly after (t), and its end is at most (t+2L-1\le t+k). It lies entirely in ({t+1,\ldots,t+k}). For (k=1), use its sole singleton. In particular its variable is absent from (mathcal F_t), even though some larger blocks intersecting the same interval have already been revealed.

For each transverse coordinate select the variable attached to this one block. In (Z_{t+k}^{(j)}) it appears exactly (L) times with coefficient (cL^{-1/4}), giving total coefficient (cL^{3/4}). Conditional on every variable except these two, the vector (Z_{t+k}) is a translate of two independent uniforms of half-width (cL^{3/4}). Its density is bounded by (1/(4c^2L^{3/2})). This conditioning contains (mathcal F_t), and averaging the other variables preserves the bound. Therefore, for (t+k\le h),

\[
 \|\operatorname{dens}(Z_{t+k}\mid\mathcal F_t)\|_\infty
 \le C_*k^{-3/2},\qquad C_*=2/c^2.
\tag{4}
\]

For (k\ge2) this follows from (L>k/4), since (4^{3/2}/4=2). For (k=1), the density bound (1/(4c^2)) is smaller. The infinite array causes no convergence problem: the defining series is absolutely convergent, and after removing the chosen variables its remaining sum is measurable with respect to the other variables.

Equivalently, for a nonnegative integrable function (g) and any (mathcal F_t)-measurable vector (a),

\[
 \mathbb E[g(Z_{t+k}-a)\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \le C_*k^{-3/2}\int_{\mathbb R^2}g(x)\,dx.
\tag{5}
\]

This conditional integral statement follows first for indicators of Borel sets and then by monotone approximation. It also justifies using the random center (a=Z'_{t+k}), since the entire second array was fixed initially.

## 4. Encounters of refined folded walks reduce to unfolded tests

Take occurrences (z,z') at first-coordinate levels (t,t'), and put (d=d_\infty(z,z')) on the torus. The first-coordinate difference has magnitude at most (n\le K/8), so it cannot wrap, and (|t-t'|\le d). The torus triangle inequality and the step bound from (3) give

\[
 d_\infty(u_t,u'_t)
 \le 2+d+2+2|t-t'|\le3d+4.
\tag{6}
\]

At equal times the deterministic drifts coincide. Each coordinate of each random deviation has magnitude at most (n/4). Thus the lifted difference between corresponding transverse base coordinates has magnitude less than (n/2+1\le K/16+1<K/2) for (K\ge8). Its periodic distance equals its ordinary absolute difference. Since the difference between two real numbers and the difference between their floors differ by less than one, (6) implies

\[
 D_{f(t)}\le3d+5.
\tag{7}
\]

For an occurrence within distance (R\ge1) of the opposing walk, (7) gives (D_{f(t)}\le a_R:=3R+5). At most two levels share the same value of (f(t)), with at most five occurrences in each. Counting both walks yields

\[
 J_{\rm occ}^{(R)}\le20\sum_{s=0}^h\mathbf1_{\{D_s\le a_R\}}.
\tag{8}
\]

For the one-sided soft count choose an opposing occurrence minimizing (d). Equation (7) gives (d\ge(D_{f(t)}-5)/3). Summing over at most ten first-walk occurrences per unfolded time yields

\[
 S_B\le H_B\sum_{s=0}^h e^{-D_s/(3B)},
 \qquad H_B=10e^{5/(3B)}.
\tag{9}
\]

This uses no independence of folded times. The duplicated endpoint (D_0=0) contributes only a bounded term.

## 5. Conditional future budgets

For the hard test take (X_s=\mathbf1_{\{D_s\le a_R\}}). The square of maximum radius (a_R) in two dimensions has area (4a_R^2). From (5),

\[
 \mathbb E[X_{t+k}\mid\mathcal F_t]\le4C_*a_R^2 k^{-3/2}.
\]

For the soft test take (X_s=e^{-D_s/(3B)}\in[0,1]). Its integral over two dimensions is

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^2}e^{-|x|_\infty/(3B)}dx
 =\int_0^\infty8r e^{-r/(3B)}dr=72B^2.
\tag{10}
\]

An independent layer-cake calculation gives the same value: it is (int_0^1 4(3B\log(1/u))^2du=36B^2\cdot2). By the integral test (sum_{k\ge1}k^{-3/2}\le1+int_1^\infty x^{-3/2}dx=3). Consequently, almost surely,

\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{s=t+1}^hX_s\mid\mathcal F_t\right]\le b,
 \quad b=12C_*a_R^2\ \text{or}\ 216C_*B^2,
\tag{11}
\]

respectively. These estimates are uniform even conditional on the second array. No future variable of the first array has been conditioned on beyond the specified filtration.

## 6. Ordered products and exponential moments

Put (B_0=1+b), and let

\[
 A_j=\mathbb E\sum_{0\le t_1<\cdots<t_j\le h}
                 X_{t_1}\cdots X_{t_j}.
\]

For (j=1), (11) at (t=0) and (X_0\le1) give (A_1\le B_0). For (j\ge2), hold (t_1,\ldots,t_{j-1}) fixed and condition the entire last-index sum on (mathcal F_{t_{j-1}}). All preceding factors are measurable, and (11) bounds the conditional last-index sum by (b). Summing then gives (A_j\le bA_{j-1}\le B_0^j). This is conditioning on the penultimate index after summing the last one; conditioning on each last factor separately would not justify the stated recursion.

Convexity gives (e^{\theta x}-1\le(e^\theta-1)x) for (0\le x\le1). Expanding the finite product and using the ordered-product bounds gives

\[
 \mathbb E e^{\theta\sum_{s=0}^hX_s}
 \le\sum_{j\ge0}\bigl((e^\theta-1)B_0\bigr)^j.
\tag{12}
\]

Choose (	heta=1/(4B_0)). Since (	heta\le1/4), the power series or convexity on ([0,1/4]) gives (e^\theta-1\le2\theta). Thus the ratio in (12) is at most (1/2), and the moment is at most two.

For the hard count, (a_R=3R+5\le4(R+1)) when (R\ge1), so

\[
 B_0=1+12C_*a_R^2
 \le(1+192C_*)(R+1)^2.
\]

Together with (8), this proves the source's claimed hard moment with the explicit, deliberately small constants

\[
 c_{\rm hit}=\frac1{80(1+192C_*)},
 \qquad C_{\rm hit}=2.
\tag{13}
\]

For the soft count, (9) proves the second moment claim with

\[
 \xi(B)=\frac1{4(1+216C_*B^2)H_B},
 \qquad C(B)=2.
\tag{14}
\]

These constants depend on no endpoint or volume parameter. The same bounds hold after fixing the whole independent opposing array, and hence hold after averaging it. If (h=0), the sums contain only (X_0\le1); the argument and bounds remain valid.

## 7. Adversarial checks and scope

The potentially dangerous blocks crossing the reveal time have already been exposed; the construction in Section 3 uses a block wholly to its right. One fresh block alone suffices, so no unjustified independence of the remaining future displacement is needed. The opponent's future positions are valid random centers because its full array belongs to every (mathcal F_t). Each coordinate supplies a separate independent uniform; one coordinate alone would give the nonsummable power (k^{-3/4}).

The folding reuses the first half of an array and creates dependence between the two legs. Equations (8) and (9) count this dependence deterministically by a factor of two; no conditional density is asserted in folded chronological order. The refined occurrence list, rather than a resampled interpolation, supplies the five-per-level bound. Equal-time transverse differences do not wrap, although individual paths or endpoints may cross the chosen torus seam. Arbitrary signs of (d_2,d_3), floor discontinuities, both endpoint levels, and the middle level are included.

The finite audit checks dyadic containment at all tested offsets, exact rational folded/refined path inequalities, and a separate finite-state conditional-law model. These checks corroborate the explicit analytic bounds; they do not approximate the infinite dyadic array or estimate the exponential moment by Monte Carlo.

No gap was found in Lemma 8.2. The environment-dependent conversion of skeletons into good nearest-neighbor routes remains unverified. In particular the deterministic detour charging inequality and the common-environment component expansion cannot be inferred merely from (13)–(14).
