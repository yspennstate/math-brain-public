# Fixed-background contour interactions

The result below supplies the cluster-expansion hypotheses left open in round 5. It concerns the truncated ensemble. A bound for untruncated contour weights, removal of truncation, and density coexistence remain separate questions.

The contour definitions are those in Sections 5–6 of [*Density coexistence for hard spheres with a long-range attractive pair interaction*, Hexagon 2610.00734v1](https://hexagonmath.org/api/v1/works/2610.00734v1/source?path=liquid-solid.tex), credited as generated by GPT-6 Astra and prompted by Qidong He. This manuscript remains unverified as a whole. The argument here proves the required numerical expansion explicitly; no novelty claim is made for the tree method.

## 1. Statement

Use the Chebyshev graph on the macrocell lattice \(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge2\). Set

\[
q_d=3^d,\qquad D=3^d-1,\qquad K=3^{m^d},\qquad B=D^2K.
\tag{1}
\]

Here each macrocell contains \(m^d\) phase cells, each with one of three labels. A contour \(\Gamma\) has a nonempty finite connected support \(S_\Gamma\), a realizable phase-cell code, and exterior phase \(c\). Write

\[
n_\Gamma=|S_\Gamma|,\quad E_\Gamma=N_1(S_\Gamma),\quad
C_\Gamma=S_\Gamma\cup\{\text{bounded complementary components}\}.
\]

Cardinalities in this note count macrocells. The geometry and conditional replacement established in rounds 3–4 are inputs: complementary collars have constant phase; bounded components have connected complement; compatible filled supports are nested or separated; and a support contained in a finite connected-complement domain has its filled support there. Compatibility means disjoint, nonadjacent supports. Each contour is incompatible with itself, including distinct occurrences of the same contour.

Let the physical interaction range be less than the macrocell side. Take the everywhere-defined truncated activities from the conditional support integrals, with cutoff

\[
0\le w_\Gamma(q)\le e^{-\tau n_\Gamma}.
\tag{2}
\]

The background space is the product of individually admissible phase-\(c\) macrostates. Cross-cell hard-core clashes are permitted in this product. They are imposed inside the physical integrals, rather than by restricting the domain of the activities. Safe fillings of positive reference mass are assumed as established in round 5, at its fixed admissible continuum parameters.

For finite \(A\), let

\[
\Xi_A(q)=\sum_{\mathcal F\text{ compatible},\ E_\Gamma\subset A\ (\Gamma\in\mathcal F)}
\prod_{\Gamma\in\mathcal F}w_\Gamma(q).
\tag{3}
\]

**Theorem.** Put

\[
\tau_*={4\over3}\bigl(1+\log B+\log(q_d+1)\bigr),\qquad
r_\tau=B e^{1-3\tau/4}.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(\tau\ge\tau_*\), the recursive activities are bounded measurable functions of their exterior interaction collars, independent of the surrounding domain. There is an everywhere-defined, measurable, lattice-translation-covariant interaction \(U_X\), indexed by nonempty finite connected macrocell sets, such that

\[
\sum_{X\ni0} e^{\tau |X|/(4q_d)}\|U_X\|_\infty
\le \epsilon_\tau:={q_dr_\tau\over1-r_\tau}
\le 2q_deB e^{-3\tau/4},
\tag{5}
\]

and, for every finite \(A\),

\[
\log\Xi_A(q)=-\sum_{X\subset A}U_X(q_X).
\tag{6}
\]

For the physical recursive ensemble only connected-complement domains are used. Once the activities have been defined globally, (6) holds for any finite catalog cutoff of the form in (3), even for perforated \(A\). It makes no assertion identifying such a catalog integral with a physical diluted ensemble on a perforated domain.

The constants depend on \(d,m\), which are fixed before \(\tau\) is chosen. The norm is uniform in microscopic backgrounds and in other parameters appearing in the untruncated integrals, provided their cutoff is (2). Translation covariance here means translation by a macrocell lattice vector; it is the covariance required by the round 5 lattice surface theorem.

## 2. Recursive locality and the product state space

The conditional definition is

\[
w_\Gamma(q)=\min\{\widehat N_\Gamma(q)/\widehat D_\Gamma(q),
e^{-\tau n_\Gamma}\},
\tag{7}
\]

where the numerator integrates support particles with the contour code and the product of truncated diluted partition functions in its bounded components, with their code phases. The denominator integrates pure phase-\(c\) support particles with the same-phase truncated component factors. The support Hamiltonian contains its internal interactions and those with the unbounded exterior. The component factors contain the interactions between support and components. Exterior-only interactions are omitted.

Induct on \(|C_\Gamma|\), simultaneously over contour types. Every contour in a bounded component has filled support strictly smaller than \(C_\Gamma\). Its definition therefore precedes (7). In a finite component the auxiliary catalog and the compatible-family sum are finite. The empty family has weight one. Safe pure-phase configurations in all its cells have positive mass and obey every hard constraint, including those against any prescribed exterior macrostates. Thus its truncated partition function is strictly positive. The same argument on the support and components proves \(\widehat D_\Gamma(q)>0\).

All integrals are finite. At fixed macrocell size a hard-core packing bound limits the particles in each individually admissible cell. The negative pair energy in a finite set of cells is bounded by the number of possible partners times that finite particle bound. Cross-cell hard-core factors only decrease integrands. A finite catalog contributes at most its number of subsets, since each truncated activity is at most one. Consequently numerator and denominator are finite for every product background, even when its exterior cells clash with each other. No exterior-only hard-core indicator is inserted into (7).

Let \(F\) be a bounded complementary component. It has no physical interaction with the unbounded exterior, since those components have no adjacent macrocells and the interaction range is less than the macrocell side. Its physical partition factor depends on support particles alone. Its internal contour factors use \(E_\Delta\subset F\); by induction their arguments also remain internal to \(F\). Therefore no component factor brings any additional dependence on the original exterior \(q\). The only such dependence in (7) is the direct interaction of support particles with exterior particles within physical range. This collar lies in \(E_\Gamma\setminus C_\Gamma\).

In particular, \(w_\Gamma\) is independent of all background particles in its own filled support. It is also independent of any surrounding recursion domain \(A\). The bounded components and their canonical catalogs are determined by the contour, and no step uses the outside boundary of \(A\). Measurability follows from measurable finite sums, parameter integrals, division by a positive denominator, and the minimum. The inductive construction commutes with macrocell translations. This proves all the locality assertions, without a mixing assumption.

## 3. Contour counting

For a fixed vertex \(y\), the number of connected supports of size \(n\) containing \(y\) is at most \(D^{2(n-1)}\). To see this, select a deterministic spanning tree of each support and perform its depth-first traversal starting at \(y\). This is a walk of length \(2(n-1)\) with at most \(D\) choices per step. The visited vertices recover the support, so different supports have different selected walks. Each support has at most \(K^n\) codes. In particular

\[
\#\{\Gamma:n_\Gamma=n,\ y\in S_\Gamma\}\le B^n.
\tag{8}
\]

Realizability and exterior type only reduce this count. Since \(x\in E_\Gamma\) implies \(S_\Gamma\cap N_1(\{x\})\ne\varnothing\),

\[
\#\{\Gamma:n_\Gamma=n,\ x\in E_\Gamma\}\le q_dB^n.
\tag{9}
\]

Likewise an incompatible \(\Delta\) has \(S_\Delta\cap N_1(S_\Gamma)\ne\varnothing\), so

\[
\#\{\Delta:n_\Delta=k,\ \Delta\not\sim\Gamma\}
\le q_dn_\Gamma B^k.
\tag{10}
\]

This includes self-incompatibility. No count of the filled volume is needed.

## 4. Connected graph coefficients and a tree bound

For an ordered tuple of contour occurrences \(\boldsymbol\Gamma=(\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_k)\), let \(G\) be its incompatibility graph on the distinct occurrence labels \(1,\ldots,k\). Thus repeated contours give edges between their occurrences. Define

\[
\varphi(\boldsymbol\Gamma)=
\sum_{H\subset G\text{ connected spanning}}(-1)^{|E(H)|},
\tag{11}
\]

with value one for \(k=1\). The following bound is sufficient:

\[
|\varphi(\boldsymbol\Gamma)|
\le\sum_{T\text{ tree on }[k]}\prod_{ij\in E(T)}
\mathbf1_{\Gamma_i\not\sim\Gamma_j}.
\tag{12}
\]

Here is a proof. Root a connected graph \(H\) at occurrence 1. Use its distance levels from 1, and give each other vertex the least-labeled neighbor in its preceding level as parent. This defines a spanning tree \(T(H)\). Given a rooted tree \(T\), let \(R(T)\) contain its edges, every edge within a level, and every edge between consecutive levels whose preceding-level endpoint has label at least that of the later vertex's parent. Then

\[
T(H)=T\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\subset H\subset R(T).
\]

Indeed such extra edges neither shorten distances from the root nor change the least preceding-level neighbor. Conversely edges skipping a level would shorten a distance, and an edge to a smaller preceding-level label would change a parent. Restricted to subgraphs of \(G\), these intervals are
\([T,R(T)\cap G]\) for spanning trees \(T\subset G\). Their alternating sums equal \((-1)^{k-1}(1-1)^j\), where \(j\) is the number of optional edges. Each interval therefore contributes either zero or \((-1)^{k-1}\). The intervals partition the connected spanning subgraphs, proving (12). This also proves the common sign \((-1)^{k-1}\) of nonzero coefficients.

## 5. Rooted sums with explicit constants

For this section use the tilted positive majorants

\[
b_\Gamma=e^{-3\tau n_\Gamma/4}.
\]

By (10),

\[
\sum_{\Delta\not\sim\Gamma}b_\Delta e^{n_\Delta}
\le q_dn_\Gamma\sum_{k\ge1}r_\tau^k
=n_\Gamma\epsilon_\tau\le n_\Gamma.
\tag{13}
\]

The last inequality is exactly \(r_\tau\le1/(q_d+1)\), equivalent to \(\tau\ge\tau_*\).

Fix a root contour \(\Gamma\). The positive rooted-tree sum omitting the root activity is at most \(e^{n_\Gamma}\). For completeness, truncate tree height at \(h\). Its sums satisfy

\[
T_\Gamma^{(0)}=1,\qquad
T_\Gamma^{(h+1)}=
\exp\left\{\sum_{\Delta\not\sim\Gamma}b_\Delta T_\Delta^{(h)}\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]

The exponential enumerates the unordered collection of child subtrees with its factorial. Equivalently, in a rooted tree with \(k-1\) other distinctly labeled vertices, partition those labels among the children; the multinomial coefficient, divided by \((k-1)!\), gives precisely the child factorials and the factorials of their subtree labels. This proves (14) with the exponential generating-function convention \(1/(k-1)!\). Contour labels themselves may repeat.

Equation (13) and induction give \(T_\Gamma^{(h)}\le e^{n_\Gamma}\). Let height and finite contour catalogs increase; all terms are positive, and every finite tree is eventually included. Equation (12) then implies

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}{1\over(k-1)!}
\sum_{\Gamma_2,\ldots,\Gamma_k}
|\varphi(\Gamma,\Gamma_2,\ldots,\Gamma_k)|
\prod_{j=2}^k b_{\Gamma_j}\le e^{n_\Gamma}.
\tag{15}
\]

To anchor a cluster at a site \(x\), use
\(\mathbf1_{x\in\cup_jE_{\Gamma_j}}\le\sum_j\mathbf1_{x\in E_{\Gamma_j}}\).
Symmetry changes \(k/k!\) into \(1/(k-1)!\); this counts occurrences even when contour labels repeat. Combining (9) and (15) gives

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}{1\over k!}\sum_{\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_k}
\mathbf1_{x\in\cup_jE_{\Gamma_j}}
|\varphi(\boldsymbol\Gamma)|\prod_j b_{\Gamma_j}
\le\sum_{\Gamma:x\in E_\Gamma}b_\Gamma e^{n_\Gamma}
\le\epsilon_\tau.
\tag{16}
\]

Since \(r_\tau\le1/(q_d+1)\le1/2\), (5)'s second inequality follows from \((1-r_\tau)^{-1}\le2\). The factor \(e\) in that inequality comes from the \(e^{n_\Delta}\) in (13), not from a numerical approximation.

## 6. The logarithm and the interaction

For a fixed finite catalog, insert a complex scalar \(z\) multiplying every activity. Formally,

\[
\Xi_A(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}{z^k\over k!}
\sum_{\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_k\in\mathcal C(A)}
\prod_jw_{\Gamma_j}\prod_{i<j}(1-\mathbf1_{\Gamma_i\not\sim\Gamma_j}).
\tag{17}
\]

Self-incompatibility eliminates repetitions in (17), so it is exactly the compatible-subset polynomial. Expanding the edge product into graphs, and uniquely splitting every graph into its connected components, gives the formal identity

\[
\Xi_A(z)=\exp\left\{
\sum_{k\ge1}{z^k\over k!}
\sum_{\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_k\in\mathcal C(A)}
\varphi(\boldsymbol\Gamma)\prod_jw_{\Gamma_j}\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]

This formal identity is also analytic at \(z=1\). If \(|z|\le e^{\tau/4}\), then, since \(n_\Gamma\ge1\),
\(|z|w_\Gamma\le e^{-3\tau n_\Gamma/4}=b_\Gamma\).
The bound (16), summed over sites in \(A\), gives absolute uniform convergence of the connected series on that disk. On its open disk it is analytic; (18) holds near zero by the formal coefficients and therefore everywhere in the disk by analytic continuation. In particular \(\Xi_A\) has no zeros there. At real nonnegative activities the series is real, \(\Xi_A(1)>0\), and (18) is its real logarithm. This proves the numerical cluster expansion without assuming a background probability law.

Assign each connected term the set

\[
X(\boldsymbol\Gamma)=\bigcup_jE_{\Gamma_j},\qquad
U_X(q_X)=-\sum_{k\ge1}{1\over k!}
\sum_{\boldsymbol\Gamma:X(\boldsymbol\Gamma)=X}
\varphi(\boldsymbol\Gamma)\prod_j w_{\Gamma_j}(q).
\tag{19}
\]

Each factor depends on coordinates in its assigned set by Section 2. Each \(E_\Gamma\) is connected. When two contours are incompatible their supports intersect or are adjacent; their enlargements consequently meet. Thus a connected incompatibility graph gives a connected \(X\). A term with disconnected graph has zero coefficient. Also

\[
|X|\le q_d\sum_j n_{\Gamma_j}.
\tag{20}
\]

Taking supremum norms before grouping and using (2), (16), and (20), proves (5). It proves absolute uniform convergence defining every \(U_X\), hence its boundedness and measurability everywhere on the product space. Translation covariance follows term by term. This argument does not average contour weights over microscopic backgrounds.

A tuple belongs to the finite catalog precisely when every \(E_{\Gamma_j}\subset A\), equivalently \(X(\boldsymbol\Gamma)\subset A\). Its activity agrees with its global activity by Section 2, and its graph coefficient is unchanged. Absolute convergence now permits grouping the numerical expansion by \(X\), giving (6) exactly.

Domain independence is essential here. For a one-contour catalog, changing its activity from \(u\) to \(v\) with the surrounding domain changes the coefficient of \(z\) in \(\log(1+zw)\) from \(u\) to \(v\). Arbitrarily small cutoffs would not repair this. Section 2 establishes independence from the domain through the actual recursive definition; it is not inferred from smallness.

Nested compatible contours have no incompatibility edge merely because of nesting. Compatible contours with overlapping \(E\)'s also have no such edge. They remain in (17); dependencies through a shared microscopic variable are retained in the functions (19). Repetitions do occur in (18)–(19). For a catalog with one contour the series is \(w-w^2/2+w^3/3-\cdots=\log(1+w)\), showing why repetitions cannot be discarded.

## 7. Consequence for the surface formula

The round 5 theorem requires an everywhere bounded measurable connected interaction, its incident norm, and the exact identity (6). These are now established for the truncated contour gas, with \(\varepsilon_U\le\epsilon_\tau\). The integral in (3) uses internal \(U\) terms. The conditioned surface theorem includes the crossing \(U\) terms as well. Every connected crossing set meets the inner boundary, so

\[
\sum_{X\text{ crossing }A}\|U_X\|_\infty
\le\epsilon_\tau|\partial A|.
\]

Consequently replacing one convention by the other changes the logarithmic partition function by at most \(\epsilon_\tau|\partial A|\), pointwise before integration. The round 5 truncated surface estimate follows with its stated additional boundary error and the explicit bound (5). This closes its Section 6 cluster-expansion condition under the established geometry, finite integrals and safe fillings.

Neither (2) nor this conclusion bounds an untruncated contour ratio by its cutoff. The next analytic issue is precisely to prove that the cutoff is inactive at coexistence. In particular no distinct-density infinite-volume Gibbs measures are constructed here.
