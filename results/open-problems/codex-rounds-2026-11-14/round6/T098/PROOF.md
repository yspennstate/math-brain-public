# Size tails for the first discarded blocks

## 1. Model and blocks

Let \(\Lambda\) be a finite integer interval, with open ends, and
\[
 H_0=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}h_iZ_i+\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\xi_i g_iX_i
       +\sum_{i,i+1\in\Lambda}J_iZ_iZ_{i+1}.
\]
The indicators are iid Bernoulli with active probability \(0<p<1\).
The fields are iid with density bounded by \(\rho<\infty\), independently
of the indicators and of \(g,J\). The latter coefficients are finite real
numbers; they can be deterministic or random, independently of the fields
and indicators. No independence between different coefficients is needed.

A site is frozen when \(\xi_f=0\). For each frozen \(f\), let \(a_f,b_f\)
be its adjacent active-run lengths, stopped at another frozen site or a
physical end. Set \(m_f=a_f+b_f\) and
\[
 C_f=[f-a_f,f+b_f],\qquad
 I_f=N_1(C_f)\cap\Lambda.
\]
The extra sites of \(I_f\), when present, are frozen controls. In particular
\(\operatorname{diam}I_f=m_f+c_f\), where \(c_f\in\{0,1,2\}\) is the
number of existing outer controls.

For each outer frozen-spin assignment \(\eta\), write \(K_f^\pm(\eta)\)
for the Hamiltonians of the two adjacent active blocks with \(Z_f=\pm1\).
Include their bonds to the controls and, for an empty adjacent block, the
corresponding direct frozen-frozen bond as a scalar. Exclude all frozen
longitudinal fields. Let \(\lambda_u^\pm(\eta)\) be their eigenvalues.
Fix \(\delta>0\) and an integer \(\ell\ge2\). A frozen site is retained if
\[
 \operatorname{diam}I_f\le\ell,\qquad
 |2h_f+\lambda_u^+(\eta)-\lambda_v^-(\eta)|\ge\delta
       \quad\hbox{for every }\eta,u,v.                    \tag{1}
\]
Let \(D\) be the discarded frozen sites, including every failure of the
diameter cutoff. This is precisely the first-step selection rule of round 5.

Define two subsets of physical sites:
\[
 B=\bigcup_{f\in D}C_f,\qquad
 E=\bigcup_{f\in D}I_f=N_1(B)\cap\Lambda.                 \tag{2}
\]
Every component below uses the physical nearest-neighbor graph: sites are
adjacent when their distance is one. The exceptional blocks are the maximal
components of \(E\). Thus overlapping or touching discarded cells are merged,
including cells that share a retained frozen control. Their actual sizes
are unrestricted. Components of \(B\) will be called cores. Distinct cores
can acquire overlapping collars; cores and exceptional blocks are different
objects.

Write \(\mathcal E_x\) for the exceptional block containing \(x\), and set
\(\mathcal E_x=\varnothing\) when \(x\notin E\). Define \(\mathcal B_x\)
in the same way for cores. Size means number of physical sites.

## 2. Bounds

Put \(t=4\rho\delta\), and suppose \(t<1\). Define
\[
 \kappa=1-(1-p)^5(1-t)^3<1.                              \tag{3}
\]
For every volume, site \(x\), and integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|\mathcal E_x|\ge n)
   \le\min\{1,\ n\kappa^{\lfloor n/5\rfloor}\}.         \tag{4}
\]
In addition, the probability that there exists any exceptional block of
size at least \(n\) is at most
\[
 \min\{1,\ |\Lambda|\kappa^{\lfloor n/5\rfloor}\}.       \tag{5}
\]
These estimates include all oversized cells and do not assert independence
of neighboring retained events before conditioning.

There is also a vanishing density bound. Set
\[
 F_p(t)=(1-p)^2\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)p^k\min(1,t4^k),
\]
\[
 b=\min\{1,F_p(t)+p^{\ell-1}[\ell-(\ell-1)p]\},
 \qquad
 \eta_r=\min\{1,(2r+1)(1-p)b+2p^r\}\quad(r\ge1).
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
For any integer \(r\ge1\), (4) strengthens to
\[
 \mathbb P(|\mathcal E_x|\ge n)
 \le\min\{1,\ 3\eta_r,\ n\kappa^{\lfloor n/5\rfloor}\}.
                                                               \tag{7}
\]

A sharper interval estimate is available for the cores. Choose
\(0<s\le1\) such that \(p16^s<1\), and put
\[
 u=\max\{t^s,4^{-s(\ell-1)}\},\quad
 M=\frac{1-p}{1-p4^s},\quad
 \lambda=p16^s+(1-p)u.                                  \tag{8}
\]
For every deterministic nonempty interval \(J\subseteq\Lambda\),
\[
 \mathbb P(J\subseteq B)\le M^2\lambda^{|J|},\qquad
 \mathbb P(|\mathcal B_x|\ge n)
       \le\min\{1,\eta_r,\ nM^2\lambda^n\}.             \tag{9}
\]
The bounds are useful as size tails when \(\lambda<1\), which holds for
all sufficiently small \(t\) and sufficiently large \(\ell\) at fixed
\(p,s\). Bound (4) needs only \(t<1\), not this additional smallness.

## 3. Conditional independence at frozen sites

Condition on the sigma-algebra \(\mathcal A\) generated by the indicators,
all fields at active sites, and \(g,J\). Conditional on each indicator
configuration, the remaining fields at frozen sites are independent with
their original common law.

The eigenvalues \(\lambda_u^\pm(\eta)\) in (1) are
\(\mathcal A\)-measurable. The adjacent-block Hamiltonians use only active
fields, transverse coefficients, bonds, and fixed frozen-spin signs. A
longitudinal field at an outer frozen control contributes the same scalar
to both sectors and cancels. The fields at all other frozen sites also
cancel as spectator scalars. Taking every outer assignment in (1) introduces
no frozen field. Thus the event \(f\in D\), for a frozen \(f\), is a
function only of \(\mathcal A\) and \(h_f\).

It follows that the events \(\{f\in D\}\) at distinct frozen sites are
independent conditional on \(\mathcal A\). This assertion concerns the
selection events, not all many-body energy denominators.

There are at most four outer assignments and \(4^{m_f}\) ordered pairs
of adjacent-block eigenstates per assignment. Each inequality
\(|2h_f+c|<\delta\) restricts \(h_f\) to an interval of length \(\delta\),
and hence has conditional probability at most \(\rho\delta\). Reverse
flips give the same intervals. Therefore the conditional discard probability
\(d_f=\mathbb P(f\in D\mid\mathcal A)\) satisfies
\[
 d_f\le
 \begin{cases}
  1,&\operatorname{diam}I_f>\ell,\\
  \min(1,t4^{m_f}),&\operatorname{diam}I_f\le\ell.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{10}
\]
This remains true with degenerate active spectra or zero transition matrix
elements; the selection event conservatively tests all pairs.

## 4. Separators for the full discarded cells

Consider any five consecutive sites \(j,j+1,\ldots,j+4\) inside
\(\Lambda\). Call this five-site interval a separator when all five sites
are frozen and the middle three \(j+1,j+2,j+3\) are retained.

On the all-frozen indicator event, each of the middle three has no adjacent
active spin. Its cell has diameter two and passes the cutoff \(\ell\ge2\).
Its only possible resonance differences are
\[
 2h_f+2J_{f-1}\eta_-+2J_f\eta_+,
       \qquad \eta_-,\eta_+\in\{-1,1\}.                 \tag{11}
\]
Each is bad with probability at most \(\rho\delta\), so retention has
conditional probability at least \(1-t\). For these three sites the
retention events are independent conditional on the bonds, since they
depend on three different longitudinal fields. Therefore, conditional on
\(g,J\), the separator event has probability at least
\[
 \sigma=(1-p)^5(1-t)^3.                                 \tag{12}
\]
It depends only on the indicators in its five sites, the three middle
fields, and the four internal bonds. Consequently separator events in
disjoint five-site intervals are independent conditional on \(g,J\).
Even arbitrarily correlated random bonds cause no difficulty after this
conditioning; (12) is uniform in their values.

The center \(j+2\) of a separator cannot belong to \(E\). To check this
directly, an active-run core \(C_f\) contains no frozen site other than
its center \(f\). Its collar \(I_f\) can contain a different frozen site
only when that site is the predecessor or successor of \(f\) in the
ordered frozen-site list. The predecessor and successor of \(j+2\) are
\(j+1,j+3\); all three are retained. No discarded cell can therefore
contain \(j+2\).

Partition any deterministic interval \(J\) of length \(n\) into
\(\lfloor n/5\rfloor\) disjoint five-site intervals, leaving at most four
sites unused. If \(J\subseteq E\), none of those intervals is a separator.
Independence conditional on \(g,J\), followed by (12), gives
\[
 \mathbb P(J\subseteq E\mid (g_i,J_i)_i)
     \le(1-\sigma)^{\lfloor n/5\rfloor}.
                                                               \tag{13}
\]
The bound does not depend on the location of the test interval. Averaging
over the coefficients gives the same unconditional inequality.

If a connected interval containing \(x\) has at least \(n\) sites, it
contains an interval of exactly \(n\) sites that contains \(x\). There
are at most \(n\) such test intervals. Applying (13) to them proves (4).
There are at most \(|\Lambda|\) test intervals of length \(n\) anywhere
in the chain; this proves (5). If \(n>|\Lambda|\), the events are empty.

## 5. Rarity of exceptional sites

First recover the fixed-site input in (6). Conditional on a specified
frozen site, its two active lengths are bounded by independent infinite
geometric runs of mass \((1-p)p^k\). Averaging the capped resonance bound
in (10) gives \(F_p(t)\). Failure of the diameter cutoff implies
\(m_f\ge\ell-1\). The convolution of the two geometric lengths has mass
\((k+1)(1-p)^2p^k\), whose tail from \(k=q\) is
\[
 (1-p)^2\sum_{k\ge q}(k+1)p^k
       =p^q[(q+1)-qp].                                  \tag{14}
\]
This follows by summing the geometric series and its derivative.
Finite physical ends only shorten the runs. A union bound gives
\[
 \mathbb P(f\in D)\le(1-p)b                              \tag{15}
\]
at each deterministic physical site \(f\).

Introduce the occupied set
\[
 O=\{i:\xi_i=1\}\cup D.
\]
The cores in (2) are exactly those components of \(O\) which contain a
discarded frozen site. Indeed every active run inside such a component
touches a discarded frozen site at one or both ends; otherwise the run
would be a separate component containing no discarded site. Conversely
each \(C_f\), \(f\in D\), lies in the occupied component of \(f\).

If \(x\in B\), choose a nearest discarded site in its occupied component.
If it is within distance \(r\), (15) and a union bound give probability
at most \((2r+1)(1-p)b\). If it is farther away, all intermediate sites
are active: a frozen intermediate site in \(O\) would itself be discarded
and nearer. Thus either the next \(r\) sites to the left or the next
\(r\) sites to the right are active. The probability of this alternative
is at most \(2p^r\); missing physical sites cannot contribute. This proves
\(\mathbb P(x\in B)\le\eta_r\). Since \(E=N_1(B)\cap\Lambda\), a union
bound over the three possible neighboring core sites gives
\[
 \mathbb P(x\in E)\le\min(1,3\eta_r).                    \tag{16}
\]
Combining (16) with (4) proves (7).

## 6. Fractional moment for the cores

Failure of the cutoff implies \(m_f\ge\ell-1\). On this event,
\(1\le4^{-s(\ell-1)}4^{sm_f}\). Otherwise
\(\min(1,t4^{m_f})\le t^s4^{sm_f}\) for \(0<s\le1\). By the two
exclusive cases of (10),
\[
 d_f\le u4^{sm_f}.                                      \tag{17}
\]
The maximum in (8), rather than a sum, is valid because the cases are
exclusive. The right side need not be capped at one for this estimate.

Fix a deterministic interval \(J\) of length \(n\). Let \(k\) be its
number of frozen sites, and let \(a=n-k\) be its number of active sites.
Let \(L_-,L_+\) count consecutive active sites just outside its two ends,
stopped at a frozen site or a physical end. Each active site inside \(J\)
can enter \(m_f\), for frozen \(f\in J\), at most twice, once for either
endpoint of its active run. An active site outside \(J\) can enter only
through the external run on its side, and can be counted only once.
Consequently
\[
 \sum_{f\in J:\xi_f=0}m_f\le2a+L_-+L_+.                 \tag{18}
\]
This also holds when \(J\) contains no frozen site, since the left side
then vanishes.

Conditional independence and (17) imply
\[
 \mathbb P(J\subseteq O\mid\mathcal A)
    =\prod_{f\in J:\xi_f=0}d_f
    \le u^k4^{s(2a+L_-+L_+)}.                           \tag{19}
\]
For a geometric run \(L\),
\[
 \mathbb E4^{sL}=(1-p)\sum_{q\ge0}(p4^s)^q
      =M.
\]
It is finite because \(p16^s<1\) implies \(p4^s<1\). The two external
runs are independent of each other and of the indicators inside \(J\);
their finite-volume truncations have moments at most \(M\). Averaging
(19) therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P(J\subseteq O)
 \le M^2\sum_{k=0}^n {n\choose k}
          [(1-p)u]^k[p16^s]^{n-k}
 =M^2\lambda^n.                                        \tag{20}
\]
Since \(B\subseteq O\), this proves the first assertion of (9).
Applying the same length-\(n\) interval argument used for (4), and then
the core-density estimate, proves the rest of (9).

## 7. Small perturbations and scope

At fixed \(p<1\), let \(\delta\downarrow0\) and \(\ell\to\infty\).
The capped geometric sum \(F_p(4\rho\delta)\) tends to zero by dominated
convergence, and so does the cutoff term in (6). Thus \(b\to0\). For
\(0<b<1\), take
\[
 r=\max\{1,\lceil\log(1/b)/\log(1/p)\rceil\}.
\]
Then \(p^r\le b\), and (16) gives exceptional-site probability
\(O_p(b[1+\log(1/b)])\). Meanwhile \(\kappa\to1-(1-p)^5<1\).
For example, once \(t\le1/2\),
\[
 \kappa\le1-(1-p)^5/8<1,                                \tag{21}
\]
uniformly in the cutoff. The polynomial prefactor in (4) can be absorbed
into an exponential at a smaller rate: if
\(a=-\log\kappa/5\), then
\[
 n\kappa^{\lfloor n/5\rfloor}
 \le\kappa^{-1}n e^{-an}
 \le\frac{2}{ae\kappa}e^{-an/2},                        \tag{22}
\]
using \(\sup_{y\ge0}y e^{-ay/2}=2/(ae)\).

These choices are compatible with the previous first rotation:
\(\delta=\epsilon^\beta\), \(0<\beta<1\),
\(\ell=\max\{2,\lceil c\log(1/\epsilon)\rceil\}\), and
\(\mu=1/\ell\), for fixed \(c>0\). Its sufficient smallness parameter
\(30e\sqrt{8/3}\,\epsilon^{1-\beta}\) tends to zero, while the exceptional
blocks above have vanishing site probability and a volume-uniform
exponential size tail. For each fixed \(p\), that size rate can be bounded
away from zero as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).

The estimates test every local state in (1); there is no energy restriction.
They describe only the first discarded blocks. The new interactions after
the first rotation can mix active labels and have renormalized denominators.
Those events need not be independent functions of unused frozen fields.
No iteration of (10), control of block growth under later rotations, or
construction of exact l-bits for nonzero transverse fields at every site
follows from these estimates.
