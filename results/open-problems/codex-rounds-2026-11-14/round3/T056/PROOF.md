# A relative-cost estimate for an amalgam

The estimate below is the deployment proposition in OpenAI, *A group without fixed price* (5 October 2026), [deployment source](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/A-group-without-fixed-price-October-5-2026/build/deployment.tex), with its [compression lemma](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/A-group-without-fixed-price-October-5-2026/build/compression.tex). This proves one intermediate estimate. It does not prove a positive lower bound for the relative quantity defined below.

## Statement and conventions

Let A and B be countable groups with a common infinite subgroup J, and suppose that Gamma = A *_J B is finitely generated. Let Gamma act freely by probability-preserving Borel automorphisms of a standard probability space (X,mu). Use right actions. Write R_L for the orbit relation of a subgroup L. For a countable family E of measure-preserving Borel partial bijections, let C(E) be the sum of the measures of their domains, counting each indexed map separately. Joins mean the equivalence relation generated by the indicated relations and maps.

Set

\[
 d_X=\inf\{C(F):F\subset R_A,\quad R_J\vee F=R_A\}.
\]

Then

\[
 \operatorname{Cost}(R_\Gamma)\ge 1+d_X.                 \tag{1}
\]

For an essentially free action, first remove the invariant null set of points with nontrivial stabilizer. Any further countable collection of almost-everywhere generation identities can also be made exact: remove the saturation of their exceptional sets under R_Gamma. Such saturations are null because the relation is enumerated by countably many measure-preserving maps. None of these restrictions changes a graphing's cost or the infimum defining it.

## 1. Component mass and complete sets

Work first on a standard Borel space Z with a finite measure lambda and a countable probability-measure-preserving equivalence relation R. Here “probability-measure-preserving” refers to preservation by its partial bijections; lambda need not have total mass one. Suppose R is enumerated by countably many measure-preserving Borel partial bijections. For a Borel subrelation S define

\[
 k(S;W)=\int_W |[z]_S|^{-1}\,d\lambda(z),\qquad
 k(S)=k(S;Z),
\]

where the integrand is zero on infinite classes. All class sizes and saturations are Borel: restrict the enumeration of R to S and count distinct values. Every Borel partial bijection inside R preserves lambda, by partitioning its domain according to the first enumerating map that agrees with it.

If W is S-invariant and its S-classes are finite, a Borel transversal D of these classes has measure k(S;W). To verify this, restrict to classes of size h. Order each finite class by a fixed Borel injection of Z into the real line. The maps taking its selected point to its first, second, ..., h-th points are partial bijections in R. Thus its h sheets all have measure lambda(D_h), while the integral on their union is their total measure divided by h. Sum over h.

For every Borel subrelation T and every e>0 there is a Borel set M meeting every T-class with

\[
 \lambda(M)\le k(T)+e.                                \tag{2}
\]

On the finite-class part choose one point per class as above. Its measure is exactly k(T). On the infinite-class part Z_infty, take increasing finite Borel partitions separating points. Let h_l(z) count the atoms of the l-th partition meeting [z]_T. For every z in Z_infty, h_l(z) tends to infinity: any prescribed finite set of distinct class members is eventually separated.

Choose 0<p<1 with p lambda(Z_infty)<e/2. At level l mark each atom independently with probability p, and let V be the union of the marked atoms inside Z_infty. The set

\[
 M_\infty=V\cup(Z_\infty\setminus[V]_T)
\]

meets every infinite class for every marking. Its expected measure is

\[
 p\lambda(Z_\infty)+
 \int_{Z_\infty}(1-p)^{h_l(z)}\,d\lambda(z).
\]

The integral tends to zero by dominated convergence. At a sufficiently large finite level some marking therefore gives lambda(M_infty)<e. Combining the finite and infinite parts proves (2). If Z_infty is empty, only the finite part is needed.

## 2. Compression with a supplied relation

Suppose S subset T subset Q subset R are Borel equivalence relations and Q=S join E with C(E)<infinity. For every e>0 there is a graphing F inside Q such that

\[
 Q=T\vee F,\qquad
 C(F)\le C(E)-k(S)+k(T)+e.                            \tag{3}
\]

Choose M as in (2) for T. Put Y=[M]_S together with the union of all infinite S-classes. Then Y is S-invariant and meets every T-class, hence every Q-class. The S-classes outside Y are finite. For each finite S-class inside Y choose one point of M in that class. The transversal calculation gives

\[
 k(S;Y)\le\lambda(M).                               \tag{4}
\]

Make a graph on the S-classes by keeping the indexed edges of E, allowing traversal in either direction. Every class has finite graph distance to Y, since Q=S join E and Y is Q-complete. The distance is Borel and constant on S-classes. For each class outside Y select one oriented E-edge descending by one in this distance. This can be done Borelly: first choose the least eligible map index and orientation, then the least eligible origin in the finite class. Call these selected edges parent edges.

The parent-edge origins form an S-transversal of Z minus Y and therefore have total measure k(S;Z minus Y). A particular indexed map instance cannot be selected in both directions: its endpoints would each have strictly smaller distance than the other. For E=(phi_i), delete each selected forward instance from its map's domain, and for each selected inverse instance delete its preimage under phi_i. The two deleted domain pieces for a fixed map are disjoint. Inverse images preserve measure. Consequently the residual indexed graphing E_0 satisfies

\[
 C(E_0)=C(E)-k(S;Z\setminus Y).                       \tag{5}
\]

Contract each finite S-class outside Y along its parent edge. More precisely, define f:Z to Y recursively by distance. On Y it is the identity. On an outside class K it has the constant value f(z'_K), where z'_K is the target of its parent edge. The recursion terminates at distance zero, so f is Borel, (z,f(z)) belongs to Q, and f sends S-related points to S-related points. It sends both ends of every parent edge to the same point.

For every residual map phi_i, partition its domain according to enumerating partial bijections psi_r and psi_s satisfying

\[
 f(z)=\psi_r(z),\qquad f(\phi_i(z))=\psi_s(\phi_i(z)),
\]

choosing the first such indices. They exist because the graph of f is inside R. On a piece P the pushed map is

\[
 \psi_s\circ\phi_i\circ\psi_r^{-1}
   :\psi_r(P)\longrightarrow\psi_s(\phi_i(P)).
\]

It is a Borel measure-preserving partial bijection in Q restricted to Y, with cost lambda(P). The indexed family F of all pushed maps thus has C(F)=C(E_0); discarding identities only lowers its cost. This partition at both endpoints is essential: f itself need not be injective.

For Q-related points of Y, push an S,E path through f. S-steps remain S-steps; parent edges disappear; every other edge becomes an F-edge. Since f fixes the endpoints, Q restricted to Y is generated by S restricted to Y and F. For arbitrary Q-related z,z', choose y in [z]_T intersect Y and y' in [z']_T intersect Y. These choices exist by T-completeness, and y,y' are Q-related. The T-steps to y,y' and the path inside Y give Q=T join F.

Finally (4) and (5) yield

\[
 C(F)\le C(E)-k(S)+k(S;Y)
       \le C(E)-k(S)+\lambda(M)
       \le C(E)-k(S)+k(T)+e,
\]

which proves (3).

Two features will be used: the measure remains unnormalized, and the relation S join E is preserved after replacing S by T, even though the graphing changes.

## 3. A finite graphing split into factor steps

Fix rho>0. There is a finite graphing E of R_Gamma, made of restrictions of nonidentity group translations, with

\[
 c=C(E)<\operatorname{Cost}(R_\Gamma)+\rho.             \tag{6}
\]

Here is the finite approximation. Start with a countable graphing within rho/2 of the infimum. Split each map into its unique group-displacement pieces; freeness makes these displacements unique, and the split does not change cost. Remove identities. Let E_N be its first N pieces and T_N their generated relation. Fix a finite generating set K of Gamma. For every s in K, the Borel sets

\[
 D_{N,s}=\{x:(x,xs)\notin T_N\}
\]

decrease to a null set. Add the s-translation restricted to D_{N,s} for every s in K. The resulting finite graphing generates all generator edges. Its extra cost tends to zero, proving (6).

Write these finitely many maps as x to x g_h on domains D_h, and choose words g_h=g_{h,1}...g_{h,l_h}, with letters in A or B and l_h>=1. Fix a factor type for each letter of J. Add l_h-1 disjoint copies of D_h to X, one for each internal vertex of its word path. Give each copy its original measure. Call the resulting finite-measure space (Z,lambda), of mass

\[
 m=1+\sum_h(l_h-1)\mu(D_h).
\]

Define P:Z to X as the identity on X and by

\[
 P(x,h,i)=xg_{h,1}\cdots g_{h,i}
\]

on the i-th added sheet. On each sheet P is a measure-preserving Borel bijection onto its image. For each subgroup L define the pullback relation

\[
 \overline R_L=\{(z,z'):(Pz,Pz')\in R_L\}.
\]

These are countable measure-preserving Borel equivalence relations: their partial bijections are enumerated by lifting each L-translation between each ordered pair of sheets, on its natural domain.

Replace every E-edge by its subdivided word path and divide the resulting indexed maps into E_A^0 and E_B^0. They lie in the corresponding pullback relations and together generate overline R_Gamma. Indeed, every original path can be subdivided, and each added point is attached along its own word path to a base point of X. Two projected-orbit-related added points can therefore be joined through base points. Their total cost is

\[
 C(E_A^0)+C(E_B^0)
 =\sum_h l_h\mu(D_h)=c+m-1.                          \tag{7}
\]

Counting map instances avoids any issue if distinct projected edges coincide.

## 4. Supplying the amalgamating relation

Let S_0 be the identity relation on Z, so k(S_0)=m. Set Q_i^n=S_n join E_i^n for i=A,B. Choose positive errors e_n whose sum is less than epsilon. Alternately process A and B. When processing i at stage n, put

\[
 S_{n+1}=Q_i^n\cap\overline R_J.
\]

This contains S_n and is contained in Q_i^n. Apply (3) to S_n, S_{n+1}, Q_i^n and E_i^n. Replace that family's maps by E_i^{n+1} with

\[
 Q_i^n=S_{n+1}\vee E_i^{n+1},\qquad
 C(E_i^{n+1})\le C(E_i^n)-k(S_n)+k(S_{n+1})+e_n.
\]

Leave the other family unchanged. The processed Q_i stays equal to its previous value, and the other Q_i grows. All maps stay in their factor pullbacks. Hence both Q_i^n increase, their join remains overline R_Gamma, and S_n increases inside overline R_J. All graphings retain finite cost. Summing the displayed inequalities and using (7) gives

\[
 C(E_A^n)+C(E_B^n)
 \le c-1+k(S_n)+\epsilon.                            \tag{8}
\]

Let S_infty and Q_i^infty be the respective unions. Since every type is processed infinitely often,

\[
 Q_i^\infty\cap\overline R_J=S_\infty,
 \qquad Q_A^\infty\vee Q_B^\infty=\overline R_\Gamma.  \tag{9}
\]

For the first equality, a pair in Q_i^n intersect overline R_J is included in S at the next stage processing i. The other inclusion follows from S_n subset Q_i^n and their monotonicity.

We claim S_infty=overline R_J. Take a pair in overline R_J and a shortest chain joining its endpoints by steps in Q_A^infty or Q_B^infty. If it has at least two steps, their types alternate, since consecutive steps of one type could be composed. No step can be in overline R_J: by (9) it would belong to S_infty, hence to both Q_i^infty, and could be composed with a neighbor. Projecting this chain to X gives an alternating word in A minus J and B minus J. Freeness identifies its displacement uniquely; its product lies in J, contradicting the normal form for an amalgam. Thus a shortest chain has at most one step, and (9) places the pair in S_infty. The identity pair is already in S_0.

Every overline R_J-class is infinite. The class of z contains the distinct base-sheet points (Pz)j for j in the infinite group J. Since S_n increases with this union, |[z]_{S_n}| tends to infinity for every z. Dominated convergence on the fixed finite-measure space therefore gives

\[
 k(S_n)\longrightarrow0.                            \tag{10}
\]

No graphing is passed to a limit here; only increasing relations and their class-size functions are used.

## 5. Projection and recovery of the A-relation

Fix a finite stage n. For each map in E_i^n partition its domain by its source and target sheets. On each piece conjugate by the two restrictions of P. This gives a Borel partial bijection in R_i with the same domain measure as the piece. Let F_i^n be the indexed family of these projected maps. Then

\[
 C(F_i^n)\le C(E_i^n).                               \tag{11}
\]

In fact equality holds if every indexed piece, including identities, is retained. Global injectivity of P is not required.

Put T_i=R_J join F_i^n. Project paths on Z between base-sheet points. S_n-steps project into R_J, while other steps project into the appropriate F_i^n. It follows that

\[
 T_A\vee T_B=R_\Gamma,
 \qquad R_J\subseteq T_i\subseteq R_i.
\]

These inclusions imply T_A=R_A. For an R_A-pair choose a shortest chain with T_A or T_B steps. If its length is at least two, it alternates and contains no R_J-step, because a step in R_J is available in both types and can be composed with a neighbor. The projected displacements then form an alternating word outside J of length at least two whose product is in A. Amalgam normal form forbids this. A single B-step with endpoints R_A-related has displacement in A intersect B=J, so it is also a T_A-step. A single A-step and a zero-step chain are already in T_A. This proves equality.

Thus F_A^n is admissible for d_X. Combining (8) and (11) gives

\[
 d_X\le C(F_A^n)
 \le C(E_A^n)+C(E_B^n)
 \le c-1+k(S_n)+\epsilon.
\]

Let n tend to infinity using (10), then let epsilon and rho tend to zero using (6). This proves (1).

## 6. Checks on the accounting and hypotheses

The constant one can also be recovered by counting saved cost directly. Subdivision added cost m-1. Up to stage n, the successive decreases of component mass total k(S_0)-k(S_n)=m-k(S_n). Subtracting this from c+m-1 leaves c-1+k(S_n), independently of the number of sheets. This agrees with the telescoping calculation (8). In particular, replacing k(S_0) by one on Z would give the wrong estimate whenever m differs from one.

For a finite atomic check, give each of h points mass q. The identity relation has component mass hq; the full relation has component mass q. A spanning tree has cost (h-1)q. Supplying the full relation deletes exactly (h-1)q, leaving zero. Thus the sign and magnitude of both component-mass terms in (3) agree with an independent tree-edge count.

If S=T, the estimate (3) permits F=E; its correction is zero. If all S-classes are infinite, k(S)=k(T)=0 and again F=E suffices. These cases also follow from the construction: Y=Z on the infinite-class part. Finite S-classes are the only classes for which parent-edge deletion is charged.

The infinitude of J is necessary for the stated constant. If J is trivial, A=B=Z/2 and Gamma is their infinite dihedral free product, the same argument has no vanishing component mass for the amalgamating relation. More concretely, in any free action restrict the A-involution to one Borel representative per two-point A-orbit. This generates R_A at cost 1/2. Conversely, every generating graphing of R_A must cover almost every point by domains and ranges of nonidentity edges, so 2C>=1. Thus d_X=1/2. Choose representatives also for the B-involution: the two restricted maps generate R_Gamma at total cost one. The asserted inequality would instead give Cost(R_Gamma)>=3/2, a contradiction. The proof above uses infinitude exactly in (10).

The argument allows atoms, nonergodic actions and noninjective sheet projection. It requires finite generation of Gamma only to obtain (6), and essential freeness for normal-form recovery. It does not give d_X>0. In particular, applying it to the manuscript's displayed amalgam does not settle the finite-model transfer or deterministic rank bound.
