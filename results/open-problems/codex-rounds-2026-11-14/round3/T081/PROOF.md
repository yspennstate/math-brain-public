# Conditional contour replacement with a microscopic background

This note verifies a finite-volume algebraic step. It proves no phase transition.

The motivating formula is Proposition 5.6 in *Density coexistence for hard spheres with a long-range attractive pair interaction*, Hexagon 2610.00734v1, credited as generated by GPT-6 Astra and prompted by Qidong He. Its [TeX source](https://hexagonmath.org/api/v1/works/2610.00734v1/source?path=liquid-solid.tex), equation labeled `eq:ratios`, has interior phase \(c_j\) in the numerator and exterior phase \(c\) in the denominator. The PDF text extraction loses that distinction. The result below is an independent conditional-integration argument for this formula, with its geometric hypotheses stated explicitly.

## 1. Setup and hypotheses

Partition space into cubes \(Q_i=L(i+[0,1)^d)\), \(i\in\mathbb Z^d\). Adjacency means \(\ell^\infty\) distance one. Suppose the interaction range, including any hard core, is strictly less than \(L\). Write \(dq_A\) for the grand-canonical configuration measure in a finite union of cubes \(A\). This measure factors over disjoint unions: the multinomial decomposition of the particle numbers cancels the factorials in the grand-canonical measure. All integrands below are nonnegative, so Tonelli's theorem applies.

Assign local phase labels, and fix a phase \(c\). Let \(\mathcal X_A^c\) denote configurations having phase \(c\) in every cube of \(A\), with all internal hard-core constraints enforced. A diluted partition function \(Z_A^c(\omega)\) additionally allows contours in the interior, and fixes a correct phase-\(c\) collar at the boundary. The exterior configuration \(\omega\) supplies this collar. Assume all finite partition functions considered are finite.

A contour \(\Gamma\) consists of a nonempty connected support \(S_\Gamma\) and a finite label code on that support. Let \(E_\Gamma^\infty\) be its unbounded complementary component, \(D_{\Gamma,1},\ldots,D_{\Gamma,k}\) its bounded complementary components, and

\[
C_\Gamma=S_\Gamma\cup\bigcup_jD_{\Gamma,j},\qquad
E_\Gamma=N_1(S_\Gamma).
\]

Here \(C_\Gamma\) is its filled support, whereas \(E_\Gamma\) is its one-cube enlargement. These are different sets. Let \(c\) be the exterior type of \(\Gamma\), and \(c_{\Gamma,j}\) the phase next to \(D_{\Gamma,j}\).

The following hypotheses specify exactly the geometry used in the proof.

1. A configuration in a diluted ensemble has a unique decomposition into its external contours, a pure phase-\(c\) background outside their filled supports, and diluted phase-\(c_{\Gamma,j}\) configurations in their bounded complementary components. Conversely this data reconstructs the configuration, subject to the physical hard-core constraints.
2. Compatible contours have nonadjacent, disjoint supports. Their filled supports are either disjoint or nested; equal filled supports for distinct compatible contours are excluded. Maximal filled supports of a compatible family are separated beyond the interaction range.
3. For a contour in a recursion domain \(A\), \(E_\Gamma\subset A\) implies \(C_\Gamma\subset A\). If a compatible contour lies strictly inside \(C_\Gamma\), its connected support lies in one component \(D_{\Gamma,j}\), and its enlargement is contained in that component. Conversely contours from the catalogs of the components are compatible with \(\Gamma\) and with contours in other components.
4. The contour code fixes phase \(c\) along the exterior collar and phase \(c_{\Gamma,j}\) along each interior collar. Hence the diluted partition functions used below have their stated boundary phases. Different complementary components cannot interact directly. No physical interaction joins a bounded component to the unbounded component across the support.
5. Every denominator in (1) below is positive. For continuum hard cores this follows if pure phase states with particles away from cube boundaries have positive configuration volume, and can fill all the required cubes.

These hypotheses are part of the theorem, not conclusions about arbitrary contour definitions. In particular, the proof does not replace a proof of boundary connectivity for a proposed contour construction. The hypotheses allow arbitrary microscopic correlations within each phase.

For each fixed finite domain, the contour catalog is finite: there are finitely many cube subsets and finitely many label codes. Catalog membership and phase labels depend only on the code, not on particle positions within a phase band.

## 2. Activities

Fix microscopic particles \(q\) in the exterior component of a contour. Let \(H_S(s\mid q)\) include interactions internal to \(S=S_\Gamma\) and between \(S\) and that exterior component, including hard-core constraints. Interactions between \(S\) and bounded complementary components are included in their partition functions. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_\Gamma^c(q)
&=\int_{\eta(s)=\eta_\Gamma}
e^{-\beta H_S(s\mid q)}
\prod_j Z_{D_{\Gamma,j}}^{c_{\Gamma,j}}(s)\,ds,\\
D_\Gamma^c(q)
&=\int_{s\in\mathcal X_S^c}
e^{-\beta H_S(s\mid q)}
\prod_j Z_{D_{\Gamma,j}}^c(s)\,ds,\\
W_\Gamma^c(q)&=N_\Gamma^c(q)/D_\Gamma^c(q).
\end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]

The exterior argument in \(W_\Gamma^c\) excludes both \(S\) and all its bounded complementary components. Only particles within the physical interaction range of \(S\) affect (1). Thus the activity depends on exterior microscopic states in \(E_\Gamma\), but not on background states in \(C_\Gamma\). This last independence is essential.

The numerator changes the phases of the interiors according to the contour code. The denominator retains the physical diluted phase-\(c\) ensembles in those interiors; it is generally not a product of pure-phase integrals.

## 3. The finite-volume identity

Let \(\mathcal C_c(A)\) be the type-\(c\) catalog with \(E_\Gamma\subset A\), and define

\[
P_A^c(\omega)=
\int_{q_A\in\mathcal X_A^c}
e^{-\beta H_A(q_A\mid\omega)}
\sum_{\mathcal F\text{ compatible in }\mathcal C_c(A)}
\prod_{\Gamma\in\mathcal F}W_\Gamma^c(q_A\omega)\,dq_A.
\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem.** Under the hypotheses above,

\[
P_A^c(\omega)=Z_A^c(\omega). \tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Induct on the number of cubes in \(A\), simultaneously for all phases and admissible microscopic exteriors. The empty domain is immediate. If there are no catalog contours, hypothesis 1 says the physical ensemble is pure phase \(c\), so (2) is also immediate.

For the inductive step, regroup the finite sum in (2) according to the contours \(\mathcal G\) whose filled supports are maximal in \(\mathcal F\). Hypothesis 2 makes their filled supports disjoint and separated. Every remaining contour is in a bounded complementary component of a unique member of \(\mathcal G\). Hypothesis 3 says that its allowed choices are precisely the catalogs of those components. Families chosen in distinct components are independently compatible. Thus this regrouping is a bijection of finite index sets; no multiplicity factor is inserted.

First fix the pure microscopic background outside \(\bigcup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal G}C_\Gamma\). For each \(\Gamma\in\mathcal G\), also temporarily fix the pure phase-\(c\) microscopic configuration \(s\) on its support. With these variables fixed, integrate the background and sum the auxiliary contours inside each \(D_{\Gamma,j}\). This produces

\[
P_{D_{\Gamma,j}}^c(s)=Z_{D_{\Gamma,j}}^c(s),
\tag{4}
\]

by induction. Each component is strictly smaller than \(A\), since the nonempty support has been removed. The collar hypotheses make the phase-\(c\) boundary condition in (4) valid: at this stage \(s\) belongs to the pure background, not to the original contour code.

The activity of a maximal contour is independent of every variable in its own filled support. It can therefore be held outside both the integrations in (4) and the remaining integration over \(s\). No interaction crosses directly between different maximal filled supports, and their activities do not depend on variables in one another's filled supports. Conditional factorization is therefore valid even if two activities depend on a shared microscopic background variable outside those supports.

For this maximal contour, the resulting integration is exactly

\[
W_\Gamma^c(q)
\int_{s\in\mathcal X_{S_\Gamma}^c}
e^{-\beta H_{S_\Gamma}(s\mid q)}
\prod_j Z_{D_{\Gamma,j}}^c(s)\,ds
=W_\Gamma^c(q)D_\Gamma^c(q)
=N_\Gamma^c(q).
\tag{5}
\]

The equality is conditional on the retained microscopic exterior \(q\). In particular, no expectation of a product has been replaced by a product of expectations.

After (5) has been performed for every member of \(\mathcal G\), its contribution becomes the pure background integral outside the filled supports, multiplied by the physical support integrals with code \(\eta_\Gamma\) and physical interior factors \(Z_{D_{\Gamma,j}}^{c_{\Gamma,j}}\). Hypothesis 1 identifies this with exactly the contribution of physical configurations whose external contours are \(\mathcal G\). For \(\mathcal G=\varnothing\), it is the physical contribution having no contours. Summing over \(\mathcal G\) proves (3). All rearrangements use finite sums and nonnegative integrals. \(\square\)

## 4. A finite-state specialization

The following specialization isolates the cancellation without contour geometry. Let \(T\) be a finite rooted tree. A node represents a support and its children represent bounded complementary components. For each node \(v\), phase \(c\in\{0,1\}\), exterior microscopic state \(b\), and support state \(s\in\{0,1\}\), choose arbitrary strictly positive weights \(a_v(c,s,b)\) and \(b_v(c,s,b)\). The first is the background support weight and the second is the contour-code support weight. Define recursively

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_v^c(b)&=\sum_s a_v(c,s,b)\prod_{u\text{ child of }v}Z_u^c(s),\\
N_v^c(b)&=\sum_s b_v(c,s,b)\prod_{u\text{ child of }v}Z_u^{1-c}(s),\\
Z_v^c(b)&=D_v^c(b)+N_v^c(b),\qquad
W_v^c(b)=N_v^c(b)/D_v^c(b).
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]

For a fixed pure-phase microscopic background \((s_v)\), all node contours are compatible: they are nested or in different branches. Their subset sum is \(\prod_v(1+W_v^c(s_{\operatorname{parent}(v)}))\), with the root's parent state fixed to \(b\). Therefore the auxiliary integral is

\[
\sum_{(s_v)}
\prod_v a_v(c,s_v,s_{\operatorname{parent}(v)})
\prod_v\bigl[1+W_v^c(s_{\operatorname{parent}(v)})\bigr].
\tag{7}
\]

It equals \(Z_{\rm root}^c(b)\). For a direct second derivation, recursively evaluate (7) from its leaves. If the subtree integrals equal \(Z_u^c(s)\), the integral at node \(v\) equals

\[
(1+W_v^c(b))\sum_s a_v(c,s,b)\prod_uZ_u^c(s)
=(1+N_v^c(b)/D_v^c(b))D_v^c(b)
=Z_v^c(b).
\]

This proves (7) independently of the geometric regrouping in Section 3.

The script `check_replacement.py` compares three exact rational calculations: the recursion (6); exhaustive enumeration of all physical support types and states; and exhaustive enumeration of the auxiliary microscopic backgrounds in (7). It also enumerates the compatible-family subsets separately in selected cases. Boundary-dependent weights retain correlations between siblings through their common parent state. These finite-state checks test the algebra; they do not certify the geometric hypotheses for a continuum contour construction.

A hand-checkable example has one root and two leaves. Every background weight equals one. At the root every defect weight also equals one. At each leaf every defect support state has weight one when its exterior state is zero, and weight three when it is one. The weights do not depend on phase. Consequently a leaf has physical partition function four or eight according to its exterior state. Thus the root denominator and numerator both equal

\[
4^2+8^2=80,
\]

and its full physical partition function is \(160\). The pure background has mass eight. The root activity is one, and both leaf activities are one or three according to the common root state. Formula (7) gives

\[
8\cdot 2\cdot\tfrac12(2^2+4^2)=160.
\]

Independently averaging the leaf activities instead gives \(8\cdot2\cdot3^2=144\). Their covariance is one, so the error is independently \(8\cdot2=16\). Reversing the dependence of the second leaf makes the physical value \(2(4\cdot8+8\cdot4)=128\); the independently averaged value remains \(144\), and the covariance becomes minus one.

Two other modifications fail in the aligned example. Replacing the root's diluted-interior denominator \(80\) by its pure-background value \(8\) gives root activity \(10\), and auxiliary integral \(11\cdot80=880\). Excluding families containing both the root contour and a nested leaf contour gives instead \(80+8=88\): the first term includes all leaf families and the second the root-only family. Neither modification preserves the physical partition function \(160\).

## 5. What this establishes

The conditional replacement is consistent with arbitrary microscopic correlations, provided the listed geometry and locality hold. In particular, nested auxiliary contours must be summed before the maximal contour denominator can cancel. Treating that denominator as a pure-phase integral, or averaging activities separately before multiplying them, changes the identity.

This verifies the algebraic replacement step in the source formulation under explicit hypotheses. It does not verify all collar and boundary-connectivity assertions, the surface-pressure bound, the subsequent contour estimates, or the existence of two infinite-volume Gibbs measures. It does not settle T081 or establish the Hexagon theorem.
