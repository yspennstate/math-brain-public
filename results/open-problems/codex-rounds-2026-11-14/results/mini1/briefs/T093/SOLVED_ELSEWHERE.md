# Literature update, 14 November 2026

The whole T093 statement was not found settled in the bounded search.

P. M. Aronow and Patrick Lopatto, *Existence of the q-Colorability Threshold for Sparse Random Graphs*, [hexagon:2610.00608v1](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00608v1), October 2026; no arXiv identifier found. Opened the primary page and [PDF](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00608v1). Theorem 1.1 claims convergence to a threshold for every fixed q >= 3. Theorem 1.2 gives a hitting-time window of order n/(log n)^(3/2). Page 4 explicitly leaves the threshold's value undetermined. This is an unrefereed preprint whose proof was not audited here. It therefore updates the existence claim provisionally and does not settle equality with the cavity prediction.

Peter Ayre, Amin Coja-Oghlan, Catherine Greenhill, *Lower bounds on the chromatic number of random graphs*, [arXiv:1812.09691v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09691), Combinatorica 42 (2022), 617–658. Opened its arXiv page and [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.09691). Theorem 1.3 proves a variational upper bound on the colourability threshold. Section 1.3 evaluates a single atom at 1/4 to obtain non-3-colourability for average degree greater than 4.697. Thus the supplied 4.9364 upper bound is outdated. This theorem proves one direction of a cavity prediction, not the matching colourability direction.

Also opened the arXiv pages of Amin Coja-Oghlan, *Upper-bounding the k-colorability threshold by counting covers*, [arXiv:1305.0177](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0177), and Amin Coja-Oghlan and Dan Vilenchik, *Chasing the k-colorability threshold*, [arXiv:1304.1063](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.1063). Their abstracts support the supplied large-q bounds. No exhaustive claim about the entire literature is intended.
