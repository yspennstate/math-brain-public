# Finite witnesses and a reduction to finitely presented groups

All logarithms are natural. For a finite random variable (V), write (H(V))
for its Shannon entropy. Set (0\log 0=0) and

\[
b(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t),\qquad
\Phi_k(t)=b(t)+t\log(k-1) \quad (k\geq2).
\]

## 1. Statement of the reduction

Let (G) be countably infinite, (A=\{1,\ldots,k\}), (k\geq2), and let
(p) be any probability vector on (A). Put (X=A^G), with measure (p^G).
For finite nonempty sets (S,D\subseteq G), a finite alphabet (B), and maps

\[
 f:A^S\longrightarrow B,\qquad d:B^D\longrightarrow A,
\]

define, for (x\in X),

\[
 Y_g(x)=f((x_{gs})_{s\in S}),\qquad
 \widehat X_g(x)=d((Y_{gt}(x))_{t\in D}),\qquad
 \varepsilon=\Pr[x_e\ne\widehat X_e].
\]

The following formula holds:

\[
\boxed{\quad h_G^{\rm Rok}(A^G,p^G)
 =\inf_{S,D,B,f,d}\bigl[H(Y_e)+\Phi_k(\varepsilon)\bigr].\quad} \tag{1}
\]

Consequently, strict failure of RBS for this finite base is equivalent to
the existence of the following finite witness:

\[
 H(Y_e)+\Phi_k(\varepsilon)<H(p). \tag{2}
\]

Every finite-base failure for a countably infinite group yields a failure
with the same base for a finitely presented infinite group. Combining this
fact with Seward's Theorems 1.10 and 1.12 yields:

**Corollary.** RBS for every countably infinite group and every standard
base is equivalent to RBS for every finitely presented infinite group and
every finite uniform base.

Formula (1) and the lifting argument below are proved directly. The last
corollary uses the two cited theorems; it is not an independent proof of them.
These statements are reductions of the open problem, not a resolution.

## 2. Repairing a local decoder

Fix (S,D,B,f,d). Given (a\in A), order (A\setminus\{a\}) by its usual
order. Define (R_g\in\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}) as follows:

* If (x_g=\widehat X_g), set (R_g=0).
* Otherwise, let (R_g) be the position of (x_g) in
  (A\setminus\{\widehat X_g\}).

Let (Z_g=(Y_g,R_g)), and let (\gamma) be the partition specified by (Z_e).
All (Z_g) are translates of this partition for the Bernoulli action
((h\cdot x)_g=x_{h^{-1}g}).

From (Z_t), (t\in D), one reads (Y_t) and hence
(\widehat X_e=d((Y_t)_{t\in D})). From (Z_e) one reads (R_e). If
(R_e=0), then (x_e=\widehat X_e); otherwise, the rank (R_e), together
with (\widehat X_e), determines (x_e). Thus (x_e) is determined by
((Z_t)_{t\in D\cup\{e\}}). Translating this identity recovers every (x_g).
The partition (\gamma) is therefore generating. In fact, these identities
hold on every configuration, not merely almost everywhere.

Let (E_e=1_{\{R_e\ne0\}}). This is a function of (R_e), has entropy
(b(\varepsilon)), and (R_e) is constant given (E_e=0). Given (E_e=1),
(R_e) has at most (k-1) possible values. The entropy chain rule gives

\[
 H(R_e)=H(E_e)+H(R_e\mid E_e)
 \leq b(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\log(k-1).
\]

This also holds at (\varepsilon=0,1), by direct interpretation. Therefore

\[
 h_G^{\rm Rok}(X,p^G)\leq H(\gamma)
 =H(Y_e,R_e)\leq H(Y_e)+\Phi_k(\varepsilon). \tag{3}
\]

Notice that (Z_e) depends only on the input coordinates in
(\{e\}\cup S\cup DS), where (DS=\{ts:t\in D,s\in S\}).
The order (ts), rather than (st), follows from (Y_t=f((x_{ts})_s)).

## 3. Approximating any finite-entropy generator

Let (\alpha) be a countable generating partition with finite entropy,
and let (U_e:X\to\mathbb N) label its atoms. Write
(U_g(x)=U_e(g^{-1}\cdot x)). The sigma algebra generated by all (U_g)
is the completed product sigma algebra. Hence, for any (\eta>0), there
are a finite nonempty (D\subseteq G) and a map
(d_0:\mathbb N^D\to A) with

\[
 \Pr[x_e\ne d_0((U_t)_{t\in D})]<\eta. \tag{4}
\]

For completeness, enumerate (G), take increasing finite subsets exhausting
it, and take the conditional expectations of (1_{\{x_e=a\}}), (a\in A),
given the corresponding (U)-names. They converge in (L^1) to those
indicators because (\alpha) generates. Selecting a symbol of maximal
conditional probability, with a fixed tie rule, gives (4). Alternatively,
approximate the finitely many events (\{x_e=a\}) in measure by sets in the
algebra of finite (U)-names and resolve overlaps by the fixed order on (A).

Choose (N) large enough that

\[
 |D|\Pr[U_e>N]<\eta.
\]

Collapse all labels (U_e>N) to a single extra label, obtaining a finite
random variable (C_e), and use the same collapse at every coordinate.
There is a decoder (d_1) on (C^D) which agrees with (d_0) whenever no
collapsed label occurs. Indeed, on such tuples all original labels are
still known; on the remaining tuples define (d_1) arbitrarily. A union
bound and shift invariance give

\[
 \Pr[x_e\ne d_1((C_t)_{t\in D})]<2\eta,\qquad
 H(C_e)\leq H(\alpha). \tag{5}
\]

Let (m) be the size of the finite alphabet of (C_e). For every
(\delta>0), there are a finite nonempty (S\subseteq G) and a map
(f:A^S\to\{\text{labels of }C_e\}) such that

\[
 \Pr[f((x_s)_{s\in S})\ne C_e]<\delta. \tag{6}
\]

To see this, use increasing finite coordinate sigma algebras exhausting the
product sigma algebra and the same finite-valued conditional-probability
argument used for (4). If (m=1), take the constant map and zero error.

Assume (m\geq2), and let (q=\Pr[Y_e\ne C_e]). Given (C_e) and the
indicator (1_{\{Y_e\ne C_e\}}), there are at most (m-1) alternatives for
(Y_e) on a mismatch. Thus

\[
 H(Y_e)\leq H(C_e)+H(Y_e\mid C_e)
 \leq H(\alpha)+b(q)+q\log(m-1). \tag{7}
\]

Use (d=d_1). By (5), (6), shift invariance, and a union bound,

\[
 \varepsilon=\Pr[x_e\ne d((Y_t)_{t\in D})]
 <2\eta+|D|\delta. \tag{8}
\]

Given (\tau>0), first choose (\eta>0) so small that
(\Phi_k(t)<\tau/2) for every (0\leq t\leq3\eta). This is possible
by continuity at zero. After (D,N,m) have been fixed, choose
(\delta>0) so that ( |D|\delta<\eta) and
(b(q)+q\log(m-1)<\tau/2) for every (0\leq q\leq\delta).
For (m=1), omit the latter requirement. Equations (7) and (8) imply

\[
 H(Y_e)+\Phi_k(\varepsilon)<H(\alpha)+\tau. \tag{9}
\]

The coordinate partition is a finite-entropy generator, so the infimum
defining (h_G^{\rm Rok}) is finite. Choose (\alpha) within any prescribed
positive tolerance of that infimum, and then let (\tau\downarrow0) in (9).
This proves that the right side of (1) is at most (h_G^{\rm Rok}).
Equation (3) proves the reverse inequality. Formula (1) follows.
The coordinate partition also gives (h_G^{\rm Rok}\leq H(p)), so (1)
proves the equivalence between strict failure and (2).

## 4. A witness lives in an infinite finitely generated subgroup

Suppose (2) holds in (G), and let (H=\langle S\cup D\rangle\leq G).
The random variables (Y_e) and (1_{\{x_e\ne\widehat X_e\}}) use only
coordinates in (H). Independent (p)-labels on (H) therefore give the
same values of (H(Y_e)) and (\varepsilon). The correction construction
of Section 2 applies unchanged to (A^H).

The group (H) cannot be finite. If it were, the full (Z)-name on (H)
would determine all input labels, so

\[
 |H|H(p)=H((x_h)_{h\in H})
 \leq H((Z_h)_{h\in H})
 \leq \sum_{h\in H}H(Z_h)
 =|H|H(Z_e)
 \leq |H|[H(Y_e)+\Phi_k(\varepsilon)]
 <|H|H(p),
\]

a contradiction. Thus (H) is infinite and finitely generated, and (3)
gives strict failure of RBS over (H). This argument uses no monotonicity
of Rokhlin entropy under factor maps.

## 5. Lifting the witness to a finitely presented group

Choose a finite generating set for (H), let (F_r\twoheadrightarrow H)
be the corresponding free-group epimorphism, and choose a representing
word (w_s) for every (s\in S), and (v_t) for every (t\in D).
Let (V) be the following finite list of words:

\[
 V=\{1\}\cup\{w_s:s\in S\}\cup\{v_t:t\in D\}
       \cup\{v_tw_s:t\in D,s\in S\}.
\]

Impose the relation (uv^{-1}=1) for every pair (u,v\in V) whose images
in (H) agree, and call the resulting finitely presented group (P).
There are finitely many relations. They hold in (H), so the original
epimorphism factors through an epimorphism (P\twoheadrightarrow H).
In particular (P) is infinite.

For (u,v\in V), equality in (H) implies equality in (P) by the imposed
relations. Conversely, equality in (P) implies equality in (H) by the
epimorphism. Thus the equality pattern on the entire finite list (V)
is exactly the same in both groups. In particular, the chosen lifts of
the distinct elements of (S) and of (D) remain distinct.

Define (f_P) and (d_P) by the same tables as (f,d), with their argument
positions indexed by these lifts. Both (Y_e) and the error indicator
depend only on the input coordinates indexed by the images of (V).
Under independent (p)-labels their joint distribution depends solely on
which entries of (V) denote the same group element: distinct coordinates
receive independent labels, and equal coordinates receive the same label.
The equality-pattern conclusion therefore gives

\[
 H(Y_e^{P})=H(Y_e^{H}),\qquad
 \varepsilon_P=\varepsilon_H.
\]

Recompute the correction partition on (A^P) from (f_P,d_P). Section 2
proves directly that it generates and has entropy at most the left side
of (2). Therefore (P) also violates RBS with base (p).

This is an existence argument. Finding all equalities in (V) is not
claimed to be algorithmic for an arbitrary presentation of (H).

### Exact checking for rational bases

Suppose (p) is rational and the finite equality pattern on
(\{e\}\cup S\cup DS) is given correctly. Enumerating independent labels
on its distinct coordinates computes (H(Y_e)) and (\varepsilon) from
rational probabilities. Choose a common denominator (Q) for (p), the
law of (Y_e), and (\varepsilon), and write

\[
 p_a=M_a/Q,\quad \Pr[Y_e=b]=N_b/Q,\quad \varepsilon=E/Q.
\]

Using (\sum_aM_a=\sum_bN_b=Q), the entropy definitions give

\[
 \exp\!\left(Q[H(Y_e)+\Phi_k(\varepsilon)-H(p)]\right)
 =\frac{Q^Q(k-1)^E\prod_aM_a^{M_a}}
 {\left(\prod_bN_b^{N_b}\right)E^E(Q-E)^{Q-E}}. \tag{10}
\]

Here zero counts contribute (0^0=1). Thus (2) can be checked by comparing
two positive integers, without a numerical tolerance for logarithms.
For a uniform finite base all these probabilities are automatically
rational. This is a finite certificate once the equality pattern is
known; it does not solve the word problem of the group.

## 6. The full conjecture, including infinite bases

The needed input from Brandon Seward, *Krieger's finite generator theorem
for actions of countable groups II*, arXiv:1501.03367v4, is as follows.
Let (c_G) be the supremum of the finite Rokhlin entropies of free ergodic
p.m.p. (G)-actions. Theorem 1.10 states, for finite base entropy,

\[
 h_G^{\rm Rok}(L^G,\lambda^G)=\min\{H(L,\lambda),c_G\}.
\]

Theorem 1.12 states that an infinite-entropy Bernoulli shift has infinite
Rokhlin entropy exactly when (G) has a free ergodic p.m.p. action of
positive Rokhlin entropy; otherwise its Rokhlin entropy is zero.

Suppose first that RBS fails for a finite-entropy standard base over (G).
Theorem 1.10 gives (c_G<H(L,\lambda)<\infty). Choose an integer (k\geq2)
with (\log k>c_G). Applying the same theorem to the uniform (k)-point
base gives Rokhlin entropy (c_G<\log k), a finite uniform-base failure.

Suppose instead that it fails for an infinite-entropy standard base.
By Theorem 1.12 there is no free ergodic p.m.p. (G)-action of positive
Rokhlin entropy. The uniform binary Bernoulli action is free and ergodic
and has finite Rokhlin entropy, so its Rokhlin entropy must be zero. It
violates RBS because its base entropy is (\log2>0).

Here freeness follows directly: for a nonidentity (g\in G), if (g) has
infinite order, an infinite sequence of coordinates along one (g)-orbit
cannot all have the same independent uniform label, except on a null
set. If (g) has finite order (m>1), there are infinitely many disjoint
orbits of size (m), and the probability of a constant label on each is
(k^{1-m}<1); independence again gives probability zero. A countable union
over (g\ne e) is null. Ergodicity follows by approximating two events by
finite-coordinate events, and choosing translates that make their finite
coordinate supports disjoint. Independence gives mixing along elements
escaping finite subsets, and an invariant event consequently has
probability equal to its square.

In either case Sections 3–5 lift a finite uniform-base failure to a
finitely presented infinite group. Thus absence of failures for finitely
presented infinite groups with finite uniform bases implies full RBS.
The reverse implication is immediate, since these are among the groups
and bases in full RBS. This proves the corollary.

## 7. Relation to prior work and limits

Lewis Bowen and Michael Chapman, *Surjunctivity does not characterize
cosoficity of invariant random subgroups*, arXiv:2511.06586, Theorem 4.1
and Section 5, give a related local entropy criterion in the IRS setting.
Their criterion uses conditional entropy and is sharper than replacing
the correction cost by the error bound (\Phi_k). No novelty is asserted
for the general principle of local witnesses.

The reduction supplies neither a positive lower bound for an unknown
non-sofic example nor a violating witness. A finite list of tests cannot
establish RBS: the windows and alphabet sizes in (1) are unbounded.
The finitely presented group (P) above depends on the hypothetical
witness; it is not a prescribed cover which must preserve entropy for
all codes.
