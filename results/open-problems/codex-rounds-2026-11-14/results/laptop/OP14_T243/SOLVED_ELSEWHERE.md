# Literature status: partial advances, whole problem not settled

Checked 2026-11-14 using web search, within the ten-minute literature budget. This filename records the requested literature check; it does **not** declare T243 solved.

- **Luis Wiederhold, Nil Ayday, Debarghya Ghoshdastidar**, *Lifting Finite-Sample Gaussian Approximation of Wide Neural Networks to Functional Convergence*, [arXiv:2610.18166v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18166v1), 13 October 2026. Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 give a supremum-norm functional W2 upper bound of order width^(-1/2) on compact input sets for one hidden layer with polynomial activation. The argument uses finite-rank polynomial sample paths. Section 4 explicitly leaves sharpness and deeper nonlinear networks open. Higher-degree polynomials are not globally Lipschitz. This is a relevant advance, not the full requested result.
- **Lucia Celli, Giovanni Peccati**, *Entropic bounds for conditionally Gaussian vectors and applications to neural networks*, [arXiv:2504.08335v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08335), revised 3 June 2025. The opened abstract describes finite-input total-variation and W2 bounds, including derivatives, under invertible limiting covariance. It does not claim a general C(K) supremum-norm rate.
- **Dario Trevisan**, *Wide Deep Neural Networks with Gaussian Weights are Very Close to Gaussian Processes*, [arXiv:2312.11737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11737), 2023. The opened abstract states inverse-width bounds for a finite input set under nondegeneracy.
- **Krishnakumar Balasubramanian, Larry Goldstein, Nathan Ross, Adil Salim**, *Gaussian random field approximation via Stein's method with applications to wide random neural networks*, [arXiv:2306.16308v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16308), revised 1 May 2024. The opened abstract already states functional W1 bounds for the supremum norm on spherical input sets, for arbitrary depth and Lipschitz activations. Thus the background should distinguish existence of functional bounds from optimal functional rates.

Only the first paper's full theorem and discussion were inspected; claims about the other three are limited to their opened abstracts. No exhaustive negative literature conclusion, and no claim that the special case below is novel.
