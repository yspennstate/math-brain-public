# A collision bound for full-state FIFO path mixtures

Fix an integer \(d\geq2\), \(0<\lambda<1\), and \(N\geq d\). Servers sample without replacement, and ties are broken uniformly. Service requirements have law \(G\) on \([0,\infty)\), with mean one. The initial state is independent of future driving randomness. Each initial queue has finitely many jobs and finite requirements.

The state of a queue contains its residual service requirement and the ordered requirements of its waiting jobs. Service age may also be retained. With no arrivals, its state follows the deterministic unit-rate FIFO service flow. Requirements of size zero leave immediately on reaching service. Write \(E\) for this standard Borel state space and \(q(x)\) for queue length. Queue length determines routing, but is not asserted to be a Markov state.

For a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(E\), write \(Q_\mu^T\) for the nonlinear FIFO queue path law on \([0,T]\), with initial law \(\mu\) and state-dependent arrival rate

\[
 b(r,\mu_t)=\lambda\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}
 p_r(\mu_t)^{d-1-j}p_{r+1}(\mu_t)^j,
 \qquad p_r(\nu)=\nu\{q\geq r\}.                 \tag{1}
\]

Here \(\mu_t\) denotes its one-time law. Paths carry the sigma field generated by all their state coordinates at all times. All path comparisons below include residual requirements, waiting requirements, and any retained ages.

Set

\[
 a=d-1,\qquad \beta=\lambda d,\qquad C=\beta a,
\]

and, for \(1\leq k\leq N\), set

\[
 B_k(T)=\frac{k(k+d-1)e^{2CT}-kd e^{CT}}2,
 \qquad \delta_{N,k}(T)=\min\{1,B_k(T)/N\}.     \tag{2}
\]

**Theorem.** Suppose \(X^N(0)\) is exchangeable and
\(L_N(0)=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{X_i^N(0)}\). Then, almost surely with respect to the law of \(L_N(0)\),

\[
 \left\|\mathcal L\bigl((X_i^N[0,T])_{i=1}^k\mid L_N(0)\bigr)
              -(Q_{L_N(0)}^T)^{\otimes k}\right\|_{\rm TV}
 \leq \delta_{N,k}(T).                         \tag{3}
\]

The convention is \(\|P-R\|_{\rm TV}=\sup_A|P(A)-R(A)|\).
Integrating (3) gives the same bound between the unconditional tagged path law and \(\mathbb E[(Q_{L_N(0)}^T)^{\otimes k}]\).

For fixed \(k,T\), (3) is \(O(N^{-1})\). It retains the random initial empirical measure.

## 1. An exact finite-system proposal construction

Give each server an independent Poisson proposal stream of rate \(\beta=\lambda d\). At a proposal to server \(i\), independently sample an ordered tuple of \(d-1\) distinct competitors uniformly from the other \(N-1\) servers. Also draw an independent uniform variable \(U\) in \([0,1]\) and a requirement with law \(G\). If any competitor has a shorter queue than \(i\), reject the proposal. Otherwise let \(j\) be the number of competitors tied with \(i\), and accept precisely when \(U\leq1/(j+1)\). An accepted proposal appends its requirement to queue \(i\).

This construction has exactly the original full-state process law. Indeed, an original arrival includes \(i\) with probability \(d/N\). Conditional on inclusion, its competitors are distributed as above and its winning probability is exactly the proposal acceptance probability. Multiplying the original arrival rate \(\lambda N\) by \(d/N\) gives \(\beta\). Thus both constructions have the same rate of arrival transitions at each server, with the same independent service mark. These rates sum to \(\lambda N\). Between transitions both constructions use the same deterministic service flow. Equivalently, along that flow the next accepted event has exponential survival function \(e^{-\lambda Nt}\), and its receiving server has the original routing probabilities. Recursion after accepted events identifies the process laws. Proposals that cause no observable state change, such as a zero requirement at an empty server, may be retained as marked events in this argument.

There are at most \(N\beta\) proposals per unit time. Hence the construction is nonexplosive on bounded intervals. Independent proposal streams do not assert independence of the accepted arrival streams.

## 2. Independent trees for the nonlinear queue

To determine one queue's entire path on \([0,T]\), first draw its rate-\(\beta\) proposal stream. At every proposal time \(s\), attach \(a=d-1\) fresh independent queue histories on \([0,s]\), which supply the competitor states just before that proposal. Each attached history is constructed by the same rule. Draw iid initial states with law \(\mu\) for every queue history in this tree, independent of all proposal streams and marks.

This recursion terminates after finitely many histories almost surely. To see this, explore backward from time \(T\). There is initially one active historical queue. Crossing a proposal of an active queue retains that queue and adds \(a\) new historical queues. If there are \(m\) active queues, proposals are crossed at rate \(\beta m\). Thus the population increases from \(m\) to \(m+a\) at that rate. Its expectation at backward time \(s\), stopped at any population threshold, is bounded by \(e^{Cs}\) by the elementary differential inequality for this birth process. Consequently the probability of reaching population \(R\) by \(T\) is at most \(e^{CT}/R\). Letting \(R\) increase proves nonexplosion. The moment calculations in Section 4 also justify this bound directly.

After the finite tree is sampled, reconstruct every history forward from its initial state. At each proposal use the competitor histories, uniform tie mark, service mark, and the FIFO rule of Section 1. This determines the root's full path.

For each fixed \(s\), a competitor subtree rooted at time \(s\) has the same law as this construction run to horizon \(s\). Its driving randomness and initial states are independent of those used for the root before that proposal. Distinct competitor subtrees are independent. Restricting a root history from horizon \(T\) to an earlier horizon discards only later proposals and their attached trees, and preserves the distribution of all retained data. Hence these constructions give consistent one-time laws \(\mu_s\).

Condition on a root proposal while its queue length is \(r\). Put \(u=p_r(\mu_s)\) and \(v=p_{r+1}(\mu_s)\). The probability of acceptance is

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom aj\frac{(u-v)^jv^{a-j}}{j+1}
 =\int_0^1[v+z(u-v)]^a\,dz.                    \tag{4}
\]

The term with \(j\) counts exactly \(j\) equal competitors and \(a-j\) longer competitors; any shorter competitor forces rejection. Multiplication by \(\beta\) turns (4) into (1), including \(u=v\), by the polynomial identity for \(u^d-v^d\). The root therefore has law \(Q_\mu^T\).

For completeness this law is unique. Formula (4) gives

\[
 \sup_r|b(r,\nu)-b(r,\eta)|
 \leq C\sup_r|p_r(\nu)-p_r(\eta)|
 \leq C\|\nu-\eta\|_{\rm TV}.                 \tag{5}
\]

The first inequality follows from
\(|x^a-y^a|\leq a|x-y|\) on \([0,1]\) and the bound on the difference of affine interpolants by their largest endpoint difference. Couple queues in two deterministic environments using the same rate-\(\beta\) proposals, uniform acceptance marks, service marks and initial state. Their probability of a first different acceptance by time \(t\) is at most the time integral of the right side of (5). For two self-consistent laws with the same initial law, put \(h(t)\) equal to that first-disagreement probability. Their one-time total variation distance is at most \(h(t)\), so
\(h(t)\leq C\int_0^t h(s)ds\), and iteration or Gronwall gives \(h(t)=0\). Their path laws agree as well. The finite-tree construction also makes \(\mu\mapsto Q_\mu^T\) measurable: condition on each finite tree shape and integrate its measurable forward reconstruction against finitely many copies of \(\mu\), then sum over the shapes. This justifies the kernels in (3).

To construct \(k\) independent roots, take \(k\) independent such trees. Their union, explored backward, starts with population \(k\) and has transitions

\[
 m\longmapsto m+a\quad\hbox{at rate }\beta m.  \tag{6}
\]

Every historical queue persists to time zero once introduced. The terminal population \(M(T)\) is therefore also the total number of distinct queue histories created in the forest.
The forest is not pruned according to queue states or proposal acceptances: every proposal reveals all its competitors. Its shape depends only on the proposal clocks, independently of its initial states and auxiliary labels.

## 3. Coupling labels, including the initial state

First fix a deterministic initial configuration \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\), and write \(L_x=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{x_i}\). Attach to each queue history in the independent forest an independent uniform label from \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\). Give that history initial state \(x_\ell\), where \(\ell\) is its attached label. These initial states are iid with law \(L_x\), even when labels coincide, because labels are independent and \(x\) is deterministic. Proposal streams and marks for different histories are still independent when their labels coincide. Thus the root path law is exactly \((Q_{L_x}^T)^{\otimes k}\). The labels alone have not made this a finite system.

Now couple this forest with the exact finite-system proposal construction. Its \(k\) root labels are a uniform ordered distinct tuple. Couple them with the forest's iid root labels by keeping the first iid tuple when distinct, and otherwise resampling until distinct. This gives the correct uniform distinct marginal and agrees with the forest whenever its root labels are distinct.

Explore proposals backward. When an active finite-system label has not previously been exposed, its proposal stream on the required past interval is a fresh independent rate-\(\beta\) Poisson stream. Couple it and its independent marks to its forest history. The continuation of the same active history uses that same stream; it is not assigned a new label.

At a proposal, the forest's \(a\) new competitor labels are iid uniform on all \(N\) labels. Generate the finite proposal's ordered competitors by retaining this tuple if it is distinct and avoids the parent label, and otherwise resampling iid tuples until that local condition holds. Conditional on this condition, a tuple is uniform among all ordered distinct competitors excluding the parent, so this procedure has exactly the marginal required in Section 1. In particular it does not exclude previously explored labels other than the parent; a collision with any previously explored label is simply declared a coupling failure.

Before the first collision anywhere in the forest labels, every new competitor is a previously unexposed finite-system server, and every local condition above holds on the first draw. All streams, marks, and initial states then agree. Reconstructing histories forward proves equality of the \(k\) complete paths. After a first failure one can finish the two constructions with their respective correct conditional distributions, without requiring further agreement. This yields a coupling whose failure event is contained in the event that some two forest histories have the same label.

The lazy exposure is legitimate because independent Poisson streams have independent restrictions to disjoint time intervals, and streams of unexposed labels are independent of all data exposed so far. Before failure no server is newly exposed twice. If a later proposal would request another history of an already exposed server, that is precisely a failure event; agreement beyond it is not used. No independence of actual routed queues is assumed.

Conditional on \(M(T)=m\), the \(m\) forest labels are iid uniform and independent of the forest shape. With \((N)_m=N(N-1)\cdots(N-m+1)\), interpreted as zero when \(m>N\), their collision probability is

\[
 1-\frac{(N)_m}{N^m}\leq\frac{m(m-1)}{2N}.    \tag{7}
\]

The inequality is the union bound on the \(\binom m2\) label pairs. For \(m>N\) its right side is at least one. Therefore the full-path distance, for a deterministic initial configuration and uniformly sampled distinct tagged labels, is at most

\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{\mathbb E[M(T)(M(T)-1)]}{2N}\right\}. \tag{8}
\]

## 4. Branching moments and the constant

For the birth process (6), put \(m_1(t)=\mathbb EM(t)\) and \(m_2(t)=\mathbb EM(t)^2\). The increments of the functions \(m\) and \(m^2\) at a jump are \(a\) and \(2am+a^2\). Multiplication by jump rate \(\beta m\) gives

\[
 m_1'=Cm_1,\qquad m_2'=2Cm_2+Ca m_1,
 \qquad m_1(0)=k,\quad m_2(0)=k^2.             \tag{9}
\]

These equations can first be applied to the stopped chain. Nonexplosion follows from the stopped first-moment bound proved above. For a fixed bounded interval, stopped second moments are bounded by the solution of the corresponding inequality with first moment at most \(ke^{Ct}\). Fatou gives a finite second moment of the unstopped chain. The generator integrands in (9) are then integrable on that interval, so the unstopped expectation identities follow by localization and monotone convergence for their nonnegative jump increments.

Solving (9), with \(A=e^{Ct}\), gives

\[
 m_1(t)=kA,\qquad
 m_2(t)=k^2A^2+ak(A^2-A).
\]

Indeed multiplication of the second equation by \(e^{-2Ct}\) gives derivative \(Ca k e^{-Ct}\), whose integral is \(ak(1-e^{-Ct})\). Thus

\[
 \mathbb E[M(t)(M(t)-1)]
 =k(k+a)A^2-k(a+1)A
 =k(k+d-1)e^{2Ct}-kd e^{Ct}.                  \tag{10}
\]

Equations (8) and (10) yield \(\delta_{N,k}(T)\).

Finally randomize the initial configuration. Conditional on its empirical measure, an exchangeable initial configuration is uniformly distributed over the distinct permutations of its multiset. The proposal construction commutes with label permutations. Consequently its fixed tagged paths have, conditional on that empirical measure, the same law as paths at uniformly sampled distinct labels. The bound (8) is uniform over deterministic configurations with that empirical measure. Integrating it proves (3).

## 5. Consequences for stationary starts

Write \(S_t\mu=\mu_t\) for the nonlinear evolution. Suppose the finite initial law is stationary and exchangeable. For any measurable \(f:E\to[0,1]\), its two-tag covariance obeys

\[
 \left|\operatorname{Cov}(f(X_1^N),f(X_2^N))
       -\operatorname{Var}\bigl((S_tL_N(0))f\bigr)\right|
 \leq\delta_{N,2}(t)+2\delta_{N,1}(t).         \tag{11}
\]

To prove this, project the path comparison to time \(t\). Under the comparison mixture, the joint product expectation is \(\mathbb E[((S_tL_N(0))f)^2]\) and the one-tag mean is \(\mathbb E[(S_tL_N(0))f]\). The two-tag product expectation differs by at most \(\delta_{N,2}\), and the one-tag means differ by at most \(\delta_{N,1}\). For numbers in \([0,1]\), \(|x^2-y^2|\leq2|x-y|\). Subtract the squared means and use stationarity to obtain (11).

There is also a conditional empirical estimate. For \(N\geq2\), put \(m=(S_tL_N(0))f\). Then

\[
 \mathbb E\left[\bigl(L_N(t)f-m\bigr)^2\mid L_N(0)\right]
 \leq\frac1N+\frac{N-1}{N}\delta_{N,2}(t).    \tag{12}
\]

Conditional on \(L_N(0)\), expand the square into diagonal and ordered off-diagonal terms and use conditional exchangeability. Each diagonal term \((f-m)^2\) is at most one. Each off-diagonal test is \(g(x,y)=(f(x)-m)(f(y)-m)\). Its expectation under \((S_tL_N(0))^{\otimes2}\) is zero. Its range is contained in
\([-m(1-m),\max\{m^2,(1-m)^2\}]\), whose length is \(\max\{m,1-m\}\leq1\). For any bounded measurable test of range length at most one, the difference of expectations is at most total variation distance, by subtracting its lower endpoint and using the layer-cake formula. Apply (3), project to time \(t\), and sum the \(N\) diagonal and \(N(N-1)\) off-diagonal terms. This proves (12).

For clarity, (12) gives a quantitative invariant-law reduction without any topological assumption. If \(f_1,\ldots,f_h:E\to[0,1]\) are measurable and \(\varphi:[0,1]^h\to\mathbb R\) is 1-Lipschitz for the sum metric, stationarity, the Lipschitz inequality, and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\[
 \left|\mathbb E\varphi((L_N(0)f_j)_{j=1}^h)
       -\mathbb E\varphi(((S_tL_N(0))f_j)_{j=1}^h)\right|
 \leq h\sqrt{\frac1N+\frac{N-1}{N}\delta_{N,2}(t)}. \tag{13}
\]

The empirical directing law is therefore approximately invariant under the nonlinear flow, in these finite collections of observables. Equations (11)–(13) do not force it to concentrate on a single measure. To pass from (13) to an invariant subsequential law in a chosen topology, tightness and continuity of the nonlinear flow in that topology still have to be justified.

## 6. Independent checks and limits of the estimate

Here is a second derivation of (10), using the full branching law. Put \(p=e^{-Ct}\). For the integer process \(J=(M-k)/a\), the rate from \(j\) to \(j+1\) is \(C(j+k/a)\). Its probabilities are

\[
 \mathbb P(J(t)=j)=\frac{(k/a)^{\overline j}}{j!}
                         p^{k/a}(1-p)^j,
 \qquad j\geq0,                              \tag{14}
\]

where the numerator is the rising factorial and its value at \(j=0\) is one. Their sum is one by the Taylor series of \((1-z)^{-k/a}\) at \(z=1-p\). They have the required initial probabilities. Differentiation, using \(p'=-Cp\), verifies the forward equations
\(P_j'=C(j-1+k/a)P_{j-1}-C(j+k/a)P_j\), with the first term absent for \(j=0\). Nonexplosion of the birth chain makes these its probabilities. The generating function of \(M\) is consequently

\[
 F(z)=z^k\left[\frac p{1-(1-p)z^a}\right]^{k/a}. \tag{15}
\]

It is differentiable near \(z=1\), since the denominator there is \(p>0\). Logarithmic differentiation gives

\[
 F'(1)=\frac{k}{p},\qquad
 (\log F)''(1)=-k+\frac{k(1-p)(a-1)}p
                    +\frac{ka(1-p)^2}{p^2}.
\]

Adding \((F'(1))^2\) to the second expression gives

\[
 F''(1)=\frac{k(k+a)-k(a+1)p}{p^2},
\]

which independently recovers the factorial moment (10). The rate parameter in this derivation is also checked directly: every proposal produces exactly \(a\) children, at per-history rate \(\beta\), so the expected population grows at rate \(\beta a=C\).

For a second check of (7), when \(m\leq N\) reveal labels successively. Conditional on the first \(j\) being distinct, the next avoids them with probability \(1-j/N\). Thus
\((N)_m/N^m=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-j/N)\). The inequality
\(1-\prod_j(1-z_j)\leq\sum_jz_j\) for \(z_j\in[0,1]\) gives the same coefficient \(m(m-1)/(2N)\). If \(m>N\), a collision is certain and the bound is still valid.

At \(T=0\), (2) reduces to the initial-sampling bound \(k(k-1)/(2N)\). For \(k=1\) it is zero. The derivative \(B_k'(0)=Ck(2k+d-2)/2\) also follows directly from the instantaneous rate \(\beta k\), multiplying the change \(\binom{k+a}{2}-\binom k2=ak+a(a-1)/2\). When \(N=d\), all local competitor tuples still exist; the capped bound remains valid. When the forest contains more than \(N\) histories, its collision probability is one and no collision-free reconstruction is claimed.

No service density, hazard assumption, second moment, bound on service support, or lower bound on queue-length class masses was used. In fact, the transient comparison needs only finite service requirements almost surely. Zero requirements cause finite cascades through a finite queue; since the explored forest and the number of proposals are finite, they do not invalidate its reconstruction. Every retained service-age coordinate agrees under the successful coupling. The initial configuration may be dependent and arbitrary before exchangeable conditioning.

For fixed \(k\), the error vanishes at \(T_N=b\log N\) whenever \(b<1/(2C)\), since its leading order is \(N^{2Cb-1}\). At \(b=1/(2C)\), this estimate no longer tends to zero. For varying \(k\), it suffices that \(k(k+d-1)e^{2CT}/N\to0\).

Under stationary starts, a deterministic product limit still requires that the variance in (11) tend to zero and that the one-server means have a unique limit. The bound supplies neither property. For an initial mixture of two empirical measures, the comparison in (3) remains a mixture of their nonlinear laws. Uniqueness of a fixed point alone does not exclude invariant laws supported on other trajectories.

The backward dependency method is related to the clan-of-ancestors construction in Maria Clara Fittipaldi, Matthieu Jonckheere, Sergio I. Lopez, *Convergence properties of many parallel servers under power-of-D load balancing*, [arXiv:1809.05373](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05373). The proof above does not use that paper's arrival-rate monotonicity or stationary-limit assertions.
