# A uniform surface-pressure bound from safe states

This note proves the surface estimate needed after the conditional replacement and geometry in rounds 3 and 4. It does not prove a liquid–vapour transition. The motivating argument is Section 4 of [*Density coexistence for hard spheres with a long-range attractive pair interaction*, Hexagon 2610.00734v1](https://hexagonmath.org/api/v1/works/2610.00734v1/source?path=liquid-solid.tex), credited as generated by GPT-6 Astra and prompted by Qidong He. The constants and the measure construction below are derived explicitly; no novelty claim is made for the entropy method.

## 1. A general theorem

Use the Chebyshev adjacency graph on \(\mathbb Z^d\). Let \((E,\alpha)\) be a standard Borel probability space. At each site the state space is a translated copy of this space. For adjacent sites impose measurable, translation-covariant pair constraints with values in \(\{0,1\}\). There are no hard constraints on nonadjacent pairs. Assume there is a measurable set \(S\subset E\) with

\[
\alpha(S)\ge e^{-\kappa}>0,
\tag{1}
\]

and every state in \(S\) satisfies every incident pair constraint against every state in \(E\). Constraints are properties of unordered pairs, so this applies with either endpoint safe. Choose a representative safe state if an everywhere-defined exterior is needed.

Let \(\Phi=(\Phi_X)\) be a translation-covariant interaction, where each \(X\) is a finite nonempty connected set and \(\Phi_X:E^X\to\mathbb R\) is measurable and bounded everywhere. Assume

\[
\sum_{X\ni0}\|\Phi_X\|_\infty\le M<\infty.
\tag{2}
\]

All energies and pressures in this theorem are dimensionless. For finite \(\Lambda\), set

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}
 &=\int I_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}(x)
    \exp\left(-\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}\Phi_X(x_X)\right)
    \alpha^{\otimes\Lambda}(dx),\\
Z_\Lambda^\omega
 &=\int I_\Lambda^\omega(x)
    \exp\left(-\sum_{X:X\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing}
                    \Phi_X((x\omega)_X)\right)
    \alpha^{\otimes\Lambda}(dx).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The first indicator includes hard constraints internal to \(\Lambda\); the second also includes those crossing its boundary. Interactions entirely outside \(\Lambda\) are omitted. The exterior \(\omega\in E^{\Lambda^c}\) need not satisfy constraints wholly outside. Let \(b_\Lambda=|\partial\Lambda|\), where the inner boundary consists of sites having an adjacent site outside.

**Theorem.** There is a stationary Gibbs measure for (3), and a real number \(p\) such that every finite \(\Lambda\) and every exterior \(\omega\) satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
-M b_\Lambda
&\le\log Z_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}-p|\Lambda|
 \le(4M+\kappa)b_\Lambda,\\
-(4M+\kappa)b_\Lambda
&\le\log Z_\Lambda^\omega-p|\Lambda|
 \le(5M+\kappa)b_\Lambda.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

In particular both absolute errors are at most \((5M+\kappa)b_\Lambda\). Along growing cubes, \(|\Lambda|^{-1}\log Z_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}\to p\). No uniqueness or correlation-decay assumption is used.

## 2. Boundary comparison and conditional density bounds

Every connected \(X\) crossing \(\Lambda\) contains a vertex of \(\partial\Lambda\): take a path in \(X\) from inside to outside and its last inside vertex. Therefore

\[
\sum_{X\text{ crossing }\Lambda}\|\Phi_X\|_\infty
\le M b_\Lambda.
\tag{5}
\]

More generally, the sum of norms of interactions meeting a finite set \(T\) is at most \(M|T|\). All sums converge absolutely, by (2).

Put \(T=\partial\Lambda\). In the free Gibbs law, condition on \(x_{\Lambda\setminus T}\). Factors independent of \(T\) cancel. Dropping hard constraints bounds its remaining conditional integral above by \(e^{M|T|}\). Restricting each \(x_i\), \(i\in T\), to \(S\) gives a lower integral \(e^{-M|T|}\alpha(S)^{|T|}\), since all constraints involving \(T\) then hold. Thus, for almost every conditioned interior,

\[
\mathbb P_{\mathrm f}(x_T\in S^T\mid x_{\Lambda\setminus T})
\ge e^{-(2M+\kappa)|T|}.
\tag{6}
\]

On this event exterior constraints also hold. The crossing soft factor lies between \(e^{-Mb_\Lambda}\) and \(e^{Mb_\Lambda}\). Its free expectation, including the exterior hard indicator, is exactly \(Z_\Lambda^\omega/Z_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}\). Consequently

\[
e^{-(3M+\kappa)b_\Lambda}
\le\frac{Z_\Lambda^\omega}{Z_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}}
\le e^{Mb_\Lambda}.
\tag{7}
\]

This also gives the everywhere upper bound

\[
\frac{d\gamma_\Lambda^\omega}{d\gamma_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}}
\le e^{(4M+\kappa)b_\Lambda}.
\tag{8}
\]

All finite partition functions are positive: restrict every interior state to \(S\). They are finite since their soft energy has absolute value at most \(M|\Lambda|\).

The same argument on all sites of a finite \(F\), with arbitrary exterior, bounds the density of its normalized conditional law relative to \(\alpha^{\otimes F}\) by

\[
e^{(2M+\kappa)|F|}.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed its numerator is at most \(e^{M|F|}\) and its denominator at least \(e^{-(M+\kappa)|F|}\). Averaging conditional laws preserves this bound for finite marginals and for conditioning on fewer exterior coordinates.

## 3. Existence of a stationary Gibbs law

Take finite-volume laws in increasing boxes with a fixed safe exterior. Their marginals on every fixed finite \(F\), once the box contains \(F\), have the bound (9). These densities lie in a bounded ball of \(L^\infty(\alpha^{\otimes F})\). The corresponding \(L^1\) space is separable: a standard Borel sigma algebra is countably generated, and simple functions from a countable generating algebra are dense. Banach–Alaoglu and this separability give a weak-* convergent subsequence. Diagonalize over the countably many finite sets \(F\).

The limits are nonnegative, integrate to one, and are consistent, since those properties are tested by bounded measurable local functions. The extension theorem gives a probability measure \(\nu\) on \(E^{\mathbb Z^d}\) with these marginals. In particular, expectations of every bounded measurable local function converge. Every hard constraint passes to the limit by testing its indicator.

For completeness the Gibbs identity also passes, although the constraints need not be continuous. Fix finite \(F\). Retain only those soft interactions meeting \(F\) whose diameter is at most \(k\), with \(k\ge1\). Every omitted set meeting \(F\) has norm total at most

\[
|F|\sum_{X\ni0:\operatorname{diam}X>k}\|\Phi_X\|_\infty
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]

The truncated conditional kernel depends on finitely many exterior states and is measurable there. Keeping identical hard constraints and changing a soft Hamiltonian by at most \(\epsilon\) changes its normalized law in total variation by at most \(e^{2\epsilon}-1\): both the unnormalized density ratio and the partition-function ratio lie in \([e^{-\epsilon},e^\epsilon]\). Thus the truncated kernels approximate the full one uniformly in exterior states.

For a bounded local observable and a bounded local exterior observable, the truncated Gibbs identity passes to the limit because both sides are bounded measurable local functions. Equation (10) then passes the full identity. A monotone-class argument extends the exterior observable to the full exterior sigma algebra. This proves DLR. The same bound (9) holds for its finite conditional laws by DLR.

Average translates of \(\nu\) over growing cubes and take another local weak-* limit using (9). Each average is Gibbs by covariance and linearity. The preceding kernel argument preserves DLR in the limit. Translating an average changes expectations of a bounded local function by at most twice its supremum times the relative size of the translated cube's symmetric difference. This tends to zero. The limit is stationary. Denote it again by \(\nu\).

## 4. Conditional entropy without a mixing hypothesis

All entropies use the product reference \(\alpha\); write \(H=-D\). For a finite set \(F\) and a sigma algebra \(\mathcal G\) on other coordinates, \(H_\nu(x_F\mid\mathcal G)\) is the average negative relative entropy of the conditional law with respect to \(\alpha^{\otimes F}\).

Every one-site conditional density, given any subset of the other sites, is at most \(C_*=e^{2M+\kappa}\), by DLR, (9), and averaging. Its entropy lies in \([-\log C_*,0]\). Thus all finite chain rules below have finite terms. The chain rule follows by writing a joint density as a marginal density times a conditional density and integrating its logarithm. Additional conditioning decreases \(H\), by convexity of \(f\mapsto\int f\log f\,d\alpha\). These facts continue to infinite conditioning sigma algebras: the conditional densities form martingales along increasing finite coordinate sets, converge in \(L^1\), and are bounded by \(C_*\); the bounded continuous function \(u\log u\) on \([0,C_*]\), with value zero at zero, then permits passage of their entropy integrals.

Use a translation-invariant lexicographic order \(\prec\) on \(\mathbb Z^d\), and its past \(P=\{i:i\prec0\}\). Put

\[
s=H_\nu(x_0\mid x_P).
\tag{11}
\]

In a chain rule for \(H_\nu(x_\Lambda)\), the earlier sites within \(\Lambda\) are a subset of the full past of each site. Therefore each summand is at least \(s\), by stationarity and monotonicity under conditioning. In the chain rule conditional on \(\Lambda^c\), the conditioning set contains the full past at every site, so each term is at most \(s\). Hence

\[
H_\nu(x_\Lambda\mid x_{\Lambda^c})
\le s|\Lambda|\le H_\nu(x_\Lambda).
\tag{12}
\]

Neither inequality bounds a correlation length.

## 5. Energy allocation and pressure

Define

\[
e=\mathbb E_\nu\sum_{X\ni0}\frac{\Phi_X(x_X)}{|X|}.
\tag{13}
\]

This converges absolutely. Translation invariance and absolutely convergent rearrangement give

\[
e|\Lambda|=\sum_{X:X\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing}
     \frac{|X\cap\Lambda|}{|X|}\mathbb E_\nu\Phi_X.
\tag{14}
\]

Subtract the internal energy sum from (14): only crossing sets remain, with coefficients between zero and one. Subtract (14) from the incident energy sum: again only crossing sets remain, with coefficients between zero and one. By (5), both discrepancies have absolute value at most \(Mb_\Lambda\).

The marginal \(\nu_\Lambda\) satisfies internal hard constraints. Relative entropy with respect to the free finite-volume Gibbs law is nonnegative. Expanding its logarithm gives

\[
\log Z_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}
\ge H_\nu(x_\Lambda)
      -\mathbb E_\nu\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}\Phi_X
\ge (s-e)|\Lambda|-Mb_\Lambda.
\tag{15}
\]

DLR gives the exact conditional identity

\[
\mathbb E_\nu\log Z_\Lambda^{x_{\Lambda^c}}
=H_\nu(x_\Lambda\mid x_{\Lambda^c})
 -\mathbb E_\nu\sum_{X:X\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing}\Phi_X
\le(s-e)|\Lambda|+Mb_\Lambda.
\tag{16}
\]

To see its sign directly, the conditional log density relative to the product reference is minus its incident energy minus \(\log Z\); integrate and negate. Hard indicators equal one under the conditional law and contribute zero there.

The lower ratio bound in (7) implies

\[
\log Z_\Lambda^{\mathrm f}
\le\mathbb E_\nu\log Z_\Lambda^{x_{\Lambda^c}}
 +(3M+\kappa)b_\Lambda.
\tag{17}
\]

Combine (15)–(17), with \(p=s-e\), to obtain the free bounds in (4). Add the two one-sided bounds in (7) to obtain its conditioned bounds. The cube boundary-to-volume ratio tends to zero, identifying \(p\) as the pressure and making it independent of the stationary law used to compute it. This proves the theorem.

If the unnormalized one-cell measure is \(z\alpha\), multiply all partition functions by \(z^{|\Lambda|}\) and replace \(p\) by \(\log z+s-e\); all surface constants are unchanged.

## 6. Substitution for the continuum restricted ensemble

Here the lattice state is the entire particle configuration in a macrocell of side \(L\), with every phase cell in its endpoint band \(c\in\{0,r\}\). Fix \(0<\delta<\min\{1,r/100\}\). Internally admissible finite configurations form a standard Borel space. Packing bounds their particle number. Let \(a<L\), and let the attractive kernel \(J_R\) have range \(R<L\), be nonnegative, normalized and smooth. There are no physical interactions between nonadjacent macrocells. The following estimates explain all inputs to the theorem; constants denoted \(C\) depend on the fixed geometry and kernel.

Spread particles uniformly in fine cubes of side \(h\). Repeating the particles of one fine cube with gaps of width \(a\) proves the packing cap

\[
0\le\rho_h\le r+\epsilon_h,
\qquad \epsilon_h=r[(1+a/h)^d-1].
\tag{18}
\]

In a dilute phase cell \(C\), \(\int_C|\rho_h|\le\delta|C|\). In a dense phase cell,

\[
\int_C|\rho_h-r|
=r|C|-N_C+2\int_C(\rho_h-r)_+
\le(\delta+2\epsilon_h)|C|.
\tag{19}
\]

A ball of radius \(2R\) intersects phase cells of total volume at most \(CR^d\), since their side is a fixed multiple \(bR\). Equations (18)–(19) give a field bound \(C(\delta+\epsilon_h)\) for the convolution of a centered fine density with \(J_R\). Integrate this against the centered density in one macrocell: its absolute pair energy, summed over all incident cells, is at most \(C(\delta+\epsilon_h)^2L^d\). Replacing fine densities by particle measures changes the incident energy by at most \(C(h/R)L^d\): moving either argument by \(O(h)\) changes the kernel by \(ChR^{-d-1}\), and packing bounds the contributing partners by \(CR^d\). Thus, with

\[
e_R=h/R+h^{-1}+R^{-d},
\tag{20}
\]

the centered dimensionless soft interaction, including an additional connected interaction of incident norm \(\varepsilon_U\), satisfies

\[
M\le C\beta(\delta^2+e_R)L^d+\varepsilon_U.
\tag{21}
\]

This bound holds on the product of individually admissible macrostates, even when particles in distinct cells clash: the estimates use the packing cap separately within each macrocell. Cross-cell hard cores remain separate indicators.

The one-cell reference is

\[
\alpha_c(dq)=z_c^{-1}\mathbf1_{\mathcal X_{Q}^c}(q)
 e^{\beta h_cN_Q}\,dq,
\quad h_c=\mu+c-\tfrac12J_R(0),
\quad\mu=-r/2+t,\quad |t|\le\delta.
\tag{22}
\]

For large \(R\), \(h_0<0<h_r\) and both are uniformly bounded. In the dilute phase use the empty configuration as safe set. Its unnormalized mass is one, while \(z_0\le e^{L^d}\).

For the dense phase choose a fixed periodic packing of density at least \(r-c\delta^2\). Such a packing is obtained by repeating a sufficiently dense large finite packing with an \(a\)-wide gap. Dilate it by \(1+c'\delta^2\). Put disjoint displacement balls of radius a small multiple of \(\delta^2\) at its centers. Retain only balls entirely at distance at least \(2a\) from the macrocell boundary. For sufficiently large \(R\), periodic counting with a boundary error \(O_\delta(\ell^{d-1})\) in a phase cell of side \(\ell=bR\) gives at least \((r-\delta)\ell^d\) particles in every phase cell, and a total count in \([(r-C\delta^2)L^d,rL^d]\). Independent displacements preserve exclusion and compatibility with every exterior. Their configuration volume is \(v_\delta^N\ge e^{-C_\delta L^d}\); the assignments of labeled particles to disjoint balls cancel the grand-canonical factorial. Packing and the unrestricted configuration volume \(e^{L^d}\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_r&\le\exp\{\beta h_r(r+O(L^{-1}))L^d+L^d\},\\
\int_S e^{\beta h_rN_Q}\,dq
&\ge\exp\{\beta h_r(r-C\delta^2)L^d-C_\delta L^d\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

Consequently both phases have

\[
\kappa\le C[\beta(\delta^2+e_R)+1+C_\delta]L^d.
\tag{24}
\]

All thresholds on \(R\) here may depend on \(\delta\) and on the fixed packing period. No uniformity in \(\delta\to0\) at fixed \(R\) is asserted. Equations (21), (24), and (4) give a surface coefficient, divided by \(\beta L^d\), at most

\[
C\left[\delta^2+e_R+\frac{1+C_\delta}{\beta}
       +\frac{\varepsilon_U}{\beta L^d}\right].
\tag{25}
\]

This coefficient is uniform in all microscopic exterior macrostates in the same phase band. Positional order and long-range correlations cause no additional entropy term.

## 7. Physical boundary terms and the truncated-ensemble application

Write \(A\) for a finite macrocell union, \(\xi_A=n_A-c\mathbf1_A dx\), and

\[
B_A=\langle\mathbf1_A,J_R\mathbf1_{A^c}\rangle.
\]

The centered free Hamiltonian is \(H_A^{\mathrm{hc}}-h_cN_A-\tfrac12\langle\xi_A,J_R\xi_A\rangle\), and its conditioned version adds \(-\langle\xi_A,J_R\xi_{A^c}\rangle\) and cross hard cores. Direct expansion gives

\[
H_{A,\mu}(q_A\mid q_{A^c})
=\widetilde H_A^c(q_A\mid q_{A^c})
 +\frac{c^2}{2}(|A|+B_A)
 -c\langle\mathbf1_A,J_R n_{A^c}\rangle.
\tag{26}
\]

Indeed \(\langle n_A,J_R\mathbf1_A\rangle+\langle n_A,J_R\mathbf1_{A^c}\rangle=N_A\), \(\langle\mathbf1_A,J_R\mathbf1_A\rangle=|A|-B_A\), and the diagonal in \(\langle n_A,J_Rn_A\rangle\) is \(J_R(0)N_A\), canceled by (22). As an independent sign check, substitute constant densities \(n_A=c\,dx\), \(n_{A^c}=c\,dx\): the boundary contribution left after the volume term is \(-c^2B_A/2\), exactly the crossing attraction plus the missing half of the internal attraction.

Let \(\widetilde p_c\) be the centered pressure in energy units. Put \(p_c^U=\widetilde p_c-c^2/2\). Equations (4) and (26) yield

\[
\log Z_A^{c,U}(q)
=\beta p_c^U|A|
 +\beta c\langle\mathbf1_A,J_R n_q\rangle
 -\frac{\beta c^2}{2}B_A+E_c(A,q),
\quad |E_c|\le(5M+\kappa)|\partial_L A|.
\tag{27}
\]

This \(p_c^U\) is also the free physical pressure. Expanding without exterior particles gives

\[
H_A^{\mathrm f}=\widetilde H_A^{c,\mathrm f}
 +\frac{c^2}{2}|A|+c\langle n_A,J_R\mathbf1_{A^c}\rangle
 -\frac{c^2}{2}B_A.
\]

The last two terms involve only boundary macrocells and are bounded by \(CL^d|\partial_L A|\), uniformly in admissible states, by packing and normalization of \(J_R\). At fixed \(R,L\), division by the volume of a growing cube makes this error vanish. The same boundary argument permits either internal or conditioned \(U\) terms in defining the bulk pressure.

For the Section 6 application, assume separately that the fixed-background contour expansion produces an everywhere bounded, measurable, translation-covariant connected interaction \(U^c\) of norm \(\varepsilon_U\), and that its finite-domain identity is

\[
\log\Xi_A^c(q)=-\sum_{X\subset A}U_X^c(q_X).
\tag{28}
\]

These are explicit remaining hypotheses here; the cluster expansion is not reproved in this note. If the original exterior has phase \(c\) only in its one-cell collar, keep it there and replace each remaining exterior macrocell by a safe phase-\(c\) state. The resulting exterior is admissible: every changed cell is safe against all unchanged states. Physical interactions in \(A\) and the linear field in (27) are unchanged because their ranges are less than \(L\). The left side of (28) depends only on interior states under that identity, so its use in the restricted integral is unchanged as well.

The integral with (28) omits crossing terms of \(U^c\); the conditioned partition function in (27) includes them. Connectedness and the norm bound give a pointwise energy difference at most \(\varepsilon_U|\partial_L A|\), hence the same logarithmic partition-function difference. Thus the truncated integral obeys (27) with error at most

\[
(5M+\kappa+\varepsilon_U)|\partial_L A|.
\tag{29}
\]

This validates the surface-pressure implication used in Section 6, conditional on (28) and its interaction norm. It does not validate the contour norm itself, the defect-energy lower bound, the final parameter inequalities, removal of truncation, or coexistence.

## 8. An exact stress-test family

On \(\mathbb Z^2\), let \(E=\{0,1,2\}\), \(\alpha\) uniform, and use interactions only on horizontal edges. State 0 is safe. States 1 and 2 on an edge are incompatible if different. The allowed edge weight is 1 if at least one endpoint is zero and \(k\) if both are the same nonzero state. For a rational \(t>1\), set

\[
k=1+2t-t^{-1},\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&k&0\\1&0&k\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{30}
\]

Here \(\kappa=\log3\) and \(M=2\log k\). Direct multiplication gives eigenpairs

\[
A(1,t,t)^T=(1+2t)(1,t,t)^T.
\]

The other eigenvalues are \(k\), on \((0,1,-1)\), and \(1-t^{-1}\), on \((-2t,1,1)\). The positive eigenvalue \(1+2t\) is strictly greater in modulus than the other two. Consequently the dimensionless pressure per site is

\[
p=\log\lambda,\qquad \lambda=(1+2t)/3.
\tag{31}
\]

For a horizontal interval of \(n\ge1\) sites,

\[
Z_n^{\mathrm f}
=\frac{\mathbf1^TA^{n-1}\mathbf1}{3^n}
=C_t\lambda^{n-1}+D_t\left(\frac{1-t^{-1}}3\right)^{n-1},
\tag{32}
\]

where

\[
C_t=\frac{(1+2t)^2}{3(1+2t^2)},\qquad D_t=1-C_t.
\]

These coefficients are squared projections onto the two symmetric eigenvectors, divided by three. At \(t=2\), \(Z_n^{\mathrm f}/\lambda^n=(5+4\cdot10^{-n})/9\). The antisymmetric eigenvalue ratio is \(k/(1+2t)=1-[t(1+2t)]^{-1}\), tending to one. Its eigenvector has nonzero variance in the stationary transfer-matrix law, so the corresponding correlation length \(-1/\log(k/(1+2t))\) diverges. The surface theorem remains valid uniformly in the finite domain; its constants depend on \(k\), as specified.

For an independent entropy check, normalize \(v=(1,t,t)\). The stationary Gibbs chain has \(\pi_i=v_i^2/(1+2t^2)\) and transition probabilities \(P_{ij}=A_{ij}v_j/[(1+2t)v_i]\). Detailed balance follows from symmetry of \(A\). Its entropy relative to the uniform one-site reference is

\[
s=-\sum_{ij}\pi_iP_{ij}\log(3P_{ij}).
\]

Its soft energy per site is \(e=-(\pi_1P_{11}+\pi_2P_{22})\log k\). Substitution of \(P\) cancels \(\log v_j-\log v_i\) by stationarity and gives \(s-e=\log\lambda\), independently of (32). Zero transition terms contribute zero. The checker compares direct configuration sums, transfer-matrix multiplication and (32), and checks the boundary constants using rational inequalities. Thus its decisive partition-function tests need no floating-point logarithms.

The finite-block entropy checks also have independent formulas. Write \(h\) for the ordinary one-step Shannon entropy rate, \(H(\pi)\) for the one-site Shannon entropy, and \(h_{n+1}=H(X_{n+1}\mid X_0)\). For a block of length \(n\), the Markov property gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_\alpha(X_1,\ldots,X_n)-ns&=H(\pi)-h\ge0,\\
H_\alpha(X_1,\ldots,X_n\mid\text{outside})-ns
 &=h-h_{n+1}\le0.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the second equality, subtract the entropy of the two endpoints from the entropy of \((X_0,\ldots,X_{n+1})\), and then subtract \(n\log3\). Its sign follows because conditioning on \(X_n\) gives entropy \(h\), whereas the earlier \(X_0\) can only give less information about \(X_{n+1}\), by the Markov property and entropy monotonicity.

Without a positive-mass safe set the theorem would be false. Take \(E=\{1,2\}\), uniform reference, no soft interaction, and permit only equal horizontal neighbors. For an interval, \(Z_n^{\mathrm f}=2^{1-n}\). Pinning the two exterior neighbors to different states gives \(Z_n^\omega=0\), even though the exterior alone can be admissible on its two disconnected half-lines. No finite uniform logarithmic boundary bound is possible. This example tests the scope of the safe-state assumption rather than the continuum model.

Connectedness of soft supports also matters. On \(\mathbb Z^2\) take a singleton state space, no hard constraints, and constant interactions \(\Phi_{\{i,i+ne_1\}}=n^{-2}\), \(n\ge1\). The incident norm is \(M=2\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}<\infty\), but most supports are disconnected. For a square of side \(N\), the pressure is \(p=-\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}\), and direct counting gives

\[
\log Z_{[1,N]^2}^{\mathrm f}-pN^2
=N\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac1n
 +N^2\sum_{n=N}^\infty\frac1{n^2}.
\]

Its ratio to the Chebyshev boundary size \(4N-4\) diverges. Thus an unweighted incident norm on arbitrary disconnected supports would not suffice. This does not contradict the theorem or the proposed contour application, whose supports are connected.
