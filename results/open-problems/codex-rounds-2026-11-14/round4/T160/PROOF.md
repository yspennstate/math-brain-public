# A finite-box response criterion for the IIC limit

The results below apply to Bernoulli bond percolation on the nearest-neighbor
cubic lattice at every fixed parameter \(0<p<1\). They give an exact
criterion for the limit in T160, but do not establish its asymptotic input.

## 1. Interior and exterior partitions

Write \(B_j=[-j,j]^3\cap\mathbb Z^3\), let \(\partial B_j\) be its
vertex boundary \(\{x:\|x\|_\infty=j\}\), and let \(E(B_j)\) be the
edges with both endpoints in \(B_j\). Fix integers \(n>m\ge1\), and set

\[
F_m=E(B_m),\qquad S_m=\partial B_m,\qquad
G_{m,n}=E(B_n)\setminus F_m.
\]

Let \(\eta\in\{0,1\}^{F_m}\) denote an interior configuration and

\[
\alpha_m(\eta)=p^{|\eta|}(1-p)^{|F_m|-|\eta|}.
\]

All these atom probabilities are positive. The interior partition

\[
\pi_m(\eta)\quad\text{of }S_m\cup\{0\}
\]

puts two terminals in the same block exactly when an open path using only
\(F_m\) joins them. Interior components containing no terminal are omitted.
In particular, if the root component misses \(S_m\), its block in this
partition is just \(\{0\}\). Let \(\mathcal P_m\) be the finite collection
of partitions realized by some interior configuration. Only these
realizable partitions will be used.

Construct an exterior partition \(\xi_{m,n}\) of \(S_m\cup\{*\}\) as
follows. Use the open edges of \(G_{m,n}\), adjoin a new terminal \(*\), and
join every vertex of \(\partial B_n\) to \(*\) by a deterministic edge.
Two terminals belong to the same block of \(\xi_{m,n}\) exactly when this
augmented exterior graph connects them. All exterior components meeting the
target are thus recorded as one marked block. This wiring does not assert
that they were connected before reaching the target.

To compare the two partitions, extend each to \(S_m\cup\{0,*\}\) by
adding its missing terminal as a singleton. Their join \(\pi\vee\xi\)
is the equivalence relation generated by both partitions. Define

\[
K_m(\pi,\xi)=\mathbf 1\{0\sim *\text{ in }\pi\vee\xi\}.
\tag{1}
\]

**Lemma 1.** For every interior and exterior configuration,

\[
\mathbf 1\{0\leftrightarrow\partial B_n\}
=K_m(\pi_m(\eta),\xi_{m,n}).                       \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** A path from the root to \(\partial B_n\) can be stopped at
its first visit to that boundary, so it lies in \(B_n\). Each change between
an \(F_m\) edge and a \(G_{m,n}\) edge takes place at a vertex of \(S_m\):
every vertex of \(B_m\setminus S_m\) has all its lattice neighbors in
\(B_m\). Break the path into its maximal interior and exterior portions.
Their endpoints give a sequence of identifications in \(\pi_m(\eta)\)
and \(\xi_{m,n}\), ending at the target terminal. Thus the right side of
(2) is one.

Conversely, a finite chain witnessing \(0\sim *\) in the join can be
realized by interior open paths and paths in the augmented exterior graph.
Concatenate these paths. Stop at the first visit to \(*\); its preceding
vertex is in \(\partial B_n\). The portion before that visit uses only
ordinary open edges and reaches the target. Removing loops gives an open
path. This also explains why wiring different target vertices creates no
false root-to-target connection. Both directions allow arbitrarily many
returns to the interior. ∎

## 2. Exterior responses and their normalization

Take probability over exterior edges only, and put

\[
h_{m,n}(\pi)=\mathbb P_p^{G_{m,n}}
   \bigl(K_m(\pi,\xi_{m,n})=1\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(\pi_\star\) be the partition with a single block
\(S_m\cup\{0\}\). This partition is realizable, for example by opening
all edges of \(F_m\). Define

\[
H_{m,n}=\{S_m\leftrightarrow\partial B_n
                 \text{ using }G_{m,n}\},\qquad
b_{m,n}=\mathbb P_p^{G_{m,n}}(H_{m,n}).
\]

An outward axis path from \((m,0,0)\) to \((n,0,0)\) shows
\(b_{m,n}>0\). By the definition of the single-block partition,

\[
h_{m,n}(\pi_\star)=b_{m,n}.                         \tag{4}
\]

If \(K_m(\pi,\xi)=1\), the block of \(*\) in \(\xi\) must contain
some boundary vertex: interior identifications cannot join anything directly
to \(*\). Therefore the event in (3) is contained in \(H_{m,n}\). Set

\[
r_{m,n}(\pi)=\frac{h_{m,n}(\pi)}{b_{m,n}},\qquad
0\le r_{m,n}(\pi)\le1,\quad r_{m,n}(\pi_\star)=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Equivalently, if \(\rho_{m,n}\) is the law of \(\xi_{m,n}\) conditional
on \(H_{m,n}\), then

\[
r_{m,n}(\pi)=\sum_\xi K_m(\pi,\xi)\rho_{m,n}(\xi).
\tag{6}
\]

The sum is finite. Thus the normalized response vector is a fixed linear
image of a probability vector on marked exterior partitions. No independence
between the blocks of an exterior partition is asserted or needed.

## 3. Exact conditional law inside a box

Let

\[
\mu_n=\mathbb P_p(\,\cdot\mid0\leftrightarrow\partial B_n),
\quad a_m(\pi)=\mathbb P_p(\pi_m=\pi),
\quad\nu_{m,\pi}=\mathbb P_p(\,\cdot\mid\pi_m=\pi)|_{F_m}.
\]

For \(\pi\in\mathcal P_m\), \(a_m(\pi)>0\). The laws
\(\nu_{m,\pi}\) have disjoint supports. Set

\[
Z_{m,n}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_m}a_m(\pi)r_{m,n}(\pi),
\qquad a_\star=a_m(\pi_\star)>0.
\tag{7}
\]

**Lemma 2.** The following identities and bounds hold:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_p(0\leftrightarrow\partial B_n)&=b_{m,n}Z_{m,n},\\
\mu_n(\eta)&=\frac{\alpha_m(\eta)r_{m,n}(\pi_m(\eta))}{Z_{m,n}},\\
\mu_n|_{F_m}&=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_m}
  \frac{a_m(\pi)r_{m,n}(\pi)}{Z_{m,n}}\nu_{m,\pi},\\
a_\star&\le Z_{m,n}\le1,\qquad
\mu_n(\pi_m=\pi_\star)\ge a_\star.
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{8}
\]

**Proof.** The two edge sets are independent under the product measure.
By Lemma 1, fixing \(\eta\) gives

\[
\mathbb P_p(\eta,0\leftrightarrow\partial B_n)
 =\alpha_m(\eta)h_{m,n}(\pi_m(\eta)).
\]

Sum over \(\eta\) and use (5) to obtain the first two identities in (8).
Group the interior atoms by their partition to obtain the third. In
particular, conditioning on the long connection does not change the
interior law further once its boundary partition has been specified, whenever
that partition has positive conditioned probability.

All responses are between zero and one and the reference response is one,
so (7) lies between \(a_\star\) and \(\sum_\pi a_m(\pi)=1\). The mass
of the reference partition under \(\mu_n\) is \(a_\star/Z_{m,n}\), proving
the last assertion. ∎

A quantitative lower bound for the fixed-box constant is

\[
a_\star\ge p^{|B_m|-1}=p^{(2m+1)^3-1}.                \tag{9}
\]

Indeed, the finite connected graph \(B_m\) has a spanning tree with
\(|B_m|-1\) edges. Requiring those edges open, with no conditions on the
others, joins every interior vertex and hence all terminals. For example,
one constructs such a tree by starting at the root and repeatedly adding
one edge to a new vertex until all vertices have been reached. No
independence between the connectivity events of different terminals is
used. Requiring every interior edge open would give the weaker bound
\(p^{|F_m|}\), where \(|F_m|=6m(2m+1)^2\).

## 4. Ratio criterion and quantitative comparison

Use total variation in the convention
\(\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|\). For fixed \(m\) and two
horizons \(n,\ell>m\), define

\[
D_m(n,\ell)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_m}a_m(\pi)
       |r_{m,n}(\pi)-r_{m,\ell}(\pi)|,
\qquad \delta_m(n,\ell)=\|\mu_n|_{F_m}-\mu_\ell|_{F_m}\|_{\rm TV}.
\]

**Theorem.** One has

\[
\frac{D_m(n,\ell)}{2+a_\star^{-1}}
\le\delta_m(n,\ell)\le\frac{D_m(n,\ell)}{a_\star}.
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently the following are equivalent:

1. The laws \(\mu_n\) converge on all cylinder events.
2. For every fixed \(m\ge1\) and every \(\pi\in\mathcal P_m\), the ratio

   \[
   \frac{h_{m,n}(\pi)}{h_{m,n}(\pi_\star)}
   \quad\text{converges as }n\to\infty.              \tag{11}
   \]

If these ratios converge to \(r_m(\pi)\), the limiting \(F_m\) law is

\[
\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_m}
 \frac{a_m(\pi)r_m(\pi)}{\sum_\sigma a_m(\sigma)r_m(\sigma)}
 \nu_{m,\pi}.                                       \tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Write \(a_\pi=a_m(\pi)\), \(r_\pi=r_{m,n}(\pi)\),
\(s_\pi=r_{m,\ell}(\pi)\), \(Z=\sum a_\pi r_\pi\), and
\(W=\sum a_\pi s_\pi\). Because the mixture components in (8) have
disjoint supports, summing absolute atom differences within each support
gives exactly

\[
2\delta_m(n,\ell)=\sum_\pi
 \left|\frac{a_\pi r_\pi}{Z}-\frac{a_\pi s_\pi}{W}\right|.
\tag{13}
\]

Also \(|Z-W|\le D_m(n,\ell)\). Add and subtract
\(a_\pi s_\pi/Z\) in (13). The triangle inequality gives

\[
2\delta_m(n,\ell)
 \le\frac{D_m(n,\ell)+|Z-W|}{Z}
 \le\frac{2D_m(n,\ell)}{a_\star}.
\]

For the converse let \(\lambda_\pi=a_\pi r_\pi/Z\) and
\(\gamma_\pi=a_\pi s_\pi/W\). These are the two conditioned partition
probability vectors. At the reference coordinate,

\[
\lambda_\star=a_\star/Z\ge a_\star,
\qquad\gamma_\star=a_\star/W\ge a_\star.
\]

Since a single event's probability difference is at most total variation,

\[
|Z-W|=\frac{a_\star|\lambda_\star-\gamma_\star|}
                   {\lambda_\star\gamma_\star}
\le a_\star^{-1}\delta_m(n,\ell).
\]

Now \(a_\pi r_\pi=Z\lambda_\pi\) and
\(a_\pi s_\pi=W\gamma_\pi\). Thus, using \(Z\le1\),

\[
D_m(n,\ell)
 \le Z\sum_\pi|\lambda_\pi-\gamma_\pi|+|Z-W|\sum_\pi\gamma_\pi
 \le(2+a_\star^{-1})\delta_m(n,\ell).
\]

This proves (10). For fixed \(m\), there are finitely many partitions,
each with positive weight \(a_\pi\). Hence being Cauchy in \(D_m\) is
equivalent to coordinatewise convergence of all responses. Equation (10)
makes this equivalent to convergence of the finite interior law.

There is also a direct inverse formula, valid for each realizable partition:

\[
r_{m,n}(\pi)
 =\frac{a_\star}{a_m(\pi)}
    \frac{\mu_n(\pi_m=\pi)}{\mu_n(\pi_m=\pi_\star)}.
\tag{14}
\]

It follows from (8), and its denominator is bounded below by \(a_\star\).
Thus convergence of cylinder probabilities implies (11) without any
subsequence extraction.

Conversely, if (11) holds for every fixed box, its reference coordinate is
one and its limiting normalizer is at least \(a_\star\). Taking limits
in (8) proves (12). Every finite edge set lies inside some \(F_m\), so
every cylinder probability converges. The resulting finite laws are
consistent: restriction commutes with taking a limit of finite sums.
The extension theorem on a countable product of two-point spaces therefore
gives a probability law with these cylinder probabilities. ∎

The same argument applies along any sequence of horizons tending to
infinity. At each fixed box, the map from response vectors with reference
coordinate one to the conditioned interior laws is injective, with inverse
(14). Equation (10) quantifies this correspondence.

As a sufficient, stronger condition, convergence of \(\rho_{m,n}\) for
every fixed \(m\) implies the limit in T160 by (6). More quantitatively,
because \(K_m(\pi,\cdot)\) is an indicator,

\[
D_m(n,\ell)\le\|\rho_{m,n}-\rho_{m,\ell}\|_{\rm TV},
\qquad
\delta_m(n,\ell)\le a_\star^{-1}
       \|\rho_{m,n}-\rho_{m,\ell}\|_{\rm TV}.          \tag{15}
\]

No converse for the full exterior partition laws is required or claimed.
All constants here depend on the fixed box; (9) gives no control uniform
in \(m\).

## 5. The root-connected boundary set does not suffice

Here is a finite witness in the cubic lattice. Let \(m=1,n=3\), and write

\[
o=(0,0,0),\quad a=(1,0,0),\quad
b=(1,1,0),\quad c=(0,1,0).
\]

Let \(\eta_0\) open only \(o-a\) among all edges in \(F_1\). Let
\(\eta_1\) also open \(b-c\), keeping every other interior edge closed.
Both root clusters have boundary set \(\{a\}\). Their other interior
components differ.

Among the exterior edges \(G_{1,3}\), open precisely the five edges of

\[
a-(2,0,0)-(2,1,0)-b,
\qquad c-(0,2,0)-(0,3,0),                            \tag{16}
\]

and close all others. Each edge displayed lies in \(G_{1,3}\). The first
path joins \(a\) to \(b\), and the second joins \(c\) to \(\partial B_3\).
With \(\eta_1\), their concatenation with \(o-a\) and \(b-c\) connects
the root to the target. With \(\eta_0\), the root component is confined to
\(o,a,(2,0,0),(2,1,0),b\), so it misses the target.

This exact exterior configuration has probability
\(p^5(1-p)^{|G_{1,3}|-5}>0\). Moreover \(\eta_1\) is obtained from
\(\eta_0\) by opening an edge, so root-to-target connectivity for
\(\eta_0\) implies it for \(\eta_1\) on every exterior configuration.
It follows that

\[
h_{1,3}(\pi_1(\eta_1))>
h_{1,3}(\pi_1(\eta_0)).                              \tag{17}
\]

In fact, \(|E(B_1)|=54\) and \(|E(B_3)|=882\), by the edge-count formula
following (9). Hence \(|G_{1,3}|=828\), and the same argument gives the
explicit lower bound

\[
h_{1,3}(\pi_1(\eta_1))-h_{1,3}(\pi_1(\eta_0))
\ge p^5(1-p)^{823}>0.
\tag{18}
\]

Thus no exact response formula depending only on the root-connected
boundary set can hold, even for a fixed pair of cubic boxes. The join in
(1) retains the component \(b-c\) and gives the correct answer.

## 6. What remains for T160

At \(p=p_c(\mathbb Z^3)\), the missing statement is the convergence of
(11) for every fixed box and every realizable boundary partition. The
identities above do not prove it. They use neither critical extinction nor
quasi-multiplicativity and supply no estimate preventing oscillation of
the normalized responses. The earlier alternating-branch tree demonstrates
why compactness and existence of subsequential limits alone cannot replace
such an estimate. No claim of literature novelty is made for this finite
conditioning argument.
