# Geometry for the conditional contour replacement

Let \(d\ge2\). On \(V=\mathbb Z^d\), join distinct vertices whose Chebyshev distance is one. All components, neighborhoods and boundaries in this note refer to this graph \(G\). Write \(N(S)=\{x:\operatorname{dist}_\infty(x,S)\le1\}\). For nonempty finite connected \(S\), let \(E(S)\) be the infinite component of \(V\setminus S\), and set

\[
F(S)=V\setminus E(S).
\]

The infinite component is unique: outside a box containing \(S\), the nearest-neighbor lattice is connected for \(d\ge2\), and every infinite component meets that exterior. The other components are finite. Thus \(F(S)\) is the support together with its finite holes. The notation \(N(S)\) and \(F(S)\) must be kept distinct.

The construction audited here assigns macro labels \(\theta_x\in\{0,r,*\}\), and calls \(x\) correct of phase \(c\in\{0,r\}\) when \(\theta_y=c\) for every \(y\in N(\{x\})\). A support is a finite connected component of the incorrect sites. Its code records all phase-cell labels in its macrocells, hence determines \(\theta\) on the support. Compatibility means that supports are disjoint and nonadjacent. These are the definitions in Section 5 of [*Density coexistence for hard spheres with a long-range attractive pair interaction*, Hexagon 2610.00734v1](https://hexagonmath.org/api/v1/works/2610.00734v1/source?path=liquid-solid.tex), credited as generated by GPT-6 Astra and prompted by Qidong He; that manuscript is unverified.

**Geometric theorem.** For this construction:

1. For each component \(D\) of \(V\setminus S\), the set \(D\cap N(S)\) is connected, correct, and has a common phase \(c_D\). All support cells adjacent to \(D\) have macro label \(c_D\). The code determines this phase.
2. Every bounded component \(D\) has connected complement. If \(A\) is finite with connected complement and \(S\subset A\), then \(F(S)\subset A\).
3. Filled supports of compatible contours are either disjoint or strictly nested. In the nested case the inner filled support and its support neighborhood belong to one bounded component of the outer support. In the disjoint case the filled supports are nonadjacent.
4. A finite compatible family splits uniquely into its maximal filled supports and independent compatible families in their bounded components. With the stated phase collars, the physical decomposition and reconstruction preserve the set of contour components.

If the physical interaction range, including the hard core, is less than the macrocell side \(L\), different complementary components cannot interact directly, and neither can distinct maximal filled supports. An activity depending on exterior particles within interaction range of its support is independent of the microscopic variables in every other maximal filled support.

The theorem supplies the geometric assumptions in the round 3 conditional replacement lemma on connected-complement recursion domains. Positivity and finiteness of its continuum partition functions remain separate analytic requirements.

## 1. Boundary connectivity

We give the graph argument explicitly. It is the specialization of the method in Ádám Timár, [*Boundary-connectivity via graph theory*, arXiv:0711.1713v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.1713v2), Lemma 2, to \(G=G^+\) equal to the Chebyshev graph. No new boundary-connectivity theorem is asserted.

First the finite cycle space of \(G\), over \(\mathbb F_2\), is generated by cycles all of whose vertices lie in one unit cube. For an edge \(xy\) changing several coordinates, choose a nearest-neighbor path from \(x\) to \(y\) that changes each differing coordinate once. This path and the original edge form a cycle within their unit cube. Adding these cycles replaces every diagonal edge in a finite cycle by nearest-neighbor edges. The resulting finite even edge set is a sum of nearest-neighbor closed walks.

Such a walk is a word in the steps \(\pm e_i\), with total displacement zero. Commute steps in different coordinate directions until steps in each direction occur together. Each interchange changes the edge set modulo two by the boundary of a unit square. Within each coordinate group, cancel consecutive inverse steps; since the net displacement in that coordinate is zero, all steps cancel. Backtracks contribute zero modulo two. Thus nearest-neighbor cycles are generated by unit squares. A square, and each diagonal replacement cycle above, has all vertices in a unit cube. Every two distinct vertices in such a cube are adjacent in \(G\).

Fix a component \(D\) of \(V\setminus S\), and let \(B=D\cap N(S)\). Every component of \(V\setminus S\) attaches to \(S\): follow a finite path from a vertex in that component to a vertex of \(S\), and use its first entrance into \(S\). In particular \(B\) is nonempty. All edges leaving \(D\) go to \(S\), because different components of \(V\setminus S\) have no edge between them.

Suppose \(B\) is disconnected, and partition it into nonempty sets \(B_1,B_2\) with no edge between them. Partition the edge cut \(\delta D\) into \(K_1,K_2\) according to which \(B_i\) contains its endpoint in \(D\). A unit-cube cycle can meet only one of \(K_1,K_2\): otherwise two of its vertices would belong to different \(B_i\), although they are adjacent. Every cycle crosses \(\delta D\) an even number of times. Hence every unit-cube generator meets \(K_1\) evenly, and so does every finite cycle of \(G\).

Choose edges \(s_1b_1\in K_1\), \(s_2b_2\in K_2\), with \(s_i\in S\), \(b_i\in D\). Join \(b_1\) to \(b_2\) by a simple path in \(D\), and \(s_1\) to \(s_2\) by a simple path in \(S\). If \(s_1=s_2\), use the length-zero path there. These two paths and the two chosen edges form a finite cycle with exactly one edge in \(K_1\). This contradicts the preceding parity conclusion. Therefore \(B\) is connected.

## 2. Constant phase collars

If \(S\) is a full component of the incorrect sites, every site in \(B=D\cap N(S)\) is correct: an incorrect neighbor of \(S\) would belong to \(S\). Two adjacent correct sites have the same phase. Indeed each lies in the other's correctness neighborhood, so both macro labels must equal both correctness phases. Connectivity of \(B\) gives its common phase \(c_D\).

For \(s\in S\) adjacent to \(b\in B\), correctness at \(b\) implies \(\theta_s=c_D\). Every boundary component has such a pair. Since the code records \(\theta_s\), it determines \(c_D\), independently of the chosen realization. If one support cell touches two complementary components, their collar phases necessarily agree; this does not invalidate the argument.

The statement is local to the boundary. A bounded component can contain other contour supports and other phases in its interior; it is not asserted to be pure throughout.

## 3. Containment in recursion domains

For any bounded component \(D\) of \(V\setminus S\), its complement is the union of \(S\) and all other complementary components. Each of those components attaches to the connected set \(S\), so this union is connected and unbounded. The same argument shows that \(F(S)\) is connected, and its complement \(E(S)\) is connected by definition.

If \(A^c\) is connected, unbounded and disjoint from \(S\), it lies entirely in one component of \(V\setminus S\). That component is \(E(S)\). Thus \(A^c\subset E(S)\), which is exactly \(F(S)\subset A\). For a finite domain,

\[
N(S)\subset A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S\subset A\text{ and }S\cap\partial A=\varnothing,
\]

where \(\partial A=\{x\in A:x\text{ has a neighbor outside }A\}\).

The connected-complement condition cannot be dropped. Set \(B_k=\{x:\|x\|_\infty\le k\}\). Label \(B_2\) by \(r\), and its exterior by \(0\). Its incorrect support is

\[
S=B_3\setminus B_1,
\qquad N(S)=B_4\setminus B_0,
\qquad F(S)=B_3.
\]

The formulas follow by inspecting the radius-one neighborhoods: only the eroded dense block \(B_1\) and the exterior of \(B_3\) are correct. Take \(A=N(S)\). Then \(N(S)\subset A\), but \(0\in F(S)\setminus A\). Here \(A^c\) has the isolated origin and the infinite exterior of \(B_4\) as different components. For \(d=2\),

\[
|S|=7^2-3^2=40,\quad |N(S)|=9^2-1=80,\quad |F(S)|=7^2=49.
\]

For \(d=3\) these numbers are \(316,728,343\). In both dimensions \(F(S)\setminus A=\{0\}\). These are exact counterexamples to an unrestricted domain hypothesis, not to the source, which explicitly excludes such domains.

## 4. Laminar filled supports

Let \(S,T\) be nonempty finite connected supports with no intersection or adjacency. The connected set \(T\) lies in one component of \(V\setminus S\).

If \(T\subset D\) for a bounded component \(D\), then \(D^c\) is connected, unbounded and disjoint from \(T\). Therefore \(D^c\subset E(T)\), giving

\[
F(T)\subset D\subset F(S).
\]

The containment is strict because \(S\subset F(S)\setminus D\). Also \(N(T)\subset D\): a neighbor of \(T\) cannot belong to \(S\), by nonadjacency, and is connected to \(T\) in \(V\setminus S\). This proves the neighborhood containment needed for the recursive catalog.

It remains to consider \(T\subset E(S)\). If \(S\) lies in a bounded component of \(V\setminus T\), the preceding argument with \(S,T\) reversed gives strict reverse nesting. Otherwise \(S\subset E(T)\). Now \(F(S)\) is connected and disjoint from \(T\), because \(T\subset E(S)\). Since it contains \(S\subset E(T)\), it lies entirely in \(E(T)\). Thus \(F(S)\cap F(T)=\varnothing\).

In this last case the filled supports cannot be adjacent. A site of \(F(S)\setminus S\) cannot have a neighbor in \(E(S)\); otherwise its bounded component would be connected to \(E(S)\). Hence every inner boundary site of \(F(S)\) belongs to \(S\). If an edge joined \(F(S)\) to \(F(T)\), its endpoints would accordingly lie in \(S\) and \(T\), contradicting compatibility. Equal filled supports are excluded by the same trichotomy.

## 5. Maximal families and reconstruction

The filled supports in a finite compatible family form a strict inclusion forest. Its maximal members are disjoint and nonadjacent. Every nonmaximal support lies in a unique bounded component of a maximal member, by Section 4; its neighborhood lies there as well. Recursing gives a unique family in each such component. Conversely a contour with \(N(T)\subset D\) is nonadjacent to the outer support. Contours in different components of the outer complement are mutually nonadjacent, since the components have no connecting edge. Families in separate maximal filled supports are also mutually compatible. These observations give both directions of the family decomposition, with no multiplicities.

For completeness, the complement of finitely many disjoint, nonadjacent filled supports \(F_1,\ldots,F_k\) is connected. For each \(F_i=F(S_i)\), its exterior vertex boundary equals \(E(S_i)\cap N(S_i)\), and is connected by Section 1. It is disjoint from every other \(F_j\) by nonadjacency. Start with any finite path between two vertices outside all the \(F_i\). For every excursion through \(F_i\), replace its segment between entrance and exit neighbors by a path on that exterior boundary. Such replacements introduce no new visit to any \(F_j\). There are finitely many original excursions, so the resulting path avoids every \(F_i\).

Consider now a finite domain \(A\) with connected complement, exterior macro labels \(c\) on \(N(A)\setminus A\), and the diluted constraint that all sites of \(\partial A\) are correct of phase \(c\). For deciding incorrect sites in \(A\), extend the macro labels to be \(c\) everywhere outside \(A\). This does not change correctness anywhere in \(A\). Each outside site adjacent to \(A\) sees only boundary sites of \(A\), whose macro labels are \(c\), so every outside site is correct. All incorrect components are therefore finite and contained away from \(\partial A\).

There is a unique set of maximal physical contour filled supports. Outside their union, all sites are correct. This complement is connected, contains the constant exterior, and consequently has phase \(c\). Within each bounded component \(D\), the boundary is correct of its collar phase \(c_D\), so the restriction is the stated diluted ensemble in \(D\). This establishes the physical decomposition.

Conversely fix the external codes, a pure phase-\(c\) background outside their filled supports, and diluted configurations of phase \(c_D\) in their components. Impose the physical hard-core constraints on the assembled particles. At each complementary boundary, the adjacent support macro labels are exactly \(c_D\). The diluted boundary constraint makes all adjacent component sites correct, with macro label \(c_D\). Thus every site immediately outside a support has the same label as in a realization of that code. Correctness depends only on immediate neighbors. The sites of the prescribed support remain incorrect, and its neighboring sites remain correct. Its connected component is therefore exactly the prescribed support; no contour is lost, added across the seam, or merged. The internal diluted configurations carry their own unique contour decompositions. This proves reconstruction and uniqueness at the level needed for the conditional integrals.

This reconstruction does not require independently chosen macrostates to satisfy crossing hard cores automatically. The physical hard-core indicator must be retained in the integrals, as in round 3.

## 6. Physical separation and the algebraic consequence

Distinct complementary components have no adjacent indices. The same is true of disjoint maximal filled supports by Section 4. For two such indices \(i,j\), some coordinate differs by at least two. The corresponding half-open cubes \(L(i+[0,1)^d)\) and \(L(j+[0,1)^d)\) have Euclidean separation at least \(L\). Hence an interaction of range less than \(L\) cannot join them. In particular no bounded component interacts directly with the unbounded component across its support. Shared exterior neighbors of different maximal supports are allowed; conditioning on their particles retains those correlations.

A contour activity integrates its support and bounded components, retaining only the unbounded exterior. Its remaining microscopic dependence is confined to exterior cubes in \(N(S)\). No other maximal filled support intersects this dependence set, since the filled supports are nonadjacent. This is precisely the independence from other filled supports used when canceling conditional ratios.

Consequently the geometry and catalog regrouping in the round 3 induction apply to these connected-complement recursion domains. Every recursive domain is smaller, and its complement is connected by Section 3. Provided the partition functions are finite and the denominators positive, summing the inner auxiliary families reconstructs each physical same-phase interior partition function before the maximal factor \(W_\Gamma D_\Gamma=N_\Gamma\) cancels. This argument retains all microscopic exterior variables until their joint integration.

No surface-pressure estimate, uniform continuum contour bound, removal of truncation, or coexistence of Gibbs measures follows from this geometric result alone.
