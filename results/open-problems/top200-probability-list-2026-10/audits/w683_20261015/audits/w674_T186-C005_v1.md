## Deterministic proof

The ordered messages preserve a<=b<=h, and each output is increasing in the total input order 000<001<011<111: A and B can only increase, and Z can only decrease. Top iteration therefore decreases; on a locally finite graph each coordinate stabilizes, and each finite input neighbourhood stabilizes, so the limit is a fixed point. Any fixed point is dominated by every iterate and hence by the limit.

For v in S(m), deleting one neighbour leaves at least k-1 incoming b messages, so b(v->u)=1 for every neighbour u. If b(u->v)=1, its other inputs plus v->u put u in S(m), supplying at least k S-neighbours to v. In the remaining case b(u->v)=0, h(u->v)=1 implies at least k-1 other incoming a messages at u; the unchanged k b messages at v give a(v->u)=1. Thus u has at least k incoming a messages. For each such w, the other k-1 a messages force h(u->w)=1, while a(w->u)=1 supplies the other h conditions and k b inputs required to put w in S(m). Every neighbour u of S(m) consequently has at least k neighbours in S(m). This proves strongness, including k=2.

For an arbitrary strong S, the proposed lower message lies in the four-state order. A vertex v in S has all other neighbours in S union N(S) and at least k-1 remaining S-neighbours. If v is outside S but adjacent to it, its at least k S-neighbours send a=1 towards v, so at least k-1 remain when one edge is omitted. These statements prove the lower message is a subsolution. Monotonicity puts it below the greatest fixed point; every v in S then satisfies the root membership test. Arbitrary unions of strong sets remain strong because a touched vertex is touched by some member of the union. This proves S(m*) equals the greatest strong set, also on infinite locally finite graphs.

## Distributional and finite-graph statements

On the Poisson tree, thinning into disjoint message types makes Z independent of B and also independent of A. A and B are nested and are not assumed independent. Accordingly p'=exp(-c(1-s))Q_k(cq), q'=exp(-c(1-s))Q_(k-1)(cq). Since A>=k-1 implies B>=k-1, the portion of h=1 outside b=1 is precisely {A>=k-1,Z>=1}; this gives the printed s equation. The nested domain is invariant, top iteration gives its largest fixed point, and bounded monotone convergence gives the limiting probabilities. The unconditioned Poisson root has the same offspring law and the a membership event, giving probability p.

At each fixed round the graph membership event uses a finite-radius neighbourhood. Local weak convergence yields the limiting expected fraction of this event; the actual finite strong core is contained in it by deterministic top domination. Taking first n to infinity and then the round count to infinity gives limsup E|K|/n<=p. If p=0, Markov's inequality excludes a linear-sized core. No exchange of these limits, finite-graph positive-core theorem, or finite-graph emptiness theorem is used here.
