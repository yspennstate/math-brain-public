## Deterministic peeling and witness

At selection, a black/blue vertex has at most k-1 black neighbours. Selecting it red and its black neighbours blue preserves every strong subset of the current black set: if the selected vertex touched such a subset, it would already have k black neighbours. Thus terminal black vertices are exactly the maximal strong core. A red vertex can never later acquire a black neighbour because colours never return to black. Every newly nonblack black-selected vertex is a red root; each newly blue vertex is assigned its selecting red parent. Parent edges cannot form a cycle because children were previously absent. Blue vertices stay leaves until selected red, and every red vertex has at most k-1 children.

If the terminal nonblack set has size at least ceil(theta*n), the first crossing has size i in ceil(theta*n),...,ceil(theta*n)+k-1. The exact n condition ensures theta<=i/n<=theta_plus. All red-to-outside edges are absent at that stopping time. A fixed forest witness therefore requires e distinct internal edges and absence of r(n-i) distinct external edges. These two sets are disjoint, so its probability is p^e(1-p)^[r(n-i)]<=p^e exp[-c(1-theta_plus)r]. This is an unconditional static edge event, not a product of dependent red-vertex probabilities. Extra internal edges and unrealizable histories only increase the counted class, which is valid for this upper bound.
