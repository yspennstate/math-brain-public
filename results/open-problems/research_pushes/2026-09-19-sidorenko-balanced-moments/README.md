19 September 2026. Owner-requested continuation by the OpenAI ChatGPT assistant.

## Mathematical status

The new result is a quantitative inequality for every finite symmetric nonnegative 1-regular kernel. A positive diagonal balancing gives a certified lower spectral edge; a nonnegative fifth-degree polynomial then bounds t_H from transfer moments of orders two through four and a certified Perron lower bound. There is also a bounded-graphon version with an unbalanced spectral bound. This is NOT a complete proof of the unrestricted Sidorenko inequality. No independent audit or first-priority certificate is claimed.

Read BALANCED_MOMENTS.md for the complete proof, scope, exact examples and limitations. Run:

    python check_balanced_moments.py

The program uses Python integers and Fraction only and regenerates balanced_moments_checks.json. It needs no external packages. No eigenvalue computed in floating point is used in an accepted comparison. The fifth trace is evaluated separately to test the examples, NOT supplied to the lower bound.

## Exact outcomes

- The constant kernel: lower bound 1, including the degenerate quadratic-optimization case.
- The normalized complete-triangle host: lower bound at least 2; zero kernel entries allowed.
- The existing four-block counterexample to an off-diagonal Gram-sign shortcut: lower bound greater than 33/25, using the displayed positive rational balancing vector. Its sup-norm deviation exceeds 5400, so it is outside the earlier half-radius local theorem.
- A generic rational five-block example: lower bound greater than 39, without balancing.
- The complete cyclic Gram matrix identity and transfer similarity are checked coefficientwise in exact arithmetic for each example, along with the independent direct density/trace identity.
- A block-diagonal abstract matrix with real spectrum, Perron value one, lower edge minus one half, and third/fourth traces greater than one nevertheless has fifth trace 4809/5000. This is not a kernel-derived transfer matrix and not a Sidorenko counterexample.

The current choice beta=t_C4 need not make the certificate positive for every W. The note gives a two-block direct-sum example. A separate exact calculation in this continuation verifies that its optimized certificate with beta=2 and a=50 is negative although its fifth trace exceeds one. Thus a universal successful parameter-selection theorem remains missing.
