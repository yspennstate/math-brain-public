## 1. Scope: does the answer answer the question as posed?

Source text (arXiv:0902.4207v2, 12 Mar 2009, authors R. N. Ganikhodzhaev and U. A. Rozikov; the 2011 IDAQP journal version adds F. Mukhamedov), extracted from the PDF, Section 8, pages 6-7:

> **8 Strictly non-Volterra QSO.** Recently in [40] a new class of non-Volterra QSOs have been introduced. These QSOs satisfy
> P_{ij,k} = 0 if k ∈ {i, j}, ∀ i, j, k ∈ E. (13)
> Such an operator is called strictly non-Volterra QSO. One can easily check that the strictly non-Volterra operators exist only for m ≥ 3.
> An arbitrary strictly non-Volterra QSO defined on S^2 (i.e., m = 3) has the form:
> x' = αy^2 + cz^2 + 2yz, y' = ax^2 + dz^2 + 2xz, z' = bx^2 + βy^2 + 2xy, (14)
> where a, b, c, d, α, β ≥ 0, a + b = c + d = α + β = 1. (15)
> **Theorem 6.** [40] 1) For any values of parameters a, b, c, d, α, β with (15) the operator (14) has a unique fixed point. Moreover the fixed point is not attractive.
> 2) The QSO (14) has 2-cycles and 3-cycles depending on the parameters (15).
> **Problem 11.** Is Theorem 6 true for m ≥ 4?

Reference [40] is "Rozikov U.A., Zhamilov U.U. On dynamics of strictly non-Volterra quadratic operators defined on the two dimensional simplex. To appear in Sbornik: Math." (= Sb. Math. 200(9), 2009).

Findings on scope:
- The definition of strictly non-Volterra used by the paper (P_{ij,k} = 0 whenever k ∈ {i,j}, with the standard QSO normalisation) is exactly (13). Match.
- Theorem 6 is stated for the m = 3 normal form (14)-(15), which is the general strictly non-Volterra operator on S^2. "Is Theorem 6 true for m ≥ 4?" can only mean: for strictly non-Volterra QSOs on S^{m-1}, m ≥ 4, do (1) uniqueness, (1') non-attractiveness of the fixed point, and (2) existence of 2- and 3-cycles depending on parameters hold? The paper reads the problem this way. Correct.
- Verdict: a referee WOULD accept the package "Theorem 6 does not extend to any m ≥ 4" as a negative answer to Problem 11 as posed, because clause (1') already fails at m = 4 (equiprobable operator, Jamilov-Ladra-Mukhitdinov 2017, literature) and clause (1) fails for every m ≥ 5 (this paper, new, verified). The residual items - uniqueness at m = 4 and the cycle clause (2) - are refinements; they are not required to answer a yes/no question whose answer is no. The paper should say this in one sentence rather than leave the reader to infer it, and it must not be read as claiming uniqueness fails at m = 4.
- One attribution error: the memo (line 3) credits Theorem 6 to "Ganikhodzhaev, Mukhamedov and Rozikov's [40]". [40] is Rozikov-Zhamilov (Jamilov) 2009, Sb. Math.; the survey's own authors are Ganikhodzhaev and Rozikov (arXiv) / with Mukhamedov (journal). Fix the citation.

## 2. Verification (exact arithmetic, my own code)

**m = 5 example.** `verify_exact.py` (sympy, rationals): the 20-coefficient operator is symmetric, nonnegative, every row sums to 1, P_{ij,k} = 0 whenever k ∈ {i,j}. V(p) = p and V(q) = q exactly for p = (1/3,1/3,1/3,0,0), q = (1/9,1/9,1/9,1/3,1/3). Full Jacobian spectrum at p: {2 (x2), -1 (x2), -2}; tangential (sum-zero subspace): {2, -1, -1, -2}, sign det(I - A) = -1. At q: full {2, -4/3, -1/3 (x2), 0}; tangential {-4/3, -1/3, -1/3, 0}, sign det(I - A) = +1. Trace DV = 0 at both (strict non-Volterra). The polynomial system V(x) = x, Σx = 1 has exactly 16 complex solutions and exactly TWO in the simplex - so "two fixed points" is exact, not just "at least two". Agrees with the memo's numbers.

Exposition error found: the memo says "the eigenvalue 2 in the radial direction is Euler's identity ... and plays no role on the simplex" right after listing the TANGENTIAL tuple (-2,-1,-1,2). The 2 in that tuple is not the radial eigenvalue (that is a fifth eigenvalue 2, present in the full spectrum {2,2,-1,-1,-2}); it is the inward transverse eigenvalue in the direction x_4 + x_5 (dV_4/dx_5 = dV_5/dx_4 = 2 at p). It plays the decisive role: it is why p is not attractive and why p has fixed-point index 0 on the simplex (next paragraph). The audit passage got the count right ("(-2,-1,-1,2) plus the radial 2"); the memo prose should be corrected to match its own artefact e0064_m5_exact.out, which prints both spectra.

Index remark needs repair. The memo/row say the m = 4 index route "would have to use the dimension" because "the five-type example shows the count can involve a saddle of index -1". The -1 is the index of p as a fixed point of the polynomial extension on the affine hull. For the Lefschetz count on the simplex Δ (Σ ind = L(V) = 1) the relevant index of an inward-REPELLING boundary fixed point is 0, and 0 + 1 = 1 is consistent; there is no index -1 in the simplex count. Confirmed two ways: (i) q alone carries index +1 and the count must be 1; (ii) in the one-parameter family below, for t < 1/2 p is the only fixed point (index 1, inward eigenvalue 2t < 1), at t = 1/2 the interior point passes through p, and for t > 1/2 p's simplex index is 0. The correct structural statement for m = 4 is in §4.

**m ≥ 6 construction.** The memo's argument (two disjoint invariant triples each carry the unique fixed point of their three-type restriction by Theorem 6) is correct as an existence proof of ≥ 2 fixed points. I built an explicit six-type operator (`family_and_m6.py`): symmetric face operator on F = {1,2,3} and on G = {4,5,6}, and every cross pair (i ∈ F, j ∈ G) lands 1/2-1/2 on the two F-types other than i. Exact solve: 32 complex solutions, exactly two in the simplex, p_F = (1/3,1/3,1/3,0,0,0) (tangential eigenvalues -1,-1,0,0,0, index +1) and p_G = (0,0,0,1/3,1/3,1/3) (tangential 2,-1,-1,-1,-1, affine index -1, simplex index 0). The F-mass map is s ↦ s(2 - s), so no interior fixed point exists. Caution for the paper: the "obvious" symmetric choice (cross pairs uniform over all four allowed types) gives a whole SEGMENT of fixed points {(1/3-t, 1/3-t, 1/3-t, t, t, t): 0 ≤ t ≤ 1/3} (sympy returned a free parameter; Newton found ~3000 distinct points). It still refutes uniqueness but the memo's "two in 27 cases, three in 3" statistic hides that degenerate members exist; the paper should give the explicit operator above rather than "choose any".

**Equiprobable identity** (row, lines 5514-5518): V_k - V_l = (x_l - x_k)[2 - m(x_k+x_l)/(m-1)]/(m-2) verified symbolically on the simplex for m = 3,4,5,6 (`equiprob.py`); tangential eigenvalues at the barycentre are -2/(m-1) (so -1 at m = 3, -2/3 at m = 4). Consistent with Theorem 6 at m = 3 and with global attraction for m ≥ 4.

## 3. Smaller and more natural examples at m = 5

**(a) A theorem instead of an example (recommend adopting).** Call the "swap extension" of a three-type strictly non-Volterra operator W (on F = {1,2,3}) by a probability vector ρ on F the five-type operator with P_{i4,5} = P_{i5,4} = 1 (i ∈ F), P_{44,5} = P_{55,4} = 1, P_{45,k} = ρ_k. Then V_4 = 2 s x_5 + x_5^2, V_5 = 2 s x_4 + x_4^2 (s = x_1+x_2+x_3), V_k = W_k(x_F) + 2ρ_k x_4 x_5. At any fixed point, V_4 - V_5 = (x_5 - x_4)(2s + x_4 + x_5) forces (x_5 - x_4)(2s + x_4 + x_5 + 1) = 0, so x_4 = x_5 = b, and b = 2(1-2b)b + b^2 gives b ∈ {0, 1/3}. b = 0: the unique face fixed point of W (Theorem 6). b = 1/3: x_F = u/3 with u = W(u)/3 + 2ρ/3 =: G(u). G maps the 2-simplex to itself and, since a QSO is 2-Lipschitz in ℓ^1 on the simplex (|V(x)-V(y)|_1 ≤ Σ P_{ij,k}(|x_i-y_i| x_j + y_i |x_j-y_j|) = 2|x-y|_1), G is a 2/3-contraction with exactly one fixed point. Hence EVERY swap extension of EVERY three-type strictly non-Volterra W by EVERY ρ has EXACTLY two fixed points. The paper's example is the case W symmetric, ρ uniform, u* = (1/3,1/3,1/3). Numerically confirmed on 300 random (W, ρ) including vertex ρ: 300/300 have exactly two fixed points, all of the predicted form, sampled ℓ^1-Lipschitz constant of W ≤ 1.95 (`swap_theorem.py`). This turns a single verified example into a two-line theorem with a Banach-fixed-point proof, and it explains the 1/3 in P_{45,k}: it is what makes u* rational.

**(b) Fewer coefficients.** 0/1 examples with 15 nonzero coefficients (the minimum possible: one target per unordered pair) exist in abundance: in a random sample of 20,000 0/1 face-invariant five-type operators, 2,600 (13%) have two fixed points; among the first 20,000 in lexicographic order, 554. Explicit one (`zero_one_numeric.py`, `swap_theorem.py`): face W with P_{11,2} = P_{22,3} = P_{33,1} = 1, P_{12,3} = P_{13,2} = P_{23,1} = 1, cross as in (a) with ρ = e_1. Fixed points: (1/3,1/3,1/3,0,0) and (u*/3, 1/3, 1/3) where u*_1 is the root ≈ 0.68548 of 3t^3 - 7t^2 - 36t + 27 (u* ≈ (0.68548, 0.20615, 0.10837)); the interior point is ≈ (0.228492, 0.068717, 0.036125, 1/3, 1/3), tangential eigenvalues (-4/3, -0.407, -0.260, 0). So "smaller" is available but costs rational coordinates; the paper's 20-coefficient operator with rational fixed points is the better display example, and (a) is the right statement.

**(c) Bifurcation family.** With P_{i4,5} = P_{i5,4} = t and the remaining cross mass (1-t)/2 on each of the two other face types (rest as in the paper), exact computation (`family_and_m6.py`) gives: the tangential spectrum at p is {2t, -1, -1, -2t}; for t ∈ (1/2, 1] exactly two fixed points, the interior one q_t = ((1-2b)/3, ·, ·, b, b) with b = (2t-1)/(4t-1) (t = 3/5: b = 1/7; t = 3/4: b = 1/4; t = 1: b = 1/3); at t = 1/2 exactly one fixed point, p, degenerate (eigenvalue 1, det = 0). So the second fixed point enters the simplex through p by a transcritical bifurcation at the boundary exactly when the inward transverse eigenvalue 2t crosses 1. This is the mechanism and would make a good figure/one paragraph.

## 4. The m = 4 case: what I tried

**Structural reduction (new, should be stated in the paper).** For m = 4: (i) no vertex is fixed (V_i(e_i) has i-th coordinate 0); (ii) no edge point (a,b,0,0), a,b > 0, is fixed, because P_{12,3} + P_{12,4} = 1 makes V_3 or V_4 positive; (iii) a point in the relative interior of a 2-face {1,2,3} is fixed only if P_{ij,4} = 0 for all i,j ≤ 3, i.e. the face is invariant, and then V_4 ≡ 0 identically, all fixed points lie in that face and are unique by Theorem 6 (two invariant faces cannot coexist: a shared pair would need two targets). Hence either the operator has a dead type and a unique fixed point, or ALL its fixed points are interior, where the Lefschetz count Σ ind = 1 applies and ind = sign det(I - DV|_T). Therefore: uniqueness at m = 4 holds if every interior fixed point has det(I - DV|_T) > 0, and fails if some operator has an interior fixed point with det < 0. Also DV(x) = 2M(x) with M(x)_{kj} = Σ_i x_i P_{ij,k} column-stochastic, zero-diagonal, with x as Perron vector (V(x) = M(x)x), and det(I - DV|_T) = 8 Π(1/2 - λ_i) over the non-Perron eigenvalues of M. At m = 3 this gives Theorem 6's uniqueness in two lines (λ_2 + λ_3 = -1 forces the product positive); at m = 4 trace alone does not suffice (e.g. (0.6,-0.8,-0.8)), so a proof must use the structure of M.

**Searches for an index ≤ 0 interior fixed point** (`m4_enum.py`, `m4_det_min.py`, `m4_det_scan.py`):
- All 5,184 vertex (0/1) four-type operators, exhaustively: each has exactly one fixed point; det(I - DV|_T) ranges over [3.158, 6.761].
- 20,000 random operators (Dirichlet on each admissible set, concentrations 0.1-3): all unique; det quantiles 1%/50%/99% = 3.95/4.65/5.75, min 3.265, none ≤ 0.
- 60 Nelder-Mead descents minimising det at the Newton fixed point over the 14-parameter polytope: min 3.1446 at a fixed point of the form (a,a,b,a), tangential eigenvalues (-1.211, -1.117, 0.328); the same value reached from many starts. (A differential-evolution run did not finish within the time budget.)
- Relaxed problem over (x, M) alone - M zero-diagonal column-stochastic, Mx = x, M_{kj} ≤ 1 - x_k, no symmetry P_{ij,k} = P_{ji,k} - SLSQP from 150 starts reaches det = 0 exactly (x uniform, M with eigenvalue 1/2 and entries 3/4 on a permutation pattern) but never below. That configuration is NOT realisable by a strictly non-Volterra P (the pair {3,4} would have to land on two targets), so the symmetry of P is what a proof of m = 4 uniqueness must use.
Conclusion: uniqueness at m = 4 remains open, but the numerical margin (det ≥ ~3.14 on the whole polytope versus the 0 needed for non-uniqueness) is large, and the reduction above turns the question into a concrete determinant inequality. I did not find a counterexample by any method; the paper's "numerically supported" is fair, and I would add the exhaustive vertex enumeration and the reduction.

- Jamilov, J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 697 (2016) 012013: a class on the 2-simplex; unique fixed point; limit set a point or infinite. m = 3 only.
- Jamilov, Discontinuity, Nonlinearity, Complexity 5(3) (2016) 263-283, "On symmetric strictly non-Volterra QSOs": abstract (publisher page, verbatim): "For a symmetric strictly non-Volterra quadratic stochastic operator on the three-dimensional simplex it is proved that this operator has a unique fixed point. A sufficient condition of attractiveness for the unique fixed point is found. ... infinitely many periodic points ... trajectories asymptotically cyclic with period two." This is m = 4 (S^3) for the SYMMETRIC subclass: uniqueness proved there, attractiveness possible - the closest prior work to the m = 4 question; the paper does not cite it and should.
- Jamilov-Ladra-Mukhitdinov, QTDS 16(3) (2017) 645-655, online 30 July 2016 (Crossref): I could not retrieve the abstract verbatim (Springer paywall redirect; Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex and Crossref carry no abstract; zbMATH 403). Consistent secondary descriptions (citing papers): the equiprobable strictly non-Volterra operator on a finite-dimensional simplex has a unique fixed point which is attracting, with a Lyapunov function giving a singleton limit set. Since at m = 3 the equiprobable operator is the a=b=...=1/2 case of (14) with tangential eigenvalues -1 (not attracting), their result is necessarily for m ≥ 4; the paper's own contraction factor (2m-1)/(m-1)^2 (verified) is an independent proof of the same thing. The paper's use of this reference is sound, but it should quote the theorem from the paper itself, not from an abstract nobody in the pipeline has seen.
- Hardin-Rozikov, QTDS 18 (2019) / arXiv:1808.00229, and Khamraev, Ukr. Math. J. 71(8) (2019): "quasi-strictly non-Volterra", four-parameter families on the 2-simplex, unique fixed point with one exception (two fixed points at e = 1 in Hardin-Rozikov). m = 3 and outside the strict class.
- Ganikhodjaev-Jusoo, AIP Conf. Proc. 2018 "Strictly non-Volterra QSO on 3-dimensional simplex": abstract concerns a Lebesgue-measure construction; no uniqueness statement for the general class. Not a resolution.
- Jamilov-Ladra, QTDS 19 (2020) art. 95 and arXiv:2601.17494 (2026): convex combinations of non-Volterra operators on S^{m-1}; not strictly non-Volterra; every trajectory converges to a fixed point (α < 1). Their 2026 introduction and reference list contain no result on strictly non-Volterra operators in dimension m ≥ 4 beyond the symmetric (m = 4) and equiprobable papers, and no mention of a multiple-fixed-point example.
No paper found gives a strictly non-Volterra QSO with more than one fixed point, and none proves uniqueness in general for m ≥ 4. The m ≥ 5 non-uniqueness and the two-face mechanism appear to be new. Later surveys/search summaries still list Problem 11 as open.
