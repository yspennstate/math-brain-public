# P0289, Ross's second conjecture: the light-traffic expansion re-derived, and what it says about Leskelä's second clause

Lane Q1 (claude-q1-attack-0912), 2026-09-12. Author-checked; not independently refereed; no novelty claimed.

## Where this stood

Leskelä, *Ross's second conjecture and supermodular stochastic ordering*, Queueing Systems 100 (2022) 213-215,
Problem 1: single-server queue, Cox arrivals with intensity λ_c(t) = λ(ct), λ stationary and ergodic, IID service,
stable; w(c) the mean stationary workload. Is w nonincreasing in c, and if not, what simple condition on λ makes it so?

The campaign's proof packet (`P0289_ross_modulation_counterexample_candidate.md`) refutes the universal clause with
the environment F(t) = 2 + cos(θX_t + θ/4), X the clockwise-only CTMC on Z/1024 at rate 1/sin θ, θ = 2π/1024, unit
service, ε = 10⁻⁴. On 09-08 the mechanism was verified and the numeric half closed (memory
`ross_second_conjecture_quantitative_half_closed_2026_09_08`), which left one named obligation: the expansion

    w(c) = εa/2 + ε² ∫₀¹ (1-u) E[F(0)F(-cu)] du + R_c,   0 ≤ R_c ≤ ε³M³ / (2(1-εM)),   uniformly in c,      (2)

had been taken from the packet as given. That is the step on which the rigour of the whole counterexample rests.
This note re-derives it from scratch and records what came out of doing so.

## The expansion, re-derived

Notation: 0 ≤ F ≤ M stationary with mean a, λ(t) = εF(ct), ρ = εM < 1, unit service, rate-one server. Given the
whole environment path, the arrivals are an inhomogeneous Poisson process with intensity λ; take the filtration
generated by the whole environment together with the arrivals up to time t, so N(t) - ∫₀ᵗ λ is a martingale.

1. Workload balance. V is the Reich workload, V(t) = sup_{u≥0} {N(t-u,t] - u}. Between arrivals d(V²) = -2V dt (also
   correct on {V = 0}); an arrival at t adds (V(t-)+1)² - V(t-)² = 2V(t-)+1. V(t-) is predictable, and the moments
   needed exist because V ≤ V̂, the M/D/1 workload of the rate-ρ Poisson process obtained by un-thinning, which has
   exponential moments. Stationarity of E V² then gives 2 E V = E[λ(0)(2V(0)+1)], i.e.
        w = εa/2 + E[λ(0) V(0)].

2. The shot lower bound. S(A) = Σ_{T ∈ A∩(-1,0]} (1+T) is the parallel-service workload. If the arrivals in (-1,0]
   are T₁ < … < T_k, the Reich supremum at u ↓ -T₁ is at least k + T₁ ≥ k + ΣTᵢ = S, so V ≥ S.

3. D = V - S is nondecreasing under the addition of an arrival. Adding one arrival at age v ≥ 1 leaves S alone and
   cannot lower V. For 0 < v < 1, S rises by 1-v; every Reich term with u > v rises by exactly 1, and every term with
   u ≤ v is dominated after the addition by the term at u = v+η, which is at least the old term plus (1-v) - η. So V
   rises by at least 1-v. Hence D(A) ≤ D(Â) whenever A ⊆ Â, and under the thinning coupling
        0 ≤ D(A) ≤ D(Â),   E D(Â) = ρ/(2(1-ρ)) - ρ/2 = ρ²/(2(1-ρ)).

4. Put together. E[λ(0)V(0)] = E[λ(0)S(0)] + E[λ(0)D(0)]; the second term is R_c ∈ [0, εM · ρ²/(2(1-ρ))], which is
   the stated bound and does not depend on c. For the first, condition on the environment and use Campbell:
   E[S(0) | env] = ∫_{-1}^0 (1+t) λ(t) dt, so E[λ(0)S(0)] = ε² ∫₀¹ (1-u) E[F(0)F(-cu)] du. That is (2).

5. The autocorrelation. E[F(0)F(t)] = 4 + ½ e^{-δ|t|} cos t with δ = tan(π/1024): the uniform average kills the
   first harmonics and the second spatial harmonic (2 ≢ 0 mod 1024), and E cos(θJ) for J Poisson(rt) is
   Re exp(rt(e^{iθ}-1)) = e^{-δt} cos t because r sin θ = 1.

Numeric checks (p0289_check.py, mpmath, 30 digits): the two-arrival interaction kernel integrates to exactly 1-d for
d ∈ {0, .25, .5, .9}, confirming the coefficient's normalisation a second way; J(2π) = 0.000972, J(3π) = 0.022520,
J(6.294) = 0.000970, J(8.9785) = 0.023600, matching the 09-08 memory; the packet's rational bounds are valid lower
bounds (true half-difference 0.01077 against the claimed 137/46080 = 0.00297, true margin 0.0094 ε² against the
claimed 2491/1612800 ε² = 0.00154 ε²). The packet is right at every step I could reach.

So the counterexample stands as author-checked by a second reader: for this stationary ergodic finite-state CTMC
intensity and deterministic unit service, w(3π) > w(2π) at ε = 10⁻⁴. The remaining doubt is prior art, not the
mathematics (Heyman's periodic-intensity counterexample to the first conjecture, Miyoshi-Rolski 2004, and the
light-traffic literature were not compared in full).

## What the re-derivation adds: the second-order term is a variance

Substituting v = cu,
    ∫₀¹ (1-u) R(cu) du = c⁻² ∫₀ᶜ (c-v) R(v) dv = ½ Var( c⁻¹ ∫₀ᶜ F(s) ds ),
where R is the autocovariance of F. Hence, exactly,

    w(c) = εa/2 + ε² [ a²/2 + ½ Var(F̄_c) ] + R_c,    F̄_c = c⁻¹ ∫₀ᶜ F,    0 ≤ R_c ≤ ε³M³/(2(1-εM)).

In words: to second order in the traffic parameter, faster modulation lowers the mean workload exactly when it
lowers the variance of the environment's average over one service time. Differentiating,
    d/dc Var(F̄_c) = (2/c³) ∫₀ᶜ (2v - c) R(v) dv,
so the low-traffic form of (1) holds if and only if ∫₀ᶜ (2v-c) R(v) dv ≤ 0 for every c > 0.

Three consequences, all elementary:

(a) If R is nonincreasing on [0,∞) the condition holds (the weight 2v-c is antisymmetric about c/2 and R is larger
    where it is negative). Every reversible stationary CTMC, for every f, has R(v) = Σ_k a_k² e^{-λ_k v}, so a
    reversible environment can never produce a second-order counterexample, whatever its monotonicity properties.
    Leskelä's Problem 3 example (the star chain, ledger P0291) is reversible and is consistent with this.
(b) The supermodular route of Bäuerle-Rolski sits above this: (2) in Leskelä's note implies R(c₁v) ≥ R(c₂v) for all
    v, i.e. R nonincreasing, hence (a).
(c) If Var(F̄_c) is strictly larger at some c₂ than at some c₁ < c₂, then w(c₁) < w(c₂) for every ε with
    εM³/(2(1-εM)) < Var(F̄_{c₂}) - Var(F̄_{c₁}) (both sides after dividing by ε²). For the packet's environment the
    derivative is positive on roughly (2π, 9); the deterministic random-phase environment 2 + cos(t + U) gives the
    undamped R(v) = ½ cos v and the same conclusion with no CTMC at all, which is presumably where Heyman-type
    examples live.

This is a partial answer to the second clause of Problem 1 - a necessary and sufficient condition for the
second-order (light-traffic) version of the monotonicity - and it explains why the counterexample had to be
non-reversible. It is NOT a condition for (1) at fixed traffic: when Var(F̄_c) is nonincreasing the third-order
remainder is only bounded, not signed, so nothing is concluded about (1) itself; and no claim is made that this
observation is new (it looks like it should be in the light-traffic literature on Cox queues, which I did not
search beyond the brain).
