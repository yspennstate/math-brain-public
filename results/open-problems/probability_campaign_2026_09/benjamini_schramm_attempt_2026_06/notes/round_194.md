# Round 194

Round 194 records the pointwise consequence of the annular-average branch.

If the super-half-log alternative fails, Round 193 gives
\[
  \frac{\sum_{a:\ d(x,a)\in I_{j,p}^{\rm gate}}\tau_p(x,a)}
       {|\{a:\ d(x,a)\in I_{j,p}^{\rm gate}\}|}
  \ge
  D_{\epsilon,\eta,J,\lambda,C_{\rm vol},\omega}
  L^{-3\eta}
  \bar\chi_p^{-2\lambda\alpha_\omega-2\theta-5\theta\alpha_\omega}.
\]
Therefore some vertex \(a\) in the expanded root annulus satisfies the same
lower bound:
\[
  \tau_p(x,a)
  \ge
  D_{\epsilon,\eta,J,\lambda,C_{\rm vol},\omega}
  L^{-3\eta}
  \bar\chi_p^{-2\lambda\alpha_\omega-2\theta-5\theta\alpha_\omega}.
\]

This gives a pointwise diagnostic criterion: excluding both super-half-log
expanded gate-annulus data and such pointwise near-half-log pairs forces
\(\mathcal R_p^*/\bar\chi_p\to0\).  The manuscript also records the caveat:
the pointwise version is lossy because geodesics always give
\(\tau_p^{\max}(n)\ge p^n\).  The annular-average formulation remains the
stronger residual target.
