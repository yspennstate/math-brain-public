# Round 454 endpoint \(L^2\) is a two-replica congestion profile

Round 454 identifies the endpoint \(L^2\) term from Round 453.

Suppose \(f(a)\) is the total \(Q\)-weighted count of skeleton prefixes
ending at \(a\in S_{u-1}(x)\), under whatever deletion scheme is being used.
Then
\[
  \|f\|_2^2=\sum_{a\in S_{u-1}(x)} f(a)^2
\]
counts ordered pairs of such skeleton prefixes with the same endpoint \(a\).
Thus a large endpoint \(L^2\) norm is exactly a two-replica boundary
congestion phenomenon.

This gives a clean trichotomy for marked annular insertions:

1. annular boundary exponent gap:
   \[
     \Gamma_{\rm ann}(G)<1/Q
   \]
   gives cut control;
2. cut control plus moderate endpoint \(L^2\) controls weighted insertion;
3. failure of moderate endpoint \(L^2\) produces a new two-replica endpoint
   congestion profile.

The third case is not a nuisance term that can be discarded.  It is structurally
close to the finite-set two-replica obstruction from the early part of the
project: two independent skeletons backscatter into the same boundary layer
vertex.  A future reduction should either:

* show that this endpoint two-replica profile is already covered by the
  existing finite-set/two-replica escape machinery; or
* add it as a named obstruction in the radial ladder and prove a separate
  exponent estimate.

This reframes the spread-return branch.  The annular transfer part is now a
boundary-vector SAW gap problem, while the endpoint concentration part is a
two-replica escape problem.  Both are familiar from earlier routes, but the
coverage-root split localizes them to the exact annuli generated by exterior
returns.
