# Round 453 cut estimates control weighted insertions via endpoint \(L^2\)

Round 453 turns the annular cut estimate into a weighted insertion bound.

Assume the cut estimate
\[
  A_{Q,u,\le L}^x(U,V)\le C\sqrt{|U||V|}
\]
for all \(U,V\subseteq S_{u-1}(x)\).  Let \(f,g\ge0\) be endpoint weights on
\(S_{u-1}(x)\).  Decompose them into dyadic level sets
\[
  U_i=\{a:2^i\le f(a)<2^{i+1}\},
  \qquad
  V_j=\{b:2^j\le g(b)<2^{j+1}\}.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \langle f,A_{Q,u,\le L}^xg\rangle
  &\le
  4C
  \left(\sum_i 2^i\sqrt{|U_i|}\right)
  \left(\sum_j 2^j\sqrt{|V_j|}\right) \\
  &\le
  4C\sqrt{N_fN_g}\,\|f\|_2\|g\|_2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(N_f,N_g\) are the numbers of nonempty dyadic levels.

For endpoint weights generated by path skeletons of length \(O(L_H)\), the
raw counts are at most exponential in \(L_H\), so
\[
  N_f,N_g=O(L_H)
\]
after ignoring zero levels.  The factor \(\sqrt{N_fN_g}\) is therefore
subexponential at the logarithmic scale used in the radial-profile criteria.

Consequently, annular cut control reduces the weighted insertion problem to
endpoint \(L^2\) control:
\[
  \langle f,A_{Q,u,\le L}^xg\rangle
  \le
  e^{o(L_H)}\,C\,\|f\|_2\|g\|_2.
\]

The remaining endpoint obstruction is now explicit.  If \(\|f\|_2\) or
\(\|g\|_2\) is too large compared to the relevant averaged skeleton mass,
then many skeletons concentrate on the same boundary vertex.  That is a
two-replica endpoint congestion event, not an annular transfer problem.
