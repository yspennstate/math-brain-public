Justification (lead). Importance and strength have to be weighed together, and the two candidates split them. Janson 20.2 is the more important question (a Probability Surveys problem and a published conjecture), but only Theorem A, the subcritical half, is proved on the page; Theorem B, the critical infinite-variance case that Thevenin actually emphasised, is a sketch that all three reviewers flagged and one showed needs a stronger growth condition. It is not finished, and its core is probabilistic, so a proof assistant could certify none of it. Hasebe-Ueda 3.3 is a problem posed in print by the people who proved the best previous bound, its answer is sharp with the extremal measure identified and a rigidity statement, it was proved twice by different routes (a Cauchy-profile moment lemma on 8 September and a Choquet two-atom reduction on 14 September), every identity was re-derived by me symbolically today and by all three reviewers, four numerical checks agree including an end-to-end mode count that does not use the criterion, and the load-bearing part is two real-polynomial inequalities in three variables that Lean can certify in full. The literature check found nothing on the free side. The classical companion is presumably known in the mixture literature and will be presented as such.
