## 2. Class arithmetic: the per-row budget and the log-factor wall

[DERIVED] from the verified facts of section 1 only. Let $C_0$ be the absolute constant in $k\le C_0 n/\log n$, and suppose the component is in the residual class, so $\ell\to\infty$ and $\ell(n-r)<rj+O(n)$.

(2a) Since $r\le k=O(n/\log n)$: $n-r=(1-o(1))n$ [this exact step is the dossier's own, S1 p.65 line 489-490]. Since $\ell\to\infty$, $n\ell\gg n$, so the basis-mass cap gives
$$rj \;\ge\; (1-o(1))\,n\ell .$$

(2b) Since $j\le k$ and $r\le k$:
$$r \;\ge\; (1-o(1))\frac{n\ell}{j}\;\ge\;(1-o(1))\frac{n\ell}{k}\;\ge\;(1-o(1))\frac{\ell\log n}{C_0},\qquad
j \;\ge\; (1-o(1))\frac{\ell\log n}{C_0}.$$
So $\min(r,j)\ge(1-o(1))\,\ell\log n/C_0$. Two remarks. First, the class's stated floor "relation corank at least five" (S6 p.123) is vacuous in the endpoint range: basis-mass forces $j\gtrsim \ell\log n\to\infty$. The name "high-corank" is quantitatively forced, not a modeling choice. Second, the same arithmetic shows $r\gtrsim\ell\log n$: class fibres are far from the maximal-defect corner.

(2c) Per-row budget. Write $b:=(n-r)\ell/k$ for the average number of bits per component row that the interface allows an encoding to spend (beyond the additive $O(j\log r+r+n)$ slack) if the total is to be $o((n-r)\ell)$ up to a factor $o(1)$. Then
$$ (1-o(1))\frac{\ell\log n}{C_0}\;\le\; b\;\le\;(1+o(1))\,\min(r,j).$$
Lower bound: $b\ge(1-o(1))n\ell/k$. Upper: $b<\frac{rj}{k}+O\!\big(\tfrac nk\big)$; $\frac{rj}{k}\le\min(r,j)$ because $\max(r,j)\le k$; and $n/k\le(1+o(1))\frac{rj}{\ell k}\le(1+o(1))\frac{\min(r,j)}{\ell}=o(\min(r,j))$.

(2d) The wall. A component row is a general-coefficient linear form certified only on the fibre; the two generic ways to name one cost $\Theta(\min(r,j))$ or more bits per row (values on $r$ independent fibre points, section 4.1; or Gale-side relation data in dimension $j$). By (2c) the budget is at most $(1+o(1))\min(r,j)$ per row and at least $\Omega(\ell\log n)$. So on the residual class, generic naming always misses the $o(\cdot)$ requirement — by a factor as small as $(1+o(1))$ at the extremal corner $j\asymp k$, $\ell\asymp rj/n$ — while any scheme that names class rows in $o\big((n-r)\ell/k\big)$ bits amortized closes the slice. In one sentence: after this analysis the entire deterministic gap is the statement that dense class rows can be named a diverging factor more cheaply than raw, and nothing more. A certificate $d_{\mathrm{cb}}\le C\ell$ names rows in $O(\ell+\log r)$ bits, overshooting the requirement by a full $\log n$ factor of slack; that slack is exactly what Layer III never uses, and it is what the weakenings below reclaim.

## 4. Worked pieces (what I actually did, and where it breaks)

### 4.1 Witness-point encoding lemma

Lemma [PROVED HERE, elementary]. Let $K$ be $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb F_p$ ($p$ odd), and let a component have $k$ rows $h_1,\dots,h_k$ (linear forms on $K^r$), each taking values in $\{\pm1\}$ on the fibre $A\subseteq\{\pm1\}^r$, with $A$ spanning $K^r$ (in-field). Then the component's row multiset is determined by: (a) some $r$ points $x_1,\dots,x_r\in A$ linearly independent over $K$ — $r^2$ bits; (b) for each row the value vector $(h_a\cdot x_1,\dots,h_a\cdot x_r)\in\{\pm1\}^r$ — $r$ bits per row. The decoder solves the nonsingular $r\times r$ system per row. Total: $r^2+kr+O(k+r+\log n)$ bits, injectively, per field.
Corollary: the number of frame-fixed components of a slice is at most $2^{\,r^2+kr+O(k+r+\log n)}$, so any slice with $kr=o((n-r)\ell)$ needs no rigidity input at all. [Same conditionality as W2.]

Where it breaks — exactly. In the residual class, (2a) gives $kr\ge rj\ge(1-o(1))(n-r)\ell$: the ratio (raw length)/(budget) is $\ge(1-o(1))\,k/j\ge1-o(1)$. So the lemma never closes an in-class slice; it reaches within a constant factor of the budget at the corner $j\asymp k$, $\ell\asymp rj/n$, and it pins the open problem to per-row compression by a factor $\omega(1)$ below raw (the wall, 2d). The circularity it fails to beat is the problem's actual core: rows are certified only on the final fibre, and the fibre exists only after all rows are known; the dossier's compression theorem (core $\le9\cdot4^\ell$, p.33/S6 Thm 1) is precisely a circularity-breaker whose price $4^\ell$ per row exceeds the budget in the open regime. This also gives a clean reason the dossier's own remark holds — "a linear theorem must use basis exchange or aggregation of the residual row space" (S6 p.122 lines 474-475): naming rows independently is information-theoretically stuck at the wall.
