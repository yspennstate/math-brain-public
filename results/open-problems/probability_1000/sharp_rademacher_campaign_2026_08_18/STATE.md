## THE ceil(k/2) TERM IS A COUNTING BOUND ON AN EXPLICIT DUAL, AND IT HOLDS FOR EVERY LINEAR TRIANGLE SYSTEM (19:0x, problem/why_ceil_k_over_two.md) — the strongest structural result since the star theorem, and it killed its own parent formula

**The dual is explicit.** For a linear system of k triangles on r coordinates (linear = no two triangles meet in TWO coordinates), D is exactly the r coordinate forms e_j (dual since p_j = ±1) plus one form per triangle f_t = e_a+e_b+e_c (dual since p_a+p_b+p_c = ±1 is precisely the NAE condition). So **|D| = r + k**. Verified with zero extras and zero missing: trees k=2,3,4 and k=6 (|D|=19); disjoint m=1,2,3; tri-cycles k=3,4,5. The campaign had been COMPUTING D for a day without writing it down.

**The bound.** valid_R needs ≤ dim = r classes, so R must collapse excess = |D| − #classes by at least (r+k) − r = **k**, exactly the triangle count. One dimension buys TWO merges because D is taken up to sign: v = e_b+e_c merges f_t with e_a AND e_b with −e_c. If two is the ceiling, excess ≤ 2d and needing excess ≥ k gives

> **d_cb ≥ ceil(k/2) for every linear triangle system.**

Trees have r=2k+1, tri-cycles r=2k, disjoint unions r=3k — all give |D|−r = k and the same bound.

**Searched for the counterexample, not for confirmations.** Max excess is exactly 2d at d=1,2,3 across 8 trees (up to 40,629 subspaces each), no violation, TIGHT not slack. The search is COMPLETE, not a sample: merges depend only on which differences lie in R, so any R has the same excess as span(differences in R), which is difference-spanned of dim ≤ d, and any such subspace is spanned by d of its own difference vectors.

**Proof status.** excess = E_c + E_t with E_c ≤ d and E_t ≤ d, both tight — the two jobs one vector does. E_c ≤ d is PROVED (signed incidence vectors of a forest are independent).
