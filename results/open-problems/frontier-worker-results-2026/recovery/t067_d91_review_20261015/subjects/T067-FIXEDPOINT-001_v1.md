# T067-FIXEDPOINT-001 v1: canonical tail limit is a BP fixed point

Status: proof (unaudited). Standard reduction, not a novelty claim.

Let q>=2, d>=2 be integers and 0<lambda<1. Broadcast a uniform root on the infinite regular d-ary tree with symmetric Potts edges. Denote the full random spin field by sigma. For each n let F_n be the sigma-algebra generated by all spins at levels at least n. These sigma-algebras decrease. Set P_n(i)=P(sigma_root=i | F_n).

By the tree Markov property, conditioning on level n makes the root independent of all later spins, so P_n equals the root posterior given level n alone. Levy's downward theorem applies to the bounded variable 1_{sigma_root=i}: P_n(i) converges almost surely and in L^1 to P_infinity(i)=P(sigma_root=i | intersection_n F_n). The cited math-brain card claim_2cc2222a supplied this exact decreasing-filtration theorem; it is not an application of the forward martingale theorem.

Let mu_n be the law of P_n. Then mu_n converges weakly to mu_infinity on the compact probability simplex. Every mu_n has uniform mean and is invariant under color permutations; those properties pass to the limit. Boundedness also gives convergence of its second moment.

For any posterior law mu with uniform mean, define the BP map T_lambda using d independent base vectors p^(1),...,p^(d), likelihood products z_i=product_j[1-lambda+q lambda p_i^(j)], posterior z_i/S with S=sum_i z_i, and weight S/q in the product expectation. This is the same map as the actual canonical recursion: mu_{n+1}=T_lambda(mu_n).

The map is weakly continuous. Each factor is at least 1-lambda>0, so S>=q(1-lambda)^d. The posterior and weight are bounded continuous functions of the finite tuple of input simplex vectors. For a bounded continuous test function h, the formula integral h(z/S)S/q d(mu^d) is continuous under weak convergence of mu on the compact simplex; finite products of weakly convergent probability laws also converge weakly. Hence T_lambda(mu_n) converges weakly to T_lambda(mu_infinity). Since mu_{n+1} has the same weak limit mu_infinity, it follows that mu_infinity=T_lambda(mu_infinity).

Thus if an independently proved local theorem excludes every nontrivial symmetric BP fixed point with second moment at most b, and a valid canonical tail bound is at most b, then the limiting law must be the uniform point mass and there is nonreconstruction. This implication is conditional: no quantitative exclusion radius b has been established by this worker.

The mathematical role of the tail bound is now precise: it bounds the second moment of a canonical fixed point. Finite grids still do not prove that fixed point is trivial.
