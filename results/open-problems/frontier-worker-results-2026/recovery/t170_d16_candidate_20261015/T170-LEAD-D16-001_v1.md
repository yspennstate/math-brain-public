## New step: a discrete horizontal boundary bound

Put m=15 and f(n)=sum_{j=0}^{n-1} popcount(j). For all n<=10000 use

 sigma(n)=30n-2f(n),       sigma(0)=0.

The certificate checks every n=2,...,10000 and every1<=b<=floor(n/2):
f(n-b)+f(b)+b<=f(n). There are exactly25000000 integer checks. Symmetry
covers the other splits; zero parts are equality.

Induction on hypercube dimension now proves that every n-vertex subset
of every binary hypercube has at most f(n) internal edges, for n<=10000:
the two slices have internal edges bounded inductively, while cross
edges form a matching of size at most min(a,b). The induction dimension
is unrestricted; each slice cardinality stays within the checked range.

Every finite subset of Z^15 has an isometric unary embedding into a finite
hypercube. Translate a finite containing box to nonnegative coordinates;
replace coordinate k by k leading ones followed by zeros in the string
of that coordinate's box length, and concatenate the15 strings. Hamming
distance equals lattice L1 distance. Thus internal nearest-neighbor
edges are preserved exactly. Degree counting gives
|boundary_edges(S)|=30n-2|internal_edges(S)|>=sigma(n).
This applies to disconnected sets as well. For n<=10000<2^14 the initial
binary segment embeds into {0,1}^15 and attains f(n) edges, so this bound
is attained in the required count range. No global hypercube theorem
or unchecked real logarithm is needed for the finite bound.
