## 1. Exact scope

Fix constants `c,r,s>0`. Let `d` tend to infinity, and let `J=(A,B)` be a finite simple bipartite graph satisfying

\[
\deg_J(a)\ge r d^2\qquad(a\in A),
\]

and

\[
\deg_J(b)\ge s d\qquad(b\in B).
\]

Let `H=J_p` be bond percolation with

\[
p=\frac{c}{d}.
\]

No upper degree bound is imposed on either side and no bounded dyadic-scale assumption is imposed on the `B` side.

The theorem below does **not** yet give the source-required long cycle. It gives a scale-free projected graph of minimum degree `Omega(d)` after pruning and therefore an `Omega(d)` **rainbow path**, which lifts to a simple alternating path in `H`. It also identifies the exact remaining obstruction to turning that path into an `Omega(d)` simple cycle: repeated physical witnesses / rainbow-cycle closure.

The restriction `d -> infinity` matters. The source Conjecture 6.1 quantifies over every fixed sufficiently large average degree and then lets host order tend to infinity. Nothing below is represented as having discharged that fixed-`d` quantifier.

## 2. Random matching projection

Condition on the percolated bipartite graph `H`. Independently for every witness `b in B`, let

\[
K_b:=\deg_H(b).
\]

On the open neighborhood `N_H(b)` choose a uniformly random maximum matching:

- if `K_b` is even, choose a uniformly random perfect matching of `N_H(b)`;
- if `K_b` is odd, first choose one unmatched vertex uniformly from `N_H(b)` and then choose a uniformly random perfect matching of the remaining `K_b-1` vertices.

For every matched pair `{a,u}` generated by witness `b`, put the simple edge `au` into a graph `Gamma` on vertex set `A`. If several witnesses generate the same simple edge, retain one graph edge and assign to it any one of the generating witnesses as its color.

### Lemma 2.1 — the witness coloring is proper

The resulting edge coloring of `Gamma` by witnesses in `B` is proper.

**Proof.** For a fixed witness `b`, all pairs generated by `b` form a matching on `A`. Therefore no two generated pairs of color `b` share an `A` endpoint. Passing to the simple graph and retaining any generating witness as the color of a multiply generated edge only removes generated pairs; it cannot create two incident edges of the same witness color. ∎

A rainbow path or rainbow cycle in `Gamma` lifts canonically to a simple alternating path or cycle in `H`: replace a colored projected edge `a u` of color `b` by `a-b-u`. Distinct projected vertices and distinct colors give distinct vertices on both bipartition classes.

## 3. B9 — randomization removes every `B`-degree scale

Fix `a in A`. Write

\[
R:=\deg_J(a)=z d^2,
\qquad z\ge r.
\]

For each `b in N_J(a)`, define a random output `U_{a,b}` as follows: it is the partner of `a` in the random maximum matching of `N_H(b)` if `a` is open to `b` and is matched there, and it is `\bot` otherwise.

The variables `(U_{a,b})_{b in N_J(a)}` are independent across different witnesses `b`, because they depend on disjoint percolation edges at the `B` coordinate together with independent matching randomizations.

Let

\[
m_b:=\Pr(U_{a,b}\ne\bot).
\]

If `t_b=deg_J(b)`, then for all sufficiently large `d`, using `t_b >= s d`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_b
&\ge p\,\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(t_b-1,p)\ge1\}\cdot\frac12\\
&\ge \frac{c}{2d}\left(1-e^{-cs/2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, conditional on `ab` being open and at least one other edge at `b` being open, the maximum matching matches `a` with probability at least `1/2` (in fact at least `2/3` except in the even cases where it is `1`). Also, for large `d`,

\[
p(t_b-1)\ge cs/2.
\]

Set

\[
\alpha:=\frac c2\left(1-e^{-cs/2}\right)>0.
\]

Then

\[
m_b\ge \frac\alpha d.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This lower bound is independent of the scale of `t_b`; it is the first place where the old dyadic witness-scale loss disappears.

## 4. B25 — double-count partner mass before collapsing collisions

For `u\ne a`, put

\[
q_{b,u}:=\Pr(U_{a,b}=u),
\qquad
x_u:=\sum_{b\in N_J(a)\cap N_J(u)}q_{b,u}.
\]

Two exact bounds are enough.

First, summing partner probabilities in the order `u` then `b`,

\[
\sum_{u\ne a}x_u
=
\sum_{b\in N_J(a)}m_b
\ge
\frac{\alpha R}{d}
=
\alpha z d.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Second, for every fixed `b,u`, the event `U_{a,b}=u` requires both open edges `ab` and `ub`, hence

\[
q_{b,u}\le p^2=\frac{c^2}{d^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
x_u\le Rp^2=c^2 z=:X.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `I_u` be the indicator that `a` and `u` are adjacent in the simple projection `Gamma`. Independence across witnesses gives

\[
\Pr(I_u=0)
=
\prod_b(1-q_{b,u})
\le e^{-x_u},
\]

so

\[
\Pr(I_u=1)\ge 1-e^{-x_u}.
\]

The function `f(x)=1-e^{-x}` is concave with `f(0)=0`. Thus, for `0<=x<=X`,

\[
f(x)\ge \frac{f(X)}X x.
\]

Using (4.1) and (4.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\deg_\Gamma(a)
&=\sum_{u\ne a}\Pr(I_u=1)\\
&\ge \frac{1-e^{-X}}X\sum_{u\ne a}x_u\\
&\ge \frac{\alpha}{c^2}\bigl(1-e^{-c^2z}\bigr)d\\
&\ge \frac{\alpha}{c^2}\bigl(1-e^{-c^2r}\bigr)d.
\end{aligned}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{\kappa:=\frac{\alpha}{c^2}\left(1-e^{-c^2r}\right)>0.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then, uniformly for every `a in A`,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\deg_\Gamma(a)\ge \kappa d.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This estimate pays neither `max_j Q_j` nor any entropy in the number of witness-degree scales.

### Lemma 4.1 — pairwise negative correlation at one projected vertex

For distinct `u,v`, the indicators `I_u,I_v` are pairwise negatively correlated. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\deg_\Gamma(a))\le \mathbb E\deg_\Gamma(a).
\]

**Proof.** A fixed witness `b` can output at most one partner for `a`. Hence

\[
\Pr(U_{a,b}\notin\{u,v\})=1-q_{b,u}-q_{b,v}
\le (1-q_{b,u})(1-q_{b,v}).
\]

Multiplying over independent witnesses,

\[
\Pr(I_u=0,I_v=0)
\le
\Pr(I_u=0)\Pr(I_v=0).
\]

For Bernoulli variables this is equivalent to `Cov(I_u,I_v)<=0`. Summing variances and covariances gives the claimed variance bound. ∎

Chebyshev and (4.4) give

\[
\Pr\left(\deg_\Gamma(a)<\frac{\kappa d}{2}\right)
\le \frac{4}{\kappa d}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Let

\[
Z:=\#\left\{a\in A:\deg_\Gamma(a)<\frac{\kappa d}{2}\right\}.
\]

No independence between different `a` is needed. By (4.5),

\[
\mathbb EZ\le \frac{4|A|}{\kappa d}.
\]

Therefore Markov gives

\[
\Pr\left(Z>\frac{|A|}{\sqrt d}\right)
\le \frac{4}{\kappa\sqrt d}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

On the complementary event,

\[
\overline d(\Gamma)
\ge
\left(1-d^{-1/2}\right)\frac{\kappa d}{2}.
\]

For all sufficiently large `d`, the standard iterative pruning lemma yields a nonempty subgraph `Gamma'` with

\[
\boxed{\delta(\Gamma')\ge \frac{\kappa d}{5}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Hence (4.7) holds with probability `1-O(d^{-1/2})` as `d->infinity`.

## 5. A self-contained long-rainbow-path consequence

### Lemma 5.1

Every properly edge-colored simple graph of minimum degree `delta` contains a rainbow path of at least `delta/2` edges.

**Proof.** Let

\[
P=v_0v_1\cdots v_\ell
\]

be a longest rainbow path. Every edge from `v_0` to a vertex outside `P` must use one of the `\ell` colors already on `P`; otherwise it extends `P`. Because the coloring is proper, at most one edge of each used color is incident with `v_0`, so there are at most `\ell` such outside edges. There are also at most `\ell` edges from `v_0` to vertices already on `P`. Thus

\[
\delta\le\deg(v_0)\le2\ell,
\]

and `\ell>=delta/2`. ∎

Combining Lemma 5.1 with (4.7), with probability `1-O(d^{-1/2})`, `Gamma'` contains a rainbow path of length at least

\[
\frac{\kappa d}{10}.
\]

It lifts to a simple alternating path in `H=J_p` of length at least

\[
\boxed{\frac{\kappa d}{5}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is a genuine scale-free path theorem for the cross core. It is **not** yet the desired cycle theorem.

## 6. B30 — exact cycle-closing defect

The only reason an ordinary projected cycle does not automatically lift is witness reuse: a projected cycle must be **rainbow** in the witness coloring in order to lift to a simple alternating cycle of `H`.

The following deterministic endpoint certificate makes this obstruction explicit.

### Lemma 6.1 — endpoint blocking for a longest rainbow path

Let `G` be a properly edge-colored simple graph of minimum degree `delta`, and let

\[
P=v_0v_1\cdots v_\ell
\]

be a longest rainbow path, with path colors `c_1,...,c_\ell`. Suppose `G` has no rainbow cycle of length at least `L`, where `2<=L<=\ell+1`. Then

\[
\boxed{\ell\ge\delta-L+2.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

More precisely, every edge `v_0v_i` with `i>=L-1` has color in `{c_1,...,c_i}`, and every edge from `v_0` to a vertex outside `P` has a color in `{c_1,...,c_\ell}`.

**Proof.** If `v_0v_i`, `i>=L-1`, had a color not in `{c_1,...,c_i}`, then the prefix

\[
v_0v_1\cdots v_i v_0
\]

would be a rainbow cycle of length `i+1>=L`, contradiction. If an edge from `v_0` to outside `P` used a color absent from the path, it would extend the longest rainbow path, contradiction.

Thus every edge incident with `v_0` is either an edge to one of the first `L-2` path vertices, contributing at most `L-2` possibilities, or is colored by one of the `\ell` path colors. Properness allows at most one incident edge of each color. Hence

\[
\deg(v_0)\le (L-2)+\ell.
\]

Since `deg(v_0)>=delta`, (6.1) follows. ∎

The same statement holds at `v_\ell` by reversing the path.

Applied to `Gamma'`, this says that failure of an `Omega(d)` rainbow cycle is no longer a degree-scale problem. It forces a directional **endpoint-color capture** pattern: most edges at the endpoint of a longest rainbow path either point very near the endpoint along the path or reuse colors already consumed by the path.

Because the coloring comes from independent random witness matchings, that endpoint-color-capture event is the next probabilistic object to attack. A theorem ruling it out at linear path length would turn (5.1) into the required simple cycle inside the cross core.

## 7. What this does and does not settle

This run removes the preceding blocker

`max_j Q_j -> 0` / infinitely many physical witness-degree scales

from the **degree-production and long-path** part of the argument. The random maximum-matching projection handles every witness degree in one formula; no dyadic gluing is required to get `Omega(d)` projected degree.

It does **not** prove an `Omega(d)` cycle. The precise remaining issue is:

> **MATCHING-RB-CLOSE.** In the random properly edge-colored projection generated by independent witness matchings above, prove that the `Theta(d)`-minimum-degree core contains a rainbow cycle of length `Theta(d)`, uniformly over all deterministic host incidence graphs satisfying the two lower-degree conditions.

A rainbow cycle of length `L` in the projection lifts to a simple cycle of length `2L` in `H`. General deterministic proper edge-colorings cannot simply be substituted here: known rainbow-cycle extremal results depend on the ambient order (for example, average degree `C log n log log n` guarantees some rainbow cycle), while the source requires constants uniform over arbitrarily large host order. The additional random matching origin of the coloring has to be used.

There is a second honest scope issue: (4.6) tends to one along `d->infinity`. The full source asks for every fixed `d>=d_0` as host order tends to infinity. A closure proof must either obtain an order-driven concentration statement at fixed `d` or bypass this projected-degree concentration step.

## 8. Literature cross-check for the new obstruction

Fresh checks on 2026-09-18:

1. Christoph–Morawski–Wigderson, arXiv:2608.19145, still surfaces as v1 dated 2026-08-19 and still states the long-cycle average-degree conjecture in its concluding remarks. A bounded search found no later explicit full resolution. This is negative evidence only.
2. Krivelevich–Samotij, *Long Paths and Cycles in Random Subgraphs of H-Free Graphs* (EJC 2014; arXiv:1303.1066), proves the near-critical long-cycle theorem under minimum-degree/H-free hypotheses. It does not settle the average-degree source target.
3. Alon–Bucić–Sauermann–Zakharov–Zamir, *Essentially tight bounds for rainbow cycles in proper edge-colourings* (PLMS 2025), proves that average degree `C log n log log n` forces a rainbow cycle in an arbitrary properly colored `n`-vertex graph. This confirms that the rainbow-closure step is a genuine extremal object, but its order dependence is unusable directly here.
4. Kim–Lee–Liu–Tran, *Rainbow Cycles in Properly Edge-Colored Graphs* (Combinatorica 2024), gives the earlier `32(log 5n)^2` rainbow-cycle bound and fixed-even-cycle results. Again, these do not give an order-free linear-length cycle at minimum degree `Theta(d)`.
5. Füredi–Kostochka–Luo, *Berge cycles in non-uniform hypergraphs* (EJC 2020), gives exact non-uniform hypergraph minimum-degree conditions for long Berge cycles. Those conditions grow exponentially with the target cycle length in the unrestricted non-uniform setting, so they do not by themselves close the random incidence problem; they reinforce that the randomness must be used.

No novelty claim is made from any search miss.
