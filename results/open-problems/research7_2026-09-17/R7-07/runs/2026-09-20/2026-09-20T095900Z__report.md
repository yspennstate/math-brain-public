## 2. Inherited setup used in this run

Let `X` denote the full rooted surviving finite-type branching-tree state, including its root type, and let

\[
K_H(X,dX')
\]

be the one-generation harmonic-descendant kernel: from a surviving root choose an infinite child with probability proportional to that child's escape conductance.

The previous C06 construction produced a stationary ergodic probability `mu` for `K_H`, equivalent to the source law conditioned on survival. On type `i` and conductance `b=B(X)`, its density has the form

\[
h_i(b)=b\,g_i(b),
\qquad
0<A_i\le g_i(b)\le L_i<\infty,
\tag{2.1}
\]

with `g_i in C^k([0,1])` for the finite `k` used in the reverse spectral-gap theorem (indeed the Stieltjes construction gives much more regularity).

Write `nu` for the original source law conditioned on survival, with any fixed source-admissible finite root-type mixture. Because the type space is finite, there are constants `c_i in (0,infinity)` such that

\[
\ell_i(b):=\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}(i,b)
=\frac{c_i}{b\,g_i(b)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\ell_i(b)\asymp b^{-1},
\qquad
\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}=\ell^{-1}\le C.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The inherited all-orders inverse-conductance estimate says

\[
E_\mu[B^{-r}]<\infty
\qquad\forall r>0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\ell\in L^p(\mu)\quad\forall p<\infty.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Let `K_H^*` be the stationary time reversal on the full tree state. The preceding reverse-projection theorem says that on functions depending only on `(type,B)`, `K_H^*` is exactly the finite-type conductance kernel `widehat Q`. For some finite `k`, `widehat Q` has a spectral gap on `C^k`:

\[
\|\widehat Q^m f-\bar\mu(f)\|_{C^k}
\le C\rho_*^m\|f\|_{C^k},
\qquad 0<\rho_*<1.
\tag{2.6}
\]

The inherited electrical information identity is

\[
I_n:=-\log\theta_T(X_n)
=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\phi(B_r)+\log B_0-\log B_n,
\qquad
\phi(b)=-\log(1-b),
\tag{2.7}
\]

with stationary dimension / entropy rate

\[
d_H=E_\mu\phi(B).
\tag{2.8}
\]

The preceding reports proved under stationary initialization

\[
\frac{I_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-\lfloor nt\rfloor d_H}{\sqrt n}
\Rightarrow \sigma_H W(t)
\tag{2.9}
\]

in `D([0,1])`, with `sigma_H^2>0` exactly outside the deterministic regular no-thinning model, and also the analytic pressure

\[
\Lambda(t)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log E_\mu e^{tI_n},
\qquad
\Lambda(t)=d_Ht+\frac12\sigma_H^2t^2+O(t^3)
\tag{2.10}
\]

for `t` near zero. The stationary MDP follows from the same expansion.

## 3. New theorem: exponential equilibration from the actual source root

### Theorem 3.1

Under the inherited finite-type hypotheses,

\[
\boxed{
\|\nu K_H^m-\mu\|_{TV}\le C e^{-cm}
\qquad(m\ge0).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is a full-state total-variation statement, even though the spectral calculation is performed only on the reversed `(type,B)` projection.

### Proof

For any stationary Markov kernel `K` with invariant law `mu`, if `nu=ell mu`, then

\[
\frac{d(\nu K^m)}{d\mu}=(K^*)^m\ell.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Here `ell` depends only on `(type,B)`. The reverse projection is exact, so

\[
(K_H^*)^m\ell=\widehat Q^m\ell.
\tag{3.3}
\]

It remains to show

\[
\|\widehat Q^m\ell-1\|_{L^1(\bar\mu)}\le Ce^{-cm}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The only issue is the `b^{-1}` singularity of `ell` at zero. Because `g_i` is positive and `C^k`, differentiation of (2.2) gives

\[
|\partial_b^j\ell_i(b)|\le C_j b^{-j-1},
\qquad 0<b\le1,
\quad 0\le j\le k.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Fix a smooth cutoff `chi` with `chi(x)=0` for `x<=1` and `chi(x)=1` for `x>=2`, and set

\[
\ell_\varepsilon(i,b)=\chi(b/\varepsilon)\ell_i(b).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then

\[
\|\ell_\varepsilon\|_{C^k}\le C\varepsilon^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

For every `r>1`, (2.4) and `ell<=C/B` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\ell-\ell_\varepsilon\|_1
&\le C E_\mu[B^{-1};B<2\varepsilon]\\
&\le C_r\varepsilon^{r-1}E_\mu[B^{-r}]
\le C_r'\varepsilon^{r-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Since `widehat Q` is Markov, it is an `L^1(bar mu)` contraction. Let

\[
f_\varepsilon=\ell_\varepsilon-\bar\mu(\ell_\varepsilon).
\]

Using `bar mu(ell)=1`, (2.6)--(3.8),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\widehat Q^m\ell-1\|_1
&\le
\|\widehat Q^m(\ell-\ell_\varepsilon)\|_1
+\|\widehat Q^m f_\varepsilon\|_1
+|\bar\mu(\ell_\varepsilon)-1|\\
&\le
2C_r'\varepsilon^{r-1}
+C\rho_*^m\varepsilon^{-(k+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Choose `epsilon=e^{-am}` with

\[
0<a<\frac{-\log\rho_*}{2(k+1)}.
\]

Then the second term in (3.9) decays exponentially. Choose `r` large enough that the first term also decays at an exponential rate. This proves (3.4), hence (3.1). `square`

### Interpretation

The stationary law is not merely equivalent to the source survival law: following the harmonic ray **forgets the source-root bias exponentially fast**. The proof uses exactly the two structures developed in the previous runs: all inverse moments at `B downarrow 0` and a reverse conductance spectral gap.

## 4. Source-root functional CLT

### Theorem 4.1

Let the tree be sampled from the original source law conditioned on survival, then sample the ray from its harmonic measure. Under `nu`,

\[
\boxed{
\left\{
\frac{I_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-\lfloor nt\rfloor d_H}{\sqrt n}
\right\}_{0\le t\le1}
\Rightarrow
\{\sigma_H W(t)\}_{0\le t\le1}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a nonregular source, `sigma_H^2>0`; in the deterministic regular no-thinning model the limit is degenerate because `I_n=n\log k` exactly.

### Proof

Fix an integer burn-in `m`. Under `nu`, the state at generation `m` has law `nu K_H^m`; by (3.1), its total-variation distance from `mu` is at most `Ce^{-cm}`. Since both future path laws use the same transition kernel `K_H`, the entire future path after generation `m` differs in total variation from a stationary future path by the same amount.

For fixed `m`, the prefix information `I_m` is finite almost surely, so

\[
\frac{I_m-md_H}{\sqrt n}\to0
\qquad \nu\text{-a.s. as }n\to\infty.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The time displacement `m/n` also vanishes. Therefore the stationary FCLT (2.9), applied to the future block, gives the Brownian limit up to an error in distribution bounded by `Ce^{-cm}`. First let `n->infinity` at fixed `m`; then let `m->infinity`. This proves (4.1). `square`

No new regeneration construction is needed for this transfer.

## 5. The source-conditioned annealed pressure equals the stationary pressure

### Theorem 5.1

For all real `t` in a sufficiently small neighborhood of zero,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log E_\nu e^{tI_n}
=\Lambda(t).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence, defining

\[
Z_n(q;T)=\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)^q,
\tag{5.2}
\]

the actual source-conditioned annealed harmonic Renyi pressure satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H(q)
:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log E_\nu Z_n(q;T)
=\Lambda(1-q)
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

for `q>0` near `1`, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H(q)
=-(q-1)d_H+\frac12\sigma_H^2(q-1)^2+O(|q-1|^3).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

On path space initialized by `nu`, the Radon--Nikodym density relative to stationary initialization is `ell(X_0)`. Thus

\[
E_\nu e^{tI_n}=E_\mu[\ell(X_0)e^{tI_n}].
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because `dmu/dnu=ell^{-1}` is bounded by (2.3), for a constant `C`,

\[
E_\mu e^{tI_n}\le C E_\nu e^{tI_n}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This gives the lower exponential-rate bound `liminf >= Lambda(t)`.

For the upper bound choose `p>1`, let `r=p/(p-1)`, and use (2.5):

\[
E_\nu e^{tI_n}
\le \|\ell\|_{L^p(\mu)}
\left(E_\mu e^{rtI_n}\right)^{1/r}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log E_\nu e^{tI_n}
\le \frac1r\Lambda(rt).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Choose `p` large enough that `rt` remains in the stationary analytic-pressure neighborhood, then let `p->infinity`, i.e. `r downarrow 1`. Continuity of `Lambda` yields the reverse inequality. This proves (5.1).

Finally, conditional on the tree,

\[
Z_n(q;T)
=E_H[e^{-(q-1)I_n}\mid T],
\tag{5.9}
\]

so averaging (5.9) under `nu` and applying (5.1) at `t=1-q` proves (5.3)--(5.4). `square`

## 6. Source-root MDP

### Theorem 6.1

Let `a_n->infinity` and `a_n/sqrt(n)->0`. For every nonregular source,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{I_n-nd_H}{a_n\sqrt n}
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

under source-conditioned initialization satisfies an MDP with speed `a_n^2` and rate

\[
\boxed{J(x)=\frac{x^2}{2\sigma_H^2}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
s_n=\theta\frac{a_n}{\sqrt n}.
\]

The stationary analytic pressure gives

\[
\frac1{a_n^2}
\log E_\mu e^{s_n(I_n-nd_H)}
\to \frac12\sigma_H^2\theta^2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed `n s_n^2=theta^2 a_n^2` and `n|s_n|^3/a_n^2=O(a_n/sqrt(n))->0`.

The bounded density `dmu/dnu` gives the same lower limit for `nu`. For the upper limit, Hölder as in (5.7) gives, for conjugate exponent `r>1`,

\[
\limsup
\frac1{a_n^2}
\log E_\nu e^{s_n(I_n-nd_H)}
\le
\frac r2\sigma_H^2\theta^2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Let `r downarrow1`. Hence the scaled cumulant generating function under `nu` is exactly `sigma_H^2 theta^2/2`. Gartner--Ellis gives (6.2). `square`

This removes stationary initialization from the fluctuation theorem at both CLT and moderate-deviation scales.

## 7. New quenched result: entropy slope and pressure sandwich

The main remaining target from the previous run was an almost-sure statement for a **fixed source tree**, not just an average over source trees. The full quenched pressure equality is still out of reach, but one can already prove its exact tangent at `q=1`.

For a fixed surviving tree `T`, define the level-`n` harmonic entropy

\[
\mathcal H_n(T)
:=-\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)\log\theta_T(v)
=E_H[I_n\mid T].
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Lemma 7.1 — source-a.s. quenched entropy slope

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathcal H_n(T)}n\to d_H
\qquad\text{for }\nu\text{-a.e. surviving }T.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

#### Proof

The stationary spectral expansion and the `L^2` electrical endpoint bound imply

\[
E_\mu[(I_n-nd_H)^2]=O(n).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since `ell in L^2(mu)`, Cauchy--Schwarz on source-initialized path space gives

\[
E_\nu|I_n-nd_H|
=E_\mu[\ell(X_0)|I_n-nd_H|]
=O(\sqrt n).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Conditional Jensen therefore yields

\[
E_\nu\left|
\frac{\mathcal H_n(T)}n-d_H
\right|
\le Cn^{-1/2}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Choose `n_m=floor(m^p)` with any `p>2`. Markov's inequality plus (7.5) is summable in `m`, so Borel--Cantelli gives

\[
\frac{\mathcal H_{n_m}(T)}{n_m}\to d_H
\quad\nu\text{-a.s.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Shannon entropy is monotone under refinement:

\[
\mathcal H_{n+1}(T)\ge\mathcal H_n(T).
\tag{7.7}
\]

Because `n_{m+1}/n_m->1`, for `n_m<=n<=n_{m+1}`,

\[
\frac{\mathcal H_{n_m}(T)}{n_{m+1}}
\le
\frac{\mathcal H_n(T)}n
\le
\frac{\mathcal H_{n_{m+1}}(T)}{n_m}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Both outer terms converge to `d_H`, proving (7.2). `square`

### Theorem 7.2 — quenched harmonic-pressure sandwich near `q=1`

Let

\[
p_n(q,T):=\frac1n\log Z_n(q;T).
\tag{7.9}
\]

There is a neighborhood `J subset (0,infinity)` of `1` such that on one `nu`-full event, simultaneously for every `q in J`,

\[
\boxed{
-(q-1)d_H
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}p_n(q,T)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}p_n(q,T)
\le
\Psi_H(q).
}
\tag{7.10}
\]

Consequently, using (5.4),

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\liminf_n p_n(q,T)+(q-1)d_H
\le
\limsup_n p_n(q,T)+(q-1)d_H
\le
\frac12\sigma_H^2(q-1)^2+O(|q-1|^3).
}
\tag{7.11}
\]

In particular both the quenched `liminf` and quenched `limsup` pressure functions have the same derivative at `q=1`:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dq}\Big|_{q=1}\underline p(q)
=
\frac{d}{dq}\Big|_{q=1}\overline p(q)
=-d_H.
}
\tag{7.12}
\]

Here (7.12) is meant through the two-sided difference quotient controlled by (7.11); existence of a common fixed-`q` limit away from `1` is **not** asserted.

#### Lower bound

For every fixed tree, Jensen's inequality and (7.1) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_n(q,T)
&=\frac1n\log E_H[e^{-(q-1)I_n}\mid T]\\
&\ge -(q-1)\frac{\mathcal H_n(T)}n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.13}
\]

Apply Lemma 7.1 to obtain the first inequality in (7.10), for every `q` simultaneously.

#### Upper bound for fixed `q`

By Theorem 5.1,

\[
E_\nu Z_n(q;T)=\exp\{n(\Psi_H(q)+o(1))\}.
\tag{7.14}
\]

Hence for every `epsilon>0`, for all large `n`,

\[
\nu\left(
Z_n(q;T)\ge e^{n(\Psi_H(q)+\varepsilon)}
\right)
\le e^{-n\varepsilon/2}.
\tag{7.15}
\]

The right side is summable, so Borel--Cantelli gives

\[
\limsup_n p_n(q,T)\le\Psi_H(q)
\tag{7.16}
\]

for `nu`-a.e. tree.

#### Simultaneous extension from rational `q` to all `q in J`

Intersect the full-probability events for rational `q in J`. For each `n` and tree `T`,

\[
q\mapsto p_n(q,T)
\]

is convex, since it is a normalized log-sum-exp of the affine functions `q log theta_T(v)`. For rational `q_-<q<q_+`, convexity gives

\[
p_n(q,T)
\le
\frac{q_+-q}{q_+-q_-}p_n(q_-,T)
+
\frac{q-q_-}{q_+-q_-}p_n(q_+,T).
\tag{7.17}
\]

Take `limsup_n`, then let rational `q_-,q_+->q`; analyticity of `Psi_H` gives the upper bound for every real `q in J`. This proves (7.10). Expansion (5.4) gives (7.11)--(7.12). `square`

## 9. Reusable lemma for the Galton--Watson speed roles

A general mechanism from Section 3 is worth exporting without changing P03's objective:

> If a forward environment chain has an equivalent stationary law `mu`, the source-start density `ell=dnu/dmu` depends on a finite-dimensional observable, the stationary time reversal closes on that observable with a `C^k` spectral gap, and `ell` has a controlled endpoint singularity dominated by all-order inverse moments, then the **forward source-start chain mixes exponentially in total variation**. The identity is `d(nu K^m)/dmu=(K^*)^m ell`; smooth truncation plus the reverse spectral gap removes the singularity.

This may be useful in speed/regeneration problems where the stationary environment is easy to analyze backward but source initialization is the quantity actually appearing in the theorem.
