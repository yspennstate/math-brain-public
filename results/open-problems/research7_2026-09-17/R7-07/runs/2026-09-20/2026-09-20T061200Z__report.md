## 2. Inherited finite-type setup and proved stationary environment

Let `D={1,...,d}`. A type-`i` parent has potential-child count `nu`,

\[
G(z)=E[z^\nu],\qquad m_*=E\nu<\infty,
\]

and each potential child independently has type `j` with probability `q_j>0` and transmits with probability `p_{ij}>0`. The failed-child mean matrix is

\[
M_{ij}=m_*q_jp_{ij},\qquad \rho=\rho(M)>1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Under the source Kesten--Stigum hypotheses retained in the previous reports, the Perron martingale limit `mathcal W` satisfies

\[
\mathcal W(T)=\rho^{-1}\sum_u\mathcal W(T_u),
\qquad
\mathcal W>0\iff\text{survival}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For simple random walk on a surviving failed tree, let

\[
B(T)=P_o^T(\text{never hit an added parent}).
\]

Writing `B_u=B(T_u)` and `S=\sum_uB_u`, the electrical recursion is

\[
B(T)=\frac{S}{1+S},
\qquad
S=\frac{B(T)}{1-B(T)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The harmonic and Perron child flows are

\[
H_T(u)=\frac{B_u}{S},
\qquad
U_T(u)=\frac{\mathcal W_u}{\rho\mathcal W(T)}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Previous C06 runs constructed a probability law

\[
\mu(d(i,T))=h_i(B(T))P_i(dT)
\tag{2.5}
\]

which is stationary and ergodic for the harmonic-descendant kernel

\[
(K_Hf)(T)=\sum_uH_T(u)f(T_u),
\tag{2.6}
\]

and is equivalent type-by-type to the source law conditioned on survival. The stationary density obeys

\[
0<A_iB\le h_i(B)\le L_iB\le L_i<\infty.
\tag{2.7}
\]

The same work established all inverse moments of `B` on survival and enough lower-tail control of `mathcal W` that

\[
X(T):=\log\frac{B(T)}{\mathcal W(T)}
\tag{2.8}
\]

has a nontrivial exponential moment under `mu`. In particular `log B`, `log mathcal W`, and `X` are in `L^2(mu)`.

The immediately preceding report used these facts to prove internally

\[
\dim_H(HARM)=\log\rho-E_\mu D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T),
\tag{2.9}
\]

with strict inequality outside the deterministic regular no-thinning source.

## 3. A route attempted and rejected: naive perturbation at the equality manifold

The previous report proposed differentiating the transfer law near the deterministic regular no-thinning equality manifold. There is a real singularity that must be respected before doing so.

The equality manifold lies at the boundary `p_{ij}=1` with deterministic `nu`. Perturbing a transmission probability to `1-epsilon a_{ij}` creates branch defects with probability `Theta(epsilon)`, but a defect at the root or another shallow generation changes the electrical recursion by an `O(1)` amount. Therefore a formal linearization of

\[
B=\frac{\sum B_u}{1+\sum B_u}
\]

around the regular fixed point misses `O(epsilon)` contributions from rare shallow defects. A valid perturbative coefficient requires a rare-defect/cluster expansion, not just differentiation of the deterministic recursion. I did not promote a formal Taylor coefficient to a theorem.

Instead, this run uses the exact electrical gauge already latent in the 2026-09-19 B12 report and pushes it through the now-proved stationary law. This gives a stronger fluctuation reduction without a small-parameter assumption.

## 4. Exact one-edge electrical coboundary

Let `T` be any transient rooted tree with unit edge conductances and at least one surviving child. For a harmonic-selected child `u`, (2.3)--(2.4) give

\[
H_T(u)
=\frac{B_u}{S}
=\frac{B_u(1-B(T))}{B(T)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the one-variable electrical potential

\[
\Phi(T):=-\log(1-B(T))
=\log(1+S(T)).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then (4.1) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
-\log H_T(u)
=\Phi(T)+\log B(T)-\log B(T_u).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the pointwise version of the conductance coboundary that appeared indirectly in the earlier B12 identity. The important new step is to use (4.3) as the complete harmonic information decomposition, rather than only as a gauge for the pair-spine kernel.

If `X_0=o,X_1,...,X_n` is a harmonic ray and

\[
\theta_T(X_n)=H_T([X_0,\ldots,X_n])
\]

is its depth-`n` harmonic cylinder mass, summing (4.3) gives the exact pathwise theorem

\[
\boxed{
-\log\theta_T(X_n)
=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\Phi(T_{X_r})
+\log B(T)-\log B(T_{X_n}).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\theta_T(v)
=\frac{B_v}{B_o}
\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(1-B_{v_r})
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

for every harmonic-active level-`n` vertex `v`.

Equation (4.5) was already implicit in the 2026-09-19 gauge report. Equations (4.3)--(4.4), combined with the stationary environment now available, are the new structural input for the remainder of this run.

## 5. Electrical entropy formula and a shorter PF--KL identity

Let

\[
\mathsf H(T)=-\sum_uH_T(u)\log H_T(u)
\tag{5.1}
\]

be the local harmonic entropy. Taking harmonic-child expectation in (4.3),

\[
\mathsf H(T)
=\Phi(T)+\log B(T)-K_H(\log B)(T).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because `log B in L^1(mu)` and `mu K_H=mu`, stationarity cancels the coboundary:

\[
\boxed{
E_\mu\mathsf H(T)
=E_\mu\Phi(T)
=E_\mu[-\log(1-B(T))].
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the finite-type harmonic dimension from the previous report has the purely electrical representation

\[
\boxed{
\dim_H(HARM)
=E_\mu\log\bigl(1+\sum_uB_u\bigr).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

There is also an exact finite-depth annealed entropy identity. Let

\[
\mathcal H_n(T)=-\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)\log\theta_T(v).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Taking harmonic expectation in (4.4) and then stationary expectation gives, for every integer `n>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
E_\mu\mathcal H_n(T)
=nE_\mu\Phi(T).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

There is no boundary `O(1)` term after stationary averaging.

The PF comparison also becomes a one-line electrical coboundary. From (2.4) and `X=log(B/mathcal W)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{H_T(u)}{U_T(u)}
&=X(T_u)-X(T)+\log\rho+\log(1-B(T)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Taking harmonic-child expectation and then `mu`-expectation, the `X` coboundary cancels:

\[
\boxed{
E_\mu D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T)
=\log\rho+E_\mu\log(1-B(T)).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Hence (5.3) immediately yields

\[
\boxed{
E_\mu\Phi
=\log\rho-E_\mu D_{KL}(H\Vert U),
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

recovering the previous PF--KL dimension identity without separately manipulating the cross entropy. This is a useful independent algebraic check on that proof chain.

## 6. Arbitrary-q harmonic partition function as a Feynman--Kac transfer

For any real `q` and fixed depth `n`, define the harmonic Renyi partition sum

\[
Z_n(q;T)=\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)^q,
\tag{6.1}
\]

where only harmonic-active vertices have positive `theta`. Every level is finite almost surely, so (6.1) is pathwise finite for fixed `n`.

Define the positive transfer operator

\[
(\mathcal K_qf)(T)
=(1-B(T))^{q-1}
(K_Hf)(T).
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 — exact all-q transfer identity

For every fixed surviving tree and every integer `n>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
Z_n(q;T)
=B(T)^{1-q}\,\mathcal K_q^n\bigl(B^{q-1}\bigr)(T).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

Under harmonic path measure,

\[
Z_n(q;T)
=E_H^T[\theta_T(X_n)^{q-1}].
\tag{6.4}
\]

By (4.5),

\[
\theta_T(X_n)^{q-1}
=B(T)^{1-q}
B(T_{X_n})^{q-1}
\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(1-B(T_{X_r}))^{q-1}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Iterating `K_H` with the multiplicative potential in (6.2) gives exactly (6.3). `square`

At `q=1`, `mathcal K_1=K_H` and `Z_n(1)=1`. Differentiating the finite sum (6.1) at `q=1` gives

\[
\boxed{
-\partial_q\log Z_n(q;T)|_{q=1}=\mathcal H_n(T),
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
\partial_q^2\log Z_n(q;T)|_{q=1}
=\operatorname{Var}_{\theta_n}\bigl(\log\theta_T(v)\bigr).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Therefore the full local `q`-geometry of harmonic measure near `q=1` is governed by the Feynman--Kac perturbation of the single stationary observable `Phi=-log(1-B)`, with `B^{q-1}` only as an endpoint gauge. This is the precise bridge from the proved dimension theorem back toward the source's boundary-fluctuation programme.

For `q=2`, (6.2) becomes the base killed-harmonic operator

\[
\mathcal K_2=(1-B)K_H
\tag{6.8}
\]

that appeared in the earlier pair-spine work, so Theorem 6.1 unifies that special operator with the entire Renyi family.

## 7. Diffusive and general diverging-scale fluctuation transfer

Let `mathbb Q` be the stationary annealed harmonic-ray law

\[
\mathbb Q(dT,d\xi)=\mu(dT)H_T(d\xi).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Write

\[
I_n:=-\log\theta_T(X_n),
\qquad
d_H:=E_\mu\Phi=\dim_H(HARM),
\qquad
\overline\Phi=\Phi-d_H.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The integrability needed below follows from the existing endpoint estimates. First, all inverse moments of `B` imply

\[
\log B\in L^r(\mu)\quad\text{for every finite }r.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Second, since `0<=B_u<=1`,

\[
0\le\Phi(T)=\log(1+S(T))\le\log(1+\nu),
\tag{7.4}
\]

and the bounded stationary density plus `E nu<infinity` imply `Phi in L^2(mu)` because `log^2(1+n)<=C(1+n)`.

From (4.4), exactly

\[
I_n-nd_H
=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\overline\Phi(T_{X_r})
+\log B(T)-\log B(T_{X_n}).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Stationarity gives the uniform endpoint estimate

\[
\left\|
\log B(T)-\log B(T_{X_n})
\right\|_{L^2(\mathbb Q)}
\le2\|\log B\|_{L^2(\mu)}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Hence for **every deterministic scale `a_n->infinity`**,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{I_n-nd_H}{a_n}
-
\frac{1}{a_n}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\overline\Phi(T_{X_r})
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in }L^2(\mathbb Q).
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

In particular, at diffusive scale,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{I_n-nd_H}{\sqrt n}
-
\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\overline\Phi(T_{X_r})
\to0
\quad\text{in }L^2.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

This is a genuine boundary-fluctuation reduction: **all nontrivial diverging-scale fluctuations of harmonic cylinder information come from the additive conductance potential `Phi=-log(1-B)`; the electrical endpoint is negligible.** No regeneration roof is needed for this reduction.

## 8. A precise remaining CLT/spectral criterion

The reduction above identifies the exact next analytic problem instead of leaving a vague `mixing` obligation.

Let `P=K_H` act on `L^2(mu)`. Suppose the source-specific Gordin summability condition holds:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n=0}^\infty
\|P^n\overline\Phi\|_{L^2(\mu)}<\infty.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then

\[
g:=\sum_{n=0}^\infty P^n\overline\Phi
\tag{8.2}
\]

converges in `L^2(mu)` and solves

\[
\overline\Phi=g-Pg.
\tag{8.3}
\]

For the stationary harmonic chain, define

\[
D_{r+1}=g(T_{X_{r+1}})-Pg(T_{X_r}).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Then `(D_r)` is a stationary ergodic square-integrable martingale-difference sequence, and

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\overline\Phi(T_{X_r})
=\sum_{r=1}^nD_r+g(T)-g(T_{X_n}).
\tag{8.5}
\]

The stationary martingale CLT therefore gives

\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt n}
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\overline\Phi(T_{X_r})
\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_H^2),
\tag{8.6}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_H^2
=E_\mu\bigl[g^2-(Pg)^2\bigr].
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

Combining (7.8),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{I_n-nd_H}{\sqrt n}
\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_H^2)
}
\tag{8.8}
\]

under the single explicit condition (8.1). Moreover (8.1) implies absolute summability of the covariance series, so

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_H^2
=\langle\overline\Phi,\overline\Phi\rangle_\mu
+2\sum_{n\ge1}
\langle\overline\Phi,P^n\overline\Phi\rangle_\mu.
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

This section is **CONDITIONAL**: (8.1) is not yet proved for the full source family. It is, however, now the exact missing stationary-fluctuation lemma.

There is a parallel spectral formulation. If the Feynman--Kac family `mathcal K_q` from (6.2) has a simple analytic leading eigenvalue `lambda(q)` on a weighted space near `q=1`, then standard perturbation gives

\[
\boxed{
-(\log\lambda)'(1)=d_H,
\qquad
(\log\lambda)''(1)=\sigma_H^2.
}
\tag{8.10}
\]

Thus a spectral gap for the branch-to-branch renewal representation would simultaneously provide the harmonic-information CLT and the quadratic expansion of the harmonic Renyi pressure at `q=1`.
