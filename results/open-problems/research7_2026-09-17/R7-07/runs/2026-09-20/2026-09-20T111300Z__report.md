## 2. Inherited setup actually used

Let `K_H` be the one-generation harmonic-descendant kernel on surviving finite-type source trees. The previous C06 reports constructed its stationary ergodic law `mu`, equivalent to the source law conditioned on survival. On root type `i` and root escape conductance `b=B(T)`,

\[
\frac{d\mu}{dP_i}(T)=h_i(B(T)),
\qquad
h_i(b)=b g_i(b),
\qquad
0<A_i\le g_i(b)\le L_i<\infty.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The Stieltjes-renewal construction gives the regularity needed below: `g_i,g_i^{-1}` are `C^k([0,1])` for every finite `k` used in the argument. The inherited endpoint theorem gives

\[
E_\mu[B^{-r}]<\infty\qquad\forall r>0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a fixed source-conditioned root mixture `nu`, the previous run established

\[
\ell:=\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}\le C B^{-1},
\qquad
\ell\in L^p(\mu)\quad\forall p<\infty.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Write

\[
\phi(b):=-\log(1-b)>0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The exact electrical information coboundary is

\[
I_n:=-\log\theta_T(X_n)
=
S_n+\log B_0-\log B_n,
\qquad
S_n:=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\phi(B_r).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Under stationary initialization, reversing a finite path block does not change the sum `S_n`. The projected stationary time-reversal on `(type,B)` is the Markov kernel `widehat Q` with Doob conjugacy

\[
\widehat Q=M_{g^{-1}}\,\mathcal T\,M_g,
\tag{2.6}
\]

where

\[
(\mathcal T f)_j(b)
=
\sum_iM_{ij}E\left[
\frac{f_i(F(b+R_i))}{1+b+R_i}
\right],
\qquad
F(x)=\frac{x}{1+x}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

The sole-infinite-child boundary is the extinction Jacobian

\[
J_{ij}=M_{ij}P(R_i=0),
\qquad
\rho(J)<1.
\tag{2.8}

The previous spectral report used high derivatives of (2.7) to prove a `C^k` spectral gap at zero tilt. The previous pressure report used analytic perturbation only near zero. The new step is that the same high-derivative mechanism works for **every negative tilt** and, because negative tilts are sub-Markov, yields a strict global contraction.

## 3. New theorem — strict spectral contraction for every negative electrical tilt

For real `t<0`, define the next-state reverse Feynman--Kac operator

\[
\widehat Q_t f:=\widehat Q(e^{t\phi}f).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The inherited electrical cancellation gives

\[
(\widehat Q_tf)_j(b)
=
\frac1{g_j(b)}
\sum_iM_{ij}E\left[
(1+b+R_i)^{t-1}
 g_i(F(b+R_i))
 f_i(F(b+R_i))
\right].
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `mathcal T_t` denote the unconjugated operator in (3.2), so `widehat Q_t=M_{g^{-1}} mathcal T_t M_g`.

### Theorem 3.1

For every fixed real `t<0`, there exist a finite integer `k=k(t)`, constants `C_t<infinity` and `0<rho_t<1`, such that

\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat Q_t^n f\|_{C^k}
\le C_t\rho_t^n\|f\|_{C^k}
\qquad(n\ge0).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
r(\widehat Q_t:C^k\to C^k)<1
\qquad\text{for every }t<0.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

Put

\[
(\mathcal R_t f)(x):=(1+x)^{t-1}f(F(x)).
\]

After `k` derivatives, the coefficient multiplying the highest derivative of `f` is exactly

\[
(1+x)^{t-1-2k}f^{(k)}(F(x));
\tag{3.5}
\]

all remaining terms contain only derivatives of order `<k` with coefficients bounded on `[0,infinity)` for fixed `t<0`. Therefore the top-derivative type matrix is

\[
A_t^{(k)}{}_{ij}
=
M_{ij}E[(1+R_i)^{t-1-2k}].
\tag{3.6}
\]

For `R_i=0` the scalar factor equals one; for every `R_i>0` it tends to zero as `k->infinity`. Dominated convergence gives

\[
A_t^{(k)}{}_{ij}\longrightarrow M_{ij}P(R_i=0)=J_{ij}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Since `rho(J)<1`, choose finite `k` with `rho(A_t^{(k)})<1`. The same weighted top-derivative norm and Landau--Kolmogorov interpolation used in the zero-tilt proof then give a Doeblin--Fortet estimate

\[
|\mathcal T_tf|_{k,w}
\le\alpha_t|f|_{k,w}+C_t\|f\|_\infty,
\qquad \alpha_t<1.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The weak powers are uniformly bounded for a stronger reason than at zero tilt. Because `phi>0` on every surviving next state and `t<0`,

\[
0<e^{t\phi}<1,
\]

so `widehat Q_t` is strictly sub-Markov pointwise and

\[
\|\widehat Q_t^n\|_{C^0\to C^0}\le1.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Conjugacy by bounded positive `g,g^{-1}` gives uniform `C^0` power bounds for `mathcal T_t`. The compact embedding `C^k -> C^0` plus (3.8)--(3.9) therefore gives quasi-compactness with essential spectral radius `<1`.

It remains to exclude spectral radius one. Suppose `r(widehat Q_t)=1`. Positivity and quasi-compactness then give a nonzero continuous nonnegative eigenfunction `f` with

\[
\widehat Q_tf=f.
\]

Choose a state `x` where `f` attains its positive maximum `M`. Then

\[
M=(\widehat Q_tf)(x)
\le M(\widehat Q_t1)(x).
\]

But every successor is surviving and has `phi>0`, hence

\[
(\widehat Q_t1)(x)
=E_x[e^{t\phi(Y_1)}]<1,
\]

a contradiction. Thus `r(widehat Q_t)<1`. Quasi-compact spectral decomposition now yields (3.3). `square`

For every `a>0`, under the stationary harmonic-ray law,

## 4. Every source harmonic Rényi moment with q>1 decays exponentially

For a fixed surviving tree let

\[
Z_n(q;T):=\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)^q.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Sampling one harmonic endpoint gives the exact identity

\[
Z_n(q;T)
=E_H^T[e^{-(q-1)I_n}].
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1

For every fixed `q>1` there exist deterministic constants `C_q<infinity` and `c_q>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
E_\nu Z_n(q;T)\le C_qe^{-c_qn}
\qquad(n\ge0).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Write `a=q-1>0`. From (2.5),

\[
e^{-aI_n}
=e^{-aS_n}\left(\frac{B_n}{B_0}\right)^a
\le B_0^{-a}e^{-aS_n}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Using `dnu/dmu=ell<=CB_0^{-1}`,

\[
E_\nu Z_n(q;T)
\le C E_\mu[B_0^{-(a+1)}e^{-aS_n}].
\tag{4.5}
\]

Choose Holder conjugates `p,s>1`. All inverse moments (2.2) give

\[
\|B^{-(a+1)}\|_{L^p(\mu)}<\infty,
\]

while Corollary 3.2 at the negative tilt `-as` gives

\[
E_\mu e^{-asS_n}\le C_{a,s}e^{-c_{a,s}n}.
\]

Therefore

\[
E_\nu Z_n(q;T)
\le
C\|B^{-(a+1)}\|_p
(E_\mu e^{-asS_n})^{1/s}
\le C_qe^{-c_qn}.
\]
This proves (4.3). `square`

### Corollary 4.2 — source-a.s. fixed-tree exponential q-mass decay

For every fixed `q>1`, there is a deterministic `gamma_q>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
Z_n(q;T)\le e^{-\gamma_q n}
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n,
\qquad \nu\text{-a.s.}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Choose `0<gamma_q<c_q` in (4.3). Markov gives

\[
\nu(Z_n(q;T)>e^{-\gamma_qn})
\le C_qe^{-(c_q-\gamma_q)n},
\]

which is summable; Borel--Cantelli proves (4.6).

This does **not** identify the exact quenched pressure at that `q`, but it rules out a zero-decay phase anywhere on the harmonic side `q>1`.

## 5. New two-spine theorem — quenched exponential common-prefix lifetime

Let two harmonic rays be sampled independently conditional on a fixed surviving tree `T`, and let `tau>=1` be their common-prefix lifetime. Earlier R7-07 work proved

\[
P_T(\tau>n)=\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)^2=Z_n(2;T).
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1

There is a deterministic `gamma_2>0` such that for `nu`-almost every surviving source tree,

\[
\boxed{
P_T(\tau>n)\le e^{-\gamma_2 n}
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently, for every deterministic `0<a<gamma_2`,

\[
\boxed{
E_T e^{a\tau}<\infty
\qquad\text{for }\nu\text{-a.e. surviving }T.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Moreover the annealed exponential moment is finite for a possibly smaller deterministic `a>0`:

\[
\boxed{
E_\nu E_T e^{a\tau}<\infty.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (5.2) is Corollary 4.2 at `q=2`. For an integer-valued positive lifetime,

\[
E_Te^{a\tau}
=1+(e^a-1)\sum_{n\ge0}e^{an}P_T(\tau>n),
\tag{5.5}
\]

so (5.2) implies (5.3). For (5.4), use the annealed bound (4.3) at `q=2` directly in (5.5). `square`

## 6. Shared-prefix information has finite moments; the remaining variance obstruction is post-split

The preceding theorem is strong enough to rule out the common prefix as the source of an order-`n` two-spine covariance.

Let, under the stationary two-ray law,

\[
C_\tau:=\sum_{r=0}^{\tau-1}\phi(B_r).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The source assumption `E nu<infinity` already gives every finite polynomial moment of `phi`, because

\[
0\le\phi(B)=\log(1+S)\le\log(1+\nu)
\]

and for each finite `m`, `log^m(1+x)<=C_m(1+x)`. The stationary density is bounded above relative to the source, so

\[
E_\mu[\phi(B)^m]<\infty\qquad\forall m<\infty.
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Lemma 6.1

For every finite integer `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{E_\mu^{(2)}[C_\tau^m]<\infty.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The same conclusion holds under source-conditioned initialization `nu`.

#### Proof sketch with the quantitative summability exposed

Choose a small `alpha>0`. Weighted Holder gives

\[
C_\tau^m
\le C_{m,\alpha}
\sum_{r\ge0}e^{\alpha mr}
1_{\{\tau>r\}}\phi(B_r)^m.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Under the stationary pair law, the first ray has stationary marginal `mu`. By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
E[1_{\{\tau>r\}}\phi(B_r)^m]
\le
P(\tau>r)^{1/2}
(E_\mu\phi^{2m})^{1/2}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The annealed version of Theorem 5.1 gives `P(tau>r)<=Ce^{-cr}`. Choose `alpha m<c/2`; (6.4)--(6.5) are summable in `r`, proving (6.3). To pass from `mu` to `nu`, multiply by the root density `ell`; since `ell in L^p(mu)` for every finite `p`, another Holder step and the same argument at a slightly higher moment give the result. `square`

Thus the entire additive electrical information accumulated before the two rays split has finite `L^2` cost (indeed all finite moments). In the proposed two-spine proof of

\[
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_H(I_n\mid T)\to\sigma_H^2,
\tag{6.6}
\]

long common pipes are no longer a candidate source of an order-`n` error. The genuinely remaining issue is the dependence of the **two selected child environments at the split branch**, induced by the common sibling normalization, and whether that dependence is killed exponentially by the already proved one-spine equilibration after the split.

This is a sharper localization than the previous report's generic “prove the split-time tail is summable” target: the split-time tail is now exponentially summable.
