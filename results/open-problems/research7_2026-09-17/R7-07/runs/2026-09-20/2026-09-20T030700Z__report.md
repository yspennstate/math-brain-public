## 3. New endpoint theorem — exponential integrability of `log(B/mathcal W)`

### Theorem 3.1

Assume (2.4)--(2.5) and `E_i mathcal W<infinity`. For every

\[
0<\theta<\min\{\alpha,1\},
\]

and every source type `i`,

\[
\boxed{
E_i\left[e^{\theta|X|};\,B>0\right]<\infty.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently the candidate stationary harmonic law also satisfies

\[
\boxed{
E_\mu e^{\theta|X|}<\infty
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

for the same range of `theta`, and hence

\[
\mu(|X|>K)\le C_\theta e^{-\theta K}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

Split into positive and negative parts.

If `X>=0`, then `B<=1` gives

\[
e^{\theta X}=
\left(\frac B{\mathcal W}\right)^\theta
\le \mathcal W^{-\theta}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The polynomial lower tail (2.5) implies

\[
E_i[\mathcal W^{-\theta};\mathcal W>0]<\infty
\qquad(0<\theta<\alpha).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Indeed the tail integral for `mathcal W^{-theta}` is dominated for large `t` by `Ct^{-alpha}`.

If `X<0`, then

\[
e^{-\theta X}
=
\left(\frac{\mathcal W}{B}\right)^\theta.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Choose `p>1` so that `theta p<=1`, possible because `theta<1`, and let `q=p/(p-1)`. Holder gives

\[
E_i\left[\mathcal W^\theta B^{-\theta};B>0\right]
\le
\left(E_i\mathcal W^{\theta p}\right)^{1/p}
\left(E_i[B^{-\theta q};B>0]\right)^{1/q}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The first factor is finite because `theta p<=1` and `E_i mathcal W<infinity`; the second is finite by (2.4). This proves (3.1).

For the stationary law, (2.6) gives `h_i(B)<=L_iB<=L_i`, so

\[
E_\mu e^{\theta|X|}
=\sum_iE_i[h_i(B)e^{\theta|X|}]
\le
\sum_iL_iE_i[e^{\theta|X|};B>0]<\infty.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Markov's inequality gives (3.3). `square`

### Why this matters

The PF--KL proof needs only logarithmic integrability. Theorem 3.1 gives a genuine exponential moment for the local electrical/PF discrepancy `X=log(B/mathcal W)`. It therefore supplies quantitative tail control for truncations used in entropy and future fluctuation arguments, rather than merely proving that the entropy integral is finite.

## 4. A sharper local KL lower bound requiring control of only two children

For one realised branch let

\[
X_u=\log\frac{B_u}{\mathcal W_u},
\qquad
U_u=\frac{\mathcal W_u}{\sum_v\mathcal W_v}.
\]

As in the preceding report define

\[
Z_t=\sum_uU_ue^{tX_u},
\qquad
Q_t(u)=\frac{U_ue^{tX_u}}{Z_t},
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The exact information-geometric identity is

\[
D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T)
=
\int_0^1t\,\operatorname{Var}_{Q_t}(X_u)\,dt.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The previous local bound controlled `|X_w|` for every surviving sibling solely to bound `Z_t`. That can be replaced by an exact endpoint identity.

### Lemma 4.1 — endpoint control of the tilt normalizer

For `0<=t<=1`,

\[
\boxed{Z_t\le Z_1^t.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
Z_1
=
\frac{\sum_uB_u}{\sum_u\mathcal W_u}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Equation (4.3) is Holder (equivalently log-convexity of `t -> log E_U e^{tX}`): since `Z_0=1`,

\[
Z_t=E_U[e^{tX}]\le(E_Ue^X)^t=Z_1^t.
\]

Equation (4.4) follows from `U_ue^{X_u}=B_u/(sum_v mathcal W_v)`. `square`

### Lemma 4.2 — two-child certificate

Suppose a branch has `nu=n` potential children and two surviving children `u!=v` such that

\[
\mathcal W_u,\mathcal W_v\ge m,
\qquad
\sum_w\mathcal W_w\le M,
\tag{4.5}
\]

\[
X_u,X_v\ge-K,
\qquad
|X_u-X_v|\ge\delta.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Set

\[
\eta:=\frac mM,
\qquad
\Lambda:=\max\left\{1,\frac n{2m}\right\}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T)
\ge
\frac12
\eta^2e^{-2K}\Lambda^{-2}\delta^2.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

### Proof

From (4.5),

\[
U_u,U_v\ge\eta.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Also every branch conductance is at most one, so

\[
\sum_wB_w\le n.
\]

The two selected children give `sum_w mathcal W_w>=2m`; hence (4.4) yields

\[
Z_1\le\frac n{2m}\le\Lambda.
\tag{4.10}
\]

By (4.3), `Z_t<=Lambda` for every `t in [0,1]`. Since `X_u,X_v>=-K`,

\[
Q_t(u),Q_t(v)
\ge
\eta e^{-K}\Lambda^{-1}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Using

\[
\operatorname{Var}_q(X)
=
\frac12\sum_{r,s}q_rq_s(X_r-X_s)^2,
\]

the ordered pair `(u,v),(v,u)` contributes at least

\[
\eta^2e^{-2K}\Lambda^{-2}\delta^2.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Integrating `t` in (4.2) gives the factor `int_0^1 t dt=1/2`, proving (4.8). `square`

### Improvement over the previous certificate

No upper or lower bound on `X_w` is required for any sibling other than the two witnesses. Large conductance/PF-ratio outliers among the remaining children cannot destroy the estimate because the entire normalizer is controlled by the endpoint ratio (4.4).

## 6. A finite-box source certificate with an explicit probability formula

The preceding result becomes genuinely finite-source data after fixing two potential-child slots.

Fix a parent type `i`, an integer `n>=2` with `P(nu=n)>0`, child types `j,k`, and Borel sets `mathcal A_j,mathcal B_k` of child subtree states satisfying, for fixed `m,M_0,K,delta>0`,

\[
T\in\mathcal A_j\cup\mathcal B_k
\Longrightarrow
m\le\mathcal W(T)\le M_0,
\quad
X(T)\ge-K,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
|X(T)-X(T')|\ge\delta
\qquad
(T\in\mathcal A_j,\ T'\in\mathcal B_k).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let

\[
r_j=P_j(\mathcal A_j),
\qquad
r_k=P_k(\mathcal B_k),
\tag{6.3}
\]

and define the one-slot control probability

\[
d_i(M_0)
:=
1-\sum_\ell q_\ell p_{i\ell}
P_\ell(\mathcal W>M_0).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus a remaining potential-child slot is acceptable iff it is absent/nontransmitted/extinct or its transmitted subtree has `mathcal W<=M_0`.

Consider the event `E_box` that

1. `nu=n`;
2. slot 1 transmits a type-`j` child with subtree in `mathcal A_j`;
3. slot 2 transmits a type-`k` child with subtree in `mathcal B_k`;
4. each remaining slot is acceptable in the sense of (6.4).

Branching independence gives the exact product lower event probability

\[
\boxed{
P_i(E_{box})
=
P(\nu=n)
(q_jp_{ij}r_j)
(q_kp_{ik}r_k)
[d_i(M_0)]^{n-2}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

On `E_box`,

\[
\sum_w\mathcal W_w\le nM_0,
\]
so Lemma 4.2 applies with

\[
M=nM_0,
\qquad
\eta=\frac m{nM_0},
\qquad
\Lambda=\max\left\{1,\frac n{2m}\right\}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Combining (5.3)--(6.6) yields the finite-box certificate

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\log\rho-\dim_H(\mathrm{HARM})
\;&\ge
\frac12
\left(\frac m{nM_0}\right)^2
 e^{-2K}
\Lambda^{-2}\delta^2
\,A_i
\frac{2me^{-K}}{1+2me^{-K}}
\\
&\qquad\times
P(\nu=n)
(q_jp_{ij}r_j)
(q_kp_{ik}r_k)
[d_i(M_0)]^{n-2},
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

whenever the inherited PF--KL identity is invoked.

Every factor in (6.7) is either a finite source parameter, one of the canonical stationary-density constants `A_i`, or a one-child probability of a bounded box in the pair `(mathcal W,X)`. No infinite-generation sibling event appears in the certificate.

## 7. Every nonregular source admits some finite-box witness

The usefulness of (6.7) would be limited if such boxes had to be guessed. R14's rigidity theorem guarantees their existence outside the equality class.

### Theorem 7.1 — finite-witness exhaustion of nonregularity

Assume the source is not the deterministic regular no-thinning case. Then there exist

\[
i,n,j,k,m,M_0,K,\delta,
\mathcal A_j,\mathcal B_k
\]

as in Section 6 such that

\[
r_j>0,\qquad r_k>0,\qquad d_i(M_0)>0,
\]

and hence the right side of (6.7) is strictly positive.

### Proof

R14's rigidity theorem says that for a nonregular source

\[
P_i(H_T\ne U_T)>0
\tag{7.1}
\]

for at least one parent type `i`.

On every finite branch, `H_T=U_T` iff the ratios `B_u/mathcal W_u` are equal across all surviving children, equivalently iff the `X_u` are all equal. Hence (7.1) implies positive probability of a branch with two surviving children `u!=v` satisfying

\[
X_u\ne X_v.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Decompose this event according to the finite value `nu=n`, an ordered pair of potential-child slots, and their types `(j,k)`. At least one such component has positive probability. Conditional on those transmitted types, the two child subtrees are independent with laws `P_j` and `P_k`.

The set `{(T,T'):X(T)ne X(T')}` is a countable union of products of bounded rational boxes separated by a positive rational gap. Because `mathcal W` is positive and finite on survival, one can simultaneously intersect with

\[
m\le\mathcal W\le M_0,
\qquad
X\ge-K,
\]

for rational `m>0`, finite `M_0,K`, without exhausting all of the positive mass. Thus some separated boxes `mathcal A_j,mathcal B_k` have `r_jr_k>0` and some `delta>0`.

Finally, enlarge `M_0` if needed. Since `mathcal W<infinity` almost surely and the type set is finite,

\[
d_i(M_0)\uparrow1
\qquad(M_0\to\infty),
\]

so `d_i(M_0)>0` for all sufficiently large `M_0`. The fixed-slot event `E_box` then has strictly positive probability by (6.5), and (6.7) is strictly positive. `square`

### Interpretation

This theorem upgrades the qualitative statement “the KL term is positive somewhere” to a **finite witness principle**: every nonregular source contains a finite-degree, two-child, bounded-box event that certifies a positive dimension deficit quantitatively. The actual constant may be very small, but it is not hidden in an infinite stationary expectation.
