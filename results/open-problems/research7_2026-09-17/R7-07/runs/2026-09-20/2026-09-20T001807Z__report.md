## 2. Source finite-type branching model

Work away from the exceptional root generation, which cannot change a boundary dimension. Let the finite type set be `D={1,...,d}`. A type-`i` parent has a common potential-child count `nu` with pgf

\[
G(z)=E[z^\nu],\qquad m_*=G'(1)<\infty.
\]

Each potential slot independently receives type `j` with probability `q_j>0`, transmits with probability `p_{ij}>0`, and, when transmitted, carries an independent forward tree with law `P_j`. The mean matrix is

\[
M_{ij}=m_*q_jp_{ij},
\qquad \rho:=\rho(M)>1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The source finite-variance assumptions imply the Kesten--Stigum hypotheses used by R14. Let `s_i=P_i(T survives)>0` and let

\[
t_i=\sum_jq_jp_{ij}s_j.
\tag{2.2}
\]

R14 proved for the extinction Jacobian

\[
J_{ij}=G'(1-t_i)q_jp_{ij}
\tag{2.3}
\]

that

\[
\rho(J)<1.
\tag{2.4}
\]

For a rooted forward tree `T`, attach one artificial parent to the root and put

\[
B(T)=P_o^T(\text{never hit the artificial parent}).
\]

If `T_u` are the transmitted child subtrees and `B_u=B(T_u)`, define

\[
C(T):=\sum_uB_u.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Then the exact electrical recursion is

\[
B(T)=\frac{C(T)}{1+C(T)},
\tag{2.6}
\]

and, on survival, the one-generation harmonic descendant kernel is

\[
(K_Hf)(T)=\sum_u\frac{B_u}{C(T)}f(T_u).

b_i:=E_{P_i}B(T)>0.

## 3. Palm sibling law

For a parent of type `i`, distinguish one potential child slot by Campbell's formula. Let `nu^*` have the size-biased law

\[
P(\nu^*=n)=\frac{nP(\nu=n)}{m_*}.
\]

Given `nu^*`, let the other `nu^*-1` slots be generated by the source rule. Let

\[
R_i=\sum_{\text{other transmitted slots }v}B(T_v)
\tag{3.1}
\]

be their total branch conductance. Then for every nonnegative measurable `Phi`,

\[
E_i\sum_{u:\tau(u)=j}\Phi(T_u,R_u)
=M_{ij}\,E[\Phi(T_j,R_i)],
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `T_j~P_j` is independent of `R_i`.

The probability that the Palm siblings contain no surviving child is

\[
P(R_i=0)
=\frac{G'(1-t_i)}{m_*}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This identity is the exact bridge from the compactification boundary below to the already-certified Jacobian `J`.

## 4. Backward conductance and the harmonic function kappa

A number `a in [0,1]` will represent the effective conductance, seen from the current root, of the entire **backward** side of a two-sided tree after the parent edge is put in series. Define

\[
F(x)=\frac{x}{1+x},\qquad x\ge0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

If the harmonic ray chooses a child while the siblings have total conductance `R`, then from the chosen child the new backward conductance is exactly

\[
a'=F(a+R).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For type `i` define

\[
\kappa_i(a)
:=E_{P_i}\left[\frac{aC(T)}{a+C(T)}\right],
\qquad 0\le a\le1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For `a>0`, `kappa_i(a)>0`. Also

\[
\frac{\kappa_i(a)}a\longrightarrow s_i
\qquad(a\downarrow0),
\tag{4.4}
\]

by dominated convergence, because `C/(a+C) -> 1_{C>0}`. Since `a<=1`,

\[
\boxed{a b_i\le\kappa_i(a)\le a.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed `C/(a+C)>=C/(1+C)=B` and `C/(a+C)<=1`.

## 5. A positive transfer kernel and an exact algebraic identity

For `a>0` define a positive kernel on type/backward-conductance states by

\[
K_{ij}(a,da')
:=M_{ij}\,E\left[
\frac{a}{a+R_i}
\,\mathbf1_{\{F(a+R_i)\in da'\}}
\right].
\tag{5.1}
\]

The key identity is

\[
\boxed{\kappa_i(a)=\sum_j\int K_{ij}(a,da')\,\kappa_j(a').}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Let a distinguished type-`j` child have forward total conductance `c=C(T_j)` and branch conductance

\[
b=B(T_j)=\frac c{1+c}.
\]

Put `x=a+R_i` and `a'=F(x)=x/(1+x)`. Direct algebra gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{a}{a+R_i}\,
\frac{a'c}{a'+c}
=
\frac{ab}{a+R_i+b}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For a realised parent tree with child branch conductances `(B_u)`, summing the right side over distinguished children gives

\[
\sum_u\frac{aB_u}{a+\sum_vB_v}
=
\frac{aC(T)}{a+C(T)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Take expectation and apply the Palm identity (3.2). This is exactly (5.2). `square`

Equation (5.2) is the finite-type analogue of the backward/forward conductance balance behind the explicit one-type harmonic-stationary measures of Lin and Rousselin.

## 6. Doob transform on a compact conductance state space

For `a>0`, define

\[
P^\kappa((i,a),d(j,a'))
=
\frac{K_{ij}(a,da')\kappa_j(a')}{\kappa_i(a)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

By (5.2), this is a Markov kernel.

### Lemma 6.1 — Feller extension to `a=0`

`P^kappa` extends to a Feller Markov kernel on the compact state space

\[
\bar E=D\times[0,1].
\]

For bounded continuous `phi`, divide the numerator of `P^kappa phi(i,a)` by `a`:

\[
\sum_jM_{ij}E\left[
\frac{\kappa_j(F(a+R_i))}{a+R_i}
\phi(j,F(a+R_i))
\right].
\tag{6.2}
\]

The integrand is bounded because `kappa_j(x)<=x` and `F(y)<=y`. If `R_i>0`, its limit is

\[
\frac{\kappa_j(F(R_i))}{R_i}\phi(j,F(R_i)).
\]

If `R_i=0`, its limit is `s_j phi(j,0)` by (4.4) and `F(a)/a->1`. The denominator satisfies `kappa_i(a)/a->s_i`. Dominated convergence therefore gives a continuous boundary value. Continuity away from zero is immediate from dominated convergence. `square`

### Lemma 6.2 — the only boundary-to-boundary channel is the extinction Jacobian

At `a=0`, the part of the extended kernel which stays at `a'=0` is the substochastic type matrix

\[
Q^0_{ij}
=
\frac{M_{ij}P(R_i=0)s_j}{s_i}
=
\frac{s_j}{s_i}G'(1-t_i)q_jp_{ij}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{Q^0=D_s^{-1}JD_s,\qquad \rho(Q^0)=\rho(J)<1.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The remaining boundary mass enters the interior `a'>0` through events `R_i>0`. From every interior state, `a'=F(a+R_i)>0`, so the interior never maps to the boundary.

## 7. Existence of an interior invariant law for the backward process

Because `P^kappa` is Feller on compact `bar E`, Krylov--Bogoliubov gives an invariant probability `pi`.

Let `beta_i=pi({i}x{0})`. Since the interior has no transition to zero, stationarity gives

\[
\beta=\beta Q^0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

But `rho(Q^0)<1`, hence the only finite nonnegative solution is

\[
\boxed{\beta=0.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Therefore every invariant probability of this form is supported on `D x (0,1]`.

Moreover, every type has positive `pi`-mass. Indeed `M_{ij}>0` for all `i,j`, and from every interior `(i,a)` the integrand in (6.1) for each target type `j` is strictly positive because `kappa_j(F(a+R_i))>0`. Invariance then forces

\[
\pi_j((0,1])>0\qquad\forall j.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Define the sigma-finite measures on `(0,1]`

\[
\lambda_j(da)=\frac{\pi_j(da)}{\kappa_j(a)}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Stationarity of `pi` under the h-transform is equivalent to the linear invariance relation

\[
\boxed{\lambda K=\lambda.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

## 8. Explicit stationary harmonic-descendant law

For a type-`j` forward tree with conductance `C=C(T)`, define

\[
H_j(C)
:=\int_{(0,1]}
\lambda_j(da)\frac{aC}{a+C}
=
\int_{(0,1]}
\pi_j(da)
\frac{aC}{(a+C)\kappa_j(a)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

By (4.5),

\[
0\le H_j(C)\le\frac{\pi_j((0,1])}{b_j}\le\frac1{b_j}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For `C>0`, (7.3) makes `H_j(C)>0`; for `C=0`, `H_j(C)=0`.

Define a measure on typed forward trees by

\[
\mu(j,dT)=H_j(C(T))P_j(dT).
\tag{8.3}
\]

It is automatically normalized:

\[
\sum_j\int H_j(C(T))P_j(dT)
=
\sum_j\int\lambda_j(da)\kappa_j(a)
=
\sum_j\pi_j((0,1])=1.
\tag{8.4}
\]

### Theorem 8.1 — stationarity

`mu` is stationary for the harmonic-descendant kernel `K_H`.

### Proof

For bounded measurable `f`, use (8.1), then cancel `C` against the harmonic split:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu K_H f
&=\sum_iE_{P_i}\left[
H_i(C)\sum_u\frac{B_u}{C}f(T_u)
\right]\\
&=\sum_i\int\lambda_i(da)E_{P_i}\left[
\sum_u\frac{aB_u}{a+C}f(T_u)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Apply the Palm identity (3.2). For a distinguished child write `c=C(T_j)`, `b=c/(1+c)`, `R=R_i`, and `a'=F(a+R)`. The algebraic identity (5.3) yields

\[
\frac{ab}{a+R+b}
=
\frac{a}{a+R}\frac{a'c}{a'+c}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu K_H f
&=\sum_{i,j}\int\lambda_i(da)
\int K_{ij}(a,da')
E_{P_j}\left[\frac{a'C(T)}{a'+C(T)}f(T)\right]\\
&=\sum_j\int(\lambda K)_j(da')
E_{P_j}\left[\frac{a'C(T)}{a'+C(T)}f(T)\right]\\
&=\mu f
\end{aligned}
\]

by (7.5). `square`

### Corollary 8.2 — equivalence and a bounded density

For every type `j`, `mu_j` is equivalent to `P_j` conditioned on survival. More strongly, its Radon--Nikodym density with respect to the unconditioned `P_j` is bounded above by `1/b_j` and is strictly positive exactly on survival.

Thus `mu` is equivalent to any full-support finite type mixture of the source survival-conditioned laws. This is precisely the equivalence obligation that was left open in the R14 PF--KL reduction.

## 9. Ergodicity of the harmonic stationary law

### Theorem 9.1 — ergodicity

`mu` is ergodic for `K_H`.

### Proof

Let `A` be a `K_H`-invariant measurable set. Then

\[
1_A(T)=\sum_u\frac{B_u}{C(T)}1_A(T_u)
\tag{9.1}
\]

for `mu`-a.e. surviving tree. All positive-conductance harmonic weights are strictly positive, so (9.1) implies that **every surviving child has the same `A`-indicator as its parent**. By Corollary 8.2, this holds source-a.s. on survival for every root type.

For type `j`, let

\[
a_j=P_j(A\mid\text{survival}).
\]

Since `rho(M)>1` and each row sum of `M` is at most `m_*`, necessarily `m_*>1`, so `P(\nu\ge2)>0`. Because `q_jp_{ij}s_j>0`, for any parent type `i` there is positive probability of two surviving transmitted type-`j` children. Conditional on that event, the two descendant trees are independent copies of `P_j(.|survival)`, while (9.1) forces their `A`-indicators to agree almost surely. Hence

\[
2a_j(1-a_j)=0,
\]

so `a_j` is `0` or `1`.

Likewise, a positive-probability event containing one surviving type-`j` child and one surviving type-`k` child forces `a_j=a_k`. Positivity of all `q_jp_{ij}s_j` therefore makes all `a_j` equal to one common value in `{0,1}`. Equivalence then gives `mu(A) in {0,1}`. `square`

This supplies the missing **equivalent stationary ergodic harmonic-descendant environment** without requiring a finite regeneration roof.

## 10. Endpoint integrability: a new polynomial lower tail for the PF martingale

R14's PF--KL theorem also requires logarithmic integrability of the Perron martingale limit. Let `r` be a positive right Perron vector and

\[
\mathcal W(T)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho^{-n}
\sum_{|v|=n}r_{\tau(v)}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Under the source finite-variance/Kesten--Stigum assumptions,

\[
E_i\mathcal W=r_i,
\qquad
\mathcal W>0\ \text{iff survival},
\tag{10.2}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal W(T)=\rho^{-1}\sum_u\mathcal W(T_u).
\tag{10.3}
\]

Define

\[
F_i^W(x)=P_i(0<\mathcal W\le x).
\]

If `0<W<=x`, then every positive child martingale is at most `rho x`, and at least one is positive. Therefore, with

\[
a_i^W(y)=\sum_jq_jp_{ij}F_j^W(y),
\]

\[
F_i^W(x)
\le
G(1-t_i+a_i^W(\rho x))-G(1-t_i).
\tag{10.4}
\]

Linearizing at `1-t_i` gives the same Jacobian `J`. Choose `gamma` with

\[
\rho(J)<\gamma<1
\]

and a positive weight vector `w` such that `Jw<=gamma_0w` for some `gamma_0<gamma`. Exactly as in the earlier conductance lower-tail proof, the quadratic Taylor remainder is absorbed for small `x`, yielding

\[
f(x)\le\gamma f(\rho x),
\qquad
f(x)=\max_iF_i^W(x)/w_i.
\tag{10.5}
\]

Iterate until `rho^n x` reaches a fixed small scale `x_0`. With

\[
\alpha=\frac{-\log\gamma}{\log\rho}>0,
\]

there is `C_W<infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{P_i(0<\mathcal W\le x)\le C_Wx^\alpha}
\tag{10.6}
\]

for every type and sufficiently small `x`.

Consequently

\[
E_i[|\log\mathcal W|;\mathcal W>0]<\infty.
\tag{10.7}
\]

For the negative part, integrate (10.6) at `x=e^{-t}`; for the positive part use `log^+ W<=W` and (10.2).

Because the density (8.2) is bounded typewise, (10.7) transfers immediately to `mu`:

\[
\boxed{E_\mu|\log\mathcal W|<\infty.}
\tag{10.8}
\]

The local harmonic entropy is also integrable:

\[
0\le -\sum_up_u\log p_u
\le\log N_+\le\log(1+\nu),
\tag{10.9}
\]

and `E log(1+nu)<infinity` follows already from `E nu<infinity`. By stationarity,

\[
E_\mu\sum_up_u|\log\mathcal W(T_u)|
=E_\mu|\log\mathcal W(T)|<\infty.
\tag{10.10}
\]

Thus the cross entropy against the PF split is integrable, and so is the one-step KL divergence required by R14.

## 12. Why the proof is not circular

The construction does not assume the stationary law it seeks. The logical order is:

1. source branching independence gives the Palm sibling kernel;
2. electrical series/parallel algebra gives the exact harmonic function `kappa` for a positive transfer kernel;
3. compact Feller theory gives an invariant law for the auxiliary backward-conductance Doob transform;
4. the already-certified strict boundary contraction `rho(J)<1` excludes invariant mass at the degenerate boundary `a=0`;
5. un-Doob-transforming that invariant law gives an explicit bounded density `H_j(C)` for a stationary forward-tree law;
6. source branching independence plus positivity proves ergodicity;
7. the same extinction Jacobian gives the missing lower-tail/log-integrability bound for `W`;
8. only then is the inherited PF--KL theorem invoked.

In particular, the earlier two-spine condition `E tau^2<infinity` is **not** used. That condition remains useful for a regeneration construction and for possible stronger fluctuation theorems, but it is no longer necessary for the one-number dimension-drop theorem.
