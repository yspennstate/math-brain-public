## 2. Inherited harmonic-descendant Markov chain

Let `K=K_H` be the harmonic-descendant kernel on surviving rooted environments,

`K f(T) = sum_u H_T(u) f(T_u)`,

where `H_T(u)` is the harmonic probability of choosing surviving child `u`. Let `mu` be the canonical stationary ergodic law constructed in the preceding C06 reports. The original source law conditioned on survival, denoted `nu`, is equivalent to `mu`.

For a harmonic ray `X_0=o,X_1,...`, write the descendant environment at level `k` as

`Y_k = T_{X_k}`.

Then under harmonic measure in a fixed tree `T`, `(Y_k)` is exactly the time-homogeneous Markov chain with kernel `K` started from `Y_0=T`.

Let

`B(T)=P_o^T(tau_partial=infinity)`

be the escape conductance and

`phi(T) = -log(1-B(T))`,

`d_H = E_mu phi`.

The exact electrical information coboundary from earlier runs is

`I_n := -log theta_T(X_n)`

`I_n - n d_H`
` = sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (phi(Y_k)-d_H)`
`   + log B(Y_0)-log B(Y_n)`.                                      (2.1)

The previous two-spine theorem gives, for every centered full-tree observable with a moment above two, exponential `L^2(mu)` decay under `K`. In particular the forward Poisson series

`u = sum_{m>=0} K^m (phi-d_H)`                                      (2.2)

converges in every finite `L^p(mu)`. Hence

`phi-d_H = u-Ku`.                                                   (2.3)

The previous Shannon corrector can be written, up to its harmless centering convention, as

`G = u + log B - E_mu log B`,

so the inherited all-finite-moment estimates for `G` and `log B` imply

`u in intersection_{p<infinity} L^p(mu)`.                           (2.4)

## 3. Forward martingale decomposition valid for every fixed starting tree

Define

`D_{k+1} = u(Y_{k+1}) - K u(Y_k)`

and

`M_n = sum_{k=0}^{n-1} D_{k+1}`.

For every fixed initial environment `T`, under the harmonic-ray law `P_T`, `(M_n)` is a martingale with respect to the descendant filtration because

`E_T[D_{k+1} | Y_0,...,Y_k]=0`.

Summing (2.3) gives

`sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(phi(Y_k)-d_H)`
` = M_n + u(Y_0)-u(Y_n)`.

Combining with (2.1), define

`W(T)=u(T)+log B(T)`.

Then the harmonic information has the exact forward martingale-coboundary decomposition

`boxed{ I_n-n d_H = M_n + W(Y_0)-W(Y_n). }`                         (3.1)

By (2.4) and the inherited inverse-conductance bounds,

`W in intersection_{p<infinity} L^p(mu)`.                           (3.2)

This forward decomposition is the key new route. It avoids needing a stationary initial tree inside the final probability statement.

## 4. Predictable quadratic variation from almost every fixed tree

The one-step conditional variance of the martingale difference is

`v(T) = E_T[D_1^2]`
`     = K(u^2)(T) - (Ku(T))^2`.                                    (4.1)

Since `u in L^2(mu)`, `v in L^1(mu)`. Under the stationary path law `P_mu`, the chain is ergodic, so Birkhoff gives

`(1/n) sum_{k=0}^{n-1} v(Y_k) -> E_mu v`                            (4.2)

almost surely. Disintegrating the stationary path law over `Y_0=T` shows that there is a `mu`-full set `A_var` such that for every `T in A_var`, (4.2) holds `P_T`-almost surely.

The deterministic constant in (4.2) is exactly the previously identified harmonic-information variance `sigma_H^2`. Indeed under the stationary law, (3.1) has an `L^2` endpoint of order `O(1)`, while

`E_mu M_n^2 = n E_mu v`.

The inherited stationary variance asymptotic is

`Var_mu(I_n)=n sigma_H^2+O(1)`.

After division by `n`, the endpoint and cross terms vanish, so

`boxed{ E_mu v = sigma_H^2. }`                                     (4.3)

For every nonregular source, the inherited variance-rigidity theorem gives `sigma_H^2>0`; the unique zero-variance case is deterministic `k`-ary branching with no thinning.

## 5. Quenched martingale Lindeberg condition

Fix `delta>0`. Since `u` has all finite moments, the stationary martingale difference satisfies

`E_mu |D_1|^{2+delta}<infinity`.                                    (5.1)

For `epsilon>0`, let

`L_n = (1/n) sum_{k=0}^{n-1}`
` E[ D_{k+1}^2 1{|D_{k+1}|>epsilon sqrt(n)} | Y_0,...,Y_k ]`.

Conditional Markov inequality gives

`E[D_{k+1}^2 1{|D_{k+1}|>epsilon sqrt(n)} | Y_k]`
` <= epsilon^{-delta} n^{-delta/2}`
`    E[|D_{k+1}|^{2+delta}|Y_k]`.

Set

`a_delta(T)=E_T |D_1|^{2+delta}`.

Then `a_delta in L^1(mu)` and

`L_n <= epsilon^{-delta} n^{-delta/2}`
`       * (1/n) sum_{k<n} a_delta(Y_k)`.                            (5.2)

Birkhoff plus disintegration therefore implies that on another `mu`-full set `A_L`, for every fixed `T in A_L`,

`boxed{ L_n -> 0  P_T-a.s. }`                                      (5.3)

This verifies the martingale functional-CLT Lindeberg condition from almost every deterministic starting environment.

## 6. The endpoint is uniformly negligible on the diffusive path scale

A scalar endpoint estimate would be enough for a one-time CLT, but the functional theorem needs

`max_{0<=k<=n} |W(Y_k)|/sqrt(n) -> 0`

in `P_T`-probability for almost every fixed start `T`.

Choose any `p>4`. Stationarity and (3.2) give

`P_mu( max_{0<=k<=n}|W(Y_k)| > epsilon sqrt(n) )`
` <= (n+1) mu(|W|>epsilon sqrt(n))`
` <= C_{p,epsilon} n^{1-p/2}`.                                    (6.1)

The right side is summable in `n` because `p>4`. Define the quenched probability

`q_n(T)=P_T(max_{k<=n}|W(Y_k)|>epsilon sqrt(n))`.

Integrating over `T~mu` gives exactly the left side of (6.1), hence

`sum_n integral q_n(T) mu(dT)<infinity`.

Tonelli implies that for `mu`-almost every fixed `T`,

`sum_n q_n(T)<infinity`, and in particular

`boxed{ max_{k<=n}|W(Y_k)|/sqrt(n) -> 0 in P_T-probability. }`       (6.2)

This is where the previously proved all-finite-moment endpoint control materially strengthens the older stationary FCLT.

## 7. Main theorem — quenched invariance principle

### Theorem 7.1

Under the finite-type branching assumptions recorded in the preceding H0008 reports, there is a set `A` with

`mu(A)=1`

such that for every fixed surviving environment `T in A`, under the harmonic measure of that single tree,

`boxed{
 { (I_floor(nt)-floor(nt)d_H)/sqrt(n) : 0<=t<=1 }
   => { sigma_H W_t : 0<=t<=1 }
}`                                                                  (7.1)

in `D([0,1])`, where `W` is standard Brownian motion.

#### Proof

Take `A=A_var intersect A_L intersect A_end`, where the three full sets are provided by Sections 4--6. For `T in A`, the martingale differences satisfy:

1. predictable quadratic variation
   `(1/n)<M>_floor(nt) -> sigma_H^2 t` for each `t`, and uniformly in the standard martingale-FCLT sense by monotonicity;
2. the conditional Lindeberg condition (5.3).

Therefore the martingale functional central limit theorem gives

`M_floor(nt)/sqrt(n) => sigma_H W_t` under `P_T`.

The difference between harmonic information and the martingale is, by (3.1),

`W(Y_0)-W(Y_floor(nt))`.

Section 6 shows its supremum over `0<=t<=1`, divided by `sqrt(n)`, tends to zero in `P_T`-probability. Slutsky in `D([0,1])` yields (7.1). `square`

Because `mu` and the original source law `nu` conditioned on survival are equivalent, `A` is also `nu`-full. Hence (7.1) is a source-almost-every fixed-tree theorem, not merely a statement averaged over random trees.

## 8. Fixed-tree harmonic boundary fluctuation corollaries

At time one, Theorem 7.1 gives, for source-a.e. surviving tree `T`,

`boxed{
 H_T( (-log theta_T(X_n)-n d_H)/(sigma_H sqrt(n)) <= x )
   -> Phi(x)
}`                                                                  (8.1)

at every continuity point `x`, whenever the source is nonregular. Here `Phi` is the standard normal distribution function.

Equivalently, a harmonic-typical depth-`n` cylinder in almost every fixed surviving tree has

`-log theta_T(X_n) = n d_H + sigma_H sqrt(n) Z_n + o_{H_T}(sqrt(n))`,

with `Z_n => N(0,1)`.                                               (8.2)

The functional form says the whole information profile along a harmonic-typical ray has Brownian diffusive fluctuations after centering by its exact dimension slope.

The deterministic regular no-thinning model is the degenerate consistency check: there `sigma_H=0` and `I_n=n log k` identically.

### Independent consistency check from the preceding all-orders cumulant theorem

The preceding report proves, on a common source-full event,

`kappa_{r,n}(T)=n gamma_r+J_r(T)+o(1)`

for every fixed `r`, with `gamma_1=d_H` and `gamma_2=sigma_H^2`. Therefore the normalized fixed-tree conditional cumulants satisfy

`Cum_1((I_n-nd_H)/sqrt(n)|T) -> 0`,

`Cum_2((I_n-nd_H)/sqrt(n)|T) -> sigma_H^2`,

`Cum_r((I_n-nd_H)/sqrt(n)|T) -> 0` for every `r>=3`.

The Gaussian moment sequence is determinate, so the scalar quenched CLT also follows by the method of moments. This is not the proof used for the functional theorem, but it independently checks the time-one conclusion.
