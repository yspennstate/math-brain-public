## 3. New split-Palm theorem: the common `S^{-2}` denominator is `L^p`-regular

The previous run isolated the only remaining two-spine issue as the pair law created when two harmonic rays choose **different** children at their first split. This section resolves the local singularity exactly.

For a type-`i` potential child, let

\[
X_i:=A_i B(T_J),
\]

where `J` has law `q`, `A_i|J=j` is Bernoulli(`p_ij`), and `T_J~P_J`; thus `X_i` is one generic transmitted conductance contribution, including zero from failed transmission/extinction. Since the source is supercritical and every relevant type has positive survival/transmission probability, for each parent type `i` there exist `delta_i,eta_i>0` with

\[
P(X_i\ge\delta_i)\ge\eta_i.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because the type set is finite, constants below may be chosen uniformly after taking minima/maxima.

Let `w_i:[0,1]->[0,infinity)` be a bounded typewise parent weight and define a parent measure

\[
\lambda_i(dT)=w_i(B(T))P_i(dT).
\]

From a parent sampled under `lambda`, form the **ordered split pair measure** by choosing two distinct children with weight `H(u)H(v)`:

\[
\Xi_w(A,C)
:=\sum_i E_{P_i}\left[
 w_i(B_{\rm par})
 \sum_{u\ne v}H(u)H(v)
 1_{\{T_u\in A,T_v\in C\}}
\right].
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — exact density and all finite `L^p` moments

For child types `j,k`, let `b=B(T)`, `c=B(T')`, `x=b+c`, and let

\[
R_{i,m}=X_{i,1}+\cdots+X_{i,m}
\]

be the sum of the other `m` potential-child contributions. Then the `(j,k)` component of `Xi_w` is absolutely continuous with respect to `mu_j otimes mu_k`, with density

\[
\boxed{
D^{(w)}_{jk}(b,c)
=
\frac{1}{g_j(b)g_k(c)}
\sum_i q_jp_{ij}q_kp_{ik}
\sum_{n\ge2}P(\nu=n)n(n-1)
E\left[
\frac{w_i(F(x+R_{i,n-2}))}{(x+R_{i,n-2})^2}
\right].
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

If `0<=w_i<=W` for every type, then

\[
\boxed{
D^{(w)}_{jk}(b,c)
\le C_W\left(1+\frac1{(b+c)^2}\right).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently, using (2.3),

\[
\boxed{
D^{(w)}\in L^p(\mu\otimes\mu)
\qquad\text{for every finite }p.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

No assumption `E nu^2<infinity` is needed.

#### Proof

Conditional on `nu=n`, exchangeability gives `n(n-1)` ordered choices of the two selected slots. For fixed selected child types `j,k`, opening their two edges contributes `q_jp_ij q_kp_ik`; their harmonic weight is

\[
\frac{bc}{(b+c+R)^2}.
\]

Relative to `P_j otimes P_k`, the resulting density is therefore the same expression as (3.3) multiplied by `bc`. Dividing by the product stationary density

\[
h_j(b)h_k(c)=bc\,g_j(b)g_k(c)
\]

cancels the two selected conductances exactly and gives (3.3).

It remains to control the apparent factorial factor `n(n-1)`. Fix a parent type and abbreviate `delta=delta_i`, `eta=eta_i`, `m=n-2`. Let `N_m~Bin(m,eta)`. By (3.1), after coupling,

\[
R_{i,m}\ge\delta N_m.
\]

For every `s>=2`, a Chernoff split at `N_m>=eta m/2` gives

\[
E[(x+R_{i,m})^{-s}]
\le
\frac{(1-\eta)^m}{x^s}
+rac{C_s}{(m+1)^s}
+C_se^{-c_sm}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For `s=2`, multiplying by `n(n-1)` yields

\[
n(n-1)E[(x+R_{i,n-2})^{-2}]
\le C\left(1+x^{-2}\right),
\tag{3.7}
\]

because `sup_n n^2(1-eta)^{n-2}<infinity`; the remaining `n^2/(n-1)^2` term is uniformly bounded. Summing against the offspring probabilities therefore costs only total mass one, not a second offspring moment. Boundedness above and below of the `g`'s gives (3.4).

Finally `(b+c)^{-2p}<=b^{-2p}` and (2.3) gives every negative moment under `mu`; hence (3.5). `square`

### Interpretation

The common sibling normalization is not a fatal pair singularity. In fact the two selected conductances cancel against the two stationary densities, leaving only a `(b+c+R)^{-2}` factor, and the same random sibling sum that creates the denominator also regularizes the `n(n-1)` number of ordered pairs. This is why finite mean offspring is enough.

## 4. The first-split pair law inherits the uniform `L^p` bound at every common-spine generation

Let

\[
(P_2f)(T)=\sum_u H_T(u)^2f(T_u)
\tag{4.1}
\]

be the same-child substochastic two-spine kernel. Its adjoint with respect to `mu` preserves functions of `(type,B)`. Since

\[
P_2=M_{(1-B)/B}\,K_H\,M_B,
\]

we have on projected functions

\[
P_2^*=M_B\,\widehat Q\,M_{(1-B)/B}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Using the explicit reverse kernel from the preceding reports, if the current child conductance is `b` and the backward sibling conductance is `R_i`, then the parent conductance is `F(b+R_i)`. Therefore

\[
(P_2^*1)_j(b)
=
\frac1{g_j(b)}
\sum_iM_{ij}E\left[
\frac{b}{b+R_i}
\frac{g_i(F(b+R_i))}{1+b+R_i}
\right].
\tag{4.3}
\]

The fixed-point identity for `g` is the same formula without the factor `b/(b+R_i)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{0\le P_2^*1\le1.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Put

\[
a_r:=(P_2^*)^r1.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Positivity and (4.4) give

\[
\boxed{0\le a_r\le1\qquad\forall r\ge0.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The unnormalized law of the common-spine parent at generation `r`, under stationary initialization, is `a_r dmu`. Relative to the original source law it therefore has typewise density

\[
w_{r,i}(b)=a_{r,i}(b)h_i(b),
\qquad
0\le w_{r,i}(b)\le L_i.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Let `Xi_r` be the unnormalized ordered law of the two distinct child environments when the first split occurs immediately after that parent. Theorem 3.1 applied to `w_r` gives a density `D_r=dXi_r/d(mu otimes mu)` satisfying, **uniformly in `r`**,

\[
\boxed{
D_r(b,c)\le C\left(1+(b+c)^{-2}\right),
\qquad
\sup_r\|D_r\|_{L^p(\mu\otimes\mu)}<\infty
\quad\forall p<\infty.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is the requested post-split `L^p` domination.

### Regularity needed for propagation after the split

For the product spectral argument below one also needs a crude derivative bound away from `b=0` or `c=0`. For every fixed integer `k`, the operator `P_2^*` is bounded on the inherited projected `C^k` space:

\[
\|P_2^*f\|_{C^k}\le L_k\|f\|_{C^k}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

To see this, use (4.3). On the `R_i=0` pipe channel the factor `b/(b+R_i)` is exactly one and all coefficients are regular. On `R_i>0`, differentiating `k` times produces only finite sums bounded by `C_k R_i^{-m}` for finite `m`; the inherited super-exponential small-positive tail of `R_i` gives every required inverse moment. Thus

\[
\|a_r\|_{C^k}\le L_k^r.
\tag{4.10}
\]

If `chi_epsilon` is a smooth cutoff supported on `b,c>=epsilon` and equal to one on `b,c>=2epsilon`, differentiating the exact density (3.3) and using the general `s>=2` form of (3.6) gives, for some finite exponent `A_k`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\chi_\epsilon(b)\chi_\epsilon(c)D_r(b,c)\|_{C^k}
\le C_k L_k^r\epsilon^{-A_k}.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

## 5. New two-spine decorrelation theorem — the reverse spectral gap propagates through a split

The reverse conductance kernel `widehat Q` has an inherited `C^k` spectral gap. Therefore the product kernel `widehat Q otimes widehat Q` has the corresponding product gap: for projected `C^k` functions `F`,

\[
\left\|(\widehat Q\otimes\widehat Q)^mF
-(\bar\mu\otimes\bar\mu)(F)\right\|_\infty
\le C\rho^m\|F\|_{C^k},
\qquad 0<\rho<1,
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `bar mu` is the `(type,B)` projection of `mu`.

The apparent mismatch is that `D_r` is singular at `(0,0)`. Equations (4.8)--(4.11) remove it: truncate at `epsilon`, use (5.1) on the smooth part, and use the super-exponential lower tail (2.3) plus the uniform all-`p` bound (4.8) on the deleted part.

### Theorem 5.1 — exponential `L^2` decay of forward conditional expectations

Let `psi(type,B)` be centered under `mu` and suppose `psi in L^{2+delta}(mu)` for some `delta>0`. Then there exist `C_psi<infinity` and `c_psi>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
\|K_H^n\psi\|_{L^2(\mu)}
\le C_\psi e^{-c_\psi n}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This holds although the **forward** process projected only to `(type,B)` need not be Markov.

#### Proof

Sample two harmonic rays independently in the same stationary tree. Conditional independence of the two rays given the tree gives

\[
\|K_H^n\psi\|_2^2
=E_\mu^{(2)}[\psi(B_n^{(1)})\psi(B_n^{(2)})].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Split according to their first separation time.

**No split / late split.** The preceding report proved

\[
P_\mu(\tau>r)\le Ce^{-cr}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Each ray separately has stationary marginal `mu`. Holder with `psi in L^{2+delta}` therefore gives, for every fixed `theta>0`,

\[
E\left[
|\psi(B_n^{(1)})\psi(B_n^{(2)})|;
\tau>\theta n
\right]
\le Ce^{-c_\theta n}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This includes the event of no split by generation `n` and every split later than `theta n`.

**Early split.** Suppose the split occurs after common generation `r<=theta n`; then the two selected child environments have unnormalized density `D_r` from (4.8), and after the split the two descendant harmonic chains evolve independently. Put `m=n-r-1`. By adjointness, because `D_r` depends only on the two projected child states,

\[
E[\psi(B_n^{(1)})\psi(B_n^{(2)});\tau=r+1]
=
\int (\widehat Q\otimes\widehat Q)^mD_r(b,c)
\psi(b)\psi(c)
\,d\bar\mu(b)d\bar\mu(c).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Choose `epsilon=e^{-beta n}` and write `D_r=D_r^{(epsilon)}+R_r^{(epsilon)}` with the smooth cutoff from (4.11). Since `psi` is centered, the constant equilibrium projection of `D_r^{(epsilon)}` contributes zero to (5.6). Thus (5.1) and (4.11) give

\[
\left|\int(\widehat Q\otimes\widehat Q)^mD_r^{(\epsilon)}\,\psi\otimes\psi\,d\bar\mu^{\otimes2}\right|
\le
C\rho^mL_k^r\epsilon^{-A_k}\|\psi\|_1^2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

For the deleted part, choose finite `p>1` with conjugate exponent compatible with the `2+delta` moment of `psi`. Markov `L^p` contraction, (4.8), and the super-exponential bound `mu(B<epsilon)<=Ce^{-c/epsilon}` imply

\[
\sup_r\|R_r^{(\epsilon)}\|_{L^p(\mu^{\otimes2})}
\le C_p e^{-c_p/\epsilon},
\tag{5.8}
\]

so its contribution to (5.6) is super-exponentially small in `n`.

Now choose `theta>0` sufficiently small and then `beta>0` sufficiently small so that

\[
(1-\theta)|\log\rho|
>\theta\log L_k+A_k\beta.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Then (5.7) is `O(e^{-cn})` uniformly over all `r<=theta n`. Summing at most `n` early split times preserves exponential decay. Combining with (5.5) proves

\[
\|K_H^n\psi\|_2^2\le Ce^{-cn},
\]

and hence (5.2), after changing the exponent. `square`

### Interpretation

This is the precise post-split decorrelation lemma that the preceding report was missing. It transfers a spectral gap that is available only for the **reverse projected conductance chain** into exponential decay of a non-Markov **forward conditional expectation on the full tree**. The mechanism is genuinely two-spine: the split-Palm `L^p` density regularizes the local dependence, while the product reverse chain kills it after the split.

## 6. New quenched Shannon-entropy corrector

The exact electrical coboundary (2.6) gives a deterministic identity for the fixed-tree level-`n` Shannon entropy

\[
\mathcal H_n(T)
:=-\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)\log\theta_T(v)
=E_H[I_n\mid T].
\]

Conditioning term by term in (2.6),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_n(T)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}K_H^r\phi(T)
+\log B(T)-K_H^n\log B(T).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
\bar\phi:=\phi-d_H,
\qquad
\bar\ell:=\log B-E_\mu\log B.
\]

Both are functions only of `(type,B)` and have moments of every finite order under `mu`: `phi(B)<=log(1+nu)` gives polynomial moments needed here, while inverse-conductance moments give all moments of `|log B|`. Theorem 5.1 therefore gives

\[
\|K_H^r\bar\phi\|_2+\|K_H^r\bar\ell\|_2
\le Ce^{-cr}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Hence the series

\[
\boxed{
G(T):=\bar\ell(T)+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}K_H^r\bar\phi(T)
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

converges absolutely in `L^2(mu)`. Equation (6.1) becomes

\[
\mathcal H_n-nd_H
=
\bar\ell+\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}K_H^r\bar\phi-K_H^n\bar\ell,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal H_n-nd_H-G\|_{L^2(\mu)}
\le Ce^{-cn}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_n\operatorname{Var}_\mu(\mathcal H_n)<\infty,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(\mathcal H_n)\to\operatorname{Var}_\mu(G).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Since the error in (6.4) is exponentially summable, Markov plus Borel--Cantelli yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_n(T)-nd_H\longrightarrow G(T)
\qquad\mu\text{-a.s.}
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

The stationary law `mu` is equivalent to the original source law conditioned on survival. Therefore the same almost-sure event has full source-conditioned measure:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_n(T)=nd_H+G(T)+o(1)
\qquad\text{for source-a.e. surviving }T.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This strictly strengthens the previous source-a.s. theorem `mathcal H_n/n -> d_H`: the fixed-tree harmonic Shannon entropy has a genuine random `O(1)` correction and no larger sublinear wandering.

### Corollary 6.1 — stationary annealed conditional variance has the CLT slope

The inherited analytic pressure theorem gives the stationary annealed variance

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(I_n)=n\sigma_H^2+O(1).
\tag{6.8}
\]

By total variance,

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(I_n)
=E_\mu\operatorname{Var}_H(I_n\mid T)
+\operatorname{Var}_\mu(\mathcal H_n(T)).
\]

Using (6.5),

\[
\boxed{
E_\mu\operatorname{Var}_H(I_n\mid T)
=n\sigma_H^2+O(1).
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus the quadratic coefficient is already correct after averaging over the stationary environment. What remains open is **self-averaging of the conditional variance itself** (and the exact source-root averaged version if one wants it without stationary weighting), not the conditional mean.

## 8. Next attack

\[
