## 3. New lemma: split-Palm decay extends to arbitrary full-tree observables

The preceding report used the two-spine decomposition to control centered conductance observables. The same argument is stronger.

### Lemma 3.1 — full-state exponential `L^2` mixing

For every `eta>0` and every centered `f in L^{2+eta}(mu)`, there are constants `C(f,eta)<infinity` and `c(eta)>0` such that

`||K^n f||_{L^2(mu)} <= C(f,eta) exp(-c n)`.  (3.1)

#### Proof

Use two conditionally independent harmonic rays in the same stationary tree. Then

`||K^n f||_2^2 = E_mu[f(T_n^(1)) f(T_n^(2))]`.  (3.2)

Decompose by their first split time `tau`.

**No split by level n.** The previous negative-tilt / collision theorem gives `P(tau>=n) <= C exp(-c n)`. Holder with `f in L^{2+eta}` makes this contribution exponentially small.

**First split at level r<n.** The preceding split-Palm theorem gives the ordered pair of distinct child environments at the split a density `D_r(type,b;type',c)` relative to `mu x mu`, with `sup_r ||D_r||_p<infinity` for every finite `p`. Crucially, `D_r` is measurable only in the projected variables `(type,B)`.

After the split the two descendant evolutions are independent. Since the adjoint of `K` acting on projected densities is the already constructed reverse kernel `widehat Q`, after `m=n-r-1` more levels the pair density is

`(widehat Q^m tensor widehat Q^m) D_r`.  (3.3)

When this density is integrated against `f tensor f`, only the projected conditional mean

`bar f(type,b)=E_mu[f | type,B=b]`

appears. Conditional expectation is contractive in `L^{2+eta}`, and `E_mu bar f=0`. Therefore the same cutoff-away-from-`B=0` plus product spectral-gap argument used in the preceding report applies without requiring `f` itself to be projected.

For early splits `r<=alpha n`, choose `alpha>0` small enough that the product spectral contraction over `m >= (1-alpha)n` beats the at-most-exponential derivative growth of the cutoff split density. For late splits `r>alpha n`, the common-prefix probability is already `O(exp(-c alpha n))`. Summing the split levels proves (3.1). `square`

This lemma is the key pivot: it turns the projected reverse spectral gap plus the split-Palm regularization into mixing for arbitrary integrable observables of the whole descendant tree.

## 4. Exact conditional-variance recursion

For an edge from `T` to child subtree `T_u`, let

`j(T,u) = -log H_T(u)`.

Define

`V_n(T)=Var_H(I_n | T)`, `V_0=0`.

The law of total variance at the first harmonic step gives the exact recursion

`V_{n+1}(T) = K V_n(T) + r_n(T)`,  (4.1)

where

`r_n(T) = Var_{u~H_T}( j(T,u) + m_n(T_u) )`.  (4.2)

Insert (2.2). The deterministic constant `n d_H` disappears inside the child variance. Put

`A(T,u)=j(T,u)+G(T_u)`,

`r_infty(T)=Var_{u~H_T}(A(T,u))`.  (4.3)

Then

`r_n(T)=Var_{u~H_T}(A(T,u)+epsilon_n(T_u))`.  (4.4)

### Lemma 4.1 — local variance increment converges exponentially in every finite moment needed here

`r_infty in L^p(mu)` for every finite `p`, and in particular

`||r_n-r_infty||_2 <= C exp(-cn)`.  (4.5)

#### Proof

The electrical edge identity gives

`j(T,u)=phi(T)+ell(T)-ell(T_u)`.  (4.6)

Under the stationary harmonic edge law, both endpoint marginals are `mu`. Equations (2.4) and (4.6) therefore give every finite moment of `A`.

By Jensen,

`r_infty^p <= (E_H[A^2|T])^p <= E_H[|A|^{2p}|T]`,

so stationarity gives `r_infty in L^p` for every finite `p`.

For random variables `X,Y` under any probability law,

`|Var(X+Y)-Var(X)| <= 2 (E X^2)^{1/2}(E Y^2)^{1/2} + E Y^2`.  (4.7)

Apply this conditionally with `X=A(T,U)` and `Y=epsilon_n(T_U)`. Taking the environmental `L^2` norm and using Jensen plus Cauchy--Schwarz yields

`||r_n-r_infty||_2 <= 2 ||A||_{L^4(edge)} ||epsilon_n||_{L^4(mu)} + ||epsilon_n||_{L^4(mu)}^2`,

which is exponentially small by (2.4). The same argument with higher moments gives an `L^{2+eta}` exponential bound for some (indeed every finite) `eta`. `square`

## 5. Main theorem: quenched conditional variance has an `O(1)` random corrector

Let

`bar r = E_mu r_infty`, `Delta_m = r_m-r_infty`, `a_m=E_mu Delta_m`.

Theorem 3.1 applies to `r_infty-bar r` and to the centered `Delta_m-a_m` because Lemma 4.1 supplies moments above two. Therefore the series

`J_0(T)=sum_{s>=0} K^s(r_infty-bar r)(T)`  (5.1)

converges in `L^2(mu)`, and

`C_0=sum_{m>=0} a_m`  (5.2)

converges absolutely.

### Theorem 5.1 — conditional-variance corrector

There exists `J in L^2(mu)`, explicitly

`J(T)=J_0(T)+C_0`,  (5.3)

such that for some `C,c>0`,

`|| V_n - n bar r - J ||_2 <= C exp(-c n)`.  (5.4)

Consequently the same convergence holds `mu`-almost surely, and by equivalence of `mu` with the original source law conditioned on survival it holds source-a.s. on survival.

#### Proof

Iterating (4.1),

`V_n = sum_{s=0}^{n-1} K^s r_{n-1-s}`.  (5.5)

Add and subtract `r_infty`:

`V_n-n bar r`
`= sum_{s=0}^{n-1} K^s(r_infty-bar r)`
`  + sum_{m=0}^{n-1} K^{n-1-m} Delta_m`.  (5.6)

The first sum converges exponentially in `L^2` to `J_0` by Theorem 3.1.

For the second sum write `Delta_m=a_m+(Delta_m-a_m)`. The scalar part converges exponentially to `C_0` because `|a_m|<=C exp(-cm)`. For the centered part, Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 4.1 give

`||K^{n-1-m}(Delta_m-a_m)||_2 <= C exp(-c(n-1-m)) exp(-c'm)`.

Summing `m=0,...,n-1` is `O(n exp(-c'' n))`, hence still exponentially small after reducing `c''`. This proves (5.4). Summability of the squared error probabilities gives almost-sure convergence by Borel--Cantelli. `square`

## 6. Identifying the slope as the harmonic-information variance `sigma_H^2`

The inherited analytic pressure / CLT theorem gives the annealed stationary variance

`Var_mu(I_n)=n sigma_H^2+O(1)`.  (6.1)

The preceding entropy-corrector theorem gives `m_n-nd_H -> G` in `L^2`, so

`Var_mu(m_n)=O(1)`.  (6.2)

Total variance yields

`E_mu V_n = n sigma_H^2+O(1)`.  (6.3)

On the other hand, Theorem 5.1 gives `E_mu V_n = n bar r + O(1)`. Therefore

`bar r = sigma_H^2`.  (6.4)

Combining (5.4) and (6.4):

`boxed{ Var_H(I_n|T) = n sigma_H^2 + J(T) + O_{L^2}(e^{-cn}). }`  (6.5)

Hence for `mu`-a.e., equivalently source-a.e. surviving, tree,

`boxed{ (1/n) Var_H(I_n|T) -> sigma_H^2. }`  (6.6)

For every nonregular source the inherited rigidity theorem gives `sigma_H^2>0`; the deterministic regular no-thinning model has `sigma_H^2=0` and (6.5) is consistent with `I_n=n log k`.

This is stronger than the four-spine target proposed in the preceding report: the environmental fluctuations of the conditional variance are not merely `o(n)` or `O(sqrt n)`; under the inherited hypotheses they have an actual random `O(1)` limit.

## 7. Fixed-tree Renyi curvature at q=1

For a fixed surviving tree define

`Z_n(q;T)=sum_{|v|=n} theta_T(v)^q`

and

`p_n(q,T)=(1/n) log Z_n(q;T)`.

Because `Z_n(1;T)=1`, differentiation at `q=1` gives exactly

`p_n'(1,T)=-(1/n) E_H[I_n|T]= -m_n(T)/n`,  (7.1)

`p_n''(1,T)=(1/n) Var_H(I_n|T)=V_n(T)/n`.  (7.2)

The entropy and variance correctors therefore imply, source-a.s. on survival,

`boxed{ p_n'(1,T) -> -d_H,   p_n''(1,T) -> sigma_H^2. }`  (7.3)

More precisely,

`p_n'(1,T) = -d_H - G(T)/n + o(1/n)`,

`p_n''(1,T) = sigma_H^2 + J(T)/n + o(1/n)`.  (7.4)

This identifies the quenched finite-level tangent and curvature at the Shannon point. It **does not** by itself prove that `p_n(q,T)` converges for any fixed `q != 1`, nor that a limiting quenched pressure is twice differentiable. Interchanging `n -> infinity` with a fixed nonzero `q-1` remains a separate uniform-cumulant problem.
