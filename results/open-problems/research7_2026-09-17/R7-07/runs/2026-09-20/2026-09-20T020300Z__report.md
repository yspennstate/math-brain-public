## 3. Harmonic-descendant kernel

Let

\[
\Omega^+=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^d\{i\}\times\{T:B(T)>0\}
\]

be the surviving typed-tree state space. For a surviving type-`i` tree, set

\[
S(T)=\sum_uB(T_u)>0.
\]

The one-generation harmonic-descendant kernel is

\[
(K_Hf)(i,T)
=\sum_{u:B(T_u)>0}\frac{B(T_u)}{S(T)}
 f(\tau(u),T_u),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `tau(u)` is the child's type.

All harmonic weights in (3.1) are strictly positive on surviving children.

The source assumptions used below are exactly the positive finite-type reduction used in the earlier reports:

- finite type set `D={1,...,d}`;
- `q_j>0` and `p_ij>0`;
- common potential-child count `nu` with finite mean `m_*=E nu`;
- mean matrix `M_ij=m_*q_jp_ij` satisfying `rho(M)>1`;
- hence every survival probability `s_j=P_j(B>0)` is positive.

Because

\[
\rho(M)\le\max_i\sum_jM_{ij}\le m_*,
\]

supercriticality implies `m_*>1`, hence

\[
P(\nu\ge2)>0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

That elementary observation supplies the independent-child collision used below.

## 4. Theorem — every equivalent stationary harmonic environment is ergodic

### Theorem 4.1

Let `mu` be a probability measure on `Omega^+` such that

1. `mu K_H=mu`;
2. `mu` is equivalent to the finite reference measure
   \[
   \mathsf P^+:=\sum_i P_i(\,\cdot\,;B>0).
   \tag{4.1}
   \]

Then every bounded `K_H`-harmonic function is `mu`-a.s. constant. In particular `mu` is an ergodic/extreme stationary law for the harmonic-descendant chain.

### Proof

Let `f` be bounded and satisfy

\[
K_Hf=f\qquad\mu\text{-a.s.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

By stationarity,

\[
\int K_H(f^2)\,d\mu=\int f^2\,d\mu.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since `K_Hf=f`,

\[
\int (K_Hf)^2\,d\mu=\int f^2\,d\mu.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Subtracting gives

\[
\int\left[K_H(f^2)-(K_Hf)^2\right]d\mu=0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The integrand is exactly the conditional variance of `f` among the surviving children under the harmonic probabilities:

\[
K_H(f^2)-(K_Hf)^2
=
\sum_u\frac{B_u}{S}
\left(f(T_u)-K_Hf(T)\right)^2\ge0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore it vanishes `mu`-a.s. Equivalence with `mathsf P^+` implies that for source-a.e. surviving tree,

\[
\boxed{
f(\tau(u),T_u)=f(i,T)
\quad\text{for every surviving child }u.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Fix any child type `j`. Choose `n>=2` with `P(nu=n)>0`, which exists by (3.2), and fix any parent type `i`. Consider the concrete positive-probability event that `nu=n` and slots 1 and 2 both transmit, both have type `j`, and both child subtrees survive. Its probability contains the strictly positive factor

\[
P(\nu=n)(q_jp_{ij}s_j)^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Conditional on the slot types/transmission/survival statuses, those two child subtrees are independent copies of `P_j(\cdot\mid B>0)`. By (4.7), their `f`-values are equal almost surely. Two independent identically distributed real random variables that are equal almost surely must have a degenerate law. Hence there is a constant `c_j` such that

\[
f(j,T)=c_j
\qquad P_j(\cdot\mid B>0)\text{-a.s.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Now fix any two types `j,k`. On the positive-probability event that slot 1 is a surviving type-`j` child and slot 2 is a surviving type-`k` child, (4.7) gives

\[
c_j=c_k.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus all `c_j` are equal. Equivalence of `mu` with the survival source completes the proof. `square`

### Why this is stronger than the earlier indicator argument

The previous C06 report sketched ergodicity through invariant indicators. The variance proof above handles **every bounded harmonic function**, which is the standard extremality criterion for a stationary Markov environment and avoids a possible ambiguity between state-invariant sets and path-space shift events.

## 5. Theorem — uniqueness of the equivalent stationary law

### Theorem 5.1

There is at most one `K_H`-stationary probability measure equivalent to `mathsf P^+`.

### Proof

Let `mu` and `nu` be two such stationary probabilities. They are equivalent, so write

\[
r=\frac{d\nu}{d\mu},\qquad r>0,\qquad\int r\,d\mu=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Run the stationary `mu`-chain `(X_0,X_1)`. Stationarity of `nu` says that for every bounded `g`,

\[
E_\mu[r(X_1)g(X_1)]
=\int rg\,d\mu
=\int rK_Hg\,d\mu
=E_\mu[r(X_0)g(X_1)].
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
E_\mu[r(X_0)\mid X_1]=r(X_1).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Use the strictly convex function

\[
\psi(x)=\frac{x^2}{1+x}=x-1+\frac1{1+x},
\qquad
\psi''(x)=\frac{2}{(1+x)^3}>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

It satisfies `0<=psi(x)<=x`, so it is integrable under `mu`. Conditional Jensen and (5.3) give

\[
E\psi(r(X_1))
\le E\psi(r(X_0)).
\tag{5.5}
\]

But `X_0` and `X_1` have the same `mu` distribution, so equality holds. Strict convexity forces

\[
r(X_0)=r(X_1)
\qquad\text{almost surely.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

For every rational `t`, the bounded indicator

\[
1_{\{r>t\}}
\]

is therefore `K_H`-harmonic `mu`-a.s. Theorem 4.1 makes it constant. Varying rational `t` shows that `r` itself is constant `mu`-a.s.; its mean is one, so `r=1`. Hence `nu=mu`. `square`

### Consequence

The harmonic stationary environment is canonical **as soon as existence plus equivalence are established**. No uniqueness/minorization theorem for the auxiliary backward-conductance chain is needed for the dimension theorem.

In particular, if two different invariant laws of the auxiliary `P^kappa` chain exist, but both are un-Doob transformed by the construction of the preceding reports into finite equivalent harmonic stationary tree laws, their induced tree laws must coincide.

## 6. Exact bridge from the transfer equation to stationary measures

The preceding uniqueness theorem becomes directly useful for R14's fixed-point equation.

Let `h=(h_i)` be nonnegative measurable functions with `h_i(0)=0`. Define a finite measure on surviving typed trees by

\[
\nu_h(d(i,T))=h_i(B(T))P_i(dT).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Assume

\[
0<\sum_iE_i[h_i(B)]<\infty
\tag{6.2}
\]

and `h_i(B)>0` almost surely on survival for every type.

### Lemma 6.1

If `mathcal L h=h` in the source-relevant sense, then

\[
\boxed{\nu_hK_H=\nu_h.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof

For bounded test `f`, start from the left side:

\[
\sum_iE_i\left[
 h_i(F(S))
 \sum_{u}\frac{B_u}{S}f(\tau(u),T_u)
\right].
\tag{6.4}
\]

Apply the ordinary finite-child Campbell/Palm decomposition by distinguishing the chosen potential child. If the distinguished child has type `j`, conductance `b=B(T)`, and the remaining Palm sibling conductance is `R_i`, then `S=b+R_i`. Therefore (6.4) equals

\[
\sum_jE_{P_j}\left[
 f(j,T)
 m_*q_j b\sum_i p_{ij}
 E\left[
 \frac{h_i(F(b+R_i))}{b+R_i}
 \right]
\right].
\tag{6.5}
\]

The bracket is exactly `(mathcal L h)_j(b)`. If `mathcal Lh=h`, (6.5) is

\[
\sum_jE_{P_j}[f(j,T)h_j(B(T))],
\]

which is `nu_h(f)`. `square`

No sibling-normalization shortcut has been used: the denominator `b+R_i` is retained exactly in (6.5).

## 7. Theorem — source-support uniqueness of the transfer fixed point

### Theorem 7.1

Let `h` and `g` be two positive source-integrable solutions of

\[
\mathcal Lh=h,
\qquad
\mathcal Lg=g,
\tag{7.1}
\]

satisfying (6.2) and positivity on survival. Then there is a constant `c>0` such that for every type `i`,

\[
\boxed{
h_i(B(T))=c\,g_i(B(T))
\quad P_i\text{-a.s. on }\{B>0\}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

If `h_i` and `g_i` are continuous on the topological support

\[
\mathcal S_i=\operatorname{supp}\mathcal L(B\mid B>0,\text{type }i),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{h_i(b)=c\,g_i(b)\quad\text{for every }b\in\mathcal S_i.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Proof

Lemma 6.1 says that the normalized versions of `nu_h` and `nu_g` are `K_H`-stationary probabilities. Positivity of the densities on survival makes both equivalent to `mathsf P^+`. Theorem 5.1 therefore says the normalized measures are identical. Undoing the normalizations gives (7.2). Continuity upgrades almost-sure equality to equality on the support, proving (7.3). `square`

### Scope qualification

This is deliberately called **source-support uniqueness**, not arbitrary pointwise uniqueness on all `b in (0,1)`. The transfer equation can be extended to values of `b` never realized by the source conductance law; changing an off-support extension may have no probabilistic meaning. For the harmonic environment and the dimension theorem, equality on the realized support is exactly the required notion.

This closes the uniqueness question posed at the end of the previous C06 report in the strongest source-relevant sense.

## 8. Global two-sided conductance comparison for the canonical density

The finite-`lambda` result of the previous report yields more than the asymptotic (2.4). Using (2.1)--(2.2),

\[
h_j(b)
=b\int_{(0,1]}\lambda_j(da)
\frac{a}{a(1-b)+b}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For `0<a<=1` and `0<b<1`,

\[
a\le\frac{a}{a(1-b)+b}\le1,
\tag{8.2}
\]

because

\[
a\le a(1-b)+b\le1.
\]

Define

\[
A_j:=\int a\,\lambda_j(da),
\qquad
L_j:=\lambda_j((0,1]).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Both are finite and strictly positive. Then

\[
\boxed{
A_j b\le h_j(b)\le L_j b
\qquad(0<b<1).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Hence the canonical harmonic stationary environment is uniformly equivalent, type by type, to the **escape-conductance-biased source measure**

\[
B(T)P_j(dT),
\tag{8.5}
\]

with bounded density ratio

\[
A_j\le\frac{h_j(B)}{B}\le L_j.
\tag{8.6}
\]

This is stronger than mere equivalence to survival conditioning and is potentially reusable in regeneration/spine estimates: stationary harmonic sampling differs from conductance size-biasing by only a bounded type-dependent factor.

## 9. A finite local certificate for a positive PF--harmonic entropy gap

The previous report proved the pointwise identity

\[
D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T)
=
\int_0^1 t\,\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{T,t}}(X_u)\,dt,
\qquad
X_u=\log\frac{B_u}{\mathcal W_u},
\tag{9.1}
\]

where `U` is the PF split and

\[
Q_t(u)=\frac{U_u e^{tX_u}}{\sum_vU_ve^{tX_v}}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

The following simple bound converts strictness into a finite local witness.

Suppose a realized branch has two children `u != v` for which, for some `eta,delta,K>0`,

\[
U_u,U_v\ge\eta,
\qquad
|X_w|\le K\text{ for all surviving }w,
\qquad
|X_u-X_v|\ge\delta.
\tag{9.3}
\]

For every `t in [0,1]`,

\[
e^{-K}\le\sum_wU_we^{tX_w}\le e^K,
\]

so

\[
Q_t(u),Q_t(v)\ge\eta e^{-2K}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Using

\[
\operatorname{Var}_q(X)
=\frac12\sum_{r,s}q_rq_s(X_r-X_s)^2,
\]

we get

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_t}(X)
\ge \eta^2e^{-4K}\delta^2.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T)
\ge
\frac12\eta^2e^{-4K}\delta^2.
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

Consequently, conditional on the inherited PF--KL dimension identity, any positive-`mu` event satisfying (9.3) gives the explicit lower bound

\[
\boxed{
\log\rho(M)-\dim_H(\mathrm{HARM})
\ge
\frac12\eta^2e^{-4K}\delta^2\,\mu(E_{\eta,\delta,K}).
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

Because the canonical `mu` is equivalent to the source survival law, a source-positive witness event is also `mu`-positive. Moreover, (8.4) gives a direct comparison to its conductance-weighted source probability.

This does not by itself replace R14's equality-rigidity theorem, but it converts any local nonregularity witness in the ratio `B/\mathcal W` into a quantitative entropy deficit.

## 11. Failed/avoided routes and what they teach

### 11.1 Auxiliary-chain uniqueness is unnecessary

A first instinct was to prove Harris recurrence or a Doeblin/minorization theorem for the backward-conductance `P^kappa` chain. That is stronger than what the dimension theorem needs and is delicate because `R_i` may have atoms and no smooth density. Theorem 5.1 shows that **tree-environment uniqueness follows from branching independence plus source equivalence**, even if the auxiliary backward variable admits more than one invariant realization.

### 11.2 Do not claim off-support fixed-point uniqueness

The fixed-point equation contains an artificial scalar argument `b`. The source sees only `b` values in the support of the escape-conductance law. A theorem asserting uniqueness on the whole interval `(0,1)` without a support/analytic-continuation hypothesis would be stronger than justified. The correct source-level statement is (7.2)--(7.3).

### 11.3 No new second-moment assumption

The ergodicity and uniqueness proofs use only the finite-mean branching source plus supercriticality and positivity. They do not reintroduce a hidden `E nu^2<infinity` hypothesis.
