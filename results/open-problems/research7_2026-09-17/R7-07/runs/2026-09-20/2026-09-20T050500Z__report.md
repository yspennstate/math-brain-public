## 2. Inherited source setup

Let the finite type set be `D={1,...,d}`. A type-`i` parent has a common potential-child count `nu` with

\[
G(z)=E[z^\nu],\qquad m_*=E\nu<\infty,
\]

and each potential child independently has type `j` with probability `q_j>0`, transmits with probability `p_{ij}>0`, and then carries an independent type-`j` forward tree. The failed-child mean matrix is

\[
M_{ij}=m_*q_jp_{ij},\qquad \rho:=\rho(M)>1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The source finite-type regime supplies the usual Kesten--Stigum hypotheses used by R14. Let `r` be a positive right Perron vector and

\[
\mathcal W(T)
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho^{-n}\sum_{|v|=n}r_{\tau(v)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal W(T)=\rho^{-1}\sum_u\mathcal W(T_u),
\qquad
\mathcal W>0\iff\text{survival}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For simple random walk on a surviving failed tree, let

\[
B(T)=P_o^T(\text{never hit an added parent}).
\]

With `B_u=B(T_u)` and `C=\sum_uB_u`, the exact electrical recursion is

\[
B(T)=\frac{C}{1+C}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The harmonic and Perron local child flows are

\[
H_T(u)=\frac{B_u}{\sum_vB_v},
\qquad
U_T(u)=\frac{\mathcal W_u}{\sum_v\mathcal W_v}
      =\frac{\mathcal W_u}{\rho\mathcal W(T)}
\tag{2.5}
\]

on surviving children.

The previous C06 reports now supply, by two algebraically conjugate but logically separate existence routes, a probability law

\[
\mu(d(i,T))=h_i(B(T))P_i(dT)
\tag{2.6}
\]

which is stationary for the harmonic-descendant kernel

\[
(K_Hf)(T)=\sum_uH_T(u)f(T_u),
\tag{2.7}
\]

ergodic, and equivalent type-by-type to the source law conditioned on survival. The direct Stieltjes-renewal construction from the immediately preceding report gives

\[
0<A_iB\le h_i(B)\le L_iB\le L_i<\infty
\quad(B>0).
\tag{2.8}
\]

It also gives the Palm sibling tail and extinction-Jacobian facts needed later:

\[
P(0<R_i\le x)\le Ce^{-c/x},
\qquad
J_{ij}=G'(1-t_i)q_jp_{ij},
\qquad
\rho(J)<1.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Finally, earlier C06 work established for some `alpha>0`, uniformly over the finite types,

\[
P_i(0<\mathcal W\le x)\le Cx^\alpha,
\tag{2.10}
\]

and all inverse moments of `B` on survival.

The purpose of Sections 3--6 is to assemble these ingredients into a complete finite-type harmonic-dimension theorem rather than leaving the PF--KL implication as a candidate chain.

## 3. All entropy/PF endpoint hypotheses are integrable under the stationary law

### Lemma 3.1 — logarithmic Perron integrability

For every type,

\[
E_\mu|\log\mathcal W|<\infty.
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

For the negative part, (2.10) implies

\[
E_i[-\log\mathcal W;0<\mathcal W\le1]
=
\int_0^\infty
P_i(0<\mathcal W\le e^{-t})\,dt
\le C\int_0^\infty e^{-\alpha t}\,dt<\infty.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For the positive part, `log^+ x<=x`, and Kesten--Stigum gives `E_i mathcal W=r_i<infinity`. Since the stationary density is bounded by `L_i`,

\[
E_\mu|\log\mathcal W|
\le
\sum_iL_iE_i[|\log\mathcal W|;\mathcal W>0]<\infty.
\]
`\square`

### Lemma 3.2 — harmonic entropy integrability

Let

\[
\mathsf H(T):=-\sum_uH_T(u)\log H_T(u).
\]

Then

\[
E_\mu\mathsf H(T)<\infty.
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

If `N_+(T)` is the number of surviving children,

\[
\mathsf H(T)\le\log N_+(T)\le\log(1+\nu)\le\nu.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The density bound (2.8) and `E nu=m_*<infinity` imply (3.3). `\square`

### Lemma 3.3 — cross entropy is integrable

The local cross entropy

\[
\mathsf C(T):=-\sum_uH_T(u)\log U_T(u)
\]

is integrable under `mu`.

#### Proof

By (2.3)--(2.5),

\[
-\log U_T(u)
=
\log\rho+\log\mathcal W(T)-\log\mathcal W(T_u).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathsf C(T)
=
\log\rho+\log\mathcal W(T)
-
\sum_uH_T(u)\log\mathcal W(T_u).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Lemma 3.1 handles the root term. By stationarity,

\[
E_\mu\sum_uH_T(u)|\log\mathcal W(T_u)|
=
E_\mu|\log\mathcal W(T)|<\infty.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Hence `mathsf C` is integrable. Since `D_KL(H||U)=mathsf C-mathsf H>=0`, the KL term is finite as well. `\square`

This closes the endpoint/integrability obligation in R14's PF--KL reduction without a finite-mean regeneration-roof assumption.

## 4. Exact-dimensionality under the harmonic stationary environment

Let `(T_n)_{n>=0}` be the stationary Markov chain with invariant law `mu` and transition `K_H`; equivalently, conditionally on `T_n`, choose a surviving child according to `H_{T_n}` and set `T_{n+1}` equal to that descendant tree. The earlier C06 variance argument proves that every bounded `K_H`-harmonic function is constant, hence this stationary chain is ergodic.

Let `xi=(xi_1,xi_2,...)` denote the corresponding harmonic ray. Cylinder consistency gives

\[
-\log H_T([\xi|n])
=
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
Y_k,
\qquad
Y_k:=-\log H_{T_k}(\xi_{k+1}).
\tag{4.1}
\]

By Lemma 3.2,

\[
E Y_0=E_\mu\mathsf H(T)<\infty.
\]

The stationary path measure is ergodic, so Birkhoff yields

\[
\boxed{
-\frac1n\log H_T([\xi|n])
\longrightarrow
h_H:=E_\mu\mathsf H(T)
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

for `mu(dT)H_T(dxi)`-almost every pair `(T,xi)`.

In the generation boundary metric used in the source/R14 reduction, radius-`e^{-n}` balls are exactly depth-`n` cylinders. Therefore (4.2) proves exact dimensionality:

\[
\boxed{
\dim_H(H_T)=h_H
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

for `mu`-almost every surviving tree. Since `mu` is equivalent to the finite-type source law conditioned on survival, the same conclusion holds for `P_i(.|survival)`-almost every tree for every root type `i`. In particular the harmonic dimension is deterministic and type-independent.

## 5. The PF--KL identity now has all hypotheses verified

Using (3.6) and stationarity,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_\mu\mathsf C(T)
&=
\log\rho
+E_\mu\log\mathcal W(T)
-E_\mu K_H(\log\mathcal W)(T)\\
&=\log\rho.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Pointwise,

\[
D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T)
=
\mathsf C(T)-\mathsf H(T).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Combining (4.3), (5.1), and (5.2),

\[
\boxed{
\dim_H(H_T)
=
\log\rho(M)
-
E_\mu D_{KL}(H_T\Vert U_T).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\dim_H(H_T)\le\log\rho(M),
\tag{5.4}
\]

and equality occurs exactly when `H_T=U_T` almost surely under `mu`, hence, by equivalence, source-almost surely on survival.

The remainder of the proof classifies that equality case directly, so this report does not merely cite the earlier R14 rigidity statement.

## 6. Equality rigidity, rederived from the source law

### Theorem 6.1

Under the positive finite-type source law above,

\[
H_T=U_T\quad\text{source-a.s. on survival}
\tag{6.1}
\]

if and only if there is an integer `k>=2` such that

\[
\boxed{
\nu\equiv k,
\qquad
p_{ij}=1\ \text{for every }i,j.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

On a surviving type-`j` descendant tree define

\[
Z_j(T):=\frac{B(T)}{\mathcal W(T)}>0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Assume (6.1). Supercriticality gives `m_*>1`, so there exists `n>=2` with `P(nu=n)>0`. For any parent type `i` and any child type `j`, the event that two specified potential-child slots both transmit, both have type `j`, and both descendant trees survive has strictly positive probability because `q_j,p_{ij},s_j>0`. Conditional on this event the two surviving type-`j` descendant trees are independent copies of the type-`j` survival law. Equality `H=U` forces their values of `B/mathcal W` to coincide. Two independent identically distributed real random variables that agree almost surely have a degenerate law. Hence

\[
Z_j(T)\equiv c_j
\tag{6.4}
\]

under the type-`j` survival law.

The analogous positive-probability event with one surviving type-`j` child and one surviving type-`ell` child forces `c_j=c_ell`. Therefore there is a common `c>0` such that

\[
B(T)=c\mathcal W(T)
\tag{6.5}
\]

on every surviving type law.

Now combine the two exact recursions. From (2.3),

\[
\sum_uB_u=c\sum_u\mathcal W_u=c\rho\mathcal W(T).
\]

Using (2.4) and (6.5),

\[
c\mathcal W(T)
=
\frac{c\rho\mathcal W(T)}{1+c\rho\mathcal W(T)}.
\]

Since `mathcal W(T)>0`,

\[
\boxed{c\rho\mathcal W(T)=\rho-1.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus `mathcal W` and `B` are deterministic on survival, with

\[
B(T)=\frac{\rho-1}{\rho}=:b_*.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Let `K` be the number of surviving transmitted children. Every surviving child has conductance `b_*`, so (2.4) gives

\[
b_*=\frac{Kb_*}{1+Kb_*}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{K=\rho}
\tag{6.8}
\]

on survival. In particular `rho` is an integer at equality.

For a type-`i` parent, each potential child independently becomes a surviving transmitted child with probability

\[
a_i:=\sum_jq_jp_{ij}s_j.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Conditional on `nu=n`, `K` is `Bin(n,a_i)`. If `0<a_i<1`, any `n>=2` with positive source probability gives positive probabilities for both `K=1` and `K=2`, contradicting the deterministic value (6.8) after conditioning on survival. Hence `a_i=1` for every `i`.

Because `q_j>0` and `p_{ij}s_j<=1`, the identity

\[
1=a_i=\sum_jq_jp_{ij}s_j
\]

forces

\[
p_{ij}=1,\qquad s_j=1
\tag{6.10}
\]

for every `i,j`. Therefore every potential child is a surviving child and `K=nu`. Since survival is now certain and `K` is deterministic, `nu` itself is deterministic, say `nu\equiv k=rho>=2`. This proves necessity.

Conversely, if `nu\equiv k>=2` and every `p_ij=1`, the unlabelled tree is the deterministic regular `k`-ary tree. Every child conductance is equal, the Perron right vector can be chosen constant, and both `H` and `U` split mass uniformly as `1/k`. Hence equality holds. `\square`

### Corollary 6.2 — strict finite-type harmonic dimension drop

For every nonregular source law satisfying the hypotheses above,

\[
\boxed{
\dim_H(H_T)<\log\rho(M)
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

source-almost surely on survival. Equality occurs exactly in the deterministic regular no-thinning case (6.2).

This is the finite-type harmonic-measure dimension-drop subclause anticipated in Kumar's Section 12.3(6), under the source branching reduction. It is **not** a solution of the full boundary-fluctuation/multifractal problem, nor a claim about regimes where the branching reduction itself is unavailable.

## 7. A new sharp endpoint theorem for the Stieltjes renewal coordinate

The preceding report constructed the stationary density through a finite invariant vector measure `Lambda^*` on `[0,infinity)` satisfying

\[
\Lambda^*=\mathcal S\Lambda^*,
\qquad
\mathcal S=(I-\mathcal A_0)^{-1}\mathcal A_+,
\tag{7.1}
\]

where exact pipes act by

\[
(\mathcal A_0\Lambda)_j
=
\sum_iJ_{ij}(\tau_1)_*\Lambda_i,
\qquad
\tau_1(u)=u+1,
\tag{7.2}
\]

and genuine branches obey

\[
\Phi_R(u)=\frac{1+u}{1+(1+u)R}\le R^{-1},
\qquad
w_R(u)=\frac1{1+(1+u)R}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The previous report proved some exponential tail. The next theorem identifies the exact **pipe spectral barrier**.

Define the reciprocal-branch moment radius

\[
\Gamma_R
:=
\sup\left\{\theta>0:
\max_iE\left[\frac{e^{\theta/R_i}}{R_i};R_i>0\right]<\infty
\right\}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

The Palm estimate (2.9) implies `Gamma_R>0`.

### Theorem 7.1 — exponential moment below the pipe barrier

For every

\[
0<\theta<\min\{\Gamma_R,-\log\rho(J)\},
\tag{7.5}
\]

the invariant renewal measure has a finite `theta`-exponential moment:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_i\int e^{\theta u}\Lambda_i^*(du)<\infty.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

#### Proof

Choose `q` with

\[
\rho(J)<q<e^{-\theta}.
\]

Set

\[
c=(I-J/q)^{-1}\mathbf1>0.
\]

Then

\[
Jc=q(c-\mathbf1)\le qc.
\tag{7.7}
\]

For positive vector measures define

\[
\|\Lambda\|_{\theta,c}
:=
\sum_i c_i\int e^{\theta u}\Lambda_i(du).
\tag{7.8}
\]

The pipe translation gives

\[
\|\mathcal A_0\Lambda\|_{\theta,c}
=e^\theta\sum_i(Jc)_i\int e^{\theta u}\Lambda_i(du)
\le qe^\theta\|\Lambda\|_{\theta,c}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

For a genuine branch, (7.3) gives

\[
w_R(u)e^{\theta\Phi_R(u)}
\le
\frac{e^{\theta/R}}{(1+u)R}.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Let

\[
k_i(u)=E_i\frac{B}{1+Bu},
\qquad
b_i=E_iB>0.
\]

As in the preceding report,

\[
k_i(u)\ge\frac{b_i}{1+u}.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Therefore, for every positive `Lambda` normalized by the conserved source mass `mathfrak m(Lambda)=1`, finite dimensionality and (7.4) imply

\[
\|\mathcal A_+\Lambda\|_{\theta,c}
\le C_\theta
\sum_i\int\frac{\Lambda_i(du)}{1+u}
\le C'_\theta\mathfrak m(\Lambda)
=C'_\theta.
\tag{7.12}
\]

Applying the pipe Green operator and (7.9),

\[
\|\mathcal S\Lambda\|_{\theta,c}
\le
\frac{C'_\theta}{1-qe^\theta}.
\tag{7.13}
\]

Use `Lambda=Lambda^*`, for which `S Lambda^*=Lambda^*` and `mathfrak m(Lambda^*)=1`, to obtain (7.6). `\square`

### Theorem 7.2 — the pipe barrier is a genuine obstruction

Assume `J` is irreducible and `rho(J)>0`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_i\int e^{\theta u}\Lambda_i^*(du)=\infty
\qquad
\text{for every }\theta\ge-\log\rho(J).
}
\tag{7.14}
\]

Thus, if `Gamma_R>=-log rho(J)`, the exact exponential-moment radius of the renewal coordinate is `-log rho(J)`.

#### Proof

Let `c>0` be a Perron right eigenvector,

\[
Jc=\rho(J)c.
\tag{7.15}
\]

Set

\[
\Xi:=\mathcal A_+\Lambda^*.
\]

The source is supercritical and has positive probability of at least two surviving children, so the genuine-branch channel is nonzero and `Xi!=0`. From (7.1),

\[
\Lambda^*=\sum_{n\ge0}\mathcal A_0^n\Xi.
\tag{7.16}
\]

With the Perron weight in (7.15), the exact pipe translation yields

\[
\|\mathcal A_0^n\Xi\|_{\theta,c}
=
(\rho(J)e^\theta)^n\|\Xi\|_{\theta,c}.
\tag{7.17}
\]

If `||Xi||_{theta,c}=infinity`, (7.14) is immediate. Otherwise it is finite and strictly positive, and Tonelli plus (7.16) gives

\[
\|\Lambda^*\|_{\theta,c}
=
\|\Xi\|_{\theta,c}
\sum_{n\ge0}(\rho(J)e^\theta)^n.
\]

The series diverges whenever `rho(J)e^theta>=1`, proving (7.14). `\square`

This identifies a source-visible obstruction: even perfect genuine-branch resetting cannot give a renewal-coordinate exponential tail beyond the cost of arbitrarily long exact pipes.

## 8. Quantitative endpoint expansion of the stationary density

Recall

\[
g_i(b):=\frac{h_i(b)}b
=
\int_0^\infty\frac{1}{1+bu}\Lambda_i^*(du).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Fix any `theta` satisfying (7.5). From (7.6), for all integers `n>=0`,

\[
\int u^n\Lambda_i^*(du)
\le C_\theta n!\theta^{-n}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Using the finite geometric identity,

\[
\frac1{1+bu}
=
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(-bu)^k
+
\frac{(-bu)^N}{1+bu},
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
 g_i(b)
=
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(-1)^k m_{i,k}b^k+R_{i,N}(b),
\quad
|R_{i,N}(b)|
\le C_\theta N!\left(\frac b\theta\right)^N,
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

where

\[
m_{i,k}:=\int u^k\Lambda_i^*(du).
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
|g_i^{(n)}(0+)|
=n!m_{i,n}
\le C_\theta (n!)^2\theta^{-n}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Thus the stationary density has a quantitative Stieltjes/factorial endpoint expansion with Gevrey-2 derivative growth. I do **not** call this analyticity at `b=0`: unbounded support of `Lambda_i^*` can place Stieltjes singularities arbitrarily close to the origin on the negative real axis.
