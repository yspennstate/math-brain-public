## 2. Inherited setup

Let `D={1,...,d}`. A type-`i` parent has potential-child count `nu`, with

\[
G(z)=E[z^\nu],\qquad m_*=E\nu<\infty,

\[
M_{ij}=m_*q_jp_{ij},\qquad \rho(M)>1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For a surviving failed tree let

\[
B(T)=P_o^T(\text{never return through an added parent edge})

be the escape conductance/probability. If the surviving transmitted children have branch conductances `B_u`, then

\[
S(T)=\sum_u B_u,
\qquad
B(T)=F(S(T)),
\qquad
F(x)=\frac{x}{1+x}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The harmonic child flow is

\[
H_T(u)=\frac{B_u}{S(T)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Previous C06 reports constructed and proved internally a canonical source-equivalent stationary and ergodic harmonic-descendant law

\[
\mu(i,dT)=h_i(B(T))P_i(dT),
\tag{2.4}

where

\[
h_i(b)=b\,g_i(b),
\qquad
0<A_i\le g_i(b)\le L_i<\infty
\quad(0\le b\le1).
\tag{2.5}

The Stieltjes-renewal construction further gives `g_i in C^infinity([0,1])` with every derivative bounded. The transfer fixed-point equation is

## 3. Exact Markov time reversal of the harmonic conductance process

Let `beta_i` denote the source law of `B(T)` under `P_i`. Project the stationary harmonic environment to type and conductance:

\[
\bar\mu_i(db)=h_i(b)\,\beta_i(db).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is a probability measure after summing over `i` by the normalization of `h`.

Consider one stationary harmonic edge: a current parent of type `i`, a harmonically selected child of type `j` with subtree conductance `b`, and Palm sibling total `R_i`. Campbell's formula gives the unnormalized joint weight

\[
M_{ij}\,P_j(dT')\,P(R_i\in dr)\,
 h_i(F(b+r))\frac{b}{b+r}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The marginal in the child subtree is exactly `h_j(b)P_j(dT')` by stationarity. Hence the conditional law of the **parent** given the child depends on the child subtree only through `(j,b)`. Therefore the time reversal of the projected process is Markov.

### Theorem 3.1 — reverse conductance kernel

For `0<b<1`, define

\[
\boxed{
\widehat Q((j,b),d(i,r))
=
\frac{M_{ij}\,b}{h_j(b)}
\frac{h_i(F(b+r))}{b+r}
P(R_i\in dr).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

With `h=bg`, this simplifies to

\[
\boxed{
\widehat Q((j,b),d(i,r))
=
\frac{M_{ij}}{g_j(b)}
\frac{g_i(F(b+r))}{1+b+r}
P(R_i\in dr).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The next reversed conductance is

\[
b'=F(b+r).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equation (2.6) says exactly that (3.4) has total mass one. Because `g_j` is positive and continuous, (3.4) extends continuously to `b=0` and `b=1`, giving a Markov kernel on the compact state space

\[
E=D\times[0,1].
\]

For vector functions `f=(f_i)`, define the Palm transfer

\[
(\mathcal T f)_j(b)
=
\sum_iM_{ij}E\left[
\frac{f_i(F(b+R_i))}{1+b+R_i}
\right].
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then `Tg=g` and the reverse Markov operator is the exact Doob transform

\[
\boxed{
(\widehat Q f)_j(b)
=
\frac{1}{g_j(b)}\,
\mathcal T(gf)_j(b).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus the previously constructed stationary density does more than prove existence: it conjugates the reverse harmonic conductance chain to the original Palm transfer operator.

### Why the reverse projection is enough

Under the stationary harmonic-ray law, the full forward environment chain is Markov and ergodic. Its time reversal is therefore Markov. Formula (3.2) shows that after projection to `(type,B)`, the reversed transition still depends only on the projected current state, hence `(type,B)` is itself a Markov chain in reverse time with kernel `widehat Q`. Since the observable `Phi=-log(1-B)` depends only on `B`, every stationary block of `Phi` may be analysed with `widehat Q`. Additive sums are unchanged by reversing the order of a finite block.

## 4. High derivatives see exactly the extinction Jacobian

The core new spectral fact is that sufficiently high derivatives suppress every genuine-branch channel and leave only the exact-pipe Jacobian `J`.

For a scalar `f`, set

\[
\mathcal Rf(x)
:=\frac{1}{1+x}f(F(x)).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Put `y=F(x)`, so `1-y=(1+x)^{-1}` and

\[
\frac{d}{dx}=(1-y)^2\frac{d}{dy}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

By induction, for each integer `k>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d^k}{dx^k}\mathcal Rf(x)
=(1+x)^{-(2k+1)}f^{(k)}(F(x))
+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}c_{k\ell}(x)f^{(\ell)}(F(x)),
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

where every `c_{k\ell}` is bounded on `[0,infinity)`. The highest derivative coefficient is exact; each differentiation contributes two powers through `d/dx=(1-y)^2d/dy`, while the original prefactor contributes one.

Define the positive matrix

\[
A^{(k)}_{ij}
:=M_{ij}E[(1+R_i)^{-(2k+1)}].
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `0<=(1+R_i)^{-(2k+1)}<=1`, dominated convergence gives

\[
\boxed{
A^{(k)}_{ij}\longrightarrow M_{ij}P(R_i=0)=J_{ij}
\qquad(k\to\infty).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because the type space is finite and `rho(J)<1`, there exists a finite `k_0` such that

\[
\boxed{\rho(A^{(k_0)})<1.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the key bridge from the old exact-pipe Jacobian to a genuine spectral statement for the harmonic conductance dynamics.

## 5. Doeblin--Fortet inequality and quasi-compactness

Fix `k=k_0`. Since `A^{(k)}` is strictly positive, choose a positive weight vector `w` and `alpha_0<1` such that

\[
(A^{(k)})^Tw\le\alpha_0w.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a vector `f=(f_i)` define

\[
|f|_{k,w}
:=\max_i w_i^{-1}\|f_i^{(k)}\|_\infty,
\qquad
\|f\|_0:=\max_i\|f_i\|_\infty.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Using (4.3)--(4.4),

\[
|\mathcal T f|_{k,w}
\le
\alpha_0|f|_{k,w}
+C_k\max_{0\le\ell<k}\max_i\|f_i^{(\ell)}\|_\infty.
\tag{5.3}
\]

On a compact interval, the Landau--Kolmogorov interpolation inequality gives, for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
\max_{\ell<k}\|f^{(\ell)}\|_\infty
\le
\epsilon\|f^{(k)}\|_\infty
+C_{\epsilon,k}\|f\|_\infty.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Choose `epsilon` so small that `alpha:=alpha_0+C_k epsilon<1`. After adjusting constants and weights,

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal T f|_{k,w}
\le
\alpha |f|_{k,w}+C\|f\|_0,
\qquad \alpha<1.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The weak norm is uniformly power-bounded. Indeed (3.7) gives

\[
\mathcal T^n
=M_g\widehat Q^{\,n}M_{g^{-1}},
\tag{5.6}
\]

where `M_g` is multiplication by `g`. Since `widehat Q` is Markov and `0<A_i<=g_i<=L_i`,

\[
\sup_{n\ge0}\|\mathcal T^n\|_{C^0\to C^0}<\infty.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Use the strong norm

\[
\|f\|_*:=\|f\|_0+|f|_{k,w}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

By (5.4), this norm controls all intermediate derivatives and is equivalent to the usual vector `C^k` norm. Its unit ball is relatively compact in `C^0` by Arzela--Ascoli. Equations (5.5) and (5.7) are therefore a standard Doeblin--Fortet/Ionescu--Tulcea--Marinescu pair, yielding

\[
\boxed{
 r_{\rm ess}(\mathcal T:C^k\to C^k)<1.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Conjugacy (3.7), with `g,g^{-1} in C^k`, gives the same conclusion for the reverse Markov operator:

\[
\boxed{
 r_{\rm ess}(\widehat Q:C^k(E)\to C^k(E))<1.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

This is a genuine spectral-gap mechanism: long exact-pipe stretches are the only channel that survives high differentiation, and their matrix is precisely the already-subcritical `J`.

## 6. No nontrivial unit-circle eigenvalues

Quasi-compactness alone permits finitely many peripheral eigenvalues. Here the source positivity rules them out directly.

Suppose

\[
\widehat Q f=\lambda f,
\qquad |\lambda|=1,
\tag{6.1}
\]

with a nonzero continuous complex-valued vector function. Choose `(j,b)` where `|f_j(b)|` attains its global maximum `M>0`. Since `widehat Q` is Markov,

\[
M=|\lambda f_j(b)|=|\widehat Q f(j,b)|
\le \widehat Q|f|(j,b)\le M.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Equality in the triangle inequality forces every successor in the support of the transition kernel to have the same phase and maximal modulus. In particular `M_{jj}>0`, so for every `r` in the support of the type-`j` Palm sibling law,

\[
 f_j(F(b+r))=\lambda f_j(b).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Fix one such `r`. Repeating the same self-type support transition gives

\[
 f_j(T_r^n(b))=\lambda^n f_j(b),
\qquad
T_r(x)=F(x+r).
\tag{6.4}
\]

If `r>0`, `T_r` is a strict contraction because

\[
0<T_r'(x)=(1+x+r)^{-2}\le(1+r)^{-2}<1.
\]

If `r=0`,

\[
T_0^n(b)=\frac{b}{1+nb}\to0.
\]

Thus in every case `T_r^n(b)` converges. Continuity of `f` implies the left side of (6.4) converges. Since `|f_j(b)|=M>0`, the sequence `lambda^n` must converge, which on the unit circle is possible only for

\[
\boxed{\lambda=1.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

There are no nontrivial Jordan blocks at eigenvalue one: a generalized eigenvector would force polynomial growth of `widehat Q^n f` in the sup norm, contradicting the Markov contraction `||widehat Q^n f||_infinity<=||f||_infinity`.

Therefore the only peripheral spectral value is `1`.

The stationary harmonic tree chain is ergodic from the previous C06 theorem. A factor of an ergodic stationary process is ergodic, and time reversal preserves ergodicity. Hence every `widehat Q`-invariant function is constant `bar mu`-almost surely. Let `Pi` be the spectral projection at eigenvalue one. For every smooth `f` with `bar mu(f)=0`,

\[
\Pi f=0\qquad\bar\mu\text{-a.s.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Combining this with quasi-compactness yields constants `C<infinity` and `r in (0,1)` such that

\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat Q^{\,n}f\|_{L^2(\bar\mu)}
\le Cr^n\|f\|_*
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

for every centered `f in C^k(E)`.

## 7. The electrical observable has an exponential stationary tail

The target observable is

\[
\phi(b):=-\log(1-b).
\tag{7.1}
\]

On an actual source tree,

\[
e^{\Phi(T)}=\frac1{1-B(T)}=1+S(T)\le1+\nu.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The stationary density obeys `h_i(B)<=L_iB<=L_i`. Since `E nu<infinity`,

\[
\boxed{
E_{\bar\mu}[e^{\phi(B)}]<\infty.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus `phi` has an exponential tail under the stationary harmonic law even though it is unbounded as `b approaches 1`.

Choose a fixed smooth truncation profile and let `phi_M` agree with `phi` on `{phi<=M}`, become constant on `{phi>=M+1}`, and interpolate smoothly in between. It extends to a `C^infinity` function on `[0,1]`. Faà di Bruno together with

\[
\phi^{(m)}(b)=\frac{(m-1)!}{(1-b)^m}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\phi_M\|_*\le C_k e^{kM}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Also, using `x^2<=C e^{x/2}` and (7.3),

\[
\boxed{
\|\phi-\phi_M\|_{L^2(\bar\mu)}
\le Ce^{-M/4}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The constants are source-dependent but independent of `M`.

## 8. Exponential reverse-Gordin estimate

Let

\[
d_H=E_{\bar\mu}\phi(B)=E_\mu\Phi,
\qquad
\bar\phi=\phi-d_H.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Because a Markov operator is an `L^2` contraction under its invariant measure,

\[
\|\widehat Q^n f\|_2\le\|f\|_2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Center `phi_M` by `bar mu(phi_M)`. From (6.7), (7.4), and (7.5),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\widehat Q^n\bar\phi\|_2
&\le
2\|\phi-\phi_M\|_2
+
\|\widehat Q^n(\phi_M-\bar\mu\phi_M)\|_2\\
&\le
Ce^{-M/4}+Cr^n e^{kM}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Choose

\[
M=\eta n,
\qquad
0<\eta<\frac{-\log r}{2k}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Then both terms decay exponentially. Hence there exist `C,c>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat Q^n\bar\phi\|_{L^2(\bar\mu)}
\le Ce^{-cn}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\ge0}
\|\widehat Q^n\bar\phi\|_2<\infty.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

This is the reverse-time Gordin condition that the preceding report was missing. It is stronger than covariance summability. Indeed

\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{Cov}(\Phi_0,\Phi_n)|
\le
\|\bar\phi\|_2\,
\|\widehat Q^n\bar\phi\|_2
\le Ce^{-cn}.
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

Thus the stationary electrical information potential has exponentially decaying correlations.

## 9. Harmonic-information central limit theorem

Define

\[
u
:=\sum_{n=0}^\infty \widehat Q^n\bar\phi
\in L^2(\bar\mu).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Then

\[
\bar\phi=u-\widehat Q u.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Let `(Y_n)` be the stationary **reversed** type-conductance Markov chain with kernel `widehat Q`. Set

\[
D_{n+1}=u(Y_{n+1})-\widehat Q u(Y_n).
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then `(D_n)` is a stationary ergodic square-integrable martingale-difference sequence and

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\bar\phi(Y_r)
=
\sum_{r=1}^{n}D_r
+u(Y_0)-u(Y_n).
\tag{9.4}
\]

The stationary martingale CLT applies, giving

\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\bar\phi(Y_r)
\Longrightarrow N(0,\sigma_H^2),
\tag{9.5}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_H^2
=E_{\bar\mu}[u^2-(\widehat Q u)^2]
\ge0.
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

Because of (8.7), the Green--Kubo series converges absolutely and

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_H^2
=\operatorname{Var}_{\bar\mu}(\phi)
+2\sum_{n\ge1}
\operatorname{Cov}(\Phi_0,\Phi_n).
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

A finite stationary block has the same additive sum after reversing its order. Therefore the law of the sum of `Phi-d_H` along the original forward harmonic ray is the same as the corresponding sum under the reversed stationary chain. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\sqrt n}
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}
(\Phi(T_{X_r})-d_H)
\Longrightarrow N(0,\sigma_H^2).
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

Finally, the exact electrical coboundary from the preceding run gives

\[
I_n:=-\log\theta_T(X_n)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\Phi(T_{X_r})
+\log B(T)-\log B(T_{X_n}).
\tag{9.9}
\]

The inherited inverse-conductance bounds imply `log B in L^2(mu)`, so the endpoint divided by `sqrt n` tends to zero in `L^2`. Slutsky therefore yields the main theorem of this run:

### Theorem 9.1 — finite-type harmonic-information CLT

Under the finite-type branching/Kesten--Stigum assumptions used throughout the C06 H0008 harmonic line,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{I_n-n d_H}{\sqrt n}
\Longrightarrow
N(0,\sigma_H^2),
\qquad
 d_H=E_\mu[-\log(1-B)].
}
\tag{9.10}
\]

The variance is given by (9.6)--(9.7). It may vanish in a degenerate source (in particular on the deterministic regular no-thinning equality model); strict positivity of `sigma_H^2` for every nonregular source is **not** proved here.

This theorem is `PROVED PARTIAL internally`: it is a complete fluctuation theorem for the finite-type harmonic-measure subproblem after Kumar's branching reduction, but it has not yet received a distinct independent audit and it does not solve the full Section 12.3(6) multifractal programme.

## 11. Material failed/avoided routes

### 11.1 Forward two-spine/Gordin norm

The earlier natural target was to prove a direct bound on `||K_H^n bar Phi||_2`. That forces control of how an entire descendant subtree retains information about its ancestor and reintroduces the pair-spine endpoint-integrability difficulties. The reverse Palm kernel eliminates this unnecessary state space: ancestor generation given a child subtree depends on the child only through its type and scalar conductance.

### 11.2 One-step Doeblin minorization

A total-variation Doeblin argument is not source-robust because the Palm sibling conductance distribution can be singular or discrete, so different incoming conductances can push forward mutually poorly aligned measures. The high-derivative transfer argument does not require a density, an atom, or an absolutely continuous sibling law.

### 11.3 Naive uniform contraction

The map `b -> F(b+R)` has derivative arbitrarily close to one on the exact-pipe channel `R=0` near `b=0`; a uniform one-step Wasserstein contraction is therefore false in the natural Euclidean metric. The matrix limit `A^(k)->J` is the correct replacement: it sums the noncontracting pipe channel through its true multitype spectral weight.
