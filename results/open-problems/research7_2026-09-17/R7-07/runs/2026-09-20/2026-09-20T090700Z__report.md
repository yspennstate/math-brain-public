## 3. The singular Feynman--Kac twist regularizes exactly after one reverse step

A direct multiplication by `exp(z phi(b))=(1-b)^{-z}` is unbounded on `C^k` when `Re z>0`; this was the apparent obstruction left by the preceding report. The reverse electrical kernel cancels it exactly.

For complex `z`, define the next-state Feynman--Kac operator

\[
\widehat Q_zf
:=
\widehat Q(e^{z\phi}f).
\tag{3.1}
\]

If `b'=F(b+r)`, then

\[
1-b'=\frac1{1+b+r},
\qquad
e^{z\phi(b')}=(1+b+r)^z.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Substituting (3.2) into (2.5),

\[
\boxed{
(\widehat Q_zf)_j(b)
=
\frac1{g_j(b)}
\sum_iM_{ij}E\left[
(1+b+R_i)^{z-1}
 g_i(F(b+R_i))
 f_i(F(b+R_i))
\right].
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Define the unconjugated tilted Palm operator

\[
(\mathcal T_zF)_j(b)
:=
\sum_iM_{ij}E\left[
(1+b+R_i)^{z-1}F_i(F(b+R_i))
\right].
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\widehat Q_z=M_{g^{-1}}\mathcal T_zM_g.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

At `z=0`, this is exactly the old Doob conjugacy `widehat Q=M_{g^{-1}} T M_g`.

### Theorem 3.1 — operator analyticity on the full half-plane `Re z < 1`

For the same finite `k` used in the inherited spectral-gap theorem,

\[
\boxed{
z\mapsto\mathcal T_z\in\mathcal B(C^k,C^k)
\text{ is holomorphic on }\{\operatorname{Re}z<1\}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The same is therefore true for `z -> widehat Q_z`.

#### Proof

Fix a compact set `K subset {Re z < 1}` and let

\[
\delta:=\sup_{z\in K}\operatorname{Re}z<1.
\]

Put `x=1+b+r>=1`. Every `m`-th `z` derivative of the scalar kernel is

\[
\partial_z^m x^{z-1}=x^{z-1}(\log x)^m.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Every `b` derivative either differentiates this factor, gaining a further negative power of `x`, or differentiates the composition `F(b+r)`, whose derivatives satisfy

\[
|F^{(ell)}(b+r)|\le C_\ell x^{-\ell-1}
\qquad(\ell\ge1).
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus for each fixed `m` and each derivative order at most `k`, the operator kernel is bounded by a finite linear combination of

\[
x^{\delta-1-a}(\log x)^m,
\qquad a\ge0.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Since `delta<1`,

\[
\sup_{x\ge1}x^{\delta-1-a}(\log x)^m<\infty.
\tag{3.10}
\]

The type space is finite and `M` is finite, so differentiation under the expectation is justified without any extra moment assumption on `R_i`. Uniform convergence of the operator-valued Taylor series on compact subsets follows from the same bounds. This proves (3.6). Multiplication by the fixed positive `C^k` functions `g` and `g^{-1}` preserves holomorphy, giving the assertion for `widehat Q_z`. `square`

This is the main structural observation of the run: the singular potential does **not** require a new weighted Banach space near zero. One reverse electrical step converts it to the bounded analytic kernel `x^{z-1}`.

## 4. Analytic leading eigenvalue and stationary pressure

At `z=0`, `widehat Q_0=widehat Q` has a simple isolated eigenvalue `1` and a genuine spectral gap on `C^k`. Analytic perturbation of an isolated simple eigenvalue therefore gives an `epsilon>0`, an analytic eigenvalue `lambda(z)`, analytic rank-one projection `Pi_z`, and operators `N_z` such that for `|z|<epsilon`,

\[
\lambda(0)=1,
\qquad
\widehat Q_z^n
=
\lambda(z)^n\Pi_z+N_z^n,
\tag{4.1}
\]

with constants `C<infinity` and `rho_*<1` satisfying

\[
\sup_{|z|<\epsilon}\|N_z^n\|_{C^k\to C^k}
\le C\rho_*^n.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For real `t` sufficiently near zero, positivity and continuity imply `lambda(t)>0`.

Let `(Y_n)` be the stationary reverse conductance chain and define

\[
S_n:=\sum_{r=1}^n\phi(Y_r).
\tag{4.3}
\]

By construction of the next-state Feynman--Kac operator,

\[
E_{\bar\mu}[e^{tS_n}]
=
\bar\mu(\widehat Q_t^n1).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `c(t):=bar mu(Pi_t 1)` is analytic and `c(0)=1`, it is nonzero for small real `t`. Equations (4.1)--(4.4) give

\[
E_{\bar\mu}[e^{tS_n}]
=
c(t)\lambda(t)^n+O(\rho_*^n).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Hence the stationary electrical pressure exists and is analytic:

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(t)
:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log E_{\bar\mu}[e^{tS_n}]
=\log\lambda(t)
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

for all real `|t|<epsilon_0` with `epsilon_0>0` sufficiently small.

In fact (4.5) is uniform for complex `z` in a smaller disk. Consequently every fixed-order cumulant has a linear asymptotic:

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_m(S_n)=n\Lambda^{(m)}(0)+O(1)
\qquad(m\ge1\text{ fixed}).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

No additional offspring moment is required for this local analytic statement; the regularization in Section 3 is what makes the operator derivatives bounded.

## 5. First and second pressure derivatives are exactly `d_H` and `sigma_H^2`

Write `P=widehat Q`, `mu=bar mu`, and

\[
h:=\phi-d_H.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Normalize the analytic right eigenfunction `v_t` by

\[
P_tv_t=\lambda(t)v_t,
\qquad
\mu(v_t)=1,
\qquad
v_0=1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The operator derivatives at zero are

\[
P'_0f=P(\phi f),
\qquad
P''_0f=P(\phi^2f),
\tag{5.3}
\]

interpreted through the regularized formula (3.3). Although `phi` itself is unbounded at `b=1`, both operators are bounded `C^k -> C^k` by Theorem 3.1.

Applying the invariant functional `mu` to the first derivative of (5.2),

\[
\boxed{
\lambda'(0)=\mu(P\phi)=\mu\phi=d_H.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The derivative of the right eigenfunction satisfies

\[
(I-P)v'_0=P\phi-d_H=Ph,
\qquad
\mu(v'_0)=0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The inherited Gordin summability gives

\[
u_G:=\sum_{n\ge0}P^nh\in L^2(\mu).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore `u_G-h=sum_{n>=1}P^nh` is a centered `L^2` solution of (5.5). The analytic eigenvector derivative `v'_0` is another centered solution. Their difference is a centered `P`-harmonic function, hence zero by ergodicity. Thus

\[
\boxed{
v'_0=\sum_{n\ge1}P^nh
\quad\text{in }L^2(\mu).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Differentiate (5.2) twice and apply `mu`. Since `mu(v'_0)=0`,

\[
\lambda''(0)
=\mu(\phi^2)+2\mu(\phi v'_0).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Subtracting `(lambda'(0))^2=d_H^2` and using (5.7),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda''(0)
&=\lambda''(0)-\lambda'(0)^2\\
&=\mu(h^2)+2\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(hP^nh).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.9}
\]

The right side is exactly the Green--Kubo / martingale variance from the preceding CLT report. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda'(0)=d_H,
\qquad
\Lambda''(0)=\sigma_H^2.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Combining with the previous variance-rigidity theorem,

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda''(0)>0
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

for every nonregular finite-type source.

Hence for such a source,

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(t)
=d_Ht+\frac12\sigma_H^2t^2+O(t^3)
\qquad(t\to0).
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is the leading-eigenvalue expansion requested by the previous run.

## 6. The electrical endpoint does not change the local pressure

The actual harmonic cylinder information differs from the stationary electrical sum by

\[
D_n:=I_n-\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\phi(B_r)
=\log B_0-\log B_n.
\tag{6.1}
\]

By (2.11), for every `a>0`,

\[
\sup_nE[e^{a|D_n|}]
\le
\sup_nE[(B_0B_n)^{-a}]
\le
E[B^{-2a}]<\infty,
\tag{6.2}
\]

where the last inequality uses Cauchy--Schwarz and stationarity.

Let

\[
\Lambda_I(t)
:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log E[e^{tI_n}]
\tag{6.3}
\]

whenever the limit exists. The uniformly exponentially integrable endpoint forces equality with the additive pressure.

### Lemma 6.1 — pressure invariance under the electrical coboundary

For every real `t` sufficiently near zero,

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_I(t)=\Lambda(t)=\log\lambda(t).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof

The forward stationary block of `phi` has the same law as the reversed block defining `S_n`, so its pressure is `Lambda`.

Fix `p>1` with conjugate exponent `q=p/(p-1)`. Holder and (6.2) give

\[
E[e^{tI_n}]
\le
\bigl(E[e^{ptS_n}]\bigr)^{1/p}
\bigl(E[e^{qtD_n}]\bigr)^{1/q},
\]

hence

\[
\limsup_n\frac1n\log E[e^{tI_n}]
\le\frac1p\Lambda(pt).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Conversely, using `S_n=I_n-D_n` with `s=t/p`,

\[
E[e^{sS_n}]
\le
\bigl(E[e^{tI_n}]\bigr)^{1/p}
\bigl(E[e^{-qsD_n}]\bigr)^{1/q},
\]

so

\[
\liminf_n\frac1n\log E[e^{tI_n}]
\ge p\Lambda(t/p).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Let `p downarrow 1`. Analyticity, hence continuity, of `Lambda` turns both bounds into `Lambda(t)`. `square`

Thus the same analytic pressure controls the actual harmonic cylinder information.

## 7. Stationary annealed harmonic Renyi pressure near `q=1`

For a surviving tree let

\[
Z_n(q;T)
:=\sum_{|v|=n}\theta_T(v)^q,
\tag{7.1}
\]

where `theta_T(v)` is the harmonic cylinder mass at level `n`.

If `V_n` is sampled from harmonic measure conditional on `T`, then

\[
P(V_n=v\mid T)=\theta_T(v),
\]

and therefore

\[
Z_n(q;T)
=E\left[
\theta_T(V_n)^{q-1}\mid T
\right]
=E\left[e^{-(q-1)I_n}\mid T\right].
\tag{7.2}
\]

Define the **stationary annealed harmonic Renyi pressure**

\[
\Psi_H(q)
:=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac1n
\log E_\mu[Z_n(q;T)].
\tag{7.3}
\]

By (6.4) and (7.2), for `q` in a neighborhood of `1`,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H(q)=\Lambda(1-q)=\log\lambda(1-q).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Hence `Psi_H` is real analytic near `q=1`, with

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H(1)=0,
\qquad
\Psi_H'(1)=-d_H,
\qquad
\Psi_H''(1)=\sigma_H^2.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

For every nonregular source,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H''(1)>0,
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

so the stationary annealed harmonic Renyi pressure is strictly convex on some neighborhood of `q=1`. More explicitly,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H(q)
=-(q-1)d_H
+\frac12\sigma_H^2(q-1)^2
+O(|q-1|^3).
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

This is a genuine new bridge toward the source's harmonic boundary-fluctuation clause. It is **not** yet a quenched almost-sure Renyi spectrum and therefore is **not** the full harmonic multifractal theorem.

## 8. Gaussian moderate deviations for harmonic information

Let `(a_n)` be any deterministic sequence with

\[
a_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{a_n}{\sqrt n}\to0.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For the reverse additive sum define

\[
X_n
:=
\frac{S_n-nd_H}{a_n\sqrt n}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For fixed real `theta`, put

\[
t_n=\theta\frac{a_n}{\sqrt n}\to0.
\tag{8.3}
\]

The uniform spectral decomposition (4.5) gives

\[
\log E[e^{t_nS_n}]
=n\Lambda(t_n)+O(1).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Therefore, using (5.12),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{a_n^2}
\log E[e^{\theta a_n^2X_n}]
&=
\frac{n}{a_n^2}
\bigl(\Lambda(t_n)-d_Ht_n\bigr)+o(1)\\
&=
\frac12\sigma_H^2\theta^2
+O\!\left(\frac{a_n}{\sqrt n}\right)
+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac1{a_n^2}
\log E[e^{\theta a_n^2X_n}]
=
\frac12\sigma_H^2\theta^2.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

For a nonregular source `sigma_H^2>0`, so the differentiable Gärtner--Ellis theorem yields an MDP with speed `a_n^2` and rate

\[
\boxed{
J(x)=\frac{x^2}{2\sigma_H^2}.
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

The same MDP holds for the actual cylinder information

\[
\boxed{
\frac{I_n-nd_H}{a_n\sqrt n}
}
\tag{8.8}
\]

because the electrical endpoint is exponentially negligible at speed `a_n^2`. Indeed, for every `epsilon,a>0`,

\[
P(|D_n|>\epsilon a_n\sqrt n)
\le
E[e^{a|D_n|}]
 e^{-a\epsilon a_n\sqrt n},
\]

and hence

\[
\frac1{a_n^2}
\log P(|D_n|>\epsilon a_n\sqrt n)
\le
- a\epsilon\frac{\sqrt n}{a_n}+o(1)
\to-\infty.
\tag{8.9}
\]

Thus the stationary harmonic-information FCLT from the preceding run now sits inside a complete Gaussian moderate-deviation window.

## 9. Local information-rate curvature

Because `Lambda` is analytic and `Lambda''(0)=sigma_H^2>0` in every nonregular model, its local Legendre transform

\[
\mathcal I(a)
:=\sup_{|t|<\epsilon_0}\{ta-\Lambda(t)\}
\tag{9.1}
\]

is smooth for `a` in a neighborhood of `d_H`. The implicit-function theorem applied to `Lambda'(t)=a` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I(d_H+\delta)
=
\frac{\delta^2}{2\sigma_H^2}
+O(|\delta|^3).
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is an **information-rate** curvature statement for a harmonic ray under the stationary environment. It should not be confused with a proof of the quenched Hausdorff multifractal spectrum of a fixed random tree.
