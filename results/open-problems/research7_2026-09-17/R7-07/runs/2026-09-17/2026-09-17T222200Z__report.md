## 5. B11 — dualize irregularity to a degree-scale transport matrix

### 5.1 Idea before proof

Do not pigeonhole a globally heavy dyadic degree cell. That would lose a factor equal to the number of scales. Instead view the edge set as a transport plan between the dyadic degree scales on the two sides. A cell is useful when it is a fixed fraction of both its row and its column, even if it is a tiny fraction of the whole graph. Such a cell should have enough internal degree on both sides to survive peeling.

Let general lower scales be

```text
R_0=r_0d^2,  S_0=s_0d,
```

and partition by total `J`-degree:

```text
A_i={a: R_i<=deg_J(a)<2R_i},     R_i=2^iR_0,
B_j={b: S_j<=deg_J(b)<2S_j},     S_j=2^jS_0.
```

Write

```text
e_ij=e(A_i,B_j),
e_i=sum_j e_ij,
f_j=sum_i e_ij.                                         (5.1)
```

### Lemma 5.1 — balanced-cell peeling

If, for fixed `alpha,beta>0`,

```text
e_ij >= alpha e_i,
e_ij >= beta f_j,                                       (5.2)
```

then `J[A_i,B_j]` contains a subgraph with more than `e_ij/2` edges and sidewise degree windows

```text
(alpha/4)R_i <= deg(a) < 2R_i,
(beta /4)S_j <= deg(b) < 2S_j.                          (5.3)
```

#### Proof

Repeatedly delete an `A_i` vertex of current cell-degree below `(alpha/4)R_i` or a `B_j` vertex below `(beta/4)S_j`. Since

```text
|A_i|R_i <= e_i,
|B_j|S_j <= f_j,
```

all `A` deletions remove fewer than

```text
(alpha/4)|A_i|R_i <= (alpha/4)e_i <= e_ij/4
```

edges, and all `B` deletions likewise remove fewer than `e_ij/4`. More than half the cell remains, with (5.3). The upper bounds come directly from the dyadic bins. `QED`

Thus a balanced cell has fixed degree ratios at most `8/alpha` and `8/beta`. What remained was to prove that fixed degree ratios, rather than exact degrees, still suffice probabilistically.

## 6. B11 continued — exact biregularity is not needed

### Theorem 6.1 — arbitrary-overlap bounded-ratio cross cores

Fix `epsilon,r_0,s_0>0` and finite `Lambda>=1`. For all sufficiently large `d`, suppose

```text
R <= deg_J(a) <= Lambda R,
S <= deg_J(b) <= Lambda S,
R >= r_0d^2,
S >= s_0d.                                               (6.1)
```

Then at `p=(1+epsilon)/d`, along every sequence with `|A|+|B|->infinity`, `J_p` a.a.s. contains a simple cycle of length `Omega(d)`.

A full proof is in the additive artifact created this run. The mechanism is as follows.

Let `m=|A|`, `M=|B|`. Degree comparison gives

```text
M >= mR/(Lambda S).                                     (6.2)
```

Partition `A` into

```text
h=max(1,floor(S/d))
```

blocks. A deterministic coloring can be chosen so that in at least half the blocks, a fixed positive fraction of all connectors have localized host degree in

```text
[ad,b_0d],                                               (6.3)
```

where `a>0,b_0<infinity` depend only on `s_0,Lambda`, and each such block satisfies

```text
|U| <= 4md/S.                                            (6.4)
```

After percolation, every good connector with at least two open neighbors in `U` selects exactly one open pair uniformly. Let `G_U` be the simple selected-pair graph. A connector chooses at most one pair in the block, so every simple cycle in `G_U` has pairwise distinct connector witnesses and lifts to a simple bipartite cycle.

For a localized connector degree `t in [ad,b_0d]`,

```text
q_t=P(Bin(t,p)>=2) >= q_*>0,                             (6.5)
```

while any particular pair has selection probability at most `C_*/d^2`. Since an `A`-pair has at most `Lambda R` common connectors, its total selection load is at most

```text
L=C_*Lambda R/d^2.                                      (6.6)
```

But connector-first mass gives

```text
sum_f x_f >= q_* M/2 >= q_*mR/(2Lambda S).              (6.7)
```

Concave occupancy then cancels the potentially unbounded `R/d^2`:

```text
E e(G_U)
 >= c m d^2/S                                           (6.8)
```

for a fixed `c>0`. Pair indicators have nonpositive covariance because one connector cannot select two different pairs in one block, so

```text
Var e(G_U) <= E e(G_U).                                 (6.9)
```

Consequently a good block has projected average degree `Omega(d)` with probability at least

```text
1-O(S/(md^2)).                                          (6.10)
```

A longest-path argument supplies an `Omega(d)` projected cycle and therefore an `Omega(d)` simple cycle in `J_p`.

The a.a.s. quantifier is uniform: if `d->infinity`, one block succeeds by (6.10); if `d` is bounded and `S/d->infinity`, many disjoint `A` blocks give independent trials; if `d,S` are bounded and `m->infinity`, the sharper form of (6.10) tends to one; and if `d,S,m` are all bounded while host order grows, one repeated connector-neighborhood type supplies an exact twin-amplification cycle.

This fully proves Theorem 6.1. No degree-ratio, codegree, or pseudorandomness factor is hidden in a numerical experiment.

**B11 outcome:** the recovery theorem is extended from exact biregularity to every fixed two-sided degree ratio, and the irregular problem is converted to a transport-cell question.

## 7. B28 — replace global regularity by what a typical incidence sees

Normalize the degree-cell matrix:

```text
P_ij=e_ij/E,
P_i=e_i/E,
Q_j=f_j/E,                                               (7.1)
```

where `E=e(J)`. Under the law of a uniformly sampled host incidence, define

```text
alpha_ij=P_ij/P_i=P(J=j | I=i),
beta_ij =P_ij/Q_j=P(I=i | J=j).                          (7.2)
```

The B11 balanced-cell condition is exactly that both conditional probabilities are bounded below.

Define the bidirectional conditional scale entropy

```text
H_bi:=H(J|I)+H(I|J)
     =sum_ij P_ij[-log alpha_ij-log beta_ij].             (7.3)
```

### Theorem 7.1 — bounded bidirectional scale entropy closes the cross branch

In the cumulative G0011 cross core (4.1), if `H_bi` remains bounded along a sequence, then `J_p` contains an `Omega(d)` simple cycle a.a.s. at the source probability.

#### Proof

If `H_bi<=H_0`, Markov applied to

```text
X=-log(alpha_IJ beta_IJ)
```

gives

```text
P(X<=2H_0)>=1/2.                                        (7.4)
```

On this event each of `alpha_IJ,beta_IJ` is at least `exp(-2H_0)`. Thus at least half the cross-edge mass lies in cells balanced in both directions by one fixed constant.

There are only `O((log n)^2)` dyadic cells because all degrees are at most `n-1`. Since the cross core carries `Omega(nd)` edges, some balanced cell has

```text
Omega(nd/(log n)^2) -> infinity                         (7.5)
```

edges. Lemma 5.1 peels it to a growing bounded-ratio core, and Theorem 6.1 gives the desired source-scale cycle. `QED`

Therefore any unresolved sequence in this cross branch must satisfy

```text
H(J|I)+H(I|J) -> infinity.                              (7.6)
```

This is stronger than “many degree scales occur.” A diagonal collection of arbitrarily many scales still has very small conditional entropy and is now solved. The true remaining obstruction requires a typical incidence to see increasingly diffuse scale mixing from both endpoints.

**B28 outcome:** an entire multiscale class, including arbitrarily many globally occupied degree scales with bounded conditional scale entropy, is closed.

## 8. B5 — add a Renyi-type parameter and differentiate

### Idea

Turn the qualitative “diffuse transport” obstruction into one scalar family. For `q>=1`, set

```text
Phi(q)=sum_ij P_ij (alpha_ij beta_ij)^(q-1).             (8.1)
```

Then

```text
Phi(1)=1,
Phi'(1)=-[H(J|I)+H(I|J)].                               (8.2)
```

At the first nonlinear point,

```text
Phi(2)
 =sum_ij P_ij alpha_ij beta_ij
 =sum_ij P_ij^3/(P_iQ_j).                               (8.3)
```

### Proposition 8.1 — positive collision mass closes the cross branch

If

```text
liminf Phi(2)>0,                                        (8.4)
```

then the cross branch has an `Omega(d)` simple cycle a.a.s. at `p=(1+epsilon)/d`.

#### Proof

Under the edge law put `Y=alpha_IJ beta_IJ in [0,1]`. If `E Y>=c`, then

```text
P(Y>=c/2)>=c/2.                                         (8.5)
```

Since each conditional probability is at most one, `Y>=c/2` forces both `alpha_IJ` and `beta_IJ` to be at least `c/2`. Hence a fixed positive mass of cells is balanced in both directions. Among only `O((log n)^2)` cells, one balanced cell grows; Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 6.1 finish. `QED`

Thus every sequence surviving the new theorem must obey the exact scalar degeneration

```text
Phi(2) -> 0.                                            (8.6)
```

Jensen also gives

```text
H_bi=E[-log Y] >= -log E[Y]= -log Phi(2),               (8.7)
```

so the collision collapse forces the entropy divergence in (7.6).

**B5 outcome:** the residual irregularity is now represented by a precise differentiable family. The full source blocker is no longer “find an approximately biregular cell”; it is specifically to eliminate or exploit `Phi(2)->0` under the cross-core semidegree and mass constraints.

It is not necessary. Theorem 6.1 permits any fixed sidewise ratios and even allows `R/d^2` to grow without bound. The occupancy calculation cancels that growth exactly.
