## 4. B1 — change category: maximal vertex-disjoint short-cycle packing gives a transversal trichotomy

### Idea before proof

The preceding edge-disjoint peeling keeps exact edge mass but allows arbitrarily complicated vertex overlap. Instead, switch categories: take a **maximal vertex-disjoint packing** of source-scale short cycles. Its vertex union is automatically a transversal of every remaining short cycle. If the packing is small, deleting that transversal leaves a high-girth graph; if this deletion destroys a positive fraction of all host edges, then an extremely small vertex set carries that edge mass. This is a cleaner route to the small-cover geometry highlighted independently by Erdős--Gallai hyperstability.

Fix `epsilon>0` and choose

`0 < theta < epsilon/(1+epsilon)`.

Fix `gamma>0`, assume `d` is large enough that `L:=floor(gamma d)+1>=3`, and write

`n=|V(G)|`, `m=e(G)=nd/2`.

Let

`C_1,...,C_q`

be a maximal family of pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles of length `<L`, and put

`U := union_i V(C_i)`, `W:=V(G)\U`, `H:=G[W]`.

Choose the packing threshold

`q_0 := theta n/(8 gamma d^2)`.

### Proposition 4.1 — short-cycle packing / high-girth / tiny-transversal trichotomy

At least one of the following holds.

**(P) Many disjoint short-cycle blocks.**

`q >= theta n/(8 gamma d^2)`.

**(H) Near-full-density high-girth remainder.**

`girth(H) >= L` and

`e(H) >= (1-theta)m`.

**(T) Tiny short-cycle transversal carrying positive edge mass.**

`girth(H) >= L`,

`|U| < theta n/(8d)`,

and

`e(G[U]) + e(U,W) > theta nd/2`.

#### Proof

If (P) holds there is nothing to prove. Assume therefore

`q < theta n/(8 gamma d^2)`.

Every packed cycle has length at most `L-1<=gamma d`, so

`|U| = sum_i |C_i|`
`<= q gamma d`
`< theta n/(8d)`.                                           `(4.1)`

Because the family is maximal under **vertex-disjointness**, every cycle of length `<L` in `G` meets `U`; otherwise it could be added to the packing. Therefore `H=G-U` has no cycle of length `<L`, hence

`girth(H) >= L`.                                             `(4.2)`

If `e(H)>=(1-theta)m`, branch (H) holds. Otherwise

`e(G[U])+e(U,W)`
`= m-e(H)`
`> theta m`
`= theta nd/2`,                                              `(4.3)`

which together with (4.1)--(4.2) is branch (T). `□`

### What B1 changes relative to the preceding report

The previous maximal **edge-disjoint** packing guaranteed positive short-cycle edge mass but could leave an enormous overlapping family with no small hitting set. Proposition 4.1 says that after switching to vertex-disjoint packing, every unresolved host has a much sharper alternative:

- `Omega(n/d^2)` genuinely vertex-disjoint short-cycle blocks; or
- a high-girth near-full-density remainder; or
- a set of fewer than `theta n/(8d)` vertices that meets **every** source-scale short cycle and is incident to more than a `theta` fraction of all host edges.

This is not a source proof, but it turns the old qualitative “hub” branch into a quantitative transversal statement.

**B1 outcome:** exact structural reduction; no metric movement.

## 5. B15 — break symmetry inside the tiny-transversal branch

### Idea before proof

Branch (T) still mixes two very different geometries. The lost edge mass may lie mostly **inside** `U`, producing a truly dense small core, or mostly **across** the cut `(U,W)`, producing a two-type incidence graph. These should not be treated symmetrically. The internal case is now completely solvable using the already-proved uniform `K>2` theorem; only the cross case survives.

Assume branch (T), and abbreviate

`e_U := e(G[U])`,

`e_X := e(U,W)`.

By (4.3),

`e_U+e_X > theta nd/2`.                                    `(5.1)`

### Proposition 5.1 — the dense-interior half of (T) is already closed

If

`e_U >= theta nd/4`,                                        `(5.2)`

then, at the source probability

`p=(1+epsilon)/d`,

`G_p` contains a cycle of length at least `d` a.a.s. as `n->infinity`, for all sufficiently large `d`.

#### Proof

From (4.1) and (5.2), the average degree `D_U` of `G[U]` satisfies

`D_U = 2e_U/|U|`
`> (theta nd/2)/(theta n/(8d))`
`= 4d^2`.                                                    `(5.3)`

Hence for all sufficiently large `d`,

`p D_U > 4(1+epsilon)d > 3`,

so

`p > 3/D_U`.                                                `(5.4)`

Apply the previous R7-07 uniform DFS theorem with multiplier `K=3` to the host `G[U]`. It gives a cycle in `(G[U])_p` of length at least

`gamma_3 D_U = D_U/24 > d^2/6 >= d`                        `(5.5)`

for `d>=6`, except with probability at most

`exp(-|U|/40)+exp(-|U|/8)`.                                `(5.6)`

It remains only to check that this failure tends to zero under the source limit. Since `G[U]` is simple,

`e_U <= |U|^2/2`.

Together with (5.2),

`|U| >= sqrt(theta nd/2)`,                                  `(5.7)`

so `|U|->infinity` as `n->infinity` (uniformly for `d>=d_0`). Thus (5.6) is `o(1)`. The resulting cycle is also a cycle of `G_p`. `□`

This is a genuine closure of one structural branch, not a closure of G0011.

### Proposition 5.2 — the surviving cross branch contains a huge two-type core

If (5.2) fails, then by (5.1)

`e_X > theta nd/4`.                                         `(5.8)`

Let `J` be the bipartite graph consisting of the cross edges between `U` and `W`. Then `J` contains a nonempty bipartite subgraph

`J'=(A,B;E')`, with `A subseteq U`, `B subseteq W`,

such that

`delta_A(J') > d^2`,                                        `(5.9)`

`delta_B(J') > theta d/8`.                                  `(5.10)`

Here `delta_A` means the minimum degree of a vertex on side `A` into `B`, and similarly for `delta_B`.

#### Proof

Write `e=e_X`. Starting from `J`, repeatedly delete

- a vertex of `U` with current degree `< e/(2|U|)`, or
- a vertex of `W` with current degree `< e/(2|W|)`.

If all edges were deleted, the total number of edges removed at deletions on the `U` side would be strictly less than

`|U| * e/(2|U|)=e/2`,

and the total removed at deletions on the `W` side would be strictly less than

`|W| * e/(2|W|)=e/2`.

Every edge is charged exactly once, when its first endpoint is deleted. Hence deleting all `e` edges would require strictly fewer than `e` edge deletions, a contradiction. Therefore a nonempty subgraph `J'` remains, and it has the claimed sidewise minimum-degree lower bounds

`delta_A(J') >= e/(2|U|)`,

`delta_B(J') >= e/(2|W|)`.                                 `(5.11)`

Now use (5.8), (4.1), and `|W|<=n`:

`e/(2|U|)`
`> (theta nd/4)/(theta n/(4d))`
`= d^2`,                                                     `(5.12)`

and

`e/(2|W|)`
`> (theta nd/4)/(2n)`
`= theta d/8`.                                               `(5.13)`

This proves (5.9)--(5.10). `□`

Two immediate size consequences are

`|B| > d^2`,                                                 `(5.14)`

`|A| > theta d/8`,                                          `(5.15)`

because a vertex on one side cannot have more neighbors than the size of the opposite side.

Thus the only tiny-transversal obstruction that survives B15 is not an arbitrary star-like hub. It contains a genuine two-type core with one side having minimum degree `>d^2` and the other minimum degree `>theta d/8`.

**B15 outcome:** the internal small-cover branch is completely closed; the residual small-cover branch is reduced to a quantitatively very supercritical bipartite core.

## 6. B21 — import two-type branching / random-bipartite machinery

### Idea before proof

The semidegrees in Proposition 5.2 are much stronger than what the original average-degree statement exposes. Before trying to solve the arbitrary irregular `J'`, quantify its two-step branching exactly and test the extremal homogeneous model. If even the complete-bipartite model failed, the reduction would be pointing toward a genuine counterexample mechanism. It does not fail: the homogeneous model is far inside the supercritical long-cycle regime. What remains is neighborhood heterogeneity/localization, not the two-type branching threshold.

### Lemma 6.1 — the residual cross core is far above the nonbacktracking threshold

Let `J'=(A,B)` satisfy (5.9)--(5.10), and let `B_J` denote its Hashimoto/nonbacktracking matrix. For sufficiently large `d`,

`rho(B_J) >= sqrt((delta_A-1)(delta_B-1))`,                  `(6.1)`

and hence at `p=(1+epsilon)/d`,

`p rho(B_J)`
`> (1+epsilon)/d * sqrt((d^2-1)(theta d/8-1))`
`= (1+epsilon) sqrt(theta d/8) (1-o(1))`.                   `(6.2)`

In particular

`p rho(B_J) -> infinity`                                    `(6.3)`

as `d->infinity`.

#### Proof

From any directed edge going from `A` to `B`, a two-step nonbacktracking continuation has at least

`(delta_B-1)(delta_A-1)`

choices; the same lower bound holds starting with the opposite orientation. Therefore, entrywise,

`B_J^2 1 >= (delta_A-1)(delta_B-1) 1`.

For a nonnegative matrix, Collatz--Wielandt (or simply the minimum-row-sum lower bound applied to `B_J^2`) gives

`rho(B_J)^2=rho(B_J^2) >= (delta_A-1)(delta_B-1)`.

Now insert (5.9)--(5.10). `□`

This is much stronger than the `1+epsilon/2` Hashimoto margin obtained for the full 2-core in the earlier run. Nevertheless, a large Hashimoto radius alone still does not force a long **simple** cycle: the previous clique-chain and short-excursion examples already warn that nonbacktracking mass can recycle locally.

### Proposition 6.2 — complete-bipartite benchmark for the residual cross scale

Fix constants `epsilon>0` and `c>0`. Let `d->infinity`, and let

`K_{a_d,b_d}`

be a complete bipartite graph with

`min(a_d,b_d) >= c d`,                                      `(6.4)`

`max(a_d,b_d) >= d^2`.                                     `(6.5)`

At

`p=(1+epsilon)/d`,                                          `(6.6)`

the percolated graph contains a cycle of length `Omega_{c,epsilon}(d)` a.a.s.

This includes the homogeneous complete-bipartite model of Proposition 5.2 with `c=theta/8`.

#### Proof

Write

`s:=min(a_d,b_d)`, `ell:=max(a_d,b_d)`.

Use the larger part as a family of independent **connector labels**. For each connector vertex `x`, expose all its incident percolation edges to the `s`-vertex side. If at least two are open, call `x` active and choose uniformly one unordered pair from its open neighbors. Because the underlying host is complete bipartite, exchangeability implies that conditional on being active, the selected pair is exactly uniform over the `C(s,2)` unordered pairs. Different connector vertices use disjoint edge variables, so their activity indicators and selected pairs are independent.

Let

`M ~ Bin(ell,q_s)`,

where

`q_s := P(Bin(s,p)>=2)`.                                    `(6.7)`

We claim that

`P(M >= 3s/4) -> 1`.                                       `(6.8)`

There are two regimes.

**Regime 1: `s <= d^(3/2)`.**

By (6.4), `sp >= c(1+epsilon)`. For all sufficiently large `d`, monotonicity of the binomial tail and the Poisson limit give a constant

`q_*=q_*(c,epsilon)>0`

such that `q_s>=q_*`. Since `ell>=d^2`,

`E M >= q_* d^2`,

while `s<=d^(3/2)`. Hence `E M/s >= q_* sqrt(d)->infinity`, and Chernoff gives (6.8).

**Regime 2: `s > d^(3/2)`.**

Now

`sp > (1+epsilon) sqrt(d)`.

Thus

`P(Bin(s,p)<=1) -> 0`

uniformly, so `q_s=1-o(1)`. Since `ell>=s`, `EM>=(1-o(1))s`, and another Chernoff bound gives (6.8).

On the event (6.8), keep only the first

`m_0:=floor(2s/3)`

active connector vertices. Their chosen pairs form an iid uniform random multigraph on `s` labeled vertices with `m_0` edges. The number of repeated sampled pairs is `O_P(1)` because `m_0=Theta(s)` while the pair space has size `Theta(s^2)`. After deleting repeats, conditional on its size the underlying simple edge set is uniform, with

`m_0-O_P(1)=(2/3+o_P(1))s`

edges. This is the classical strictly supercritical Erdős--Rényi regime of average degree `4/3+o(1)>1`; the standard supercritical random-graph long-cycle theorem gives a constant `eta>0` such that a.a.s. the auxiliary simple graph has a cycle of length at least `eta s`.

Finally, every auxiliary edge on that cycle came from a **different connector vertex**, because we selected at most one pair from each connector. Replacing an auxiliary edge `{u,v}` labeled by `x` with the open two-edge path `u-x-v` therefore lifts the auxiliary simple cycle to a simple cycle in the original percolated complete bipartite graph, of twice the auxiliary length. Hence the lifted cycle has length at least

`2 eta s >= 2 eta c d`,

which is `Omega_{c,epsilon}(d)`. `□`

### What the benchmark rules out

The complete-bipartite stress test includes the most violently unbalanced two-type geometry: one side may be only `Theta(d)` while the other is at least `d^2` and can be arbitrarily larger. The proposition says that **part-size imbalance itself is not the obstruction**. In the complete model, independent connector labels create a supercritical random graph on the smaller side and long cycles lift with distinct labels.

The exact place the proof fails for the arbitrary `J'` of Proposition 5.2 is now clear. If connector neighborhoods vary adversarially, then conditional on activation the chosen pair is uniform only inside that connector's own neighborhood, not globally. The resulting labeled auxiliary edges can concentrate in many incompatible local regions. A proof for arbitrary `J'` therefore needs a **heterogeneous rainbow/connector expansion theorem**, not another global branching calculation.

This meshes with the 2026 Erdős--Gallai hyperstability picture: small vertex-cover components are a genuine extremal structural class at large constant `K`, but the near-critical problem requires a much sharper analysis of how random two-edge transmissions through the cover overlap.

**B21 outcome:** exact diverging two-type Hashimoto margin plus a complete proof of the homogeneous complete-bipartite benchmark; arbitrary neighborhood heterogeneity remains open.

4. **Tiny transversal, cross core:** there is a bipartite subgraph `J'=(A,B)` with

   `delta_A>d^2`,

   `delta_B>theta d/8`,

   `|B|>d^2`,

   `|A|>theta d/8`,

   `p rho(B_J) >= (1+epsilon) sqrt(theta d/8)(1-o(1)) -> infinity`.

This is strictly narrower than the preceding hub-versus-blocks reduction. In particular a one-vertex or `o(d)`-vertex star hub can no longer be the residual small-cover obstruction: after pruning, the cover side itself has `Omega(d)` vertices and every opposite-side vertex has `Omega(d)` cover neighbors.

## 8. Failure analysis and exact remaining gaps

### Gap 1 — many vertex-disjoint short-cycle blocks

The event that an entire `Theta(d)` host cycle survives has probability exponentially small in `d`, so simple independence across the packed blocks is not uniform enough when `d=d(n)` grows. One must use the blocks as **transmission gadgets**, not demand intact cycles.

### Gap 2 — high-girth branch uniformity

Nelson's fixed-parameter theorem gives the source multiplier for every fixed sufficiently large `d`, but its host-order threshold is not controlled uniformly for `mu=Theta(d)` and additive supercriticality `Theta(1/d)`. This remains a real quantifier gap.

### Gap 3 — arbitrary two-type cross core

The cross core has enormous two-step branching, but a branching calculation does not control how connector neighborhoods overlap. Complete bipartite blocks are benign; the missing theorem must distinguish benign block concentration from a truly adversarial overlap pattern while preserving a long **simple** cycle.

### Gap 4 — no universal multiplier improvement

None of the above proves the remaining three branches for all hosts. Therefore `K_long<=2` is not improved and the frozen metric remains unchanged.

## 9. Prior-art caution

The maximal-packing/transversal argument is elementary and sits conceptually close to classical Erdős--Pósa and to the small-vertex-cover side of Christoph--Müyesser--Wigderson's hyperstability theorem. It is not claimed as a novel general graph-theoretic principle.

Likewise the complete-bipartite benchmark is an application of standard supercritical random-graph behavior after a connector projection. Its value here is diagnostic: it proves that the exact semidegree scales produced by Proposition 5.2 are sufficient in the homogeneous model and isolates neighborhood heterogeneity as the next issue. No publication-level novelty claim is made from a bounded negative search.
