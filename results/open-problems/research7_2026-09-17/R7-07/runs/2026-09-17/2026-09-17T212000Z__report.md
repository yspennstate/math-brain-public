## 5. B3 — prove a stronger witness-respecting deterministic statement

The first idea is to replace mere projected degree by a local object that already remembers distinct witnesses.

For `a in A`, define `nu_F(a)` in a bipartite graph `F=(A,B)` as the largest `k` for which there are distinct connectors

```text
b_1,...,b_k in B
```

and distinct target vertices

```text
x_1,...,x_k in A\{a}
```

such that every two-edge path

```text
a-b_i-x_i
```

lies in `F`.

### Lemma 5.1 — link-matching Dirac lemma

If `nu_F(a)>=k` for every `a in A`, then `F` contains a simple cycle of length at least `k+1`.

### Proof

Choose a longest simple path in `F` whose two endpoints lie in `A`:

```text
P=a_0 c_1 a_1 ... c_m a_m.
```

Such a path exists when `k>=1`. At endpoint `a_m`, take `k` witness-disjoint two-edge paths

```text
a_m-b_i-x_i,
```

with all `b_i` distinct and all `x_i` distinct.

By maximality of `P`, it is impossible that both `b_i` and `x_i` lie outside `P`, because then appending `b_i,x_i` would give a longer A-to-A path. Hence for each `i`, at least one of `b_i,x_i` lies on `P`.

Choose

```text
z_i=b_i if b_i lies on P,
z_i=x_i otherwise.
```

The `z_i` are `k` distinct vertices of `P`: the chosen B-type vertices are distinct, the chosen A-type vertices are distinct, and vertices of opposite bipartition classes cannot coincide. Among `k` distinct vertices before endpoint `a_m`, one has path-distance at least `k` from `a_m`.

If that far vertex is `b_i`, then the subpath from `b_i` to `a_m`, together with the edge `a_m b_i`, is a simple cycle of length at least `k+1`. If it is `x_i`, then `b_i` is outside `P`; the subpath from `x_i` to `a_m`, together with `a_m b_i x_i`, is a simple cycle of length at least `k+2`. This proves the lemma. `QED`

This strengthens the previous Hall language: a global lower bound on these local link-matchings is already enough for a source-scale cycle. The probabilistic difficulty is to build such witness capacity coherently without union-bounding an `exp(-Omega(d))` local failure over arbitrary host order.

## 6. B9 — defer witness allocation until after percolation

The decisive change is not to pre-pair deterministic incidences. Pre-pairing before percolation pays the old factor `1/2` at the threshold. Instead, allocate a connector only **after** seeing its open neighborhood.

The full proof below uses an even more conservative rule: inside a localized A-block, each connector is allowed to contribute **at most one projected edge**. Therefore every projected cycle is automatically rainbow in connector labels and lifts to a simple cycle in the bipartite graph.

### Theorem 6.1 — arbitrary-overlap biregular cross-core theorem

Fix `epsilon,theta>0`. There are `d_0` and `gamma>0` depending only on them such that the following holds. Let `J=(A,B)` be `(R,S)`-biregular with

```text
R>=d^2/2,
S>=theta d/16,
d>=d_0,
```

and let `J_p` retain incidences independently with `p=(1+epsilon)/d`. Along every sequence with `|A|+|B| -> infinity`, `J_p` a.a.s. contains a simple cycle of length at least `gamma d`.

### Proof, part I: localization

Write

```text
m=|A|, M=|B|.
```

Biregularity gives

```text
mR=MS.                                               (6.1)
```

Set

```text
h=max(1,floor(S/d)).                                 (6.2)
```

If `h=1`, use `U_1=A`. If `h>=2`, randomly color A-vertices with `h` colors and let `U_j` be a color class. For fixed `b,j`,

```text
t_{b,j}=|N_J(b) intersect U_j| ~ Bin(S,1/h).
```

When `h>=2`, `d<=S/h<=2d`. When `h=1`, `theta d/16<=S<2d`. Put

```text
a_0=min(1,theta/16),
a=a_0/2,
beta=4.
```

Chernoff gives

```text
P(t_{b,j} notin [a d,beta d]) <= delta_d,
delta_d=2 exp(-c_0 d),                              (6.3)
```

for `c_0=c_0(theta)>0`, while `m>=S` and `m/h>=d` for `h>=2`, so

```text
P(|U_j|>2m/h) <= exp(-c_1 d).                       (6.4)
```

The expected number of connectors violating (6.3) in a fixed class is at most `delta_d M`. Markov therefore gives probability at most `sqrt(delta_d)` that more than `sqrt(delta_d)M` connectors are bad. Taking `d_0` large, a fixed class is simultaneously size-good and connector-good with probability at least `3/4`. Averaging over the random coloring yields a deterministic coloring with at least `h/2` good classes. For each such class `U`,

```text
|U| <= 4md/S,                                       (6.5)
```

and at least `eta M` connectors, with `eta>=1/2`, have localized base degree in `[ad,beta d]`.

### Proof, part II: one selected pair per connector

Fix a good block `U` and only those `eta M` good connectors. For such a connector `b`, after percolation let

```text
D_b=|N_{J_p}(b) intersect U|.
```

If `D_b<2`, select nothing. If `D_b>=2`, independently select one unordered open pair uniformly from the `C(D_b,2)` possibilities. Let `G_U` be the simple graph on `U` consisting of the selected pairs, with duplicates collapsed.

Because each connector selects at most one pair in this block, any simple cycle in `G_U` has pairwise distinct connector witnesses and lifts to a simple cycle of twice the length in `J_p`.

For a connector whose localized base degree is `t in [ad,beta d]`, let

```text
q_t=P(Bin(t,p)>=2).
```

For large `d`, `p<=1/2`. The exactly-two term gives the uniform positive bound

```text
q_t >= C(t,2)p^2(1-p)^(t-2)
    >= q_*,
q_*=[a^2(1+epsilon)^2/4] exp(-2 beta(1+epsilon))>0.  (6.6)
```

By exchangeability, every base pair inside that connector's localized neighborhood has unconditional selection probability

```text
r_{b,e}=q_t/C(t,2) <= C_*/d^2,
C_*=4/a^2.                                          (6.7)
```

The equality follows because whenever `D_b>=2` exactly one pair is selected and all `C(t,2)` base pairs are symmetric.

### Proof, part III: occupancy survives arbitrary codegree overlap

For a pair `e subset U`, define

```text
x_e=sum_b r_{b,e}.
```

A pair has at most `R` common connectors because either endpoint has J-degree `R`. Thus

```text
x_e <= L:=C_*R/d^2.                                 (6.8)
```

On the other hand, summing selection probabilities connector-first gives the exact mass identity

```text
sum_e x_e=sum_b q_{t_b} >= eta M q_*.               (6.9)
```

Let `I_e` indicate that pair `e` appears in `G_U`. Independence across connectors gives

```text
E I_e=1-prod_b(1-r_{b,e}) >= 1-exp(-x_e).           (6.10)
```

Since `f(x)=1-exp(-x)` is concave with `f(0)=0`, for `0<=x<=L`,

```text
f(x) >= [f(L)/L]x.                                  (6.11)
```

Writing `Z_U=e(G_U)`, equations (6.1), (6.8)--(6.11) yield

```text
E Z_U
 >= [1-exp(-L)]/L * eta M q_*
 = eta q_*[1-exp(-L)] m d^2/(C_*S).                 (6.12)
```

Because `R>=d^2/2`, `L>=C_*/2`, hence `[1-exp(-L)]` has a fixed positive lower bound. Therefore for some constant `c_2=c_2(epsilon,theta)>0`,

```text
E Z_U >= c_2 m d^2/S.                               (6.13)
```

This is the key arbitrary-overlap estimate. It uses no codegree regularity: concentrated codegrees merely saturate the concave occupancy function instead of destroying all selected edges.

### Proof, part IV: negative covariance and a long projected cycle

For distinct pairs `e,f`, a single connector cannot select both. Thus

```text
P(I_e=0,I_f=0)
 =prod_b(1-r_{b,e}-r_{b,f})
 <=prod_b(1-r_{b,e})(1-r_{b,f})
 =P(I_e=0)P(I_f=0).
```

Since

```text
Cov(I_e,I_f)
 =P(I_e=0,I_f=0)-P(I_e=0)P(I_f=0),
```

we have pairwise nonpositive covariance. Consequently

```text
Var Z_U <= E Z_U.                                    (6.14)
```

Chebyshev gives

```text
P(Z_U<(1/2)E Z_U)
 <=4/E Z_U
 <=4S/(c_2md^2)
 <=4/(c_2d^2).                                      (6.15)
```

On success, (6.5) and (6.13) imply

```text
average_degree(G_U)>= (c_2/4)d.                     (6.16)
```

A graph of average degree `D` has a nonempty subgraph of minimum degree at least `D/2`; a longest path in a graph of minimum degree `delta` closes to a cycle of length at least `delta+1`. Therefore `G_U` has a cycle of length at least `c_3d`, for `c_3=c_2/8` after harmless integer rounding. It lifts to a simple cycle of length at least `2c_3d` in `J_p`.

## 7. B30 — localize scale, then identify and repair the gluing failure

A single localized block proves the result with probability `1-O(d^-2)`. That is enough when `d->infinity`, but it is not by itself an `a.a.s.` statement for fixed large `d` as host order grows. This is precisely the scale-gluing issue B30 is meant to expose.

The localization from Section 6 supplies at least `h/2` disjoint good A-blocks. Different blocks use disjoint sets of percolation edge variables, even though they may share B-vertices, because an incidence is determined by its A-endpoint. The auxiliary one-pair choices are also taken independently across blocks. Therefore the block-success events are independent.

If `h->infinity`, (6.15) gives

```text
P(no good block produces a long cycle)
 <= [4/(c_2d^2)]^(h/2) -> 0.                        (7.1)
```

once `d_0` is large enough that the base is below one.

Now consider a sequence where `d` stays bounded and `h` stays bounded. Then `S` stays bounded.

If `m=|A|->infinity`, (6.13) gives `E Z_U->infinity` in a good block, and (6.15) tends to zero directly.

If `m` also stays bounded, host order can tend to infinity only because `R` and hence `M=mR/S` tend to infinity. There are finitely many `S`-subsets of bounded `A`, so one neighborhood type `T subset A`, `|T|=S`, occurs for `L_T->infinity` twin connectors. Fix a cyclic ordering of the vertices of `T` and split those twin connectors into `S` disjoint groups. Assign one group to each consecutive pair in the cyclic order. Each connector witnesses its assigned pair with probability `p^2`, independently inside the group. Since `p` is bounded below in this bounded-`d` regime and every group size tends to infinity, every cyclic pair obtains an open, group-distinct witness with probability tending to one. Thus `J_p` contains a simple cycle of length `2S`, and

```text
2S >= (theta/8)d.                                   (7.2)
```

These cases exhaust every host-order sequence: if failure probability did not tend to zero, take a subsequence; either `d`, then `h`, then `m` is unbounded, or all three are bounded and the twin argument applies. Hence Theorem 6.1 is proved with

```text
gamma=min(2c_3,theta/8)>0.
```

## 8. What failed, and what is now genuinely removed

### Failed route A: predetermined pairing

Pairing deterministic neighbors before percolation makes a projected edge require two prescribed open incidences. The effective two-step branching factor pays an artificial factor about `1/2` and becomes subcritical near `p=(1+epsilon)/d` for small epsilon.

**Removed:** choose the pair only after seeing the open neighborhood.

### Failed route B: keep every post-percolation pair

This preserves density, but one connector can label many projected edges. An ordinary projected cycle may then reuse a connector and fail to lift to a simple bipartite cycle.

**Removed:** each connector contributes at most one selected pair per localized block.

### Failed route C: use one global selected graph

When `d` is fixed and a small number of high-degree connector types grow with host order, a one-shot `1-O(d^-2)` success estimate is not asymptotically one.

**Removed:** B30 localization produces many independent A-block trials when `S/d` grows, while the bounded-block cases are handled by growing block size or the exact twin-neighborhood amplification.

### Failed route D: generic Berge-cycle theorem as a black box

The non-uniform hypergraph of open connector neighborhoods does not automatically satisfy the uniformity/2-connectivity/minimum-degree hypotheses of the strongest linear Dirac-type Berge-cycle theorems. The 2020 fully non-uniform minimum-degree theorem has an exponential-in-cycle-length threshold and is too weak here.

**Usefulness retained:** the Berge-cycle viewpoint correctly identifies that distinct connector witnesses are the core issue, but the proof above needs a problem-specific occupancy/localization argument.
