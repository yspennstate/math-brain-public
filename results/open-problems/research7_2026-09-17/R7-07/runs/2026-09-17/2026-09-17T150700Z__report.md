## 4. Counterexample/obstruction first: the lower barrier 1 has a short self-contained cycle-count proof

Although the lower barrier is standard, it is useful to pin it to the exact metric without importing random-graph giant-component theory.

**Lemma 4.1 (sharp lower barrier).** For every fixed `K<1` and every fixed `gamma>0`, there is a sequence of host graphs of average degree `d -> infinity` such that at `p=K/d` the probability of a cycle of length at least `gamma d` tends to zero. Hence no source theorem can have threshold multiplier below 1.

**Proof.** Take `G=K_{d+1}`, whose average degree is exactly `d`, and put `n=d+1`. Let `C_l` be the number of `l`-cycles of `G_p`. Then

`E C_l = (n)_l p^l/(2l) <= (np)^l/(2l)`.

Since `np=K(d+1)/d -> K<1`, choose `q` with `K<q<1`; for all sufficiently large `d`, `np<=q`. Therefore

`P(exists a cycle of length >= gamma d)`
`<= sum_{l=ceil(gamma d)}^{d+1} E C_l`
`<= sum_{l=ceil(gamma d)}^infinity q^l/(2l)`
`<= q^{ceil(gamma d)}/(2 ceil(gamma d)(1-q)) -> 0`.

This proves the claim. `□`

So the hard direction really is the upper inequality `K_long<=1`; there is no slack hidden in the definition.

## 5. B18 — translate the source threshold into the cycle-bearing Hashimoto operator

The earlier `G0011-A001` lost a factor two by converting average degree `d` to a subgraph of minimum degree only `d/2`. The first new move is to stop pruning to minimum degree and instead pass to the nonbacktracking operator, which is intrinsically insensitive to leaves and records cycle-bearing growth.

### 5.1 The 2-core loses no average degree

**Lemma 5.1.** Let `G` have average degree `d>2`, and let `C` be its 2-core. Then `C` is nonempty and its average degree `d_C` satisfies `d_C >= d`.

**Proof.** Obtain `C` by repeatedly deleting a vertex of current degree at most 1. Suppose `r` vertices are deleted. Each deletion removes at most one edge, so if `G` has `n` vertices and `m=dn/2` edges, then

`m_C >= m-r`, `n_C=n-r`.

Consequently

`2m_C-d n_C >= 2(m-r)-d(n-r) = (d-2)r >=0`,

hence `2m_C/n_C >= d`, provided `C` is nonempty. If every vertex were deleted, then `m<=n`, contradicting `m=dn/2>n`. Thus `C` is nonempty. `□`

This is already a precise correction to the factor-two pruning route: deleting only the genuinely non-cycle-bearing degree-0/1 material preserves, rather than halves, the average degree.

### 5.2 Exact nonbacktracking supercriticality at `(1+epsilon)/d`

For a graph `H` of minimum degree at least 2, let `B_H` be its Hashimoto/nonbacktracking matrix indexed by directed edges: `B_{e,f}=1` exactly when the head of `e` is the tail of `f` and `f` is not the reverse of `e`.

**Theorem 5.2.** If `H` has minimum degree at least 2 and average degree `bar d`, then

`rho(B_H) >= bar d - 1`.

A complete proof is given in Section 6 rather than cited as a black box.

Combining Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 gives, for every host `G` of average degree `d>2`,

`rho(B_C) >= d_C-1 >= d-1`.

At the exact source percolation scale `p=(1+epsilon)/d`,

`p rho(B_C) >= (1+epsilon)(1-1/d)`.

In particular, once

`d >= 2(1+epsilon)/epsilon`,

we have the uniform strict margin

`p rho(B_C) >= 1+epsilon/2`.

**Interpretation.** Every hypothetical counterexample to G0011 is already uniformly *supercritical* for the nonbacktracking operator on its 2-core. The average-degree conjecture is therefore not blocked by a hidden factor-two branching threshold. The factor-two loss is an artefact of minimum-degree pruning.

This does not prove the conjecture: the missing theorem is a uniform conversion from `p rho(B)>1+delta` plus the global degree-mass condition to an `Omega(d)` cycle, robust to localization and short-cycle recycling.

## 6. B25 — double-count nonbacktracking paths; full entropy proof of `rho(B)>=bar d-1`

This is the strongest new exact derivation in the run.

Let `H=(V,E)` have minimum degree at least 2, `m=|E|`, and average degree `bar d=2m/|V|`. Put the uniform probability measure on its `2m` directed edges. Consider the nonbacktracking Markov chain on directed edges with transition

`Q(e,f)=B(e,f)/(deg(head(e))-1)`.

### 6.1 Uniform directed-edge measure is stationary

Fix `f=(v,w)`. Its predecessors are precisely `e=(u,v)` with `u != w`, of which there are `deg(v)-1`. Hence

`sum_e [1/(2m)] Q(e,f)`
`= (deg(v)-1) [1/(2m)] [1/(deg(v)-1)]`
`= 1/(2m)`.

Thus every time marginal of the chain is uniform on directed edges.

### 6.2 Convert path counts to an exponential moment

Let `N_k = 1^T B^k 1`, the number of directed nonbacktracking sequences `(e_0,...,e_k)` with `k` transitions. Along the stationary chain put `D_i=deg(head(e_i))`. A particular admissible sequence has chain probability

`[1/(2m)] prod_{i=0}^{k-1} 1/(D_i-1)`.

Therefore, summing the identity

`1 = 2m * P(sequence) * prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(D_i-1)`

over all admissible sequences yields the exact formula

`N_k = 2m E[ prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(D_i-1) ]`.

By Jensen applied to the exponential,

`E[ prod(D_i-1) ]`
`= E exp(sum log(D_i-1))`
`>= exp(sum E log(D_i-1))`.

Stationarity gives

`E log(D_i-1)`
`= sum_{v in V} [deg(v)/(2m)] log(deg(v)-1)`
`= [1/(|V| bar d)] sum_v deg(v) log(deg(v)-1)`.

Now `f(x)=x log(x-1)` is convex on `[2,infinity)`, because

`f''(x)=(x-2)/(x-1)^2 >=0`.

Jensen over the vertex degrees therefore gives

`(1/|V|) sum_v deg(v) log(deg(v)-1)`
`>= bar d log(bar d-1)`.

Consequently `E log(D_i-1)>=log(bar d-1)`, and hence

`N_k >= 2m (bar d-1)^k`.                                    `(6.1)`

Finally, `N_k <= (2m) ||B^k||_1`; taking `k`th roots and using Gelfand's formula yields

`rho(B) >= bar d-1`.                                         `(6.2)`

This proves Theorem 5.2. `□`

### 6.3 The same count survives percolation with an exponential margin

Let `X_k` count directed nonbacktracking sequences `(e_0,...,e_k)` all of whose underlying undirected edges are retained in `H_p`. A walk may reuse an edge after a detour, so if it uses `r` distinct underlying edges then `r<=k+1`; since `0<p<=1`, its survival probability `p^r` is at least `p^{k+1}`. Thus from (6.1),

`E X_k >= p^{k+1} N_k`
`>= 2m p [p(bar d-1)]^k`.                                  `(6.3)`

For the 2-core of a G0011 host and `d>=2(1+epsilon)/epsilon`, this becomes

`E X_k >= 2m_C p (1+epsilon/2)^k`.                          `(6.4)`

So the exact source scaling produces exponentially many open nonbacktracking walks **in expectation**, uniformly over arbitrary degree inhomogeneity.

### 6.4 Sanity check on the main irregular family

For `K_{a,b}`, the average degree is `d=2ab/(a+b)`. At source scale `p=(1+epsilon)/d`, the two-step nonbacktracking offspring product is

`p^2(a-1)(b-1)`
`= (1+epsilon)^2 ((a+b)^2/(4ab)) (1-1/a)(1-1/b)`.

Since `(a+b)^2/(4ab)>=1` and `min(a,b)>=d/2`, this is `>1` by a uniform margin for sufficiently large `d`. Thus extreme bipartite imbalance does not recreate the factor-two obstruction either; it strengthens alternating nonbacktracking growth.

## 7. B30 — localize exactly why exponential nonbacktracking growth still does not give the source cycle

Equation (6.4) is not a solution because expectation can be concentrated on rare configurations and because nonbacktracking walks can recycle through short cyclic regions. The obstruction can be written exactly.

Let `W_k` be the deterministic set of length-`k` transition nonbacktracking directed walks in the 2-core, and let `E(W)` denote the set of underlying undirected edges used by `W`. Then

`E[X_k^2] = sum_{W,W' in W_k} p^{|E(W) union E(W')|}`.       `(7.1)`

The first moment is large because of (6.4). A direct Paley–Zygmund route requires controlling (7.1) relative to `(E X_k)^2`. Every shared edge between two candidate walks costs roughly a factor `1/p ~ d`; repeated traversal inside a localized short-cycle region can therefore dominate the second moment even though the global average-degree branching factor is excellent.

This identifies a sharper missing ingredient than the old “large component -> long cycle” wording:

> **Missing overlap lemma.** At scales `k=Theta(d)`, either the weighted overlap sum (7.1) is controlled strongly enough to produce many spatially distinct open nonbacktracking trajectories, or the deterministic host contains a localized short-excursion structure whose total degree mass is large enough to be attacked by a separate dense-core/finite-block argument.

The split is mathematically natural because any two simple paths that diverge and first rejoin produce a simple cycle equal to the union of their divergent segments. Therefore a host/percolation outcome with no `Omega(d)` cycle can only support large path multiplicity through repeated **short** divergence/rejoin excursions. The factor-two minimum-degree pruning never sees this distinction; the Hashimoto formulation does.

I did not prove the required uniform weighted-overlap bound. Claiming that (6.4) alone implies a long cycle would be invalid.

## 8. A precise reduction for the next run

The preceding lemmas reduce a substantial part of G0011 to the following cycle-specific statement.

**Hashimoto localization principle (HLP), desired, not proved.** For every `delta>0` there should exist `c(delta)>0` such that a finite graph `H` with minimum degree at least 2, average degree at least `d`, and `p rho(B_H)>=1+delta`, together with the degree-mass condition inherited from being the 2-core of an average-degree-`d` host, has in `H_p` a cycle of length at least `c(delta)d` a.a.s.; equivalently, any failure family must exhibit quantitatively localized nonbacktracking Perron mass/overlap.

If HLP is proved with `delta=epsilon/2`, Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 immediately give G0011 for all `d>=2(1+epsilon)/epsilon` (after matching the source's uniform asymptotics). This is noncircular: HLP is not assumed anywhere in the derivation of the Hashimoto margin.

A useful next attack is to prove a **localization dichotomy** for the stationary nonbacktracking path measure: either its collision/overlap probability through time `c d` is small enough for a second-moment path-spreading argument, or at least a fixed fraction of stationary directed-edge mass lies in a vertex set of `O_epsilon(d)` vertices. In the second branch, one can invoke the dense finite-block/robust Erdős–Gallai machinery rather than global DFS.
