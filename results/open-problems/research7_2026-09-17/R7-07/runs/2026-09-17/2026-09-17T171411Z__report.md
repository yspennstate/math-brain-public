## 4. B3 — prove something stronger: remove articulation ambiguity exactly

The previous cyclic clique-chain stress test suggested that the average degree may be distributed over many small dense blocks. Before trying probabilistic arguments inside such blocks, it is useful to know exactly how much average degree can be lost by passing to a genuine 2-connected block.

### Lemma 4.1 — a near-full-average 2-connected block always exists

Let `G` be a finite simple graph of average degree `d>2`. Then `G` has a 2-connected block `B` whose average degree satisfies

`d_bar(B) > d-1`.

More precisely, if `C` is a connected component of `G` with average degree `d_C>=d`, then one block `B` of `C` satisfies

`e(B)/(v(B)-1) > d_C/2`,

and consequently `v(B)>d_C` and

`2e(B)/v(B) > d_C-1 >= d-1`.

#### Proof

Choose a connected component `C` with average degree `d_C>=d`; such a component exists because the average degree of `G` is the vertex-weighted average of the component average degrees.

Write the blocks of `C` as `B_1,...,B_s`, counting each bridge as a two-vertex block. Standard block-tree bookkeeping gives two exact identities:

`e(C) = sum_i e(B_i)`,

`v(C)-1 = sum_i (v(B_i)-1)`.

Therefore the weighted average of the ratios

`r_i := e(B_i)/(v(B_i)-1)`

with weights `v(B_i)-1` is

`sum_i e(B_i) / sum_i(v(B_i)-1)`
`= e(C)/(v(C)-1)`
`= d_C v(C)/(2(v(C)-1))`
`> d_C/2`.

Hence some block `B` has

`e(B)/(v(B)-1) > d_C/2`.                              `(4.1)`

A bridge block has ratio exactly `1`, while `d_C/2>1`, so the chosen block is not a bridge; it is a genuine 2-connected block.

Since `B` is simple,

`e(B) <= v(B)(v(B)-1)/2`.

Combining this with (4.1) gives `v(B)>d_C`. Finally,

`d_bar(B)=2e(B)/v(B)`
`> d_C (v(B)-1)/v(B)`
`= d_C - d_C/v(B)`
`> d_C-1`.

This proves the lemma. `□`

### Why this matters and why it does not solve the source

This removes one possible explanation for the factor-two difficulty: articulation points themselves do **not** force us to throw away half the average degree. One can always enter a 2-connected block retaining all but an additive `1` in average degree.

However, this is not yet a probabilistic reduction with the correct a.a.s. quantifier. A graph can consist of many `Theta(d)`-vertex dense blocks. A particular selected block then has only a bounded-away-from-one chance of producing the required percolated structure, while the source conclusion comes from amplification across many blocks. Conversely, if one selected block grows with the host order, one needs a theorem inside a single large 2-connected irregular block. Thus the block lemma fixes the deterministic density loss but exposes a size/multiplicity dichotomy rather than closing the problem.

**B3 outcome:** new exact structural lemma; source remains OPEN.

## 5. B3 — a sharp-multiplier theorem for the high-girth / near-full-average branch

Nelson's theorem gives a particularly clean way to settle one branch at the conjectured multiplier.

Fix `epsilon>0`. Choose a constant

`0 < theta < epsilon/(1+epsilon)`.                       `(5.1)`

For example, `theta=epsilon/[4(1+epsilon)]` works. For a fixed sufficiently large `d`, define

`mu_d := (1-theta)d`,

`p_d := (1+epsilon)/d`,

and

`Delta_d := p_d - 1/(mu_d-1)`.                           `(5.2)`

Because (5.1) is equivalent to

`(1+epsilon)(1-theta)>1`,

we have `Delta_d>0` for all sufficiently large `d`.

### Theorem 5.1 — high-girth branch, at the source multiplier, for fixed d

Fix `epsilon,theta` as above, fix a sufficiently large `d` for which `Delta_d>0`, and fix `gamma>0`. Let `(H_n)` be any sequence of finite simple graphs with `|V(H_n)|=n`, average degree at least `(1-theta)d`, and girth at least

`L_d := floor(gamma d)+1`.

Then, at every `p>=p_d=(1+epsilon)/d`,

`P((H_n)_p contains a cycle of length >=L_d) -> 1`

as `n->infinity`.

#### Proof

It is enough by monotonicity to work at `p=p_d`.

Let `zeta>0` be arbitrary. Choose

`eta := min{zeta, Delta_d/2}`.

Then

`p_d >= 1/(mu_d-1) + eta`.

Nelson's Theorem 4.2, applied with average-degree parameter `mu_d` and additive margin `eta`, gives an integer `N=N(mu_d,eta)` such that for every `n>=N`, a `p_d`-random spanning subgraph of `H_n` contains a circuit with probability at least `1-eta>=1-zeta`.

Every circuit in `H_n` has length at least the girth, hence at least `L_d`. Therefore

`P((H_n)_{p_d} has a cycle of length >=L_d) >= 1-zeta`

for all `n>=N`. Since `zeta>0` was arbitrary, the probability tends to one. `□`

### Exact limitation

This theorem is deliberately stated **for fixed `d`**. Nelson's `N(mu,eta)` is not uniform in the regime relevant here, where

`mu=Theta(d)` and `eta=Theta(1/d)`.

Consequently Theorem 5.1 does not by itself handle arbitrary source sequences with `d=d(n)->infinity`. Calling it a proof of G0011 would be wrong. What it does prove is that, at every fixed sufficiently large `d`, a counterexample sequence cannot retain almost all of its average degree while having source-scale girth.

That nonuniformity is now an explicit gap rather than an implicit one.

## 6. B12 — conserved edge mass: short-cycle peeling gives an exact dichotomy

The previous run's nonbacktracking analysis said that repeated short divergence/rejoin excursions are the obstruction. The most literal way to quantify that statement is to conserve the host edge mass while removing those excursions.

Keep `epsilon,theta,d,gamma` as in Section 5 and put

`L := floor(gamma d)+1`.

Starting from `G`, repeatedly choose any cycle of length `<L` and delete **all edges of that cycle**. Stop when there is no cycle of length `<L`. Let the deleted cycles be

`C_1,...,C_r`,

with lengths `ell_1,...,ell_r`, and let the final graph be `H`.

The cycles are pairwise edge-disjoint by construction. Most importantly, there is an exact conservation law:

`m = e(H) + sum_{i=1}^r ell_i`,                         `(6.1)`

where `m=e(G)=nd/2`.

The final graph has

`girth(H) >= L`.                                          `(6.2)`

### Proposition 6.1 — source-scale high-girth versus linear short-cycle mass

For every such peeling, at least one of the following holds.

**A. High-girth density branch.**

`e(H) >= (1-theta)m`.

Then `H` has average degree at least `(1-theta)d` and girth at least `L`. For each fixed sufficiently large `d`, Theorem 5.1 implies that `H_p`, hence `G_p`, contains a cycle of length at least `L>gamma d` a.a.s. at `p>=(1+epsilon)/d`.

**B. Short-cycle mass branch.**

`e(H) < (1-theta)m`.

Then by (6.1)

`S := sum_i ell_i = m-e(H) > theta m = theta nd/2`.       `(6.3)`

Since every deleted cycle has

`ell_i <= L-1 = floor(gamma d) <= gamma d`,

we also have

`r >= S/(gamma d) > theta n/(2gamma)`.                   `(6.4)`

Thus failure of the high-girth branch forces not merely one or a few exceptional short cycles but a family of **linearly many edge-disjoint cycles** whose total edge length is a positive fraction of the entire host edge set.

#### Proof

Everything except the probabilistic conclusion in branch A follows directly from the stopping rule and the exact edge partition (6.1). Branch A invokes Theorem 5.1. `□`

### Interpretation

For fixed `d`, this is a genuine reduction of any remaining counterexample sequence to branch B. The obstruction has acquired a quantitative form:

`positive fraction of host edge mass = edge-disjoint short-cycle mass`.

This is substantially sharper than saying merely that the host has many triangles or a high Hashimoto overlap. It also aligns with Nelson's classical proof architecture, so no novelty claim is made for the peeling idea itself. The source-specific point is the scaling `L=Theta(d)` and the explicit identification of the branch that survives at the sharp multiplier.

**B12 outcome:** exact monovariant/conservation reduction; no change to `K_long` because branch B and the `d(n)` uniformity problem remain.

## 7. B24 — identify the extremal object inside the surviving packing branch

The edge-disjoint family in branch B can overlap very heavily in vertices. The cyclic clique-chain from the preceding run suggests one extreme (many locally dense blocks); an opposite extreme is a fan of many short cycles through a small hub set. The following exact load calculation separates those possibilities.

For every vertex `v` define its cycle load

`t(v) := |{ i : v in V(C_i) }|`.

Because each cycle contributes one incidence at each of its vertices,

`sum_v t(v) = sum_i ell_i = S`.                          `(7.1)`

Combining (7.1) with (6.3) gives

`(1/n) sum_v t(v) > theta d/2`.                           `(7.2)`

Therefore branch B always contains at least one vertex `v` with

`t(v) > theta d/2`.                                       `(7.3)`

Moreover the cycles are edge-disjoint, so the two edges used by distinct cycles at a common vertex are all distinct. Hence

`2 t(v) <= deg_G(v)`                                      `(7.4)`

for every `v`. Thus the packing branch necessarily has a vertex lying on `Omega(d)` edge-disjoint short cycles.

That alone is not enough: in a roughly regular dense block, every vertex can have cycle load `Theta(d)`. To split the two geometries more sharply, build the **cycle-intersection graph** `J`: its vertices are the cycles `C_i`, with two cycles adjacent when they share a host vertex.

### Proposition 7.1 — super-hub versus many vertex-disjoint short-cycle blocks

Fix any threshold `T>=1`. In branch B, one of the following holds:

1. **hub:** some host vertex belongs to at least `T` cycles `C_i`; or
2. **blocks:** there is a pairwise vertex-disjoint subfamily of the `C_i` of size at least

`r / (gamma d (T-1)+1)`.

Using (6.4), the second alternative contains more than

`theta n / [2gamma (gamma d (T-1)+1)]`                  `(7.5)`

pairwise vertex-disjoint short cycles.

#### Proof

Assume the hub alternative fails, so `t(v)<=T-1` for every host vertex.

Fix one packed cycle `C_i`. Every other packed cycle meeting `C_i` must meet it at some vertex `v in V(C_i)`. At a fixed such vertex there are at most `t(v)-1<=T-2` other packed cycles. Therefore

`deg_J(C_i)`
`<= sum_{v in V(C_i)} (t(v)-1)`
`<= ell_i (T-1)`
`<= gamma d (T-1)`.

Thus

`Delta(J) <= gamma d(T-1)`.

Every finite graph of maximum degree `D` has an independent set of size at least `|V|/(D+1)` by the greedy algorithm. Applying this to `J` gives an independent set of size at least

`r/(gamma d(T-1)+1)`.

An independent set in `J` is exactly a pairwise vertex-disjoint subfamily of the packed cycles. This proves the proposition. `□`

### A useful concrete specialization

Take `T=d` (for integer `d`; harmless ceilings can be inserted otherwise). Then every packing-branch host satisfies one of:

- a vertex lies on at least `d` pairwise edge-disjoint short cycles, hence by (7.4) has host degree at least `2d`; or
- there are at least

`theta n / [2gamma (gamma d(d-1)+1)] = Omega_{epsilon,gamma}(n/d^2)`

pairwise vertex-disjoint short-cycle blocks.

This is exactly the kind of partition-valued obstruction suggested by the preceding clique-chain stress test. Static localization to one globally heavy Perron set was too strong; the correct residual objects can be either many disjoint metastable blocks or a highly recycled hub through which many short excursions pass.

### Why this still does not close branch B

Neither side alone supplies the desired long cycle.

- A vertex-disjoint family of short cycles gives independent local opportunities, but opening an entire `Theta(d)`-length cycle has probability exponentially small in `d`; naive amplification is insufficient when `d` grows.
- A high-load hub can support many short cycles without supporting a long simple cycle through the hub, because any simple cycle can use the hub only once. The extremal warning is the same phenomenon visible in `K_{2,N}`-type fans: huge local nonbacktracking/cycle multiplicity can coexist with bounded circumference inside the fan itself.

The next argument must use the **connections between** these packed gadgets, not merely count them.

**B24 outcome:** an exact residual hub-versus-blocks dichotomy with quantitative bounds; source remains OPEN.

## 8. What changed mathematically this run

The universal numerical metric did not move, but the unresolved class became substantially narrower.

The chain of rigorous implications is now:

1. average degree `d>2` loses only an additive `1`, not a factor two, when one passes to a suitable 2-connected block (Lemma 4.1);
2. at the exact source multiplier `(1+epsilon)/d`, a near-full-average, source-scale-high-girth remainder is enough for the long-cycle conclusion for every fixed sufficiently large `d` (Theorem 5.1, via Nelson);
3. greedily removing all source-scale-short cycles conserves edge mass exactly; if the high-girth remainder does not retain `(1-theta)` of the edge mass, then a positive fraction `theta` of **all host edges** lies in a linearly large family of edge-disjoint short cycles (Proposition 6.1);
4. that short-cycle family necessarily has average vertex cycle-load `>theta d/2`, and quantitatively decomposes into either a super-hub or `Omega(n/d^2)` vertex-disjoint short-cycle blocks (Proposition 7.1).

So the remaining sharp-threshold problem is no longer an undifferentiated average-degree irregularity problem. It is the problem of proving that a near-critical percolation cannot remain trapped forever in a positive-edge-mass collection of short, overlapping cycle gadgets without eventually stitching them into a simple cycle of length `Omega(d)`.

## 9. Failure analysis and exact gaps

### Gap 1 — uniformity in d

Theorem 5.1 uses Nelson's Theorem 4.2 with

`mu=(1-theta)d`,

`eta < (1+epsilon)/d - 1/((1-theta)d-1) = Theta_epsilon(1/d)`.

The resulting host-order threshold `N(mu,eta)` is not controlled uniformly as `d` grows. The source allows sequences in which `d=d(n)` varies, so a fixed-`d` a.a.s. theorem is PARTIAL only.

### Gap 2 — short-cycle mass can be highly dependent

The cycles in Proposition 6.1 are edge-disjoint but need not be vertex-disjoint. The cycle-load/hub theorem measures that dependence but does not yet neutralize it.

### Gap 3 — local cycles are not enough

Even `Omega(n/d^2)` vertex-disjoint cycles of length at most `gamma d` do not directly yield one cycle of length `gamma d` after percolation. The event that one whole host cycle survives is the wrong event to amplify. One must instead exploit supercritical **path transmission through the gadgets** and then close a long loop globally.

## 10. Prior-art caution

Nelson's paper is especially important to the novelty assessment. His proof of the ordinary-circuit threshold already combines:

- a covering-tree survival estimate;

- a maximal family of edge-disjoint short circuits;

Therefore Lemma/Proposition 6.1 should be viewed as a source-scaled reorganization of a classical architecture, not as a claimed new technique. The potentially new content in this track would have to be the `Theta(d)`-scale stitching theorem needed for branch B, or a uniform-in-`d` strengthening sufficient to close G0011.
