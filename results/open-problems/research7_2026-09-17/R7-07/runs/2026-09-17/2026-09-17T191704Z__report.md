## 4. B19 — make the reduction algorithmic: retain half the cross-edge mass while preserving large semidegrees

### Idea before proof

The previous cross-core pruning guaranteed only that a nonempty high-semidegree subgraph survived. That is enough for a branching-number calculation but too weak for a global cycle argument: the core might in principle contain only a tiny residue of the positive edge mass. Use a fixed-threshold peeling algorithm that budgets only one quarter of the original cross edges to deletions on each side. This should leave both the same two-type geometry and a linear-in-`n d` edge reservoir.

### Proposition 4.1 — edge-retaining two-type core

Assume (1.1)--(1.2), and let `J=(U,W)` be the cross graph. There is a nonempty bipartite subgraph

`J_*=(A,B;E_*)`, `A subseteq U`, `B subseteq W`,

such that

`e(J_*) > e/2 > theta n d/8`,                               `(4.1)`

`delta_A(J_*) > d^2/2`,                                     `(4.2)`

`delta_B(J_*) > theta d/16`.                                `(4.3)`

#### Proof

Keep the original number `e=e(J)` fixed during the procedure. Repeatedly delete

- any current vertex of `U` whose current degree is `< e/(4|U|)`; or
- any current vertex of `W` whose current degree is `< e/(4|W|)`.

Every original `U`-vertex is deleted at most once, so the total number of edges removed at `U`-deletion steps is strictly less than

`|U| * e/(4|U|) = e/4`.

Likewise, all `W`-deletion steps remove strictly fewer than `e/4` edges in total. Hence strictly fewer than `e/2` edges are deleted altogether. The terminal subgraph therefore has more than `e/2` edges, proving (4.1), and every surviving vertex satisfies the corresponding threshold.

For `a in A`, use (1.1)--(1.2):

`deg_{J_*}(a) >= e/(4|U|)`
`> (theta n d/4)/(theta n/(2d))`
`= d^2/2`.

For `b in B`, use `|W|<=n`:

`deg_{J_*}(b) >= e/(4|W|)`
`> (theta n d/4)/(4n)`
`= theta d/16`.

This proves (4.2)--(4.3). `□`

In particular

`|B| > d^2/2`,                                              `(4.4)`

`|A| > theta d/16`.                                         `(4.5)`

The important improvement over the previous core is (4.1): the residual two-type geometry now carries a positive fraction of the original host edge mass, rather than merely existing.

**B19 outcome:** exact constructive peeling theorem; the cross obstruction can henceforth be assumed to satisfy (4.1)--(4.3).

## 5. B11 — dualize to the two-step projection and prove an overlap-insensitive degree bound

### Idea before proof

A cycle in a bipartite graph alternates between the two sides. Compress two steps `A -> B -> A` into one projected move. The obvious fear is that adversarial overlap among the connector neighborhoods `N(b)` could collapse the number of distinct projected neighbors even though the Hashimoto branching number is huge. The codegree identity below shows that this local collapse cannot happen in expectation: every possible codegree profile gives `Omega(d)` projected degree at the source probability.

Fix the edge-retaining core `J_*=(A,B)` and put

`D_A:=delta_A(J_*)`, `D_B:=delta_B(J_*)`.

Thus

`D_A>d^2/2`, `D_B>theta d/16`.                              `(5.1)`

Percolate the edges with

`p=(1+epsilon)/d`.                                          `(5.2)`

Define the random simple two-step projection `Q_p` on vertex set `A`: distinct `a,a'` are adjacent in `Q_p` iff there is some `b in B` for which both `ab` and `a'b` survive.

For fixed `a in A`, set

`r:=deg_{J_*}(a)`,

`c_{a,a'}:=|N_{J_*}(a) cap N_{J_*}(a')|`.

### Proposition 5.1 — exact codegree formula and uniform projected-degree lower bound

For every fixed `a in A`,

`E deg_{Q_p}(a)`
`= sum_{a'!=a} [1-(1-p^2)^{c_{a,a'}}]`                     `(5.3)`

and

`E deg_{Q_p}(a)`
`>= (D_B-1) [1-(1-p^2)^r]`                                 `(5.4)`

`>= (theta d/16-1) [1-exp(-(1+epsilon)^2/2)]`.              `(5.5)`

In particular the expectation is `Omega_{theta,epsilon}(d)` uniformly over every neighborhood-overlap pattern.

#### Proof

For a fixed pair `a,a'`, every common connector `b` supplies the event that the two distinct edges `ab,a'b` are both open. For different common connectors these events use disjoint original edges, hence are independent. This gives (5.3).

Double-count length-two host walks starting at `a`:

`sum_{a'!=a} c_{a,a'}`
`= sum_{b in N(a)} (deg(b)-1)`
`>= r(D_B-1)`.                                               `(5.6)`

Let

`f(x)=1-(1-p^2)^x`, `x>=0`.

The function is increasing, concave, and satisfies `f(0)=0`. Also every codegree obeys `0<=c_{a,a'}<=r`. Therefore the chord from `(0,0)` to `(r,f(r))` gives

`f(c_{a,a'}) >= (c_{a,a'}/r) f(r)`.

Summing and using (5.6) proves (5.4).

Finally, `r>=D_A>d^2/2`, so

`p^2 r > (1+epsilon)^2/2`,

and

`1-(1-p^2)^r >= 1-exp(-p^2 r)`
`> 1-exp(-(1+epsilon)^2/2)`.

Together with `D_B>theta d/16`, this gives (5.5). `□`

This is stronger than the previous spectral-radius observation in a different direction. The Hashimoto calculation said the average two-type branching operator is far supercritical. Proposition 5.1 says even an individual fixed `A`-vertex cannot have its entire two-step mass hidden by pathological codegrees.

## 6. B19 continued — an explicit two-stage exploration gives concentrated `Omega(d)` *distinct* projected neighbors

The expectation bound still permits a heavy-tail objection. The next lemma removes that objection for every fixed starting vertex, with an exponential-in-`d` failure probability.

### Proposition 6.1 — local distinct-reachability theorem

There are constants `alpha=alpha(theta,epsilon)>0`, `beta=beta(theta,epsilon)>0`, and `d_0` such that for every `d>=d_0` and every fixed `a in A`,

`Pr[ a has at least alpha d distinct neighbors in Q_p ] >= 1-3 exp(-beta d)`. `(6.1)`

One valid explicit choice is

`eta := 1-exp(-(1+epsilon)^2/8)`,

`alpha := theta eta/80`,                                    `(6.2)`

with some positive `beta` smaller than the constants appearing below.

#### Proof

Choose exactly

`R=floor(d^2/3)`                                             `(6.3)`

distinct connectors `b` from `N(a)`; this is possible for large `d` by (5.1). For every selected `b`, fix in advance a set

`S_b subseteq N(b)\{a}`, |S_b|=k:=floor(theta d/32)`.        `(6.4)`

Again this is possible for large `d` because `D_B>theta d/16`.

Expose first only the `R` edges from `a` to the selected connectors, and let

`Z={b: ab is open}`, `M=|Z|`.

Then

`M ~ Bin(R,p)`, `mu:=E M=Rp=Theta(d)`.                      `(6.5)`

By the standard multiplicative Chernoff bounds,

`Pr[M<mu/2] <= exp(-mu/8)`,

`Pr[M>2mu] <= exp(-mu/3)`.                                  `(6.6)`

Condition on a realization of `Z` satisfying `M>=mu/2`. For each `a'!=a`, let

`r_{a'}:=|{b in Z: a' in S_b}|`.

Now expose precisely the edges `ba'` with `b in Z` and `a' in S_b`. Let `Y_{a'}` indicate that at least one of those edges is open. Conditional on `Z`, the variables `Y_{a'}` for distinct `a'` are independent, because they depend on pairwise disjoint sets of original edges. Moreover

`Pr[Y_{a'}=1 | Z]=1-(1-p)^{r_{a'}}`.                         `(6.7)`

We have

`sum_{a'!=a} r_{a'} = M k`,                                 `(6.8)`

and every `r_{a'}<=M`. The function `g(x)=1-(1-p)^x` is concave with `g(0)=0`, so for `0<=x<=M`,

`g(x) >= (x/M) g(M)`.

Hence, writing `Y=sum Y_{a'}` for the number of distinct reached vertices,

`E[Y|Z]`
`= sum g(r_{a'})`
`>= k g(M)`.                                                 `(6.9)`

For sufficiently large `d`, `R>=d^2/4`. Since `M>=mu/2=Rp/2`,

`pM >= p^2 R/2 >= (1+epsilon)^2/8`.                         `(6.10)`

Therefore

`g(M)=1-(1-p)^M >= 1-exp(-pM) >= eta`.                      `(6.11)`

Also `k>=theta d/40` for sufficiently large `d`, so

`E[Y|Z] >= theta eta d/40`.                                 `(6.12)`

For a sum of independent, non-identically distributed Bernoulli variables, the lower-tail Chernoff bound gives

`Pr[Y < E[Y|Z]/2 | Z] <= exp(-E[Y|Z]/8)`
`<= exp(-theta eta d/320)`.                                 `(6.13)`

Combining (6.6) and (6.13), and weakening constants, yields (6.1) with `alpha=theta eta/80`. Every counted `a'` has an open length-two path `a-b-a'`, hence is a genuine neighbor in `Q_p`. `□`

### Interpretation

This proposition survives the two extremal overlap patterns that looked dangerous:

- if the chosen connector neighborhoods overlap almost completely, the concavity step still gives `Theta(d)` distinct reachable `A`-vertices;
- if they are diffuse, the same estimate follows from the total multiplicity identity (6.8).

Thus **arbitrary local neighborhood overlap is not itself the missing ingredient**. A fixed vertex sees source-scale breadth with exponentially high probability.

**B19 outcome:** explicit two-stage exploration algorithm plus a full concentration theorem; no metric movement because local breadth does not yet glue into a witness-distinct long cycle.

## 7. B26 — hunt the sporadic failure: the exact obstruction is not breadth but reuse of connector witnesses

### 7.1 Exact hypergraph/Berge-cycle reformulation

Given a realization of `J_{*,p}`, form a hypergraph `H_p` on vertex set `A` with one labelled hyperedge

`E_b := N_{J_{*,p}}(b)`

for every `b in B`.

A length-`ell` Berge cycle in `H_p` is a sequence of distinct vertices

`a_1,...,a_ell`

and distinct hyperedges

`E_{b_1},...,E_{b_ell}`

such that

`a_i,a_{i+1} in E_{b_i}`

with indices modulo `ell`.

### Proposition 7.1 — exact lifting equivalence

`J_{*,p}` has a simple cycle of length `2 ell` if and only if `H_p` has a Berge cycle of length `ell`.

#### Proof

A simple alternating bipartite cycle

`a_1 b_1 a_2 b_2 ... a_ell b_ell a_1`

has distinct `A`-vertices and distinct `B`-vertices, and exactly says that `a_i,a_{i+1}` belong to `E_{b_i}`. Conversely, a Berge cycle gives those two open incidence edges for each distinct witness `b_i`; the resulting alternating sequence is a simple cycle in `J_{*,p}`. `□`

Thus the surviving cross branch is no longer vaguely an “irregular bipartite” problem. Its exact source-scale subproblem is:

> prove that this random incidence hypergraph has a Berge cycle of length `Omega(d)` under (4.1)--(4.3).

### 7.2 Why the ordinary two-step projection is insufficient

The simple projection `Q_p` forgets witness labels. One connector `b` with many open neighbors produces a clique in `Q_p`. That clique may contain many ordinary cycles even if every edge of such a cycle is witnessed by the **same** `b`; those cycles do not lift to a simple bipartite cycle. Therefore Propositions 5.1 and 6.1 deliberately stop short of claiming source progress from an ordinary projected cycle.

This is the precise gluing failure left by B11.

### 7.3 Proper-colour reduction exposes a second factor-two barrier

There is a tempting repair: for each host connector `b`, pair its neighbors into a matching and color every projected pair by the witness `b`. Every color class is then a matching, so the projected host multigraph is properly edge-coloured. A projected pair survives exactly when its two original incidence edges survive; because the pairs use disjoint incidence edges within each `b`, and distinct connectors use disjoint incidence edges, these pair-survival events are mutually independent with probability `p^2`.

However this clean independence pays a factor two in the host degree. If `a=|A|`, `b=|B|`, and `e_*=e(J_*)`, the total number `M_0` of paired projected host edges satisfies

`M_0 = sum_{v in B} floor(deg(v)/2)`
`>= (e_*-b)/2`.                                             `(7.1)`

Since `b<=e_*/D_B`,

`2M_0/a >= (e_*/a)(1-1/D_B)`
`>= D_A(1-1/D_B)`
`> (d^2/2)(1-o(1))`.                                        `(7.2)`

At projected retention probability `p^2`, the guaranteed average-degree scale is therefore only

`p^2 * 2M_0/a > ((1+epsilon)^2/2)(1-o(1)).`                 `(7.3)`

For small `epsilon`, the right-hand side is below `1`. Thus the most straightforward “make every witness color proper by disjoint pairing, then apply ordinary random-graph/rainbow machinery” discards exactly enough incidence information to lose the near-critical regime. De-duplication of parallel projected pairs can only lose more.

This is a useful failed route rather than a proof gap hidden by notation: independence plus proper witness colours is too expensive if obtained by using every incidence edge at most once.

### 7.4 What the sporadic search actually rules out

The previous report already proved the complete-bipartite residual-scale model has an `Omega(d)` cycle a.a.s.; therefore maximal codegree / complete neighborhood overlap is **not** a counterexample mechanism. Proposition 6.1 now shows that no intermediate codegree distribution can even suppress one-vertex source-scale two-step breadth. The remaining obstruction is global:

- retain enough of the repeated-incidence information to stay above the `K=1` threshold;
- while selecting a long cycle whose connector labels are all distinct.

This is exactly a Berge/rainbow **witness-distinct gluing** problem.

The 2024--2025 properly edge-coloured rainbow-cycle theorems do not immediately close it. Their hypotheses concern a proper colouring of an ordinary graph and their thresholds depend on the number of projected vertices; the natural full two-section here has each witness color spanning a clique, hence is not properly coloured. The disjoint-pairing operation repairs properness but incurs the factor-two loss (7.3).

**B26 outcome:** no source counterexample found; high overlap, low overlap and arbitrary local overlap all fail as purely local obstructions. The surviving exact obstruction is global distinct-witness gluing, and the naive proper-colour repair reproduces a quantitative factor-two barrier.
