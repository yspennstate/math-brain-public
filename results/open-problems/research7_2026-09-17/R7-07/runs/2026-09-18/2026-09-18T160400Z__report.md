## 2. Exact blocking formula

The current obstruction is no longer the false pointwise quantity `rho_Q`. The preceding run proved that pointwise growing-modulus mixing fails because full-affine-span two-valued rows can have exponentially larger-than-uniform null probability, while their **aggregate** contribution is exponentially negligible after the entropy of choosing the row is included.

For a cyclic modulus `Q`, let

\[
S_Q^{(\ge3)}(A)
:=
\#\Big\{y\in(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)^n:
 yA=0,
 y\text{ is surjective},
 |\{y_1,\ldots,y_n\}|\ge3
\Big\}.
\]

The exact primitive-null-vector surjection inequality already on file is

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{\det A=0\}}
\le
\frac{|\operatorname{Sur}(\operatorname{cok}A,\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)|}{\varphi(Q)}.
\]

The preceding report also disposed of the exactly two-valued portion by an exponentially decaying aggregate estimate. Therefore the sharpest current **next-movement** blocker is the following source-relevant averaged statement:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,S_Q^{(\ge3)}(A_{n,3})\le n^{o(1)}
\quad\text{for some squarefree }Q=n^{1/3-o(1)}
\text{ with }\varphi(Q)=Qn^{o(1)}.
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

`(BLOCK)` would imply

\[
\Pr(A_{n,3}\text{ singular})
\le
n^{-1/3+o(1)}
\]

up to the already exponentially negligible two-valued term, hence would move the frozen metric strictly beyond `1/4` to `a_3>=1/3-o(1)`.

This is **not** asserted proved here. It is the precise next obstruction selected by the cumulative work. It is also deliberately weaker than the full source endpoint `a_3=1`.

## 3. Why the quarter exponent is the present analytic frontier

The last movement run used rowwise Cramer tilting, exceptional-row compatibility, and localized shifted-theta estimates to obtain `a_3>=1/4`. The one-row undirected Fourier suppression has the form

\[
\exp(-c n/p^3),
\]

because one effective row has `N_i\asymp n/p` steps and the robust inverse phase gap is only `c/p^2`. After the successful removal of the old global type-count payment, the remaining finite-character/orbit complexity is of size `\exp(O(p\log p))`; the cumulative Gaussian/cumulant control also carries the condition `p^4(\log n)^C=o(n)`.

Thus the current one-prime architecture naturally stops at approximately `p=n^{1/4-o(1)}`. This is not evidence that the source exponent is `1/4`; it is a precise statement that the successful route to `1/4` does not contain a mechanism that produces more than one-row suppression or that averages the medium residue profiles at a larger modulus.

Any improvement now has to do something qualitatively absent from that route: either obtain collective suppression from the symmetric edge constraints, or abandon worst-row control and compare **profile entropy against profile-specific pairing cost**.

## 4. Complete post-`1/4` route inventory and exact failure of each route

The following inventory is complete for the three ordinary no-movement runs after the `a_3>=1/4` movement. Earlier successful methods are retained as the frontier described above; they are not relabelled failures.

### Route 1 — B14: quotient the structured Fourier centers by symmetry

Idea: use the symmetric `S_p` action and row-coupling to identify the many structured Fourier saddles as duplicate copies, reducing their entropy.

Exact failure: for each residue-center shift `c_k`, the row characteristic function has an exact period, and on every feasible row target the full Fourier coefficient integrand has the same period. The `p` structured row centers are therefore genuine phase-coherent translates, and the product representation has corresponding center tuples that cannot simply be divided out as redundant orbits.

What remains missing: a **collective** identity coupling several row phases before factorization. Ordinary rowwise symmetry does not supply it.

### Route 2 — B21: move to the integer cokernel and use several accessible primes

Idea: replace one prime beyond the quarter scale by a squarefree cyclic group built from several primes each within the already controlled quarter scale. Real singularity forces a primitive integer null vector, hence nontrivial modular kernels at every prime.

Success: exact deterministic surjection inequalities and CRT reductions were obtained.

Failure as a progress route: fixed-finite-group cokernel asymptotics do not give error terms uniform when the target group grows polynomially with `n`. The relevant observable is also unbounded, so bare contiguity or fixed-group convergence cannot be promoted to the required growing-modulus expectation without new quantitative work.

### Route 3 — B27: reduce four-prime control to one mixed-nullity moment

Idea: for quarter-scale primes `p_i`, bound

\[
\mathbb E\prod_i L_{p_i}(A),
\qquad
L_p(A)=\frac{|\ker(A\bmod p)|-1}{p-1},

at the twin scale.

Success: the mixed-moment inequality is exact and calibrated correctly to the real-singular event.

Failure: no available argument controls **simultaneous modular singularity without real singularity**, especially large-nullity tails and correlations between the kernels at different primes. The reduction changed the location of the gap but did not estimate it.

### Route 4 — B5: make the modulus size a continuation parameter

Idea: write `Q=n^{\theta+o(1)}` and seek uniform finite-group mixing, so a cyclic-surjection first moment would imply `a_3>=theta`.

Exact failure in the imported fixed-group architecture: a displayed occupancy condition in the Mészaros proof has the scale `m_q(c)>10|V|^2`; for a balanced vector with occupancy about `n/Q`, this already enforces approximately

A separate non-typical-vector union-bound component produces the same qualitative barrier. More importantly, those results explicitly allow constants to depend on the fixed target group. Thus fixed-group convergence does not automatically become a theorem at `Q=n^{1/3-o(1)}`.

### Route 5 — B12: use exact regularity invariants

Idea: constrain every modular null row by conservation laws.

Success: every surjective modular null row for `gcd(d,Q)=1` has zero coordinate sum and full affine span; every primitive real null vector reduces to a surjective modular null vector for every `Q`.

Failure: these invariants are much too weak for pointwise anticoncentration. The two-valued equitable-cut rows from the next run satisfy both invariants while having null probability exponentially larger than `Q^{-(n-1)}`. Therefore zero sum + full affine span do not identify the probabilistically typical rows.

### Route 6 — B25: count all source-compatible affine rows exactly

Idea: use Mobius inversion to count zero-sum full-affine-span vectors and see whether candidate entropy itself provides a saving.

Success: the candidate count is essentially exactly `Q^{n-1}` for the useful squarefree regimes.

Failure: that is the opposite of an entropy saving. It proves that the problem is entirely in the **probability weight assigned to different residue profiles**, not in a hidden deficit in the number of candidate rows.

### Route 7 — B20: seek a pointwise growing-modulus mixing theorem

Idea: prove

\[
Q^{n-1}\Pr(A_{n,3}y=0)=n^{o(1)}
\]

uniformly over zero-sum, full-affine-span rows.

Exact failure: the theorem is false. Two-valued full-affine-span rows correspond in the cubic model to equitable cuts in which every vertex has exactly one neighbour in the minority class. A fixed such row has probability of order `\sqrt n(4/9)^n`, so after multiplication by `Q^{n-1}` the proposed uniform bound is enormous for any polynomial `Q`.

This was a refutation of the auxiliary pointwise milestone, **not** a refutation of Vu's source conjecture.

### Route 8 — B28: replace pointwise control by averaged profile control

Idea: sum the exceptional rows instead of taking a supremum.

Success: the complete two-valued stratum has expected surjection mass

\[
\mathbb E S_Q^{(2)}(A)
\asymp
\varphi(Q)\left(\frac{2^{4/3}}3\right)^n

up to the explicit polynomial/constant prefactor already proved, and `2^{4/3}/3<1`. Hence the exact family that destroys pointwise mixing is harmless after averaging.

Failure: this closes **only** support size two. No existing argument interpolates from this low-alphabet exact counting regime to the near-uniform local-CLT regime. The entire medium-entropy region with at least three residues remains unbounded at polynomially growing `Q`.

### Route 9 — B3: prove a stronger structural classification

Success: the two-level family was completely identified with equitable cuts, and the cubic total mass was evaluated.

Failure as a source-progress route: the classification gives no structure theorem for three or more residue values. In particular it does not constrain the medium-profile region in `(BLOCK)`.

## 5. Missing ingredient — what a successful proof must contain that no route contains

Every current route handles one of two ends of the residue-profile spectrum:

1. **very low alphabet / high structure:** exact combinatorial classification can beat entropy (support size two is now done);
2. **near uniform / high entropy:** finite-field Fourier/local-CLT machinery gives strong anticoncentration, but the present undirected quantitative implementation reaches only the quarter scale.

What is absent is a theorem for the interval between them.

A proof that moves `a_3` beyond `1/4` through the cokernel route needs a **profile-sensitive inverse anticoncentration theorem for the undirected symmetric configuration model at growing modulus**. In one useful form, it would associate to each residue occupancy profile `nu` a cost `Psi(nu)` such that

\[
\sum_{y:\,\operatorname{prof}(y)=\nu}
\Pr(A_{n,3}y=0)
\le
\exp\{-n\Psi(\nu)+o(n)\},
\]

with a uniform inequality strong enough that, after summing all profiles with support at least three, the total is `n^{o(1)}` at `Q=n^{1/3-o(1)}`.

The required theorem must contain an **inverse statement**: whenever a profile has anomalously weak anticoncentration, it must collapse to an explicitly structured low-alphabet/local-constraint family whose aggregate entropy can be counted and shown harmless. Neither the quarter-scale rowwise Fourier argument nor the exact two-level counting argument supplies this interpolation.

Equivalently, the missing ingredient is not another Taylor coefficient or another exact affine invariant. It is an entropy-versus-pairing-cost dichotomy that is uniform in a polynomially growing modulus and respects the undirected symmetry `A=A^T`.

## 7. Nearest solved analogue in the literature

The nearest quantitative solved analogue is the **directed** fixed-degree regular model of Hoi H. Nguyen and Amanda Pan, *A note on the singularity probability of random directed d-regular graphs*, arXiv:2308.06461, published in *European Journal of Combinatorics* in 2024. They prove

\[
\Pr(A\text{ singular})\le n^{-1/3+o(1)}
\]

for the directed model. Their method studies singularity modulo a prime and proves an inverse-type decay theorem for the characteristic function, related to an inverse Kneser problem. This is exactly the scale currently targeted by `(BLOCK)`.

What their setting has that the undirected source lacks is an exposure structure compatible with directed row randomness. In the undirected graph, the identity `A_{ij}=A_{ji}` couples the row constraints through the same edge variables. The R7-07 quarter-exponent proof handles this by symmetric type enumeration and rowwise tilting, but that representation does not preserve enough independence to import the directed inverse theorem unchanged at the `n^{1/3}` modulus scale.

The freshest nearby follow-up found on 2026-09-18 is Yukun He and Jiaoyang Huang, *The oriented Kesten--McKay law for random regular digraphs*, arXiv:2609.05297, submitted 2026-09-04. It again concerns the directed model and uses finite-field anticoncentration for shifted inverse compressions together with fixed-rank transposition arguments to obtain hard-edge control. It strengthens the case that finite-field inverse anticoncentration is the right neighboring technology, but it does not solve Vu's fixed-degree undirected sharp exponent.

Huang's 2018 undirected/directed invertibility paper remains the basic source for the finite-field local-CLT approach, while Mészaros and Nguyen--Wood give fixed-finite-group sandpile/cokernel distributions. Their group size is fixed in the relevant asymptotics; none supplies `(BLOCK)` for polynomially growing cyclic groups.

A bounded fresh 2026-09-18 search did not surface a theorem proving

\[
\Pr(A_{n,d}\text{ singular})=n^{-d+2+o(1)}
\]

for the fixed undirected model. That is negative evidence only; it is not a novelty certificate.
