# 4. B1 — exact Fourier–Hafnian identity for the cubic configuration model

Work first with the cubic configuration model: `3n` labelled half-edges partitioned three per vertex, with a uniform perfect matching `M`. Let `A_M` be the multigraph adjacency matrix, using the usual convention that a loop contributes `2` to its diagonal entry.

For a prime `p`, put `G=F_p^2`, write `z=(t,y)`, and define

\[
B((t,y),(t',y'))=ty'+t'y,
\qquad
K(z,w)=e_p(B(z,w)).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For fixed `t,y in F_p^n`, every matched half-edge pair `{h,h'}` contributes

\[
t_{v(h)}y_{v(h')}+t_{v(h')}y_{v(h)}.
\]

Therefore, exactly,

\[
t^TA_My
=
\sum_{\{h,h'\}\in M}
B((t_{v(h)},y_{v(h)}),(t_{v(h')},y_{v(h')})).
\tag{4.2}
\]

If the matched half-edges lie at the same vertex, (4.2) contributes `2t_i y_i`, so the loop convention is correct without a hidden correction.

Fourier inversion gives

\[
1_{\{A_My=0\}}=p^{-n}\sum_{t\in F_p^n}e_p(t^TA_My).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `\mathcal K^{(t,y)}` be the `3n x 3n` symmetric half-edge matrix whose off-diagonal entry at `(h,h')` is the kernel in (4.1) evaluated at the states attached to the incident vertices. Since a Hafnian is the sum of products over all perfect matchings,

\[
\operatorname{Haf}(\mathcal K^{(t,y)})
=
\sum_M e_p(t^TA_My).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence the fixed-row probability has the exact collective formula

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm conf}(A_My=0)
=
\frac{p^{-n}}{(3n-1)!!}
\sum_{t\in F_p^n}\operatorname{Haf}(\mathcal K^{(t,y)}).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Summing over `y` and using a formal Wick functional `\mathcal W_K` with covariance `K`, with

\[
P(Z)=\sum_{z\in G}Z_z^3,
\]

gives the aggregate identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\rm conf}|\ker(A_M\bmod p)|
=
\frac{p^{-n}}{(3n-1)!!}\mathcal W_K[P(Z)^n].
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is algebraic Wick pairing, not a positivity claim. It validates the STUCK2 representation exactly and keeps the undirected matching constraint instead of replacing it by independent rows.

**B1 outcome:** positive. The growing-modulus first moment is now one exact tensor contraction rather than a product of rowwise local approximations.

# 5. B11 — flat edge spectrum: a rigorous no-go for the naive spectral version

Normalize the edge kernel by

\[
U=p^{-1}K
\]

as an operator on `l^2(F_p^2)`, and let `Rf(z)=f(-z)`. Character orthogonality gives

\[
K^2=p^2R,
\qquad
KK^*=p^2I,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{U^2=R,\qquad U^4=I,\qquad U\text{ is unitary}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

For odd `p`, direct diagonal traces are

\[
\operatorname{tr}U=\operatorname{tr}U^2=\operatorname{tr}U^3=1.
\]

Thus the four possible eigenvalues `{1,-1,i,-i}` have multiplicities

\[
\boxed{
 m_1=\frac{p^2+3}{4},
\quad
 m_{-1}=m_i=m_{-i}=\frac{p^2-1}{4}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every eigenvalue has modulus one and every eigenspace has dimension `Theta(p^2)`. Therefore the hoped-for theorem cannot come from multiplying edge spectral gaps: **there is no spectral gap**.

The cubic copy tensor

\[
D(x_1,x_2,x_3)=1_{\{x_1=x_2=x_3\}}
\]

has the exact Fourier dual

\[
\boxed{
U^{\otimes3}D(w_1,w_2,w_3)
=p^{-1}1_{\{w_1+w_2+w_3=0\}}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Its Frobenius norm is preserved exactly:

\[
\|D\|_F^2=p^2
=
\left\|p^{-1}1_{\{w_1+w_2+w_3=0\}}\right\|_F^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

So neither edge operator norm nor one-vertex Frobenius norm contains the missing decay.

**B11 first outcome:** negative but decisive. The naive “diagonalize `K` and harvest a spectral contraction” route is exhausted by (5.1)-(5.4).

# 6. B11 positive replacement — projective scaling gives multiplicative-charge sectors

The modular first moment is naturally divided by `p-1` because nonzero kernel vectors are counted projectively. The Fourier kernel has exactly the same symmetry. For `lambda in F_p^*`, set

\[
g_\lambda(t,y)=(\lambda^{-1}t,\lambda y).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then `B(g_lambda z,g_lambda w)=B(z,w)`, and hence `K` commutes with the induced multiplicative action.

The orbits on `F_p^2` are the origin, the two nonzero coordinate axes, and the `p-1` hyperbolas

\[
O_r=\{(t,y):ty=r\},\qquad r\in F_p^*.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently the trivial-character sector has dimension `p+2`, every nontrivial multiplicative-character sector has dimension `p+1`, and

\[
(p+2)+(p-2)(p+1)=p^2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is an exact reduction from one `p^2`-state transform to `p-1` charge sectors of only `p+O(1)` states.

For a multiplicative character `eta`, parameterize a hyperbola by `(a,r/a)` and let `e_{r,eta}` be the corresponding normalized character vector. Then for `r,s !=0`, a direct change of variables yields

\[
\boxed{
\langle e_{s,\eta},Ue_{r,\eta}\rangle
=
\frac1p\sum_{x\ne0}\eta(x)e_p(sx+r/x).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the generic charge block is a normalized twisted Kloosterman transform. The classical Weil bound gives

\[
\boxed{
|\langle e_{s,\eta},Ue_{r,\eta}\rangle|
\le 2p^{-1/2}
\qquad(rs\ne0).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The trivial/origin/axis sector has exceptional nearly nondecaying coordinates; generic hyperbola sectors have square-root **entrywise** cancellation. This does not contradict unitarity: the block operator norm may still equal one. The gain is delocalization, not a spectral gap.

Because the cubic copy tensor is invariant under simultaneous multiplicative scaling on its three legs, its character coefficient vanishes unless

\[
\boxed{\eta_1\eta_2\eta_3=1.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

With an oriented edge convention, opposite ends carry inverse charge. Therefore the Fourier–Hafnian network decomposes exactly into multiplicative-character **flows** on the cubic graph.

A connected cubic graph has cycle-space dimension

\[
|E|-|V|+1=n/2+1,

so exponentially many such flows exist. Multiplying the pointwise `p^{-1/2}` bound edge-by-edge and then naively summing flows can lose the entire gain. The new missing theorem is therefore a multilinear/inverse statement: a nontrivial charge flow that avoids the axis block should be unable to saturate the unitary norm at too many vertices unless it belongs to a small structured family.

**B11 second outcome:** positive structural reduction. The correct dual object is a charge-conserving Kloosterman tensor network, not a collection of decaying eigenmodes.

# 7. B30 — exact one-prime gluing failure after edge diagonalization

Orient every edge. Absorb `U` into the head half-edge so that the edge tensor `K=pU` becomes `p` times an identity contraction. At a vertex of indegree `k`, the local tensor becomes

\[
T_k=(U^{\otimes k}\otimes I^{\otimes(3-k)})D.
\]

The four tensors are explicit:

\[
T_0=1_{\{x_1=x_2=x_3\}},
\]

\[
T_1(w,x_2,x_3)=p^{-1}e_p(B(w,x_2))1_{\{x_2=x_3\}},

\[
T_2(w_1,w_2,x)=p^{-2}e_p(B(w_1+w_2,x)),

\[
T_3(w_1,w_2,w_3)=p^{-1}1_{\{w_1+w_2+w_3=0\}}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Direct counting gives the exact local-norm identity

\[
\boxed{\|T_k\|_F=p\quad(k=0,1,2,3).}
\tag{7.2}
\]

So the apparent `p^{-1}` and `p^{-2}` factors are precisely offset by support growth. Any proof that takes absolute values vertex-by-vertex, or uses only edge operator norm plus local Frobenius norms, cannot see the desired growing-modulus decay.

The mixed tensors `T_1,T_2` disappear exactly when every vertex has indegree `0` or `3`. Such an orientation exists iff the graph is bipartite. In the cubic configuration model, for one balanced bipartition the all-crossing matching probability is

\[
\frac{(3n/2)!}{(3n-1)!!}

\frac{2^{3n/2}}{\binom{3n}{3n/2}}
=(1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{3\pi n}{2}}2^{-3n/2}.

After a union bound over balanced bipartitions,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm conf}(G\text{ bipartite})
\le
\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+o(1)\right)2^{-n/2}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Conditioning on simplicity changes this only by a fixed factor for cubic configurations. Hence the clean copy/zero-sum gluing regime is exponentially rare in the source model.
