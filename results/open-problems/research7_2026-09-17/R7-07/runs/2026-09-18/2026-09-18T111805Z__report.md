# Idea before code

The `1/6` proof still paid several worst-case errors separately. The new idea is to recognize that the symmetric type sum is already governed by Huang's exact centered Gaussian, so odd cubic corrections should be **averaged before they are bounded**. Under the isotypic decomposition, their variances are only `O(p^2)` in normalized coordinates, and one exact conservation law removes the common row-energy mode from the denominator correction.

After those central losses disappear, the first remaining obstruction is genuinely noncentral: a row outside the structured characteristic-function neighborhoods is suppressed only by `exp(-c n/p^3)`, while the symmetric type problem has `p^2`-dimensional entropy. A new exact dual-support lemma shows the congruence theta problem has the same `exp(-c n/p^3)` scale. Balancing this against `exp(O(p^2 log n))` gives the new prime scale `p=n^{1/5-o(1)}`.

No numerical or solver evidence is used; the new claims are analytic and combinatorial and are proved on the page.

# Method B5 — parameterize the central perturbation and average it

Retain the cumulative exact isotypic variables

\[
Q(A)=\frac{dnp^2}{4}\|H\|_F^2+\frac{np}{2}\|r\|^2,
\]

\[
X=\sqrt n\,pH,
\qquad Y=\sqrt{np}\,r,
\qquad Z_{ij}=X_{ij}+Y_j/\sqrt p,
\]

and introduce

\[
W_{ij}=\sqrt n\,pA_{ij}=X_{ij}+(Y_i+Y_j)/\sqrt p.
\]

A reusable Wick lemma was proved: for any centered Gaussian array `G` with `O(p^2)` coordinates and bounded covariance operator,

\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_a G_a^3\right)=O(p^2).
\tag{B5.1}
\]

Indeed

\[
E(G_a^3G_b^3)=9C_{aa}C_{bb}C_{ab}+6C_{ab}^3,
\]

and the two double sums are respectively bounded by `||C||op ||diag C||^2=O(p^2)` and `max|C_ab| tr(C^2)=O(p^2)`.

The Stirling entropy cubic has the scale

\[
\Psi_{St,3}
=c_{3,d}\frac p{\sqrt n}\sum_{ij}W_{ij}^3
+c'_{3,d}\frac{\sqrt p}{\sqrt n}\sum_iY_i^3,
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{Var}\Psi_{St,3}=O_d(p^4/n).
\tag{B5.2}
\]

It is odd. On Huang's broad type ball its absolute size is `o(Q)`, so pairing `G` and `-G` and using

\[
0\le\cosh x-1\le x^2e^{|x|}/2
\]

shows its normalized Gaussian cost is only `1+O_d(p^4/n)+o(1)`, not the pointwise cubic maximum.

The same argument applies to the previously recovered one-row Edgeworth cubic after symmetric gluing:

\[
\Psi_{loc,3}=O_d(p/\sqrt n)\sum_{ij}Z_{ij}^3,
\]

which again has variance `O_d(p^4/n)`.

The even Stirling quartic contributes

\[
O_d\left(\frac{p^2}{n}\sum W_{ij}^4\right),
\]

whose Gaussian expectation is `O_d(p^4/n)`. Higher entropy terms gain another `o(1)` relative factor because `max|p^2A_ij|+max|pr_i|=o(1)` on the broad equidistributed ball.

Finally, Nguyen--Pan's fourth-moment remainder is integrated rather than maximized. With row energies `S_i=sum_j Z_ij^2`, the target part is bounded by

\[
C_d\left[\frac pn\sum_iS_i^2+
\frac{p^2}{n}\sum_{ij}Z_{ij}^4\right].
\]

Under the quadratic Gaussian,

\[
E\sum_iS_i^2=O_d(p^3),
\qquad E\sum_{ij}Z_{ij}^4=O_d(p^2),
\]

so the normalized fourth-order cost is `O_d(p^4 log n/n)` after a standard Gaussian-core/tail split. This removes the old `p^6/n` barrier.

**B5 outcome:** every central Stirling/cubic/quartic correction is harmless well beyond the `1/5` scale.

# Method B12 — exact conservation in the row-denominator tensor

The exact row-normalization correction is

\[
E_2(A)=\frac d2\sum_iS_i\frac{x_i}{1+x_i},
\qquad x_i=pr_i=\sqrt{p/n}\,Y_i.
\]

Its leading odd term is proportional to

\[
P:=\sum_iY_iS_i.
\]

Writing `R_i=sum_j X_ij^2`, direct expansion gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
P=\sum_iY_iR_i+\frac2{\sqrt p}Y^TXY.
}
\tag{B12.1}
\]

The common term `||Y||^2/p` in each `S_i` disappears because `sum_iY_i=0`. This is the conserved mode missed by the pointwise argument.

Conditioning on `X` and using that `Y` is Gaussian on `1^perp`,

\[
E_Y\left(\sum_iY_iR_i\right)^2
=\|P_{1^\perp}R\|^2.
\]

Permutation symmetry gives equal means for the `R_i`, and each quadratic row energy has variance `O_d(p)`, hence the first term has variance `O_d(p^2)`. The quadratic-form identity

\[
E_Y(Y^TXY)^2=2\operatorname{tr}X^2+(\operatorname{tr}X)^2
\]

makes the second term only `O_d(p)` after its `4/p` factor. Thus

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}P=O_d(p^2).}
\tag{B12.2}
\]

The leading denominator fluctuation therefore has variance `O_d(p^3/n)`, while the even remainder has expectation `O_d(p^3/n)`. The exact denominator changes normalized mass by `1+O_d(p^3/n)+o(1)`.

**B12 outcome:** the `p^3/sqrt(n)` pointwise denominator obstruction in the `1/6` proof is artificial; the global conserved row mode removes it.

# Method B24 — exact extremal dual support and the real `1/5` barrier

The exact congruence homomorphism has mod-`p` weighted-row characters `beta in F_p^p`. On an off-diagonal count coordinate `(i,j)` the character numerator is

\[
\beta_i j+\beta_j i,
\]

and on the diagonal half-coordinate it is `2 beta_i i`.

A new exact support lemma proves that every nonzero `beta` acts nontrivially on at least `p-1` independent upper-triangular count coordinates.

- If `beta_0 !=0`, the `p-1` coordinates `(0,j)`, `j!=0`, are all nonzero.
- If `beta_0=0` and the nonzero support inside `{1,...,p-1}` has size `k`, the `k` diagonal coordinates plus the `k(p-1-k)` cross coordinates give
  \[
  k+k(p-1-k)=k(p-k)\ge p-1.
  \]

Because `gcd(p,d)=1`, a nonzero `a/p` frequency cannot be cancelled modulo integers by an added mod-`d` frequency `b/d`. Therefore the squared Euclidean distance of every character with nontrivial mod-`p` part from the integer dual lattice satisfies

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{dist}^2\ge c_d/p.}
\tag{B24.1}
\]

In count coordinates the Gaussian precision is `Theta_d(p^2/n)`, hence Poisson summation yields

\[
\left|S_\chi/S_1\right|
\le
\exp\{C_dp^2\log n-c_dn/p^3\}.
\tag{B24.2}
\]

Differentiated Poisson summation gives the same exponential suppression for every fixed polynomial moment required by B5/B12, so those cancellations hold on the **exact congruence theta sum**, not only in the continuous Gaussian.

Independently, Nguyen--Pan's inverse characteristic theorem applied to the identical one-row `U_{d,p}` walk says that a row frequency outside the `p` structured neighborhoods has characteristic modulus bounded away from one by `c/p^2`. Since an undirected row contains `n_i asymp n/p` iid row-walk increments, its outer contribution is

\[
\exp\{-c n_i/p^2\}=\exp\{-c n/p^3\}.
\tag{B24.3}
\]

The symmetric type entropy is at most `exp(O(p^2 log n))`. Both (B24.2) and (B24.3) are therefore negligible under the common sufficient condition

\[
\boxed{p^5\log n=o(n).}
\tag{B24.4}
\]

**B24 outcome:** after the central averaging improvements, the first surviving barrier is an outer-frequency/type-complexity competition, and its natural polynomial endpoint is `1/5`.

# Main theorem proved this run

Choose by Bertrand a prime

\[
p\asymp \frac{n^{1/5}}{(\log n)^2}.
\]

Then `p^5 log n/n ->0`. The central errors from B5/B12 vanish, the exact congruence and outer-frequency errors vanish by B24, Huang's non-sparse non-equidistributed classes remain exponentially small by their class-specific bounds, and the cumulative sparse support event remains `O_d(n^{2-d})` because `p^3 log n=o(n)`.

Thus the expected number `N_ns` of nonzero finite-field kernel vectors outside the sparse event is `O_d(1)`. If the matrix is singular over `F_p` outside that event, its kernel contains at least `p-1` nonzero vectors, so

\[
\Pr(A\text{ singular over }F_p)
\le O_d(n^{2-d})+O_d(1/p).
\]

Real singularity of an integer adjacency matrix implies singularity modulo every prime. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(A_{n,d}\text{ singular over }\mathbb R)
\le O_d(n^{2-d})+n^{-1/5+o(1)}
}
\]

for every fixed `d>=3`; in particular

\[
\boxed{a_3\ge1/5.}
\]

This is a source-matching metric improvement but **not** the source endpoint `a_3=1`.
