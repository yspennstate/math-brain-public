## B15 — break AP symmetry and classify every local zero-sum type

Work over a prime field `F_p` with `p>7`. Let the coordinate support be three distinct residues `S={a,b,c}`. At any vertex, the three neighbour labels form a multiset from `S` summing to zero. There are only three algebraic possibilities:

1. `000`, requiring `0 in S`;
2. a double-single multiset `(r,r,s)`, requiring `2r+s=0`;
3. the all-distinct multiset `(a,b,c)`, requiring `a+b+c=0`.

No other partition of three positions exists. Undirected edge balance then classifies feasible combinations of these local types.

If the all-distinct type is the only type, each vertex sends exactly one half-edge to each colour. Symmetry `H_{ab}=H_{ba}` therefore forces all three colour classes to have equal size.

If all-distinct coexists with a double-single relation, say `a+b+c=0` and `2a+b=0`, then `c=a`, contradicting distinct support. If it coexists with `000`, then one support value is zero and the other two are negatives, giving the AP support `u{-1,0,1}`.

If all-distinct is absent, one local type cannot be feasible: any double-single or `000` type omits at least one support colour, so no half-edge from any source vertex aims at that colour, contradicting edge balance because the omitted colour class is nonempty. Hence at least two local types occur. If one is `000`, the other relation is among the two nonzero values and gives `u{0,1,-2}`. If both are double-single relations, after relabelling they are `2a+b=0` and `2b+c=0`, hence `S=u{1,-2,4}`. A third distinct double-single relation would close the cycle and force `9a=0`, impossible for `p>7`; sharing the doubled or the single value forces a support collision.

Thus every exactly-three-valued modular null row belongs, up to nonzero scalar multiplication, to one of four exhaustive classes:

\[
\boxed{
Z:\ a+b+c=0;\qquad
AP:\ u\{-1,0,1\};\qquad
C0:\ u\{0,1,-2\};\qquad
C4:\ u\{1,-2,4\}.
}
\]

Here `Z` means the generic zero-sum case with only the all-distinct local type; AP is removed from it and handled separately.

**Outcome:** the arbitrary support-three problem collapses to four additive-resonance shapes. There is no hidden fifth local pattern for `p>7`.

## B14 — quotient the group action and solve Z/AP completely

The scalar action of `F_p^*` is free on every nonzero row, so divide by `p-1`. The number of unordered distinct zero-sum triples is

\[
\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}6,
\]

because there are `p^2` ordered zero-sum triples and exactly `3p-2` ordered triples with a repeated coordinate. Of these, `(p-1)/2` are AP supports. Hence the number of generic Z supports is

\[
\frac{(p-1)(p-5)}6.
\]

For one generic Z support, edge balance forces the three colour classes to have size `N=n/3`. For a fixed row, every vertex has one neighbour of each colour, and the exact configuration-model probability is

\[
\Pr(Ay=0)=
\frac{6^n((N-1)!!)^3(N!)^3}{(3n-1)!!}.
\]

Multiplying by `n!/(N!)^3` rows for that support gives

\[
R_n=\frac{n!6^n((N-1)!!)^3}{(3n-1)!!}
\sim2\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac23\right)^n.
\]

These pairings are automatically simple: a vertex has at most one stub aimed at a fixed colour, ruling out loops and parallel edges. Thus, after division by the scalar orbit and conditioning on simplicity, all generic Z rows contribute only

\[
O\!\left(p\sqrt n(2/3)^n\right)=e^{-\Omega(n)}
\]

for polynomially bounded `p`.

For AP, the preceding noncircular theorem applies unchanged:

\[
\mathbb E_{\rm simple}\frac{S_p^{AP}(A)}{p-1}
=\frac{8}{9n}+o(n^{-1}).
\]

Its leading term is exactly `u(e_i-e_j)`, i.e. a false-twin pair; all AP profiles with two or more coordinates of each nonzero sign are collectively `o(n^{-1})`.

**Outcome:** generic zero-sum support triples are exponentially negligible, while AP contributes exactly the conjectural `n^{-1}` scale with false twins as the first-order mechanism.

## B25 — exact C0 contingency count and entropy bound

Normalize C0 to `{0,1,-2}`. If the class sizes are `N_0,N_1,N_{-2}`, and `x_s` counts source vertices of colour `s` using local type `(1,1,-2)`, flow symmetry gives

\[
x_1=2x_{-2},\qquad x_0=3(N_{-2}-x_{-2}),\qquad N_1=2N_{-2}.
\]

Put `N_{-2}=N`, `N_1=2N`, `N_0=n-3N`, and `t=x_{-2}`. Then

\[
x_{-2}=t,\qquad x_1=2t,\qquad x_0=3(N-t),
\]

with

\[
\max(0,2N-n/3)\le t\le N,
\]

and `t` even from the `-2` diagonal pairing. For an exact-support-three row take `1<=N<n/3`.

The exact favourable pairing count for fixed class labels and fixed `t` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{N,t}={}&
\binom{n-3N}{3(N-t)}\binom{2N}{2t}\binom Nt3^{3N}
[6(N-t)]![3(N-t)]!(2t)!\\
&\times(3n-18N+9t-1)!!(4t-1)!!(t-1)!!.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore the normalized projective expectation is exactly

\[
M^{C0}_{n,N}
=\frac{n!}{(n-3N)!(2N)!N!}
\frac{\sum_tF_{N,t}}{(3n-1)!!}.
\]

With `alpha=N/n`, `tau=t/n`, uniform Stirling expansion has derivative

\[
\frac{\partial R_{C0}}{\partial\tau}
=\frac32\log\frac{\tau(1-6\alpha+3\tau)}{3(\alpha-\tau)^2}.
\]

It is strictly increasing in `tau`, so its unique zero `tau=3alpha^2` is a minimum; the rate maximum is at a feasible endpoint. The upper endpoint `tau=alpha` has

\[
\boxed{
R_{C0,+}(\alpha)
=-\frac12h(3\alpha)
+3\alpha\log\left(\frac{2^{4/3}}3\right)<0.
}
\]

The other endpoint is smaller by one half of a binary entropy term, both for `alpha<=1/6` and after the complementary parametrization for `alpha>=1/6`. Moreover

\[
R_{C0,+}(\alpha)\le \frac{3\alpha}{2}\log(3\alpha).
\]

For fixed `N,t`, direct expansion gives

\[
M^{C0}_{n,N,t}=\Theta(n^{-3N+3t/2}).
\]

Since `t` is even, the largest fixed-`N` order is `n^{-3}`, attained only at the first sparse possibilities `(N,t)=(1,0)` and `(2,2)`. The displayed entropy envelope handles growing `N`, and fixed positive density has a strict exponential gap. Hence

\[
\boxed{\sum_NM^{C0}_{n,N}=O(n^{-3})}
\]

in the configuration model and, after the bounded simplicity conditioning factor, in the uniform simple cubic graph.

## B25 continued — exact C4 count and a uniform exponential gap

Normalize C4 to `{1,-2,4}` with local types

\[
T_1=(1,1,-2),\qquad T_2=(-2,-2,4).
\]

Let `L=n/3`, `m=N_4`, and `t` be the number of colour-4 source vertices of type `T_1`. Edge balance uniquely forces

\[
N_1=2(L-m),\quad N_{-2}=L+m,\quad N_4=m,
\]

and

\[
x_1=2(L-m-t),\quad x_{-2}=L-m+t,\quad x_4=t.
\]

The exact pairing count is

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_{m,t}={}&
\binom{2(L-m)}{2t}\binom{L+m}{2m-t}\binom mt3^n
[2(L-m+t)]!(2t)!(2m-t)!\\
&\times[4(L-m-t)-1]!![L+3m-t-1]!![m-t-1]!!,
\end{aligned}
\]

with `0<=t<=min(m,L-m)` and `m-t` even. Thus

\[
M^{C4}_{n,m}
=\frac{n!}{[2(L-m)]!(L+m)!m!}
\frac{\sum_tG_{m,t}}{(3n-1)!!}.
\]

Writing `beta=m/n`, `tau=t/n`, the rate derivative simplifies to

\[
\frac{\partial R_{C4}}{\partial\tau}
=\frac12\log
\frac{(\beta-\tau)(1/3-\beta+\tau)^2}
{\tau^2(1/3+3\beta-\tau)}.
\]

The stationarity polynomial factors exactly as

\[
-\frac19(1-3\beta+9\tau)(3\beta^2-\beta+\tau),
\]

so the unique feasible maximizer is `tau=beta(1-3beta)`. Substitution yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{C4}^*(\beta)={}&
\frac13(1-3\beta)\log(1-3\beta)
+\frac23(1+3\beta)\log(1+3\beta)\\
&-\beta\log\beta-(1+\beta)\log3
+(4/3-4\beta)\log2.
\end{aligned}
\]

With `x=3beta`, its derivative has the sign of

\[
\log\frac{(1+x)^2}{16x(1-x)}.
\]

The only critical points are `(7+-4sqrt(2))/17`. The smaller one is the only interior local maximum. Elementary rigorous logarithm bounds on `0.078<x_-<0.080` give `R_{C4}^*(x_-)<-0.10`, while both endpoint limits are `log(2^{4/3}/3)<0`. Therefore the whole C4 family has a uniform exponential gap:

\[
\boxed{\sum_mM^{C4}_{n,m}=e^{-\Omega(n)}}.
