## 4. B1 — exact rowwise Cramér tilting

For a symmetric type `M=(m_ij)`, let

\[
n_i=d^{-1}\sum_jm_{ij},\qquad q_{ij}=m_{ij}/(dn_i),\qquad t_{ij}=q_{ij}-1/p.
\]

In the cumulative notation `u_ij=pA_ij-r_i`, one has exactly

\[
t_{ij}=\frac{u_{ij}}{1+pr_i}.
\]

On the broad equidistributed ball the previous isotypic diagonalization gives

\[
\sum_i\|u_i\|^2\le C p^2\log n/n,
\qquad N_i:=n_i=(1+o(1))n/p.
\]

Let `X` be one uniform `U_{d,p}` step. For each row there is a unique small exponential tilt

\[
P_{\eta_i}(X=x)=e^{\langle\eta_i,x\rangle}P_0(X=x)/M(\eta_i)
\]

with `E_{eta_i}X=dq_i`. Since the covariance on `1^perp` has eigenvalues `Theta_d(1/p)`,

\[
\|\eta_i\|_2\le C_dp\|t_i\|_2
\le C_dp^2\sqrt{\log n/n}=o(1)
\]

through the quarter-power scale with logarithmic slack.

The exact Radon--Nikodym identity is

\[
P_0\left(\sum_{a=1}^{N_i}X_a=m_i\right)
=e^{-N_iI_i}
P_{\eta_i}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{N_i}(X_a-dq_i)=0\right).
\]

Thus the row target rate `e^{-N_i I_i}` is removed **before** dividing the Fourier integral into structured and outer pieces.

The second new lemma is that Nguyen--Pan's inverse gap survives this tilt. Rather than use additive total variation, use

\[
1-|E_\rho e^{i\langle s,X\rangle}|^2
=E_{\rho\otimes\rho}[1-\cos\langle s,X-Y\rangle].
\]

The tilted and uniform step weights are `e^{o(1)}`-comparable, so this nonnegative phase energy is multiplicatively comparable. Therefore outside the same structured neighborhoods

\[
|\phi_{\eta_i}(s)|\le1-c_d/p^2.
\]

For `N_i\asymp n/p`, the outer centered integral is `<=exp(-c n/p^3)`. The central tilted local mass is at least `exp(-C p log n)` under `p^3 log^C N_i=o(N_i)`, by the same Gaussian determinant and moment expansion as the Nguyen--Pan one-row local calculation, uniformly under the `o(1)` tilt. Hence the **relative** row outer error is

\[
\epsilon_{row}
\le\exp[-c n/p^3+C p\log n].
\]

The crucial gain is that this bound no longer contains the row's target energy.

## 5. B15 — the exceptional-row compatibility calculation

For each fixed symmetric type, write a row probability as `P_i=C_i+R_i`, where `C_i` is the rate-factored structured contribution and `R_i` the outer contribution. The tilted lemma gives

\[
|R_i|\le\epsilon_{row}C_i
\]

uniformly over all rows, including a row carrying almost all of the type displacement. Therefore

\[
\left|\prod_iP_i-\prod_iC_i\right|
\le[(1+\epsilon_{row})^p-1]\prod_iC_i
\le2p\epsilon_{row}\prod_iC_i.
\]

Under `p^4 log n=o(n)`,

\[
p\epsilon_{row}
\le\exp[-c n/p^3+C p\log n+\log p]=o(1).
\]

This is a pointwise multiplicative statement **before summing over `M`**. The `exp(O(p^2 log n))` symmetric-type count is therefore never paid against the one-row inverse exponent. This is the exact place where the old `1/5` barrier disappears.

## 6. B30 — localized shifted-theta estimate

The preceding B24 theorem proved that every nontrivial weighted-row mod-`p` character has shifted dual distance squared at least `c_d/p`; pure mod-`d` characters are farther. The finite character group has size

\[
d^{p-1}p^p=\exp(O_d(p\log p)).

Poisson summation gives a shifted dual Gaussian theta sum. Localize at a nearest shifted dual point. That one point costs

\[
e^{-c n/p^3}.

Every **other** shifted dual point is a fixed positive distance away by the dimension-free dual spacing, so the entire remaining shell-packed tail costs

\[
e^{-c n/p^2+C p^2\log n}.

Thus for every nontrivial character

After summing the `exp(O(p log p))` characters, both errors are `o(1)` under `p^4 log n=o(n)`.
