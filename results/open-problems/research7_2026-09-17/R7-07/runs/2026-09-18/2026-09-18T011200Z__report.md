## 5. New theorem: arbitrary `A`-degree dispersion is harmless when the witness side is one-scale

### Theorem 5.1 — one-sided bounded-scale witness theorem

Fix constants

```text
c>0, r>0, s_0>0, Lambda>=1.
```

For all sufficiently large `d`, suppose `J=(A,B)` is a finite simple bipartite graph satisfying

```text
deg_J(a) >= r d^2                 for every a in A,       (5.1)
S <= deg_J(b) <= Lambda S          for every b in B,       (5.2)
S >= s_0 d.                                                (5.3)
```

There is **no upper bound** on the `A` degrees, and `S/d` need not be bounded above. Let

```text
p=c/d.
```

Then there exists `gamma=gamma(c,r,s_0,Lambda)>0` such that, along every sequence of such hosts with `|A|+|B|->infinity`, the percolated graph `J_p` contains a simple cycle of length at least `gamma d` with probability tending to one.

This is a theorem-sized claim; the proof follows in full.

## 6. B8 — discretize the witness side rather than the infinitely many `A` scales

Set

```text
h=ceil(S/d).
```

Independently color every vertex of `A` uniformly with one of `h` colors, writing `A_1,...,A_h` for the color classes. For a fixed incidence `ab`, conditional on `a in A_j`,

```text
t_{b,j}:=|N_J(b) cap A_j|
        =1+Bin(deg_J(b)-1,1/h).                           (6.1)
```

From `S<=deg(b)<=Lambda S`, `S>=s_0d`, and `h=ceil(S/d)`, there are constants

```text
0<alpha<beta<infinity,
zeta>0
```

depending only on `s_0,Lambda` for which, uniformly over all incidences,

```text
P(alpha d <= t_{b,color(a)} <= beta d) >= 1-2e^{-zeta d}.  (6.2)
```

This is the standard multiplicative Chernoff inequality applied to (6.1): its mean is bounded above and below by fixed positive multiples of `d`.

For a fixed `a`, let `Y_a` be the number of incident `B` vertices whose block degree fails (6.2). Then

```text
E_color Y_a <= 2e^{-zeta d} deg_J(a),
P_color(Y_a>deg_J(a)/2) <= 4e^{-zeta d}.                 (6.3)
```

Hence some deterministic coloring has at least `3|A|/4` vertices `a` for which at least half of their incident witnesses have block degree in `[alpha d,beta d]`. Fix this coloring. Call such vertices **good**.

For each color block separately, expose only the percolation edges from `B` to that block. If a witness `b` has block degree `t in [alpha d,beta d]` and at least two open neighbors in the block, choose one unordered open pair uniformly. Put every selected pair into a simple projected graph `Gamma_j` on `A_j`, and let

```text
Gamma = disjoint union_j Gamma_j.                        (6.4)
```

A witness may select one pair in several different color blocks, but any cycle of `Gamma` lies in one single block. Inside that block the witness selected at most one pair, so distinct projected cycle edges have distinct `B` witnesses and the projected cycle lifts to a simple bipartite cycle in `J_p`.

For `t in [alpha d,beta d]`, let

```text
theta_t=P(Bin(t,c/d)>=2).
```

The exactly-two term gives a uniform positive lower bound `theta_t>=theta_*>0` for all large `d`. By symmetry among the `binom(t,2)` host pairs, every fixed pair in that witness block is selected with probability

```text
theta_t/binom(t,2) >= q_0/d^2                           (6.5)
```

for a constant `q_0>0` depending only on `alpha,beta,c`.

**B8 outcome:** the number of `A`-degree scales has vanished completely. Only the one-scale witness side is discretized, and every relevant local witness neighborhood has `Theta(d)` vertices.

## 7. B20 — exact worst-case collision bound for the representative process

Fix a good `a`, lying in color block `A_j`. Let `R_a` be the number of localized witnesses incident to `a`. By goodness and (5.1),

```text
R_a >= deg_J(a)/2 >= (r/2)d^2.                          (7.1)
```

For each `u in A_j\{a}`, let

```text
c_u = #{ localized b : a,u in N_J(b) cap A_j }.         (7.2)
```

Then

```text
0<=c_u<=R_a,                                             (7.3)
```

and, because every localized witness containing `a` has at least `alpha d` vertices in the block,

```text
sum_u c_u
 = sum_{localized b containing a}(t_{b,j}-1)
 >= R_a(alpha d-1).                                      (7.4)
```

Let `I_u` indicate that at least one such witness selected `{a,u}`. Distinct witnesses act independently, and by (6.5)

```text
E I_u >= 1-(1-q)^{c_u},
q=q_0/d^2.                                               (7.5)
```

Put

```text
f(x)=1-(1-q)^x.
```

`f` is increasing and concave with `f(0)=0`; consequently its graph lies above the chord from `0` to `R_a`:

```text
f(x) >= (x/R_a)f(R_a),  0<=x<=R_a.                      (7.6)
```

Summing (7.5) and using (7.4)-(7.6),

```text
E deg_Gamma(a)
 >= sum_u f(c_u)
 >= (f(R_a)/R_a)sum_u c_u
 >= (alpha d-1)f(R_a).                                  (7.7)
```

Furthermore, (7.1) implies

```text
f(R_a)
 >= 1-(1-q_0/d^2)^{(r/2)d^2}
 >= 1-exp(-q_0r/2).                                     (7.8)
```

Thus some fixed `kappa=kappa(c,r,s_0,Lambda)>0` satisfies

```text
E deg_Gamma(a) >= kappa d                               (7.9)
```

for every good `a`.

This calculation is deliberately worst-case in the codegrees. Even if all common-neighbor mass concentrates as strongly as a simple graph permits, concavity forces `Omega(d)` distinct projected partners. The putative `1/k` loss from `k` `A`-degree scales is therefore an artefact of choosing one degree cell too early.

### Pairwise negative correlation

For each projected host pair `e`, let `I_e` be the indicator that at least one eligible block-witness selected `e`. For distinct pairs `e!=f`, each elementary block-witness `(b,j)` either sees at most one of them or has mutually exclusive outcomes `e` and `f`. Different block-witness variables are independent because they use disjoint percolation edges and independent auxiliary pair choices. Thus

```text
P(I_e=0,I_f=0)
 = product_{b,j}(1-x_{b,e}-x_{b,f})
 <= product_{b,j}(1-x_{b,e})(1-x_{b,f})
 = P(I_e=0)P(I_f=0),                                    (7.10)
```

so

```text
Cov(I_e,I_f)<=0.                                        (7.11)
```

If `X=e(Gamma)=sum_e I_e`, then

```text
Var X <= E X.                                            (7.12)
```

At least `3|A|/4` vertices are good, so summing (7.9),

```text
E X >= (3kappa/8)|A|d.                                  (7.13)
```

Chebyshev gives

```text
P(X < EX/2)
 <= 4/EX
 <= 32/(3kappa |A|d).                                   (7.14)
```

On the complementary event the average degree of `Gamma` is at least `(3kappa/8)d`. Because `Gamma` is the disjoint union of its color graphs, some `Gamma_j` has at least that average degree. Standard iterative deletion yields a nonempty subgraph of that block with minimum degree at least `(3kappa/16)d`. Taking a longest path and the neighbor of its first vertex furthest along the path produces a projected cycle of length at least

```text
(3kappa/16)d+1.                                         (7.15)
```

It lifts to a simple bipartite cycle of at least twice that length in `J_p`.

Whenever `d->infinity` or `|A|->infinity`, (7.14) tends to zero.

### Fixed-`d`, bounded-`A` tail

It remains to respect the source's host-order a.a.s. quantifier. If `d` and `|A|` remain bounded while `|B|->infinity`, there are only finitely many possible exact neighborhoods `N_J(b) subseteq A`. Some neighborhood type `T` of size at least `S>=s_0d` therefore occurs for an unbounded number of twin witnesses. For each fixed pair `e subseteq T`, every twin selects `e` with one fixed positive probability after percolation and representative choice. With `k` twins,

```text
P(no twin selects e)=(1-q_T)^k ->0.                     (7.16)
```

A finite union bound over the pairs of `T` gives the projected clique `K_T` with probability tending to one. For the chosen sufficiently large `d_0`, `|T|>=3`; this clique has a cycle of length at least `s_0d`, which lifts to a simple bipartite cycle of length at least `2s_0d`.

Equations (7.14)-(7.16) complete Theorem 5.1 along every host-order-divergent sequence.

**B20 outcome:** the natural representative process does not stall in the one-sided class. Its exact collision functional is concave in the pair codegrees, and the extremal concentration regime is still source-solvable.

#### Proof

```text
E/m >= r_0d^2,
```
