## New mathematics

The preceding run showed that a product of modular nullity factors can certify real singularity. This run identifies exactly what a multi-prime proof must control and removes a previously hidden counting ambiguity.

### B12 — exact conservation and affine-span theorem

Let `A` be the adjacency matrix of a finite simple `d`-regular graph, let `V=Z/QZ`, and assume `gcd(d,Q)=1`. If a row `y in V^n` is surjective and satisfies `yA=0`, then

\[
\boxed{\sum_i y_i=0}
\]

and the smallest coset of a subgroup containing the coordinates of `y` is all of `V`.

The proof is deterministic. Regularity gives `A1=d1`, so `0=yA1=d sum_i y_i`; invertibility of `d` modulo `Q` gives zero sum. If all coordinates were in `gamma+H`, then each coordinate of `yA` would lie in `d gamma+H`. Since `yA=0`, one gets `d gamma in H`, hence `gamma in H`; surjectivity then forces `H=V`.

A second exact lemma gives the correct real-to-modular bridge. If an integer matrix `A` is singular over the rational field, choose a primitive integral left-null vector `x`. Bézout shows `x mod Q` is surjective for every integer `Q>=2`; multiplying by the `phi(Q)` units produces `phi(Q)` distinct surjective modular null vectors. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf1_{\{\det A=0\}}
\le
\frac{|\operatorname{Sur}(\operatorname{cok}A,Z/QZ)|}{\varphi(Q)}.
}
\]

For squarefree `Q=prod p`, Chinese remaindering gives the exact factorization

\[
|\operatorname{Sur}(\operatorname{cok}A,Z/QZ)|
=
\varphi(Q)\prod_{p|Q}L_p(A),
\qquad
L_p(A)=\frac{|\ker(A\bmod p)|-1}{p-1}.
\]

Thus the previous four-prime mixed moment is exactly one normalized cyclic cokernel-surjection moment, not merely an analogy.

### B25 — exact double count of all source-compatible cyclic test rows

For `y in (Z/QZ)^n`, define `MinCos(y)` to be its smallest containing subgroup coset and let

\[
N_{\rm aff}(Q,n)
=
#\{y:\sum_i y_i=0,\ MinCos(y)=Z/QZ\}.
\]

The new exact formula is

\[
\boxed{
N_{\rm aff}(Q,n)
=
\sum_{g|Q}\mu(g)\gcd(n,g)(Q/g)^{n-1}.
}
\]

For squarefree `Q`,

\[
\boxed{
N_{\rm aff}(Q,n)
=Q^{n-1}
\prod_{p|Q}\left(1-\frac{\gcd(n,p)}{p^{n-1}}\right).
}
\]

Proof: put `D(y)=gcd(Q,y_2-y_1,...,y_n-y_1)`. Full affine span is `D(y)=1`. If `g|D(y)`, all coordinates are congruent to one residue `a mod g`; the zero-sum equation is solvable exactly for `gcd(n,g)` choices of `a`, and then leaves `(Q/g)^{n-1}` choices. Möbius inversion gives the formula. Hence, for squarefree polynomial-size moduli whose prime factors tend to infinity, `N_aff(Q,n)=Q^{n-1}(1-o(1))`. Proper-coset aliases therefore do **not** create the growing-modulus entropy loss.

This is stronger and more explicit than the fixed-group candidate count needed in the Nguyen–Wood moment calculation: it is exact for every `Q`, including growing `Q`.

### Pointwise mixing reduction

Define

\[
\rho_Q(n)=
\sup_{y:\sum y_i=0,\ MinCos(y)=V}
Q^{n-1}\Pr(A_{n,d}y=0).
\]

The two theorems above immediately imply

\[
\mathbb E|\operatorname{Sur}(\operatorname{cok}A,Z/QZ)|
\le \rho_Q(n)
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(\det A=0)\le\frac{\rho_Q(n)}{\varphi(Q)}.
}
\]

So a full growing-cokernel distribution is unnecessary. It is enough to prove one pointwise modular mixing bound `rho_Q(n)=n^{o(1)}` at one useful modulus.

### B5 — introduce the modulus exponent and find the actual proof-architecture barrier

Write `Q=n^{theta+o(1)}` with `phi(Q)=Q n^{o(1)}`. Then

\[
\rho_Q(n)=n^{o(1)}
\Longrightarrow
\Pr(A_{n,3}\text{ singular})\le n^{-\theta+o(1)}
\Longrightarrow
a_3\ge\theta.
\]

Thus `theta` is a direct continuation parameter for the frozen metric. The cumulative one-prime proof reaches `theta=1/4`.

I then checked the closest finite-group mixing proof instead of assuming it stays uniform as `Q` grows. Mészáros explicitly fixes the finite group `V` and states that his constants may depend on `V`; his Section 6 uniformity is in the graph degree `d`, not in group size. More sharply, his undirected Lemma 7.7 requires

\[
m_q(c)>10|V|^2
\]

for every residue. For a typical vector `m_q(c)~n/Q`, that displayed step requires

\[
\boxed{Q^3\ll n}.
\]

A second displayed part of the fixed-group proof has non-typical suppression `exp(-c n^{2 eta-1})` against roughly `n^{Q+1}` permutation classes, with `1/2<eta<alpha<2/3`; a direct uniformization again cannot pass `Q=n^{1/3+o(1)}`. These observations are **necessary restrictions on that proof architecture**, not a claim that its other `V`-dependent constants have been made uniform up to `n^{1/3}`.

The previous four-quarter-prime `Q=n^{1-o(1)}` plan is therefore algebraically correct but cannot be obtained merely by making the existing Mészáros proof quantitative. The revised first milestone is a direct simple-cubic-graph bound

\[
\rho_Q(n)=n^{o(1)}
\quad\text{for one squarefree }Q=n^{1/3-o(1)},
\]

which would move the frozen exponent from `1/4` to `1/3-o(1)`.

## Failures and exact gap

B25 eliminated candidate-count entropy completely, leaving the probability/mixing term untouched.
