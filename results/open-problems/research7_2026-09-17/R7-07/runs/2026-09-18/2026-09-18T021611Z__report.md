## 5. B9 — random maximum matchings normalize all witness scales at once

### 5.1 Idea

Do not thin witnesses into degree classes. After percolation, let each physical witness use all of its open neighborhood but only through a matching. This has two desired effects simultaneously:

1. every witness of any degree scale contributes pair capacity without a dyadic pigeonhole;
2. within one witness, the generated projected pairs are disjoint, so the witness labels form a proper edge coloring and witness reuse becomes visible rather than hidden.

### 5.2 Construction

Let `H=J_p`. Conditional on `H`, independently for each `b in B`, let

```text
K_b = deg_H(b).
```

If `K_b` is even, choose a uniformly random perfect matching on `N_H(b)`. If `K_b` is odd, choose one unmatched vertex uniformly and a uniformly random perfect matching of the remaining vertices. Put every matched `A`-pair into a projected graph `Gamma` on `A`. If several witnesses generate the same simple edge, retain one edge and color it by any one of its generating witnesses.

For a fixed witness `b`, its generated pairs form a matching. Hence no two incident projected edges can receive the same witness color. The resulting witness coloring is therefore **proper**.

A rainbow projected path/cycle lifts to a simple alternating path/cycle in `H`: replace a colored edge `a-u` of witness color `b` by `a-b-u`. The projected vertices are distinct and rainbow colors make all intermediate `B` witnesses distinct.

### 5.3 Scale-free one-witness lower bound

Fix `a in A` and write

```text
R=deg_J(a)=z d^2,  z>=r.
```

For `b in N_J(a)`, let `U_{a,b}` be the matching partner of `a` if edge `ab` is open and `a` is matched by witness `b`, and let it be `bot` otherwise. Different `b` give independent outputs.

Put `t_b=deg_J(b)` and `m_b=P(U_{a,b}!=bot)`. Conditional on `ab` being open and at least one other open neighbor being present, a uniform maximum matching matches `a` with probability at least `1/2`. Since `t_b>=sd`, for all large `d`,

```text
m_b
 >= p P(Bin(t_b-1,p)>=1)/2
 >= [c/(2d)] [1-exp(-cs/2)]
 = alpha/d,
```

where

```text
alpha := (c/2)(1-exp(-cs/2)) > 0.                        (5.1)
```

The key point is that (5.1) is independent of `t_b`: very large and merely `Theta(d)` witnesses live in the same estimate.

**B9 outcome:** the previous `max_j Q_j` witness-scale loss disappears from degree production.

## 6. B25 — count partner mass twice and absorb all pair collisions

For every `u!=a`, define

```text
q_{b,u}=P(U_{a,b}=u),
x_u=sum_{b common to a,u} q_{b,u}.
```




### 6.1 Negative correlation and a high-degree projected core

For distinct partners `u,v`, a single witness can output at most one partner for `a`, so

```text
1-q_{b,u}-q_{b,v} <= (1-q_{b,u})(1-q_{b,v}).
```

Multiplying over independent witnesses gives

```text
P(I_u=0,I_v=0) <= P(I_u=0)P(I_v=0),
```

which is equivalent for Bernoulli variables to

```text
Cov(I_u,I_v)<=0.
```

Therefore

```text
Var(deg_Gamma(a)) <= E deg_Gamma(a).                      (6.5)
```

Chebyshev with (6.3) yields

```text
P(deg_Gamma(a)<kappa d/2) <= 4/(kappa d).                 (6.6)
```

Let `Z` be the number of `A` vertices violating the `kappa d/2` degree threshold. Then

```text
E Z <= 4|A|/(kappa d),
P(Z>|A|/sqrt(d)) <= 4/(kappa sqrt(d)).                    (6.7)
```

Thus, with probability `1-O(d^{-1/2})` as `d->infinity`, `Gamma` has average degree at least `(1-o(1)) kappa d/2`; iterative deletion gives a nonempty subgraph `Gamma'` satisfying

### 6.2 Long rainbow path

Every properly edge-colored simple graph of minimum degree `delta` has a rainbow path of at least `delta/2` edges. Indeed, take a longest rainbow path of length `ell`. At one endpoint, at most `ell` incident edges lead back to path vertices. Any edge to a vertex outside the path must use one of the `ell` already-used colors, and proper coloring permits at most one incident edge of each such color. Hence `delta<=2ell`.

Applying this to `Gamma'`, with joint probability `1-O(d^{-1/2})` there is a rainbow projected path with at least


edges. It lifts to a simple alternating path in `H` of length at least

```text
kappa d/5.                                                (6.9)
```

## 7. B30 — localize the only remaining gluing failure: rainbow-cycle closure

An ordinary projected cycle may reuse the same physical witness as the color of two separated projected edges and then fail to lift to a simple bipartite cycle. Therefore the exact remaining condition is not another degree-scale condition: the projected cycle must be **rainbow**.

### Lemma 7.1 — endpoint blocking for a longest rainbow path

Let `G` be properly edge-colored with minimum degree `delta`, and let

```text
P=v_0 v_1 ... v_ell
```

be a longest rainbow path with path colors `c_1,...,c_ell`. Suppose there is no rainbow cycle of length at least `L`, where `2<=L<=ell+1`. Then

```text
ell >= delta-L+2.                                        (7.1)
```

More precisely, if `i>=L-1`, every chord `v_0v_i` has color among `c_1,...,c_i`; otherwise the prefix `v_0...v_i` together with that chord is a rainbow cycle of length `i+1>=L`. Every edge from `v_0` outside `P` must use a color already appearing on all of `P`, or it would extend the longest rainbow path. Thus every edge at `v_0` is either one of at most `L-2` edges to the first `L-2` path vertices or has one of the `ell` path colors. Properness gives at most one incident edge of each color, hence

```text
deg(v_0) <= (L-2)+ell,
```

which proves (7.1). The same certificate holds at the other endpoint.
