## 5. B6 — exact cube-zero deformation

For integers `0 <= s <= r <= n`, define

\[
\mathcal N_3(n;r,s)=
\{A\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times n}:A^3=0,\ \operatorname{rank}A=r,\ \operatorname{rank}A^2=s\}.
\]

For `A^3=0`, the kernel increments are

\[
b_1=n-r,\qquad b_2=r-s,\qquad b_3=s.
\]

They are nonincreasing, giving

\[
s\le r/2,
\qquad
r-s\le n-r,
\qquad
r\le\frac{n+s}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — rank-refined cube-zero count

For every feasible `n,r,s`,

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal N_3(n;r,s)|
\le
\binom nr^2\binom rs^2
(n+1)^{2rs-s^2}
2^{2nr-2r^2+rs}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

For `s=0`, this reduces exactly to the prior square-zero bound

\[
|\mathcal N_3(n;r,0)|
\le
\binom nr^2 2^{2r(n-r)}.
\tag{3}
\]

### Proof

Choose row and column sets `I,J`, each of size `r`, such that

\[
B=A_{I,J}
\]

is nonsingular. There are at most `binom(n,r)^2` choices. Put

\[
R=A_{I,*}\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times n},
\qquad
C=A_{*,J}\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times r}.
\]

Then `R` and `C` both have rank `r`, `C_{I,*}=B`, and the exact CUR identity gives

\[
A=C B^{-1}R.
\tag{4}
\]

There are at most `2^{rn}` possibilities for `R`; once `R,J` are fixed, `B=R_{*,J}` is fixed.

Define

\[
G=RC=(A^2)_{I,J},
\qquad
T=B^{-1}G.
\tag{5}
\]

Then

\[
A^2=C T B^{-1}R,
\tag{6}
\]

and the outer factors have full column/full row rank, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}G
=
\operatorname{rank}T
=
\operatorname{rank}A^2=s.
\tag{7}
\]

Also

\[
A^3=C T^2 B^{-1}R,
\tag{8}
\]

hence

\[
A^3=0\iff T^2=0.
\tag{9}
\]

Let `pi_I` be coordinate projection to the coordinates in `I`, and write

\[
q=\dim \pi_I(\ker R).
\]

Since `T^2=0` and `rank T=s`, `dim ker T=r-s`. If `v in ker T`, then

\[
R(Cv)=Gv=BTv=0,
\]

while

\[
\pi_I(Cv)=Bv.
\]

Because `B` is invertible, the image of `ker T` under `v -> Bv` has dimension `r-s`; therefore

\[
\boxed{q\ge r-s.}
\tag{10}
\]

Now fix `R` and `G`. For every selected basis column `c_j` of `C`, both its `I` coordinates and its `R`-image are fixed:

\[
(c_j)_I=B_{*,j},
\qquad
R c_j=G_{*,j}.
\tag{11}
\]

Differences of two real solutions lie in

\[
V_R=\{h\in\ker R:h_I=0\}.
\]

Rank-nullity and (10) give

\[
\dim V_R=(n-r)-q\le n-2r+s.
\tag{12}
\]

An affine `d`-plane meets the sign hypercube in at most `2^d` points, so each basis column has at most

\[
2^{n-2r+s}
\]

possibilities and all `r` columns together have at most

\[
2^{r(n-2r+s)}.
\tag{13}
\]

It remains to count `G`. Every entry of `G=RC` is a sum of `n` signs, hence takes one of exactly `n+1` integer values. A rank-`s` `r x r` matrix is determined by choosing one nonsingular `s x s` minor and its `s` full rows plus `s` full columns. Thus

\[
\#G
\le
\binom rs^2(n+1)^{2rs-s^2}.
\tag{14}
\]

This deliberately ignores the additional relation `(B^{-1}G)^2=0`, so it is an upper bound.

Multiplying the choices of `(I,J)`, `R`, `G`, and `C`, while (4) reconstructs `A` uniquely, gives exactly (2). QED.

**B6 outcome: SUCCESS.** The square-zero reveal survives exactly, with one new middle state `G=(A^2)_{I,J}`.

## 6. B27 — uniform low-second-rank half exponent

The binary part of (2) has exponent

\[
h(r,s)=2nr-2r^2+rs.
\]

Completing the square gives

\[
\boxed{
h(r,s)
=-2\left(r-\frac n2-\frac s4\right)^2
+\frac{n^2}{2}+\frac{ns}{2}+\frac{s^2}{8}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Hence uniformly in `r`,

\[
h(r,s)\le\frac{n^2}{2}+\frac{ns}{2}+\frac{s^2}{8}.
\tag{16}
\]

The middle-state count contributes at most

\[
(2rs-s^2)\log_2(n+1)
\le 2ns\log_2(n+1)
\tag{17}
\]

bits. The two binomial factors cost only `O(n)` bits, and summing over at most `n^2` rank pairs is negligible on the quadratic scale.

Therefore:

### Theorem 6.1 — uniform mesoscopic regime

If `s_n log n=o(n)`, then

\[
\boxed{
\#\{A\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times n}:A^3=0,\ \operatorname{rank}A^2\le s_n\}
\le2^{n^2/2+o(n^2)}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{A:A^3=0,\ \operatorname{rank}A^2\le n/(\log n)^2\}
\le2^{n^2/2+O(n^2/\log n)}.
}
\tag{19}
\]

This includes `s=0`, every fixed second rank `s=O(1)`, and every growing regime `s=o(n/log n)`.

Thus any cube-zero sign-matrix family capable of violating the candidate corrected half exponent must live in the genuinely mesoscopic/macroscopic range

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A^2)\not=o(n/\log n).
\tag{20}
\]

## 7. B20 — exact reason the reveal still stops

For `A^2=0`, the middle state is identically `G=0`; there is no middle-state entropy.

For `A^3=0`, once `G` is fixed the sign-column fibers remain sufficiently small. The exact unresolved cost is the naive integer-state enumeration

\[
\boxed{
\log_2\#G
\le
(2rs-s^2)\log_2(n+1)+O(n).
}
\tag{21}
\]

When `s log n=o(n)`, this is subquadratic and Theorem 6.1 follows. When `s=Theta(n)`, it becomes `Theta(n^2 log n)` and destroys the desired scale.

This `log n` loss is not a geometric-dimension obstruction. It comes from encoding each degree of freedom of a rank-`s` integer matrix by one scalar from an interval of length `Theta(n)`. But the relevant `G` are not arbitrary bounded integer matrices: they are attainable products

\[
G=RC
\]

of sign matrices and obey

\[
(B^{-1}G)^2=0.
\tag{22}
\]

The missing ingredient is therefore a **binary-entropy/compression theorem for attainable middle products**, not another fixed Jordan-profile dimension calculation.

**B20 outcome: SUCCESSFUL BLOCKER ISOLATION.** The algorithm fails in exactly one quantified place: the `log n` bits per middle-product degree of freedom in (21).
