# 2. B11 — exact Fourier dualization of characteristic-polynomial collisions

The previous run reduced a matching corrected exponent to a characteristic-polynomial collision problem. Fix an odd prime `p` and write

`chi_M(t)=t^n+c_1(M)t^{n-1}+...+c_n(M)`

over `F_p`. Let

`c(M)=(c_1(M),...,c_n(M)) in F_p^n`,

`nu(y)=P(c(M_n)=y)`,

and for an independent copy `M_n'`,

`C_{n,p}=P(c(M_n)=c(M_n'))=sum_y nu(y)^2`.                    (2.1)

For `x in F_p`, define `e_p(x)=exp(2 pi i x/p)`, and for `xi in F_p^n` define

`Phi_xi = E_M e_p(<xi,c(M)>)`.                                (2.2)

## Theorem 2.1 — exact Parseval identity

One has

`C_{n,p}=p^{-n} sum_{xi in F_p^n} |Phi_xi|^2`.                (2.3)

### Proof

The unnormalized finite Fourier transform of `nu` is

`hat nu(xi)=sum_y nu(y)e_p(<xi,y>)`.

Because `nu` is the pushforward of the uniform sign-cube measure through `M -> c(M)`, this equals `Phi_xi`. Parseval on the finite abelian group `F_p^n` gives

`sum_y |nu(y)|^2=p^{-n}sum_xi |hat nu(xi)|^2`,

which is (2.3). QED.

Thus the desired near-maximal collision entropy

`C_{n,p_n} <= 2^{-n^2+o(n^2)}`                               (2.4)

is exactly a statement that the total nonzero Fourier mass of the nonlinear characteristic-coefficient map is quadratically small.

## Theorem 2.2 — one-row exact product formula

Fix a row `i` and condition on every matrix entry outside that row. For every Fourier frequency `xi`, there are coefficients

`b,a_1,...,a_n in F_p`

such that for the remaining row `x=(x_1,...,x_n) in {+/-1}^n`,

`<xi,c(M)> = b + sum_j a_j x_j`.                              (2.5)

Consequently

`| E_{row i} e_p(<xi,c(M)>) |
 = product_j |cos(2 pi a_j/p)|`.                              (2.6)

### Proof

Up to signs, `c_k(M)` is the sum of all `k x k` principal minors of `M`. A principal minor not containing index `i` is constant under variation of row `i`; a principal minor containing `i` is a determinant and is linear in that row. Hence every `c_k`, and therefore every linear combination `<xi,c(M)>`, is affine-linear in the row, which proves (2.5). Independence of the Rademacher row coordinates then gives

`E_x e_p(b+sum_j a_jx_j)
 =e_p(b) product_j (e_p(a_j)+e_p(-a_j))/2
 =e_p(b) product_j cos(2pi a_j/p)`.

Taking moduli gives (2.6). QED.

### Outcome

This identifies the exact inverse-Littlewood--Offord interface. If many `a_j` avoid zero modulo `p`, one row contracts the Fourier coefficient. But quadratic collision entropy would require controlled contraction through order `n` successive dependent row exposures. After integrating one row, the remaining conditional Fourier object is not simply another fixed affine Rademacher phase. No multistage inverse theorem was proved that prevents the cofactor coefficient systems from repeatedly becoming structured. Therefore B11 produced a sharp reduction but no movement in (1.4).

# 3. B11/B21 bridge — Schur--Krylov reconstruction and the exact noncircularity loss

A second exact algebraic identity clarifies how much information the characteristic polynomial can recover from one missing column.

Write over a field `K`

`M = [[a,r^T],[c,B]]`,

where `B` is `(n-1)x(n-1)` and `r,c in K^{n-1}`.

## Theorem 3.1 — Schur--adjugate formula

As a polynomial identity in `t`,

`chi_M(t)=(t-a)chi_B(t)-r^T adj(tI-B)c`.                      (3.1)

This is the determinant expansion of `tI-M` across the first row/column block and does not require invertibility of `tI-B`.

Define

`L_{B,r}(c)=r^T adj(tI-B)c`                                  (3.2)

as a linear map from `K^{n-1}` to polynomials of degree at most `n-2`.

## Theorem 3.2 — rank is the Krylov dimension

`rank L_{B,r}
 = dim span{r,B^Tr,...,(B^T)^{n-2}r}`.                        (3.3)

In particular, if `r` is cyclic for `B^T`, then `L_{B,r}` is injective. Hence, given `B,r,a` and `chi_M`, the missing column `c` is uniquely determined.

### Proof

Put `m=n-1`. The adjugate has the Faddeev--LeVerrier triangular expansion

`adj(tI-B)=sum_{k=0}^{m-1} t^{m-1-k} Q_k(B)`,                (3.4)

where every `Q_k(X)` is monic of degree `k` plus a linear combination of lower powers. Therefore the coefficient row vectors of `r^T adj(tI-B)` are

`r^TQ_0(B),...,r^TQ_{m-1}(B)`.                              (3.5)

Because

`Q_k(B)=B^k + span{I,B,...,B^{k-1}}`,                        (3.6)

the change of basis from

`r^T,r^TB,...,r^TB^{m-1}`

to the family in (3.5) is unit triangular. The two families have the same span and the same rank. Transposing gives exactly (3.3). QED.

### Noncircularity check

This theorem is useful only for an enriched invariant. The source event provides `chi_M`; it does **not** provide `B`, `r`, or `a`. If one reveals all three, the number of revealed sign choices is already

`2^{(n-1)^2+(n-1)+1}=2^{n^2-n+1}`,                           (3.7)

which saves only linear entropy relative to `2^{n^2}`. Therefore it would be circular to claim that typical Krylov cyclicity makes the bare characteristic polynomial almost injective at the quadratic scale. It makes the last column reconstructible only after almost the whole matrix has been supplied separately.

This explicit noncircularity check is retained because a superficially similar reconstruction argument could otherwise generate an invalid `2^{-Theta(n^2)}` conclusion.

# 5. B28 — classify the unavoidable structured fibers first

Before seeking near-injectivity of the characteristic-polynomial map, there is an exact universal source of cospectrality that must be factored out.

For `epsilon=(epsilon_1,...,epsilon_n) in {+/-1}^n`, define

`D_epsilon=diag(epsilon_1,...,epsilon_n)`.

Then `M -> D_epsilon M D_epsilon` preserves sign entries and preserves the characteristic polynomial by similarity.

## Theorem 5.1 — exact diagonal-switching orbit size

For every sign matrix `M`,

`|{DMD : D diagonal with +/-1 entries}| = 2^{n-1}`.            (5.1)

### Proof

`D` and `-D` induce the same conjugation, so there are at most `2^{n-1}` images. Conversely suppose

`DMD=D'MD'`.

Let `E=D'D=diag(e_1,...,e_n)`. Then `EME=M`, so for every `i,j`,

`e_i e_j M_{ij}=M_{ij}`.

Every sign entry is nonzero, hence `e_i e_j=1` for every pair. Thus all `e_i` are equal and `E=I` or `E=-I`. Therefore `D'=D` or `D'=-D`, exactly the two representatives of the same conjugation. QED.

## Corollary 5.2 — exact collision floor

For every odd prime `p`,

`C_{n,p} >= 2^{-n^2+n-1}`.                                   (5.2)

### Proof

Partition the `N=2^{n^2}` sign matrices by their characteristic coefficient vector modulo `p`, with fiber sizes `m_f`. Every nonempty fiber is a union of the switching orbits from Theorem 5.1, so `m_f>=d=2^{n-1}`. Then

`C_{n,p}=N^{-2}sum_f m_f^2
        >=N^{-2}d sum_f m_f
        =d/N
        =2^{-n^2+n-1}`.

QED.

This universal symmetry changes the collision probability by only a subquadratic factor. Thus the target collision exponent `1` remains information-theoretically possible after quotienting or tolerating these mates.

# 6. B28 — structured-exception theorem tailored to F0012

The previous R7-07 run established the archimedean restriction that every Gaussian-integral eigenvalue of an `n x n` sign matrix lies in

`D_n={z in Z[i]: |z|<=n}`.

Consequently the number of possible Gaussian-integral spectral multisets, hence of admissible characteristic polynomials, is

`B_n <= binom(n+(2n+1)^2-1,n)=2^{O(n log n)}`.                (6.1)

Let `A_n` denote this admissible polynomial family.

## Theorem 6.1 — structured-exception/small-fiber criterion

Suppose there are sign-matrix sets `S_n` and constants `sigma,tau in [0,1]` such that

`|S_n| <= 2^{(sigma+o(1))n^2}`,                               (6.2)

and uniformly for every `f in A_n`,

`#{M notin S_n : chi_M=f}
 <= 2^{(tau+o(1))n^2}`.                                      (6.3)

Then

`u_GI >= 1-max(sigma,tau)`.                                  (6.4)

In particular, `sigma,tau<=1/2` imply `u_GI=1/2` when combined with the already-proved explicit lower family.

### Proof

Every matrix in `E_n` either belongs to `S_n` or belongs to one of the admissible fibers. Hence

`#E_n
 <= |S_n| + |A_n| max_{f in A_n} #{M notin S_n:chi_M=f}`.

Using (6.1)--(6.3),

`#E_n
 <= 2^{(sigma+o(1))n^2}
   +2^{O(n log n)}2^{(tau+o(1))n^2}
 =2^{(max(sigma,tau)+o(1))n^2}`.

Divide by `2^{n^2}` and apply definition (1.2) to obtain (6.4). QED.

### Sharp stress test

The explicit source-refuting signed-shear construction already has

`3^{floor(n/2)}2^{ceil(n^2/2)} = 2^{n^2/2+O(n)}`              (6.5)

distinct Gaussian-integral-spectrum sign matrices. Thus any structured exceptional set meant to absorb the entire known obstruction necessarily has quadratic size exponent at least `1/2`. The threshold in Theorem 6.1 is therefore naturally saturated by the construction, rather than being an artifact of the proof.

### Outcome

Theorem 6.1 is more F0012-specific than a global collision theorem: it asks for small fibers only on `2^{o(n^2)}` admissible spectra after isolating one structured set. It would give the first positive lower endpoint as soon as `max(sigma,tau)<1`. No such global classification was proved this run, so the frozen metric remains unchanged.
