## 8. (e) Nearest solved analogue and what it has that this problem lacks

The nearest same-ensemble solved analogue is Eberhard's 2022 characteristic-polynomial theorem for iid sign/integer-entry matrices. Its architecture is:

1. translate global irreducibility/Galois questions to local finite-field eigenvalue statistics;
2. prove strong finite-field singularity estimates;
3. control correlations of finitely many distinct eigenvalue events (`m < c log n` in the local theorem);
4. feed those local statistics into a prime-ideal/Galois argument.

What that proof has is a **low-complexity global obstruction**: reducibility or insufficient transitivity can be detected through bounded/logarithmic-order local statistics. What `(BLOCK)` lacks is precisely such a low-order witness. Complete splitting over `Q(i)` is an `n`-eigenvalue / complete-flag condition, and the desired probability is exponentially small on the `n^2` scale. Correlation control for only `O(log n)` local eigenvalue events cannot simply be multiplied into a quadratic-scale entropy bound. Moreover, the same Bernoulli entries determine reductions at all primes, so naive primewise independence is unavailable.

Thus the portable part of Eberhard is **not** the final Galois argument. The potentially portable part is the idea of revealing the matrix through a local/codimension stratification and proving that atypically structured subspaces pay an entropy cost. The missing upgrade is from `O(log n)`-order correlation control to a mechanism that accumulates `Theta(n^2)` bits of loss.
