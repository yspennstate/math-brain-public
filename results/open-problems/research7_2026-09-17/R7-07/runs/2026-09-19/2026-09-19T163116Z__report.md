## 5. Method B28 — two-spine martingale: exact pipes contribute zero quadratic variation

### 5.1 Same-child pair kernel

Fix one transient rooted tree `T`. At a surviving vertex `v`, write

`S_v=sum_u B_u`,

`p_v(u)=B_u/S_v`,

and

`N_v=#{u:B_u>0}`.

Define the substochastic pair kernel

`(P_2 f)(v)=sum_u p_v(u)^2 f(u)`.

It describes two independent harmonic rays while they continue to choose the same child. Killing occurs when the rays choose different children. The one-step split probability is

`Delta_v=1-sum_u p_v(u)^2`.

Let `L` be their common-prefix generation and set

`tau=L+1`.

Then

`P_T(tau>n)=C_n(T)`,

where `C_n` is the level-`n` harmonic collision mass from the preceding run, and therefore

`E_T tau=sum_n C_n=1/B_o`.

### 5.2 Exact Poisson equation

Set

`V_v=1/B_v`.

Then

`P_2 V(v)`

`=sum_u (B_u^2/S_v^2)(1/B_u)`

`=1/S_v`

`=V_v-1`,

because `V_v=1+1/S_v`.

Thus

`boxed: P_2 V=V-1`.

Equivalently, if `X_k` is the killed pair chain and `V(cemetery)=0`,

`M_n=V(X_{n wedge tau})+n wedge tau`

is the conditional-expectation martingale

`M_n=E_T[tau | F_n]`.

### 5.3 Exact branch quadratic variation

A second one-line computation gives

`P_2(V^2)(v)`

`=sum_u (B_u^2/S_v^2)(1/B_u^2)`

`=N_v/S_v^2`.

Since `(P_2V)^2=1/S_v^2`, the conditional variance of the one-step martingale increment is exactly

`boxed: a(v)=(N_v-1)/S_v^2`.

Therefore

`E_T[tau^2]<infinity`

if and only if

`E_T sum_{k<tau} (N_{X_k}-1)/S_{X_k}^2 < infinity`,

and in that case the martingale isometry yields

`boxed: E_T[tau^2] = 1/B_o^2 + E_T sum_{k<tau}(N_{X_k}-1)/S_{X_k}^2`.

The equivalence is rigorous: `M_n=E[tau|F_n]`; bounded expected quadratic variation is equivalent to `L^2` boundedness of this conditional-expectation martingale, which is equivalent to `tau in L^2`. If the quadratic-variation expectation diverges, `tau` is not square-integrable and both sides are infinite in the extended sense.

This is the main B28 structural gain. If `N_v=1`, the local quadratic-variation contribution is **exactly zero**. Exact pipes may enlarge the mean overlap, but after the natural `B_o^2` normalization they are not the source of the second-moment excess. Only genuine positive branches contribute.

### 5.4 Exact deterministic branch-sum identity

For a level-`n` vertex `v`, the preceding harmonic-cylinder identity is

`theta(v)=(B_v/B_o) product_{r<n}(1-B_{v_r})`.

The probability the pair chain reaches `v` is `theta(v)^2`. Multiplying the quadratic-variation term by `B_o^2` gives

`B_o^2 theta(v)^2 (N_v-1)/S_v^2`

`=(N_v-1) product_{r=0}^{n}(1-B_{v_r})^2`.

Hence, in `[1,infinity]`,

`boxed:`

`B_o^2 E_T[tau^2]`

`=1 + sum_{v:B_v>0}(N_v-1) product_{r=0}^{|v|}(1-B_{v_r})^2`.

Since `tau=L+1`, the preceding killed-semigroup target can also be rewritten exactly as

`(B_o^2/2)E[(L+1)(L+2)]`

`=(1/2)(B_o^2 E[tau^2]+B_o)`.

Thus the new branch sum is not an adjacent proxy; it is exactly equivalent to the missing finite-roof/two-ray gate.

## 7. Method B21 — import the many-to-two / two-spine transfer

For a vector of nonnegative measurable functions `h=(h_i)` with `h_i(0)=0`, define the finite source-absolutely-continuous measure

`Lambda_h(f)=sum_i E_i[h_i(B(T)) f(i,T)]`.

The same-child pair kernel on typed rooted trees is

`(P_2f)(i,T)=sum_u (B_u/S(T))^2 f(type(u),T_u)`.

### Theorem 7.1 — exact two-spine conductance transfer

`Lambda_h P_2 = Lambda_{L_2 h}`,

where, for `b>0`,

`boxed:`

`(L_2 h)_j(b)`

`=m_* q_j b^2 sum_i p_ij E[ h_i((b+R_i)/(1+b+R_i))/(b+R_i)^2 ]`,

and `(L_2h)_j(0)=0`.

#### Derivation

Expand `Lambda_h(P_2f)` over the `nu` exchangeable potential child slots of a type-`i` parent. Distinguish slot 1 and absorb the factor `nu` by passing to `hat nu`. Conditional on the distinguished slot having type `j`, being transmitted, and carrying a descendant tree of conductance `b`, the siblings contribute `R_i`; parent conductance is

`(b+R_i)/(1+b+R_i)`,

and the probability that the two harmonic rays both choose the distinguished child is

`b^2/(b+R_i)^2`.

Averaging and collecting the output child type `j` gives the formula.

Taking `f=1` gives the exact mass identity

`boxed:`

`sum_j E_j[(L_2h)_j(B)]`

`=sum_i E_i[h_i(B)(1-Delta(T))]`

`=||Lambda_h||-Lambda_h(Delta)`.

Thus `L_2` is positive and substochastic in the natural source-mass norm, with the exact loss equal to the two-ray split probability.

### 7.2 The compactification boundary is not a new operator

The size-biased sibling law satisfies

`P(R_i=0)=G'(1-t_i)/m_*`.

Therefore the `R_i=0` portion of Theorem 7.1 is

`boxed:`

`(L_{2,0}h)_j(b)=sum_i J_ij h_i(b/(1+b))`.

So the compactification boundary of the two-spine problem is exactly `J^T` together with the translation

`1/b -> 1/b + 1`.

R14 already proved `rho(J)<1`. Hence the exact-pipe boundary has a genuine spectral gap; the only remaining issue is whether nonzero sibling conductances tending to zero can form an approximate boundary eigenstate.

## 8. Weighted compactification calculation

Let `ell` be a positive left Perron vector of `J`,

`sum_i J_ij ell_i = rho(J) ell_j`.

Fix

`0<eta<-log rho(J)`

and consider the boundary weight

`W_i(b)=ell_i b exp(eta/b)`.

For the exact pipe map

`phi(b)=b/(1+b)`,

one has

`1/phi(b)=1/b+1`,

and therefore

`W_i(phi(b))/W_i(b)=exp(eta)/(1+b)`.

Consequently the exact-pipe part has formal weighted-sup contraction factor at most

`rho(J) exp(eta)<1`.

For a positive sibling conductance `R`, put

`g_R(b)=(b+R)/(1+b+R)`.

Then

`1/g_R(b)-1/b`

`=1-R/[b(b+R)]`.

After the pair factor `b^2/(b+R)^2` is included, the weighted ratio contains

`exp(eta) exp(-eta R/[b(b+R)])`.

Thus any fixed positive `R` is crushed as `b downarrow0`; only `R` going to zero together with `b` can imitate the exact-pipe boundary. But Section 6 gives exactly an `exp(-c/R)` source-Palm bound for that small-positive-`R` channel.

This reduces the unresolved proof to a concrete three-piece operator estimate:

1. `R=0`: strict contraction from `rho(J)exp(eta)<1`;
2. `0<R<=r`: exponentially small source-Palm mass `O(exp(-c/r))`;
3. `R>r`: strong exponential damping `exp(-eta R/[b(b+R)])` near the compactification boundary.

A rigorous Lasota--Yorke/Doeblin--Fortet inequality on a weighted conductance-density space containing the initial density `h_i(b)=b^2` would therefore imply

`r(L_2)<1`,

`sum_n n E_i[B^2 P_2^n1]<infinity`.

That would close the two-ray second moment, the failed-excursion moment, and the finite regeneration-roof gate from preceding R7-07 runs.

## 9. B28 validation — there is no honest finite unit eigenmeasure in the interior

Suppose a nonzero finite source-absolutely-continuous conductance-density measure `Lambda_h` satisfied

`Lambda_hP_2=Lambda_h`.

The exact mass identity would force

`Lambda_h(Delta)=0`.

Thus `Delta=0` almost surely under `Lambda_h`, meaning every visited environment has exactly one positive-conductance child. Stationarity would force an infinite sole-positive-child pipe with full `Lambda_h` probability.

But the source probability of an infinite sole-positive-child pipe is zero: finite pipe probabilities are governed by powers of the extinction Jacobian `J`, and `rho(J)<1`. Absolute continuity then gives the same null event under `Lambda_h`, a contradiction.

Therefore no nonzero finite source-absolutely-continuous unit eigenmeasure exists. Any failure of a spectral gap must be caused by **approximate eigenmass escaping toward `B=0` through increasingly near-pipe configurations**, exactly the channel controlled in Sections 6-8.

This does not by itself prove `r(L_2)<1`: positive operators can have spectral radius one without an interior eigenvector. It does rule out the wrong obstruction and justifies targeting boundary tightness/quasi-compactness rather than searching for another stationary finite density.
