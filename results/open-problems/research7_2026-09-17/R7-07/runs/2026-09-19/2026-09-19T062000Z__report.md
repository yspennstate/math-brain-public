## 5. B11 — dualize the middle product into a rank core

For `0 <= s <= r <= n`, define

\[
\mathcal R_n(r,s)
=
\{A\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times n}:
\operatorname{rank}A=r,
\ \operatorname{rank}A^2=s\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By Sylvester's rank inequality, any realized pair satisfies

\[
s\ge 2r-n,
\qquad
 t:=n-2r+s\ge0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — binary rank-core bound

For every `n,r,s`,

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal R_n(r,s)|
\le
\binom nr^2\binom rs^2
2^{2nr-2r^2+2rs-s^2}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

No nilpotency assumption is used.

### Proof

Fix `A in mathcal R_n(r,s)`. Choose row and column index sets `I,J subset[n]`, each of size `r`, such that

\[
B=A_{I,J}
\]

is nonsingular. There are at most `binom(n,r)^2` choices. Put

\[
R=A_{I,*}\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times n},
\qquad
C=A_{*,J}\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times r}.
\]

Then `R` has full row rank, `C` has full column rank, `C_{I,*}=B`, and the exact CUR identity is

\[
A=C B^{-1}R.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Define

\[
G=RC.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Since

\[
A^2=C B^{-1}G B^{-1}R
\]

and the outer factors have full column/full row rank,

\[
\operatorname{rank}G=\operatorname{rank}A^2=s.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Choose `P,Q subset[r]`, each of size `s`, such that the rank core

\[
H=G_{P,Q}=R_P C_Q
\tag{5.7}
\]

is nonsingular. This adds at most `binom(r,s)^2` choices.

Expose all of `R`, at cost at most

\[
2^{rn}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Then `B=R_{*,J}` is fixed. Expose the `s` columns `C_Q`. Their coordinates on `I` are fixed by `C_{I,*}=B`, so they cost at most

\[
2^{s(n-r)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Now define

\[
\Pi=I_n-C_QH^{-1}R_P.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Because `H=R_PC_Q`, direct multiplication gives

\[
R_P\Pi=0,
\qquad
\Pi C_Q=0,
\qquad
\Pi^2=\Pi.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Let `bar P=[r]\setminus P` and `bar Q=[r]\setminus Q`. Since `rank G=s` and `H` is an invertible rank core, the Schur complement vanishes:

\[
G_{\bar P,\bar Q}
=G_{\bar P,Q}H^{-1}G_{P,\bar Q}.
\]

Substituting `G=RC` yields the exact constraint

\[
\boxed{R_{\bar P}\Pi C_{\bar Q}=0.}
\tag{5.12}
\]

The matrix `R_{\bar P}\Pi` has rank exactly `r-s`. Indeed, for any row vector `x`,

\[
x\Pi=0
\Longleftrightarrow
x=xC_QH^{-1}R_P,
\]

so the kernel of `x -> xPi` is exactly `row(R_P)`. The `r` rows of `R` are independent, hence the `r-s` rows indexed by `bar P` are independent modulo `row(R_P)`; their projected images are therefore independent.

Consider one remaining column `c_j`, `j in bar Q`. Its `I`-coordinates are fixed:

\[
(c_j)_I=B_{*,j},
\tag{5.13}
\]

and it obeys

\[
R_{\bar P}\Pi c_j=0.
\tag{5.14}
\]

The `r-s` equations in (5.14) remain independent after the coordinates in `I` are fixed. Suppose otherwise. Then there is a nonzero row vector

\[
y\in\operatorname{row}(R_{\bar P}\Pi)
\]

supported entirely on `I`. From `Pi C_Q=0`,

\[
yC_Q=0.
\]

Using the actual remaining columns of the present matrix `A` in (5.12),

\[
yC_{\bar Q}=0.
\]

Thus `yC=0`. But `y` is supported on `I` and `C_{I,*}=B`, so

\[
0=yC=y_I B.
\]

Since `B` is nonsingular, `y_I=0`, contradiction.

There are `n-r` free coordinates after (5.13) and `r-s` independent equations from (5.14). Hence the affine solution space has dimension exactly

\[
(n-r)-(r-s)=n-2r+s=t.
\tag{5.15}
\]

An affine `d`-plane intersects `{+1,-1}^N` in at most `2^d` points, so each remaining column has at most `2^t` choices and the `r-s` remaining columns together contribute at most

\[
2^{(r-s)t}.
\tag{5.16}
\]

Multiplying (5.8), (5.9), (5.16) and the index choices gives

\[
|\mathcal R_n(r,s)|
\le
\binom nr^2\binom rs^2
2^{rn+s(n-r)+(r-s)(n-2r+s)}.
\]

The exponent simplifies to

\[
r n+s(n-r)+(r-s)(n-2r+s)
=2nr-2r^2+2rs-s^2,
\]

which proves (5.3). QED.

**B11 outcome: SUCCESS.** The entire `(n+1)^{2rs-s^2}` middle-state factor from the preceding run disappears. The improvement is a genuine change in the proof mechanism, not a sharper estimate of the same integer-state enumeration.

## 7. B24 — exact extremizers for affine cube fibers

The last step of Theorem 5.1 used only the universal fact that a `d`-dimensional affine subspace contains at most `2^d` sign points. To determine whether that step hides a quadratic improvement, classify its extremizers.

### Lemma 7.1 — sharp `3/4` gap for one affine form

Let

\[
\phi(x)=b+\sum_{k=1}^d a_kx_k,
\qquad
x\in\{\pm1\}^d.
\]

Either

\[
\phi\equiv 1,
\quad
\phi\equiv-1,
\quad
\phi=x_j,
\quad\text{or}\quad
\phi=-x_j
\tag{7.1}
\]

for some `j`, in which case `|phi(x)|=1` on the entire cube, or

\[
\boxed{
\#\{x\in\{\pm1\}^d:|\phi(x)|=1\}
\le 3\,2^{d-2}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

If two coefficients `a_i,a_j` are nonzero, condition on all remaining coordinates. On the resulting two-dimensional square the four values are

\[
c\pm a_i\pm a_j.
\]

All four cannot lie in `{+1,-1}`. If they did, `phi^2` would be the constant `1` on this square, but its `x_i x_j` Fourier coefficient equals `2a_i a_j`, which is nonzero. Hence at most three of the four points survive. Averaging over all conditioned coordinates gives (7.2).

If exactly one coefficient `a_j` is nonzero, the two possible values are `b+a_j` and `b-a_j`. If both have absolute value one, solving the four sign choices forces `b=0` and `a_j=+/-1`; otherwise at most half of the cube survives. If all coefficients vanish, either `b=+/-1` or no point survives. QED.

### Theorem 7.2 — affine-plane cube extremizers

Let `L subset R^N` be an affine subspace of affine dimension `d`. Exactly one of the following holds:

1. `|L cap {+1,-1}^N|=2^d`, and after choosing `d` pivot coordinates every remaining coordinate on `L` is a constant `+/-1` or `+/-` one pivot coordinate;
2. 
   \[
   \boxed{|L cap {+1,-1}^N|\le3\,2^{d-2}.}
   \tag{7.3}
   \]

### Proof

Choose `d` coordinate functionals whose linear parts form a basis of the dual of the direction space of `L`. Projection to those coordinates is an affine isomorphism `L -> R^d`. A sign point of `L` therefore projects injectively into `{+1,-1}^d`. Every nonpivot coordinate is an affine form in the pivot variables.

If one of those forms is not one of the four cube-compatible forms in (7.1), Lemma 7.1 permits at most `3/4` of the `2^d` pivot sign assignments. If every nonpivot coordinate is cube-compatible, every one of the `2^d` pivot sign assignments lifts to a sign point of `L`. QED.

For a fixed exposed `(R,C_Q,P,Q)` in Theorem 5.1, all remaining columns lie in affine spaces with common direction

\[
D=
\{h\in\mathbf R^n:
 h_I=0,\ R_{\bar P}\Pi h=0\},
\qquad
\dim D=t.
\tag{7.4}
\]

If `D` is not of the signed-partition/cube type classified by Theorem 7.2, every remaining-column fiber gains a factor at most `3/4`, hence the total improves by

\[
\left(\frac34\right)^{r-s}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

If a fiber contains more than `3/4` of the maximum `2^t` sign points, it actually contains all `2^t`, and its direction is exactly of signed-partition type.

This is a sharp structural classification, but it also gives a no-go result: (7.5) saves only `O(n)` bits because `r-s=O(n)`. It cannot repair a positive `Theta(n^2)` excess in the dangerous wedge. In particular, when `t=0` the direction is automatically zero-dimensional and therefore extremal.
