## 3. B25 — resolved fresh-edge trials remove the nesting error

The new idea is to count only fresh-edge trials whose predecessors have actually returned.

Starting at a regeneration vertex `v_0`, define trial `r` as follows. After trial `r-1` has failed and returned across its candidate edge, wait until the walk next crosses an edge `(x_r,y_r)` into a vertex `y_r` never previously visited. Let

\[
\rho_r=\inf\{t>\sigma_r:X_t=x_r\},
\]

where `sigma_r` is the candidate crossing time. Trial `r` succeeds if `rho_r=infinity`; otherwise it fails and only then is trial `r+1` allowed to start.

Because `y_r` is unvisited, the branching property gives, conditional on its type `j`, a fresh forward subtree with law `P_j`. Thus

\[
P(\rho_r=\infty\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_r-},J_r=j)=b_j.
\]

With

\[
b_*:=\min_jb_j>0,
\]

this yields the **valid** geometric estimate

\[
\boxed{P(N>r)\le(1-b_*)^r,}
\]

where `N` is the index of the first successful resolved trial. The distinction is crucial: this controls the **number of sequentially resolved failed excursions**, not `H` directly.

For a failed trial let

\[
D_r=\max_{\sigma_r\le t\le\rho_r}
(|X_t|-|y_r|)_+
\]

be its downward exploration depth. Pathwise,

\[
\boxed{
H\le1+\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}(1+D_r).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The proof is deterministic. A failed trial can increase the currently explored maximal generation by at most `1+D_r`; between its return and the next fresh-edge crossing the walk remains inside the already explored set; the final successful candidate lies only one edge beyond the accumulated explored set.

## 4. New finite-roof theorem

For a fresh trial of type `j`, let `F` be the event of return across the candidate edge and define

\[
\ell_j
:=E_{P_j}E^T[D\mathbf1_F],
\qquad
\ell_*:=\max_j\ell_j.
\]

Also put

\[
c_j=(1-b_j)+\ell_j,
\qquad c_*:=\max_jc_j.
\]

### Theorem R7-07-H0008-A — failed-excursion moment implies finite exit-level roof

If

\[
 b_*>0,\qquad \ell_*<\infty,
\]

then the next fresh-edge regeneration exists almost surely and

\[
\boxed{
EH\le1+\frac{c_*}{b_*}
=1+\frac{\max_j(1-b_j+\ell_j)}{b_*}<\infty.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

The resolved-trial estimate gives `P(N>=r)<=(1-b_*)^(r-1)`. From (3.1), Tonelli, and the fresh law at every actual trial,

\[
\begin{aligned}
EH
&\le1+\sum_{r\ge1}
E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{N\ge r\}}(1+D_r)
\mathbf1_{\{\rho_r<\infty\}}
\right]\\
&\le1+c_*\sum_{r\ge1}P(N\ge r)\\
&\le1+c_*\sum_{r\ge1}(1-b_*)^{r-1}
=1+\frac{c_*}{b_*}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This also shows `N<infinity` almost surely. `square`

N(t)=\max\{n:S_n\le t\},
\]

and put `m=E_pi H_0` in `[1,infinity]`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N(t)}t\to
\begin{cases}
1/m,&m<\infty,\\
0,&m=\infty,

\tag{7.1}
