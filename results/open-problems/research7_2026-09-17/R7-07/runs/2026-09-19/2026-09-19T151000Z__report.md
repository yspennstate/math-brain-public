## 4. Method B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED

### 4.1 Fixed-tree setup

Work first on one locally finite transient rooted tree `T` with root `o` and an artificial parent joined to `o` by a unit-resistance edge. For every vertex `v`, let `B_v` be the probability, starting at `v` in its forward subtree, never to cross to its parent. If `u` ranges over children of `v`, set

\[
S_v=\sum_{u\succ v}B_u.
\]

Electrical reduction gives

\[
B_v=\frac{S_v}{1+S_v},
\qquad
S_v=\frac{B_v}{1-B_v}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The harmonic exit ray chooses a child with probability

\[
p_v(u)=\frac{B_u}{S_v}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For a level-`n` vertex `v`, let `theta(v)` be its harmonic cylinder mass. Then

\[
theta(v)=\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}p_{v_r}(v_{r+1}),
\qquad o=v_0\prec\cdots\prec v_n=v.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The killed harmonic operator is

\[
(\mathcal Kf)(v)
=(1-B_v)\sum_{u\succ v}p_v(u)f(u).
\tag{4.4}
\]

### 4.2 Exact Doob transform of the conductance linearization

Define

\[
(\mathscr Lg)(v)=(1-B_v)^2\sum_{u\succ v}g(u).
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is the positive derivative/linearization of the conductance recursion `S/(1+S)` with respect to its child conductances.

For every nonnegative `f`,

\[
\boxed{
B_v(\mathcal Kf)(v)=\mathscr L(Bf)(v).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_v(\mathcal Kf)(v)
&=B_v(1-B_v)\sum_u\frac{B_u}{S_v}f(u)\\
&=(1-B_v)^2\sum_uB_uf(u),
\end{aligned}
\]

because `B_v/S_v=1-B_v`. Iteration yields

\[
\boxed{
B_o\mathcal K^n1(o)=\mathscr L^nB(o).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus the previously isolated Feynman--Kac operator is not an unrelated auxiliary object: after multiplication by `B`, it is exactly the tangent dynamics of the electrical-conductance recursion.

### 4.3 Harmonic-mass telescoping

From (4.1)-(4.2),

\[
p_v(u)=\frac{B_u(1-B_v)}{B_v}.
\]

Hence for `|v|=n`,

\[
\boxed{
theta(v)
=\frac{B_v}{B_o}
\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(1-B_{v_r}).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Put

\[
A_n(T):=B_o\mathcal K^n1(o),
\qquad
C_n(T):=\sum_{|v|=n}\theta(v)^2.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Expanding the killed path weight and using (4.8),

\[
\boxed{
A_n
=\sum_{|v|=n}B_v\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(1-B_{v_r})^2
=B_o^2\sum_{|v|=n}\frac{\theta(v)^2}{B_v}.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

### 4.4 Energy-layer conservation law

I proved the exact deterministic identity

\[
\boxed{
A_n-A_{n+1}=B_o^2C_n.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

For a fixed level-`n` vertex let

\[
P_v=\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(1-B_{v_r})^2.
\]

Its contribution to `A_n` is `P_vB_v`. The combined contribution of its children to `A_{n+1}` is

\[
P_v(1-B_v)^2\sum_uB_u
=P_v(1-B_v)^2S_v
=P_vB_v(1-B_v).
\]

The difference is `P_vB_v^2`, which by (4.8) equals `B_o^2 theta(v)^2`. Summing over the generation proves (4.11).

There is a second exact conservation law. The harmonic cylinder masses `theta(v)` are precisely the unit electrical currents through the unit-resistance edges entering `v` when one unit of current is sent from the artificial parent to infinity. Hence the unit-current energy is

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}C_n.
\]

The effective conductance of the complete branch from the artificial parent through `o` to infinity is `B_o`; therefore its effective resistance, and hence the unit-current energy, is `1/B_o`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\ge0}C_n=\frac1{B_o}.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Combining (4.11)-(4.12) gives

\[
\boxed{
A_n=B_o^2\sum_{m\ge n}C_m.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

### 4.5 The killed-semigroup gate is exactly a two-ray overlap moment

Let `xi,eta` be two conditionally independent harmonic exit rays in the same tree and let

\[
L=\sup\{m\ge0:\xi_m=\eta_m\}
\]

be their common-prefix generation. Then

\[
P(L\ge m\mid T)=C_m(T).
\tag{4.14}
\]

Tonelli and (4.13) therefore give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n\ge0}B_o\mathcal K^n1(o)
&=B_o^2\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)C_m\\
&=\boxed{\frac{B_o^2}{2}

\tag{4.15}
