### Theorem 5.1 — square-conductance conjugation

Define

`c(b)=(b/(1-b))^2`.

Let `C` be multiplication by `c`. Then

`boxed: L_2 C = C T`,

where

`boxed:`

`(Tg)_j(b)`

`=(1-b)^2 m_* q_j sum_i p_ij E[g_i(F_b(R_i))].`

### Complete derivation

Because

`F_b(r)/(1-F_b(r))=b+r`,

`c(F_b(r))=(b+r)^2`.

`(L_2 Cg)_j(b)`

`=m_*q_j b^2 sum_i p_ij E[c(F_b(R_i))g_i(F_b(R_i))/(b+R_i)^2]`

`=c(b)(1-b)^2m_*q_j sum_i p_ij E[g_i(F_b(R_i))]`.

No estimate is used. The denominator disappears identically.
