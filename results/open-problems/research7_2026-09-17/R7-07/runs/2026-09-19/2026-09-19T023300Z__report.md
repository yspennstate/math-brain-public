## 4. B12 — find what is conserved: the rational flag is actually an integral Gaussian-lattice flag

### Idea before code

The previous run reduced the event to existence of a complete `Q(i)`-rational invariant flag, leaving a possible denominator-height problem. The first question is whether those denominators are intrinsic. They are not: `Z[i]` is a Euclidean PID, so a primitive eigenvector splits off as a direct summand and induction triangularizes over `Z[i]` itself.

### Theorem 4.1

For `R=Z[i]` and `M in M_n(R)`, the following are equivalent:

1. `chi_M` splits into linear factors in `R[x]`;
2. there exists `U in GL_n(R)` with `U^{-1} M U` upper triangular over `R`.

#### Proof

The reverse implication is immediate. For the forward implication, choose a root `lambda in R`. Over `K=Q(i)`, `M-lambda I` has nonzero kernel. Clear denominators in a nonzero kernel vector to obtain `v in R^n`, then divide its coordinates by their Gaussian gcd. The resulting `v` is primitive and still satisfies `Mv=lambda v`.

A primitive vector over a PID extends to an `R`-basis of `R^n`. In such a basis,

`M = [[lambda,*],[0,B]]`

with `B in M_{n-1}(R)`, and

`chi_B(x)=chi_M(x)/(x-lambda)`.

The quotient polynomial again splits over `R`; induction triangularizes `B` by a Gaussian-unimodular matrix. Extending that basis change by the first coordinate triangularizes `M`. QED.

Thus every exceptional integer sign matrix preserves a complete flag of **primitive free direct summands**

`0=L_0<L_1<...<L_n=Z[i]^n`.

The rational flag of the previous run can always be chosen as the scalar extension of an integral Gaussian lattice flag.

### Exact conserved consequences

First, for every rational prime `p == 1 mod 4`, choose a Gaussian prime `pi|p`. Reducing the unimodular triangularization modulo `pi` keeps the change of basis invertible, so `M mod p` is triangularizable over `F_p` and its characteristic polynomial splits completely over `F_p`.

For every prime `p == 3 mod 4`, reduction modulo the inert prime gives a triangularization over `F_{p^2}`. Hence `chi_M mod p` has no irreducible factor of degree greater than two over `F_p`.

Second, the characteristic-polynomial discriminant is a signed square. If the roots are `lambda_j in Z[i]`, then

`D=prod_{r<s}(lambda_r-lambda_s) in Z[i]`

and the integer discriminant is `D^2`. Writing `D=a+bi`, integrality of `D^2` forces `ab=0`, hence

`Delta(chi_M)=+q^2` or `-q^2`

for an integer `q`, including `q=0` for repeated roots.

## 5. B24 — find the extremal object: opposite coordinate orders are exactly optimal in the triangular shear class

### Fixed-shear ternary encoding

For `n=2m`, use the fixed shear

`S=[[I,0],[I,I]]`

and write

`M=[[A,B],[C,D]]`.

Under the zero-lower-block constraint

`C+D=A+B`,

define

`X=(A+B)/2`, `Y=(D-B)/2`.

The sign constraint gives entrywise states

`(-1,0),(1,0),(0,-1),(0,1),(0,0)`

for `(X_ij,Y_ij)`. The four nonzero states each have one sign-quadruple preimage and `(0,0)` has two. Therefore `supp X` and `supp Y` are disjoint, and a pair has exactly

`2^{m^2-|supp X|-|supp Y|}`

sign-matrix preimages. Moreover

`S^{-1} M S = [[2X,B],[0,2Y]]`.

### Exact two-order theorem

Let `sigma,tau` be coordinate orders in which `X,Y` respectively are upper triangular, and let `D(sigma,tau)` be their Kendall inversion distance. The exact weighted partition is

`Z(sigma,tau)`
`=6^m 12^{binom(m,2)-D(sigma,tau)} 16^{D(sigma,tau)}`.      (5.1)

Proof: a diagonal coordinate contributes `6`. For an unordered pair ordered the same way by `sigma,tau`, the forward cell allows both ternary variables (`6`) and the reverse cell neither (`2`), giving `12`. If the two orders disagree, one directed cell allows only `X` (`4`) and the other only `Y` (`4`), giving `16`. Multiply independently over all cells.

Since `16>12`, (5.1) is maximized when the orders are exact reverses. Thus

`T_m=6^m16^{binom(m,2)}=4^{m(m-1)}6^m`.                    (5.2)

This proves that the original opposite-order fixed-shear construction was not merely convenient: it is the exact extremizer over **every pair of coordinate triangularizations**.

A ternary matrix is permutation-upper-triangular iff its off-diagonal support digraph is acyclic. Taking the union over at most `(m!)^2` pairs of topological orders yields

`#(all compatible permutation-triangular pairs)`
`<= (m!)^2 T_m`
`=2^{2m^2+O(m log m)}`.                                    (5.3)

Thus this entire route cannot construct more than `2^{2m^2+o(m^2)}` exceptional `2m x 2m` sign matrices and cannot improve the leading probability exponent below `1/2`. The `2^m` signed diagonal shears from the preceding run alter this by only `2^{O(m)}`.

This is a **route-level no-go**, not a global upper bound on `E_{2m}`.

`n=2k+q`, `q>=1`,

and use an `(k+q) x k` shear matrix whose top `k x k` block is diagonal with signs and whose final `q` rows are zero.
