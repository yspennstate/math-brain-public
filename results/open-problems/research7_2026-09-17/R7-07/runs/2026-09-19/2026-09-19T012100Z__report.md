## 4. B3 — stronger restricted theorem: rational flags and the sharp fixed-stratum half exponent

### Idea before derivation

The lower construction has exponent `1/2`, suggesting that the corrected sharp exponent may be `1/2`. Instead of immediately trying to bound the whole exceptional set, first ask what can be proved uniformly on one *fixed triangularizing flag*. If every individual flag stratum has half entropy and one stratum already attains half entropy, the only remaining issue is the arithmetic entropy of the union of flags.

Put `K=Q(i)`. For a complete `K`-rational flag

`0=V_0<V_1<...<V_n=K^n`, `dim_K V_j=j`,

let

`b_F={T in M_n(K): T(V_j) subset V_j for every j}`.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact flag characterization

For every integer matrix `M`, the following are equivalent:

1. `Spec(M) subset Z[i]`;
2. `chi_M` splits over `Q(i)`;
3. `M` preserves a complete `Q(i)`-rational flag.

Proof: `(1)=>(2)` is immediate. `(2)=>(3)` is the standard triangularizability criterion over a field; induct on dimension using a `Q(i)` eigenvector and the quotient. For `(3)=>(1)`, an adapted basis makes `M` upper triangular over `Q(i)`, so every eigenvalue lies in `Q(i)`. Because `M` is integral, its eigenvalues are algebraic integers. The ring of integers of `Q(i)` is `Z[i]`, so the eigenvalues are Gaussian integers.

Thus `E_n` is **exactly** the sign-cube projection of rational complete-flag incidence. This is not merely a sufficient construction.

### Lemma 4.2 — hypercube section

If `W` is a real `d`-dimensional linear subspace of `R^N`, then

`|W cap {+-1}^N| <= 2^d`.

Choose `d` coordinate projections whose restrictions to `W` are injective; cube points map injectively into `{+-1}^d`.

### Theorem 4.3 — every fixed rational flag has at most half entropy

The complex flag-preserving algebra has dimension

`d=n(n+1)/2`.

For

`W_F=b_F(C) cap M_n(R)`,

one has `dim_R W_F <= d`: real-linearly independent real matrices remain complex-linearly independent. Applying the hypercube-section lemma gives

`|b_F cap {+-1}^{n x n}| <= 2^{n(n+1)/2}`.

Hence a single rational flag can never carry more than a `1/2+o(1)` fraction of the `n^2` sign entropy.

### Theorem 4.4 — the fixed-flag half exponent is itself sharp

For `n=2m`, take

`S=[[I,0],[I,I]]`

and one fixed sign shear `R=I` from the original R7-07 construction. Writing

`M=[[A,B],[C,D]]`,

impose

- `A+B` upper triangular;
- `C+D=A+B`;
- `D-B` lower triangular.

Let `J` reverse the second `m` basis vectors and put `Q=S diag(I,J)`. Then

`Q^{-1} M Q = [[A+B, BJ],[0, J(D-B)J]]`

is upper triangular, so **all matrices in this family preserve one fixed rational complete flag**.

The count is exact:

- every off-diagonal ordered position contributes four sign choices;
- every diagonal position satisfies `c+d=a+b`, which has six sign quadruples.

Therefore the one fixed flag contains

`4^{m(m-1)} 6^m`

`2^{n^2/2 + (n/2) log_2(3/2)}`.

So the fixed-flag upper theorem is sharp at the `n^2` scale. The source's original exponent `1` cannot be rescued by a stronger estimate on any one Borel/flag stratum; the only possible extra suppression lies in controlling the union of rational flags.

## 5. B14 — signed-permutation quotient is exactly exponent-neutral

### Idea before derivation

The source itself notes in a nearby normality problem that `n!=2^{o(n^2)}` permutations can be used without changing an `n^2` exponent. For F0012 the natural symmetry is larger: conjugation by signed permutation matrices. Quotient the rational flags by this group before trying to count them.

Let

`G_n={DP: D diagonal with +-1 entries, P permutation}`.

Conjugation maps the sign cube to itself and preserves spectrum. For `n>=2`, the kernel of the conjugation action is exactly `{+-I}`, hence its effective size is

`|Gamma_n|=2^{n-1} n! = 2^{O(n log n)} = 2^{o(n^2)}`.

It acts on rational flags and satisfies

`b_{gF}=g b_F g^{-1}`.

Define:

- `kappa_n`: minimum number of individual `Q(i)`-rational complete flags whose Borel intersections cover `E_n`;
- `q_n`: minimum number of `Gamma_n`-orbits of rational flags whose full orbit-strata cover `E_n`.

Then exactly

`q_n <= kappa_n <= |Gamma_n| q_n`.

Therefore

`limsup log_2(kappa_n)/n^2 = limsup log_2(q_n)/n^2`.

The symmetry quotient can simplify classification but cannot alter the desired leading exponent.

Combining the fixed-flag theorem with a minimal cover yields

`|E_n| <= kappa_n 2^{n(n+1)/2}`,

hence

`P(E_n) <= kappa_n 2^{-n(n-1)/2}`.

If

`c_flag := limsup log_2(kappa_n)/n^2`,

## 6. B29 — projectivize: exact incidence on the complete flag variety

### Idea before derivation

Eigenvector coordinates can have arbitrarily large denominators, so an affine chart risks mistaking a coordinate blow-up for a new mathematical obstruction. Pass directly to the projective complete flag variety.

The complete flag variety `Fl_n/Q(i)` is projective. Define

`I_n(Q(i))={(M,F): M in {+-1}^{n x n}, F in Fl_n(Q(i)), M V_j subset V_j for all j}`.

By Theorem 4.1, projection to `M` is exactly `E_n`.

Thus there is **no missing geometric boundary**: all rational eigenline/flag degenerations are already represented projectively. The hard part is arithmetic — how many rational flag strata (modulo the exponent-neutral signed-permutation symmetry) are genuinely needed to cover the exceptional sign matrices?

A second exact reduction reaches the same point through characteristic polynomials. Gershgorin gives `|lambda|<=n` for every eigenvalue of a sign matrix. There are at most `(2n+1)^2` Gaussian integers in the containing square. Hence the number of possible spectral multisets, and thus characteristic polynomials, among `E_n` is at most

`binom((2n+1)^2+n-1,n)=2^{O(n log n)}`.

Therefore it would suffice to prove the uniform fiber estimate

`max_p #{M in {+-1}^{n x n}: chi_M=p} <= 2^{n^2/2+o(n^2)}`

for the admissible split Gaussian-integer polynomials `p`.

The fresh literature check also exposes why a purely algebraic-dimension argument is insufficient. In the full finite-field matrix space, the fixed characteristic polynomial `x^n` already supports `q^{n^2-n}` nilpotent matrices (Fine--Herstein/Gerstenhaber), and Reiner's 1961 theorem counts general prescribed-characteristic-polynomial fibers. A half-exponent theorem must therefore exploit the **real sign-hypercube intersection**, not just the defining equations of a characteristic-polynomial fiber.

**B29 outcome:** projective compactification converts the unknown into a clean arithmetic incidence/covering problem; there is no unaccounted affine boundary route.
