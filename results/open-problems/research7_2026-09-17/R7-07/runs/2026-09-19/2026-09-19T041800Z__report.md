## 5. B18 — canonical kernel filtration and exact fixed-filtration entropy

Let `A` be nilpotent and set

\[
b_j=\dim K_j-\dim K_{j-1}.
\]

### 5.1 Kernel increments are nonincreasing

The map induced by `A`,

\[
K_{j+1}/K_j\longrightarrow K_j/K_{j-1},
\]

is injective: if `x in K_{j+1}` and `Ax in K_{j-1}`, then

\[
A^j x=A^{j-1}(Ax)=0,
\]

so `x in K_j`. Therefore

\[
b_{j+1}\le b_j,
\qquad \sum_j b_j=n.
\]

Thus `b` is exactly the conjugate-partition data of the nilpotent Jordan type.

### 5.2 Exact dimension formula

For a fixed filtration with increments `b_j`, define

\[
\mathcal L(K_\bullet)=\{T:T K_j\subset K_{j-1}\ \forall j\}.
\]

Choose complements `E_j` with `K_j=K_{j-1}\oplus E_j` and `dim E_j=b_j`. The restriction `T|_{E_j}` can be chosen arbitrarily in `Hom(E_j,K_{j-1})`, independently for each `j`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dim\mathcal L(K_\bullet)
&=\sum_j b_j\dim K_{j-1}\\
&=\sum_jb_j\sum_{\ell<j}b_\ell\\
&=\sum_{\ell<j}b_\ell b_j\\
&=\frac12\left(n^2-\sum_jb_j^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Every nilpotent matrix with canonical kernel filtration `K_\bullet` belongs to this space because `A K_j\subset K_{j-1}`.

### 5.3 Hypercube intersection

If `L` is a `D`-dimensional real linear subspace of `R^N`, then

\[
|L\cap\{\pm1\}^N|\le2^D.
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, the coordinate functionals span `L^*`; select `D` whose restrictions form a basis. Projection to those coordinates is injective on `L`, and a sign vector has only `2^D` projected patterns.

Applying (2) to (1) gives the exact fixed-filtration bound

\[
\boxed{
\#\bigl(\mathcal L(K_\bullet)\cap\{\pm1\}^{n\times n}\bigr)
\le2^{(n^2-\sum_jb_j^2)/2}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Let `F_b(n)` denote the set of **realized canonical kernel filtrations** of profile `b`. Then

\[
\boxed{
N_b(n)\le |F_b(n)|\,2^{(n^2-\sum_jb_j^2)/2}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Since the number of profiles is the partition number `p(n)=exp(O(sqrt(n)))`, the profile sum itself costs only `2^{o(n^2)}`. The remaining obstruction is now precise: control **realized-filtration entropy**, not Jordan-profile entropy. In particular,

\[
\log_2|F_b(n)|\le\frac12\sum_jb_j^2+o(n^2)
\tag{5}
\]

uniformly in `b` would imply the desired nilpotent-fiber upper bound `(R)`.

### 5.4 The half exponent is already sharp inside the nilpotent fiber

Return to the earlier `n=2m` shear

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\I&I\end{pmatrix},
\]

for which

\[
S^{-1}MS=
\begin{pmatrix}
A+B&B\\ C+D-A-B&D-B
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Impose `C+D=A+B`, require `A+B` upper triangular and `D-B` lower triangular, and now force both diagonal blocks to have zero diagonal. Each off-diagonal scalar position has exactly four sign choices. On a diagonal position the equations

\[
a+b=0,\qquad d-b=0,\qquad c+d=a+b
\]

have exactly two sign solutions. Thus the family has exactly

\[
4^{m(m-1)}2^m
=2^{2m^2-m}
=2^{n^2/2-n/2}
\tag{6}
\]

distinct matrices. After reversing the coordinate order in the lower block, the conjugated matrix is strictly upper triangular, hence nilpotent. Therefore

\[
\boxed{N_0(2m)\ge2^{2m^2-m}.}
\tag{7}
\]

So `(R)`, if proved, is sharp at quadratic scale. For odd `n`, `N_0(n)=0` because a nilpotent matrix has trace zero while the sum of an odd number of `+/-1` diagonal entries cannot be zero.

## 6. B19 — sequential reveal proves the entire square-zero stratum

For `1<=r<=n/2`, let

\[
Z_{n,r}=\{A\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times n}:A^2=0,\operatorname{rank}A=r\}.
\]

### Theorem

\[
\boxed{|Z_{n,r}|\le\binom nr^2 2^{2r(n-r)}.}
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\#\{A\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times n}:A^2=0\}\le2^{n^2/2+O(n)}.}
\tag{9}
\]

### Proof

Fix `A in Z_{n,r}`. Choose row and column sets `I,J` of cardinality `r` so that

\[
B=A_{I,J}
\]

is nonsingular. There are at most `binom(n,r)^2` choices of `(I,J)`.

Expose the basis rows

\[
R=A_{I,*}\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times n}.
\]

There are at most `2^{rn}` choices and `rank(R)=r` because `B` is invertible.

For each basis column `c_j=A_{*,j}`, `j in J`, the relation `A^2=0` gives

\[
R_i\cdot c_j=(A^2)_{ij}=0
\]

for every `i in I`, hence

\[
c_j\in\ker R,
\qquad \dim\ker R=n-r.
\]

Its coordinates on `I` are already fixed by the exposed row matrix:

\[
\pi_I(c_j)=A_{I,j}=B_{*,j}.
\]

Because the `r` vectors `B_{*,j}`, `j in J`, form a basis of `R^r`, any actual completion forces the restricted projection

\[
\pi_I|_{\ker R}:\ker R\to\mathbf R^r
\]

to have rank `r`. Therefore every nonempty affine fiber

\[
\{x\in\ker R:\pi_I(x)=B_{*,j}\}
\]

has dimension

\[
(n-r)-r=n-2r.
\]

By the affine version of the hypercube-section lemma, each such fiber contains at most `2^{n-2r}` sign vectors. Hence all `r` basis columns together have at most

\[
2^{r(n-2r)}
\]

choices after `R` is fixed.

Finally, the rank-`r` matrix is uniquely reconstructed from its basis rows and columns by the exact CUR identity

\[
A=A_{*,J}\,B^{-1}\,A_{I,*}.
\]

Thus for one `(I,J)` there are at most

\[
2^{rn}2^{r(n-2r)}=2^{2r(n-r)}
\]

matrices, proving (8) after multiplying by `binom(n,r)^2`.

Since `A^2=0` implies `im(A) subset ker(A)`, we have `r<=n/2`; therefore `2r(n-r)<=n^2/2`. Summing (8), using `binom(n,r)<=2^n`, yields

\[
\sum_{r\le n/2}|Z_{n,r}|
\le(n+1)2^{2n}2^{n^2/2}
=2^{n^2/2+O(n)},
\]

which proves (9). QED.

**B19 outcome:** SUCCESS. The complete square-zero stratum has the desired half-exponent upper bound. This is a theorem-sized restricted result, not the all-nilpotent target.
