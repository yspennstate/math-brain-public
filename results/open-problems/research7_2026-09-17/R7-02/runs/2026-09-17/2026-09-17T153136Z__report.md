## 5. Method B18 — say it in another language

### 5.1 A bijection between critical laws and a fixed-mean tilted simplex

Let `p_k=P(X=k)` be any law satisfying (C), and put

\[
A:=G(m)=\sum_{k\ge0}m^kp_k.
\]

Define the `m`-tilted law

\[
\nu(k):=\frac{m^kp_k}{A},\qquad k\ge0.
\]

Then `nu` is a probability distribution and the critical identity is exactly

\[
\mathbf E_\nu[K]=\frac{\mathbf E[Xm^X]}{\mathbf E[m^X]}=\frac1{m-1}. \tag{T1}
\]

Moreover normalization of the original law gives

\[
1=\sum_kp_k=A\sum_k\nu(k)m^{-k},
\]

hence

\[
A=\frac1{\mathbf E_\nu[m^{-K}]}. \tag{T2}
\]

Conversely, take **any** probability distribution `nu` on `Z_+` with mean `1/(m-1)`, put

\[
A:=\frac1{\mathbf E_\nu[m^{-K}]},\qquad
p_k:=A\nu(k)m^{-k}.
\]

Then `sum p_k=1`, `G(m)=A`, and

\[
(m-1)\mathbf E[Xm^X]=(m-1)A\mathbf E_\nu[K]=A=G(m).
\]

Thus critical initial laws are in exact bijection with a fixed-mean simplex of tilted laws. This is useful because it separates the critical constraint from the exponentially weighted normalization; extremal critical examples become ordinary finite-support moment problems for `nu`.

### 5.2 A backward-stable recurrence for every `s<m`

Let

\[
u_n:=\mathbf P(X_n>0),\qquad
\pi_n:=\frac{1-G_n(0)^m}{m}.
\]

Since `G_n(0)=1-u_n`,

\[
\pi_n=\frac{1-(1-u_n)^m}{m}=u_n+O(u_n^2). \tag{P}
\]

Fix `s in (0,m)`, `s != 1`, and define

\[
d_n(s):=G_n(s)-1,\qquad
r:=\frac ms>1,\qquad
c:=\frac{m(s-1)}s.
\]

The exact source pgf recursion gives

\[
G_{n+1}(s)=\frac1sG_n(s)^m+\Bigl(1-\frac1s\Bigr)G_n(0)^m.
\]

After substituting `G_n(s)=1+d_n` and `G_n(0)^m=1-m\pi_n`, this becomes

\[
d_{n+1}=r d_n-c\pi_n+R_s(d_n), \tag{R}
\]

where

\[
R_s(x):=\frac{(1+x)^m-1-mx}{s}. \tag{NR}
\]

For `x>-1`, convexity of `(1+x)^m` gives `R_s(x)>=0`, and `R_s(x)=O(x^2)` as `x->0`.

Because the critical system has zero free energy, the source's bounded-moment lemma gives bounded `G_n(s)` for each fixed `1<s<m`; for `0<s<1` boundedness is automatic. Therefore iterating (R) **backwards** is legitimate:

\[
d_n=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r^{-j-1}\bigl(c\pi_{n+j}-R_s(d_{n+j})\bigr). \tag{BR+}
\]

For `s<1`, it is cleaner to write `h_n:=1-G_n(s)=-d_n>=0`, `a:=m(1-s)/s>0`; then

\[
h_n=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r^{-j-1}\bigl(a\pi_{n+j}+R_s(-h_{n+j})\bigr). \tag{BR-}
\]

The forward recursion is unstable because `r>1`; the backward representation is a contraction. This is the key category change.

### 5.3 Conditional theorem: Conjecture 1 alone forces Conjecture 4(a) and Conjecture 2

**Theorem.** Assume the critical hypothesis (C). Suppose only that the source's Conjecture 1 holds with some constant `C>0`, i.e.

\[
u_n=\mathbf P(X_n>0)\sim Cn^{-2}. \tag{U}
\]

Then, for every fixed `s in (0,m)`, `s!=1`,

\[
G_n(s)-1\sim C\frac{m(s-1)}{m-s}\,n^{-2}. \tag{A}
\]

Consequently, if `C=4/(m-1)^2` as in source Conjecture 1, source Conjecture 4(a) follows. Moreover the conditional law of `X_n` given `X_n>0` converges to the geometric law

\[
\mathbf P(Y=k)=\frac{m-1}{m^k},\qquad k\ge1,
\]

so source Conjecture 2 follows as well.

**Proof.** By (P), `pi_n~Cn^{-2}`.

First take `1<s<m`. Then `d_n>=0` and `c>0`. From (BR+) and `R_s>=0`,

\[
0\le d_n\le c\sum_{j\ge0}r^{-j-1}\pi_{n+j}. \tag{B1}
\]

Since `pi_k~Ck^{-2}`, the right side is `O(n^{-2})`; hence `d_n=O(n^{-2})` and therefore `R_s(d_n)=O(n^{-4})`. Also dominated convergence applies to the geometrically weighted sequence because, for all large `n`,

\[
\frac{\pi_{n+j}}{\pi_n}\le 4\frac{n^2}{(n+j)^2}\le4,
\]

while `r^{-j-1}` is summable. Thus

\[
\sum_{j\ge0}r^{-j-1}\pi_{n+j}
\sim\pi_n\sum_{j\ge0}r^{-j-1}
=\frac{\pi_n}{r-1}. \tag{B2}
\]

The nonlinear remainder contributes only

\[
\sum_{j\ge0}r^{-j-1}R_s(d_{n+j})=O(n^{-4})=o(\pi_n). \tag{B3}
\]

Substitution in (BR+) yields

\[
d_n\sim\frac{c}{r-1}\pi_n
=\frac{m(s-1)}{m-s}\pi_n
\sim C\frac{m(s-1)}{m-s}n^{-2}.
\]

Now take `0<s<1`. Then `h_n=1-G_n(s)>=0` and, pointwise,

\[
0\le 1-s^{X_n}\le \mathbf 1_{\{X_n>0\}},
\]

so

\[
0\le h_n\le u_n=O(n^{-2}). \tag{B4}
\]

Hence `R_s(-h_n)=O(n^{-4})`. Applying the same dominated-convergence argument to (BR-) gives

\[
h_n\sim\frac{a}{r-1}\pi_n
=C\frac{m(1-s)}{m-s}n^{-2},
\]

which is equivalent to (A). This proves the first assertion.

Finally, if

\[
M_n(s):=\mathbf E[s^{X_n}\mid X_n>0],
\]

then

\[
G_n(s)=1-u_n+u_nM_n(s),
\qquad
M_n(s)=1+\frac{d_n(s)}{u_n}.
\]

For every `s in (0,1)`, (A) gives

\[
M_n(s)\longrightarrow
1+\frac{m(s-1)}{m-s}
=\frac{(m-1)s}{m-s},
\]

which is exactly the pgf of the geometric distribution `P(Y=k)=(m-1)m^{-k}`, `k>=1`. Convergence of pgfs on `(0,1)` to this proper pgf implies weak convergence on the positive integers. QED.

**Outcome of B18.** The source text had written that, *if* the ratio `(G_n(s)-1)/P(X_n>0)` had a limit, the limit would have to be the geometric one. The backward contraction above removes that extra ratio-limit assumption: the survival asymptotic alone forces the ratio limit. This does not prove Conjecture 1, but it collapses two further conjectural consequences onto it.

## 6. Method B24 — find the extremal object: exact obstruction to global monotonicity

The obvious route from source Proposition 3 to 4(b) is a monotone-density/Tauberian argument: if `epsilon_n=G_n(m)-1` were eventually decreasing, the exact logarithmic sum asymptotic (S) would force its pointwise constant. ### 6.1 General finite-support critical family

Fix `m>=2` and an integer `L>=2`. Put

\[
t=\frac1{(m-1)L},\qquad \delta=m^{-L},\qquad z=1-t+t\delta.
\]

Take the tilted law

\[
\nu=(1-t)\delta_0+t\delta_L.
\]

It has mean `tL=1/(m-1)`, so the inverse tilt gives the exact critical law

\[
\mathbf P(X_0=0)=\frac{1-t}{z},\qquad
\mathbf P(X_0=L)=\frac{t\delta}{z}. \tag{E1}
\]

Indeed

\[
G_0(m)=z^{-1},
\qquad
\mathbf E[X_0m^{X_0}]=\frac{Lt}{z}=\frac1{(m-1)z},
\]

so `(m-1)E[X_0m^{X_0}]=G_0(m)` exactly.

Let `q=P(X_0=0)=(1-t)/z`. At `s=m` the pgf recursion gives

\[
G_1(m)=\frac1mG_0(m)^m+\frac{m-1}{m}q^m.
\]

Hence

\[
G_1(m)-G_0(m)=\frac{z^{-m}}mF_{m,L}, \tag{E2}
\]

where

\[
F_{m,L}=1+(m-1)(1-t)^m-mz^{m-1}. \tag{E3}
\]

Set temporarily `delta=0`, so `z=1-t`. Then

\[
F_m(t,0)=1-(1-t)^{m-1}(1+(m-1)t), \tag{E4}
\]

and differentiation yields the exact identity

\[
\frac{d}{dt}F_m(t,0)=m(m-1)t(1-t)^{m-2}>0\quad(0<t<1). \tag{E5}
\]

Therefore

\[
F_m(t,0)=m(m-1)\int_0^t u(1-u)^{m-2}\,du
\ge\frac{m(m-1)}2t^2(1-t)^{m-2}. \tag{E6}
\]

On the other hand, since `z=(1-t)+t delta` and `x^(m-1)` has derivative at most `m-1` on `[0,1]`,

\[
0\le z^{m-1}-(1-t)^{m-1}\le(m-1)t\delta. \tag{E7}
\]

Combining (E3),(E6),(E7),

\[
F_{m,L}\ge
m(m-1)t\left[\frac12t(1-t)^{m-2}-\delta\right]. \tag{E8}
\]

Because `t=1/((m-1)L)` decays only polynomially in `L` while `delta=m^{-L}` decays exponentially, the bracket is positive for all sufficiently large `L`. Therefore:

**Proposition (global monotonicity is false for every branching factor).** For every integer `m>=2` there exists a finitely supported critical law satisfying (C) for which

\[
G_1(m)>G_0(m).
\]

Thus neither criticality nor finite support implies that `epsilon_n=G_n(m)-1` is decreasing from time zero.

This is an obstruction to a proof route, **not** a counterexample to source Conjecture 4(b), which is asymptotic.

## 7. Method B20 — find exactly why the obvious algorithm fails, then salvage the weakest sufficient condition

Define

\[
\varepsilon_n:=G_n(m)-1,
\qquad
\pi_n:=\frac{1-G_n(0)^m}{m}.
\]

The source pgf recursion at `s=m` gives the **exact increment formula**

\[
\varepsilon_{n+1}-\varepsilon_n
=(m-1)\bigl(H_m(\varepsilon_n)-\pi_n\bigr), \tag{I1}
\]

with

\[
H_m(x):=\frac{(1+x)^m-1-mx}{m(m-1)}
=\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{m-2}{6}x^3+O(x^4). \tag{I2}
\]

Thus eventual monotonicity is not mysterious: it is **exactly** the eventual inequality

\[
\pi_n\ge H_m(\varepsilon_n). \tag{I3}
\]

The exact `m=2,L=3` witness above has

\[
\varepsilon_0=\frac7{17},\qquad
\pi_0=\frac{33}{578},\qquad
H_2(\varepsilon_0)=\frac{49}{578},
\]

so (I3) fails and the increment is exactly `8/289`.

### 7.1 A sharp conditional Tauberian theorem for 4(b)

Assume now the source's third tilted moment condition

\[
\mathbf E[X_0^3m^{X_0}]<\infty.
\]

As noted above, the source proves

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i=\frac{2}{m-1}\log n+O(1). \tag{I4}
\]

I claim that a much weaker pointwise estimate than the full survival conjecture suffices to turn (I4) into Conjecture 4(b).

**Conditional theorem.** If

\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\pi_n}{\varepsilon_n^2}>\frac12, \tag{I5}
\]

then

\[
\varepsilon_n\sim\frac{2}{m-1}\frac1n. \tag{I6}
\]

**Proof.** Since `epsilon_n->0`, (I2) implies

\[
\frac{H_m(\varepsilon_n)}{\varepsilon_n^2}\to\frac12.
\]

Condition (I5) therefore implies (I3) for every sufficiently large `n`. By (I1), `epsilon_n` is eventually nonincreasing.

Set `c=2/(m-1)` and `S_n=sum_{i<=n}epsilon_i`. By (I4), `S_n=c log n+O(1)`. Fix `lambda>1`. Eventual monotonicity gives, for large `n`,

\[
S_{\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor}-S_n
\le (\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor-n)\varepsilon_n,
\]

hence

\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n\varepsilon_n
\ge c\frac{\log\lambda}{\lambda-1}. \tag{I7}
\]

Likewise, on the interval from `floor(n/lambda)+1` through `n`, every term is at least `epsilon_n`, so

\[
S_n-S_{\lfloor n/\lambda\rfloor}
\ge (n-\lfloor n/\lambda\rfloor)\varepsilon_n,
\]

and therefore

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\varepsilon_n
\le c\frac{\lambda\log\lambda}{\lambda-1}. \tag{I8}
\]

Letting `lambda downarrow 1` in (I7),(I8) yields `n epsilon_n -> c`, which is (I6). QED.

### 7.2 What is actually missing now

The source predicts

\[
\pi_n\sim\mathbf P(X_n>0)\sim\frac{4}{(m-1)^2n^2}
\]

and 4(b) predicts

\[
\varepsilon_n^2\sim\frac{4}{(m-1)^2n^2},
\]

so the conjectural ratio is `pi_n/epsilon_n^2 -> 1`. The conditional theorem needs only a strict eventual lower margin above **1/2**. This is substantially weaker than proving the full survival constant.

The source's current rigorous tools do not obviously provide that pointwise lower ratio. Proposition 4 controls a logarithmic average of `pi_n n^2`; Theorem 3 controls `epsilon_n` between constant multiples of `1/n`; neither prevents a log-time oscillation of the ratio. The B24 family shows why a purely static critical-law inequality cannot prove (I3): a critical law can place tiny original mass at a very large state and have `pi << epsilon^2`, causing `epsilon` to increase. Any proof of (I5) must therefore use **dynamical smoothing of the recursion**, not criticality alone.

That is the exact obstruction identified this run.
