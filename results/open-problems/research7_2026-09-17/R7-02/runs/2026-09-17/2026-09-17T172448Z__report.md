## 4. Method B1 — change category to an autonomous critical tilted-law dynamics

Define the probability law

$$
\widehat\nu_n(k):=\frac{m^k\mathbf P(X_n=k)}{A_n},\qquad k\in\mathbf Z_+ . \tag{4.1}
$$

Criticality is preserved by the recursion, so

$$
\mathbf E_{\widehat\nu_n}K
=\frac{mG_n'(m)}{A_n}
=\frac1{m-1}
=:\mu \tag{4.2}
$$

for every `n`. Thus the evolving tilted law lives on the fixed affine slice of probability measures having mean `mu`.

Let

$$
r_n:=\widehat\nu_n(0),\qquad
 t_n:=1-r_n,\qquad
 \rho_n:=\widehat\nu_n^{*m}.
$$

### Proposition 4.1 — exact autonomous tilted-law map

Set

$$
R_n:=\frac{1}{1+(m-1)r_n^m},
\qquad
\lambda_n:=mR_n=\frac{m}{1+(m-1)r_n^m}. \tag{4.3}
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\widehat\nu_{n+1}(0)=R_n\,[m\rho_n(0)+\rho_n(1)]
} \tag{4.4}
$$

and, for every `k>=1`,

$$
\boxed{
\widehat\nu_{n+1}(k)=R_n\rho_n(k+1).
} \tag{4.5}
$$

Moreover

$$
\boxed{
\lambda_n=\frac{A_n^m}{A_{n+1}}.
} \tag{4.6}
$$

#### Proof

Write `p_n(k)=P(X_n=k)`. Since

$$
p_n(k)=A_n\widehat\nu_n(k)m^{-k},
$$

the sum `S_n=X_n^{(1)}+...+X_n^{(m)}` satisfies

$$
\mathbf P(S_n=j)=A_n^m m^{-j}\rho_n(j). \tag{4.7}
$$

For `k>=1`, the recursion gives `X_{n+1}=k` iff `S_n=k+1`, and therefore

$$
\widehat\nu_{n+1}(k)
=\frac{m^k}{A_{n+1}}A_n^m m^{-(k+1)}\rho_n(k+1)
=\frac{A_n^m}{mA_{n+1}}\rho_n(k+1). \tag{4.8}
$$

At zero, `X_{n+1}=0` iff `S_n` is 0 or 1, hence

$$
\widehat\nu_{n+1}(0)
=\frac{A_n^m}{A_{n+1}}
\left[\rho_n(0)+\frac1m\rho_n(1)\right]. \tag{4.9}
$$

Normalize the right sides of (4.8)–(4.9). Their total mass is

$$
\frac{A_n^m}{mA_{n+1}}
\left[m\rho_n(0)+\rho_n(1)+\sum_{j\ge2}\rho_n(j)\right]
=
\frac{A_n^m}{mA_{n+1}}[1+(m-1)r_n^m].
$$

It equals one, proving (4.3) and (4.6), and then (4.4)–(4.5). QED.

This is genuinely stronger structure than the scalar endpoint recurrence: after tilting, the shape dynamics is autonomous and the normalizing scalar `A_n` disappears except through the exact cocycle (4.6).

## 5. Method B11 — dualize to moment recurrences and obtain a coercive Lyapunov function

Define the tilted second moment

$$
M_n:=\mathbf E_{\widehat\nu_n}[K^2]. \tag{5.1}
$$

It is finite under (2.1).

### Proposition 5.1 — exact second-moment recursion

For every `n`,

$$
\boxed{
M_{n+1}=\lambda_n M_n-\mu,
\qquad \mu=\frac1{m-1}.
} \tag{5.2}
$$

#### Proof

Under `rho_n`, let `S=K_1+...+K_m` with iid `K_i~widehat nu_n`. From (4.5),

$$
M_{n+1}=R_n\sum_{j\ge2}(j-1)^2\rho_n(j). \tag{5.3}
$$

Now

$$
\mathbf E S=m\mu,
\qquad
\mathbf E S^2=mM_n+m(m-1)\mu^2.
$$

Since `mu=1/(m-1)`, direct expansion gives

$$
\sum_{j\ge2}(j-1)^2\rho_n(j)
=mM_n-\mu-r_n^m. \tag{5.4}
$$

Also `R_n(\mu+r_n^m)=\mu`, because `R_n^{-1}=1+(m-1)r_n^m`. Substituting into (5.3) yields

$$
M_{n+1}=mR_nM_n-\mu=\lambda_nM_n-\mu.
$$

QED.

The key point is that (5.2) has a sign structure absent from the endpoint scalar equation.

### Theorem 5.2 — monotone tilted variance scale and exact coercivity

For every critical nonconstant trajectory satisfying (2.1),

$$
\boxed{M_{n+1}\ge M_n.} \tag{5.5}
$$

More exactly, define

$$
B_m(r):=\lambda(r)-1-(m-1)(1-r),
\qquad
\lambda(r):=\frac{m}{1+(m-1)r^m}. \tag{5.6}
$$

Then `B_m(r)>=0` on `[0,1]`, and

$$
\boxed{
M_{n+1}-M_n
=B_m(r_n)M_n
+(m-1)t_n^2\operatorname{Var}_{\widehat\nu_n}(K\mid K>0).
} \tag{5.7}
$$

#### Proof

First,

$$
\lambda(r)-1
=\frac{(m-1)(1-r^m)}{1+(m-1)r^m}. \tag{5.8}
$$

A direct subtraction gives

$$
B_m(r)
=
\frac{(m-1)r[1-mr^{m-1}+(m-1)r^m]}
{1+(m-1)r^m}. \tag{5.9}
$$

The bracket

$$
f(r):=1-mr^{m-1}+(m-1)r^m
$$

satisfies `f(1)=0` and

$$
f'(r)=m(m-1)r^{m-2}(r-1)\le0
$$

on `[0,1]`; hence `f(r)>=0`, proving `B_m(r)>=0`.

Next, because the tilted mean is fixed at `mu`, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
\mu^2
=\bigl(\mathbf E[K\mathbf1_{K>0}]\bigr)^2
\le t_nM_n. \tag{5.10}
$$

From (5.2),

$$
M_{n+1}-M_n
=[\lambda_n-1]M_n-\mu. \tag{5.11}
$$

Insert `(m-1)t_n` and use `(m-1)mu^2=mu`:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{n+1}-M_n
&=B_m(r_n)M_n+(m-1)(t_nM_n-\mu^2)\\
&=B_m(r_n)M_n
 +(m-1)t_n^2\operatorname{Var}(K\mid K>0),
\end{aligned}
$$

which is (5.7). Both terms are nonnegative. QED.

Equation (5.7) is the distributional coercivity that the preceding scalar fake profile did not encode. It says that growth of the tilted second moment has two independent nonnegative sources: a strictly nonlinear zero-mass term and the conditional positive-tail variance.

## 7. Method B21 — import a Tauberian/log-product argument and pin the reciprocal second-moment average

The source's Propositions 1 and 2 imply, under (2.1),

$$
\prod_{i=0}^{n}A_i^{m-1}=\Theta(n^2). \tag{7.1}
$$

Equation (6.1), (6.3), and boundedness of `A_n` therefore give

$$
\boxed{J_n=\Theta(n^2).} \tag{7.2}
$$

We next locate `M_n` on its natural linear scale.

### Lemma 7.1 — `M_n=Theta(n)`

Let

$$
u_n:=1-\mathbf E_{\widehat\nu_n}[m^{-K}]
=1-\frac1{A_n}
=\frac{\varepsilon_n}{1+\varepsilon_n}. \tag{7.3}
$$

For `K>0`,

$$
\frac{m-1}{m}\le 1-m^{-K}<1.
$$

Hence

$$
\frac{m-1}{m}t_n\le \nu_n\le t_n. \tag{7.4}
$$

By (3.1), `nu_n=Theta(1/n)`, so

$$
t_n=\Theta(n^{-1}). \tag{7.5}
$$

Cauchy (5.10) now yields `M_n>=c n`.

For the upper bound, Cauchy in the form

$$
M_n^2=(\mathbf E K^2)^2
\le (\mathbf E K)(\mathbf E K^3)
=\mu\,\mathbf E K^3 \tag{7.6}
$$

reduces matters to the third tilted moment. If `m>=3`, for every integer `k>=1`,

$$
k(k+1)((m-1)k-1)\ge(m-2)k^3, \tag{7.7}
$$

so `E K^3<=J_n/(m-2)=O(n^2)`. If `m=2`, the polynomial equals `k^3-k`, hence `E K^3=J_n+E K=J_n+1=O(n^2)`. Therefore `M_n=O(n)`. Together with the lower bound,

$$
\boxed{M_n=\Theta(n).} \tag{7.8}
$$

### Theorem 7.2 — sharp reciprocal harmonic law

Under the critical condition and (2.1),

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac1{M_j}
=(m-1)\log N+O(1).
} \tag{7.9}
$$

#### Proof

Let

$$
Z_n:=\frac{J_n}{M_n}. \tag{7.10}
$$

From (6.5),

$$
\frac{Z_{n+1}}{Z_n}
=\frac{\lambda_nM_n}{M_{n+1}}
=\frac{M_{n+1}+\mu}{M_{n+1}}
=1+\frac{\mu}{M_{n+1}}. \tag{7.11}
$$

By (7.2) and (7.8), `Z_n=Theta(n)`, and therefore

$$
\log Z_N=\log N+O(1). \tag{7.12}
$$

Iterating (7.11),

$$
\log Z_N-\log Z_0
=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log\left(1+\frac{\mu}{M_j}\right). \tag{7.13}
$$

Since `M_j>=c j`,

$$
\sum_{j\ge1}\frac1{M_j^2}<\infty. \tag{7.14}
$$

The elementary inequality

$$
0\le x-\log(1+x)\le\frac{x^2}{2},\qquad x\ge0,
$$

then shows that replacing each logarithm in (7.13) by `mu/M_j` changes the partial sums by a bounded amount. Combining with (7.12),

$$
\mu\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac1{M_j}=\log N+O(1).
$$

Because `mu=1/(m-1)`, this is (7.9). QED.

This is a new exact-scale constraint of a different kind from the source's harmonic law for `epsilon_n`: every genuine trajectory must carry a **monotone** tilted second moment on linear scale whose reciprocal has the universal logarithmic coefficient `m-1`.

### Corollary 7.3 — source-language second-derivative consequence

Criticality gives

$$
M_n
=\frac{m^2G_n''(m)+mG_n'(m)}{A_n}
=\frac{m^2G_n''(m)}{A_n}+\mu. \tag{7.15}
$$

Since `A_n=1+O(1/n)` and `M_n=Theta(n)`, we obtain

G_n''(m)=\Theta(n), \tag{7.16}
$$

and moreover

$$
\frac1{G_n''(m)}
=\frac{m^2}{M_n}+O(n^{-2}). \tag{7.17}
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac1{G_j''(m)}
=m^2(m-1)\log N+O(1).
} \tag{7.18}
$$

This gives a sharp universal harmonic coefficient for the reciprocal curvature of the critical generating function at the endpoint `s=m`.

## 8. Why this does not yet prove Conjecture 4(b)
