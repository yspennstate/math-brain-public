## 4. Method B6 — deform from the conjectured profile

The conjectured endpoint profile is

\[
\varepsilon_n\sim \frac{c}{n},\qquad c:=\frac{2}{m-1}. \tag{4.1}
\]

Define the harmonic number

\[
H_N:=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac1n
\]

and the logarithmic-scale defect

\[
D_N:=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{(n\varepsilon_n-c)^2}{n}. \tag{4.2}
\]

The key observation is that the first variation around `c/n` cancels in the summed endpoint equation. What remains is a **positive quadratic defect**.

### Theorem 4.1 — harmonic defect identity

Assume the critical condition and (3.5). Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{H_N}\sum_{n=1}^N n\pi_n
=
\frac{4}{(m-1)^2}
+
\frac{1}{2H_N}
\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{(n\varepsilon_n-\frac2{m-1})^2}{n}
+o(1).
} \tag{4.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\frac1{\log N}\sum_{n=1}^N n\pi_n
\ge \frac{4}{(m-1)^2}.
} \tag{4.4}
\]

Since `pi_n<=nu_n`, also

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\frac1{\log N}\sum_{n=1}^N n\mathbf P(X_n>0)
\ge \frac{4}{(m-1)^2}.
} \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the **conjectured survival constant appears rigorously as the sharp lower constant for the logarithmic harmonic average**, under the third tilted-moment hypothesis.

### Proof

Multiply (3.3) by `n` and sum from `1` to `N`:

\[
\sum_{n=1}^N n\pi_n
=
\sum_{n=1}^N nQ_m(\varepsilon_n)
-
\frac1{m-1}\sum_{n=1}^N n(\varepsilon_{n+1}-\varepsilon_n). \tag{4.6}
\]

The second sum telescopes exactly:

\[
\sum_{n=1}^N n(\varepsilon_{n+1}-\varepsilon_n)
=N\varepsilon_{N+1}-\sum_{n=1}^N\varepsilon_n. \tag{4.7}
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{n=1}^N n\pi_n
=
\sum_{n=1}^N nQ_m(\varepsilon_n)
+
\frac1{m-1}
\left(\sum_{n=1}^N\varepsilon_n-N\varepsilon_{N+1}\right). \tag{4.8}
\]

Because `m` is fixed and `epsilon_n=O(1/n)`, the polynomial (3.2) has the expansion

\[
Q_m(x)=\frac{x^2}{2}+O(x^3) \qquad (x\to0). \tag{4.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{n=1}^N nQ_m(\varepsilon_n)
=
\frac12\sum_{n=1}^N n\varepsilon_n^2+O(1), \tag{4.10}
\]

because

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}n\varepsilon_n^3=O\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}\right)<\infty. \tag{4.11}
\]

Set `x_n=n epsilon_n`. Then

\[
\sum_{n=1}^N n\varepsilon_n^2
=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{x_n^2}{n}. \tag{4.12}
\]

Expanding around `c=2/(m-1)`,

\[
\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{x_n^2}{n}
=
D_N+2c\sum_{n=1}^N\varepsilon_n-c^2H_N. \tag{4.13}
\]

By (3.6), `N epsilon_{N+1}=O(1)`. By (3.7),

\[
\frac1{H_N}\sum_{n=1}^N\varepsilon_n\to c, \tag{4.14}
\]

since `H_N~log N`. Insert (4.10) and (4.13) into (4.8), divide by `H_N`, and use (4.14):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{H_N}\sum_{n=1}^Nn\pi_n
&=\frac{D_N}{2H_N}
+c\frac{\sum_{n\le N}\varepsilon_n}{H_N}
-\frac{c^2}{2}
+\frac1{m-1}\frac{\sum_{n\le N}\varepsilon_n}{H_N}
+o(1)\\
&=\frac{D_N}{2H_N}
+c^2-\frac{c^2}{2}+\frac{c}{m-1}+o(1)\\
&=\frac{D_N}{2H_N}+\frac4{(m-1)^2}+o(1),
\end{aligned}
\]

because `c=2/(m-1)`. This proves (4.3). Since `D_N>=0`, (4.4) follows. Finally (3.4) implies (4.5). QED.

### Finite-`N` lower inequality

There is also a direct inequality before taking limits. Since every coefficient of `Q_m(x)-x^2/2` is nonnegative for `x>=0`,

\[
Q_m(x)\ge \frac{x^2}{2}. \tag{4.15}
\]

Thus (4.8) gives

\[
\sum_{n=1}^Nn\pi_n
\ge
\frac12\sum_{n=1}^Nn\varepsilon_n^2
+
\frac1{m-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^N\varepsilon_n-N\varepsilon_{N+1}\right). \tag{4.16}
\]

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz yields

\[
\sum_{n=1}^Nn\varepsilon_n^2
=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{(n\varepsilon_n)^2}{n}
\ge
\frac{\left(\sum_{n=1}^N\varepsilon_n\right)^2}{H_N}. \tag{4.17}
\]

Equations (4.16)–(4.17), together with (3.6)–(3.7), already imply the sharp averaged lower bound (4.4). The defect identity (4.3) records exactly how much is lost by Cauchy–Schwarz.

## 5. Method B13 — a monotone defect and an equivalence at harmonic scale

By definition,

\[
D_{N+1}-D_N=rac{((N+1)\varepsilon_{N+1}-c)^2}{N+1}\ge0. \tag{5.1}
\]

So `D_N` is a genuine monovariant: it measures accumulated failure of the endpoint sequence to follow the conjectured `c/n` profile on logarithmic scales.

Theorem 4.1 gives the exact asymptotic equivalence

\[
\frac1{H_N}\sum_{n=1}^Nn\pi_n
\longrightarrow \frac4{(m-1)^2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{D_N}{H_N}\longrightarrow0. \tag{5.2}
\]

The reverse direction is immediate from (4.3); the forward direction is the same identity because the error is `o(1)`.

If `D_N/H_N->0`, then for every fixed `delta>0`, weighted Chebyshev gives

\[
\frac1{H_N}
\sum_{\substack{n\le N:\\|n\varepsilon_n-c|\ge\delta}}
\frac1n
\le
\frac{D_N}{\delta^2H_N}\longrightarrow0. \tag{5.3}
\]

Thus exact harmonic survival asymptotics would force

\[
n\varepsilon_n\to\frac2{m-1}
\]

in **logarithmic density**, even before one proves ordinary pointwise convergence. This is a precise intermediate milestone between the source's logarithmic Cesaro information and Conjecture 4(b).

The deformation picture is therefore second-order: after the source fixes the weighted mean of `n epsilon_n`, the only surviving leading-order obstruction is the nonnegative quadratic energy `D_N`.

## 6. Method B30 — localize the gluing failure across logarithmic scales

The previous run asked whether the scalar endpoint dynamics itself might force eventual pointwise regularity. The answer is no: the endpoint recurrence plus the known `Theta(1/n)` and logarithmic-Cesaro facts admit persistent log-periodic oscillations.

### Proposition 6.1 — abstract scalar oscillatory obstruction

Fix

\[
c=\frac2{m-1}
\]

and choose a sufficiently small fixed `a>0`. For all sufficiently large `n`, define

\[
\widetilde\varepsilon_n
:=\frac{c+a\sin(\log n)}{n}. \tag{6.1}
\]

Then:

1. `tilde epsilon_n>0` and `tilde epsilon_n=Theta(1/n)`;
2. 
   \[
   \sum_{n\le N}\widetilde\varepsilon_n=c\log N+O(1); \tag{6.2}
   \]
3. if
   \[
   \widetilde\pi_n
   :=Q_m(\widetilde\varepsilon_n)
   -\frac{\widetilde\varepsilon_{n+1}-\widetilde\varepsilon_n}{m-1}, \tag{6.3}
   \]
   then `tilde pi_n>0` and `tilde pi_n<1/m` for all sufficiently large `n`, and the exact endpoint recurrence (3.1) holds identically with tildes;
4. nevertheless
   \[
   n\widetilde\varepsilon_n=c+a\sin(\log n) \tag{6.4}
   \]
   does not converge.

Hence **no proof using only** the scalar endpoint recurrence, positivity of `pi_n`, `epsilon_n=Theta(1/n)`, and the source's logarithmic-Cesaro information can establish Conjecture 4(b). Some genuinely distributional input is necessary.

### Proof

Take `0<a<c/2`. Then (1) is immediate. For (2),

\[
\sum_{n\le N}\frac{\sin(\log n)}n=O(1), \tag{6.5}
\]

because the integral is bounded,

\[
\int_1^N\frac{\sin(\log x)}x\,dx
=1-\cos(\log N), \tag{6.6}
\]

and the sum-minus-integral error is bounded since the derivative of `sin(log x)/x` is `O(x^{-2})`. Therefore (6.2) holds.

Let

\[
b(t):=c+a\sin t.
\]

For `t=log n`, Taylor expansion of the smooth function `b(log x)/x` gives

\[
\widetilde\varepsilon_{n+1}-\widetilde\varepsilon_n
=\frac{a\cos t-b(t)}{n^2}+O(n^{-3}), \tag{6.7}
\]

uniformly in `t`. Also

\[
Q_m(\widetilde\varepsilon_n)
=\frac{b(t)^2}{2n^2}+O(n^{-3}). \tag{6.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
n^2\widetilde\pi_n
=
\frac12 b(t)^2
+
\frac{b(t)-a\cos t}{m-1}
+o(1), \tag{6.9}
\]

uniformly in `t`.

At `a=0`, the right side is the positive constant

\[
\frac12c^2+\frac{c}{m-1}
=\frac4{(m-1)^2}. \tag{6.10}
\]

By uniform continuity on the compact circle, all sufficiently small fixed `a>0` preserve a strictly positive lower bound in (6.9). Hence `tilde pi_n>0` eventually. Equation (6.9) also gives `tilde pi_n=O(n^{-2})`, hence `tilde pi_n<1/m` eventually. By its definition (6.3), the endpoint recurrence holds exactly. But (6.4) oscillates forever, proving (4).

For eventual `0<tilde pi_n<1/m`, one may even define

\[
\widetilde z_n:=(1-m\widetilde\pi_n)^{1/m}\in(0,1),
\]

so the scalar pair `G_n(m)=1+tilde epsilon_n`, `G_n(0)=tilde z_n` satisfies the **exact `s=m` pgf update algebraically**. This does **not** assert the existence of a probability distribution whose full pgf has those two values at every `n`, much less a genuine Derrida–Retaux trajectory. Proposition 6.1 is an obstruction to scalar-only proofs, not a counterexample to the source conjecture. QED.
