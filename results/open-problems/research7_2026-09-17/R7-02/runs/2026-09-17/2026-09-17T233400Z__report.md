## 3. B6 — full distinct-prime product theorem, with complete proof

### Theorem

Let `p,q` be primes. Let `A,B subset Z` be complete residue systems modulo `p,q`, respectively, with

`gcd(A-A)=gcd(B-B)=1`.

If `T` tiles `Z^2` by `A x B`, then

`(p,0)+T=T` or `(0,q)+T=T`.

The primes need not be equal.

### Proof

For row fibres and column fibres write

`S_y={x:(x,y) in T}`, `T_x={y:(x,y) in T}`.

Khetan Lemma 3.6, which is stated for arbitrary finite factors `A,B`, gives for all `n,m in Z`

`R_n=disjoint_union_{b in B} S_{n-b}`

as an `A`-tiling of `Z`, and

`C_m=disjoint_union_{a in A} T_{m-a}`

as a `B`-tiling of `Z`.

By Khetan Lemma 3.3 / Corollary 3.4 and the gcd assumptions,

`R_n=pZ+r_n`, `C_m=qZ+s_m`

for residue sequences `r_n in Z/pZ` and `s_m in Z/qZ`.

If `s_m` is constant, say `s_m=s`, every anchor row has residue `s mod q`: for `(x,y) in T` and every `a in A`, `T_x subset C_{x+a}=qZ+s`. Fix a nonempty row `S_y` and `b in B`, and set `n=y+b`. In the decomposition of `R_n`, a nonempty summand `S_{n-b'}` must have `n-b'=s mod q`. Since `B` is a complete residue system modulo `q` and the chosen `b` already satisfies that congruence, it is the unique such `b'`. Therefore `S_y=R_n=pZ+r_n`. Every nonempty row is `p`-periodic, hence `(p,0)` is a period of `T`.

Suppose instead that `s` is nonconstant and put `I=Image(s) subset Z/qZ`; then `|I|>=2`. A row `y` is nonempty exactly when `y mod q in I`. One implication follows from `T_x subset C_{x+a}`; conversely if `y mod q=s_m`, then `y in C_m`, so one of the column fibres in its disjoint-union decomposition contains `y`.

For such a nonempty row and any `b,c in B`,

`S_y subset R_{y+b}=pZ+r_{y+b}` and `S_y subset R_{y+c}=pZ+r_{y+c}`.

Since `S_y` is nonempty, the two cosets coincide, so

`r_{y+b}=r_{y+c}`

for all `b,c in B` whenever `y mod q in I`. Apply Khetan Lemma 3.7 with prime `q`, tile `B`, residue set `I`, and the map `r:Z -> X` with the **arbitrary codomain** `X=Z/pZ`. Its hypotheses are now exact, hence `r_n` is constant, say `r_n=r`.

Every anchor column then has residue `r mod p`: if `(x,y) in T`, then `x in S_y subset R_{y+b}=pZ+r`. Fix a nonempty column `T_x`, choose `a in A`, and set `m=x+a`. In

`C_m=disjoint_union_{a' in A} T_{m-a'}`,

any nonempty summand has column index `r mod p`. Since `A` is a complete residue system modulo `p`, exactly one `a'` has `m-a'=r mod p`, namely the chosen `a`. Therefore `T_x=C_m=qZ+s_m`. Every nonempty column is `q`-periodic, so `(0,q)` is a period of `T`. QED.

## 4. B14 — exact GL_2(Z) / full-affine-span normalization

The two-rich branch has the form

`F=x_0+{a u+b v:a in A,b in B}`

for independent primitive `u,v in Z^2`. Let `d_A=gcd(A-A)` and `d_B=gcd(B-B)`. Directly from differences,

`<F-F>=d_A Z u+d_B Z v`.

Therefore

`[Z^2:<F-F>] = |det(u,v)| d_A d_B`.

Full affine span makes this index one. Since every factor on the right is a positive integer,

`|det(u,v)|=d_A=d_B=1`.

Thus the coordinate change is genuinely unimodular and both gcd hypotheses are forced by the source's affine-span assumption; they were not silently added. This also matches the index mechanism in Khetan's product-structure lemma.

## 5. B22 — the precise mixed-prime obstruction and why the six-cell proof bypasses it

The source's Lemma 3.15 is genuinely prime-power. When `|F|=p^2`, the dilation equations are available for every `alpha` coprime to `p`. On a rational kernel circle with parameter `r=c/d`, the proof writes `p^m` for the full `p`-power dividing `d` and chooses

`beta=d/p^m`.

This `beta` is coprime to `p`, so it is an allowed dilation. The resulting root-of-unity equation gives `p`-divisible sections.

At `|F|=6`, however, the dilation theorem supplies only `alpha` coprime to **both** `2` and `3`. For a prospective `p=2` argument, `d/2^{v_2(d)}` can be divisible by `3`; for a prospective `p=3` argument, `d/3^{v_3(d)}` can be even. Hence the crucial `beta` need not be among the available dilation indices. The prime-square divisibility lemma cannot simply be imported into the six-cell case.

The R7-02 rich-direction lemma avoids exactly this obstruction by using only `alpha=1`. Infinitely many roots then make the one-variable Laurent polynomial identically zero and force one common phase sum to vanish on every parallel section. This gives the weaker conclusion “no nonempty singleton section.” For a six-point set and two transverse rich directions, that is already enough: the simple bipartite section-incidence graph has six edges and minimum degree two, hence is `K_{2,3}` or `C_6`. The phase equations rule out `C_6`, leaving the product case handled above.

This identifies the source's arithmetic obstruction and the mechanism by which the six-cell proof goes around it, rather than claiming the prime-power lemma extends unchanged.
