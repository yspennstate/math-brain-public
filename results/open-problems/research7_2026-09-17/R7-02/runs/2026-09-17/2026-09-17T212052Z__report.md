## 3. Method B2 — attack the negation: a six-cell counterexample needs two transverse rich spectral directions

Let `X=cl(Z^2·T)` and choose an ergodic invariant probability measure `mu` on `X`. Let `nu` be the spectral measure of the origin cylinder as in Khetan. Since `|F|=6`, the dilation lemma gives, for `nu`-almost every `xi != 0`,

`sum_{a in F} chi_{alpha a}(xi)=0`

for every integer `alpha` with `gcd(alpha,6)=1`.

Take a minimal finite set `Delta` with

`supp(nu) subset union_{g in Delta} ker chi_g`.

Call `g in Delta` **rich** if infinitely many points of `ker chi_g` satisfy all of the dilation-zero equations.

The “at most one rich direction” half of Khetan's prime-square proof is actually cardinality-free. Replacing the index condition `gcd(alpha,p)=1` there by `gcd(alpha,6)=1`, the same argument gives

`supp(nu) subset ker chi_{g0} union S`

with `S` finite. Minimality of `S` makes each residual atom have irrational `chi_{g0}`-value; Khetan Lemma 3.1 then forces `g0·f=f`, and Theorem 3.11 yields a 1-periodic tiling in the orbit closure. No primality is used in this branch after the dilation equations are available.

Therefore a counterexample can only survive if there are at least two transverse rich directions. The rest of this run closes that branch as well.

### B2 outcome

The negation is reduced to the two-rich-direction case, and that case is eliminated below. This is source-scale movement rather than a finite sample obstruction.

## 4. Method B18 — translate rich directions into root-of-unity section equations

Let `g=nh` be rich with `h` primitive. The first part of Khetan's Lemma 3.15 proof does not need prime cardinality. The kernel `ker chi_g` is a finite union of circles

`{r h + t h^perp : t in R} mod Z^2`

with rational `r=c/d`. Infinitely many common zeros lie on one such circle. Use only `alpha=1`, which is allowed because `1` is coprime to `6`. The Laurent polynomial in `z=e^{2 pi i t}`

`sum_{a in F} exp(2 pi i r <a,h>) z^{<a,h^perp>}`

has infinitely many unit-circle zeros and is therefore identically zero. Grouping coefficients by `<a,h^perp>` gives, for every section `E` of `F` by a line parallel to `g`,

`sum_{a in E} zeta_g^{<a,h>} = 0`,

where `zeta_g=exp(2 pi i c/d)` is common to all parallel sections.

In particular **no nonempty rich-direction section can be a singleton**.

Now take two transverse rich directions. Form the bipartite incidence graph whose left vertices are sections in the first direction, right vertices sections in the second direction, and whose six edges are the six points of `F`. The graph is simple because two nonparallel lines meet in at most one point, and every vertex has degree at least two.

With exactly six edges, each side has at most three vertices. A degree-four vertex would require at least four vertices on the other side, impossible. Hence the only possibilities are:

1. `K_{2,3}` (or its transpose), with section-size patterns `(3,3)` and `(2,2,2)`;
2. a `3 x 3` degree-two incidence graph, necessarily a single `C6`.

The exact finite enumeration in `paper_khetan_ec6/check_incidence.py` was executed and gave exactly eight labelled matrices: one `K_{2,3}`, one transpose, and six `3 x 3` complements of permutation matrices. The output is archived in `incidence_check.txt`.

### Excluding C6 exactly

Label the three rows `R_1,R_2,R_3` and columns `C_1,C_2,C_3` so that row `R_i` misses column `C_i`. For the first rich direction, affine product coordinates make the common section phase factor as

`zeta^{<R_i cap C_j,h>} = lambda_i z_j`.

The nonzero row factor `lambda_i` cancels. The three two-point vanishing equations become

`z_2+z_3=0`,
`z_1+z_3=0`,
`z_1+z_2=0`.

The first two imply `z_1=z_2`; the third then gives `2 z_1=0`, impossible because `z_1` is a unit complex number. Thus **C6 cannot occur**.

Hence the two-rich-direction incidence is exactly `K_{2,3}`.

## 5. Method B30 — localize the surviving K2,3 geometry and close the gluing step

Let `u,v` be primitive integer vectors in the two rich directions. Since every one of the `2 x 3` line intersections is occupied, after translating one point to the origin,

`F = {a u + b v : a in A, b in B}`

with `|A|=3`, `|B|=2` (or swapped) and `A,B subset Z`.

Full affine span gives exactly

`<F-F> = gcd(A-A) Z u + gcd(B-B) Z v = Z^2`.

Therefore

`|det(u,v)|=1`, `gcd(A-A)=gcd(B-B)=1`.

So these are genuine unimodular product coordinates.

For the three-point factor, the common rich-section phase gives a unit `z` with

`z^{a_1}+z^{a_2}+z^{a_3}=0`.

Three unit complex numbers sum to zero only as an equilateral triple. Hence all ratios are cube roots of unity and `z^{3(a_i-a_j)}=1` for all `i,j`. Since `gcd(A-A)=1`, Bezout gives `z^3=1`; the zero sum excludes `z=1`, so `z` is a primitive cube root. The three exponents are distinct modulo `3`, hence **A is a complete residue system modulo 3**.

For the two-point factor, `gcd(B-B)=1` forces its two elements to differ by `1`, so **B is a complete residue system modulo 2**.

The remaining apparent blocker was that Khetan Proposition 3.9 is written for a product of two complete residue systems of the **same** prime size. The proof extends to distinct primes.

### Distinct-prime product-periodicity proposition

Let `p,q` be primes. Let `A` be a complete residue system modulo `p`, `B` a complete residue system modulo `q`, with `gcd(A-A)=gcd(B-B)=1`. Then every `(A x B)`-tiling `T` of `Z^2` has period `(p,0)` or `(0,q)`.

Proof. With row fibres `S_y` and column fibres `T_x`, define as in Khetan

`R_n = disjoint_union_{b in B} S_{n-b}`,
`C_m = disjoint_union_{a in A} T_{m-a}`.

Then `R_n` is an `A`-tiling of `Z` and `C_m` a `B`-tiling. The prime one-dimensional theorem gives

`R_n=pZ+r_n`, `C_m=qZ+s_m`.

If `r` is constant, translate so every `R_n=pZ`. Then all anchors have first coordinate in `pZ`. Put `W=(y^q-1)T`. The tiling identity gives `A(x)W=0`. Because `W` is supported on `pZ x Z` and `A` is a complete residue system modulo `p`, all exponents `a+pk` are distinct; coefficient comparison forces `W=0`, hence `(0,q)` is a period. The constant-`s` case symmetrically gives period `(p,0)`.

If `s` is nonconstant, set `I=Image(s) subset Z/qZ`; then `|I|>=2`. A row `y` is nonempty iff `y mod q in I`. For every nonempty row and every `b,c in B`,

`S_y subset R_{y+b}=pZ+r_{y+b}` and `S_y subset R_{y+c}=pZ+r_{y+c}`,

so the nonempty intersection forces

`r_{y+b}=r_{y+c}`.

Khetan Lemma 3.7 applies with prime `q`, tile `B`, set `I`, and arbitrary codomain `X=Z/pZ`; it forces `r` to be constant. The previous paragraph gives period `(0,q)`. QED.

Applying this with `(p,q)=(3,2)` (or swapped) shows that **every tiling in the two-rich-direction case is itself 1-periodic**.

### B30 outcome

The exact gluing obstruction disappears: the only possible two-rich six-point geometry is a distinct-prime `3 x 2` product, and the product-periodicity proof works for distinct primes because Khetan Lemma 3.7 allows an arbitrary codomain.
