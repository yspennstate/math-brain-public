## 3. Method B25 — direct double-count/fibre proof for the actual `3 x 2` product

The two-rich-direction work from the preceding run reduced the surviving geometry to a unimodular product

`F=x_0+{a u+b v : a in A, b in B}`

where `|A|=3`, `|B|=2`, `gcd(A-A)=gcd(B-B)=1`, the common rich phase makes `A` a complete residue system modulo `3`, and `B` is two consecutive integers, hence a complete residue system modulo `2`.

The only product theorem actually needed is therefore the following.

### Proposition

If `A` is a complete residue system modulo `3`, `B={b_0,b_1}` with `|b_1-b_0|=1`, and `T` tiles `Z^2` by `A x B`, then either `(3,0)` or `(0,2)` is a period of `T`.

### Proof

For row fibres and column fibres put

`S_y={x:(x,y) in T}` and `T_x={y:(x,y) in T}`.

For every `n,m in Z`, unique tiling gives the disjoint unions

`R_n = disjoint_union_{b in B} S_{n-b}`,
`C_m = disjoint_union_{a in A} T_{m-a}`.

Thus `R_n` is an `A`-tiling of `Z`, while `C_m` is a `B`-tiling of `Z`.

By the prime one-dimensional tiling theorem imported by Khetan,

`R_n=3Z+r_n`, with `r_n in Z/3Z`.

For `B` no theorem is needed. After translation it is `{0,1}`, and the one-dimensional tiling equation is

`1_C(k)+1_C(k-1)=1`.

Hence every `B`-tiling is a parity class and

`C_m=2Z+s_m`, with `s_m in Z/2Z`.

There are two cases.

**Case 1: `s_m` is constant.** Say `s_m=s`. Every anchor `(x,y)` satisfies `y in C_{x+a}=2Z+s` for every `a in A`, so every nonempty row has `y=s mod 2`. Fix such a row and choose `b in B`; with `n=y+b`, among the two summands of `R_n`, only `S_y` can be nonempty because the two choices of `b` give opposite row parity. Therefore

`S_y=R_n=3Z+r_n`.

Every nonempty row is `3`-periodic, so `(3,0)` is a period of `T`.

**Case 2: `s_m` is nonconstant.** Its image is both parity classes. Therefore every row is nonempty: given `y`, choose `m` with `s_m=y mod 2`; then `y in C_m`, so one of the column fibres in its disjoint-union representation contains `y`.

For every `y`, the nonempty set `S_y` lies in both

`R_{y+b_0}=3Z+r_{y+b_0}` and `R_{y+b_1}=3Z+r_{y+b_1}`.

The two cosets must coincide. Since `|b_1-b_0|=1`, this forces `r_n` to be constant, say `r_n=r`.

Then every anchor column is `r mod 3`. Fix a nonempty column `T_x` and choose `a in A`; put `m=x+a`. In

`C_m = disjoint_union_{a' in A} T_{m-a'}`,

only one summand can be nonempty, because `A` has exactly one representative of each residue modulo `3` and all nonempty columns are `r mod 3`. That unique summand is `T_x`. Hence

`T_x=C_m=2Z+s_m`.

Every nonempty column is `2`-periodic, so `(0,2)` is a period of `T`.

This proves the proposition. QED.

### B25 outcome

The exact two-rich six-cell branch is now closed **without** the previous distinct-prime formal-series generalization. The repaired argument needs only the source's prime one-dimensional tiling input for the three-point factor and the elementary two-point alternating equation.

## 4. Method B11 — spectral dualization of the at-most-one-rich branch

The other delicate part of the first draft was phrased as “Khetan's at-most-one-rich argument is cardinality-free.” Rather than relying on that prose transfer, I reduced it directly to a finite set of one-dimensional spectral eigenvalues.

Let `f` be the `{0,1}`-valued origin-cylinder indicator on an ergodic invariant measure supported by the tiling orbit closure. For every `alpha` coprime to `6`, let

`P_alpha(xi)=sum_{a in F} chi_{alpha a}(xi)`.

The dilation tiling equations imply that outside the trivial character the spectral measure of `f` lies in every zero set `Z(P_alpha)`. Write

`Z_* = intersection_{gcd(alpha,6)=1} Z(P_alpha)`.

Take the finite character-kernel cover `Delta` furnished by the spectral setup used in Khetan. If at most one `g_0 in Delta` is rich, then for every other `h` the set

`ker chi_h intersect Z_*`

is finite. Hence

`supp(nu) subset ker chi_{g_0} union S`

for finite `S`.

Now absorb every torsion eigenvalue of translation by `g_0`: if `chi_{g_0}(s)` is a root of unity for some `s in S`, let `K` be a common multiple of its order and replace `g_0` by `g=K g_0`. Those atoms move into `ker chi_g`. What remains is a finite set of spectral atoms whose `g`-eigenvalues are non-roots-of-unity. Grouping equal eigenvalues gives

`f=f_0+phi_1+...+phi_m`,

with

`U_g f_0=f_0`,
`U_g phi_j=gamma_j phi_j`,

where the `gamma_j` are distinct non-roots-of-unity.

This converts the branch into the finite-state recurrence statement proved next.

### B11 outcome

The spectral branch is reduced to a finite-dimensional, one-translation problem. All torsion spectral atoms are absorbed into an enlarged kernel; the only possible obstruction consists of finitely many distinct non-torsion eigenvalues.

## 5. Method B19 — finite-alphabet recurrence kills the non-torsion atoms

### Lemma

Let an invertible measure-preserving `Z`-action have a finite-valued function

`f=f_0+sum_{j=1}^m phi_j`

with `Uf_0=f_0` and `U phi_j=gamma_j phi_j`, where the `gamma_j` are pairwise distinct and no `gamma_j` is a root of unity. Then every `phi_j=0` almost surely, hence `Uf=f`.

### Proof

After discarding a countable union of null sets, all translated eigenfunction identities hold pointwise for every integer. Fix such an `x` and set

`a_n=f(U^n x)=f_0(x)+sum_j gamma_j^n phi_j(x)`.

This sequence takes values in a finite alphabet. It satisfies the order `m+1` recurrence with characteristic polynomial

`Q(z)=(z-1) product_j (z-gamma_j)`.

The leading and constant coefficients of `Q` are nonzero, so any actual block

`(a_n,...,a_{n+m})`

determines uniquely both its next and previous entry. Since only finitely many such blocks are possible, two blocks repeat. Forward and backward recurrence determinism therefore makes the whole bi-infinite sequence periodic: for some `L>0`,

`a_{n+L}=a_n` for all `n`.

Subtracting the spectral formulas gives

`sum_j phi_j(x)(gamma_j^L-1) gamma_j^n=0`.

For `n=0,...,m-1`, this is a Vandermonde system. Distinctness of the `gamma_j` makes the matrix invertible, so

`phi_j(x)(gamma_j^L-1)=0` for every `j`.

Because no `gamma_j` is a root of unity, `gamma_j^L != 1`; hence every `phi_j(x)=0`. QED.

Applying the lemma gives `U_g f=f` almost surely. Every translated coordinate indicator `U_v f` is also `g`-invariant because the `Z^2` translations commute. Intersecting the full-measure sets over countably many `v in Z^2`, almost every configuration in the invariant measure satisfies

`x(v+g)=x(v)` for every `v`.

Thus the orbit closure contains a one-periodic `F`-tiling.

### B19 outcome

The at-most-one-rich branch now has an explicit self-contained final mechanism; it does not need to invoke the previous draft's prose-level transfer through Khetan's irrational-phase lemma and periodic-cylinder theorem.

## 6. C6 phase factorization stress check

I also rewrote the potentially ambiguous `zeta^{v_j}` notation in the six-cycle exclusion. On a rich component of `ker chi_{nh}`, write

`xi=rho h+t h^perp`

with rational `rho`. The section phase is literally

`exp(2 pi i rho <a,h>)`.

For the intersections of two transverse parallel-line families, affine product coordinates give

`x_{ij}=x_*+alpha_j h+beta_i v`,

so

`exp(2 pi i rho <x_{ij},h>)=lambda_i z_j`.

In a `C6`, row `i` misses column `i`; after cancelling `lambda_i != 0`, the three row equations are

`z_2+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_2=0`,

which are inconsistent for nonzero phases. Thus the earlier `C6` exclusion remains valid after replacing the shorthand by a literal exponential factorization.
