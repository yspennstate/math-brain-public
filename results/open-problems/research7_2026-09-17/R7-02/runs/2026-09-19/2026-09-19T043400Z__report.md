## 3. Main theorem — sharp degree-three bound

### Theorem
For every Boolean function `f:{-1,1}^n->{-1,1}` of Fourier degree at most three,

`sum_i |fhat({i})| <= 3/2`.

Three-bit majority attains equality. Therefore

`sum_i fhat({i}) <= 3/2 < sqrt(3)`,

which proves the full all-`n` weak source inequality at degree three and also establishes the source's proposed majority value for `d=3`.

### 3.1 B11 — coefficient lattice

Put `g=(1-f)/2`, so `g` is `{0,1}`-valued, and let `z_i=(1-x_i)/2`. Because coordinatewise affine substitution preserves multilinear degree,

`g(z)=sum_{T, |T|<=d} a_T prod_{i in T} z_i`.

Möbius inversion gives

`a_T=sum_{U subseteq T} (-1)^{|T|-|U|} g(1_U)`,

so every `a_T` is an integer. Substituting `z_i=(1-x_i)/2` shows every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of `g` belongs to `2^{-d} Z`; because `f=1-2g`, every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of `f` belongs to

`2^{1-d} Z`.                                                    (3.1)

At `d=3`, every nonzero singleton coefficient has magnitude at least `1/4`.

Let `A={i:fhat({i})!=0}`, `m=|A|`, and flip coordinate signs so every active singleton coefficient is positive. Write

`B=sum_i |fhat({i})|`.

Parseval plus (3.1) gives

`1 >= sum_{i in A} fhat({i})^2 >= m/16`,

hence

`m<=16`.                                                        (3.2)

Now restrict every inactive coordinate. If `f_y` is the restriction corresponding to assignment `y`, then for `i in A`

`E_y [ f_yhat({i}) ] = fhat({i})`.

Thus for

`L(y)=sum_{i in A} f_yhat({i})`

we have `E_y L(y)=B`; some restriction `h=f_{y_*}` therefore satisfies

`L(h)>=B`.                                                      (3.3)

This reduces arbitrary original dimension `n` to at most sixteen coordinates without assuming that the original function is a junta.

**B11 outcome:** the originally contemplated `f^2=1` convolution equations were not needed. The `{0,1}` Möbius dual representation yields the exact granularity needed for a finite universal reduction.

### 3.2 B14 — permutation quotient

For the Boolean degree-three restriction `h` on `m` coordinates, average over coordinate permutations. The symmetric average preserves the signed singleton sum.

Let layer `k` consist of cube points with exactly `k` minus signs. Define

`C_k=binom(m,k)`,

`a_k=sum_{x in layer k}h(x)`,

`g_k=a_k/C_k`.

Booleanity gives the exact lattice condition

`a_k in {-C_k,-C_k+2,...,C_k}`.                                (3.4)

A symmetric Fourier polynomial of degree at most three is a linear combination of the first four Krawtchouk polynomials. Hence `g_k` is an ordinary polynomial in `k` of degree at most three, and so

`g_{k+4}-4g_{k+3}+6g_{k+2}-4g_{k+1}+g_k=0`.                   (3.5)

The signed singleton objective is exactly

`L(h)=E[h(X) sum_i X_i]`

`    =2^{-m} sum_{k=0}^m C_k g_k (m-2k)`.                     (3.6)

Therefore every actual Boolean degree-three function maps into an exact finite lattice of cubic layer sequences.

**B14 outcome:** the all-`n` problem is compressed to a finite exact necessary-condition problem for each `m<=16`.
