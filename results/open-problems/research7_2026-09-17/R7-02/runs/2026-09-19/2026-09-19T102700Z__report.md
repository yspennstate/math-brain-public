## 3. Exact blocking formula: minimal-counterexample restriction deficits

Assume for diagnosis that the degree-five weak inequality is false. Choose a counterexample with the **minimum number `n` of relevant coordinates**, then flip coordinates so every singleton coefficient is nonnegative. Write

`a_i = fhat({i}) >= 0`, `L = sum_i a_i > sqrt(5)`.

Degree-five Boolean granularity gives every nonconstant Fourier coefficient in `(1/16) Z`. Put

`S = 16 L`, `s_i = 16 a_i`.

Then `S` is an integer and a counterexample has

`S >= 36`,

because `35/16 < sqrt(5) < 36/16`.

### 3.1 Every variable of a minimal counterexample has positive singleton coefficient

Suppose a relevant coordinate `i` had `a_i=0`. For the two restrictions `f_i^+` and `f_i^-`, the inherited signed singleton sums on the remaining coordinates average to `L-a_i=L`. Hence one restriction has signed singleton sum at least `L>sqrt(5)`. Reorienting its remaining coordinates can only increase that signed sum to its singleton `l1` mass, producing a degree-five counterexample with fewer relevant variables. This contradicts minimality.

Therefore every relevant coordinate is singleton-active:

`n = # {i : a_i>0}` and every `s_i` is a positive integer.

The complete twelve-variable theorem from the preceding run gives `n>=13`.

### 3.2 One-coordinate restriction formula

Let

`b_ij = fhat({i,j})`,

`t_i = 16 sum_{j != i} b_ij`,

so `t_i` is an integer. Restricting `x_i=sigma` gives inherited signed singleton sum

`L_i^sigma = L-a_i + sigma sum_{j != i} b_ij`

and therefore

`16 L_i^sigma = S-s_i + sigma t_i`.                         (3.1)

By minimality, neither restriction is a counterexample. Its singleton `l1` mass is at most `sqrt(5)`, hence its inherited signed sum is at most `sqrt(5)`. That signed sum is itself a multiple of `1/16`, so in fact

`S-s_i + t_i <= 35`,

`S-s_i - t_i <= 35`.                                       (3.2)

Equivalently,

`boxed: s_i >= S-35 + |t_i|`.                               (3.3)

Summing (3.3) over all relevant coordinates gives the exact diagnostic blocker

`boxed: sum_i |t_i| <= S - n(S-35)`.                        (3.4)

In particular `S >= n(S-35)`. Since `n>=13`, `S>=38` is impossible. Thus every minimal degree-five counterexample is forced into only two lattice masses:

`boxed: S in {36,37}`.                                      (3.5)

More precisely,

- if `S=37`, then `13 <= n <= 18` and `sum_i |t_i| <= 37-2n`;
- if `S=36`, then `13 <= n <= 36` and `sum_i |t_i| <= 36-n`.

So the all-dimensional source obstruction is already finite in the following precise sense: a counterexample would have a minimal representative on at most 36 relevant variables, with singleton mass exactly `36/16` or `37/16`.

This is a STUCK-diagnosis reduction, not a claim that `d=5` is solved; the frozen metric remains `D_weak=4`.

### 3.3 Full deficit-polynomial form

The one-coordinate inequalities are the first member of a larger exact family. Let `T` be any nonempty set of coordinates and fix `x_i=sigma_i` for `i in T`. Define the inherited signed singleton sum on the unfixed variables, in units of `1/16`, by

`G_T(sigma) = 16 sum_{j notin T} fhat_{T=sigma}({j})`.

Expanding restriction Fourier coefficients gives

`G_T(sigma) = sum_{A subseteq T} R_{T,A} chi_A(sigma)`,

where

`R_{T,A} = 16 sum_{j notin T} fhat(A union {j})`.            (3.6)

Because every nontrivial restriction has fewer relevant variables than the chosen minimal counterexample, it is not a counterexample. Hence

`G_T(sigma) <= 35` for every sigma.                          (3.7)

Define the integer-valued deficit polynomial

`D_T(sigma)=35-G_T(sigma) >= 0`.

Its mean is

`delta_T := E D_T = sum_{i in T} s_i - (S-35)`.             (3.8)

For every nonempty `A subseteq T`, nonnegativity alone gives

`|R_{T,A}| = |E[D_T chi_A]| <= E D_T = delta_T`.            (3.9)

Equation (3.9) is the clean form of the missing structural constraint: **every row-sum of every higher Fourier layer visible through a restriction is bounded by the tiny singleton deficit of that restriction.** For `T={i}`, it is exactly (3.3).
