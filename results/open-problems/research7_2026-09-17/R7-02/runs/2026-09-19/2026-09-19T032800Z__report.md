## 5. B2 — attack the negation first

The first attempted counterexample mechanism was to make many positive linear Fourier coefficients live on several weakly interacting quadratic components. Write a degree-at-most-two Boolean function as

`f(x)=a + sum_i b_i x_i + sum_{i<j} c_ij x_i x_j`.

For each coordinate,

`D_i f(x_{\ne i}) := (f(x_i=1)-f(x_i=-1))/2

Since `f` is Boolean, every derivative value lies in `{-1,0,1}`. Thus each row of the quadratic coefficient matrix is an affine map of a cube into only three points. This kills the natural multi-component counterexample route and leads to a complete degree-two classification.

## 6. B16 — complete all-n degree-two theorem

### Theorem

If `f:{-1,1}^n->{-1,1}` has Fourier degree at most two, then

`sum_i |\hat f({i})| <= 1`.

In particular,

`sum_i \hat f({i}) <= 1 <= sqrt(2)`.

This is stronger than the weak P0169 inequality at degree two.

### Lemma: affine cube maps into `{-1,0,1}`

Let

`g(y)=beta + sum_r gamma_r y_r`,  `y_r in {-1,1}`,

and assume `g(y) in {-1,0,1}` for every cube point. Then:

1. `sum_r |gamma_r| <= 1`;
2. at most two `gamma_r` are nonzero;
3. with two nonzero coefficients, both have magnitude `1/2` and `beta=0`;
4. with one nonzero coefficient, either `|gamma|=1, beta=0`, or `|gamma|=1/2, beta=+/-1/2`;
5. with none, `beta in {-1,0,1}`.

**Proof.** Flip coordinate `r` while keeping all others fixed. The two values of `g` remain in `{-1,0,1}`, while their difference is `2 gamma_r`. Therefore each nonzero `|gamma_r|` is `1/2` or `1`.

The maximum and minimum values of the affine part are obtained by choosing each sign to agree or disagree with `gamma_r`; their difference is

`2 sum_r |gamma_r|`.

Because all values of `g` lie in `[-1,1]`, this diameter is at most two. Hence `sum_r |gamma_r|<=1`. Since each nonzero magnitude is at least `1/2`, there are at most two.

If there are two, both magnitudes are `1/2`; the unshifted values are `-1,0,0,1`, so translating all four into `{-1,0,1}` forces `beta=0`. If there is one coefficient of magnitude one, its two values are `beta-1,beta+1`, forcing `beta=0`. If there is one coefficient of magnitude `1/2`, the two values differ by one and therefore must be `{-1,0}` or `{0,1}`, forcing `beta=-1/2` or `+1/2`. The zero-coefficient case is immediate. QED.

### Graph structure of a quadratic Boolean function

Apply the lemma to every `D_i f`. Form a graph `G` on the coordinates with edge `ij` iff `c_ij !=0`. Then:

- every vertex has degree at most two;
- graph degree two forces `b_i=0` and both incident `|c_ij|=1/2`;
- graph degree one gives either incident `|c_ij|=1` with `b_i=0`, or incident `|c_ij|=1/2` with `|b_i|=1/2`;
- graph degree zero gives `b_i in {-1,0,1}`.

Now decompose the nonconstant polynomial over connected components of `G`, treating an isolated vertex with nonzero `b_i` as an active singleton:

`f(x)=a + sum_C h_C(x_C)`.

There can be at most one active component. Indeed, if two active components `C,D` existed, choose two distinct range values `u_1<u_2` of `h_C` and `v_1<v_2` of `h_D`. Fix every other component. Among the four sums, `u_1+v_1` is the strict minimum and `u_2+v_2` the strict maximum, while at least one cross-sum is strictly intermediate. Therefore `f` would attain at least three values, contradicting Booleanity.

The unique active graph component has maximum degree two, hence is a singleton, path, or cycle.

- Singleton: `|b_i|=1`, so total linear `l1` mass is one.
- Cycle: all vertices have degree two, hence all `b_i=0`.
- Path with at least two edges: internal vertices force every edge magnitude to `1/2`; internal `b_i=0`; each endpoint contributes magnitude at most `1/2`, so total at most one.
- Single-edge path: if the edge magnitude is one, both endpoint linear coefficients vanish; if it is `1/2`, each endpoint contributes at most `1/2`. Again the total is at most one.

Thus in every case

`sum_i |b_i|<=1`.

Since `b_i=\hat f({i})`, the theorem follows. QED.

This is a theorem-sized claim with the full proof written above; it is not inferred from the finite checker.

**B16 outcome:** all-`n` degree-two theorem proved; frozen metric moves.
