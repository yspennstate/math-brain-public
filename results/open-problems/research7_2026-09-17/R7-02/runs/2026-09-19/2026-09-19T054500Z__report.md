## 2. Main mathematical result: a complete all-n degree-four theorem

### Theorem

For every Boolean function `f:{-1,1}^n->{-1,1}` of Fourier degree at most four,

`sum_i |fhat({i})| <= 2`.                                             (2.1)

Consequently

`sum_i fhat({i}) <= 2 = sqrt(4)`.

Thus the weak source inequality is completely proved at `d=4`, uniformly in `n`, and

`D_weak : 3 -> 4`.

The proof has an exact finite core on at most sixteen coordinates and a written saturation argument excluding every larger active set.

## 3. Granularity and orientation

For a Boolean degree-`d` function, every nonconstant Fourier coefficient belongs to

`2^(1-d) Z`.                                                          (3.1)

Indeed, with `g=(1-f)/2` and zero-one coordinates `z_i=(1-x_i)/2`, the multilinear coefficients of `g` are integers by Möbius inversion. Converting back to the `x` basis gives denominator at most `2^d` for `g`, hence denominator at most `2^(d-1)` for every nonconstant coefficient of `f`.

At `d=4`, every nonzero singleton coefficient therefore has magnitude at least `1/8`.

Flip input coordinate signs so all nonzero singleton coefficients are positive. Degree and Booleanity are preserved, and the signed singleton sum becomes the original `l1` singleton mass. Let

`A={i:fhat({i})>0}`, `m=|A|`.

Then

`fhat({i}) >= 1/8` for all `i in A`.                                  (3.2)

## 4. B18/B25: exact finite core for m<=16

Let `h` be any Boolean degree-four function on `m<=16` variables. Symmetrize over coordinate permutations. If `b_k` is the number of `+1` values in Hamming layer `k`, put

`r_k=b_k/binom(m,k)`.

The symmetric average has degree at most four, so `r_k` is a quartic polynomial in `k`. Write it in normalized Newton form

`r(k)=sum_{j=0}^4 u_j binom(k,j)/binom(m,j)`.                          (4.1)

Multiplying by `binom(m,k)` gives

`b_k=sum_{j=0}^4 u_j binom(m-j,k-j)`.                                 (4.2)

The equations for `k=0,1,2,3,4` are triangular with diagonal one, so every `u_j` is an integer. The Boolean layer conditions are

`0<=b_k<=binom(m,k)`.                                                  (4.3)

For `K~Bin(m,1/2)`, the signed singleton objective is

`L(h)=E[h(X) sum_i X_i]=2 E[r(K)(m-2K)]`.

The exact binomial identity

`E[(m-2K) binom(K,j)/binom(m,j)] = -j/2^j`

therefore yields

`L(h)=-u_1-u_2-(3/4)u_3-(1/2)u_4`.                                  (4.4)

This turns the finite core into an integer-lattice optimization, not an enumeration of all Boolean truth tables.

## 5. B3/B25: lift the finite core to arbitrary dimension

Fix any `T subseteq A` with `|T|<=16` and restrict every coordinate outside `T`. Every restriction `f_y` is Boolean of degree at most four. By (4.5),

`sum_{i in T} (f_y)hat({i}) <=2` for every `y`.

Averaging the restricted Fourier coefficients over `y` recovers the original ones, so

`sum_{i in T} fhat({i}) <=2`.                                        (5.1)

If `m<=16`, Theorem (2.1) follows immediately. Assume `m>=17`.

By granularity, any 16 active coordinates have total singleton mass at least `16/8=2`; by (5.1) they have at most two. Hence every 16-subset saturates and, in fact,

`fhat({i})=1/8` for every active `i`.                                 (5.2)

For a fixed 16-subset `T`, write

`L_T(y)=sum_{i in T}(f_y)hat({i})`.

Every `L_T(y)<=2`, but its average is exactly two by (5.2). Therefore

`L_T(y)=2` for **every** outside assignment `y`.                      (5.3)

Now fix arbitrary values of every originally inactive coordinate and call the resulting function `g` on the `m` active coordinates. Equation (5.3) implies every 16-subset of singleton coefficients of `g` sums to two. Since `m>=17`, comparison of two 16-subsets differing in one coordinate gives

`ghat({i})=1/8` for every `i=1,...,m`.                                (5.4)

Thus it suffices to consider a Boolean degree-four function on exactly `m` variables with all singleton coefficients equal to `1/8`.

Expanding the identically constant function (5.3) in the outside variables gives, for every 16-subset `T` and every nonempty `R` disjoint from `T` with `|R|<=3`,

`sum_{i in T} ghat(R union {i})=0`.                                  (5.5)

If real numbers indexed by a set of size at least 17 sum to zero on every 16-subset, then they are all zero: compare two 16-subsets differing by one index to see all entries agree, then use one 16-subset sum. Applying this to (5.5) gives

- for `m>=18`, all level-two coefficients vanish;
- for `m>=19`, all level-three coefficients vanish;
- for `m>=20`, all level-four coefficients vanish.                     (5.6)

If `m>=20`, only levels zero and one remain. Then `g` is affine with singleton derivative `1/8`, impossible for a Boolean function because a Boolean discrete derivative belongs to `{-1,0,1}`. Hence

`m<=19`.                                                               (5.7)

## 6. Eliminate m=17,18,19 exactly

Scale Fourier coefficients by eight and write the odd part of `8g` as

`H(x)=sum_i x_i + sum_{i<j<k} c_{ijk} x_i x_j x_k`,

where `c_{ijk}=8 ghat({i,j,k})`.

A global sign flip cancels all even Fourier levels, so

`8g(x)-8g(-x)=2H(x)`.

Since the left side belongs to `{-16,0,16}`,

`H(x) in {-8,0,8}` for every cube point.                              (6.1)

### m=19

All triple coefficients vanish by (5.6), so `H(1,...,1)=19`, contradicting (6.1).

### m=18

For each pair `{j,k}`, the complement has exactly 16 coordinates; (5.5) gives

`sum_{i notin {j,k}} c_{ijk}=0`.

Sum these equations over all pairs. Every triple coefficient is counted three times, hence the total triple coefficient sum is zero. Therefore

`H(1,...,1)=18`, contradicting (6.1).

### m=17

Put

`C=sum_{i<j<k}c_{ijk}`, `v_0=H(1,...,1)=17+C`.

For each `j`, let `L_j` be the sum of triple coefficients containing `j`, and let `v_j` be the value of `H` after flipping only coordinate `j`. Then

`v_j=v_0-2(1+L_j)`,

so

`L_j=(v_0-v_j)/2-1`.                                                  (6.2)

Every triple coefficient appears in exactly three `L_j`, so

`sum_j L_j=3C=3(v_0-17)`.                                             (6.3)

Summing (6.2) over the seventeen coordinates and comparing with (6.3) gives

`sum_{j=1}^{17}v_j=11v_0+68`.                                        (6.4)

By (6.1), every `v_j` and `v_0` is a multiple of eight. The left side of (6.4) is therefore divisible by eight, whereas the right side is congruent to `68=4 mod 8`. Contradiction.

Thus `m=17,18,19` are impossible. Together with (4.5) and the `m>=20` contradiction, this proves (2.1) for every `n`.
