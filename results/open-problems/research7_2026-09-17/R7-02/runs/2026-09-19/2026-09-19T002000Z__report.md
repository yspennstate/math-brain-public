## 3. B30 — canonical conditional two-point compression

The earlier proof used separate hand-built bounded comparators below and at the endpoint.  A single construction handles every integrable `N`.

### Lemma 3.1

Let `N` be nonnegative integer-valued with finite mean `mu>0`, and let

\[
p_0=P(N=0)<1,
\qquad q=1-p_0,
\qquad
m=E[N\mid N>0]=\frac{\mu}{q}.
\]

Write

\[
k=\lfloor m\rfloor,
\qquad
\theta=m-k\in[0,1).
\]

Define `L` by

\[
P(L=0)=p_0,
\]

and conditionally on `L>0`,

\[
P(L=k\mid L>0)=1-\theta,
\qquad
P(L=k+1\mid L>0)=\theta.
\]

Then

\[
P(L=0)=P(N=0),
\qquad
EL=EN=\mu,
\qquad
\boxed{L\le_{\rm cx}N}.
\]

### Proof

The mean calculation is exact:

\[
EL=q[(1-\theta)k+\theta(k+1)]=qm=\mu.
\]

Let `phi` be a convex function on the nonnegative integers for which the expectations exist, and extend it piecewise linearly to a convex function `bar phi` on `[0,infinity)`.  Conditional Jensen gives

\[
E[\phi(N)\mid N>0]
=E[\bar\phi(N)\mid N>0]
\ge \bar\phi(m)
=(1-\theta)\phi(k)+\theta\phi(k+1)
=E[\phi(L)\mid L>0].
\]

The two laws have the same atom `p_0` at zero, so adding the common term `p_0 phi(0)` yields

\[
E\phi(L)\le E\phi(N).
\]

Since their means agree, this is precisely `L<=cx N`.  Equivalently, it is enough to check the stop-loss generators `(x-t)_+`, all of which are integrable because `EN<infinity`.  This proves the lemma.

The comparator is exactly source-valid: the cookie environment whose first `L` cookies are deterministic-right and whose remaining cookies are fair is iid across sites, has bounded depth at most `k+1`, has drift `EL=mu`, is `(POS1)`, and is `(WEL)` because the event `L=0` has probability `p_0>0` and then all left transition probabilities equal `1/2` (while right transition probabilities are always positive).

Hence every arbitrary `N` with `1<EN<=2` has a bounded source-valid comparator at the **same drift parameter**.

**B30 outcome: SUCCESS.**  This one conditional compression replaces all special comparator formulas and eliminates the earlier artificial drift-strengthening step.

## 4. B11 — exact stop-loss dual proof of the branching transfer

To verify the direction of the convex-order transfer directly, fix an integer threshold `t>=0` and define

\[
\varphi_t(x)=(x-t)_+,
\qquad
H_t(r)=E(S_r-t)_+.
\]

### Lemma 4.1

For every `r>=0`,

\[
H_t(r+1)-H_t(r)=E\,q_t(S_r),
\]

where

\[
q_t(s)=
\begin{cases}
2^{s-t},&s<t,\\
1,&s\ge t.
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently `H_t` is increasing and convex in `r`.

### Proof

Condition on `S_r=s`.  If `s>=t`,

\[
E[(s+\xi-t)_+-(s-t)_+]=E\xi=1.
\]

If `s<t`, put `d=t-s>=1`.  Since `P(\xi\ge j)=2^{-j}` for `j>=1`,

\[
E(\xi-d)_+
=\sum_{j=d+1}^{\infty}P(\xi\ge j)
=\sum_{j=d+1}^{\infty}2^{-j}
=2^{-d}=2^{s-t}.
\]

This proves the increment formula.  The function `q_t` is increasing, and under the natural coupling `S_{r+1}>=S_r`; therefore `E q_t(S_r)` is nondecreasing in `r`.  Hence the increments of `H_t` are nondecreasing, so `H_t` is convex, and they are nonnegative, so it is increasing.

Now, for an emigration law `M`, define

\[
(K_M\varphi_t)(x)
=E\,H_t((x+1-M)_+).
\]

Because `H_t` is convex and nondecreasing, the map

\[
m\mapsto H_t((x+1-m)_+)
\]

is convex for each fixed `x`, while

\[
x\mapsto H_t((x+1-m)_+)
\]

is increasing convex for fixed `m`.  The order `L<=cx N` therefore gives

\[
\boxed{K_L\varphi_t(x)\le K_N\varphi_t(x)}
\qquad\forall x,t\in\mathbb Z_+.
\]

The integer stop-loss functions together with an affine increasing term generate every increasing convex function on `Z_+` by nonnegative second differences.  Thus each kernel preserves increasing convexity and the pointwise kernel inequality extends to all increasing convex test functions.

Starting both chains from zero and inducting gives

\[
\boxed{Z_n^L\le_{\rm icx}Z_n^N\qquad\forall n\ge0},
\]

hence, in particular,

\[
\boxed{E_0Z_n^L\le E_0Z_n^N\qquad\forall n.}
\]

No boundedness or higher moment of `N` is used; `EN<infinity` is sufficient.

**B11 outcome: SUCCESS.**  The transfer is now checked at the exact dual generators of increasing-convex order, with an explicit geometric-offspring increment formula.
