## 3. Method B18 — express the proof as two distinct ordered compressions

### Idea before derivation

The cumulative proof had accumulated several couplings. The useful simplification is to separate them into two operations with different order structures:

1. **Bernoulliize and front-load** the original stack: exact representation followed by a pathwise order.
2. **Compress the random emigration law at fixed mean**: a convex-order operation, not a pathwise one.

This makes it impossible to accidentally use stochastic domination where only convex order is true.

### Derivation

For a positive stack set

\[
p_i=\omega(0,1,i),\qquad d_i=2p_i-1.
\]

Conditionally on that stack take independent

\[
A_i\sim {\rm Bernoulli}(d_i),\qquad U_i\sim {\rm Bernoulli}(1/2)
\]

and set

\[
B_i=A_i+(1-A_i)U_i.
\]

Then

\[
P(B_i=1\mid p)=d_i+(1-d_i)/2=p_i,
\]

so this is exactly the original quenched cookie toss. With

\[
N=\sum_iA_i,
\]

moving all positions with `A_i=1` to the front can only decrease, simultaneously for every `m`, the number of failures before the `m`-th success. Therefore

\[
F_m^{\rm orig}\ge G_{(m-N)_+}\quad\forall m,
\]

and the backward chains can be coupled so that

\[
\boxed{V_n^{\rm orig}\ge Z_n^N\quad\forall n.}
\]

This is the first, **pathwise**, order.

Now let `mu=EN`, `p0=P(N=0)`, `q=1-p0`, `m=mu/q`, `k=floor(m)`, and `theta=m-k`. Define bounded `L` by

\[
P(L=0)=p_0,
\]

and conditional on `L>0`,

\[
P(L=k)=1-\theta,\qquad P(L=k+1)=\theta.
\]

Conditional Jensen gives

\[
\boxed{L\le_{cx}N,\qquad EL=EN,\qquad P(L=0)=P(N=0).}
\]

The `L` environment is finitely many deterministic-right cookies followed by fair cookies, hence source-valid bounded depth at the same drift.

For every increasing convex `phi`, with `S_r` a sum of `r` mean-one geometric variables,

\[
h(r)=E\phi(S_r)
\]

is increasing convex. Hence `m -> h((x+1-m)_+)` is convex and the transition kernels satisfy

\[
K_L\phi(x)\le K_N\phi(x).
\]

They also preserve increasing convexity in `x`, so induction gives

\[
\boxed{Z_n^L\le_{icx}Z_n^N\quad\forall n,}
\]

therefore `EZ_n^L<=EZ_n^N`. This is the second, **convex-order**, comparison.

For the bounded comparator with `1<mu<=2`, source Theorem 5.4 gives `E sigma_L<infinity` and `EA_L=infinity`. The first-return generating-function identity

\[
U_r(s)=\frac{B_r(s)}{1-F(s)}
\]

applied first to bounded rewards `r_R(z)=z wedge R` forces

\[
\liminf_{s\uparrow1}(1-s)\sum_n EZ_n^Ls^n=\infty.
\]

The finite-time mean domination transfers this to `Z^N`. If `EA_N<infinity`, then `E sigma_N<infinity` because `sigma_N<=A_N+1`, and the same Abel identity would give a finite limit, contradiction. Thus

\[
\boxed{EA_N=\infty\qquad(1<EN\le2,\ P(N=0)>0).}
\]

Pathwise front-loading then gives `EA_orig=infinity`, and the regeneration relation gives `v=0`.

A further B18 simplification handles the supercritical branch in the same backward-process language. If `delta>2`, choose a finite truncation `M` with

\[
\delta_M=E\sum_{i\le M}d_i>2.
\]

The bounded truncation has finite expected cycle area. Coupling all deeper original positive cookies against fair truncation cookies makes the original success sequence stronger, so

\[
V_n^{\rm orig}\le V_n^{(M)}
\]

pathwise. Therefore the original cycle area is finite too, proving positive speed. This also handles `delta=infinity` and removes dependence on a separate numerical-speed monotonicity theorem.
