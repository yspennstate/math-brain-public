### 5.3 The Yanev–Yanev line is a substantially closer collision risk than the earlier abstract-only screen showed

The accessible full-text rendering of George P. Yanev and Nikolay M. Yanev, *Limit Theorems for Branching Processes with Random Migration Stopped at Zero* (1997), DOI `10.1007/978-1-4612-1862-3_25`, explicitly allows, in a positive generation, **a random number of emigrating families** (plus possible individual emigration), no migration, or immigration. Its Theorem 2.1 derives extinction-time asymptotics in terms of a migration criticality parameter. Public full text:
`https://www.researchgate.net/publication/252745153_Limit_Theorems_for_Branching_Processes_with_Random_Migration_Stopped_at_Zero`.

Our scalar recursion can be *structurally* read as a critical BGW process with parent-count migration `1-N`: `N=0` adds one reproductive family, `N=1` gives no parent-count migration, and `N>=2` removes `N-1` families before reproduction, clipped at zero. That makes the 1995–1997 Yanev–Yanev family a genuine theorem-level overlap candidate, not merely background bibliography.

However, this run **does not claim equivalence or prior resolution**. The exact hypotheses labelled (2.1)/(2.2), the parameter translation, their mixture convention for the migration regimes, and—most importantly—the implication from their survival/moment asymptotics to our required **cycle-area divergence** have not yet been matched line by line. A search hit is not a theorem mapping.

The older 1983/84 Yanev–Mitov life-period model is visibly narrower in its displayed definition: the migration variable there is in `{-1,0,1}`. Vatutin 1977 and subsequent refinements remain relevant but the current accessible metadata is insufficient for a source-level identification.

Patrick Carper's 5 September 2026 preprint `arXiv:2609.06311`, *A Branching Process with Geometric Emigration and the "Have Your Cookie and Eat It" Random Walk*, concerns geometric emigration and therefore remains a special-family overlap, not the arbitrary-`N` theorem.
