## Method B19 — constructive truncation closes the `delta>2` source clause

For finite `M`, replace every cookie after the `M`-th at every site by a placebo cookie `1/2`, keeping the first `M` cookies unchanged. Denote the truncated environment by `omega^(M)`. It preserves `(IID)`, `(WEL)`, and `(POS1)`, and now satisfies `(BD)`. Its drift is

\[
\delta_M=\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^M d_i.
\]

Because every `d_i>=0`, monotone convergence gives

\[
\delta_M\uparrow\delta,
\]

including when `delta=+infinity`. Hence if `delta>2`, some finite `M` has `delta_M>2`.

The bounded-stack source theorem then gives strictly positive speed `v_M>0` for the truncated walk. Pathwise,

\[
\omega(x,1,i)\ge \omega^{(M)}(x,1,i)
\]

for every site and visit index. Both limiting speeds exist under the source LLN, and the source's monotonicity proposition therefore yields

\[
\boxed{v\ge v_M>0.}
\]

This is a full proof of one of the two source clauses, under the exact source hypotheses and including `delta=+infinity`. It is the metric movement `UNKNOWN/UNMEASURED -> 1/2`.

## Method B19/B16 — reduce all of `delta<2` to the single critical case `delta=2`

Let `delta<2` and put `c_i=E(1-d_i)`. Since `sum_i E d_i=delta<infinity`, we have `E d_i->0`, hence `c_i->1` and `sum_i c_i=infinity`. Put `r=2-delta>0`. Choose finitely many coefficients `a_i in [0,1/2]` satisfying

\[
\sum_i a_i c_i=r.
\]

Define a strengthened stack coordinatewise by

\[
p_i^+=p_i+a_i(1-p_i),
\qquad
d_i^+=d_i+a_i(1-d_i).
\]

Only finitely many cookie coordinates change. The transformation preserves `(IID)` and `(POS1)`. It also preserves `(WEL)` because, for changed coordinates,

\[
1-p_i^+=(1-a_i)(1-p_i)>0
\]

whenever the original left-step probability is positive. Its expected total drift is exactly

\[
\delta^+
=\delta+\sum_i a_iE(1-d_i)
=2.
\]

The strengthened law dominates the original law. Therefore source monotonicity gives

\[
0\le v(P)\le v(P^+).
\]

Consequently, if the critical theorem `delta=2 => v=0` is proved, every `delta<2` law automatically has zero speed. Together with the positive half already proved, the entire source now reduces to

\[
\boxed{\delta=2\Longrightarrow v=0.}
\]

This reduction imposes no extra stack-length or higher-moment hypothesis.

## Method B16 — exact unbounded-stack branching transition

The historical intake attempt guessed that the bounded branching asymptotics should survive unbounded positive stacks but did not prove the tail/moment step. I replaced that guess with an exact stopping-time calculation.

In a fresh site stack let independent uniforms generate signs `B_i in {+1,-1}` with conditional mean `d_i`. Let `T_m` be the trial index of the `m`-th success and

\[
F_m=T_m-m
\]

be the number of failures before the `m`-th success. Under `(IID)`, the backward edge-crossing chain uses independent site copies through

\[
V_{k+1}=F^{(k)}_{V_k+1}.
\]

For finite `delta`, define

\[
D_m=\sum_{i\le T_m}d_i,
\qquad
R_m=\sum_{i>T_m}d_i,
\qquad
r_m=ER_m.
\]

Let

\[
M_n=\sum_{i=1}^n(B_i-d_i).
\]

Because every success probability is at least `1/2`, couple with fair successes from the same uniforms. Then `T_m` is dominated by the fair negative-binomial stopping time of mean `2m`, so optional stopping is legitimate. At `T_m`,

\[
m-F_m=D_m+M_{T_m}.
\]

Taking expectations gives the **exact identity**

\[
\boxed{EF_m=m-\delta+r_m.}
\]

Since `T_m>=m` and all drift terms are nonnegative,

\[
0\le r_m
\le E\sum_{i>m}d_i
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
E[V_{k+1}-V_k\mid V_k=x]
=1-\delta+r_{x+1}
=1-\delta+o(1).
}
\]

At the unresolved critical point, the exact asymptotic drift is `-1`.

## New finite-mean integrability lemma

A possible obstruction was that the random total stack drift

\[
D_\infty=\sum_i d_i
\]

may have only a finite first moment, not a finite second moment. That turns out not to destroy second-order transition control.

Let `G_m` be the total number of fair trials needed for `m` fair successes in the coupling. Then

\[
T_m\le G_m,
\qquad
D_m\le\min(D_\infty,G_m),
\]

and `D_infty` is independent of `G_m`. For

\[
h(t)=E[\min(D_\infty,t)^2],
\]

integrability of `D_infty` alone implies `h(t)/t->0`: for `A<t`,

\[
h(t)
\le A^2+tE[D_\infty 1_{\{D_\infty>A\}}].
\]

Divide by `t`, let `t->infinity`, then `A->infinity`. Also `h(t)/t<=E D_infty`. Since `G_m/m` has bounded second moments and `G_m->infinity` in probability,

\[
\frac1m E h(G_m)
=E\left[\frac{h(G_m)}{G_m}\frac{G_m}{m}\right]
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{E D_m^2=o(m).}
\]

No second moment of `D_infty` was used.

## Universal variance asymptotics and one-step CLT

The stopped martingale square isometry gives

\[
E M_{T_m}^2
=E\sum_{i\le T_m}(1-d_i^2).
\]

Now

\[
ET_m=2m-\delta+r_m
\]

and

\[
0\le E\sum_{i\le T_m}d_i^2\le E D_\infty=\delta,
\]

so

\[
E M_{T_m}^2=2m+O(1).
\]

Since `F_m=m-D_m-M_{T_m}`, the new `E D_m^2=o(m)` lemma and Cauchy-Schwarz yield

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(F_m)=2m+o(m).}
\]

Therefore, for the branching chain,

\[
\boxed{
E[V_{k+1}-V_k\mid V_k=x]=1-\delta+o(1),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(V_{k+1}\mid V_k=x)=2x+o(x).
}
\]

There is also a one-step Gaussian limit. The predictable quadratic variation

\[
Q_m=\sum_{i\le T_m}(1-d_i^2)
\]

satisfies `Q_m/(2m)->1` in probability: the variance estimate gives `T_m/(2m)->1`, while `0<=T_m-Q_m<=D_infty=o_p(m)`. The martingale increments are bounded, so Lindeberg is automatic. Thus

\[
M_{T_m}/\sqrt{2m}\Longrightarrow N(0,1).
\]

The consumed-drift fluctuation is negligible because `E D_m^2=o(m)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_m-(m-\delta+r_m)}{\sqrt{2m}}
\Longrightarrow N(0,1).
}
\]

At `delta=2`, arbitrary positive unbounded stacks with finite expected total drift therefore have the same first two large-state branching coefficients and the same one-step Gaussian scaling as the bounded model. Unboundedness alone cannot defeat the conjecture by changing those coefficients.
