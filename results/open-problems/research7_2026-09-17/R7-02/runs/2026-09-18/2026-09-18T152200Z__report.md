## 5. Exact implication from Wu's theorem to the source's two-point conjecture

The implication is short but worth recording explicitly so the status change is source-matching rather than title-matching.

Let `H_t` denote the source's narrow-wedge KPZ height after subtracting the deterministic parabola and applying its standard 1:2:3 space/height scaling. Wu's theorem gives, in particular, locally uniform convergence in distribution of the narrow-wedge spatial profile to the one-time directed-landscape/Airy-sheet profile. Under the standard normalization this limiting one-time profile is the Airy_2 process `A_2` (with the deterministic parabola already removed by the source's definition of `xi`).

Fix `w in R`. The map

`E_w : C_loc(R) -> R^2,   f |-> (f(0), f(w))`

is continuous for the locally uniform topology. Therefore the continuous-mapping theorem yields

`(H_t(0), H_t(w)) => (A_2(0), A_2(w))`.

The Airy_2 finite-dimensional distribution is characterized by the extended-Airy-kernel Fredholm determinant. Hence, at every pair `(s_1,s_2)` of continuity points (indeed the Airy_2 two-point law is continuous),

`P(H_t(0) <= s_1, H_t(w) <= s_2)`

converges to the exact determinant written by Quastel–Spohn in (2.9). Matching `H_t(w)` back to their spatial coordinate `2 lambda_t^2 w` gives precisely the source's two-point statement.

Thus no additional unproved compactness, interchange of limits, or multi-point tightness lemma is missing: locally uniform process convergence is strictly stronger than the fixed two-coordinate convergence requested in F0138.
