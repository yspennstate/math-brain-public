# Method B11 — square-root dualization identifies the exact equality obstruction

A tempting route is to feed the preceding first-two-moment asymptotics into a generic Lamperti criterion. Doing so reveals why that route does not quite close the endpoint.

Fix `c>0` and transform the backward process by

\[
Y=\sqrt{V+c}.
\]

At state `x`, set

\[
y=\sqrt{x+c},\qquad
\eta=\sqrt{W+c}-\sqrt{x+c}.
\]

There is an exact identity

\[
\boxed{\eta=\frac{\Delta}{2y}-\frac{\eta^2}{2y}.}
\]

Indeed, with `a=sqrt(W+c)` and `b=y`,

\[
\Delta=(a-b)(a+b),
\]

and

\[
\frac{\Delta}{a+b}
=
\frac{\Delta}{2b}-\frac{(a-b)^2}{2b}.
\]

Moreover

\[
0\le \eta^2
=
\frac{\Delta^2}{(\sqrt{W+c}+\sqrt{x+c})^2}
\le
\frac{\Delta^2}{x+c}.
\]

The preceding one-step CLT plus convergence of the normalized second moments makes `Delta^2/x` uniformly integrable, while `Delta/x -> 0` in probability. Therefore

\[
E[\eta^2\mid x]\longrightarrow \frac12.
\]

Taking conditional expectation in the exact identity and using `E Delta=-1+o(1)` yields

\[
\boxed{
E[\eta\mid x]
=
-\frac{3}{4y}+o(y^{-1}),
\qquad
E[\eta^2\mid x]\to\frac12.
}
\]

Thus in the standard asymptotically-zero-drift notation the transformed chain has

\[
\mu=\frac34,\qquad b=\frac12.
\]

The corresponding invariant-moment threshold is

\[
\frac{2\mu}{b}-1=2.
\]

But the moment needed for speed is exactly `Y^2=V+c`. Therefore the desired invariant mean lies **at the equality boundary**, not on either strict side of the generic Lamperti moment theorem. This explains precisely why the first two asymptotic coefficients, even supplemented by the one-step Gaussian limit, are insufficient by themselves. It also blocks an invalid shortcut: one cannot infer the critical speed merely from matching the bounded model’s drift and variance coefficients.

Outcome: **useful obstruction diagnosis; no metric movement**.

# Method B24 — Bernoulliization and front-loading reduce the full critical source to one scalar emigration law

This is the main structural result of the run.

## B24.1 Bernoulliize every positive cookie

For a source-valid site stack let

\[
p_i=\omega(0,+1,i),\qquad
d_i=2p_i-1\in[0,1].
\]

Conditionally on that stack introduce independent marks

\[
A_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(d_i)
\]

and independent fair bits

\[
C_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2).
\]

Define the actual success bit by

\[
B_i=
\begin{cases}
1,&A_i=1,\\
C_i,&A_i=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Then conditionally on the original stack,

\[
P(B_i=1\mid\omega)
=d_i+(1-d_i)/2=p_i,
\]

and the `B_i` are independent. Hence enlarging the environment in this way changes neither the annealed ERW law nor the backward branching-like process law. Every positive cookie can be represented as a random deterministic-right cookie or a fair cookie.

Let

\[
N=\sum_{i\ge1}A_i.
\]

At the critical source value `delta=2`, Tonelli gives

\[
\boxed{EN=E\sum_i d_i=2,}
\]

so `N<infinity` almost surely.

The source weak-ellipticity condition supplies positive probability of a stack on which every `p_i<1`, hence every `d_i<1`. Because `sum_i d_i<infinity` almost surely at finite `delta`, on that event

\[
\prod_i(1-d_i)>0.
\]

This is the conditional probability that every mark vanishes. Thus

\[
\boxed{P(N=0)>0.}
\]

## B24.2 Move all deterministic successes to the front

Fix one realization of the marks and of the fair bits. Replace each adjacent pattern

`[fair bit C, deterministic 1]`

by

`[deterministic 1, fair bit C]`.

After the first position the cumulative success count can only increase; after the second it is unchanged. Iterating adjacent interchanges moves all deterministic successes to the front and weakly increases the cumulative number of successes at every prefix.

If `F_m` is the number of failures before the `m`-th success, this gives the pathwise simultaneous inequality

\[
\boxed{F_m^{\rm front}\le F_m^{\rm original}\quad\text{for every }m.}
\]

The front-loaded site contains exactly `N` deterministic-right cookies and then fair cookies. Because `F_m` is itself nondecreasing in `m`, the backward chains can be coupled from their common start `0` so that

\[
\boxed{Z_k^{\rm front}\le Z_k^{\rm original}\quad\text{for all }k.}
\]

## B24.3 Positive recurrence and stationary stochastic order

At `delta=2`, choose a finite `M` such that

The finite-depth truncation is a bounded positive cookie environment with drift parameter greater than one. Its backward BLP is positive recurrent by the bounded theory. Adding deterministic successes only decreases the backward process, so the full binaryized process returns to zero no later and is also positive recurrent.

For the front-loaded chain choose `M` with

\boxed{\pi_{\rm front}\le_{\rm st}\pi_{\rm original}.}

# Method B5 — solve an unbounded one-parameter emigration family exactly

The reduction suggests attacking RE(2) first on explicit unbounded laws. Let

\[
P(N=n)=p q^n,\qquad n\ge0,
\]

with `q=1-p`, and parameterize by its mean

\[
\delta=\frac qp,
\qquad
p=\frac1{\delta+1},
\qquad
q=\frac\delta{\delta+1}.
\]

This is a genuinely unbounded source-valid `(IID)+(WEL)+(POS1)` family because `P(N=0)=p>0`.

Let

\[
T(z)=\frac1{2-z},
\]

the pgf of a mean-one geometric variable on the nonnegative integers. Given `Z=x`, putting `m=x+1`,

\[
Z'=S_{(m-N)_+},
\]

where `S_r` is a sum of `r` iid such geometric variables. Directly summing the geometric law of `N` gives

\[
\boxed{
E[z^{Z'}\mid Z=x]
=
\frac{pT(z)^{x+2}+q^{x+2}(T(z)-1)}{T(z)-q}.
}
\]

For `delta>1` let `Phi(z)=E_pi z^Z` be the stationary pgf. Stationarity gives, with `a=T(z)`,

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(z)(a-q)=pa^2\Phi(a)+q^2(a-1)\Phi(q).
}
\]

Now conjugate the Möbius map to translation:

\[
z=1-1/u,
\qquad
\Psi(u)=\Phi(1-1/u).
\]

Because

\[
T(1-1/u)=1-1/(u+1),
\]

the stationary equation becomes

\[
\Psi(u+1)=A_\delta(u)\Psi(u)
+
\frac{\delta^2}{\delta+1}\Psi(\delta+1)\frac{u+1}{u^2},
\]

where

\[
A_\delta(u)=\frac{(u-\delta)(u+1)}{u^2}.
\]

Since `Psi(u)->1`, the coefficient of a possible harmonic-size nonzero increment must cancel, which forces

\[
\boxed{\Psi(\delta+1)=1-\delta^{-2}.}
\]

Therefore, for the deficit

\[
d(u)=1-\Psi(u),
\]

the equation collapses to

\[
\boxed{
d(u+1)=A_\delta(u)d(u)+\frac1{u^2}.
}
\]

## Critical parameter `delta=2`

At `delta=2`, `Psi(3)=3/4`, so `d_3=1/4`, and for integer `n>=3`,

\[
d_{n+1}
=
\frac{(n-2)(n+1)}{n^2}d_n+rac1{n^2}.
\]

Define

\[
g_n=\frac{(n-2)(n-1)}n d_n.
\]

Then

\[
g_{n+1}-g_n
=
\frac{n-1}{n(n+1)}
=
-\frac1n+\frac2{n+1},
\]

with `g_3=1/6`. Hence

\[
\boxed{g_n=H_{n-1}+\frac2n-2,}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
1-\Phi(1-1/n)
=
\frac{n(H_{n-1}-2)+2}{(n-2)(n-1)}.
}
\]

In particular,

\[
n[1-\Phi(1-1/n)]\sim\log n\to\infty.
\]

For every nonnegative integer-valued `Z`,

\[
\frac{1-z^Z}{1-z}\uparrow Z
\quad\text{as }z\uparrow1.
\]

Thus the critical invariant mean is infinite:

\[
\boxed{E_\pi Z=\infty,\qquad v=0.}
\]

## Supercritical parameter `delta>2`

Variation of constants uses the exact product

\[
\prod_{k=m}^{n-1}A_\delta(k)
=
\frac{\Gamma(n-\delta)\Gamma(m)}{\Gamma(m-\delta)\Gamma(n)}\frac nm.
\]

The product is asymptotic to `(m/n)^(delta-1)` up to the fixed `m` coefficient, and summing the `1/k^2` forcing gives

\[
\boxed{u d(u)\longrightarrow\frac1{\delta-2}.}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{E_\pi Z=\frac1{\delta-2}}
\]

and the exact speed is

\[
\boxed{
v(\delta)
=
\frac1{1+2/(\delta-2)}
=
\frac{\delta-2}{\delta},
\qquad \delta>2.
}
\]

Together with monotonicity and the critical calculation, this family has

\[
\boxed{
v(\delta)=0\quad(\delta\le2),
\qquad
v(\delta)=\frac{\delta-2}{\delta}\quad(\delta>2).}
\]

In particular the right derivative at the threshold is `v'(2+)=1/2`.

This is a complete exact computation for one genuinely unbounded source-valid family, but not a proof of RE(2) for arbitrary `N`.
