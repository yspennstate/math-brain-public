# Method B21 — exact convex-order comparator and kernel theorem

Put

\[
p_0=P(N=0)>0,
\qquad
a=\min\{p_0/2,1/6\}.
\]

Define `N_*` by

\[
P(N_*=0)=a,
\qquad
P(N_*=2)=1-3a,
\qquad
P(N_*=3)=2a. \tag{1}
\]

Then `E N_*=2`.

## Lemma 1 — `N_* <=cx N`

Because both variables have mean two, it is enough to compare the stop-loss transforms at integer thresholds.

At `k=0`, both are two. At `k=1`,

\[
E(N-1)_+=EN-P(N\ge1)=1+p_0,
\]

while

\[
E(N_*-1)_+=(1-3a)+2(2a)=1+a\le1+p_0.
\]

At `k=2`, equality of the mean to two gives

\[
E(N-2)_+=E(2-N)_+=2p_0+P(N=1)\ge2a,
\]

whereas

\[
E(N_*-2)_+=2a.
\]

For `k>=3`, the `N_*` stop-loss transform is zero. Since integer-valued stop-loss transforms are affine between consecutive integer thresholds, the inequalities hold at every real threshold. Hence

\[
\boxed{N_*\le_{cx}N.} \tag{2}
\]

This is a complete proof, not a numerical check.

## Lemma 2 — random sums convert the emigration convex order into population increasing-convex order

Let

\[
S_r=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_r,
\qquad S_0=0.
\]

For an increasing convex `phi` define

\[
h(r)=E\,\phi(S_r).
\]

Then `h` is increasing, because `S_{r+1}=S_r+xi_{r+1}>=S_r`. It is also convex. Indeed, with

\[
g(s)=E[\phi(s+\xi)-\phi(s)],
\]

convexity of `phi` makes `g` nondecreasing, and therefore

\[
h(r+1)-h(r)=Eg(S_r)
\]

is nondecreasing in `r`.

For an emigration law `L` write

\[
(K_L\phi)(x)=E\phi\!\left(S_{(x+1-L)_+}\right). \tag{3}
\]

For fixed `x`, the map

\[
n\mapsto h((x+1-n)_+)
\]

is convex: `n -> (x+1-n)_+` is convex and `h` has a convex nondecreasing piecewise-linear extension. Applying (2) yields

\[
\boxed{K_{N_*}\phi(x)\le K_N\phi(x)\quad\forall x.} \tag{4}
\]

For fixed emigration value `l`, the map

\[
x\mapsto h((x+1-l)_+)
\]

is increasing convex; averaging in `l` shows that `K_L` preserves increasing convexity.

## Proposition 3 — finite-time ICX domination

Let `D` be the chain (RE) with emigration `N_*` and `Z` the chain with emigration `N`, both started at zero. Then

\[
\boxed{D_n\le_{icx}Z_n\quad\text{for every }n.} \tag{5}
\]

The induction is exact. If it holds at time `n`, then for increasing convex `phi`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\phi(D_{n+1})
&=E(K_{N_*}\phi)(D_n)\\
&\le E(K_N\phi)(D_n)\\
&\le E(K_N\phi)(Z_n)\\
&=E\phi(Z_{n+1}),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the first inequality uses (4), and the second uses that `K_N phi` is increasing convex. Taking `phi(x)=x` gives

\[
\boxed{E_0D_n\le E_0Z_n\quad\forall n.} \tag{6}
\]

B21 outcome: successful theorem; this is the central new mechanism.

# Method B30 — glue to one source-valid bounded model

The law `N_*` has support `{0,2,3}`. Construct a cookie environment by taking, independently at each site, `N_*` deterministic-right cookies followed by fair cookies.

This environment satisfies all hypotheses of the source's bounded theorem:

- `(IID)` by construction;
- `(BD)` with depth three;
- `(POS1)` trivially;
- drift `delta_*=EN_*=2`;
- `(WEL)` in the exact source sense. Right probabilities are always positive, while on the event `N_*=0`, of probability `a>0`, every cookie has positive left probability.

Its backward branching chain is exactly `D`. The bounded theorem gives speed zero at `delta_*=2`. The standard backward-BLP speed identity says that a finite invariant mean would give

\[
v_*={1\over1+2E_{\pi_*}D}>0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{E_{\pi_*}D=+\infty.} \tag{7}
\]

The comparator chain is irreducible and aperiodic and is positive recurrent because `delta_*=2>1` in the bounded backward-BLP theory. Hence its law from zero converges to `pi_*`. For every cutoff `R`,

\[
E_0(D_n\wedge R)\to E_{\pi_*}(D\wedge R). \tag{8}
\]

Since the right side tends to infinity with `R`, (8) implies

\[
\boxed{E_0D_n\to+\infty.} \tag{9}
\]

Now return to arbitrary `N`. Its chain is positive recurrent: choose `M` with `E(N wedge M)>1`; the bounded-emigration chain with `N wedge M` is positive recurrent, while emigration `N` only decreases the population and hence returns to zero no later.

The arbitrary kernel is stochastically monotone in the state. If its invariant law `pi_N` had finite mean, couple a chain `Z^(0)` started from zero with a stationary chain `Z^(pi)` using the same emigration and offspring variables. Then

\[
Z_n^{(0)}\le Z_n^{(\pi)}
\]

pathwise, so

\[
E_0Z_n\le E_{\pi_N}Z<\infty
\]

uniformly in `n`. But (6) and (9) imply

\[
E_0Z_n\ge E_0D_n\to\infty,
\]

a contradiction. Thus

\[
\boxed{E_{\pi_N}Z=+\infty} \tag{10}
\]

for every `N` with `EN=2` and `P(N=0)>0`. This proves RE(2).
