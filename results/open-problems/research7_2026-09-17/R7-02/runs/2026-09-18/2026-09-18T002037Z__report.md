## 3. B3 — strongest six-edge statement and the exact obstruction beyond six

### 3.1 Six edges with two no-singleton section families

Let the two transverse section families of a finite point set be the two sides of a simple bipartite incidence graph, with points as edges. If there are six points and every nonempty section in either direction contains at least two points, the graph has six edges and minimum degree at least two.

The classification is elementary and complete:

1. each side has at most three vertices because the sum of its degrees is six;
2. neither side has one vertex, because a simple graph with one vertex on one side gives degree at most one on the other side;
3. if one side has two vertices, every opposite vertex has degree exactly two, so there are three opposite vertices and the graph is `K_{2,3}`;
4. if both sides have three vertices, every degree is exactly two; a simple two-regular bipartite graph on six vertices must be the single cycle `C6`.

Hence only

`K_{2,3}`, `K_{3,2}`, or `C6`

can occur.

### 3.2 Exact semiprime family showing this local mechanism is genuinely six-cell-specific

This run found a full family, not just an eight-cell toy obstruction.

**Proposition.** For every odd prime `q>=5`, there exists an exact full-affine-span cluster `F_q subset Z^2`, `|F_q|=2q`, such that:

- all nonempty horizontal and vertical sections have size at least two;
- with `zeta=exp(2*pi*i/6)`, every horizontal section satisfies `sum zeta^x=0`;
- every vertical section satisfies `sum (-1)^y=0`;
- the section-incidence graph is
  `K_{2,3} disjoint_union ((q-3)/2 copies of K_{2,2})`, hence is non-product for `q>=5`;
- `F_q` exactly tiles `Z^2` by the lattice `qZ x 2Z`.

**Construction and proof.** Partition the residues modulo `q` into one triple and `(q-3)/2` pairs. Put residue `0` in the triple and residue `1` in one pair. Assign phase classes modulo six `0,2,4` to the triple and a pair `a,a+3` to every pair block; make the phase of residue `1` equal to `1`, so its pair mate has phase `4`. Since `gcd(q,6)=1`, CRT gives a unique `x_r mod 6q` for every residue `r mod q` with the assigned phase. Choose the representatives `x_0=0` and `x_1=1`.

For the block indexed by `j`, place its `x_r` values on both rows `2j` and `2j+1`. The triple rows have phase sum

`1+zeta^2+zeta^4=0`,

and each pair row has phase sum

`zeta^a+zeta^(a+3)=0`.

Each vertical section contains two consecutive heights, so its `(-1)^y` sum is zero. The incidence graph is exactly the disjoint union claimed.

Modulo `qZ x 2Z`, every residue `r mod q` occurs exactly once at each parity, so the `2q` points are a complete coset-representative set and

`Z^2 = F_q direct_sum (qZ x 2Z)`.

Thus `F_q` is exact. Because `(0,1)` is a point difference above `x_0=0`, and the point above `x_1=1` differs from `(0,0)` by `(1,2j)` for some `j`, subtracting `2j(0,1)` gives `(1,0)`. Hence the difference lattice is all of `Z^2` and the affine span is full.

At `q=3` there are no pair blocks left after the triple: the graph is exactly `K_{2,3}`. So the local two-phase/no-singleton rigidity used by the six-cell proof is sharply tied to the six-edge count; for all later distinct-semiprime sizes the same local `alpha=1` information admits exact full-span non-product tiles.

This proposition is **not** a counterexample to an orbit-closure theorem at size `2q`; the constructed tile has an explicit periodic lattice tiling. Its role is to identify exactly what the six-cell incidence argument uses and where it stops generalizing.

## 4. B15 — remove all post-incidence phase arithmetic

The previous R7-02 distinct-prime theorem assumed the one-dimensional factors were already complete residue systems. Exactness makes this assumption unnecessary.

### Theorem: intrinsic distinct-prime product periodicity

Let `p,q` be primes, let `A,B subset Z` satisfy

`|A|=p`, `|B|=q`, `gcd(A-A)=gcd(B-B)=1`,

and suppose `T` tiles `Z^2` by `A x B`. Then

`(p,0)+T=T` or `(0,q)+T=T`.

**Proof.** By Khetan Lemma 3.6, for every `n` the union of row fibres

`R_n = disjoint_union_{b in B} S_{n-b}`

is an `A`-tiling of `Z`, and every analogous column union `C_m` is a `B`-tiling. Prime-cardinality one-dimensional rigidity (Khetan Lemma 3.3 / Corollary 3.4), together with the gcd-one assumptions, gives

`R_n=pZ+r_n`, `C_m=qZ+s_m`.

Reducing `A direct_sum R_n=Z` modulo `p` forces `A` to contain exactly one representative of every residue modulo `p`; likewise `B` is a complete residue system modulo `q`. Therefore the corrected distinct-prime product theorem from the previous R7-02 run applies, using Lemma 3.7 with arbitrary codomain, and yields one of the two coordinate periods. QED.

### Consequence for the six-cell `K_{2,3}` branch

If two transverse line families on a full-span exact six-point cluster have incidence graph `K_{2,3}`, let `u,v` be their primitive directions. Translating one intersection to zero gives

`F={a u+b v : a in A, b in B}`, `|A|=3`, `|B|=2`.

The index of the generated difference lattice is

`|det(u,v)| gcd(A-A) gcd(B-B)`.

Full affine span makes this index one, so `u,v` are a unimodular basis and both gcds are one. The intrinsic theorem with primes 3 and 2 makes every tiling by `F` one-periodic.

Crucially, **no root-of-unity classification is needed after `K_{2,3}` has been found**. The spectral phase equations only have to force the incidence graph; exactness and one-dimensional tiling rigidity handle the arithmetic endpoint.

## 5. B27 — quantifier swap and deterministic six-section theorem

### 5.1 Weighted incidence lemma

Let a simple bipartite graph have six edges and minimum degree at least two. Assume one side has nonzero row factors `lambda_i` and the other has nonzero column weights `z_j` such that

`lambda_i sum_{j~i} z_j = 0`

for every row.

The six-edge classification leaves only `K_{2,3}` (or transpose) and `C6`. In a `C6`, relabel the three rows and three columns so row `i` misses column `i`. The equations become

`z_2+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_2=0`.

The first two give `z_1=z_2`; the third gives `2z_1=0`, contradicting `z_1 != 0`. Therefore `C6` is impossible.

### 5.2 Deterministic six-section closure theorem

**Theorem.** Let `F` be exact, full-span, and have six points. Suppose two transverse directions satisfy:

1. every nonempty section in either direction has at least two points;
2. in one direction the phase coefficient at an occupied row-column intersection factors as a nonzero row factor times a nonzero column factor, and the coefficient sum vanishes on every row.

Then every `F`-tiling is one-periodic.

**Proof.** The incidence graph has six edges and minimum degree two. The weighted lemma excludes `C6`, leaving `K_{2,3}` or its transpose. B15 then turns that product geometry into a one-periodic tiling. QED.

This theorem swaps the spectral quantifiers for a finite deterministic interface: spectral theory now only has to deliver two no-singleton directions and one rank-one row phase system.

## 6. New cardinality-free theorem for the other spectral branch

This is the strongest result of the run because it removes a hidden dependence question in the proof candidate.

### Theorem: at most one rich spectral direction implies periodicity for arbitrary finite `|F|`

Let `N=|F|` for any finite exact cluster and use Khetan's orbit-closure spectral setup. Let `Delta` be a minimum finite cover from Theorem 3.16. For each `alpha` coprime to `N`, put

`M_alpha(xi)=sum_{a in F} chi_{alpha a}(xi)`.

Call `g in Delta` rich if

`ker chi_g intersect intersection_{gcd(alpha,N)=1} Z(M_alpha)`

is infinite. If at most one member of `Delta` is rich, then the orbit closure contains a one-periodic `F`-tiling.

**Proof.** The dilation lemma is valid for arbitrary finite `F`. Therefore every support point away from zero lies in the common zero set of all `M_alpha`, `gcd(alpha,N)=1`. If at most one kernel direction `g_0` has infinitely many such points, all other kernel intersections contribute only finitely many support points. Hence

`supp(nu) subset ker chi_{g_0} union S`

for finite `S` (if no direction is rich, choose any `g_0`; the statement is stronger).

As in Khetan's Case 2, among all positive multiples of `g_0` and finite exceptional sets with this property choose one minimizing `|S|`, and make `S` disjoint from the kernel. Every `s in S` is then an isolated support atom. Also `chi_{g_0}(s)` cannot be a root of unity: if it had order `k`, moving from `g_0` to `k g_0` absorbs `s` into the kernel and decreases `|S|`.

Partition `S` by the common value `gamma_E=chi_{g_0}(s)`. Under the spectral isometry, translation by `n g_0` gives

`(n g_0).f-f = sum_E (gamma_E^n-1) phi_E`.

On a common full-measure set this holds pointwise for every `n>=0`, and the left side is integer-valued because it is the difference of two `{0,1}` indicators. Every `gamma_E` is not a root of unity. Khetan Lemma 3.1 therefore forces the right side to vanish for every `n`. At `n=1`,

`g_0.f=f` in `L^2`.

The origin cylinder is one-periodic modulo `mu`; Khetan Theorem 3.11 then gives one-periodicity for `mu`-almost every point of the orbit closure. QED.

The proof uses **no prime-square arithmetic** and no Lemma 3.15. For the six-cell target one merely replaces Khetan's `gcd(alpha,p)=1` indexing by the source-valid family `gcd(alpha,6)=1` supplied by the same general dilation lemma.

## 7. Two-rich six-cell branch using only `alpha=1`

Take a rich direction `g=n h`, with `h` primitive. Khetan Lemma 3.2 writes its character kernel as finitely many circles. One circle contains infinitely many common mask zeros. Parameterize it by

`xi(t)=r h+t h^perp`.

At the allowed dilation `alpha=1`, the mask along that circle is the Laurent polynomial

`M_1(xi(t)) = sum_{a in F} exp(2*pi*i*r<a,h>) z^{<a,h^perp>}`,

`z=exp(2*pi*i*t)`.

Infinitely many roots force this Laurent polynomial to vanish identically. Grouping coefficients by `<a,h^perp>` yields, on **every** nonempty line section parallel to `h`,

`sum_{a in section} exp(2*pi*i*r<a,h>)=0`.

Therefore no such section is a singleton.

With two distinct rich directions, minimum-cardinality `Delta` makes them transverse. Apply this argument to both to obtain the two no-singleton section families. For the first direction, an occupied grid intersection can be written

`a_ij=a_0+s_i h_1+t_j h_0`.

Its phase factors exactly as

`exp(2*pi*i*r<a_ij,h_0>) = lambda_i z_j`,

where both factors are nonzero. Hence the row section equations have precisely the rank-one form required by the deterministic theorem. That theorem gives `K_{2,3}`, and B15 gives one-periodicity.

Thus the two-rich branch at `|F|=6` is closed **without** trying to extend Khetan's prime-power cyclotomic Lemma 3.15 to mixed primes.
