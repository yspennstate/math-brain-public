## 3. B11 — exact spanning-forest duality for one loop-erased ant

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite connected weighted graph with terminals `s,t` and positive conductances `c_e`. By the weighted Wilson/LERW representation, the loop-erased `s`-to-`t` path has the law of the unique `s`-to-`t` path in a weighted random spanning tree.

Let

\[
\tau(c)=\sum_{T\text{ tree}}\prod_{f\in T}c_f,
\]

and let `\mathcal F_{s,t}` be the spanning two-forests with exactly two components, one containing `s` and one containing `t`. For `e=uv`, define

\[
\Phi_e^\times(c)
=
\sum_{\substack{F\in\mathcal F_{s,t}\\u,v\text{ in distinct components}}}
\prod_{f\in F}c_f.
\]

If `p_e(c)` is the probability that the loop-erased path reinforces `e`, then deleting `e` gives a weight-preserving bijection, up to the factor `c_e`, between spanning trees whose `s`-`t` path contains `e` and the forests counted by `\Phi_e^\times`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\frac{p_e(c)}{c_e}=\frac{\Phi_e^\times(c)}{\tau(c)}.}
\]

Writing `\Phi_{s,t}` for the total weighted `s`-`t` two-forest partition function and using Kirchhoff's identity

\[
R_{s,t}(c)=\frac{\Phi_{s,t}(c)}{\tau(c)},
\]

this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{p_e(c)}{c_e}
=R_{s,t}(c)\,
\Pr_{\mathbb F_{s,t}}(u,v\text{ separated}).
}
\]

This gives an exact dual formulation of every loop-erased reinforcement probability.

### Exact terminal-pair identity and stochastic martingale

If an interior vertex `i` is adjacent to both terminals by `si` and `it`, then under any `s`-`t` separating two-forest, `i` lies on exactly one terminal side. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{p_{si}}{c_{si}}+\frac{p_{it}}{c_{it}}=R_{s,t}(c).
}
\]

Consequently, for any two such vertices `i,j`, in the actual reinforced stochastic process,

\[
M_{ij}(n)=
H_{W_{si}(n)-1}+H_{W_{it}(n)-1}
-H_{W_{sj}(n)-1}-H_{W_{jt}(n)-1}
\]

is an exact martingale, because reinforcing an edge of current raw weight `W` increments `H_{W-1}` by `1/W`, and the two terminal-pair ratios are identical. This generalizes the five-edge harmonic martingale from the preceding run.

## 4. B25 — forest factorization on the balanced suspension manifold

Consider a **two-terminal suspension graph**: terminals `s,t`, a finite interior set `I`, both terminal edges `si,it` for every `i\in I`, and an arbitrary finite internal graph `H` on `I`. Every `s-i-t` route is a geodesic of length two, whereas every edge of `H` is non-geodesic.

Write terminal conductances `l_i=c_{si}`, `r_i=c_{it}` with

\[
\sum_i l_i=\sum_i r_i=1,
\]

and internal conductances `h_e`.

On the balanced manifold

\[
l_i=r_i=a_i>0,
\qquad \sum_i a_i=1,
\]

the effective resistance is exactly

\[
\boxed{R_{s,t}=2.}
\]

Indeed, potential `0` at `s`, `1` at `t`, and `1/2` at every interior vertex puts zero voltage across all internal edges and carries total current `1/2`.

Now restrict an `s`-`t` separating two-forest to `H`. Each resulting internal component `C` has exactly one terminal attachment. The total weight for attaching it to `s` is

\[
A(C)=\sum_{i\in C}a_i,
\]

and the weight for attaching it to `t` is the same. Conditional on the internal forest, component root labels are therefore independent fair `s/t` coins. Thus the induced internal-forest measure has weight proportional to

\[
\left(\prod_{e\in F_H}h_e\right)
\prod_{C\in\operatorname{Comp}(F_H)}2A(C).
\]

For any positive internal edge `e=ij`, the dual identity then gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{p_e}{h_e}
=\Pr(i,j\text{ lie in different internal-forest components})<1.
}
\]

Strictness holds because `h_e>0` gives positive forest weight to configurations containing `e`, on which `i,j` lie in the same component. Therefore every positive non-geodesic internal edge has strictly negative mean drift on the balanced manifold:

\[
\boxed{p_e-h_e<0.}
\]

Meanwhile terminal symmetry gives `p_{si}=p_{it}=a_i`.

## 5. New theorem — global convergence of the loop-erased mean ODE on every suspension graph

Consider the stochastic-approximation mean ODE

\[
\dot c_e=p_e(c)-c_e.
\]

Assume every terminal coordinate starts positive, with both terminal sums normalized to one. Put

\[
q_i=l_i(0)r_i(0),
\qquad
S=\sum_j\sqrt{q_j},
\qquad
a_i=\frac{\sqrt{q_i}}{S}.
\]

### Theorem

For **every finite internal graph `H`**, including arbitrarily non-series-parallel ones,

\[
\boxed{l_i(t)\to a_i,\qquad r_i(t)\to a_i}
\]

for every interior vertex, and

\[
\boxed{h_e(t)\to0}
\]

for every internal non-geodesic edge.

### Proof

The terminal-pair identity gives

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log(l_i r_i)
=
\frac{p_{si}-l_i}{l_i}
+
\frac{p_{it}-r_i}{r_i}
=R_{s,t}-2,
\]

independently of `i`. Hence product ratios are invariants: for a scalar `\lambda(t)>0`,

\[
\boxed{l_i(t)r_i(t)=\lambda(t)q_i.}
\]

By Thomson's principle every unit `s`-`t` flow spends at least unit energy on the terminal cut at `s`:

\[
\sum_i\frac{f_{si}^2}{l_i}
\ge
\frac{(\sum_i f_{si})^2}{\sum_i l_i}=1,
\]

and likewise at least unit energy on the disjoint terminal cut at `t`. Internal edges add nonnegative energy. Thus

\[
\boxed{R_{s,t}\ge2,}
\]

so `\lambda(t)` is nondecreasing. But Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\sqrt{\lambda(t)}S
=
\sum_i\sqrt{l_i(t)r_i(t)}
\le1,
\]

hence `\lambda(t)\le S^{-2}`.

Suppose `\lambda_\infty<S^{-2}`. Then eventually the Hellinger overlap `\sum_i\sqrt{l_ir_i}` stays below `1-\eta`. Since `\lambda` is already bounded away from zero and every `q_i>0`, all terminal coordinates stay uniformly positive. Internal coordinates stay bounded because `\dot h_e\le1-h_e`. On this compact set, equality `R_{s,t}=2` is impossible: equality in both cut Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities forces the minimizing flow to have `f_{si}=l_i` and `f_{it}=r_i`, while the total energy budget leaves zero internal energy; flow conservation then gives `l_i=r_i` for all `i`, contradicting the positive Hellinger gap. Hence `R_{s,t}-2` has a strictly positive minimum there, forcing `\log\lambda` to grow linearly and contradicting boundedness. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\lambda_\infty=S^{-2}.}
\]

It follows that `\sum_i\sqrt{l_ir_i}\to1` and therefore

\[
\sum_i(\sqrt{l_i}-\sqrt{r_i})^2
=2-2\sum_i\sqrt{l_ir_i}\to0.
\]

Together with `l_ir_i\to a_i^2`, this proves `l_i,r_i\to a_i`.

Finally fix an internal edge `e` and `\varepsilon>0`. At the limiting balanced terminal vector `(a,a)`, B25 proved `p_e/h_e<1` whenever `h_e>0`. On the compact region `h_e\ge\varepsilon` this inequality has a uniform margin `\delta(\varepsilon)>0`. Since terminal coordinates converge to `(a,a)`, eventually

\[
\dot h_e
=h_e\left(\frac{p_e}{h_e}-1\right)
\le-\frac{\delta}{2}h_e
\]

whenever `h_e\ge\varepsilon`. Thus eventually `h_e` cannot cross upward through `\varepsilon`, so `\limsup h_e\le\varepsilon`. Letting `\varepsilon\downarrow0` gives `h_e(t)\to0`. ∎

This is a genuine theorem for a broad non-series-parallel class, but it is a **mean-ODE theorem**, not the full stochastic source theorem.

## 7. Exact remaining source-level gluing failure

The source problem is stochastic. The theorem above eliminates an interior **mean-field** obstruction for every suspension graph, but does not yet prove that the normalized reinforced process avoids boundary faces on which one or more geodesic terminal coordinates vanish.

Those boundary faces contain real non-geodesic ODE equilibria: if only a longer simple path retains positive normalized weights, every loop-erased ant can follow it and the corresponding mean field can be stationary. Therefore one cannot conclude the source theorem merely from interior ODE convergence.

The exact unresolved statement for this graph class is now:

> Starting from strictly positive edge weights, prove that the loop-erased reinforced stochastic process has probability zero of converging to any boundary face that gives positive limiting normalized mass to an internal non-geodesic edge.

The harmonic-number martingales from B11 are a concrete new tool for this boundary-avoidance problem, but no complete almost-sure exclusion is proved in this run.
