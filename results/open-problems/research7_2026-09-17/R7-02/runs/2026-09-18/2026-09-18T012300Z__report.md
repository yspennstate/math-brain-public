## 4. Why the two mathematical branches currently close

### 4.1 At most one rich direction: the source's second case is arithmetic-free

The strongest simplification from the previous run can be stated more abstractly than the source's `p^2` theorem.

The source Case 2 uses only the following inputs:

It does **not** use the prime-power vanishing-sum theorem, section divisibility, or the equality `|F|=p^2` after the case split. Replacing the valid dilation index set `{alpha:gcd(alpha,p)=1}` by `{alpha:gcd(alpha,6)=1}` leaves the argument unchanged. So this branch has no surviving six-specific arithmetic gap.

### 4.2 Two rich directions: the six-edge core is finite and exact

Let `g=nh`, with `h` primitive, be rich. The kernel `ker chi_g` is a finite union of circles. Infinitely many common zeros therefore lie on one circle. Restrict only `M_1` to that circle. The result is a Laurent polynomial in one unit-circle parameter. Infinitely many distinct zeros force that Laurent polynomial to vanish identically. Grouping coefficients by the parallel sections of `F` gives one common nonzero phase weight and zero sum on **every** nonempty section. Hence there are no singleton sections.

Two rich directions from the minimum cover are transverse. Their nonempty parallel sections form the two vertex classes of a simple bipartite incidence graph; the six points of `F` are its six edges. Minimum degree is at least two. The only possibilities are `K_{2,3}`, its transpose, and `C6`.

For `C6`, choose one rich direction's phase. Geometrically, intersections of two transverse parallel-line families have additive coordinates, so the phase at an occupied intersection factors as a nonzero row factor times a nonzero column factor. Relabel the `C6` so row `i` misses column `i`. After cancelling row factors the three row equations are

`z2+z3=0`, `z1+z3=0`, `z1+z2=0`.

They force `z1=0`, impossible for a phase. Therefore `C6` is excluded.

In the surviving `K_{2,3}` case, choosing primitive direction vectors `u,v` gives

`F = x0 + {a u + b v : a in A, b in B}`

with `|A|=3`, `|B|=2`. Full affine span gives the exact lattice-index identity

`1 = |det(u,v)| gcd(A-A) gcd(B-B)`,

so the basis is unimodular and both gcds are one. Since `F` is exact, the row/column fibre decomposition supplies one-dimensional prime-cardinality tilings. Their rigidity first forces the factors to be complete residue systems and then, by Khetan Lemma 3.7 with arbitrary codomain, forces one of the residue maps to be constant. Hence every tiling has a coordinate period.

This closes the finite branch without the defective formal Laurent cancellation and without Khetan Lemma 3.15.
