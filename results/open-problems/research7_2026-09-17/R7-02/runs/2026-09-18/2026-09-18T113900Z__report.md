## 4. Method B11 — dualize the lost series invariant

The chord destroys equality of the two edges in each subdivided branch, so the preceding scalar series state is gone. The seven path incidence vectors nevertheless admit an exact dual certificate: assign

\[
c_{sx}=c_{yt}=c_{ab}=\frac12,
\qquad c_e=0\text{ on all other edges}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every non-geodesic path contains exactly two of `{sx,yt,ab}`, while `R` contains none. Therefore every bad path has cost one and `R` has cost zero. In particular

\[
S_n:=\frac{w_{sx}+w_{yt}+w_{ab}}2
=\frac32+n-R_n.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define

\[
t_n=n+\frac32,
\qquad Y_n=\frac{R_n+1/2}{t_n}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `I_{n+1}` indicates that the next reinforced path is `R`, then exactly

\[
Y_{n+1}-Y_n=\frac{I_{n+1}-Y_n}{t_n+1}.
\tag{4.4}

This is the replacement invariant suggested by B11.

## 5. Method B16 — exact finite UST drift certificate

The exact checker enumerates all 35 spanning trees of the eight-edge graph and constructs `Z` and `N_R` over the integers. After substituting (3.1), the polynomial

\[

\[
\boxed{q_R>\frac{R+1/2}{n+3/2}=Y_n.}
\tag{5.2}

\[
\sum_n\frac{q_R(n)-Y_n}{t_n+1}<\infty
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.3}

Also `q_R>=1/(2t_n)`, so conditional Borel–Cantelli yields

\[
R_n\to\infty\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.4}
