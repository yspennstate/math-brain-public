## 3. B10 — derandomize the spectral branch down to one mask and one translation

### 3.1 Centering removes the trivial character cleanly

Let `X` be the orbit closure of an `F`-tiling `T`. Every point of `X` remains an `F`-tiling: for each `v`,

`Phi_v(x)=sum_{a in F}x(v-a)`

is a continuous cylinder function, equal to one on the orbit and hence on its closure.

An invariant probability measure on `X` exists without any source theorem: average a point mass over square Følner sets in `Z^2`, take a weak-star convergent subsequence by compactness, and note that translation changes only a boundary fraction tending to zero. Passing to an ergodic component gives an ergodic invariant `mu` supported on `X`.

Let `f(x)=x(0)`. The tiling equation gives

`sum_{a in F}U_a f=1`.

Taking expectation and `|F|=6` yields

`E_mu f=1/6`.

Put `f_c=f-1/6`. Then

`sum_{a in F}U_a f_c=0`.

For the original mask

`M(xi)=sum_{a in F}chi_a(xi)`,

the spectral measure `nu_c` of `f_c` therefore satisfies

`support(nu_c) subset Z(M)`.

Also

`||f_c||_2^2=Var(f)=1/6-1/36=5/36>0`,

so `nu_c` is nonzero. The trivial-character boundary is therefore completely separated from the nonconstant proof.

### 3.2 Only the finite kernel cover is needed

Use the finite character-kernel cover from the previously checked Khetan spectral interface and choose a cardinality-minimal `Delta` with

`support(nu_c) subset union_{g in Delta} ker chi_g`.

Call `g` **M-rich** when

`ker chi_g intersect Z(M)`

is infinite.

This definition uses only the original mask `M=M_1`. The entire downstream proof can now be split according to whether at most one or at least two members of `Delta` are M-rich. Khetan's dilation theorem is no longer needed after the finite cover has been obtained.

### 3.3 At most one M-rich direction

Assume at most one member `g_0` is M-rich. If none is rich, choose any `g_0`; `Delta` is nonempty because `nu_c` is nonzero. For every other `h`,

`ker chi_h intersect Z(M)`

is finite. Hence

`support(nu_c) subset ker chi_{g_0} union S`

for finite `S`.

Among all positive multiples `kg_0` admitting such a finite residual, choose one minimizing the residual cardinality; call the resulting vector `g`. Remove residual points already in `ker chi_g`. Then each remaining residual point is a genuine isolated spectral atom, and its eigenvalue `chi_g(s)` is not a root of unity. The proof is exact: a point not in support can be deleted; a finite residual point separated from the closed kernel is isolated and therefore atomic; if `chi_g(s)` has order `q`, passing from `g` to `qg` enlarges the kernel and absorbs `s`, contradicting residual minimality.

Group residual atoms with equal eigenvalue. Then

`f = 1/6 + f_0 + sum_{j=1}^m phi_j`,

with

`U_g f_0=f_0`, `U_g phi_j=gamma_j phi_j`,

where the `gamma_j` are distinct and non-torsion.

The finite-alphabet recurrence lemma from prior R7-02 work now gives a completely elementary endpoint. For almost every `x`,

`a_n=f(U_g^n x)=c(x)+sum_j gamma_j^n phi_j(x)`

is a bi-infinite sequence in the two-letter alphabet `{0,1}`. It satisfies the recurrence with characteristic polynomial

`(z-1) product_j(z-gamma_j)`.

Because the leading and constant coefficients are nonzero, a block of length `m+1` determines both its next and previous symbol. Only finitely many such blocks exist, so one repeats and the entire bi-infinite sequence is periodic with some period `L`. Therefore

`sum_j phi_j(x)(gamma_j^L-1)gamma_j^n=0`.

Taking `n=0,...,m-1` gives an invertible Vandermonde system; since no `gamma_j` is torsion, every `phi_j(x)=0`. Thus

`U_g f=f` almost surely.

Translations commute, so every coordinate function `U_v f` is also `g`-invariant. On a countable full-measure intersection,

`x(v+g)=x(v)` for every `v in Z^2`.

Hence almost every such `x` is a one-periodic point of `X`, and Section 3.1 already showed every point of `X` is an `F`-tiling.

**B10 outcome:** the one-rich branch is now downstream-dilation-free and also no longer requires Khetan's irrational-phase lemma or a separate periodicity-transfer theorem.

## 4. B20 — attack the hidden proof-transfer assumptions

### 4.1 Exact parallel-kernel compression

A previous draft used the shorthand “minimal kernel covers have pairwise nonparallel members.” The exact proof is:

if `g_1=m h`, `g_2=n h` with primitive `h`, and `L=lcm(|m|,|n|)`, then

`ker chi_{m h} union ker chi_{n h} subset ker chi_{L h}`.

Indeed `m<h,xi>` integral implies `L<h,xi>` integral, and similarly for `n`. Thus two parallel kernels can be replaced by one, contradicting cardinality minimality. Distinct members of a minimum cover are therefore genuinely transverse.

This removes a hidden geometry assumption from the two-rich branch.

### 4.2 One Laurent polynomial already gives the rich-section equations

Let `g=nh` be M-rich with primitive `h`. Its kernel is a finite union of one-dimensional torus components. One component contains infinitely many zeros of the original mask `M`. Parameterize that component as

`xi(t)=xi_0+t h^perp`.

Then

`M(xi(t))=sum_{a in F} exp(2 pi i<a,xi_0>) z^{<a,h^perp>}`,

`z=exp(2 pi i t)`,

is a Laurent polynomial with infinitely many unit-circle zeros, hence is identically zero. Grouping coefficients by `<a,h^perp>` gives for every nonempty section `E` parallel to `h`

`sum_{a in E} exp(2 pi i<a,xi_0>)=0`.

Therefore no such section is a singleton.

With two rich transverse kernels, the section-incidence graph is a simple six-edge bipartite graph of minimum degree at least two. The already executed exact finite certificate leaves only `K_{2,3}` / `K_{3,2}` and `C6`.

For `C6`, linearity of the phase on the affine intersection grid gives rank-one weights `lambda_i z_j`; cancelling the nonzero row factors leaves

`z_2+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_2=0`,

which forces a nonzero phase to equal zero. Thus `C6` is impossible.

The surviving `K_{2,3}` geometry is a `3 x 2` product in two primitive transverse directions. Full affine span forces the direction basis to be unimodular and both one-dimensional factor difference gcds to be one.

### 4.3 The product fibre decomposition also needs no imported lemma

For a product tile `A x B` and tiling complement `T`, define row fibres `S_y` and column fibres `T_x`. Fixing a target row coordinate `n`, unique representation of `(x,n)` immediately gives

`Z = A direct_sum (disjoint_union_{b in B} S_{n-b})`.

Fixing a column coordinate gives the analogous `B`-tiling. Thus the fibre decomposition is a one-line consequence of unique product tiling, not a source dependency.

Only the already source-checked prime-cardinality one-dimensional theorem is needed for the three-point factor. It gives each `A`-complement as a coset of `3Z`, and hence `A` itself is a complete residue system modulo three. The two-point factor is elementary because gcd one forces consecutive integers, whose complements are parity classes. The exact binary residue-profile argument from the preceding run then gives period `(3,0)` or `(0,2)`.

**B20 outcome:** the six-cell proof candidate no longer needs any downstream use of the dilation family. It also removes three additional imported interfaces: Khetan Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.11 and Lemma 3.6. ## 5. B29 — boundary cases and points at infinity

I explicitly checked the cases most likely to disappear in spectral shorthand.

1. **Origin atom:** center first. The nonconstant spectral measure is annihilated by `M`, while the mean is exactly `1/6`.
2. **Empty nonconstant spectrum:** impossible because `Var(f)=5/36`.
3. **No finite residual:** if `support(nu_c) subset ker chi_g`, periodicity follows immediately from `U_g f_c=f_c`.
4. **Residual point already in the kernel:** remove it; it is not exceptional.
5. **Torsion residual:** multiply `g` by the torsion order and absorb the point. Minimal residual cardinality forbids this case.
6. **Coincident residual eigenvalues:** group them before applying the recurrence; Vandermonde is applied to distinct eigenvalues, not distinct torus points.
7. **Residual accumulation:** impossible away from the closed kernel because the residual is finite. Each residual support point is therefore isolated and atomic; all accumulation belongs to the kernel projection.
8. **Parallel rich directions:** impossible in a minimum cover by the explicit lcm kernel-compression lemma.
9. **Orbit-closure limit not satisfying the tiling equation:** impossible because every tiling equation is a closed cylinder condition.
10. **Existence of the invariant ergodic measure:** square Følner averages give an invariant measure; an ergodic component supplies the measure used in the spectral argument.

No boundary case produced a counterexample or a fatal gap.
