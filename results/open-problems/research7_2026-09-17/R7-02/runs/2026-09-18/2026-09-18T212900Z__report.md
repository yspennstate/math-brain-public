### B20 — exact obstruction to an ordinary pathwise comparator

The natural alternative would be to seek a bounded same-mean law that is stochastically above or below `N`, because ordinary stochastic order would give a direct pathwise emigration coupling. This is impossible except when the laws are identical.

If `X<=st Y` are nonnegative integer-valued and `EX=EY<infinity`, then

\[
EY-EX
=\sum_{k\ge1}\bigl(P(Y\ge k)-P(X\ge k)\bigr)=0.
\]

Every summand is nonnegative, so every summand vanishes and `X` and `Y` have the same distribution. Hence a distinct bounded comparator at the same mean can never be obtained through stochastic domination.

For an explicit critical witness, take

\[
P(N=0)=1/3,\qquad P(N=3)=2/3.
\]

The universal endpoint comparator is then

\[
P(L_2=0)=1/6,
\quad P(L_2=2)=1/2,
\quad P(L_2=3)=1/3.
\]

At threshold one, `P(L_2>=1)=5/6>2/3=P(N>=1)`, while at threshold three, `P(N>=3)=2/3>1/3=P(L_2>=3)`. The laws are incomparable in stochastic order.

This isolates why convex order is the correct category. For increasing convex `phi`, if `S_r` is the sum of `r` geometric(1/2) offspring and `h(r)=E phi(S_r)`, then `h` is increasing convex. Hence

\[
n\mapsto h((x+1-n)_+)
\]

is convex, so `L<=cx N` implies `K_L phi(x)<=K_N phi(x)`, and the transition kernel preserves increasing convexity in `x`. Inductively,

\[
\boxed{Z^L_n\le_{\rm icx} Z^N_n\quad\text{for every }n,}
\]

in particular `E Z^L_n <= E Z^N_n`.

**B20 outcome: SUCCESS.** The failure of the pathwise same-mean algorithm is now proved exactly, and the convex-order route is shown to be structurally necessary rather than stylistic.

### B25 — exact first-return / Abel identity, eliminating the renewal-reward black box

For any scalar chain `Z` started at zero, let

\[
\sigma=\inf\{n\ge1:Z_n=0\}.
\]

For a nonnegative reward `r`, define

\[
u_n=E_0r(Z_n),
\quad
b_n=E_0[r(Z_n);\sigma>n],
\quad
f_k=P_0(\sigma=k).
\]

Counting the expected reward at time `n` according to whether the first cycle is still alive or first returned at time `k` gives the exact convolution

\[
\boxed{u_n=b_n+\sum_{k=1}^n f_k u_{n-k}.}
\]

Therefore, for `0<s<1`,

\[
U_r(s):=\sum_{n\ge0}u_ns^n,
\quad
B_r(s):=\sum_{n\ge0}b_ns^n,
\quad
F(s):=E_0s^\sigma
\]

satisfy

\[
\boxed{U_r(s)=\frac{B_r(s)}{1-F(s)},}
\]

with

\[
B_r(s)=E_0\sum_{n<\sigma}r(Z_n)s^n.
\]

If `E sigma<infinity` and the first-cycle reward `A_r=sum_{n<sigma}r(Z_n)` is integrable, then

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{s\uparrow1}(1-s)U_r(s)
=\frac{EA_r}{E\sigma}.
}
\]

Apply this to the bounded comparator with `r_R(x)=x wedge R`. Its cycle length has finite mean and its untruncated total progeny has infinite mean, so letting `R` grow forces

\[
\lim_{s\uparrow1}(1-s)
\sum_{n\ge0}E_0Z^L_ns^n=\infty.
\]

Fixed-time increasing-convex domination transfers this Abel divergence to `Z^N`.

The arbitrary `N` chain also has `E sigma_N<infinity`: since `EN=mu>1`, choose finite `M` with `E(N wedge M)>1`; the bounded chain with emigration `N wedge M` has finite mean return time by Theorem 5.4, and the full-`N` chain has more emigration and is pathwise no larger. If `EA_N` were finite, the same exact Abel identity with reward `r(x)=x` would instead give the finite limit `EA_N/E sigma_N`, a contradiction. Therefore

\[
\boxed{EA_N=\infty\quad\text{for every }1<EN\le2,\ P(N=0)>0.}
\]

This proof uses neither an invariant distribution nor a Cesaro renewal-reward theorem.

**B25 outcome: SUCCESS.**
