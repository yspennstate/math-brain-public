### Exact source scope

Let `G=(V,E)` be any finite graph with distinct marked vertices `N` (nest) and `F` (food). Edge weights are initially positive (the parent paper takes all weights initially equal to one). Successive walkers start at `N`, move as a random walk with transition probabilities proportional to current edge weights, and are stopped at their first visit to `F`. After a walk, one of two reinforcement rules is applied:

1. **uniform-geodesic rule:** among the shortest `N`–`F` paths contained in the subgraph visited by that walk, choose one uniformly and add one to every edge on that path;
2. **loop-erased rule:** reverse the `N`–`F` trajectory and loop-erase it as in the parent paper, producing a simple `F`–`N` path, then add one to every edge on that path.

The 2025 source’s main conjecture is that, on **every finite graph**, the only edges whose weights grow linearly are edges lying on a geodesic between `N` and `F`. Thus a source-level refutation may be a single finite graph/rule with a non-geodesic edge having positive linear asymptotic weight; a proof must cover the stated general finite-graph scope for both rules.

The stronger parent formulation is Daniel Kious, Cécile Mailler, Bruno Schapira, **Finding geodesics on graphs using reinforcement learning**, *Annals of Applied Probability* 32(5) (2022), 3889–3929, DOI `10.1214/21-AAP1777`, arXiv `2010.04820`, Conjecture 1.1. It asks for almost-sure convergence of every normalized edge weight and, for the loop-erased rule, positive limiting weight exactly on the union of geodesics. This stronger parent scope is useful but is not silently substituted for the canonical 2025 source statement.

## Published partial boundary and prior internal attempt

The parent paper proves the loop-erased conjecture on every series-parallel graph (Theorem 1.3) and the uniform-geodesic conjecture on the five-edge non-series-parallel losange graph (Theorem 1.4).
