## B11 — dualize through coefficient granularity and active coordinates

Instead of attacking all `n` coordinates directly, exploit the arithmetic dual information imposed by Booleanity and low degree.

For `f:{-1,1}^n->{-1,1}` of degree at most `d`, put `g=(1-f)/2`, change variables `z_i=(1-x_i)/2`, and expand `g` in the multilinear `z`-basis. Möbius inversion makes every coefficient in this basis an integer. Substituting back into the Fourier basis shows every nonconstant Fourier coefficient of `f` lies in `2^(1-d) Z`. Hence at degree three every nonzero singleton coefficient has magnitude at least `1/4`.

After sign-flipping coordinates, all nonzero singleton coefficients may be assumed positive. Parseval then bounds their number by 16. If `A` is this active set and `B=sum_{i in A} fhat({i})`, average over restrictions of all inactive coordinates. The signed singleton sum of the restricted function has expectation exactly `B`, so some restriction on the active coordinates has signed singleton sum at least `B`. Thus the universal degree-three question reduces, without changing the source quantifiers, to an exact finite family on at most 16 coordinates.

This is a reduction, not yet a finite-`n` surrogate: the reduction must be proved uniformly for arbitrary original `n`.

## B14 — quotient by the full coordinate-permutation group

For a Boolean restriction `h` on `m<=16` coordinates, average `h` over all coordinate permutations. The symmetrized function remains degree at most three, takes values in `[-1,1]`, and preserves the signed singleton sum.

Let `k` denote the number of `-1` coordinates. For each Hamming layer define the integer layer sum

`a_k = sum_{x: #minus(x)=k} h(x)`

and normalized layer average

`g_k = a_k / binom(m,k)`.

Necessarily `|a_k| <= binom(m,k)` and `a_k` has the same parity as `binom(m,k)`. A symmetric multilinear polynomial of degree at most three is a linear combination of Krawtchouk polynomials of degrees 0,1,2,3; equivalently `g_k` is a polynomial of degree at most three in `k`. Hence its fourth finite difference vanishes:

`g_{k+4} - 4 g_{k+3} + 6 g_{k+2} - 4 g_{k+1} + g_k = 0`.

The objective is exactly

`L(h)=sum_i hhat({i}) = 2^{-m} sum_k binom(m,k) g_k (m-2k)`.

Therefore every actual Boolean degree-three `h` produces an integer-lattice cubic layer sequence. Enumerating all admissible first four layer sums and propagating the exact recurrence gives a finite *superset* of symmetrized Boolean possibilities; an upper bound on this superset is therefore a valid upper bound on every actual `h`.

## B24 — identify and force the extremizer

Three-bit majority has singleton coefficients `1/2,1/2,1/2`, hence signed and absolute singleton sum `3/2`. It is the candidate extremizer.

The finite exact layer calculation should determine, for each `1<=m<=16`, the largest objective among all integer-lattice cubic layer sequences. The hoped-for pattern is:

- for `m<=9`, maximum at most `3/2`;
- for `m>=10`, even a weaker upper bound `<=2` suffices, because an original function with `m` active singleton coefficients has `B>=m/4>=5/2` by granularity, contradicting the restriction upper bound.

Thus the checker need not classify realizability of every cubic layer sequence. It needs only exact rational enumeration of the necessary layer constraints, with no floating-point arithmetic. If those bounds hold, they combine with the all-`n` reduction to give a complete proof of the sharp degree-three theorem.
