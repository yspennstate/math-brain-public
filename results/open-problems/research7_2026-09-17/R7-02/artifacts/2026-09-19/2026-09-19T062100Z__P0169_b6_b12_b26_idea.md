## B6 — deform the degree-four saturation mechanism to degree five

For a Boolean degree-five function, every nonconstant Fourier coefficient is in `(1/16) Z`. After orienting input signs, every active singleton coefficient is therefore at least `1/16`, and the signed singleton objective is itself a multiple of `1/16`.

Since `35/16 = 2.1875 < sqrt(5) < 36/16 = 2.25`, the weak source inequality at `d=5` would follow from the stronger lattice statement `sum_i |fhat({i})| <= 35/16`.

Symmetrizing on `m` variables gives exact integer Newton data

`r(k)=sum_{j=0}^5 u_j binom(k,j)/binom(m,j)`,

`b_k=sum_{j=0}^5 u_j binom(m-j,k-j)`,

with every `u_j` integral and `0<=b_k<=binom(m,k)`. The signed singleton objective is

`L=-u_1-u_2-(3/4)u_3-(1/2)u_4-(5/16)u_5`.

The first exact test is the integer-lattice relaxation through at least `m=35`. If its maximum is at most `35/16` at `m=35`, every 35 active coordinates in any larger oriented function must saturate. Pointwise saturation under every outside restriction would force, for every 35-subset `T` and every nonempty disjoint `R` with `|R|<=4`,

`sum_{i in T} fhat(R union {i})=0`.

Comparing 35-subsets would annihilate level 2 for `m>=37`, level 3 for `m>=38`, level 4 for `m>=39`, and level 5 for `m>=40`, making `m>=40` impossible. Residual support sizes would then be finite cases.

If the 35-variable relaxation exceeds `35/16`, its exact witness diagnoses what breaks the degree-four mechanism.

## B12 — identify the invariant carried by saturation

The useful invariant is pointwise block saturation, not only total singleton mass. When a finite-core bound is saturated after every outside restriction, the block linear mass is an identically constant function of the outside coordinates; all nonconstant outside Fourier coefficients vanish, giving the subset-sum identities above.

For residual supports near the annihilation threshold, scale Fourier coefficients by 16. The global sign-flip difference isolates the odd part:

`16 f(x)-16 f(-x)=2 H_odd(x)`,

so `H_odd(x)` is constrained to `{-16,0,16}`. Summed complement identities may then force mod-16 contradictions analogous to the degree-four `m=17` argument.

## B26 — hunt the sporadic case first

A degree-five counterexample to the weak inequality must have oriented linear mass at least `36/16=9/4`, because the objective lies on the `1/16` lattice. Before trying to prove universality, search exact structured families for a Boolean degree-five function with mass at least `9/4`: symmetric or nearly symmetric profiles, compositions of the degree-four seven-variable `7/4` example, address/multiplexer functions, and realizable witnesses suggested by the layer relaxation.

A genuine mass `>=9/4` witness refutes the weak `d=5` clause. A bounded failed search is only evidence.
