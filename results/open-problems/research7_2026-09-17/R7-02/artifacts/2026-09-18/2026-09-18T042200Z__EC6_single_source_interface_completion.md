`sum_{a in F} U_a f_c=0`,

## 2. B21 — finite alphabet plus finite spectral residual implies a genuine period

### Lemma 2.1 (finite alphabet forces every active isolated eigenvalue to be torsion)

Let `U` be an invertible measure-preserving transformation and let `f` take values in a finite set. Suppose

`f=f_0+sum_{j=1}^m phi_j`,

where `Uf_0=f_0`, `U phi_j=gamma_j phi_j`, and the nonzero `gamma_j != 1` are pairwise distinct. Then every `gamma_j` for which `phi_j` is nonzero is a root of unity. Consequently there is `q>=1` such that `U^q f=f` almost surely.

### Proof

Take a common full-measure set on which all translated eigenfunction identities hold for every integer. Fix `x` there and define

`a_n=f(U^n x)=f_0(x)+sum_j gamma_j^n phi_j(x)`, `n in Z`.

The sequence takes values in a finite alphabet and satisfies the linear recurrence with characteristic polynomial

`Q(z)=(z-1) product_j (z-gamma_j)`.

Its leading and constant coefficients are nonzero, so any block of `m+1` consecutive values determines uniquely both the next and previous values. There are only finitely many possible such blocks. Two blocks repeat, and forward/backward determinism makes the entire bi-infinite sequence periodic, say with period `L=L(x)>0`.

Thus for every integer `n`,

`sum_j phi_j(x)(gamma_j^L-1) gamma_j^n=0`.

For `n=0,...,m-1`, the Vandermonde matrix on the distinct `gamma_j` is invertible. Hence `phi_j(x)(gamma_j^L-1)=0` for every `j`. If `phi_j(x) !=0`, then `gamma_j^L=1`.

Only finitely many `gamma_j` occur globally. Let `q` be the lcm of the orders of those roots of unity whose eigenfunctions are not identically zero. Then `U^q phi_j=phi_j` for all active `j`, while `U^q f_0=f_0`. Therefore `U^q f=f` almost surely. QED.

## 3. B3 — prove the remaining one-dimensional prime-three input from scratch

### Lemma 3.1 (three-point exact tilings of Z are 3-periodic)

Let `A subset Z` have `|A|=3` and `gcd(A-A)=1`. If `A direct_sum T=Z`, then `A` is a complete residue system modulo `3` and `T` is a coset of `3Z`.

### Proof

Translate `A` so that `A={0,a,b}`, `0<a<b`, `gcd(a,b)=1`. Let `t_n=1_T(n)`. Unique tiling is exactly

`t_n+t_{n-a}+t_{n-b}=1` for every `n in Z`.   (1)

Consider the finite state `S_n=(t_{n-b+1},...,t_n) in {0,1}^b`. Equation (1) determines the next bit by

`t_{n+1}=1-t_{n+1-a}-t_{n+1-b}`,

and determines the preceding bit by

`t_{n-b}=1-t_n-t_{n-a}`.

Thus state evolution is deterministic in both time directions on visited states. Since there are finitely many states, one repeats; forward and backward determinism imply that `t` is bi-infinite periodic. Let `q` be a period.

Reduce (1) modulo `q`. Summing over one period gives `3 |T mod q|=q`, so `3` divides `q`. Let `zeta=exp(2 pi i/q)`. Fourier transforming the cyclic tiling identity gives, for every nonzero frequency `k`,

`(1+zeta^{ka}+zeta^{kb}) T_hat(k)=0`.   (2)

If `|z|=1` and `1+z^a+z^b=0`, then the three unit vectors form an equilateral triangle. Hence `z^{3a}=z^{3b}=1`. Since `gcd(a,b)=1`, Bezout gives `z^3=1`; because the sum is zero, `z !=1`. Thus every nontrivial unit-circle zero of `1+z^a+z^b` is a primitive cube root of unity.

There must be at least one nontrivial `q`-th-root zero of the mask. Otherwise (2) makes every nonzero Fourier coefficient of the binary periodic sequence `t` vanish, so `t` would be the constant `1/3`, impossible. Therefore the primitive cube roots are mask zeros, and `A` has all three residues modulo `3`.

Moreover those two primitive cube roots are the only possible nontrivial `q`-th-root mask zeros. Equation (2) therefore shows that the Fourier support of `t` is contained in the three frequencies `0,q/3,2q/3`. Every character at those frequencies is invariant under translation by `3`, so Fourier inversion gives `t_{n+3}=t_n` for every `n`. The density is `1/3`, hence exactly one residue class modulo `3` is occupied. Therefore `T` is a coset of `3Z`. QED.

## 4. The resulting one-interface six-cell theorem

### Theorem 4.1

Let `F subset Z^2` have `|F|=6` and full affine span. Let `T` be an exact `F`-tiling and `X=cl(Z^2 . T)`. Assume only the finite character-kernel spectral cover supplied by Khetan Theorem 3.16. Then `X` contains a one-periodic `F`-tiling.

### Proof

Sections 1-2 prove the conclusion if the compressed finite kernel cover has at most one rich direction. Assume it has two transverse rich directions `h_1,h_2`.

For one rich vector `g=nh` with primitive direction `h`, a component of `ker chi_g` contains infinitely many zeros of the original mask `M`. Parameterize that one-dimensional component by `xi(t)=xi_0+t h^perp`. Then

`M(xi(t))=sum_{x in F} exp(2 pi i <x,xi_0>) z^{<x,h^perp>}`

is a Laurent polynomial in `z=exp(2 pi i t)` with infinitely many unit-circle zeros, hence is identically zero. Grouping coefficients by `<x,h^perp>` shows that on every nonempty section `E` of `F` parallel to `h`,

`sum_{x in E} exp(2 pi i <x,xi_0>)=0`.

No such section can be a singleton.

Apply this to both transverse rich directions. Build the bipartite incidence graph whose vertices are the nonempty parallel sections in the two directions and whose six edges are the six points of `F`. The graph is simple because two transverse lines meet in at most one point. Every vertex has degree at least two. Full affine span rules out having only one section on either side. Thus each side has two or three vertices. With six edges, the only possibilities are `K_{2,3}` or `K_{3,2}`, or `C_6` when both sides have three vertices.

The `C_6` case is impossible. Relabel the three rows and columns so row `i` misses column `i`. On the affine intersection grid the phase factors as `lambda_i z_j` with all factors nonzero. The three row cancellation equations become

`z_2+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_3=0`, `z_1+z_2=0`.

The first two give `z_1=z_2`; the third gives `2z_1=0`, contradiction.

Hence the incidence graph is `K_{2,3}` up to swapping directions. Choose primitive integer direction vectors `u,v`; after translation

`F={a u+b v : a in A, b in B}`

with `|A|=3`, `|B|=2`. Put `d_A=gcd(A-A)`, `d_B=gcd(B-B)`. The subgroup generated by `F-F` is `d_A Z u + d_B Z v`, whose index in `Z^2` is `|det(u,v)| d_A d_B`. Full affine span makes this index one. Hence `u,v` are a unimodular basis and `d_A=d_B=1`. In these coordinates the tile is `A x B` with the hypotheses of Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2.

For an anchor set `T subset Z^2` tiling by `A x B`, define row fibres `S_y={x:(x,y) in T}` and column fibres `T_x={y:(x,y) in T}`. For every `n,m`, uniqueness gives

`R_n = disjoint_union_{b in B} S_{n-b}`, with `A direct_sum R_n=Z`,

`C_m = disjoint_union_{a in A} T_{m-a}`, with `B direct_sum C_m=Z`.

By Lemmas 3.1-3.2 there are residues `r_n in Z/3Z`, `s_m in Z/2Z` such that `R_n=3Z+r_n`, `C_m=2Z+s_m`.

If `s_m` is constant, every occupied row has the same parity. Fix an occupied row `S_y` and `b in B`, and put `n=y+b`. In `R_n`, any nonempty summand `S_{n-b'}` must have the occupied parity, so `b'` has the same parity as `b`. Since `B` contains exactly one representative of each parity, `b'=b`. Thus `S_y=R_n` is a full coset of `3Z`. Every occupied row is invariant under horizontal translation by `3`, so `(3,0)` is a period.

If `s_m` is nonconstant, its binary image contains both parities. Every row is then occupied: for the parity of a given `y`, choose `m` with `s_m` equal to it; since `C_m=2Z+s_m`, membership of `y` in `C_m` produces an anchor on row `y`. Write `B={b_0,b_1}`. Since `gcd(B-B)=1`, `|b_1-b_0|=1`. Every nonempty row `S_y` lies in both cosets `R_{y+b_0}` and `R_{y+b_1}`, so those cosets coincide. Their indices differ by one, hence `r_n` is constant. Therefore every anchor has one fixed first-coordinate residue modulo `3`. Repeating the uniqueness argument in `C_m`, using that `A` has exactly one representative of each residue modulo `3`, shows every occupied column is a full coset of `2Z`. Thus `(0,2)` is a period.

In both cases the original tiling itself is one-periodic; in particular its orbit closure contains a one-periodic tiling. QED.
