## 1. Idea before calculation

The preceding R7-05 theorem solved the infinite-stack gluing problem for finite-state spatial Markov environments by inserting a bounded phase Poisson corrector into the Lamperti function `k^{-beta}`. The finite-state hypothesis entered only through one fact: the centered local drift had a bounded coboundary corrector.

The natural deformation is therefore not to approximate an arbitrary dependent environment by a finite-state chain. It is to retain exactly the property used by the proof.

Let `Y_n` be the centered finite-prefix drift of a deliberately weakened positive-cookie baseline and let `F_n` be the sigma-field generated by the spatial environment through site `n`. If

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}\left\|E[Y_{n+r}\mid\mathcal F_n]\right\|_\infty<\infty,
\tag{1.1}
\]

then the Gordin series gives a bounded random corrector. The key observation is that this corrector is adapted to the **actual environmental history**. Consequently the resulting high-population survival estimate remains true after an arbitrary finite stopping time of the forward branching process. This is exactly what is needed at the exit-biased high boundary; no density estimate for the exit environment is necessary.

Summable `phi`-mixing is a concrete infinite-memory sufficient condition for (1.1), so the theorem below strictly extends the preceding finite-state Markov result along the dependence axis.

## 2. Stationary positive infinite-stack setting

Let

\[
\eta_x=(p_{x,1},p_{x,2},\ldots),\qquad x\in\mathbb Z,
\]

be a stationary ergodic two-sided sequence of positive cookie stacks, with

\[
\frac12\le p_{x,i}\le1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Write

\[
\alpha_{x,i}=2p_{x,i}-1\in[0,1].
\]

Assume the stationary mean total drift is

\[
\bar\delta
:=
E\left[\sum_{i\ge1}\alpha_{0,i}\right]
\in(1,\infty].
\tag{2.2}
\]

By monotone convergence this is also

\[
\bar\delta
=
\lim_{m\to\infty}
E\left[\sum_{i=1}^m\alpha_{0,i}\right].
\tag{2.3}
\]

For `M>=1`, let the neutral-tail truncation be

\[
p^{(M)}_{x,i}
=
\begin{cases}
p_{x,i},&i\le M,\\
1/2,&i>M.
\end{cases}
\tag{2.4}
\]

The forward branching process uses the stack at successive spatial sites. If `V_n=k`, expose the independent cookie coins at site `n` and let `V_{n+1}` be the number of successes before the `k`-th failure. Cookie-coin uniforms are independent of the whole environment. State zero is absorbing.

The full and truncated forward survival probabilities will be denoted by `h` and `h_M`; the corresponding annealed never-return probabilities of the excited walk are `p_esc^(infinity)` and `p_esc^(M)`.

## 3. A weakened finite-depth baseline

Fix

\[
0<\beta<\bar\delta-1
\tag{3.1}
\]

when `bar(delta)<infinity`; if `bar(delta)=infinity`, fix any finite `beta>0`.

Choose `m_0` so that

\[
d_{m_0}
:=
E\sum_{i=1}^{m_0}\alpha_{0,i}
>1+\beta.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Choose `t in (0,1)` sufficiently close to one that

\[
\bar\delta_q:=t d_{m_0}>1+\beta.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Define the stationary weakened baseline

\[
q_{x,i}
=
\begin{cases}
\frac12+t\left(p_{x,i}-\frac12\right),&i\le m_0,\\
\frac12,&i>m_0.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.4}
\]

and its site drift

\[
\delta_x^q
=t\sum_{i=1}^{m_0}\alpha_{x,i}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then

\[
\frac12\le q_{x,i}\le\frac{1+t}{2}<1,
\qquad
E\delta_0^q=\bar\delta_q>1+\beta.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For every `M>=m_0`, coordinatewise

\[
q_{x,i}\le p^{(M)}_{x,i}\le p_{x,i}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus one common finite-depth baseline is dominated by both the full stack and every sufficiently deep truncation.

Set

\[
Y_n=\delta_n^q-\bar\delta_q.
\tag{3.8}
\]

This is bounded and centered.

## 4. Projective-corrector hypothesis and the Gordin identity

Let

\[
\mathcal F_n=\sigma(\eta_j:j\le n).
\]

Assume the bounded projective condition

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
\left\|E[Y_r\mid\mathcal F_0]\right\|_\infty<\infty.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

By stationarity the same bound holds after every spatial shift. Define

\[
G_n
=
\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
E[Y_{n+r}\mid\mathcal F_n].
\tag{4.2}
\]

The series converges in `L^infinity`, and

\[
\|G_n\|_\infty\le C_G<\infty
\tag{4.3}
\]

uniformly in `n`. Moreover

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[G_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]
&=
\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}E[Y_{n+1+r}\mid\mathcal F_n]\\
&=G_n-Y_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Define

\[
c_n=-\beta G_n.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then `c_n` is adapted, uniformly bounded, and satisfies the exact cancellation identity

\[
\boxed{
E[c_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]
=c_n+\beta Y_n
=c_n+\beta(\delta_n^q-\bar\delta_q).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the infinite-memory analogue of the finite-state Poisson equation from the previous R7-05 theorem.

### Summably phi-mixing corollary to the hypothesis

Suppose the stationary stack sequence is `phi`-mixing and, in the standard two-sided convention,

\[
\sum_{r\ge1}\phi(r)<\infty.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `Y_r` is a bounded centered function of the stack at site `r`, the standard conditional mixing estimate gives

\[
\left\|E[Y_r\mid\mathcal F_0]\right\|_\infty
\le 2\|Y_0\|_\infty\phi(r).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Hence (4.7) implies (4.1). In particular, the theorem below applies to summably `phi`-mixing stack processes without any finite-state or Markov assumption.

## 5. Large-population moments for the baseline

For `k>=m_0`, the finite-depth positive baseline has the exact one-step pgf

\[
E[z^{V_{n+1}}\mid\mathcal F_n,V_n=k]
=T(z)^k B_n^q(z),
\qquad
T(z)=\frac1{2-z},
\tag{5.1}
\]

where

\[
B_n^q(z)
=
\prod_{i=1}^{m_0}
\left((1-t\alpha_{n,i})+t\alpha_{n,i}z\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
V_{n+1}\stackrel d=G_1+\cdots+G_k+Z_n,
\tag{5.3}
\]

conditional on `F_n`, where `G_i` are i.i.d. geometric counts of successes before one fair failure,

\[
P(G_i=j)=2^{-(j+1)},\qquad j\ge0,
\tag{5.4}
\]

and `Z_n` is a sum of at most `m_0` Bernoulli variables with mean `delta_n^q`, independent of the `G_i` conditional on the environment.

Let

\[
\Delta_n=V_{n+1}-V_n.
\]

Uniformly in the environmental history and in `k>=m_0`,

\[
E[\Delta_n\mid\mathcal F_n,V_n=k]=\delta_n^q,
\tag{5.5}
\]

\[
E[\Delta_n^2\mid\mathcal F_n,V_n=k]=2k+O(1),
\tag{5.6}
\]

\[
E[|\Delta_n|^3\mid\mathcal F_n,V_n=k]=O(k^{3/2}),
\tag{5.7}
\]

and a geometric Chernoff bound gives constants `C_0,c_0>0`, depending only on `m_0,t`, such that

\[
P(|\Delta_n|>k/2\mid\mathcal F_n,V_n=k)
\le C_0e^{-c_0k}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

## 6. B6 — deform the finite-state corrector into a bounded random corrector

Let `H_n` be the joint filtration containing the environment through site `n`, all cookie randomness already used, and `V_n`. Choose

\[
a>2\sup_n\|c_n\|_\infty+2
\tag{6.1}
\]

and define the adapted function

\[
F_n(k)
=(k+a)^{-\beta}
\left(1+\frac{c_n}{k+a}\right).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
\frac12(k+a)^{-\beta}
\le F_n(k)
\le\frac32(k+a)^{-\beta}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — projective-corrector drift cancellation

Uniformly over admissible environmental histories,

\[
\boxed{
E[F_{n+1}(V_{n+1})\mid\mathcal H_n]-F_n(V_n)
=
\beta(\beta+1-\bar\delta_q)
(k+a)^{-\beta-1}
+o(k^{-\beta-1})
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

as `k=V_n -> infinity`.

#### Proof

Put `y=k+a`. On `|Delta_n|<=k/2`, Taylor expansion gives

\[
(V_{n+1}+a)^{-\beta}
=y^{-\beta}
-\beta y^{-\beta-1}\Delta_n
+\frac{\beta(\beta+1)}2y^{-\beta-2}\Delta_n^2
+R_n,
\tag{6.5}
\]

where, by (5.7),

\[
E[|R_n|\mid\mathcal H_n]
=O(k^{-\beta-3/2}).
\tag{6.6}
\]

The complementary event contributes exponentially little by (5.8). Therefore (5.5)-(5.6) yield

\[
E[(V_{n+1}+a)^{-\beta}\mid\mathcal H_n]
=
y^{-\beta}
+\beta(\beta+1-\delta_n^q)y^{-\beta-1}
+o(y^{-\beta-1}).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Likewise

\[
E[(V_{n+1}+a)^{-\beta-1}\mid\mathcal H_n]
=y^{-\beta-1}+o(y^{-\beta-1}).
\tag{6.8}
\]

Conditional on `F_n`, the current-site cookie uniforms determining `V_{n+1}` are independent of the environment variables determining `c_{n+1}`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
&E[c_{n+1}(V_{n+1}+a)^{-\beta-1}\mid\mathcal H_n]\\
&\qquad=
E[c_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]
\left(y^{-\beta-1}+o(y^{-\beta-1})\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.9}
\]

The boundedness of `c` makes the error uniform. Subtracting `F_n(k)` and using (4.6), the coefficient of `y^{-beta-1}` is

\[
\beta(\beta+1-\delta_n^q)
+\beta(\delta_n^q-\bar\delta_q)
=
\beta(\beta+1-\bar\delta_q),
\]

which proves (6.4). QED.

Because `bar(delta)_q>1+beta`, the coefficient in (6.4) is strictly negative. Thus there exists a deterministic `L<infinity` such that whenever `V_n>L`,

\[
E[F_{n+1}(V_{n+1})\mid\mathcal H_n]
\le F_n(V_n).
\tag{6.10}
\]

## 7. B28 — a history-typical bound, valid after adaptive exits

For a deterministic or random starting time `sigma`, define

\[
\tau_L^{(\sigma)}
=
\inf\{n\ge\sigma:V_n\le L\}.
\]

Apply optional stopping to the nonnegative supermartingale

\[
F_{n\wedge\tau_L^{(\sigma)}}(V_{n\wedge\tau_L^{(\sigma)}}).
\]

On the hitting event, (6.3) gives

\[
F_{\tau_L^{(\sigma)}}(V_{\tau_L^{(\sigma)}})
\ge\frac12(L+a)^{-\beta},
\]

while at the starting time

\[
F_\sigma(k)\le\frac32(k+a)^{-\beta}.
\]

Therefore, for every finite stopping time `sigma` and every `k>L`, almost surely on `{V_sigma=k}`,

\[
\boxed{
P_q(\tau_L^{(\sigma)}<\infty\mid\mathcal H_\sigma)
\le
3\left(\frac{L+a}{k+a}\right)^\beta.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Extinction forces a visit to population at most `L`. Hence the baseline survival probability, **conditional on the actual environmental history at the stopping time**, satisfies

\[
\boxed{
P_q(V_n>0\text{ for all }n\ge\sigma\mid\mathcal H_\sigma,V_\sigma=k)
\ge
1-C_\beta(1+k)^{-\beta}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is the point at which the proof genuinely goes beyond the finite-state argument. We do not require a bound uniform over every deterministic environment. We require only a bound along histories that occur under the stationary process, and (7.2) remains true after the adaptive high-population exit that biases those histories.

## 8. B30 — one-scale gluing of infinite stacks to finite truncations

Fix `K>=2`. Starting from a population `1<=k<K`, the censored next population, with outputs

\[
\{0,1,\ldots,K-1,H\},
\]

where `H` means at least `K`, is determined once either the `k`-th failure or the `K`-th success appears. Therefore at most

\[
k+K-1\le2K-2
\tag{8.1}
\]

cookies at the current site are inspected.

Consequently, if

\[
M\ge2K-2,
\tag{8.2}
\]

the full environment and its neutral-tail truncation have **identical stopped dynamics below population `K`**, pathwise under the natural common-uniform coupling. Let

\[
\tau_K
=
\inf\{n:V_n=0\text{ or }V_n\ge K\}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

As established in the preceding R7-05 finite-visibility theorem, positive cookies imply `tau_K<infinity` almost surely; before exit, the first `K` tosses are all successes with conditional probability at least `2^{-K}`.

On the high-exit event `V_{tau_K}>=K`, both the full process and the depth-`M` process dominate the same baseline `q`. Apply (7.2) at the stopping time `tau_K` to the baseline continued from the common exit history. Conditional on that exit history, both survival probabilities lie between

\[
1-C_\beta K^{-\beta}
\quad\text{and}\quad1.
\tag{8.4}
\]

On the zero-exit event both survival probabilities are zero. Since their stopped paths and exit histories are identical, averaging gives

\[
\boxed{
0\le h-h_M\le C_\beta K^{-\beta}
\qquad(M\ge2K-2).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Choose

\[
K_M=\left\lfloor\frac M2\right\rfloor+1.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Then `2K_M-2<=M`, and hence

\[
\boxed{
0\le h-h_M\le C_\beta M^{-\beta}.
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

The standard finite-interval forward/backward identity used in the earlier R7-05 work transfers the same bound to the walk's never-return probability:

\[
\boxed{
0\le
p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}-p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}
\le C_\beta M^{-\beta},
}
\tag{8.8}
\]

for all sufficiently large `M`. Positivity already gives monotonicity in `M`, so

\[
\boxed{
p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}\uparrow p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}.}
\tag{8.9}
\]

## 9. Main theorem

### Theorem 9.1 — projective-corrector infinite-stack continuity

Consider a stationary ergodic positive cookie environment satisfying (2.1)-(2.2). Fix `beta<bar(delta)-1` and construct the weakened finite-prefix baseline (3.4). If its centered drift sequence satisfies the bounded projective condition (4.1), then the neutral-tail truncations satisfy

\[
0\le
p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}-p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}
\le C_\beta M^{-\beta}
\tag{9.1}
\]

for all sufficiently large `M`, and therefore

\[
p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}\uparrow p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

If `bar(delta)=infinity`, (9.1) holds for every prescribed finite `beta>0` after choosing a sufficiently long finite prefix.

Cookies of strength one are allowed in this continuity theorem.

### Corollary 9.2 — summably phi-mixing infinite stacks

If the stationary stack sequence is summably `phi`-mixing, then (4.1) holds for every bounded finite-prefix drift, so Theorem 9.1 applies. Thus the finite-state Markov hypothesis of the preceding infinite-stack theorem can be replaced by a genuinely infinite-memory mixing hypothesis.

### Corollary 9.3 — exact functional-resolvent limit under upper ellipticity

Assume in addition that the original cookies are uniformly bounded away from one:

\[
p_{x,i}\le1-\kappa
\quad\text{a.s. for some }\kappa>0.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then every depth-`M` truncation falls under the previously proved arbitrary-stationary finite-depth functional-resolvent theorem. Let `R_M` denote that exact environment-valued resolvent expression. Combining that theorem with (9.1) yields

\[
\boxed{
 p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}
 =\lim_{M\to\infty}R_M,
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

and the truncation part of the approximation error is `O(M^{-beta})` for every `beta<bar(delta)-1`.

This corollary is an exact computational characterization for a broad stationary infinite-memory subclass. Without the upper-ellipticity assumption, Theorem 9.1 still proves continuity, but this note does **not** claim that the earlier finite-depth functional resolvent applies unchanged to deterministic cookies `p=1`.

## 10. What this does and does not close

This theorem removes the specific finite-state dependence obstruction isolated by the previous report and proves that exit bias is harmless whenever the finite-prefix drift has a bounded projective corrector. In particular, summably `phi`-mixing environments are covered.

\[
