## New theorem A — ballistic LLN gives arbitrary mesoscopic tilted contraction

Fix a compact set `U` of unit directions for which

`c_U := inf_{u in U} v·u >0`.

For `theta>=0`, define

`(K_{theta,u}f)(omega)
 = sum_e omega(0,e) exp(-theta e·u) f(tau_e omega)`.

For every target `rho in (0,1)` there exist `alpha>0` and `n_0` such that, for all `n>=n_0`, all `u in U`, and `theta_n=alpha/n`,

`E_P[K_{theta_n,u}^n 1]
 = E_0 exp(-alpha X_n·u/n)
 <= rho`.                                                     (A1)

Proof.  Put `Delta_n=||X_n/n-v||`.  Then `Delta_n->0` a.s. and `Delta_n<=2`.  Uniformly in `u in U`,

`exp(-alpha X_n·u/n)
 <= exp(-alpha c_U) exp(alpha Delta_n)`.

Dominated convergence gives `E exp(alpha Delta_n)->1`.  Choose `alpha` so that `exp(-alpha c_U)` is arbitrarily small and then take `n` large.  QED.

This is rate-free.  It uses only the bounded-step ballistic strong law.  In particular it survives precisely where a fixed `theta` large-deviation tilt may fail.

## New theorem B — high-density finite-range contraction field

For fixed `alpha,n,u`, let

`Z_{n,u}(omega):=K_{alpha/n,u}^n1(omega)`.

For `q in (0,1)`, call site `x` `u`-good if

`Z_{n,u}(tau_x omega)<=q`.

By Markov and (A1),

`P[x is u-bad] <= rho/q`.                                    (B1)

For any fixed finite directional star `S subset U`,

`P[x is bad for at least one u in S] <= |S|rho/q`.           (B2)

Since `rho` is arbitrary, the defect density can be made arbitrarily small.  Also `Z_{n,u}(tau_x omega)` depends only on environment variables within nearest-neighbour distance `<n` of `x`; therefore the good/bad field is finite-range dependent, with separated sites independent once their supports are disjoint.

This is a genuine infinite-environment/renormalized seed, not another periodic model.

## New theorem C — finite-horizon corrector with controlled oscillation

Set `theta=alpha/n`, `a=a_u=max_{|e|_1=1}|e·u|`, and define

`H_{n,u}:=sum_{j=0}^{n-1}K_{theta,u}^j1`.                    (C1)

It has deterministic bounds

`1 <= H_{n,u}(omega) <= n exp(alpha a)`.                     (C2)

Moreover

`K_{theta,u}H_{n,u}=H_{n,u}-1+Z_{n,u}`.                     (C3)

Thus at a good site,

`K H <= H-(1-q) <= H`,                                      (C4)

while at an arbitrary site,

`K H <= exp(alpha a) H`.                                    (C5)

So the mesoscopic corrector is truly superharmonic on a high-density set and has a fully explicit worst-case multiplicative defect.

## New theorem D — defect-charged global supermartingale

Let

`B_m^u=sum_{k=0}^{m-1} 1_{ {X_k is u-bad} }`                (D1)

be the bad-site local time.  Then

`M_m
 = exp(-theta X_m·u)
   H_{n,u}(tau_{X_m}omega)
   exp(-alpha a B_m^u)`                                      (D2)

is a nonnegative quenched supermartingale for every environment.

At a good current site, (C4) gives the supermartingale inequality without loss.  At a bad current site, `B_m` increases by one and the factor `exp(-alpha a)` exactly cancels the worst factor `exp(alpha a)` in (C5).

This is the key new point: bad sites do not invalidate the corrector.  Their entire effect is represented by one explicit occupation counter.

## New theorem E — exact wrong-way bound in terms of sparse-defect local time

For `b,L>0`, let

`tau_L=tilde T_{-bL}^u wedge T_L^u`,

`A_L^u={tilde T_{-bL}^u<T_L^u}`.

Optional stopping applied to (D2) gives, for every integer `m>=0`,

`P_0(A_L^u, B_{tau_L}^u<=m)
 <= n exp(alpha a)
    exp(-alpha bL/n + alpha a m)`.                           (E1)

With

`m_L=floor(bL/(2an))`,

we obtain

`P_0(A_L^u)
 <= n exp(alpha a) exp(-alpha bL/(2n))
    + P_0(B_{tau_L}^u>bL/(2an)-1)`.                          (E2)

This is the main mathematical delta of the run.

Consequently, if for every nearby `u` and every `b>0` the sparse-defect occupation obeys

`L^(d-1) P_0(B_{tau_L}^u>bL/(2a_u n)-1) ->0`,               (E3)

then `(mathcal P^(d-1))|ell` follows.  Guerra's endpoint theorem yields `(T)|ell` in the i.i.d. setting, and the known equivalence machinery then gives the effective polynomial condition in the source's `M>15d+5` range.

Thus the full source problem is now reduced to a sharply formulated anti-trapping assertion for an **arbitrarily sparse finite-range defect field that is itself canonically generated by the walk's tilted n-step kernel**.

## What remains and why high density is not enough by itself

The bad marginal in (B1) can be made as small as desired, but the walk is not independent of that field.  It can preferentially enter or revisit rare bad regions.  Therefore a Bernoulli-density statement alone does not justify replacing the second term of (E2) by a standard percolation probability.

This is also the exact point at which Guerra's finite-scale seed is stronger than a one-start annealed estimate: his condition controls an expected supremum of quenched wrong-exit probabilities over a whole entrance region.  The present construction controls pointwise tilted transfer and charges the adaptive selection bias by `B_m^u` instead of hiding it.

The remaining bridge is no longer “find an infinite-state corrector.”  A controlled mesoscopic corrector has now been explicitly constructed.  The missing theorem is:

`rare finite-range tilted defects + ballistic iid spatial geometry
 => sufficiently small probability of linear defect local time`.             (GAP)

A direct proof of (GAP) at exponent `d-1`, or a finite-scale version strong enough for Guerra's `(W)` seed, would close the route.

## Next attack

The next run should coarse-grain **defect local time**, not repeat periodic examples or the old admission loop.

Two concrete possibilities:

1. Cluster route.  Quantify the finite-range defect-cluster tail as the defect marginal tends to zero and combine it with a worst-case uniform-elliptic escape-time bound from a finite cluster.  Because the defect marginal can be chosen arbitrarily small, test whether the cluster exponent can dominate the exponential-in-cluster-size residence cost strongly enough to obtain an arbitrary polynomial occupation tail.

2. Crossing-count corrector.  Modify `H_{n,u}` on a coarse lattice so that the martingale pays once per **new bad cluster/block crossed**, rather than once per time step spent there.  Long chains of finite-range bad blocks then have direct exponential spatial tails, avoiding the repeated-return amplification that currently appears in `B_m^u`.

The second route would be the stronger pivot if technically viable.
