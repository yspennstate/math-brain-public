## New theorem A — exact source event reduction to consecutive-level depths

Fix a unit direction `u` with `v_u=v·u>0`. Put

`a=max_i |u_i|`

and choose a nearest-neighbor step `e_*` with `e_*·u=a`.

Define the `a`-spaced hitting times

`sigma_m=inf{n:X_n·u>=ma}`

and depths

`D_m=(ma-inf_{sigma_m<=n<=sigma_{m+1}}X_n·u)_+`.

For `N_L=ceil(L/a)`, every path satisfies

`{tilde T_{-bL}^u<T_L^u}
 subset union_{m=0}^{N_L-1}{D_m>=bL+ma}`.                    (A1)

Indeed, at the time of the wrong-way hit choose the largest completed `a`-level `m`; the hit occurs before `sigma_{m+1}` and the excursion minimum is at most `-bL`.

Therefore

`P_0(tilde T_{-bL}^u<T_L^u)
 <= sum_{m<N_L}P_0(D_m>=bL+ma)`.                              (A2)

This gives the exact source-facing sufficient criterion

`L^(d-1) sum_{m<N_L}P(D_m>=bL+ma) -> 0`                      (A3)

for every `b>0` and every nearby `u`. By Guerra's degree-`d-1` theorem, (A3) closes `(T)|ell`, after which Guerra–Ramírez supplies the standard effective polynomial ballisticity regime and hence the source-requested `M>15d+5` conclusion.

A convenient stronger sufficient condition is uniform `d`th-moment integrability:

`sup_m E[D_m^d 1_{D_m>R}] -> 0`.                              (A4)

More simply, `sup_m E[D_m^p]<infinity` for some `p>d` yields

`P_0(tilde T_{-bL}^u<T_L^u)=O(L^(1-p))`.

Thus the level route now has a precise intermediate theorem, rather than the vague goal “control selection bias.”

## New theorem B — forced-prefix minorization

Uniform ellipticity alone gives a sharp one-sided comparison in the opposite direction.

For every fixed `m>=0` and `r>=0`,

`P_0(D_m>=r) >= kappa^m P_0(D_0>=r)`.                         (B1)

Proof: force the first `m` steps to be `e_*`. Their quenched probability is at least `kappa^m`. On this event `sigma_m=m` exactly. From `me_*`, require the shifted copy of `{D_0>=r}`. This combined path event is contained in `{D_m>=r}`. The prefix probability is bounded below pointwise, so no independence from the future event is needed; spatial translation invariance then gives (B1).

Integrating tails yields, for every `p>0`,

`E[D_m^p]>=kappa^m E[D_0^p]`.                                 (B2)

Hence if the initial depth has an infinite `p`th moment, **every fixed deterministic far-ahead level also has that infinite moment**.

This is a decisive correction to an overly optimistic reading of the previous level-Palm compactness result. A finite-moment weak Palm limit cannot arise because some fixed sufficiently large level suddenly has a better moment class. Any improvement must be non-uniform in the joint `m,r` limit, with tail mass escaping as `m->infinity`.

## New theorem C — exact Tauberian price of averaging levels

Let

`nu_N=(1/N)sum_{m<N}Law(D_m)`.

Summing (B1) gives

`nu_N([r,infinity))
 >= [(1-kappa^N)/(N(1-kappa))]P(D_0>=r)`.                     (C1)

Thus

`P(D_0>=r)
 <= [N(1-kappa)/(1-kappa^N)]nu_N([r,infinity))
 <= N nu_N([r,infinity))`.                                   (C2)

So the previous desired anti-selection comparison is available with an explicit order-`N` loss. In particular,

`N(r) r^(d-1) nu_{N(r)}([r,infinity))->0`                     (C3)

is enough to obtain

`r^(d-1)P(D_0>=r)->0`.

The remaining difficulty is no longer hidden: a merely weak level-Palm limit does not control the joint scale required in (C3), and controlling `D_0` alone still does not automatically control all history-biased failed regeneration attempts.

## New theorem E — a negative fixed-time tilt constructs the infinite-environment corrector

For `theta>0`, set

`g_n(theta)=n^(-1)log E_0[e^{-theta X_n·u}]`,

`Lambda_u(theta)=limsup_n g_n(theta)`.

If

`Lambda_u(theta)<0`,                                           (E1)

then there are `c,C>0` with

`P_0(exists n:X_n·u<=-L)<=Ce^{-cL}`.                          (E2)

Moreover the canonical random function

`H_{theta,u}(omega)=sum_{n>=0}E_{0,omega}[e^{-theta X_n·u}]`   (E3)

is finite almost surely, belongs to `L^1(P)`, and satisfies

`K_{theta,u}H=H-1<=H`,                                        (E4)

where

`(K_{theta,u}h)(omega)=sum_e omega(0,e)e^{-theta e·u}h(tau_e omega)`.

The proof is direct. (E1) makes the annealed series in (E3) summable. One Markov step shifts the series and gives (E4). For (E2), if `X_n·u<=-L` then `n>=L/a`, and Markov's inequality followed by the exponentially summable bound in (E1) gives an exponentially summable union bound.

Because `H>=1`, all negative moments of `H` are automatic. Therefore (E1) manufactures the positive integrable multiplicative corrector sought by the earlier project work. This is an actual infinite-environment construction, not another finite-periodic Perron calculation.

## Exact analytic gap and obstruction

Positive velocity does give

`g_n'(0)=-(1/n)E[X_n·u] -> -v_u<0`.                           (F1)

A sufficient bridge from (F1) to (E1) is a uniform small-tilt curvature estimate. Under the `theta`-tilted endpoint law,

`g_n''(theta)=n^(-1)Var_{n,theta}(X_n·u)`.                     (F2)

Hence a bound

`sup_{n large, 0<=theta<=theta_0}g_n''(theta)<=C`              (F3)

lets Taylor's theorem choose a fixed small `theta>0` with `g_n(theta)<=-(v_u/4)theta` for all large `n`; then Theorem E applies.

But the derivative-limit interchange is not a consequence of an LLN alone. As an abstract obstruction, let

`Y_n=vn` with probability `1-e^{-sqrt(n)}`

and

`Y_n=-an` with probability `e^{-sqrt(n)}`.

Coupled independently, the exceptional probabilities are summable, so `Y_n/n->v` almost surely and in mean, while for every fixed `theta>0`,

`n^(-1)log E[e^{-theta Y_n}] -> theta a>0`.                   (F4)

This is **not an RWRE counterexample**. It shows precisely why positive velocity cannot be inserted mechanically into the finite-state spectral proof: subexponentially rare linear wrong-way deviations can destroy every fixed negative tilt while leaving the LLN untouched. The source's own comment about the slope of the rate function at zero points at this same non-uniformity.
