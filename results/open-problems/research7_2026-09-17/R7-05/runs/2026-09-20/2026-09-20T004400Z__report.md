## New proof I — finite-cone correctors remove the lateral-confinement obligation

For a unit direction `u`, set

`(K_{theta,u}h)(omega)
 = sum_e omega(0,e) exp(-theta e.u) h(tau_e omega)`.

Assume that, for a target direction `v`, an orthonormal basis `v,e_2,...,e_d` and some `eta>0`, positive correctors with positive and negative moments exist for the finite directional star

`F(v,eta)={v} union {(v+/- eta e_j)/sqrt(1+eta^2): 2<=j<=d}`.

Then for every `M>0` and `b>0`,

`L^M P_0(H_{bL}^{-v}<H_L^v) -> 0`.                            (1)

Proof: choose a box

`B_L={-bL<x.v<L, |x.e_j|<RL}`

with `R>2/eta`. A path that hits the back slab before the front must have its first box exit through either the back face or a side face. The back face is negative in direction `v`. On the positive `e_j` side, use

`u_j^-=(v-eta e_j)/sqrt(1+eta^2)`;

there

`x.u_j^- <= (1-eta R)L/sqrt(1+eta^2) = -c_*L`.

On the negative side use `u_j^+`. The inherited `L^p` corrector theorem makes each one of the finitely many non-front face probabilities `o(L^{-M})`; their union is also `o(L^{-M})`. This proves (1).

Consequently, if such integrable correctors exist for every direction in an open neighborhood of `ell`, then after shrinking the neighborhood each target direction has a finite star inside it and `(P*)_M|ell` holds for every `M>0`.

**Progress:** the separate lateral-confinement obligation in the preceding report is eliminated. The corrector route now needs only a controlled corrector on an open cone.

## New proof II — additive Poisson correction exponentiates to the required multiplicative corrector

Let

`(P_env f)(omega)=sum_e omega(0,e)f(tau_e omega)`

and

`d_u(omega)=sum_e omega(0,e)e.u`.

Suppose a measurable `chi` satisfies, for constants `delta>0,C<infinity`,

`d_u + P_env chi - chi >= delta`,                             (2)

and every corrected one-step increment obeys

`|e.u + chi(tau_e omega)-chi(omega)| <= C`.                   (3)

If additionally

`E exp(r|chi|)<infinity`                                      (4)

for some `r>0`, then for all sufficiently small `theta>0`,

`h_theta=exp(-theta chi)`

satisfies

`K_{theta,u}h_theta <= h_theta`                               (5)

and has both a positive and negative moment.

Indeed, with

`Y_e=e.u+chi(tau_e omega)-chi(omega)`,

(2) gives `sum_e omega(0,e)Y_e>=delta`, while `|Y_e|<=C`. Therefore

`K h/h = sum_e omega(0,e)e^{-theta Y_e}`

and

`e^{-theta y} <= 1-theta y +(theta^2 C^2/2)e^{theta C}`.

For small enough `theta`, the conditional average is at most one. Exponential integrability of `chi` then provides both required moments of `h_theta`.

This turns the multiplicative-corrector problem into a more structured infinite-environment target: construct an additive coboundary whose corrected drift is pointwise positive and whose oscillation is controlled.

## New proof III — a canonical Feynman--Kac corrector

For any `theta,u`, define

`H_{theta,u}(omega)=sum_{n>=0}(K_{theta,u}^n 1)(omega)
                   =sum_{n>=0}E_{0,omega}[e^{-theta X_n.u}]`.

Whenever this is finite,

`K_{theta,u}H_{theta,u}=H_{theta,u}-1 <= H_{theta,u}`.         (6)

Also `H>=1`, so all negative moments are automatically bounded by one. Therefore any positive moment

`E[H_{theta,u}^s]<infinity`

is sufficient for the corrector hypothesis in the finite-cone theorem. This gives a canonical object to construct rather than an unspecified Perron eigenfunction.

## New obstruction — positive mean drift alone does not survive the finite-to-infinite-state limit

I constructed an irreducible positive recurrent aperiodic countable-state Markov-additive process with increments in `{-1,+1}` such that both signs have conditional probability at least a fixed `kappa>0`, the stationary mean increment is positive, but **no** positive tilted superharmonic corrector has any positive stationary moment.

The chain has one good state `G` and traps `T_{n,k}`, `1<=k<=n`. From `G`, with small probability `epsilon a_n` enter `T_{n,n}`, where `a_n=c n^{-3}`; otherwise remain at `G`. Inside a trap count down deterministically to `G`. At `G`, the increment is `+1` with probability `1-kappa`; in a trap the increment is `-1` with probability `1-kappa`. Choosing `epsilon E[N]<1` makes the stationary good-state fraction exceed the trap fraction, hence the asymptotic mean speed is positive.

At a trap state the tilted one-step factor is

`m_-(theta)=(1-kappa)e^{theta}+kappa e^{-theta}>1`.

Any positive `h` with `K_theta h<=h` must satisfy

`h(T_{n,n}) >= m_-(theta)^n h(G)`.                            (7)

But the stationary mass of `T_{n,n}` is proportional to `a_n~n^{-3}`, hence for every `s>0`,

`E_pi[h^s] >= C sum_n n^{-3}m_-(theta)^{sn}=infinity`.        (8)

Moreover `||K_theta^r||_infinity >= m_-(theta)^r`, so the `l^infinity` spectral radius exceeds one.

This is **not** an i.i.d. RWRE counterexample. It decisively shows that the earlier finite-state Perron argument cannot be passed to an infinite state space using only positive stationary mean drift, bounded increments and a uniform two-sided increment probability. Rare adverse phases can force exponentially large corrector oscillation. A source proof must exploit the spatial i.i.d. geometry to rule out exactly this phenomenon.
