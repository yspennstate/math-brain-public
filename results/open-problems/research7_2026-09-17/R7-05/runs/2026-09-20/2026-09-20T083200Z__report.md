# 2. New theorem: positive velocity gives a uniform annealed finite-box front exit

Use Guerra's geometry. Fix a rotation `R` with `R e_1=ell` and write

`w = R^{-1} v = (w_1,w_perp)`, with `w_1=v.ell>0`.

For `c>0`, define

`B1(c,M)=R([0,M] x [0,3cM]^{d-1}) intersect Z^d`,

`B2(c,M)=R((-M,12M/11) x (-cM,4cM)^{d-1}) intersect Z^d`,

and let `partial^+ B2` be the frontal boundary at longitudinal coordinate at least `12M/11`, exactly as in Guerra Definition 1.8.

For `x in B1(c,M)`, put

`q_{M,x}(omega) = P_{x,omega}( X_{T_{B2(c,M)}} notin partial^+ B2(c,M) )`.

Choose

`c > (12/(11 w_1)) max_{2<=j<=d}|w_j| + 2`.

(The harmless `+2` is only to leave a strict cone margin.)

### Theorem C04.11 — density-one Guerra seed

Under the source ballistic strong law `X_n/n -> v`,

`boxed{ sup_{x in B1(c,M)} E_P q_{M,x} -> 0 as M -> infinity. }`      `(2.1)`

Consequently, with `N_M=|B1(c,M)|`,

`boxed{ E_P[ (1/N_M) sum_{x in B1(c,M)} q_{M,x} ] -> 0. }`             `(2.2)`

More strongly, for every fixed `eta>0`, if

`Bad_M(eta,omega)={x in B1(c,M):q_{M,x}(omega)>eta}`,

then

`boxed{ E_P[ |Bad_M(eta,omega)| / N_M ] -> 0. }`                       `(2.3)`

Thus, at large scales, a density tending to one of starting points in Guerra's seed box are quenched-`eta`-good in expected spatial density. The missing source-level upgrade is not one-point ballisticity; it is the conversion of this density-one statement into the quenched supremum required by `(W)`.

### Proof

The proof is deliberately rate-free.

In `R`-coordinates every start `x in B1(c,M)` is at longitudinal distance at least `M` from the back face, at least `M/11` from the front face, and at least `cM` from every transverse side of `B2(c,M)`.

The deterministic ray `x+t v` reaches the front at time

`t_f(x)=(12M/11-(R^{-1}x)_1)/w_1`,

so

`M/(11w_1) <= t_f(x) <= 12M/(11w_1)`.

Before that time its transverse displacement in coordinate `j>=2` is at most

`12M |w_j|/(11w_1)`,

which is strictly less than the available side margin `cM` by the choice of `c`. Hence there exists a deterministic `epsilon>0`, depending only on `v,ell,c`, such that **every** path whose long-time empirical velocity stays within `epsilon` of `v` exits the box through the front, uniformly for all relative starting positions `x in B1(c,M)`, once `M` is large compared with its finite initial segment.

Under the ballistic strong law, for the translated walk started at the origin there is an a.s. finite random `N_epsilon` such that

`|X_n/n-v|_2 < epsilon` for all `n>=N_epsilon`.

Because steps are nearest-neighbour, no path can reach any face of the box before a deterministic time `delta M`, where `delta>0` depends only on the fixed geometric margins. Therefore, for every allowed start `x`, the non-front exit event is contained, for all sufficiently large `M`, in

`{N_epsilon > delta M}`.

The annealed increment law is translation invariant, so the same upper bound applies to every `x in B1(c,M)`. Hence

`sup_x E q_{M,x} <= P_0(N_epsilon>delta M) -> 0`,

which proves (2.1). Averaging proves (2.2). Finally Markov's inequality pointwise in `x` gives

`E[|Bad_M(eta)|/N_M]`

`= (1/N_M) sum_x P(q_{M,x}>eta)`

`<= (1/(eta N_M)) sum_x E q_{M,x}`

`<= eta^{-1} sup_x E q_{M,x} -> 0`,

proving (2.3). `square`

### What this does and does not prove

Equation (2.3) is a genuine finite-environment consequence of positive velocity and requires no regeneration moment beyond the strong law. It is still weaker than Guerra's seed because

`E sup_x q_{M,x}`

can stay large even when the expected density of starts with `q_{M,x}>eta` goes to zero. A vanishingly sparse collection of very bad starting locations is enough to defeat the supremum.

This is now the precise ``quenched spike'' problem.

# 3. New decisive obstruction: finite-mean regeneration and stationarity do not remove quenched/start spikes

The previous reports repeatedly reduced wrong-way slab exits to the initial regeneration/backtracking block. Positive velocity does imply finite mean for the **successful inter-regeneration cycle** in each nearby direction with `v.u>0`. It is tempting to hope that this finite mean plus stationarity already forces a useful initial backtracking tail. The next theorem shows that this is false even at the level of an abstract bounded-step stationary regenerative process.

### Theorem C04.12 — equilibrium residual-life obstruction

There exists a stationary ergodic process `(Y_n)_{n in Z}` with increments in `{-1,+1}` such that

1. `Y_n/n -> v_* >0` almost surely;
2. its regenerative cycle length has finite mean;
3. nevertheless, if

`R_0 = max_{m>=0} (Y_0-Y_m)_+`

is the future backward depth from a stationary time origin, then along all large integer `L`,

`boxed{ P(R_0>=L) >= c/log L, }`                                      `(3.1)`

and therefore

`L^p P(R_0>=L) -> infinity`

for every fixed `p>0`.

This process is **not** an i.i.d. spatial RWRE and is not a counterexample to P0487. Its role is to prove that positive speed + bounded steps + stationary ergodicity + finite-mean regeneration cannot by themselves yield the polynomial wrong-way estimate. The product spatial environment must enter essentially.

### Construction and proof

Let `B` be a positive integer random variable with

`P(B=b)=C/[b^2 (log(b+1))^2]`, `b>=1`,

where `C` normalizes the law. Then

`P(B>=m) asymp 1/[m (log m)^2]`,

`E B < infinity`, while `E B^2=infinity`.

A regenerative cycle with parameter `B=b` consists of the word

`(-1)^b (+1)^{b+1}`.

Its length and net displacement are

`S=2B+1`,     `Delta=+1`.

Concatenate i.i.d. cycles in both time directions and take the standard equilibrium (stationary renewal) version: the cycle containing time zero has the size-biased law

`P_*(B=b)=(2b+1)P(B=b)/E S`,

and conditional on that cycle, the origin is uniform among its `2b+1` time locations. This produces a stationary ergodic bounded-increment process.

The renewal-reward theorem gives

`boxed{ v_* = E Delta / E S = 1/E(2B+1) >0. }`

Now condition on a cycle of size `B=b`. If time zero falls in the descending part with at least `L` negative increments still ahead, then `R_0>=L`. There are `(b-L+1)_+` such positions. Therefore the equilibrium size bias cancels exactly with the uniform position in the cycle:

`P(R_0>=L)`

`>= sum_b [(2b+1)P(B=b)/E S] * [(b-L+1)_+/(2b+1)]`

`= E[(B-L+1)_+]/E S`

`= [1/E S] sum_{m>=L} P(B>=m)`.

Since `P(B>=m) asymp 1/[m(log m)^2]`, the integral test gives

`sum_{m>=L} P(B>=m) asymp 1/log L`.

This proves (3.1). `square`

### Interpretation for P0487

A typical stationary time sees a **size-biased residual cycle**. A single a.s.-finite but heavy initial residual does not affect the almost-sure asymptotic speed, yet it can completely destroy every polynomial initial backtracking estimate. This exactly explains why finite mean of later regeneration cycles cannot, by itself, justify the step

`positive speed => polynomial tail of the initial regeneration/backtracking block`.

The source's i.i.d. **spatial** product structure must supply an anti-size-bias / anti-spike mechanism that is absent in generic regenerative theory.

# 4. A sharper source-facing target: bypass polynomial rates and prove one finite seed

The preceding theorem suggests a more efficient target than continuing to force an asymptotic `(P^{d-1})` estimate directly.

Let

`A_M(omega)=(1/N_M) sum_{x in B1(c,M)} q_{M,x}(omega)`

and

`S_M(omega)=sup_{x in B1(c,M)} q_{M,x}(omega)`.

Theorem C04.11 proves

`E A_M -> 0`.

Guerra's Theorem 1.9 says that it is enough to find one finite `M>1/lambda_1` with

`E S_M < lambda_1`.

Therefore **any** i.i.d.-spatial anti-spike estimate of the form

`boxed{ E S_M <= Phi(E A_M) + o_M(1), }`                              `(AS)`

along an unbounded sequence of scales, with a deterministic modulus `Phi(t)->0` as `t->0`, would close the full source implication. No explicit polynomial rate for `E A_M` is needed.

A concrete regeneration-flavoured sufficient version would be

`E S_M <= C E_reg[ S_u 1_{S_u>c_0 M} ] + eta(M)`,                     `(AS-reg)`

where `S_u` is the spatial radius (or duration) of a fresh successful regeneration cycle, `E_reg S_u<infinity` follows from positive speed, and `eta(M)->0`. The right side then tends to zero with **no rate assumption**, so some finite scale satisfies Guerra's seed.

`(AS)` and `(AS-reg)` are **targets, not proved claims**. Their point is that the theorem-sized missing statement is now weaker than the previously targeted degree-`d-1` tail estimate.

# 5. New exact de-supremization lemma for a randomized block scheme

There is one clean way to remove adaptive entry-point selection if the geometry can be arranged to support it.

### Lemma C04.13 — random-shift averaging

Let `J` be a finite abelian group, let `(a_j)_{j in J}` be arbitrary numbers in `[0,1]`, let `I` be an arbitrary `J`-valued random variable, and let `U` be independent and uniform on `J`. Then, conditionally on everything except `U`,

`boxed{ E_U[a_{I+U}] = |J|^{-1} sum_{j in J} a_j. }`                  `(5.1)`

The entry index `I` may be chosen adversarially from the entire previously exposed path and environment; the independent shift removes that bias exactly.

Proof: for fixed `I=i`, the map `U -> i+U` is a permutation of `J`. `square`

For P0487 the intended `J` is a transverse set of block offsets. Equation (5.1) says that an auxiliary independently shifted block decomposition would see the **spatial average seed** `A_M`, not the quenched supremum `S_M`, even when the physical entry point was selected by the past. Because Theorem C04.11 already makes `E A_M` arbitrarily small, a valid randomized-block renormalization of this type would bypass the missing anti-spike estimate altogether.

The unresolved geometric obligation is substantial and explicit: construct a sequence of independently shifted forward boxes such that

1. every macroscopic non-front exit is witnessed by failures of the selected local boxes;
2. the current entry remains in a uniformly buffered admissible part of each selected box for every shift;
3. the shifts can be chosen independently of the exposed environment/path without invalidating the covering; and
4. overlap between successive boxes does not reintroduce a hidden quenched supremum.

No such covering lemma is claimed here. This is the next attack because it directly targets the only difference between the proved density-one seed and Guerra's sufficient finite-scale seed.
