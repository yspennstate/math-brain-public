## 1. Inherited result and exact target of this run

The preceding checkpoint proved two facts for a compact cone `U` about `ell`, with

`inf_{u in U} v.u > 0`.

First, positive velocity gives a finite cone-backtracking radius

`R_U := sup_{n>=0} sup_{u in U} (-X_n.u)_+ < infinity` almost surely,

hence

`p_s(U):=P_0(R_U>=s-1) -> 0` as `s->infinity`.

Second, if descending levels are separated by a fixed width `s`, then the **first** attempt to cross a newly reached lower slab is fresh. Its success indicator is adaptively dominated by Bernoulli parameter `p_s(U)`. Therefore a wrong-way trajectory can avoid exponential cost only if almost every descending slab is eventually crossed after its first fresh attempt has failed: the residual mechanism is dense recycling.

The unresolved point was that a recycled slab may be tried many times, and its eventual successful attempt is selected after observing part of that slab. The goal here is to quantify exactly how many retries and how much posterior overlap are required before that selection can defeat the fresh one-attempt estimate.

# 2. Attempt decomposition inside one recycled slab

Fix `u in U`, let

`a(u)=max_{|e|_1=1}|e.u| <= 1`,

and choose `s>2`. Put

`h_j=-js`,

`sigma_j=inf{n>=0:X_n.u<=h_j}`.

On `{sigma_j<infinity}`, define attempts recursively. The first attempt starts at

`S_{j,1}=sigma_j`.

If `S_{j,r}` is defined, let

`E_{j,r}=inf{n>S_{j,r}: X_n.u>h_j or X_n.u<=h_{j+1}}`.

The attempt is **successful** if the lower boundary is reached first. If it fails, define the next re-entry time

`S_{j,r+1}=inf{n>E_{j,r}:X_n.u<=h_j}`

when this is finite. On the event `{sigma_{j+1}<infinity}`, there is a unique finite successful-attempt index

`A_j=min{r: attempt r is successful}`.

For a given attempt starting at a stopping time `S`, let `N_z(e)` be all departure counts from site `z` before `S`, and let `M_z(e)` be the departure counts during that attempt. Define its departure-overlap charge

`I=sum_z min( |N_z|, |M_z| )`,

where `|N_z|=sum_e N_z(e)` and similarly for `M_z`.

For the successful attempt in slab `j`, write its charge as `I_j`.

# 3. New theorem C04.18 — bounded-retry / bounded-overlap crossing is exponentially sparse

Let `mu` be the one-site environment law and `kappa>0` the uniform ellipticity constant. Recall the exact posterior factorization proved in the earlier replica-overlap checkpoint: for any observed finite path and any virtual future path `eta`, its posterior predictive likelihood is at most

`kappa^{-I} * P_fresh(eta)`,

where `I` is the shared-departure overlap between the observed past and `eta`.

### Lemma C04.18a — one attempt

At the start of **any** slab attempt, regardless of all previous recycling, for every integer `m>=0`,

`boxed{`

`P_0( attempt succeeds and I<=m | full history at the attempt start )`

`    <= kappa^{-m} p_s(U).`

`}`                                                                  `(3.1)`

#### Proof

Condition on the full annealed path history up to the attempt start. The posterior environment remains sitewise factorized with the count-vector tilts from the replica-overlap theorem. For each successful attempt path whose overlap satisfies `I<=m`, the posterior/fresh likelihood ratio is at most `kappa^{-m}`. Summing over all such finite successful path cylinders therefore gives at most `kappa^{-m}` times the corresponding probability under a completely fresh i.i.d. environment.

Under that fresh law the start has projection in `[h_j-a(u),h_j]`. A successful attempt must descend by at least

`s-a(u) >= s-1`

before returning above `h_j`. Dropping the upper stopping restriction can only enlarge its probability, so the fresh success probability is at most

`P_0(R_U>=s-1)=p_s(U)`.

This proves (3.1). `square`

### Lemma C04.18b — first K attempts

For integers `K>=1`, `m>=0`, define

`G_j(K,m)={sigma_{j+1}<infinity, A_j<=K, I_j<=m}`.

Then

`boxed{ P_0(G_j(K,m) | G_{sigma_j}) <= K kappa^{-m} p_s(U). }`        `(3.2)`

Indeed, union over the first `K` attempt indices and apply (3.1) at each corresponding stopping time.

Because `G_j(K,m)` is decided by the time `sigma_{j+1}` is reached, the same conditional-mgf iteration as in the preceding fresh-slab theorem applies across distinct `j`. Therefore, if

`q=K kappa^{-m}p_s(U)<delta`,

then for every `k`,

`boxed{`

`P_0( sum_{j<k} 1_{G_j(K,m)} >= delta k )`

`    <= exp[-k D(delta||q)].`

`}`                                                                  `(3.3)`

No independence after recycling is asserted or needed.

### Source-facing consequence

On a wrong-way event reaching `k_L=Theta(L)` descending slabs, except for probability `exp(-cL)`, fewer than a `delta` fraction of the slabs can be crossed with **both** at most `K` attempts and successful-attempt overlap at most `m`, provided `s` was chosen so that `K kappa^{-m}p_s(U)<delta`.

Thus a subexponential counterexample cannot consist predominantly of bounded-retry, bounded-overlap recycled crossings.

# 4. New theorem C04.19 — bounded average complexity is exponentially impossible

The previous statement upgrades from pointwise caps to average caps.

Fix arbitrary finite constants `K_0>=1` and `m_0>=0`. Because `p_s(U)->0`, choose a fixed slab width `s=s(K_0,m_0,U)` so large that

`4K_0 kappa^{-4m_0} p_s(U) < 1/4`.                                  `(4.1)`

For a wrong-way path crossing `k` slabs, suppose simultaneously

`(1/k) sum_{j<k} A_j <= K_0`,                                      `(4.2)`

`(1/k) sum_{j<k} I_j <= m_0`.                                      `(4.3)`

By Markov's deterministic counting inequality, at most `k/4` indices have `A_j>4K_0`, and at most `k/4` have `I_j>4m_0`. Hence at least `k/2` slabs satisfy

`A_j<=4K_0` and `I_j<=4m_0`.

Apply (3.3) with `delta=1/2`, `K=4K_0`, `m=4m_0`. For `k_L=floor(bL/s)-1`, there is `c=c(b,K_0,m_0,U,s)>0` such that

`boxed{`

`P_0(`

` T_{-bL}^u<T_L^u,`

` (1/k_L)sum_{j<k_L} A_j <= K_0,`

` (1/k_L)sum_{j<k_L} I_j <= m_0`

`) <= exp(-cL),`

`}`                                                                  `(4.4)`

uniformly for `u in U`.

This is a genuinely rate-free structural theorem. For every prescribed finite complexity caps `K_0,m_0`, one may choose a **fixed** mesoscopic width after those caps are fixed, and every wrong-way path whose recycled crossings stay within those average caps is exponentially unlikely.

Consequently, along any subsequence on which the source wrong-way probability is only subexponential, the conditional wrong-way law cannot keep the pair

`( average attempt count per descended slab, average successful-attempt overlap per descended slab )`

jointly tight across the corresponding hierarchy of fixed mesoscopic scales.

This strictly sharpens the preceding `dense recycling` statement: recycling must become quantitatively more complicated, not merely occur.

# 5. New theorem C04.20 — a decisive no-go for the obvious retry closure

There is an important limitation to (3.1): one cannot close the problem simply by balancing the rarity of a fresh successful attempt against a geometric bound on the number of retries.

For each attempt, uniform ellipticity itself gives a deterministic lower bound on clean success. Choose a coordinate step `e_-(u)` with

`e_-(u).u=-a(u)`.

Since `a(u)>=1/sqrt(d)`, forcing at most

`N_s=ceil(s sqrt(d))+2`

successive `e_-(u)` steps reaches the lower boundary while staying below the upper boundary. Hence every attempt, under every posterior history, has conditional clean-success probability at least

`r_s:=kappa^{N_s}>0`.                                                `(5.1)`

Therefore

`P_0(sigma_{j+1}<infinity, A_j>K | G_{sigma_j}) <= (1-r_s)^K`.       `(5.2)`

Combining (5.2) with the first-`K` overlap estimate would give the natural bound

`P_0(sigma_{j+1}<infinity, I_j<=m | G_{sigma_j})`

`<= (1-r_s)^K + K kappa^{-m}p_s(U)`.                                `(5.3)`

But this route is **provably incapable of giving a contraction** from the available information. The same forced path used in (5.1) is a subset of the fresh backtracking event defining `p_s(U)`, so

`p_s(U)>=r_s`.                                                       `(5.4)`

Consequently, for every integer `K>=1` and every `m>=0`, Bernoulli's inequality gives

`(1-r_s)^K + K kappa^{-m}p_s(U)`

`>= (1-r_s)^K+Kr_s`

`>= 1`.                                                              `(5.5)`

Thus **no optimization in `K` can make (5.3) nontrivial**. This is a decisive obstruction to the most obvious continuation of the packing/retry idea: a one-attempt upper bound plus an environment-uniform per-attempt success floor can never control eventual success after arbitrary recycling. The retry mechanism must be charged by something that accumulates more efficiently than the final successful-attempt overlap.

This no-go prevents future runs from silently reintroducing exactly the same circular estimate in a different notation.

# 6. New bridge C04.21 — entropy anti-selection replaces the crude `kappa^{-I}` distortion

The exact posterior structure suggests a different quantity: **information gained about the strip**, rather than raw overlap count.

Let `P` be a prior law on any environment block, `Q<<P` a posterior law, and let `f` be any measurable function with

`0<=f<=1`, `E_P f<=p<1`.

Write `H=D(Q||P)` for relative entropy. Then

`boxed{ E_Q f <= [H+log 2]/log(1/p). }`                              `(6.1)`

### Proof

The entropy variational inequality gives, for every `lambda>0`,

`lambda E_Q f <= H + log E_P exp(lambda f)`.

Since `0<=f<=1`, convexity yields

`exp(lambda f) <= 1+f(exp(lambda)-1)`,

hence

`E_P exp(lambda f) <= 1+p(exp(lambda)-1)`.

Choose `lambda=log(1/p)`. Then the last expression is `2-p<=2`, proving (6.1). `square`

### Application to a recycled slab

Let `f` be the **quenched one-attempt clean-crossing probability** of the strip from the current entrance point, viewed as a function of the strip environment alone. Under the fresh strip law its mean is at most `p_s(U)`. If the strip posterior at a later retry has relative entropy `H_strip`, then

`boxed{`

`P_0(next attempt succeeds | current history)`

`<= [H_strip+log 2]/log(1/p_s(U)).`

`}`                                                                  `(6.2)`

Thus turning a very small fresh crossing probability into an order-one predictive crossing probability requires acquiring order `log(1/p_s(U))` nats of information about that slab. Unlike `kappa^{-I}`, this cost does not automatically blow up exponentially with repeated visits.

There is also an exact sitewise posterior decomposition. If `N_z(e)` are all observed departure counts from site `z`, then

`mu_{N_z}(dp)= [prod_e p(e)^{N_z(e)} / Phi(N_z)] mu(dp)`,

and for a strip `S`,

`boxed{ H_strip = sum_{z in S} D(mu_{N_z}||mu). }`                   `(6.3)`

At a fixed site, the posterior depends on the entire path only through its `2d`-component departure-count vector. Up to a deterministic path horizon `T`, that vector has at most polynomially many possible values, so

`E D(mu_{N_z}||mu) = I(omega_z; path history) <= 2d log(T+2d)`.       `(6.4)`

Equation (6.4) is deliberately only an averaged single-site statement; no pathwise `O(log T)` posterior-KL bound is claimed for an arbitrary prior `mu`.

The important new direction is that repeated recycling concentrated on a small set of sites can only create posterior information at logarithmic expected rate per site, whereas retries spread over many new sites create a spatial-packing branch. This is exactly the packing-versus-overlap dichotomy sought in the preceding report, but with **Bayesian information** replacing the too-crude factor `kappa^{-I}`.

# 7. What changed mathematically

The inherited statement said: source-relevant wrong-way paths must recycle almost every descending slab.

This run proves substantially more:

1. For every fixed attempt cap `K` and overlap cap `m`, slabs successfully crossed within both caps are adaptively Bernoulli-dominated with parameter `K kappa^{-m}p_s(U)`.
2. Therefore wrong-way paths with simultaneously bounded **average** retry count and bounded **average** successful-attempt overlap are exponentially unlikely at a suitable fixed scale.
3. The obvious attempt to remove the retry cap using only uniform ellipticity is mathematically dead: the best geometric-tail-plus-union bound is always at least one because the forced-path retry hazard is already contained in the fresh backtracking probability.
4. A replacement anti-selection inequality based on relative entropy is available and measures the actual information the path has learned about a recycled strip.

These are infinite-environment iid statements. No periodic example is being substituted for the original theorem.
