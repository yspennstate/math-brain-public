## New theorem 1 — positive velocity imposes a finite Cesaro budget on level-excursion depth

Fix any unit direction `u` with `v_u:=v dot u>0`, and put

`a_u=max_i |u_i|`.

Define threshold times

`T_k^u=inf{n>=0:X_n dot u>=k}`

and the depth between consecutive threshold crossings

`D_k^u=(k-inf_{T_k^u<=n<=T_{k+1}^u} X_n dot u)_+`.

Because every step changes `X dot u` by at most `a_u`, every path satisfies

`2D_k^u+1 <= a_u (T_{k+1}^u-T_k^u)`.                         (1)

Indeed the path must accumulate at least `D_k^u` downward projection variation to reach the minimum and then at least `D_k^u+1` upward variation to reach level `k+1`.

Summing (1) gives

`2 sum_{k<N}D_k^u + N <= a_u T_N^u`.                         (2)

Positive velocity implies the inverse hitting-time law

`T_N^u/N -> 1/v_u` almost surely.

Therefore

`limsup_N (1/N)sum_{k<N}D_k^u
 <= c_u := (1/2)(a_u/v_u-1) < infinity`.                       (3)

This is a genuinely infinite-environment statement derived directly from the source hypothesis. It says deep level excursions may occur, but positive velocity gives them a finite pathwise Cesaro depth budget.

## New theorem 2 — finite-mean level-Palm limits

Define the deterministic annealed empirical depth laws

`nu_N^u=(1/N)sum_{k<N} Law_0(D_k^u)`.

The family is tight. For every `A>0`, the pathwise inequality

`A N^{-1}#{k<N:D_k^u>=A} <= N^{-1}sum_{k<N}D_k^u`

combined with bounded reverse Fatou gives

`limsup_N nu_N^u([A,infinity)) <= c_u/A`.                     (4)

For every fixed truncation `R`, the variables

`N^{-1}sum_{k<N}(D_k^u wedge R)`

are bounded by `R` and have almost-sure limsup at most `c_u`, so

`limsup_N int (x wedge R) nu_N^u(dx) <= c_u`.                 (5)

Hence any weakly convergent subsequence has a limit `nu_u` with

`int x nu_u(dx) <= c_u`.                                      (6)

Consequently

`r nu_u([r,infinity)) -> 0`.                                  (7)

Thus positive velocity itself manufactures an asymptotic **typical far-ahead level-crossing law with finite mean backward depth**. In dimension two, (7) is exactly the `1/r` little-o scale needed by the Guerra bridge — but it holds under the level-Palm law, not yet under the early failed-regeneration law appearing in `R_u`.

## New theorem 3 — exact incoming-harmonic-measure bias in a coordinate direction

For `u=e_1`, let `H_k={x:x_1=k}` and let `Q_k` be the law of the shifted environment `tau_{X_{T_k}}omega` at first entrance to `H_k`.

Then

`dQ_k/dP = f_k`,

where

`f_k(omega)=sum_{x in H_k} P_{-x,omega}(X_{T_0}=0)`.          (8)

This follows by expanding over the entrance point `x`, shifting the i.i.d. environment by `-x`, and observing that the shifted event is first entrance from `-x` to `H_0` at the origin. In particular `E_P f_k=1`.

If

`q_r(omega)=P_{0,omega}(T_{-r}<T_1)`,

then the strong Markov property gives the exact relation

`P_0(D_k^{e_1}>=r)=E_P[f_k q_r]`.                             (9)

With `bar f_N=N^{-1}sum_{k<N}f_k`,

`nu_N^{e_1}([r,infinity))=E_P[bar f_N q_r]`.                  (10)

Therefore, along a subsequence realizing a finite-mean level-Palm limit,

`r * lim_j E_P[bar f_{N_j}q_r] -> 0`.                         (11)

## Decisive obstruction: positive speed alone does not give the needed uniform integrability

A bounded-step deterministic path can have positive velocity while its level-excursion depths lose first-moment mass at moving scales.

At every ordinary integer level `k`, advance immediately to `k+1`. At square levels `k=j^2`, first walk backward `j` steps, return to `j^2`, then advance. Then

`D_{j^2}=j`, `D_k=0` otherwise.

Up to level `N`, the extra excursion time is

`2 sum_{j<=sqrt N} j = N+O(sqrt N)`,

so the full path has limiting speed `1/2`; during the `j`th setback the relative depth is only `j/j^2`, so the pointwise speed limit is not destroyed.

Yet, for every fixed `R`,

`lim_{N->infinity} (1/N)sum_{k<N}D_k 1_{D_k>R}=1/2`.          (14)

The empirical depth laws converge weakly to `delta_0`, while a positive amount of first-moment mass escapes to larger and larger depths. This is **not an RWRE counterexample**. It proves that the missing step cannot be obtained from positive speed plus bounded increments alone; an i.i.d.-spatial anti-selection argument is genuinely necessary.
