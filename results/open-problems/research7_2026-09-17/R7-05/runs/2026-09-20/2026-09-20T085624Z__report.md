## 1. Inherited result

The preceding report proved a rate-free, density-one version of Guerra's finite seed from positive velocity: for suitably wide boxes, the annealed non-front exit probability tends to zero uniformly over deterministic starts, hence the expected spatial density of quenched-bad starts tends to zero. The missing upgrade was from spatial average/density to the quenched supremum required by Guerra's finite seed.

Earlier reports also reduced the source problem to tails of the initial/failed-regeneration backtracking radius and developed mesoscopic correctors. Those arguments left open the possibility that a very slow trap could support a wrong-way exit without paying a fixed exponential cost per macroscopic displacement.

The new theorem below shows that this is the **only** possibility: any wrong-way exit that occurs in time `O(L)` is already exponentially unlikely under positive velocity, uniformly for nearby directions.

# 2. New theorem C04.14 — every finite-speed wrong-way exit is exponentially unlikely

Let `I_a` be Varadhan's annealed level-1 rate function for `X_n/n`. Assume

`X_n/n -> v` almost surely under `P_0`, with `v.ell>0`.

Choose a compact neighborhood `U` of `ell` in the unit sphere so small that

`c_U := inf_{u in U} v.u > 0`.

For `u in U` and `b>0`, define the back hitting time

`T_{-bL}^u := inf{ n>=0 : X_n.u <= -bL }`.

Then for every fixed `A<infinity` and `b>0` there exist constants `c=c(A,b,U)>0` and `L_0<infinity` such that, for every `L>=L_0`,

`boxed{ sup_{u in U} P_0( T_{-bL}^u <= A L ) <= exp(-c L). }`          `(2.1)`

Consequently,

`boxed{ sup_{u in U} P_0( T_{-bL}^u < T_L^u, T_{-bL}^u <= A L ) <= exp(-cL). }` `(2.2)`

Thus the entire wrong-way slab-exit event occurring at any fixed finite macroscopic speed already has the exponential decay demanded by condition `(T)`.

### Proof

The annealed LDP has a good convex rate function `I_a` on the nearest-neighbor velocity domain

`D={x in R^d: |x|_1<=1}`.

Under a deterministic nonzero limiting velocity, Varadhan's zero-set description gives

`Z := {x:I_a(x)=0} subset {t v: 0<=t<=1}`,

with either `Z={v}` or `Z=[0,v]` in the standard non-nestling/nestling alternatives.

Fix `A,b,U`. Set

`K_{A,b,U}:={ y in D : min_{u in U} y.u <= -b/A }`.

This is compact. It is disjoint from `Z`: if `y=t v` with `t>=0`, then

`min_{u in U} y.u = t min_{u in U} v.u >= t c_U >=0`,

whereas every `y in K_{A,b,U}` has a projection at most `-b/A` in some direction of `U`. Therefore, goodness and lower semicontinuity of `I_a` give

`r:=inf_{y in K_{A,b,U}} I_a(y)>0`.                                    `(2.3)`

By the LDP upper bound, for all sufficiently large integers `n`,

`P_0( X_n/n in K_{A,b,U}) <= exp(-(r/2)n)`.                            `(2.4)`

Now fix `u in U`. If `T_{-bL}^u=n<=AL`, then

`X_n.u/n <= -bL/n <= -b/A`,

so `X_n/n in K_{A,b,U}`. Also, because each nearest-neighbor step has projection of magnitude at most one,

`n>=bL`.

Hence, for all large `L`,

`P_0(T_{-bL}^u<=AL)`

`<= sum_{n=ceil(bL)}^{floor(AL)} P_0(X_n/n in K_{A,b,U})`

`<= sum_{n=ceil(bL)}^{floor(AL)} exp(-(r/2)n)`

`<= C_r exp(-(rb/2)L)`.

Absorb the constant into the exponential for large `L`. The same `r` works for every `u in U`, proving (2.1). Equation (2.2) follows because the wrong-way-before-front event is a subset. `square`

### Why this is genuinely source-facing

This theorem uses only the source assumptions plus Varadhan's LDP/zero-set theorem. It is not a periodic approximation and not a finite-state surrogate. It settles, uniformly through a full neighborhood of `ell`, every back exit whose time divided by slab width stays bounded.

# 3. Quenched-start corollary — finite-speed quenched spikes are also harmless

The preceding theorem can be upgraded to the kind of many-start control that appears in finite-box criteria.

Let `Gamma_L subset Z^d` be any deterministic set with

`|Gamma_L| <= C L^D`

for fixed `C,D`. For `x in Gamma_L`, let

`q_{L,A,x}^u(omega)`

`:= P_{x,omega}( inf{n<=AL:(X_n-x).u<=-bL}<infinity )`.

By stationarity of the i.i.d. environment law and Theorem C04.14,

`E q_{L,A,x}^u <= exp(-cL)`

uniformly in `x` and `u in U`. Since `sup_x a_x <= sum_x a_x` for nonnegative numbers,

`boxed{ E[ sup_{x in Gamma_L} q_{L,A,x}^u ] <= C L^D exp(-cL) <= exp(-c' L) }` `(3.1)`

for large `L`, uniformly in `u in U`.

Therefore the **quenched-supremum spike problem from the preceding report cannot come from finite-speed backtracking**. Even polynomially many adaptively unfavorable starting points are harmless in this sector after annealing.

A corresponding statement applies to Guerra's non-front finite-box exit completed by time `AL`: choose the transverse aspect ratio wide enough that the entire zero-velocity ray segment `[0,v]` stays strictly inside the front cone for every `u` in a small compact neighborhood. Then the normalized displacements associated with back or side exits before time `AL` form a compact set separated from `Z`, and the same LDP-plus-polynomial-union argument gives an exponentially small expected quenched supremum for that fast sector.

# 4. New theorem C04.15 — any failure of `(T)` must be carried by creeping exits

Fix `u in U` and `b>0`. Write

`W_L(u,b):={T_{-bL}^u<T_L^u}`

and, for `A<infinity`,

`W_L^{fast}(A):=W_L intersect {T_{-bL}^u<=AL}`,

`W_L^{slow}(A):=W_L intersect {T_{-bL}^u>AL}`.

Suppose the exponential wrong-way estimate fails for this pair `(u,b)`, i.e.

`limsup_{L->infinity} (1/L) log P_0(W_L(u,b)) = 0`.                  `(4.1)`

Then for **every** finite `A`,

`boxed{ limsup_{L->infinity} (1/L) log P_0(W_L^{slow}(A)) = 0. }`      `(4.2)`

### Proof

The upper bound in (4.2) is automatic because probabilities are at most one. For the lower bound, Theorem C04.14 gives a `c_A>0` with

`P_0(W_L^{fast}(A)) <= exp(-c_A L)`.

By (4.1), there is a sequence `L_k->infinity` and numbers `epsilon_k downarrow0` such that

`P_0(W_{L_k}) >= exp(-epsilon_k L_k)`.

For large `k`, `epsilon_k<c_A/2`, hence

`P_0(W_{L_k}^{slow}(A))`

`>= exp(-epsilon_k L_k)-exp(-c_A L_k)`

`>= (1/2) exp(-epsilon_k L_k)`.

Dividing logarithms by `L_k` and sending `k->infinity` gives the lower limsup zero. `square`

### Diagonal interpretation

Taking `A=1,2,3,...` and diagonal subsequences shows that any hypothetical counterexample to `(T)` under positive velocity must have subexponential wrong-way mass on trajectories whose back-hitting times satisfy

`T_{-bL}^u/L -> infinity`.

Equivalently, the source obstruction is forced into trajectories whose **effective velocity while reaching the wrong face tends to zero**. There is no finite nonzero wrong-way velocity left to analyze.

This is a strict narrowing of P0487: the problem is now a zero-velocity/creeping-backtrack problem.

# 5. Exact variational identity exposing the rate-function slope at the origin

The source says Open Question 3.20 is intimately linked to the slope of the large-deviation rate function at the origin. The preceding theorem gives a path-level reason. The following elementary convex identity makes the link precise.

For a fixed nearby direction `u`, define the one-dimensional contraction of the annealed rate function

`J_u(s):=inf{ I_a(x): x.u=s, x in D }`.

In the hard nestling case, `J_u(0)=0` and `J_u(s)>0` for `s<0`. By convexity, the function

`f_u(epsilon):=J_u(-epsilon)`, `epsilon>=0`,

is convex near zero with `f_u(0)=0`. Therefore `f_u(epsilon)/epsilon` is nondecreasing in `epsilon>0`, and the one-sided slope

`sigma_u := lim_{epsilon downarrow0} J_u(-epsilon)/epsilon`

exists in `[0,infinity]`.

A path that covers backward distance `bL` in time `tL` has endpoint velocity `-b/t` in the scalar `u` coordinate, so its formal LDP action per `L` is

`t J_u(-b/t)`.

With the substitution `epsilon=b/t`,

`boxed{ inf_{t>=b} t J_u(-b/t) = b sigma_u. }`                         `(5.1)`

Indeed,

`t J_u(-b/t)=b [J_u(-epsilon)/epsilon]`,

and as `t` ranges from `b` to infinity, `epsilon` ranges from `1` down to `0`; the infimum is the right-hand slope at zero.

### What (5.1) proves and what it does not

Equation (5.1) is an exact variational identity, not a proof of `(T)`. It says:

- every bounded `t` has strictly positive action, which is exactly the sector rigorously closed by Theorem C04.14;
- if `sigma_u=0`, arbitrarily slow wrong-way velocities have arbitrarily small formal exponential cost;
- if `sigma_u>0`, the **rate function itself** predicts a positive cost `b sigma_u` even as `t->infinity`.

The remaining nontrivial step is a uniform-in-time passage from the fixed-time LDP to the hitting probability. Standard LDP upper bounds give errors on the scale `n=tL`; when `t->infinity`, such errors need not be `o(L)`. That is precisely why the source's slope-at-zero statement is not a tautological consequence of the LDP.

Thus the source's comment about the slope at the origin can be sharpened as follows: **the only sector not already controlled by ordinary fixed-speed large deviations is the sector in which the optimized LDP action converges to the one-sided slope at zero.**

# 6. Interaction with the 2026 origin-LDP paper

The recent Drewitz--Ramirez--Saglietti--Zheng analysis is directly relevant to this narrowed target. Their dominant event at the origin uses mesoscopic regions approximating suitable periodic environments; in the nestling setting, trapping/slowdown near zero velocity is the natural atypical mechanism. This is consistent with Theorem C04.15: a failure of `(T)` cannot be caused by a fixed negative speed, so it would have to exploit precisely a near-zero-velocity mechanism.

But the origin-LDP mechanism does **not** by itself produce a wrong-way slab exit. A trap can consume macroscopic time while keeping the walker spatially near its trap center; after the waiting period, the walk still has to make a macroscopic displacement to the back face. Therefore generic slowdown estimates are too coarse. What is needed is an **oriented slowdown estimate**: a quantitative statement that a trajectory which both spends superlinear-in-`L` time and ultimately exits through the wrong face still pays an exponential cost in `L`, uniformly over nearby directions.

This distinction prevents a false shortcut from the 2026 rate-at-origin theory to P0487.

# 7. New conditional closure criterion — only the slow oriented piece remains

For fixed `u,b,A`, decompose

`P_0(W_L)=P_0(W_L^{fast}(A))+P_0(W_L^{slow}(A))`.

The first term is already `<=exp(-c_A L)` by Theorem C04.14. Therefore the following is sufficient for condition `(T)` in a neighborhood of `ell`:

There exist a compact neighborhood `U` of `ell` and, for every `b>0`, some finite `A=A(b)` and `c_s(b)>0` such that

`boxed{ sup_{u in U} P_0(T_{-bL}^u<T_L^u, T_{-bL}^u>A L) <= exp(-c_s(b)L) }` `(7.1)`

for all sufficiently large `L`.

Under (7.1), the two pieces give the source-valid annealed exponential wrong-way slab estimate `(T)|ell`; established downstream implications then recover the standard `(P)_M|ell` regime `M>15d+5`.

This is **CONDITIONAL**, not claimed proved.

The point is that (7.1) is substantially narrower than the original problem: it concerns only slow paths that eventually make an oriented macroscopic backtrack.

# 8. Failed route learned from this run

A tempting next step is to bound `W_L^{slow}(A)` by the bare slowdown event `{T_{-bL}^u>A L}` or by long residence in a trap. That is too strong and loses the essential orientation. In a nestling environment, a mesoscopic trap may create long residence with relatively modest cost while the eventual exit is still overwhelmingly forward. Such a bound would attack a phenomenon that can be much more common than the source wrong-way event.

Therefore future work should retain the conjunction

`slow AND eventually exits backward before forward`.

This is the main methodological correction from the origin-LDP comparison.

# 9. Next concrete attack

The next useful proof target is an **oriented trap-erasure / last-departure lemma**.

For a slow wrong-way path, identify a last mesoscopic region in which the path spends a large fraction of its excess time before beginning the macroscopic displacement that eventually reaches the back face. Then split:

1. after the last long residence, the remaining backward displacement is made in `O(L)` steps; this sector should be controlled by Theorem C04.14 after exposing/resampling fresh environment ahead of the displacement;
2. the remaining backward displacement is itself slow; recurse on a geometrically smaller sequence of spatial scales.

The desired quantitative output is a recursion for the oriented slow event, not for generic slowdown, of schematic form

`S_L(A) <= exp(-cL) + sum_j p_j S_{theta_j L}(A_j)`

with total recursive weight strictly below one after exploiting i.i.d. spatial freshness. A successful version would turn repeated creeping into either an exponentially costly sequence of fresh unfavorable regions or a finite-speed terminal backtrack already covered by C04.14.

The complementary rate-function version is to obtain a uniform-in-time hitting estimate realizing the positive one-sided slope `sigma_u`; the exact identity (5.1) specifies the target exponent.
