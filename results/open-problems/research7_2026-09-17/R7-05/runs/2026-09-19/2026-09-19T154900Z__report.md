## 4. New mathematics — B16 / B19 / B25

A complete derivation is preserved in the artifact named below. The useful reduction is as follows.

Let `Z=Z^D` be the critical random measure, `W=Z(D)` and `M=Z/W`. Conditional on `Z`, the extremal leaders form a Poisson process with intensity

`Z(dx) exp(-alpha h) dh`.

Thus, for the maximum `(X,T)`,

`P(X in dx,T in dt | Z)
 = Z(dx) exp(-alpha t)
   exp[-(W/alpha)exp(-alpha t)] dt.`

Writing `s(t)=exp(-alpha t)/alpha`, the averaged joint density is

`rho(dx,t)
 = exp(-alpha t) E[Z(dx)e^{-s(t)W}].`  (4.1)

### B16 — exact smallest countermodel to a scalar reduction

The height density is

`f_T(t)=exp(-alpha t)E[W e^{-s(t)W}]`,

but the conditional location law is

`mu_t(A)
 = E[W M(A)e^{-s(t)W}] / E[W e^{-s(t)W}].`  (4.2)

On a two-point space, take the same total mass `W in {1,2}` with probabilities `1/2,1/2` in two models. In model A set `M(a)=1/2` always. In model B set `M(a)=3/4` for `W=1` and `M(a)=1/4` for `W=2`. Both models have the same `W` law and the same unconditional normalized mean `E M(a)=1/2`, but

`mu_s^B(a)=((3/4)e^{-s}+(1/2)e^{-2s})/(e^{-s}+2e^{-2s})`

differs from `1/2` except at `s=log 2`.

Therefore total-mass information, even supplemented by the unweighted position marginal, cannot determine the joint maximum law. This is an obstruction to a proof route, not a DGFF counterexample.

### B19 — exact Laplace–Palm reconstruction

For measurable `A` and `s>0`, define

`G_A(s)=E[Z(A)e^{-sW}]`.

Equation (4.1) gives the exact two-way correspondence

`rho(A,t)=e^{-alpha t}G_A(e^{-alpha t}/alpha)`.

The height marginal gives

`G_D(s)=E[W e^{-sW}]=-L'(s)`,

where `L(s)=E[e^{-sW}]`. Hence the height law determines the scalar law of total mass, while the source joint density is equivalent to the **entire measure-valued first Laplace–Palm transform**

`E[Z(dx)e^{-sZ(D)}]`,  `s>0`.

This makes precise why an explicit law of `Z(D)` alone does not close Problem 16.4.

### B25 — a second count and exact independence criterion

Counting the void above level `t` shows that, with `E~Exp(1)` independent of `Z`,

`T=(1/alpha)[log(W/alpha)-log E]`,

while conditionally on `Z` the maximizing location has law `M` and is independent of `E`.

Let `q_A(w)=E[M(A)|W=w]`. From (4.2), `X` and `T` are independent with location law `mu` iff

`E[W(q_A(W)-mu(A))e^{-sW}]=0`

for all `s>0` and measurable `A`. Exponential tilting makes the corresponding signed measure finite, and uniqueness of Laplace transforms gives the equivalence

`X independent of T`

iff

`E[M(A)|W]=mu(A)` almost surely for every measurable `A`.

Thus position-height dependence is exactly a Laplace-visible dependence of the normalized critical measure on its total mass. Any PDE solution route has to encode this spatial-total-mass coupling, not merely the total-mass distribution.
