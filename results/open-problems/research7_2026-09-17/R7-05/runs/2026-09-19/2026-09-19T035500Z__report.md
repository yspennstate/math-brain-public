## 6. B11 — dualize: complete Bernoulli aggregation theorem

Let `q=(q_0,...,q_n)` be a target law for `S=sum_i X_i`, and sort the marginal probabilities as

`p_(1)>=...>=p_(n)`.

Define

`R_q(m)=sum_{k=0}^n q_k min(k,m)`.

### Theorem

There exists a joint law of Bernoulli variables with marginals `p_i` and sum law `q` if and only if

`sum_i p_i = sum_k k q_k`

and, for every `m=1,...,n-1`,

`sum_{i=1}^m p_(i) <= R_q(m)`.

Equivalently, for every subset `A subseteq [n]`,

`sum_{i in A}p_i <= sum_k q_k min(k,|A|)`,

plus total-mean equality.

### Proof — necessity

Pointwise,

`sum_{i in A} X_i <= min(S,|A|)`.

Take expectations. For fixed `|A|=m`, the maximal left side is the sum of the `m` largest `p_i`. Total-mean equality is `E[S]=sum_i E[X_i]`.

### Proof — sufficiency

For each `k`, let

`Delta(n,k)=conv{1_A: |A|=k}`

be the hypersimplex. A vector in `Delta(n,k)` is exactly the inclusion-probability vector of some random `k`-subset. Thus the desired coupling exists precisely when

`p in C_q := sum_k q_k Delta(n,k)`.

For an arbitrary vector `c`, permute coordinates so that `c_1>=...>=c_n`; the corresponding first `m` coordinates of `p` form some `m`-subset, so their sum is at most the sum of the `m` largest marginal probabilities and hence at most `R_q(m)`.

The support function satisfies

`h_{Delta(n,k)}(c)=c_1+...+c_k`,

so

`h_{C_q}(c)=sum_k q_k(c_1+...+c_k)`.

Abel summation gives

`c dot p = c_n sum_i p_i + sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(c_m-c_{m+1}) sum_{i=1}^m p_i`

and

`c_1+...+c_k = c_n k + sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(c_m-c_{m+1})min(k,m)`.

Using total-mean equality and the prefix/subset inequalities yields

`c dot p <= h_{C_q}(c)`

for every `c`. If `p` were outside the closed convex set `C_q`, strict separation would produce some `c` with `c dot p>h_{C_q}(c)`, contradiction. Thus `p in C_q`. Decompose `p=sum_k q_k u^(k)` with `u^(k) in Delta(n,k)`, sample `K~q`, sample a conditional random `k`-subset realizing `u^(k)`, and set `X_i=1_{i in A}`. This realizes the prescribed marginals and sum law. QED.

### Useful corollaries

For `n=3`, after sorting `p_1>=p_2>=p_3`, feasibility is exactly

`p_1+p_2+p_3=q_1+2q_2+3q_3`,

`p_1<=1-q_0`,

`p_1+p_2<=q_1+2q_2+2q_3`.

For equal Bernoulli margins `p_i=p`, **every** cardinality law on `{0,...,n}` with mean `np` is realizable: conditional on `S=k`, choose a uniformly random `k`-subset.

## 7. B5 — parameterize the boundary

Fix the Bernoulli margins and two candidate sum laws `q,q'` with the same required mean. Put

`q(lambda)=(1-lambda)q+lambda q'`.

Each feasibility slack is affine:

`s_m(lambda)=R_{q(lambda)}(m)-sum_{i=1}^m p_(i)`,

with

`s'_m(lambda)=sum_k(q'_k-q_k)min(k,m)`.

Therefore the exact feasible set of `lambda in [0,1]` is an intersection of one-dimensional half-spaces, hence a closed interval, and every endpoint is witnessed by saturation of a top-`m` inequality. In this Bernoulli slice there is no hidden nonlinear membership obstruction.
