## 4. B1 — solve the complete one-dimensional equality problem

For the centrally symmetric one-dimensional source scope, write

`K=[-a,a]`, `a>0`,

and

`Lambda=[lambda]`.

Since `B_2^1=[-1,1]`,

`phi(s)=2 min{a exp(lambda s),1}`.

Put

`u=log a`, `v=lambda t`.

Then

`log phi(s)=log 2 + min{log a + lambda s,0}`,

and the exact logarithmic midpoint gap is

`Gamma(u,v) = log(phi(t/2)^2/[phi(0)phi(t)])`

`= 2 min{u+v/2,0} - min{u,0} - min{u+v,0}`.

This can be evaluated completely. If `u` and `u+v` have the same weak sign, `log phi` is affine along the whole segment and `Gamma=0`. If they have opposite strict signs, the segment crosses the unique kink and

`Gamma = min{|u|,|u+v|} > 0`.

Therefore

`phi(t/2)^2 = phi(0)phi(t)`

**if and only if**

`(log a)(log a + lambda t) >= 0`.

Equivalently, equality holds exactly when the endpoint radii `a` and `a exp(lambda t)` lie on the same side of the unit radius, allowing an endpoint to equal one. Equality is strict precisely when the scalar dilation crosses the unit-ball boundary between the endpoints.

This is a complete exact one-dimensional classification.

## 7. B6 — why this correction does not settle maximal-intersection uniqueness

The printed biconditional is stronger than the implication actually needed in the maximal-intersection uniqueness argument.

In the intermediate-volume uniqueness setup one has

- `K` neither contained in the unit ball nor containing it;
- a determinant-preserving diagonal variation, so `tr Lambda=0`;
- equality forced by maximality.

What the uniqueness argument needs is essentially the necessity statement

`equality + intermediate position + tr Lambda=0 => Lambda=0`.

It does **not** need the converse assertion that a containment condition or scalar `Lambda` automatically gives equality.

Therefore the dimension-one false positive refutes the literal source wording but does not itself refute maximal-intersection uniqueness. HARD7-01's two-dimensional false-negative is more relevant to the necessity direction, but its own determinant-one deformation shows that the particular slack-coordinate box used there is not globally maximal.
