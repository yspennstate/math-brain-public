## B1 — change category: exact Bernoulli skeleton after freezing heavy magnitudes

### Counterexample/obstruction first

A tempting monotonicity claim is false: increasing the absolute amplitudes of a Rademacher sum can increase, not decrease, concentration. For instance,

`epsilon_1+3 epsilon_2`

has four distinct values, each of mass `1/4`, whereas

`3 epsilon_1+3 epsilon_2`

has an atom of mass `1/2` at zero. Therefore the fact that every discrete-Pareto magnitude satisfies `R_j>=1` does not justify comparing the weighted sum with an all-unit Rademacher sum. The proof must preserve an actual independent constant-amplitude subfamily or use weighted arithmetic structure.

### Exact thinning lemma

For deterministic coefficients `v=(v_1,...,v_n)`, independent pairs `(epsilon_j,R_j)`, and

`J={j:R_j=1}`,

let

`Q(Y,r)=sup_w P(|Y-w|<=r)`

be the Levy concentration function. Then conditional on `v` and all magnitudes,

`P(|sum_j epsilon_j R_j v_j-w|<=r | v,R)
 <= Q(sum_{j in J} epsilon_j v_j,r)`                            (1.1)

for every radius `r` and deterministic translation `w`.

**Proof.** Split the weighted sum as

`S_J + T`,

where

`S_J=sum_{j in J} epsilon_j v_j`

and

`T=sum_{j notin J} epsilon_j R_j v_j`.

Condition additionally on every sign outside `J`. Then `T-w` is deterministic and the conditional small-ball probability is at most the supremum over all translations of the law of `S_J`, namely `Q(S_J,r)`. Average over the outside signs. QED.

Thus every heavy magnitude in the tested column can be removed from the one-dimensional small-ball estimate by retaining only the independent unit-magnitude coordinates.

### Matrix form for a fixed real shift

For this part fix a **real** Hermitization shift `z` and define the raw matrix

`M_n(z)=X_n-a_n z I`,

so `A_n(z)=a_n^{-1}M_n(z)`.

Let `C_i` be column `i`, let

`H_i=span{C_j:j!=i}`,

and choose a unit real vector `v_i` orthogonal to `H_i`. Since column `i` is independent of the other columns, `v_i` is independent of every random variable in column `i`. Also

`dist(C_i,H_i) >= |<C_i,v_i>|`,

and

`<C_i,v_i>
 = sum_j epsilon_{ji}R_{ji}v_{i,j} - a_n z v_{i,i}`.

Let

`J_i={j:R_{ji}=1}`.

Applying (1.1) gives the exact conditional bound

`P(dist(C_i,H_i)<=r | v_i,(R_{ji})_j)
 <= Q(sum_{j in J_i}epsilon_{ji}v_{i,j},r)`.                    (1.2)

The tested-column heavy tail is therefore gone. The remaining difficulty is the arithmetic structure of a random normal vector after restriction to an independent Bernoulli(`p_*`) coordinate mask.

Aizenman–Germinet–Klein–Warzel's Bernoulli decomposition shows that the category change is not peculiar to discrete Pareto: every nondegenerate real law has a Bernoulli-component representation with uniformly positive conditional Bernoulli variance after a suitable parameter choice. For the full source class that produces a weighted rather than constant-amplitude skeleton, so I do not claim the full endpoint from it here.

## B11 — dualize: random span, stretched-scale spread, and the correct LCD size

Fix `q in (0,q_*)` and put

`rho_n=exp(-n^q/8)`.                                             (2.1)

Call a unit vector `rho_n`-compressible on `delta n` coordinates if it lies within Euclidean distance `rho_n` of a vector supported on at most `delta n` coordinates.

### Lemma 1 — stretched-scale spread in the real discrete-Pareto benchmark

There is `delta>0` such that, almost surely for all sufficiently large `n`, simultaneously:

1. every unit vector `x` satisfying

   `||M_n(z)x|| <= a_n exp(-n^q)`                               (2.2)

   has at least `delta n` coordinates with

   `|x_j| >= rho_n/sqrt(n)`;                                   (2.3)

2. for every column `i`, every unit normal `v_i in H_i^perp` has at least `delta n` coordinates satisfying the same lower bound (2.3).

**Proof.** Nondegeneracy of the entry law implies, after a fixed deterministic rescaling, a one-dimensional concentration gap

`sup_lambda P(|xi-lambda|<=1) <= 1-beta`                         (2.4)

for some `beta>0`. Tikhomirov's strict-rectangular no-moment theorem supplies constants `u,v>0` such that every `n x s` or `(n-1) x s` random block with `s<=delta n` has least singular value at least `u sqrt(n)`, except with probability `exp(-v n)`, uniformly under the relevant deterministic **real** diagonal deformation, once `delta` is small enough. Union over the deleted column and all supports costs

`n sum_{s<=delta n} binom(n,s)
 = exp((H(delta)+o(1))n)`.

Choose `delta` so that `H(delta)<v/2`. Borel–Cantelli then gives, almost surely eventually, the simultaneous tall-block lower bound for all supports required below.

Independently, the Pareto tail gives

`P(max_{j,k}|xi_{jk}|>exp(n^q/64))
 <= C n^2 exp(-alpha n^q/64)`,                                 (2.5)

which is summable. Since `a_n` is subexponential on this scale,

`||M_n(z)|| <= exp(n^q/16)`                                    (2.6)

almost surely eventually.

Suppose a unit near-kernel vector `x` is within `rho_n` of an `S`-supported vector `x_S`, `|S|<=delta n`. Then

`||M_n(z)x||
 >= ||M_n(z)_{[:,S]}x_S|| - ||M_n(z)||rho_n
 >= u sqrt(n)||x_S|| - exp(-n^q/16)`.                          (2.7)

For large `n`, `||x_S||>=1-rho_n` and the right side exceeds `u sqrt(n)/3`, contradicting (2.2). Hence every such near-kernel vector is incompressible at radius `rho_n`. If fewer than `delta n` coordinates had magnitude at least `rho_n/sqrt(n)`, deleting those coordinates would leave a vector of norm `<rho_n`, producing a forbidden sparse approximation. This proves part 1.

For a unit normal `v_i`, use `H_i^*v_i=0` and the same tall-block bound for the `(n-1) x s` submatrices of `H_i^*`. A `rho_n`-compressible normal would imply

`u sqrt(n)||(v_i)_S||
 <= ||H_i^*||rho_n
 <= exp(-n^q/16)`,                                             (2.8)

which is impossible for large `n`. The same coordinate-count argument proves part 2. QED.

**Scope boundary.** The Tikhomirov input used here is stated for real random matrices and real deterministic deformation. Lemma 1 is therefore claimed only for the fixed **real-shift** benchmark. Extending this exact step to arbitrary complex `z`, or replacing it with a complex no-moment analogue, remains source-level work.

### Independent mask captures stretched-scale mass

For each `i`, choose any `delta n` spread coordinates `S_i` of `v_i`. The mask `J_i` lives in the tested column and is independent of `v_i`. Conditional on `v_i`,

`|J_i cap S_i| ~ Binomial(delta n,p_*)`.

Chernoff yields

`P(|J_i cap S_i|<p_*delta n/2 | v_i) <= exp(-c n)`.             (2.9)

On the complement,

`||(v_i)_{J_i}||_2
 >= sqrt(p_*delta n/2) rho_n/sqrt(n)
 = c_0 rho_n`.                                                  (2.10)

Thus the independent unit-magnitude skeleton retains stretched-exponential norm with exponentially high probability.

### Main improvement: polynomial LCD is enough

The STUCK1 heuristic suggested that one might need arithmetic complexity on the scale `exp(n^q)`. That is much stronger than necessary.

For a nonzero real vector `y`, write `LCD_{L,gamma}(y/||y||)` for the Rudelson–Vershynin least common denominator

`inf{theta>0:
 dist(theta y/||y||,Z^m)<min(gamma theta,L)}`.                  (2.11)

The standard Rademacher LCD small-ball theorem gives, for a unit vector `u`,

`Q(sum_j epsilon_j u_j,eps)
 <= C eps + C exp(-cL^2)`                                      (2.12)

whenever `eps>=c_1/LCD_{L,gamma}(u)`. If

`D=LCD_{L,gamma}(u)`

and the desired radius `t` is smaller than `c_1/D`, monotonicity of `Q` lets us apply (2.12) at the larger radius `c_1/D`:

`Q(sum_j epsilon_j u_j,t)
 <= Q(sum_j epsilon_j u_j,c_1/D)
 <= C/D+C exp(-cL^2)`.                                        (2.13)

Consequently it is sufficient to prove, for some fixed `eta>0`,

`LCD_{L_n,gamma}((v_i)_{J_i}/||(v_i)_{J_i}||)
 >= n^(1+eta)`,                                                 (2.14)

outside a summable failure event, with

`L_n=K sqrt(log n)`                                             (2.15)

and `K` large. Equations (2.13)-(2.15) give a tested-column small-ball probability `O(n^(-1-eta))`.

This lowers the required arithmetic scale from stretched-exponential to merely polynomial.

## B30 — one scale only: complete conditional bridge from polynomial LCD to the desired floor

Define the raw target scale

`tau_n=a_n exp(-n^q)`.                                          (3.1)

On the spread event of Lemma 1, if some unit `x` obeys

`||M_n(z)x||<=tau_n`,

then there is a set `I(x)` of at least `delta n` coordinates satisfying (2.3). For every `i in I(x)`, the elementary invertibility-via-distance relation gives

`dist(C_i,H_i)
 <= ||M_n(z)x||/|x_i|
 <= tau_n sqrt(n)/rho_n
 =: r_n`,                                                       (3.2)

so

`r_n=a_n sqrt(n) exp(-7n^q/8)`.                                (3.3)

Hence deterministically

`1_{s_min(M_n(z))<=tau_n and spread-good}
 <= (1/(delta n)) sum_{i=1}^n
       1_{dist(C_i,H_i)<=r_n}`.                                (3.4)

The factor `1/(delta n)` is crucial: after expectation there is **no extra union-bound factor `n`**.

On the mask-mass event (2.10), normalize the retained Rademacher sum. Its small-ball radius is at most

`t_n:=r_n/||(v_i)_{J_i}||
 <= C a_n sqrt(n) exp(-3n^q/4)
 = exp(-3n^q/4+O(log n)).                                      (3.5)

Thus `t_n` is eventually smaller than `n^{-A}` for every fixed `A`.

### Theorem — one-scale polynomial-LCD bridge

Fix a real `z`, `0<q<q_*`, and `eta>0`. Suppose that for some sufficiently large `K`, with the tested-column mask `J_i`, the random normal satisfies

`b_n := P(
 LCD_{K sqrt(log n),gamma}((v_i)_{J_i}/||(v_i)_{J_i}||)<n^(1+eta),
 ||(v_i)_{J_i}||>=c_0 rho_n
)`                                                               (3.6)

and

`sum_n b_n<infinity`.                                           (3.7)

Then

`sum_n P(s_min(A_n(z))<exp(-n^q))<infinity`,                    (3.8)

and therefore

`s_min(A_n(z))>=exp(-n^q)`                                      (3.9)

almost surely for every sufficiently large `n`.

**Proof.** The failures of the spread lemma are summable by the rectangular exponential bound plus Borel–Cantelli, and the mask-mass failures in (2.9) are exponentially summable. On the good event, (3.5) is eventually much smaller than `n^(-1-eta)`. If the LCD is at least `n^(1+eta)`, apply (2.13) with `L_n=K sqrt(log n)` and choose `K` so that

`exp(-cL_n^2)<=n^(-1-eta)`.

Using (1.2),

`P(dist(C_i,H_i)<=r_n | good arithmetic data)
 <= C n^(-1-eta)`.                                             (3.10)

The arithmetic-bad contribution is `b_n`. Taking expectations in (3.4), the sum over `i` is cancelled by the factor `1/(delta n)`, and we obtain

`P(s_min(M_n(z))<=tau_n)
 <= C b_n + C n^(-1-eta)
    + (summable stretched/exponential errors)`.                 (3.11)

The right side is summable by (3.7). Borel–Cantelli yields

`s_min(M_n(z))>a_n exp(-n^q)`

eventually. Divide by `a_n` to get (3.9). QED.

### Why this is substantive progress despite the binary metric staying zero

Before this run, the recovery milestone was a vague request for heavy-tail-compatible random-normal anti-structure at a denominator scale comparable to `exp(n^q)`. The theorem above shows that this was vastly stronger than necessary. The full one-scale probability estimate closes as soon as one can prove a **polynomial** thinned-LCD lower bound `n^(1+eta)` with summable failure.

Two mechanisms create this improvement:

1. the target small-ball radius is already stretched-exponentially below every inverse polynomial, so evaluating the LCD theorem at the larger scale `1/LCD` loses nothing essential;
2. the spread of a near-null vector across `delta n` coordinates permits averaging in (3.4), avoiding an extra factor of `n` in the probability estimate.

The geometric part of the recovery action is therefore complete for the fixed real-shift discrete-Pareto benchmark. The residual issue is arithmetic.

## Exact remaining gap and noncircularity check

The benchmark is **not solved**. The next missing theorem is exactly:

> For the random normal `v_i` to the span of the other heavy-tailed columns, and for the independent Bernoulli coordinate mask `J_i={j:R_{ji}=1}`, prove for some `eta>0` that
>
> `LCD_{K sqrt(log n),gamma}((v_i)_{J_i}/||(v_i)_{J_i}||)
>  >= n^(1+eta)`
>
> outside a sequence of events with summable probabilities.

The dependence is noncircular: `v_i` is measurable with respect to all columns except `i`, while `J_i` and the tested signs are functions of column `i`, hence independent of `v_i`. The strict-rectangular lower bound used to prove spread concerns tall submatrices and does not assume the square least-singular statement being sought. The LCD hypothesis in the conditional bridge is explicitly left unproved rather than being smuggled into the geometry lemma.

Known square random-normal arithmetic theorems under finite second-moment/Hilbert–Schmidt budgets do not automatically apply to this fixed infinite-variance distribution. The strict-rectangular no-moment theorem supplies the geometric spread but not the one-dimensional arithmetic unstructure of the square random normal. That is now the exact boundary.

A second source-level gap is that the geometric input was used only for **real** deterministic shift `z`. C0031's Hermitization needs Lebesgue-a.e. complex `z`. No complex-shift extension is claimed here.
