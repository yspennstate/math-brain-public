## 4. Mathematical setup

## 5. B2 — attack the negation: a monotone counterdiagnostic invisible to the full singular principal part

The earlier campaign noted that one critical Laplace pole is insufficient. I strengthened that obstruction.

For

`0 < epsilon < alpha/sqrt(alpha^2+1)`,

define

`F_epsilon(h)=exp(alpha h)(1+epsilon sin h)`.

Then

`F_epsilon'(h)=exp(alpha h)[alpha+epsilon(alpha sin h+cos h)]`.

Since the minimum of `alpha sin h+cos h` is `-sqrt(alpha^2+1)`, this derivative is strictly positive. Thus the model retains the elementary source-shape property that `F` is positive and increasing.

Nevertheless

`q_epsilon(h)=1+epsilon sin h`

has no limit. Its transform is explicit:

`G_epsilon(alpha+s)
 =(alpha+s)[1/s + epsilon/(1+s^2)]`

`= alpha/s + 1 + epsilon(alpha+s)/(1+s^2)`.

The epsilon-dependent term is analytic through `s=0`. Hence every such oscillatory model has exactly the same complete negative-power singular principal part at the critical point as the convergent epsilon=0 model:

`Sing_{s=0} G_epsilon(alpha+s)=alpha/s`.

**Outcome B2:** not only the pole residue, but the whole meromorphic principal part at criticality is insufficient to recover the source limit. Any successful route must import local-in-depth regularity not encoded by the singular transform coefficients.

## 6. B18 — translate the problem: exact critical truncated-mass identity

## 7. B24 — extremal/missing regularity: a sufficient Tauberian closure theorem

I isolated a source-plausible regularity condition that would make the averaged growth implication reversible.

### Proposition

Let `F>=0` be nondecreasing, `q(H)=exp(-alpha H)F(H)` and

`S(H)=q(H)-F(0)+alpha integral_0^H q(h)dh`.

Assume

`S(H)/H -> L in (0,infinity)`

and for some fixed `delta in (0,1)`

`omega_delta(H):=sup_{u in [(1-delta)H,H]} |q(u)-q(H)| -> 0`.

Then

`boxed{ q(H)->L/alpha. }`

### Proof

Since `q>=0`,

`alpha integral_0^H q(h)dh
 =S(H)-q(H)+F(0)
 <=S(H)+F(0)`.

Thus the Cesaro means of `q` are bounded. The terminal-window mean

`M_delta(H):=(1/(delta H)) integral_{(1-delta)H}^H q(u)du`

is therefore bounded as well. By the oscillation hypothesis,

`|M_delta(H)-q(H)|<=omega_delta(H)`,

so `q(H)=O(1)` and hence `q(H)/H->0`.

Divide the exact critical-mass identity by `H` to obtain

`A(H):=(1/H) integral_0^H q(h)dh -> L/alpha`.

Now

`M_delta(H)
 =[A(H)-(1-delta)A((1-delta)H)]/delta -> L/alpha`.

Finally

`|q(H)-L/alpha|
 <=omega_delta(H)+|M_delta(H)-L/alpha| ->0`.

This proves the proposition.

**Outcome B24:** a precise remaining bridge is now available.
