## 5. B2 — attack the negation: deterministic vacancy separator

Let

\[
R_{L,H}=\{0,1,\ldots,L\}\times\{0,1,\ldots,H\},
\qquad L\ge2,\ H\ge1.
\]

Use nearest-neighbor square-lattice adjacency, and let `*` adjacency mean Chebyshev distance one (nearest-neighbor or diagonal).

A configuration

\[
\eta:R_{L,H}\to\{0,1,2\}
\]

is stable if it satisfies (4.1). Impose opposing side data

\[
\eta(0,y)=1,\qquad \eta(L,y)=2
\quad(0\le y\le H).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
V(\eta)=\{x:\eta(x)=0\}.
\]

### Theorem — Dobrushin vacancy separator

Every stable `eta` satisfying (5.1) has a `*`-connected vacancy path from the bottom side of the rectangle to the top side.

### Proof

Assume not. The deterministic matching-lattice crossing dichotomy on a rectangular square-grid says that if a vertex set `A` has no `*`-connected top--bottom crossing, then its complement has a nearest-neighbor left--right crossing. One direct proof explores the nearest-neighbor component of `A^c` attached to the left side: if it misses the right side, its external vertex boundary is a `*` chain in `A` joining top to bottom.

Apply this with `A=V(eta)`. Under the negation there is therefore a nearest-neighbor path

\[
x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_m
\]

through occupied sites from the left side to the right side. Every path label is in `{1,2}`. By (5.1),

\[
\eta(x_0)=1,\qquad \eta(x_m)=2.
\]

Let `j` be the first index with `eta(x_j)=2`. Then `eta(x_{j-1})=1` and `x_{j-1},x_j` are nearest neighbors, contradicting stability (4.1). Hence the vacancy `*` crossing must exist. QED.

Every such crossing has at least `H+1` vertices. Therefore

\[
\boxed{|V(\eta)|\ge H+1}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and, on a fixed-width strip,

\[
\boxed{\frac{|V(\eta)|}{|R_{L,H}|}\ge\frac1{L+1}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is a complete deterministic finite theorem. It is **not** a source-level coexistence theorem because the opposing side data are artificial.

## 6. B11 — dualize: a forbidden occupied crossing is the whole certificate
