**Theorem.** If any one of `r,s,t` is `0` or `1`, the finite P0462 coupling exists.

For `s=1`, write `H_2=span{u}`. Stochastic domination for `H_1 subset H` gives a monotone coupling

`P(B={e})`

## F. B11 — dualize: complement coupling, exact Farkas criterion, signed Plücker lift

### F1. Three-color partition equivalence

The source problem is equivalent in finite dimension to this symmetric statement:

> Do `P^{H_1}`, `P^{H_2}`, and `P^K` admit a coupling `(A,B,D)` that partitions `E` almost surely?

Indeed, in any source coupling fixed cardinalities force `A cap B=empty`; with `D=E\(A union B)`, complement duality gives `D~P^K`. Conversely a partition coupling has

`A union B=E\D~P^{K^perp}=P^H`.

This removes the asymmetric “union has a third law” presentation and turns the problem into a three-color marginal realization problem.

### F2. Exact finite Farkas/Kantorovich criterion

For three fixed-cardinality laws `mu_1,mu_2,mu_3`, a partition coupling exists iff for every real triple of set functions `f_1,f_2,f_3`,

`E_{mu_1} f_1 + E_{mu_2} f_2 + E_{mu_3} f_3`
`>= min_{E=A disjoint-union B disjoint-union D}`
`   [f_1(A)+f_2(B)+f_3(D)].`

Necessity is pointwise expectation. Sufficiency is finite LP separation/Farkas duality: infeasibility of nonnegative partition weights with the three given marginals produces exactly a violating triple of marginal potentials.

This fixes the certificate standard for future computation. A floating-point solver output is not a refutation; a candidate must be converted to explicit exact subspaces/minors plus an exact violating potential inequality.

### F3. Canonical signed coupling in the real finite case

Suppose

`R^n=H_1\oplus H_2\oplus K`

and choose `Q=[U V W] in SO(n)` with the three column blocks spanning the three subspaces. For each row partition `E=A disjoint-union B disjoint-union D`, define

`omega(A,B,D)`
`=epsilon(A,B,D) det(U_A) det(V_B) det(W_D)`,

where `epsilon` is the row-permutation sign.

Full Laplace expansion gives

`sum omega = det Q = 1`.

Fix `A`. Expanding the complementary minor over its `V/W` block partition and then using Jacobi's complementary-minor identity with `Q^{-1}=Q^T` gives

`sum_{B,D} omega(A,B,D)=det(U_A)^2`.

The same argument for fixed `B` or fixed `D` gives

`sum_{A,D} omega=det(V_B)^2`,
`sum_{A,B} omega=det(W_D)^2`.

Thus there is always a **canonical signed** three-color coupling with exactly the desired projection-DPP marginals in the real orthogonal case. The entire finite difficulty is positivity: can signed mass be moved in the kernel of the marginal map until all atoms are nonnegative?

## G. B5 — parameter deformation: explicit positive Givens families

Start from a coordinate decomposition

`E=S_1 disjoint-union S_2 disjoint-union S_3`.

Choose `i in S_a`, `j in S_b` from two colors and apply an ambient Givens rotation through angle `theta` in `span(e_i,e_j)`, fixing all other coordinates.

For color `a`, the projection DPP keeps every unaffected coordinate and selects `i` versus `j` with probabilities

`cos^2(theta)`, `sin^2(theta)`.

Color `b` makes the complementary choice. Therefore an exact common coupling is simply:

- keep all unaffected colors fixed;
- with probability `cos^2(theta)` keep the original colors of `i,j`;
- with probability `sin^2(theta)` swap them.

This solves the source coupling for every angle on this one-parameter family. Products of Givens rotations on pairwise disjoint coordinate pairs are likewise solved by independent swaps.

Hence a six-coordinate obstruction cannot be a single local cross-color rotation of a coordinate decomposition, nor a product of noninteracting such rotations. It must use genuinely interacting/noncommuting multi-coordinate mixing.
