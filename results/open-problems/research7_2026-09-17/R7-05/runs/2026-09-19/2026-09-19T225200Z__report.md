## 4. B30 — finite-state Markov-additive theorem

Let `E` be finite. From internal state `i`, one step selects a next state `j` and a bounded additive increment `z`, with joint subprobability kernel `p_ij(dz)`. Define

`P_ij = integral p_ij(dz)`.

Assume `P` irreducible and let `pi` be its stationary law. The stationary mean increment is

`mu = sum_i pi_i sum_j integral z p_ij(dz)`.

For `theta` near zero define the tilted matrix

`K_theta(i,j) = integral exp(-theta z) p_ij(dz)`.

### Theorem

If `mu>0`, then there exist `theta>0`, `r<1` and a strictly positive vector `h` satisfying

`K_theta h = r h`.

Consequently, for the additive coordinate `S_n` and two-sided slab stopping levels

`tau_- = inf{n:S_n<=-L}`,

`tau_+ = inf{n:S_n>=L}`,

one has from every initial internal state

`P(tau_-<tau_+) <= (h_max/h_min) exp(-theta L)`.       (4.1)

### Complete proof

Bounded increments make `K_theta` analytic near zero. At zero, `K_0=P`. The eigenvalue `1` of a finite irreducible stochastic matrix is simple, with left eigenvector `pi` and right eigenvector `1` after normalization.

Let `r(theta)` be its analytic Perron eigenvalue continuation. Simple-eigenvalue differentiation gives

`r'(0)=pi^T K'_0 1`.

Since

`K'_0(i,j) = - integral z p_ij(dz)`,

we obtain

`r'(0)=-mu<0`.

Therefore `r(theta)<1` for all sufficiently small positive `theta`. Perron--Frobenius provides a strictly positive right eigenvector `h`.

Set

`Y_n=exp(-theta S_n)h(J_n)`.

Then

`E[Y_{n+1}|F_n]
 = exp(-theta S_n)(K_theta h)(J_n)
 = r(theta)Y_n
 <= Y_n`.

Thus `Y_n` is nonnegative supermartingale. For `tau=tau_- wedge tau_+` and every `N`, optional stopping at `tau wedge N` gives

`E Y_{tau wedge N} <= Y_0 <= h_max`.

On `{tau_-<tau_+,tau<=N}`,

`S_tau<=-L`

and hence

`Y_tau>=exp(theta L)h_min`.

Therefore

`P(tau_-<tau_+,tau<=N)
 <= (h_max/h_min)exp(-theta L)`.

Letting `N` increase proves (4.1). No assumption that the two-sided stopping time is almost surely finite is required. QED.

## 5. Periodic RWRE corollary

A deterministic nearest-neighbor environment periodic under a finite-index lattice has a finite quotient state `J_n`; displacement in a fixed direction is a bounded Markov-additive functional of that quotient chain. Uniform ellipticity makes the quotient irreducible.

The ergodic theorem for the finite quotient chain identifies its stationary mean additive increment with the limiting velocity component `v.ell`.

Therefore

`v.ell>0`

implies the exponential wrong-way slab bound (4.1).

Because the tilted finite matrix varies continuously with the direction and `v.ell` remains positive for all directions sufficiently close to `ell`, the same spectral inequality holds uniformly on a small directional neighborhood. ## 6. B15 — exact two-phase `d=2` example with negative local drift

Define a deterministic environment depending only on the parity of the first coordinate.

Even `x_1`:

`omega(+e1)=1/2`, `omega(-e1)=1/10`, `omega(+e2)=omega(-e2)=1/5`.

Odd `x_1`:

`omega(+e1)=1/4`, `omega(-e1)=7/20`, `omega(+e2)=omega(-e2)=1/5`.

Every nearest-neighbor transition has probability at least `1/10`, so the environment is uniformly elliptic.

The local first-coordinate drift is

`2/5`

on even phases but

`-1/10`

on odd phases. Thus the old pointwise positive-drift hypothesis fails on half the lattice phases.

The parity chain changes phase with probability `3/5` and remains in the same phase with probability `2/5`, independent of the current parity. Its stationary law is therefore `(1/2,1/2)`. Hence the limiting first-coordinate speed is exactly

`v_1=(1/2)(2/5-1/10)=3/20>0`.          (6.1)

For a tilt `theta>0`, define

`a_0=(1/2)e^{-theta}+(1/10)e^{theta}`,

`a_1=(1/4)e^{-theta}+(7/20)e^{theta}`.

The tilted parity matrix is

`[[2/5,a_0],[a_1,2/5]]`,

with Perron eigenvalue

`r(theta)=2/5+sqrt(a_0a_1)`.

Writing `y=e^{2theta}` gives the exact identity

`a_0a_1 = 1/(8y)+1/5+7y/200`.

The condition `r(theta)<1` is therefore

`1/(8y)+1/5+7y/200 < 9/25`.

Multiplication by `200y` yields

`7y^2-32y+25<0`,

that is

`(y-1)(7y-25)<0`.

Hence the complete positive-tilt interval is

`0<theta<(1/2)log(25/7)`.          (6.2)

A positive right Perron vector is

`h=(sqrt(a_0),sqrt(a_1))`.

Thus for every `theta` in (6.2),

`P_omega(H_{-L}^{e1}<H_{+L}^{e1})
 <= sqrt(max(a_0,a_1)/min(a_0,a_1)) exp(-theta L)`.          (6.3)

This exact example demonstrates that local backward phases are compatible with positive speed and exponential slab control once a nonconstant multiplicative corrector is used.
