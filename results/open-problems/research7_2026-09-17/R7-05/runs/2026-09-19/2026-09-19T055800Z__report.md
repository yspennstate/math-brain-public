## 4. B2 — obstruction first: exponent plus edge-density matching is impossible

Matching the canonical degree exponent forces

`gamma = m/(2m+delta).`   (4.1)

For affine BPA in the current-book convention, if `E_n` is the edge count and `e_n=E[E_n]`, then conditional independence gives

`e_n=(1+gamma/(n-1))e_{n-1}+beta.`   (4.2)

Writing `lambda=beta/(1-gamma)` and `u_n=e_n-lambda n`, one gets exactly

`u_n=(1+gamma/(n-1))u_{n-1}`,

hence `u_n=O(n^gamma)` and

`e_n/n -> beta/(1-gamma).`   (4.3)

Under the exponent match (4.1), standard admissibility `beta<=1` therefore implies

`lambda_B <= (2m+delta)/(m+delta).`   (4.4)

But fixed PA creates exactly `m` new edges per arriving vertex, and for every `m>=2`, `delta>0`,

`m-(2m+delta)/(m+delta)`

`= [m(m-2)+(m-1)delta]/(m+delta) >0.`   (4.5)

Equivalently the affine intercept formally needed to match both invariants is

`beta_density=m(m+delta)/(2m+delta)`,

and

`beta_density-1=[m(m-2)+(m-1)delta]/(2m+delta)>0.`   (4.6)

So **no standard affine conditionally-independent PA model in the positive-threshold fixed-`m` regime can simultaneously match both the FPA power-law exponent and its asymptotic edge density**.

This corrects an easy possible overinterpretation of cross-model comparisons: same-exponent comparisons do not isolate conditional dependence while holding mean edge density fixed, because the standard BPA parameter class forbids that stronger calibration.

## 5. B11 — exact threshold equality classification

For matched exponent (4.1), the BPA threshold is

`p_B(gamma,beta)=(1/2-gamma)/(beta+sqrt(beta^2+beta gamma)).`   (5.1)

It is strictly decreasing in `beta`. Therefore `beta=1` gives the smallest threshold achievable inside the standard affine BPA class.

At `beta=1`,

`p_B,min(m,delta)`

`= delta / {2[(2m+delta)+sqrt((2m+delta)(3m+delta))]}.`   (5.2)

### m>=3

Let

`D_F=m(m+delta)+sqrt(m(m-1)(m+delta)(m+delta+1))`,

`D_B=(2m+delta)+sqrt((2m+delta)(3m+delta)).`

The first summands obey

`m(m+delta)-(2m+delta)=m(m-2)+(m-1)delta>0.`

The difference of the square-root radicands is

`m^2(m^2-7) + m(2m+3)(m-2)delta + (m^2-m-1)delta^2`,

whose coefficients are all positive for `m>=3`. Thus `D_F>D_B` and

`p_F(m,delta)<p_B,min(m,delta).`

Since `p_B` decreases with `beta`, the exact all-intercept result is

`boxed: p_F(m,delta) < p_B(matched gamma,beta)`

for every `m>=3`, `delta>0`, `0<beta<=1`.   (5.3)

Thus for `m>=3` there is **no** standard affine same-exponent BPA with the same numerical positive threshold; fixed-outdegree PA has strictly smaller `p_c` than every admissible affine BPA in this comparison.

### m=2: a sharp crossover

For `m=2`, equality of `p_F` and the extremal `p_B(gamma,1)` reduces exactly to

`delta + sqrt(2(delta+2)(delta+3)) = sqrt((delta+4)(delta+6)).`   (5.4)

Two reversible squarings reduce this to

`q(delta)=delta^4+10delta^3+24delta^2-36=0.`   (5.5)

For `delta>0`, `q'(delta)>0`, while `q(0)<0` and `q(sqrt(6))>0`, so there is exactly one positive root:

`delta_* = 1.01208790493121... .`   (5.6)

The exact sign classification is:

- `0<delta<delta_*`: `p_B(gamma,1)<p_F`;
- `delta=delta_*`: equality at `beta=1`;
- `delta>delta_*`: `p_F<p_B(gamma,1)` and hence `p_F<p_B(gamma,beta)` for every admissible `beta<=1`.

When `0<delta<delta_*`, continuity and monotonicity in `beta` imply a unique admissible equality intercept. If

`C=(1/2-gamma)/p_F`,

then solving `beta+sqrt(beta^2+beta gamma)=C` gives

`beta_*=C^2/(gamma+2C).`   (5.7)

Equivalently, with `D_F` above,

`beta_*=D_F^2/[(2m+delta)(m+2D_F)].`   (5.8)

For `m=2`, this satisfies `beta_*<1` below `delta_*`, equals one at `delta_*`, and exceeds one above it.

Therefore the neighboring R08 witness `m=2, delta=2, beta=1` lies in a regime where its inequality is not an isolated example: `delta=2>delta_*`, so the fixed threshold is smaller than the threshold of **every** admissible same-exponent affine BPA.
