## 3. B24 — extremal object: independent finite-variance smoothing closes the hard edge for a genuine no-density subclass

The mathematical idea before any implementation was to identify the strongest heavy-tailed subclass for which conditioning leaves an ordinary iid smoothed-analysis problem.

Assume

`xi = H + sigma Z`,

where `H` satisfies the same alpha-stable-domain hypotheses as the source, `Z` is independent of `H`, centered, non-degenerate and finite-variance, and `sigma>0`. After scaling take `E|Z|^2=1`. Since `alpha<2`, the finite-variance component does not alter the stable tail domain.

This class includes purely atomic examples, e.g. integer-valued Pareto-type `H` plus an independent Rademacher `Z`; hence it is genuinely outside the source's bounded-density clause.

### Theorem proved this run

For the nested iid matrix `X_n=(xi_ij)` and stable normalization `a_n`, for every bounded Borel set `K subset C`, there exists a finite exponent `B` such that on one probability-one event,

` s_min(a_n^{-1}X_n-zI) >= n^{-B}`

for all sufficiently large `n` for Lebesgue-a.e. `z in K`.

In particular, for every fixed `q>0`, eventually

` s_min(a_n^{-1}X_n-zI) >= exp(-n^q)`

for Lebesgue-a.e. `z` on one probability-one event.

### Complete proof

Write `H_n=(H_ij)`, `Z_n=(Z_ij)` and

`X_n-a_n zI = sigma [Z_n+B_n(z)]`,

`B_n(z)=sigma^{-1}(H_n-a_n zI)`.

Stable-domain regular variation gives `P(|H|>t)<=t^{-alpha+o(1)}`. Choose `C>3/alpha`. Then

`P(max_{i,j<=n}|H_ij|>n^C)
 <= n^2 P(|H|>n^C)
 <= n^{2-alpha C+o(1)}`,

which is summable. Therefore almost surely eventually

`max|H_ij|<=n^C`

and hence

`||H_n||<=n^{C+1}`.

Because `a_n` is regularly varying with index `1/alpha`, for every `epsilon>0`, eventually `a_n<=n^{1/alpha+epsilon}`. Thus for every bounded `K` there is a deterministic `gamma` with

`sup_{z in K} ||B_n(z)|| <= n^gamma`

almost surely eventually.

Condition on the full heavy core `(H_ij)`. Tao–Vu's shifted-iid theorem, in the form quoted by Cook Theorem 1.8, says that for every desired failure exponent `R>0`, there is `beta=beta(R,gamma)` such that uniformly over deterministic complex `B` with `||B||<=n^gamma`,

`P_Z(s_min(Z_n+B)<=n^{-beta}) <= C n^{-R}`.

Choose `R>2`. For large `n`, conditional on the heavy core,

`E_Z[ integral_K 1_{s_min(Z_n+B_n(z))<=n^{-beta}} dz | H ]
 <= |K| C n^{-R}`.

Summing over `n`, Tonelli gives finite conditional expectation. Thus for almost every noise realization and Lebesgue-a.e. `z`, only finitely many failures occur. Unconditioning gives the one-event/a.e.-shift statement.

Finally

`s_min(a_n^{-1}X_n-zI)
 = (sigma/a_n)s_min(Z_n+B_n(z))
 >= sigma a_n^{-1}n^{-beta}
 >= n^{-B}`

for some finite `B`. QED.

### Consequence for the ESD

The cumulative C0031 derivation has already isolated the only missing no-density a.s. step as the lower-log / ultra-hard-edge defect below `exp(-n^q)`, with all larger singular-value scales controlled a.s. under the source hypotheses. The theorem above removes that defect completely for the independent-smoothing subclass. Therefore the source Hermitization argument yields

`mu_{a_n^{-1}X_n} => mu_alpha` almost surely

for this subclass, without bounded density.

This is a complete restricted theorem but not a source-wide solution, so `BC_AS` remains 0.

## 4. B18 — another language: exact fair edge-flow representation of the symmetric discrete-Pareto benchmark

The original STUCK1 benchmark was

`P(xi=k)=P(xi=-k)=p_k`,

`p_k=c_alpha k^{-1-alpha}`.

A stronger decomposition than the previous unit-mask thinning is available.

For `k>=1`, define

`w_k = 2(p_{k+1}-p_{k+2}+p_{k+3}-...)`.

Since `(p_k)` decreases to zero, the alternating-series theorem gives `w_k>0`, and

`w_{k-1}+w_k=2p_k` for `k>=2`.

Set

`w_0=2p_1-w_1>0`.

Place weight `w_0` on the central edge `{-1,+1}` and weight `w_k` on each of the two ordinary edges `{k,k+1}` and `{-k,-(k+1)}`. The incident edge weights at every vertex `+/-k` sum to `2p_k`. The total edge weight is 1 because the total weighted degree is 2.

Sample an edge according to its weight and then select one endpoint by an independent fair sign. The selected endpoint has exactly the law of `xi`. Writing the endpoints around their midpoint yields the exact representation

`xi = Y + A epsilon`,

where `epsilon` is a fair Rademacher independent of `(Y,A)` and

`A in {1/2,1}`.

Equivalently,

`2xi = Z + K epsilon`,

with integer `Z` and `K in {1,2}`.

This is a theorem-sized exact identity, not an approximation.

For the matrix, conditional on the latent edges,

`2(X_n-a_n zI)=D_n(z)+K_n circ E_n`,

where `E_n` has iid fair signs, `K_ij in {1,2}`, and `D_n(z)=Z_n-2a_nzI` is deterministic conditional on the latent edges.

To keep the signs independent while obtaining a polynomial core, truncate the latent edge/midpoint variables, not the final `xi`. Their tail is of the same Pareto order. With `C>3/alpha`, the event that some latent midpoint in the `n x n` block exceeds `n^C` occurs only summably often. Conditional on the complementary event, `D_n(z)` has polynomial norm for bounded `z`.

Thus the discrete-Pareto benchmark is reduced to a clean bounded-profile smoothed theorem:

> For arbitrary polynomial-norm deterministic complex cores `D_n` and deterministic amplitude profiles `K_ij in {1,2}`, obtain a summable polynomial (or merely stretched-exponential-scale) lower-tail bound for `s_min(D_n+K circ E_n)`.

This is narrower than the previous heavy-tailed random-normal LCD theorem because every random coordinate is now bounded and fair.

## 5. Existing theorem boundary for the bounded profile

If `K` were constant, Tao–Vu's shifted-iid theorem would immediately give arbitrary polynomial confidence and the benchmark hard edge would close.

For variable `K`, scale by 2 and view `A=K/2 in {1/2,1}` as Cook's standard-deviation profile. Its threshold graph is complete, hence super-regular. Cook Theorem 1.26 allows an arbitrary polynomial deterministic mean profile, but the probability bound has an unavoidable displayed term

`sqrt(log n/n)`.

That is not summable and is exactly too weak for the full-sequence Borel–Cantelli step. Cook Remark 1.16 explicitly says stronger Littlewood–Offord tools may improve the probability estimate but are not pursued in that paper.

Therefore the precise residual benchmark problem is no longer “heavy tails” in a tested column. It is:

`dense bounded non-iid Bernoulli amplitudes + arbitrary polynomial complex deterministic core + summable hard-edge confidence`.

The edge-flow representation also explains why Theorem 1 in this report works while discrete Pareto remains open: in Theorem 1, conditioning leaves iid finite-variance noise independent of the heavy core; in the edge-flow representation, the amplitude profile is coupled to the latent core.

## 6. B27 — swap quantifiers: a.e. z via Fubini

A naive grid approximation of `a_n z` cannot solve the generic complex-shift problem: an `O(1)` raw perturbation is vastly larger than the desired polynomial hard-edge floor, so Weyl perturbation cannot transfer an integer or lattice shift to a generic fixed `z`.

Hermitization only needs the property for Lebesgue-a.e. `z` on one probability-one sample event, not uniformly over every `z`. Theorem 1 exploits the correct order of quantifiers: condition on the heavy core, apply a bound uniform over deterministic perturbations, integrate failure indicators over `z`, then use Tonelli and Borel–Cantelli. No continuum union bound is paid.

The same Fubini closure will apply to the edge-flow benchmark as soon as the bounded-profile Bernoulli theorem is upgraded from `sqrt(log n/n)` to a summable confidence level.

## 7. Failed routes / limitations

1. The independent-smoothing theorem does not cover a general stable-domain atom because probability measures can be convolution-indecomposable; the existence of a Bernoulli decomposition with state-dependent amplitude is not the same as an independent additive convolution factor.
2. The fair edge-flow representation does not make the amplitude profile iid after conditioning on the heavy core; `K` is coupled to the latent edge/midpoint. Therefore Tao–Vu's shifted-iid theorem cannot simply be invoked conditionally.
3. Cook Theorem 1.26 is structurally applicable to the dense bounded profile but supplies only a nonsummable `sqrt(log n/n)` error term.
4. The restricted convolution subclass result is not evidence that the full source endpoint is closed, and no independent audits exist.
