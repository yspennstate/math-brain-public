## 4. B4 — exact adaptive-block theorem

`Delta = (1/2) I_+ - (5/6) I_-`,

where `I_+,I_-` are iid copies of `I=int_0^1 B(t)^2 dt`, and

`I=sum_{r>=1} Z_r^2/(pi^2 r^2)`.

Conditioning on all coordinates except `Z_1` shows `I` has no atoms: a level equation leaves at most two values of the continuously distributed `Z_1`. Its support is all `(0,infinity)`: finite Gaussian coordinates can place a finite partial sum in any prescribed positive interval while a sufficiently remote nonnegative tail is simultaneously small with positive probability. Thus `Delta` is nonatomic and has both signs.

Set

`q_* = P(Delta<0) = P(I_+ < (5/3) I_-)`.

Exchangeability gives `P(I_+<I_-)=1/2`; full support gives positive mass on `I_-<I_+<(5/3)I_-`, hence `q_*>1/2`. Full support also gives positive mass on `I_+>(5/3)I_-`, hence `q_*<1`. Therefore

`1/2 < q_* < 1`.

For iid block innovations `Delta_1,...,Delta_K`, let `x_j=1` mean monotone and `0` crossed. Relative innovation cost is `sum x_j Delta_j`. Without an external orientation constraint, the almost-sure unique optimizer is

`x_j = 1{Delta_j<0}`,

`K^{-1} sum x_j -> q_*` a.s.

`p_*=q_*/2 in (1/4,1/2)`.
