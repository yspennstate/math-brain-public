## E. Methods B2 / B11 / B5 and outcomes

### B2 — negate the naive horizontal-monotonicity selection rule

Two exact two-by-two examples show that arbitrarily small vertical perturbations can reverse which horizontal pairing is optimal.

Take `X1=(0,0)` and `X2=(1,0)`.

If `Y1=(2,0)`, `Y2=(3,epsilon)`, then

`C_mono = 2 + sqrt(4+epsilon^2)`

and

`C_cross = 1 + sqrt(9+epsilon^2)`.

Since `sqrt(9+x)-sqrt(4+x)` decreases strictly from `1`, one has

`C_cross < C_mono` for every `epsilon>0`.

If instead `Y1=(2,epsilon)`, `Y2=(3,0)`, the same derivative comparison gives

`C_mono < C_cross` for every `epsilon>0`.

The inequalities are strict and persist under small generic perturbations. Thus no local pairwise theorem saying “small vertical mismatch forces horizontal sorting/translation” can solve P0336.

### B11 — solve the quadratic fiber cell problem

Set the natural scaled vertical displacement `Z=sqrt(n) H`. At quadratic order the excess energy is

`Z^2 / (2D)`.

On a vertical fiber, every admissible horizontal coupling has `A~U[0,1]`, `B~U[2,3]`, hence

`E D = E(B-A) = 2`.

For a prescribed conditional mean `m=E Z`, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

`(E Z)^2 <= E(Z^2/D) E D = 2 E(Z^2/D)`, hence

`E[ Z^2/(2D) ] >= m^2/4`.

Equality holds iff

`Z = (m/2) D` almost surely.

Crucially, for **every** horizontal coupling of the two uniform horizontal marginals, setting `Z=(m/2)(B-A)` attains equality. Therefore the complete quadratic fiber-cell minimizer set contains every horizontal coupling.

This is a full exact lemma and materially changes the route: second-order Gamma-limit energy alone cannot identify the source limit.

### B5 — add anisotropy and differentiate

`c_lambda(D,H)=sqrt(D^2 + lambda H^2)`,

`c_lambda(D,rD)-D = D( sqrt(1+lambda r^2)-1 )`.

Since every fiber coupling has `E D=2`,

`E[c_lambda(D,rD)-D] = 2( sqrt(1+lambda r^2)-1 )`,
