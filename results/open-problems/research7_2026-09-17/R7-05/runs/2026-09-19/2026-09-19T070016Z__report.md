## 3. Fresh prior-art check

The key later source is Alex Zhai, **“Exponential concentration of cover times,”** *Electronic Journal of Probability* 23 (2018), DOI `10.1214/18-EJP149`, arXiv `1407.7617`.

Zhai's Theorem 1.1 gives exponential GFF concentration for cover time on general graphs; Remark 1.4 says the proof is actually for finite electrical networks. In Section 4 the exact weighted form is written with

\[
c_{\rm tot}=\sum_{x,y}c_{xy},

and yields universal constants with center \(c_{\rm tot}M^2/2\) and fluctuation scale

c_{\rm tot}(\sqrt{\lambda R}\,M+\lambda R).

## 4. B1 — change category: chain to electrical network/GFF

Fix one source-admissible symmetric reversible chain with transition matrix \(P=(p_{xy})\). Use the electrical network

\[
c_{xy}=p_{xy}.
\]

Uniform reversibility makes \(P\) symmetric, and row sums equal one. Hence

\[
c_x=1,
\qquad
c_{\rm tot}=\sum_{x,y}c_{xy}=n.
\tag{3}
\]

Zhai explicitly allows self-loops. The continuous-time network walk has the same embedded chain and independent mean-one exponential holding times. Thus expected continuous-time hitting/cover times equal expected embedded-chain step counts; all source quantities retain their values.

Let \(\eta\) be a pinned GFF and set

\[
\zeta_x=\eta_x-\frac1n\sum_v\eta_v.
\]

Then \(\sum_x\zeta_x=0\), differences are unchanged, and

\[
E(\zeta_x-\zeta_y)^2=R_{\rm eff}(x,y).
\tag{4}
\]

The centered GFF is invariant under the chain's transitive symmetry, so

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_x)=\sigma^2
\]

for every `x`. Averaging (4) in `y`, using the zero-sum covariance identity, gives

\[
\frac1n\sum_yR_{\rm eff}(x,y)=2\sigma^2.
\tag{5}
\]

## 5. B18 — effective-resistance translation gives an exact identity

The commute identity in the network is

\[
E_xT_y+E_yT_x=c_{\rm tot}R_{\rm eff}(x,y)=nR_{\rm eff}(x,y).
\]

Aldous–Fill symmetry gives equality of the two hitting-time terms, hence

\[
E_xT_y=\frac n2R_{\rm eff}(x,y).
\tag{6}
\]

Average in `y` under the uniform stationary law. By the source definition \(E_\pi T_x=\tau_0\), so (5) and (6) yield the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\tau_0=n\sigma^2.}
\tag{7}
\]

Now let

\[
M=E\max_x\eta_x=E\max_x\zeta_x.
\]

No coordinate independence is needed for the Gaussian log-sum-exp bound:

\[
M\le\sigma\sqrt{2\log n}.
\tag{8}
\]

Therefore Zhai's deterministic center satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{c_{\rm tot}}2M^2
=\frac n2M^2
\le n\sigma^2\log n
=\tau_0\log n.
}
\tag{9}
\]

This is the sharp source constant before any error term is estimated.

## 6. B3 — stronger quantitative form of Open Problem 7.6

By source Lemma 7.2(ii) and (6),

\[
\frac n2R
=\max_{x,y}E_xT_y
\le2\tau_0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{R\le4\sigma^2.}
\tag{10}
\]

Zhai's exponential tail (2), integrated by layer cake, gives

\[
E\left|\tau_{\rm cov}-\frac n2M^2\right|
\le Cn(\sqrt R\,M+R)
\tag{11}
\]

for a universal `C`. Combining (7), (8), and (10),

\[
n\sqrt R\,M
\le2\sqrt2\,\tau_0\sqrt{\log n},
\qquad
nR\le4\tau_0.
\tag{12}
\]

Using (9)–(12),

\[
\boxed{
EC
\le \tau_0\log n+C\tau_0(\sqrt{\log n}+1)
=\tau_0\log n\left(1+O((\log n)^{-1/2})\right).
}
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the source statement (AF7.6) follows, and the derivation is quantitatively stronger.

No unknown cover-time estimate is fed back into the proof: the inputs are the source symmetry identities, source Lemma 7.2(ii), the commute identity, Zhai's published network theorem, and the elementary Gaussian maximum inequality. In particular, I did **not** use Zhai's later corollary involving \(t_{\rm hit}/t_{\rm cov}\), so there is no circular need to know the cover-time scale in advance.
