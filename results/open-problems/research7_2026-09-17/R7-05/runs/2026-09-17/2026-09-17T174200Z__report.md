## 4. Exact blocking formula

The remaining local obstruction from the previous report can be stated without ambiguity.

Put the averaged traceless matrix and transverse deformation in the common eigenbasis

`Bbar = diag(beta,-beta)`, `beta>|a|`,

`D(q) = [[a,b],[q/b,-a]]`, `b != 0`,

and write the two environmental matrices as

`B_s(q)=Bbar+sD(q)`, `s in {+1,-1}`.

Let

`D_0 := beta^2-a^2 > 0`.

For `q>0`, the individual top eigenvalues are

`beta_+(q)=sqrt((beta+a)^2+q)`,

`beta_-(q)=sqrt((beta-a)^2+q)`,

and the slow-switching baseline relevant to the all-Hurwitz threshold is

`S(q):=(beta_+(q)+beta_-(q))/2`.

Let `F(q,r)` be the top Lyapunov exponent of the traceless pair at common switching rate `r`. Define the excess

`Delta(q,r):=F(q,r)-S(q)`.

Also define

`P(q):=beta_+(q) beta_-(q)`,

`L(q):=D_0-q`.

The previous R7-05 algebra gives the exact overlap expression

`K(q) = [P(q)+L(q)]/[2P(q)]`

and the exact identity

`P(q)^2-L(q)^2 = 4 beta^2 q`.

Consequently

`1-K(q) = 2 beta^2 q / [P(q)(P(q)+L(q))]`,

so, as `q -> 0+`,

`log K(q) = - beta^2 q / D_0^2 + O(q^2)`.

For each **fixed** `q>0`, the published slow-switching expansion used in the previous report gives

`Delta(q,r) = (r/2) log K(q) + o_q(r)` as `r -> 0+`.

For each **fixed** `r>0`, the transverse projective calculation from the previous report gives

`Delta(q,r)`

`= - q * beta^2 r/[2D_0(D_0+beta r)] + o_r(q)` as `q -> 0+`.

These two one-parameter formulas agree in their common formal first term. But they do **not** by themselves control an arbitrary path `r=r(q)->0`.

The exact missing statement is therefore the following uniform factorization/remainder estimate:

```text
Delta(q,r) = (r/2) log K(q) + R(q,r),
R(q,r) = o(q r) uniformly as (q,r)->(0,0) through q>0, r>0.
```

Equivalently, one needs a joint factorization

```text
Delta(q,r) = q r G(q,r),
G(q,r) -> - beta^2/(2 D_0^2)
```

as `(q,r)->(0,0)` from the positive quadrant.

That is the blocking formula. If it were established, then `Delta(q,r)<0` throughout a full punctured rectangle `0<q<q_0`, `0<r<r_0`, and the last local all-Hurwitz boundary layer near the common-invariant-line locus would disappear. Without it, a logically possible curve `r=r(q)->0` can hide inside the nonuniform remainders of both separate asymptotic statements.

## 6. The missing ingredient no previous route contains

Every successful local argument so far is one-parameter: either the matrices are fixed while switching slows, or the switching rate is fixed while the matrices approach the common-line boundary. The corner is singular in **both** parameters:

1. as `r->0`, the environmental chain develops long dwell intervals and the stationary projective law approaches a mixture of modewise invariant directions;
2. as `q->0`, those modewise attracting directions coalesce with the common invariant line and the transverse coupling that creates the response vanishes.

A proof must therefore contain something none of the prior routes contains:

> a **joint, quantitative renewal/contraction estimate** for the projective PDMP that remains uniform while the switching time diverges and the two attracting projective directions coalesce, and whose error vanishes with both `q` and `r`.

The useful target is not generic continuity. It is an estimate with the correct product structure, for example

`|R(q,r)| <= C q r (q+r)`

in a fixed small rectangle, or any bound implying `R(q,r)=o(qr)` uniformly. Such a bound would encode the fact that the contextual excess vanishes on **both** coordinate axes and that the first nonzero mixed term has the already-known negative coefficient.

Analyticity at each fixed nondegenerate parameter value is not enough. Separate differentiability along the two axes is also not enough. The missing ingredient is uniform control through the degenerate corner.

## 7. Simplest unresolved special case

The cleanest test of the missing mechanism is obtained by removing every inessential parameter and retaining only the transverse coupling. Set

`beta=1`, `a=0`, `b=1`, `c=q>0`.

Then

```text
B_s(q) = [[1, s], [s q, -1]],    s in {+1,-1}.
```

For this family the slow threshold and overlap data simplify exactly to

`S(q)=sqrt(1+q)`,

`P(q)=1+q`,

`L(q)=1-q`,

`K(q)=1/(1+q)`.

The projective Riccati dynamics is simply

`z' = s q - 2z - s z^2`,

with the two attracting modewise directions approaching one another as `q->0`.

The slow expansion becomes

`F(q,r)-sqrt(1+q) = -(r/2) log(1+q) + o_q(r)`.

The **simplest still-unproved statement** is therefore:

```text
There exist q_0>0 and r_0>0 such that
F(q,r) < sqrt(1+q)
for every 0<q<q_0 and every 0<r<r_0.
```

Equivalently, prove in this single normalized one-dimensional PDMP that

`F(q,r)-sqrt(1+q) = -(q r)/2 + o(qr)`

uniformly in the positive slow corner.

This is deliberately narrower than the full contextual problem. It is the smallest case that still contains the exact nonuniformity responsible for the present stall. Deciding it will tell us whether the obstruction is merely technical or whether a genuine slow boundary layer can survive even in the most symmetric family.

## 8. Nearest solved analogue and exactly what it has that we lack

The nearest solved analytic analogue is the slow-switching expansion in Monmarché–Schreiber–Strickler (2025). For a **fixed** collection of matrices satisfying the paper's hypotheses, their expansion identifies the first correction to the average of the modewise Perron exponents. In the present two-state symmetric normalization this yields

`Delta(q,r) = (r/2) log K(q) + o_q(r)`.

That theorem supplies precisely the correct first derivative in `r` for each fixed `q>0`. What the current problem lacks is a version whose constants are controlled as `q->0`, where the two Perron directions coalesce and the problem approaches a reducible common-line boundary.

Lawley–Mattingly–Reed (2014) is the nearest qualitative stability analogue. It proves slow-switching stability when each individual matrix is Hurwitz and fast-switching stability when the average is Hurwitz. What it lacks for this purpose is a quantitative threshold uniform under the coalescing-eigendirection perturbation and, more importantly, the mixed `q r` asymptotic needed to compare with the contextual threshold.

Monmarché–Strickler (2025) supplies a nearby fast-regime phenomenon in which all convex combinations can be stable while a suitable switching rate has positive Lyapunov exponent. That confirms that the contextual obstruction is not a trivial convexity principle, but its high-frequency construction does not resolve the present slow `q>0`, `r->0` corner or the planar multi-band question.

Thus the nearest literature has the **one-parameter perturbation machinery**; the missing piece is its **uniform degeneration analysis** when the projective fixed points merge.
