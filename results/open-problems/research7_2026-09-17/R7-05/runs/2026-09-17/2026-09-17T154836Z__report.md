## 4. B3 — prove something stronger: opposite first-order endpoint corrections in the 2 x 2 Perron/Metzler subclass

The old numerical route tried to make the traceless response curve `F(r)` rise above its slow and fast baselines at both ends, hoping that two endpoint-born humps would survive a Hurwitz-compatible scalar threshold. The following theorem shows that this mechanism is algebraically impossible in a natural rigorous subclass.

### Theorem 4.1 — endpoint-sign opposition

Consider two irreducible Metzler `2 x 2` matrices under symmetric two-state continuous-time Markov switching with common jump rate `r`. Remove arbitrary scalar parts from each matrix; these scalar parts contribute only an `r`-independent stationary average, so they do not affect the first endpoint corrections. Denote the resulting traceless matrices by `B_0,B_1`, and write

`Bbar = (B_0+B_1)/2`.

Let

`beta_i > 0`

be the Perron/spectral-abscissa eigenvalue of `B_i`, and let

`beta > 0`

be that of `Bbar`. Let `F(r)` be the top Lyapunov exponent of the traceless switched pair.

Then there is a similarity basis in which

`Bbar = [[beta,0],[0,-beta]]`

and

`D := (B_0-B_1)/2 = [[a,b],[c,-a]]`.

Put

`q := b c`.

The rigorous slow/fast Markovian expansions from Monmarche–Schreiber–Strickler specialize to

`F(r) = (beta_0+beta_1)/2 + (r/2) log K + o(r)` as `r -> 0`,

and

`F(r) = beta + q/(2r) + o(1/r)` as `r -> infinity`,

where

`K = (ell_0^+ v_1^+)(ell_1^+ v_0^+) > 0`

is the product of the two Perron-eigenline transition overlaps, with `ell_i^+ v_i^+ = 1`.

Moreover,

`sign(log K) = -sign(q)`.

Thus the slow and fast first-order corrections always have opposite signs, unless both first-order coefficients vanish.

### Proof

The only imported ingredients are the published asymptotic formulas. Everything relating their coefficients is elementary `2 x 2` algebra and is derived here.

Because the two environmental states have equal common jump rate `r`, the normalized switching matrix used in the 2025 paper has `p=1/2` and its frequency parameter is `omega=2r`: each physical off-diagonal rate is `omega/2=r`.

For the slow expansion, Theorem 1, equation (10), gives

`Lambda_M(omega) = (beta_0+beta_1)/2 + c_s omega + o(omega)`.

For two equally weighted environments,

`c_s = (1/4)[ log(ell_1^+ v_0^+) + log(ell_0^+ v_1^+) ]`

`    = (1/4) log K`.

Substituting `omega=2r` gives

`F(r) = (beta_0+beta_1)/2 + (r/2) log K + o(r)`.

For the fast expansion, Proposition 2, equation (16), gives at `p=1/2`

`c_f = (1/4){ ellbar (B_0-B_1)^2 vbar - [ellbar(B_0-B_1)vbar]^2 }`,

where `vbar,ellbar` are the normalized dominant right/left eigenvectors of `Bbar`.

In the chosen eigenbasis of `Bbar`, take

`vbar=e_1`, `ellbar=e_1^T`.

Since

`B_0-B_1 = 2D`

and every traceless `2 x 2` matrix satisfies

`D^2=(a^2+bc)I`,

we obtain

`c_f = (1/4)[4(a^2+bc)-4a^2] = bc = q`.

Equation (11) therefore becomes

`F(r) = beta + q/omega + o(1/omega)`

`     = beta + q/(2r) + o(1/r)`.

It remains to compare `q` with `K`.

The two matrices are

`B_0 = [[beta+a,b],[c,-beta-a]]`,

`B_1 = [[beta-a,-b],[-c,-beta+a]]`.

Therefore

`beta_0^2 = (beta+a)^2 + q`,

`beta_1^2 = (beta-a)^2 + q`.

Define

`L := beta^2-a^2-q`.

A trace calculation gives

`tr(B_0 B_1)=2L`.

For a traceless hyperbolic `2 x 2` matrix `B_i`, the dominant rank-one spectral projector is

`P_i^+ = (I+B_i/beta_i)/2`.

Since `P_i^+=v_i^+ ell_i^+`,

`K = tr(P_0^+ P_1^+)`.

Consequently

`K = 1/2 + tr(B_0B_1)/(4 beta_0 beta_1)`

`  = (beta_0 beta_1 + L)/(2 beta_0 beta_1)`.

Now compute exactly

`(beta_0 beta_1)^2 - L^2`

`= [((beta+a)^2+q)((beta-a)^2+q)] - (beta^2-a^2-q)^2`

`= 4 beta^2 q`.

This is the decisive identity.

If `q>0`, then

`beta_0 beta_1 > |L|`,

hence

`0 < K < 1`,

so `log K<0`.

If `q<0`, then

`beta_0 beta_1 < |L|`.

Irreducible Metzler Perron eigenvectors are strictly positive, so `K>0`. The alternative `L < -beta_0 beta_1` would make `K<0`, impossible. Therefore

`L > beta_0 beta_1`,

which gives

`K>1`,

so `log K>0`.

Finally, if `q=0`, the squared identity gives `beta_0 beta_1=|L|`. Again `K>0` excludes `L=-beta_0 beta_1`, so `L=beta_0 beta_1` and `K=1`. Both first-order endpoint coefficients then vanish.

Hence

`sign(log K) = -sign(q)`,

with simultaneous zero in the degenerate case. QED.

### What this theorem does and does not establish

It rigorously rules out a tempting *mechanism*: in the irreducible two-dimensional Perron/Metzler subclass, one cannot make `F` initially bend upward away from both its slow and fast limiting values. One endpoint's first correction is up exactly when the other's is down.

It does **not** prove the stronger contextual Malrieu variant impossible. Two separated interior humps could still be born away from both asymptotic regimes, and Malrieu's source allows non-Metzler real matrices. The theorem therefore remains PARTIAL relative to the strengthened all-Hurwitz target.

It also explains why endpoint-biased searches are structurally poor: they optimize a configuration that the first-order endpoint geometry forbids in this entire subclass.

## 5. B14 — exploit projective symmetry: an exact flux/entropy identity for the complementary elliptic regime

The Perron/Metzler theorem above uses projective fixed points. To avoid remaining trapped in that category, I derived an exact identity in the complementary regime where the traceless angular flows have no zeros.

Let

`B=[[a,b],[c,-a]]`,

and for

`u(theta)=(cos theta,sin theta)`,

`u_perp(theta)=(-sin theta,cos theta)`,

define the radial and angular fields

`g_B(theta) = <B u,u>`,

`v_B(theta) = <B u,u_perp>`.

Direct calculation gives

`g_B(theta) = a cos(2 theta) + (b+c)/2 sin(2 theta)`,

`v_B(theta) = (c-b)/2 + (b+c)/2 cos(2 theta) - a sin(2 theta)`.

Therefore the following pointwise identity holds for **every** traceless real `2 x 2` matrix:

`v_B'(theta) = -2 g_B(theta)`.                                          (5.1)

Now take the symmetric two-mode switching pair `B_0,B_1` at rate `r`, and suppose each angular field `v_i` is nowhere zero on the projective circle. This is exactly the no-real-eigendirection/elliptic situation for a traceless `2 x 2` matrix.

Let `rho_i(theta)` be a stationary density of `(theta,I)`. It satisfies

`-(v_0 rho_0)' + r(rho_1-rho_0)=0`,

`-(v_1 rho_1)' + r(rho_0-rho_1)=0`.

Adding the equations shows that the total projective probability flux

`J := v_0 rho_0 + v_1 rho_1`

is constant in `theta`.

The top radial exponent is

`F(r)= integral [g_0 rho_0 + g_1 rho_1] dtheta`.

Using (5.1),

`F(r) = -(1/2) sum_i integral v_i' rho_i dtheta`.

Because each `v_i` is nowhere zero, `log|v_i|` is smooth on the projective circle. Put

`y_i=v_i rho_i`.

Then

`v_i' rho_i = (log|v_i|)' y_i`.

Periodic integration by parts gives

`F(r) = (1/2) sum_i integral log|v_i| y_i' dtheta`.

The stationary equations say

`y_0' = r(rho_1-rho_0)`,

`y_1' = r(rho_0-rho_1)`.

Hence the exact identity

`F(r) = (r/2) integral (rho_1-rho_0) log|v_0/v_1| dtheta`.              (5.2)

No discretization, perturbation expansion, or numerical approximation enters (5.2).

### Interpretation

Equation (5.2) moves the elliptic two-matrix problem from radial growth to a signed transport/entropy-like pairing between

1. the mode-imbalance density `rho_1-rho_0`, and
2. the log angular-speed ratio `log|v_0/v_1|`.

Thus any multi-hump construction in the elliptic/no-zero-speed regime must make the invariant mode imbalance change its correlation with one fixed log-speed contrast as `r` varies. The scalar radial functions have disappeared from the final formula.

This gives a genuinely different route from the previous Fourier-corrector certificates and from the Perron endpoint expansions.

`h > max((beta_0+beta_1)/2, beta_bar)`.
