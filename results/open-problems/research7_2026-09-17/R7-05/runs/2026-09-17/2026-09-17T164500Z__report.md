## 4. B6 — deform to the first-order-degenerate point: it is an exactly flat common-line boundary

The preceding report put the averaged traceless matrix into the eigenbasis

`Bbar = diag(beta,-beta)`, `beta>0`,

and wrote

`D=(B0-B1)/2 = [[a,b],[c,-a]]`,

with invariant scalar

`q=bc`.

The slow and fast first-order corrections both vanish when `q=0`. The natural question was whether a second-order switching hump can bifurcate there.

The first new result is that the `q=0` boundary is much more rigid than merely first-order degenerate.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact flatness on the common-invariant-line boundary

Assume `beta>|a|` and `q=bc=0`. Then for the symmetric two-state Markov switching between

`B_+ = Bbar+D`,

`B_- = Bbar-D`,

at any common jump rate `r>0`, the top Lyapunov exponent is exactly

`F(r)=beta`.

In particular it is independent of `r`. Therefore no scalar shifts making both endpoint matrices and their average strictly Hurwitz can produce a switched instability on this boundary.

### Proof

If `q=0`, at least one of `b,c` vanishes.

Suppose first `c=0`. Then

`B_+ = [[beta+a,b],[0,-beta-a]]`,

`B_- = [[beta-a,-b],[0,-beta+a]]`.

Both matrices preserve the line spanned by `e_1`; the cocycle is upper triangular in one common basis.

For a stationary symmetric two-state chain, the asymptotic fraction of time in each mode is `1/2`, independently of the jump rate. Therefore the Lyapunov exponent along the common invariant line is

`(1/2)(beta+a)+(1/2)(beta-a)=beta`.

The exponent on the one-dimensional quotient is

`(1/2)(-beta-a)+(1/2)(-beta+a)=-beta`.

Triangular off-diagonal shear does not change the two Lyapunov exponents: the exponents of a triangular linear cocycle are the time averages of its diagonal entries, equivalently the exponents on the invariant flag and quotient. Since `beta>0`, the top exponent is `beta`.

If instead `b=0`, the same argument applies to the common lower-triangular invariant flag. Hence `F(r)=beta` for every `r>0` whenever `q=0`.

For the stronger contextual scalar shift, at `q=0` one has

`beta_+=beta+a`, `beta_-=beta-a`

because `beta>|a|`, and therefore

`(beta_++beta_-)/2=beta=beta_bar`.

Strict Hurwitz feasibility requires `h>beta`, whereas the switched exponent after the scalar shift is `beta-h<0` for every rate. Thus no all-Hurwitz instability occurs on the boundary. QED.

### Consequence

The first-order-degenerate set is not a plausible birthplace for two switching humps by an intrinsic second-order effect at `q=0`: the full response curve is exactly flat there. Any bifurcation must come from leaving the common-invariant-line locus.

## 5. B21 — import linear-response thinking, but derive the response self-containedly in the projective Riccati chart

I next perturb transversely away from the common-line boundary and compute the exact first response of `F(r)` without a numerical stationary solve.

Fix

`beta>|a|`, `b != 0`,

and use `q` itself as the transverse parameter by setting

`c=q/b`.

Write the two modes with `s in {+1,-1}` as

`B_s(q) = [[beta+s a, s b], [s c, -beta-s a]]`.

The symmetric environment `s_t` flips sign at rate `r`.

### 5.1 A uniformly attracting projective strip for small `q`

In the affine projective chart

`z=x_2/x_1`,

the Riccati equation is exactly

`z' = s c - 2(beta+s a) z - s b z^2`.                 (5.1)

Put

`delta=beta-|a|>0`.

Choose `M>1/(2 delta)` large enough that `2 delta M>1`, and then choose `|c|` small enough that

`|b| M^2 |c| < 2 delta M-1`.

At the upper boundary `z=M|c|`, for either mode,

`z' <= |c|-2 delta M|c|+|b|M^2 c^2 <0`.

At the lower boundary `z=-M|c|`,

`z' >= -|c|+2 delta M|c|-|b|M^2 c^2 >0`.

Hence the strip

`|z| <= M|c|`

is invariant for both deterministic mode flows and therefore for every switching realization.

Moreover, inside this strip the derivative of the right-hand side with respect to `z` satisfies

`partial_z f_s(z) = -2(beta+s a)-2 s b z <= -2 delta+2|b|M|c|`.

After shrinking `|c|` once more, this is at most `-delta<0`. Thus two projective trajectories driven by the same switching path contract exponentially. There is consequently a unique attracting stationary projective law inside this strip, and it is the top Oseledets branch continuously inherited from `e_1` at `q=0`.

This gives all regularity needed below directly; no black-box analyticity theorem is required.

### 5.2 Exact first derivative at `q=0`

For `c != 0`, put

`z=cw`.

Equation (5.1) becomes

`w' = s -2(beta+s a)w - s b c w^2`.                  (5.2)

The invariant strip gives `|w|<=M` uniformly as `c->0`. Any stationary subsequential limit as `c->0` is therefore a stationary law of the linear PDMP

`w' = s -2(beta+s a)w`,                              (5.3)

with `s` flipping at rate `r`. The contraction in the preceding paragraph makes the stationary law of (5.3) unique, so the full stationary laws converge.

Let under that limiting law

`M_1=E[w]`,

`N_1=E[s w]`.

Applying the stationary generator to `w` gives

`0 = -2 beta M_1 -2 a N_1`,                          (5.4)

because `E[s]=0`.

Applying it to `s w` gives one unit from the forcing `s*s=1`, the continuous drift, and a jump contribution `-2r s w`:

`0 = 1 -2(beta+r)N_1 -2a M_1`.                      (5.5)

Eliminating `M_1` yields

`N_1 = beta / ( 2[ beta(beta+r)-a^2 ] )`.

Writing

`D_0 := beta^2-a^2 >0`,

this is

`N_1(r)= beta / (2(D_0+beta r))`.                    (5.6)

Along the attracting projective branch,

`d/dt log|x_1| = beta+s a+s b z`.

The environment has symmetric marginal, so `E[s]=0`, and bounded `z` makes the norm exponent equal to the `x_1` exponent. Hence

`F(q,r)=beta+b E[s z]`.

Since `z=cw` and `bc=q`, equations (5.2)–(5.6) give the complete transverse derivative

`F(q,r)=beta + q * beta/(2(D_0+beta r)) + o(q)`       (5.7)

for every fixed `r>0`.

The coefficient is positive and strictly decreasing in `r`.

### 5.3 Stronger exact moment identity: the nonlinear correction has a sign

The first-order formula can be strengthened. Work directly with the stationary small-`q` branch of (5.1). Put

`M=E[z]`,

`N=E[s z]`.

Stationarity of `z` gives

`0=-2 beta M-2aN-b E[s z^2]`.                        (5.8)

Stationarity of `s z` gives

`0=c-2(beta+r)N-2aM-b E[z^2]`.                      (5.9)

Eliminate `M` between (5.8) and (5.9). Since `q=bc`, one obtains the exact identity

`F(q,r)-beta = bN`

`= beta q/(2(D_0+beta r))`

`  - beta b^2/(2(D_0+beta r)) * E[(1-(a/beta)s) z^2]`.    (5.10)

Because `beta>|a|`,

`1-(a/beta)s >0`

for both modes. Therefore the nonlinear term in (5.10) is always nonpositive, and

`F(q,r)-beta <= beta q/(2(D_0+beta r))`.              (5.11)

This is an exact inequality, not merely the first derivative.

In particular, for every sufficiently small negative `q` for which the attracting projective branch above is the top branch,

`F(q,r) < beta`

for **every** `r>0`. Since the contextual Hurwitz threshold is at least `beta_bar=beta`, no all-Hurwitz switched instability can occur anywhere in rate space on this negative-`q` side of the common-line boundary.

This is stronger than the fixed-rate perturbative conclusion.

## 6. Compare the response with the exact Hurwitz-feasibility threshold

For the family above, the individual spectral abscissae near `q=0` are

`beta_+(q)=sqrt((beta+a)^2+q)`,

`beta_-(q)=sqrt((beta-a)^2+q)`.

Hence

`S(q):=(beta_+(q)+beta_-(q))/2`

has expansion

`S(q)=beta + q * beta/(2D_0) + O(q^2)`.               (6.1)

The average remains exactly

`Bbar=diag(beta,-beta)`,

so the scalar-shift threshold is

`T(q)=max(S(q),beta)`.

For sufficiently small `q>0`, `T(q)=S(q)`. Combining (5.7) and (6.1), for every fixed `r>0`,

`F(q,r)-T(q)`

`= q[ beta/(2(D_0+beta r)) - beta/(2D_0) ] + o(q)`

`= -q * beta^2 r / [2D_0(D_0+beta r)] + o(q)`.       (6.2)

Thus a positive-`q` all-Hurwitz instability cannot bifurcate from `q=0` at any fixed positive switching rate.

For sufficiently small `q<0`, `T(q)=beta`, so (5.7) gives

`F(q,r)-T(q)`

`= q * beta/[2(D_0+beta r)] + o(q) <0`,              (6.3)

and the exact inequality (5.11) actually upgrades this to the all-rate statement already noted.

### Theorem 6.1 — local no-bifurcation theorem

Under `beta>|a|` and a transverse perturbation `c=q/b` with fixed `b!=0`:

1. At `q=0`, `F(r)=beta` for every rate.
2. For each fixed `r>0`, there exists `epsilon_r>0` such that `0<|q|<epsilon_r` implies
   `F(q,r)<T(q)`.
3. On the small negative-`q` side, after shrinking the projective strip neighborhood once, `F(q,r)<T(q)` holds for every `r>0` simultaneously.

Therefore no stronger all-Hurwitz instability band can be born at a finite positive rate by an infinitesimal transverse deformation of this common-invariant-line boundary. QED, by Theorem 4.1, (5.10)–(5.11), and (6.1)–(6.3).

This is a genuinely stronger obstruction than the previous endpoint-sign theorem: it controls the whole fixed-rate interior to first order and one full transverse side exactly in rate.

## 7. B29 — compactify rate space and identify the precise remaining gluing failure

The rate variable naturally has two boundary points, `r=0` and `r=infinity`. The first-response coefficient in (5.7) is simple enough to inspect on the compactification.

Define

`N(r)=beta/[2(D_0+beta r)]`.

For `q>0`, the one-sided first derivative of the contextual excess at `q=0` is

`A_+(r)=N(r)-N(0)`

`= - beta^2 r/[2D_0(D_0+beta r)] <0` for `r>0`.      (7.1)

Although this vanishes at `r=0`, dividing by `r` gives

`A_+(r)/r = -beta^2/[2D_0(D_0+beta r)]`,

which extends continuously to the slow corner with strictly negative value

`-beta^2/(2D_0^2)`.

This exactly matches the small-`q` expansion of the slow-switching overlap coefficient from the preceding report. Indeed, with

`P=beta_+(q) beta_-(q)`,

`L=D_0-q`,

`K=(P+L)/(2P)`,

and the exact identity

`P^2-L^2=4 beta^2 q`,

one finds

`log K = - beta^2 q/D_0^2 + O(q^2)`.

Since the published slow expansion is

`F(q,r)=S(q)+(r/2)log K+o(r)`,

the corner term is

`F-S = - q r beta^2/(2D_0^2) + higher order`,

exactly the `r->0` limit of (6.2).

For `q<0`, the one-sided excess coefficient is

`A_-(r)=N(r)>0`;

multiplying by `r` gives

`r A_-(r) -> 1/2` as `r->infinity`.                  (7.2)

Thus

`F(q,r)-beta ~ q/(2r)`

at the fast corner, matching the published fast-switching coefficient from the previous report.

### What the compactification proves

There is no hidden first-order sign reversal at either rate boundary:

- on the positive-`q` side, the fixed-rate negative excess glues to the slow endpoint through the strictly negative normalized coefficient (7.1);
- on the negative-`q` side, the fixed-rate negative excess glues to the fast endpoint through (7.2).

The remaining gap is now sharply two-parameter and higher-order:

> Prove uniform remainder control in the corner `(q,r)->(0,0)` on the positive-`q` side, sufficient to compare the `O(qr)` negative term with all higher terms; equivalently, rule out a boundary layer `r=r(q)->0` in which a positive excess could emerge despite the negative first normalized coefficient.

The negative-`q` side does not have this gap because the exact moment identity (5.10) already gives an all-rate strict inequality.

This is the exact gluing failure left by B29. It is substantially smaller than the previous broad question of where two interior humps might occur.
