# Method B29 — add the points at infinity

For a right-only fresh shell of width `g` inside current span `N`, set `s=g/N` and

`U=max_h (L_h-(N-h))_+`,

`V=max_h (R_h-h)_+`, `h=0,...,g-1`.

The boundary needs two charts: `V/g` for ordinary near-side growth, and `(U/N,V/N)` for rare macroscopic excursions.

## B29 result 1: exact limiting cross mass

For each fixed `s in (0,1)` as `N->infinity`, `g/N->s`,

`P(V/g<=z) -> z/(1+z)`,

`P(U=0) -> 1-s`,

and

`P(U>uN) -> s/(1+u)`.

These follow directly by turning the exact shell products into Riemann sums using `P(L>n)~P(R>n)~1/n`:

`sum_{h<g}1/(h+zg) -> int_0^1 dx/(x+z)`,

`sum_{h<g}1/(N-h) -> int_0^s dt/(1-t)`,

and

`sum_{h<g}1/(N(1+u)-h) -> int_0^s dt/(1+u-t)`.

So in the macroscopic limiting kernel the one-sided face emits a cross-revival with exactly mass `s`.

## B29 result 2: source-specific excursion law

Reuse the previously proved finite-variance bivariate regular variation

`n P(L>an,R>bn) -> J(a,b)`,

where

`J(a,b)=a^(-1)H(b/a)`

and for nearest-neighbour finite variance

`H(t)=1+1/t-[pi/(1+t)]csc(pi/(1+t))`.

Then, conditional on a cross-revival and letting `s downarrow0` after the macroscopic `N` limit,

`(U/N,V/N) => (X,Y)`

with

`P(X>u,Y>v)=J(1+u,v)`.

In particular

`P(X>u)=1/(1+u)`,

`P(Y>v)=H(v)`.

Because `H(v)=1-(pi^2/6)v+O(v^2)` at zero, `Y>0` almost surely and its inverse logarithm is integrable.

### Outcome

The candidate dangerous event is not “`U=O(N)` while `V=O(g)`”.  Conditional on cross revival, the same-frog dependence forces a nondegenerate `V/N` limit.  Thus adding `U` to the projective fresh-shell ratio changes its logarithm by only `O(1)` per cross event, not by `log(1/s)`.

# Method B13 — find what only decreases

Use the projective fresh-shell coordinate.  The current one-sided state has ratio `s=g/N`; the next ratio is `(U+V)/(N+U+V)`.

To avoid a finite-state logarithmic singularity at direct absorption, regularize the numerator by `(U+V) vee 1`; this does not change the macroscopic fixed-`s` limit because absorption probability vanishes there.

The `V`-only limiting drift is exact.  Writing `Z=V/g`, the B29 near chart gives the beta-prime law `P(Z<=z)=z/(1+z)`, hence

`E log Z=0`,

and the preceding exact integral gives

`E log(1+sZ)= -s log s/(1-s)`.

Therefore

`B(s)=s log s/(1-s)=-s log(1/s)+o(s)`.

The correction from restoring `U` is

`C=log(1+U/V)-log(1+U/(N+V))`.

It vanishes unless `U>0`, an event of probability `s+o(s)`.  Conditional on that event, B29 gives the pair `(X,Y)`.  The correction converges to

`c(X,Y)=log(1+X/Y)-log(1+X/(1+Y))`.

It has finite mean because

`c(X,Y)<=log(1+X)+log(1+1/Y)`,

`E log(1+X)=1`,

and `1-H(y)=(pi^2/6)y+O(y^2)` makes `E log(1+1/Y)<infinity`.

Hence there is a finite `kappa_H>0` with

`E C = kappa_H s+o(s)`.

Combining terms gives the new theorem

`D(s)=-s log(1/s)+kappa_H s+o(s)`.

Thus for all sufficiently small `s`,

`D(s)<0`.

## Outcome

# Method B26 — hunt the sporadic bounded-shell case

Let

`a=P(L=0,R=0)`.

For one frog with geometric lifetime, `a>=E(1-pi)>0` because death before the first jump forces both displacements to zero.

For every fixed `M`, whenever `1<=g<=M`, the fresh shell contains at most `M` independent unexposed sites.  If all their marks are `(0,0)`, the next extension is `(U,V)=(0,0)`.  Therefore

`P(absorb next step | past, 1<=g<=M) >= a^M`.

By applying this at successive stopping times, the probability of surviving `k` visits to the bounded-shell set is at most `(1-a^M)^k`.  Consequently:

**if the chain visits a fixed bounded-shell set infinitely often, it is absorbed almost surely.**
