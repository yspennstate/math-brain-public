## 4. Recovery target from the previous diagnosis

For a positive infinite stack, let `h_s(k)` be forward-process survival from population `k` when the current spatial phase is `s`, and let `h_{M,s}(k)` be survival after neutralizing cookies deeper than `M`.

The previous run proved the exact finite-core identity: if

\[
\tau_K=\inf\{n:V_n=0\text{ or }V_n\ge K\}
\]

and `M>=2K-2`, then the full and truncated lifted chains have exactly the same stopped kernel below `K`. Hence all truncation error appears only after high-population exit.

For arbitrary stationary ergodic dependence, the future environment at `tau_K` is exit-biased. The stuck report therefore selected the finite-state Markov case, where the current phase is a finite sufficient statistic for the future.

> Can one prove a high-population survival estimate **uniform in the Markov phase**, strong enough that the random exit bias becomes harmless?

The answer is yes.

## 5. Model for the recovery theorem

Let `S={1,...,d}` be finite and `(S_x)` a stationary irreducible spatial Markov chain with transition matrix `K` and stationary law `pi`, so

\[
\pi K=\pi,
\qquad \pi_s>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

State `s` carries a deterministic infinite positive stack

\[
(p_{s,1},p_{s,2},\ldots),
\qquad \frac12\le p_{s,i}\le1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Put

\[
\alpha_{s,i}=2p_{s,i}-1,
\qquad
\delta_s=\sum_{i\ge1}\alpha_{s,i},
\qquad
\bar\delta=\sum_s\pi_s\delta_s>1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The total drift may be infinite. Cookies equal to one are allowed.

At phase `s`, let `F_s(k)` be the number of successes before the `k`-th failure in the stack's independent cookie coins. The lifted forward chain uses the current phase's stack and then moves the phase by `K`:

\[
V_{n+1}=F_{S_n}(V_n),
\qquad
S_{n+1}\sim K(S_n,\cdot).
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is an index shift of the convention in the previous finite-state artifact and leaves the physical spatial process unchanged.

## 6. Exact finite-core visibility, now with phase

Fix `K_0>=2`. Starting from `1<=k<K_0`, censor the next population at the set

\[
\{0,1,\ldots,K_0-1,H\},
\]

where `H` means at least `K_0`.

The censored result is known once either the `k`-th failure or the `K_0`-th success has occurred. This takes at most

\[
k+K_0-1\le2K_0-2
\tag{6.1}
\]

coin tosses. Therefore, whenever

\[
M\ge2K_0-2,
\tag{6.2}
\]

the full and `M`-truncated lifted chains have exactly the same stopped transition kernel below `K_0`, including the Markov phase.

Moreover, before exit, the first `K_0` tosses at a site are all successes with conditional probability at least `2^{-K_0}` because all cookies are positive. Hence

\[
P_{s,k}(\tau_{K_0}>n)
\le(1-2^{-K_0})^n,
\tag{6.3}
\]

so the stopped-core exit time is almost surely finite. No upper ellipticity is used.

## 7. Build a common finite-depth transient baseline

Fix

\[
0<\beta<\bar\delta-1
\tag{7.1}
\]

when `bar(delta)<infinity`; if `bar(delta)=infinity`, fix any finite `beta>0`.

Define the finite-prefix stationary drift

\[
\bar d_m
=
\sum_s\pi_s\sum_{i=1}^m\alpha_{s,i}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

By monotone convergence, `bar d_m ↑ bar delta`. Choose `m_0` so that

\[
\bar d_{m_0}>1+\beta.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Choose `t<1` sufficiently close to one that

\[
\bar\delta_q:=t\bar d_{m_0}>1+\beta.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Define the weakened finite-depth stack

\[
q_{s,i}
=
\begin{cases}
1/2+t(p_{s,i}-1/2),&i\le m_0,\\
1/2,&i>m_0.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Then

\[
1/2\le q_{s,i}\le(1+t)/2<1,
\]

and for every `M>=m_0`,

\[
q_{s,i}\le p^{(M)}_{s,i}\le p_{s,i}
\tag{7.6}
\]

coordinatewise. Under a common-uniform coupling, forward populations are monotone in every cookie strength. If `g_s(k)` is survival for `q`, then

\[
\boxed{g_s(k)\le h_{M,s}(k)\le h_s(k).}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Thus it suffices to prove `g_s(k)->1` uniformly in the finite phase.

## 8. B1 — change category: construct a phase-corrected Lamperti Lyapunov function

For the finite-depth baseline `q`, put

\[
\delta_s^q
=t\sum_{i=1}^{m_0}\alpha_{s,i}.
\]

For every population `k>=m_0`, the exact one-step pgf has the form

\[
T(z)^kB_s^q(z),
\qquad
T(z)=\frac1{2-z},
\tag{8.1}
\]

so equivalently

\[
V'\stackrel d=G_1+\cdots+G_k+Y_s,
\tag{8.2}
\]

where `G_i` are i.i.d. geometric counts of successes before one fair failure,

\[
P(G_i=j)=2^{-(j+1)},\quad j\ge0,
\]

and `Y_s` is a bounded Bernoulli sum carrying the finite cookie drift.

Uniformly over the finite phase set,

\[
E[V'-k\mid s,k]=\delta_s^q,
\tag{8.3}
\]

\[
E[(V'-k)^2\mid s,k]=2k+O(1),
\tag{8.4}
\]

\[
E|V'-k|^3=O(k^{3/2}),
\tag{8.5}
\]

and a standard geometric Chernoff bound gives

\[
P(|V'-k|>k/2\mid s,k)\le C_0e^{-c_0k}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

A scalar negative power `k^{-beta}` does not work phasewise when some `delta_s^q` is small. The new move is to correct it by the Markov phase.

Because

\[
\pi(\bar\delta_q\mathbf1-\delta^q)=0,
\]

the finite Poisson equation

\[
\boxed{
(I-K)c
=
\beta(\bar\delta_q\mathbf1-\delta^q)
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

has a solution, unique after imposing `pi c=0`.

Choose `a>2||c||_infinity+2` and set

\[
F(s,k)
=(k+a)^{-\beta}
\left(1+\frac{c_s}{k+a}\right)>0.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Let `Delta=V'-k` and `y=k+a`. Taylor expansion on `|Delta|<=k/2`, together with (8.3)-(8.6), gives

\[
E[(V'+a)^{-\beta}\mid s,k]
=
y^{-\beta}
+\beta(\beta+1-\delta_s^q)y^{-\beta-1}
+o(y^{-\beta-1}),
\tag{8.9}
\]

uniformly in `s`, and

\[
E[(V'+a)^{-\beta-1}\mid s,k]
=
y^{-\beta-1}+o(y^{-\beta-1}).
\tag{8.10}
\]

The next phase is distributed by `K(s,.)`, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[F(S',V')\mid s,k]
={}&y^{-\beta}\\
&+y^{-\beta-1}
\left[\beta(\beta+1-\delta_s^q)+(Kc)_s\right]
+o(y^{-\beta-1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.11}
\]

Subtracting

\[
F(s,k)=y^{-\beta}+c_sy^{-\beta-1}
\]

and using

\[
(Kc-c)_s
=\beta(\delta_s^q-\bar\delta_q)
\tag{8.12}
\]

from the Poisson equation yields the phase-independent coefficient

\[
\boxed{
E[F(S',V')\mid s,k]-F(s,k)
=
\beta(\beta+1-\bar\delta_q)y^{-\beta-1}
+o(y^{-\beta-1}).
}
\tag{8.13}
\]

Because `beta+1<bar delta_q`, there exists `L` such that for every phase and every `k>L`,

\[
E[F(S',V')\mid s,k]\le F(s,k).
\tag{8.14}
\]

Let

\[
\sigma_L=\inf\{n:V_n\le L\}.
\]

Then `F(S_{n wedge sigma_L},V_{n wedge sigma_L})` is a nonnegative supermartingale. With

\[
m_L=\min_{s,0\le j\le L}F(s,j)>0,
\]

optional stopping gives

\[
P_{s,k}(\sigma_L<\infty)
\le \frac{F(s,k)}{m_L}
\le C_\beta(1+k)^{-\beta}.
\tag{8.15}
\]

Extinction requires entering population zero and hence `{0,...,L}`, so

\[
\boxed{
1-g_s(k)\le C_\beta(1+k)^{-\beta}
}
\tag{8.16}
\]

uniformly over every Markov phase `s`.

This is the missing phase-uniform high-population safety estimate.

## 9. B23 — attempt the negating long-persistence mechanism

The most natural two-state obstruction is a chain with a highly persistent weak phase `B` and a compensating strong phase `G`. Conditioning on a high-population exit could plausibly select the start of a long bad block, so the exit-biased future law might have much larger extinction probability than a stationary sample.

At the uncorrected scalar level that danger is real: a weak phase may have `delta_B^q<1`, so the local drift of `k^{-beta}` can have the wrong sign.

However, in a finite irreducible Markov environment that persistence effect is exactly finite-dimensional. The Poisson corrector enforces

\[
\beta(\beta+1-\delta_s^q)+(Kc-c)_s
=
\beta(\beta+1-\bar\delta_q)<0
\tag{9.1}
\]

for every phase simultaneously. A very persistent bad phase can increase `||c||`, changing only the finite cutoff `L` and the constant in (8.16); it cannot change the leading negative coefficient as long as the stationary drift surplus is positive.

Thus the selected counterexample attempt fails for a structural theorem-level reason. If the full stationary-ergodic source fails to admit this continuation, the obstruction must lie beyond finite-state Markov persistence, for example in an unbounded/nonuniform corrector under adaptive shifts.

## 10. B24 — extremal object: one weakened finite-depth baseline dominates the boundary defect

The common baseline `q` from Section 7 satisfies, for every sufficiently large `M`, every phase `s`, and every population `j`,

\[
1-h_s(j)\le1-g_s(j),
\qquad
1-h_{M,s}(j)\le1-g_s(j).
\]

By (8.16),

\[
\boxed{
\max\{1-h_s(j),1-h_{M,s}(j)\}
\le C_\beta(1+j)^{-\beta}.
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

So the useful extremal boundary object is not a worst exit-biased environment. It is the single radially weakened finite-depth Markov chain `q`, which controls both the full infinite stack and every sufficiently deep truncation pointwise in phase.

This makes the exit-induced phase distribution irrelevant to the upper bound.

## 11. Main theorem — finite-state Markov infinite-stack gluing

Set

\[
K_M=\lfloor M/2\rfloor+1.
\tag{11.1}
\]

Then `2K_M-2<=M`, so the full and truncated chains have the same stopped law at `tau_{K_M}`.

For either survival function `r` in `{h,h_M}`, harmonicity and a.s. finiteness of the stopped-core exit give

\[
r_s(1)
=
E_{s,1}\left[
 r_{S_{\tau_{K_M}}}(V_{\tau_{K_M}});
 V_{\tau_{K_M}}\ge K_M
\right].
\tag{11.2}
\]

On the high-boundary event, (10.1) yields

\[
1-C_\beta(1+K_M)^{-\beta}
\le
r_{S_{\tau_{K_M}}}(V_{\tau_{K_M}})
\le1.
\tag{11.3}
\]

Let

\[
a_{K_M}(s)=P_{s,1}(V_{\tau_{K_M}}\ge K_M)
\]

for the common stopped core. Then both full and truncated survival lie in the same interval

\[
a_{K_M}(s)
[1-C_\beta(1+K_M)^{-\beta}]
\le r_s(1)\le a_{K_M}(s).
\tag{11.4}
\]

Using monotonicity `h_M<=h`,

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\sup_s\bigl(h_s(1)-h_{M,s}(1)\bigr)
\le C_\beta M^{-\beta}
}
\tag{11.5}
\]

for every `beta<bar delta-1` and all sufficiently large `M` (with arbitrary finite `beta` if `bar delta=infinity`).

For the excited walk itself, condition on the origin phase `r`; the first right step has probability `p_{r,1}`, and the site-1 phase is `s` with probability `K(r,s)`. The standard finite-interval forward/backward identity gives

\[
p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}
=
\sum_{r,s}\pi_r p_{r,1}K(r,s)h_s(1),
\tag{11.6}
\]

and for every sufficiently large transient truncation,

\[
p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}
=
\sum_{r,s}\pi_r p_{r,1}K(r,s)h_{M,s}(1).
\tag{11.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
0\le p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}-p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}
\le C_\beta M^{-\beta},
}
\tag{11.8}
\]

and in particular

\[
\boxed{
p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}\uparrow p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}.}
\tag{11.9}
\]

This completes the recovery action selected by the stuck report, and it does so for every finite-state irreducible Markov spatial environment, not only the two-state benchmark.

## 12. Consequence for the cumulative exact formula

The earlier finite-state Markov artifact already gives an exact finite-dimensional resolvent for every finite-depth truncation with stationary average drift above one. Let that exact depth-`M` formula be denoted by

\[
\mathcal F_M(K,\pi,\{p_{s,i}:i\le M\}).
\]

Since the finite-prefix stationary drift increases to `bar delta>1`, all sufficiently large truncations are transient and the finite-depth formula applies. Equation (11.8) therefore proves

\[
\boxed{
 p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}
=
\lim_{M\to\infty}
\mathcal F_M(K,\pi,\{p_{s,i}:i\le M\}),
}
\tag{12.1}
\]

with truncation error `O(M^{-beta})` for every `beta<bar delta-1`.

This is a genuine extension along the source's positive stationary-ergodic axis. It strictly contains the finite-state finite-depth theorem and the two-state infinite-stack recovery benchmark.
