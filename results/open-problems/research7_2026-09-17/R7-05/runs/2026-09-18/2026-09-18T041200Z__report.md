## Method B1 — change mathematical category: random closure lattice

For site marks `J_x=[x-L_x,x+R_x]`, define on finite integer intervals

`Phi(I)=conv(union_{x in I} J_x)`.

The breadth-first activation interval is exactly the monotone iteration `I_{k+1}=Phi(I_k)` from `{0}`.  A finite interval is closed iff it is a fixed point.  Closed intervals containing the origin are closed under both intersection and union and hence form a distributive lattice.  On extinction, the final activated interval is the least fixed point containing the origin.

The proof is elementary but useful: it changes H0024 from a static random-interval counting problem into a monotone random fixed-point problem.  This is a new route, not a restatement of the previous closed-hull moment bounds.

**Outcome:** rigorous structural theorem; no source closure.

## Method B20 — find why the natural breadth-first algorithm fails, and extract its exact kernel

Suppose the current activated interval is `[A,B]`, `N=B-A`, and the previous interval was `[A+ell,B-r]`.  The fresh shell consists of `ell` new sites on the left and `r` on the right.  Set `g=ell+r`, translate `A` to zero, and write

`F(a,b)=P(L<=a,R<=b)`.

If `U,V` are the next left/right extension counts, then for integers `u,v>=0` the exact conditional law is

`P(U<=u,V<=v | N,ell,r)`

`= product_{j=0}^{ell-1} F(j+u,N-j+v)`

`  * product_{j=N-r+1}^{N} F(j+u,N-j+v)`.        (1)

The state update is exactly

`(N,ell,r) -> (N+U+V,U,V)`.

This makes `(N,ell,r)` an exact Markov state compression.  It also identifies the failure of the naive program: when the fresh shell is macroscopic, both coordinates in `F` are of comparable order, so the transition law depends on the *joint* tail of `(L,R)`.  The source's one-sided asymptotic alone cannot determine equality.

**Outcome:** exact kernel and exact location of the missing information.

## Method B21 — import a Lamperti/renewal viewpoint

### A. Universal interior thin-shell theorem

Assume abstract critical marginals

`P(L>n)~1/n`, `P(R>n)~1/n`,

with arbitrary same-site dependence.  Consider states with

`N->infinity`, `g=ell+r->infinity`, `g/N->0`, `ell/g->theta in (0,1)`.

From (1), the far-side constraint at a left fresh site has tail only `O(1/N)`, so over `O(g)` sites its total effect is `o(1)`; similarly on the other side.  The near-side products give harmonic-sum limits.  Consequently

`(U/g,V/g) => (theta Z_1,(1-theta)Z_2)`,       (2)

where `Z_1,Z_2` are independent and

`P(Z_i<=z)=z/(1+z)`, `z>0`.

Thus the same-site dependence disappears in this interior thin-shell scaling.

The limiting fresh-shell multiplier is

`M_theta=theta Z_1+(1-theta)Z_2`.

Since `E log Z_i=0`, weighted AM-GM gives

`E log M_theta >0` for every `0<theta<1`.      (3)

The artifact supplies an explicit positive lower bound on compact subintervals of `(0,1)` using the event `{Z_1>=2,Z_2<=1/2}`, which has probability `1/9`.

Therefore equality is not a globally zero-drift phenomenon: a thin shell that retains nontrivial mass on both sides has strictly positive logarithmic growth.  The unresolved critical mechanism is forced toward the one-sided faces or bounded-shell states.

### B. Exact one-sided boundary scales

For a right-only shell of size `g=sN` with `g->infinity` and `s->0`,

`V/g => Z`, `P(Z<=z)=z/(1+z)`,

while the far-left revival probability is

`P(U>0)~s`,

and conditionally on revival

`U/N => W`, `P(W>u)=1/(1+u)`.

The near-side projective normalization is exactly

`E log(1+sZ) = -s log s/(1-s)`.               (4)

So the negative normalization term is `-s log(1/s)` to first order, while a rare cross-revival occurs with probability order `s` and carries a logarithmic jump order `log(1/s)`.  Both mechanisms therefore live on the same critical scale.

For the second-order marginal family

`q_n=(1/n)(1+a/log n+O(log^{-2}n))`,

the one-sided beta-prime multiplier has local log drift

`(pi^2/6) a/log g + o(1/log g)`.              (5)

This is the correct Lamperti-scale boundary problem; a first-order comparison cannot settle it.

### C. New source-specific theorem: Brownian bivariate critical tail

In the actual finite-variance branch of H0024, at `beta=1/2`, let a single frog have maximal right/left displacements `D^+,D^-`.  For every fixed `x,y>0`, the source's Proposition 3.4 argument extends from a one-sided hitting time to the time required to hit both levels.  The extension is valid because the two-sided completion time dominates either one-sided hitting time, so all of the source's small-time and Potter-bound domination estimates remain available, while Donsker convergence gives convergence of the pair of Brownian hitting times.

The resulting exact joint asymptotic is

`n P(D^+>=nx,D^->=ny)/ell(n^2) -> K_sigma(x,y)`,

with

`K_sigma(x,y)`

`=sqrt(2)sigma [1/x+1/y-(pi/(x+y)) csc(pi x/(x+y))]`.       (6)

The proof evaluates the Brownian completion-time Laplace transform by first exiting `(-y,x)` and then using strong Markov to hit the opposite endpoint.  The remaining integral is evaluated with the digamma reflection identity.

Normalizing `y=1`, define

`H(t)=1+1/t-[pi/(1+t)]csc(pi/(1+t))`.          (7)

Then the critical-tail conditional ratio is `H(t)`.  In particular

`H(1)=2-pi/2=0.4292036732...`,

and

`H(t)=1-(pi^2/6)t+(pi^2/3)t^2+O(t^3)` as `t down to0`.       (8)

The same ratio holds for the *site maxima* over a random number `eta` of frogs under only the source hypothesis `0<E eta<infinity`: the finite-mean expansion `E[1-(1-z)^eta]~(E eta)z` applies to the left, right, and union rare events and cancels the occupancy factor.

This is the most source-specific advance of the run.  A site that is capable of a macroscopic opposite-side revival is not behaving like an independent second radius.  In the Brownian branch its other-side reach remains macroscopic on the logarithmic scale, with failure probability only `(pi^2/6)t+o(t)` at relative scale `t`.

The coincidence of `pi^2/6` in (5) and (8) is recorded as a structural clue only; it is **not** promoted to a phase-boundary proof.

**Outcome:** complete finite-variance bivariate tail theorem plus exact shell kernel and interior drift theorem; full critical-line phase remains open.
