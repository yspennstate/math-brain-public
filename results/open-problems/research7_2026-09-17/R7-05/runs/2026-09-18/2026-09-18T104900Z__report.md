## 4. Exact blocking formula

The cumulative reductions now isolate one scalar with no ambiguity.

Work at the first unresolved depth `M=3`. Let `Z` be the standard backward branching chain, and let

\[
S=\{0,1\},
\qquad
\tau_S=\inf\{n\ge0:Z_n\in S\},
\qquad
\tau_S^+=\inf\{n\ge1:Z_n\in S\}.
\]

The preceding report proved that if

\[
B_{rs}=P_r(Z_{\tau_S^+}=s),\qquad r,s\in S,
\]

then the only remaining boundary ratio is

\[
\kappa=\frac{B_{01}}{B_{10}}=\frac{\pi_1}{\pi_0},
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the exact escape formula is

\[
\boxed{
 p_{\rm esc}
 =\mathbb E[p_1]\,
   \frac{\delta-1}{c_0+c_1\kappa}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Here

\[
c_0=
\mathbb E\!\left[p_1(2p_2-1)+(2p_3-1)(p_1+p_2-p_1p_2)\right],
\]

\[
c_1=\mathbb E[p_1p_2(2p_3-1)].
\]

Thus a universal depth-three formula is equivalent, within this reduction, to evaluating `kappa` from the cookie law.

The most useful way to expose the obstruction is as a bounded Dirichlet problem. Define

\[
h(i)=P_i(Z_{\tau_S}=1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
h(0)=0,
\qquad
h(1)=1,
\]

and for every `i>=2`, by the Markov property,

\[
\boxed{
h(i)=\sum_{j\ge0}P(i,j)h(j).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Positive recurrence/irreducibility in the right-transient bounded-cookie regime ensures that `S` is hit almost surely, so this is the unique bounded solution with the displayed boundary values.

Starting from the boundary and taking one step before applying `h` gives

\[
\boxed{
B_{01}=P(0,1)+\sum_{j\ge2}P(0,j)h(j),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

while the complementary hitting probability gives

\[
\boxed{
B_{10}=P(1,0)+\sum_{j\ge2}P(1,j)(1-h(j)).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore the exact unresolved scalar is

\[
\boxed{
\kappa
=
\frac{P(0,1)+\sum_{j\ge2}P(0,j)h(j)}
{P(1,0)+\sum_{j\ge2}P(1,j)(1-h(j))}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is the present **blocking formula**. It is not a vague request for another stationary moment: it is a concrete one-dimensional boundary-value problem for the backward chain. The trace identity `pi_0 B_01=pi_1 B_10` is exact, but using it to evaluate `kappa` is circular because `pi_1/pi_0` is precisely the unknown ratio.

The previous PGF/difference-equation obstruction and (4.7) are two representations of the same unresolved degree of freedom. In the transformed stationary PGF equation, the homogeneous solution behaves as

\[
H(u)\asymp u^{1-\delta},
\]

so for `delta>1` it already decays at infinity and the normalization `G(1^-)=1` cannot force its amplitude to zero. In the censored-chain language, that surviving amplitude is reflected by the freedom in `kappa`. The useful lesson is that another first-order normalization or polynomial drift identity is unlikely to eliminate the missing scalar unless it genuinely determines the Dirichlet flux in (4.7).
