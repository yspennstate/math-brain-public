## 4. New idea before code: finite population cannot see arbitrarily deep cookies

The previous obstruction was

\[
\sup_M\inf_Na_{M,N}\le\inf_N\sup_Ma_{M,N},
\]

with no reason for equality from finite-horizon path convergence alone. The new route does not try to exchange those limits directly. Instead it cuts the forward branching process at a high population `K` and asks how many cookies one step below `K` can possibly inspect.

For a fresh site let `S_k` be the number of successes before the `k`-th failure. If `1<=k<K`, stop the coin sequence when either the `k`-th failure or the `K`-th success occurs. This stopping time is at most

\[
\boxed{k+K-1\le2K-2.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Hence the censored one-step output `0,1,...,K-1` or `H=[K,infinity]` is determined by the first `2K-2` cookies and nothing deeper.

Therefore the full infinite stack and every neutral-tail truncation with

\[
M\ge2K-2
\tag{4.2}
\]

have **exactly the same stopped transition kernel below population `K`**. This is an exact gluing fact, not a limiting approximation.

Also, because every positive cookie has success probability at least `1/2`, from every `k<K` the event that the first `K` tosses are all successes has probability at least `2^{-K}` and forces a jump to population at least `K`. Thus the stopping time

\[
\tau_K=\inf\{n:V_n=0\text{ or }V_n\ge K\}
\]

has

\[
P(\tau_K>n)\le(1-2^{-K})^n,
\tag{4.3}
\]

so it is almost surely finite even if some cookie strengths equal one.

This was the key B30 localization step.

## 5. A common transient baseline that forces the high boundary to become asymptotically safe

Let
