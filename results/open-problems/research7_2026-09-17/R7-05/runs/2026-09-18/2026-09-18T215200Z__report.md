## B2 — obstruction/counterexample search first

A natural source-valid obstruction is a nonsmooth/atomic heavy-tail law causing infinitely many exactly singular dimensions. The two simplest atomic mechanisms are actually summable.

For independent entries `xi,xi'`, define the collision probability

`rho=P(xi=xi')=sum_x P(xi=x)^2<1`

for every fixed nondegenerate law. Two prescribed iid rows of length `n` agree with probability `rho^n`; hence

`P(exists i<j with row_i=row_j) <= n(n-1)rho^n/2`,

whose sum over `n` is finite. If `q=P(xi=0)<1`,

`P(exists zero row) <= n q^n`,

also summable. Thus Borel–Cantelli rules out repeated rows and zero rows as mechanisms for infinitely many singular dimensions in the natural nested iid-array coupling.

This is deliberately not overstated: other arithmetic linear dependencies and very small nonzero singular values remain. The result merely removes the most obvious atom-based negation and confirms that the hard issue is quantitative lower-log mass, not just exact zeros.

## B11 — dualize the missing quantity

Let `A_n=a_n^{-1}X_n`, let `nu_{n,z}` be the empirical singular-value measure of `A_n-zI`, and define

`U_n(z)=-int log s dnu_{n,z}(s)`

and for `eta>0`

`U_{n,eta}(z)=-(1/2)int log(s^2+eta^2)dnu_{n,z}(s)`.

For a.e. `z` the matrices are nonsingular simultaneously for all `n`, and the nonnegative regularization defect is

`R_{n,eta}(z)=U_n(z)-U_{n,eta}(z)
 = int (1/2)log(1+eta^2/s^2)dnu_{n,z}(s)`.

Writing

`F_{n,z}(t)=nu_{n,z}([0,t])`,

Tonelli gives the exact identity

`R_{n,eta}(z)
 = int_0^infinity F_{n,z}(t) eta^2/[t(t^2+eta^2)]dt`.              (1)

Proof: for `s>0`,

`(1/2)log(1+eta^2/s^2)
 = int_s^infinity eta^2/[t(t^2+eta^2)]dt`,

then interchange the two nonnegative integrals. An atom at zero makes both sides infinite, so the extended identity is still valid.

Equation (1) is a useful change of target. A summable bound on the single `s_n` is sufficient but not logically the only route: one can instead control the **integrated small-singular counting profile**. A single extraordinarily small singular value carries only mass `1/n`; its damage is its logarithmic depth times that mass.

For `eta=e^{-J}`, the genuinely dangerous part `0<t<=eta` becomes exactly

`R_{n,eta}^{low}(z)
 = int_J^infinity F_{n,z}(e^{-u})/[1+e^{-2(u-J)}]du`,

so

`(1/2)int_J^infinity F_{n,z}(e^{-u})du
 <= R_{n,eta}^{low}(z)
 <= int_J^infinity F_{n,z}(e^{-u})du`.                             (2)

The correct discrete lower-log energy is therefore

`E_n(z;J)=sum_{m>=ceil(J)}F_{n,z}(e^{-m})`,

comparable to the integral in (2) up to a first-bin error. This is a sharper object than the binary event `{s_n<n^{-c}}` and is the main new reduction of this run.

Within the source's Hermitization route, the remaining pathwise interchange is precisely to force

`lim_{eta downarrow0} limsup_n R_{n,eta}(z)=0`                     (3)

for Lebesgue-a.e. `z`, almost surely, after fixed-eta convergence is established.

## B5 — add a smoothing parameter and identify exactly where it stops

Define for any square matrix `M`

`Phi_eta(M)=(1/(2n))log det(MM^*+eta^2I)`.

A complete deterministic calculation gives the Frobenius Lipschitz estimate

`|Phi_eta(M)-Phi_eta(N)| <= ||M-N||_F/(2 eta sqrt(n))`.             (4)

Indeed, along `M_t=M+t(N-M)`, the derivative is the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing with `(M_tM_t^*+eta^2I)^{-1}M_t`; its singular values are `s/(s^2+eta^2)<=1/(2eta)`, so its Frobenius norm is at most `sqrt(n)/(2eta)`.

Now smooth each raw entry by an independent fixed Gaussian: `xi^(sigma)=xi+sigma g`. The smoothed law has bounded density and the same alpha-stable tail domain, so the source's bounded-density a.s. theorem applies to it. At normalized matrix scale,

`A_n^(sigma)-A_n=sigma a_n^{-1}G_n`.

Under an infinite Gaussian-array coupling, `||G_n||_F=n(1+o(1))` a.s. Therefore (4) yields, for every fixed `sigma,eta>0`,

`|U_{n,eta}^{(sigma)}(z)-U_{n,eta}(z)|
 <= [sigma/(2eta)] [sqrt(n)/a_n](1+o(1)) -> 0`,                  (5)

because `a_n` is regularly varying with index `1/alpha>1/2`.

Thus density smoothing transfers **all fixed-eta regularized potentials** asymptotically to the nonsmooth source law. It does not settle the source because nonnormal eigenvalues are not deterministically stable under tiny perturbations and removing `eta` in (5) is exactly the lower-log defect (3). This gives a useful no-churn conclusion: another proof that only smooths the entry law, without controlling (1)/(2), cannot close C0031.
