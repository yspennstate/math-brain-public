# 5. B12 — exact projective-time / log-span clock

Index generations after the finite initial transient at which the activated span is positive.  Write

`N_k` = current span,

`g_k=ell_k+r_k` = shell activated in the preceding transition,

`s_k=g_k/N_k`.

On a nonabsorbed generation, `g_k>=1`; after the initial positive-span transient one has `0<s_k<1`.

Because `g_k` is exactly the increment in activated span,

`g_k=N_k-N_{k-1}`.                                               (5.1)

Hence

`N_{k-1}=N_k(1-s_k)`,                                            (5.2)

so

`log N_k-log N_{k-1}=-log(1-s_k)`.                               (5.3)

Summing gives the exact identity

`log(N_K/N_0)=sum_{k=1}^K -log(1-s_k)`.                           (5.4)

### Consequence

On eternal survival, the integer spans strictly increase at every generation, so `N_K->infinity`.  Thus the sum on the right of (5.4) diverges.

If instead

`sum_k s_k<infinity`,                                             (5.5)

then `s_k->0`; eventually `s_k<=1/2`, and

`-log(1-s_k)<=2s_k`.                                              (5.6)

The right side of (5.4) would converge, contradiction.  Therefore every eternally surviving path satisfies

`boxed(sum_k s_k=infinity)`.                                     (5.7)

This is the B12 conserved structure: the projective shell ratio is an exact clock for multiplicative span growth.  Any event whose one-step conditional hazard is bounded below by a fixed multiple of `s_k` has infinite cumulative hazard on survival.

# 6. B3 — prove the stronger scale-burst statement

Fix an integer `M>=1`.

At generation `k`, translate the current activated interval to `[0,N_k]`.  Call a fresh site **M-crossing** if its frog mark reaches across the opposite old boundary and at least `M N_k` further beyond it.

For a left fresh site at position `j` with `0<=j<ell_k`, it is sufficient that

`R>(M+1)N_k-j`.                                                   (6.1)

Since the threshold in (6.1) is at most `(M+1)N_k`, the exact tail (4.2) gives

`P(site is M-crossing | F_k)
 >= 1/((M+1)N_k+2)`.                                              (6.2)

For a right fresh site the reflected condition using `L` has the same lower bound.  The left/right coordinates at **one** site may be dependent, but the marks at the `g_k` different fresh sites are independent.

Let `C_k^(M)` be the event that at least one fresh site is M-crossing in generation `k`.  Put

`q_k=1/((M+1)N_k+2)`.                                             (6.3)

Then

`P(C_k^(M)|F_k)
 >=1-(1-q_k)^(g_k)
 >=1-exp(-g_k q_k)`.                                              (6.4)

For all sufficiently large `N_k`,

`q_k>=1/[2(M+1)N_k]`.                                             (6.5)

Also `g_k<=N_k`, so `g_k q_k<=1/(M+1)<=1`.  Since `1-e^{-x}>=x/2` on `[0,1]`,

`P(C_k^(M)|F_k)
 >=g_k/[4(M+1)N_k]
 =[1/(4(M+1))]s_k.                                                (6.6)

By (5.7), on eternal survival

`sum_k P(C_k^(M)|F_k)=infinity`.                                  (6.7)

The generation-`k` fresh shell consists of previously unexposed iid site marks.  Therefore the standard Levy conditional Borel-Cantelli theorem applies to the adapted events `C_k^(M)`: on the event that the predictable series (6.7) diverges,

`C_k^(M) occurs infinitely often a.s.`                            (6.8)

Now an M-crossing produces extension at least `M N_k`, so

`g_{k+1}>=M N_k`.                                                 (6.9)

Since `N_{k+1}=N_k+g_{k+1}`,

`s_{k+1}=g_{k+1}/N_{k+1}
 >=M/(M+1)`.                                                       (6.10)

Thus for every fixed integer `M`, on eternal survival,

`limsup_k s_k>=M/(M+1)` a.s.                                     (6.11)

Intersect the probability-one statements over the countable set `M=1,2,...`.  Because always `s_k<=1`,

`boxed(P(limsup_{k->infinity} g_k/N_k=1 | eternal survival)=1)`.  (6.12)

This is the **scale-burst theorem**.

Equivalent formulation: for every fixed factor `A>1`, an eternally surviving trajectory makes multiplicative span jumps of factor at least `A` infinitely many times almost surely.
