## 4. B29 — uniform Abel correctors strictly enlarge the dependent class

The preceding theorem used a bounded projective/Gordin corrector under

\[
\sum_{j\ge0}\|E[Y_j\mid\mathcal F_0]\|_\infty<\infty,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `Y_j=delta_j^q-bar(delta)_q` is the centered drift of a deliberately weakened finite-cookie prefix with

\[
\bar\delta_q>1+\beta.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The idea here is to use an Abel resolvent and require only that its boundary error vanish uniformly.

For `0<r<1`, define

\[
G_{r,n}=\sum_{j\ge0}r^jE[Y_{n+j}\mid\mathcal F_n].
\tag{4.3}
\]

It is bounded for every fixed `r`.  A direct shift gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
E[G_{r,n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]
=\frac{G_{r,n}-Y_n}{r}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Setting

\[
c_{r,n}=-\beta rG_{r,n}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
E[c_{r,n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]-c_{r,n}
=\beta Y_n-\beta(1-r)G_{r,n}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the exact projective cancellation survives, with only one Abel residual.

Assume the **uniform Abel condition**

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon(r):=
\left\|(1-r)G_{r,0}\right\|_\infty\to0
\quad(r\uparrow1).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Choose `r` so that

\[
\varepsilon(r)<\frac12(\bar\delta_q-1-\beta).
\tag{4.7}
\]

For fixed such `r`, choose `a` large and define

\[
F_n(k)=(k+a)^{-\beta}
\left(1+\frac{c_{r,n}}{k+a}\right).
\tag{4.8}
\]

The finite-prefix branching increment has the same uniform conditional moments used in the prior theorem:

\[
E\Delta_n=\delta_n^q,
\qquad
E\Delta_n^2=2k+O(1),
\qquad
E|\Delta_n|^3=O(k^{3/2}).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Taylor expansion of (4.8), with (4.5), yields

\[
\boxed{
E[F_{n+1}(V_{n+1})\mid\mathcal H_n]-F_n(V_n)
=
\beta\bigl(\beta+1-\bar\delta_q-(1-r)G_{r,n}\bigr)
(k+a)^{-\beta-1}
+o(k^{-\beta-1}).
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

The coefficient is uniformly negative by (4.7).  Therefore, above a deterministic level `L`, `F_n(V_n)` is a nonnegative supermartingale.  Optional stopping at any finite stopping time `sigma` gives

\[
\boxed{
P_q(\text{eventual extinction}\mid\mathcal H_\sigma,V_\sigma=k)
\le C_\beta(1+k)^{-\beta}.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

The cumulative exact visibility theorem says that below population `K` the full stack and its neutral-tail depth-`M` truncation have identical censored dynamics whenever `M>=2K-2`.  Apply (4.11) after their common high-population exit and take `K=floor(M/2)+1`.  This gives

\[
\boxed{
0\le p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}-p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}
\le C_\beta M^{-\beta},
\qquad
p_{\rm esc}^{(M)}\uparrow p_{\rm esc}^{(\infty)}.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Projective norm summability implies (4.6) by dominated convergence, but not conversely.

A strict example is an irrational rotation `T x=x+alpha mod 1` with Haar phase and two-cookie finite-prefix drift encoded by

\[
p_1(x)=0.85+0.05\cos(2\pi x),
\qquad
p_2(x)=0.85+0.05\sin(2\pi x).
\tag{4.13}
\]

The centered drift is a finite Fourier combination.  The present stack reveals the phase, so

\[
E[Y_j\mid\mathcal F_0]=Y(T^j x),

whose `L^infinity` norm does not decay; (4.1) therefore diverges.  But for each Fourier mode

## 5. B2 — destroy the corrector hypothesis without destroying continuity

To test whether UAC might follow from bare stationary ergodicity, take a stationary alternating-renewal process with unbounded finite-mean block lengths.  Let the centered finite-prefix drift be `+a` or `-a` on alternating blocks, and encode the residual block state in the cookie stack while preserving the same total drift.

For each `r` close to one there are positive-probability states with a visible constant-sign residual block of length `L` much larger than `(1-r)^(-1)`.  On a positive block,

\[
(1-r)G_{r,0}
\ge a(1-r)\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^j-\|Y_0\|_\infty r^L.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Choosing `L>>(1-r)^(-1)` makes the first term close to `a` and the second negligible, so

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{r\uparrow1}
\|(1-r)G_{r,0}\|_\infty>0.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus bare stationary ergodicity does not imply UAC.

This attack does **not** produce a source counterexample.  If the cookie stack in this construction has finite depth, truncation becomes exactly identical once `M` exceeds that depth.  Therefore UAC and the earlier projective condition are sufficient proof devices, not necessary conditions. ## 6. B27 — exact pathwise tail defect and harmonic reduction

Grand-couple the full positive stack and every depth-`M` neutral-tail truncation with the same site/visit uniforms.  Let `E_M` be survival of the depth-`M` forward chain and `E_infinity` survival of the full chain.  Positivity gives

\[
E_M\subseteq E_{M+1}\subseteq E_\infty.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
h_M(\omega,k)=P_{\omega,k}(E_M),
\quad
h(\omega,k)=P_{\omega,k}(E_\infty),
\quad
h_*(\omega,k)=\lim_M h_M(\omega,k).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
h_*(\omega,k)
=P_{\omega,k}\left(\bigcup_ME_M\right)
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

and therefore the exact residual is

\[
\boxed{
d(\omega,k):=h(\omega,k)-h_*(\omega,k)
=P_{\omega,k}\left(E_\infty\cap\bigcap_ME_M^c\right).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

So truncation continuity fails **if and only if** there is positive probability that the full process survives while every fixed finite-depth truncation dies.  This is a much sharper source-level target than another sufficient mixing condition.

Under the additional upper-ellipticity assumption

\[
p_{x,i}\le1-\kappa,
\tag{6.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
h_*(\omega,k)=E_\omega[h_*(\theta\omega,S_k)].}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Subtracting the full survival equation shows

\[
\boxed{d(\omega,k)=E_\omega[d(\theta\omega,S_k)].}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus `d` is a bounded nonnegative harmonic function for the full environment-population skew product.

Let

\tau_K=\inf\{n:V_n=0\text{ or }V_n\ge K\}.

While `1<=V_n<K`, (6.4) gives a conditional chance at least `kappa^(K-1)` that the next population is zero, hence `tau_K<infinity` almost surely.  Optional stopping the bounded martingale `d(theta^n omega,V_n)` gives

\tag{6.7}
