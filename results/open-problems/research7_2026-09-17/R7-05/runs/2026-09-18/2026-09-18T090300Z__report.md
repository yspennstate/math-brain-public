## Method B2 — attack the negation first

The exact two-cookie formula rules out a formula depending only on the total drift `delta`.

For a deterministic two-cookie stack `(p_1,p_2,1/2,...)`,

\[
\delta=2(p_1+p_2-1),
\qquad
p_{\rm esc}=\frac{\delta-1}{2p_2-1}
\quad(\delta>1).
\]

The two source-valid environments

\[
(p_1,p_2)=\left(\frac45,\frac45\right),
\qquad
(p_1,p_2)=\left(\frac7{10},\frac9{10}\right)
\]

both have `delta=6/5`, but their escape probabilities are respectively

\[
\frac13\quad\text{and}\quad\frac14.
\]

Thus even at depth two, total drift is insufficient. Any successful general formula must retain finer cookie-stack information.

## Method B11 — dualize to the backward branching chain

Let `F_{x,m}` be the number of failures before the `m`-th success in the independent Bernoulli stack at site `x`, and define the standard backward branching chain

\[
Z_0=0,\qquad Z_{k+1}=F_{k+1,Z_k+1}.
\]

For the i.i.d. bounded-cookie class with `delta>1`, this chain is irreducible positive recurrent; write `pi` for its unique stationary law.

The new exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
p_{\rm esc}=\mathbb E[\omega(0,1)]\,\pi(0).
}
\]

The proof is finite-interval and pathwise before the stationary limit. Start the ERW at `1` and stop at `{0,n}`. Reading the successful crossing backward from `n`, set `U_n=0` and

\[
U_j=F_{j,U_{j+1}+1},\qquad j=n-1,\ldots,1.
\]

The walk hits `n` before `0` iff `U_1=0`. By i.i.d. reversal of the site stacks,

\[
U_1\overset d=Z_{n-1},
\]

hence

\[
P_1(T_n<T_0)=P(Z_{n-1}=0).
\]

Right transience makes `{T_n<T_0}` decrease to `{T_0=\infty}`, while positive recurrence/aperiodicity gives `P(Z_{n-1}=0)->pi(0)`. Multiplying by the independent first-step probability at the origin proves the formula. Reflection gives the corresponding left-transient identity.

Two exact consequences are

\[
\pi(0)=\frac1{E_0\tau_0^+}
\]

and the zero-state balance equation

\[
\pi(0)=\sum_{i\ge0}\pi(i)\,
\mathbb E\left[\prod_{j=1}^{i+1}\omega(0,j)\right].
\]

The latter exposes the present blocker: outside the special two-cookie cancellation, `pi(0)` sees the full stationary tail of the countable-state chain.

## Method B5 — deform from the solved depth-two case

For deterministic positive two-cookie biases

\[
p_i(t)=\frac12+t b_i,
\qquad b_i>0,
\]

put `delta_1=2(b_1+b_2)`. Then `delta(t)=t delta_1` and the transience threshold on this ray is `t_c=1/delta_1`. Zerner's exact formula gives, for `t>t_c`,

\[
p_{\rm esc}(t)=\frac{t\delta_1-1}{2tb_2},
\qquad
p_{\rm esc}'(t)=\frac{1}{2b_2t^2}>0.
\]

In particular the escape probability turns on linearly at the threshold:

\[
p_{\rm esc}(t)
=
\frac{\delta_1^2}{2b_2}(t-t_c)+O((t-t_c)^2).
\]

Via the B11 identity,

\[
\pi_t(0)=
\frac{t\delta_1-1}{2tb_2(1/2+tb_1)}.
\]

This gives a sharp benchmark for `M>=3`: the real missing object is the parameter dependence of the stationary zero atom. For finite truncations, stationary differentiation obeys

\[
\pi_t'(I-P_t)=\pi_tP_t',\qquad \pi_t'\mathbf1=0,
\]

but passing this to the infinite chain near the recurrence boundary requires an ergodic/resolvent estimate not proved here. I do not promote the formal infinite-state differentiation to a theorem.
