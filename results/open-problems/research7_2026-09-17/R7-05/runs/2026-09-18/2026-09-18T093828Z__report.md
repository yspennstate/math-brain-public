## 4. Method B3 — prove something stronger: arbitrary finite-depth boundary reduction

Consider an i.i.d. site stack

\[
(p_1,\ldots,p_M,1/2,1/2,\ldots),\qquad M\ge2,
\]

under the standard weak-ellipticity assumptions, and let

\[
\delta=\mathbb E\sum_{j=1}^M(2p_j-1)>1.
\]

For a fresh site let `F_m` be the number of failures before its `m`-th success, annealed over the random stack. Let the standard backward chain have transition law

\[
Z_{n+1}=F_{n+1,Z_n+1},
\]

and stationary law `pi`.

Define the finite boundary

\[
S=\{0,1,\ldots,M-2\}.
\]

For `r in S`, put

\[
m_r=\mathbb E[F_{r+1}],
\qquad
c_r=m_r+\delta-r-1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact finite censored-chain formula

Let

\[
\tau_S^+=\inf\{n\ge1:Z_n\in S\}
\]

and define the `(M-1) x (M-1)` censored transition matrix

\[
B_{rs}=\mathbb P_r(Z_{\tau_S^+}=s),\qquad r,s\in S.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `nu` be the stationary probability vector of `B`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\pi_r=
\frac{\delta-1}{\sum_{j\in S}\nu_jc_j}\,\nu_r,
\qquad r\in S.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 p_{\rm esc}
 =\mathbb E[p_1]
 \frac{(\delta-1)\nu_0}{\sum_{j\in S}\nu_jc_j}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the entire infinite stationary-law problem, at every finite cookie depth, reduces exactly to the return kernel of a finite boundary set.

### 4.1 Proof of the arbitrary-depth finite-mean-free balance

Let

\[
K=Y_1+\cdots+Y_M
\]

be the number of successes among the first `M` cookie tosses at a fresh site, with `a_k=P(K=k)`. For every `i>=M-1`, after the first `M` trials there are `M-K` failures and `i+1-K` successes still required from fair Bernoulli trials. Hence

\[
\mathbb E[s^{F_{i+1}}]
=Q(s)T(s)^{i+1},
\tag{4.5}
\]

where

\[
T(s)=\frac1{2-s},
\qquad
Q(s)=\sum_{k=0}^M a_k s^{M-k}(2-s)^k.
\]

Write

\[
G(s)=\sum_{i\ge0}\pi_i s^i,
\qquad
P_r(s)=\mathbb E[s^{F_{r+1}}].
\]

Splitting the stationary PGF at `S` gives the exact equation

\[
G(s)=
\sum_{r\in S}\pi_rP_r(s)
+Q(s)T(s)
\left(
G(T(s))-\sum_{r\in S}\pi_rT(s)^r
\right).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Put `x=1-s`. The same termwise estimate proved in the previous depth-three run gives, without assuming `E_pi Z<infinity`,

\[
\frac{G(T(s))-G(s)}{1-s}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Further,

\[
Q(1-x)=1+\delta x+O(x^2),
\qquad
T(1-x)=1-x+O(x^2),
\]

and for fixed `r`,

\[
P_r(1-x)=1-m_rx+o(x),
\qquad
T(1-x)^r=1-rx+O(x^2).
\]

Divide (4.6) by `x` after moving the tail term to the left and let `x downarrow 0`. Equation (4.7) removes the potentially infinite stationary first moment. The resulting exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r\in S}\pi_rc_r=\delta-1.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

For `M=3`, this is exactly the previous `C_0 pi_0+C_1 pi_1=delta-1` identity.

**B3 outcome:** the depth-three balance is not an isolated cancellation: every finite cookie depth has only `M-1` exceptional boundary masses, constrained by one exact drift balance independent of stationary moments.

## 5. Method B12 — find what is conserved: boundary harmonic measures

For each `s in S`, define

\[
h_s(i)=\mathbb P_i(Z_{\tau_S}=s),
\qquad
\tau_S=\inf\{n\ge0:Z_n\in S\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Positive recurrence and irreducibility imply `tau_S<infinity` almost surely. Hence

\[
h_s(r)=\mathbf1_{\{r=s\}},\qquad r\in S,
\]

and the harmonicity relation

\[
Ph_s(i)=h_s(i),\qquad i\notin S.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `r in S`, one step followed by the first return to `S` gives

\[
Ph_s(r)=B_{rs}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Because `0<=h_s<=1`, stationarity may be applied with no moment assumption:

\[
0=\sum_i\pi_i(Ph_s(i)-h_s(i))
=\sum_{r\in S}\pi_r(B_{rs}-\mathbf1_{\{r=s\}}).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\pi_SB=\pi_S.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

where `pi_S=(pi_r)_{r in S}`. Since `B` is an irreducible finite stochastic matrix, its invariant probability vector `nu` is unique, so

\[
\pi_r=a\nu_r.
\]

Substituting this into (4.8) gives

\[
a=\frac{\delta-1}{\sum_{r\in S}\nu_rc_r},
\]

which proves (4.3)–(4.4).

## 6. Method B29 — add the point at infinity: exact finite brackets for the censored kernel

Fix `N>=M` and let

\[
T_N=\{M-1,M,\ldots,N-1\}.
\]

For `s in S`, define

\[
\ell_{s,N}(i)=\mathbb P_i(Z_{\tau_{S,N}}=s),
\]

where

\[
\tau_{S,N}=\inf\{n\ge0:Z_n\in S\text{ or }Z_n\ge N\}.
\]

Also define

\[
e_N(i)=\mathbb P_i(Z_{\tau_{S,N}}\ge N).
\]

The strong Markov property and `0<=h_s<=1` give the exact sandwich

\[
\boxed{
\ell_{s,N}(i)\le h_s(i)\le\ell_{s,N}(i)+e_N(i).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

On the finite set `T_N`, the lower and error functions solve

\[
\ell_{s,N}(i)
=P(i,s)+\sum_{j\in T_N}P(i,j)\ell_{s,N}(j),
\tag{6.2}
\]

\[
e_N(i)
=P(i,[N,\infty))+\sum_{j\in T_N}P(i,j)e_N(j).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Inserting these bounds after the first transition from `r in S` gives lower and upper bounds on every `B_{rs}`.

Because `tau_S<infinity` almost surely, the maximum state visited before `tau_S` is finite almost surely, so for each fixed `i`

\[
e_N(i)\downarrow0.
\]

The one-step tail from any fixed boundary state also tends to zero. Hence the finite brackets converge entrywise to the exact censored matrix `B`.

For a finite-support random stack whose cookie probabilities are rational, every transition probability in (6.2)–(6.3), including the tail `P(i,[N,infinity))`, is rational: after the finite cookies, the remaining failures have a negative-binomial law with parameter `1/2`. Thus every finite bracket is an exact-rational certificate.

This gives a deterministic certified arbitrary-precision algorithm for the finite-depth i.i.d. problem. It is mathematically stronger than a simulation, but it remains an approximation scheme rather than a universal closed symbolic evaluation, so it does not move `M_closed`.
