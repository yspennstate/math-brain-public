# Mathematical research

## Idea before code

The preceding run showed that finite expected closed-hull count forces survival, and produced source-valid survival examples at first-order equality. The immediate temptation is to prove the converse by showing the closed-hull expectation diverges. That cannot be logically sufficient: a finite almost-sure random variable can have infinite mean.

The new route is therefore to identify the **stationary set of seeds that survive**, derive an exact mass-transport identity for its gaps, and then ask how the closed-hull probabilities constrain that point process. This produces a conserved quantity (B12). Breaking left/right symmetry then reveals the correct second-order functional (B15). Finally, a scale-by-scale second-moment calculation pinpoints exactly where the strict proof loses its gluing power at equality (B30).

No checker is useful here; all claims are theorem-sized and are proved on the page.

## B12 — conserved mass of the survival skeleton

Let `((L_x,R_x))` be iid finite directed interval marks and let `H(x)` be the final activation hull from seed `x`. Define

`S={x in Z: H(x) is infinite}`,
`theta=P(0 in S)`.

Because `S` is a translation-equivariant factor of an iid environment, it is stationary and ergodic.

Let

`N_0=# {finite closed intervals containing 0}`.

### Theorem 1 — exact closed-hull/survival dichotomy

`theta=0` if and only if `N_0=infinity` almost surely; `theta>0` if and only if `N_0<infinity` almost surely.

### Proof

If `theta=0`, then by translation invariance each fixed site survives with probability zero, and countability gives `S=empty` almost surely. Thus every `H(x)` is finite and closed. Begin with `I_0=H(0)=[a_0,b_0]`. Given finite closed `I_k=[a_k,b_k]`, the hull `H(b_k+1)` is finite, closed, contains `b_k+1`, and hence overlaps or is adjacent to `I_k`. Their union is again a closed interval and strictly extends the right endpoint. Iterating gives infinitely many finite closed intervals containing zero, so `N_0=infinity`.

If `theta>0`, ergodicity gives positive asymptotic density of `S`, so survival sites occur on both sides of zero. No finite closed interval can contain a survival site, because such an interval would trap the process started there. If zero itself survives then `N_0=0`; otherwise the nearest survival sites `s_-<0<s_+` confine every finite closed interval containing zero to `[s_-+1,s_+-1]`, leaving only finitely many possibilities. Thus `N_0<infinity`. QED.

Since translation invariance gives

`E N_0=sum_{n>=0}(n+1)p_n`,

this theorem explains exactly why divergence of the old series is not a converse: survival can have `N_0<infinity` almost surely but `E N_0=infinity` through heavy gaps.

### The conserved gap identity

Assume `theta>0`, and for `d>=1` set

`g_d=P(0 in S, S cap {1,...,d-1}=empty, d in S)`.

Then

`sum_d g_d=theta`,                                                  (B12.1)

and the exact mass-transport identity is

`sum_d d g_d=1`.                                                    (B12.2)

Proof of (B12.2): each survival point sends one unit of mass to every integer in the half-open interval from itself to the next survival point. Every integer receives exactly one unit from the survival point immediately to its left (or itself). Translation-invariant mass transport equates expected sent and received mass.

Moreover, if `0` and `d` are consecutive survival sites, every interior seed `x in {1,...,d-1}` has a finite interval hull that cannot contain or cross either endpoint survival site. Therefore `H(x) subseteq[1,d-1]`, so every mark based in the gap stays in the gap. Hence `[1,d-1]` is closed and

`g_d<=p_{d-2}` for `d>=2`.                                         (B12.3)

Combining (B12.2)-(B12.3), for every `M>=1`,

`1<=M theta+sum_{d>M}d p_{d-2}`,

or

`theta >= (1/M)[1-sum_{d>M} d p_{d-2}]_+`.                         (B12.4)

This recovers the previous finite-intensity survival theorem but also identifies the invariant it was hiding: unit spatial mass is partitioned among gaps of the stationary survival skeleton.

**B12 outcome:** genuine structural progress. The exact remaining converse problem is now a stationary point-process/gap problem, not merely a divergent-series calculation.

## B15 — break symmetry: the logarithmic correction is additive

Let

`q_n^L=P(L>n)`, `q_n^R=P(R>n)`,
`A_L(n)=prod_{k=0}^n(1-q_k^L)`, `A_R(n)=prod_{k=0}^n(1-q_k^R)`.

Assume `P(L=0,R=0)>0` and

`q_n^L=(1/n)(1+a_L/log n+O(1/log^2 n))`,                            (B15.1)

`q_n^R=(1/n)(1+a_R/log n+O(1/log^2 n))`.                            (B15.2)

No independence between `L` and `R` at a site is assumed.

### Theorem 2 — two-boundary hull asymptotic up to constants

For

`p_n=P([0,n] closed)=prod_{k=0}^n F(k,n-k)`,

one has

`p_n asymp A_L(n)A_R(n)
    asymp 1/[n^2(log n)^(a_L+a_R)]`.                               (B15.3)

### Proof

For the upper bound,

`F(u,v)<=1-q_u^L`, `F(u,v)<=1-q_v^R`.

Splitting the product at `floor(n/2)` gives

`p_n<=A_L(floor(n/2))A_R(n-floor(n/2)-1)`.                          (B15.4)

From (B15.1)-(B15.2), summing logarithms gives

`A_L(n) asymp n^-1(log n)^(-a_L)`,
`A_R(n) asymp n^-1(log n)^(-a_R)`,                                 (B15.5)

so the right side of (B15.4) is within a fixed constant of `A_L(n)A_R(n)`.

For the lower bound, write `j(u,v)=P(L>u,R>v)`. Then

`F(u,v)=1-q_u^L-q_v^R+j(u,v)
       >=(1-q_u^L)(1-q_v^R)-q_u^Lq_v^R`.                           (B15.6)

Away from finitely many endpoint indices, factor this as the product of the two marginals times

`1-r_{u,v}`,

where

`r_{u,v}=q_u^Lq_v^R/[(1-q_u^L)(1-q_v^R)]`.

The critical tails imply

`sum_{k=K}^{n-K}r_{k,n-k}=O((log n)/n)=o(1)`,                       (B15.7)

so the product of these correction factors is bounded away from zero. The finitely many endpoint ratios are bounded below for large `n` because, for fixed `k`, `F(k,n-k)->P(L<=k)` (and symmetrically at the right endpoint), while `P(L=0,R=0)>0` guarantees positive endpoint marginals. Therefore

`p_n>=c A_L(n)A_R(n)`                                              (B15.8)

for large `n`, proving (B15.3). QED.

### Consequence

If

`a_L+a_R>1`,                                                        (B15.9)

then

`sum_n(n+1)p_n<infinity`,

so survival has positive probability. Thus the second-order functional exposed by the hull method is

`a_L+a_R`,

not a maximum or minimum of the two side corrections. In the source-symmetric case `a_L=a_R=a`, this becomes exactly `a>1/2`, explaining the threshold in the preceding source-valid equality family.

This is a survival theorem only. No extinction converse is claimed for `a_L+a_R<=1`.

**B15 outcome:** breaking symmetry resolves which combination of the two boundary corrections the rigorous mechanism sees: it is additive.

## B30 — one logarithmic scale at a time and locate the gluing failure

Put

`A=a_L+a_R`.

Let `W_m` count closed intervals `[-i,j]` containing zero with

`e^m<=i+j<e^(m+1)`.

By (B15.3),

`E W_m
 =sum_{e^m<=n<e^(m+1)}(n+1)p_n
 asymp m^(-A)`.                                                     (B30.1)

Therefore the total expected closed-hull count changes summability exactly at `A=1`. For `A<=1`, however, Theorem 1 says this expectation divergence is compatible both with extinction (`N_0=infinity` a.s.) and with survival accompanied by a finite but heavy-tailed `N_0`.

The old strict proof also fails at a precise logarithmic place. Let

`Z_N=# {[-i,j] closed: N<=i,j<=2N}`.

Then

`E Z_N asymp (log N)^(-A)`.                                        (B30.2)

For two candidates, if their left and right endpoint differences are `d,e`, simultaneous closure forces a closed intersection core plus a left wing satisfying one-sided left constraints and a right wing satisfying one-sided right constraints. These sets of sites are disjoint, giving

`P(I and I' closed)<=p_core A_L(d-1)A_R(e-1)`.                     (B30.3)

Summation yields

`E Z_N^2
 <= E Z_N + C N^2 p_{Theta(N)}
    (sum_{d<=N}A_L(d-1))(sum_{e<=N}A_R(e-1))`.                     (B30.4)

In the symmetric unresolved source regime `a_L=a_R=a<=1/2`,

`A_L(d)asymp1/[d(log d)^a]`,
`sum_{d<=N}A_L(d)asymp(log N)^(1-a)`,
`p_{Theta(N)}asympN^-2(log N)^(-2a)`.

Thus

`E Z_N^2/(E Z_N)^2 <= C(log N)^2`,                                 (B30.5)

including the smaller diagonal contribution. Paley-Zygmund therefore gives only

`P(Z_N>0)>=c/(log N)^2`,                                           (B30.6)

which tends to zero. This is not an estimate of the true probability from above; it says the existing second-moment machinery has lost the uniform positive scale probability that drove the strict-case tail-zero-one argument.

**B30 outcome:** the equality obstruction is now localized. The missing theorem must control dependence among nested hull events across logarithmic scales, or equivalently impose additional constraints on the stationary survival-gap law beyond

`sum g_d=theta`, `sum d g_d=1`, `g_d<=p_{d-2}`.                    (B30.7)

This is a cross-scale renewal/capacity problem, not another first-order tail estimate.
