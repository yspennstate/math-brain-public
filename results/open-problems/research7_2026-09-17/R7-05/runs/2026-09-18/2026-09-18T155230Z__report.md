## 4. Reused finite-depth algebra

For a fixed finite cookie depth `M`, write

\[
\alpha_{x,i}=2p_{x,i}-1,
\qquad
T(z)=\frac1{2-z},
\]

and in a deterministic stack

\[
B_x(z)=\prod_{i=1}^M((1-\alpha_{x,i})+\alpha_{x,i}z).
\]

If `S_k` is the number of successes before the `k`-th failure at the site, then for every `k>=M` the exact one-site pgf is

\[
\boxed{P_{x,k}(z)=T(z)^kB_x(z).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For positive cookies `B_x` is itself a pgf, so declaring (4.1) at every `k>=0` defines a genuine auxiliary stochastic kernel. The true and auxiliary positive-population rows differ only for

\[
\mathcal B=\{1,\ldots,M-1\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This row-localization is the cumulative algebraic input for B21 and B4 below; no i.i.d. factorization is used in those sections.

## 5. B7 — take the limit: exact obstruction to infinite cookie depth

Fix any deterministic positive environment `omega` and truncate after depth `M` by

\[
\omega^{(M)}(x,i)=
\begin{cases}
\omega(x,i),&i\le M,\\
1/2,&i>M.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
\omega^{(M)}\le\omega^{(M+1)}\le\omega.
\]

Let

\[
e(\eta)=P_{0,\eta}[X_n>0\text{ for every }n>0].
\]

Zerner's Theorem 16 gives

\[
e(\omega^{(M)})\uparrow e_*(\omega)\le e(\omega).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Now define the finite-horizon event

\[
E_N=\{X_1>0,\ldots,X_N>0\}.
\]

A length-`N` trajectory can consume at most `N` cookies at a site, so for every `M>=N`,

\[
\boxed{P_{0,\omega^{(M)}}(E_N)=P_{0,\omega}(E_N).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Set `a_{M,N}=P_{0,omega^(M)}(E_N)`. Continuity from above of the decreasing events `E_N` gives

\[
e(\omega^{(M)})=\inf_Na_{M,N},
\]

while (5.2) gives

\[
e(\omega)=\inf_N\sup_Ma_{M,N}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 e_*(\omega)
 =\sup_M\inf_Na_{M,N}
 \le
 \inf_N\sup_Ma_{M,N}
 =e(\omega).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus every finite horizon stabilizes **exactly**, but source-level infinite-depth closure requires the minimax equality

\[
\boxed{
\sup_M\inf_Na_{M,N}\stackrel?=\inf_N\sup_Ma_{M,N}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is not a bookkeeping gap. Zerner explicitly posed continuity of the return probability in `omega` as an open problem. Equality in this monotone truncation direction is a concrete instance of that continuity issue. Therefore the cumulative all-finite-depth theorem cannot be sent to `M=infinity` by an unjustified limiting argument.

B7 outcome: **precise obstruction found, no full-source closure.**

## 6. B21 — replace the finite-state Poisson equation by a Gordin decomposition

I next removed finite-state/Markov dependence from the transfer asymptotic.

Assume a deterministic finite depth `M`, a two-sided stationary ergodic stack process, and for some `kappa>0`

\[
\frac12\le p_{x,i}\le1-\kappa,
\qquad 1\le i\le M,
\tag{6.1}
\]

with neutral cookies afterward. Set

\[
\delta_x=\sum_{i=1}^M(2p_{x,i}-1),
\qquad
\bar\delta=E\delta_0>1.
\]

Assume the stack sequence is summably phi-mixing:

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\phi(n)<\infty.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For the reversed centered drift `Y_j=delta_{-j}-bar(delta)` and past filtration `G_j`, the standard phi-mixing conditional estimate gives

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\|E[Y_n\mid\mathcal G_0]\|_\infty<\infty.
\]

Therefore the bounded stationary series

\[
G_j=\sum_{m=0}^\infty E[Y_{j+m}\mid\mathcal G_j]
\]

is well defined. Since

\[
E[G_{j+1}\mid\mathcal G_j]=G_j-Y_j,
\]

with

\[
D_{j+1}=G_{j+1}-E[G_{j+1}\mid\mathcal G_j]
\]

we have the exact Gordin decomposition

\[
\boxed{Y_j=G_j-G_{j+1}+D_{j+1},}
\tag{6.3}
\]

where `(D_{j+1})` is a bounded martingale-difference sequence. Exact-SHA readback of the main artifact caught a wording slip that had incorrectly grouped `G` with the martingale differences; the separate immutable erratum fixes the sentence while leaving (6.3) and all mathematics unchanged.

For harmonic weights `a_j=(u+j)^(-1)`, summation by parts yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_jY_j
={}&a_0G_0-a_{n-1}G_n\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(a_j-a_{j-1})G_j
+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_jD_{j+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The coboundary terms converge absolutely, while the martingale transform converges almost surely and in `L^2` since `sum a_j^2<infinity`. Bounded martingale differences and conditional Hoeffding further give uniform exponential moments for every fixed exponential parameter.

Define

\[
b_\eta(v)=B_\eta(1-v^{-1})
=\prod_{i=1}^M\left(1-\frac{\alpha_i(\eta)}v\right).
\]

Uniformly in the stack,

\[
\log b_\eta(v)=-\frac{\delta(\eta)}v+O(v^{-2}).
\]

For the reversed stationary stack sequence `eta_j`, let

\[
W_n(u)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}b_{\eta_j}(u+j).
\]

Equations above imply

\[
\boxed{n^{\bar\delta}W_n(u)\to L(u)\in(0,\infty)}
\tag{6.5}
\]

almost surely and in every fixed finite `L^q`, and hence

\[
\boxed{EW_n(u)\sim C(u)n^{-\bar\delta}.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is the actual annealed transfer product because the fixed-environment `n`-step auxiliary pgf is

\[
T^n(z)^k
\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}
B_{\theta^m\omega}(T^{n-1-m}(z)),
\]

whose environment block, after `u=(1-z)^{-1}` and stationarity recentering, has the law of the reversed sequence in `W_n`.

Fixed derivatives satisfy

\[
\left|\frac{d^r}{dv^r}\log b_\eta(v)\right|
\le C_rv^{-r-1},
\]

so coefficient extraction gives, for fixed populations `k,j`,

\[
\boxed{E\,\mathsf Q_\omega^{(n)}(k,j)=O(n^{-\bar\delta}).}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Since `bar(delta)>1`, all fixed low-state auxiliary Green sums are annealed-summable.

B21 outcome: **successful extension from finite-state Markov dependence to a genuine infinite-memory summably phi-mixing class.** The statement is annealed after stationarity recentering; no false fixed-origin quenched limit is asserted.

## 7. B4 — prove a weaker statement completely: functional resolvent for arbitrary stationary dependence

The finite-state theorem made the defect system finite-dimensional by carrying a finite environment state. Here I keep the **entire environment** as state instead. Finite dimensionality is unnecessary for uniqueness if the low-population boundary operator is contractive.

Assume only stationary spatial dependence, fixed finite depth `M>=2`, and (6.1). No mixing, Markov, finite-state, or independence assumption is used in this section.

Lift the forward population chain to `(omega,k)` with deterministic environment shift `omega -> theta omega`. Let `K_omega` be the true one-site population kernel and `Q_omega` the positive auxiliary kernel from (4.1). Put

\[
\mathcal A=\{0,1,\ldots,M-1\},
\qquad
\mathcal B=\{1,\ldots,M-1\}.
\]

For `k>=M`, let `tau` be the first auxiliary hit of `A`, set

\[
r(\omega,k)=P^{\mathcal Q}_{\omega,k}(\tau=\infty),
\]

and for a bounded vector function `f=(f_1,...,f_{M-1})` define

\[
(Hf)(\omega,k)=
E^{\mathcal Q}_{\omega,k}
[f_{V_\tau}(\theta^\tau\omega);
\tau<\infty,V_\tau\in\mathcal B]
\]

for `k>=M`, while `(Hf)(omega,k)=f_k(omega)` on `B`. Then `||Hf||_infinity<=||f||_infinity`.

If `s(omega,k)` is true forward survival, true and auxiliary rows agree until first entry into `A`, so

\[
\boxed{s(\omega,k)=r(\omega,k)+(Hs_B)(\omega,k),\qquad k\ge M.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For a true boundary state `b in B`, define

\[
a_b(\omega)
=\sum_{j\ge1}K_\omega(b,j)r(\theta\omega,j)
\]

and

\[
(\mathcal Mf)_b(\omega)
=\sum_{j\ge1}K_\omega(b,j)(Hf)(\theta\omega,j).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{(I-\mathcal M)s_B=a.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The key new point is a uniform contraction. From boundary population `b`, next population zero means the first `b` Bernoulli trials are all failures. The upper ellipticity in (6.1) therefore gives

\[
K_\omega(b,0)\ge\kappa^b\ge\kappa^{M-1}=:\varepsilon_M>0.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\|\mathcal M\|_{\infty\to\infty}\le1-\varepsilon_M<1.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
s_B=(I-\mathcal M)^{-1}a
=\sum_{r=0}^\infty\mathcal M^ra,
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

with uniform remainder

\[
\left\|
s_B-\sum_{r=0}^R\mathcal M^ra
\right\|_\infty
\le
\frac{(1-\varepsilon_M)^{R+1}}{\varepsilon_M}\|a\|_\infty.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The sitewise no-return/forward-survival identity does not require independence, so in the right-transient positive regime

\[
\boxed{
p_{\rm esc}
=E[p_{0,1}(\omega)s(\theta\omega,1)].
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Under dependence the expectation in (7.6) must not be factored.

Thus arbitrary stationary spatial dependence at **fixed finite depth** has an exact contractive functional resolvent whenever cookie strengths are uniformly bounded away from one. This is genuinely beyond the preceding finite-state Markov theorem, although the environment-valued boundary function is infinite-dimensional.

If the B21 mixing assumptions are added, (6.7) gives a useful convergence rate for the auxiliary first-entrance piece:

\[
E P^{\mathcal Q}_{\omega,k}
(\tau>N,\tau<\infty,V_\tau\in B)
=O(N^{1-\bar\delta}).
\tag{7.7}
\]

So this subclass has two proved approximation mechanisms: polynomial time-truncation of the auxiliary first-entrance operator and geometric Neumann truncation in the true defect boundary.

B4 outcome: **complete restricted theorem with full proof; source remains PARTIAL.**
