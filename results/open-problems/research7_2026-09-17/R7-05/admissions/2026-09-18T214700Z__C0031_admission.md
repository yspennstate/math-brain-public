## Outside source and exact endpoint

Outside source: Charles Bordenave and Djalil Chafaï, *Around the circular law*, Probability Surveys **9** (2012), 1–89, arXiv:1109.3343v4, last revised 13 March 2012, DOI 10.1214/11-PS183. Primary source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.3343 .

Exact locator: Section 6.6 discussion immediately following Theorem 6.2 (PDF p. 53 in the public version; the campaign intake records this as `Section 6.6 remark, p. 39` under its acquired-PDF pagination). Theorem 6.2 states, in the heavy-tailed i.i.d. setup of Section 6 with tail index `alpha in (0,2)`, that the empirical eigenvalue measure of the appropriately `n^(−1/alpha)`-scaled matrix converges weakly in probability to the deterministic heavy-tailed circular-law limit `mu_alpha`; if the entry law has a bounded density, the convergence is almost sure. The authors then explicitly state that they believe the theorem can be upgraded to almost-sure weak convergence in general, and identify their obstruction as slow in-probability control of small singular values.

Canonical source-level closure criterion for C0031: **under the same Section-6 heavy-tail hypotheses used for the in-probability assertion of Theorem 6.2, prove almost-sure weak convergence of the empirical eigenvalue measures to `mu_alpha` without adding the bounded-density assumption or another hypothesis not present in that source endpoint.** A proof merely for bounded-density, smooth, Gaussian-regularized, symmetric-special, or rectangular ensembles is a restricted result and does not close C0031.

## Hypotheses / quantifiers preserved

The target concerns square `n x n` matrices whose entries are i.i.d. and lie in the heavy-tailed alpha-stable domain considered by Bordenave–Chafaï, with `0 < alpha < 2`, and the same deterministic normalization used in their heavy-tail theorem. The limiting measure `mu_alpha` is deterministic. The requested strengthening changes only the convergence mode: from convergence in probability to almost-sure weak convergence. It does **not** permit imposing a bounded density; bounded density is already the source's sufficient special case.

Where the intake paraphrase says `n^(−1/alpha)`, any slowly-varying normalization detail in the primary theorem is to be inherited exactly from the primary source rather than silently discarded. No claim below depends on replacing the source normalization by a stricter one.
