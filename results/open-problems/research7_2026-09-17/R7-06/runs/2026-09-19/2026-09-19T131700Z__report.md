## B3 — deterministic sublinear minimal polynomial

Let the distinct integral eigenvalues of an integral-spectrum symmetric sign matrix `M` be

\[
\theta_1<\cdots<\theta_r
\]

with multiplicities `m_j`. Every such matrix obeys

\[
\sum_i\lambda_i^2=\operatorname{tr}(M^2)=n^2.
\]

Earlier R7-06 work proved the exact parity restriction: for even `n` all integral eigenvalues are even; for odd `n`, exactly one eigenvalue counted with multiplicity is odd.

For `q` distinct even integers, let `E(q)` be their minimum possible square sum. If `q=2s+1`, the minimizer is `0,±2,...,±2s` and

\[
E(2s+1)=8\sum_{k=1}^s k^2
=\frac{q(q^2-1)}3.
\]

If `q=2s`, one minimizer is `0,±2,...,±2(s-1),2s`, giving

\[
E(2s)=\frac{q(q^2+2)}3.
\]

Hence always

\[
E(q)\ge\frac{q(q^2-1)}3.
\]

Therefore, for even `n`,

\[
\boxed{r(r^2-1)\le3n^2,}
\]

and for odd `n`,

\[
\boxed{1+\frac{(r-1)((r-1)^2-1)}3\le n^2.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{r=O(n^{2/3}).}
\]

Since `M` is real symmetric, its minimal polynomial is square-free and equals

\[
g(t)=\prod_{j=1}^r(t-\theta_j),
\]

so every F0011 target matrix satisfies the exact integer matrix identity

\[
\boxed{g(M)=0,\qquad \deg g=O(n^{2/3}).}
\]

This is deterministic and does not use a semicircle law.

The two-root boundary is especially informative. If the only roots are `theta,phi`, put `s=theta+phi`, `p=theta phi`; then

\[
M^2-sM+pI=0.
\]

Taking diagonal entries gives

\[
n-sM_{ii}+p=0.
\]

If `s!=0`, every diagonal entry is forced to the same sign. If `s=0`, then `theta=-phi`, `theta^2=n`, and

\[
\boxed{M^2=nI.}
\]

That is exactly the symmetric-Hadamard branch, and integrality forces `n` to be a square. Thus the hoped-for stronger universal `exp(O(n log n))` fixed-fiber estimate already contains a difficult Hadamard-counting boundary at `r=2`; I do not claim that stronger estimate.

## B11 — fixed spectra as an integral projector lattice

Write

\[
\chi_M(t)=\prod_j(t-\theta_j)^{m_j},
\qquad
g(t)=\prod_j(t-\theta_j),
\qquad
f(t)=\chi_M(t)/g(t).
\]

For deletion of vertex `i`, let

\[
\chi_i(t)=\det(tI-M^{(i)}).
\]

Interlacing reduces every eigenvalue multiplicity by at most one, so `chi_i` is divisible by `f`. Since both are monic integral polynomials,

\[
\boxed{\chi_i(t)=f(t)q_i(t),\qquad q_i\in\mathbb Z[t],\quad \deg q_i=r-1.}
\]

Let `R(t)=(tI-M)^{-1}` and let `P_j` denote the spectral projector onto the `theta_j` eigenspace. Cramer's rule and the spectral theorem give

\[
R_{ii}(t)=\frac{q_i(t)}{g(t)}
=\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{w_{ij}}{t-\theta_j},
\]

where

\[
\boxed{w_{ij}=(P_j)_{ii}=\frac{q_i(\theta_j)}{g'(\theta_j)}.}
\]

The exact constraints are

\[
w_{ij}\ge0,
\qquad \sum_jw_{ij}=1,
\qquad \sum_j\theta_jw_{ij}=M_{ii}\in\{\pm1\},
\qquad \sum_iw_{ij}=m_j.
\]

The determinant derivative identity also gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_iq_i(t)=\frac{\chi_M'(t)}{f(t)}
=\sum_jm_j\prod_{k\ne j}(t-\theta_k).
}
\]

Because every `q_i(theta_j)` is an integer and `0<=w_ij<=1`, a fixed spectrum admits at most

\[
\prod_j(|g'(\theta_j)|+1)
\le \exp(O(r^2\log n))
\]

possible rooted deletion polynomials. This does not yet count full matrices; it converts the rooted spectral information into an explicit finite lattice.

There is also a simultaneous exact finite-field statement. If a prime `p` does not divide

\[
\Delta(g)=\prod_{j<k}(\theta_j-\theta_k),
\]

in particular if `p>2n`, then `g mod p` is square-free. The exact identity `g(M)=0` implies `M mod p` is diagonalizable and

\[
\boxed{
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\ker(M-\theta_jI)=m_j
\quad\text{for every }j.
}
\]

Thus a fixed integral spectrum imposes its entire multiplicity vector as simultaneous exact corank constraints at every good prime, not merely complete splitting of the characteristic polynomial.

The integer scaled projectors

\[
A_j:=\prod_{k\ne j}(M-\theta_kI)
\]

satisfy

\[
\boxed{
A_j=g'(\theta_j)P_j,
\quad A_j^2=g'(\theta_j)A_j,
\quad A_jA_k=0\ (j\ne k).
}
\]

A direct obstruction to importing the newest one-vector walk-matrix/SNF cospectral machinery also appears. For any seed `v`,

\[
M^kv=\sum_j\theta_j^kP_jv,
\]

so the Krylov space has dimension at most `r`. A block with `k` seeds can span all of `R^n` only if

\[
\boxed{k\ge\max_jm_j\ge n/r=\Omega(n^{1/3}).}
\]

Hence every one-vector walk matrix is singular on every repeated-spectrum target; by the prior R7-06 theorem, this covers every F0011 instance for `n>=6`. The 2026 generalized-cospectral-mate results built around a nonsingular walk matrix therefore have a genuine hypothesis mismatch here.

There is still a rank-one bridge. In the first-row switching normalization, write `d=M_11`, `c=1-d`, `e=(1,...,1)^T`; then

\[
e=(M+cI)e_1.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
e^T(tI-M)^{-1}e
=(t+c)^2R_{11}(t)-(t+2c)-d,}
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
\det(tI-M-sJ)
=\chi_M(t)\bigl(1-s e^T(tI-M)^{-1}e\bigr).
}
\]

So the failure of the one-vector SNF route is controllability, not lack of access to generalized rank-one perturbation spectra.

Finally, bare mod 2 carries no new annihilator information. For even `n`, parity gives `g(t)=t^r mod 2`, while `M=J mod2` and `J^2=0`; for odd `n`, `g(t)=t^{r-1}(t+1) mod2`, `M=J mod2`, and `J^2=J`. Thus any adaptation of the recent finite-field/rational-canonical strategy must use a nontrivial `2`-adic lift such as mod 4, or odd primes.

## B19 — reconstruct the switching class from a codimension-three spectral deck

For a subset `S` of vertices, define

\[
\chi_S(t)=\det(tI-M^{(S)}).
\]

I proved the following exact reconstruction theorem.

### Theorem

The labeled collection

\[
\{\chi_S(t): |S|\le3\}
\]

determines a symmetric sign matrix up to diagonal sign switching.

### Proof

Jacobi's complementary principal-minor identity gives

\[
\frac{\chi_S(t)}{\chi_\varnothing(t)}
=\det R(t)_{S,S},
\qquad R(t)=(tI-M)^{-1}.
\]

For `S={i}`, this determines `R_ii`. Since

\[
R_{ii}(t)=t^{-1}+M_{ii}t^{-2}+O(t^{-3}),
\]

it recovers every diagonal sign.

For `S={i,j}`,

\[
\frac{\chi_{ij}}{\chi_\varnothing}
=R_{ii}R_{jj}-R_{ij}^2,
\]

so it recovers every `R_ij(t)^2`.

For three distinct indices,

\[
\frac{\chi_{ijk}}{\chi_\varnothing}
=R_{ii}R_{jj}R_{kk}
+2R_{ij}R_{ik}R_{jk}
-R_{ii}R_{jk}^2
-R_{jj}R_{ik}^2
-R_{kk}R_{ij}^2.
\]

All terms except the triple product are already known, so the triple-deletion spectrum recovers

\[
R_{ij}(t)R_{ik}(t)R_{jk}(t).
\]

For `i!=j`,

\[
R_{ij}(t)=M_{ij}t^{-2}+O(t^{-3}),
\]

hence the coefficient of `t^{-6}` in the triple product is

\[
\tau_{ijk}=M_{ij}M_{ik}M_{jk}.
\]

The earlier exact switching lemma states that the diagonal signs together with all triangle products `tau_ijk` determine the labeled matrix up to diagonal sign conjugation. QED.

This is an application of classical Jacobi/principal-minor reconstruction machinery; no novelty priority is inferred from a bounded search.

The theorem is sharp in a useful sense. On the symmetric-Hadamard branch `H^2=nI`,

\[
R(t)=\frac{tI+H}{t^2-n},
\]

so

\[
R_{ii}(t)=\frac{t+d_i}{t^2-n},
\qquad
R_{ij}(t)^2=\frac1{(t^2-n)^2}.
\]

Thus, once the spectrum and diagonal vector are fixed, the entire one- and two-deletion spectral deck is independent of every off-diagonal Hadamard sign. But the triple deletion satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\chi_{ijk}(t)}{\chi_\varnothing(t)}
=\frac{
(t+d_i)(t+d_j)(t+d_k)
-(t+d_i)-(t+d_j)-(t+d_k)
+2\tau_{ijk}
}{(t^2-n)^3},
}
\]

so the first level that sees the switching-invariant triangle bit is exactly level three. This is an information-theoretic obstruction to any universal vertex-deleted or pair-deleted spectral reconstruction strategy.

The same mechanism gives a recursive row-extension formulation. Write

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}d&x^T\\x&B\end{pmatrix},
\qquad x\in\{\pm1\}^{n-1},
\]

and let `mu_a` and `Q_a` be the distinct eigenvalues and orthogonal spectral projectors of `B`. Schur complementation yields

\[
\frac{\chi_M(t)}{\chi_B(t)}
=t-d-\sum_a\frac{\|Q_ax\|^2}{t-\mu_a}.
\]

Hence, once `B` and the target full characteristic polynomial are fixed, every permissible sign border vector `x` must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\|Q_ax\|^2
=-\operatorname*{Res}_{t=\mu_a}
\frac{\chi_M(t)}{\chi_B(t)}
\quad\text{for every }a,
}
\]

with the natural cancellation interpretation for common roots. Conversely, these residues plus the asymptotic `t-d` term recover the target characteristic polynomial.

This isolates the concrete next counting problem: at each exposure step, how many sign vectors can realize one prescribed energy in every eigenspace of the already-exposed principal block?

**B19 outcome:** an exact reconstruction hierarchy and a new row-extension formulation of the entropy bottleneck; source still open.
