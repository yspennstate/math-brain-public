## 4. Method B4 — prove a weaker statement completely: exact smooth boundary germ

The first question was not to invert the full law again, but to determine rigorously what happens at the solvable endpoint `a=0`.

For every `x>=0` and every integer `N>=0`, the finite geometric identity is

\[
\frac1{1+x}
=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-x)^n+\frac{(-x)^{N+1}}{1+x}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Substitute `x=aU` into (4.1) and take expectations.  Every moment exists by (3.5), so no infinite-series interchange is required.  One obtains the exact finite Taylor formula

\[
\boxed{
q_\beta(a)
=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^n\mu_n a^n
+(-1)^{N+1}a^{N+1}
\mathbb E\frac{U^{N+1}}{1+aU}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because `0<(1+aU)^{-1}<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\left|q_\beta(a)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^n\mu_n a^n\right|
\le \mu_{N+1}a^{N+1},
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

and the sign of the remainder yields the global alternating enclosures

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n=0}^{2m+1}(-1)^n\mu_n a^n
\le q_\beta(a)
\le
\sum_{n=0}^{2m}(-1)^n\mu_n a^n.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

In particular `q_beta` is `C^infinity` at zero and

\[
\boxed{q_\beta^{(n)}(0+)=(-1)^n n!\mu_n.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Hence its formal Taylor coefficients are

\[
a_n=(-1)^n
\left[
\frac{\Gamma(1+n/\beta)}{\Gamma(n+1)c_\beta^{n/\beta}}
\right]^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Their successive-ratio asymptotic is

\[
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
\sim
(\beta c_\beta)^{-2/\beta}n^{2/\beta-2}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `beta<1`, the exponent is positive and the ratio tends to infinity.  Thus the formal Taylor series has **radius zero** for every heavy-tail index under consideration.  There is no contradiction with `C^infinity`: (4.2) is a genuine finite-order asymptotic expansion with a signed remainder, while the infinite formal series diverges at every nonzero `a`.

Stirling applied to (4.6) further gives

\[
\log|a_n|
=\left(\frac2\beta-2\right)\log(n!)+O(n).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Therefore the exact Gevrey order of the boundary germ is

\[
\boxed{\sigma_\beta=\frac2\beta-2.}
\tag{4.9}

## 5. Method B19 — make it an algorithm: certified arbitrary-index evaluation and inversion

The goal here was to replace an uncontrolled implicit solve by a rigorous one-dimensional evaluation procedure valid for every real `beta`, including irrational indices.

### 5.1 Mellin representation

For `0<Re(s)<beta`, Tonelli and the change `a=Tu` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty a^{s-1}q_\beta(a)\,da
&=\mathbb E\int_0^\infty a^{s-1}\frac{T}{a+T}\,da\\
&=\mathbb E[T^s]\int_0^\infty\frac{u^{s-1}}{1+u}\,du.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The stable positive moment is

\[
\mathbb E S^s
=c_\beta^{s/\beta}
\frac{\Gamma(1-s/\beta)}{\Gamma(1-s)},
\tag{5.2}
\]

and Euler's beta integral is

\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{u^{s-1}}{1+u}\,du
=\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\widehat q_\beta(s)
=c_\beta^{2s/\beta}
\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s/\beta)^2}{\Gamma(1-s)},
\quad 0<\Re s<\beta.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Mellin inversion on any vertical line `c in (0,beta)` gives

\[
q_\beta(a)
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\widehat q_\beta(c+it)a^{-c-it}\,dt.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This representation contains Gamma functions and elementary operations only; the stable random variables and BCC fixed-point unknown have disappeared from the evaluator.

### 5.2 Exponential contour truncation

Choose `c=beta/2`.  Fixed-line Stirling estimates give a finite constant `K_beta` such that

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat q_\beta(\beta/2+it)|
\le K_\beta(1+|t|)^{\beta-1}e^{-\pi|t|/\beta}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The exponential factor is transparent: `Gamma(s)` contributes `e^{-pi|t|/2}`, the two `Gamma(1-s/beta)` factors contribute `e^{-pi|t|/beta}`, and `1/Gamma(1-s)` contributes `e^{+pi|t|/2}`.  A rigorous `K_beta` can be constructed deterministically by ball/interval evaluation on a finite `t` interval and an explicit Stirling remainder beyond it.

Let

\[
q_{\beta,T}(a)=\frac{a^{-\beta/2}}{2\pi}
\int_{-T}^{T}
\widehat q_\beta(\beta/2+it)e^{-it\log a}\,dt.
\tag{5.7}
\]

For `T>=1`, monotonicity of `t^{beta-1}` yields

\[
\boxed{
|q_\beta(a)-q_{\beta,T}(a)|
\le
\frac{K_\beta\beta}{\pi^2}
 a^{-\beta/2}T^{\beta-1}e^{-\pi T/\beta}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Differentiation under the absolutely convergent contour gives

\[
q_\beta'(a)
=-\frac1{2\pi a}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
(\beta/2+it)\widehat q_\beta(\beta/2+it)a^{-\beta/2-it}\,dt,
\tag{5.9}
\]

with an analogous exponentially small explicit truncation bound.  For example, one may take

\[
\boxed{
|q_\beta'(a)-q_{\beta,T}'(a)|
\le
\frac{2K_\beta}{\pi}a^{-\beta/2-1}
\frac{T^\beta e^{-\pi T/\beta}}{\pi/\beta-\beta/T}.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

The finite contour is analytic and pole-free, so validated interval/Taylor, Gauss, or tanh-sinh quadrature closes the compact part to arbitrary precision.

### 5.3 Certified inversion

The physical squared radius is

\[
\boxed{
r(a)=a\left(\frac{q_\beta(a)}{C_\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}.}
\tag{5.11}
\]

It is strictly increasing.  Indeed, from the original expectation,

\[
-aF_\beta'(a)
=\beta\,\mathbb E\left[
\frac{a/S}{S'+a/S}(S'+a/S)^{-\beta}
\right]
<\beta F_\beta(a),
\tag{5.12}
\]

hence

\[
0<-a\frac{q_\beta'(a)}{q_\beta(a)}<\beta.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Differentiating (5.11),

\[
\boxed{
r'(a)=
\left(\frac{q_\beta(a)}{C_\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}
\left(1+\frac a\beta\frac{q_\beta'(a)}{q_\beta(a)}\right)>0.}
\tag{5.14}
\]

Also `r(a)->0` as `a downarrow 0`; the already proved large-`a` law `r(a)^beta=beta log a+O(1)` gives `r(a)->infinity`.  Therefore `a -> r(a)` is a global increasing bijection.

Since `M'(r)=pi g_alpha(sqrt(r))`, (3.2) yields

\[
\boxed{
g_\alpha(\sqrt{r(a)})
=-\frac{q_\beta'(a)}{\pi r'(a)}.}
\tag{5.15}
\]

Equations (5.8), (5.10), (5.11), and (5.14) therefore give a terminating validated algorithm: bracket `a` by dyadic search, shrink by interval bisection, increase contour length/validated quadrature precision whenever an interval is too wide, then evaluate (5.15).  For every fixed `0<alpha<2`, `rho>0`, and `epsilon>0`, this returns rigorous intervals of width at most `epsilon` for `a(rho^2)`, the CDF tail, and the density.

## 6. Method B23 — do what the proposer did not expect: prove the obstruction is intrinsic

The preceding rational-index run produced finite Meijer-G formulas and finite differential equations.  The natural question was whether failure at irrational `beta` was merely an algebraic inconvenience.

### 6.1 Rational beta is holonomic

Let `beta=p/q` in lowest terms.  The Mellin transform (5.4) satisfies the exact finite shift law

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\widehat q_\beta(s+p)}{\widehat q_\beta(s)}
=(-1)^p(\beta c_\beta)^{2q}
\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(s+j)^2}
{\prod_{k=0}^{q-1}(s+\beta k)^2}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

A rational Mellin shift is equivalent under Mellin inversion to a finite linear differential equation with polynomial coefficients in `a`; this is the finite ODE constructed explicitly in the preceding run.  Therefore `q_beta` is D-finite for rational beta.

### 6.2 Irrational beta cannot be D-finite

Suppose `beta` is irrational and, toward contradiction, `q_beta` satisfies a nonzero finite linear differential equation with polynomial coefficients on the positive axis.  Since `q_beta` is `C^infinity` at zero, its formal jet from (4.6) must satisfy the associated formal differential equation.  Consequently its coefficient sequence `(a_n)` is P-recursive: for some polynomials `P_0,...,P_m`, not all zero,

\[
\sum_{j=0}^mP_j(n)a_{n+j}=0
\tag{6.2}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.

Let `d_j=deg P_j` for every nonzero `P_j` and let `p_j` be its leading coefficient.  Equation (4.7) iterated over each fixed `j` gives

\[
\frac{a_{n+j}}{a_n}
=(-A_\beta)^j n^{j\sigma_\beta}(1+o(1)),
\quad
A_\beta=(\beta c_\beta)^{-2/\beta},
\quad
\sigma_\beta=\frac2\beta-2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

After dividing (6.2) by `a_n`, its `j`th summand has asymptotic order

\[
p_j(-A_\beta)^j n^{d_j+j\sigma_\beta}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For a finite sum of such terms to vanish, the maximal exponent among

\[
E_j=d_j+j\sigma_\beta
\]

must occur for at least two distinct indices.  If `i!=j` tie, then

\[
\sigma_\beta=\frac{d_i-d_j}{j-i}\in\mathbb Q.
\tag{6.5}
\]

But `sigma_beta=2/beta-2` is irrational whenever beta is irrational.  Thus the maximum is unique and its nonzero leading coefficient cannot cancel.  Contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
q_\beta(a)\text{ is D-finite in }a
\iff
\beta\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

A finite Meijer-G function is D-finite, and this class remains D-finite under algebraic prefactors and algebraic pullbacks.  Hence for irrational beta,

\[
\boxed{
q_\beta\text{ cannot be represented by a finite Meijer-G expression
with algebraic prefactor and algebraic change of variable.}
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This theorem explains the exact rational/irrational divide seen in the previous Meijer-G construction.  The rational collapse is not merely a failure of imagination: irrational Gamma slopes create a genuinely non-holonomic auxiliary transform.  The natural arbitrary-index representation is therefore Mellin--Barnes/Fox-H-type rather than a finite Meijer-G one.

The conclusion is deliberately narrow.  It does **not** prove that every conceivable notion of a symbolic closed form for the final physical density is impossible.

**B23 outcome:** a new exact structural theorem.  Rational beta is precisely the holonomic side; irrational beta is provably non-holonomic by a dominant-balance obstruction in the exact boundary coefficients.
