## 4. B5 — add beta and differentiate: all-radius analytic index dependence

The new idea is to treat the stability index itself as the deformation parameter, rather than taking another spatial coefficient.  Fix a compact `J=[beta_-,beta_+]` inside `(0,1)` and a Mellin line `Re s=sigma` with `0<sigma<beta_-`. Thus `q_beta(a)` and `H_beta(a)` are jointly real analytic in `(beta,a)` on `(0,1)x(0,infinity)`.

The exact first derivative of the Mellin kernel is

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\beta\log\widehat q_\beta(s)
=\frac{2s}{\beta^2}
\left[
\psi(1-s/\beta)-\log\Gamma(1-\beta)-\beta\psi(1-\beta)
\right].
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\beta H_\beta(a)
=H_\beta(a)[\log a+\psi(1-\beta)-\psi(1+\beta)]
+\frac{a^\beta}{C_\beta}\partial_\beta q_\beta(a).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because `H_beta'(a)>0`, the analytic implicit-function theorem gives joint real analyticity of the inverse

\[
A_\beta(x)=H_\beta^{-1}(x)

and the exact sensitivity

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\beta A_\beta(x)

\tag{4.3}

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\beta a_\beta(\rho)

## 5. B17 — exact Fox-H class and a sharp Thorin obstruction

Matching the Mellin kernels to the standard Fox-H definition gives exact named-function formulas:

\[
\boxed{
q_\beta(a)=
H_{2,2}^{1,2}\!\left[
\frac{a}{c_\beta^{2/\beta}}
\,\middle|\,
\begin{matrix}
(0,1/\beta),(0,1/\beta)\\
(0,1),(0,1)
\end{matrix}
\right],
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_\beta(t)
=\frac{c_\beta^{2(\beta-1)/\beta}}{C_\beta}
H_{3,3}^{1,2}\!\left[
\frac{t}{c_\beta^{2/\beta}}
\,\middle|\,
\begin{matrix}
(1-1/\beta,1/\beta),(1-1/\beta,1/\beta),(0,1)\\
(\beta-1,1),(\beta-1,1),(0,1)
\end{matrix}
\right].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For rational beta the slopes are commensurate, so Gauss multiplication reduces these to finite Meijer-G expressions; this mechanism has prior-art analogues in stable-law work and is not itself claimed novel.

The stronger new point is a class obstruction.  Put

\[
h_\beta(t)=t\sigma_\beta(t).
\]

In the standard Thorin-Bernstein characterization, a CBF written as `H(z)=z int sigma(t)/(z+t) dt` belongs to TBF only when a version of `w(t)=t sigma(t)` is nondecreasing (equivalently `H'` is Stieltjes).

From the Mellin transform, the pole at `s=1` gives

\[
\sigma_\beta(t)=\frac\beta t+\cdots.
\]

For `beta != 1/2`, the next double pole at `s=1+beta` has coefficient

\[
\boxed{
K_\beta
=-\frac{\beta\Gamma(1-\beta)^2\Gamma(2\beta)}
{\Gamma(1-2\beta)\Gamma(\beta)^2},
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_\beta(t)
=\frac\beta t+K_\beta t^{-1-\beta}\log t+O(t^{-1-\beta}).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For `beta>1/2`, `Gamma(1-2 beta)<0`, hence `K_beta>0`, and therefore

\[
h_\beta'(t)
=K_\beta t^{-\beta-1}(1-\beta\log t)+O(t^{-\beta-1})<0
\]

for all sufficiently large `t`.  This violates the Thorin monotonicity condition.  Thus

\[
\boxed{
1/2<\beta<1
\Longrightarrow
H_\beta\in\mathrm{CBF}\setminus\mathrm{TBF}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equivalently, for every `1<alpha<2`, the BCC physical radius-power map is complete Bernstein but not Thorin-Bernstein.  At and below `beta=1/2` this asymptotic sign argument does not prove positive Thorin membership, and none is claimed.

**B17 outcome: PROVED exact all-index Fox-H representations and a strict CBF/TBF separation throughout the upper half `1<alpha<2`.**

## 6. B28 — rational indices are exactly the holonomic ones for the canonical density

For rational `beta=p/q`, (5.2) reduces to a finite Meijer-G function, hence `sigma_beta` is D-finite.

For irrational beta, suppose `sigma_beta` were D-finite.  A polynomial-coefficient ODE, after Mellin transform and integration by parts, produces a nontrivial finite integer-shift recurrence

\[
\sum_{k=k_-}^{k_+}R_k(s)\widehat\sigma_\beta(s+k)=0,
\tag{6.1}
\]

with rational `R_k`, because

\[
\mathcal M[t^m f^{(j)}](s)
=(-1)^j(s+m-j)_j\widehat f(s+m-j).
\tag{6.2}
\]

But for irrational beta, (3.5) has an uncancelled double pole at every

\[
\boxed{s_n=1+n\beta,\qquad n=1,2,\ldots.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Choose a nonzero coefficient `R_k0` and a large `n` avoiding its finitely many zeros/poles.  The corresponding term in (6.1) has a double pole at `s=1+n beta-k0`.  Another shifted term could share it only if

\[
(m-n)\beta=k_1-k_0\in\mathbb Z,
\]

which irrationality forbids unless `m=n` and `k_1=k_0`.  Hence that pole cannot cancel, contradicting (6.1).  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_\beta\text{ is D-finite}
\iff \beta\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Together with the previous q-beta dichotomy, the transform now has a precise phase diagram: rational indices form a dense measure-zero exceptional set with finite Meijer-G/linear-ODE data and differential-algebraic local inverse; irrational indices form the full-measure typical set with genuinely nonholonomic Fox-H/Mellin-Barnes data.  B5 proves that the laws and inverse branches nevertheless vary smoothly through this representation-class transition.  I do **not** claim that the irrational inverse itself is non-D-algebraic; that remains unproved.

**B28 outcome: PROVED D-finiteness of the canonical deterministic CBF density exactly at rational indices.**
