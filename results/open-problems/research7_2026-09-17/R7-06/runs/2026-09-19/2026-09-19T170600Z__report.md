# Method B6 — deform to the constant row-sum/design point

The prior report proposed conditioning on exact or near-exact row-sum types on the two-eigenvalue core. In fact diagonal switching collapses that apparent type space completely.

Let

\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{n\times n},\qquad H^2=nI.
\]

Fix coordinate 1, put `d=H_{11}`, and define a diagonal sign matrix

\[
D_{11}=1,\qquad D_{ii}=dH_{1i}\quad(i>1).
\]

Then

\[
\widetilde H=dDHD
\]

is symmetric, satisfies `\widetilde H^2=nI`, and has first row and column all `+1`, since for `i>1`

\[
\widetilde H_{1i}=dH_{1i}(dH_{1i})=1,
\quad
\widetilde H_{11}=dH_{11}=1.
\]

Write

\[
\widetilde H=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\mathbf1^T\\
\mathbf1&B
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Multiplying blocks in `\widetilde H^2=nI` gives exactly

\[
\boxed{B\mathbf1=-\mathbf1,\qquad B^2=nI-J.}
\tag{1}
\]

Set

\[
G=\frac{J+B}{2}\in\{0,1\}^{v\times v},\qquad v=n-1.
\]

Then from (1)

\[
\boxed{G\mathbf1=\frac{n-2}{2}\mathbf1}
\tag{2}
\]

and, using `J^2=vJ`, `JB=BJ=-J`,

\[
4G^2=J^2+JB+BJ+B^2=nI+(n-4)J,
\]
so

\[
\boxed{G^2=\frac n4I+\frac{n-4}{4}J.}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus the normalized core is precisely a symmetric Hadamard 2-design incidence matrix with parameters

\[
(v,k,\lambda)=
\left(n-1,\frac{n-2}{2},\frac{n-4}{4}\right).
\]

Conversely (2)-(3) imply that `B=2G-J` satisfies (1), hence recover the normalized symmetric Hadamard matrix. Therefore this is an equivalence, not merely a necessary condition.

For integral spectrum on the two-eigenvalue branch, `\sqrt n` must be integral. Writing the nontrivial Hadamard square order as

\[
n=4t^2,
\]
we get the exact fixed design system

\[
\boxed{
v=4t^2-1,\quad
G\mathbf1=(2t^2-1)\mathbf1,\quad
G^2=t^2I+(t^2-1)J.
}
\tag{4}
\]

**B6 outcome.** The proposed arbitrary row-sum type is not a useful remaining degree of freedom on the normalized symmetric-Hadamard branch: diagonal switching sends every orbit to this one constant design type. The next rigidity must therefore come from the exact intersection equation (4), not from classifying many row-sum vectors.

# Method B24 — identify the extremal finite-field fiber

Reduce (4) modulo two. Since `v` and `2t^2-1` are odd,

\[
G\mathbf1=\mathbf1\pmod2.
\]

If `t` is odd, `G^2=I` over `\mathbb F_2`; define `N=G+I`. If `t` is even, `G^2=J`; define `N=G+J`. In the even case `GJ=JG=J` and `J^2=J` because `v` is odd. In both parity cases we obtain

\[
\boxed{N=N^T,\qquad N^2=0,\qquad N\mathbf1=0.}
\tag{5}
\]

For fixed parity of `t`, `G\mapsto N` is affine and injective. Therefore every normalized design from (4) lies in

\[
\mathcal N_v=
\{N\in\operatorname{Sym}_v(\mathbb F_2):N^2=0,\ N\mathbf1=0\}.
\tag{6}
\]

The extremal object can be described exactly. Put `v=2m+1`, `V=\mathbb F_2^v`, `e=\mathbf1`, and `W=e^\perp`. Since `e\cdot e=v=1` in `\mathbb F_2`, we have

\[
V=\langle e\rangle\perp W.
\]

For `x\in W`, `x\cdot x=e\cdot x=0`, and `W` is nondegenerate because `W^\perp=\langle e\rangle` but `e\notin W`. Hence `W` is a `2m`-dimensional symplectic space.

For `N\in\mathcal N_v`, let `U=\operatorname{im}N`. Symmetry and `Ne=0` imply `U\subset W` and

\[
\ker N=U^\perp.
\]

The square-zero identity says `U\subseteq\ker N`; therefore `U` is totally isotropic and

\[
\operatorname{rank}N\le m.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the largest possible rank is `m`, and its image is a Lagrangian subspace of `W`. This is the exact B24 extremal finite-field fiber.

# Method B25 — double count by isotropic image and internal symmetric form

Fix an `r`-dimensional totally isotropic `U\subset W` and choose a basis matrix `X\in\mathbb F_2^{v\times r}` with column space `U`. Then

\[
X^TX=0,\qquad X^Te=0.
\]

I claim that square-zero symmetric `N` with image exactly `U` and `Ne=0` are in bijection with invertible symmetric `r\times r` matrices `S` through

\[
\boxed{N=XSX^T.}
\tag{8}
\]

Proof: symmetry implies a map with image `U` annihilates `U^\perp=\ker X^T`, so it factors uniquely through the surjective map `X^T:V\to\mathbb F_2^r`; injectivity of `X` gives a unique `S`. Transposing forces `S=S^T`, and exact rank `r` forces `S` invertible. Conversely, symmetric invertible `S` gives

\[
N^2=XS(X^TX)SX^T=0,
\qquad
Ne=XSX^Te=0,
\]
and image `U`.

Let `h_r` be the number of invertible symmetric `r\times r` binary matrices. Let `I(m,r)` be the number of `r`-dimensional totally isotropic subspaces in a `2m`-dimensional symplectic binary space. Then

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal N_{2m+1}|=\sum_{r=0}^m I(m,r)h_r.}
\tag{9}
\]

The isotropic-subspace count follows directly by choosing an ordered isotropic basis. After `i` vectors, the next vector has `2^{2m-i}-2^i` choices in the current orthogonal complement outside the current span. Dividing by `|GL_r(2)|` gives

\[
\boxed{
I(m,r)=
\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}
\frac{2^{2m-2i}-1}{2^{r-i}-1}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Hence uniformly for `r\le m`,

\[
\log_2 I(m,r)
=2mr-\frac32r^2+\frac12r+O(1).
\tag{11}
\]

The trivial bound `h_r\le2^{r(r+1)/2}` inserted into (9)-(11) yields

\[
I(m,r)h_r
\le
2^{r(2m+1-r)+O(1)}.
\tag{12}
\]

The exponent `r(v-r)` is increasing on `0\le r\le m`, so its maximum is attained at `r=m` and equals

\[
m(m+1)=\frac{v^2-1}{4}.
\]

Therefore

\[
|\mathcal N_v|
\le
2^{(v^2-1)/4+O(\log v)}.
\tag{13}
\]

This upper exponent is sharp. For `r=m`, the isotropic spaces are Lagrangians and

\[
I(m,m)=\prod_{j=1}^m(2^j+1)
=2^{m(m+1)/2+O(1)}.
\tag{14}
\]

There are at least `2^{m(m-1)/2-O(m)}` invertible symmetric `m\times m` matrices: for even `m`, take all nondegenerate alternating forms; for odd `m`, take `1\oplus A` with `A` nondegenerate alternating on `m-1` coordinates. The standard `GL/Sp` orbit count gives that exponent. Consequently the `r=m` layer alone gives

\[
|\mathcal N_v|
\ge
2^{(v^2-1)/4-O(v)}.
\tag{15}
\]

Combining (13) and (15),

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal N_v|=2^{v^2/4+O(v)}.}
\tag{16}
\]

Using the classical exact count of nonsingular symmetric binary matrices upgrades (16) to the sharper constant-factor scale

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal N_{2m+1}|=\Theta(2^{m(m+1)})
=\Theta(2^{(v^2-1)/4}).}
\tag{17}
\]

I do not claim (17) as new finite-field enumeration; Brouwer--Gow--Sheekey provide strong prior art for this nilpotent/self-adjoint counting framework. The F0011-relevant conclusion is instead that the **first modular relaxation itself genuinely contains quadratic entropy with exponent 1/4**.
