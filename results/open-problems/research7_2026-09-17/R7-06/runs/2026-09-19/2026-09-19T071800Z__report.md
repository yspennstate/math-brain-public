## 4. B15 — the beta-th radius map is complete Bernstein

### Idea before calculation

Complexifying `R_beta` directly introduces an awkward `1/beta` power. The natural single-valued object is instead

\[
\boxed{
H_\beta(a)=R_\beta(a)^\beta
=\frac{a^\beta q_\beta(a)}{C_\beta}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The physical inverse is equivalent to `H_beta(a)=r^beta`.

### Theorem proved

For every `0<beta<1`, `H_beta` is a nonconstant **complete Bernstein function**. Consequently its canonical holomorphic extension to

\[
\Omega=\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]
\]

strictly maps the upper half-plane into the upper half-plane and the lower half-plane into the lower half-plane.

### Complete derivation

The earlier fixed-point elimination used

\[
F_\beta(a)=\mathbb E(S'+a/S)^{-\beta},
\qquad q_\beta(a)=C_\beta F_\beta(a).
\]

Since

\[
S'+a/S=(a+SS')/S,
\]

we have

\[
H_\beta(a)=a^\beta F_\beta(a)
=\mathbb E\left[S^\beta
\left(\frac a{a+SS'}\right)^\beta\right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define the positive locally finite measure

\[
\nu_\beta(B)
=\mathbb E\left[S^\beta\mathbf 1_{\{SS'\in B\}}\right].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Local finiteness follows from

\[
F_\beta(1)=\int_0^\infty(1+t)^{-\beta}\nu_\beta(dt)<\infty:
\]

for a compact `[u,v] subset (0,infinity)`,

\[
\nu_\beta([u,v])
\le(1+v)^\beta F_\beta(1)<\infty.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
H_\beta(a)=\int_0^\infty
\left(\frac a{a+t}\right)^\beta\nu_\beta(dt).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

For every fixed `t>0`,

\[
\phi_t(z)=\left(\frac z{z+t}\right)^\beta
\]

is complete Bernstein. Indeed, if `Im z>0`,

\[
\Im\frac z{z+t}=\frac{t\Im z}{|z+t|^2}>0,
\]

and the principal beta-th power keeps its argument in `(0,beta*pi) subset (0,pi)`. It is also positive on the positive axis. The Pick characterization therefore applies.

Truncate `nu_beta` to `[1/n,n]`. Each finite positive integral of the `phi_t` is complete Bernstein. These truncations increase pointwise to the finite function `H_beta` on `(0,infinity)`, and the complete-Bernstein class is closed under such pointwise limits. Thus `H_beta` is complete Bernstein. It is nonconstant because `R_beta` is strictly increasing. By the open mapping theorem, the weak Pick inequality becomes strict in each open half-plane.

### Global root theorem

For every `x>0`,

\[
\boxed{H_\beta(z)=x}
\tag{4.6}
\]

has exactly one solution in the **entire slit plane** `Omega`, and it is positive real.

A nonreal point in the upper/lower half-plane cannot map to positive real because of the strict Pick sign. On `(0,infinity)`, `H_beta` is strictly increasing, `H_beta(0+)=0`, and `H_beta(a)->infinity`. The last fact follows either from the previously proved surjectivity of `R_beta`, or directly by restricting (4.2) to `{S<=a,S'<=1}` and using the logarithmic growth of the truncated beta-moment `E[S^beta;S<=a]`.

## 5. B8 — finite models expose the heavy-tail topology

The proposed finite-discretization attack produced both a counterexample to the naive route and a topology-preserving repair.

### 5.1 Naive discretization of `T` necessarily fails globally

Replace the law of `T` by positive atoms:

\[
q_N(a)=\sum_{j=1}^Np_j\frac{t_j}{a+t_j},
\qquad \sum_jp_j=1.
\]

Then the imitated radius power is

\[
H_N^{\rm naive}(a)=\frac{a^\beta q_N(a)}{C_\beta}.
\]

Because the finite atomic law has finite first moment,

\[
H_N^{\rm naive}(a)
\sim\frac{\sum_jp_jt_j}{C_\beta}a^{\beta-1}\to0
\qquad(a\to\infty),
\]

while `H_N^naive(0+)=0` and it is positive in between. Hence it has a positive interior maximum and at least one positive critical point; for values below that maximum it generically has multiple positive preimages. Finite-atom discretization of `T` therefore manufactures the branch pathology we are trying to remove.

This identifies the mechanism: finite atoms erase the stable infinite-tail structure that makes the true `H_beta` grow like a logarithm and remain globally invertible.

### 5.2 Correct discretization of the generalized Stieltjes measure

Discretize `nu_beta` in (4.5), not the probability law of `T`. For

\[
\nu_N=\sum_{j=1}^Nw_j\delta_{t_j},\qquad w_j,t_j>0,
\]

set

\[
H_N(a)=\sum_{j=1}^Nw_j\left(\frac a{a+t_j}\right)^\beta.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every `H_N` is complete Bernstein and strictly increasing from `0` to `W_N=sum_jw_j`. For every `0<x<W_N`, `H_N(z)=x` has exactly one slit-plane root, positive real.

The measure truncations

\[
\widetilde H_n(a)
=\int_{1/n}^{n}
\left(\frac a{a+t}\right)^\beta\nu_\beta(dt)
\]

increase pointwise to `H_beta(a)`. Their total masses tend to infinity because, by monotone convergence,

\[
\nu_\beta((0,\infty))
=\lim_{a\to\infty}H_\beta(a)=\infty.
\]

Fix `x>0`, let `a_*=H_beta^{-1}(x)`, and let `a_n` solve `\widetilde H_n(a_n)=x` once the truncated mass exceeds `x`. Then

\[
\boxed{a_n\downarrow a_* .}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The proof is order-theoretic: `\widetilde H_n<=H_beta` gives `a_n>=a_*`; the functions increase with `n`, so the roots decrease; and for any `epsilon>0`, `H_beta(a_*+epsilon)>x`, hence eventually `\widetilde H_n(a_*+epsilon)>x` and `a_n<a_*+epsilon`.

Positive atomic quadrature of the finite truncations retains the complete-Bernstein property. This gives a finite approximation scheme that preserves, rather than destroys, the true branch topology.

## 6. B25 — argument-principle first moment gives a fixed-contour inverse

The global root theorem allows the remaining inverse to be written as a canonical contour transform.

Define

\[
\chi(w)=\left(\frac{1+w}{1-w}\right)^2,
\qquad |w|<1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This maps the unit disk conformally onto `Omega`. For `x>0`, set

\[
G_{\beta,x}(w)=H_\beta(\chi(w))-x.
\]

The slit-plane root theorem says that `G` has exactly one zero in the disk,

\[
w_*=\chi^{-1}(H_\beta^{-1}(x))
=\frac{\sqrt{H_\beta^{-1}(x)}-1}
{\sqrt{H_\beta^{-1}(x)}+1}
\in(-1,1),
\]

and it is simple.

Use the **fixed** circle sequence `s_n=1-1/n`. For every sufficiently large `n`, the circle encloses `w_*`; at most one circle can pass exactly through it. The argument principle gives

\[
\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|w|=s_n}
 w\frac{G_{\beta,x}'(w)}{G_{\beta,x}(w)}\,dw
=w_*.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\omega_\beta(x)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|w|=1-1/n}
 w\,
\frac{H_\beta'(\chi(w))\chi'(w)}
{H_\beta(\chi(w))-x}
\,dw
=w_*.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

If one circle meets the zero, omit that one term; it has no effect on the limit. Crucially, the contour family is specified independently of the unknown root.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
H_\beta^{-1}(x)
=\chi(\omega_\beta(x))
=\left(\frac{1+\omega_\beta(x)}{1-\omega_\beta(x)}\right)^2.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The forward function in the contour is itself explicit. From (3.4), for any `0<sigma<beta`,

\[
\boxed{
H_\beta(z)
=\frac{z^\beta}{C_\beta}
\frac1{2\pi i}
\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}
 c_\beta^{2s/\beta}
\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s/\beta)^2}{\Gamma(1-s)}
 z^{-s}\,ds.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Stirling decay is `exp(-pi|Im s|/beta)` up to polynomial factors, so the principal-branch integral extends locally uniformly over the slit plane because `|arg z|<pi<pi/beta`.

Equations (6.2)–(6.4) remove the positive root/fixed-point solve. This is not merely “use a root finder”: the complete-Bernstein theorem proves the global one-root count, and the conformal disk supplies a contour exhaustion fixed without knowing the root.

## 7. Direct physical CDF and density

Let `rho>0` be physical radius, set

\[
r=\rho^2,\qquad x=r^\beta=\rho^\alpha,
\]

and define

\[
w(\rho)=\omega_\beta(\rho^\alpha),
\qquad
a(\rho)=\chi(w(\rho)).
\]

Then `H_beta(a)=r^beta`. From `H_beta=a^beta q_beta/C_beta`,

\[
q_\beta(a)=C_\beta(r/a)^\beta.
\]

Using the CDF identity (3.3), the exact physical radial tail becomes

\[
\boxed{
1-\mu_\alpha\{|z|\le\rho\}
=C_\beta
\left[
\rho\frac{1-w(\rho)}{1+w(\rho)}
\right]^\alpha.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

There is no self-consistent unknown or hidden root solve: `w(rho)` is the fixed-contour transform (6.2).

For density, write `Q_beta(r)=C_beta r^beta a(r)^(-beta)`. Differentiate `H_beta(a(r))=r^beta`:

\[
a'(r)=\frac{\beta r^{\beta-1}}{H_\beta'(a)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{Q_\beta'(r)}{Q_\beta(r)}
=\frac\beta r-
\frac{\beta^2r^{\beta-1}}{aH_\beta'(a)}.
\]

Since `M_beta'(r)=pi g_{2beta}(sqrt r)=-Q_beta'(r)`, the planar density is

\[
\boxed{
 g_\alpha(\rho)
=\frac{\beta\,[1-\mu_\alpha\{|z|\le\rho\}]}{\pi\rho^2}
\left[
\frac{\beta\rho^\alpha}
{a(\rho)H_\beta'(a(\rho))}-1
\right],
\qquad \rho>0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

At `rho=0`, the existing exact origin formula applies. Thus the new contour inversion supplies an all-positive-radius transform formula and joins continuously to the already known origin value.
