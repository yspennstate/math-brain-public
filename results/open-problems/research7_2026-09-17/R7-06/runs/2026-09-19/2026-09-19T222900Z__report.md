## 4. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: complete rigidity of simplicial correlation-face Gram roots

Define the sign-correlation polytope

\[
\mathcal C_r^{\pm}
=\operatorname{conv}\{xx^T:x\in\{\pm1\}^r\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the classical Bernoulli correlation/cut-polytope object in sign coordinates; the object itself is not claimed novel.

### Theorem 4.1 — simplicial-face Gram-root classification

Let

\[
C=[c_1\ \cdots\ c_r]\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times r}
\]

be invertible and put

\[
K=CC^T,
\qquad q=K/r=\frac1r\sum_{j=1}^r c_jc_j^T.
\]

Assume the `r` correlation vertices

\[
v_j=c_jc_j^T
\]

are exactly the vertices of a simplicial face `F` of `\mathcal C_r^{\pm}`. Then every sign matrix `C'` with

\[
C'C'^T=K
\]

is obtained only by column permutation and independent column sign changes:

\[
\boxed{C'=C\Pi\Delta}.\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence the sign-Gram-root fiber has exactly

\[
\boxed{2^r r!}\tag{4.3}
\]

members.

### Proof

The point

\[
q=\frac1r(v_1+\cdots+v_r)
\]

has strictly positive barycentric coordinates relative to all vertices of the simplex `F`, so `q` lies in `relint(F)`.

For another root `C'=[c'_1\cdots c'_r]`,

\[
q=\frac1r\sum_j c'_jc_j'^T
\]

is a convex representation of the same point by vertices of `\mathcal C_r^{\pm}`. Since every polytope face is exposed, take a supporting affine functional `ell` with value `alpha` on `F` and strictly smaller value on every vertex outside `F`. Averaging gives

\[
\alpha=\ell(q)=\frac1r\sum_j\ell(c'_jc_j'^T).
\]

Every summand is at most `alpha`, hence every one equals `alpha`; all vertices used by `C'` lie in `F`.

Because `F` is a simplex, barycentric coordinates are unique. The coefficient of each `v_i` in the original representation is `1/r`. In the new equal-weight representation it is the multiplicity of `v_i` divided by `r`, so each `v_i` must occur exactly once. Finally

\[
xx^T=yy^T,\qquad x,y\in\{\pm1\}^r
\]

implies `y=±x`. Thus the columns of `C'` are exactly the columns of `C`, permuted and independently sign-flipped, proving (4.2). Conversely all such signed permutations preserve `CC^T`. Since invertibility forbids two columns of `C` from agreeing up to sign, the action is free and the exact count is `2^r r!`. ∎

### Finite face certificate

A candidate root satisfies the theorem if one finds symmetric `A` and `alpha` with

\[
c_j^TAc_j=\alpha\quad(1\le j\le r),
\]

\[
x^TAx<\alpha
\quad\text{for every other sign class }\{\pm x\},
\tag{4.4}
\]

and the matrices `c_jc_j^T` are affinely independent. Then `Z\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(AZ)` exposes exactly these vertices and affine independence makes the face simplicial. This is an exact finite certificate, not a numerical heuristic.

### Corollary 4.2 — fixed-half completion entropy is subquadratic on this regime

Fix a symmetric sign `P`. If

\[
K_P=2rI-P^2>0
\]

and one sign Gram root of `K_P` satisfies Theorem 4.1, then the total number of sign pairs `(C,D)` completing this fixed `P` is at most

\[
\boxed{2^r r!=2^{O(r\log r)}}.\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed Theorem 4.1 lists every possible `C`, and (3.5) uniquely fixes `D`; failures of the sign or symmetry condition only reduce the count.

**B3 outcome:** any quadratic completion entropy for a *fixed* half `P` must be carried by Gram points `K_P/r` lying in non-simplicial or otherwise multiply represented regions of the correlation polytope. ## 5. B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: relabeling does not explain the quadratic orthogonal entropy

### Theorem 5.1 — exact orthogonal-group size

For `r=2k`,

\[
\boxed{
|O_I(2k,2)|
=2^{2k-1}|Sp(2k-2,2)|
=2^{2k-1+(k-1)^2}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(2^{2i}-1).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log_2|O_I(r,2)|=\frac{r^2}{2}-\frac r2+O(1).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Let `B(x,y)=x^Ty` on `V=F_2^{2k}` and let `e` be the all-ones vector. In characteristic two,

\[
B(x,x)=\sum_i x_i=B(x,e).
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `A^TA=I`, then for all `x`,

\[
B(Ax,e)=B(Ax,Ax)=B(x,x)=B(x,e)=B(Ax,Ae),
\]

so nondegeneracy gives `Ae=e`.

Set `W=e^perp`. Because `2k` is even, `e in W`; (5.3) shows that the restriction of `B` to `W` is alternating, with radical exactly `<e>`. Hence

\[
\overline W=W/\langle e\rangle
\]

is a symplectic space of dimension `2k-2`, giving a homomorphism

\[
\rho:O_I(2k,2)\to Sp(\overline W).
\]

Choose a nondegenerate alternating complement `U` of `<e>` in `W` and choose `f` with `B(e,f)=1` and `B(f,U)=0`. Every symplectic isometry of `U` extends by fixing `e,f`, so `rho` is onto.

For `A` in the kernel,

\[
Au=u+\phi(u)e\qquad(u\in U)
\]

for a unique `phi in U*`, while

\[
Af=f+v+\alpha e.
\]

The condition `B(Af,Au)=0` reads

\[
B(v,u)+\phi(u)=0
\]

for all `u`. Nondegeneracy on `U` determines `v` uniquely from `phi`, while `alpha` is free. Hence

\[
|\ker\rho|=2^{2k-2}\cdot2=2^{2k-1}.
\]

Finally, counting ordered symplectic bases recursively gives

\[
|Sp(2s,2)|=(2^{2s}-1)2^{2s-1}|Sp(2s-2,2)|
=2^{s^2}\prod_{i=1}^s(2^{2i}-1).
\]

Take `s=k-1` to obtain (5.1), and expand the product to obtain (5.2). ∎

## 6. B22 — READ THE FAILURES: singular cross blocks are endpoint spectral modes plus an invertible core

The generic argument from the preceding run fails precisely when `C` is singular. The failure can be decomposed exactly.

### Theorem 6.1 — endpoint-mode decomposition

Let

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix}=H^T,
\qquad H^2=s^2I_{2r},\quad s>0.
\]

Put

\[
E=\ker C^T,
\qquad F=\ker C,
\qquad k=\operatorname{corank}C.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{E=E_s(P)\oplus E_{-s}(P)},\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{F=E_s(D)\oplus E_{-s}(D)}.\tag{6.2}
\]

Moreover `E^perp` and `F^perp` are invariant, the restriction

\[
C_0:C|_{F^\perp}:F^\perp\to E^\perp
\]

is invertible, and with `P_0=P|_{E^perp}`, `D_0=D|_{F^perp}`,

\[
\boxed{D_0=-C_0^{-1}P_0C_0},\tag{6.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{C_0C_0^T=s^2I-P_0^2>0}.\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

The block equations from `H^2=s^2I` are

\[
P^2+CC^T=s^2I,
\qquad PC+CD=0,
\qquad C^TC+D^2=s^2I.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus

\[
\ker C^T=\ker(CC^T)=\ker(s^2I-P^2).
\]

Since `P` is symmetric, the spectral theorem identifies this kernel exactly with its `+s` and `-s` eigenspaces, proving (6.1). The same argument proves (6.2).

Those endpoint spaces are invariant, and symmetry makes their orthogonal complements invariant. Also

\[
\operatorname{ran}C=(\ker C^T)^\perp=E^\perp.
\]

Restricting `C` to `F^perp` therefore gives an injective map between equal `(r-k)`-dimensional spaces and hence an isomorphism. Restriction of `PC+CD=0` gives (6.3); restriction of `CC^T=s^2I-P^2` gives (6.4), with strict positivity because all endpoint eigenspaces were removed. ∎

### Corollary 6.2 — spectral multiplicity imbalance forces cross-block corank

Let

\[
e_\pm=\dim E_{\pm s}(P),
\qquad f_\pm=\dim E_{\pm s}(D).
\]

The invertible cores have opposite trace by (6.3), so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}H
=s[(e_+-e_-)+(f_+-f_-)].
}\tag{6.6}
\]

Since each signed difference has absolute value at most `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{corank}C
\ge \frac{|\operatorname{tr}H|}{2s}.
}\tag{6.7}
\]

If the full matrix has `+s` multiplicity `a` and `-s` multiplicity `2r-a`, then

\[
\operatorname{tr}H=s(2a-2r),
\]

and hence every such half split satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{corank}C\ge |a-r|.
}\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus all trace/multiplicity imbalance is carried by endpoint-supported singular modes. In particular the smallest exceptional family, `corank C=1`, consists of one endpoint eigenline on each side plus an `(r-1)`-dimensional invertible-cross core.
