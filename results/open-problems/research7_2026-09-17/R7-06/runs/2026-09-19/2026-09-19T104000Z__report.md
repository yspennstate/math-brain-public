## Source and exact closure criterion

Primary source: Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, *Probability Surveys* 18 (2021), 179–200, DOI `10.1214/20-PS346`; original arXiv `2005.02797` submitted 2020-05-06. Locator: **Conjecture 9.4 (Integral spectrum), Section 9, PDF p. 15**.

Let `M_n^sym` be the real symmetric `n×n` matrix whose upper-triangular entries are independent Rademacher signs. The source conjecture is

`P(M_n^sym has all n eigenvalues in Z)=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}` as `n→∞`.

Full closure is that asymptotic statement. A finite classification or a `2^{-c n^{3/2}}` estimate is PARTIAL only.

## B2 — attack the negation first: a deterministic mod-2 obstruction

Write `χ_M(x)=det(xI-M)`. Since every sign is `1 mod 2`,

`M ≡ J_n (mod 2)`.

The all-ones matrix has

`χ_{J_n}(x)=x^{n-1}(x-n)`.

Hence over `F_2`,

`χ_M(x) ≡ x^n` for even `n`,

and

`χ_M(x) ≡ x^{n-1}(x+1)` for odd `n`.

Suppose the spectrum is integral, `χ_M(x)=∏(x-λ_i)` with every `λ_i∈Z`. Reducing that factorization modulo `2`, the multiplicity of `x+1` is the number of odd eigenvalues. Therefore:

- **even `n`: every integral eigenvalue must be even**;
- **odd `n`: exactly one integral eigenvalue is odd**.

If `c_k` is the `k`th elementary symmetric coefficient of the eigenvalues, this further gives `2^k|c_k` for even `n` and `2^{k-1}|c_k` for odd `n`.

There is also a useful general exact rejection principle: if the spectrum is integral then, for every prime `p`, `χ_M mod p` must split completely into linear factors over `F_p`. Thus one irreducible factor of degree greater than one modulo any prime certifies non-integrality. I have **not** claimed independence of these modular tests or an asymptotic exponent from them yet.

Outcome B2: **new exact arithmetic obstruction; source conjecture not resolved.**

## B14 — find the group acting: exact switching quotient

Let

`G_n={diag(d_1,...,d_n):d_i=±1}/{±I}`

act by `M↦DMD`. Because `D^{-1}=D`, this is similarity and preserves the spectrum.

If `DMD=M`, then for every `i≠j`, nonzero `M_ij` forces `d_i d_j=1`; hence all `d_i` are equal and `D=±I`. Thus the quotient action is free and every orbit has exactly `2^{n-1}` matrices.

Every orbit has a unique gauge with first-row off-diagonal entries all `+1`: take `d_1=1`, `d_j=M_{1j}`. Therefore the exact number of switching-normalized representatives is

`2^{n+C(n-1,2)}=2^{n(n+1)/2-(n-1)}`.

Let `I_n` be the number of these normalized representatives with integral spectrum. Then exactly

`P(M_n^sym integral)=I_n / 2^{n(n+1)/2-(n-1)}`.

Since the denominator exponent is `(n^2-n+2)/2=n^2/2+O(n)`, Vu's conjecture is equivalent to the deterministic entropy statement

`I_n=2^{o(n^2)}`.

This is a useful change of target: the hard upper bound is precisely subquadratic entropy of integral-spectrum switching classes.

As a lower check, all rank-one symmetric sign matrices are `σuu^T`, with `σ=±1` and `u∈{±1}^n` modulo `u↦-u`. There are exactly `2^n` of them, with spectrum `{σ n,0,...,0}`. They form two switching orbits and give

`P(integral)≥2^{-(n^2-n)/2}`,

which already matches the conjectured leading exponent from below.

Outcome B14: **source conjecture reformulated exactly as `I_n=2^{o(n^2)}`; no asymptotic upper bound yet.**
