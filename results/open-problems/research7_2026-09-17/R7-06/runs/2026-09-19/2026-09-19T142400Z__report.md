## 4. B12 — exact counterexample to a uniform small-branch theorem

### Idea before code

Try to make all spectral energy constraints collapse onto one low-complexity equation. The maximally degenerate principal block `B=J_k` has only two eigenspaces, so it is the natural adversary to the proposed simultaneous-energy anti-concentration statement.

### Theorem 4.1

Let `a>=1` be an integer and set

\[
k=a(a+1),\qquad n=k+1.
\]

For any balanced sign vector

\[
x\in\{\pm1\}^k,\qquad \sum_i x_i=0,
\]

define

\[
M_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&x^T\\x&J_k\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}(M_x)
=\{k,\ a+1,\ -a,\ 0^{(k-2)}\}.
}
\]

Hence, after the entire principal block `J_k`, the new diagonal sign, and the entire target spectrum are fixed, the number of valid next sign rows is

\[
\boxed{
\binom{k}{k/2}
=2^{k-\frac12\log_2 k+O(1)}.
}
\]

### Proof

Let `1` denote the all-ones vector in `R^k`. Balance gives `x perp 1`, and `||x||^2=k`.

Every vector `u` perpendicular to both `1` and `x` satisfies

\[
J_ku=0,\qquad x^Tu=0,
\]

so `(0,u)` is a zero eigenvector. This gives zero with multiplicity `k-2`.

Also `(0,1)` is an eigenvector with eigenvalue `k`, because `x^T1=0` and `J_k1=k1`.

The remaining invariant two-dimensional space is generated by the top coordinate and `(0,x/sqrt(k))`. On it,

\[
M_x\sim
\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt{k}\\\sqrt{k}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its characteristic polynomial is

\[
t^2-t-k
=t^2-t-a(a+1)
=(t-(a+1))(t+a).
\]

All eigenvalues are therefore integers and the displayed multiplicities total `k+1`.

There are exactly `binom(k,k/2)` balanced sign vectors. Stirling gives the entropy asymptotic. QED.

### Energy interpretation

For `B=J_k`,

\[
Q_k=\frac1kJ_k,
\qquad
Q_0=I-\frac1kJ_k.
\]

Every balanced `x` has exactly

\[
\|Q_kx\|^2=0,
\qquad
\|Q_0x\|^2=k.
\]

Thus all of the row-extension residue constraints from the previous run reduce to one balancing condition, leaving almost all `k` sign bits free.

### B12 outcome

**Exact negative result for the intended local lemma.** A uniform bound of the form

\[
\#\{x:\text{prescribed full spectral extension}\}=2^{o(k)}
\]

is false. This does not refute Vu 9.4; it says a proof cannot obtain its entropy saving independently at every exposed row.

## 5. B21 — conditional entropy can remain essentially maximal

\[

The B12 family gives a sharp obstruction. With `m=k=a(a+1)`, fixed `B_m=J_k`, fixed diagonal sign `1`, and fixed target spectrum from Theorem 4.1, choose the next row uniformly among all admissible rows. Then

\[
H(X_{m+1}\mid B_m,\lambda_{m+1})
=\log_2\binom{k}{k/2}

So the conditional entropy can be

This identifies precisely what the recursive proof would have to change. The needed estimate cannot be a worst-case local branching bound. ## 6. B29 — arbitrary square-order Hadamards embed into one symmetric integral spectral fiber

### Idea before code

The previous run had noticed that symmetric Hadamard matrices form a difficult boundary family. The row-entropy obstruction suggests something stronger: use a general Hadamard matrix as an entire cross block, so orthogonality supplies the projection-energy constraints automatically while symmetry is restored by placing its transpose in the opposite block.

### Theorem 6.1 — Hadamard-to-F0011 embedding

Let

\[
k=q^2
\]

be a perfect square, and let `H` be **any** real Hadamard matrix of order `k`:

\[
H\in\{\pm1\}^{k\times k},\qquad HH^T=kI_k.
\]

No symmetry assumption on `H` is made.

Normalize the first column to all `+1` by row sign changes, and write

\[
H=[1_k\mid X],\qquad X\in\{\pm1\}^{k\times(k-1)}.
\]

Then

\[
X^TX=kI_{k-1},
\qquad
X^T1_k=0,
\qquad
XX^T=kI_k-J_k.
\]

Define the symmetric sign matrix of order

\[
N=2k-1
\]

by

\[
\boxed{
M(H)=
\begin{pmatrix}
J_{k-1}&X^T\\
X&J_k
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}M(H)
=\{k^{(2)},(-1)^{(1)},q^{(k-2)},(-q)^{(k-2)}\}.
}
\]

Moreover the map from normalized `H` to `M(H)` is injective.

### Proof

Since `X^T1_k=0` and `rank(X)=k-1`, the image of `X` is exactly

\[
W=1_k^\perp.
\]

Put

\[
U=X/q.
\]

The identity `X^TX=kI` makes `U:R^{k-1}->W` an orthogonal isomorphism.

First, the bottom all-ones direction is an eigenvector of `M(H)` with eigenvalue `k`: its cross term is annihilated by `X^T`, and `J_k1_k=k1_k`.

Now identify `W` with a second copy of `R^{k-1}` through `U`. On the remaining space

\[
R^{k-1}\oplus W,
\]

`M(H)` becomes

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
J_{k-1}&qI\\qI&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Decompose `R^{k-1}` into the all-ones line and its orthogonal complement. On each of the `k-2` orthogonal directions the induced `2 x 2` block is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\q&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

so `+q` and `-q` each occur `k-2` times.

On the all-ones direction the block is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}k-1&q\\q&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose characteristic polynomial is

\[
t^2-(k-1)t-k=(t-k)(t+1).
\]

This contributes a second `k` and one `-1`. The total multiplicity is `2k-1`, and every eigenvalue is integral because `q` is an integer.

Finally the cross block of `M(H)` is exactly `X`, and the normalized Hadamard is `[1_k|X]`, so the map is injective. QED.

### Counting corollary

Let `mathcal H(k)` denote the number of labeled real Hadamard matrices of order `k`. Row-sign normalization of the first column has fibers of exactly `2^k`, so there are

\[
\frac{\mathcal H(k)}{2^k}
\]

normalized first-column Hadamards. All inject into the **same** integral spectral fiber at dimension `N=2k-1`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\#\{M=M^T\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N}:\operatorname{Spec}M=\lambda_k\}
\ge \frac{\mathcal H(k)}{2^k},
}
\]

with

\[
\lambda_k=\{k^{(2)},(-1)^{(1)},q^{(k-2)},(-q)^{(k-2)}\}.
\]

If Vu 9.4 holds, the total number of integral-spectrum symmetric sign matrices of order `N` is

\[
2^{N(N+1)/2}
2^{-(1/2+o(1))N^2}
=2^{o(N^2)}.
\]

Substituting `N=2k-1` and using the injection yields the rigorous one-way implication

\[
\boxed{
\text{Vu 9.4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal H(k)=2^{o(k^2)}
\text{ along every perfect-square Hadamard order }k.
}
\]

This strictly strengthens the previous R7-06 boundary observation, which involved only symmetric Hadamards: **every** real Hadamard matrix of square order now participates, even when `H` itself is not symmetric.

### Why this matters for amortization

The one-row B12 example could in principle have been a local curiosity. The Hadamard embedding shows that high-entropy spectral compatibility has a coherent block form. Both diagonal blocks of `M(H)` are fixed all-ones matrices; all variation is in the cross block `X`, yet the full spectrum remains fixed.

Thus an amortized B29 proof must control a family whose entropy is at least the entropy of arbitrary square-order Hadamard matrices. It cannot simply sum independent one-row anti-concentration savings without addressing the orthogonality structure that makes many rows jointly compatible.

This does not solve the source and does not show equivalence; it identifies a genuine hard core that the current route must survive.
