
applied to (2.2), with `x=beta y` in the two `Gamma(1-s/beta)` factors, gives the exact Levy-Khintchine representation


where

```text
m_beta=(2/beta)(log c_beta+gamma_E)-2gamma_E,
```

and

```text
nu_beta(dx)
 =1_{x>0} k_{beta,+}(x) dx/x
  +1_{x<0} k_-(|x|) dx/|x|,

k_{beta,+}(x)=2/(exp(beta x)-1)-1/(exp(x)-1),
k_-(x)=1/(exp(x)-1).                                   (3.3)
```

Near zero the canonical functions are `(2/beta-1)/x+O(1)` and `1/x+O(1)`, so the fully compensated integral is legitimate; at infinity they decay exponentially.

## 4. B13 — a monotone physical invariant for the entire `0<alpha<=1` range

Thomas Simon proves that a positive beta-stable variable is multiplicatively strongly unimodal iff `beta<=1/2`, and records the equivalent statement that `log S` is strongly unimodal.  By Ibragimov, that means the density of `log S` is log-concave.  The logistic term `log(E_1/E_2)` has density

```text
1/[4 cosh^2(x/2)],
```

which is log-concave.  Therefore, for `beta<=1/2`, the density of

```text
X_beta=log S+log S'+log(E_1/E_2)                       (4.1)
```

is log-concave by closure under convolution.

Define

```text
lambda_beta(a)=-a q_beta'(a)/q_beta(a).                (4.2)
```

By (2.1), `lambda_beta(e^x)` is exactly the hazard rate of `X_beta`.  The survival function of a log-concave density is log-concave, so its hazard is nondecreasing.  Hence for every `0<beta<=1/2`,

```text
lambda_beta(a) is nondecreasing,
lambda_beta(a)->0 as a downarrow 0,
lambda_beta(a)->beta as a->infinity.                   (4.3)
```

The right endpoint is also certified by the already proved auxiliary tail

```text
q_beta(a)=const*a^(-beta)[beta log a+O(1)].            (4.4)
```

Now define the physical squared-radius tail elasticity

```text
E_beta(r)=-r Q_beta'(r)/Q_beta(r).                     (4.5)
```

Differentiating the exact radius map gives

```text
d log R_beta/d log a=(beta-lambda_beta)/beta,
```

and therefore

```text
E_beta(R_beta(a))
 = beta lambda_beta(a)/(beta-lambda_beta(a)).          (4.6)
```

The right side is an increasing function of `lambda_beta`.  Consequently, for every

```text
0<alpha<=1,
```

`E_beta(r)` is nondecreasing from zero to infinity.  Equivalently,

```text
t -> log Q_beta(exp t)
```

is concave.

In ordinary physical radius, let

```text
Qbar_alpha(rho)=1-mu_alpha{|z|<=rho}=Q_beta(rho^2).
```

Since `-Qbar_alpha'(rho)=2 pi rho g_alpha(rho)`, the invariant is

```text
H_alpha(rho)
 =-rho Qbar_alpha'(rho)/Qbar_alpha(rho)
 =2 pi rho^2 g_alpha(rho)/Qbar_alpha(rho)
 =2 E_beta(rho^2),                                     (4.7)
```

so `H_alpha` is nondecreasing throughout `0<alpha<=1`.  Equivalently, the BCC physical tail is geometrically log-concave:

```text
Qbar_alpha(rho_1^theta rho_2^(1-theta))
 >=Qbar_alpha(rho_1)^theta Qbar_alpha(rho_2)^(1-theta).
```

This is a source-object theorem, not only a property of the auxiliary parameter.

**Exact route failure:** Simon's MSU theorem stops exactly at `beta=1/2`.  It proves that `log S` is not log-concave above that point, so this proof cannot extend directly to `1<alpha<2`.  It does **not** prove that the convolution (4.1) itself loses log-concavity there; the second log-stable term and logistic smoothing could in principle restore it.  Thus B13 yields a real restricted theorem but not all-index closure.

## 5. B27 — rational holonomic formulas provably become unboundedly complex near irrational beta

Earlier work proved that the auxiliary `q_beta(a)` is D-finite for rational beta and non-D-finite for irrational beta.  B27 tests whether the rational ODEs might nevertheless have one bounded-complexity form along rational approximants.

Define `h(beta)` as the least `N` for which there exists a nonzero polynomial-coefficient linear ODE

```text
Sum_{j=0}^m P_j(a) q_beta^(j)(a)=0                    (5.1)
```

with `m<=N` and `deg P_j<=N`; set `h(beta)=infinity` if there is none.  The earlier finite Mellin-shift equation at `beta=p/q` in lowest terms has order at most `2q` and polynomial degree at most `p`, hence

```text
h(p/q)<=2q.                                            (5.2)
```

The exact Mellin transform

```text
hat q_beta(s)
=c_beta^(2s/beta)
 Gamma(s)Gamma(1-s/beta)^2/Gamma(1-s),
 0<Re(s)<beta,                                         (5.3)
```

also proves a continuity lemma.  On a compact beta interval `J subset (0,1)`, choose an inversion line `Re(s)=c<min J`.  Stirling gives uniform exponential decay `exp[-pi|Im s|/beta]` up to polynomial factors.  Any fixed number of `a`-derivatives only introduces polynomial factors in `s`, so dominated Mellin inversion shows that

```text
(beta,a) -> q_beta^(j)(a)
```

is continuous, locally uniformly for positive `a`, for every fixed `j`.

Now let irrational `beta_0` be fixed and rationals `beta_n->beta_0`.  If `h(beta_n)` failed to tend to infinity, some subsequence would admit equations (5.1) with one common order/degree bound `N`.  Pad all coefficient tuples to the same finite-dimensional coefficient space and normalize their Euclidean coefficient norm to one.  Compactness gives a subsequence converging to a nonzero limiting polynomial tuple `(P_0,...,P_N)`.  Local-uniform derivative continuity permits passage to the limit in the exact equations, yielding

```text
Sum_{j=0}^N P_j(a) q_{beta_0}^(j)(a)=0,
```

a nontrivial D-finite equation at irrational beta.  This contradicts the established irrational non-D-finiteness theorem.  Therefore

```text
boxed:  beta_n rational -> irrational beta_0
        implies h(beta_n)->infinity.                  (5.4)
```

Thus the denominator growth in the rational Meijer-G/ODE construction is not merely cosmetic: **some finite linear holonomic complexity must diverge along every rational approximation of every irrational index.**  Equation (5.2) is an explicit `O(q)` upper construction; (5.4) does not claim a matching linear lower bound.

**Exact B27 boundary:** this proves blow-up only for the linear holonomic/D-finite class.  The physical rational tails are known to be D-algebraic, and no non-D-algebraicity theorem is currently proved at irrational beta.  Therefore a uniformly bounded *nonlinear* differential-algebraic schema is not ruled out by this run.  I do not upgrade the linear theorem into a stronger claim.
