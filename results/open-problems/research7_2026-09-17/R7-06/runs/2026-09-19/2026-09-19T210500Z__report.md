## 4. A new exact identity used by all three attacks

Use unnormalised convolution

\[
(f*f)(a)=\sum_x f(x)f(a+x).
\]

For `a=0`, `(f*f)(0)=h` and `\widehat f(0)=h`. For `a\ne0`, either `(f*f)(a)=0`, or `a\in Q+Q` and (2) gives `\widehat f(a)=0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{(f*f)(a)\widehat f(a)=h^2 1_{\{0\}}(a).}
\tag{3}
\]

Taking the unnormalised Fourier transform and using

\[
\widehat{f*f}=\widehat f^{\,2},
\qquad
\widehat{\widehat f}=2^m f,
\]

gives, for every `\xi\in G`,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{q\in Q}\widehat f(\xi+q)^2=h^2.}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus every translate of `Q` carries exactly the same total squared Fourier mass. This is an exact weighted-tiling identity, not a fit or heuristic. It does not alone count the family, but it supplies a global invariant that was absent from the earlier row-exposure formulation.

## 5. Method B4 — prove something weaker completely

### 5.1 A divisibility obstruction for additive subspaces

Let `V\le G` have dimension `d` and suppose

\[
V\setminus\{0\}\subseteq Q+Q.
\tag{5}
\]

Then `\widehat f(v)=0` for every nonzero `v\in V`. Push `f` to the quotient `G/V^\perp\cong V^*`. The Fourier transform of the coset-count function is exactly `\widehat f|_V`: its value at zero is `h`, and all nonzero Fourier coefficients vanish. Fourier inversion therefore makes all `2^d` coset counts equal to `h/2^d`. Hence

\[
\boxed{2^d\mid h,\qquad d\le\nu_2(h).}
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, if an affine subspace `A=x+V` is contained in `Q`, then `A+A=V\subseteq Q+Q`, so

\[
\boxed{\dim A\le\nu_2(h).}
\tag{7}
\]

On the square-order branch `h=4t^2`, this gives `\dim A\le2+2\nu_2(t)`. Large affine cubes are therefore impossible; for odd `t` the maximum possible affine-cube dimension is only two.

### 5.2 Complete additive-rich counting theorem

Define the translation stabilizer

\[
V_Q=\{v\in G:Q+v=Q\}.
\]

For every fixed `\varepsilon>0`, consider all `h=2m+2` element subsets with

\[
|V_Q|\ge m^\varepsilon.
\tag{8}
\]

Since translations act freely, `|V_Q|=2^d` divides `h`; hence

\[
\varepsilon\log_2m\le d\le\log_2h=O(\log m).
\tag{9}
\]

For fixed `d`, the number of possible subspaces is

\[
{m\brack d}_2
=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{2^m-2^j}{2^d-2^j}
\le2^{d(m-d+1)}.
\tag{10}
\]

For a fixed `V`, every `V`-invariant `Q` is a union of `k=h/2^d` cosets, so even without enforcing `0\in Q`, spanning, self-principality, or exact stabilizer,

\[
\#Q\le\binom{2^{m-d}}{k}
\le2^{h(m-d)/2^d}.
\tag{11}
\]

Summing the `O(\log m)` possible dimensions gives

\[
\boxed{
\#\{Q:|Q|=2m+2,\ |V_Q|\ge m^\varepsilon\}
\le
2^{O_\varepsilon(m^{2-\varepsilon}+m\log m)}
=2^{o(m^2)}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

This is a complete theorem, and it counts a larger labelled family than the self-principal/fixed-trace family of interest. Therefore it proves the desired subquadratic bound on every self-principal subfamily with polynomial-size translation symmetry.

**B4 outcome:** real progress on a restricted regime. Any hypothetical `2^{\Omega(m^2)}` self-principal obstruction family must be additively primitive: its translation stabilizer is smaller than every fixed power of `m`, and it cannot contain a linear-dimensional affine block. This remains PARTIAL toward F0011.

## 6. Method B9 — randomize, but keep the dependence honest

Fix an ordered external basis

\[
A=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)
\]

of `G`, independently of `Q`. Define `E_A` by the simultaneous conditions

\[
\widehat{1_Q}(a_i)=0,
\qquad
\widehat{1_Q}(a_i+a_j)=0
\quad(i<j).
\tag{13}
\]

These say that the `m+1` columns

\[
1,\chi_{a_1}|_Q,\ldots,\chi_{a_m}|_Q
\]

are pairwise orthogonal.

### 6.1 Exact sequential probability bound

Use an iid ordered model: `q_0=0`, while `q_1,\ldots,q_{h-1}` are independent uniform points of `G`. Because `A` is a basis, the entries of the `m` nonconstant sign columns after their first fixed `+1` entry are independent Rademachers.

Expose column `c_i` after `c_0,\ldots,c_{i-1}` have become pairwise orthogonal. Their tails in `\mathbb R^{h-1}` are linearly independent. Indeed, if a combination of the tails vanishes, then

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_jc_j=s e_0,
\qquad s=\sum_j\alpha_j.
\]

Taking inner products with `c_k` gives `h\alpha_k=s` for every `k<i`, so all coefficients equal `s/h`; summing gives `s=is/h`, and `i<h` forces `s=0`.

Therefore orthogonality of the next random cube vertex to the previous `i` columns cuts out an affine subspace of codimension exactly `i` in `\mathbb R^{h-1}`. Any codimension-`i` affine subspace contains at most `2^{h-1-i}` vertices of the cube. Thus

\[
\Pr(c_i\perp c_0,\ldots,c_{i-1}\mid\text{earlier orthogonality})\le2^{-i}.
\]

Multiplying,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{\rm iid}(E_A)
\le2^{-\sum_{i=1}^m i}
=2^{-m(m+1)/2}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

This derivation never declares the pairwise orthogonality equations independent.

### 6.2 Transfer to uniform sets

Let `D` be the event that the `h` sampled points are distinct. The union bound gives

\[
\Pr(D)\ge1-\binom h2 2^{-m}.
\tag{15}
\]

Conditioned on `D`, forgetting the ordering gives a uniform `h`-element set containing zero. Hence, whenever the denominator is positive,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{Q}(E_A)
\le
\frac{2^{-m(m+1)/2}}
{1-\binom h2 2^{-m}}
=2^{-m^2/2+O(m)}.
}
\tag{16}
\]

This is the rigorous quadratic rarity that the earlier heuristic row-exposure idea lacked.

### 6.3 Why this still does not close the restricted count

Every completed self-principal `Q` contains some internal vector-space basis, and that internally chosen basis satisfies (13). But Theorem (16) freezes `A` *before* `Q` is chosen. Summing naively over all ordered ambient bases costs

\[
|GL(m,2)|=2^{m^2+O(m)},
\]

which overwhelms the `2^{-m^2/2}` gain. The remaining gap is thus exactly **adaptive-basis entropy**, not a lack of anti-concentration for a fixed basis.

**B9 outcome:** a source-relevant quadratic anti-concentration theorem for the fixed-basis half-Hadamard constraints, plus an exact statement of why it cannot yet be promoted to the endogenous-basis case.

## 7. Method B23 — do what the expected proof does not: try to build a quadratic family

I tested the obvious mechanisms that could invalidate the restricted `2^{o(m^2)}` count.

### 7.1 Unrestricted `GL(m,2)` orbit is not an entrywise-preserving family

Suppose a linear map `T` preserves the principal Walsh entries under the natural correspondence:

\[
(-1)^{(Tx)\cdot(Ty)}=(-1)^{x\cdot y}
\quad(x,y\in Q).
\tag{17}
\]

Then

\[
x^T(T^TT-I)y=0
\]

for all `x,y\in Q`. Since `Q` spans in both variables, bilinearity extends this to all of `G`, forcing

\[
\boxed{T^TT=I.}
\tag{18}
\]

So the straightforward linear-coordinate orbit that preserves the matrix entrywise is only the already-known orthogonal gauge, not `2^{\Theta(m^2)}` genuine entropy. This does not rule out accidental self-principality of some nonorthogonal image; it rules out the simplest orbit mechanism.

### 7.2 The obvious orthonormal seed is impossible

Suppose `u_1,\ldots,u_s\in Q` are orthonormal over `\mathbb F_2`. For each pair `i\ne j`, the frequencies `u_i`, `u_j`, and `u_i+u_j` are nonzero elements of `Q+Q`, so their three Fourier coefficients vanish. If `N_{ab}` counts points of `Q` with coordinate pattern `(u_i\cdot q,u_j\cdot q)=(a,b)`, those three zero equations plus the total count force

\[
N_{00}=N_{01}=N_{10}=N_{11}=h/4=(m+1)/2.
\tag{19}
\]

But the seed points `0,u_1,\ldots,u_s` already contribute `s-1` points to pattern `00`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{s\le(m+3)/2.}
\tag{20}
\]

In particular, for `m\ge5` no self-principal `Q` can contain an orthonormal basis. The easiest coordinate construction dies immediately.

### 7.3 Affine/product constructions leave the slice

Large affine blocks are excluded by (7), and polynomial translation symmetry has already been counted subquadratically by (12). Ordinary products also leave the special extremal size law: if two factors separately satisfy `|Q_i|=2m_i+2`, then

\[
|Q_1\times Q_2|=(2m_1+2)(2m_2+2)
>2(m_1+m_2)+2
\]

for nontrivial factors. Thus the standard spectral-set product mechanism does not generate a quadratic family inside the `|Q|=2m+2` self-principal slice.

**B23 outcome:** no counterfamily was found. More importantly, three natural quadratic-entropy mechanisms fail for exact structural reasons. A true counterfamily, if it exists, must be additively primitive, must avoid simple orthonormal seed geometry, and cannot be the obvious `GL`-gauge or product orbit. This is not a proof of nonexistence.
