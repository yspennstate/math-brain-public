## 2. New proof I — exact sign-Gram counting formula for singular roots

For sign classes

\[
\mathcal V_r=\{[x]:x\in\{\pm1\}^r\},\qquad A_{[x]}=xx^T,
\]

and a positive-semidefinite sign Gram matrix `K`, let

\[
\mathfrak M_r(K)=
\left\{m:\mathcal V_r\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
\sum_vm_v=r,
\ \sum_vm_vA_v=K
\right\}.
\]

Then, with

\[
R_r(K)=\#\{C\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times r}:CC^T=K\},
\]

I proved the exact all-corank formula

\[
\boxed{
R_r(K)=2^r\sum_{m\in\mathfrak M_r(K)}
\frac{r!}{\prod_vm_v!}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The proof simply groups the ordered columns of `C` by sign class: the multinomial coefficient chooses class positions and `2^r` chooses column representatives. Positive definiteness forces every multiplicity to be `0` or `1`, recovering the preceding support formula. Thus singular Gram roots are now represented exactly rather than silently discarded.

## 3. New proof II — fixed-`P` endpoint freedom depends only on corank

I used the elementary cube-section lemma

\[
\boxed{|L\cap\{\pm1\}^M|\le2^{\dim L}}
\tag{3.1}
\]

for every affine subspace `L`: choose `dim L` coordinate functionals that are injective on its direction space.

Now fix the first half `P` and a cross block `C` in

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix},
\qquad H^2=NI,
\]

and put `k=corank C`. If `D,D'` are symmetric solutions of `PC+CD=0`, then

\[
C(D-D')=0.
\]

Therefore `D-D'` is a symmetric operator supported on `ker C`, a vector space of dimension exactly `binom(k+1,2)`. By (3.1), for fixed `(P,C)` there are at most

\[
\boxed{2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}}
\tag{3.2}
\]

sign choices for `D`. The quadratic equation `C^TC+D^2=NI` can only reduce this number.

This removes the previous distinct-nonzero-singular-value hypothesis: repeated singular values are **not** a separate conditioned endpoint obstruction.

## 4. New proof III — every singular Gram fiber reduces to a positive-definite Gram fiber

Let

\[
R_r^+=\max_{L>0}R_r(L),
\]

where the maximum is over positive-definite Gram matrices of square sign matrices. If `rank K=r-k`, I proved

\[
\boxed{
R_r(K)\le
\binom rk\,2^{2rk}R_r^+.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Proof encoding: from a root `C`, choose a canonical set of `r-k` independent sign columns `B` and store the other `k` columns `E`. Extend `B` by `k` sign columns `F` to an invertible sign matrix `Ctilde=[B,F]`, possible because the sign cube spans `R^r`. For fixed `(K,E,F)`,

\[
\widetilde C\widetilde C^T=K-EE^T+FF^T>0
\]

is fixed, so at most `R_r^+` choices remain. The index set, `E`, and `F` cost at most `binom(r,k)2^{2rk}`. The encoding recovers `C`.

Combining (3.2) and (4.1), a fixed first half `P` with cross-block corank `k` has at most

\[
\boxed{
\binom rk\,2^{2rk+\binom{k+1}{2}}R_r^+
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

sign completions.

## 5. Main new structural theorem — a balanced cut always has corank at most `sqrt N`

Assume the integral two-eigenvalue boundary

\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N},
\qquad H^2=NI,
\qquad N=2r,
\qquad q=\sqrt N\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let the `+q,-q` multiplicities be `r+delta,r-delta`, with `delta>=0`. Since the diagonal is sign-valued,

\[
2q\delta=|\operatorname{tr}H|\le N,
\qquad \delta\le q/2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Take the smaller eigenspace `U`, of dimension `r-delta`. Its projector is one of

\[
\Pi=\frac12(I\pm H/q),
\]

so every coordinate leverage score obeys

\[
\Pi_{ii}\ge\frac12-\frac1{2q}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the coordinate matroid of `U`,

\[
r_U(A)=\operatorname{rank}\Pi[A,A]
\ge\operatorname{tr}\Pi[A,A]
\ge\left(\frac12-\frac1{2q}\right)|A|.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The two-copy matroid-union formula therefore gives two disjoint independent coordinate sets of total size at least `N-q`. Extend them to opposite sides of a balanced partition `[N]=S sqcup T`, `|S|=|T|=r`. Then

\[
r_U(S)+r_U(T)\ge N-q.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For the cross block `C=H[S,T]`, the kernel of `C^T` consists of supported-on-`S` vectors whose `H`-image remains supported on `S`. Since `H^2=NI`, this subspace is `H`-invariant and splits across the two eigenspaces. Using coordinate-matroid duality for `U^perp`, the exact dimension is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{corank}C
=N-\delta-r_U(S)-r_U(T).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Together with (5.5),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{corank}C\le q-\delta\le\sqrt N.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is the key movement of the run: **every** matrix in the hard two-eigenvalue boundary admits a balanced half cut with only `O(sqrt N)` singular endpoint dimension. There is no genericity assumption and no exclusion of repeated singular values.

## 6. Consequence — singular endpoint costs only subquadratic entropy over positive Gram fibers

Choose a canonical balanced cut satisfying (5.7), set `k=corank C`, and condition on its principal half `P`. From (4.2),

\[
\#\{(C,D)\text{ completing }P\}
\le
R_{N/2}^+
\binom{N/2}{k}
2^{Nk+\binom{k+1}{2}}.
\]

Since `k<=sqrt N`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{(C,D)\text{ completing a fixed }P\}
\le
R_{N/2}^+
2^{N^{3/2}+O(N+\sqrt N\log N)}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Selecting the cut itself costs only `binom(N,N/2)=2^{O(N)}` possibilities. Thus the **entire singular/corank endpoint is now reducible to positive-definite sign-Gram fibers with `o(N^2)` extra entropy at the conditioned half-completion level**.

This is a stronger all-corank statement than the prior corank-one generic theorem and directly addresses the owner's request not to exclude the singular endpoint.

## 7. What remains genuinely hard

Equation (6.1) does **not** prove Vu's conjecture or even a full subquadratic count for the two-eigenvalue boundary.

First, `R_r^+` contains the orthogonal Gram point `K=rI`; its roots are Hadamard matrices. Ferber–Jain–Zhao prove only a constant quadratic-exponent improvement for the total number of Hadamard matrices and explicitly state that even `2^{o(r^2)}` would be very interesting and require new ideas. Therefore a uniform `R_r^+=2^{o(r^2)}` theorem is itself Hadamard-hard.

Second, a naive sum over all symmetric sign principal halves `P` costs

\[
2^{r(r+1)/2}=2^{N^2/8+O(N)},
\]

so a local Gram-fiber theorem alone cannot close the global fixed-spectrum count. The missing bridge is now cleaner:

\[
\boxed{
\text{couple principal-half seed entropy to Gram-root entropy globally, rather than paying them independently.}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The most promising next attacks are a many-good-cut/double-counting inequality, or a theorem proving that non-Hadamard-like positive Gram fibers are subquadratic while the Hadamard-like locus has additional global rigidity.
