## 1. Source target and inherited claim

Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 9.4, Section 9 / PDF p. 15, arXiv `2005.02797`, DOI `10.1214/20-PS346`.

For the symmetric Rademacher matrix `M_n^sym`, with diagonal and upper-triangular entries independent fair signs and the lower triangle filled by symmetry,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}(M_n^{\rm sym})\subset\mathbb Z\bigr)
=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently, the total number of labelled symmetric sign matrices with integral spectrum is `2^{o(n^2)}`. The source is a total-spectrum event, not a fixed-spectrum statement. Earlier F0011 work already observed that the number of possible integral spectra is only `2^{O(n)}`, so fixed-spectrum control at `2^{o(n^2)}` scale would suffice after summing spectra; that observation is inherited, not claimed new here.

The active restricted family is

\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N},\qquad H^2=NI,
\tag{1.2}
\]

with `N=2r` on the integral branch. For a balanced cut

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\ C^T&D\end{pmatrix},
\tag{1.3}
\]

the preceding run reduced every entropy-near-extremal fixed-half-rowspace fiber to

\[
K:=CC^T=\lambda I+R,
\qquad q:=\operatorname{rank}R=o(r),
\tag{1.4}
\]

with

\[
|\lambda-r|\le\frac{rq}{r-q},\qquad 0\preceq K\preceq2rI,
\tag{1.5}
\]

and proved the exact parity implication

\[
q<r-2\Longrightarrow\lambda\in4\mathbb Z.
\tag{1.6}
\]

It also classified `q=1` as impossible and `q=2`, exposing the saturated Ehlich--Wojtas/D-optimal branch. The unresolved question was whether the low-rank defect itself can still carry quadratic entropy once `q` grows, and whether the singular endpoint can be kept inside the same treatment.

## 2. Method 1 — lift the mod-four rank lemma to an exact two-class defect skeleton

Put

\[
\delta:=r-\lambda=R_{ii}.
\]

For row `i` of `C`, let `w_i` be the parity of its number of minus signs. The previous exact sign-Gram identity and `lambda in 4Z` give

\[
\boxed{
\frac R2\equiv
\left(\frac r2\bmod2\right)J+w\mathbf1^T+\mathbf1w^T
\pmod2.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Writing `A={i:w_i=0}` and `B={i:w_i=1}` yields an exact residue partition:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
r\equiv0\pmod4:&R_{ij}\equiv2\pmod4\iff i,j\text{ lie in opposite classes},\\[1mm]
r\equiv2\pmod4:&R_{ij}\equiv2\pmod4\iff i,j\text{ lie in the same class}.
\end{array}}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is all-dimensional and does not assume `C` invertible. It supplies the discrete skeleton needed to analyze small semidefinite shells geometrically.

## 3. Method 2 — semidefinite shell geometry

### 3.1. The entire `|delta|=2` shell collapses to defect two

Assume `|delta|=2` and `R` is semidefinite with the sign of its diagonal. Then

\[
S:=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\delta)}2R\succeq0
\]

is an integral Gram matrix with diagonal one. Thus `S_ij in {-1,0,1}`. In a Gram representation by unit vectors, `+-1` means parallel/antiparallel and `0` means orthogonal.

Here `r=2 mod 4`, so (2.2) says same-parity pairs have odd inner product and opposite-parity pairs have even inner product. Hence every row in one parity class lies on one line and the two classes are orthogonal. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}R\le2.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The inherited trace/spectral-cap constraint forces rank at least two in the nonzero branch for `r>2`, so

\[
\boxed{q=2.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus there is **no hidden higher-rank semidefinite family at distance two**. The previous exact q=2 classification is the whole shell, including the negative singular endpoint. Its labelled Gram-skeleton count is only `2^{O(r)}`.

### 3.2. The `|delta|=4` shell has only two structural forms

Now assume `|delta|=4` and again take

\[
S:=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\delta)}2R\succeq0.
\]

Then `S` is integral PSD with diagonal two. In a Gram representation, every vector has norm squared two. Here `r=0 mod 4`. Same-parity inner products are even, hence in `{-2,0,2}`, so distinct direction classes inside one parity class are orthogonal. Opposite-parity inner products are odd, hence exactly `+-1`.

Collapse parallel/antiparallel vectors to direction classes. If one parity class is empty, then after switching and permutation

\[
\boxed{
S=2\operatorname{diag}(J_{a_1},\ldots,J_{a_q}),
\qquad a_1+\cdots+a_q=r.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is an exact orthogonal-block blow-up and may have arbitrary rank.

If both parity classes are nonempty, choosing one representative from each direction class gives

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}2I_a&E\\E^T&2I_b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad E\in\{\pm1\}^{a\times b}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

A new covariance-packing lemma gives `a,b<=4`: if two internally orthogonal equal-norm clouds of norm squared `d` have every cross inner product of magnitude at least `c`, then

\[
\boxed{\max(a,b)\le(d/c)^2.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Indeed the cross-Gram matrix `E` has `||E||_op<=d` by PSD of the full Gram, while `||E||_F^2>=abc^2` and `rank(E)<=min(a,b)`, so `||E||_op^2>=c^2 max(a,b)`.

Here `d=2,c=1`, so `a,b<=4`. A finite singular-value argument then sharpens this to

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}G\le4.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The nontrivial cases are `(a,b)=(3,2)` and `(3,3)`: the PSD bound `||E||_op<=2` forces squared singular values `(4,2)` in the first case and `(4,4,1)` in the second. If `a=4`, Frobenius norm saturation forces every nonzero singular value to equal two. Thus every two-parity core has rank at most four. For large inherited orders the trace bound forces rank at least four, hence exactly four.

## 4. Method 3 — count the Gram skeletons, not just classify a local defect

The preceding geometry gives a source-scale entropy statement for the **intermediate defect matrix**.

For `|delta|=2`, a parity label and switching sign for each row specify the defect up to the already forced two-block sizes, giving `2^{O(r)}` possibilities.

For `|delta|=4`:

- one parity class gives a labelled set partition plus row signs, at most
  \[
  r^r2^r=2^{O(r\log r)};
  \]
- two parity classes have at most four direction classes per side, so parity/direction/sign labels contribute only `2^{O(r)}`, with a finite `2^{16}` cross-core choice.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\#\{R:\ R\text{ semidefinite, sign-Gram-derived, }|r-\lambda|\le4\}
\le2^{O(r\log r)}=2^{o(r^2)}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The `delta=0` case is just `R=0`. Consequently the first two nonzero semidefinite arithmetic shells, together with the scalar shell, cannot carry quadratic entropy through the **choice of Gram skeleton**.

This statement includes the singular/corank endpoint: nowhere was `K` assumed invertible, and negative semidefinite shells may have zero Gram eigenvalues.

## 5. What this does and does not solve

The new bound is a real bridge, but it is not the source theorem. It separates the remaining difficulty cleanly:

1. **Gram-skeleton entropy** is now `2^{o(r^2)}` throughout the semidefinite shells `|delta|<=4`.
2. **Root multiplicity inside one Gram skeleton** remains open. At `R=0` it is already ordinary Hadamard counting; at `|delta|=2` it contains saturated D-optimal/Ehlich--Wojtas roots; at one-parity `|delta|=4` it is a sign-root problem for mutually orthogonal equidistant row blocks.
3. **Higher shells and indefinite low-rank defects** are not covered by the small-norm PSD geometry.
4. General integral spectra outside the centered two-eigenvalue family are still not controlled.

Thus the theorem advances the obligation from “classify arbitrary low-rank defect matrices” to the more precise root-fiber recursion problem.
