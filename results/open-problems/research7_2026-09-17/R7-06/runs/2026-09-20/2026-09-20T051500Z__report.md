\boxed{

\tag{1.1}

\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{2r\times2r},

## 2. Exact good-cut theorem

For a balanced cut `S sqcup T=[2r]`, write

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix},
\qquad K=CC^T=2rI-P^2,
\]

and define

\[
\mathcal T(S)=\|PC\|_F^2.
\]

The block identities give

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T(S)=\operatorname{tr}(K(2rI-K))
=2r^3-\operatorname{tr}(P^4).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

I proved the following exact average. Let

\[
a=\frac12\operatorname{tr}H.
\]

For a uniformly random balanced cut,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_S\mathcal T(S)
=
\frac{r^4}{2r-1}
-
\frac{2a^2r(r-1)}{(2r-3)(2r-1)}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The proof is an exact without-replacement second-moment calculation. For a fixed ordered cross pair `i in S`, `j notin S`, set `b_k=H_{ik}H_{kj}`. Orthogonality of rows `i,j` gives `sum_k b_k=0`. Conditional on the cross event, `S\setminus{i}` is exactly a half-sample of the other `2r-2` signs. If `H_ii=H_jj`, its conditional square expectation is

\[
\frac{r(r-2)}{2r-3};
\]

if `H_ii=-H_jj`, it is

\[
\frac{r^2-2}{2r-3}.
\]

Counting same-sign and opposite-sign diagonal pairs gives (2.2).

On the centered branch `a=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_S\mathcal T(S)=\frac{r^4}{2r-1}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Hence every completed centered matrix admits a balanced cut with

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T(S)\ge\frac{r^4}{2r-1}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

There are only `binom(2r,r)=2^{O(r)}` balanced cuts. By relabelling symmetry, if `A_{S_0}` is the set of centered matrices for which a fixed standard cut satisfies (2.4), then

\[
\boxed{
H_{\rm sym,centered}(2r)
\le
\binom{2r}{r}\,|A_{S_0}|.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

This is a useful reduction: **one may impose the high-energy condition on a fixed cut at only subquadratic overhead**. The previous `T=0` embedded-Hadamard cut therefore cannot by itself block a cut-selected counting proof.

## 3. The selected cut is quantitatively interior to the Gram cone

Let `lambda_1,...,lambda_r in [0,2r]` be the eigenvalues of `K` for a cut satisfying (2.4). Because

\[
\mathcal T=\sum_i\lambda_i(2r-\lambda_i),
\]

and `lambda(2r-lambda)<=7r^2/16` outside `[r/4,7r/4]`, at least

\[
\boxed{
q\ge\frac{r(2r+7)}{9(2r-1)}>\frac r9
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

eigenvalues lie in `[r/4,7r/4]`.

Also

\[
\operatorname{tr}K^2=2r^3-\mathcal T
\le r^3\frac{3r-2}{2r-1},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{erank}(K)
:=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}K)^2}{\operatorname{tr}K^2}
\ge
r\frac{2r-1}{3r-2}
=\left(\frac23+o(1)\right)r.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus after canonical cut selection the hard family has a linear-dimensional bulk separated from both endpoint eigenvalues. This does not by itself give `2^{o(r^2)}`, but it removes the exact endpoint from the selected-cut formulation and supplies a deterministic interior-energy constraint for the next entropy argument.

## 4. Hypercube-rich flag theorem

Let `V<=R^n` have dimension `d`, and write

\[
Q(V)=V\cap\{\pm1\}^n.
\]

Odlyzko's classical bound gives `|Q(V)|<=2^d`. Define

\[
\sigma(V)=d-\log_2|Q(V)|.
\]

### Exact saturation

If `|Q(V)|=2^d`, choose `d` pivot coordinates giving an isomorphism `pi:V->R^d`. Since `pi` maps the `2^d` cube points injectively into `{+-1}^d`, it is bijective there. Any ambient coordinate is therefore a linear form

\[
L(x)=\sum c_i x_i
\]

taking values in `{+-1}` on the entire discrete cube. Expanding

\[
L(x)^2
=
\sum_i c_i^2+2\sum_{i<j}c_ic_jx_ix_j
\equiv1
\]

forces `c_ic_j=0` for all `i ne j` and `sum c_i^2=1`. Hence

\[
\boxed{L(x)=\pm x_j.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore the ambient coordinates split into signed copies of the `d` pivots.

If, moreover, `V` contains an ordered orthogonal sign basis `R in {+-1}^{d x n}`, `RR^T=nI`, and `m_j` denotes the number of ambient coordinates copying pivot `j`, then writing the pivot block as `Y` gives

\[
Y\operatorname{diag}(m_1,\ldots,m_d)Y^T=nI.
\]

Because the matrix is square, the corresponding column identity gives `m_j d=n` for every `j`, so

\[
\boxed{d\mid n,\qquad m_j=n/d,\qquad Y^TY=dI.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus `Y` is an order-`d` Hadamard matrix, and conversely every order-`d` Hadamard matrix lifts through these fixed signed coordinate groups. A saturated Hadamard tail is therefore **literally a smaller-order Hadamard completion problem**, not merely a qualitative structured case.

### Near saturation

Suppose instead

\[
|Q(V)|\ge2^{d-s}.
\]

In pivot coordinates, let an ambient coordinate have support size `m`:

\[
L(x)=\sum_{j\in S}a_jx_j,
\qquad |S|=m.
\]

At least a `2^{-s}` fraction of the full cube maps to actual cube points of `V`, so

\[
\Pr(L(\varepsilon)\in\{\pm1\})\ge2^{-s}.
\]

The Erdos-Littlewood-Offord/Sperner atom bound gives

\[
\sup_t\Pr(L(\varepsilon)=t)
\le2^{-m}\binom{m}{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
\le\sqrt{\frac2{\pi m}}.
\]

Using the two target atoms gives

\[
\boxed{
m\le\frac8\pi4^s.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

So near-maximal cube intersection implies **uniformly sparse coordinate dependence**.

## 5. Sparse Gram-root normal form

Suppose the near-saturated `V` contains a full orthogonal sign tail

\[
R\in\{\pm1\}^{d\times n},\qquad RR^T=nI.
\]

After placing the pivots first, the previous lemma yields

\[
\boxed{
R=Y[I_d\ A],
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `Y` is an invertible `d x d` sign matrix and every column of `A` has support at most `(8/pi)4^s`.

Orthogonality gives

\[
Y(I+AA^T)Y^T=nI.
\]

Because `Y` is square and invertible,

\[
\boxed{
Y^TY=n(I+AA^T)^{-1}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the main recursive bridge of the run: a near-saturated row-flag tail becomes a **square sign-Gram-root problem coupled to a column-sparse coefficient matrix**. It matches the existing C05 Gram-fiber machinery rather than introducing a disconnected structure theory.

For an ordered Hadamard row flag `w_1,...,w_n`, let

\[
V_k=\operatorname{span}(w_1,...,w_k)^\perp,
\qquad d=n-k.
\]

The next row has at most

\[
|Q(V_k)|=2^{d-\sigma(V_k)}
\]

choices. Hence every step has an explicit dichotomy:

* large `sigma(V_k)` pays entropy immediately;
* small `sigma(V_k)` forces the sparse Gram-root normal form (5.1)-(5.2);
* zero `sigma(V_k)` recurses exactly to a smaller Hadamard completion.

This is the missing *kind* of mechanism needed to compound losses across scales. It does not yet prove that the losses compound enough.

## 7. Failed shortcut and why it matters

A tempting interpretation of the previous endpoint construction was that the centered family might have to be counted by directly controlling the `T=0` singular cut. The exact average (2.3) disproves that need: `T=0` can be a bad representation of a completed matrix even when the matrix necessarily possesses a high-energy cut.

The opposite shortcut also fails: merely knowing that a good cut has `Omega(r)` interior Gram eigenvalues still gives only constant-factor anti-concentration gains under the current Ferber-Jain-Zhao one-step machinery. The new ingredient must therefore be **recursive compression of low-deficiency row flags**, not further optimization of a single fixed anti-concentration constant.
