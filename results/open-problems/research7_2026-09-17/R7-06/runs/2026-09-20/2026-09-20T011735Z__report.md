## 2. New theorem I — determinant deficit forces near-Hadamard seed structure

Take an invertible balanced half cut `N=2r`,
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K:=CC^T=2rI-P^2>0.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Since `P` is an `r x r` sign matrix,
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P^2)=r^2.
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal E(P)
=\sum_{i\ne j}\langle P_{i,*},P_{j,*}\rangle^2
=\|P^2-rI\|_F^2.
\tag{2.2}
\]
I proved
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{r^r}{\det K}
\ge \frac{\mathcal E(P)}{4r^2}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Proof: write
\[
A=(P^2-rI)/r.
\]
Then `tr A=0`, the positivity of `K` gives `spec(A) subset [-1,1)`, and
\[
K/r=I-A.
\]
For every `a in [-1,1)`,
\[
-\log(1-a)-a\ge a^2/4.
\]
Summing over the eigenvalues of `A` and using `tr A=0` gives (2.3).

Consequences:

\[
\det K\ge r^r e^{-\varepsilon r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E(P)\le4\varepsilon r^3.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Hence for any `tau>0`, at most `4 epsilon r^2/tau^2` ordered row pairs have correlation at least `tau sqrt(r)` in magnitude.

At exact maximum volume,
\[
\boxed{
\det K=r^r
\iff K=rI
\iff P^2=rI.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Thus the orthogonal Gram point does **not** coexist with an arbitrary symmetric sign seed: it recursively forces the principal half itself to be symmetric Hadamard-type. This is an exact seed/fiber coupling that was absent from the previous worst-fiber bound.

## 3. New theorem II — every genuine four-column positive-Gram trade is a closed-quadruple-type switch

For a positive-definite sign Gram matrix `K`, an invertible root `C` has `r` distinct column sign classes and determines a support set `S(C)` with
\[
K=\sum_{[x]\in S(C)}xx^T.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Suppose two roots of the same `K` have supports sharing exactly `r-4` sign classes. Cancelling the common columns leaves `r x 4` sign matrices `U,V` with
\[
UU^T=VV^T.
\tag{3.2}
\]
I proved that either the replacement is only a signed permutation, or, up to signed permutations on the four columns,
\[
\boxed{V=U H_4/2}
\tag{3.3}
\]
for a `4 x 4` Hadamard matrix `H_4`, and necessarily
\[
\boxed{u_1\circ u_2\circ u_3\circ u_4=\pm\mathbf1.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The proof uses a four-dimensional cube-isometry classification. Because the four removed columns come from an invertible root they are independent, so `V=UQ` for an orthogonal `Q`. Restricting to four independent rows gives nonsingular sign matrices `A,B` with `AA^T=BB^T`. A nonsingular `4 x 4` sign matrix has determinant magnitude `8` or `16`. The determinant-8 Gram embedding is unique up to signed column permutation. In the determinant-16 case both matrices are Hadamard, and `Q=(1/4)A^TB` has entries in `{0,±1/2,±1}`; orthogonality forces either a monomial matrix or a half-Hadamard matrix. The latter maps a sign row back to a sign row exactly on one parity class, yielding (3.4).

Therefore the local support graph of a fixed positive Gram fiber, with edges corresponding to four-column replacement, is exactly the closed-quadruple-type switching graph.

## 4. New obstruction — local four-column switching does not generate the whole Gram fiber

The preceding classification suggested a tempting intermediate reduction: perhaps every Gram fiber could be navigated by successive four-column closed-quadruple switches. A fresh literature check supplies a concrete counterexample.

Abiad and Peters, *Switching graphs and Hadamard matrices*, The Art of Discrete and Applied Mathematics 9(2), P2.06, published 21 May 2026, DOI `10.26493/2590-9770.1840.c3u`, report that among the 60 equivalence classes of order-24 Hadamard matrices, **two have no closed quadruple**.

Take the transpose `C` of a representative from one such class. It has no closed column quadruple, but
\[
CC^T=24I.
\]
Any order-24 Hadamard matrix `C'` from another inequivalent class also satisfies `C'C'^T=24I`; inequivalence implies it cannot differ from `C` only by signed column permutation. So the same positive Gram fiber contains a genuinely different root even though `C` has no four-column support-trade neighbor.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\text{positive Gram-fiber ambiguity can be genuinely nonlocal.}
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
A strategy that only counts or iterates closed-quadruple moves is therefore insufficient. This is a counterexample to that intermediate reduction, **not** a counterexample to Vu's conjecture.

The classical switching operation itself is not claimed as new; it goes back at least to Orrick, *Switching Operations for Hadamard Matrices*, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 22 (2008), DOI `10.1137/050641727`. I also checked the 5 June 2026 Crnković-Egan-Švob general switching framework, which confirms that switching remains an active broad structural language rather than an enumeration theorem of the kind needed here.
