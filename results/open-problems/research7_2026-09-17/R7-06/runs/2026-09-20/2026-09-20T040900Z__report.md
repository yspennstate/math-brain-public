## 2. New lemma — robust spectral subspaces suffice

Let `A` be an `m x R` real matrix with columns `a_j` satisfying `||a_j||^2<=L`. Suppose the `q`-th largest eigenvalue of `AA^T` is at least `tau`. Project onto the top `q` eigenspace and call the projected matrix `B`.

Then

\[
BB^T\succeq\tau I_q.
\]

For every `d`-dimensional subspace `W<=R^q`,

\[
\sum_j\|P_{W^\perp}b_j\|^2
=\operatorname{tr}(P_{W^\perp}BB^T)
\ge\tau(q-d).
\]

A single column contributes at most `L`; hence at least `(tau/L)(q-d)` columns lie outside every `W`. The vector-matroid base-packing criterion gives

\[
\boxed{
\left\lfloor\frac\tau L\right\rfloor
\text{ pairwise disjoint bases of }R^q.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is the key strengthening over the preceding run: **a full minimum spectral edge is unnecessary**. One can throw away all soft directions and anti-concentrate only on a robust spectral subspace.

If `ell` is even and `ell<=tau/L`, Ferber-Jain-Zhao Theorem 1.10 therefore gives, uniformly in the inhomogeneous target `b`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{x\in\{\pm1\}^R:Ax=b\}
\le2^R p_\ell^q,
\qquad
p_\ell=2^{-\ell}\binom\ell{\ell/2}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

## 3. The F0011 Gram cone always has many robust directions

Fix any symmetric sign seed `P` for which

\[
K=2rI-P^2=CC^T\succeq0
\tag{3.1}
\]

has a sign root. Since `P` and `C` have sign rows,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}K=r^2,
\qquad0\preceq K\preceq2rI.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

In particular

\[
\boxed{\rho:=\operatorname{rank}K\ge r/2.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Choose, as a deterministic function of `K`, a principal positive-definite row basis `I` of size `rho`, and expose those independent rows of a root `C`. For every prefix of `m<=rho` selected rows, its Gram matrix `K_m` has

\[
\operatorname{tr}K_m=mr,
\qquad0<K_m\preceq2rI_m.
\tag{3.4}
\]

For the coarse theorem artifact, let `q_m` be the number of eigenvalues at least `(3/10)r`. Then

\[
mr\le q_m(2r)+(m-q_m)(3r/10),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{q_m\ge(7/17)m.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This holds for every independent prefix, every fixed seed, and every corank.

## 4. Uniform all-corank quadratic saving

Let

\[
M=\lfloor r/100\rfloor.
\]

At prefix size `1<=m<=M`, the coefficient columns are sign vectors in `R^m`, so their squared norms equal `m`. Projecting onto the `q_m` robust eigendirections in (3.5), Lemma 2.1 gives at least

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{(3/10)r}{m}\right\rfloor\ge30
\]

disjoint bases.

Set

\[
p_{30}=2^{-30}\binom{30}{15},
\qquad
a_{30}=\log_2(1/p_{30})\approx2.7912135978.

The FJZ atom theorem bounds the next-row choices by

\[
2^r p_{30}^{q_m}
\le2^{r-a_{30}(7/17)m}.

The ordinary affine-cube/Odlyzko factor is `2^{r-m}`. Therefore each of these early extensions gains an extra factor

\[
2^{-g_0m},
\qquad
\boxed{g_0=(7/17)a_{30}-1\approx0.1493232461>0.}
\tag{4.1}

\[
\begin{aligned}
\#\{(C,D):\text{complete fixed }P\}

\boxed{
2^{r(r+1)/2-g_0M(M+1)/2}

The equality uses the exact cancellation

\[
\rho r-\binom\rho2+\binom{k+1}{2}=r(r+1)/2.

Consequently every fixed seed satisfies

This is a genuine change from the preceding run: the quadratic entropy saving is now **uniform over the whole allowed Gram cone, including every singular/corank endpoint**.

## 5. Stronger spectral-profile form proved in this report

The fixed `(3/10,30,r/100)` choice is only a convenient corollary. The same proof gives an exact profile inequality.

At an independent prefix of size `m`, fix any even integer `ell` satisfying `ell m<r`, and let `q_m(ell)` count the eigenvalues of `K_m` at least `ell m`. Using trace `mr` and cap `2r`,

\[
mr
\le q_m(\ell)(2r)+\bigl(m-q_m(\ell)\bigr)\ell m,
\]

which yields

\[
\boxed{
q_m(\ell)
\ge
m\frac{r-\ell m}{2r-\ell m}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The projected column norms are at most `m`, so the threshold `tau=ell m` gives `ell` disjoint bases exactly. Put

\[
a_\ell:=\log_2\frac{2^\ell}{\binom\ell{\ell/2}}.
\]

Compared with the Odlyzko extension factor, this prefix therefore saves at least

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_\ell(m)
:=m\left[
 a_\ell\frac{r-\ell m}{2r-\ell m}-1
\right]
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

bits whenever the bracket is positive. Hence the fixed-seed theorem admits the uniform refinement

\[
\boxed{
\log_2\#\{(C,D)\mid P\}
\le
\frac{r(r+1)}2
-
\sum_{m<r/2}
\max_{\substack{\ell\ge2\ {m even}\\ \ell m<r}}
\bigl(\Delta_\ell(m)\bigr)_+.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Only the prefixes that actually occur are used; since `rho>=r/2`, every `m<r/2` is available in the independent-basis exposure. No soft-eigenvalue assumption enters (5.3).

Even without optimizing over `ell`, taking `ell=30` all the way to the zero of the bracket strictly improves the coarse constant. Let

\[
x_*:=\frac{a_{30}-2}{a_{30}-1},
\qquad
c_*:=x_*/30\approx0.0147239763.
\]

A Riemann-sum evaluation of (5.2) for `m<=c_*r` gives

\[
\sum_m(\Delta_{30}(m))_+
=
\bigl(C_{30}+o(1)\bigr)r^2,
\tag{5.4}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
C_{30}
=
\frac1{30^2}
\left[
\frac{a_{30}-1}{2}x_*^2
-a_{30}\left(2\log\frac2{2-x_*}-x_*\right)
\right]
\approx1.6118620\times10^{-5}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The logarithm in (5.5) is natural because it comes from integrating the rational profile; `a_30` remains a binary-logarithmic entropy constant. Thus the asymptotic fixed-seed exponent can already be sharpened from (4.4) to

\[
\boxed{
\log_2\#\{(C,D)\mid P\}
\le
\left(\frac12-C_{30}+o(1)\right)r^2.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The point of (5.3), more than the small numerical improvement, is that the missing problem is now an optimization/stability question for an explicit spectral-profile functional rather than an unstructured soft-edge exception.

## 7. Exact mixed energy and a counterexample to a rank-only endpoint reduction

The same block equations give the exact invariant

\[
\boxed{
T(P,C):=\operatorname{tr}(K(2rI-K))
=\|PC\|_F^2
=2r^3-\operatorname{tr}(P^4).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus `T>=0`, and `T=0` iff `PC=0`, equivalently `Spec(K) subset {0,2r}`. If `r` is even, every nonzero entry of `PC` has magnitude at least 2 and therefore `|supp(PC)|<=T/4`. If `r` is odd, every entry is odd, so `T>=r^2`.

At `T=0`, trace plus the spectral cap force

\[
\operatorname{rank}P=\operatorname{rank}C=r/2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

This maximally singular endpoint is nevertheless not a low-complexity nuisance. Put `r=2s`; let `A,E` be symmetric Hadamard matrices of order `s`, and let `B` be any Hadamard matrix of order `s`. Define

\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}A&A\\A&A\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C=\begin{pmatrix}B&B\\-B&-B\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}E&-E\\-E&E\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then `PC=CD=0` and direct multiplication gives

\[
P^2+CC^T=2rI,
\qquad
C^TC+D^2=2rI.
\]

Therefore

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix}
\tag{7.4}
\]

is a symmetric Hadamard matrix of order `4s=2r`, with `T=0` and `corank C=r/2`. The displayed blocks recover `(A,B,E)`, so the construction is injective and gives the labelled-count relation

\[
\boxed{
H_{\rm sym}(4s)\ge H_{\rm sym}(s)^2 H(s),
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

whenever the left ingredients exist; in particular, after fixing `A=E`,

\[
\boxed{H_{\rm sym}(4s)\ge H(s).}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This explicitly refutes the proposed intermediate hope that the maximal-corank endpoint should admit a merely polynomial, `2^{O(r log r)}`, or rank-only count. It contains a smaller-order general Hadamard counting problem.

There is also a useful hardness implication for the source conjecture. Sylvester gives symmetric Hadamards for every power of two. Taking `s=4^j`, the matrix in (7.4) has order `4s`, whose square root is integral. Therefore Vu's full Conjecture 9.4 would imply

\[
\boxed{H(4^j)=2^{o((4^j)^2)}}
\tag{7.7}
\]

along the powers-of-four orders. Ferber-Jain-Zhao explicitly identify even `H(n)=2^{o(n^2)}` as a major stronger target than their present constant-quadratic improvement. Thus the remaining F0011 obstruction is not an artifact of this proof organization: the source conjecture contains a genuinely Hadamard-hard enumeration slice.

No external novelty claim is made for the block construction itself; (7.5)-(7.7) are recorded as project deductions/obstructions.

## 8. What failed and what the failure teaches

The preceding `lambda_min(K)>=gamma r` split was too coarse. A single soft eigenvalue could defeat it while leaving almost all covariance directions robust. The projected theorem resolves that failure by using the whole spectral profile.

A second tempting route — use rank loss to control singular fibers — is now decisively insufficient. The endpoint affine freedom exactly cancels the rank-adaptive root saving at the Odlyzko level, and the explicit construction (7.3) embeds arbitrary Hadamard matrices into the maximal-corank endpoint. Any future singular theorem must use more than dimension/corank.

The new profile inequality (5.3) does not close the centered two-eigenvalue family: it produces a fixed positive quadratic saving, not a saving tending to the whole quadratic exponent. Repeatedly optimizing the same one-step atom inequality may improve the constant but cannot be assumed to yield `o(N^2)` without a new recursive structural mechanism.
