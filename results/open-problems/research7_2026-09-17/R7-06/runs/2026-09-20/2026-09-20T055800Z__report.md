## 1. Exact outside source and inherited claim

Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 9.4, Section 9 / PDF p. 15, arXiv `2005.02797`, DOI `10.1214/20-PS346`.

For the symmetric Rademacher matrix `M_n^sym`, with diagonal and upper-triangular entries independent fair signs and lower triangle filled by symmetry,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}(M_n^{\rm sym})\subset\mathbb Z\bigr)
=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently, the total number of labelled symmetric sign matrices with integral spectrum must be `2^{o(n^2)}`.

The active restricted family remains the centered symmetric two-eigenvalue / symmetric-Hadamard boundary

\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N},\qquad H^2=NI.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The immediately previous report proved a good-cut theorem plus a near-Odlyzko-saturation inverse statement. If a `d`-dimensional sign-spanned subspace `V<=R^n` has cube deficiency

\[
\sigma(V)=d-\log_2|V\cap\{\pm1\}^n|\le s,
\]

then after choosing pivot coordinates every nonpivot coordinate depends on at most `(8/pi)4^s` pivots. It also identified exact saturation with literal smaller-order Hadamard recursion. What remained was to turn this sparse normal form into an entropy theorem and then count symmetric completions after the half-rowspace itself is fixed.

## 2. New theorem A — finite-alphabet compression of rich cube sections

Write the rich subspace in pivot coordinates as

\[
V=\{(x,A^Tx):x\in\mathbb R^d\}.
\]

If one nonpivot coordinate has support `S`, `|S|=k`, the sign points in `V` span the pivot space, hence there are `k` sign points whose restrictions to `S` form an invertible sign matrix `M`. The coefficient vector solves

\[
Ma=z,\qquad z\in\{\pm1\}^k.
\]

Cramer's rule and Hadamard's determinant inequality imply that every nonzero coefficient is rational with numerator and denominator bounded by `k^{k/2}`. Therefore, if

\[
t=\min\left(d,\left\lceil\frac8\pi4^s\right\rceil\right),
\qquad D_t=\lceil t^{t/2}\rceil,
\]

all coefficients belong to the finite alphabet

\[
\mathcal R_t=\{p/q:q\ne0,\ |p|,|q|\le D_t\}.
\]

This gives the explicit count

\[
\boxed{
\#\mathcal V_{n,d}(s)
\le
\binom nd
\left(
1+\sum_{k=1}^{t}\binom dk|\mathcal R_t|^k
\right)^{n-d}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\log_2\#\mathcal V_{n,d}(s)
\le
n+O\!\left((n-d)[t\log(d+1)+t^2\log(t+2)]\right).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For `d=Theta(n)`, the condition

\[
16^s(s+1)=o(n)
\]

therefore implies

\[
\boxed{\#\mathcal V_{n,d}(s)=2^{o(n^2)}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

A convenient explicit sufficient range is

\[
s\le(1/4-\varepsilon)\log_2 n
\]

for any fixed `epsilon>0`.

This is the first current C05 result that counts the **near-saturated subspaces themselves** at the source-scale `2^{o(n^2)}` rather than merely describing their coordinates sparsely.

## 3. New theorem B — a small support core

For any nontrivial coordinate form `L` involving at least two pivot variables,

\[
\Pr(L(\varepsilon)\in\{\pm1\})\le3/4.
\]

The support-size-two case is elementary; for support at least three it follows from the two-atom Littlewood-Offord/Sperner bound. Pairwise-disjoint support constraints are independent, so if `m` nontrivial supports are disjoint then

\[
2^{-s}\le(3/4)^m,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{m\le s/\log_2(4/3).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

A maximal matching of nontrivial supports therefore yields a hitting set `U` satisfying

\[
\boxed{
|U|\le
 t\left\lceil\frac{s}{\log_2(4/3)}\right\rceil,
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

meeting every nontrivial coordinate dependence. Thus low deficiency forces both a finite coefficient alphabet and concentration around a small exceptional pivot core.

## 4. New theorem C — fixed half-rowspace entropy is a principal-angle multiplicity functional

Let `N=2r`, fix the coordinate half-space `E`, and put

\[
Q=H/\sqrt N,
\qquad V=QE.
\]

Then `Q=Q^T`, `Q^2=I`. Let the principal angles between `E` and `V` have multiplicities

- `a` at angle `0`;
- `b` at angle `pi/2`;
- `m_theta` at each distinct interior angle.

I proved that for fixed `E,V`, all symmetric orthogonal involutions `Q` with `QE=V` lie in a linear subspace of dimension

\[
\boxed{
L(V)=a(a+1)+b^2+
\sum_{\theta\in(0,\pi/2)}\frac{m_\theta(m_\theta+1)}2.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\#\{H\text{ symmetric Hadamard}:QE=V\}\le2^{L(V)}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The proof is a CS-decomposition calculation. On an interior principal-angle block, the fixed pair `(E,V)` leaves only a symmetric orthogonal matrix `S` in a space of dimension `m(m+1)/2`; the endpoint blocks contribute `a(a+1)` and `b^2`. A linear `L`-dimensional space contains at most `2^L` sign points.

A useful algebraic way to see why the angle blocks are canonical is to set `B=P_EQ|_E`. Since `P_V=QP_EQ`,

\[
B^2=P_EP_VP_E.
\]

Thus the eigenspaces of the principal-angle operator are invariant for `B`; on the angle-`theta` block `B=cos(theta) S` with `S` symmetric orthogonal. This gives the block parametrization used in the proof.

## 5. The singular/corank endpoint is explicit, not omitted

For the block cut

\[
Q=\frac1{\sqrt N}
\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix},
\]

principal-angle geometry gives

\[
\operatorname{singval}(C/\sqrt N)=\{\sin\theta_i\},
\qquad
\operatorname{singval}(D/\sqrt N)=\{\cos\theta_i\}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{a=\operatorname{corank}C,\qquad b=\operatorname{corank}D.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The earlier all-corank theorem gives a balanced cut with `a<=sqrt N`. Consequently the angle-zero contribution `a(a+1)` is only `O(N)` on a good cut.

Define

\[
M(V)=\max\left(b,\max_{\theta\in(0,\pi/2)}m_\theta\right).
\]

Then

\[
L(V)
\le a(a+1)+M(V)r+\frac{M(V)+1}{2}r.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus, on a low-corank cut,

\[
M(V)=o(r)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L(V)=o(N^2).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The quadratic fixed-subspace obstruction is therefore no longer generic cross-block singularity. It is **macroscopic repeated principal-angle multiplicity**, including a possible macroscopic `pi/2` block.

## 6. Combined source-scale restricted theorem

Combining the two entropy mechanisms gives a clean restricted theorem.

Fix `epsilon>0`. Consider centered symmetric Hadamard matrices of order `N=2r` for which some balanced cut has associated half-rowspace `V` satisfying

\[
\operatorname{corank}C\le\sqrt N,
\]

\[
\sigma(V)\le(1/4-\epsilon)\log_2N,
\]

and

\[
M(V)=o(r).
\]

Then the whole branch has cardinality

\[
\boxed{2^{o(N^2)}.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Indeed, cut choice costs only `2^{O(N)}`, Theorem A gives only `2^{o(N^2)}` possible half-rowspaces, and Theorem C gives `2^{o(N^2)}` completions for each fixed half-rowspace.

This is genuinely at the entropy scale required by Vu's source conjecture, but it is only a **restricted fixed-spectrum theorem**. It does not cover arbitrary integral spectra and does not yet cover the complementary centered-Hadamard regimes.

## 7. Remaining gap and next attack

The remaining obstruction is now more specific than in the preceding run.

1. **High-deficiency branch.** If relevant row-tail/cut subspaces have deficiency above the finite-alphabet range, one needs to prove that this deficiency accumulates over sufficiently many nested exposures/cuts to produce a quadratic entropy payment.
2. **Repeated-angle branch.** If a good cut has one principal angle of multiplicity `rho r`, formula (4.1) permits quadratic entropy inside that block. The next main target is a **multiplicity-or-recursion theorem**: show that a macroscopic repeated-angle block forces a smaller sign-Gram/symmetric-Hadamard instance with contracting squared dimensions, or itself incurs a quadratic entropy loss.

The previous good-cut energy theorem guarantees a linear-dimensional interior spectral bulk on a selected centered cut, but it does not force those interior angles to be distinct. A single highly repeated interior angle is therefore the sharpest immediate bottleneck.
