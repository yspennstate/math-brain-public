## 1. Exact source and inherited bottleneck

Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 9.4, arXiv `2005.02797`, DOI `10.1214/20-PS346`.

For the symmetric Rademacher matrix `M_n^sym`, with independent fair signs on and above the diagonal,

\[
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}(M_n^{\rm sym})\subset\mathbb Z\bigr)
=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}.
\]

Equivalently, the total integral-spectrum count should be `2^{o(n^2)}`. The source does **not** fix a spectrum. The current C05 line is only a restricted fixed two-eigenvalue bridge.

\[

where both metrics are direct sums of `I+J`. The missing theorem was to exploit the fact that `T` comes from an actual sign root, rather than count arbitrary metric similarities.

## 2. Main new theorem — the scaled inverse core is ternary

Applying the same contrast construction to `C^T` gives

\[
C^TF=2ES,
\qquad
S\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d\times d}.
\]

Composing with `C^TC E=(r-4\varepsilon)E` and `CC^TF=(r-4\varepsilon)F` gives the exact inverse identities

\[
\boxed{ST=TS=sI_d.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{sT^{-1}=S\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d\times d}.}
\]

In particular `s` is an integer, so this nontrivial one-parity branch forces

\[
\boxed{r\equiv0\pmod4.}
\]

This is substantially stronger than the inherited metric-isometry equation.

## 3. Dense low-rank inverse defect

For row/column anchors `g_i,h_j`, the core entries are

\[
T_{g,h}=\frac{C_{g,h}-C_{g,h_j}}2,
\qquad
S_{h,g}=\frac{C_{g,h}-C_{g_i,h}}2.
\]

Hence

\[
D:=T-S^T
\]

satisfies

\[
D_{g,h}=\frac{C_{g_i,h}-C_{g,h_j}}2\in\{-1,0,1\}
\]

and decomposes as the difference of a matrix with at most `q` distinct rows and one with at most `q` distinct columns. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}D\le2q.}
\]

Since `S^T=T-D` and `TS=ST=sI`,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(TT^T-sI)\le2q,}
\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(T^TT-sI)\le2q.}
\]

So `T` has singular value `sqrt(s)` with multiplicity at least

\[
\boxed{d-2q=r-3q=(1-o(1))r.}
\]

The sign-reconstructible core therefore has a giant **exact Euclidean** singular-value plateau, not just a flat spectrum in the `I+J` metric.

## 4. Midpoint normal form

Put

\[
X=(T+S^T)/2=T-D/2.
\]

The inverse equations imply the exact completed-square identities

\[
\boxed{XX^T=sI+DD^T/4,}
\qquad
\boxed{X^TX=sI+D^TD/4.}
\]

The local four-sign reconstruction gives a mixed alphabet:

* if `D_ij=0`, then `X_ij in {-1,0,1}`;
* if `|D_ij|=1`, then `X_ij in {-1/2,+1/2}`.

Thus the hard fiber is a low-rank-perturbed ordinary orthogonality problem with an entry alphabet tied directly to the defect support.

## 5. Ordinary Gram skeletons now have subquadratic entropy

Let `L_G,L_H` be the `q x d` block-incidence matrices on row/column contrast coordinates. Then

\[
G_G=I+L_G^TL_G,
\qquad
G_H=I+L_H^TL_H.
\]

Define

\[
Q=L_GT.
\]

Every entry of `Q` is a block sum of a contrast vector and is therefore in `{-1,0,1}`. Expanding the metric equation gives

\[
\boxed{
T^TT=sI+sL_H^TL_H-Q^TQ.
}
\]

In particular, for each column `t_j`,

\[
\boxed{\|t_j\|_0=2s-\|Q_j\|_2^2,}
\qquad
2s-q\le\|t_j\|_0\le2s,
\]

and for two columns

\[
t_j\cdot t_k=
\begin{cases}
 s-Q_j\cdot Q_k,&\text{same column block},\\
 -Q_j\cdot Q_k,&\text{different column blocks}.
\end{cases}
\]

For a fixed partition, `Q` has only `3^(qd)` possibilities. Labelled partitions and anchor/switching data cost only `2^{o(r^2)}` when `q=o(r)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\text{possible ordinary column Gram skeletons }T^TT\}=2^{o(r^2)}.
}
\]

The reverse core gives the same conclusion for `TT^T`. Thus any surviving quadratic entropy must lie **inside a fixed two-sided ordinary ternary Gram fiber**, not in the Gram skeletons.

## 6. The low-rank defect is global, not a few exceptional cells

Writing `P=L_HS`, trace identities give

\[
\|T\|_F^2=2sd-\|Q\|_F^2,
\qquad
\|S\|_F^2=2sd-\|P\|_F^2.
\]

Combining these with the midpoint normal form gives

\[
\boxed{
\|D\|_F^2=2sd-\|Q\|_F^2-\|P\|_F^2.
}
\]

Because `D` is ternary,

\[
\boxed{
2d(s-q)\le |\operatorname{supp}D|\le2sd.
}
\]

Hence, in the inherited `q=o(r)` regime,

\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{supp}D|/d^2=1/2+o(1).
}
\]

This explicitly kills the tempting intermediate reduction “low rank means only a few bad entries, delete them and obtain a weighing matrix.” The defect has low **algebraic rank** but is necessarily dense on about half of all cells.

At the same time its entropy is still subquadratic: rank-`<=2q` ternary matrices number at most

\[
2^{O(qd+q\log d)}=2^{o(r^2)}.
\]

So the defect itself is also not the quadratic-entropy reservoir.

## 7. Flat cofactors and modular arithmetic

The determinant identity gives

\[
|\det T|=s^{d/2}.
\]

Since `T^{-1}=S/s`,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{adj}(T)=\pm s^{d/2-1}S.}
\]

Every cofactor of `T` is therefore exactly `0` or `+-s^(d/2-1)`.

If `alpha_1|...|alpha_d` are the Smith invariants of `T`, the integrality of `sT^{-1}` implies

\[
\boxed{\alpha_i\mid s\quad\forall i,}
\]

and their product is `s^(d/2)`. For `p^e || s`, this yields

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{F_p}(T)\le\lfloor d/2\rfloor.}
\]

If `p` divides `s` exactly once, then `d` is even and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{F_p}(T)=d/2.}
\]

If `d` is odd, every prime exponent of `s` is even, so

\[
\boxed{s\text{ is a perfect square}.}
\]

These give exact arithmetic rejection tests for any proposed core.
