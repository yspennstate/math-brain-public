## 1. Exact outside source and inherited claim

Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 9.4, Section 9 / PDF p. 15, arXiv `2005.02797`, DOI `10.1214/20-PS346`.

For the symmetric Rademacher matrix `M_n^sym`, whose entries on and above the diagonal are independent fair signs and whose lower triangle is filled by symmetry,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}(M_n^{\rm sym})\subset\mathbb Z\bigr)
=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]
Equivalently, source closure requires the total number of labelled symmetric sign matrices with integral spectrum to be `2^{o(n^2)}`.

The previous C05 report had reached two useful but still restricted facts: a uniform fixed-Gram small-row entropy estimate and a Walsh/swap-symmetric-bent reduction for one exact maximum-volume cross-block class. The two-eigenvalue boundary remained the cleanest hard test case:
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix}=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{2r\times2r},
\qquad H^2=2rI,
\]
with
\[
K:=CC^T=2rI-P^2.
\tag{1.2}
\]
The singular endpoint had already been reduced to endpoint freedom plus a Gram fiber, but the positive Gram-fiber theorem still did not beat the correct half-triangular Odlyzko exponent.

A normalization correction is worth recording: the preceding bound
\[
\log_2|\mathcal R(K)|\le r^2-\frac58r\log_2r+O(r)
\]
is valid but, for `K>0`, is dominated by the classical row-by-row Odlyzko bound `r(r+1)/2`. The useful benchmark is therefore exponent `1/2`, not exponent `1`. This run beats that benchmark by a **constant quadratic amount** whenever the Gram lower edge is bounded away from zero.

## 2. New theorem A — exact rank-adaptive bound including every corank

For
\[
\mathcal R(K)=\{C\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times r}:CC^T=K\},
\qquad \rho=\operatorname{rank}K,
\]
I proved
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal R(K)|
\le2^{\rho r-\binom\rho2}
=2^{\rho(2r-\rho+1)/2}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Proof idea, fully written in the theorem artifact: choose from `K` a fixed principal `rho x rho` positive-definite row basis. Expose those basis rows in order. At basis step `s`, the new sign row obeys `s-1` independent affine equations, so an affine-cube projection gives at most `2^{r-s+1}` choices. Once the basis rows are fixed, every dependent row is uniquely reconstructed from `K_{jI}K_{II}^{-1}`. Multiplication gives (2.1).

This is useful at the endpoint because previous C05 work proved that if `k=corank C`, then for fixed `(P,C)` the admissible symmetric `D` lie in an affine space of dimension at most `binom(k+1,2)`. Setting `rho=r-k`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\#\{(C,D)\text{ completing fixed }P\}
&\le2^{\rho r-\binom\rho2}\,2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\\
&=\boxed{2^{r(r+1)/2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]
So **all coranks are handled uniformly**, with no distinct-singular-value hypothesis. The same calculation also identifies the exact limitation of rank-only reasoning: the root entropy saved by losing rank can be canceled by endpoint affine freedom. A singular improvement must use the sign and quadratic endpoint equations, not just dimension.

A direct global corollary for the symmetric two-eigenvalue/Hadamard boundary is
\[
\boxed{
\#\{H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{2r\times2r}:H^2=2rI\}
\le2^{r(r+1)}
=2^{N^2/4+O(N)}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Indeed there are `2^{r(r+1)/2}` symmetric sign choices for `P`, and (2.2) bounds every conditioned completion. This is still quadratic and therefore only PARTIAL toward F0011.

## 3. New theorem B — spectral base-packing

Let `A` be an `m x r` sign matrix satisfying
\[
\lambda_{\min}(AA^T)\ge\gamma r.
\]
For every `d`-dimensional subspace `W`,
\[
\sum_j\|P_{W^\perp}a_j\|^2
=\operatorname{tr}(P_{W^\perp}AA^T)
\ge\gamma r(m-d).
\]
Each sign column has squared norm `m`, so at least `(gamma r/m)(m-d)` columns lie outside `W`. The standard vector-matroid base-packing criterion therefore yields
\[
\boxed{
\text{at least }\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma r}{m}\right\rfloor
\text{ pairwise disjoint bases of }\mathbb R^m.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
This is deterministic and is the bridge from covariance control to anti-concentration.

## 4. New theorem C — a constant quadratic saving for every well-conditioned positive Gram fiber

Ferber-Jain-Zhao, *On the number of Hadamard matrices via anti-concentration*, CPC 31 (2022), Theorem 1.10, prove the inhomogeneous atom inequality
\[
\sup_u\Pr\!\left(\sum_j\varepsilon_j a_j=u\right)
\le
\left(2^{-\ell}\binom\ell{\ell/2}\right)^{(r_1+\cdots+r_\ell)/\ell}
\tag{4.1}
\]
for an even `ell`-block partition with block ranks `r_i`. At `ell=4`, four full-rank blocks give `(3/8)^m`.

Let `K>0` be a sign Gram matrix with
\[
\lambda_{\min}(K)\ge\gamma r.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Expose a root `C` row by row. After `m` rows, the next row must solve an inhomogeneous system
\[
A_mx=b_m.
\]
Because `A_mA_m^T` is a principal submatrix of `K`, Cauchy interlacing preserves the same lower edge `gamma r`. For
\[
1\le m\le M:=\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma r}{4}\right\rfloor,
\]
(3.1) gives four disjoint bases of the coefficient columns. Thus FJZ gives at most
\[
2^r(3/8)^m=2^{r-m}(3/4)^m
\]
choices for the next sign row, improving the Odlyzko factor `2^{r-m}`. For later rows use Odlyzko. Multiplication gives
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal R(K)|
\le
2^{r(r+1)/2}\left(\frac34\right)^{M(M+1)/2}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\log_2|\mathcal R(K)|
\le
\left(\frac12-c_*\gamma^2\right)r^2+O_\gamma(r),
\qquad
c_*:=\frac{\log_2(4/3)}{32}\approx0.01297.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the substantive bridge of the run: the published FJZ atom theorem was designed around Hadamard/orthogonality counting, while the new spectral base-packing lemma makes it apply to **inhomogeneous fixed-Gram row constraints for arbitrary positive `K` with a lower spectral edge**.

For the F0011 half-completion `K=2rI-P^2`, if
\[
\|P\|_{op}^2\le(2-\gamma)r,
\tag{4.5}
\]
then `K>=gamma r I`; `C` is invertible, `D=-C^{-1}PC` is forced, and (4.3) bounds all completions of that fixed seed. If one merely sums over all symmetric sign seeds `P`, the corresponding well-conditioned subfamily satisfies
\[
\#H_{\rm well-cond}
\le
2^{r(r+1)}\left(\frac34\right)^{M(M+1)/2}
=
2^{(1-c_*\gamma^2)r^2+O_\gamma(r)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
In full order `N=2r`, this is a strict constant improvement below the `N^2/4` exponent for every fixed `gamma>0`. It is not yet subquadratic.

## 5. Inverse consequence — the remaining positive obstruction is forced to the soft edge

Suppose positive sign Gram matrices `K_r` have nearly baseline-size fibers,
\[
\log_2|\mathcal R(K_r)|
\ge(1/2-\varepsilon_r)r^2,
\qquad \varepsilon_r\to0.
\]
Then (4.4) forces
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\min}(K_r)/r=O(\sqrt{\varepsilon_r})+o(1).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Equivalently, for the inherited half-completion, threatening positive fibers must satisfy
\[
\boxed{\|P\|_{op}^2=(2-o(1))r.}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus the unresolved positive cone has been localized to a **spectrally near-singular soft-edge layer**.

This also explains a failure of the previous determinant-deficit route. A determinant deficit can be concentrated in one very small eigenvalue, so it need not create either a fixed lower edge or a comparable Frobenius defect. Log-determinant control alone cannot bridge the soft edge.

## 6. Singular endpoint and what is still missing

The singular endpoint was not excluded. Equation (2.2) controls every corank with the same half-triangular baseline, and the earlier balanced-cut theorem gives an additional low-corank representation on the symmetric Hadamard boundary. The new calculation is stronger conceptually: it shows that **pure rank deficiency is not itself the missing saving**, because endpoint freedom can exactly absorb that rank gain in the worst affine count.

The full source gap remains substantial. Vu requires `2^{o(N^2)}` total integral-spectrum matrices. The new theorem still leaves a positive quadratic exponent even away from the soft edge, and summing over the seed cannot be done independently if one wants source closure.

The next attack should therefore be a **soft-edge inverse theorem**, not another finite census:

1. let `v` be a near-kernel direction for a large Gram fiber with `lambda_min(K)=o(r)`;
2. translate the common near-kernel into concentration of the same linear form across many sign rows/columns;
3. apply inverse Littlewood-Offord/arithmetic-structure reasoning to show that either the fiber loses quadratic entropy or `v` belongs to a low-complexity exceptional family;
4. separately count that exceptional family, or construct a genuine quadratic-size counterfamily and thereby refute the intermediate reduction.

A second promising formulation exposes a symmetric completion directly. At row `m+1` of `[P\ C]`, symmetry fixes the first `m` coordinates of the `P` part; the unknown-coordinate coefficient Gram is
\[
2rI_m-P_{[m],[m]}^2.
\]
The same base-packing theorem applies whenever that principal soft edge is bounded below. A suitable ordering/paving theorem for the principal blocks of `P` could therefore turn the present fixed-Gram result into a direct symmetric-Hadamard entropy theorem.
