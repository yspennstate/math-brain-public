## 1. Exact outside source and inherited claim

Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 9.4, Section 9 / PDF p. 15, arXiv `2005.02797`, DOI `10.1214/20-PS346`.

For the symmetric Rademacher matrix `M_n^sym`, whose entries on and above the diagonal are independent fair signs and whose lower triangle is filled by symmetry,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}(M_n^{\rm sym})\subset\mathbb Z\bigr)
=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]
Equivalently, because there are `2^{n(n+1)/2}` labelled symmetric sign matrices, source closure requires a total integral-spectrum count `2^{o(n^2)}`.

The immediately preceding R7-06 result handled the two-eigenvalue boundary
\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N},\qquad H^2=NI,
\]
by finding a balanced cut `N=2r` whose cross block has corank at most `sqrt(N)`, and reducing the singular/corank branch to positive-definite sign-Gram fibers at only `2^{O(N^{3/2})}` additional conditioned entropy. It then proved the determinant/seed coupling
\[
\log\frac{r^r}{\det K}
\ge\frac{\|P^2-rI\|_F^2}{4r^2},
\qquad K=CC^T=2rI-P^2>0,
\]
and showed that local four-column Gram trades do not generate whole fibers.

The missing implication was a genuinely global entropy control on positive Gram fibers, especially the exact or near-Hadamard regime.

## 2. New theorem A — exact small-row marginals give a uniform positive-Gram entropy loss

For
\[
\mathcal R(K)=\{C\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times r}:CC^T=K\},\qquad K>0,
\]
let `R_i` be the rows of uniform `C`.

Right signed-column permutations preserve the fiber. This makes the two-row marginal exact:
\[
\boxed{
H_2(R_i,R_j)
=r+\log_2\binom r{(r+K_{ij})/2}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Applying Shearer's inequality and binary-entropy curvature gives
\[
\boxed{
\log_2|\mathcal R(K)|
\le
r^2-\frac{\|K-rI\|_F^2}{4r(r-1)\ln2}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
For the inherited half-completion Gram matrix `K=2rI-P^2`, this becomes the direct seed/fiber coupling
\[
\boxed{
\log_2|\mathcal R(2rI-P^2)|
\le
r^2-\frac{\|P^2-rI\|_F^2}{4r(r-1)\ln2}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Thus if the seed has correlation energy `Omega(r^4)`, the fiber automatically loses `Omega(r^2)` bits. This is an all-dimensions theorem rather than a census observation.

A four-row refinement is uniform even at `K=rI`. For four rows, after dividing by a reference row, the eight coordinate-pattern counts have seven Fourier parameters. The six pairwise Gram entries plus the total determine all but one; the final parameter is the fourth-order overlap
\[
T_{ijkl}=\sum_s R_{i,s}R_{j,s}R_{k,s}R_{l,s}.
\]
There are at most `r+1` possible values of `T`. Conditional on `T`, signed-column symmetry makes the row quartet uniform over one eight-cell type, whose central multinomial size is at most
\[
C\,8^r r^{-7/2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
H_2(R_i,R_j,R_k,R_l)
\le4r-\frac52\log_2r+O(1).
\]
A second Shearer application yields the uniform theorem
\[
\boxed{
\log_2|\mathcal R(K)|
\le r^2-\frac58r\log_2r+O(r)
\quad\text{for every positive sign Gram }K.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
Using the inherited exact relation
\[
|\mathcal R(K)|=2^r r!\,|\mathcal B(K)|
\]
for sign-class support representations,
\[
\boxed{
\log_2|\mathcal B(K)|
\le r^2-\frac{13}{8}r\log_2r+O(r).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
This does not yet make the exponent `o(r^2)`, but it removes a uniform, rigorously quantified amount of entropy from **every** positive Gram fiber, including the Hadamard point.

## 3. New theorem B — the Sylvester max-volume fixed-spectrum branch is a swap-symmetric bent-function problem

At exact maximum volume `K=rI`, the inherited logdet theorem forces
\[
P^2=rI,\qquad CC^T=rI.
\]
Hence both `P` and `C` are Hadamard matrices.

Take the canonical hard cross-block class `r=2^m` and normalize `C` to the Sylvester/Walsh matrix
\[
W_{x,y}=(-1)^{x\cdot y},\qquad x,y\in\mathbb F_2^m,
\qquad W^2=rI.
\]
The block equation forces
\[
D=-\frac1rWPW.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Write `P_{x,y}=(-1)^{f(x,y)}`. Then the entries of `WPW` are exactly the Walsh transform of the Boolean function `f` on `2m` variables. Consequently
\[
\boxed{
D\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times r}
\iff f\text{ is bent},
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
P=P^T
\iff f(x,y)=f(y,x).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Thus every actual max-volume completion in this normalized cross class injects into the swap-symmetric bent functions, with the additional seed-Hadamard condition `P^2=rI`.

Because the cross block is invertible, `D=-C^{-1}PC` implies `tr D=-tr P`; hence the full matrix has trace zero and its two eigenvalues have equal multiplicity `r`. For the F0011 integral-spectrum branch `N=2r=2^{m+1}`, this requires `m` odd, so `sqrt(N)` is integral. This is therefore a genuine fixed-spectrum slice, not merely an unrestricted Hadamard count.

## 4. New theorem C — swap symmetry improves the bent-function exponent to `1/4`

Rothaus's classical theorem says that a bent function on `2m>2` variables has algebraic degree at most `m`.

A monomial is indexed by `(A,B)` with degree `|A|+|B|`; the matrix swap sends `(A,B)` to `(B,A)`. Among all monomials of degree at most `m`, let
\[
M_m=\sum_{d=0}^m\binom{2m}{d}
=2^{2m-1}+\frac12\binom{2m}{m},
\]
and let
\[
F_m=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\binom mj
\]
be the fixed monomials. The number of swap orbits is exactly
\[
O_m=\frac{M_m+F_m}{2}
=\left(\frac14+O(m^{-1/2})\right)r^2.
\]
Therefore the normalized Walsh branch has at most
\[
\boxed{
2^{(1/4+O(m^{-1/2}))r^2}
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
possible principal seeds `P` before even imposing `P^2=rI`.

Allowing the entire Sylvester Hadamard equivalence orbit for the cross block costs only
\[
(2^r r!)^2=2^{O(r\log r)}.
\]
Hence the full labelled max-volume branch with cross block Sylvester-equivalent satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\#\text{completions}
\le2^{(1/4+o(1))r^2}
=2^{(1/16+o(1))N^2}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
This is a leading-quadratic entropy improvement on a canonical exact hard fixed-spectrum family. It is still not `2^{o(N^2)}`, so it is **restricted progress**, not closure.

For context, Potapov's 2024 general bent-function upper bound has logarithmic exponent `(11/32+o(1))2^{2m}`. The `1/4` here comes from the extra swap symmetry forced by the symmetric-matrix problem.

## 5. Counterfamily test — the raw Maiorana-McFarland freedom collapses completely under swap symmetry

The obvious huge bent family is
\[
f(x,y)=x\cdot\pi(y)+g(y),
\]
with `pi` a permutation of `F_2^m` and `g` arbitrary. I tested whether this family could defeat the preceding reduction by supplying quadratically many symmetric completions.

It cannot. Swap symmetry holds if and only if
\[
\boxed{
\pi(y)=Ay+c,
\qquad
g(y)=c\cdot y+c_0,
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
where `A` is an invertible symmetric `m x m` binary matrix, `c in F_2^m`, and `c_0 in F_2`.

Proof: setting `x=0` gives `g(y)=c\cdot y+c_0` with `c=pi(0)`. Writing `phi(y)=pi(y)+c`, symmetry reduces to
\[
x\cdot\phi(y)=y\cdot\phi(x).
\]
Taking `x=e_i` makes every coordinate of `phi` linear, so `phi(y)=Ay`; the displayed identity is exactly `A=A^T`, and bijectivity is exactly `A` invertible.

Thus the raw symmetric Maiorana-McFarland subfamily has exactly
\[
\#\{A=A^T\in GL(m,2)\}\,2^{m+1}
\le2^{m(m+1)/2+m+1}
=2^{O((\log r)^2)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Every member gives a genuine valid max-volume completion, but this family is far too small to be a quadratic-entropy obstruction. This statement is only about the raw Maiorana-McFarland representation, not its completed affine-equivalence closure.
