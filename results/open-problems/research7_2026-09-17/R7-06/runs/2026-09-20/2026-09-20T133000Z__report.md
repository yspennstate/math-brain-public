## 1. Source and inherited reduction

Vu's Conjecture 9.4 asks, for the symmetric Rademacher matrix `M_n^sym`,

\[
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}(M_n^{\rm sym})\subset\mathbb Z\bigr)
=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}.
\]

The source is the total integral-spectrum event, not a fixed-spectrum statement. C05 remains a restricted centered two-eigenvalue bridge.

The immediately preceding C05 theorem reduced the entropy-dangerous `|r-lambda|=4`, `q=o(r)` kernel-orientation fiber to one binary matrix `X`, low-rank defect mask `E`, anchor signs `U,V`, and the exact signed integer relation

\[
(V\odot X)(U\odot(X\oplus E))^T=sI.
\]

It also proved that

\[
A=X\odot(1-E)
\]

is an exact `s`-regular bipartite graph, hence has `s` disjoint perfect matchings. Modulo `2`, when `s` is odd,

\[
X+E=X^{-T},
\qquad E=X+X^{-T},
\qquad \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}E=o(r).
\]

A tempting next reduction was to try to control the orientation using only this low-rank finite-field defect. The present theorem disproves that reduction explicitly.

## 2. Fixed rank-two, zero-diagonal, half-dense defect

Let `d=2m` with `m` odd and work over `F_2^d`. Put

\[
u=(\mathbf1_m,0),\qquad v=(0,\mathbf1_m).
\]

Then

\[
u^Tu=v^Tv=1,\qquad u^Tv=0.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
E=uv^T+vu^T
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&J_m\\
J_m&0
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

This single fixed defect satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}E=2,
\qquad \operatorname{diag}E=0,
\qquad |\operatorname{supp}E|=d^2/2.
}
\]

Thus it simultaneously has the three qualitative properties that a pure finite-field attack would hope to exploit: tiny rank, zero diagonal, and macroscopic density.

## 3. Seed and symplectic orbit

Set

\[
X_0=I+uv^T.
\]

Because `v^Tu=0`, `(uv^T)^2=0`, so `X_0^{-1}=X_0`. Consequently

\[
X_0^{-T}=I+vu^T
\]

and

\[
\boxed{X_0+X_0^{-T}=E.}
\]

Let

\[
L=\operatorname{span}\{u,v\},\qquad W=L^\perp.
\]

The plane `L` is nondegenerate. Since `u+v=\mathbf1_d`, every `w in W` has even weight, hence `w^Tw=0`; the restriction of the standard form to `W` is therefore nondegenerate alternating. Thus

\[
\dim W=d-2=2a,
\qquad a=m-1,
\]

and `W` is a symplectic space.

For every `R in Sp(W)`, extend `R` by the identity on `L` to an orthogonal transformation `Q_R` of `F_2^d` and put

\[
X_R=Q_RX_0.
\]

Because `Q_R` fixes `u,v` and is orthogonal,

\[
X_R^{-T}=Q_RX_0^{-T}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{X_R+X_R^{-T}=E}
\]

for the **same fixed defect** `E`. The map `R -> X_R` is injective.

## 4. Quadratic orientation entropy survives fixed rank two

The exact symplectic group order is

\[
|Sp(2a,2)|=2^{a^2}\prod_{i=1}^a(2^{2i}-1).
\]

Hence

\[
\log_2|Sp(2a,2)|=2a^2+a+O(1)
=\frac12d^2-O(d).
\]

So this one fixed defect has at least

\[
\boxed{2^{d^2/2-O(d)}}
\]

distinct solutions of

\[
X+X^{-T}=E.
\]

This is a source-scale quadratic family even though `rank(E)=2`, `diag(E)=0`, and its support density is exactly `1/2`.

## 5. Consequence for the F0011 strategy

This is an explicit counterexample to the intermediate statement

> low rank + dense support + zero diagonal, together with `X+E=X^{-T}` over `F_2`, should imply only `2^{o(d^2)}` orientations.
