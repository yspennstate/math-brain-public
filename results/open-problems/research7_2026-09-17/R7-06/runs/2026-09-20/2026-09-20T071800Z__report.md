## 1. Exact source and inherited bottleneck

Van H. Vu, *Recent progress in combinatorial random matrix theory*, Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 9.4, Section 9 / PDF p. 15, arXiv `2005.02797`, DOI `10.1214/20-PS346`.

For the symmetric Rademacher matrix `M_n^sym`, with diagonal and upper-triangular entries independent fair signs and the lower triangle filled by symmetry,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}(M_n^{\rm sym})\subset\mathbb Z\bigr)
=2^{-(1/2+o(1))n^2}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Equivalently, the total number of labelled symmetric sign matrices with integral spectrum must be `2^{o(n^2)}`. The active restricted family is still

\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N},\qquad H^2=NI,
\tag{1.2}
\]

with `N=2r`.

The preceding run proved two ingredients for a balanced half-space `E`, `Q=H/sqrt(N)`, `V=QE`:

1. near-Odlyzko-saturated half-rowspaces form a `2^{o(N^2)}` family in the explicit low-deficiency regime;
2. for fixed `V`, the completion count satisfies

\[
F(V)\le 2^{L(V)},
\]

where

\[
L(V)=a(a+1)+b^2+
\sum_{\theta\in(0,\pi/2)}\frac{m_\theta(m_\theta+1)}2
\tag{1.3}
\]

and `a,b,m_theta` are principal-angle multiplicities. A previously proved good-cut theorem supplies

\[
a=\operatorname{corank}C\le\sqrt{2r}
\tag{1.4}
\]

for the cross block `C` in

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\C^T&D\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The sharp unresolved point was a macroscopic repeated principal angle. This run attacks that branch directly.

## 2. New theorem A — trace locks the endpoint mass

Because `C` and `D` are both `r x r` sign matrices while their normalized singular values are respectively `sin(theta_i)` and `cos(theta_i)`, one has the exact identities

\[
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^r\sin^2\theta_i=\frac r2,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\cos^2\theta_i=\frac r2.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular

\[
\boxed{a\le r/2,\qquad b\le r/2.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This matters because the endpoint `pi/2` block has coefficient `b^2` in (1.3); (2.2) prevents it from silently replacing an almost-full interior block in a near-maximal entropy profile.

## 3. New theorem B — near-maximal fixed-rowspace entropy forces one `(r-o(r))`-fold interior angle

Let

\[
m=\max_{\theta\in(0,\pi/2)}m_\theta,
\qquad q=r-m,
\qquad B_r=\frac{r(r+1)}2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If a good-cut fixed rowspace has

\[
\log_2F(V)\ge B_r-\Delta_r,
\qquad \Delta_r=o(r^2),
\tag{3.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{m=r-o(r),\qquad q=o(r).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The proof first shows that if `m<=3r/4`, then

\[
\boxed{L(V)\le \frac{7}{16}r^2+O(r),}
\tag{3.4}
\]

which leaves a fixed quadratic gap below `B_r=(1/2)r^2+O(r)`. Once `q<=r/4`, concentrating all remaining interior multiplicity gives the quantitative inverse bound

\[
\boxed{
B_r-L(V)\ge\frac58rq-2a^2-a.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Since `F(V)<=2^{L(V)}` and `a<=sqrt(2r)`, this implies

\[
\boxed{
q\le\frac{8}{5r}\bigl(\Delta_r+2a^2+a\bigr)=o(r).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus the completion entropy cannot remain within `o(r^2)` of its maximal principal-angle parameter dimension unless essentially the entire half-space lies in one interior principal-angle block.

## 4. New theorem C — that dominant angle is an exact low-rank near-Hadamard deformation

Let `theta_*` have multiplicity `m=r-q` and put

\[
\lambda=2r\sin^2\theta_*.
\]

Then `lambda` is an eigenvalue of `K=CC^T` of multiplicity `r-q`, and the block equations from `H^2=2rI` imply

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(CC^T-\lambda I)=q,}
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(C^TC-\lambda I)=q,}
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(P^2-(2r-\lambda)I)=q,}
\tag{4.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(D^2-(2r-\lambda)I)=q.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The trace `tr(CC^T)=r^2`, together with `0<=CC^T<=2rI`, gives

\[
\boxed{|\lambda-r|\le\frac{rq}{r-q}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore the entropy-dangerous branch is an `o(r)`-rank deformation of the smaller Hadamard equations, with scalar `lambda=r+o(r)`.

This explicitly retains the singular endpoint. Since

\[
a+b+\sum_{\theta\ne\theta_*}m_\theta=q,
\tag{4.6}
\]

all cross-block kernel directions, all `pi/2` endpoint directions and all other interior angles are part of the same `o(r)` exceptional sector. No invertibility assumption on `C` is used.

## 5. New theorem D — arithmetic collapse of the repeated scalar

Because `K=CC^T` is integer symmetric, if an eigenvalue `lambda` has multiplicity `m>r/2`, its minimal polynomial degree `d` satisfies

\[
dm\le r.
\]

Hence `d=1` and

\[
\boxed{\lambda\in\mathbb Z.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

There is a further parity restriction. Modulo two, `K` is the zero matrix if `r` is even and the all-ones matrix if `r` is odd. Its mod-two characteristic polynomial is therefore `x^r` in the even case and `x^{r-1}(x-1)` in the odd case. Consequently any integer eigenvalue of multiplicity greater than one must be even:

\[
\boxed{m>1\Longrightarrow\lambda\in2\mathbb Z.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Combining (4.5) with the spacing of even integers shows that in the near-extremal regime there are only

\[
\boxed{O(q+1)}
\tag{5.3}
\]

possible repeated scalars. Thus the remaining defect branch is not a continuum of angles; it is a low-rank perturbation around a short discrete list of near-`r` scalar Gram values.

## 6. Exact extremizer = literal smaller Hadamard recursion

At zero defect, `q=0`, the trace identity forces

\[
\lambda=r,\qquad \theta_*=\pi/4,
\]

and the block equations become

\[
\boxed{CC^T=C^TC=rI,\qquad P^2=D^2=rI,\qquad PC+CD=0.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus `C` is a Hadamard matrix of order `r`, while `P,D` are symmetric Hadamard matrices of order `r`. Conversely any compatible triple with (6.1) reconstructs a symmetric Hadamard of order `2r`.

Writing `S(r)` for the number of labelled symmetric Hadamards and `H(r)` for the number of labelled Hadamards,

\[
\boxed{A_{\rm full}(2r)\le S(r)^2H(r)}
\tag{6.2}
\]

for a fixed balanced cut. So the exact principal-angle entropy extremizer is **literally a smaller Hadamard recursion**, not merely analogous to one.

This also makes the hardness boundary honest: the `q=0` member of the new low-rank defect class already contains ordinary Hadamard enumeration. A generic `2^{o(r^2)}` theorem for all `q=o(r)` would therefore have to address that known hard core rather than avoid it.

## 7. Current literature and prior-art correction

The canonical source remains Vu, arXiv `2005.02797` / Probability Surveys 18 (2021), Conjecture 9.4. A bounded fresh search on 20 September 2026 did not surface a proof of the exact conjecture; this is negative search evidence only.

Ferber--Jain--Zhao, *On the number of Hadamard matrices via anti-concentration* (published online 10 September 2021; CPC 31 (2022), 455--477), remains the relevant general asymptotic boundary: it gives a constant quadratic improvement in Hadamard enumeration, not `2^{o(n^2)}`.

The fresh search also found relevant prior art for the cube-section side. Melo--Winter, *Intersection patterns of linear subspaces with the hypercube* (JCTA 164 (2019), 60--71; arXiv `1712.01763`), already prove that the second-largest possible `k`-dimensional cube intersection is `(3/4)2^k`. Groenland--Johnston, *Intersection sizes of linear subspaces with the hypercube* (arXiv `1810.02729`; retrieved version dated 22 March 2026), classify the large intersection cardinalities more sharply. Therefore the earlier C05 qualitative deficiency-gap observation is **not** treated as an external novelty claim. The principal-angle/low-rank recursion result above is separate.

Valtonen, *Classification of Symmetric Hadamard Matrices Up to Order 32*, arXiv `2608.06920v2`, revised 10 September 2026, is finite classification and does not provide the needed asymptotic entropy theorem.
