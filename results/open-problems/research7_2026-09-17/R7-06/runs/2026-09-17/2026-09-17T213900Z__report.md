## 5. B11 — half-shift product inequality by Jacobi duality

### Lemma 5.1
For every `c>0` and every real `t`,

`vartheta_c(t) vartheta_c(t+1/2)
 >= vartheta_c(0) vartheta_c(1/2)`.                              (5.1)

Also

`vartheta_c(t) <= vartheta_c(0)`.                               (5.2)

### Proof

Set

`q=exp(-pi^2/c)`.

Poisson summation gives

`vartheta_c(t)=sqrt(pi/c) theta_3(pi t,q)`,

`vartheta_c(t+1/2)=sqrt(pi/c) theta_4(pi t,q)`.                  (5.3)

Use the exact Jacobi duplication identity

`theta_3(z,q) theta_4(z,q)
 = theta_4(0,q^2) theta_4(2z,q^2)`.                             (5.4)

For real `w`, the triple product is

`theta_4(w,Q)=prod_{n>=1}(1-Q^{2n})
 (1-2Q^{2n-1} cos(2w)+Q^{4n-2})`.                              (5.5)

Every `w`-dependent factor in (5.5) is minimized when `cos(2w)=1`; hence

`theta_4(w,Q)>=theta_4(0,Q)`.                                   (5.6)

Apply (5.6) to (5.4), then evaluate (5.4) at `z=0`:

`theta_3(z,q)theta_4(z,q)
 >=theta_4(0,q^2)^2

Multiplying by `pi/c` proves (5.1).  Finally, the Poisson Fourier series of `vartheta_c` has positive cosine coefficients, so it is maximized at an integer, proving (5.2).  QED.

## 6. B25 — exact conductor-27 coefficient domination by an odd-mode injection

The key new arithmetic inequality is stronger than merely comparing a few theta values numerically.

### Lemma 6.1
For every `b>0`,

`vartheta_{27b}(1/6)/vartheta_{27b}(1/3)
 > vartheta_b(0)/vartheta_b(1/2)`.                               (6.1)

Equivalently,

`B/A > U0/U1`.                                                   (6.2)

### Proof

Let

`r=exp(-pi^2/(27b)) in (0,1)`.

By Poisson summation, the left side of (6.1) is

`L(r)=theta_3(pi/6,r)/theta_3(pi/3,r)`,                          (6.3)

while the right side is

`R(r)=theta_3(0,r^27)/theta_4(0,r^27)`.                          (6.4)

For `0<x<1`, set

`h(x)=log((1+x)/(1-x))`.                                        (6.5)

The function `h` is positive and strictly increasing.  In the Jacobi triple product for `theta_3`, if `m=2n-1` is odd, the ratio of the `pi/6` factor to the `pi/3` factor is

`(1+r^m+r^(2m))/(1-r^m+r^(2m))`.                                (6.6)

Use

`1+x+x^2=(1-x^3)/(1-x)`,

`1-x+x^2=(1+x^3)/(1+x)`,

to obtain

`log[(1+x+x^2)/(1-x+x^2)] = h(x)-h(x^3)`.                       (6.7)

The corresponding absolutely convergent sum telescopes by residue class:

`log L(r)
 = sum_{m odd}[h(r^m)-h(r^(3m))]
 = sum_{k odd, 3 not divides k} h(r^k)`.                         (6.8)

On the other hand, the triple products at zero give

`log R(r)=2 sum_{m odd} h(r^(27m))`.                             (6.9)

Now perform an explicit injection.  For each positive odd `m`, choose the two indices

`k1(m)=27m-2`, `k2(m)=27m-4`.                                   (6.10)

They are positive and odd, neither is divisible by three, and all selected indices are pairwise distinct: their residues modulo 27 are respectively 25 and 23.  Moreover

`k1(m)<27m`, `k2(m)<27m`.                                       (6.11)

Since `0<r<1`, smaller exponent means larger argument of the increasing function `h`; therefore

`h(r^(k1(m)))+h(r^(k2(m)))
 > 2 h(r^(27m))`.                                                (6.12)

The left side of (6.12), summed over positive odd `m`, uses only a subset of the positive terms in (6.8), while the right side is exactly (6.9).  Hence

`log L(r)>log R(r)`,

which proves (6.1)--(6.2).  QED.

This is the main B25 move: the conductor-27 scale separation is not estimated asymptotically; two explicit residue-allowed odd modes below each `27m` mode dominate it term by term after the exact logarithmic product rewrite.
