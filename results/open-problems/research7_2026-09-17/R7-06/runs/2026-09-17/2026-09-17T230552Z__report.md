## 3. Recovered starting equations for the final recovery

Hardin–Tenpas transform the `A2` fundamental triangle to

`D = {(x,y): 1/2 <= y <= 1, -1 <= x <= b(y)},`

`b(y)=4y^3-3y=T_3(y)`.

For `m=3`, the two nonidentity interpolation nodes are

`p=(-1/2,1),    q=(-1,1/2)`.

The complete `A2`-degree `<6` invariant witness space is

`g=c0+A alpha+B beta+C gamma+Dcoef delta`,

with nonconstant coefficients required to be nonnegative. I write the fourth free coefficient as `Dcoef` in prose when needed to avoid confusing it with the transformed domain `D`. The exact basis is

`alpha=(-1+2y(x+y))/3`,

`beta=(-1+2x(x-3y+4y^3))/3`,

`gamma=[2T_2(x)T_2(y)+T_4(y)]/3`,

`delta=[T_3(x)y+xT_5(y)+T_2(x)T_4(y)]/3`.

For any transformed Gaussian `F=F_a`, imposing

`g(p)=F(p),  g(q)=F(q),  g_x(p)=F_x(p)`

eliminates `c0,A,B` exactly:

`A=(3/2)F_x(p)+2C+Dcoef/2`,

`B=(1/2)F_x(p)+C+Dcoef/2-(2/3)[F(p)-F(q)]`,

`c0=F(p)+B/2`.

Thus the entire low-degree certificate family is truly two-dimensional after the forced contacts. The previous exact necessary walls (I)–(III), together with `C,Dcoef,B>=0`, provide a bounded parameter wedge whenever they are consistent.

## 4. B8 — discretize the certificate problem: exact affine response and a finite infeasibility principle

### 4.1 Exact response kernels

Because the eliminated coefficients depend affinely on `C,Dcoef`, differentiating `g` with respect to those two free variables gives two **universal polynomial response kernels**, independent of the Gaussian parameter:

`K_C := partial g / partial C = 1/2 + 2 alpha + beta + gamma`,

`K_D := partial g / partial Dcoef = 1/4 + (1/2)alpha + (1/2)beta + delta`.

Therefore, if `H_00` denotes the residual at `C=Dcoef=0`, then for every fixed `a`

`boxed{ H_{C,Dcoef}(x,y)=H_00(x,y)-C K_C(x,y)-Dcoef K_D(x,y). }`       (4.1)

This identity is exact. It turns continuum witness selection into a two-variable **semi-infinite linear feasibility problem**: every domain point contributes one affine half-plane in `(C,Dcoef)`.

### 4.2 The C-response factors globally

Exact symbolic elimination gives

`boxed{
K_C(x,y)
 = [(2y-1)(2y+1)(4x^2+4xy+4y^2-3)]/6.
}`                                                               (4.2)

Since `y>=1/2` on the transformed domain, the first two factors are nonnegative. Away from the bottom endpoint `y=1/2`, the sign of `K_C` is therefore exactly the sign of

`x^2+xy+y^2-3/4`.                                                (4.3)

So the effect of increasing `C` changes sign across one explicit conic. This is a concrete reason a single monotone `C(a)` argument was unlikely to cover the compact middle regime: the same coefficient pushes the residual in opposite directions on the two sides of (4.3).

On the slanted Weyl boundary `x=b(y)=T_3(y)`, (4.2) becomes

`boxed{
K_C(b(y),y)
 = [(2y-1)^3(2y+1)^3(4y^2-3)]/6.
}`                                                               (4.4)

Thus the C-response changes sign on that boundary exactly at

`y=sqrt(3)/2`                                                     (4.5)

in addition to the endpoint zero at `y=1/2`. The target nodes themselves lie on the response-zero geometry forced by interpolation.

For comparison, the other free direction has the exact boundary restriction

`boxed{
K_D(b(y),y)
 = [(2y-1)^2(2y+1)^2
    (128y^6-224y^4+104y^2-5)]/12.
}`                                                               (4.6)

The global `K_D` polynomial does not acquire an equally useful one-conic factorization, so the `Dcoef=0` face identified below is not merely cosmetic: it removes the more complicated response direction from the first post-small-`a` atlas cell.

### 4.3 Compact wedge + Helly gives a finite obstruction certificate

Let `W_a` be the set cut out by the already proved necessary constraints (I)–(III) and coefficient nonnegativity. If `W_a` is empty, the low-degree family is already exactly ruled out by finitely many necessary inequalities.

Assume `W_a` is nonempty. Constraint (II) gives a finite upper bound on `Dcoef`; constraint (III), together with that bound and `C>=0`, gives a finite upper bound on `C`. Hence `W_a` is a compact convex polygonal set.

For each `z=(x,y)` in the compact transformed domain, define the closed half-plane

`P_z(a)={ (C,Dcoef): H_00(z)-C K_C(z)-Dcoef K_D(z) >= 0 }`.

The low-degree witness family is feasible exactly when

`W_a intersect (intersect_{z in D} P_z(a)) != empty`.            (4.7)

If (4.7) is empty, then the open complements of the `P_z(a)` cover the compact set `W_a`, so a **finite** subcover exists. Applying Helly's theorem in `R^2` to that finite family together with `W_a` shows that low-degree infeasibility has a certificate consisting of at most three convex constraints from this family (where `W_a` itself may be one of the three).

This does **not** discretize the positive proof: the witnessing domain points still have to be located and certified. It does, however, prove that failure of the five-dimensional low-degree ansatz is not an intrinsically infinite-dimensional phenomenon. If that ansatz fails at a fixed Gaussian parameter, there is a finite active obstruction of dimension at most three. ## 5. B19 — make witness selection an algorithm: a scalar active-contact equation on the Dcoef=0 face

`z_s=(T_3(s),s),   1/2<s<1`,                                    (5.2)

with `K_C(z_s) != 0`. Equality of the residual at that point forces

`C = H_00(z_s)/K_C(z_s)`.                                       (5.3)

All functions involved are `A2`/Weyl invariant. The slanted boundary is the fixed set of the corresponding reflection. `boxed{

}`                                                               (5.4)

Equivalently,

`H_00'(s) K_C(s) - H_00(s) K_C'(s)=0`.                          (5.5)

This is the key B19 reduction: at each Gaussian parameter `a`, the proposed first active atlas branch on `Dcoef=0` is found by solving **one scalar equation in one scalar boundary coordinate**.
