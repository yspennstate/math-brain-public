## 5. B14 — exploit the A2 symmetry: the omega*_9 problem has only two nontrivial interpolation nodes

Hardin–Tenpas work in

`Delta_A2 = {(x1,x2): 0 <= x1 <= 1/2, 0 <= x2 <= x1/sqrt(3)}`

with change of variables

`t1 = cos(2 pi x1),   t2 = cos(2 pi x2/sqrt(3))`.

Thus the transformed triangular domain is

`D = {(t1,t2): 1/2 <= t2 <= 1, -1 <= t1 <= b(t2)}`,  
`b(t2)=4 t2^3-3 t2`,

because the slanted boundary `x2=x1/sqrt(3)` becomes `t1=cos(3 arccos t2)=4t2^3-3t2`.

For the family `omega*_{m^2}`, Hardin–Tenpas give interpolation nodes

`(cos(pi k1/m), cos(pi k2/m))`

with `0 <= 3 k2 <= k1 <= m` and `k1=k2 (mod 2)`. For `m=3`, the nodes are exactly

`(1,1)`,  `p=(-1/2,1)`,  `q=(-1,1/2)`.

The identity node `(1,1)` is excluded in Corollary 13, so **omega*_9 requires equality at only two nontrivial nodes `p,q`**.

Now use their `A2`-degree. Let

`v'=[1,1/sqrt(3)]`, `v''=[2,0]`,

with degree `D(k0 v'+k1 v'')=2k0+3k1`. Their Section 3.3 observes that all modes of degree `<2m` are allowed for the `m^2` family. At `m=3`, the only degree `<6` pairs `(k0,k1)` are

`(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1)`.

Therefore the complete low-degree invariant ansatz is five-dimensional:

`g = c0 + A alpha + B beta + C gamma + D delta`,  with `A,B,C,D >= 0`,

where

`alpha = (-1+2 t2(t1+t2))/3`,

`beta = (-1+2 t1(t1-3t2+4t2^3))/3`,

`gamma = [2 T2(t1)T2(t2)+T4(t2)]/3`,

`delta = [T3(t1)t2+t1 T5(t2)+T2(t1)T4(t2)]/3`.

Here `gamma=P_{2v'}` and `delta=P_{v'+v''}`. The displayed formulas follow directly by averaging the corresponding Fourier characters over the dihedral `A2` orbit and pairing opposite vectors into cosines.

This removes the arbitrary-polynomial ambiguity from the first exact finite attack: the natural `A2`-degree `<6` problem is a five-coefficient positive-definite minorant problem.

## 6. B16 — exact small-case reduction to two free coefficients

Let `F` denote the transformed periodized Gaussian potential `F~_{a,A2}` from Hardin–Tenpas, fix `a>0`, and write

`Fp=F(p)`, `Fq=F(q)`, `Fr=F(r)`, `Delta=Fp-Fq`,

where `r=(-1,1)`, and let `F1,F2,F22` denote partial derivatives in `t1,t2`.

Assume a low-degree magic interpolant `g` exists, meaning

1. `A,B,C,D >= 0`,
2. `g <= F` on the full transformed triangular domain,
3. `g(p)=F(p)` and `g(q)=F(q)`.

### 6.1 Tangency forced at p

The point `p=(-1/2,1)` lies in the relative interior of the top boundary segment `t2=1`. Along that boundary `t1` varies on both sides of `p`. Thus for `h=F-g >=0` with `h(p)=0`, necessarily

`h_1(p)=0`, i.e. `g_1(p)=F_1(p)`.

The exact basis value vectors are

`V(p) = (1,0,-1/2,0,0)`,

`V(q) = (1,-1/2,1,-1/2,-1/2)`,

and

`V_1(p)=(0,2/3,0,-4/3,-1/3)`.

Solving the two equality conditions and this forced tangency for `c0,A,B` leaves only `C,D` free:

`A = (3/2) F1(p) + 2C + D/2`,

`B = (1/2) F1(p) + C + D/2 - (2/3) Delta`,

`c0 = Fp + B/2`.

Since Hardin–Tenpas Lemma 15 gives `F1(p)>0`, positivity of `A` is automatic once `C,D>=0`. Positivity of `B` remains an explicit scalar constraint.

### 6.2 Inward derivative condition at p

The domain lies below `t2=1`. Moving inward as `t2=1-s`, `s>=0`, the nonnegative contact function `h` must have

`d/ds h(p_1,1-s)|_{s=0} >=0`,

so `g_2(p) >= F_2(p)`.

Exact differentiation of the invariant basis gives

`V_2(p)=(0,1,-3,4,-13/2)`.

After the elimination above,

`g_2(p)=3C-(15/2)D+2 Delta`.

Hence every such low-degree certificate must satisfy

`C >= [F2(p)-2 Delta]/3 + (5/2)D`.                         (I)

### 6.3 Curved-boundary second derivative at q

At `q=(-1,1/2)`, the slanted boundary is

`t1=b(t2)=4t2^3-3t2`.

Writing `t2=1/2+s`, one has `b'(1/2)=0` and `b''(1/2)=6`. Also all five low-degree basis functions have zero `t2` derivative at `q`, while Hardin–Tenpas Lemma 15 gives `F_2(q)=0`. Therefore first-order contact along this boundary is automatic.

The second derivative of `h(b(1/2+s),1/2+s)` at zero must be nonnegative, giving

`[F22(q)+6F1(q)] - [g22(q)+6g1(q)] >= 0`.

The exact basis derivatives are

`V_1(q)=(0,1/3,-2,4/3,7/3)`,

`V_22(q)=(0,4/3,-8,16/3,28/3)`.

After elimination,

`g22(q)+6g1(q) = -5F1(p) + (40/3)Delta +15D`.

Thus

`D <= [F22(q)+6F1(q)+5F1(p)-(40/3)Delta]/15`.             (II)

This condition is notable because **C cancels exactly**.

### 6.4 The top-left corner gives a second independent upper wall

At `r=(-1,1)`,

`V(r)=(1,-1/3,-1/3,1,-1/3)`.

Substitution gives

`g(r)=Fp-(5/12)F1(p)+(1/2)C-(5/12)D-Delta/9`.

The global minorant requirement at this one point already forces

`C <= 2(Fr-Fp)+(5/6)F1(p)+(2/9)Delta+(5/6)D`.             (III)

Together, (I) and (III) imply an explicit upper bound on `D`; combining that with (II), `D>=0`, `C>=0`, and `B>=0` reduces the first low-degree omega*_9 search to an exact **two-parameter feasibility wedge** before any continuum positivity proof is attempted.

This is the main new mathematical result of the run. It does not prove the wedge is nonempty for every Gaussian `a`, and even a failure of this low-degree ansatz would only kill this certificate family, not refute Cohn–Kumar.
