`alpha=(-1+2y(x+y))/3`,

## 4. B5 — add the Gaussian parameter and inspect Fourier shells

### 4.1 Exact orbit polynomial formula

For the `A2` orbit represented by integers `k1>=k2>=0` of the same parity, direct dihedral averaging gives

`P_{k1,k2}(x,y) = (1/3)[`

`  T_{k1}(x)T_{k2}(y)`

` +T_{(k1-k2)/2}(x)T_{(3k1+k2)/2}(y)`

` +T_{(k1+k2)/2}(x)T_{(3k1-k2)/2}(y) ].`

The exact checker recovers `alpha,beta,gamma,delta` from `(1,1),(2,0),(2,2),(3,1)` respectively.

In the normalization relevant to the Poisson expansion, the squared dual norms are

`|P_{1,1}|^2=4/3`,
`|P_{2,0}|^2=4`,
`|P_{2,2}|^2=16/3`,
`|P_{3,1}|^2=28/3`.

The **first Fourier orbit not contained in the degree-<6 interpolation space** is

`eta := P_{3,3}`

with squared norm `12`. The next shell has squared norm `16`, represented by `P_{4,0}`. Thus there is a strict spectral gap of `4` in squared norm after the first forbidden shell.

### 4.2 Why this shell controls `a -> 0`

By Poisson summation, the transformed periodized Gaussian admits an orbit expansion

`F_a = c_a + sum_O w_O(a) P_O`,

with every nonconstant coefficient positive and

`w_O(a) = K_a |O| exp(-pi^2 |v_O|^2/a)`

up to the common positive normalization `K_a` and the orbit normalization convention. Only positivity and relative exponential scales are used below.

Consequently, after the four allowed low shells, the leading omitted term as `a -> 0` is `w_eta(a) eta`, while the sum of all subsequent shells is smaller by a relative factor

`O(exp(-4 pi^2/a))`

in every fixed polynomial-derivative norm, including `C^2(D)`. The derivative growth of Chebyshev orbit polynomials is polynomial in the shell index, while the Gaussian Fourier coefficient decay is exponential, so the full differentiated tail has the same gap.

This suggests solving the finite interpolation problem first for the single forbidden mode `eta`, then treating all later shells perturbatively.

## 5. B20 — the obvious fully saturated Hermite algorithm fails

A natural algorithm is to impose, in addition to the required value contacts at `p,q` and top tangency at `p`, equality in both local necessary walls from the preceding run: match `F_2(p)` and match the second derivative along the curved boundary at `q`.

For the single shell `eta=P_{3,3}`, these five linear conditions uniquely determine the low-degree interpolant. Exact elimination gives

`[c0,A,B,C,D]=[-2,-10,-6,-4,-4]`.

The resulting residual factors exactly as

`R_sat = eta-h_sat`

`      = (1/3)(2y-1)(2y+1)(2xy+2y^2-1)(4x^2+4xy+4y^2-3)`.

On the top boundary `y=1`,

`R_sat(x,1)=(2x+1)^3`.

Hence `R_sat<0` for every `-1<=x<-1/2`.

**Conclusion:** saturating both local necessary walls is not merely suboptimal; it is structurally impossible already for the first forbidden Fourier shell. This kills the most natural two-parameter selection algorithm rather than spending further runs tuning it numerically.

## 6. B30 — repair one shell exactly, then localize the continuum proof

### 6.1 A repaired shell interpolant

Keep the required contacts `g(p)=eta(p)`, `g(q)=eta(q)`, `g_1(p)=eta_1(p)`, but choose

`C=-8`, `D=-6`.

The three contact equations then give

`h_eta = -9/2 -19 alpha -11 beta -8 gamma -6 delta`.

Define the residual

`R_eta := eta-h_eta`.

The following invariant identities hold exactly:

`gamma = -1-2alpha-2beta+6alpha^2`,

`delta = 1+alpha+2beta-6alpha^2+3alpha beta`,

`eta = -2-9alpha-6beta-18alpha beta+36alpha^3`.

Therefore the repaired residual collapses to

`R_eta = 1/2 + beta + 12 alpha^2 + 36 alpha^3`.          (6.1)

This simplification is the central algebraic observation of the run.

### 6.2 No interior minimum

The Jacobian of the orbit-coordinate map is

`J = det d(alpha,beta)/d(x,y)`

`  = -(8/9)(x-y)(x-4y^3+3y)`.

In the interior of `D`, one has `x<b(y)=4y^3-3y<=y`, so both factors are nonzero and `J!=0`.

But from (6.1),

`partial R_eta / partial beta = 1`.

Thus, in the interior where `(alpha,beta)` are valid local coordinates, `R_eta` has **no critical point**. Any global minimum must lie on the boundary of `D`.

### 6.3 Top boundary

At `y=1`, exact factorization gives

`R_eta(x,1) = (2x+1)^2(16x+17)/6 >= 0`

for `-1<=x<=1`, with equality only at `p=(-1/2,1)`.

### 6.4 Left boundary

At `x=-1`,

`R_eta(-1,y)`

` = (2y-1)^2(16y^4-32y^3-4y^2+16y+5)/6`.

Put `u=2y-1 in [0,1]`. The quartic factor becomes

`u^4-7u^2-2u+9`.

Its degree-4 Bernstein coefficients on `[0,1]` are exactly

`[9, 17/2, 41/6, 4, 1]`,

all strictly positive. Hence the left boundary residual is nonnegative, with equality only at `q=(-1,1/2)`.

### 6.5 Slanted boundary

On `x=b(y)=4y^3-3y`,

`R_eta(b(y),y)`

` = (2y-1)^2(2y+1)^2 Q(y^2)/6`,

where

`Q(z)=256z^4-256z^3-16z^2+48z+1`, `z in [1/4,1]`.

The exact degree-4 Bernstein coefficients of `Q` on four rational subintervals are:

- `[1/4,1/2]`: `[9,19/2,53/6,7,5]`;
- `[1/2,2/3]`: `[5,11/3,61/27,29/27,49/81]`;
- `[2/3,3/4]`: `[49/81,10/27,17/54,1/2,1]`;
- `[3/4,1]`: `[1,5/2,41/6,16,33]`.

All are positive. Therefore `Q>0` throughout `[1/4,1]`, and the slanted-boundary residual is nonnegative, again with equality only at `q`.

Combining the absence of interior critical points with the three boundary calculations proves

**Lemma 6.1.** `R_eta>=0` on all of `D`, with zero set exactly `{p,q}`.

### 6.6 Nondegenerate boundary contacts

Exact derivatives at the two zeros are

at `p`:

`R_eta(p)=0`, `R_x(p)=0`, `R_y(p)=-3`, `R_xx(p)=12`;

at `q`:

`R_eta(q)=0`, `R_x(q)=3`, `R_y(q)=0`, `R_yy(q)=12`.

Thus `p` has strict positive inward slope plus positive tangential quadratic curvature, while `q` has strict positive rightward slope plus positive vertical quadratic curvature. This makes the shell certificate stable under sufficiently small `C^2` perturbations that preserve the exact interpolation contacts.

## 7. New theorem: omega*_9 is Gaussian-optimal for all sufficiently small a

**Theorem 7.1.** There exists `a0>0` such that for every Gaussian parameter `0<a<a0`, the Hardin--Tenpas configuration `omega*_9=omega(A2,(1/3)A2)` minimizes the `A2`-periodized Gaussian energy `F_{a,A2}` among all 9-point `A2`-periodic configurations.

This is a genuine new finite-parameter theorem, but it is only PARTIAL relative to the source and relative to the frozen `m_sq` metric.

### Proof

Group the Poisson/Fourier expansion as

`F_a = L_a + w_eta eta + T_a`,

where `L_a` consists of the constant mode and the four allowed low modes `alpha,beta,gamma,delta`, `w_eta>0` is the grouped coefficient of `eta=P_{3,3}`, and `T_a` contains shells of squared norm at least `16`.

Write the low coefficients as `w_alpha,w_beta,w_gamma,w_delta`. Define

`C_a = w_gamma - 8 w_eta`,

`D_a = w_delta - 6 w_eta`,

and then define `A_a,B_a,c_a` from the exact three contact formulas applied to the **full** `F_a`:

`A_a=(3/2)F_{a,1}(p)+2C_a+D_a/2`,

`B_a=(1/2)F_{a,1}(p)+C_a+D_a/2-(2/3)(F_a(p)-F_a(q))`,

`c_a=F_a(p)+B_a/2`.

Set

`g_a=c_a+A_a alpha+B_a beta+C_a gamma+D_a delta`.

By construction,

`g_a(p)=F_a(p)`, `g_a(q)=F_a(q)`, `g_{a,1}(p)=F_{a,1}(p)`.

Linearity of the contact formulas shows that the allowed low shells cancel exactly in `F_a-g_a`, while the `eta` contribution is exactly `w_eta R_eta`. The tail contributes a linear contact correction using only `1,alpha,beta`. Hence

`F_a-g_a = w_eta R_eta + E_a`,

with

`||E_a||_{C^2(D)} / w_eta = O(exp(-4 pi^2/a)) -> 0`.       (7.1)

The same decomposition gives the nonconstant coefficients of `g_a`:

`A_a = w_alpha -19 w_eta + o(w_eta)`,

`B_a = w_beta -11 w_eta + o(w_eta)`,

`C_a = w_gamma -8 w_eta`,

`D_a = w_delta -6 w_eta`.

Because their squared norms satisfy

`4/3, 4, 16/3, 28/3 < 12`,

each low-shell coefficient dominates `w_eta` exponentially as `a->0`. Therefore

`A_a,B_a,C_a,D_a > 0`

for all sufficiently small `a`. Thus `g_a` is positive definite in the Hardin--Tenpas sense.

It remains to prove the global minorant inequality. Divide by `w_eta` and write

`H_a=(F_a-g_a)/w_eta = R_eta + eps_a`,

where `||eps_a||_{C^2(D)}->0` by (7.1). The construction gives exact contacts

`H_a(p)=H_a(q)=0`, `H_{a,x}(p)=0`.

Moreover every low invariant has zero `y`-derivative at `q`, and the transformed Gaussian has `F_{a,y}(q)=0`, so also

`H_{a,y}(q)=0`.

Choose disjoint small neighborhoods of `p` and `q`.

Near `p`, `R_y(p)=-3` and `R_xx(p)=12`. By `C^2` convergence, for all sufficiently small `a`, throughout a fixed small neighborhood one has `H_{a,y}< -1` and `H_{a,xx}>4`. Along the top boundary `y=1`, exact `H_a(p)=H_{a,x}(p)=0` and the second-derivative bound imply `H_a(x,1)>=2(x+1/2)^2`. Moving inward decreases `y`, and `H_{a,y}< -1`, so the residual can only increase. Hence `H_a>=0` near `p`.

Near `q`, `R_x(q)=3` and `R_yy(q)=12`. Again by `C^2` convergence, on a fixed small neighborhood one has `H_{a,x}>1` and `H_{a,yy}>4`. Along the left boundary `x=-1`, exact `H_a(q)=H_{a,y}(q)=0` yields `H_a(-1,y)>=2(y-1/2)^2`; moving right into `D` increases the residual because `H_{a,x}>1`. Hence `H_a>=0` near `q`.

On the compact complement of those two neighborhoods, Lemma 6.1 gives a strictly positive minimum `min R_eta>0`, and uniform convergence in (7.1) preserves positivity for sufficiently small `a`.

Therefore `F_a-g_a>=0` on all of `D` for `0<a<a0`, while `g_a` is positive definite and has equality at the two nonidentity `omega*_9` difference nodes. The Hardin--Tenpas linear-programming criterion then proves global 9-point `A2`-periodic Gaussian optimality of `omega*_9`. QED.

## 8. What this theorem does and does not prove

It **does prove** the first rigorous parameter interval for the next square-family configuration `omega*_9`: all sufficiently small Gaussian parameters are covered by one explicit leading-shell mechanism.

It **does not prove**:

- `omega*_9` for all `a>0`;
- universal optimality of `omega*_9` for every completely monotone potential;
- the full Cohn--Kumar conjecture among arbitrary density-one configurations;
- any explicit numerical value of `a0` in this run.

The fixed metric therefore cannot move from 2 to 3 yet.

The useful conceptual gain is that the small-`a` endpoint is no longer merely a numerical regime: the first forbidden Fourier shell has an exact positive residual certificate, and the strict shell gap makes the full Gaussian tail perturbative.
