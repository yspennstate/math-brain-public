## 4. B12 — exact RKHS identity for arbitrary many defects

### 4.1 Positive-definite feature representation

The periodized Gaussian `V_a` is positive definite on the torus `R^2/Lambda`, because its Poisson expansion has strictly positive Fourier coefficients. Hence there is a real or complex Hilbert space `H_a` and a feature map `phi_a` such that

`<phi_a(x),phi_a(y)> = V_a(x-y)`.                                (4.1)

Label the subgroup points as `g_1,...,g_9 in G`, and let an arbitrary competing labelled configuration be `x_1,...,x_9`. Define chord vectors

`u_i := phi_a(x_i)-phi_a(g_i)`.                                  (4.2)

Let

`Phi_G := sum_{g in G} phi_a(g)`.

The included-diagonal energy is

`tilde U_a(X):=sum_{i,j} V_a(x_i-x_j)=||sum_i phi_a(x_i)||^2`.

The diagonal contribution is always `9 V_a(0)`, so the difference of included-diagonal energies equals twice the difference of unordered energies. Thus

`2[U_a(X)-U_a(G)]
 = 2 <Phi_G, sum_i u_i> + ||sum_i u_i||^2`.                     (4.3)

Now use the finite-subgroup identity

`sum_{g in G} V_a(x-g)=F_{a,Lambda'}(x)`.                        (4.4)

Because `g_i in Lambda'` and `F_{a,Lambda'}` is `Lambda'`-periodic,

`<Phi_G,u_i>
 = F_{a,Lambda'}(x_i)-F_{a,Lambda'}(g_i)
 = -D_{Lambda',a}(x_i-g_i)`.                                    (4.5)

Substituting (4.5) into (4.3) gives the exact many-defect identity

`boxed{
2[U_a(X)-U_a(G)]
 = ||sum_{i=1}^9 u_i||^2
   - 2 sum_{i=1}^9 D_{Lambda',a}(x_i-g_i).
}`                                                                    (4.6)

Also, directly from the feature map,

`||u_i||^2 = 2 D_{Lambda,a}(x_i-g_i)`.                           (4.7)

### 4.2 Interpretation

For one defect, (4.6)-(4.7) reduce exactly to

`2 Delta U = 2(D_Lambda-D_Lambda') >= 0`,

which is the previous theorem.

For several defects, the first term is not `sum ||u_i||^2`; it is

`||sum_i u_i||^2`.

Therefore the sole new obstruction is **Hilbert-space cancellation among defect chords**. The one-defect theorem controls each chord individually but cannot prevent different chords from pointing against one another in feature space.

This is stronger and cleaner than saying vaguely that “cross terms appear”: equation (4.6) is an exact global formula for every labelled nine-point competitor and every Gaussian scale.

A useful equivalent two-defect formula follows immediately. Take basepoints `g_1=0`, `g_2=d`, move them to `x,y`, and leave all others fixed. Put

`q_a(z):=D_{Lambda,a}(z)-D_{Lambda',a}(z) >= 0`.

Then

`boxed{
Delta U_a
 = q_a(x) + q_a(y-d)
   + C_a(x,y;d),
}`                                                                    (4.8)

where the exact mixed term is

`C_a(x,y;d)
 = V_a(x-y)-V_a(x-d)-V_a(y)+V_a(d)`.                             (4.9)

Indeed, (4.9) is precisely

`<phi_a(x)-phi_a(0), phi_a(y)-phi_a(d)>`.

This formula also explains why a plain Cauchy–Schwarz repair is inadequate. The previous B24 calculation showed that the one-defect ratio `D_{Lambda'}/D_Lambda` has supremum one as `a -> infinity`; hence the positive margin `q_a` can be arbitrarily small relative to the chord norm `D_Lambda`. Bounding the mixed term only by `sqrt(D_Lambda D_Lambda)` cannot be absorbed uniformly by `q_a(x)+q_a(y-d)`.

So sequentially “healing one point at a time” is not a proof algorithm: after the first point is moved, the second move sees the exact cross term (4.9), whose sign is uncontrolled by the isolated one-point theorem.

## 5. B3 — a complete joint theorem for one two-defect orbit

The RKHS identity identifies cancellation, but it does not yet exploit quotient geometry. The next idea is to restrict **how** two defects move while keeping them genuinely joint: translate both selected subgroup points by the same vector. For one nontrivial quotient orbit, the previous nested-honeycomb theorem then closes the problem exactly.

### 5.1 General rigid-subset translation identity

Let `S subset G`, `|S|=k`. For `t in R^2/Lambda`, define the configuration

`X_t := (S+t) union (G\S)`,

interpreted with multiplicity if a translated point collides with an unshifted point. Internal interactions inside `S+t` are unchanged by rigid translation, as are interactions inside `G\S`. Hence only cross interactions change.

For `c in G`, define the ordered difference count

`N_S(c):=#{(s,s') in S^2 : s-s'=c}`.                             (5.1)

Then `N_S(0)=k` and `N_S(c)=N_S(-c)`. Define nonnegative quotient weights

`w_S(c):=k-N_S(c)`.                                               (5.2)

### Proposition 5.1 — rigid translation is a weighted vacancy theta problem

For every `S`, every `t`, and every `a>0`,

`boxed{
U_a(X_t)-U_a(G)
 = R_{S,a}(t)-R_{S,a}(0),
}`                                                                    (5.3)

where

`R_{S,a}(t):=sum_{c in G} w_S(c) V_a(t+c)`.                       (5.4)

#### Proof

The cross interaction at displacement `t` is

`Cross_S(t)=sum_{s in S} sum_{g in G\S} V_a(t+s-g)`.

Add and subtract the terms with `g in S`:

`Cross_S(t)
 = sum_{s in S} sum_{g in G} V_a(t+s-g)
   - sum_{s,s' in S} V_a(t+s-s')`.

By the finite-subgroup identity (4.4), and because `s in Lambda'`, the first double sum is `k F_{a,Lambda'}(t)`. Grouping the second double sum by `c=s-s'` gives

`Cross_S(t)
 = k sum_{c in G} V_a(t+c)
   - sum_{c in G} N_S(c)V_a(t+c)
 = sum_{c in G} [k-N_S(c)] V_a(t+c)
 = R_{S,a}(t)`.

All non-cross interactions cancel between `X_t` and `G`, proving (5.3). QED.

This formula is exact and turns a many-body question into a translated theta problem on a weighted finite quotient.

### 5.2 Specialize to two subgroup points

Take `S={0,d}` with `d != 0` in `G`. Since every nonzero element of `G ~= (Z/3Z)^2` has order three,

`N_S(0)=2`, `N_S(d)=N_S(-d)=1`, and `N_S(c)=0` otherwise.

Thus

`w_S(0)=0`,
`w_S(+-d)=1`,
`w_S(c)=2` on the other six nonzero quotient classes.             (5.5)

Choose a triangular basis `e1,e2` of `Lambda` with

`|e1|=|e2|=1`, `<e1,e2>=1/2`.

As in the preceding report, introduce the index-three triangular sublattice

`M={m e1+n e2 : m == n (mod 3)}`.                                (5.6)

The scaled nonzero quotient `Lambda/3Lambda\{0}` decomposes as

`H1 := Lambda\M`

and

`H2 := M\3Lambda
    = (3Lambda+(e1+e2)) union (3Lambda-(e1+e2))`.                 (5.7)

Here `H1` consists of six cosets and `H2` of two cosets; each is a regular honeycomb point set with `0` at a primitive-hexagon center.

Now select the two subgroup points whose difference is in the two-element quotient orbit

`d = +- (e1+e2)/3  (mod Lambda)`.                                (5.8)

After scaling `y=3t`, `b=a/9`, the two low-weight classes `+-d` become precisely `H2`. Therefore (5.4)-(5.5) become

`boxed{
R_{S,a}(t)
 = 2 Theta_{H1,b}(y) + Theta_{H2,b}(y),
qquad y=3t,\ b=a/9,
}`                                                                    (5.9)

where `Theta_{H,b}(y)=sum_{u in H} exp(-b|y+u|^2)`.

### Theorem 5.2 — rigid two-defect stability on the deep quotient orbit

Let `S={0,(e1+e2)/3}` modulo `Lambda`, or any image of this pair under the dihedral symmetry of `A2`. Translate both selected points by the same `t`, leaving the other seven subgroup points fixed. Then for every `a>0`,

`U_a(X_t) >= U_a(G)`,                                             (5.10)

with equality modulo `Lambda` only for the trivial translation `t in Lambda`.

#### Proof

By Proposition 5.1 and (5.9),

`U_a(X_t)-U_a(G)
 = 2[Theta_{H1,b}(3t)-Theta_{H1,b}(0)]
   +[Theta_{H2,b}(3t)-Theta_{H2,b}(0)].`                          (5.11)

Bétermin's Proposition 5.3 gives that the center of a primitive regular honeycomb is a global minimizer of its translated Gaussian theta function at every Gaussian scale; in the point case this applies directly to both `H1` and `H2`. Hence each bracket in (5.11) is nonnegative.

For equality, the previous nested-honeycomb analysis gives the common center-translation set as `3Lambda`: centers of `H1` form `M`, centers of `H2` form `3Lambda`, and `3Lambda subset M`. Thus simultaneous equality forces `3t in 3Lambda`, i.e. `t in Lambda`, the trivial torus translation. QED.

### Corollary 5.3 — completely monotone extension

Under the standard summability assumptions making the periodic energy finite, let `f(r)=int_0^infty exp(-a r) dmu(a)` be a completely monotone squared-distance potential with positive Bernstein measure `mu`. Integrating (5.10) against `mu` gives

`U_f(X_t) >= U_f(G)`.                                             (5.12)

For any nonconstant such potential whose representing measure charges positive scales, the Gaussian strictness yields strict inequality for nontrivial `t` whenever the integral is finite.

This is the first theorem in this track controlling a **genuinely joint two-defect motion** rather than one moved point. It remains a restricted two-dimensional family inside the full nine-point configuration space and therefore does not move `m_sq`.

## 6. B20 — exact classification of where the nested-honeycomb gluing fails

### 6.1 Decompose the weight pattern

After scaling by three, the weights are again one on `+-e1` and two on the other six nonzero residue classes. Write this weighted set as

`(all eight nonzero cosets with weight one)
 + (the six nonzero cosets other than +-e1 with one extra copy)`.

The first term is exactly the vacancy set `Lambda\3Lambda`, already proved in the previous run to have its Gaussian potential minimized at zero by the `H1 disjoint_union H2` honeycomb decomposition.

For the second term, define the index-three sublattice

`M0 := {m e1+n e2 : n == 0 (mod 3)} = <e1,3e2>`.                 (6.2)

Modulo `3Lambda`, `M0` consists of the line `{0,+-e1}`. Therefore

`Lambda\M0`

is exactly the union of the six residue classes that receive the extra weight.

Consequently, the **only missing inequality** for the near rigid pair is

`boxed{
Theta_{Lambda\M0,b}(y) >= Theta_{Lambda\M0,b}(0)
\quad for all b>0,\ y in R^2.
}`                                                                    (6.3)

Because

`Theta_{Lambda\M0,b}=Theta_{Lambda,b}-Theta_{M0,b}`,

(6.3) is equivalently

`boxed{
D_{M0,b}(y) >= D_{Lambda,b}(y)
\quad for all b>0,\ y.
}`                                                                    (6.4)

Unlike `M` in the successful 6+2 decomposition, `M0=<e1,3e2>` is **not** an equilateral triangular sublattice. Its two-coset complement is not a regular honeycomb, so Bétermin Proposition 5.3 does not apply. This is the precise gluing failure of the previous method.

### 6.2 Exact reduction to one-dimensional Jacobi theta factors

The blocker (6.3) has further exact structure. Put

`e1=(1,0)`, `e2=(1/2,sqrt(3)/2)`,

and write `y=(X,Y)`. Let

`theta_b(z):=sum_{m in Z} exp(-b(z+m)^2)`.

Every point of `Lambda` is `(m+n/2, (sqrt(3)/2)n)`. The complement `Lambda\M0` is exactly the rows with `3` not dividing `n`. Split those rows into even and odd `n`. A direct reindexing gives

`Theta_{Lambda\M0,b}(X,Y)
 = theta_b(X) A_{0,b}(Y)
   + theta_b(X+1/2) A_{1,b}(Y)`,                                  (6.5)

where

`A_{0,b}(Y)
 := sum_{k in Z, 3 not divides k}
    exp(-b (Y+sqrt(3)k)^2)`,                                      (6.6)

and

`A_{1,b}(Y)
 := sum_{k in Z, k not == 1 (mod 3)}
    exp(-b (Y+sqrt(3)(k+1/2))^2)`.                               (6.7)

Proof of the indexing: for `n=2k`, the horizontal shift `n/2=k` is integral and `3 not divides n` is `3 not divides k`; for `n=2k+1`, the horizontal shift is `k+1/2`, and `2k+1 == 0 (mod 3)` exactly when `k == 1 (mod 3)`.

Thus the unresolved near-orbit theorem is no longer a two-dimensional combinatorial mystery. It is an explicit inequality between two products of ordinary one-dimensional Gaussian theta sums. No sign proof for (6.3)-(6.7) is claimed in this run.

### 6.3 Why the sequential one-point algorithm still does not close the near orbit

One might instead move the two points one after another and invoke the one-point theorem twice. Formula (4.8) proves exactly why that inference is invalid: after the first move, the second move pays the mixed finite difference `C_a`. The isolated margin `q_a` is not uniformly comparable to the chord norm because the previous run proved

`sup_{a>0,z notin Lambda} D_{Lambda',a}(z)/D_{Lambda,a}(z)=1`.

Therefore there is no uniform positive constant `delta` for which

`q_a(z) >= delta D_{Lambda,a}(z)`

on all scales. A Cauchy–Schwarz estimate on `C_a` therefore cannot be absorbed from one-point information alone. Any sequential-healing proof must exploit additional sign/geometry, not just the one-defect theorem.

This exact route-kill is useful: it prevents the next run from spending time on a false black-box induction.
