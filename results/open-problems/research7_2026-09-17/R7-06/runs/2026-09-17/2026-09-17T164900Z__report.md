### 4.1 Global packing endpoint: every competitor has separation at most 1/3

Let

`d(omega) := min_{i!=j, v in A2} |x_i-x_j+v|`

be the minimum torus separation of distinct points. The lift of `omega` to the plane is an `A2`-periodic packing with nine centers per `A2` cell. Disks of radius `d(omega)/2` centered at all lifted points form a planar periodic equal-disk packing.

Since the shortest-vector-one triangular lattice has cell area

`area(A2)=sqrt(3)/2`,

the packing density is

`Delta(omega)
 = [9 pi (d/2)^2] / [sqrt(3)/2]
 = (3 sqrt(3) pi / 2) d^2.`

The sharp planar circle-packing bound is

`Delta <= pi/sqrt(12) = pi/(2 sqrt(3)).`

Therefore

`9 d^2 <= 1`, hence `d(omega)<=1/3=r`.

The standard equality case in the planar hexagonal-packing theorem forces the lifted packing to be a triangular lattice. Because it also contains `A2` as a period lattice and has index nine over `A2`, modulo translations there are only finitely many such equality configurations. Indeed any superlattice `L''` with `A2 subset L''` and `[L'':A2]=9` lies in `(1/9)A2`, so it corresponds to one of finitely many order-nine subgroups of `(1/9 A2)/A2`.

Let `M` denote this finite equality set modulo translation. Every member of `M` is congruent to a triangular lattice of the same density, so every radial Gaussian gives it the same energy as `omega*_9`.

For a displacement field `u:G->R^2`, the first variation of a contact length on the edge `(x,x+s_j)` is

`ell'_{x,j} = n_j dot (u_{x+s_j}-u_x)`.

### 4.3 Rigidity gives a linear stability inequality for the packing radius

Fix one `omega^0 in M`, choose the translation gauge `sum_i u_i=0`, and write a nearby configuration as `x_i=x_i^0+u_i` after the unique small relabeling.

For the standard component, define the vector `L u` of first contact-length variations. By (4.1) and the spectral gap of the finite contact graph, there is `c_0>0` such that

`||L u||_2 >= c_0 ||u||_2`                                      (4.2)

on the translation quotient.

There is also an exact equilibrium identity

`sum_edges (L u)_e = 0`.                                        (4.3)

To see it, orient every contact in the three positive step directions. Then

`sum_{x,j} n_j dot (u_{x+s_j}-u_x)
 = sum_j n_j dot (sum_x u_{x+s_j}-sum_x u_x)=0`.

From (4.2)--(4.3), some contact has first variation at most `-c_1||u||_2` for a fixed `c_1>0`. Taylor expansion of its length gives

`|r n_e + (u_j-u_i)|
 <= r - c_1||u||_2 + C||u||_2^2`.

After shrinking the neighborhood,

`r-d(omega) >= c dist(omega,M)`                                 (4.4)

for some `c>0`. The other finitely many components of `M` satisfy the same statement after rotation/congruence; taking the minimum constant gives a uniform version of (4.4).

Thus near the densest-packing equality set, packing deficit controls geometric distance **linearly**, not merely quadratically.

### 4.4 Any Gaussian competitor that beats omega*_9 is O(1/a) from M

For `omega*_9`, every difference from a fixed site runs through `L'=(1/3)A2`. Therefore

`E_a(omega*_9)
 = 9 sum_{z in L'\{0}} exp(-a|z|^2).`

For `a>=1`, set the finite constant

`C_0 := 9 sum_{z in L'\{0}} exp[-(|z|^2-r^2)].`

Then

`E_a(omega*_9) <= C_0 exp(-a r^2).`                              (4.5)

Now let `omega` be any putative competitor with

`E_a(omega) <= E_a(omega*_9)`.

A nearest pair at torus distance `d=d(omega)` contributes in both orientations, so

`E_a(omega) >= 2 exp(-a d^2).`                                  (4.6)

Combining (4.5)--(4.6),

`a(r^2-d^2) <= log(C_0/2).`

Since `d<=r`,

`0 <= r-d <= [log(C_0/2)]/(a r).`                               (4.7)

Hence `d(omega)->r` uniformly for all such putative competitors.

Configuration space modulo translation and permutation is compact, `d` is continuous, and its equality set `{d=r}` is exactly `M`. Therefore (4.7) first puts every putative competitor inside the fixed neighborhood where (4.4) applies. Then (4.4) upgrades the conclusion to

`dist(omega,M) <= K/a`                                          (4.8)

for a fixed constant `K` independent of large `a`.

This converts a global energy comparison into a very small neighborhood problem.

### 4.5 Uniform local convexity on every fixed K/a neighborhood

It remains to prove that no nontrivial configuration satisfying (4.8) can lower the Gaussian energy.

This is the direct finite analogue of the large-parameter nearest-neighbor Hessian mechanism in Leblé's local theorem, and here it can be proved from (4.1) without importing an unquantified local radius.

Fix `K`. Around one `omega^0 in M`, take a translation-gauged displacement `u` with

`||u|| <= K/a`.

For a nearest-neighbor edge `e=(i,j)`, write

`z_e = r n_e + (u_j-u_i)`.

The Hessian of a single Gaussian pair is exactly

`D^2 exp(-a|z|^2)[w,w]
 = exp(-a|z|^2) [4a^2(z dot w)^2 - 2a|w|^2].`                   (4.9)

Because `|u_j-u_i|=O_K(1/a)`, the weighted rigidity map

`w -> (z_e dot (w_j-w_i))_e`

is an `O_K(1/a)` operator perturbation of

`w -> (r n_e dot (w_j-w_i))_e`.

By (4.1), after taking `a` sufficiently large depending on `K`,

`sum_{e in E_nn} [z_e dot (w_j-w_i)]^2
 >= (r^2/16) sum_{e in E_nn}|w_j-w_i|^2.`                      (4.10)

Also, uniformly on this `K/a` ball,

`exp(-a r^2-C_K) <= exp(-a|z_e|^2) <= exp(-a r^2+C_K)`          (4.11)

for a fixed `C_K`.

Using (4.9)--(4.11), the nearest-neighbor shell Hessian satisfies

`Q_nn(w)
 >= exp(-a r^2)
    [ (a^2 r^2/4) exp(-C_K) - 2a exp(C_K) ]
    sum_{e in E_nn}|w_j-w_i|^2.`                               (4.12)

Thus its positive part is order `a^2 exp(-a r^2)`, whereas its transverse negative part is only order `a exp(-a r^2)`.

All remaining nonzero differences of the triangular lattice have a strict distance gap above `r`; the second shell has distance `sqrt(3)r`. On a `K/a` displacement ball, the total absolute Hessian contribution of every non-nearest shell is bounded by

`C'_K a^2 exp[-a (sqrt(3)r)^2 + C'_K] ||w||^2`,                (4.13)

using the Gaussian lattice tail and `|w_i-w_j|^2<=C||w||^2`. The nearest-neighbor contact graph has a fixed spectral gap on the translation quotient, so

`sum_{E_nn}|w_j-w_i|^2 >= lambda_0 ||w||^2`

for some `lambda_0>0`.

Since `(sqrt(3)r)^2-r^2=2r^2>0`, (4.13) is exponentially smaller than the positive `a^2 exp(-a r^2)` term in (4.12). Therefore, for every fixed `K`, there exists `A_K` such that for all `a>A_K`,

`D^2 E_a(omega^0+u)[w,w] > 0`

for every nontranslation `w` and every `||u||<=K/a`.

The base triangular configuration `omega^0` is a critical point by inversion/dihedral symmetry. Hence `E_a` is strictly convex on this translation-gauged `K/a` ball and has its unique minimum at `omega^0` there.

Apply this to the fixed `K` from (4.8), and to each of the finitely many components of `M`. For all sufficiently large `a`, a putative global competitor must lie in one of these balls, but strict local convexity says it cannot have lower energy. This proves Theorem 4.1. QED.
