## 5. B2 — attack the negation: exact defect-lattice identity

Set

`x=0`, `y=e_1`, `z=2e_1`,

and delete the two axial edges `xy` and `yz` from `Z^2`, obtaining the slit/defect graph `H`.

Let the five cluster-partition probabilities of `{x,y,z}` under parameter-`p` percolation on `H` be

- `c`: all three connected;
- `a`: `xy|z`;
- `a'`: `yz|x`;
- `b`: `xz|y`;
- `s`: all three separate.

Restore `xy` and `yz` independently with probability `p`; write `q=1-p`.

A direct five-case conditioning gives the exact identity

`tau_1(p)-tau_2(p) = q [ a - q b + p s ].`

Writing

`Delta_H=P_H(x~y)-P_H(x~z)=a-b`

and

`D_H=P_H(x !~ y AND y !~ z)=b+s`,

the identity becomes

`boxed: tau_1-tau_2 = q [ Delta_H + p D_H ].`

The derivation is fully written in the accompanying artifact.

### B2 outcome

A counterexample at the very first separation must satisfy the stronger defect inequality

`Delta_H < -p D_H <= 0`.

Thus it is not enough for the two-edge-deleted lattice merely to reverse adjacent and farther connectivities: it must reverse them by a margin at least `p D_H`. This is a concrete obstruction-first normal form for the first source milestone.

## 7. B16 — exact small case: two certified parameter intervals

### 7.1 Low density

The direct edge gives `tau_1(p)>=p`.

Every `0 -> 2e_1` connection contains a self-avoiding path of even length. There is exactly one length-2 path; for every larger length `m`, the number of self-avoiding paths is at most the number of non-backtracking paths `4*3^(m-1)`. Hence for `p<1/3`,

`tau_2(p) <= p^2 + 108p^4/(1-9p^2)`.

So `tau_1>=tau_2` whenever

`99p^3+9p^2+p-1 <= 0`.

The cubic is strictly increasing on `[0,infinity)` and has one positive root

`p_- = 0.176409928591824...`.

Therefore

`boxed: tau_1(p)>=tau_2(p) for 0<p<=p_- .`

This explicit certificate is not claimed novel; the 2015 theorem is qualitatively stronger in the sufficiently-small-`p` regime.

### 7.2 High density: full connectivity, not the truncated function

Put `q=1-p`. For `p>1/2`, uniqueness of the infinite open cluster implies that disconnection of two fixed vertices produces a finite closed cutset around at least one of them. Minimal cutsets correspond to simple closed dual circuits.

For separation one, the length-4 and length-6 separating circuits contribute

`S_1(q)=2q^4+6q^6`.

For separation two, they contribute

`S_2(q)=2q^4+8q^6`.

Reason: length-4 circuits isolate an endpoint; length-6 circuits surround an adjacent two-vertex domino. For adjacent terminals, each endpoint has only three dominoes containing exactly one terminal, while for distance-two terminals each endpoint has four.

There are at most ten such small circuits. Distinct small minimal cutsets have union size at least seven, so Bonferroni gives an error at most `45q^7` for each `F_n=1-tau_n` relative to `S_n`.

Every longer separating dual circuit crosses the straight primal segment between the terminals. A crude non-backtracking count gives, for `n=1,2`,

`L_n(q) <= (2n/3)(3q)^8/(1-3q)`.

Consequently

`tau_1-tau_2 = F_2-F_1`

`>= q^6[2-90q-2*3^8 q^2/(1-3q)].`

For every `q<=1/200` the bracket is positive (at `q=1/200` it is greater than `1.21`). Hence

`boxed: tau_1(p)>tau_2(p) for 199/200 <= p <1`,

with equality at `p=1`.

This is a full-connectivity fixed-separation Peierls certificate, not an inference from the known high-density **truncated** theorem. I have not certified publication novelty of this elementary bound.
