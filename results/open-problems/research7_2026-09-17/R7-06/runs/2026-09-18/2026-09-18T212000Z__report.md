## 5. Method B2 — attack the negation of the historical obstruction

Put

\[
\beta=\alpha/2\in(0,1),\qquad r=|z|^2,
\]

and let `S,S'` be independent positive beta-stable variables with

\[
\mathbb E e^{-xS}=e^{-\Gamma(1-\beta)x^\beta}.
\]

The BCC scalar equation is

\[
1=\mathbb E\left(\frac{S}{r+y_*(r)^2SS'}\right)^\beta
=\mathbb E\bigl(r/S+y_*(r)^2S'\bigr)^{-\beta}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every `q>0`, their negative-moment integral yields

\[
m_q:=\mathbb E S^{-q}
=
\frac{\Gamma(1+q/\beta)}
{\Gamma(1+q)\Gamma(1-\beta)^{q/\beta}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

At `r=0`, with `y_0=y_*(0)`, implicit differentiation of (5.1), justified by these finite negative moments, gives the exact new formula

\[
\boxed{
 y_*'(0)
=-\frac{m_1m_{\beta+1}}
{2m_\beta y_0}
},
\qquad
\boxed{
 (y_*^2)'(0)
=-\frac{m_1m_{\beta+1}}{m_\beta}
}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

So the scalar fixed point is not merely usable away from zero; its first radial response is closed in Gamma functions.

## 6. Method B14 — exploit isotropy/radial symmetry

Let

\[
Y(r)=y_*(r)^2,
\qquad T=SS',
\qquad \widetilde g_\alpha(r)=g_\alpha(\sqrt r).
\]

BCC equation (4.11) and Lemma 4.5 are exactly

\[
\pi\widetilde g_\alpha(r)
=(Y-rY')\,
\mathbb E\frac{T}{(r+YT)^2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Using `E[T^{-1}]=m_1^2`, `E[T^{-2}]=m_2^2` and (5.3), differentiation at zero gives

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde g_\alpha'(0)
=
\frac{2}{\pi}Y(0)^{-2}
\left(
\frac{m_1^3m_{\beta+1}}{m_\beta}-m_2^2
\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

After substituting (5.2), this becomes

\[
\boxed{
 c_{\alpha,1}
=
\frac{2}{\pi}
\left(
\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)}
\right)^{4/\alpha}
\left[
\frac{2}{\alpha}\Gamma(1+2/\alpha)^4
-\frac14\Gamma(1+4/\alpha)^2
\right].
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 g_\alpha(\rho)
=g_\alpha(0)+c_{\alpha,1}\rho^2+o(\rho^2).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The coefficient is strictly negative for every `0<alpha<2`.  Writing `x=2/alpha>1`, its sign reduces to

\[
x\Gamma(x+1)^4-\frac14\Gamma(2x+1)^2<0.
\]

For

\[
R(x)=\Gamma(2x+1)/\Gamma(x+1)^2,
\]

the function `log(R(x)/(2 sqrt(x)))` vanishes at `x=1` and has positive derivative for `x>=1`, using the digamma duplication identity and

\[
\psi(x+1)-\psi(x+1/2)
=
\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-(x+1/2)t}}{1+e^{-t/2}}dt
<\frac1{x+1/2}.
\]

Hence `R(x)>2 sqrt(x)` for `x>1`.  Thus

\[
\boxed{c_{\alpha,1}<0\quad(0<\alpha<2)}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

This proves a source-relevant local-shape theorem absent from the source statement: every BCC density has a strict quadratic local maximum at the origin.
