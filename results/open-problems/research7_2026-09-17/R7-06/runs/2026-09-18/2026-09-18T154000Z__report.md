## 3. B3 — stronger quenched theorem: critical inverse-square scenery is a Bellman opacity barrier

Set in `Z^5`

\[
w(x)=(1+|x|^2)^{-1},
\qquad
f(x)=\tfrac12\log(1+|x|^2).
\]

The Hessian identity

\[
D^2f(x)=\frac{I}{1+|x|^2}-\frac{2xx^T}{(1+|x|^2)^2}
\]

and the central-difference formula imply a constant `C_5` with

\[
(Pf)(x)-f(x)\le C_5w(x)
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb Z^5.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `T_R` be first exit from the radius-`R` ball, `sigma` any stopping time, `tau=T_R wedge sigma wedge n`, and `p=P_0(T_R<=sigma wedge n)`. Bounded-time Dynkin gives

\[
\mathbb E_0 f(X_\tau)
\le C_5\mathbb E_0\sum_{j<\tau}w(X_j).
\]

On the exit event `f(X_tau)>=0.5 log(1+R^2)` and `f>=0` otherwise. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_0\sum_{j<\tau}w(X_j)
\ge
\frac{p}{2C_5}\log(1+R^2).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Now suppose all rewards before exit satisfy `z(x)<=-Aw(x)` and the terminal continuation on exit is nonnegative and capped by `B`. Any stopping strategy has payoff at most

\[
Bp-A\mathbb E_0\sum_{j<\tau}w(X_j)
\le
p\left[B-\frac{A}{2C_5}\log(1+R^2)\right].
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
A\log(1+R^2)\ge2C_5B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{Bellman value at the origin}=0.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is an all-stopping-rules quenched theorem, stronger than the preceding expectation-level killed-kernel estimate.

For an *uncapped* terminal continuation `H`, split `H=(H wedge B)+(H-B)_+`. The capped part has nonpositive value by (3.3), so the exact residual obstruction is

\[
\boxed{
V_H(0)
\le
\mathbb E_0[(H_{T_R}(X_{T_R})-B)_+;\,T_R\le n].
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus this route does not hide the failure: once the inverse-square core is strong enough, only a continuation overshoot above the cap can defeat it.

## 5. B28 — the exact smooth Weibull tilt is typically opaque

Now take the source-valid special law

\[
a_y\asymp(1+|y|)^{-3},
\qquad
\sum_{|y|\le R}a_y^{5/3}\asymp\log R.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Tilt the product law by

\[
\frac{dP_\theta}{dP}
\propto
\exp\left[-\theta\sum_{|y|\le R}a_y\zeta(y)\right].
\tag{5.2}
\]

At one site the tilted density is proportional to

\[
\exp(-|x|^{5/2}-hx),
\qquad h=\theta a_y.

Its unique mode is

\[
t(h)=\left(\frac{h}{5/2}\right)^{2/3},
\qquad x_*=-t(h).
\tag{5.3}
\]

A direct convex-potential gap calculation at `-t/2` proves

\[
P_h\{X>-t/2\}\le Ce^{-ct^{5/2}}
\quad(t\text{ large}).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the modal negative profile is

\[
t_y\asymp\theta^{2/3}(1+|y|)^{-2},
\tag{5.5}
\]

exactly the B3 inverse-square barrier.

Calibrate the tilt so that its modal weighted Green deficit is `kappa s`. Writing `A=theta^{2/3}`, (5.1) gives

\[
A\asymp\frac{\kappa s}{\log R}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

At the critical radius `R^2 asymp s/log s`, define

\[
R_*=R/[D(\log R)^{1/5}].

For `|y|<=R_*`, the mode obeys `t_y>=cD^2(log R)^{2/5}`, hence (5.4) gives failure probability at most `R^{-cD^5}` per site. A union bound over `O(R^5)` sites, with `D` large, proves

\[
\boxed{

\zeta(y)\le-cA(1+|y|^2)^{-1}

\tag{5.7}

The tilt also has the correct information scale.
