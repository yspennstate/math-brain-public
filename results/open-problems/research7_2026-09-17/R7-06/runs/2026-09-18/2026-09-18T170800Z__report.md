## 2. B5 — exact localized susceptibility

For deterministic scenery `z`, let

\[
F_n(z)=\sup_{\tau\le n}\mathbb E_0\sum_{j<\tau}z(X_j).
\]

Fix a finitely supported nonnegative profile `h` and define

\[
F_{n,h}(\lambda;z)=F_n(z-\lambda h).
\]

A finite-horizon deterministic Markov stopping policy has a deterministic occupation vector `mu_pi`; hence `F_{n,h}` is the maximum of finitely many affine policy values

\[
L_\pi(z)-\lambda\sum_xh(x)\mu_\pi(x).
\]

Therefore, if the tie at `lambda` is resolved by choosing among optimal policies one with minimal `h`-weighted occupation,

\[
\boxed{
-\partial_{\lambda+}F_{n,h}(\lambda;z)
=
\mathbb E_0\sum_{j<\tau_{\lambda,h}^z}h(X_j).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Since every stopped occupation is bounded coordinatewise by the finite Green kernel, the difference quotients are deterministically dominated. Thus for

\[
A_{n,h}(\lambda)=\mathbb E F_{n,h}(\lambda;\zeta)
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
-\partial_{\lambda+}A_{n,h}(\lambda)
=\chi_{n,h}(\lambda)
:=\mathbb E\mathbb E_0\sum_{j<\tau_{\lambda,h}^{\zeta}}h(X_j),
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and the exact finite-amplitude interpolation formula

\[
\boxed{
A_{n,h}(0)-A_{n,h}(\Lambda)
=\int_0^\Lambda\chi_{n,h}(s)ds.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

This closes the formal envelope/tie-handling gap left in the preceding report.

## 3. B18 — inverse-square susceptibility is a terminal-radius observable

Take

\[
w_R(x)=\frac{{\bf1}_{\{|x|<R\}}}{1+|x|^2},
\qquad
H_R(y)=\sum_xG(y,x)w_R(x),
\]

where `G` is the infinite Green kernel on `Z^5`. Then `(I-P)H_R=w_R`. Dynkin's identity at the bounded Bellman stopping time gives the exact pathwise relation

\[
\boxed{
-\partial_{\lambda+}F_{n,w_R}(\lambda;z)
=H_R(0)-\mathbb E_0H_R(X_{\tau_{\lambda,w_R}^z}).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, if `mu` is stopped occupation and `q` the terminal distribution after a nonempty path, direct flow counting gives

\[
(I-P)\mu=c\delta_0-q,
\qquad
\boxed{
\mu(x)=cG(0,x)-\sum_yq(y)G(y,x).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the missing cancellation is exactly a **terminal sink** correction to the raw Green source.

Using the standard five-dimensional asymptotic

\[
G(0,x)=a_5|x|^{-3}+O(|x|^{-5}),
\]

shell summation gives, uniformly in `R` and `y`,

\[
\boxed{
H_R(0)-H_R(y)
=
\kappa_5\log(1+\min\{|y|,R\})+O(1),
\qquad \kappa_5=a_5|S^4|>0.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The proof is in the artifact: inside radius `R/4`, the outer scales from `|y|` to `R` contribute `kappa_5 log(R/(1+|y|))`, while all inner-scale and Green-asymptotic errors are uniformly summable; outside radius `R/4`, the critical convolution is `O(1)`.

Combining (3.1) and (3.3) yields

\[
\boxed{
\chi_{n,w_R}(\lambda)
=
\kappa_5\mathbb E_{\zeta,0}
\log(1+\min\{|X_{\tau_{\lambda,w_R}^{\zeta}}|,R\})+O(1).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is the main conceptual advance of the run: **the critical logarithm is exactly the expected logarithmic terminal scale of the Bellman-optimal stopped path, up to an additive constant.** The slowly varying source question is therefore a terminal-sink localization problem rather than merely a Green-tail estimate.

## 4. B25 — score identity and a sharp no-go for generic first-order bounds

For the smooth Weibull recovery law define

\[
\psi(t)=\frac52\operatorname{sgn}(t)|t|^{3/2}.
\]

At fixed finite horizon, ties between distinct policy occupation vectors lie on finitely many affine hyperplanes and hence have product-law probability zero. Integration by parts against `e^{-|t|^{5/2}}dt` gives, for the mean optimal occupation

\[
m_{\lambda,h}(x)=\mathbb E\mu_{\lambda,h}^{\zeta}(x),
\]

the exact score representation

\[
\boxed{
 m_{\lambda,h}(x)
=
\mathbb E[(F_{n,h}(\lambda;\zeta)-A_{n,h}(\lambda))\psi(\zeta(x))].
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Hence the same local derivative is counted a second way:

\[
\boxed{
\chi_{n,h}(\lambda)
=
\operatorname{Cov}\left(
F_{n,h}(\lambda;\zeta),
\sum_xh(x)\psi(\zeta(x))
\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The normalized coordinate scores form an orthonormal family. Bessel's inequality, tensorized Poincare, and the previously proved uniform square-summability of the Green kernel imply

\[
\boxed{
\sum_xm_{\lambda,h}(x)^2\le C_{\rm occ}<\infty
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

uniformly in horizon, penalty and profile. We also always have

\[
0\le m_{\lambda,h}(x)\le G(0,x).
\tag{4.4}
\]

These bounds imply only

\[
\chi_{n,w_R}(\lambda)\le C(1+\log R).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Crucially, that logarithm cannot be removed from these two generic constraints alone. On a dyadic shell `D_k`, define

\[
\theta_k=2^{-2k}\sum_{x\in D_k}m(x).
\]

Green domination permits `theta_k=O(1)`, while the global `l^2` cost of maintaining `theta_k` at constant order on shell `k` is only `O(2^{-k})`, a summable sequence. The comparison profile `m^*(x)=cG(0,x)` for sufficiently small fixed `c` obeys both generic constraints and has

\[
\sum_xw_R(x)m^*(x)=\Theta(\log R).
\]

Therefore no proof based only on pointwise Green domination plus the first-score `l^2` estimate can yield `o(log R)`. The missing information must use the Bellman source-sink structure in (3.2), i.e. actual terminal-measure cancellation across scales.
