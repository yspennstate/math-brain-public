Y(a)=F(a)^{1/\beta},\qquad

## 4. Method B1 — change category: Stieltjes and complete-Bernstein structure

Let `T=SS'`.  From the exact CDF identity of the preceding run,

\[
q(a):=Q(r(a))=\mathbb E\frac{T}{a+T}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### 4.1 `q` is Stieltjes

Equation (4.1) is exactly

\[
q(a)=\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{\nu(dt)}{a+t},
\qquad
\nu(dt)=t\,\mathbb P(T\in dt).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The Stieltjes integrability condition holds because

\[
\int\frac{\nu(dt)}{1+t}
=\mathbb E\frac{T}{1+T}<1.
\]

Consequently

\[
(-1)^nq^{(n)}(a)
=n!\,\mathbb E\frac{T}{(a+T)^{n+1}}>0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.2 Both the CDF and its odds are complete Bernstein

Define

\[
m(a)=M(r(a))=1-q(a).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{m(a)}a=\mathbb E\frac1{a+T}
\tag{4.4}
\]

is Stieltjes, so `m` is a complete Bernstein function.

More strongly, define CDF odds

\[
h(a)=\frac{m(a)}{q(a)}=q(a)^{-1}-1.
\tag{4.5}
\]

A Stieltjes function maps the upper half-plane into the lower half-plane.  Thus `1/q` maps the upper half-plane into itself; subtracting the real constant one preserves that mapping.  Since `0<q(a)<1` for positive `a`, `h(a)>0`.  The analytic characterization of complete Bernstein functions therefore proves that `h` is itself CBF.

The radial law is reconstructed exactly as

\[
\boxed{M(r(a))=\frac{h(a)}{1+h(a)},\qquad
Q(r(a))=\frac1{1+h(a)}}
\tag{4.6}
\]

and, from (3.1),

\[
\boxed{r(a)^\beta=\frac{a^\beta}{C_\beta(1+h(a))}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

### 4.3 A negative theorem: the inverse leaves the Bernstein hierarchy

Using (3.4),

\[
h(a)\sim\frac{a^\beta}{C_\beta\beta\log a}.
\]

If `x=a^beta`, this is

\[
h(a)\sim\frac{x}{C_\beta\log x}.
\]

Hence regular-variation inversion gives

\[
\boxed{h^{-1}(t)\sim[C_\beta t\log t]^{1/\beta}.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Because `1/beta>1`, `h^{-1}(t)/t -> infinity`.  Every Bernstein function vanishing at zero is concave, so its ratio to `t` is nonincreasing and it is `O(t)`.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{h^{-1}\text{ is not a Bernstein function for any }0<\beta<1.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

**B1 outcome.**  The CDF has a canonical Stieltjes/CBF structure, but this category does not magically invert the source law: its inverse provably exits the Bernstein hierarchy.  This is useful obstruction information rather than another implicit restatement.

## 5. Method B12 — conserved Mellin shift and a direct rational-index ODE

Let

\[
\beta=p/q\in(0,1),\qquad \gcd(p,q)=1.
\]

The exact Mellin transform (3.2) obeys the finite shift recurrence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\widehat F(s+p)}{\widehat F(s)}
=(-1)^p(\beta c_\beta)^{2q}
\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(s+j)^2}
{\prod_{k=0}^{q-1}(s+\beta k)^2}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This follows directly from the Gamma functional equation.  Define

\[
D_a=a\frac d{da},
\]

\[
P_p(D)=\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(D-j)^2,
\qquad
R_{p,q}(D)=\prod_{k=0}^{q-1}(D+p-\beta k)^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because Mellin sends `D_a` to multiplication by `-s` and sends multiplication by `a^p` to a shift `s -> s+p`, (5.1) is equivalent to the exact linear ODE

\[
\boxed{
P_p(D_a)F
=(-1)^p\frac{a^p}{(\beta c_\beta)^{2q}}
R_{p,q}(D_a)F.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The new point is to eliminate `a` as well.

Set

\[
\mathcal D=r\frac d{dr},\qquad
\sigma=\mathcal D\log Q.
\]

From

\[
r=a(Q/C_\beta)^{1/\beta},
\]
if

\[
L=\frac{d\log r}{d\log a},
\]
then

\[
L=1+\frac\sigma\beta L,
\qquad
\boxed{L=\frac\beta{\beta-\sigma}.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus on radial pullbacks

\[
D_a=\mathscr A_Q,
\qquad
\boxed{
\mathscr A_Q=
\frac\beta{\beta-\mathcal D\log Q}\,\mathcal D.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Also

\[
r^\beta=a^\beta Q/C_\beta
\]

implies

\[
\boxed{a^p=r^p(C_\beta/Q)^q.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Substitution into (5.3), with `F=Q/C_beta`, yields the direct nonlinear radial equation

\[
\boxed{
P_p(\mathscr A_Q)Q
=(-1)^p\Lambda_\beta r^pQ^{-q}
R_{p,q}(\mathscr A_Q)Q,
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_\beta=
\left(\frac{\Gamma(\beta)}
{\beta\Gamma(1-\beta)}\right)^q.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This equation contains neither the PWIT fixed-point unknown `y_*`, nor the ratio coordinate `a`, nor a stable expectation.  It is a finite nonlinear ODE directly for the radial tail whenever `alpha/2` is rational.

The origin data begin with

\[
Q(0)=1,
\qquad
\boxed{Q'(0)=-m_1^2C_\beta^{1/\beta}},
\tag{5.9}
\]

where

\[
m_u=\frac{\Gamma(1+u/\beta)}
{\Gamma(1+u)c_\beta^{u/\beta}}.
\]

The identity in (5.9) uses `beta C_beta m_{beta+1}=m_1`; all higher finite origin derivatives are already explicit by `M_jet=+infinity`.

For the exactly checkable alpha=1 case, `beta=1/2`, `(p,q)=(1,2)` and `Lambda=4`, so (5.7) reduces to

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr A_Q^2Q
=-4rQ^{-2}
(\mathscr A_Q+1)^2
(\mathscr A_Q+\tfrac12)^2Q.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

The previously derived exact Struve/Bessel solution satisfies the corresponding `a`-equation identically; this is an internal consistency check on the shift constants, not an independent audit.

**B12 outcome.**  Every rational `alpha/2` now has a finite direct radial ODE, in addition to the preceding Meijer-G parameterization.  Rational cases therefore no longer hide any self-consistent PWIT variable.

## 6. Method B29 — compactify infinity and derive all tail orders

The right-half-plane singularities of (3.2) occur at

\[
s=n\beta,\qquad n\ge1.
\]

Normally they are double poles from `Gamma(1-s/beta)^2`.  If `n beta=m` is a positive integer, the zero of `1/Gamma(1-s)` reduces the pole to first order.  This is the resonance mechanism; when `beta=p/q`, it occurs exactly when `q` divides `n`.

For nonresonant `n` define

\[
A_n=
\frac{\beta^2 c_\beta^{2n-1}\Gamma(n\beta)}
{\Gamma(1+\beta)\Gamma(1-n\beta)((n-1)!)^2},
\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
B_n=-A_n\left[
\psi(n\beta)+\psi(1-n\beta)
+\frac2\beta(\gamma_E-H_{n-1}+\log c_\beta)
\right].
\tag{6.2}
\]

At a resonance `m=n beta in N`, define

\[
C_n=(-1)^{m+1}
\frac{\beta^2c_\beta^{2n-1}((m-1)!)^2}
{\Gamma(1+\beta)((n-1)!)^2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The Laurent expansion at a nonresonant pole follows from

\[
\Gamma(1-s/\beta)
=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}
\left[-\frac\beta\epsilon+H_{n-1}-\gamma_E+O(\epsilon)\right],
\]

where `s=n beta+epsilon`.  At a resonance,

\[
\frac1{\Gamma(1-s)}=(-1)^m(m-1)!\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2)
\]

cancels one pole order.  Shifting the inverse-Mellin contour to the right contributes minus each residue.  Stirling gives, on every fixed pole-avoiding vertical line,

\[
|\widehat F(\sigma+it)|
\le C_\sigma(1+|t|)^{K_\sigma}e^{-\pi|t|/\beta},
\]

so the shift can be repeated to arbitrary finite order.  Therefore for every fixed `N`, and `sigma_N` between `N beta` and `(N+1) beta` avoiding poles,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
F(a)
={}&\sum_{\substack{1\le n\le N\\n\beta\notin\mathbb N}}
 a^{-n\beta}(A_n\log a+B_n)\\
&+\sum_{\substack{1\le n\le N\\n\beta\in\mathbb N}}
 C_na^{-n\beta}
+O(a^{-\sigma_N}).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The first pole gives exactly

\[
A_1=\beta,\qquad B_1=\eta_\beta,
\]

recovering the preceding leading theorem.  At `beta=1/2`, the first resonance is `n=2` and the formula gives

\[
\boxed{C_2=\pi/2,}
\tag{6.5}
\]

matching the next term in the exact alpha=1 special-function expansion.

Now compactify infinity with

\[
x=r^\beta,\qquad y=a^{-\beta}.
\]

Since `x=a^beta F=F/y`, equation (6.4) becomes, to arbitrary finite order,

\[
\boxed{
x=-\log y+\eta_\beta
+\sum_{n\ge2}y^{n-1}\Phi_n(\log y),}
\tag{6.6}
\]

where nonresonantly

\[
\Phi_n(\ell)=-(A_n/\beta)\ell+B_n,
\]

and resonantly `Phi_n=C_n`.

The leading solution is `y_0=e^(eta_beta-x)`.  Finite-order asymptotic reversion gives explicit polynomials `P_j`, `deg P_j<=j`, satisfying for every fixed `N`

\[
\boxed{
y=e^{\eta_\beta-x}
\left[1+\sum_{j=1}^NP_j(x)e^{-jx}
+O(x^{N+1}e^{-(N+1)x})\right].}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Because `Q=C_beta F=C_beta x y`, the full radial-tail transseries is

\[
\boxed{
Q(r)=D_\beta x e^{-x}
\left[1+\sum_{j=1}^NP_j(x)e^{-jx}
+O(x^{N+1}e^{-(N+1)x})\right],
\quad x=r^\beta,
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

where `D_beta=C_beta e^(eta_beta)`.

For a nonresonant second pole,

\[
P_1(x)=e^{\eta_\beta}
\left[(A_2/\beta)(x-\eta_\beta)+B_2\right].
\tag{6.9}
\]

For `beta=1/2`, resonance gives

\[
P_1(x)=2e^{-\gamma_E}.
\]

Hence the alpha=1 squared-radius tail is sharpened to

\[
\boxed{
1-M(r)=2e^{-\gamma_E}\sqrt r\,e^{-\sqrt r}
\left[1+2e^{-\gamma_E}e^{-\sqrt r}
+O(\sqrt r\,e^{-2\sqrt r})\right].
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Termwise differentiation gives every finite exponential order of the planar density as well.

**B29 outcome.**  The far-tail correction is no longer a single leading exponent: the complete Mellin pole lattice fixes the full finite-order transseries, with an explicit periodic resonance pattern for rational beta.
