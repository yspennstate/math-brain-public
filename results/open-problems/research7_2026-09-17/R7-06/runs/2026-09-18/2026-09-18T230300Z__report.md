## 4. Method B11 — dualize to an exact one-transform CDF

\[

\tag{4.1}
\]

For `0<Re s<beta`, Tonelli and the beta integral give

\[
\mathcal Mq(s)=\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)E[T^s].
\]

The stable fractional-moment formula yields

\[
\mathcal Mq(s)=c_\beta^{2s/\beta}
\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s/\beta)^2}{\Gamma(1-s)}.

\[
\mathcal Mq(s)=C_\beta\mathcal MF(s).

\[
\boxed{1-M(r(a))=C_\beta F(a).}
\tag{4.3}

\[
\boxed{1-M(r(a))=C_\beta\frac{r(a)^\beta}{a^\beta}.}

\[

## 5. Method B7 — take the far-radius limit and correct the printed exponent

### 5.1 Internal source inconsistency

The source's own stable-density expansion is

\[
g_\beta(x)=\beta x^{-\beta-1}+O(x^{-2\beta-1}).
\]

Multiplying by `x^beta` and integrating from 1 to `t` gives

\[
\boxed{E[S^\beta;S\le t]=\beta\log t+C+O(t^{-\beta}).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The printed Lemma 4.8 omits the factor `beta` in front of `log t`.  That missing factor propagates into the printed Lemma 4.10.  Under the same normalization, its leading relation should be

\[
\boxed{r^\beta=\beta\log(ry_*^{-2})+O(1),}
\tag{5.2}
\]

not `r^beta=log(ry_*^-2)+O(1)`.

Since `a=ry_*^{-2}`, (5.2) predicts `log a ~ r^beta/beta`, and therefore the exponential scale `exp(-r^beta)` in squared radius, rather than `exp(-beta r^beta)`.

### 5.2 Mellin proof and explicit constant

The correction can be derived independently from the exact Mellin transform (3.2).  At the first right-hand singularity `s=beta`, write `s=beta+epsilon`.  Expanding the Gamma factors gives

\[
\mathcal MF(\beta+\epsilon)
=\frac\beta{\epsilon^2}+\frac{B_\beta}{\epsilon}+O(1),
\tag{5.3}
\]

with

\[
B_\beta=2\gamma_E+2\log\Gamma(1-\beta)
+\beta\{\psi(\beta)+\psi(1-\beta)\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

A Mellin contour shift to the right, with Stirling decay on vertical lines and the next pole avoided, yields

\[
\boxed{F(a)=a^{-\beta}(\beta\log a+\eta_\beta)
+O(a^{-\beta-\delta})}
\tag{5.5}
\]

for any sufficiently small fixed `delta>0`, where

\[
\boxed{\eta_\beta=-2\gamma_E-2\log\Gamma(1-\beta)
-\beta\{\psi(\beta)+\psi(1-\beta)\}.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Equation (3.1) becomes

\[
\boxed{r(a)^\beta=\beta\log a+\eta_\beta+o(1).}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Substitute this into the exact CDF formula (4.4).  Then

\[
\boxed{1-M(r)\sim D_\beta r^\beta e^{-r^\beta},}
\tag{5.8}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
D_\beta=\frac{\Gamma(1+\beta)}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}
\exp[-2\gamma_E-\beta\{\psi(\beta)+\psi(1-\beta)\}].
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Differentiating via the differentiated Mellin asymptotic gives

\[
M'(r)\sim\beta D_\beta r^{2\beta-1}e^{-r^\beta}.
\]

Because `M'(r)=pi g_alpha(sqrt(r))`, the planar density therefore satisfies

\[
\boxed{

\sim A_\alpha\rho^{2\alpha-2}e^{-\rho^\alpha},
\qquad
A_\alpha=\frac\beta\pi D_\beta,
\quad\beta=\alpha/2.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

The printed BCC Theorem 1.3 has the same polynomial power but exponential `exp[-(alpha/2)rho^alpha]`.  The direct source inconsistency (5.1)–(5.2) and the independent Mellin derivation (5.3)–(5.10) agree on the corrected exponent.

## 6. Method B30 — rational beta is a standard Meijer-G law

Let `beta=p/q` in lowest terms and define

\[
\mathcal A_m=\{0,1/m,\ldots,(m-1)/m\}.
\]

Set `s=pt` in the Mellin inversion (3.2) and apply Gauss multiplication to `Gamma(pt)`, `Gamma(1-pt)`, and both factors `Gamma(1-qt)`.  In the standard Meijer-G convention this gives

\[
\boxed{
F_{p/q}(a)=
\frac{q(2\pi)^{1-q}}{C_\beta}
G_{2q,2p}^{p,2q}\!\left(
Z\middle|
\begin{matrix}
\mathcal A_q,\mathcal A_q\\
\mathcal A_p,\mathcal A_p
\end{matrix}
\right),
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

where the first copy of `A_p` belongs to the `m=p` lower numerator block, all `2q` upper parameters belong to the `n=2q` numerator block, and

\[
\boxed{Z=\frac{q^{2q}a^p}{p^{2p}\Gamma(1-\beta)^{2q}}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The low-denominator specializations include:

- `beta=1/2`: `Z=16a/pi^2`, recovering the Struve/Bessel representation after standard Meijer-G reduction;
- `beta=1/3`: a `G_{6,2}^{1,6}` with upper parameters `(0,1/3,2/3)` repeated twice and lower `(0,0)`;
