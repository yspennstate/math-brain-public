## 5. B5 — add a parameter and differentiate: exact two-edge deformation

### Idea

Instead of changing the bulk parameter and simultaneously changing the law of every punctured configuration, first freeze the bulk defect law and vary the two deleted axial edges independently. This cleanly separates local monotonicity from the genuinely global dependence hidden in the defect partition masses.

Let `H=Z^2\{xy,yz}` with all remaining edges at bulk parameter `p`. On `{x,y,z}` denote partition probabilities

- `c`: all three connected;
- `a`: `xy|z`;
- `a'`: `yz|x`;
- `b`: `xz|y`;
- `s`: all separate.

Restore `xy` with probability `r` and `yz` with probability `t`, independently of `H`.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact two-edge deformation identity

\[
\boxed{
D(r,t):=\mathbb P(x\leftrightarrow y)-\mathbb P(x\leftrightarrow z)
=(1-t)[a-(1-r)b+rs].
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
\Delta_H=a-b,
\qquad
D_H=b+s,
\]

\[
\boxed{D(r,t)=(1-t)(\Delta_H+rD_H).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Condition on the five terminal partitions in `H`.

- `xyz`: both target indicators equal one; contribution `0`.
- `xy|z`: `x<->y` is already true; `x<->z` occurs iff `yz` is restored; conditional difference `1-t`.
- `yz|x`: both `x<->y` and `x<->z` occur iff `xy` is restored; difference `0`.
- `xz|y`: `x<->z` is already true. The vertex `y` joins the common cluster unless **both** restored path edges fail, so the difference is `-(1-r)(1-t)`.
- `x|y|z`: `x<->y` needs `xy`, probability `r`; `x<->z` needs both path edges, probability `rt`; difference `r(1-t)`.

Multiplying by partition masses and summing gives (5.1); regrouping gives (5.2). QED.

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\partial_rD(r,t)=(1-t)(b+s)\ge0.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the sign is independent of the far-edge parameter `t<1`; changing `t` only multiplies the difference by `1-t`. Increasing the **near** edge parameter `r` can only improve the comparison.

If `b>a`, define

\[
r_*(H)={b-a\over b+s};
\tag{5.4}
\]

and take `r_*=0` if `b<=a`. For `t<1`, the first comparison is equivalent to `r>=r_*(H)`. In the homogeneous model, where the defect law itself is `H_p` and `r=t=p`, the exact source milestone becomes

\[
\boxed{p\ge r_*(H_p).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This does not solve the problem, but it isolates the only global quantity the local edge interpolation cannot control.

## 7. B25 — double-count / local bijection: a finite-energy constraint and its deficit

### Idea

The previous critical proof identified the all-four-connected puncture orbit `epsilon` as the dangerous negative contribution. Try to map an all-connected configuration to a triple-plus-singleton configuration by closing edges at a terminal that is a leaf of a minimal connecting tree.

For each terminal `w in {x,u,z,d}`, let `E_w` be the event that the **other three** terminals are connected after deleting the three remaining `K`-edges incident to `w`. The central edge to `y` is already absent in `K`.

### Proposition 7.1 — leaf-surgery inequality

\[
\boxed{\delta\ge {q^3\over2}\epsilon.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof

On the `epsilon` event all four puncture terminals lie in one open cluster. Choose a finite open tree minimal under edge deletion while still spanning the four terminals. Every leaf of a minimal Steiner tree is a terminal, and any finite tree has at least two leaves. If `w` is such a leaf, removing `w` from the tree leaves the other three terminals connected along edges that avoid `w`; therefore `E_w` holds.

Pointwise,

\[
\sum_w \mathbf1_{E_w}\ge2\mathbf1_{\epsilon}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

By the `D_4` symmetry of the punctured square lattice, all four `P(E_w)` are equal, hence

\[
P(E_d)\ge\epsilon/2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The event `E_d` is defined after deleting the three noncentral edges incident to `d`, so it is independent of their Bernoulli states. If `E_d` occurs and all three such edges are closed, `d` is isolated while `x,u,z` stay connected. This is exactly one specified `delta` partition and has probability `q^3P(E_d)`. Thus (7.1) follows. QED.

### Lemma 8.1 — fixed-point connectivity is continuous at a nonpercolating parameter

Let `G` be locally finite and suppose

\[
P_{p_0}(x\leftrightarrow\infty)=0.
\]

For fixed `w`, `p -> P_p(x<->w)` is continuous at `p_0`.

### Proof

Let `Lambda_R` be finite boxes increasing to `G`, containing `x,w`. Let `A_R` be the event that `x` connects to `w` by a path entirely in `Lambda_R`, and `B_R` the event that `x` connects inside `Lambda_R` to its boundary. Both are finite cylinder events, so their probabilities are polynomial, hence continuous, in `p`.

For `A={x<->w}`,

\[
A_R\subseteq A,
\qquad
A\setminus A_R\subseteq B_R.
\tag{8.1}
\]

At `p_0`, `P(B_R)` decreases to `P(x<->infinity)=0`. Given `eps>0`, choose `R` with `P_{p0}(B_R)<eps`. For `p` sufficiently close to `p0`, finite-cylinder continuity gives

\[
|P_p(A_R)-P_{p0}(A_R)|<eps,
\qquad
P_p(B_R)<2eps.
\]

Using (8.1) gives

\[
P_p(A)\le P_{p0}(A)+3eps
\]

and, since `P_{p0}(A)-P_{p0}(A_R)<=eps`, also

\[
P_p(A)\ge P_{p0}(A)-2eps.
\]

Hence continuity. QED.
