## 4. B18 — both zero-response arcs become one cubic-theta interval

The two nontrivial zero arcs are

\[
\Gamma_-(\theta)=(\cos(\theta+2\pi/3),\cos\theta),
\quad0\le\theta\le\pi/3,
\]

and

\[
\Gamma_+(\theta)=(\cos(\theta-2\pi/3),\cos\theta),
\quad0\le\theta\le\pi/6.
\]

On `Gamma_-` put

\[
t=\cos(2\theta+\pi/3),
\]
while on `Gamma_+` put

\[
t=\cos(2\theta-\pi/3).
\]

Direct trigonometric reduction gives on **both** branches

\[
\boxed{\alpha=(t-1/2)/3},
\qquad
\boxed{\beta=(2t^2-2t-1)/3}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The first branch covers `t in [-1,1/2]`; the second covers `t in [1/2,1]`. In physical lattice coordinates `u=Ae_1+Be_2`, `Gamma_-` is `A=1/3`, and `Gamma_+` is `A+B=1/3`. The Weyl map

\[
(A,B)\mapsto(A+B,-B)
\]

maps the second line to the first while preserving `A2` and the Gaussian. Hence the restriction of `F_a` to the **entire** zero skeleton is one scalar function `f_a(t)` on `[-1,1]`.

Poisson summation gives, with

\[
q=e^{-4\pi^2/(3a)}\in(0,1),
\]

a positive scalar multiple of

\[
\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb Z}q^{m^2-mn+n^2}e^{2\pi i(mA+nB)}.
\]

At `A=1/3`, after `n -> -n`, this is the Hirschhorn--Garvan--Borwein cubic theta

\[
b(q,z)=\sum_{m,n}\omega^{m-n}q^{m^2+mn+n^2}z^n,
\qquad
z=\omega e^{-2\pi iB},\quad\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]

Their equation (1.22) gives

\[
b(q,z)
=(q;q)_\infty(q^3;q^3)_\infty
\frac{(zq;q)_\infty(z^{-1}q;q)_\infty}
{(zq^3;q^3)_\infty(z^{-1}q^3;q^3)_\infty}.
\]

Since `Re(z)=-t`, cancellation of the indices divisible by three yields the exact product

\[
\boxed{
 f_a(t)=A_a
 \prod_{\substack{n\ge1\\3\nmid n}}
 (1+2tq^n+q^{2n}),

}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the useful B18 translation: the formerly two-dimensional conic obstruction is a one-variable cubic-theta product with every factor affine in the interpolation coordinate.

## 5. B4 — complete all-parameter theorem on the zero skeleton

For `-1<=t<=1`, every factor in (4.2) obeys

\[
1+2tq^n+q^{2n}\ge(1-q^n)^2>0
\]

and has positive `t`-slope. For every fixed `k>=1`, uniform product differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{
 f_a^{(k)}(t)=
 k! f_a(t)
 \sum_{\substack{n_1<\cdots<n_k\\3\nmid n_j}}
 \prod_{j=1}^k
 \frac{2q^{n_j}}{1+2tq^{n_j}+q^{2n_j}}>0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Uniformity follows from

\[
\sum_{3\nmid n}\frac{2q^n}{(1-q^n)^2}<\infty.
\]

Therefore `f_a` is **strictly absolutely monotone** on the complete interval for every `a>0`.

By (4.1), `(Pi F_a)|_{Z_K}` is a polynomial of degree at most two in `t`. It matches `f_a` at `t=-1` (`q`) and `t=1/2` (`p`). It also matches the `t` derivative at `p`, because on either branch at `theta=0`, `dy/dtheta=0` and `(dx/dtheta)/(dt/dtheta)=1/2`, while `Pi` matches `F_x(p)`. Thus it is exactly the quadratic Hermite interpolant with nodes `(-1;1/2,1/2)`.

The Newton remainder is

\[
\boxed{
 f_a(t)-(\Pi F_a)(t)
 =f_a[-1,1/2,1/2,t](t+1)(t-1/2)^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The third divided difference is strictly positive because `f_a'''>0`; the node product is nonnegative on all of `[-1,1]`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
 R_0=(I-\Pi)F_a\ge0

}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equality occurs only at the forced contact points `p` and `q`.

This **completely removes the zero-response condition** from the previous exact scalar feasibility criterion, uniformly over the entire Gaussian parameter axis.

## 6. B30 — identify where gluing can and cannot fail

At every regular point of `Z_K` other than `p,q`, (5.3) is strict while `K_C` changes sign transversely. Therefore

\[
R_0/K_C\to+\infty\quad\hbox{from }K_C>0,
\qquad
R_0/K_C\to-\infty\quad\hbox{from }K_C<0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Such points cannot support a finite `C_+` minimizer or `C_-` maximizer.

The contact `p` is a double zero, so write

\[

Expanding the exact polynomial (3.1),

\[
K_C=3v+2h^2+2hv+10v^2+O(|h|^2|v|+|h||v|^2+|v|^3).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Write `r_y=R_{0,y}(p)` and `r_xx=R_{0,xx}(p)`. Since `R_0(p)=R_{0,x}(p)=0`,

\[
R_0=r_yv+\tfrac12r_{xx}h^2+O(|h||v|+v^2+|h|^3).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The zero curve has

\[
v=-\frac23h^2+O(h^3).
\]

The strict Hermite residual from (5.2) has a positive quadratic leading term there, so

\[
\boxed{\tfrac12r_{xx}-\tfrac23r_y>0}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\boxed{r_y/3<r_{xx}/4}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

On the parabolic blow-up `v=-lambda h^2`,

\[
\frac{R_0}{K_C}\longrightarrow
L(\lambda)=
\frac{\tfrac12r_{xx}-\lambda r_y}{2-3\lambda},
\]

and

\[
L'(\lambda)=
\frac{(3r_{xx}-4r_y)/2}{(2-3\lambda)^2}>0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus the finite local limits on the `K_C>0` side begin at `r_xx/4` and increase to `+infinity`, while those on the `K_C<0` side increase from `-infinity` toward `r_y/3`. The strict gap (6.4) means the double contact `p` itself cannot reverse the required envelope order.

\[
\boxed{
K_C=\frac43 s(s+1)(2s^2+2su+2u^2-3u).

\tag{6.6}

Hence the denominator is cubic on the natural cusp scaling `u~s^2`; the local numerator requires a separate cusp compactification. Away from the zero set, a genuine global active point in either sign component can also still determine `C_-` or `C_+`.

**B30 outcome: SUCCESS as localization.** The gluing problem is no longer allowed to fail on the regular zero arcs or at `p`; it has been pushed to the `q` cusp and/or genuine global active points.
