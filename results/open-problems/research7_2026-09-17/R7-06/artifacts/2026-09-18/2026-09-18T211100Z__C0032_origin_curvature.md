# C0032: exact first nonconstant coefficient of the heavy-tailed circular-law density

Status: **PARTIAL** toward the Bordenave–Chafaï explicit-density problem.  This does **not** identify the full all-radius density.

## 1. Source equations and notation

Use the notation of Bordenave–Caputo–Chafaï, *Spectrum of non-Hermitian heavy tailed random matrices*, arXiv:1006.1713v5, Section 4.2.  Put

\[
\beta=\alpha/2\in(0,1),\qquad r=|z|^2.
\]

Let `S,S'` be independent positive beta-stable random variables normalized by

\[
\mathbb E e^{-xS}=\exp\{-\Gamma(1-\beta)x^\beta\},\qquad x\ge0.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The source's scalar function `y_*(r)` is the unique positive solution of

\[
1=\mathbb E\left(\frac{S}{r+y_*(r)^2SS'}\right)^\beta.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The source proves `y_*` is `C^1`, and its equation (4.11), followed by Lemma 4.5, gives the radial density.  If

\[
Y(r):=y_*(r)^2,\qquad T:=SS',
\]

and

\[
\widetilde g_\alpha(r):=g_\alpha(\sqrt r),
\]

then

\[
\pi\widetilde g_\alpha(r)
=
\bigl(Y(r)-rY'(r)\bigr)
\,\mathbb E\frac{T}{(r+Y(r)T)^2}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The source computes only `g_alpha(0)` explicitly.  The purpose here is to compute the exact first nonconstant radial coefficient.

## 2. Negative moments of the source-normalized stable law

For every `q>0`, Mellin inversion of (1.1), equivalently source equation (4.10), gives

\[
m_q:=\mathbb E S^{-q}
=
\frac{1}{\Gamma(q)}\int_0^\infty x^{q-1}
 e^{-\Gamma(1-\beta)x^\beta}\,dx
=
\frac{\Gamma(1+q/\beta)}
{\Gamma(1+q)\Gamma(1-\beta)^{q/\beta}}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular all negative moments that occur below are finite.  At `r=0`, (1.2) becomes

\[
1=Y(0)^{-\beta}m_\beta,
\]

so

\[
y_0:=y_*(0)=m_\beta^{1/(2\beta)},
\qquad
Y_0:=Y(0)=m_\beta^{1/\beta}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is the source's origin computation.

## 3. B2 — negate the historical obstruction: the scalar equation differentiates exactly

The historical campaign attempt C0032-A001 said that away from the origin the PWIT equation did not reduce to a scalar equation.  Equation (1.2) shows that obstruction is false: the original paper already scalarizes at every radius.

Rewrite (1.2) as

\[
F(r,y):=
\mathbb E\bigl(r/S+y^2S'\bigr)^{-\beta}-1=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

At `(0,y_0)`, dominated differentiation is justified by the finite negative moments (2.1).  We get

\[
F_r(0,y_0)
=-\beta m_1m_{\beta+1}y_0^{-2\beta-2},
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
F_y(0,y_0)
=-2\beta m_\beta y_0^{-2\beta-1}\ne0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore the one-sided derivative at the physical boundary `r=0` is

\[
\boxed{
 y_*'(0)
=-\frac{m_1m_{\beta+1}}
{2m_\beta y_0}
}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
Y'(0)=2y_0y_*'(0)
=-\frac{m_1m_{\beta+1}}{m_\beta}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is already an exact Gamma-function quantity by (2.1).

## 4. B14 — radial symmetry makes the density derivative close on negative moments

Define

\[
H(r):=\mathbb E\frac{T}{(r+Y(r)T)^2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because `Y` is `C^1` and `m_1,m_2<infinity`, dominated differentiation at zero gives

\[
H(0)=Y_0^{-2}m_1^2,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
H'(0)
&=-2\,\mathbb E\left[
T\,(Y_0T)^{-3}\bigl(1+Y'(0)T\bigr)
\right]\\
&=-2Y_0^{-3}\left(m_2^2+Y'(0)m_1^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `P(r)=Y(r)-rY'(r)`.  Although the source only needs `Y` to be `C^1`, this is enough to evaluate the right derivative of `P` at zero:

\[
\frac{P(r)-P(0)}r
=
\frac{Y(r)-Y_0}{r}-Y'(r)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence differentiating (1.3) at zero does **not** require `Y''(0)`:

\[
\pi\widetilde g_\alpha'(0)=Y_0H'(0).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Substituting (3.5) into (4.3) yields the closed moment formula

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde g_\alpha'(0)
=
\frac{2}{\pi}Y_0^{-2}
\left(
\frac{m_1^3m_{\beta+1}}{m_\beta}-m_2^2
\right).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Now insert (2.1).  Since

\[
Y_0^{-2}
=\bigl(\Gamma(1+\beta)\Gamma(1-\beta)\bigr)^{2/\beta},
\tag{4.7}
\]

and

\[
\frac{m_{\beta+1}}{m_\beta}
=
\frac{1}{\beta}\Gamma(1+1/\beta)
\Gamma(1-\beta)^{-1/\beta},
\tag{4.8}
\]

one obtains after cancellation

\[
\boxed{
 c_{\alpha,1}:=\widetilde g_\alpha'(0)
=
\frac{2}{\pi}
\left(
\frac{\Gamma(1+\beta)}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}
\right)^{2/\beta}
\left[
\frac1\beta\Gamma(1+1/\beta)^4
-\frac14\Gamma(1+2/\beta)^2
\right].
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Thus, with `rho=|z|`,

\[
\boxed{
 g_\alpha(\rho)
=g_\alpha(0)+c_{\alpha,1}\rho^2+o(\rho^2)
\qquad(\rho\downarrow0).
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

In terms of `alpha` alone,

\[
\boxed{
 c_{\alpha,1}
=
\frac{2}{\pi}
\left(
\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/2)}
\right)^{4/\alpha}
\left[
\frac{2}{\alpha}\Gamma(1+2/\alpha)^4
-\frac14\Gamma(1+4/\alpha)^2
\right].
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

This is the first nonconstant exact coefficient beyond the source's explicit origin value.

## 5. Strict sign: the origin has negative quadratic curvature for every 0<alpha<2

The prefactor in (4.9) is positive.  Put

\[
x=1/\beta>1.
\]

The bracket in (4.9) is

\[
x\Gamma(x+1)^4-\frac14\Gamma(2x+1)^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

It is strictly negative.  Indeed set

\[
R(x)=\frac{\Gamma(2x+1)}{\Gamma(x+1)^2},
\qquad
f(x)=\log\frac{R(x)}{2\sqrt x}.
\]

We have `f(1)=0`.  Using the duplication identity for the digamma function,

\[
f'(x)
=\psi(x+1/2)-\psi(x+1)+2\log2-\frac1{2x}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Also

\[
\psi(x+1)-\psi(x+1/2)
=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-(x+1/2)t}}{1+e^{-t/2}}\,dt
<\frac1{x+1/2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore, for `x>=1`,

\[
f'(x)>
2\log2-\frac1{2x}-\frac1{x+1/2}
\ge 2\log2-\frac12-\frac23>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence `R(x)>2 sqrt(x)` for every `x>1`, which is exactly

\[
\Gamma(2x+1)^2>4x\Gamma(x+1)^4.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{c_{\alpha,1}<0\quad\text{for every }0<\alpha<2.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

So every BCC heavy-tailed circular-law density has a strict quadratic local maximum at the origin:

\[
\boxed{
 g_\alpha(\rho)<g_\alpha(0)
\quad\text{for all sufficiently small }\rho>0.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This local-shape conclusion is not part of the source theorem, which gives the value at zero but not the first radial curvature.
