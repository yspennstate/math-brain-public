## 1. Inherited reduction and the precise bridge attempted here

The previous R7-06 theorem treats the two-eigenvalue boundary
\[
H=H^T\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N},\qquad H^2=NI,
\]
and proves that some balanced cut `N=2r`,
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}P&C\\ C^T&D\end{pmatrix},
\]
has `corank(C)<=sqrt(N)`. It also reduces every singular cross-block Gram fiber to a positive-definite sign-Gram fiber with only `2^{O(N^{3/2})}` additional conditioned entropy. Thus singular endpoints are not omitted here: they are inherited into the same positive-fiber bottleneck at subquadratic cost.

For an invertible cross block,
\[
K:=CC^T=2rI-P^2>0.
\tag{1.1}
\]
The missing global bridge is that one cannot pay independently for all seeds `P` and then the worst Gram-root fiber of `K`. This note proves two structural facts aimed directly at that bridge:

1. a quantitative identity-level theorem showing that a high-volume cross Gram matrix forces the seed `P` to be near-Hadamard, with an exact determinant deficit controlled by row-correlation energy;
2. a classification of the first possible nontrivial positive-Gram support ambiguity: every support-distance-four trade is exactly a closed-quadruple-type switch. A 2026 order-24 Hadamard classification fact then gives a concrete counterexample to the stronger intermediate hope that all Gram ambiguity is generated by those local four-column moves.

## 2. Determinant deficit controls the seed's correlation energy

### Theorem 2.1 (logdet-to-near-Hadamard rigidity)

Let
\[
P=P^T\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times r}
\]
and assume
\[
K:=2rI-P^2>0.
\]
Define the off-diagonal row-correlation energy
\[
\mathcal E(P)
:=\sum_{i\ne j}\langle P_{i,*},P_{j,*}\rangle^2.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{r^r}{\det K}
\ge \frac{\mathcal E(P)}{4r^2}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\det K\le r^r\exp\!\left(-\frac{\mathcal E(P)}{4r^2}\right).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Moreover
\[
\det K=r^r
\iff K=rI
\iff P^2=rI.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Proof

Because every entry of `P` is a sign,
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P^2)=\|P\|_F^2=r^2.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Set
\[
A:=\frac{P^2-rI}{r}.
\]
Then `A=A^T`, `tr A=0`, and from `K>0`, every eigenvalue `a` of `A` lies in `[-1,1)`. Also
\[
\frac Kr=I-A.
\]
Therefore
\[
\log\frac{r^r}{\det K}
=-\log\det(I-A)
=\sum_{i=1}^r -\log(1-a_i).
\]
Since `sum_i a_i=0`,
\[
\log\frac{r^r}{\det K}
=\sum_{i=1}^r\bigl[-\log(1-a_i)-a_i\bigr].
\tag{2.5}
\]
For every `a in [-1,1)`,
\[
-\log(1-a)-a\ge\frac{a^2}{4}.
\tag{2.6}
\]
Indeed, for
\[
g(a)=-\log(1-a)-a-a^2/4,
\]
one has
\[
g'(a)=\frac{a(1+a)}{2(1-a)}.
\]
Thus `g` decreases from `g(-1)=1-log 2-1/4>0` to `g(0)=0` on `[-1,0]`, and increases from zero on `[0,1)`.

Applying (2.6) in (2.5),
\[
\log\frac{r^r}{\det K}
\ge\frac14\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
=\frac1{4r^2}\|P^2-rI\|_F^2.
\tag{2.7}
\]
The diagonal of `P^2` is exactly `r`, hence
\[
\|P^2-rI\|_F^2
=\sum_{i\ne j}(P^2_{ij})^2
=\sum_{i\ne j}\langle P_{i,*},P_{j,*}\rangle^2
=\mathcal E(P).
\]
This proves (2.1)-(2.2).

Equality `det K=r^r` forces the left side of (2.1) to vanish, hence `A=0`, i.e. `P^2=rI`, and then `K=rI`. Conversely `P^2=rI` gives `K=rI` and equality. QED.

### Corollary 2.2 (high determinant implies overwhelmingly small pair correlations)

If
\[
\det K\ge r^r e^{-\varepsilon r},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal E(P)\le4\varepsilon r^3.}
\tag{2.8}
\]
Consequently, for every `tau>0`, the number of ordered pairs `i ne j` satisfying
\[
|\langle P_{i,*},P_{j,*}\rangle|\ge\tau\sqrt r
\]
is at most
\[
\boxed{\frac{4\varepsilon}{\tau^2}r^2.}
\tag{2.9}
\]
Thus an asymptotically maximal determinant cross Gram forces an asymptotically near-Hadamard seed in an explicit averaged sense.

### Corollary 2.3 (the exact maximum-volume point recurses into Hadamard structure)

At the maximum determinant point `K=rI`, both
\[
P^2=rI,\qquad CC^T=rI.
\]
So the hardest exact-volume point does not permit an arbitrary seed: the principal half `P` is itself a symmetric Hadamard-type sign matrix, while `C` is an ordinary Hadamard matrix. This is an exact seed/fiber coupling that is lost if one simply replaces every positive Gram fiber by the global maximum `R_r^+`.

## 3. Positive Gram fibers as support decompositions

For a positive-definite sign Gram matrix `K`, every root
\[
C\in\{\pm1\}^{r\times r},\qquad CC^T=K,
\]
is invertible. Hence no two columns of `C` agree up to sign. Passing to sign classes `[x]={x,-x}`, a root determines an `r`-element support set `S(C)` and
\[
K=\sum_{[x]\in S(C)}xx^T.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The preceding R7-06 work proved the exact root count
\[
R_r(K)=2^r r!\,|\mathcal B(K)|,
\]
where `mathcal B(K)` is the family of such `r`-element support sets.

The first possible nontrivial equality between two supports uses four removed and four inserted sign classes. The next theorem completely classifies that local ambiguity.

## 4. Exact classification of four-column sign-Gram trades

### Theorem 4.1 (support-distance-four ambiguity is closed-quadruple switching)

Let `C,C'` be invertible `r x r` sign matrices with
\[
CC^T=C'C'^T.
\]
Assume their sign-class supports have exactly `r-4` classes in common. After cancelling those common classes, write the remaining columns as
\[
U=[u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4],\qquad V=[v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4],
\]
so
\[
UU^T=VV^T.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Then exactly one of the following occurs.

1. `V=UM` for a signed permutation matrix `M`, so the two four-column supports were not genuinely different.
2. Up to signed permutations of the four old and four new columns,
\[
\boxed{V=U\frac{H_4}{2},}
\tag{4.2}
\]
where `H_4` is a `4 x 4` Hadamard matrix, and
\[
\boxed{u_1\circ u_2\circ u_3\circ u_4=\pm\mathbf1.}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Thus every genuine four-column Gram-support trade is a closed-quadruple-type switch. In the Hadamard case this is exactly the classical closed-quadruple phenomenon.

### Proof

Because `C` is invertible, every four-column subset is linearly independent, so `rank U=4`. Equation (4.1) implies that there is an orthogonal `Q in O(4)` with
\[
V=UQ.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Choose four linearly independent rows of `U` and form a nonsingular sign matrix `A in {+-1}^{4x4}`. On the same rows of `V` we get a sign matrix `B=AQ`, with
\[
AA^T=BB^T.
\tag{4.5}
\]

We use the elementary classification of nonsingular `4 x 4` sign simplices. After sign/permutation normalizations, a nonsingular `4 x 4` sign matrix has determinant magnitude `8` or `16`. This follows by dephasing the first row and column, subtracting the first row from the remaining three, and reducing to a nonsingular `3 x 3` zero-one determinant, whose absolute value is `1` or `2`.

If `|det A|=8`, the row Gram can be normalized to the type having one row with inner product `2` with each of three mutually orthogonal rows. Normalize those three rows by signed column permutations to
\[
(1,1,1,1),\quad(1,1,-1,-1),\quad(1,-1,1,-1).
\]
The remaining sign row is uniquely forced by the three inner products `2`, namely `(1,1,1,-1)`. Hence every cube embedding of this ordered Gram configuration differs only by a signed column permutation. Therefore `Q` is monomial, case 1.

If `|det A|=16`, then `A` is Hadamard and (4.5) forces `B` to be Hadamard. Hence
\[
Q=A^{-1}B=\frac14A^TB.
\]
Every entry of `Q` lies in `{0,+-1/2,+-1}`. Since `Q` is orthogonal, either it has an entry of magnitude one, in which case orthogonality recursively forces `Q` to be a signed permutation, or it has no such entry. In the latter case each row has norm one using four coordinates each of size at most `1/2`, so every entry has magnitude exactly `1/2`; thus
\[
Q=R/2
\]
for a `4 x 4` Hadamard matrix `R`. After signed permutations we may take `R=H_4`.

It remains to use the fact that *every* row `x` of `U` is a sign vector and `xQ` is again a sign vector. For the standard Walsh `H_4`,
\[
xH_4/2\in\{\pm1\}^4
\iff x_1x_2x_3x_4=-1,
\]
up to the harmless sign normalizations already made. Indeed the eight even-parity sign vectors are precisely the signed rows of `H_4`, whose Walsh transforms have one coordinate of magnitude `2` and three zeros after division by two, while the eight odd-parity sign vectors transform to sign vectors. Therefore every row of `U` has the same four-coordinate product, which is exactly (4.3). QED.

### Corollary 4.2 (local Gram-fiber graph)

Make a graph whose vertices are positive-definite sign-Gram support representations of one fixed `K`, joining two supports when their symmetric difference has eight sign classes. Then every edge is a closed-quadruple-type switch. A root with no closed column quadruple is isolated in this **local** fiber graph.

This is useful, but it is not a global connectivity theorem.

## 5. A concrete counterexample to the local-switch-only intermediate reduction

A tempting strengthening would be:

> every positive sign-Gram fiber is generated from any one root by successive four-column closed-quadruple switches.

That statement is false.

Abiad and Peters, *Switching graphs and Hadamard matrices*, The Art of Discrete and Applied Mathematics 9(2), P2.06 (published 21 May 2026, DOI `10.26493/2590-9770.1840.c3u`, arXiv:2410.10638), report that among the 60 equivalence classes of Hadamard matrices of order 24, **two have no closed quadruple**.

Take the transpose `C` of a representative from one such class, so `C` has no closed column quadruple. Nevertheless
\[
CC^T=24I,
\]
and any Hadamard matrix `C'` from another inequivalent order-24 class also satisfies
\[
C'C'^T=24I.
\]
Since inequivalent Hadamards cannot differ merely by signed column permutation, the Gram fiber of `24I` contains a genuinely different root from `C`. By Theorem 4.1, `C` has no four-column support-trade neighbor at all.

Therefore:
\[
\boxed{\text{Gram-fiber ambiguity can be genuinely nonlocal; closed-quadruple moves do not generate every fiber from every root.}}
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is a source-backed finite-dimensional counterexample to that intermediate reduction, not a counterexample to Vu's conjecture.

The classical closed-quadruple switching operation itself goes back at least to Orrick, *Switching Operations for Hadamard Matrices*, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 22 (2008), 31-50, DOI `10.1137/050641727`. No novelty claim is made for the classical switching operation.

## 6. What the two theorems change in the entropy attack

The positive-fiber bottleneck now has a sharper two-axis structure.

### 6.1 Volume axis

For an actual invertible half cut,
\[
K=2rI-P^2.
\]
If `det K` is close to the Hadamard maximum `r^r`, Theorem 2.1 forces `P` itself to have very small total row-correlation energy. At the exact maximum `K=rI`, `P` recursively satisfies `P^2=rI`.

Thus one cannot simultaneously treat `P` as an arbitrary symmetric sign seed and `K` as a maximum-volume Gram point. This is a genuine seed/fiber coupling.

### 6.2 Trade axis

The first nontrivial local support degeneracy is completely understood and is classical closed-quadruple switching. But (5.1) proves that the Gram fiber can contain roots that are separated by genuinely higher-order/global trades even when a starting root has no four-column move.

Therefore an entropy proof based only on enumerating local switches is insufficient. A viable next theorem must control **global cut-metric/correlation-polytope decompositions**, not only the degree of the local switch graph.

### 6.3 Singular endpoint

The singular branch is not being dropped. The previous balanced-cut theorem gives `corank C=O(sqrt N)` for some cut and reduces its completion entropy to a positive-definite Gram fiber with only `2^{O(N^{3/2})}` extra cost. Hence the current positive-fiber structural theorem is exactly the residual bottleneck after the singular endpoint has already been compressed to subquadratic overhead.

## 7. Remaining gap and next attack

Nothing here bounds every positive Gram fiber by `2^{o(r^2)}`; that would already contain the notoriously difficult Hadamard count at `K=rI`. Nor does the determinant deficit alone yet control root multiplicity: the previous exact `r<=6` census already showed that a nonorthogonal Gram point can have larger support multiplicity than the orthogonal point at small size.

The new precise bridge target is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{prove an entropy bound for low-determinant/global-trade fibers, while high-determinant fibers force a near-Hadamard seed.}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

A concrete next approach is to encode (3.1) as an integer decomposition of a correlation/cut metric and prove one of the following:

- **volume-deficit theorem:** if `log(r^r/det K)>=eta r`, then `|mathcal B(K)|<=2^{o(r^2)}` uniformly for fixed `eta>0`;
- **global-trade theorem:** any fiber of size `2^{Omega(r^2)}` forces a large additive/cut-ideal structure in every root, strong enough to couple back to a structured principal seed;
- **counterexample search:** construct a family of positive Gram matrices with definite determinant deficit and `2^{Omega(r^2)}` support fibers. Such a family would refute the volume-deficit route and force a different bridge.

Theorem 4.1 says that merely iterating four-column switches cannot substitute for one of these global statements.
