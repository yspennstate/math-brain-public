## 1. Exact one-row obstruction to uniform conditional anti-concentration

Let `a>=1` be an integer and put

\[
k=a(a+1),\qquad n=k+1.
\]

The integer `k` is even. Let `B=J_k` be the all-ones `k x k` matrix, let `d=1`, and choose any balanced sign vector

\[
x\in\{\pm1\}^k,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^k x_i=0.
\]

Define

\[
M_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&x^T\\x&J_k\end{pmatrix}.
\]

### Theorem 1

Every such `M_x` has the same integral spectrum

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}(M_x)
=\{k,\ a+1,\ -a,\ 0^{(k-2)}\}.
}
\]

Consequently, after fixing the entire principal block `B=J_k`, the new diagonal entry `d=1`, and the complete target spectrum, the number of admissible sign rows is at least

\[
\boxed{\binom{k}{k/2}=2^{k-\frac12\log_2 k+O(1)}.}
\]

In particular, no proof of F0011 can rely on a uniform statement saying that every prescribed integral spectral extension leaves only `2^{o(k)}` possible next sign rows.

### Proof

Write `1` for the all-ones vector in `R^k`. Since `x` is balanced, `x\perp1`, and `||x||^2=k`.

On the `(k-2)`-dimensional space

\[
U=\{u:u\perp1,\ u\perp x\},
\]

we have `J_k u=0` and `x^T u=0`; therefore `(0,u)` is a zero eigenvector of `M_x`. Thus zero has multiplicity at least `k-2`.

The vector `(0,1)` is an eigenvector with eigenvalue `k`, because `x^T1=0` and `J_k1=k1`.

It remains to restrict `M_x` to the two-dimensional space spanned by the top coordinate and `(0,x/sqrt(k))`. In that orthonormal basis the matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt{k}\\ \sqrt{k}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its characteristic polynomial is

\[
t^2-t-k=t^2-t-a(a+1)=(t-(a+1))(t+a).
\]

This gives the asserted spectrum, with total multiplicity

\[
(k-2)+1+2=k+1=n.
\]

Finally, the number of balanced sign vectors is exactly `binom(k,k/2)`, and Stirling's formula gives the entropy estimate. QED.

### Relation to the row-extension residue equations

For `B=J_k`, the spectral projectors are

\[
Q_k=\frac1kJ_k,\qquad Q_0=I-\frac1kJ_k.
\]

Every balanced sign vector satisfies

\[
\|Q_kx\|^2=0,\qquad \|Q_0x\|^2=k.
\]

Thus the simultaneous exact energy constraints obtained in the previous R7-06 run collapse here to a single balancing equation. Their conditional entropy is essentially the full `k` bits of the new row.

This is an exact obstruction to a **per-step** anti-concentration theorem, not an obstruction to a global amortized proof.

## 2. A global block obstruction: every square-order Hadamard family embeds in one integral spectral fiber

Let `k=q^2` be a perfect square and let `H` be an arbitrary real Hadamard matrix of order `k`:

\[
H\in\{\pm1\}^{k\times k},\qquad HH^T=kI_k.
\]

No symmetry assumption on `H` is made.

By multiplying rows by signs, normalize the first column to be `1_k`. Write

\[
H=[\,1_k\mid X\,],\qquad X\in\{\pm1\}^{k\times(k-1)}.
\]

Hadamard orthogonality gives the exact identities

\[
X^TX=kI_{k-1},\qquad X^T1_k=0,\qquad XX^T=kI_k-J_k.
\]

Now form the symmetric sign matrix of order

\[
N=2k-1
\]

by

\[
\boxed{
M(H)=
\begin{pmatrix}
J_{k-1}&X^T\\
X&J_k
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

### Theorem 2

For every normalized Hadamard matrix `H` of square order `k=q^2`, the matrix `M(H)` has the same integral spectrum

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}M(H)
=
\{k^{(2)},\ (-1)^{(1)},\ q^{(k-2)},\ (-q)^{(k-2)}\}.
}
\]

The map `H -> M(H)` is injective on normalized Hadamard matrices.

### Proof

The image of `X` is exactly

\[
W=1_k^\perp,
\]

because `X^T1_k=0` and `X` has rank `k-1`. Define

\[
U=\frac1qX:\mathbb R^{k-1}\to W.
\]

Since `X^TX=kI`, `U` is an orthogonal isomorphism.

The bottom direction `1_k` is invariant under `M(H)`: the cross term vanishes because `X^T1_k=0`, while `J_k1_k=k1_k`. Hence it contributes one eigenvalue `k`.

Identify the bottom space `W` with a second copy of `R^{k-1}` by `U`. On

\[
\mathbb R^{k-1}\oplus W
\]

the matrix becomes

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
J_{k-1}&qI_{k-1}\\
qI_{k-1}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Split the top copy as

\[
\mathbb R^{k-1}=\operatorname{span}\{1_{k-1}\}\oplus1_{k-1}^\perp.
\]

On each of the `k-2` orthogonal directions, the induced two-dimensional block is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\q&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

with eigenvalues `+q` and `-q`. This produces each of those eigenvalues with multiplicity `k-2`.

On the remaining two-dimensional direction generated by `1_{k-1}` and its `U`-image, the block is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}k-1&q\\q&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its characteristic polynomial is

\[
t^2-(k-1)t-k=(t-k)(t+1),
\]

so it contributes `k` and `-1`. Together with the bottom all-ones eigenvector, `k` has multiplicity two. All eigenvalues are integers because `q=sqrt(k)` is an integer.

Finally, the cross block of `M(H)` is exactly `X`, and the normalized Hadamard is exactly `[1_k|X]`. Hence `M(H)` determines `H`, proving injectivity. QED.

## 3. Counting consequence: Vu 9.4 contains a subquadratic Hadamard-counting problem

Let `\mathcal H(k)` denote the number of labeled real `k x k` Hadamard matrices. Row-sign normalization of the first column has fibers of exactly size `2^k`, so the number of normalized first-column Hadamards is

\[
\frac{\mathcal H(k)}{2^k}.
\]

By Theorem 2, all of these inject into one fixed integral spectral fiber of symmetric sign matrices of order `N=2k-1`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\#\{M\in\{\pm1\}^{N\times N}:M=M^T,\ \operatorname{Spec}M=\lambda_k\}
\ge \frac{\mathcal H(k)}{2^k},
}
\]

where

\[
\lambda_k=\{k^{(2)},(-1)^{(1)},q^{(k-2)},(-q)^{(k-2)}\}.
\]

If Vu Conjecture 9.4 is true, then the total number of integral-spectrum symmetric sign matrices of order `N` is

\[
2^{N(N+1)/2}\,2^{-(1/2+o(1))N^2}=2^{o(N^2)}.
\]

Taking `N=2k-1`, Theorem 2 therefore implies the rigorous conditional consequence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H(k)=2^{o(k^2)}
\quad\text{along every perfect-square Hadamard order }k.
}
\]

This is stronger than the previous R7-06 observation based only on **symmetric** square-order Hadamards: the present embedding accepts **every** real Hadamard matrix of square order and places it inside a symmetric integral-spectrum sign matrix.

It is only a one-way reduction. It does not assert that Vu 9.4 is equivalent to Hadamard counting, nor that the non-Hadamard part of a spectral fiber is controlled.

## 4. Why this blocks the naive B21/B29 entropy plan

Theorem 1 shows that, even after exposing the full principal block and the exact target spectrum, a single extension step may retain

\[
k-O(\log k)
\]

bits of entropy. Thus a uniform row-by-row `o(k)` branching estimate is false.

Theorem 2 shows that the obstruction can persist in a highly organized global block: after fixing both all-ones diagonal blocks, the cross block may range over an arbitrary normalized square-order Hadamard family while the full symmetric matrix keeps one fixed integral spectrum.

Accordingly, a successful B29 amortization must do at least one of the following:

1. prove that Hadamard-like high-entropy extension stages are globally rare;
2. obtain a subquadratic count for the structured Hadamard-like exceptional family; or
3. exploit spectral constraints that couple different exposure stages more strongly than the separate projection-energy equations.

The existing Hadamard anti-concentration theorem of Ferber–Jain–Zhao gives only a fixed quadratic-exponent improvement,

\[
\mathcal H(k)\le 2^{(1-c_H)k^2/2},
\]

whereas they explicitly conjecture `\mathcal H(k)=2^{O(k log k)}` and note that even `2^{o(k^2)}` would already require new ideas. Hence the reduction above reaches a recognized unresolved counting scale rather than an already available estimate.
