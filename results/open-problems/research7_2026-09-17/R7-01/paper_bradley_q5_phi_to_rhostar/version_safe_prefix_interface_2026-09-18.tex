\section{A version-safe prefix-interface lemma for the Peyre tensorization step}

This note isolates and repairs the only audit-sensitive interface left in the
candidate proof of Bradley Question~5.  The point is not that arbitrary enlarged
conditioning preserves conditional independence---it does not.  The point is that
the prefix sigma-field required by Peyre has a special \emph{side-separated}
form around an intervening Markov separator.

\subsection{Side-separated conditioning preserves a product disintegration}

\paragraph{Lemma (side-separated conditioning).}
Let $A,B,R$ be random elements of standard Borel spaces and suppose
\[
 A\perp\!\!\!\perp B\mid R.
\]
Let $C=c(A,R)$ and $D=d(B,R)$ be arbitrary measurable functions.  Then
\[
 A\perp\!\!\!\perp B\mid (R,C,D).
\tag{1}
\]
More precisely, regular conditional laws may be chosen so that for
$\mathsf{Law}(R,C,D)$-almost every $(r,c,d)$,
\[
 \mathsf{Law}(A,B\mid R=r,C=c,D=d)
 =\mathsf{Law}(A\mid R=r,C=c)\otimes
  \mathsf{Law}(B\mid R=r,D=d).
\tag{2}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Disintegrate the conditional product law as
\[
 \mathsf{Law}(A,B\mid R=r)=\alpha_r\otimes\beta_r
\]
for $\mathsf{Law}(R)$-almost every $r$.  Under the product measure
$\alpha_r\otimes\beta_r$, the statistic $C=c(A,r)$ depends only on the first
coordinate and $D=d(B,r)$ only on the second.  Disintegrating the two factors
separately therefore gives conditional kernels $\alpha_{r,c}$ and
$\beta_{r,d}$ and the product kernel
$\alpha_{r,c}\otimes\beta_{r,d}$.  This kernel has the defining integral
property for the conditional law of $(A,B)$ given $(R,C,D)$: it is immediate
for rectangles in the $A,B$ variables and product test functions of $(R,C,D)$,
and the monotone-class theorem extends it to the full product sigma-field.
This proves (2), hence (1). \qed

The lemma is deliberately side-separated.  It makes no claim for a statistic
which mixes information from both sides, such as $c(A,B,R)$.

\subsection{Finite interlacings}

Let $(X_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z}$ be a Markov chain on a standard Borel state space.
Fix finite nonempty disjoint $S,T\subset\mathbb Z$ with
$\operatorname{dist}(S,T)\ge n$.  Order $S\cup T$ chronologically and merge
consecutive selected indices of the same color into maximal monochromatic
blocks.  Enumerate the $S$-blocks chronologically by $U_1,\dots,U_I$ and the
$T$-blocks by $V_1,\dots,V_J$, where each $U_i$ and $V_j$ denotes the vector of
chain coordinates in that block.  For each pair set
\[
 \mathcal H_{ij}
 =\sigma(U_{i'}:i'<i,\;V_{j'}:j'<j).
\tag{3}
\]
For $s<t$ let $K_{s,t}(x,\cdot)$ be a fixed regular transition kernel.  Let
$A_{s,t}$ be a Borel set of full $\mu_s=\mathsf{Law}(X_s)$ measure on which the
robust local Hilbertian coefficient of the $s\to t$ channel is bounded by
$\kappa(n)$ whenever $t-s\ge n$; this is the row-good set constructed in the
main proof and its SDPI sharpening.

\paragraph{Theorem (finite prefix-interface lemma).}
For every fixed finite interlacing $S,T$, the subjective correlations required
by Peyre's minimal-hypothesis theorem satisfy
\[
 \boxed{
 \{U_i:V_j\}_{\mathcal H_{ij}}
 \le \kappa(n)\,
 \mathbf 1\{U_i,V_j\text{ are adjacent in the chronological block path}\}.
 }
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover, the pairwise almost-sure statements in (4) can be realized on one
simultaneous probability-one event for this fixed finite $S,T$.

\paragraph{Proof: adjacent pair.}
Assume first that $U_i$ occurs immediately before $V_j$.  Put
$s=\max U_i$ and $t=\min V_j$ (identifying a block with its set of selected
indices in this display).  Then $t-s\ge n$.  Because the two blocks are
adjacent and same-color blocks are indexed chronologically, every coordinate
appearing in $\mathcal H_{ij}$ occurs strictly before the earlier block $U_i$.
Consequently the Markov property gives, under a regular conditional law given
$\mathcal H_{ij}=h$, for almost every $h$,
\[
 U_i\longrightarrow X_s\longrightarrow X_t\longrightarrow V_j,
\tag{5}
\]
and the middle transition remains the fixed kernel $K_{s,t}$; only the input
law of $X_s$ changes.

There is no hidden support assumption here.  Since
$\mu_s(A_{s,t}^c)=0$,
\[
 \mathbb P(X_s\notin A_{s,t}\mid\mathcal H_{ij})=0
 \quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the conditional input law of $X_s$ is supported on $A_{s,t}$ for almost
every prefix $h$.  By the definition of the robust local coefficient and HGR
data processing along (5),
\[
 \rho(U_i,V_j\mid\mathcal H_{ij}=h)
 \le \rho(X_s,X_t\mid\mathcal H_{ij}=h)
 \le \kappa(K_{s,t})\le\kappa(n)
\tag{7}
\]
for almost every $h$.  This is exactly Peyre's subjective bound.  The case
$V_j$ immediately before $U_i$ is identical after reversing the color labels;
the conditioning field is again strictly earlier than the earlier block.

\paragraph{Proof: nonadjacent pair.}
Suppose $U_i$ lies before $V_j$ and they are not adjacent.  The $T$-block
immediately following $U_i$ has index strictly smaller than $j$, so that entire
intervening block is contained in $\mathcal H_{ij}$.  Choose a time $r$ in
that intervening block.  Then $X_r$ is $\mathcal H_{ij}$-measurable,
$U_i$ lies entirely in the pre-$r$ path, and $V_j$ lies entirely in the
post-$r$ path.

For a Markov chain, the pre-$r$ and post-$r$ paths are conditionally independent
given $X_r$.  Split the remaining generators of $\mathcal H_{ij}$ according
to which side of $r$ they occupy.  Because every generator is a vector of chain
coordinates, its sigma-field can be split at $r$; hence there exist
\[
 \mathcal C^-\subseteq\sigma(X_k:k\le r),\qquad
 \mathcal C^+\subseteq\sigma(X_k:k\ge r)
\]
such that
\[
 \mathcal H_{ij}=\sigma(X_r)\vee\mathcal C^-\vee\mathcal C^+.
\tag{8}
\]
Apply the side-separated-conditioning lemma with the pre-$r$ path as $A$, the
post-$r$ path as $B$, $R=X_r$, and with random elements generating
$\mathcal C^-$ and $\mathcal C^+$.  It follows that the two path sides remain
conditionally independent given $\mathcal H_{ij}$.  Therefore
\[
 \{U_i:V_j\}_{\mathcal H_{ij}}=0.
\tag{9}
\]
If $V_j$ lies before $U_i$, use the intervening $S$-block and the same argument.

There are only finitely many pairs $(i,j)$.  Each adjacent or nonadjacent
argument has a full-measure set of admissible prefix histories.  Pull these
sets back to the sample space and take their finite intersection.  This gives
one probability-one event on which every pairwise assertion needed for this
fixed interlacing is valid.  No universal null-set assertion over all possible
interlacings is made or needed. \qed

\subsection{Passage to arbitrary interlacings}

\paragraph{Corollary.}
Any uniform finite-interlacing correlation bound deduced from (4), in
particular the Peyre arcsine path bound
\[
 \rho\bigl(\sigma(X_s:s\in S),\sigma(X_t:t\in T)\bigr)
 \le \Psi(\kappa(n)),
 \qquad
 \Psi(r)=\sin\!\left((2\arcsin r)\wedge\frac\pi2\right),
\tag{10}
\]
extends from finite $S,T$ to arbitrary countable $S,T\subset\mathbb Z$ with
$\operatorname{dist}(S,T)\ge n$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Choose increasing finite subsets $S_m\uparrow S$ and $T_m\uparrow T$.  For
centered $f\in L^2(\sigma(X_s:s\in S))$ and
$g\in L^2(\sigma(X_t:t\in T))$, set
\[
 f_m=\mathbb E[f\mid\sigma(X_s:s\in S_m)],\qquad
 g_m=\mathbb E[g\mid\sigma(X_t:t\in T_m)].
\]
The martingale convergence theorem gives $f_m\to f$ and $g_m\to g$ in $L^2$.
The finite bound applies uniformly to $(f_m,g_m)$; covariance and variance are
continuous under $L^2$ convergence, so the same bound passes to $(f,g)$.
Taking the supremum over $f,g$ proves (10). \qed

\subsection{Why the side-separated formulation matters}

Peyre explicitly warns that subjective correlation can behave wildly when the
reference sigma-field is changed: independent variables can become maximally
correlated after conditioning on a statistic involving both sides.  Therefore
the informal phrase ``adding conditioning does not destroy the factorization''
should never be used as a general principle.  What is valid here is the more
specific statement proved above: after conditioning on a genuine Markov
separator, the additional prefix information decomposes into one statistic of
the past side and one statistic of the future side.  Conditioning a product
disintegration on such a product statistic preserves its product structure.

independent audit and not a novelty certificate.  It leaves the frozen source
metric unchanged at $C_{\phi\to\rho^*}=1$ while making the tensorization
interface hypothesis-exact and null-set safe.
