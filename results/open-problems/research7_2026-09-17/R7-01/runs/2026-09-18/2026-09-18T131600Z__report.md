## 3. B5 — forcing exponent phase diagram and the exact crossover derivative

The idea is to replace the one source scaling by

`f_epsilon ~ c epsilon^alpha`, `c>0`, `alpha>0`,                     (3.1)

and compare the three clocks

`epsilon^{-1}` (network synchronization),
`f_epsilon^{-1}` (forcing),
`epsilon^{-2}` (martingale diffusion).                              (3.2)

The exact recursions are

`Y_{n+1}-Y_n=f_epsilon(pbar-Y_n)+epsilon eta_{n+1}`,                 (3.3)

and, on `H={w:pi^Tw=0}`,

`W_{n+1}=T_epsilon W_n+f_epsilon Pp+epsilon Pxi_{n+1}`,
`T_epsilon=I-epsilon B-f_epsilon I`,
`B=(I-Q)|_H`.                                                        (3.4)

Because `B` is Hurwitz, `||T_epsilon^m||<=C exp(-a epsilon m)` whenever `f_epsilon=o(epsilon)`.

### Theorem 3.1 — two sharp dynamical crossover exponents

I proved the following complete power-law phase diagram.

1. **`0<alpha<1`.** On `t=f_epsilon n`,

   `Z_floor(t/f_epsilon) -> p+e^{-t}(z_0-p)`                         (3.5)

   uniformly on compact time intervals in probability.

2. **`alpha=1`.** If `f_epsilon/epsilon->c`, then on `s=epsilon n`,

   `Z_floor(s/epsilon) -> z_c(s)`,
   `z_c'=(Q-I)z_c+c(p-z_c)`.                                        (3.6)

   The equilibrium is

   `z_c^*=1pbar+c(B+cI)^(-1)Pp`.                                    (3.7)

3. **`1<alpha<2`.** On `t=f_epsilon n`, for every `delta>0`,

   `Z_floor(t/f_epsilon) -> 1[pbar+(pi^Tz_0-pbar)e^{-t}]`            (3.8)

   uniformly on `[delta,T]`; if the initial state is already synchronized, `delta=0` is allowed.

4. **`alpha=2`.** The source-critical forced Wright–Fisher limit (1.1) holds.

5. **`alpha>2`.** On `t=epsilon^2 n`, forcing disappears and

   `dY_t=sqrt(kappa Y_t(1-Y_t))dB_t`.                               (3.9)

Hence `alpha=1` is the deterministic network/forcing transition and `alpha=2` is the deterministic-forcing/diffusion transition.

### Proof mechanism

For `alpha<1`, put

`S_epsilon=(1-epsilon-f_epsilon)I+epsilon Q`.

Since `Q` is row-stochastic,

`||S_epsilon^m||_infinity<=(1-f_epsilon)^m`.                         (3.10)

On `m<=T/f_epsilon`, Doob gives

`E max ||epsilon sum_{k<=m}xi_k||^2
 <= C_T epsilon^2/f_epsilon ->0`.                                   (3.11)

Abel summation transfers this to the filtered convolution because

`||S-I|| sum_j||S^j||=O((epsilon+f_epsilon)/f_epsilon)=O(1)`.        (3.12)

The deterministic Euler generator is

`-I+(epsilon/f_epsilon)(Q-I) -> -I`,                                (3.13)

which yields (3.5).

At `alpha=1`, the Euler matrix is

`I+epsilon[(Q-I)-cI]+o(epsilon)`                                    (3.14)

and the accumulated noise over `O(epsilon^-1)` steps has variance `O(epsilon)`, proving (3.6). Projecting its equilibrium onto the Perron and transverse subspaces yields (3.7).

For `1<alpha<2`, the scalar recursion (3.3) has vanishing martingale contribution because `epsilon^2/f_epsilon->0`; meanwhile the transverse initial state decays as

`exp[-a epsilon t/f_epsilon]`,                                      (3.15)

the forcing-induced transverse displacement is `O(f_epsilon/epsilon)->0`, and the filtered transverse martingale vanishes by the same Abel argument. This gives (3.8). Cases `alpha=2` and `alpha>2` use the cumulative martingale-problem proof, with limiting forcing coefficient respectively `c` and `0`.

## 4. B18 — translate the source-critical limit into Jacobi boundary theory

For `c>0` and `0<pbar<1`, define

`a=2c pbar/kappa`, `b=2c(1-pbar)/kappa`.                            (4.1)

After the deterministic time change `tau=kappa t`, (1.1) has the standard Wright–Fisher/Jacobi generator

`(1/2)y(1-y)phi''
 +(1/2)[a(1-y)-by]phi'`.                                             (4.2)

Therefore its invariant density is exactly

`const * y^(a-1)(1-y)^(b-1)`,                                      (4.3)

that is,

`Y_infinity ~ Beta(a,b)`.                                            (4.4)

The stationary variance is

`Var(Y_infinity)=kappa pbar(1-pbar)/(2c+kappa)`.                     (4.5)

The Feller boundary thresholds are correspondingly exact:

`0 attainable <=> 2c pbar<kappa`,                                   (4.6)

`1 attainable <=> 2c(1-pbar)<kappa`.                               (4.7)

At equality or above the corresponding endpoint is inaccessible from the interior. In the positive-mutation accessible regime these are the standard regular/instantaneously reflecting Jacobi boundaries. This turns the source's qualitative “stay away versus touch the extreme values” question into two explicit inequalities.

For homogeneous forcing `p=q1`, `pbar=q` for every network and only

`kappa=||pi||_2^2`, `1/N<=kappa<1`,                                 (4.8)

retains network dependence in the slow boundary law. In particular, doubly stochastic networks have `kappa=1/N` and minimize the limiting slow variance.
