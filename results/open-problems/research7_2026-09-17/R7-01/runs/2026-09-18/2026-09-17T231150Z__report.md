# 3. B22 first: the exact failure mode in the old prose

The previous proof package said, in the nonadjacent-block case, that after conditioning on an intervening Markov separator, “adding to the conditioning sigma-field further variables from the past and from the future side does not destroy that factorization.”  Read literally as a general statement about enlarged conditioning, that sentence is too broad.

Peyre's own Examples 3.1.7--3.1.9 show why: if `A` and `B` are independent, conditioning on a statistic which mixes information from both sides can make them maximally correlated.  Thus the implication

\[
A\perp B\mid R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A\perp B\mid(R,Z)
\]

is false for an arbitrary `Z`.

The D0033 argument survives because its actual prefix field has much more structure.  Once an intervening selected state `X_r` is included, every other prefix coordinate lies either on the pre-`r` path or on the post-`r` path.  It is therefore a join of **two side-separated sigma-fields**, not an arbitrary mixed statistic.  The right lemma is the following.

# 4. Core repair theorem: side-separated conditioning

## Lemma 4.1

Let `A,B,R` be random elements of standard Borel spaces with

\[
A\perp\!\!\!\perp B\mid R.
\]

Let `C=c(A,R)` and `D=d(B,R)` be arbitrary measurable sidewise statistics.  Then

\[
\boxed{A\perp\!\!\!\perp B\mid(R,C,D).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

More precisely, regular conditional laws can be chosen so that for almost every `(r,c,d)`,

\[
\mathsf{Law}(A,B\mid r,c,d)
=
\mathsf{Law}(A\mid r,c)\otimes
\mathsf{Law}(B\mid r,d).
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Disintegrate conditional on `R=r`:

\[
\mathsf{Law}(A,B\mid R=r)=\alpha_r\otimes\beta_r
\]

for almost every `r`.  Under the product measure `alpha_r tensor beta_r`, the statistic `C=c(A,r)` depends only on the first factor and `D=d(B,r)` only on the second.  Disintegrate the two factors separately by these statistics, producing kernels `alpha_{r,c}` and `beta_{r,d}`.  Their product `alpha_{r,c} tensor beta_{r,d}` satisfies the defining conditional-integral identity for rectangles in `(A,B)` and product test functions in `(R,C,D)`.  A monotone-class argument extends the identity to the full product sigma-field.  Hence (4.2), and therefore (4.1).  QED.

This is precisely the safe boundary exposed by B22: **side-separated conditioning preserves a product disintegration; arbitrary mixed conditioning need not.**

# 5. B27: the simultaneous finite-interlacing prefix lemma

Fix finite nonempty disjoint `S,T` with `dist(S,T)>=n`.  Merge chronological selected times into maximal monochromatic blocks and enumerate the two colors as

\[
U_1,\dots,U_I,\qquad V_1,\dots,V_J.
\]

For each pair let

\[
\mathcal H_{ij}
=
\sigma(U_{i'}:i'<i,\;V_{j'}:j'<j).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `K_{s,t}` be fixed regular Markov transition kernels, and for each relevant pair `(s,t)` let `A_{s,t}` be the full-`Law(X_s)` row-good set used by the already-proved robust local HGR bound.

## Theorem 5.1 — version-safe finite prefix interface

For every fixed finite interlacing,

\[
\boxed{
\{U_i:V_j\}_{\mathcal H_{ij}}
\le
\kappa(n)\,
\mathbf 1\{U_i,V_j\text{ are adjacent in the chronological block path}\}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The pairwise almost-sure statements can be realized on one simultaneous probability-one event for this fixed `S,T`.

### Adjacent blocks

Suppose `U_i` immediately precedes `V_j`, and let `s=max U_i`, `t=min V_j`.  Every coordinate in `H_ij` is strictly earlier than the earlier block.  Conditional on an admissible prefix value `h`, the Markov property gives

\[
U_i\longrightarrow X_s\longrightarrow X_t\longrightarrow V_j,
\tag{5.3}
\]

and conditioning changes only the input law of `X_s`, not the transition `K_{s,t}`.  The row-good support is version-safe because

\[
\mathbb P(X_s\notin A_{s,t}\mid\mathcal H_{ij})=0\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus, for prefix-almost every `h`, the conditional input law is supported on the same good set, and HGR data processing gives

\[
\rho(U_i,V_j\mid\mathcal H_{ij}=h)
\le
\rho(X_s,X_t\mid\mathcal H_{ij}=h)
\le\kappa(K_{s,t})\le\kappa(n).
\tag{5.5}
\]

The case with `V_j` immediately before `U_i` is identical.

### Nonadjacent blocks

Suppose `U_i` is earlier than `V_j` and they are nonadjacent.  The `T`-block immediately after `U_i` has index `<j`, hence is included in `H_ij`.  Choose `r` in that intervening block.  Then `X_r` is `H_ij`-measurable, `U_i` is entirely on the pre-`r` side, and `V_j` is entirely on the post-`r` side.

The Markov property gives conditional independence of the pre- and post-`r` paths given `X_r`.  Split every remaining generator of `H_ij` at `r`.  Since the generators are finite coordinate vectors, there exist side sigma-fields

\[
\mathcal C^-\subseteq\sigma(X_k:k\le r),
\qquad
\mathcal C^+\subseteq\sigma(X_k:k\ge r)
\]

with

\[
\mathcal H_{ij}=\sigma(X_r)\vee\mathcal C^-\vee\mathcal C^+.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Lemma 4.1 now applies to the two path sides.  Therefore they remain independent given `H_ij`, and in particular

\[
\boxed{\{U_i:V_j\}_{\mathcal H_{ij}}=0.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The reverse chronological orientation is symmetric.

### One finite full-measure event, not an unjustified universal one

There are only `I*J` pairs.  Pull each pair's prefix-almost-sure good set back to the sample space and intersect these finitely many probability-one events.  This gives a single probability-one event on which all pairwise statements required by Peyre hold for the fixed finite `S,T`.  **No simultaneous null-set claim over infinitely many interlacings is made.**

### Infinite interlacings

For arbitrary countable `S,T`, take increasing finite subsets `S_m,T_m`.  If `f` and `g` are square-integrable variables measurable with respect to the full two color sigma-fields, the finite-coordinate conditional expectations `f_m,g_m` converge in `L^2`.  A uniform finite-interlacing correlation bound therefore passes to `f,g` by continuity of covariance and variance.  Hence the finite prefix lemma is enough for the full definition of `rho-star(n)`.

This theorem is written in full in the new additive package artifact
`paper_bradley_q5_phi_to_rhostar/version_safe_prefix_interface_2026-09-18.tex`.
