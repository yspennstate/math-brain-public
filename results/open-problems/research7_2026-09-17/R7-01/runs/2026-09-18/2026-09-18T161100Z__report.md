## 4. B4 — uniform matched initial-layer theorem

### New theorem

For every fixed `T<infinity`, uniformly over initial states in `[0,1]^N`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\left\|
Z_{\lfloor t/\varepsilon^2\rfloor}^\varepsilon
-
\left[
\mathbf1Y^\varepsilon(t)
+e^{-(t/\varepsilon)B}W_0^\varepsilon
\right]
\right\|
=O_{\mathbb P}\!\left(
\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is uniform **through time zero**.

### Proof — semigroup term

Iteration of (3.1) gives

\[
W_n^\varepsilon
=T_\varepsilon^nW_0^\varepsilon
+f_\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T_\varepsilon^jPp
+\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^nT_\varepsilon^{n-k}P\xi_k^\varepsilon.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `E_epsilon=exp(-epsilon B)`. Since `f_epsilon=O(epsilon^2)`, finite-dimensional Taylor expansion gives

\[
\|T_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon\|\le C\varepsilon^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Both semigroups satisfy exponentially stable bounds on the transverse hyperplane. The telescoping identity

\[
T_\varepsilon^n-E_\varepsilon^n
=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
T_\varepsilon^{n-1-j}
(T_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon)
E_\varepsilon^j
\]

therefore yields

\[
\|T_\varepsilon^n-E_\varepsilon^n\|
\le Cn\varepsilon^2e^{-a\varepsilon(n-1)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Writing `x=epsilon n`, the right side is bounded by a constant times

\[
\varepsilon x e^{-ax},
\]

so its supremum over all `n` is `O(epsilon)`. If `n=floor(t/epsilon^2)`, then

\[
0\le t/\varepsilon-n\varepsilon<\varepsilon,
\]

and stable-semigroup Lipschitz continuity over that final interval gives

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t\ge0}
\|T_\varepsilon^{\lfloor t/\varepsilon^2\rfloor}
-e^{-(t/\varepsilon)B}\|
\le C\varepsilon.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The compact state space uniformly bounds `W_0^epsilon`, so the initial-state approximation error is `O(epsilon)`.

### Proof — forcing term

By (3.2), uniformly in `n`,

\[
\left\|
f_\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T_\varepsilon^jPp
\right\|
\le C f_\varepsilon\sum_{j\ge0}e^{-a\varepsilon j}
\le C\frac{f_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}
=O(\varepsilon).
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof — stochastic convolution

For fixed `n`, put

\[
N_n^\varepsilon
=\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^n
T_\varepsilon^{n-k}P\xi_k^\varepsilon.
\]

For each fixed coordinate/dual direction the summands are martingale differences with deterministic coefficient bounds

\[
C\varepsilon e^{-a\varepsilon(n-k)}.
\]

The squared increment bounds sum to

\[
C\varepsilon^2\sum_{j\ge0}e^{-2a\varepsilon j}
\le C\varepsilon.
\]

Azuma–Hoeffding and a fixed-dimensional coordinate union bound therefore give

\[
\mathbb P(\|N_n^\varepsilon\|>u)
\le C_1\exp\left(-\frac{u^2}{C_2\varepsilon}\right).
\tag{4.7}
\]

There are `O(epsilon^{-2})` relevant indices up to slow time `T`, so another union bound gives

\[
\mathbb P\left(
\max_{n\le T/\varepsilon^2}\|N_n^\varepsilon\|>u
\right)
\le C_T\varepsilon^{-2}
\exp\left(-\frac{u^2}{C_2\varepsilon}\right).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Taking `u=L sqrt(epsilon log(1/epsilon))` with `L` sufficiently large proves

\[
\max_{n\le T/\varepsilon^2}\|N_n^\varepsilon\|
=O_{\mathbb P}\left(
\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Equations (4.5), (4.6), and (4.9) inserted into (4.2) prove (4.1).

### Consequence: synchronized starts are exactly the ordinary-vector case

If `Z_0^epsilon -> z_0` and `z_0=1(pi^Tz_0)`, (4.1) plus the scalar diffusion theorem gives the ordinary vector slow limit `1Y` on `[0,T]`.

If the limiting initial transverse component is nonzero, the slow vector family cannot have an ordinary `J_1` limit retaining the true time-zero state. One rigorous way to see the contradiction is to combine: (i) the actual time-zero value converges to the nonsynchronized `z_0`; (ii) for every `delta>0`, the transverse supremum on `[delta,T]` converges to zero in probability; and (iii) any putative `J_1` limit compatible with the vanishing mesh-size jumps and these positive-time restrictions would be consensus-valued arbitrarily close to zero and hence, by right continuity, consensus-valued at zero. ## 6. B30 — logarithmic overlap and gluing

Choose

\[
n_\varepsilon^*
=\left\lceil
K\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)
\right\rceil,
\qquad
\tau_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2n_\varepsilon^*
=K\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon^2).
\tag{6.1}
\]

On `m<=n_epsilon^*`, the exact slow recursion has deterministic accumulated forcing

\[
O(n_\varepsilon^*f_\varepsilon)
=O(\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon))
\]

and martingale second moment

\[
O(\varepsilon^2n_\varepsilon^*)
=O(\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)).
\]

Doob's inequality therefore gives

\[
\max_{m\le n_\varepsilon^*}
|Y_m^\varepsilon-Y_0^\varepsilon|
=O_{\mathbb P}\left(
\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Combining (6.2) with the matched theorem yields the fast-side approximation

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le\tau_\varepsilon}
\left\|
Z_{\lfloor t/\varepsilon^2\rfloor}^\varepsilon
-
\left[
\mathbf1Y_0^\varepsilon
+e^{-(t/\varepsilon)B}W_0^\varepsilon
\right]
\right\|
=O_{\mathbb P}\left(
\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For `t>=tau_epsilon`, exponential stability gives

\[
\|e^{-(t/\varepsilon)B}W_0^\varepsilon\|
\le C\varepsilon^{aK},
\]

so the slow-side approximation is

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\tau_\varepsilon\le t\le T}
\|Z_{\lfloor t/\varepsilon^2\rfloor}^\varepsilon-
\mathbf1Y^\varepsilon(t)\|
=O_{\mathbb P}\left(
\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right)
+O(\varepsilon^{aK}).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

At the matching time itself,

\[
Z_{n_\varepsilon^*}^\varepsilon-
\mathbf1Y_0^\varepsilon
=O_{\mathbb P}\left(
\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right)
+O(\varepsilon^{aK}).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Hence the two asymptotic descriptions genuinely overlap: synchronization is complete while the slow diffusion has not yet moved macroscopically. If `K>1/(2a)`, the deterministic layer term is smaller than `sqrt(epsilon)`.
