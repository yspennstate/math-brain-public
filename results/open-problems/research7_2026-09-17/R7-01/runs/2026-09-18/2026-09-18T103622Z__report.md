## 4. B3 — prove something stronger: one cut-parity theorem gives all 85 known cut facets

`w^r_e = |Delta^r_e|^2`.

`Q_X = U D_X U*`.

This is a rank-`|X|` projection on the six `H1⊕H2⊕H3`-adapted coordinates. Split those coordinates into three two-element blocks `B_r`, sample the projection DPP

`S_X ~ P^{Q_X}`,

and set

`N_r = |S_X cap B_r| in {0,1,2}`.

Because `Q_X` is a projection,

`N_1+N_2+N_3=|X|` almost surely.                              (4.1)

For each color define

`c_r(X)=sum_{|e cap X|=1} w^r_e`                                 (crossing mass)

and

`t_r(X)=sum_{e subset X}w^r_e + sum_{e subset X^c}w^r_e`          (noncrossing mass).

### Theorem 4.1 (exact cut-parity representation)

` t_r(X)=Pr(N_r is even),   c_r(X)=Pr(N_r is odd). `              (4.2)

Proof: Cauchy--Binet gives

`det (Q_X)_{rr} = sum_{e subset X} w^r_e = Pr(N_r=2)`,

while

`det(I_2-(Q_X)_{rr}) = sum_{e subset X^c} w^r_e = Pr(N_r=0)`.

`lambda_000 = 1-(p1+p2+p3)/2`,
