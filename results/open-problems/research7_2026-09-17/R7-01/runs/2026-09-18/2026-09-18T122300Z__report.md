## 3. B6 — deform to a solvable fast time: exact initial-layer theorem

The preceding proof established that nonsynchronized starts prevent full vector convergence at slow time zero, but did not identify the layer. The natural deformation is to change from slow time `t=e^2n` to fast time

`s=en`.

Let

`P=I-1pi^T`, `W_n=PZ_n`, `H={w:pi^Tw=0}`,

and put

`B=(I-Q)|_H`.                                                        (3.1)

Because `Q` is irreducible, `1` is a simple eigenvalue and every other eigenvalue has real part strictly less than one. Hence every eigenvalue of `B` has strictly positive real part, with no diagonalizability or aperiodicity assumption.

The exact projected recursions are

`Y_{n+1}-Y_n=f_e(pbar-Y_n)+e eta_{n+1}`,                              (3.2)

and

`W_{n+1}=T_eW_n+f_ePp+ePxi_{n+1}`,
`T_e=I-eB-f_eI`.                                                      (3.3)

### Theorem 3.1 (deterministic synchronization layer)

Assume `Z_0^e -> z_0`, and define

`y_0=pi^Tz_0`, `w_0=z_0-1y_0`, `Zhat^e(s)=Z^e_floor(s/e)`.

Then for every finite `S`,

`sup_{0<=s<=S}|pi^TZhat^e(s)-y_0| ->0`                               (3.4)

and

`sup_{0<=s<=S}||PZhat^e(s)-exp(-sB)w_0|| ->0`                        (3.5)

in probability. Therefore

`Zhat^e(s) -> 1y_0+exp(-sB)w_0`                                     (3.6)

uniformly on compact fast-time intervals in probability.

### Proof

For `m<=S/e`, summing (3.2) gives a deterministic drift bounded by

`m f_e <= S f_e/e=O(e)`.                                             (3.7)

The martingale part has second moment at most `Ce^2m<=C_Se`, so Doob's maximal inequality yields

`E max_{m<=S/e}|e sum_{k<m}eta_{k+1}|^2 <= C_Se`.                    (3.8)

This proves (3.4).

Iterating (3.3),

`W_m=T_e^mW_0+f_e sum_{k<m}T_e^kPp
     +e sum_{k=1}^mT_e^{m-k}Pxi_k`.                                  (3.9)

Stability of `B` gives constants `C,a>0` with

`||T_e^k||_H<=Ce^{-ae k}`.                                           (3.10)

Hence the forcing term is `O(f_e/e)=O(e)`. Also, uniformly on compact `s` intervals,

`T_e^floor(s/e) -> exp(-sB)`,                                       (3.11)

because `T_e=I-eB+o(e)`.

For the stochastic convolution let

`M_m=e sum_{k=1}^mPxi_k`.

Doob gives `E max_{m<=S/e}||M_m||^2=O(e)`. Abel summation gives

`sum_{k=1}^mT_e^{m-k}(M_k-M_{k-1})
 =M_m+(T_e-I)sum_{k=1}^{m-1}T_e^{m-1-k}M_k`.                         (3.12)

Since `||T_e-I||=O(e)` and `sum_j||T_e^j||=O(e^-1)`, the right side is bounded by `C max_{k<=m}||M_k||`, hence tends uniformly to zero in probability. Equations (3.9)–(3.12) prove (3.5). QED.

This completely identifies the initial layer. Its endpoint is the Perron average of the initial condition; the matrix `Q` only controls the transient semigroup `exp(-sB)`.

## 5. B21 — import fast-slow stochastic averaging: a transverse OU theorem

The deterministic layer suggests a second scale. Once the system is within `sqrt(e)` of consensus, the transverse fluctuations do not disappear on fast time; they converge to an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.

### Theorem 5.1 (fast transverse OU fluctuation)

Fix `y in (0,1)` and assume

`Z_0^e=y1+sqrt(e)v_0^e`, `v_0^e in H`, `v_0^e->v_0`.                  (5.1)

Define

`V^e(s)=e^-1/2 W^e_floor(s/e)`.                                     (5.2)

Then for each finite `S`,

`(Y^e_floor(s/e),V^e(s)) => (y,V_s)`                                (5.3)

in `D([0,S],R x H)`, where

`dV_s=-BV_s ds+sqrt(y(1-y))P dB_s^(N)`, `V_0=v_0`.                  (5.4)

### Proof

Equation (3.8) gives

`sup_{s<=S}|Y^e_floor(s/e)-y| ->0`                                  (5.5)

in probability. Dividing (3.3) by `sqrt(e)` gives one-step increments

`Delta V_k=-eBV_k-f_eV_k+f_ee^-1/2Pp+sqrt(e)Pxi_{k+1}`.              (5.6)

Over `O(e^-1)` fast steps, the two `f_e` contributions are respectively `O(f_e/e)=O(e)` and `O(f_e/e^(3/2))=O(sqrt(e))`, so both vanish.

The driving martingale

`M^e(s)=sum_{k<floor(s/e)}sqrt(e)Pxi_{k+1}`                          (5.7)

has jumps `O(sqrt(e))`. Conditional independence gives

`<M^e>_s=sum_{k<floor(s/e)} e P D(QZ_k^e) P^T`,                      (5.8)

where `D(q)=diag(q_j(1-q_j))`.

The stable linear recursion gives tightness of `V^e`. Hence `W^e=sqrt(e)V^e=o_p(1)` on compact fast intervals, while (5.5) gives `Y^e->y`. Since

`QZ_k^e=Y_k^e1+QW_k^e`,                                              (5.9)

(5.8) converges to

`s y(1-y)PP^T`.                                                      (5.10)

Lindeberg is automatic from bounded jumps, so the martingale FCLT yields

`M^e => sqrt(y(1-y))P B^(N)`.                                       (5.11)

The drift Riemann sums in (5.6) converge to `-int_0^sBV_u du`, and the linear limiting martingale problem is unique. This proves (5.3)–(5.4). QED.

### Stationary fast covariance

At a frozen slow coordinate `y`, the limiting OU process has covariance

`C_y=y(1-y) int_0^infinity e^{-sB}PP^T e^{-sB^T} ds`,                (5.12)

or equivalently

`BC_y+C_yB^T=y(1-y)PP^T`, `C_y pi=0`.                               (5.13)

Thus the network has a clean two-scale signature:

- slow diffusion: topology enters through `pi`, `pbar`, and `kappa=||pi||_2^2`;
- fast transverse fluctuation: topology enters through the full stable operator `B` and the Lyapunov covariance (5.12).

For doubly stochastic `Q`, `pi=N^-1 1` and `kappa=1/N`. If `Q` is also symmetric, then on `1^perp`,

`C_y=(y(1-y)/2)B^-1`.                                                (5.14)
