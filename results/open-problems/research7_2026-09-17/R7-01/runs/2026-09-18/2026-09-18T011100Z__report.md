## 2. B3 — direct standard-Borel theorem for the full alternating-block interface

### Idea before calculation

The remaining proof-risk was not the scalar constant. It was whether Peyre's prefix-conditioning theorem and the Markov property really give the required sparse path matrix for an arbitrary nonstationary Markov chain. The cleanest way to remove that ambiguity is to state and prove exactly the finite-interlacing theorem on a standard-Borel state space.

### Robust local coefficient

For `s<t`, let

\[
K_{s,t}(x,\cdot)=\Pr(X_t\in\cdot\mid X_s=x)
\]

be a regular transition kernel. The previously proved row lemma gives a full-`mu_s` Borel set `A_{s,t}` such that

\[
\|K_{s,t}(x,\cdot)-K_{s,t}(x',\cdot)\|_{TV}
\le 2\phi(t-s)
\quad(x,x'\in A_{s,t}).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Define `kappa(s,t)` as the supremum HGR maximal correlation of the channel `K_{s,t}` over **all** input laws supported on `A_{s,t}`, and

\[
\kappa(n)=\sup_{t-s\ge n}\kappa(s,t).
\]

Because arbitrary input-event conditionals and the unconditional output law are mixtures of the same row family, (2.1) gives a one-sided phi coefficient at most `2 phi(t-s)`. Bradley's inequality

\[
\rho(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
\le 2\sqrt{\phi(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\phi(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)}
\le2\sqrt{\phi(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)}
\]

therefore yields

\[
\boxed{\kappa(n)\le 2\sqrt{2\phi(n)}}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

This bound is robust under any past conditioning because that conditioning can change the input law but cannot move it off a full-marginal set.

### Alternating-block theorem

Let finite `S,T subset Z` satisfy `dist(S,T)>=n`. Order `S union T` chronologically and merge consecutive selected times of the same colour into maximal blocks

\[
B_1<B_2<\cdots<B_m.
\]

Let the S-blocks be `U_1,...,U_r` and T-blocks `V_1,...,V_q`. For Peyre's minimal-prefix theorem, the relevant conditioning field for `(U_i,V_j)` is

\[
\mathcal H_{ij}=\sigma(U_{i'}:i'<i,\ V_{j'}:j'<j).
\tag{2.3}
\]

There are two cases.

**Nonadjacent opposite-colour blocks.** Suppose `U_i<V_j` but they are not adjacent. An intervening T-block belongs to `H_ij`. Choose a state coordinate in the last such intervening block. Everything else in `H_ij` lies on or before that separator. The Markov property makes the path to the left independent of the future path conditional on the separator state; adding conditioning measurable on the left/separator side preserves that factorization. Therefore the subjective maximal correlation of `U_i,V_j` given `H_ij` is exactly zero. The reversed temporal order is symmetric.

**Adjacent blocks.** Suppose `U_i` is immediately before `V_j`. Let `s` be the last selected time of `U_i` and `t` the first selected time of `V_j`. Then `t-s>=n`. All variables in `H_ij` are strictly before `U_i`, so conditional on `H_ij` the transition from `X_s` to `X_t` is still the same kernel `K_{s,t}`. Moreover

\[
\Pr(X_s\in A_{s,t}\mid\mathcal H_{ij})=1
\]

a.s. Hence conditional data processing along

\[
U_i\to X_s\to X_t\to V_j
\]

gives subjective maximal correlation at most `kappa(n)`.

Thus Peyre's subjective-correlation matrix is supported **exactly on a path**, with all nonzero entries at most `kappa(n)`. The path singular norm is bounded by

\[
2\kappa(n)\cos\frac{\pi}{m+1}<2\kappa(n).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Peyre Theorem 3.3.1 and its Section 3.4(a) Minimal Hypotheses therefore give

\[
\boxed{
\rho(\sigma(X_s:s\in S),\sigma(X_t:t\in T))
\le2\kappa(n).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

No stationarity, reversibility, irreducibility, recurrence, or time-homogeneous kernel is used.

Combining (2.2) and (2.5), if `phi(n0)<1/32`, then

\[
\rho^*(n_0)\le4\sqrt{2\phi(n_0)}<1.
\]

The extension from finite to arbitrary countable interlacings follows by approximating `L^2` functions with conditional expectations onto increasing finite coordinate sets. Bradley Theorem 3.3(6) finishes the source proof.

**B3 outcome:** the key tensorization interface now has a complete standard-Borel proof in the exact nonstationary scope of Bradley's Section 3.

## 3. B15 — deliberately break stationarity, homogeneity, and reversibility

### Idea

If the candidate proof secretly used the symmetric finite-state toy calculations from earlier runs, it should fail on an explicitly time-inhomogeneous chain whose one-step kernels are themselves nonreversible. Build such a family and check it independently against Bradley's older rho-star criterion.

Take state space `Z/3Z`, positive `a,b,c` with `a+b+c=1` and `b!=c`, and cyclic shift matrix `C`. Define

\[
K^+=aI+bC+cC^2,
\qquad
K^-=aI+cC+bC^2,
\]

using `K+` at even times and `K-` at odd times. Both are doubly stochastic, so a two-sided Markov law with uniform one-time marginals exists. But a time shift swaps the kernels, so the path law is not strictly stationary when `K+ != K-`; each one-step kernel is nonreversible since a clockwise transition has probability `b` while the reverse has probability `c`.

Let `eta=min(a,b,c)>0`. Every row satisfies the Doeblin minorization

\[
K^\pm(i,\cdot)\ge3\eta\,u(\cdot),
\]

where `u` is uniform. Hence every step has Dobrushin contraction at most `1-3eta`, and row differences after `n` steps are at most `(1-3eta)^n`. Thus the chain is phi-mixing exponentially.

Independently, because both one-time marginals are uniform,

\[
\frac{\Pr(X_k=i,X_{k+1}=j)}{\Pr(X_k=i)\Pr(X_{k+1}=j)}
=3K_k(i,j)\ge3\eta.
\]

Any event-event likelihood ratio is a weighted average of the atom ratios, so `psi'(1)>=3eta>0`. Bradley Theorem 3.3(7) therefore independently says this nonstationary/nonreversible chain is rho-star mixing.

**B15 outcome:** the candidate theorem survives a source-valid family that removes the symmetry assumptions most likely to have been hidden in an earlier derivation.

## 4. B26 — exact `kappa=1/2` boundary witness

### Idea

The previous path-norm argument has a formal threshold `kappa<1/2`. Determine whether equality is a real Markov obstruction or only a limitation of the abstract tensorization step.

Consider the stationary binary symmetric Markov chain on `{-1,+1}`:

\[
\Pr(Y=y\mid X=x)=\frac{1+rxy}{2},\qquad0<r<1.
\]

Allow an arbitrary input law with `m=E X`. Since binary centered `L^2` spaces are one-dimensional, HGR maximal correlation equals ordinary correlation. We have

\[
E[Y\mid X]=rX,
\quad EY=rm,
\quad E[XY]=r,
\]

so

\[
\rho(X,Y)
=r\sqrt{\frac{1-m^2}{1-r^2m^2}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `z=m^2`, the squared multiplicative factor has derivative

\[
\frac{d}{dz}\frac{1-z}{1-r^2z}
=
\frac{r^2-1}{(1-r^2z)^2}<0.
\]

Therefore the robust one-step coefficient is exactly

\[
\boxed{\kappa_r(1)=r}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

At `r=1/2`, the local robust coefficient is **exactly** the abstract threshold. Nevertheless all transition probabilities are positive, so the chain is finite-state, irreducible and aperiodic. Bradley Theorem 3.1 states that every such stationary finite-state Markov chain is rho-star mixing.

Hence

\[
\boxed{\kappa=1/2\text{ is not a necessary Markov threshold.}}
\]

It is only the point where the completely abstract path-matrix estimate stops giving a strict uniform contraction. Markov compatibility can beat it.

**B26 outcome:** no further attempt to sharpen the `1/2` path threshold is needed for Question 5; the source proof only needs eventual `kappa(n)<1/2`, which phi mixing already guarantees.
