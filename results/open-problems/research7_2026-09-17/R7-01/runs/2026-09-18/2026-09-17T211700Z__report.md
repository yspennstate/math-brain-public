# 4. Idea before code

The previous proof has two quantitative steps:

1. convert one-sided phi control into robust local maximal-correlation control,
   \[
   \kappa(n)^2\le \delta(n)\le 2\phi(n);
   \]
2. tensorize the local HGR coefficient across an alternating interlacing path.

The preceding run showed that step 2 has a first-order factor-two obstruction in a symmetric binary Markov chain. What remained unclear was whether the **square root in step 1** is merely a proof artifact. The counterexample-first idea is to design a stationary Markov kernel whose one-step dependence is carried by a rare reveal event: phi should count the rare event linearly, while HGR should see the square root of its probability.

The useful additional constraint is to make the process exactly one-dependent. That lets the full interlaced coefficient be computed rather than bounded, eliminating any ambiguity caused by long paths.

# 5. B2 — attack the negation: refute any better-than-square-root universal phi-to-rho-star rate

Fix
\[
0<\varepsilon<\frac12.
\]
Let the six-state space be
\[
\mathsf E=\{-1,+1\}\times\{-1,0,+1\}.
\]
Write a state as `x=(u,v)`. Let
\[
\mu(+1)=\mu(-1)=\frac12,
\]
and
\[
\nu(+1)=\nu(-1)=\varepsilon,
\qquad
\nu(0)=1-2\varepsilon.
\]
Set
\[
\pi=\mu\otimes\nu.
\]
Under `pi`, the coordinates `U,V` are independent and centered and
\[
\mathbb E_\pi U^2=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\pi V^2=2\varepsilon.
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{
K((u,v),(u',v'))=\pi(u',v')\bigl(1+uv'\bigr).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Because `uv'` belongs to `{-1,0,+1}`, (5.1) is nonnegative. Its row sum is one because `E_pi V=0`. Also
\[
\sum_{u,v}\pi(u,v)K((u,v),(u',v'))
=\pi(u',v')\bigl(1+v'\mathbb E_\pi U\bigr)
=\pi(u',v'),
\]
so `pi` is stationary.

For `x=(u,v)` and `z=(\tilde u,\tilde v)`, a direct two-step calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
K^2(x,z)
&=\sum_{(a,b)}\pi(a,b)(1+ub)\,\pi(z)(1+a\tilde v)\\
&=\pi(z)\left(1+u\mathbb E b+\tilde v\mathbb E a
+u\tilde v\mathbb E[ab]\right)\\
&=\pi(z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{K^2=\Pi},
\tag{5.3}
\]
where every row of `Pi` is `pi`. Since every atom of `pi` is positive, every two-step transition is positive. The chain is therefore irreducible and aperiodic, and its stationary process is one-dependent.

For a fixed row `x=(u,v)`, the signed density of `K(x,.)-pi` with respect to `pi` is `uv'`. Hence
\[
\|K(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}
=\frac12\mathbb E_\pi|V|
=\varepsilon.
\tag{5.4}
\]
For the stationary Markov chain, conditioning any past event changes the future through the one-step row mixture; continuation cannot increase total variation. Therefore
\[
\phi(1)\le\varepsilon.
\]
Equality is attained by the past event `{U_0=+1}` and future event `{V_1=+1}` because
\[
\mathbb P(V_1=+1\mid U_0=+1)=2\varepsilon,
\qquad
\mathbb P(V_1=+1)=\varepsilon.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{\phi(1)=\varepsilon.}
\tag{5.5}
\]
By (5.3),
\[
\boxed{\phi(n)=0\quad(n\ge2).}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This family already has the desired rare-event shape. B18 below computes its full `rho-star` coefficient exactly and shows that
\[
\rho^*(1)=\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.
\]
Consequently no universal inequality
\[
\rho^*(1)=o\!\left(\sqrt{\phi(1)}\right)
\]
can hold over stationary Markov chains. In particular, no universal power bound `rho-star <= C phi^alpha` can hold uniformly near zero with `alpha>1/2`.

**B2 outcome:** success. A source-compatible stationary Markov family refutes every better-than-square-root universal pointwise rate, while remaining exactly phi-mixing and rho-star-mixing.

# 6. B24 — find the extremal object: exact saturation of the local HGR/Dobrushin inequality

The one-step rows depend only on the sign `u`. Their total-variation diameter is
\[
\boxed{\delta(K)=2\varepsilon.}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Indeed the `u=+1` and `u=-1` rows differ only by the sign of the `v'` tilt, and the resulting TV distance is `2 epsilon`.

Now compute the robust HGR coefficient of the channel under an arbitrary input law. Since the kernel depends on the input only through the binary coordinate `U`, extra information in the input `V` cannot improve the channel contraction beyond the `U` channel. Let
\[
m=\mathbb E U.
\]
Then the output coordinate `V'` has
\[
\mathbb P(V'=+1)=\varepsilon(1+m),
\]
\[
\mathbb P(V'=-1)=\varepsilon(1-m),
\]
\[
\mathbb P(V'=0)=1-2\varepsilon.
\]
The posterior mean of the binary input is
\[
\mathbb E[U\mid V'=+1]=1,
\quad
\mathbb E[U\mid V'=-1]=-1,
\quad
\mathbb E[U\mid V'=0]=m.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[U\mid V'])
=2\varepsilon(1-m^2),
\]
while
\[
\operatorname{Var}(U)=1-m^2.
\]
Whenever the input is nondegenerate,
\[
\rho(U,V')^2
=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[U\mid V'])}
{\operatorname{Var}(U)}
=2\varepsilon.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Thus the robust local coefficient is independent of the nondegenerate input prior and satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\kappa(1)^2=2\varepsilon=\delta(K)=2\phi(1).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is exact equality in both local inequalities used by the D0033 source proof. Hence the square-root loss in converting one-sided phi to robust local HGR is not merely a slack proof step: it is attained by a finite irreducible aperiodic stationary Markov chain.

**B24 outcome:** success. The extremal local mechanism is a rare exact reveal: phi pays the reveal probability, while HGR pays its square root.

# 7. B18 — translate the chain into an independent-edge model and compute rho-star exactly

For each integer `t`, independently sample an edge pair
\[
(A_t,B_{t+1})
\]
with joint law
\[
\boxed{
q(a,b)=\mu(a)\nu(b)(1+ab).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Its marginals are `mu` and `nu`. Define
\[
X_t=(A_t,B_t).
\tag{7.2}
\]
Because `A_t` and `B_t` belong to two different independent edge pairs, `X_t` has law `pi`. Conditional on `X_t=(u,v)`, the next `A_{t+1}` is an independent `mu` variable and
\[
\mathbb P(B_{t+1}=b\mid A_t=u)=\nu(b)(1+ub),
\]
so the transition law of (7.2) is exactly (5.1).

For one edge, `A` is a symmetric sign and
\[
\mathbb E[A\mid B]=B.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Indeed `B=+1` forces `A=+1`, `B=-1` forces `A=-1`, and `B=0` leaves `A` symmetric. Since every centered square-integrable function of the binary variable `A` is a scalar multiple of `A`, the one-edge HGR maximal correlation is
\[
\rho(A,B)^2
=\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[A\mid B])
=\mathbb E B^2
=2\varepsilon.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Thus
\[
\rho(A,B)=\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.
\]

Now take arbitrary disjoint index sets `S,T`. Each observed state `X_t` consists of `A_t` from edge `t` and `B_t` from edge `t-1`. The edge pairs are mutually independent over `t`. On any edge, the `S` and `T` sigma-fields either see variables on only one side, or, exactly when one endpoint `t` and `t+1` lies in `S` and the other lies in `T`, they see the correlated pair `(A_t,B_{t+1})` split across the two sides.

Maximal correlation tensorizes over independent pairs: the maximal correlation of the product sigma-fields is the supremum of the maximal correlations of the crossing factors. Therefore
\[
\rho\bigl(\sigma(X_s:s\in S),\sigma(X_t:t\in T)\bigr)
\le\sqrt{2\varepsilon},
\tag{7.5}
\]
with equality as soon as there is a crossing adjacent edge. Taking `S={0}`, `T={1}` gives
\[
\boxed{\rho^*(1)=\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.}
\tag{7.6}
\]
If `dist(S,T)>=2`, there is no crossing edge, so the two sigma-fields are functions of disjoint independent edge-pair families. Hence
\[
\boxed{\rho^*(n)=0\quad(n\ge2).}
\tag{7.7}
\]
Combining (5.5) and (7.6),
\[
\boxed{\rho^*(1)^2=2\phi(1).}
\tag{7.8}
\]

**B18 outcome:** success. The independent-edge translation converts a potentially difficult interlaced-sigma-field problem into exact product HGR tensorization and computes the full coefficient, not just a lower bound.

## 8. What this adds to the previous quantitative picture

The preceding run's symmetric two-state chain established a different sharpness mechanism. With local coefficient `r`, it has
\[
\phi=r/2,\qquad \kappa=r,
\]
and long alternating even/odd sums give
\[
\rho^*\ge\frac{2r}{1+r^2}=2r+O(r^3).
\]
Thus the factor two in path amplification is first-order sharp in a stationary Markov family.

The present six-state chain instead gives
\[
\kappa=\sqrt{2\phi},
\qquad
\rho^*=\kappa,
\]
so the square-root local conversion is exactly sharp, while there is no further long-path amplification because the edge factorization makes the dependence one-dependent.

Therefore the two principal inequalities in the current quantitative proof are **separately sharp in stationary finite-state Markov chains**, but the two examples do not simultaneously saturate them. The unresolved quantitative sharpening is now a compatibility problem:

> How large can long alternating-path amplification be in a stationary Markov chain that is simultaneously near-extremal for `kappa^2 <= 2 phi`?

This quantitative compatibility question is stronger than, and not needed for, Bradley's source question. It does not change the frozen source metric.
