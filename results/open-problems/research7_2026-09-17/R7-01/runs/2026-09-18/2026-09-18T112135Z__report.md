## 3. B2 — attack the negation: the source's singular vector term cannot survive

The source's heuristic contains the interaction drift on a scale at which it appears formally singular. I first attacked the possibility that a genuinely N-dimensional interacting diffusion survives.

Set `epsilon=r_r` and `f_e=r_f`. The recursion is

`Z_{n+1}^e-Z_n^e
 = epsilon(Q-I)Z_n^e + f_e(p-Z_n^e)+epsilon xi_{n+1}^e`,        (3.1)

where `xi_{n+1}^e=X_{n+1}^e-QZ_n^e` is a bounded martingale difference.

On the source's diffusive scale `n ~ t/epsilon^2`, the deterministic interaction has effective strength `epsilon/epsilon^2=1/epsilon`. Let `lambda !=1` be any eigenvalue of the irreducible row-stochastic matrix `Q`. The corresponding deterministic one-step multiplier is

`1-epsilon(1-lambda)-f_e`.

Since irreducibility makes eigenvalue 1 simple and every other eigenvalue satisfies `Re(lambda)<1`, over `t/epsilon^2` steps the magnitude behaves as

`exp(-(1-Re(lambda))t/epsilon+O(t)) -> 0`.

This remains true for periodic `Q`; for example a period-two mode `lambda=-1` decays like `exp(-2t/epsilon)`. Therefore the source's singular term is not a finite vector drift to be interpreted literally. It enforces an infinitely fast synchronization constraint. Any correct limit must live on the one-dimensional Perron consensus manifold.

This B2 obstruction search did not refute the source conjecture; it refuted the naive N-dimensional interpretation of its heuristic and exposed the right reduced state space.

## 4. B11 — dualize: exact Perron projection kills the singular drift

Let `pi` be the unique positive stationary probability row-vector of `Q`:

`pi^TQ=pi^T`, `sum_j pi_j=1`.

Define

`Y_n^e=pi^T Z_n^e`,  `pbar=pi^T p`,

and project (3.1) by `pi^T`. The interaction disappears exactly:

`Y_{n+1}^e-Y_n^e=f_e(pbar-Y_n^e)+epsilon eta_{n+1}^e`,                 (4.1)

`eta_{n+1}^e=pi^T xi_{n+1}^e`.

Conditional independence of the Bernoulli action coordinates gives, if `q_n=QZ_n^e`,

`E[(eta_{n+1}^e)^2|F_n]
 = sum_j pi_j^2 q_{n,j}(1-q_{n,j})`.                                 (4.2)

Thus the dual Perron vector is not merely convenient notation: it produces the unique slow coordinate and removes the singular interaction identically.

To justify replacing the right side of (4.2) by a function of `Y` alone, put

`P=I-1 pi^T`, `W_n^e=PZ_n^e=Z_n^e-1Y_n^e`.

Because `P` commutes with `Q`,

`W_{n+1}^e=T_e W_n^e+f_ePp+epsilon Pxi_{n+1}^e`,                     (4.3)

where on `H={w:pi^Tw=0}`,

`T_e=I-epsilon(I-Q)-f_eI`.

The matrix `B=(I-Q)|_H` has spectrum strictly in the open right half-plane. A Lyapunov quadratic form `M` on `H` therefore gives, for small epsilon,

`T_e^*MT_e <= (1-a epsilon)M`.                                      (4.4)

Consequently

`E||W_n^e||^2 <= C exp(-a epsilon n)||W_0^e||^2+C epsilon`,           (4.5)

and hence for every fixed `T`,

`epsilon^2 sum_{n<T/epsilon^2}E||W_n^e|| ->0`.                       (4.6)

The proof is elementary: in the `M`-norm the deterministic part contracts by order epsilon per step; the bounded martingale noise contributes order `epsilon^2` per step; the forcing cross term costs `O(f_e^2/epsilon)=O(epsilon^3)` because `f_e=O(epsilon^2)`.

A stronger uniform statement follows by iterating (4.3):

`W_n^e=T_e^nW_0^e+f_e sum_{j<n}T_e^jPp
       +epsilon sum_{k=1}^nT_e^{n-k}Pxi_k^e`.                         (4.7)

The first term is `O(exp(-a epsilon n))`; the forcing sum is `O(f_e/epsilon)=O(epsilon)`. For each fixed n, every coordinate of the martingale convolution is a martingale sum whose squared increment bounds total `O(epsilon)`, so Azuma–Hoeffding gives

`P(||noise convolution at n||>u) <= C exp(-c u^2/epsilon)`.

Union over only `O(epsilon^{-2})` diffusive-scale time indices still tends to zero. Therefore, for every `0<delta<T`,

`sup_{delta<=t<=T}||Z^e_{floor(t/epsilon^2)}-1Y^e(t)|| ->0`           (4.8)

in probability; if the initial vectors themselves approach the consensus manifold, the same is true from `t=0`.

## 5. Identification of the limiting quadratic variation

Set

`kappa=sum_j pi_j^2`.

Since `QZ_n^e=Y_n^e 1+QW_n^e` and `h(x)=x(1-x)` is 1-Lipschitz on `[0,1]`, (4.2) implies

`|E[(eta_{n+1}^e)^2|F_n]-kappa Y_n^e(1-Y_n^e)| <= C||W_n^e||`.       (5.1)

Combining with (4.6),

`epsilon^2 sum_{n<T/epsilon^2}
 |E[(eta_{n+1}^e)^2|F_n]-kappa Y_n^e(1-Y_n^e)| ->0`                  (5.2)

in `L^1`.

Thus the fast modes contribute no residual diffusion at order one: the quadratic variation is exactly the Perron-weighted Bernoulli variance after synchronization.

## 6. Complete diffusion-limit theorem

Assume

`f_e/epsilon^2 -> c in [0,infinity)`

and `Y_0^e=pi^T Z_0^e -> y_0`. Define

`Y^e(t)=Y^e_{floor(t/epsilon^2)}`.

Then

`Y^e => Y` in `D([0,T],[0,1])` for every finite `T`,                  (6.1)

where the limit is the Jacobi/Wright–Fisher diffusion

`dY_t=c(pbar-Y_t)dt+sqrt(kappa Y_t(1-Y_t))dB_t`, `Y_0=y_0`.          (6.2)

Here is the full identification argument. From (4.1),

`Y^e=Y_0^e+A^e+M^e`,

where the drift increment is `f_e(pbar-Y_n^e)` and the martingale increment is `epsilon eta_{n+1}^e`. On a macroscopic interval of length theta, drift variation and predictable quadratic variation are both `O(theta+epsilon^2)`, and jumps are `O(epsilon)`. Compact containment is automatic because `Y^e in [0,1]`. Aldous' criterion gives tightness and every subsequential limit is continuous.

For `phi in C^3([0,1])`, Taylor expansion gives

`E[phi(Y_{n+1}^e)-phi(Y_n^e)|F_n]
 = f_e(pbar-Y_n^e)phi'(Y_n^e)
 + (epsilon^2/2) v(Z_n^e)phi''(Y_n^e)
 + O(epsilon^3+f_e^2)`,                                              (6.3)

with `v` equal to the conditional variance in (4.2). Summing over `O(epsilon^{-2})` steps makes the remainder `o(1)`. The first term converges because `f_e/epsilon^2->c`; the second converges by (5.2). Every subsequential limit therefore solves the martingale problem

`Lphi(y)=c(pbar-y)phi'(y)+(kappa/2)y(1-y)phi''(y)`.                   (6.4)

The associated one-dimensional SDE (6.2) has unique weak law: the drift is Lipschitz and the square-root Jacobi coefficient has the standard Yamada–Watanabe modulus. Hence all subsequences have the same limit, proving (6.1).

Equation (4.8) then yields the vector conclusion. If `Z_0^e-1Y_0^e ->0`,

`Z^e_{floor(t/epsilon^2)} => 1Y_t`

in `D([0,T],[0,1]^N)`. For arbitrary nonsynchronized initial data, this holds on every `[delta,T]`, `delta>0`. The absence of full `[0,T]` convergence in that case is a genuine initial-layer issue: a continuous consensus-valued limit cannot simultaneously retain a nonsynchronized vector at time zero.

## 7. B24 — sharp extremal topology constant

The limit depends on the network only through

`pbar=pi^Tp`, `kappa=||pi||_2^2`.

Because `pi` is a probability vector,

`1/N <= kappa < 1`                                                    (7.1)

for irreducible `N>=2`. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the lower bound, and equality holds exactly for uniform `pi` (in particular, every irreducible doubly stochastic network). The supremum over irreducible networks is 1, approached by stationary distributions concentrating arbitrarily strongly at one state, but it is not attained because every `pi_j` is positive.

Thus the balanced network minimizes diffusion strength by a factor `1/N`, while highly imbalanced networks approach the single-agent Wright–Fisher noise. This sharp constant is a useful independent sanity check against the 2017 network CLT, whose variance likewise decreases with network size and is minimized in the doubly stochastic case.
