## 3. Method B18 — exact transportation/Farkas formulation of the `2+2` case

Let `E` be finite and `dim H1=dim H2=2`. For two-subsets `A,B` and four-subsets `T`, define

`a_A=P^{H1}(X1=A)`,

`b_B=P^{H2}(X2=B)`,

`c_T=P^H(X=T)`.

A coupling is exactly a nonnegative table `x_{A,B}` over disjoint two-subsets satisfying

`sum_{B disjoint A} x_{A,B}=a_A`,

`sum_{A disjoint B} x_{A,B}=b_B`,

`sum_{A disjoint B, A union B=T}x_{A,B}=c_T`.

The last constraint is the union marginal. Projection-DPP cardinalities make disjointness automatic in any source-valid solution.

Farkas' lemma gives an exact dual. Let `alpha_A,beta_B,gamma_T` be real. A dual certificate must satisfy

`alpha_A+beta_B+gamma_{A union B}>=0`

for every disjoint pair. Eliminating `gamma` optimally gives the necessary-and-sufficient family

`sum_A a_A alpha_A + sum_B b_B beta_B`

`>= sum_T c_T min_{A disjoint B, A union B=T}(alpha_A+beta_B)`.       (3.1)

Thus an exact rational orthogonal decomposition plus an exact strict reverse inequality in (3.1) would be a finite certified refutation. Conversely, proving (3.1) for every pair of potentials settles the finite `2+2` case.

Outcome B18: **the first unresolved source rank is reduced to an explicit finite multimarginal transport inequality.** This is not yet source closure.

## 4. Method B21 — source-known monotone coupling kills all additive dual witnesses

The key imported fact is only the already-published stochastic domination `P^{H2} <=st P^H` for `H2 subset H`, not the desired union theorem.

### Theorem

For any finite-rank orthogonal decomposition `H=H1\oplus H2`, no Farkas separator can be additive on either marginal. More precisely, if

`alpha_A=sum_{i in A}r_i`,

then (3.1), with the obvious rank-generalized partition sizes, holds for every `beta`. The symmetric statement holds if `beta` is additive.

### Proof

Choose by stochastic domination and Strassen a monotone coupling `(B,T)` with

`B~P^{H2}`, `T~P^H`, `B subset T`.

Put `D=T\B`. Deterministic DPP cardinalities give `|D|=dim H1`. For each coordinate `i`,

`P(i in D)=P(i in T)-P(i in B)`

`=(P_H)_{ii}-(P_{H2})_{ii}=(P_{H1})_{ii}`.

Hence, for additive `alpha`,

`E alpha_D = sum_i r_i(P_{H1})_{ii}=E_{A~P^{H1}}alpha_A`.

Also `E beta_B=E_{B~P^{H2}}beta_B`. Pointwise, since `(D,B)` is one admissible partition of `T`,

`min_{A disjoint B', A union B'=T}(alpha_A+beta_{B'}) <= alpha_D+beta_B`.

Taking expectations proves (3.1). QED.

For the `2+2` case, therefore, a counterexample must involve genuine pair interaction on **both** sides. Pair potentials of the form

`alpha_{ij}=r_i+r_j`

can be quotiented out. The first relevant invariants are four-point cycle curvatures such as

`alpha_{ij}+alpha_{kl}-alpha_{ik}-alpha_{jl}`.

Outcome B21: **all one-body dual obstruction routes are eliminated noncircularly**. The remaining search lives in the interaction/cycle quotient.

## 5. Method B25 — the minimal six-point uncolored obstruction vanishes exactly

Take `E={1,...,6}` in the `2+2` case and set

`H3=H^perp`.

Then `dim H3=2` and

`H1\oplus H2\oplus H3=C^6`.

By projection-DPP complement duality, the original coupling is equivalent to a joint law on a three-color partition

`E=X1 disjoint-union X2 disjoint-union X3`,

with `Xr~P^{Hr}` and each `|Xr|=2`.

For each color `r` and edge `{i,j}`, write

`w^r_ij=det(P_r[{i,j}])=P^{Hr}(Xr={i,j})`,

and define the uncolored weight

`m_ij=w^1_ij+w^2_ij+w^3_ij`.

### Theorem: `m` is always in the perfect-matching polytope of `K6`

First,

`sum_{j != i}m_ij=sum_r(P_r)_{ii}=1`,

because `P_1+P_2+P_3=I`.

For `K6`, the only nontrivial blossom constraints under these degree equations are

`m(E(S))<=1` for every `|S|=3`.

Fix such an `S` and let

`A_r=P_S P_r P_S`,

a positive-semidefinite `3x3` compression. Then

`A_1+A_2+A_3=I_3`, `rank A_r<=2`.

The sum of its principal two-minors is `e_2(A_r)`, so

`m(E(S))=sum_r e_2(A_r)`.                                      (5.1)

Because `det A_r=0`,

`det(I-A_r)=1-tr A_r+e_2(A_r)`.

Summing and using `sum_r tr A_r=3` gives

`sum_r e_2(A_r)=det(A_2+A_3)+det(A_1+A_3)+det(A_1+A_2)`.       (5.2)

Now consider

`p(t1,t2,t3)=det(t1A_1+t2A_2+t3A_3)`.

Every coefficient of this cubic is nonnegative: factor `A_r=C_rC_r^*`, concatenate the matrices `sqrt(t_r)C_r`, and apply Cauchy-Binet; every coefficient is then a sum of squared minors. Since each pure determinant `det A_r` vanishes, the three pair determinants in (5.2) are disjoint collections of the nonnegative two-color monomials of `p(1,1,1)`. The remaining three-color coefficient is also nonnegative. Therefore

`sum_r e_2(A_r)<=det(A_1+A_2+A_3)=1`.

This proves every blossom inequality and hence the theorem.

Thus at the first ambient size not already covered by the source, **ordinary matching geometry cannot be the obstruction**. Any failure must be a genuinely colored synchronization failure.

### A quantitative DPP support constraint

Put orthonormal bases of `H1,H2,H3` as three two-row blocks of a `6x6` unitary `U`. The block Laplace expansion has exactly 90 ordered pair partitions and gives

`1=|det U| <= sum_{A,B,C partition E} sqrt(w^1_A w^2_B w^3_C)`.

Hence some rainbow partition has

`w^1_A w^2_B w^3_C >= 1/8100`.                               (5.3)

So a genuine orthogonal `2+2+2` instance always has a positive rainbow support atom. This still does not construct the full coupling because subtracting an atom need not preserve the Pluecker/orthogonality structure.
