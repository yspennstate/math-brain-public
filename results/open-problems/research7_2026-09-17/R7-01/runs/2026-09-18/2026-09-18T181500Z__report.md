## 5. B1 — arbitrary-clock theorem

Assume `Z_0^epsilon -> z_0`, put `y_0=pi^Tz_0`, `w_0=Pz_0`.

### A. Finite interaction clock

If

\[
D_\varepsilon\to d\in[0,\infty),
\]

then `V_epsilon,F_epsilon ->0` and

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\|\widetilde Z^\varepsilon(t)-e^{dt(Q-I)}z_0\|
\xrightarrow{P}0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Proof: write

\[
S_\varepsilon=I+\varepsilon(Q-I)-f_\varepsilon I
\]

and iterate

\[
Z_n=S_\varepsilon^nZ_0
+f_\varepsilon\sum_{j<n}S_\varepsilon^jp
+\varepsilon\sum_{k\le n}S_\varepsilon^{n-k}\xi_k.
\]

Because `S_epsilon` is nonnegative with row sum `1-f_epsilon`, its infinity norm is at most one. Euler convergence gives

\[
S_\varepsilon^{\lfloor m_\varepsilon t\rfloor}
\to e^{dt(Q-I)}
\]

uniformly on compact time intervals. The forcing is at most `O(m_epsilon f_epsilon)=O(F_epsilon)=o(1)`. The martingale transform has total squared increment bound `O(m_epsilon epsilon^2)=O(V_epsilon)=O(epsilon)`; Azuma plus a union bound gives a uniform `o_P(1)` remainder.

### B. Intermediate clock

If

\[
D_\varepsilon\to\infty,
\qquad
V_\varepsilon\to0,
\]

then

\[
\sup_{t\le T}|\pi^T\widetilde Z^\varepsilon(t)-y_0|\to0
\quad\text{in probability},
\tag{5.2}
\]

while for every `delta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\delta\le t\le T}
\|\widetilde Z^\varepsilon(t)-\mathbf1y_0\|
\to0
\quad\text{in probability}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The Perron-coordinate forcing is `O(F_epsilon)=o(1)` and its martingale quadratic variation is `O(V_epsilon)=o(1)`. For the transverse term, the spectral estimate

\[
\|T_\varepsilon^n\|\le Ce^{-a\varepsilon n}
\]

makes the initial state vanish like `exp(-aD_epsilon delta)`, the deterministic forcing is `O(f_epsilon/epsilon)=O(epsilon)`, and the stochastic convolution is uniformly `o_P(1)`. If `w_0!=0`, the vector cannot have an ordinary càdlàg limit on `[0,T]` which preserves `z_0` at time zero and equals `1y_0` at every positive time.

### C. Finite nonzero diffusive clock

If

\[
V_\varepsilon\to v\in(0,\infty),
\]

then `D_epsilon=V_epsilon/epsilon -> infinity` and

\[
\pi^T\widetilde Z^\varepsilon(t)
=Y_{\rm slow}^\varepsilon(V_\varepsilon t)
\Rightarrow Y_{vt}.
\]

Therefore the exact tangent limit is

\[
\boxed{
 d\widetilde Y_t
 =cv(\bar p-\widetilde Y_t)dt

}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Transverse collapse holds uniformly on every positive-time compact interval. For the source clock `v=1`, this is exactly the Jacobi/Wright–Fisher process already proved cumulatively.
