## 5. Why rank one works and rank two is the first genuinely new phenomenon

This is the cleanest diagnosis of the missing mechanism.

Suppose `H'=H \oplus [u]` is a rank-one extension. Stochastic domination gives a monotone coupling `X subset X'`. The difference `D=X'\X` is a **singleton**. Its complete law is therefore determined by its one-point probabilities. Orthogonality gives

`P(i in D) = (P_[u])_{ii}`,

which already specifies the rank-one DPP `P^[u]`. Thus no higher-order compatibility condition remains.

For a rank-two increment, the difference `D` is a **two-set**. One-point probabilities only determine its vertex degrees

`P(i in D)=(P_{H2})_{ii}`.

But the required law is determined by the full family of pair probabilities

`P(D={i,j}) = |Delta^{H2}_{ij}|^2`.

Those are genuine second-order Pluecker data and are not forced by the one-point marginals. This is precisely why the rank-one monotone-coupling argument stops at total rank four, and why all additive/one-body dual witnesses have already been eliminated while nonlinear two-body colored facets remain.

In short: **rank one is solved because a singleton law equals its one-point marginals; rank two is hard because a pair law contains irreducible two-point Pluecker information.**

## 9. Nearest solved analogue and the precise extra structure it has

The nearest solved analogue is Lyons's rank-one/codimension-one increment theorem from the 2003 source.

There the monotone coupling produces a one-point difference. A one-point distribution is fully determined by its coordinate marginals, so the diagonal identity

`diag(P_{H\oplus[u]}-P_H)=diag(P_[u])`
