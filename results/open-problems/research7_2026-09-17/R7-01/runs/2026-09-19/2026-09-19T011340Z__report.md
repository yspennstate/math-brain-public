## 5. Complete B12 derivation: exact conservation identity

Assume exponential service with rate `mu`, and put

\[
\rho=\lambda/\mu,
\qquad
M=\sum_{i=1}^nN_i,
\qquad
B=\sum_{i=1}^nB_i,
\qquad B_i=\mathbf1_{\{N_i>0\}}.
\]

A parent arrival occurs at rate `n lambda/k` and increases `M` by exactly `k`. Departures occur at total rate `mu B` and decrease `M` by one. Stationarity of `M` gives

\[
E B=n\rho.
\]

For `M^2`, one parent arrival contributes

\[
(M+k)^2-M^2=2kM+k^2,
\]

while one departure contributes

\[
(M-1)^2-M^2=-2M+1.
\]

Thus

\[
0
=2n\lambda EM+n\lambda k
-2\mu E[MB]+\mu EB.
\]

Using `EB=n lambda/mu`,

\[
2\mu E[MB]
=2n\lambda EM+n\lambda(k+1),
\]

and subtracting `EM EB` yields

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(M,B)=\frac{n\rho(k+1)}2.
}
\]

Every marginal is the ordinary M/M/1 geometric law, so

\[
EN_i=\frac\rho{1-\rho},
\qquad
EB_i=\rho,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,B_i)=\rho.
\]

Exchangeability gives a common off-diagonal covariance `c_NB`. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(M,B)
=n\rho+n(n-1)c_{NB},
\]

which implies the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
c_{NB}
=\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,B_j)
=\frac{\rho(k-1)}{2(n-1)},
\qquad i\ne j.
}
\]

The stationary queue-length vector is associated by the standard monotone graphical construction: all coordinates are monotone in the same direction in each independent arrival primitive and, after reversing the coordinate order of each independent potential-service primitive, likewise in each service primitive. Association passes to the stationary weak limit. Since `B_i` and `(N_i-1)_+=N_i-B_i` are increasing functions of `N_i`,

\[
0\le\operatorname{Cov}(B_i,B_j)
\le\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,B_j).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
0\le c_{BB}:=\operatorname{Cov}(B_i,B_j)
\le\frac{\rho(k-1)}{2(n-1)}.
}
\]

Summing covariances gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(B)
\le n\rho(1-\rho)+\frac{n\rho(k-1)}2.
}
\]

The previous run proved, as `(k-1)/(n-1)->0`,

\[
c_{BB}
=\rho^2C_{\rho,\mu}\lambda\frac{k-1}{n-1}
+O((k/n)^2),
\]

with

\[
C_{\rho,\mu}
=\int_0^\infty\langle Z,P_tZ\rangle_\pi^2dt>0.
\]

The exact upper bound now gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{2\rho^2\mu}
\le C_{\rho,\mu}
\le\frac1{2\rho^2\mu}.
}
\]

This upper bound is substantially sharper near heavy traffic than the earlier generic spectral-gap upper bound.

## 6. Complete B18 derivation: hypergeometric-mixture TV theorem

Because the stationary law is permutation invariant, conditional on `B=m` the busy set is uniform over the `m`-subsets of `[n]`. Thus a fixed tagged `k`-vector is hypergeometric conditional on

\[
P_n=B/n.
\]

Let `P_busy` denote its law and let `P_tilde` be the law obtained by drawing `k` iid Bernoulli(`P_n`) variables conditional on `P_n`.

Couple sampling with and without replacement until the first repeated sampled index. The collision probability is at most

\[
\frac{k(k-1)}{2n},
\]

so

\[
d_{TV}(P_{busy},P_{tilde})
\le\frac{k(k-1)}{2n}.
\]

Let `Q_k=Ber(rho)^{otimes k}`. For Bernoulli affinity

\[
A(p,\rho)=\sqrt{p\rho}+\sqrt{(1-p)(1-\rho)},
\]

with `H^2=1-A`,

\[
H^2(Ber(p)^{\otimes k},Q_k)
=1-A(p,\rho)^k
\le k(1-A(p,\rho)).
\]

Also

\[
1-A(p,\rho)
\le\frac{(p-\rho)^2}{2\rho(1-\rho)}.
\]

Convexity of squared Hellinger distance in its first argument gives

\[
H^2(P_{tilde},Q_k)
\le\frac{k\operatorname{Var}(P_n)}{2\rho(1-\rho)}.
\]

Since `d_TV <= sqrt(2) H`, and the B12 variance bound gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(P_n)
\le\frac{\rho[(1-\rho)+(k-1)/2]}n,
\]

we obtain the explicit rigorous estimate

\[
\boxed{
 d_{TV}(P_{busy},Q_k)
\le
\frac{k(k-1)}{2n}
+
\sqrt{
\frac{k[(1-\rho)+(k-1)/2]}
{n(1-\rho)}
}.
}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
k^2/n\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_{TV}(P_{busy},Ber(\rho)^{\otimes k})\to0.
}
\]

This is a genuine growing-dimensional theorem beyond the previous full-workload cubic regime, but only for the busy/idle observable and exponential service. It is therefore PARTIAL relative to the source.

## 7. B7 failure and the exact unresolved square-root converse

The natural converse attempt starts from the previous fixed-pair expansion

\[
c_{BB}
=a_{\rho,\mu}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2),
\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac{k-1}{n-1},
\qquad a_{\rho,\mu}>0,
\]

which suggests selecting `m~n/k` coordinates. Then `(m-1)c_BB` is order one, so their normalized busy-count variance differs by order one from the product law.

For every **fixed** selected dimension `r`, the exact arrival generator can be written

\[
L_r=L_0+
\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq[r]\\|A|\ge2}}
\gamma_{|A|}\prod_{i\in A}(T_i-I),
\qquad
\gamma_b=\lambda\frac{(k-1)_{b-1}}{(n-1)_{b-1}}.
\]

The product resolvent gives fixed-`r` centered moment orders `O_r(epsilon)`, `O_r(epsilon^2)`, `O_r(epsilon^2)` for distinct pairs, triples, and quadruples. But those constants are not proved uniform when `r=m~n/k -> infinity`. Therefore the desired bound

\[
E\left(\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{i=1}^m(B_i-\rho)\right)^4=O(1)
\]

has **not** been established. Without such uniform integrability, variance inflation cannot be converted into a bounded-test-function or total-variation separation.

The initial artifact accidentally crossed this fixed-dimension/growing-dimension boundary. Exact-SHA readback exposed the issue before the run report was finalized, and the immutable erratum explicitly withdraws the converse. The valid `L^2` diagnostic remains

\[

\frac{c_{BB}}{\rho(1-\rho)}
\right]^2,
\]

so the pair-Walsh contribution is nonvanishing when `k^2/n` has a positive finite limit. This alone does **not** imply a TV lower bound.
