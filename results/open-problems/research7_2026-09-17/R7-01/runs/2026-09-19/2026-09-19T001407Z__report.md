## 5. B4 derivation: exact stationary product defect in the exponential case

Assume exponential service of rate `mu`, so `rho=lambda/mu in (0,1)`. Let `N=(N_1,...,N_k)` be the tagged queue-length vector. For a fixed nonempty tagged subset `A`, `|A|=a`, a parent job hits exactly `A` at rate

\[
r_A
=\frac{n\lambda}{k}
\frac{\binom{n-k}{k-a}}{\binom nk}.
\]

Let `L` be the true tagged generator and `L_0` the generator of `k` independent M/M/1 queues with arrival rate `lambda` and departure rate `mu`. The departure parts agree. Let

\[
\pi(m)=(1-\rho)\rho^m,
\qquad
q=\pi^{\otimes k},
\]

and define

\[
B_i=1_{\{N_i>0\}},
\qquad
Z_i=\frac{B_i}{\rho}-1,
\qquad
v=E_q Z_i^2=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}.
\]

For a simultaneous arrival to `A`, the adjoint jump score under `q` is

\[
\rho^{-|A|}\prod_{i\in A}B_i-1
=
\prod_{i\in A}(1+Z_i)-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
g_{n,k}:=\frac{L^*q}{q}
=
\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}r_A
\left(\prod_{i\in A}(1+Z_i)-1\right)
-
\lambda\sum_{i=1}^k Z_i.
\]

For every nonempty `B subseteq K` with `|B|=b`, the coefficient of `Z_B=prod_{i in B}Z_i` is the rate of parent jobs hitting every member of `B`:

\[
\gamma_b
=\frac{n\lambda}{k}\frac{(k)_b}{(n)_b}
=
\lambda\frac{(k-1)_{b-1}}{(n-1)_{b-1}}.
\]

The `b=1` coefficient is exactly `lambda`, so all first-order marginal terms cancel against the independent generator. Hence

\[
\boxed{
 g_{n,k}
 =
 \sum_{b=2}^k
 \gamma_b
 \sum_{|B|=b} Z_B.
}
\]

Distinct `Z_B` monomials are orthogonal under `q`, because one coordinate in the symmetric difference appears to the first power and has mean zero. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\|g_{n,k}\|_{L^2(q)}^2
=
\sum_{b=2}^k
\binom{k}{b}\gamma_b^2 v^b.
}
\]

The pair term is

\[
T_2
=
\binom{k}{2}
\lambda^2\left(\frac{k-1}{n-1}\right)^2v^2.
\]

Moreover

\[
\frac{T_{b+1}}{T_b}
=
\frac{k-b}{b+1}
\left(\frac{k-b}{n-b}\right)^2v.
\]

Thus if `k=o(n^(2/3))`, the ratio is uniformly `o(1)` over the relevant tail and `sum_{b>=3}T_b=o(T_2)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|g_{n,k}\|_{L^2(q)}^2
=
\frac{\lambda^2}{2}
\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}\right)^2
\frac{k^4}{n^2}(1+o(1)),
}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\|g_{n,k}\|_{L^2(q)}
=
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}
\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}
\frac{k^2}{n}(1+o(1)).
}
\]

This is an exact statement about the queue-length product-law stationarity defect. It does **not** by itself imply a workload-TV lower bound at `k asy sqrt(n)`, because the inverse-generator control must remain uniform even while the total interaction-event rate grows.

## 6. B21 derivation: exact two-queue linear response

For two tagged queues, the common-parent arrival rate is exactly

\[
\gamma
=
\lambda\frac{k-1}{n-1}.
\]

Each queue keeps marginal arrival rate `lambda`, so single-coordinate arrivals occur at rate `lambda-gamma`. The pair generator is

\[
L_\gamma=L_0+\gamma W,
\]

with

\[
(Wf)(x,y)
=f(x+1,y+1)-f(x+1,y)-f(x,y+1)+f(x,y).
\]

Under the product geometric law `q_2=pi otimes pi`, `L_0` is reversible. Each shift is bounded on `L^2(q_2)`, and the mean-zero inverse `(-L_0)^(-1)` is bounded by the positive M/M/1 spectral gap. The adjoint interaction score is

\[
\boxed{
W^*1
=
\rho^{-2}B_1B_2
-\rho^{-1}B_1
-\rho^{-1}B_2
+1
=Z_1Z_2.
}
\]

Hence the stationary density has the `L^2` expansion

\[
h_\gamma
=1+\gamma(-L_0)^{-1}(Z_1Z_2)+O(\gamma^2),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_{p_\gamma}[Z_1Z_2]
=
\gamma C_{\rho,\mu}+O(\gamma^2),
}
\]

where

\[
C_{\rho,\mu}
=
\langle Z_1Z_2,(-L_0)^{-1}Z_1Z_2\rangle_{q_2}>0.
\]

If `P_t` denotes the one-dimensional M/M/1 semigroup and

\[
c(t)=\langle Z,P_tZ\rangle_\pi,
\]

then product factorization gives

\[
\boxed{
C_{\rho,\mu}
=
\int_0^\infty c(t)^2\,dt.
}
\]

Now `c(0)=v=(1-rho)/rho`. The Dirichlet form of `Z` has only the edge `0<->1` contributing, so

\[
\langle Z,-L_1Z\rangle_\pi
=(1-\rho)\lambda\rho^{-2}
=\mu v.
\]

By the spectral theorem, `c(t)/v` is a Laplace transform of a probability measure with mean `mu`; Jensen gives

\[
c(t)\ge v e^{-\mu t}.
\]

The exact one-dimensional M/M/1 `L^2` gap is

\[
(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt\lambda)^2,
\]

so

\[
c(t)\le v e^{-(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt\lambda)^2t}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{v^2}{2\mu}
\le
C_{\rho,\mu}
\le
\frac{v^2}{2(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt\lambda)^2}.
}
\]

Because `B_i=1_{W_i>0}` is directly observable from the workload and every marginal has `P(B_i=1)=rho`,

\[
E[Z_1Z_2]
=
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(B_1,B_2)}{\rho^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(B_1,B_2)
=
\rho^2 C_{\rho,\mu}\lambda\frac{k}{n}(1+o(1))
}
\]

for every sequence `k=o(n)`. In particular, for sufficiently small `gamma`,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(B_1,B_2)
\ge
\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{4\mu}\gamma.
\]

This is the rigorous smooth counterpart of the atomic-service diagonal obstruction: exponential marks erase the singular diagonal, but the common-parent dependence survives at first order in the specific-pair common-arrival rate.

## 7. B28 derivation: busy-count variance and the square-root scale

Let

\[
S_k=\sum_{i=1}^k B_i.
\]

Exchangeability yields

\[
\operatorname{Var}(S_k)
=
k\rho(1-\rho)
+k(k-1)\operatorname{Cov}(B_1,B_2).
\]

Substituting the pair expansion gives, uniformly for `k=o(n)`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(S_k)
=
k\rho(1-\rho)
+
\rho^2 C_{\rho,\mu}\lambda
\frac{k(k-1)^2}{n-1}
+O\!\left(\frac{k^4}{n^2}\right).
}
\]

Thus, if `k->infinity` and `k=o(n)`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(S_k)
=
k\rho(1-\rho)
+
\rho^2 C_{\rho,\mu}\lambda
\frac{k^3}{n}(1+o(1)).
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(S_k)}{k\rho(1-\rho)}
=
1+A_\rho\frac{k^2}{n}
+o\!\left(\frac{k^2}{n}\right),
\qquad
A_\rho
=
\frac{\rho\lambda C_{\rho,\mu}}{1-\rho}>0.
}
\]

The explicit bounds on `C` imply

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1-\rho}{2}
\le
A_\rho
\le
\frac{1+\sqrt\rho}{2(1-\sqrt\rho)}.
}
\]

So `k^2/n` is a rigorous smooth-dependence fluctuation parameter. At `k^2/n -> tau in (0,infinity)`, the busy-count variance is inflated by a fixed nonzero factor `1+A_rho tau+o(1)` compared with the independent product.

This does **not** yet prove that the workload-vector TV distance stays bounded away from zero at `k asy sqrt(n)`. The bounded-support conversion from the variance difference alone only gives a lower bound of order `k/n`, which still vanishes at the square-root scale. A sharp TV statement needs concentration/CLT or a bounded likelihood-ratio statistic.

## 8. Exact failure/gap

The old atomic proof detects one common parent event by a positive-diagonal singularity. For exponential service that detector is absent. The new analysis shows that the first smooth score is pairwise and has natural aggregate size `k^2/n`, but a second-moment signal can in principle be carried by rare events and therefore cannot be promoted to source-level non-independence without additional control.

The exact missing ingredient is one of the following equivalent-strength routes:

1. prove a uniform fourth-moment bound and a CLT for `S_k` in the critical `k^2/n=Theta(1)` window;
2. construct a bounded degenerate U-statistic with controlled variance whose mean separates the true and product laws at that scale;
3. obtain a cluster/likelihood expansion for the stationary law that is uniform even when the total number of pair-collision parent events diverges.

No numerical evidence, source simulation, generator residual, or variance discrepancy is being mislabeled as a full TV theorem.
