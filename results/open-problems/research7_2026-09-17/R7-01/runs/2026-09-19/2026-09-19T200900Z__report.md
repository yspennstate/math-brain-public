## 4. New derivation I — exact one-particle and full Wiener semigroup

Let `H=L^2(0,1)` and let `mu` be Wiener measure on `C_0[0,1]`. For

`W(h)=int_0^1 h(s) dB_s`,

conditioning the future unit window on the current window gives, for `0<=t<=1`,

`P_t W(h)=W(U_t h)`

with

`(U_t h)(r)=1_{r>=t} h(r-t)`.

For `t>=1`, `U_t=0`. Therefore `(U_t)` is the nilpotent right-shift contraction semigroup on `H`.

For normalized Gaussian exponential vectors

`Exp(h)=exp(W(h)-||h||^2/2)`,

the fresh-noise covariance cancels exactly, giving

`P_t Exp(h)=Exp(U_t h)`.

Hence the full stationary moving-window semigroup is

`P_t = Gamma(U_t)`,

the Gaussian second quantization of the killed right-shift semigroup.

The one-particle generator is

`A h = -h'`,

with domain

`D(A)={h in H^1(0,1): h(0)=0}`.

This representation is exact and replaces the earlier heuristic OU-plus-skew picture.

## 5. New derivation II — exact generator pre-form and boundary symmetric energy

On the dense exponential core, define

`E(Exp(h),Exp(k)) = - d/dt|_{0+} <P_t Exp(h),Exp(k)>`.

Since `<Exp(h),Exp(k)>=exp(<h,k>)`, one obtains

`E(Exp(h),Exp(k)) = -<Ah,k> exp(<h,k>)`

and therefore

`E(Exp(h),Exp(k)) = (int_0^1 h'(s)k(s) ds) exp(<h,k>)`.

Its symmetric part is a pure boundary trace. Because `h(0)=k(0)=0`,

`[E(Exp(h),Exp(k))+E(Exp(k),Exp(h))]/2`

`= (1/2) h(1)k(1) exp(<h,k>)`.

In particular,

`E(Exp(h),Exp(h)) = (1/2)h(1)^2 exp(||h||^2)`.

So the old “no symmetric part” diagnosis is false. The symmetric dissipation is degenerate but nonzero, and is concentrated at the outgoing boundary of the unit window rather than being the bulk Malliavin/OU gradient energy.

## 6. New derivation III — exact strong-sector obstruction

The natural pre-form does not satisfy a standard strong-sector inequality. This can be proved with an explicit sequence, without numerics.

Let

`h_n(s)=sqrt(2) sin(n pi s)`

and

`k_n(s)=sqrt(2) sin((n+1) pi s)`.

They lie in `D(A)`, have unit `L^2` norm, are orthogonal, and vanish at both endpoints. Thus

`E(Exp(h_n),Exp(h_n))=E(Exp(k_n),Exp(k_n))=0`,

while

`int_0^1 h_n'(s)k_n(s) ds`

`=2 n pi int_0^1 cos(n pi s) sin((n+1) pi s) ds`

`=2n(1+1/(2n+1))`.

Also `||Exp(h_n)||_2^2=||Exp(k_n)||_2^2=e` and `<Exp(h_n),Exp(k_n)>=1`. Therefore the diagonal shifted energies

`E_1(u,u)=E(u,u)+||u||_2^2`

are both the fixed value `e`, but

`E_1(Exp(h_n),Exp(k_n))`

`=1+2n(1+1/(2n+1)) -> infinity`.

Hence no constant `C` can make

`|E_1(u,v)| <= C E_1(u,u)^{1/2} E_1(v,v)^{1/2}`

hold on this core. The obstacle is therefore genuinely **non-sectorial**. This sharply explains why a direct classical nonsymmetric Ma--Roeckner construction is not automatic and points toward generalized Dirichlet forms.

## 7. New derivation IV — finite-memory resolvent

For `t>=1`, the future unit Brownian window is independent of the current window, so

`P_t f=mu(f)`

for every `f in L^2(mu)`. Thus for every `alpha>0`,

`R_alpha f = int_0^1 e^{-alpha t} Gamma(U_t)f dt + e^{-alpha}/alpha * mu(f)`.

For centered `f`, the second term is zero. The resolvent is therefore a finite-memory integral; the hard issue is not long-range semigroup complexity but the non-sectorial path-space capacity theory.

If `tau_S` is a first hitting time, the source functional obeys

`int_0^infty e^{-t} P(tau_S<=t) dt = E[e^{-tau_S}; tau_S<infinity]`.

So `Cap_MW` is already a discounted hitting potential for this exact process. A rigorous generalized-form/capacity correspondence, however, still has to be proved before equating this with a variational capacity of a completed analytic form.
