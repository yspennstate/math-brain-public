# 4. Mathematical idea before derivation

The preceding work revealed an apparent split:

- service laws with a positive atom have an exact **cubic** threshold `k^3/n`;
- exponential service has an exact **square-root** threshold `k^2/n`.

A tempting interpretation would be that atoms are the whole reason for the cubic obstruction and that every atomless service law should be governed by the smoother square-root mechanism.

This run attacks that interpretation directly.

The correct singular object is not the diagonal produced by equal atomic service marks. What matters is whether a recent common parent can place a pair of workloads on a **Lebesgue-null residual surface** that the independent stationary M/G/1 product cannot see.

If a positive part of the service law lives on a compact set `C` of upper Minkowski dimension `<1/2`, then the common-residual set

\[
E_{C,\epsilon}
=
\{(x-t,y-t):x,y\in C,\ 0<t<\epsilon\}
\]

still has planar Lebesgue measure zero. That makes the old singular-support argument work even when the service distribution has **no atoms at all**.

The three required new methods are therefore:

1. **B5 — add a parameter:** expose the support-dimension parameter `d`; the covering proof succeeds for `d<1/2` and becomes critical at `d=1/2`.
2. **B6 — deform to a solved point:** use a scaled atomless Cantor law `X=a+varepsilon Y_r` and send `varepsilon->0`, approaching deterministic service in weak/moment topology without losing the cubic threshold for any positive scale.
3. **B23 — do what the proposer did not expect:** refute the natural “atomless implies exponential-like threshold” heuristic by an explicit atomless singular-continuous service class.

No code precedes or replaces the argument; this is a theorem-sized symbolic derivation.

# 5. New theorem

Consider the same limited fork–join model. Parent jobs arrive at rate

\[
\Lambda_n=\frac{n\lambda}{k},
\]

choose a uniform `k`-subset of `n` queues, and send iid service requirements with law `G`. Assume

\[
m_1=E[X]>0,
\qquad
E[X^2]<\infty,
\qquad
\rho=\lambda m_1<1.
\]

Fix a tagged set of exactly `k` queues. Let `P_{n,k}` be its joint stationary workload law and `pi_lambda` the one-dimensional stationary M/G/1 workload law.

### Theorem

Suppose there exists a compact set

\[
C\subset(0,\infty)
\]

such that

\[
q:=G(C)>0,
\qquad
\overline{\dim}_M(C)<\frac12.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}(P_{n,k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k})\to0
 \iff
 k^3/n\to0.
}
\]

This includes bounded **atomless singular-continuous** service laws.

## 6. B5 — support dimension and the singular residual surface

Let

\[
a=\min C>0
\]

and fix `0<epsilon<a`. Define

\[
E_{C,\epsilon}
=
\{(x-t,y-t):x,y\in C,\ 0<t<\epsilon\}.
\]

Choose a number `d` satisfying

\[
\overline{\dim}_M(C)<d<1/2.
\]

For all sufficiently small `delta`, the set `C` can be covered by at most

\[
A\delta^{-d}
\]

intervals of length `delta`. Cover `(0,epsilon)` by `O(delta^{-1})` intervals of the same length. Then

\[
C\times C\times(0,\epsilon)
\]

has a cover by

\[
O(\delta^{-2d-1})
\]

three-dimensional boxes of side `delta`.

The map

\[
\Phi(x,y,t)=(x-t,y-t)
\]

is Lipschitz. Each source box maps into a planar square of area `O(delta^2)`. The total planar area of the resulting cover is therefore

\[
O(\delta^{-2d-1})O(\delta^2)
=O(\delta^{1-2d})\to0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Leb}_2(E_{C,\epsilon})=0.}
\]

This exposes the exact boundary of this method: at upper Minkowski dimension `1/2`, the exponent `1-2d` becomes zero and the covering argument no longer proves nullity.

## 7. Independent product assigns zero mass to the surface

For any finite-mean service law, the Pollaczek–Khinchine geometric-sum representation expresses a stationary M/G/1 workload as a geometric sum of equilibrium service variables. The equilibrium distribution has density

\[
\frac{\overline G(t)}{E[X]}.
\]

Therefore `pi_lambda` has the idle atom `1-rho` at zero and an absolutely continuous restriction to `(0,infinity)`.

Since `E_{C,epsilon}` lies in the positive quadrant and has planar Lebesgue measure zero,

\[
(\pi_\lambda\otimes\pi_\lambda)(E_{C,\epsilon})=0.
\]

For finite `k`, let

\[
A_{n,\epsilon}
=
\bigcup_{i<j}\{(W_i,W_j)\in E_{C,\epsilon}\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k}(A_{n,\epsilon})=0.}
\]

## 8. The true fork–join law gives the surface positive mass

Let

\[
N=|S\cap K|
\]

be the number of tagged queues hit by one parent and define

\[
p_n=P(N\ge2),
\qquad
R_n=\frac{n\lambda}{k}p_n.
\]

Thus multi-hit tagged parents form a Poisson process of rate `R_n`.

For a fixed tagged coordinate, the tagged-singleton stream has rate

\[
\lambda_e
=
\lambda
\frac{\binom{n-k}{k-1}}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}.
\]

Assume `R_n>0`. Let `U` be the backward age of the most recent tagged multi-hit parent. Then

\[
P(U\in du)=R_ne^{-R_nu}\,du.
\]

Choose two of that parent's tagged destinations independently of the pre-arrival workload state. Wang et al.'s stationary association theorem implies that the selected pair is simultaneously idle immediately before the parent with probability at least

\[
(1-\rho)^2.
\]

The two iid service marks both fall in `C` with probability `q^2`.

By definition of the most recent multi-hit parent, there is no later multi-hit event. The only later arrivals to either selected queue are their two independent tagged-singleton streams. Conditional on `U=u`, no later arrival hits either selected queue with probability

\[
e^{-2\lambda_eu}.
\]

If additionally `u<epsilon<a`, then both selected workloads remain positive and equal

\[
(X_i-u,X_j-u)
\]

for service marks `X_i,X_j in C`, so their pair belongs to `E_{C,epsilon}`.

Integrating gives

\[
\boxed{
P_{n,k}(A_{n,\epsilon})
\ge
(1-\rho)^2q^2
\frac{R_n}{R_n+2\lambda_e}
\left(1-e^{-(R_n+2\lambda_e)\epsilon}\right).
}
\]

Since the independent product gives `A_{n,epsilon}` probability zero,

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}(P_{n,k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k})
\ge
(1-\rho)^2q^2
\frac{R_n}{R_n+2\lambda_e}
\left(1-e^{-(R_n+2\lambda_e)\epsilon}\right).
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus TV convergence forces

\[
R_n\to0.
\]

## 9. Hypergeometric lower bound forces the cubic condition

The cumulative P0429 proof gives a self-contained exact bound. With

\[
Y=(N)_2=N(N-1),
\]

hypergeometric factorial moments satisfy

\[
E[(N)_r]=\frac{(k)_r^2}{(n)_r},
\qquad r=2,3,4,
\]

and

\[
Y^2=(N)_4+4(N)_3+2(N)_2.
\]

Writing `b=k^2/n`, for `n>=6`,

\[
EY^2\le8(1+b)^2EY,
\qquad
EY\ge b^2/4.
\]

Paley–Zygmund at level zero yields

\[
p_n=P(N\ge2)
\ge\frac1{128}\min(b^2,1).
\]

Therefore

\[
R_n
\ge
\frac{\lambda}{128}\frac nk
\min\left(\frac{k^4}{n^2},1\right).
\]

If `k^2/n<=1`,

\[
R_n\ge\frac{\lambda}{128}\frac{k^3}{n};
\]

if `k^2/n>=1`,

\[
R_n\ge\frac{\lambda}{128}.
\]

Hence

\[
R_n\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k^3/n\to0.
\]

Together with (8.1), this proves necessity. The already-proved universal cubic upper bound supplies sufficiency. The theorem follows.

Fix

Let `C_r` be the two-branch Cantor attractor

\[
C_r=rC_r\cup(1-r+rC_r)

and `nu_r` its equal-weight Bernoulli self-similar measure. Then `nu_r` is atomless and

\[
\dim_M(C_r)=\frac{\log2}{\log(1/r)}<1/2.

\[
X=a+\varepsilon Y_r,
\qquad Y_r\sim\nu_r.

This is bounded, atomless and singular-continuous. Its support is a translate/rescaling of `C_r`, so it has the same Minkowski dimension. For every stable arrival rate,

\boxed{

Yet as `varepsilon->0`,

\[
X\Rightarrow a

and every finite moment converges to the corresponding deterministic-service moment. The atomless family therefore deforms continuously toward the original deterministic obstruction without changing its exact asymptotic threshold for any positive deformation scale.

# 11. B23 — what the new example rules out

The exponential theorem might have suggested:

> once the service law is atomless, the singular collision obstruction disappears and the natural threshold becomes `k=o(sqrt(n))`.

The Cantor family disproves that principle. Atomlessness is not the deciding regularity property.

The cumulative source picture is now:

1. **full finite-second-moment class:** universal threshold exponent exactly `1/3`;
2. **positive atoms:** exact cubic threshold;
3. **positive-mass service components supported on compact sets of upper Minkowski dimension `<1/2`:** exact cubic threshold, including atomless singular-continuous laws;
4. **exponential service:** exact square-root threshold.

The next conceptual boundary is smoothness/transversality of the service law, not merely presence or absence of atoms.

No claim is made here for the Minkowski-critical case `dim_M C=1/2`, for general singular laws of larger support dimension, or for arbitrary absolutely continuous non-exponential laws.
