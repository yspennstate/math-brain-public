## 4. New mathematical reconstruction: every orbit is asymptotically periodic up to coordinate permutation

The earlier intake attempt used Shannon entropy as a Lyapunov function. The stronger direct majorization argument is worth preserving because it identifies how much of the natural trajectory question is already structurally controlled.

For

\[
S_k(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k x_{[i]},
\]

bistochasticity gives

\[
S_k(x_{n+1})\le S_k(x_n)
\]

for every `k`. Hence each `S_k(x_n)` converges. Therefore the sorted vectors `x_n^downarrow` converge coordinatewise to a single decreasing vector `y`.

Every omega-limit point of the unsorted trajectory must therefore be a coordinate permutation of `y`, so the omega-limit set is finite, with cardinality at most

\[
\frac{m!}{\prod_a m_a!},
\]

where the `m_a` are multiplicities of equal coordinates of `y`.

Continuity makes the finite omega-limit set invariant. It is onto under `V`: if `x_{n_j}->z`, compactness of the predecessors gives a subsequence `x_{n_j-1}->w` and continuity gives `V(w)=z`. Thus `V` permutes the omega-limit points. Separation of the finitely many omega-points plus uniform continuity rules out late switching between distinct cycles: eventually the nearest omega-point follows the finite permutation exactly. Hence the omega-limit set is one periodic orbit, and after a phase shift

\[
x_{kp+r}\to z_r,
\qquad V(z_r)=z_{r+1\pmod p}.
\]

This proof needs only continuity plus `V(x) prec x`, not quadraticity. I do **not** claim it as novel: the recovered older E0066 memo and later literature already point to this trajectory theorem. Its value in this run is admission diagnosis: the broad source prompt has a strong general structural answer, while the genuinely unresolved residual issues are finer classification questions not specified by Problem 13.

The complete proof is archived in the new admission artifact.
