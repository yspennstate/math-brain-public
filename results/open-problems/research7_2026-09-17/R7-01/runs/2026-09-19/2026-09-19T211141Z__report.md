## 4. New exact derivation I — mixing below one-half forces a diameter bound

Let `G` be a finite group, let `mu` be a probability measure on `G`, and write

`nu_t = mu^{*t}`

for the law after `t` steps from the identity. Let `U` be uniform on `G`, and set

`A_t = supp(nu_t)`.

If

`||nu_t-U||_TV <= epsilon`,

then, because `nu_t(G\A_t)=0`,

`||nu_t-U||_TV >= U(G\A_t) = 1-|A_t|/|G|`.

Therefore

`|A_t| >= (1-epsilon)|G|`.

If `epsilon<1/2`, then `|A_t|>|G|/2`. For any `g in G`, the two sets `A_t` and `g A_t` each contain more than half of `G`, so they intersect. Thus there are `a_1,a_2 in A_t` with

`a_1 = g a_2`,

hence

`g = a_1 a_2^{-1}`.

If `mu` is supported on a generating set `S`, each `a_i` is a positive word of length `t` in `S`, and `a_2^{-1}` is a word of length `t` in `S^{-1}`. Consequently

`diam Cay(G,S union S^{-1}) <= 2t`.

This is exact and requires no reversibility, spectral estimate, or representation theory.

### Consequence for Diaconis's nested conjecture

A universal

`t_mix = O(n^3 log n)`

bound for every generating pair of `A_n` would imply

`diam Cay(A_n,{a,b,a^{-1},b^{-1}}) = O(n^3 log n)`

for every generating pair `(a,b)`.

So the source conjecture would, as a deterministic consequence, give a polynomial diameter theorem for every two-generator presentation of `A_n`. This is a substantial group-growth obligation, not a short mixing calculation.

## 5. New exact derivation II — universal two-generator support lower bound

For a two-point increment law, at time `t` there are at most `2^t` positive words, hence

`|A_t| <= 2^t`.

Combining with the previous support inequality, total variation at most `epsilon` requires

`2^t >= (1-epsilon)|G|`.

Thus

`t_mix(epsilon) >= log_2((1-epsilon)|G|)`.

For `G=A_n`, `|A_n|=n!/2`. At the conventional threshold `epsilon=1/4`,

`t_mix(1/4) >= log_2(3 n!/8)`.

Stirling's formula yields

`t_mix(1/4) >= n log_2 n - (log_2 e)n + O(log n)`.

Hence any two-generator walk starts with an exact information/support lower bound of order `n log n`, while the source's proposed upper scale is `n^3 log n`. The remaining factor `n^2` is structural; support counting cannot bridge it.

These two derivations are preserved as reusable mathematics, but neither closes the source.

## 6. Dated current-literature check — 2026-09-20 local / 2026-09-19 UTC

The bounded check covered the original/current source, exact phrases around the nested `A_n` conjecture, random-pair permutation-group diameter, and the recent random-Cayley cutoff literature.

1. **Diaconis (2013).** arXiv currently exposes v1, and the Bernoulli publication/DOI matches the source metadata. Printed Question 2 contains both the broad arbitrary-group question and the `A_n` “any generating pair” conjecture.

2. **Helfgott–Seress (2014), Annals of Mathematics.** For arbitrary generating sets of `Sym(n)` or `Alt(n)`, they prove the quasipolynomial bound

   `diam = exp(O((log n)^4 log log n))`.

   Their abstract explicitly describes polynomial diameter as a longstanding conjecture. The polynomial consequence derived in Section 4 is therefore far beyond a routine known general bound.

3. **Babai–Hetyei, random pairs.** For a uniformly random pair in `S_n`, the generated `S_n/A_n` Cayley graph has with high probability diameter at most

   `exp((1/2+o(1))(log n)^2)`.

   This is a random-pair diameter result, not a worst-case “any generating pair” mixing theorem.

4. **Hermon–Olesker-Taylor, JEMS 2026.** `Cutoff for almost all random walks on Abelian groups`, JEMS 28 (2026), 1913–1977, DOI `10.4171/JEMS/1743`, establishes cutoff in broad random-generator regimes for all finite Abelian groups and gives nilpotent comparison consequences.

5. **Hermon–Huang (2024).** `Cutoff for random Cayley graphs of nilpotent groups`, arXiv `2403.12355`, treats bounded-rank/bounded-class nilpotent groups for growing random generating sets and proves comparison results for some general generator sets.

6. **Huang–Rao (2025).** `Cutoff for random walks on dihedral groups`, arXiv `2510.19942`, treats a virtually Abelian family in a growing-generator regime.

These are important partial answers to the broad theme, but no theorem recovered in this bounded check matches either the arbitrary finite-group programme in full or Diaconis's worst-case two-generator `A_n` bound. Search failure is not treated as proof of openness or novelty.
