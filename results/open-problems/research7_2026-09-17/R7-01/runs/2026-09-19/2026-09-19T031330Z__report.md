## 3. B3 — prove the stronger L2 likelihood-ratio theorem

Specialize to exponential service rate `mu`, so `rho=lambda/mu`. Let

\[
q_k=\pi^{\otimes k},\qquad \pi(m)=(1-\rho)\rho^m.
\]

For the forward shift `S_i` and `U_i=S_i-I`, the tagged generator has the exact expansion

\[
L=L_0+W,
\qquad
W=\sum_{b=2}^k\gamma_b
\sum_{|B|=b}\prod_{i\in B}U_i,
\]

with

\[
\boxed{\gamma_b=\lambda\frac{(k-1)_{b-1}}{(n-1)_{b-1}}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For one geometric coordinate,

\[
\|U^*\|\le c_\rho:=1+\rho^{-1/2},
\qquad
U^*1=z,
\qquad
\|z\|_2^2=v_\rho:=\frac{1-\rho}{\rho},
\]

and `U^*` preserves the mean-zero subspace. The one-dimensional M/M/1 spectral gap is

\[
g_\rho=(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt\lambda)^2.
\]

Decompose `L^2(q_k)` into ANOVA sectors `H_A`, indexed by coordinate support `A subseteq[k]`. If `|A|=r`, then

\[
H=-L_0\ge r g_\rho\quad\text{on }\mathcal H_A.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For

\[
T_b=\sum_{|B|=b}\prod_{i\in B}U_i^*,
\]

a term that meets the input support in `m=b-s` coordinates and creates `s` new coordinates maps ANOVA level `r` to `r+s`. Fixing input and output supports, its block norm is bounded by

\[
\binom rm c_\rho^m v_\rho^{s/2}.
\]

The support-incidence matrix has row degree `binom{k-r}{s}` and column degree `binom{r+s}{s}`. Inserting `H^{-1/2}` on both sides and using (3.2) therefore gives

\[
\frac{\binom rm c_\rho^m v_\rho^{s/2}}
{g_\rho\sqrt{r(r+s)}}
\sqrt{\binom{k-r}{s}\binom{r+s}{s}}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For `s=0`, (3.3) is at most `c_rho^b k^(b-1)/(b! g_rho)`. For `s>=1`, elementary binomial bounds plus `r+s<=(s+1)r` yield

\[
(3.3)\le
\frac{c_\rho^{b-s}v_\rho^{s/2}(s+1)^{(s-1)/2}}
{(b-s)!s!g_\rho}
 k^{b-1}.
\]

Since `s! >= (s/e)^s`, choosing

\[
A_\rho=2\max\{1,c_\rho,2e\sqrt{v_\rho}\}
\]

and summing `s=0,...,b` gives the dimension-uniform bound

\[
\boxed{
\|H^{-1/2}T_bH^{-1/2}\|
\le\frac{A_\rho^b}{g_\rho}k^{b-1}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The key gain is `k^(b-1)`, not `k^b`: the product-chain energy pays for one combinatorial degree.

If `k<=n/2`, then

\[
\gamma_b\le\lambda(2k/n)^{b-1}.
\]

Writing `delta=k^2/n` and `K=W^*`, (3.4) sums geometrically to

\[
\boxed{
\theta_{n,k}:=\|H^{-1/2}KH^{-1/2}\|
\le
\frac{2\lambda A_\rho^2}{g_\rho}
\frac{\delta}{1-2A_\rho\delta}
=O_\rho(\delta)
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

whenever `2A_rho delta<1`. This is the missing STUCK1 relative-form estimate, now proved for the full interaction rather than only its pair part.

## 4. B11 — dual stationary equation and coercive inversion

The exact product-law forcing is

\[
D=K1
=\sum_{b=2}^k\gamma_b\sum_{|B|=b}\prod_{i\in B}z_i.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The ANOVA monomials are orthogonal, hence

\[
\|D\|_2^2
=\sum_{b=2}^k\binom kb\gamma_b^2v_\rho^b.
\tag{4.2}
\]

With `epsilon=k^3/n^2`, the same coefficient bound gives, whenever `4v_rho epsilon<1`,

\[
\|D\|_2^2
\le
\frac{4\lambda^2v_\rho^2\delta^2}
{1-4v_\rho\epsilon}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Every term in `D` has ANOVA level at least two, so

\[
\boxed{
\|H^{-1/2}D\|_2
\le
\frac{\sqrt2\lambda v_\rho\delta}
{\sqrt{g_\rho}\sqrt{1-4v_\rho\epsilon}}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Writing the stationary density relative to `q_k` as `1+h`, the adjoint stationarity equation is

\[
\boxed{(H-K)h=D.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

On the centered form domain, (3.5) makes the form of `H-K` coercive whenever `theta_{n,k}<1`. Lax–Milgram therefore yields a unique centered form-domain solution satisfying

\[
\|H^{1/2}h\|_2
\le\frac{\|H^{-1/2}D\|_2}{1-\theta_{n,k}}.
\]

Using the product gap once more,

\[
\boxed{
\|h\|_2
\le
\frac{\sqrt2\lambda v_\rho\delta}
{g_\rho(1-\theta_{n,k})\sqrt{1-4v_\rho\epsilon}}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The variational solution initially defines a finite signed invariant measure `(1+h)q_k`. It has mass one and finite total variation. For `k<=n/2` the tagged chain is irreducible because singleton tagged arrivals and single-queue departures have positive rates. The source-stable system has a unique stationary probability. Any finite signed invariant measure of mass one for an irreducible positive recurrent continuous-time countable-state chain equals that stationary probability: apply the invariant measure to `P_t`, use `P_t(x,y)->pi_true(y)`, and pass the limit under the finite total variation sum. Hence the variational density is the **actual** stationary density; positivity need not be assumed.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 d_{TV}(P^N_{n,k},q_k)
\le
\frac{\lambda v_\rho}{\sqrt2g_\rho}
\frac{k^2/n}
{(1-\theta_{n,k})\sqrt{1-4v_\rho k^3/n^2}}
=O_\rho(k^2/n).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This proves full stationary queue-length asymptotic independence for exponential service whenever `k=o(sqrt n)`.

For exponential service, conditional on the queue-length vector, memorylessness and independent task marks give the same coordinatewise kernel in both systems: zero queues have workload zero and a queue with `m>=1` tasks has `Gamma(m,mu)` total residual workload. TV contracts under this kernel. Thus

\[
\boxed{
 d_{TV}(P^W_{n,k},Q^W_k)
\le d_{TV}(P^N_{n,k},q_k)
=O_\rho(k^2/n).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This closes the exact simplest unresolved case named by STUCK1.
