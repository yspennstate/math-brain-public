## 3. B25 — exact workload/busy covariance

Let

\[
B_i=1_{\{W_i>0\}},\qquad
S=\sum_{i=1}^nW_i,\qquad
B=\sum_{i=1}^nB_i.
\]

Each marginal is M/M/1, so

\[
E B_i=\rho,
\qquad
\bar w:=E W_i=\frac{\rho}{\mu(1-\rho)}.
\]

A parent arrival adds

\[
Y=X_1+\cdots+X_K,
\quad
E Y=K/\mu,
\quad
E Y^2=K(K+1)/\mu^2.
\]

Between arrivals `S` decreases at rate `B`. Stationarity of `S` gives `E B=n rho`. Stationarity of `S^2` gives

\[
0=-2E[SB]+\frac{n\lambda}{K}
\left(2E[S]\frac K\mu+\frac{K(K+1)}{\mu^2}\right),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(S,B)=\frac{n\rho(K+1)}{2\mu}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because `W_iB_i=W_i`,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(W_i,B_i)
=E[W_i](1-\rho)=\rho/\mu.
\]

Exchangeability then forces, exactly for every `i\ne j`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(W_i,B_j)
=\frac{\rho(K-1)}{2\mu(n-1)}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Write

\[
\eta_n=(K-1)/(n-1).
\]

The cross signal is `rho eta_n/(2 mu)`.

## 4. B14 — fixed 3/4-coordinate cluster control

For a fixed set of `r<=4` tagged queue lengths, the exponential projected process is autonomous. Relative to `r` independent M/M/1 queues its generator is

\[
L^{(r)}=L_0^{(r)}+
\sum_{b=2}^r\gamma_b
\sum_{|A|=b}\prod_{i\in A}(S_i-I),
\]

with exact coefficient

\[
\gamma_b
=\lambda\frac{(K-1)_{b-1}}{(n-1)_{b-1}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For fixed `b`, `gamma_2=lambda eta_n` and `gamma_b=O(eta_n^{b-1})` for `b>=3`.

Let `q_r=pi^r` be the geometric product law, `H_r=-L_0^{(r)}`, `K_r=W_r^*`, and `D_r=K_r1`. The product chain is reversible with gap

\[
g_\rho=(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt\lambda)^2>0.
\]

Since `r<=4` is fixed and the one-coordinate shift adjoints are bounded on `L^2(pi)`,

\[
\|H_r^{-1}K_r\|=O(\eta_n).
\]

For small `eta_n`, the exact stationary density `1+h_r` therefore satisfies the convergent equation

\[
h_r=(I-H_r^{-1}K_r)^{-1}H_r^{-1}D_r,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\|h_r\|_2=O(\eta_n),\quad r\le4.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For `r=4`, product ANOVA gives a stronger pure-level estimate. `D_4` has support-level 2/3/4 pieces of sizes `O(eta_n)`, `O(eta_n^2)`, `O(eta_n^3)`. Because `H_4^{-1}` preserves support and the perturbation operator is `O(eta_n)`, the Neumann expansion yields

\[
\boxed{
\|\Pi_4h_4\|_2=O(\eta_n^2).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Exponential memorylessness maps queue lengths to workloads through the same coordinatewise Gamma kernel in the true and product systems. Consequently, any fixed 3-coordinate product with product expectation zero has true expectation `O(eta_n)`, while any fixed 4-coordinate product of four centered one-coordinate workload functions has true expectation `O(eta_n^2)`.

These are fixed-coordinate statements only; their constants never depend on the growing block size used next.

## 5. B17 — bounded-second-moment TV separator, no CLT needed

For any two laws `P,Q` and any square-integrable statistic `F`,

\[
\boxed{
|E_PF-E_QF|^2
\le
2d_{TV}(P,Q)(E_PF^2+E_QF^2).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This follows by Cauchy-Schwarz with densities `p,q` and

\[
(p-q)^2/(p+q)\le |p-q|.
\]

Thus a nonzero mean shift plus uniformly bounded second moments suffices for positive TV separation. A beta-binomial/Curie-Weiss critical-window CLT is unnecessary.

Define

\[
A_i=W_i-\bar w,
\qquad C_i=B_i-\rho,
\qquad
T_m=\frac1m\sum_{i\ne j\le m}A_iC_j.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Under the product workload law, `E T_m=0`. By (3.2), under the true law

\[
\boxed{
E T_m=(m-1)\frac{\rho\eta_n}{2\mu}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `m eta_n<=c_0`, then

\[
\boxed{
E_PT_m^2+E_QT_m^2\le C(\rho,\mu,c_0).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The complete count is by overlap size of the two ordered pairs in `T_m^2`:

- 2 distinct coordinates: `O(m^2)` terms, each `O(1)` from fixed workload moments, hence `O(1)` after `m^{-2}`;
- 3 distinct coordinates: `O(m^3)` terms, each has product expectation zero and true expectation `O(eta_n)` by (4.2), hence total `O(m eta_n)`;
- 4 distinct coordinates: `O(m^4)` terms, each is a pure product of four centered coordinate functions and has true expectation `O(eta_n^2)` by (4.3), hence total `O((m eta_n)^2)`.

This proves (5.4) without any growing-dimensional resolvent estimate.

## 6. Necessity at and above the square-root scale

Suppose `K^2/n` does not tend to zero. Along a subsequence let

\[
K^2/n\ge\varepsilon>0.
\]

If `eta_n` stays bounded below, the two-coordinate statistic `(W_1-bar w)(B_2-rho)` has true mean at least a positive constant by (3.2), product mean zero, and uniformly bounded second moments. Equation (5.1) gives a positive two-coordinate TV gap, hence a positive full `K`-coordinate TV gap.

Otherwise pass to a further subsequence with `eta_n->0`. Pick `0<c<epsilon/2` and set

\[
m_n=\lfloor c/\eta_n\rfloor.
\]

Then `m_n eta_n->c`, and

\[
\frac{m_n}{K}
\sim\frac{c(n-1)}{K(K-1)}
\le c/\varepsilon+o(1)<1,
\]

so `m_n<=K` eventually. Equations (5.3)-(5.4) give

\[
E_PT_{m_n}\to\frac{\rho c}{2\mu}>0,
\qquad E_QT_{m_n}=0,
\]

with bounded second moments. Equation (5.1) therefore yields

\[
\liminf d_{TV}(P_{m_n},Q_{m_n})>0.
\]

TV decreases under marginalization, hence the full `K`-workload TV distance also has positive liminf. Therefore

\[
 d_{TV}(P^W_{n,K},Q^W_K)\to0
\Longrightarrow K^2/n\to0.
\]

Together with the previous run's sufficiency, this proves (2.2).
