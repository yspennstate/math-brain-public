## 6. New derivation A — uniform renewal FLLN for endogenous polling intervals

Let `X_1,X_2,...` be iid positive interarrival times with

`mu = E[X_1] in (0,infinity)`, `lambda=1/mu`,

and let `N(t)` be the renewal count. For every fixed `T<infinity`,

`Delta_S(T) = sup_{0<=t<=T} |N(St)/S - lambda t| -> 0`

almost surely as `S->infinity`.

**Proof.** The renewal SLLN gives `N(u)/u -> lambda` a.s. Fix an outcome where this holds. Choose a finite grid `0=t_0<...<t_m=T`. At every grid point,

`N(S t_j)/S -> lambda t_j`.

For `t in [t_j,t_{j+1}]`, monotonicity gives

`N(S t_j)/S <= N(S t)/S <= N(S t_{j+1})/S`.

Hence the uniform error on that interval is bounded by the larger endpoint error plus `lambda(t_{j+1}-t_j)`. First let `S->infinity`, then let the grid mesh tend to zero. This proves the uniform statement.

Now take **arbitrary random endpoints** `0<=U_S<=V_S<=ST`; they may be stopping times and may depend on the same arrivals and the queueing evolution. Pathwise,

`|(N(V_S)-N(U_S))/S - lambda(V_S-U_S)/S|`

`<= |N(V_S)/S-lambda V_S/S| + |N(U_S)/S-lambda U_S/S|`

`<= 2 Delta_S(T) -> 0`.

Thus no independence of polling epochs and renewal arrivals is required for the finite-horizon fluid LLN. The circular dependence highlighted by the source is not the first-order arrival-count obstruction.

## 7. New derivation B — residual renewal gaps are o(S) under only a first moment

Let renewal epochs be `T_n=X_1+...+X_n` and forward recurrence time

`R(t)=T_{N(t)+1}-t`.

For every fixed `T`,

`sup_{0<=t<=ST} R(t)/S -> 0`

almost surely.

**Proof.** Since `E[X_1]<infinity`, the tail-sum criterion and Borel–Cantelli give `X_n/n->0` a.s. From this,

`max_{1<=k<=n} X_k/n -> 0`

almost surely: split the maximum into a fixed initial block and a tail on which `X_k<=epsilon k<=epsilon n`. The renewal SLLN also gives `N(ST)=O(S)` a.s. Therefore the largest interarrival interval intersecting `[0,ST]` is `o(S)`, and every forward recurrence time on this horizon is bounded by that largest interval. Division by `S` gives the claim.

For a stationary/equilibrium renewal process the forward-residual tail is

`P(R_e>x)=(1/mu) int_x^infinity P(X_1>u) du`,

which is a proper probability distribution whenever `E[X_1]<infinity`, even when `E[X_1^2]=infinity` and consequently `E[R_e]=infinity`.

Therefore **infinite mean residual life does not imply non-tight residual state**, and at fluid scale a second interarrival moment is not needed for this step.

## 8. New derivation C — deterministic fluid cycle balance for gated/exhaustive queues

Write

`rho_i=lambda_i E[B_i]`, `rho=sum_i rho_i<1`,

and deterministic switch-over times `S_i=s_i S`, with `s=sum_i s_i`. In any periodic deterministic unit-service-rate fluid cycle, let `C` be cycle duration and `V_i` the class-i visit duration. Work conservation gives

`C=sS+sum_i V_i`.

Flow conservation over one full cycle gives

`V_i=rho_i C`.

Consequently

`C/S = s/(1-rho)`,

`V_i/S = rho_i s/(1-rho)`.

For **exhaustive service**, the fluid work at visit start equals `rho_i(C-V_i)` and net drain during service is `1-rho_i`, so

`V_i = rho_i(C-V_i)/(1-rho_i) = rho_i C`.

For **gated service**, the gate serves exactly the class-i fluid accumulated since the previous gate, which spans one whole cycle, so directly `V_i=rho_i C`.

Thus the first-order deterministic cycle duration and visit fractions are uniquely forced by conservation; the renewal interarrival law disappears except through rates.
