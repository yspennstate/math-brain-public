## 3. Exact blocker

The cleanest unresolved source-valid case is exponential service with rate `mu`. Let

\[
q=\pi^{\otimes k}
\]

be the product of the one-queue stationary M/M/1 laws, and decompose the tagged generator as

\[
L=L_0+W,
\]

where `L_0` is the product generator with independent rate-`lambda` arrivals and rate-`mu` services, while `W` replaces a small fraction of independent arrivals by common-parent subset arrivals.

For

\[
B_i=\mathbf 1_{\{N_i>0\}},\qquad Z_i=B_i/\rho-1,
\]

the previous exponential-service run proved the exact product-law adjoint score

\[
\boxed{
D_{n,k}:=\frac{L^*q}{q}
=\sum_{b=2}^k\gamma_b\sum_{|A|=b}\prod_{i\in A}Z_i,
\qquad
\gamma_b=\lambda\frac{(k-1)_{b-1}}{(n-1)_{b-1}}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The Walsh chaoses in (3.1) are orthogonal under `q`, and for `k=o(n^(2/3))` the pair term dominates:

\[
\boxed{
\|D_{n,k}\|_{L^2(q)}
=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}\frac{k^2}{n}(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the **smooth** departure from product form has natural scale `k^2/n`, suggesting a square-root critical window, not the cubic `k^3/n` scale forced by singular atoms.

If the stationary tagged law has density `1+h` with respect to `q`, stationarity formally gives the exact Poisson equation

\[
\boxed{
-L_0 h=D_{n,k}+\mathcal K_{n,k}h,
\qquad
\mathcal K_{n,k}f:=q^{-1}W^*(qf).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The independent product M/M/1 generator has a dimension-free spectral gap

\[
g_\rho=(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt\lambda)^2>0.
\]

Equation (3.3) exposes the missing estimate. A dimension-uniform relative-form/resolvent bound of schematic form

\[
\boxed{
\|(-L_0)^{-1}\mathcal K_{n,k}f\|_2
\le C_\rho\frac{k^2}{n}\|f\|_2
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

(or an equivalent Dirichlet-form inequality strong enough to close a Neumann argument) would imply

\[
\|h\|_2=O_\rho(k^2/n)
\]

when `k^2/n -> 0`, and hence by `d_TV <= (1/2)||h||_2` would upgrade **full workload** asymptotic independence for exponential service to `k=o(sqrt(n))`.

What is missing is not another fixed-pair coefficient. It is precisely a **growing-dimensional operator estimate** such as (3.4). The birth-shift part of `W` is not obviously bounded on raw `L^2(q)`, so the product spectral gap alone does not justify the Neumann series. This is the same uniformity wall that invalidated the previous attempt to substitute a growing block size into a fixed-dimensional perturbation expansion.

There is a second, converse-side issue at the square-root window. The previous runs proved

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(B_i,B_j)
=a_{\rho,\mu}\frac{k}{n}+O((k/n)^2),\qquad a_{\rho,\mu}>0,
\]

for a fixed pair, and hence the busy-count fluctuation scale suggests order-one overdispersion when `k^2/n` is order one. But a variance or chi-square signal alone does not imply a positive total-variation separation without a dimension-uniform moment/CLT statement. Thus the putative square-root threshold requires two separate closures:

1. a **sufficiency** estimate such as (3.4) for full workloads when `k^2/n -> 0`;
2. a **necessity** mechanism, likely a critical-window CLT or bounded-test-function limit for the busy count, when `k^2/n` stays bounded away from zero.

The next recovery must attack only one of these rather than mixing them. The selected action below attacks the sufficiency side first because it has the sharper exact equation.
