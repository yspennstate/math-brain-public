## Substantive correction: the source scale is n^{-1/2}, not n^{-1}

Chatterjee's random-assignment lower bound is at the `n^{-1/2}` fluctuation scale in the paper's Definition 1.1 sense. The scale is now independently confirmed for a concrete model by Gilles Mordant's August 5, 2026 preprint *A central limit theorem for the random assignment problem*, arXiv:2608.05123, which proves for i.i.d. Uniform[0,1] costs

\[
\sqrt n(C_n-\zeta(2))\Rightarrow
N(0,4\zeta(2)-4\zeta(3)).
\]

Therefore the old `E0039-A001` statement that the conjectured order is `n^{-1}` is a material error and must not be inherited. Its proposed connection to the last `1/n^2` terms of the exact mean formula is likewise not a valid fluctuation baseline.

The Mordant theorem does **not** close E0039: Chatterjee explicitly notes that compactly supported uniform laws are outside `P^+(1)`, and a single distribution is not honestly the source's requested "general class".

A bounded literature check dated 2026-09-19 found no positive source-matching broad-class minimum-assignment CLT. The 2025 Ahlberg-Deijfen-Lo CLT concerns stable matching, not minimum assignment, and was excluded as a different object. Search misses were not interpreted as proof of openness.

## New derivation: deterministic nonlinear-transfer lemma

The durable admission artifact proves the following elementary but useful lemma. For

\[
C_{id}(U)=\min_\pi\sum_i U_{i\pi(i)},\qquad
C_g(U)=\min_\pi\sum_i g(U_{i\pi(i)}),
\]

assume `|g(u)-a u| <= K u^2` on the region containing all entries selected by the two respective optimal permutations `pi_0` and `pi_g`. Define

\[
S_0=\sum_iU_{i\pi_0(i)}^2,\qquad
S_g=\sum_iU_{i\pi_g(i)}^2.
\]

Testing each optimizer against the opposite objective gives the exact deterministic sandwich

\[
-KS_g\le C_g-aC_{id}\le KS_0,
\]

hence

\[
|C_g-aC_{id}|\le K\max(S_0,S_g).
\]

This explicitly handles optimizer switching; no assumption that a monotone nonlinear transform preserves the minimizing permutation is needed.

For a smooth cost law `F` with `lambda=f(0)>0`, set `g=F^{-1}`. Taylor inversion gives

\[
g(u)=\lambda^{-1}u+O(u^2).
\]

Consequently, if one can prove

\[
\sqrt n\max(S_0,S_g)\to0
\]

in probability together with localization of both optimal assignments to the Taylor region, then

\[
\sqrt n(C_n^F-\lambda^{-1}C_n^U)\to0
\]

in probability. Mordant's uniform CLT would then transfer by Slutsky to a genuine smooth class, yielding

\[
\sqrt n(C_n^F-\lambda^{-1}\zeta(2))
\Rightarrow
N\!\left(0,\lambda^{-2}[4\zeta(2)-4\zeta(3)]\right).
\]

Thus the broad-class problem is reduced along this route to a precise selected-edge localization/integrability statement, not to the exact-mean recursion emphasized in the old intake attempt.

A stronger sufficient condition is that the largest uniform coordinate selected by either optimizer is `o_p(n^{-1/2})`, because `sum u_i^2 <= (max u_i) sum u_i` and the total optimum is tight on constant scale. The expected natural maximum is much smaller, around logarithmic-over-n scale, but no theorem establishing the needed bound for both optimizers was proved or positively recovered here.
