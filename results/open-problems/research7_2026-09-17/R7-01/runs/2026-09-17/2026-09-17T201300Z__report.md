# 5. B11 — dualize to a robust local HGR profile and use Peyre's arcsine theorem

For a Markov kernel `K`, define the robust local maximal-correlation coefficient

\[
\kappa(K)
:=
\sup_{\lambda}\rho(U,V),
\qquad U\sim\lambda,\quad V\mid U=x\sim K(x,\cdot),
\]

where the supremum is over input laws supported on the chain's full-measure row-good set. Let

\[
\kappa(n):=\sup_{t-s\ge n}\kappa(K_{s,t}).
\]

The previous run's Dobrushin/chi-square lemma immediately gives

\[
\kappa(K)^2\le\delta(K),
\qquad
\kappa(n)^2\le\Delta(n)\le\min\{1,2\phi(n)\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define

\[
\Psi(r)=\sin\left((2\arcsin r)\wedge\frac\pi2\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\Psi(r)=2r\sqrt{1-r^2}\quad(0\le r\le2^{-1/2}),
\qquad
\Psi(r)=1\quad(r\ge2^{-1/2}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

## Theorem 5.1 — robust-HGR arcsine bound

For every lag `n`,

\[
\boxed{\rho^*(n)\le\Psi(\kappa(n)).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

Fix finite disjoint index sets `S,T` with `dist(S,T)>=n`. Order their union chronologically and merge consecutive selected indices of the same color into maximal monochromatic blocks. Write the `S` blocks as `U_i` and the `T` blocks as `V_j` in chronological order. After shifting one index if necessary, `U_i` can be adjacent only to `V_i` and `V_{i-1}`.

Use exactly the prefix conditioning allowed by Peyre Section 3.4(a): condition the pair `(U_i,V_j)` only on earlier `U` blocks and earlier `V` blocks.

If `U_i,V_j` are nonadjacent in the chronological block path, those prefixes contain an intervening time-state separator. The Markov property makes the tested past and future blocks conditionally independent, so their subjective maximal correlation is zero.

If they are adjacent, let `s` be the final time in the earlier block and `t` the first time in the later block. The prescribed prefix conditioning is prior to that transition. It may change the input distribution of `X_s`, but it leaves the transition kernel `K_{s,t}` unchanged and cannot put positive mass on a row-null set. Therefore, by the definition of robust `kappa` and Markov data processing,

\[
\rho(U_i,V_j\mid\text{prescribed prefixes})
\le\kappa(K_{s,t})\le\kappa(n).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Pad the two finite block sequences by deterministic variables and index them by `Z`. Peyre's Theorem 3.3.10 applies with

\[
\varepsilon(0)=\varepsilon(-1)=\kappa(n),
\qquad
\varepsilon(z)=0\quad(z\notin\{0,-1\}).
\]

Hence

\[
\arcsin\rho(\sigma(U_i:i),\sigma(V_j:j))
\le
(2\arcsin\kappa(n))\wedge\frac\pi2,
\]

which is exactly (5.4). Taking the supremum over finite interlacings and the usual finite-coordinate `L^2` approximation gives the full sigma-field coefficient. QED.

Combining (5.1) and (5.4), whenever `phi(n)<=1/4`,

\[
\boxed{
\rho^*(n)
\le
2\sqrt{2\phi(n)(1-2\phi(n))}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This strictly improves the previous quantitative bound

\[
2\sqrt{2\phi(n)}
\]

for every `0<phi(n)<1/4`. It also makes the direct bound nontrivial throughout `phi(n)<1/4` rather than only at the smaller threshold implicit in the old crude path estimate.

**B11 outcome:** success. The alternating path is governed by an exact arcsine tensorization function of the robust local HGR coefficient.

# 6. B5 — add a parameter and differentiate: can the path factor two be beaten inside Markov chains?

Consider the stationary two-state chain `X_k in {-1,+1}` with

\[
P(X_{k+1}=X_k)=\frac{1+a}{2},
\qquad
P(X_{k+1}=-X_k)=\frac{1-a}{2},
\qquad 0<a<1,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and stationary law `(1/2,1/2)`. Then

\[
E[X_{k+h}\mid X_k]=a^hX_k,
\qquad
E[X_iX_j]=a^{|i-j|}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Fix lag `n` and put `r=a^n`. The `n`-step channel is a binary symmetric channel with parameter `r`.

For an arbitrary input law with mean `m`, both input and output are binary, so their maximal correlation is their absolute Pearson correlation. Direct calculation gives

\[
\rho(U,V)^2
=\frac{r^2(1-m^2)}{1-r^2m^2}
\le r^2,
\tag{6.3}
\]

with equality at `m=0`. Thus the robust local coefficient is exactly

\[
\boxed{\kappa(n)=r.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The source phi coefficient is also exact:

\[
\boxed{\phi(n)=\frac r2.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Indeed, conditioning the past can only change the posterior on `X_0`. The `n`-step channel multiplies its bias by `r`, so the total-variation distance of `X_n` from stationarity is at most `r/2`; future continuation cannot increase TV. Equality is attained by conditioning on `X_0=+1` and testing `X_n=+1`.

The weak-edge derivative question is now precise: does a long interlacing amplify a local coefficient `r` with derivative strictly below 2 at `r=0`? B25 answers no.

**B5 outcome:** success. It reduces the possible improvement to an explicit derivative at the independent chain `a=0`.

# 7. B25 — count covariance pairs twice: a matching first-order lower bound

For `N>=1`, take

\[
S_N=\{0,2n,\ldots,2Nn\},
\qquad
T_N=\{n,3n,\ldots,(2N-1)n\},
\]

and the centered admissible functions

\[
F_N=\sum_{i=0}^{N}X_{2in},
\qquad
G_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}X_{(2j+1)n}.
\]

Using `E[X_iX_j]=r^{|i-j|/n}` on these sampled times,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(F_N)
=(N+1)+2\sum_{h=1}^{N}(N+1-h)r^{2h}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(F_N)}N
\longrightarrow
1+2\sum_{h\ge1}r^{2h}
=\frac{1+r^2}{1-r^2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The same limit holds for `Var(G_N)/N`.

For the cross term, each bulk odd site sees even sites at signed odd sampled distances. Hence

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(F_N,G_N)}N
\longrightarrow
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}r^{|2k-1|}
=\frac{2r}{1-r^2}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The boundary correction is `O(1)` because the omitted geometric tails are summable even after summing over distance from the two ends. Consequently

\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Corr}(F_N,G_N)
=\boxed{\frac{2r}{1+r^2}}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Since every finite `N` gives an admissible interlacing at separation `n`,

\[
\boxed{
\rho^*(n)\ge\frac{2a^n}{1+a^{2n}}
=\frac{4\phi(n)}{1+4\phi(n)^2}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The new arcsine upper bound with the exact robust local coefficient gives, for `r<=1/sqrt(2)`,

\[
\rho^*(n)\le2r\sqrt{1-r^2}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Thus

\[
2r-2r^3+O(r^5)
\le \rho^*(n)
\le2r-r^3+O(r^5),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\rho^*(n)=2r+O(r^3)}
\qquad(r\downarrow0).
\tag{7.7}
\]

So the coefficient **2** in the weak-edge expansion of the global alternating-path step is genuinely first-order sharp even inside stationary irreducible aperiodic two-state Markov chains. It is not merely an artifact of allowing arbitrary abstract families in Peyre's theorem.

This does **not** prove sharpness of the full source bound as a function of `phi`. In this binary family,

\[
\kappa=r,\qquad \Delta=r=2\phi,
\]

so the universal local conversion `kappa^2 <= Delta` is very loose for small `r`. The remaining quantitative gap is therefore localized sharply: can a stationary Markov family simultaneously approach the erasure-type local equality `kappa^2 ~= Delta` and the long-path factor-two equality?

**B25 outcome:** success. The global factor two cannot be improved to any smaller first-order universal coefficient if one works only from robust local HGR information.
