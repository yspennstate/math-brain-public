# 4. B1 — change category: the reset-age kernel is a scaled coisometry

The previous report introduced the chain

`P(i,0)=p`, `P(i,i+1)=q`, `p=1-q`, `0<q<1`,                    (4.1)

on `N_0`, with stationary law

`pi(j)=p q^j`.                                                     (4.2)

It proved exactly that its forward phi coefficient is `q^n`, its reverse phi coefficient is `1` at every finite lag, and its lower-psi coefficient is `0` at every finite lag. I now change category from transition probabilities to the Hilbert-space Markov operator.

Let

`H := L^2_0(pi) = {f: sum_j pi(j)f(j)=0, sum_j pi(j)f(j)^2<infinity}`.

Write `K` for the one-step Markov operator on `H`:

`(Kf)(i) = E[f(X_1)|X_0=i] = p f(0)+q f(i+1)`.                    (4.3)

Its Hilbert adjoint is conditional expectation backward in time.

## Theorem 4.1 — exact coisometry identity

For every `f in H`,

`(K^* f)(0)=0`,

`(K^* f)(j)=f(j-1)` for `j>=1`,                                   (4.4)

and consequently

`||K^* f||_2^2 = q ||f||_2^2`,                                    (4.5)

`K K^* = q I_H`.                                                   (4.6)

In particular, `K^*/sqrt(q)` is an isometry and `K/sqrt(q)` is a coisometry.

### Proof

Under stationarity, the reversed one-step kernel is determined by

`pi(i)P(i,j)=pi(j)P^*(j,i)`.

If `j>=1`, the only predecessor of `j` is `j-1`, and

`P^*(j,j-1)=pi(j-1)q/pi(j)=1`.

If `j=0`, every state can precede zero and

`P^*(0,i)=pi(i)p/pi(0)=pi(i)`.

Therefore, for centered `f`,

`(K^*f)(0)=sum_i pi(i)f(i)=0`,

and for `j>=1`, `(K^*f)(j)=f(j-1)`. This proves (4.4).

Now

`||K^*f||_2^2`
` = sum_{j>=1} p q^j f(j-1)^2`
` = q sum_{i>=0} p q^i f(i)^2`
` = q ||f||_2^2`,

which is (4.5). Finally, using (4.4), for every `i>=0`,

`(K K^*f)(i)`
` = p(K^*f)(0)+q(K^*f)(i+1)`
` = q f(i)`.

Hence `K K^*=qI_H`. QED.

## Corollary 4.2 — exact ordinary maximal correlation

For every integer `d>=1`,

`rho(sigma(X_0),sigma(X_d)) = q^(d/2)`.                            (4.7)

### Proof

For a stationary Markov chain, the maximal correlation of the two endpoint sigma-fields is the operator norm of `K^d` on `H`. From (4.5), `||K^*||=sqrt(q)`, hence `||K||=sqrt(q)` and `||K^d||<=q^(d/2)`.

On the other hand, repeated use of (4.6) gives

`K^d (K^*)^d = q^d I_H`.                                          (4.8)

For any unit vector `g in H`, put `f=(K^*)^d g/q^(d/2)`. By (4.5), `||f||=1`, and by (4.8),

`K^d f = q^(d/2) g`.

Thus `||K^d||>=q^(d/2)`, proving equality and (4.7). QED.

### Outcome of B1

The stress-test chain is not merely exponentially rho-mixing. Its one-cut dependence is algebraically rigid: all nonzero singular directions of the backward operator contract by the same factor `sqrt(q)`. This is substantially more structure than generic phi-mixing and is the feature to exploit against interlacing.

# 5. B19 — make it an algorithm: an exact revealing-erasure channel

The `d`-step transition admits an exact decomposition that is stronger than total-variation mixing.

Put

`Q := q^d`.                                                        (5.1)

Starting from `X_t=i`, during the next `d` steps either there is no reset, of probability `Q`, or at least one reset. Therefore

`P^d(i,j)=p q^j` for `0<=j<=d-1`,

plus an atom of mass `Q` at `j=i+d`.                               (5.2)

Define the future survival flag

`E := 1_{X_{t+d}>=d}`.                                             (5.3)

Then:

1. `E` is measurable from the **right endpoint alone**;
2. `P(E=1)=Q` and `E` is independent of `X_t` and of the entire past up to time `t`;
3. on `{E=1}`, `X_{t+d}-d=X_t` exactly;
4. on `{E=0}`, `X_{t+d}` has law
   `nu_d(j)=p q^j/(1-Q)`, `0<=j<d`, independently of the entire past up to `t`.

Thus the channel is an exact **reveal-or-refresh erasure channel**: with probability `Q` it reveals the input perfectly (shifted by `d`), and otherwise it outputs independent fresh noise. The erasure/reveal flag is itself observable at the output.

This gives an exact interlaced calculation rather than a bound.

## Theorem 5.1 — exact first nontrivial interlaced coefficient

Let

`U=X_0`, `V=X_d`, `W=X_{2d}`.

Then

`rho(sigma(V), sigma(U,W))^2 = 2Q-Q^2`,                            (5.4)

or equivalently

`rho(sigma(X_d), sigma(X_0,X_{2d})) = sqrt(2 q^d-q^(2d))`.         (5.5)

### Proof

Let `f(V)` be centered and square-integrable. Apply the reveal-or-refresh decomposition to the transition from `V` to `W`, and let

`E=1_{W>=d}`.

The flag `E` is independent of `(U,V)`, with `P(E=1)=Q`. On `{E=1}`, `V=W-d` is known exactly. On `{E=0}`, `W` is independent of `(U,V)`. Hence

`E[f(V)|U,W]`
` = 1_E f(V) + 1_{E^c} E[f(V)|U]`.                                (5.6)

The two terms live on disjoint events, and `E` is independent of `(U,V)`, so

`||E[f(V)|U,W]||_2^2`
` = Q ||f||_2^2 + (1-Q)||E[f(V)|U]||_2^2`.                         (5.7)

But `E[f(V)|U]=(K^d f)(U)`. Therefore, for `||f||_2=1`,

`||E[f(V)|U,W]||_2^2`
` = Q + (1-Q)||K^d f||_2^2`.                                      (5.8)

Taking the supremum over unit `f` and using Corollary 4.2,

`rho(sigma(V),sigma(U,W))^2`
` = Q+(1-Q)||K^d||^2`
` = Q+(1-Q)Q`
` = 2Q-Q^2`.                                                       (5.9)

The norm is genuinely attained: if `g` is any unit vector in `H` and `f=(K^*)^d g/sqrt(Q)`, then `||f||=1` and `||K^d f||=sqrt(Q)`. QED.

### Corollary 5.2 — exact single-hole coefficient against the whole exterior

Let

`F^- = sigma(X_k: k<=0)`,

`F^+ = sigma(X_k: k>=2d)`.

Then

`rho(sigma(X_d), F^- join F^+) = sqrt(2 q^d-q^(2d))`.              (5.10)

### Proof

For a Markov chain, conditional on the boundary pair `(X_0,X_{2d})`, the interior variable `X_d` is conditionally independent of the exterior tails `sigma(X_k:k<0)` and `sigma(X_k:k>2d)`. Hence for every square-integrable `f(X_d)`,

`E[f(X_d)|F^- join F^+] = E[f(X_d)|X_0,X_{2d}]`.

The conditional-expectation operator, and therefore its norm/maximal correlation, is the same as in Theorem 5.1. QED.

### Quantitative meaning

Ordinary one-cut maximal correlation at distance `d` is `sqrt(Q)`. The first genuinely two-sided/interlaced geometry amplifies this only to

`sqrt(2Q-Q^2) = sqrt(2) sqrt(Q)(1+O(Q))`.                          (5.11)

So the simplest interlacing does **not** expose a hidden nondecaying correlation. In particular, the reset-age chain cannot fail rho*-mixing through a single point trapped between two remote exterior tails.

## Why the constructive recursion still fails globally

The same erasure identity exposes the precise algorithmic failure when one adds arbitrarily many alternating blocks. Suppose a target sigma-field `B` contains a boundary variable `V`, and one adds to the opposite side an output of a reveal-or-refresh channel from `V` with reveal probability at most `Q`. For a centered `B`-measurable variable `F`, conditioning on the reveal flag gives the generic estimate

`||E[F | A join new_output]||_2^2`
` <= Q ||F||_2^2 + (1-Q)||E[F|A]||_2^2`.                           (5.12)

Thus, if one tracks only a single scalar correlation bound `r`, the naive recursion is

`r_new^2 <= Q+(1-Q)r_old^2`.                                      (5.13)

After `L` alternating additions this permits

`r_L^2 <= 1-(1-Q)^L`,                                              (5.14)

which tends to one as `L -> infinity` for every fixed `Q>0`. This is exactly the historical alternation-count obstruction in a sharper form: **the elementary erasure algorithm is locally exact but globally loses all information because it forgets that different reveals belong to the same regenerative one-dimensional ancestry structure**.

Equation (5.13) is therefore not a route to the full source theorem; repeating it would be checker/lemma churn rather than progress.

# 6. B21 — import the analogous-field mechanism: Bradley's pure-death rho* proof

Bradley's 2015 INAR paper supplies a near-perfect comparison object. In its Lemma 2.4 he studies

`zeta_k = zeta_0 prod_{i=1}^k eta_i`,                              (6.1)

where the `eta_i` are iid Bernoulli with survival probability `a`. This is a pure-death ancestry thread: once `zeta` hits zero, it stays zero forever.

For arbitrary interlaced index sets `S,T` separated by `m`, Bradley partitions their union into alternating blocks

`Q_1,Q_2,...,Q_L`,                                                 (6.2)

with successive blocks at least `m` apart. He encodes each block in one variable `Y_i` whose distinguished value zero means that the pure-death thread is zero on the whole block. Two properties then hold:

1. **absorbing:** `Y_{i-1}=0` forces `Y_i=0`;
2. **high-probability death across each gap:** conditional on all previous blocks, `P(Y_i=0 | past) >= 1-a^m`.

His Lemma 2.3 then gives a bound on the dependence between the odd and even block joins **independent of `L`**, and Lemma 2.2 converts that bound to maximal correlation. This is exactly what the naive recursion (5.13) lacks.

## The reset-age chain differs at one precise place

For the reset-age chain, the ancestry carried by `X_t` also dies across a gap with probability `1-Q`. But after death, a **new** age/ancestry begins at the next reset. Therefore the natural zero/fresh state is **not absorbing** across later alternating blocks.

That is the full gluing obstruction:

- the cross-gap old ancestry is a pure-death thread and fits Bradley's Lemma 2.4;
- but the chain continually launches replacement threads;
- scalar recursion counts every replacement as a new chance to reveal the opposite color and accumulates to one;
- Bradley's Csaki-Fisher tensorization works for INAR because the relevant particle/survival components can be separated into independent pieces, whereas the reset-age process has a single succession of regenerative ancestry intervals whose deterministic-time block allocation is random.

This comparison is stronger than the previous vague statement that “arbitrary interlacings are hard.” It identifies the exact missing theorem.

## Missing lemma isolated by this run

A sufficient next ingredient for the reset-age stress test is a **regenerative alternating-block tensorization lemma** of the following shape.

Let a stationary one-dimensional chain have exact transitions

`state -> shifted copy with probability Q`,

`state -> fresh independent state with probability 1-Q`,           (6.3)

with the branch flag observable at the right endpoint. For every two finite sets `S,T` whose cross-color distance is at least one step, partition `S union T` into maximal monochromatic blocks. Prove a bound

`rho(sigma(X_s:s in S), sigma(X_t:t in T)) <= R(Q)`                 (6.4)

where `R(Q)` is independent of the number of blocks and `R(Q)->0` as `Q->0`.

For the actual `d`-spaced reset-age transitions, `Q=q^d`; any such lemma would immediately prove that the reset-age stress test is rho*-mixing.

Theorem 5.1 determines the unavoidable local scale:

`R(Q) >= sqrt(2Q-Q^2)`                                             (6.5)

because the three-point configuration is an admissible interlacing. Thus the correct dimension-free theorem, if true, should live on the `sqrt(Q)` scale, not the `Q` scale.

I have **not** proved (6.4) in this run. The absorbing-state hypothesis in Bradley's Lemma 2.3 cannot simply be dropped: doing so would silently assume away precisely the repeated-regeneration effect that has to be controlled.
