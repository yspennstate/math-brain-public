# 4. Idea before code

No checker was written because the new step is theorem-sized and analytic rather than a finite computation.

The previous run's obstacle was that local reveal/refresh bounds accumulated with the number of alternating blocks. The escape is to stop iterating a scalar recurrence and instead preserve the **geometry of the interlacing**.

For any finite disjoint index sets `S,T`, order `S union T` in time and merge consecutive selected indices of the same color into maximal monochromatic blocks. These blocks form an alternating path.

There are then two structural facts:

1. **Only adjacent opposite-color blocks can have nonzero subjective correlation once previous blocks are conditioned on.** Any nonadjacent pair has a whole previously exposed opposite-color block between it, and one state in that intervening block is an exact Markov separator.

2. **An adjacent block pair has a uniform conditional correlation bound depending only on the time gap, not on how the past has been conditioned.** Phi-mixing implies a total-variation diameter bound on the transition rows. Diameter, unlike closeness to a particular stationary marginal, survives arbitrary changes of the input distribution.

Thus the subjective-correlation matrix is a weighted path, not a dense matrix. Its operator norm is at most twice the edge weight, independent of the number of alternating blocks. Peyre's tensorization theorem then gives the desired uniform interlaced bound.

This simultaneously resolves the reset-age stress test and the full source question.

# 5. Lemma 1 — phi controls the essential diameter of every Markov transition family

Fix integers `s<t` and put `g=t-s`. Let

- `mu_s = Law(X_s)`,
- `mu_t = Law(X_t)`,
- `K_{s,t}(x,.) = Law(X_t in . | X_s=x)`

be a regular conditional transition kernel.

Use total variation in the convention

`||nu-eta||_TV = sup_B |nu(B)-eta(B)|`.

## Lemma

There is a Borel set `A_{s,t}` of full `mu_s` measure such that, for every `x in A_{s,t}`,

`||K_{s,t}(x,.) - mu_t||_TV <= phi(g)`.

Consequently, for all `x,x' in A_{s,t}`,

`||K_{s,t}(x,.) - K_{s,t}(x',.)||_TV <= 2 phi(g)`.          (5.1)

## Proof

Fix a Borel set `B` and define

`h_B(x)=K_{s,t}(x,B)-mu_t(B)`.

For any Borel `D` with `mu_s(D)>0`, the event `{X_s in D}` belongs to the past sigma-field `F_{-infty}^s`, while `{X_t in B}` belongs to `F_t^infty`. Therefore the definition of `phi(g)` gives

`|(1/mu_s(D)) integral_D h_B dmu_s| <= phi(g)`.             (5.2)

If `h_B > phi(g)+epsilon` on a positive-`mu_s` set, take that set as `D` in (5.2), contradiction. The same argument applied to `-h_B` gives

`|h_B(x)| <= phi(g)`

for `mu_s`-almost every `x`.

To choose one null set for all Borel `B`, take a countable algebra `C` generating the Borel sigma-field of `R`, for example finite unions of rational half-open intervals, and intersect the full-measure sets just obtained for all `B in C`. Call the intersection `A_{s,t}`.

For fixed `x in A_{s,t}`, the class of Borel `B` satisfying

`|K_{s,t}(x,B)-mu_t(B)| <= phi(g)`

is closed under monotone limits and contains `C`; the monotone class theorem therefore extends the bound to every Borel `B`. This proves the first assertion. The triangle inequality gives (5.1). QED.

### Why this lemma is the important reformulation

The unconditional marginal `mu_t` will change after conditioning the earlier history, so a proof based only on closeness to that marginal is fragile. The **pairwise row diameter** in (5.1) is intrinsic to the transition family and survives any later change of the input law.

# 6. Lemma 2 — the row-diameter bound survives arbitrary past conditioning

Fix the same `s<t`, and let `lambda` be **any** probability law supported on `A_{s,t}`. Define a two-step experiment

`U ~ lambda`,

`V | (U=x) ~ K_{s,t}(x,.)`.

## Lemma

Under this law,

`phi(sigma(U),sigma(V)) <= 2 phi(g)`,                       (6.1)

and hence

`rho(U,V) <= a_g := min(1, 2 sqrt(2 phi(g)))`.              (6.2)

## Proof

Put `delta=2 phi(g)`. By (5.1), for every Borel `B`, the set of numbers

`{K_{s,t}(x,B) : x in A_{s,t}}`

has diameter at most `delta`.

For any nonnull event `C` determined by `U`, the law of `V` conditional on `C` is one convex mixture of the rows `K_{s,t}(x,.)`, while the unconditional law of `V` is another convex mixture of exactly the same row family. For each Borel `B`, two convex combinations of numbers in an interval of length `delta` differ by at most `delta`. Thus

`|P(V in B | C)-P(V in B)| <= delta`

uniformly in `B,C`, proving (6.1).

Bradley's equation (1.13), applied under this conditional-input probability law, then gives

`rho(U,V) <= 2 sqrt(phi(sigma(U),sigma(V))) <= 2 sqrt(2 phi(g))`.

Maximal correlation is at most one, proving (6.2). QED.

## Conditional-support point

If `lambda` is the conditional law of `X_s` given any earlier-history sigma-field, it is supported on `A_{s,t}` almost surely: the unconditional probability of `X_s notin A_{s,t}` is zero, so its conditional probability is zero almost surely.

Therefore (6.2) is available after **arbitrary conditioning on the earlier path**. This is the exact property needed by Peyre's subjective-correlation theorem.

# 7. Main theorem — a uniform source-level rho-star bound

## Theorem

For every real-state Markov chain `X=(X_k,k in Z)` in Bradley's Section-3 setting and every integer `n>=1`,

`rho_star(n) <= min(1, 4 sqrt(2 phi(n)))`.                  (7.1)

Consequently every phi-mixing Markov chain is rho-star-mixing.

## Proof, finite interlacings

Take nonempty finite disjoint `S,T subset Z` with

`dist(S,T)>=n`.

Order `S union T` chronologically and merge consecutive selected indices of the same color into maximal monochromatic blocks. The result is a finite sequence of blocks

`B_1 < B_2 < ... < B_m`

whose colors alternate.

Enumerate the S-blocks chronologically as

`S_1,...,S_I`

and the T-blocks chronologically as

`T_1,...,T_J`.

Let

`U_i=(X_k)_{k in S_i}`,

`V_j=(X_k)_{k in T_j}`.

For a pair `(i,j)`, Peyre Section 3.4(a) tells us to measure the subjective maximal correlation of `U_i,V_j` after conditioning only on

`H_ij = sigma(U_i' : i'<i, V_j' : j'<j)`.                  (7.2)

We now identify every entry of the resulting correlation matrix.

### 7.1 Adjacent opposite-color blocks

Suppose `S_i` is immediately followed by `T_j` in the full block order. Put

`s=max S_i`,

`t=min T_j`.

Since every S index is at distance at least n from every T index,

`t-s >= n`.

Every random variable in `H_ij` occurs before the block `S_i`. Condition on an almost-everywhere value `H_ij=h`. The Markov property gives, under that conditional law,

`U_i -> X_s -> X_t -> V_j`.                                 (7.3)

Conditioning the earlier history changes the input law of `X_s` but does not change the transition kernel `K_{s,t}`. By the conditional-support observation in Section 6 and Lemma 2,

`rho(X_s,X_t | H_ij=h) <= a_{t-s} <= a_n`,

where

`a_n=min(1,2 sqrt(2 phi(n)))`

and `phi` is nonincreasing in the lag.

Maximal-correlation data processing along (7.3) gives

`rho(U_i,V_j | H_ij=h) <= a_n`.

Therefore Peyre's subjective correlation satisfies

`{U_i:V_j}_{H_ij} <= a_n`.                                  (7.4)

The identical argument applies when a T-block is immediately followed by an S-block.

### 7.2 Nonadjacent opposite-color blocks have subjective correlation exactly zero

Suppose `S_i` lies chronologically before `T_j`, but they are not adjacent blocks. Since the block colors alternate, the T-block immediately following `S_i` lies strictly between them. Its T-index is `<j`, so the entire intermediate T-block belongs to `H_ij`.

Choose one time `r` in that intermediate block. Conditional on `X_r`, the Markov property factors the pre-r path from the post-r path. Conditioning additionally on any variables already contained in `H_ij` preserves this factorization: variables in `H_ij` lie either in the past factor or in the future factor, and conditioning coordinates separately inside two conditionally independent factors leaves the remaining factors conditionally independent.

Hence for almost every value of `H_ij`,

`U_i` and `V_j` are independent,

so

`{U_i:V_j}_{H_ij}=0`.                                       (7.5)

If `T_j` lies before `S_i`, the same argument uses the intervening S-block, whose S-index is `<i` and is therefore already in `H_ij`.

### 7.3 The subjective-correlation matrix is a path

Let `epsilon_ij` be `a_n` when S-block `i` and T-block `j` are adjacent in the full chronological block path, and zero otherwise. Equations (7.4)-(7.5) verify Peyre's minimal hypotheses.

Every row and every column of `epsilon` has at most two nonzero entries, each at most `a_n`. Therefore

`||epsilon||_infty <= 2 a_n`,

`||epsilon||_1 <= 2 a_n`,

and the Schur/operator-norm estimate gives

`||epsilon||_{2->2} <= sqrt(||epsilon||_1 ||epsilon||_infty) <= 2 a_n`.   (7.6)

Peyre's Theorem 3.3.1 now yields

`rho(sigma(U_1,...,U_I), sigma(V_1,...,V_J)) <= min(1,2a_n)`

and hence

`rho(sigma(X_s:s in S), sigma(X_t:t in T))`

`<= min(1,4 sqrt(2 phi(n)))`.                                (7.7)

The bound is independent of the number, sizes, or shapes of the blocks.

## Infinite interlacings

Bradley's rho-star supremum allows arbitrary separated subsets of `Z`. For any such `S,T`, the maximal correlation of the two countable vectors is the supremum over finite subvectors. Equivalently, every square-integrable function measurable with respect to the full coordinate sigma-field is approximated in `L^2` by its conditional expectations onto increasing finite-coordinate sigma-fields. Thus the uniform finite-set bound (7.7) passes to arbitrary `S,T`.

Taking the supremum in the definition of `rho_star(n)` proves (7.1).

Finally, if `phi(n)->0`, then the right side of (7.1) tends to zero. Therefore

`phi-mixing => rho-star-mixing`

for Markov chains. QED.

# 10. Stress checks, possible failure points, and what remains unverified

I explicitly stress-checked the following places because each could otherwise hide a circularity.

### 10.1 The row null set does not depend on the future event

A countable generating algebra plus monotone-class extension makes the `A_{s,t}` null set simultaneous over all Borel future events.

### 10.2 Conditioning the past cannot charge the exceptional row set

Since `P(X_s notin A_{s,t})=0`, every regular conditional law of `X_s` given the earlier history assigns zero mass to the exceptional set almost surely.

### 10.3 Nonadjacent subjective independence is compatible with Peyre's exact conditioning field

The Section 3.4(a) conditioning field contains all earlier same-color blocks, not the whole system. With chronological ordering, a nonadjacent pair always has an intervening opposite-color block whose within-color index is already smaller, hence that block is contained in the prescribed conditioning field. No illegal future revelation is inserted.

### 10.4 Additional conditioned coordinates do not break separation

Given an intervening state `X_r`, the whole pre-r and post-r paths factor. Conditioning further on coordinates belonging to one factor and coordinates belonging to the other factor preserves the product structure of the remaining pieces.

### 10.5 Adjacent blocks use only past conditioning

If two blocks are adjacent in the full order, every block with smaller within-color index lies before the earlier of the two. Therefore the conditional law keeps the original future transition kernel. This is where the deliberate chronological ordering matters.

### 10.6 Infinite sets do not create a new issue

Maximal correlation of countable random vectors is the supremum over finite subvectors by standard `L^2` approximation; Peyre also records this finite-coordinate principle. Thus the finite uniform bound is enough for Bradley's full rho-star definition.

### 10.7 External dependency is real

The proof is not self-contained from Bradley alone: it relies crucially on Peyre 2010 Theorem 3.3.1 plus Section 3.4(a). That theorem must be checked independently against the exact subjective-correlation formulation. I reopened the relevant pages and used the minimal-hypothesis statement literally, but this is still R7-01 self-review, not an independent audit.

### 10.8 Novelty is unverified
