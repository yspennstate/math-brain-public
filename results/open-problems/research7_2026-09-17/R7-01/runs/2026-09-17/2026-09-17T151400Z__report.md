# 3. B18 — say it in another language: overlap of forward and reverse transition densities

The previous report's blocker was that forward phi-mixing gives only **additive** row closeness to stationarity, while lower-psi requires a **relative lower bound**. The useful translation is to look at the forward and time-reversed rows simultaneously. Their overlap multiplies through Chapman–Kolmogorov and converts two additive bounds into a relative lower bound.

Let `(X_k)_{k in Z}` be a strictly stationary Markov chain on a standard Borel state space, with stationary law `pi` and transition kernel `P`. Let `P^<-` denote the time-reversal kernel. For integers `n,m>=1`, define

`delta_+(n) := ess sup_x ||P^n(x,.)-pi||_TV`,

`delta_-(m) := ess sup_z ||(P^<-)^m(z,.)-pi||_TV`.

Under Bradley's stationary Markov-chain reduction these are the forward phi coefficient and the time-reversed phi coefficient, respectively.

## Theorem 3.1 — forward/reverse overlap bound

For every `n,m>=1`,

`psi'(n+m) >= max(0, 1-delta_+(n)-delta_-(m)).`                     (3.1)

Consequently, if for some `n,m`

`delta_+(n)+delta_-(m) < 1`,                                        (3.2)

then the chain is rho*-mixing.

### Proof

By stationarity, for each fixed lag `r`, `P^r(x,.) << pi(.)` for `pi`-a.e. `x`. Indeed, if `pi(A)=0`, then

`0 = pi(A) = integral P^r(x,A) pi(dx)`,

so `P^r(x,A)=0` for `pi`-a.e. `x`. Choose transition densities

`k_r(x,y) := dP^r(x,.)/dpi (y)`

on a common full-measure set.

The time-reversal identity

`pi(dx) P^m(x,dy) = pi(dy) (P^<-)^m(y,dx)`

implies, for `pi x pi`-a.e. `(y,z)`,

`k_m(y,z) = k_m^<-(z,y)`,                                          (3.3)

where `k_m^<-` is the density of the reversed `m`-step kernel with respect to `pi`.

For a probability density `h` with respect to `pi`,

`||h pi-pi||_TV = 1 - integral min(h,1) dpi`.                       (3.4)

This follows because both `h pi` and `pi` have total mass one and

`(1/2) integral |h-1| dpi = integral (1-h)_+ dpi = 1-integral min(h,1)dpi`.

Now fix regular points `x,z`, and set

`a_x(y):=min(k_n(x,y),1)`,

`b_z(y):=min(k_m^<-(z,y),1)`.

Then `0<=a_x,b_z<=1` and by (3.4),

`integral a_x dpi >= 1-delta_+(n)`,

`integral b_z dpi >= 1-delta_-(m)`.                                (3.5)

Chapman–Kolmogorov and (3.3) give

`k_{n+m}(x,z)`
` = integral k_n(x,y) k_m(y,z) pi(dy)`
` = integral k_n(x,y) k_m^<-(z,y) pi(dy)`
` >= integral a_x(y)b_z(y) pi(dy)`.

For numbers `a,b in [0,1]`,

`ab-(a+b-1)=(1-a)(1-b)>=0`,

hence `ab>=a+b-1`. Therefore, using (3.5),

`k_{n+m}(x,z) >= integral a_x dpi + integral b_z dpi - 1`
`                 >= 1-delta_+(n)-delta_-(m)`.                     (3.6)

If the right side is positive, the joint law of `(X_0,X_{n+m})` has density at least that constant with respect to `pi x pi`. Equivalently,

`P(X_0 in A, X_{n+m} in B) >= c pi(A)pi(B)`

for every positive-`pi` measurable `A,B`, with

`c=1-delta_+(n)-delta_-(m)>0`.

Thus `psi'(n+m)>=c>0`. Bradley's Theorem 3.3(7) says that a stationary Markov chain with positive lower-psi coefficient at one lag is rho*-mixing. This proves (3.1) and the consequence. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — bi-phi Markov chains

If both the forward chain and its time reversal are phi-mixing, then the chain is rho*-mixing.

**Proof.** Choose `n,m` so that `delta_+(n)<1/2` and `delta_-(m)<1/2`, then apply Theorem 3.1. QED.

### Corollary 3.3 — reversible phi-mixing Markov chains

If the chain is reversible and phi-mixing, then it is rho*-mixing. Quantitatively,

`psi'(2n) >= max(0,1-2 phi(n)).`                                   (3.7)

**Proof.** Reversibility gives `delta_-(n)=delta_+(n)=phi(n)`. Apply Theorem 3.1. Since `phi(n)->0`, the right side is positive for all sufficiently large `n`. QED.

This is a genuine extension of the preceding finite-state theorem: no finite-state, atomicity, or stationary-law pointwise minorization assumption is used.

# 4. B20 — find why the algorithm fails: a maximally one-sided phi-mixing kernel

Theorem 3.1 suggests trying to finish the source by proving that forward phi-mixing forces enough reverse mixing. That is false in the strongest possible way, even for an elementary countable-state chain.

Fix `q in (0,1)` and put `p=1-q`. On `N_0={0,1,2,...}` define

`P(i,0)=p`,

`P(i,i+1)=q`,                                                       (4.1)

and no other transitions.

Interpretation: at each step, with probability `p` reset the age to zero; otherwise increase the age by one.

## Proposition 4.1 — exact stationary law and forward phi rate

The chain has stationary distribution

`pi(j)=p q^j`, `j>=0`,                                              (4.2)

and its forward phi coefficient is exactly

`phi_+(n)=q^n`.                                                     (4.3)

### Proof

For `j=0`,

`sum_i pi(i)P(i,0)=p sum_i pi(i)=p=pi(0)`.

For `j>=1`, the only predecessor is `j-1`, hence

`sum_i pi(i)P(i,j)=pi(j-1)q=pq^j=pi(j)`.

So (4.2) is stationary.

Starting from state `i`, after `n` steps either there has been at least one reset, or there has been none. If the last reset occurred exactly `j` steps before the endpoint, where `0<=j<=n-1`, the final state is `j` and that event has probability `p q^j`. If no reset occurred, which has probability `q^n`, the final state is `i+n`. Thus

`P^n(i,j)=p q^j` for `0<=j<=n-1`,

plus an additional atom `q^n` at `j=i+n`, and zero elsewhere.       (4.4)

The first `n` coordinates coincide exactly with `pi`. On the tail `{n,n+1,...}`, `P^n(i,.)` is the point mass `q^n delta_{i+n}`, while `pi` has total mass `q^n`. Therefore

`||P^n(i,.)-pi||_TV = q^n ||delta_{i+n}-pi(. | >=n)||_TV`
`                    = q^n(1-p q^i)`.                              (4.5)

Taking the supremum over `i` gives `q^n`. Under the stationary Markov reduction this is exactly `phi_+(n)`. QED.

## Proposition 4.2 — the reverse phi coefficient is maximally bad

For every finite `n>=1`,

`phi_-(n)=1`.                                                       (4.6)

### Proof

Detailed balance for time reversal gives the reverse transitions explicitly. From state `j>=1`, the previous state is deterministically `j-1`; from state zero, the previous state has law `pi`. Indeed,

`P^<-(j,j-1)=pi(j-1)q/pi(j)=1`, `j>=1`,

and

`P^<-(0,i)=pi(i)p/pi(0)=pi(i)`.

Thus if the reversed chain starts at any `j>=n`, after `n` steps it is deterministically at `j-n`. Its total variation distance from `pi` is

`1-pi(j-n)=1-p q^{j-n}`,

whose supremum over `j` is 1. Hence (4.6). QED.

## Proposition 4.3 — lower-psi is identically zero at finite lags

For every `n>=1`,

`psi'(n)=0`.                                                        (4.7)

### Proof

Every singleton has positive stationary mass. Fix a starting state `i`; by (4.4), at lag `n` the only state at or above `n` that can have positive probability is `i+n`. Choose any `j>=n` with `j != i+n`. Then

`P^n(i,{j})=0` while `pi({j})>0`.

Taking the positive-probability events `{X_0=i}` and `{X_n=j}` in the definition of `psi'` gives ratio zero. QED.

### Meaning of the example

This example realizes exactly the obstruction left by the first report:

- forward phi-mixing is exponentially fast;
- stationary-law minorization fails at every lag;
- lower-psi is zero at every finite lag;
- the reverse phi coefficient is as bad as possible at every lag.

Therefore any attempt to settle Question 5 by upgrading forward phi to lower-psi **without using interlaced structure directly** is doomed. The example is **not** claimed to be a rho*-counterexample. Its rho* behavior remains to be decided.

# 5. B30 — one direction at a time: what any counterexample must look like

The source's Theorem 4.1(3)(c) supplies a sharp dichotomy. A phi-mixing stationary chain is strongly mixing and therefore mixing in the ergodic-theoretic sense. Time reversal preserves ergodic-theoretic mixing. Apply Theorem 4.1(3)(c) to the reversed stationary sequence: either

`phi_-(n) -> 0`,

or

`phi_-(n)=1 for every n>=1`.                                       (5.1)

The first branch is impossible for a source counterexample, because Corollary 3.2 would then imply rho*-mixing. Hence:

## Theorem 5.1 — necessary structure of every D0033 counterexample

If Bradley Question 5 has a negative answer, every source-valid counterexample must satisfy

`phi_+(n)->0` but `phi_-(n)=1` for every `n>=1`.                     (5.2)

In particular, no reversible chain can be a counterexample.

### Proof

Forward phi-mixing is the source assumption. If the reversed phi coefficients tend to zero, Corollary 3.2 proves rho*-mixing, contradicting counterexample status. Bradley's stationary mixing dichotomy leaves only the second branch of (5.1). QED.

The reset-age chain (4.1) shows that (5.2) is not internally contradictory: such extreme one-sided behavior occurs in a simple exponentially phi-mixing Markov chain. Therefore the source question has now been localized to a genuinely narrower regime: **maximally one-sided Markov chains**.

There is also an important reason the reset-age model is a useful stress test rather than merely a pathology. It is the age process of i.i.d. Bernoulli resets, so each `X_t` is a causal function of an i.i.d. driving sequence with an exponentially decaying backward coding radius. That representation makes it plausible that rho* may still decay even though reverse phi and lower-psi are maximally bad. Determining its rho* coefficient is therefore the cleanest next decision problem: either it yields the desired counterexample, or it teaches which extra mechanism beyond bidirectional phi controls arbitrary interlacings.

## 7. Exact remaining gap

The source question is now reduced to:

> Can a strictly stationary Markov chain with `phi_+(n)->0` and `phi_-(n)=1` for all finite `n` fail rho*-mixing?

The simplest concrete instance is the reset-age kernel (4.1). The exact missing calculation is not ordinary two-time correlation; it is the supremum of maximal correlations between sigma-fields generated by **arbitrary interlaced finite index sets separated by distance n**.

The difficulty is that a single reset creates a directional regeneration for future blocks, but an interlaced coloring can repeatedly place variables on both sides of random reset locations. Any proof must turn the i.i.d. reset structure into a dimension-free contraction that does not degrade with the number of alternating blocks.
