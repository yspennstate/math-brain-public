# 4. Exact blocking formula

The preceding run reduced the cleanest putative counterexample to the stationary reset-age chain

`P(i,0)=p`, `P(i,i+1)=q`, `p=1-q`, `0<q<1`,

with stationary law

`pi(j)=p q^j`.

It proved:

- exact forward `phi(n)=q^n`;
- reverse `phi_-(n)=1` for every finite `n`;
- lower-psi equal to zero at every finite lag;
- exact ordinary maximal correlation `rho(X_0,X_d)=q^(d/2)`;
- exact one-hole interlaced coefficient
  `rho(X_d ; X_0,X_{2d}) = sqrt(2q^d-q^(2d))`, and the same coefficient against the whole two-sided exterior.

The source-level obstruction now begins only when the number of alternating blocks is unbounded.

Fix an integer gap `d>=1` and sample at multiples of `d`:

`Y_j := X_{jd}`,

`Q := q^d`.

The exact `d`-step transition has a disjoint-support reveal/refresh representation. Let `E_j` be Bernoulli(`Q`) and let `Z_j` have law

`nu_d(z) = p q^z/(1-Q)`, `z=0,...,d-1`,

with the pairs `(E_j,Z_j)` independent over `j` and independent of the past. Then the sampled chain can be generated by

`Y_j = Y_{j-1}+d` if `E_j=1`,

`Y_j = Z_j` if `E_j=0`.                                      (4.1)

The branch is observable from the right endpoint:

`E_j = 1_{Y_j>=d}`.                                            (4.2)

Thus a zero of `E_j` kills the previous ancestry and launches a fresh independent seed, whereas a one transmits the previous state perfectly.

For the simplest alternating singleton geometry define

`A_N = sigma(Y_0,Y_2,...,Y_{2N})`,

`B_N = sigma(Y_1,Y_3,...,Y_{2N-1})`,

and

`r_N(Q,d) := rho(A_N,B_N)`.                                    (4.3)

The previous exact three-point theorem says

`r_1(Q,d)^2 = 2Q-Q^2`.                                         (4.4)

The elementary reveal/refresh conditioning step gives the scalar recurrence pattern

`r_new^2 <= Q + (1-Q) r_old^2`.                                (4.5)

Iterating only this scalar information yields

`r_N(Q,d)^2 <= 1-(1-Q)^N`,                                     (4.6)

which becomes useless after taking the supremum over `N`:

`sup_N [1-(1-Q)^N] = 1` for every `Q>0`.                        (4.7)

This is the exact blocking formula for the current route. The local dependence is `O(sqrt(Q))`, but scalarizing after each alternation charges a fresh reveal probability every time and loses the one-dimensional renewal structure.

The decisive stress-test quantity is therefore

`R_d := sup_{N>=1} r_N(q^d,d)`.                                 (4.8)

A proof that `R_d -> 0` would close the equally spaced alternating-singleton obstruction for the reset-age chain. A lower bound `limsup_d R_d > 0` would produce the correct kind of non-decaying interlaced signal and would be a serious counterexample mechanism. Neither direction is currently proved.

I do **not** redefine the frozen source metric to `R_d`; (4.8) is only the simplest unresolved diagnostic quantity inside D0033.

# 5. What the renewal representation really says

Equation (4.1) gives more information than the failed recurrence (4.5). The iid Bernoulli edge variables `E_j` split the sampled path into renewal runs. Inside one run of consecutive `E=1` edges:

- the state is transported deterministically by adding `d` at each step;
- the residue modulo `d` is conserved;
- at the first `E=0` boundary, the old ancestry is destroyed and a fresh seed `Z` is drawn independently.

Equivalently, every selected coordinate belongs to a random ancestry interval. Distinct ancestry intervals are independent once their renewal boundaries and fresh seeds are treated correctly.

This is precisely the structure that (4.5) throws away: it treats every possible reveal as an independent new charge against the correlation budget even when several reveals belong to the same single ancestry interval.

The hoped-for dimension-free argument therefore has to tensorize over **renewal intervals**, not over observed coordinates.

There are two dangerous shortcuts which the next proof must not take.

### 5.1 Revealing the whole renewal partition to both colors is not a valid upper-bound trick

The variables `E_j` are not a neutral deterministic partition; each is observable at its right endpoint and therefore belongs naturally to only one side of an alternating coloring. If one conditions on, or adjoins, the entire `E`-configuration to both sigma-fields, one gives the two sides extra common randomness. Maximal correlation is monotone under enlarging sigma-fields, so this can make the upper bound strictly worse and in the extreme can create correlation one for reasons unrelated to the original problem.

Thus the random partition cannot simply be made common and then passed to a product theorem.

### 5.2 Csaki-Fischer tensorization cannot be applied before the random partition is separated measurably

Conditioned on a fixed renewal partition, different renewal intervals are independent, and maximal correlation of a product of genuinely independent pairs tensorizes by the maximum component correlation. That is exactly the behavior wanted.

But the interval decomposition is itself random and is only partially visible from each color class. The missing step is a **one-sided measurable disintegration** that preserves which side owns each boundary flag while still exposing enough independence to apply tensorization.

This is the sharp mathematical distinction between the reset-age chain and the absorbing pure-death model.

### B2 — attack the negation

Tried the obvious infinite-state counterexample family with rare exact memory and stationary refresh. It fails because any decomposition

Localized any counterexample to kernels whose transition densities have arbitrarily deep relative holes on stationary sets whose masses shrink at the phi scale.

**Failure mode:** phi controls additive probability error, while the sufficient lower-psi mechanism needs a relative lower bound. Semigroup structure alone did not bridge that gap.

# 7. Missing ingredient

The missing result can now be stated without vague language.

> **Regenerative alternating-block tensorization problem.** For the reveal/refresh path (4.1), control maximal correlation between the two colors of an arbitrary alternating finite path by a function `R(Q)` independent of the number of color blocks, with `R(Q)->0` as `Q->0`, while respecting the one-sided measurability of the renewal flags.

What is missing is not another estimate on a single transition. Every one-step and one-hole quantity is already exactly known. What is missing is a decomposition of the `L^2` spaces of the two color sigma-fields into pieces indexed by ancestry intervals in which:

1. different ancestry intervals contribute orthogonally or tensorize by a maximum rather than a sum;
2. renewal boundaries are not illegally revealed to both sides;
3. regeneration after a boundary is treated as a new independent component rather than as a new additive chance of failure.

In operator language, the missing theorem should replace the scalar recursion (4.5) by an operator-valued recursion whose norm is stable under concatenating arbitrarily many alternating blocks.

That is the exact ingredient absent from all nine routes so far.

# 8. Simplest unresolved case

The simplest honest unresolved case is **not** another single trapped coordinate. That case is solved exactly.

It is the finite, equally spaced, alternating singleton lattice

`0, d, 2d, ..., 2Nd`,

colored even versus odd, with `N` unbounded.

The question is whether

`sup_N rho( sigma(X_0,X_{2d},...,X_{2Nd}),
            sigma(X_d,X_{3d},...,X_{(2N-1)d}) ) -> 0`             (8.1)

as `d->infinity`.

This is the first geometry where the alternation count can diverge while every cross-color gap remains `d`. It already contains the complete scalar accumulation obstruction (4.6), but removes irregular spacings, mixed block sizes, and arbitrary source-kernel complications.

If (8.1) fails for the reset-age chain, the failure supplies exactly the sort of coherent many-block signal needed for a negative answer to Bradley Question 5. If (8.1) holds, it identifies the mechanism needed before returning to arbitrary interlacings.

No claim is made here that equally spaced alternation is automatically extremal for all interlacings; proving such an extremality statement would itself require a new argument.

# 9. Nearest solved analogue

The nearest solved analogue is Bradley's 2015 INAR/pure-death survival component.

For a pure-death thread

`zeta_k = zeta_0 prod_{i=1}^k eta_i`,

with iid Bernoulli survivals, the zero state is absorbing. Bradley groups arbitrary interlaced index sets into alternating blocks, encodes each block by a variable with a distinguished zero value, and uses the fact that once a thread is zero it remains zero. That monotone absorption prevents every later block from launching a fresh copy of the old dependence. Independent component structure then permits block-count-independent maximal-correlation control.

The reset-age chain has the same death probability across a gap but a different post-death rule:

`old ancestry dies -> fresh independent ancestry starts immediately`.

That single replacement is enough to invalidate the absorbing-state step. It is therefore the nearest solved analogue in which the exact missing hypothesis is visible, rather than merely a vaguely related rho*-mixing theorem.

## B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED

Inside every renewal run of (4.1), the ancestry seed and the residue `Y_j mod d` are conserved while the block age increases deterministically. Build an orthogonal decomposition of centered observables according to the renewal interval carrying their ancestry. The target is to make independent intervals tensorize by a maximum norm rather than accumulate through (4.5).

Concrete question: can the conditional-expectation operator from odd to even coordinates be decomposed into a direct sum or martingale-difference series indexed by the *leftmost live ancestry interval*, with orthogonal fresh-seed increments?

## B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY ON PURPOSE

Anchor the finite alternating path immediately after a forced refresh edge (`E_0=0`) instead of working stationary/two-sided from the start. This removes the random ancestral phase at the left boundary and turns the interval decomposition into a forward renewal construction. Derive a finite-`N` norm recursion there, then examine whether the boundary effect vanishes uniformly as the anchor is moved away.

The point is not to assume a common renewal partition; it is to make one boundary deterministic and leave all later flags with their actual one-sided ownership.

## B27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS

Do not try to prove `rho*(d)->0` over all interlacings and all `d` simultaneously. For fixed `d` (equivalently fixed `Q=q^d`), first determine or bound

`R_d = sup_N r_N(Q,d)`

uniformly over path length `N`; only after that take `d->infinity`.

The previous failure came from taking the local small-`Q` estimate before the supremum in `N`. Reversing those quantifiers forces the proof to solve the exact uniformity problem instead of hiding it in an iteration.
