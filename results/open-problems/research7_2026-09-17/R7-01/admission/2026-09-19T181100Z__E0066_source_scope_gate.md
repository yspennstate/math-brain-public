## 3. Mathematical reconstruction of the general orbit structure

The majorization hypothesis itself gives a stronger structural statement than the old entropy-only attempt. This derivation is included to determine what mathematical content of the broad prompt is already controlled; it is **not claimed novel**.

### Proposition

Let `V:S^{m-1}->S^{m-1}` be continuous and satisfy `V(x) prec x` for every `x`. For every initial state `x_0`, there exist a vector `y` and a periodic orbit

\[
z_0,z_1,\dots,z_{p-1},\qquad V(z_r)=z_{r+1\pmod p},
\]

such that every `z_r` is a coordinate permutation of `y`, and, after a phase shift,

\[
x_{kp+r}\longrightarrow z_r\qquad (k\to\infty)
\]

for each `r`. In particular

\[
p\le \frac{m!}{\prod_a m_a!},
\]

where the `m_a` are the multiplicities of the distinct coordinate values of `y`.

### Proof

For `1<=k<=m`, define

\[
S_k(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k x_{[i]},\qquad S_0(x)=0.
\]

Since `x_{n+1}=V(x_n) prec x_n`, each sequence `S_k(x_n)` is nonincreasing and bounded below, hence has a limit `s_k`. Therefore the decreasing rearrangements converge coordinatewise:

\[
(x_n)_{[k]}=S_k(x_n)-S_{k-1}(x_n)\to y_k:=s_k-s_{k-1}.
\]

So every accumulation point of the original unsorted trajectory is a coordinate permutation of the same vector `y`. The omega-limit set `Omega(x_0)` is nonempty by compactness and is contained in the finite set of distinct coordinate permutations of `y`; hence `Omega(x_0)` is finite.

Continuity gives `V(Omega) subseteq Omega`. It is also onto `Omega`: if `z` is a limit of `x_{n_j}`, take a convergent subsequence of the predecessors `x_{n_j-1}->w`; then `w in Omega` and continuity gives `V(w)=z`. Thus `V` permutes the finite set `Omega`.

It remains to rule out several disjoint cycles in `Omega`. Let the distinct points of `Omega` have minimum pairwise separation `3 delta>0`. Uniform continuity gives `epsilon<=delta` such that `d(u,v)<epsilon` implies `d(Vu,Vv)<delta`. For all sufficiently large `n`, `x_n` lies within `epsilon` of `Omega`; the nearest omega-point is then unique. If `x_n` is near `z`, `x_{n+1}` must be within `delta` of `V(z)` and also within `epsilon` of its unique nearest omega-point, so that nearest point is exactly `V(z)`. Hence, from some time onward, the index of the nearest omega-point follows one cycle of the finite permutation `V|_Omega`. Since every point of `Omega` must be visited infinitely often in the limiting sense, `Omega` is exactly that one cycle. Each phase subsequence has a unique accumulation point and therefore converges to it. This proves the proposition.

This argument uses only continuity plus `V(x) prec x`; quadraticity is not needed.

## 4. Dated later-literature check

The following bounded checks were performed on **2026-09-19**.

1. **Abdulghafor, Shahidi, Zeki, Turaev (2016), “Dynamics of doubly stochastic quadratic operators on a finite-dimensional simplex,” Open Mathematics 14, 509–519, DOI 10.1515/math-2016-0045.** The paper explicitly treats the dynamics of the same majorization-based doubly stochastic QSO class. It proves convergence when there are no periodic points, proves that a non-permutation operator on the two-dimensional simplex has no interior periodic points, shows the analogous statement fails in higher dimension, and classifies the extreme-point dynamics in dimension two.

2. **Mirmukhsin Makhmudov (2021), “Description of the Set of Strictly Regular Quadratic Bistochastic Operators and Examples,” Discontinuity, Nonlinearity, and Complexity 10(3), 425–433, DOI 10.5890/DNC.2021.09.006; arXiv:2001.10444.** The abstract states that almost all QBOs are strictly regular, describes the strictly regular part of the QBO polytope, proves density, and gives nontrivial examples.

3. A bounded 2024–2026 search also found later papers on **b-bistochastic** or special low-dimensional subclasses, including a 2026 one-dimensional b-bistochastic dynamics paper. These are related terminology/subclass results, not a source-wide classification of all finite-dimensional QBOs.
