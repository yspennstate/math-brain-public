# P0429: a cubic stationary collision threshold

## Statement proved in this artifact

Consider the Wang–Harchol-Balter–Jiang–Scheller-Wolf–Srikant limited fork–join model. There are `n` FCFS unit-speed queues. Jobs form a Poisson process of rate

\[
\Lambda_n=\frac{n\lambda}{k},
\]

where each job chooses a uniform `k`-subset of the `n` servers and sends one task to each chosen server. Task sizes are iid with law `G`, mean `m_1`, finite second moment `m_2`, and `\rho=\lambda m_1<1`. Fix any tagged set `K` of exactly `k` queues. Let `P_{n,k}` be their stationary workload law, and let `\pi_\lambda` be the stationary workload law of one M/G/1 queue with task rate `\lambda` and service law `G`.

### Theorem A — full finite-second-moment source class

Define

\[
H_G:=\frac{m_1}{1-\rho}+\frac{\lambda m_2}{2(1-\rho)^2}.
\]

For all sufficiently large `n` in the regime relevant below,

\[
\boxed{
 d_{\rm TV}\!\left(P_{n,k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k}\right)
 \le 2\lambda H_G\,k\left[1-\frac{\binom{n-k}{k-1}}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\right]
 \le 2\lambda H_G\frac{k(k-1)^2}{n-1}.
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{k^3/n\to0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{\rm TV}(P_{n,k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k})\to0.}
\]

Thus under the **full source assumptions**, `k=o(n^(1/3))` suffices. This improves the source theorem's `o(n^(1/4))` exponent without assuming exponential service.

### Theorem B — deterministic-service obstruction

Take the allowed service law `G=delta_1` and any fixed `0<lambda<1/2`. If

\[
d_{\rm TV}(P_{n,k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k})\to0,
\]

then necessarily

\[
\boxed{k^3/n\to0.}
\]

In particular, no power exponent strictly larger than `1/3` can be guaranteed uniformly over the full finite-second-moment source class. The frozen exponent metric therefore moves from `1/4` to the sharp universal value `1/3`.

This does **not** yet prove that every fixed service law has the same threshold. In fact, the Wang et al. simulations suggest substantially more independence for exponential service. The source-wide, service-law-dependent classification remains open; the honest status is PARTIAL.

## Proof of Theorem A

### 1. Split the parent Poisson process by its intersection with the tagged set

For a job with selected server set `S`, write `A=S cap K`. Independent marking of the parent Poisson process by `A` splits it into independent Poisson processes indexed by subsets `A subseteq K`.

For a fixed tagged queue `i`, a task arrival to `i` has total rate `lambda`. Conditional on `i in S`, the other `k-1` chosen servers form a uniform `(k-1)`-subset of the remaining `n-1` servers. Therefore the probability that **none** of the other `k-1` tagged queues is selected is

\[
a_{n,k}:=\frac{\binom{n-k}{k-1}}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}.
\]

Hence jobs whose tagged intersection is exactly `{i}` form a Poisson stream of rate

\[
\lambda_e=\lambda a_{n,k},
\]

and all arrivals to `i` belonging to jobs that hit at least one other tagged queue form an independent Poisson substream of rate

\[
\delta_n:=\lambda-\lambda_e=\lambda(1-a_{n,k}).
\]

The singleton streams `{A={i}}`, one for each `i in K`, are mutually independent because they are disjoint thinnings of the parent Poisson process.

A union bound, conditional on `i in S`, gives

\[
1-a_{n,k}
=P(S\cap(K\setminus\{i\})\ne\varnothing\mid i\in S)
\le (k-1)\frac{k-1}{n-1}
=\frac{(k-1)^2}{n-1}.
\]

### 2. Stationary influence interval of one removed arrival

Consider one stationary M/G/1 queue at task rate `lambda`. Mark an independent Poisson substream of its arrivals at rate `delta`; later we will take `delta=delta_n`. Couple the full queue `W` and the queue `W^0` obtained by deleting every marked arrival, using the same unmarked arrivals and service marks on the whole two-sided stationary input. Monotonicity gives `W^0(t)<=W(t)`.

For every marked arrival at time `T_j`, let

\[
E_j:=\inf\{t\ge T_j:W(t)=0\}
\]

be the next empty epoch of the **full** queue. If `W(0) != W^0(0)`, then at least one marked arrival since the last full empty epoch is still relevant, hence

\[
\{W(0)\ne W^0(0)\}
\subseteq
\bigcup_j\{T_j\le0<E_j\}.
\]

Therefore, by Markov's inequality for the number of covering influence intervals and Campbell's formula,

\[
P(W(0)\ne W^0(0))
\le E\sum_j 1_{\{T_j\le0<E_j\}}
=\delta\,E^0(E_0-T_0),
\]

where `E^0` is Palm expectation for the marked Poisson substream.

By PASTA, immediately before a marked arrival the workload has the ordinary stationary M/G/1 law. If its value is `W` and the marked task has service requirement `X`, the post-arrival amount of work is `W+X`. Starting with deterministic workload `y`, the expected time to empty in a stable M/G/1 queue is

\[
E\tau_y=\frac{y}{1-\rho}.
\]

Indeed, until the emptying time the server works continuously at speed one, while arriving work has mean rate `rho`; taking expectations in the workload balance (or equivalently using the standard busy-period decomposition) gives `E tau_y = y + rho E tau_y`.

The Pollaczek–Khinchine mean stationary workload is

\[
EW=\frac{\lambda m_2}{2(1-\rho)}.
\]

Consequently

\[
E^0(E_0-T_0)
=\frac{E(W+X)}{1-\rho}
=\frac{m_1}{1-\rho}+\frac{\lambda m_2}{2(1-\rho)^2}
=:H_G<\infty.
\]

Thus the stationary perturbation estimate is

\[
\boxed{P(W(0)\ne W^0(0))\le\delta H_G.}
\]

The key difference from the earlier deterministic-horizon proof is that each discrepancy is charged only for its **actual stationary influence lifetime**. There is no union bound over the maximum of `k` queue lookback times.

### 3. Original tagged vector versus the singleton-only vector

Delete, from each tagged queue, every arrival whose parent job hits at least two tagged queues. What remains is exactly the collection of `k` mutually independent singleton streams of rate `lambda_e`, so the resulting stationary vector has law

\[
\pi_{\lambda_e}^{\otimes k}.
\]

The collision incidence stream at each coordinate has rate `delta_n`. Applying the influence estimate coordinatewise and then a union bound over the `k` coordinates gives a coupling with

\[
P(\mathbf W\ne\mathbf W^0)\le k\delta_n H_G.
\]

Hence

\[
d_{TV}(P_{n,k},\pi_{\lambda_e}^{\otimes k})\le k\delta_n H_G.
\]

### 4. Restore the missing marginal rate independently

Starting from `k` independent M/G/1 queues of rate `lambda_e`, independently superpose on each queue a Poisson stream of rate `delta_n` with iid service law `G`. The resulting queues are independent M/G/1 queues of rate `lambda`, hence have product law `pi_lambda^(otimes k)`.

Applying the same influence-interval estimate coordinatewise gives

\[
d_{TV}(\pi_{\lambda_e}^{\otimes k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k})
\le k\delta_n H_G.
\]

The triangle inequality now yields

\[
d_{TV}(P_{n,k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k})
\le2k\delta_nH_G
=2\lambda H_G k(1-a_{n,k}).
\]

Using `1-a_{n,k}<=(k-1)^2/(n-1)` proves Theorem A.

## Proof of Theorem B

Set every task service time identically equal to one and fix `0<lambda<1/2`. Each marginal queue is M/D/1 with load `rho=lambda`.

### 1. Multi-hit parent jobs

Let

\[
N=|S\cap K|
\]

for one parent job. Then `N` has the hypergeometric law obtained by intersecting two `k`-subsets of an `n`-set. Let

\[
p_n=P(N\ge2),\qquad
R_n=\Lambda_n p_n=\frac{n\lambda}{k}p_n.
\]

By Poisson thinning, jobs hitting at least two tagged queues form a Poisson process of rate `R_n`. The per-coordinate singleton stream still has rate `lambda_e=lambda a_{n,k}` and is independent of the multi-hit stream.

### 2. A singular event forced by the most recent multi-hit job

Let `U` be the backward time from zero to the most recent multi-hit parent job. For `R_n>0`,

\[
P(U\in du)=R_n e^{-R_nu}\,du.
\]

At that job choose, using an auxiliary independent randomization, two of its tagged destination queues uniformly from the hit set. By exchangeability this produces a uniform unordered pair of tagged queues, independent of the pre-arrival state.

At a Poisson arrival epoch each selected marginal queue is busy with probability `lambda`. Therefore, without assuming independence between the two queues,

\[
P(\text{both selected queues idle immediately before the job})
\ge1-2\lambda>0.
\]

By definition of `U`, there is no later multi-hit job. The singleton streams into the selected pair are independent Poisson processes of rate `lambda_e`, so conditional on `U=u` the probability of no later singleton arrival to either queue is `exp(-2 lambda_e u)`.

If both queues were idle at the multi-hit arrival, then both receive deterministic service requirement one. If also there is no later arrival to either and `u<1`, their workloads at time zero are both exactly `1-u>0`.

Thus, for every fixed `0<epsilon<1`, if

\[
E_{n,\epsilon}:=\{\exists i<j:\ 0<W_i(0)=W_j(0)<1\},
\]

then

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{n,k}(E_{n,\epsilon})
&\ge (1-2\lambda)\int_0^\epsilon
R_n e^{-(R_n+2\lambda_e)u}\,du\\
&=(1-2\lambda)\frac{R_n}{R_n+2\lambda_e}
\left(1-e^{-(R_n+2\lambda_e)\epsilon}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

(The event name retains `epsilon` only to remember the construction window; the displayed equality event itself is enough for the TV separation.)

### 3. The independent product gives zero mass to that event

For an M/D/1 queue the Pollaczek–Khinchine geometric-sum representation writes the stationary workload as a geometric sum of equilibrium service times. For deterministic unit service the equilibrium service time is Uniform(0,1). Hence the workload has one atom at zero and an atomless distribution on `(0,infinity)`.

Therefore, under `pi_lambda^(otimes k)`, any two distinct coordinates have probability zero of being equal at a positive value. Since `k` is finite for each `n`,

\[
\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k}(E_{n,\epsilon})=0.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
d_{TV}(P_{n,k},\pi_\lambda^{\otimes k})
\ge(1-2\lambda)\frac{R_n}{R_n+2\lambda_e}
\left(1-e^{-(R_n+2\lambda_e)\epsilon}\right).
}
\]

If `R_n` fails to converge to zero, the right-hand side is bounded away from zero along a subsequence because `0<=lambda_e<=lambda`.

### 4. `R_n -> 0` forces the cubic condition

Put `Y=(N)_2=N(N-1)`. Hypergeometric factorial moments give, for `r=2,3,4`,

\[
E[(N)_r]=\frac{(k)_r^2}{(n)_r}.
\]

Also

\[
Y^2=(N)_4+4(N)_3+2(N)_2.
\]

For `n>=6`, writing `a=k^2/n`,

\[
\frac{E(N)_3}{E(N)_2}=\frac{(k-2)^2}{n-2}\le2a,
\]

and

\[
\frac{E(N)_4}{E(N)_2}
=\frac{[(k-2)(k-3)]^2}{(n-2)(n-3)}\le4a^2.
\]

Hence

\[
EY^2\le8(1+a)^2 EY.
\]

Moreover `(k-1)>=k/2` for `k>=2`, so

\[
EY=\frac{[k(k-1)]^2}{n(n-1)}\ge\frac{a^2}{4}.
\]

Paley–Zygmund at level zero now gives

\[
p_n=P(Y>0)\ge\frac{(EY)^2}{EY^2}
\ge\frac{a^2}{32(1+a)^2}
\ge\frac1{128}\min(a^2,1).
\]

Therefore

\[
R_n=\frac{n\lambda}{k}p_n
\ge\frac{\lambda}{128}\frac nk
\min\left(\frac{k^4}{n^2},1\right).
\]

If `k^2/n<=1`, this says

\[
R_n\ge\frac\lambda{128}\frac{k^3}{n}.
\]

If `k^2/n>=1`, then

\[
R_n\ge\frac\lambda{128}\frac nk\ge\frac\lambda{128}.
\]

Thus `R_n -> 0` is possible only if `k^3/n -> 0`. Since TV convergence would force `R_n -> 0` by the singular-event lower bound, Theorem B follows.

`M_alpha = largest alpha for which the full finite-second-moment source assumptions imply independence for every k=o(n^alpha)`.

\boxed{M_\alpha=1/3.}
