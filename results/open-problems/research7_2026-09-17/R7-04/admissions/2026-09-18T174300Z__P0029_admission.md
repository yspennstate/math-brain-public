**Title:** *My favorite open problems*.  
**Year/version:** undated living author webpage; current official version checked 2026-09-18. The repository source manifest records that the page includes a 2024 update elsewhere, but that does not provide a publication year for Problem 9.  
**Primary URL:** https://sites.math.washington.edu/~burdzy/open_mathjax.php  
**Exact locator:** Problem 9, heading “Do peaks of random labelings repell each other?”

The source defines a labeling of a graph with `N` vertices as a bijection to `{1,...,N}` and a peak as a vertex whose label is larger than every adjacent label. For a uniformly random labeling of the `n` by `n` discrete square, conditioned on having **exactly two peaks**, let `rho_n` be their graph distance. The exact source question is:

> Does `rho_n / n` converge in distribution to `0` as `n -> infinity`?

The conditioning, square geometry, exactly-two-peak quantifier, graph distance, and normalization by the side length `n` are all part of the source scope. A torus, ladder, tree, unconditioned labeling, or fixed finite `n` is not the source problem.

## Exact source closure criterion

A source-level **YES** requires a proof under the exact conditioned square-grid law that

`rho_n / n -> 0` in distribution.

Because `0 <= rho_n/n <= 2`, this is equivalent to convergence in probability to `0`, and also equivalent to

`E[rho_n/n | exactly two peaks] -> 0`.

A source-level **NO** requires a rigorous refutation of that convergence, for example constants `c, eps > 0` and infinitely many `n` such that

`P(rho_n >= c n | exactly two peaks) >= eps`,

or any other theorem implying a positive limsup away from zero. Fixed finite-grid data, simulations, or an unproved asymptotic heuristic do not close the source.
