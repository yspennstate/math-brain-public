Author: **Rick Durrett**.

Title: **Coexistence in stochastic spatial models**.

Publication: *The Annals of Applied Probability* **19** (2009), no. 2, 477–496; DOI `10.1214/08-AAP590`.

Public preprint: arXiv `0906.2293`.

Exact source locator: **Problem 4**, in Example 2.1 / the sexual reproduction discussion, PDF p. 11 in the source layout used by the intake.

Exact source target:

> Consider the sexual reproduction model with beta > 4.5. Show that when the stirring rate epsilon^{-2} is large there is a unique nontrivial stationary distribution.

The immediately preceding source definition is the binary sexual-reproduction process with death `1 -> 0` at rate 1 and, at a vacant site having `k` occupied sites among its `n` neighbours, birth rate

`beta * k(k-1)/(n(n-1))`,

with nearest-neighbour exchanges at rate `epsilon^{-2}` in the fast-stirring version. Equivalently, a vacant site samples two distinct neighbours and becomes occupied when both are occupied. The source's mean-field reaction is

`f(u) = -u + beta u^2(1-u)`.

For `beta>4.5`, the source's travelling-wave argument lies on the survival side of the fast-stirring threshold and Durrett–Neuhauser's theorem gives `beta_c(epsilon) -> 4.5` as `epsilon -> 0`; Problem 4 asks for uniqueness, not merely existence/survival.

## Exact closure criterion

For each fixed `beta>4.5`, prove that there exists `epsilon_0(beta)>0` such that for every `0<epsilon<epsilon_0(beta)` the fast-stirred sexual-reproduction process has exactly one invariant probability law other than the absorbing all-zero law.

A proof only of existence, survival, convergence of densities as `epsilon->0`, uniqueness for a modified model, or uniqueness only after adding an extra hypothesis is PARTIAL and does not close E0010.

## Fixed source-matching mathematical metric

Let `I^nt_{beta,epsilon}` be the set of nontrivial stationary laws. Once attractiveness is established, let `bar_nu_{beta,epsilon}` denote the upper invariant law and define

`Q_SR(beta,epsilon) = sup_{mu in I^nt_{beta,epsilon}} [ bar_nu_{beta,epsilon}(eta(0)) - mu(eta(0)) ]`.

For any stationary `mu`, attractiveness gives `mu <=_st bar_nu`. If `Q_SR(beta,epsilon)=0`, then every nontrivial stationary law has the same one-site marginal as `bar_nu`; in a monotone coupling, the nonnegative sitewise disagreement has mean zero at every site, hence vanishes a.s. on the countable lattice, so `mu=bar_nu`. Conversely uniqueness implies `Q_SR=0`. Thus source closure is equivalent to showing `Q_SR(beta,epsilon)=0` for all sufficiently small epsilon.

## Prior repository work and correction

Useful retained point: density control alone does not establish uniqueness; a coupling/complete-convergence mechanism is needed.

Important correction discovered during admission: E0010-A001 says the sexual-reproduction model is “not attractive”. For the source flip rates this is false. In a Harris graphical construction:

- a death mark sets one coordinate to 0;
- a birth mark associated to a site and an ordered pair of distinct neighbours applies the Boolean map `eta(x) <- eta(x) OR (eta(y) eta(z))`;
- a stirring mark swaps the same two coordinates in both coupled configurations.

Every one of these maps preserves coordinatewise order. Hence the fast-stirred process is attractive. This materially reopens a monotone-coupling route that the prior attempt incorrectly discarded.
