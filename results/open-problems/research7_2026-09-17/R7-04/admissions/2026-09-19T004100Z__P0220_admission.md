## Exact outside source

**David Aldous**, *A one-dimensional drift-jump particle process*, problem page posted February 2018:

`https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/left_Hamm.html`

The source fixes, consistently in every finite `k`, particles

`0 < X_1(t) < ... < X_k(t)`

with two dynamics:

1. a unit-rate space-time Poisson process on `(0,∞) x (0,∞)`; at an event `(x_0,t_0)`, the closest particle to the right of `x_0`, if one exists, jumps to `x_0`;
2. between events every particle obeys `dX_i(t)/dt = X_i(t)`.

The source states that the finite-k laws are consistent, that the process has a unique stationary distribution by soft arguments, and asks:

> **Problem. Give some (fairly explicit) description of the stationary distribution.**

Historical source locator: David Aldous and Persi Diaconis, unfinished 1993 notes *Patience Sorting, Longest Increasing Subsequences*, Section 4.1 (printed pp. 36–37 in the currently hosted PDF) and Section 4.2 for `k=2`:

`https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/patience.pdf`

The notes establish, for fixed `k`, the patience-pile top labels `B(n,i)` and their stabilized limit `B(i)`, and equation (42)

`(X_1(∞),...,X_k(∞)) =_d (Gamma(B(1)),...,Gamma(B(k)))`,

where `Gamma(j)` is the j-th point of an independent rate-one Poisson process. They then derive a balance equation and an explicit scalar-series density for `k=2`, while remarking that useful information is hard to extract in general.
