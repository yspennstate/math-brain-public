## 6. B6 — a full reversible-neighborhood exclusion theorem

The first new result is stronger than the usual reversible case.

### Theorem

Let `Q0` be any finite irreducible reversible CTMC generator. There is a neighborhood `U` of `Q0` in finite generator space such that for every irreducible `Q in U`, every `c>0`, and every nonzero centered real `g`,

`<g,H_Q(c)g>_piQ < 0`.

Hence no second-order P0290 counterexample can approach a fixed reversible doubly monotone generator through arbitrarily small nonreversible perturbations.

### Proof architecture

The proof, written in full in the artifact, has three scale regimes.

**Small scale.**

`H_Q(0)=Sym_pi(Q)/6`.

For every stationary generator,

`<h,Qh>_pi = -1/2 sum_{i,j} pi_i q_ij (h_j-h_i)^2`.

At an irreducible generator this is strictly negative on the centered unit sphere. Finite-dimensional compactness and continuity of the stationary law make the negativity uniform for nearby `Q` and all sufficiently small `c`.

**Intermediate compact scale.**

At reversible `Q0`, the centered eigenvalues are `-alpha_j`, `alpha_j>0`. Put

`phi(x)=(e^{-x}-1+x)/x^2`.

Then the derivative eigenvalue is

`-alpha_j g0(alpha_j c)`,

where

`g0(x)=[x-2+(x+2)e^{-x}]/x^3 > 0`.

Strict positivity follows because the numerator has derivative `1-(x+1)e^{-x}>0`. On any compact `c` interval the largest derivative eigenvalue is therefore bounded away from zero on the negative side. Continuous conjugation by `D_pi^{1/2}` and projection onto the centered subspace preserve this gap for nearby nonreversible generators.

**Large scale.**

On the centered subspace,

`Q Psi(cQ) = c^{-2} Q^{-1} + 2 c^{-3} Q^{-2} + exponentially small`.

The leading inverse form is strictly negative: if `h=Q^{-1}g`,

`<g,Q^{-1}g>_pi = <Qh,h>_pi < 0`.

Near `Q0` the inverse-form gap and the spectral gap stay uniformly positive in magnitude, so the `c^{-3}` and exponential terms are dominated for all sufficiently large `c`. This completes the global-in-`c` neighborhood theorem.

This rules out an entire qualitative counterexample strategy: tiny circulation perturbations of reversible/birth-death models cannot work at second order.

## 7. B14 — reflection symmetry splits the exact four-state family

For the recovered four-state family, let `R` reverse the state order. The exact identity

`Q_a^T = R Q_a R`

For the entire function `F_c(Q)=Q Psi(cQ)`,

`F_c(Q_a)^T = R F_c(Q_a) R`.

Therefore

`H_Qa(c)=1/2(F_c+R F_c R)`

In the orthonormal basis

`e=(1,1,1,1)/2`,
`u=(1,-1,-1,1)/2`,
`v=(1,1,-1,-1)/2`,
`w=(1,-1,1,-1)/2`,

`e,u` are reflection-even, `v,w` are reflection-odd, and `e` is the constant kernel vector. Thus the centered derivative operator splits exactly as

`[ h_+(a,c) ] direct-sum H_-(a,c)`,

Consequently the **all-observable** derivative is nonpositive on this entire family if and only if the three scalar conditions
