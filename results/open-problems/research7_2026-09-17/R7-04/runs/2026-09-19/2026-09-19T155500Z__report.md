## 3. B21 — current monotone-spectrum literature changes the search geometry

A fresh literature search found a directly relevant 2026 result not used in the earlier P0290 reports:

Primary pages:

- `https://journals.uwyo.edu/index.php/ela/article/view/10159`
- `https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01314`

Their Theorem 5.2 proves that every eigenvalue of an `n x n` monotone stochastic matrix, `n>=4`, belongs to the Karpelevich eigenvalue region `Theta_{n-1}` of `(n-1) x (n-1)` stochastic matrices. The paper also records that `3 x 3` monotone stochastic matrices have real spectrum.

This produces a source-relevant continuous-time corollary. If `Q` is an `n`-state stochastically monotone generator and `lambda=-alpha+i beta` is a nonzero eigenvalue, then

\[
\boxed{
|\beta|\le \cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{n-1}\right)\alpha.
}
\]

Proof: for every `t>0`, `P_t=e^{tQ}` is monotone stochastic, so `e^{t lambda}` lies in `Theta_{n-1}`. Choose an `(n-1)`-state stochastic matrix `S_t` having that eigenvalue. Then `S_t-I` is an `(n-1)`-state Markov generator with eigenvalue `e^{t lambda}-1`. The classical Dmitriev--Dynkin/Karpelevich generator-sector theorem gives the `(n-1)`-state cotangent bound; divide by `t` and let `t downarrow 0`.

Because source admissibility includes forward monotonicity, every P0290 hidden chain obeys this restriction. Double monotonicity may make the true sector smaller.
