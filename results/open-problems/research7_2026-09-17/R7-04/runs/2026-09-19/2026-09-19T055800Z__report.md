## 4. B3 — prove something stronger: direct source-native invariance

### 4.1 Exact finite-k generator

For

\[
D_k=\{0<x_1<\cdots<x_k<\infty\},\qquad x_0=0,
\]

the stated dynamics give, for smooth compactly supported `f`,

\[
\boxed{
(L_k f)(x)
=
\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\partial_i f(x)
+
\sum_{i=1}^k\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}
\left[f(x^{i\leftarrow y})-f(x)\right]dy.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The jump intervals have total length `x_k`, so the jump loss is exactly `-x_k f`.

### 4.2 Correct adjoint and a defect in the historical equation (43)

The drift divergence is

\[
-\sum_{i=1}^k\partial_i(x_i p)
=-k p-\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\partial_i p.
\]

A jump that lands at target coordinate `x_i` can come from any pre-jump coordinate `z` in `(x_i,x_{i+1})`, where `x_{k+1}=infinity`. Therefore a stationary smooth density satisfies

\[
\boxed{
x_kp
=-k p-\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\partial_i p
+
\sum_{i=1}^k\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}
 p(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1},z,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_k)dz.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The unfinished notes print `-p_k` in equation (43), not `-k p_k`. Thus the printed equation is missing `(k-1)p_k` for every `k>=2`. The issue is exactly the divergence of the `k`-dimensional vector field `(x_1,...,x_k)`: its divergence is `k`, not `1`.

This is not a numerical objection. If a positive density satisfied both the actual-process equation (4.2) and the printed equation, subtraction would give `(k-1)p_k=0`. In particular the source's own positive `k=2` density cannot obey both.

### 4.3 Independent invariance of the patience-sorting limit law

Let

\[
\mu_k=\mathcal L(\Gamma(B(1)),\ldots,\Gamma(B(k))).
\]

The source finite-time representation is

\[
(X_1(t),\ldots,X_k(t))
\overset d=
(\Gamma(B(M_{e^t},1)),\ldots,\Gamma(B(M_{e^t},k))).

Because `M_{e^t}->infinity` in probability and the first `k` stabilized pile-top labels converge as in source equation (41), the finite-time laws `mu_{k,t}` converge weakly to `mu_k`.

The finite-`k` semigroup `P_s` is Feller. For initial states converging in `D_k`, all particles up to a fixed time `s` stay below one deterministic bound because jumps are leftward and the drift multiplies by at most `e^s`. Only finitely many Poisson points in the resulting bounded space-time rectangle can matter. Under the common-point coupling, almost surely no event falls exactly on a just-before-event particle coordinate; therefore the strict interval decisions stabilize for all sufficiently nearby initial states, and induction over the finite event list gives pathwise continuity at time `s`.

The source construction is the same time-homogeneous process, so

\[
\mu_{k,t+s}=\mu_{k,t}P_s.
\]

For bounded continuous `f`, Feller continuity and weak convergence yield

\[
\int P_s f\,d\mu_k
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}\int P_s f\,d\mu_{k,t}
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}\int f\,d\mu_{k,t+s}
=
\int f\,d\mu_k.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\mu_kP_s=\mu_k\quad\text{for every }s>=0.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This proves stationarity directly, without using the printed equation (43), without the disputed multi-time Schur kernel, and without circular appeal to uniqueness.
