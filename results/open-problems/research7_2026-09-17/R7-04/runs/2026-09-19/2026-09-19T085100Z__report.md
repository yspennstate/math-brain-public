## 6. B18 — translate the light-traffic coefficient into a variance

Let `m=E[f(X_0)]`, `g=f-m`, and

`C(t)=E[g(X_0)g(X_t)]`.

Then

`J_f(c)=m^2/2+J_g(c)`,

where

`J_g(c)=int_0^1 (1-u) C(cu)du`.

Define

`A_c = (1/c) int_0^c g(X_t)dt`.

Stationarity makes scalar covariance even. Double-counting the pair `(s,t)` in `[0,c]^2` gives

`Var(A_c)
 = (1/c^2) int_0^c int_0^c C(t-s)dsdt
 = (2/c^2) int_0^c (c-r)C(r)dr
 = 2J_g(c)`.

Hence the exact translation is

`boxed( J_g(c) = (1/2) Var[ c^{-1} int_0^c g(X_t)dt ]. )`

So a second-order P0290 reversal is exactly an increase of the variance of a time average when the averaging window is enlarged.

This reformulation also explains why stationarity alone is insufficient: the spectral filter of a finite time average is a squared-sinc kernel, not a pointwise monotone function of the window size. The hidden-chain order restrictions must supply the missing rigidity.

## 7. B2 — exact three-state spectral obstruction

Write an ordered three-state generator as

```
Q = [ -(a+b)      a          b
          c     -(c+d)       d
          e        f       -(e+f) ].
```

For the ordered states `0<1<2`, forward stochastic monotonicity has exactly the two nontrivial generator inequalities

`b<=d`, `c>=e`.

Set

`u=d-b>=0`, `v=c-e>=0`.

The two nonzero eigenvalues solve

`lambda^2+S lambda+P=0`,

where

`S=a+b+c+d+e+f`

and

`P=(ce+cf+de)+(ae+af+bf)+(ad+bc+bd)`.

Exact expansion yields

`boxed( S^2-4P = (a-f-u+v)^2 + 4uv >=0. )`

Therefore every irreducible three-state **forward** stochastically monotone CTMC already has real nonzero spectrum. Since a source-admissible doubly stochastically monotone chain is certainly forward monotone, this applies a fortiori to P0290.

Thus the oscillatory complex-eigenvalue mechanism behind the broader P0289 counterexample cannot occur on three hidden states.

This theorem exactly explains the earlier computational observation rather than merely extending the same random search.

## 8. B25 — double-count the time-average square and solve the two-mode case

Real spectrum by itself does not force the covariance to be pointwise decreasing: an observable can have one negative exponential coefficient. The relevant smoothed coefficient nevertheless remains monotone.

### Two-mode lemma

Suppose a real stationary covariance has

`C(t)=A e^{-alpha|t|}+B e^{-beta|t|}`,

with `0<alpha<beta`. Define

`phi(x)=(e^{-x}-1+x)/x^2`

and

`g_0(x)=-phi'(x)=[x-2+(x+2)e^{-x}]/x^3`.

Two elementary derivative identities are

`x^4 g_0'(x)=6-2x-(x^2+4x+6)e^{-x} <=0`,

and

`x^2 (x^2 g_0(x))' = 2-(x^2+2x+2)e^{-x} >=0`.

For the first inequality, on `0<x<3` set

`F(x)=x^2+4x+6-(6-2x)e^x`.

Then `F(0)=F'(0)=0` and

`F''(x)=2[1-(1-x)e^x]>=0`

because `(1-x)e^x` decreases from one. For `x>=3` the sign is immediate. The second inequality follows from `2e^x>=2+2x+x^2`.

Hence `g_0` decreases while `x^2g_0(x)` increases.

Positive definiteness of the stationary covariance gives a nonnegative spectral density

`A alpha/(alpha^2+omega^2)+B beta/(beta^2+omega^2)`.

Its numerator is exactly

`alpha beta(A beta+B alpha)+(A alpha+B beta)omega^2`,

so necessarily

`A beta+B alpha>=0`,
`A alpha+B beta>=0`.

Now

`J(c)=A phi(alpha c)+B phi(beta c)`

has derivative

`J'(c)=-A alpha g_0(alpha c)-B beta g_0(beta c)`.

If both coefficients are nonnegative, the sign is immediate. If `A<0<B`, the first spectral inequality gives `B/(-A)>=beta/alpha`; then monotonicity of `x^2g_0(x)` gives `J'<=0`. If `B<0<A`, the second spectral inequality gives `A/(-B)>=beta/alpha`; then monotonicity of `g_0` gives `J'<=0`. Both coefficients cannot be negative.

Therefore every positive-definite two-real-exponential covariance has nonincreasing time-average variance.

### Apply to the three-state chain

For distinct nonzero eigenvalues the centered three-state covariance has exactly the two-exponential form above, so `J_f(c)` is nonincreasing.

At a repeated-eigenvalue boundary, perturb an allowed monotone jump rate (for example `d -> d+epsilon`) and take a sequence of positive epsilons for which the nonzero spectrum is distinct. Irreducibility and forward monotonicity remain valid. Stationary distributions and matrix exponentials vary continuously with the finite generator, so the corresponding `J_f` converge pointwise. A pointwise limit of nonincreasing functions is nonincreasing.

Thus:

**Theorem.** For every irreducible stationary three-state stochastically monotone CTMC `X` and every deterministic real observable `f`,

`boxed( c -> int_0^1(1-u)E[f(X_0)f(X_{cu})]du is nonincreasing. )`

This is stronger than needed for P0290 because it requires only forward monotonicity and allows arbitrary real `f`.
