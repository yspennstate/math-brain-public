## 5. B2 — attack the negation: exact two-point rank geometry

Let

`P(X=a)=p`, `P(X=b)=q=1-p`, with `0<a<b`,

and put

`d=b-a`, `m=E[X]=a+q d`.

For attained service `t<a`, conditional residual service is `a-t` with probability `p` and `b-t` with probability `q`. In the equivalent Gittins stopping formulation,

`r_G(t)=inf_{Delta>0} E[min(X-t,Delta) | X>t] / P(X-t<=Delta | X>t)`.

If `Delta<a-t`, the completion probability is zero. If `a-t<=Delta<b-t`, the ratio equals

`[p(a-t)+q Delta]/p`,

which is strictly increasing in `Delta`, so the minimum on that interval is attained at `Delta=a-t` and equals `(a-t)/p`. If `Delta>=b-t`, the best such choice is `Delta=b-t`, which gives `m-t`. Therefore

`r_G(t)=min{(a-t)/p, m-t}`, `0<=t<a`.

For `a<=t<b`, survival reveals the job is the long type, hence

`r_G(t)=b-t`.

SERPT is immediate:

`r_S(t)=m-t`, `0<=t<a`,

`r_S(t)=b-t`, `a<=t<b`.

Therefore its nondecreasing envelope is

`r_MS(t)=m`, `0<=t<a`,

`r_MS(t)=max{m,d}`, `a<=t<b`.

Since

`d-m = p d-a`,

the exact qualitative threshold is

`theta := p(b-a)/a = p d/a`.

## 6. B18 — translate the problem into phase priorities

### Theorem A: exact equality region

If

`theta<=1`, equivalently `p(b-a)<=a`,

then M-SERPT and Gittins are sample-path identical and both equal FCFS. Thus for every stable load,

`E[T_MS]=E[T_G]`.

**Proof.** The condition gives `d<=m`, so the M-SERPT rank is identically `m`; with FCFS ties, M-SERPT is FCFS.

For Gittins, `p d<=a` is equivalent to `a/p>=m`, because

`a/p>=m`
`<=> a>=p(a+q d)`
`<=> q a>=p q d`
`<=> a>=p d`.

Hence a fresh job has `r_G(0)=m`. Once it receives any positive service while `t<a`, its rank is at most `m-t<m`; after age `a`, if it is long, its rank is `b-t<=d<=m` and decreases thereafter. Thus once Gittins starts a job, that job never becomes worse than a fresh waiting job of rank `m`. Inducting through completions, later arrivals never preempt it. Therefore Gittins also serves in arrival order, exactly FCFS. QED.

This is an all-load result, not a light-traffic approximation.

### Theorem B: exact phase reduction in the only possible two-point gap region

Assume

`theta>1`, equivalently `p d>a`,

and define

`c=b-a/p`.

Then `a<c<b`.

For M-SERPT, young jobs `[0,a)` have rank `m`, and the revealed long residual `[a,b)` has rank `d>m`. Hence M-SERPT is the two-level feedback discipline

`H0=[0,a)` high priority,

`L=[a,b)` low priority.

Each job is served continuously through its first `a` units; a long job is then demoted for its entire residual `d`, and demoted jobs are FCFS in demotion order.

For Gittins, because `p d>a`, one has `a/p<m`. For every `t<a`,

`(a-t)/p-(m-t)=(a/p-m)-(q/p)t<0`,

so

`r_G(t)=(a-t)/p`.

At age `a`, the rank jumps to `d>a/p`, so fresh work outranks the newly revealed long job. For `t>=a`, the rank is `b-t`. It exceeds the fresh rank `a/p` exactly when

`t<c=b-a/p`,

and is no worse than fresh work exactly when `t>=c`. Hence Gittins is the three-level feedback discipline

`H0=[0,a)` high,

`L=[a,c)` low,

`H1=[c,b)` high.

The middle low phase has exact length

`c-a=d-a/p=a(theta-1)/p`,

and the rescued terminal high phase has exact length

`b-c=a/p`.

Thus the **only structural difference** between the two policies on a two-point service law is that Gittins promotes the final `a/p` units of a long job back above fresh work, while M-SERPT leaves all `d` residual units below fresh work. QED.
