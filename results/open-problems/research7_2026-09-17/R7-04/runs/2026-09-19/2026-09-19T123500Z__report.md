# 8. B28 — singular slow faces cannot create a leading three-state resonance

The previous run observed that tiny apparent positive derivative values near reducible points disappeared under more stable evaluation. This run proves a structural reason such faces are dangerous.

Let `Q_0` have a strictly positive stationary law `pi` and closed communicating classes

`C_1<...<C_m`

that are contiguous ordered blocks. Let `Q_1` have the same stationary law and

`Q_epsilon=Q_0+epsilon Q_1`,

assumed irreducible and doubly stochastically monotone for small positive `epsilon`.

Let `Pi` be the `L^2(pi)` projection onto class-constant functions. Define the effective block generator

\[
\bar Q=\Pi Q_1\Pi,
\]

or

\[
\bar q_{rs}
=\frac1{\pi(C_r)}
\sum_{i\in C_r}\sum_{j\in C_s}\pi_i(q_1)_{ij}.
\]

Its stationary law is `bar pi_r=pi(C_r)`.

### Averaging lemma

For every fixed `t>0`,

\[
\exp\{t(Q_0/\varepsilon+Q_1)\}
\to
\Pi e^{t\bar Q}\Pi.
\]

Because all states have positive stationary mass, `Q_0` has no transient states; zero is semisimple, its kernel is exactly the class-constant subspace, and all eigenvalues on the complement have negative real part. Variation of constants gives fast decay of the complement and the slow equation `dot y=Pi Q_1 Pi y+O(epsilon)`; finite-dimensional Gronwall then yields the limit.

### Derivative limit

For centered `g`, let

\[
C_\varepsilon(s)
=\langle g,e^{s(Q_0/\varepsilon+Q_1)}g\rangle_\pi.
\]

For every `s>0`, this converges to the quotient covariance. Stationary Markov semigroups are `L^2(pi)` contractions, so the covariances are uniformly bounded.

Put

\[
K_\varepsilon(t)=J_{g,Q_\varepsilon}(t/\varepsilon).
\]

For any bounded covariance `C`, after changing variables one has the exact identity

\[
K'(t)=\frac1{t^3}\int_0^t(2s-t)C(s)\,ds.
\]

This avoids differentiating the singular semigroup. Dominated convergence gives

\[
K_\varepsilon'(t)\to J'_{\Pi g,\bar Q}(t).
\]

The chain rule gives `K_epsilon'(t)=epsilon^{-1}J'_{g,Q_epsilon}(t/epsilon)`, hence

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\varepsilon^{-1}J'_{g,Q_\varepsilon}(t/\varepsilon)
=J'_{\Pi g,\bar Q}(t).
}
\]

### Double monotonicity passes to the quotient

An increasing function on the ordered class space lifts to an increasing function on the original ordered state space. The scaled `Q_epsilon` semigroups are stochastically monotone; passing to the averaging limit makes the quotient semigroup stochastically monotone. Repeating the argument for stationary reversals shows that `bar Q^*` is also stochastically monotone.

If `m<=3`, the earlier complete R7-04 three-state theorem applies, giving

\[
J'_{\Pi g,\bar Q}(t)\le0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\varepsilon^{-1}J'_{g,Q_\varepsilon}(t/\varepsilon)\le0.
}
\]

So a perturbation away from such a reducible face cannot create a **positive leading-order `O(epsilon)` slow-scale derivative**. If the quotient derivative is strictly negative, the full derivative is negative for small epsilon. If the quotient derivative vanishes, any possible positivity is necessarily subleading `o(epsilon)` and must be established by a higher-order exact analysis rather than floating eigensolver output.

The B16 checker verifies that all nine reducible certified rays have at most three contiguous classes, so this theorem applies to perturbations away from every one of those certified rays, subject to remaining in the fixed-uniform source cone and becoming irreducible.
