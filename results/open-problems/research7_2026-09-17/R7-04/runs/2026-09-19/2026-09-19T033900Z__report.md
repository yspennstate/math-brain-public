## 4. Method B8 — finite Fredholm determinants with a certified quantitative limit

The previous exact stationary-law theorem gives, for finitely many constraints `(k_a,t_a)`,

\[
S:=P(X_{k_a}>t_a\ \forall a)

\[
H_k=\{k-\tfrac12,k+\tfrac12,k+\tfrac32,\ldots\}
\]

and the extended kernel is

\[
K((t,x),(s,y))
=
\frac1{(2\pi i)^2}
\oint\!\oint
\frac{e^{z-t/z-w+s/w}}{z-w}

\]

with the standard contour nesting according to time order.

This run makes the infinite half-line determinant quantitatively finite. For `L>=max_a k_a`, let

\[
H_k^{(L)}=\{j-\tfrac12:j=k,\ldots,L\},
\qquad
A_L=\bigsqcup_a\{t_a\}\times H_{k_a}^{(L)},
\]

and

\[
D_L:=\det(I-K|_{A_L}).
\]

Then

\[
D_L\downarrow S
\]

and, more strongly,

\[
\boxed{
0\le D_L-S
\le
\sum_a P\{\operatorname{Pois}(t_a)\ge L+1\}.

\tag{B8.1}
\]

### Proof

`D_L` is exactly the probability that no Maya particle lies in the retained finite half-lines at the observation times. The only way the truncated gap can occur while the full gap fails is that, at some `t_a`, there is a Maya particle at or to the right of `L+1/2`. \[
\Lambda_1(t_a)\le |\Lambda(t_a)|

and the total number of boxes in rate-one poissonized Plancherel growth is exactly `Pois(t_a)`, a union bound gives (B8.1). This is source-independent of any trace-class assertion and is a complete quantitative proof.

The determinant dimension is only

\tag{B26.1}

\[

\tag{B26.2}
\]

### Proof of the skew formula

After removing the first `m` cells in column one, the skew diagram consists of two vertical chains: `q` cells in column two and `q+r-m` remaining cells in column one. Standard tableaux are their order-preserving interleavings. The row constraints say that at every prefix the number selected from column two may exceed the number selected from the remaining first column by at most `m`. There are `binom(2q+r-m,q)` unrestricted interleavings. The reflection principle bijects the paths whose first excess is `m+1` with paths counted by `binom(2q+r-m,q-m-1)`. Subtraction proves (B26.2).

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&P(X_1>s,X_2>t,X_3>u)\\

\sum_{q,r\ge0}\sum_{m=0}^{q+r}
\frac{f^{(2^q,1^r)}}{(2q+r)!}
\frac{a^m}{m!}
\frac{f^{(2^q,1^r)/(1^m)}b^{2q+r-m}}
{(2q+r-m)!}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{B26.3}
\]

This is a completely positive explicit triple-survival series.
