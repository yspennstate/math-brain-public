## 2. Exact outside source and dated status check

Primary current source:

David Aldous, *A one-dimensional drift-jump particle process*, problem page posted February 2018:

`https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/left_Hamm.html`.

The source considers, consistently for every finite `k`, particles

`0 < X_1(t) < ... < X_k(t)`

with a unit-rate space-time Poisson input: an event `(x_0,t_0)` moves the closest particle to the right of `x_0` to `x_0`, and between events every coordinate obeys `dX_i/dt=X_i`. Aldous states that the process has a unique stationary law and asks for a **fairly explicit description of that stationary distribution**.

Historical source:

David Aldous and Persi Diaconis, unfinished 1993 notes *Patience Sorting, Longest Increasing Subsequences*, Sections 4.1–4.2:

`https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Research/OP/patience.pdf`.

The notes prove the distributional representation

`(X_1(∞),...,X_k(∞)) =_d (Gamma(B(1)),...,Gamma(B(k)))`,

where `B(i)` are stabilized patience-sorting pile-top labels and `Gamma(j)` is the `j`-th point of an independent rate-one Poisson process. They explicitly solve the discrete `k=2` pile-label law as

`P(B(2)=b)=1/[b(b-2)!]`, `b>=2`,

and convert it into a scalar infinite-series formula.

Dated status/literature reconciliation performed on **2026-09-19**:

## 4. Method B2 — obstruction/counterexample search first

`T_j := inf{n : (Lambda_n)_1 >= j}`.

A tempting simplification would be to model the successive hitting times `(T_j)` as a one-dimensional Markov chain in the particle index `j`. That compression is false.

## 5. Method B18 — translate the source to Plancherel first-row hitting times

### Theorem 5.1 — patience pile tops are the first row of the RSK insertion tableau

For a permutation `sigma=(sigma_1,...,sigma_n)`, run patience sorting by placing each incoming card `x` on the leftmost pile whose top is strictly larger than `x`, or creating a new rightmost pile. Let `B(n,j)` be the `j`-th pile top. If `P(sigma)` is the Robinson–Schensted insertion tableau under ordinary row insertion, then

`B(n,j)=P(sigma)(1,j)`

for every existing pile.

**Proof.** Induct over incoming cards. Row insertion replaces the leftmost first-row entry strictly larger than `x`, bumping the old entry downward; if none is larger it appends `x`. Patience sorting performs exactly the same update to the pile-top vector. Lower pile contents and lower tableau rows do not affect that first-row/top-vector update. Therefore the vectors agree after every insertion. QED.

### Theorem 5.2 — stabilized pile-top labels equal Plancherel first-row hitting times in law

Let `B(j)` be the stabilized Aldous–Diaconis pile-top labels and define

`T_j=inf{n:(Lambda_n)_1>=j}`.

Then jointly for every fixed `k`,

`(B(1),...,B(k)) =_d (T_1,...,T_k)`.

**Proof.** RSK bijects a uniform permutation with a pair `(P,Q)` of standard Young tableaux of the same shape. For any fixed tableau `T` of shape `lambda`, exactly `f^lambda` permutations have `P=T`, and exactly `f^lambda` have `Q=T`, hence the marginal tableau laws of `P` and `Q` agree. By Theorem 5.1, the pile-top labels are the first-row entries `P(1,j)`. Revealing `Q` in increasing entry order produces standard Plancherel growth, and `Q(1,j)` is exactly the first time its first row reaches length `j`. Thus the finite vectors have the same law; pass to the stabilized labels. QED.

### Theorem 5.3 — the stationary drift-jump vector is a poissonized-Plancherel hitting vector

Let `N_x` be an independent rate-one Poisson process and set

`Lambda(x)=Lambda_{N_x}`,

`tau_j=inf{x>=0:Lambda_1(x)>=j}`.

Then

`(X_1,...,X_k) =_d (tau_1,...,tau_k)`

for every fixed `k`.

**Proof.** Aldous–Diaconis equation (42) gives the stationary vector as `(Gamma(B(1)),...,Gamma(B(k)))`. Replace `B` in law by `T` using Theorem 5.2. Since `Gamma(T_j)=inf{x:N_x>=T_j}=tau_j`, the joint identity follows. QED.

This is the principal category change of the run: the stationary particle vector is exactly the vector of top-row hitting times of rate-one poissonized Plancherel growth.

## 6. Method B25 — all-coordinate determinant marginals

Let `L_n` be the LIS length of a uniform permutation of `n` letters and let `u_m(n)` be the number of permutations in `S_n` with LIS at most `m`.

### Lemma 6.1

For every `k>=1` and integer `n>=0`,

`P(B(k)>n)=P(L_n<k)=u_{k-1}(n)/n!`.

**Proof.** The `k`-th pile is absent after `n` cards exactly when patience sorting created fewer than `k` piles. The number of piles equals the LIS length. Stabilization preserves all atoms up to time `n`, yielding the stated tail identity. QED.

As a low-dimensional source check, when `k=2` the only permutation with LIS `<2` is the decreasing permutation, so `P(B(2)>n)=1/n!` and therefore

`P(B(2)=b)=1/(b-1)!-1/b!=1/[b(b-2)!]`,

exactly the Aldous–Diaconis formula.

### Theorem 6.2 — exact Bessel/Toeplitz stationary survival formula

For every `k>=1` and `x>=0`,

`P(X_k>x) = exp(-x) det[ I_{i-j}(2 sqrt(x)) ]_{i,j=1}^{k-1}`,

where `I_r` is the modified Bessel function, `I_{-r}=I_r`, and the `k=1` determinant is empty and equals one.

**Proof.** Since `X_k=Gamma(B(k))` in law,

`{X_k>x}={N_x<B(k)}`.

Condition on `N_x=n` and apply Lemma 6.1:

`P(X_k>x)=e^{-x} sum_{n>=0} u_{k-1}(n) x^n/(n!)^2`.

Gessel's determinant identity states

`sum_{n>=0} u_m(n) y^(2n)/(n!)^2 = det[I_{i-j}(2y)]_{i,j=1}^m`.

Set `m=k-1`, `y=sqrt(x)`. QED.

Thus

- `P(X_1>x)=e^{-x}`;
- `P(X_2>x)=e^{-x} I_0(2sqrt(x))`;
- `P(X_3>x)=e^{-x}[I_0(2sqrt(x))^2-I_1(2sqrt(x))^2]`.

## 7. Positive exact full-joint representation

The marginal theorem is not the endpoint. The Plancherel translation also gives an exact positive finite-dimensional formula for the whole process.

Let

`0=t_0<t_1<...<t_r`

and nested Young diagrams

`empty=lambda^(0) subset lambda^(1) subset ... subset lambda^(r)`.

Write `n_j=|lambda^(j)|`, `d_j=n_j-n_(j-1)`, and `f^(mu/lambda)` for the number of standard Young tableaux of skew shape `mu/lambda`.

Then

`P(Lambda(t_j)=lambda^(j), j=1,...,r)`

`= exp(-t_r) [f^(lambda^(r))/n_r!]`

`  * product_{j=1}^r [ f^(lambda^(j)/lambda^(j-1)) (t_j-t_(j-1))^(d_j) / d_j! ]`.

**Proof.** A discrete `d=m-n` step Plancherel transition from `lambda` to `mu` is obtained by summing over the `f^(mu/lambda)` standard skew-tableau paths. Along each one-box path, the transition products telescope to

`[f^mu/f^lambda] [n!/m!]`.

Multiply these discrete multi-step transitions by the independent Poisson increment probabilities `e^{-Delta t}(Delta t)^d/d!`; across the time slabs all intermediate dimensions and factorial ratios telescope to the displayed expression. QED.

Using the Plancherel Schur specialization `rho_t`, with

`s_(mu/lambda)(rho_t)=f^(mu/lambda)t^(|mu|-|lambda|)/( |mu|-|lambda| )!`,

the formula is a Schur-process weight. Therefore, for arbitrary ordered times and thresholds,

`P(X_(k_j)>t_j for all j)=P(Lambda_1(t_j)<k_j for all j)`

is an exact positive nested-partition sum obtained by restricting `lambda^(j)_1<k_j` in the formula above.

Under particle coordinates `z_i=lambda_i-i`, the event `lambda_1(t)<k` is the absence of particles from the half-line `{k-1,k,...}` at time `t`. Okounkov–Reshetikhin's Schur-process theorem therefore implies that these joint stationary survival events are Fredholm gap determinants of the corresponding specialized space-time determinantal kernel.
