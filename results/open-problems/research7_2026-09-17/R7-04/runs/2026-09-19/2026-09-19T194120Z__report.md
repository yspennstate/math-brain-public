## 5. B6 — exact stationary response, no simulation

Assume \(pd>a\), so \(d>m>h\) and \(a<c<b\).

### M-SERPT, short job

At worst future rank \(m\), a later fresh job ties and loses by FCFS order, so new \(m\)-work is zero. Every old job contributes exactly its first \(a\) units and never recycles. Thus SOAP gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[T_{\rm MS}\mid X=a]
=
a+\frac{\lambda a^2}{2(1-\lambda a)}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### M-SERPT, long job

Its worst future rank is \(d\). Every new arrival contributes exactly \(a\) units before completing or reaching rank \(d\); every old job remains no worse than \(d\), so its 0-old work is the full \(X\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[T_{\rm MS}\mid X=b]
=
\frac{\lambda\mathbb E[X^2]}
{2(1-\rho)(1-\lambda a)}
+
\frac{b}{1-\lambda a}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Gittins, short job

Its worst future rank is \(h=a/p\). A later fresh job ties and gives no new work. An old job contributes its first \(a\) units; if long, it is discarded at age \(a\) when its rank jumps to \(d>h\), then returns at age \(c=b-h\) and contributes one recycled terminal interval of length \(h\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[T_G\mid X=a]
=
a+
\frac{\lambda(a^2+qh^2)}
{2(1-\lambda a)}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the SOAP paper's published \(a=2,p=q=1/2,b=14\) case this becomes
\(2+6\lambda/(1-2\lambda)\), exactly its displayed value.

### Gittins, long job

Its waiting term is the same as in (5.2). Until age \(c\), new jobs contribute \(a\)-work; on the final interval \([c,b)\), whose length is \(h\), new jobs contribute no delaying work. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[T_G\mid X=b]
=
\frac{\lambda\mathbb E[X^2]}
{2(1-\rho)(1-\lambda a)}
+
\frac{c}{1-\lambda a}
+h.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For the same published \(2/14\) example this becomes

\[
\frac{50\lambda}{(1-8\lambda)(1-2\lambda)}
+\frac{10}{1-2\lambda}+4,
\]

again exactly the SOAP value.

## 6. B11 — exact occupation/charging identity

Subtract the size-conditional formulas.

For short jobs,

\[
\mathbb E[T_{\rm MS}-T_G\mid X=a]
=
-\frac{\lambda qh^2}{2(1-\lambda a)}.
\]

For long jobs,

\[
\mathbb E[T_{\rm MS}-T_G\mid X=b]
=
\frac{h}{1-\lambda a}-h
=
\frac{\lambda ah}{1-\lambda a}.
\]

Weight by \(p,q\) and use \(h=a/p\). The cancellation is exact:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E T_{\rm MS}-\mathbb E T_G
=
\frac{q\lambda a^2}{2p(1-\lambda a)}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is a complete source-relevant identity, not a numerical diagnostic. It says the entire two-point M-SERPT penalty is one positive occupation charge: the long-job gain from Gittins's terminal rescue exceeds its extra short-job delay by precisely (6.1).

## 7. B29 — solve the entire two-point global optimization

Scale \(a=1\) and define

\[
\theta=\frac{p(b-a)}a>1,\qquad
s=p+(1-p)\theta,\qquad
\rho=\lambda\mathbb EX.
\]

Then \(\lambda=\rho p/s\). Set

\[
R(p,\theta,\rho)=\frac{\mathbb E T_{\rm MS}}{\mathbb E T_G}.
\]

From (6.1),

\[
R=1+\frac{D}{\mathbb E T_G},
\qquad
D=\frac{(1-p)\rho}{2(s-p\rho)}.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal N
=
2p(1-\rho)(s-p\rho)\,\mathbb E T_G>0.
\]

Exact differentiation of the positive closed-form response expressions gives

\[
\frac{\partial R}{\partial\theta}
=
\frac{-2p\rho(p-1)^2(\rho-1)}{\mathcal N^2}
\left(
p[\rho(p+1)-2]-(1-p)(2-\rho)\theta
\right).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The prefactor is positive. The bracket decreases in \(\theta\) and, at \(\theta=1\), equals

\[
\rho(1+p^2)-2<0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\partial_\theta R<0}
\]

throughout \(0<p,\rho<1,\theta>1\). Hence the supercritical two-point supremum lies in the one-sided limit \(\theta\downarrow1\).

That limit is

\[
R_1(p,\rho)=1+\delta(p,\rho),
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\delta(p,\rho)
=
\frac{p\rho(1-p)(1-\rho)}
{(1-p\rho)[2-\rho(p^2-p+1)]}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The second denominator is at least 1. Also
\(1-p\rho\ge1-p\) and \(1-p\rho\ge1-\rho\), so \(\delta\) extends continuously by 0 even at the corner \((1,1)\). It vanishes on the whole boundary and is positive inside. Thus its global maximum is interior.

The two interior derivative numerators are

\[
F=p^4\rho^2-2p^3\rho^2+2p^2\rho+2p\rho-4p-\rho+2,
\]

\[
G=p^3\rho^2-2p^2\rho^2-\rho^2+4\rho-2.
\]

At a critical point \(F=G=0\), while

\[
F-pG=(p\rho-1)(2p+\rho-2).
\]

Because \(p\rho<1\),

\[
\rho=2(1-p).
\]

Substitution into \(G=0\) gives

\[
Q(p)=2p^5-8p^4+10p^3-6p^2+1=0.
\]

Moreover

\[
Q'(p)=2p(p-2)(5p^2-6p+3)<0
\]

for \(0<p<1\), since the quadratic factor has discriminant \(-24\). As

\[
Q(1/2)=5/16>0,\qquad Q(1)=-1<0,
\]

there is exactly one root \(p_\star\in(1/2,1)\). Therefore the unique interior critical point is the global maximizer:

\[
p_\star
=
0.65713914089196252219614876194473389499\ldots,
\]

\[
\rho_\star
=
2(1-p_\star)
=
0.68572171821607495560770247611053221002\ldots.
\]

The exact sharp two-point supremum is

\[
\boxed{
C_2
=
1+
\frac{(1-p_\star)^2(2p_\star-1)}
{(p_\star^2-2p_\star+2)(2p_\star^2-2p_\star+1)}
=
1.06017342191276796060451535444764110226\ldots .
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

It is approached as \(\theta\downarrow1\) from above. At \(\theta=1\) itself, the previous theorem says both policies are FCFS and the ratio is exactly 1, so the supercritical supremum is not attained on that boundary.
