## 4. B19 — constructive nucleation in every nontrivial stationary law

The first new result is a finite-energy/nucleation theorem that does **not** assume spatial translation invariance.

### Theorem 4.1 — finite-cylinder nucleation

Let `mu` be any stationary probability law for the source process with

\[
\mu(\{\mathbf0\})=0.
\]

Then for every finite set `Lambda` of lattice sites,

\[
\boxed{
\mu\{\eta:\eta(x)=1\text{ for every }x\in\Lambda\}>0.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

First, a stationary law with no atom at zero cannot charge configurations with finitely many particles.  Let

\[
N(\eta)=\sum_x\eta(x).
\]

If `1<=N(eta)=m<infty`, then for any fixed `t>0`

\[
P_\eta(\eta_t=\mathbf0)>0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, because the interaction range is finite and only finitely many particles are present, only finitely many stirring and birth clocks can create a descendant before all old particles die if all stirring marks incident to the occupied sites are suppressed.  Require those finitely many potentially activating birth/stirring marks not to occur before `t`, and require every one of the `m` death clocks to ring before `t`.  This graphical event has strictly positive probability and sends the configuration to zero.

If `mu(N<infty)>0`, then the measurable function `eta -> P_eta(eta_t=0)` is strictly positive on a set of positive `mu`-mass.  Hence

\[
P_\mu(\eta_t=\mathbf0)
=
\int P_\eta(\eta_t=\mathbf0)\,\mu(d\eta)>0.
\]

Stationarity would imply `mu({0})>0`, a contradiction.  Thus

\[
\boxed{\mu(N=\infty)=1.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since the lattice is countable,

\[
\{N\ge2\}=
\bigcup_{x\ne y}\{\eta(x)=\eta(y)=1\},
\]

so there are fixed sites `x!=y` satisfying

\[
\mu(\eta(x)=\eta(y)=1)>0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Now fix `Lambda`.  Choose a large finite connected buffer `V` containing `x,y,Lambda`, the paths to be used below, and enough additional sites for finitely many births.  Partition the event in (4.4) by the finitely many restrictions `eta|_V`.  Some finite pattern `sigma` with two occupied sites has positive cylinder probability.

From that fixed pattern there is a finite legal control word taking the system to a state that is occupied on all of `Lambda`.  Nearest-neighbour transpositions on a finite connected graph generate every permutation of a fixed occupation pattern.  Hence two existing particles can be moved to a legal pair of birth parents around a vacant target.  Fire that two-parent birth to increase the particle count by one.  Repeat until at least `|Lambda|` particles are available, and then permute `|Lambda|` occupied sites onto `Lambda`.  If enough particles were already available, skip the birth stage.

Realize this finite word in a finite time interval by requiring the prescribed stirring/birth marks in disjoint small windows, suppressing deaths of particles needed by the word, and suppressing competing marks in a finite interaction collar and across its boundary.  Only finitely many positive-rate clocks are constrained, so this graphical event has probability `c_sigma>0`, independently of the configuration outside the protected collar.  Therefore

\[
P_\mu(\eta_T\equiv1\text{ on }\Lambda)
\ge \mu(\eta|_V=\sigma)c_\sigma>0.
\]

By stationarity the left side equals the time-zero cylinder probability, proving (4.1). QED.

The important limitation is quantitative: (4.1) supplies a genuine seed in every nontrivial stationary law, but the seed probability need not be bounded below uniformly in the law, location, block size or `epsilon`.

## 5. B23 — a uniform stable-branch contraction gap and a Palm lower bound

The second attack asks exactly how much low-branch Palm mass a persistent discrepancy would need.

Define the larger root of the ideal disagreement threshold

\[
a_*(\beta)
=
\frac{1+\sqrt{1-3/\beta}}3,
\qquad
2a_*-3a_*^2=\frac1\beta.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every `beta>4.5`,

\[
\rho_+>\frac23,
\qquad
a_*<\frac23,
\tag{5.2}
\]

so there is a strict branch gap.  Set

\[
\theta_\beta=\frac{\rho_++a_*}{2},
\qquad
\kappa_\beta
=1-\beta(2\theta_\beta-3\theta_\beta^2)>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Because `theta_beta>1/2`, for every admissible `d>=0` and every `a>=theta_beta`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\psi(a,d)
&=2a-3a^2+d(1-3a)-d^2\\
&\le 2a-3a^2\\
&\le2\theta_\beta-3\theta_\beta^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
a\ge\theta_\beta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta\psi(a,d)\le1-\kappa_\beta
\text{ for every admissible }d.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus entering the stable common-one basin gives a strict disagreement-contraction margin that is **uniform in the disagreement fraction**.

A second exact estimate controls the worst possible reproduction anywhere.  On the triangle

\[
a\ge0,\qquad d\ge0,\qquad a+d\le1,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{\psi(a,d)\le\frac13.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For `a>=1/3`, the derivative of `psi` in `d` at zero is `1-3a<=0`, so the maximum over `d>=0` is `2a-3a^2<=1/3`.  For `a<1/3`, the unconstrained maximizing value is

\[
d=\frac{1-3a}{2}
\]

and gives

\[
\psi_{\max}(a)=\frac{1+2a-3a^2}{4},
\]

which increases on `[0,1/3]` and equals `1/3` at `a=1/3`.  This proves (5.5).

Now work in the exact ideal color-exchangeable Palm closure and suppose a nonzero stationary discrepancy exists.  Let

\[
p_{\rm bad}=P^D(a<\theta_\beta)
\]

under discrepancy Palm probability.  On the good event, normalized reproduction is at most `1-kappa_beta` by (5.4); globally it is at most `beta/3` by (5.5).  Stationarity needs mean reproduction at least one, hence

\[
1\le
(1-p_{\rm bad})(1-\kappa_\beta)
+p_{\rm bad}\frac\beta3.
\]

Rearranging gives the fixed positive lower bound

\[
\boxed{
P^D(a<\theta_\beta)
\ge
c_\beta
:=
\frac{\kappa_\beta}{\beta/3-1+\kappa_\beta}
>0.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is a stronger description of the obstruction than “the low branch might occur.”  Any persistent disagreement must put a **beta-dependent positive fraction of its Palm mass** in low-common-one environments.  The prior relative-stirring error can be absorbed into (5.6) once it is smaller than a fixed fraction of `kappa_beta`; no `1/Q` denominator is needed in this algebraic step.

## 7. New exact structural theorem — every discrepancy has an infinite backward disagreement ancestry

The graphical construction supplies a way to attack the relative/Palm problem directly rather than paying the Bayes factor in (6.2).

### Theorem 7.1 — backward discrepancy ancestry

In the basic monotone coupling, suppose

\[
D_t(x)=\xi_t(x)-\eta_t(x)=1.
\]

For every earlier time `s<t`, there exists a backwards graphical path from `(x,t)` to some `(y,s)` such that the currently followed site is a disagreement immediately before each genealogical jump.  If a birth creates a new disagreement at its target, at least one of its two parents was already a disagreement immediately before that birth.

Consequently, in a two-sided stationary ordered coupling, under the Palm law conditioned on `D_0(0)=1`, there is a backward disagreement ancestry reaching every time `-T`, `T>0`.

#### Proof

Trace backwards the most recent graphical event affecting the site currently followed.

**Stirring.**  A stirring mark swaps the two endpoint states in both coupled processes.  If the followed endpoint is a disagreement after the swap, the corresponding opposite endpoint immediately before the swap was a disagreement.  Move the ancestry there.

**Death.**  A common death mark sends an occupied target to zero in both processes and cannot create a discrepancy.  Therefore a discrepancy existing after a death cannot have begun at that death; either it was created later or was transported later.  There is no genealogical source at a death mark.

**Birth.**  If the discrepancy already existed at the target and survives the mark, keep following the target backwards.  If instead the mark creates a discrepancy, the upper process accepted a two-parent birth while the lower process did not.  Under coordinatewise order, at least one of the two upper occupied parents is absent in the lower process.  That parent is a disagreement immediately before the birth; choose it and continue backwards.

**Between marks.**  The discrepancy persists at the same site.

The graphical construction is locally finite and the one-lineage tracing procedure is nonexplosive on every bounded time interval, so it reaches every prescribed earlier time.  In a two-sided stationary construction, finite backward paths exist for every integer horizon; a diagonal/Konig-lemma selection gives a compatible ancestry to all past times. QED.

The theorem converts the branch-selection problem into a path event.  A present discrepancy cannot appear out of nothing; it must be supported by an indefinitely long backward chain of prior discrepancies.

## 8. The new residual lemma: a relative bad-corridor theorem

Combine the stable-branch contraction (5.4), fixed bad-Palm mass requirement (5.6), and backward ancestry theorem.  After the previous relative-stirring estimates have made the local tagged color law close to its mixed limit, a macroscopic block with common-one density at least `theta_beta` is strictly hostile to discrepancy reproduction.  Therefore an infinite backward ancestry must repeatedly find a connected corridor of low/common-one-bad blocks.

A sufficient source-level closure statement is now the following **relative moving-path estimate**:

> There exist block scales, `C<infinity`, and `lambda<1` such that, uniformly over every stationary ordered coupling with `Q>0` and over approximate-maximal disagreement sites, the discrepancy-Palm probability that its backward ancestry traverses `m` consecutive low-common-one/bad blocks is at most `C lambda^m`.

If this holds, Theorem 7.1 says a present discrepancy would require such a corridor for every `m`, while `C lambda^m -> 0`; hence `Q=0`.  This directly proves uniqueness without ever dividing an ordinary bad-block probability by `Q`.

The distinction is exact:

- ordinary block estimate: `P(B)<=delta`, which becomes `delta/Q` under Palm conditioning and is insufficient;
- required estimate: bound the **ancestry-conditioned** bad corridor from the start, relative to the discrepancy event itself.
