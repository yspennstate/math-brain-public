## 4. B30 — exact local discrepancy obstruction without exchangeability

Let the source birth neighborhood `N_x` have size `n` and be symmetric/translation covariant.  Define

\[
K_A(x)=\sum_{y\in N_x}A_y,
\quad K_D(x)=\sum_{y\in N_x}D_y,
\quad K_C(x)=n-K_A(x)-K_D(x),
\]

and

\[
r_n(k)=\frac{k(k-1)}{n(n-1)}.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact local discrepancy generator

The reaction part of the coupled generator is

\[
\boxed{
R D_x
=-D_x
-\beta D_x r_n(K_A(x))
+\beta C_x\{r_n(K_A(x)+K_D(x))-r_n(K_A(x))\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The three terms are respectively discrepancy death, lower-only catch-up birth when the target is a discrepancy, and upper-only birth when both target states are vacant.  This identity is exact.

For integers `a,d>=0`, `a+d<=n`,

\[
\boxed{r_n(a+d)-r_n(a)\le\frac{2d}{n}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, adding `d` newly upper-occupied neighbors creates at most `2d(n-1)` ordered parent pairs, out of `n(n-1)`.

### Theorem 4.2 — exact translation-invariant Palm neighbor obstruction

Assume now the ordered stationary coupling is spatially translation invariant and `q=E D_0>0`.  Define

\[
g_{\beta,n}(k)
=-1-\beta\frac{k(k-1)}{n(n-1)}
+2\beta\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then under discrepancy Palm probability,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E^D[g_{\beta,n}(K_A(0))]\ge0.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Complete proof

Translation invariance makes the expected stirring term at the origin zero.  Taking expectations in (4.1) gives

\[
0=-q-\beta E[D_0r_n(K_A)]
+\beta E[C_0\{r_n(K_A+K_D)-r_n(K_A)\}].
\]

By (4.2), the last expectation is at most `(2/n)E[C_0K_D]`.  Translation invariance and `N_0=-N_0` give the exact mass transport

\[
E[C_0K_D(0)]
=\sum_{z\in N_0}E[C_0D_z]
=\sum_{z\in N_0}E[D_0C_{-z}]
=E[D_0K_C(0)].
\]

Since `K_C<=n-K_A`,

\[
E[C_0K_D]\le E[D_0(n-K_A)].
\]

Substitution yields

\[
0\le E[D_0g_{\beta,n}(K_A)],
\]

and division by `q` proves (4.4). QED.

The function is strictly decreasing because

\[
g(k+1)-g(k)
=-\frac{2\beta k}{n(n-1)}-\frac{2\beta}{n}<0.
\]

Let

\[
m_{\beta,n}=\max\{k:g_{\beta,n}(k)\ge0\},
\quad
\gamma_{\beta,n}=-g_{\beta,n}(m_{\beta,n}+1)>0.
\]

Since `g(0)=2beta-1`, (4.4) immediately gives

\[
\boxed{
P^D(K_A\le m_{\beta,n})
\ge
\frac{\gamma_{\beta,n}}{2\beta-1+\gamma_{\beta,n}}>0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus a nonzero translation-invariant stationary discrepancy must spend a fixed positive fraction of Palm mass in explicitly common-one-poor local neighborhoods.  This removes the local-exchangeability assumption from the earlier Palm obstruction, but the translation-invariance hypothesis is extra relative to the full source and is therefore kept explicitly as a restriction.

## 6. B27 — a quantifier-swapped forward influence theorem

The previous run proposed bounding a bad corridor under terminal discrepancy Palm conditioning.  This run replaces that with a stronger and cleaner statement that does not condition on the endpoint.

### Theorem 6.1 — uniform influence path criterion

Consider a coarse directed space-time graph with maximum in-degree and out-degree `B`.  Suppose that for every deterministic path and **every** admissible incoming ordered state/history, the conditional probability, using fresh graphical marks in the next slab, that discrepancy influence follows a specified next coarse edge is at most `r`, uniformly over all prior history.  Suppose also that discrepancies admit a subadditive influence representation through these paths.  If

\[
\boxed{Br<1,}
\tag{6.1}
\]

then every stationary ordered coupling has zero discrepancy field.

#### Complete proof

Iterated conditioning bounds the probability of any fixed length-`m` discrepancy influence path by `r^m`; independence is not needed because the conditional estimate is uniform over the full revealed past.  At most `B^m` paths of length `m` terminate at a given coarse cell, so the column mass of the length-`m` influence kernel is at most `(Br)^m`.

Let `q_v` be the stationary discrepancy probability in coarse cell `v` and `Q=sup_vq_v`.  Subadditivity gives

\[
q_v\le\sum_uq_uK_m(u,v)
\le Q\sum_uK_m(u,v)
\le Q(Br)^m.
\]

Taking the supremum yields `Q<=Q(Br)^m`.  If `Q>0`, division contradicts `Br<1` for large `m`; hence `Q=0`. QED.

This theorem avoids the `delta/Q` problem because the quantifiers are reversed: the route must bound every possible continuation from an incoming discrepancy **before** any terminal discrepancy is selected.

For E0010 the hypotheses are not yet proved.  The existing fast-stirring block construction gives high ordinary probability of mature high-density descendants; it does not automatically give the worst-history discrepancy-influence continuation bound required above.

## 7. B21 — disagreement percolation import and the dependence correction

Van den Berg--Maes disagreement percolation provides the correct broad analogy: uniqueness can follow by controlling paths that transmit a disagreement.  However, Häggström's 2001 counterexample shows that one cannot merely remove independence and conclude uniqueness from the absence of an infinite disagreement path under an arbitrary dependent coupling.  That caveat is directly relevant to E0010.

Accordingly, the present route does **not** claim that a small ordinary bad-block probability makes blocks independent under discrepancy ancestry.  Theorem 6.1 instead demands a history-uniform conditional continuation bound.  This is a branching-random-walk/path-counting criterion robust to dependence because dependence is already included in the worst-case one-step constant `r`.

This import corrects the route rather than solving the source.
