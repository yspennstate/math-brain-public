# B3 — prove something stronger: all maximum-degree-two witness graphs

The preceding C6 theorem suggested that long cycles were the wrong place to look for failure. Instead of checking `C7,C8,...` separately, this run proves one theorem for **every** finite graph witness family of maximum degree at most two.

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph, `m=|E|`, and let

`A_G={K subseteq V: K contains both endpoints of at least one edge of G}`.

Thus `A_G^c` is the independent-set event.

## Theorem

If `Delta(G)<=2` and

`g(alpha^2):=-alpha^2 log(1-alpha^2) <= 1/2`,

then for every `0<p<1` and every `alpha p`-spread `nu`,

`W_alpha(nu)(A_G) <= mu_p(A_G)`.

In particular this holds for every `alpha<=alpha_*`.

## Proof, thinned side

Sample `J~nu`, then alpha-thin it to `K`. Conditional on `J`, for each graph edge `e subseteq J`, the event that `e` is not fully retained is decreasing in the independent retention bits. Harris/FKG gives

`P(K in A_G^c | J) >= (1-alpha^2)^{e_G(J)}`,

where `e_G(J)` is the number of graph edges induced by `J`.

With `L=-log(1-alpha^2)`, Jensen gives

`W_alpha(nu)(A_G^c) >= exp(-L E[e_G(J)]).`

Spreadness applied to every two-element edge yields

`E[e_G(J)] = sum_{e in E} nu(H_e) <= m(alpha p)^2`.

Therefore

`W_alpha(nu)(A_G^c) >= exp(-m p^2 g(alpha^2)).`                 (1)

This bound is source-valid for arbitrary dependence in `nu`.

## Proof, product side: paths

Set

`t=1-p^2/2`.

Let `P_k` be the probability that a Bernoulli-p occupied path with `k` edges has no occupied edge. Conditioning at an endpoint gives

`P_k=(1-p)P_{k-1}+p(1-p)P_{k-2}`,

with `P_0=1` and `P_1=1-p^2`. The exact supersolution identity is

`t^2-(1-p)t-p(1-p)=(p^2/4)((1-p)^2+1)>0`.                    (2)

Since `P_0<=t^0` and `P_1<=t`, induction in the recurrence gives

`P_k<=t^k`                                                     (3)

for every path.

## Proof, product side: cycles

Let `lambda_+>0>lambda_-` be the roots of

`x^2-(1-p)x-p(1-p)=0`.

The two-state cyclic transfer matrix gives exactly

`Z_k:=P(C_k has no occupied edge)=lambda_+^k+lambda_-^k`.      (4)

Identity (2) implies `t>=lambda_+`. If `k` is odd, the negative eigenmode subtracts, so

`Z_k<=lambda_+^k<=t^k`.

If `k>=4` is even, write

`a=lambda_+/t<=1`, `rho=|lambda_-|/lambda_+<1`.

Then

`Z_k/t^k=a^k(1+rho^k)<=a^4(1+rho^4)=Z_4/t^4`.                 (5)

Thus all cycles reduce to the four-cycle. Here

`Z_4=1-4p^2+4p^3-p^4`,

and the exact positive certificate is

`(t^4-Z_4)/p^2`

`=2(1-p)^6+8p(1-p)^5+(25/2)p^2(1-p)^4+10p^3(1-p)^3`

` +(9/2)p^4(1-p)^2+p^5(1-p)+(1/16)p^6 >=0`.                  (6)

Consequently every cycle satisfies `Z_k<=t^k`.

## Proof, gluing components

Every maximum-degree-two graph is a disjoint union of paths, cycles and isolated vertices. Product measure factorizes, so (3) and (6) give

`mu_p(A_G^c) <= t^m <= exp(-m p^2/2).`                         (7)

If `g(alpha^2)<=1/2`, then by (1) and (7),

`W_alpha(nu)(A_G^c) >= exp(-m p^2 g(alpha^2))`

`>= exp(-m p^2/2) >= mu_p(A_G^c)`.

Taking complements proves the theorem.

## High-degree star obstruction to scalar edge-count gluing

For the star `K_{1,d}`, the product no-edge probability is exactly

`Z_d(p)=(1-p)+p(1-p)^d`:                                      (8)

either the center is absent, or it is present and all leaves are absent.

`[-log Z_d(p)]/(d p^2) ->0`.                                   (9)

`mu_p(A_G^c)<=exp(-c |E(G)|p^2)`

for all graphs. Since the geometry-free FKG/Jensen bound (1) depends only on `|E|p^2`, an edge-count-only proof necessarily fails under unbounded branching.

`=1-4p^2+4p^3-p^4=Z_4`.                                      (10)
