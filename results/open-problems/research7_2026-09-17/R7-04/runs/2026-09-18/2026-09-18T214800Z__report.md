## 3. B1 — change category: exact positive first-merge decomposition

Let `G=(V,E)` be any finite connected simple graph. Read a labeling in descending-label order. A revealed vertex is a **birth** iff it has no neighbor among earlier revealed vertices; births are exactly peaks.

For every nonempty seed set `T`, let `F_G(T)` be the number of orders of `V\T` which, appended after `T` is already revealed, create no new birth. With `partial T={x notin T:N(x) intersects T}`, this satisfies

`F_G(V)=1`,

`F_G(T)=sum_{x in partial T} F_G(T union {x})`.            (3.1)

If `C` induces a connected subgraph and `r in C`, set

`h_C(r):=F_{G[C]}({r})`,                                  (3.2)

the number of successive orderings of `C` rooted at `r`.

For nonadjacent `r,v`, let `W_G(r,v)` count full orders whose births are exactly `r` first and `v` second. Suppose `S` induces exactly two connected components `A,B`; define

`M_G(S)={x notin S: x has a neighbor in A and a neighbor in B}`

and

`g_G(S)=sum_{x in M_G(S)} F_G(S union {x})`.               (3.3)

### Theorem 3.1 — ordered first-merge identity

For distinct nonadjacent `r,v`,

`W_G(r,v)
 = sum_S binom(|S|-1,|A|-1) h_A(r) h_B(v) g_G(S)`,        (3.4)

where the sum is over all `S` whose two components `A,B` contain `r,v`, respectively.

### Proof

Take any order counted by `W_G(r,v)`. Immediately after `v` is born, the revealed graph has exactly two components. Every later vertex before the components merge is not a birth, so it attaches to exactly one current component. Since the final graph is connected, there is a unique first merge step. Let `S=A union B` be the revealed set just before that step and `x` the merge vertex. Then there is no `A`--`B` edge and `x` is adjacent to both components.

The restriction of the prefix order to `A` is a successive order starting at `r`, hence has `h_A(r)` choices. The restriction to `B` is a successive order starting at `v`, hence has `h_B(v)` choices. Put `a=|A|`, `b=|B|`, `s=a+b`. The global first vertex is fixed as `r`; the remaining `a-1` vertices of the rooted `A` order and all `b` vertices of the rooted `B` order may be interleaved in exactly `binom(s-1,a-1)` ways. Because there is no cross-edge, the first `B` vertex `v` is a birth and every later `A` or `B` vertex has an earlier internal neighbor, so `v` is the unique second birth before merging.

Choose `x in M_G(S)` and append any suffix counted by `F_G(S union {x})`. The suffix has no further birth. Conversely, any such data determine a unique full order with births exactly `r,v`, and every such full order has a unique first-merge state `S,x`. Thus the classes are disjoint and exhaustive, proving (3.4). QED.

This proves the candidate `(REC)` from STUCK1 with its exact ordered interleaving factor.

## 4. The source-useful strengthening: exact positive unordered pair mixture

For the actual unordered peak pair let

`U_G({r,v})=W_G(r,v)+W_G(v,r)`.

For a fixed two-component state with `|A|=a`, `|B|=b`, `s=a+b`, the two orientations have coefficients

`binom(s-1,a-1)` and `binom(s-1,b-1)=binom(s-1,a)`.

Pascal's identity gives their sum `binom(s,a)`. Therefore

`U_G({r,v})
 = sum_S binom(|S|,|A|) h_A(r)h_B(v)g_G(S)`.            (4.1)

Every summand is nonnegative. The alternating independent-set cancellation that blocked the previous run is therefore **not intrinsic to the actual pair law**.

Define

`R(C)=sum_{u in C} h_C(u)`,

`mu_C(u)=h_C(u)/R(C)`,

and for a two-component state `S=A union B`,

`Omega_G(S)=binom(|S|,|A|) R(A)R(B)g_G(S)`.              (4.2)

Summing (4.1) over unordered pairs yields

`Z_2(G)=# {orders with exactly two peaks}
       =sum_{S:kappa(G[S])=2} Omega_G(S)`.               (4.3)

More strongly, conditional on the pre-merge state `S=A union B`, the two peak locations are independent:

`P(R_A=r,R_B=v | S, two peaks)=mu_A(r)mu_B(v)`.          (4.4)

This is immediate because the only root-dependent factor in (4.1) is `h_A(r)h_B(v)`.

Hence the actual two-peak distance has the exact positive representation

`E[rho | two peaks]
 = [sum_S Omega_G(S) E_{mu_A x mu_B} d_G(R_A,R_B)]
   / [sum_S Omega_G(S)]`.                                (4.5)

Equation (4.5) is the main structural advance of this run. The square source is now a positive pre-merge shape problem rather than a signed correlation problem.

There is a second exact simplification. If `p_1(C)` is the probability that a uniform order of connected `C` has exactly one peak, then `R(C)=|C|! p_1(C)`. Thus, if `s=|S|`,

`Omega_G(S)=s! p_1(A)p_1(B)g_G(S)`.                     (4.6)

The component-size interleaving entropy cancels exactly into the two one-peak probabilities.

## 5. A new positive description of each component root law

For connected `C` with at least two vertices,

`h_C(r)=sum_{y~_C r} F_{G[C]}({r,y})`.                  (5.1)

Proof: classify a successive order rooted at `r` by its unique second vertex `y`; it must be adjacent to `r`, and after `(r,y)` the remaining choices are exactly the no-birth suffixes counted by `F_{G[C]}({r,y})`.

Define symmetric edge conductances

`c_C({x,y})=F_{G[C]}({x,y})`.

Then

`mu_C(r)
 = [sum_{y~r} c_C({r,y})]
   / [2 sum_{e in E(C)} c_C(e)]`.                        (5.2)

So `mu_C` is the stationary vertex measure of a reversible nearest-neighbor walk with these exact conductances. In particular `max_r mu_C(r)<=1/2` for every connected `C` with at least two vertices. This bound is sharp on stars but is not alone an `n`-scale separation estimate.

## 6. B13 — monovariant and exact neutral-growth count

Fix a contributing first-merge state `S=A union B` with roots `r in A`, `v in B`. Promote `v` to be second in the pre-merge interleaving; this is valid because there are no `A`--`B` edges. During the resulting two-front growth let

`delta_t = d_G(A_t,B_t)`.

Adding one boundary vertex to one component cannot increase `delta_t` and can reduce it by at most one. Therefore

`Phi_t=|A_t|+|B_t|+delta_t`                              (6.1)

is nondecreasing.

Initially `delta_0=d_G(r,v)`. Immediately before the first merge, the two components have no edge between them but have a common neighbor, so their distance is exactly `2`. If `s=|S|`, among the `s-2` pre-merge additions there are therefore exactly

`d_G(r,v)-2` distance-decreasing steps,                  (6.2)

and exactly

`s-d_G(r,v)` distance-neutral steps.                     (6.3)

Hence

`d_G(r,v)<=|S|`.                                         (6.4)

The obstruction is now concrete: close roots can occur inside large pre-merge states only through many neutral/interface-growth additions. An asymptotic proof must compare the positive weight of those neutral histories with histories that spend a linear number of steps actually closing root separation.

## 7. B19 — exact recursion and merge hazard

For a two-component state `S=A union B`, partition its frontier into `L_A(S)` (adjacent only to A), `L_B(S)` (adjacent only to B), and the common boundary `M_G(S)`. Then (3.1) becomes

`F_G(S)
 = g_G(S)
 + sum_{y in L_A(S)} F_G(S union {y})
 + sum_{y in L_B(S)} F_G(S union {y})`.                  (7.1)

Under the uniform no-further-birth suffix law from `S`, the probability that the very next vertex is the first merge is exactly

`lambda_G(S)=g_G(S)/F_G(S)`.                             (7.2)

Thus the relevant positive process is an absorbing Markov growth process on two-component states. Its unresolved state information is not merely component sizes or root distance: the `F`-weighted interface geometry enters through `g_G(S)` and through the conductance root laws (5.2).
