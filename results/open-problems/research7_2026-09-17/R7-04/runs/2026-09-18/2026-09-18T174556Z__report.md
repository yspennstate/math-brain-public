## 5. Method B18 — translate peaks into restart times

Write a labeling as its descending-label vertex order

`v_1,...,v_N`, `N=n^2`,

and let `S_k={v_1,...,v_k}`. The new vertex `v_k` is a peak of the completed labeling exactly when

`N(v_k) ∩ S_{k-1} = empty`.

Reason: every previously revealed vertex has a larger label and every unrevealed vertex has a smaller label. Hence a vertex is a local maximum iff none of its neighbors occurred earlier.

Therefore:

> **The peaks of a labeling are exactly the births/restarts of the descending reveal process.**

This is a bijective exact translation. A two-peak labeling is a permutation with exactly two births, and peak distance is the distance between the two birth vertices.

This is stronger for the present purpose than the previous campaign's acyclic-orientation formulation because it keeps the time of the second peak visible.

## 6. Method B25 — exact double count and subset recurrence

For `S subset V` and `r in S`, let `A_S(r)` be the number of descending orders of `S` with exactly one birth, rooted at `r`. Then

`A_{ {r} }(r)=1`,

and appending `v notin S` preserves the one-birth state iff `N(v)∩S != empty`.

Let `B_S` count orders of `S` with exactly two births, and let `M_S` sum the distance between their two births. If `v` is appended to a one-birth state and `N(v)∩S=empty`, it creates the second birth and

`B_{S union {v}} += A_S(r)`,

`M_{S union {v}} += d(r,v) A_S(r)`.

After two births, only vertices touching the current revealed set may be appended; otherwise a third birth would occur. Hence at the full vertex set,

`B_V = #{two-peak labelings}`,

`M_V = sum of twin-peak distances over those labelings`,

and `M_V/B_V` is the exact conditioned mean distance.

There is also a factorized ordered-pair formula. Let `F(T)` be the number of no-further-birth completions from seed set `T`, with

`F(V)=1`,

`F(T)=sum_{v notin T, N(v)∩T != empty} F(T union {v})`.

For ordered nonadjacent peaks `(r,v)`, first/high peak `r` and second/lower peak `v`, define `W(r,v)` as the number of full labelings with those ordered peak identities. Then

`W(r,v) = sum_{S: r in S, v notin S, N(v)∩S=empty} A_S(r) F(S union {v}).`   (6.1)

The prefix ordering, second-birth choice, and suffix completion are unique, so (6.1) is an exact partition of the labelings. Consequently

`B_V=sum_{r!=v}W(r,v)`,

`M_V=sum_{r!=v} d(r,v) W(r,v)`.

For every connected graph and every nonadjacent `r,v`, `W(r,v)>0`: reveal `r`, then `v`, then grow the revealed union by boundary vertices. Thus the square-grid problem is a **weight-profile** question, not a support question.

## 8. New structural bottleneck

Formula (6.1) separates the old weighted-orientation obstruction into two exact objects:

1. `A_S(r)`: a rooted one-birth/connected-growth prefix count;
2. `F(S union {v})`: a no-further-birth completion count from the two-seed state.

There are `Theta(n^4)` macroscopically separated vertex pairs but only `o(n^4)` pairs at distance `o(n)`. Therefore a sufficiently uniform comparison of the aggregate weights `W(r,v)` across nonadjacent pairs would force macroscopic peak separation and refute the source convergence. The unresolved issue is whether `W(r,v)` can vary strongly enough with separation/location to overcome that pair entropy.

Burdzy–Pal's regular-tree theorem gives an analogous phenomenon in a solvable geometry: conditioned pair weights away from the root are comparable in a fixed-degree regular tree, and their twin-peak distance concentrates on the tree scale. That is suggestive, not a transferable grid theorem.
