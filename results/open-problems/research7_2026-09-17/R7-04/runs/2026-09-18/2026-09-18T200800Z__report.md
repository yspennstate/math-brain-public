## 4. B3 — prove something stronger: exact actual two-peak pair law

The previous report controlled the actual pair weight by a rank-2 completion weight up to a factor `N-1`.  The stronger goal this run was to eliminate that surrogate entirely.

Let `G=(V,E)` be finite connected, `|V|=N`.  For an independent set `I`, write `N[I]` for its closed neighbourhood and define

`b(empty)=1`,

`b(I) = |N[I]|^{-1} sum_{x in I} b(I\{x})`.                 (4.1)

### Lemma 4.1

In a uniformly random relative order, the probability that every `x in I` precedes all neighbours of `x` equals `b(I)`.

**Proof.**  The earliest element of `N[I]` is uniform.  On the event in question it must be some `x in I`.  Conditional on that choice, because `I` is independent, deleting `x` leaves the same precedence constraints for `I\{x}` and a uniform residual order.  Summing over the possible earliest `x` gives exactly (4.1).  QED.

Fix the highest peak `r`, equivalently condition that `r` is first in descending label order.  An additional peak can only lie in

`U_r := V \ N[r]`.

For `x in U_r`, let `B_x` be the event that `x` precedes every neighbour.  Then `B_x` is precisely the event that `x` is an additional peak.  For independent `I subset U_r`, the root lies outside `N[I]`, so Lemma 4.1 gives

`P(intersection_{x in I} B_x | r first)=b(I)`.              (4.2)

For nonadjacent `r,v`, let `W(r,v)` count full descending orders with exactly two peaks, highest peak `r` and the other peak `v`.

### Theorem 4.2 — exact root-pinned one-defect formula

`W(r,v)=(N-1)! C(r,v)`,                                    (4.3)

where

`C(r,v)= sum_{I independent, I subset U_r, v in I}
           (-1)^(|I|-1) b(I).`                              (4.4)

**Proof.**  Exactly one additional peak, namely `v`, is the event

`B_v intersect intersection_{x in U_r\{v}} complement(B_x)`.

Apply inclusion-exclusion.  A term indexed by `J subset U_r\{v}` vanishes unless `I=J union {v}` is independent; if independent, (4.2) gives probability `b(I)`.  Reindexing gives (4.4).  After fixing `r` first there are `(N-1)!` equiprobable residual orders, proving (4.3).  QED.

Therefore the **actual** ordered pair law conditioned on exactly two peaks is

`P((r,v) | exactly two peaks)
 = C(r,v) / sum_{a,b} C(a,b)`,                               (4.5)

and, in particular,

`E[rho_n/n | two peaks]
 = [sum_{r,v} d(r,v) C_n(r,v)]
   / [n sum_{r,v} C_n(r,v)].`                                (4.6)

This removes the previous polynomial promotion loss completely.  It is a source-relevant exact reduction, but not an asymptotic solution.

A companion identity also gives the previous rank-2 completion count:

`F({r,v})/(N-2)!
 = sum_{I independent, I cap (N[r] union N[v]) = empty}
      (-1)^|I| b(I).`                                       (4.7)

Thus the true pair law and rank-2 law are two evaluations of the same independent-set machinery.
