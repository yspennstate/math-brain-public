## B18 — reliability / cluster reformulation

Let the distinct minimal witnesses of a general up-class `A` be `B_1,...,B_m`, and for nonempty `F subseteq [m]` let

`U(F)=union_{j in F} B_j`.

For every deterministic `J`, finite inclusion-exclusion gives

`theta_{J,alpha}(A)
 = sum_{empty != F subseteq [m]}
   (-1)^{|F|+1} alpha^{|U(F)|} 1_{U(F) subseteq J}.`                  (4)

Therefore

`W_alpha(nu)(A)
 = sum_{empty != F subseteq [m]}
   (-1)^{|F|+1} alpha^{|U(F)|} nu(H_{U(F)}),`                         (5)

while

`mu_p(A)
 = sum_{empty != F subseteq [m]}
   (-1)^{|F|+1} p^{|U(F)|}.`                                         (6)

This identifies the gluing failure exactly. Spreadness gives only

`nu(H_U)<=(alpha p)^{|U|}`.                                          (7)

In (5), (7) helps on odd witness clusters, whose coefficients are positive, but is in the wrong direction on even clusters, whose coefficients are negative. Thus a direct termwise comparison with (6) is impossible without lower information on selected union-marginals, or without replacing the alternating expansion by a positive majorant.

The previous disjoint, Janson, sunflower, triangle and path proofs can all be viewed as ways to restore precisely that missing information. B27 below constructs the simplest general positive majorant in the source's own Hahn-Banach dual language.

## B27 — swap the quantifiers and construct one dual law for all J

Talagrand's dual form uses

`h(I,J)=(alpha p)^(-|I|) 1_{I subseteq J}` for nonempty `I`,

and `h(emptyset,J)=1`. It asks for one probability measure `eta`, independent of `J`, satisfying

`theta_{J,alpha}(A)
 <= mu_p(A) integral h(I,J)deta(I)`                                  (8)

for every `J`.

### Explicit cover-supported dual theorem

Let `C` be any finite family of nonempty subsets of `S` with

`A subseteq union_{I in C} H_I`,                                     (9)

and set `q=alpha^2p`. If

`sum_{I in C} q^{|I|} <= mu_p(A)`,                                   (10)

then (8) holds, hence `W_alpha(nu)(A)<=mu_p(A)` for every `alpha p`-spread `nu`.

Proof: write `m_A=mu_p(A)` and

`M=m_A^{-1} sum_{I in C}q^{|I|}<=1`.

Put mass `q^{|I|}/m_A` on every `I in C` and the leftover mass `1-M` at `emptyset`. If an `alpha`-thinning of `J` enters `A`, (9) implies that at least one `I in C` contained in `J` survives, so the union bound gives

`theta_{J,alpha}(A)
 <= sum_{I in C:I subseteq J} alpha^{|I|}.`                           (11)

For the constructed `eta`,

`m_A integral h(I,J)deta(I)
 = m_A(1-M)
   + sum_{I in C:I subseteq J}(alpha p)^(-|I|)q^{|I|}`

`=m_A(1-M)+sum_{I in C:I subseteq J}alpha^{|I|}`,                     (12)

because `q/(alpha p)=alpha`. Equation (12) dominates (11), proving the dual inequality for all `J` at once. Integrating against an `alpha p`-spread law finishes the source inequality. `QED`

Define the principal-upset cover cost

`Phi_q(A)=min_C sum_{I in C}q^{|I|}`

over finite nonempty-set covers satisfying (9). Then every source counterexample must satisfy the strict necessary condition

`Phi_{alpha^2p}(A)>mu_p(A)`.                                         (13)

This is not an equivalence: when (13) holds, a more efficient dual law may still share probability mass across many `J`-constraints.

### Witness-count barrier

Let the minimal witnesses be `B_1,...,B_m` and let

`k=min_j |B_j|`.

Using the minimal witnesses as the cover,

`Phi_{alpha^2p}(A)
 <= sum_j(alpha^2p)^{|B_j|}

Since `A` contains one principal upset of size `k`,

`mu_p(A)>=p^k`.

Therefore

`m alpha^{2k}<=1`                                                     (14)

is a complete sufficient condition for source domination on this `A`, uniformly over all `alpha p`-spread laws. Equivalently, a counterexample must have

`m>alpha^{-2k}`.                                                       (15)

Throughout the only viable universal range `alpha<=alpha_*`, this implies
