# New theorem: oriented-star conflict domination

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and

`A_G={K: K contains both endpoints of at least one edge of G}`.

Fix an orientation `D` of all edges. For every vertex `v` of positive outdegree let

`N^+(v)={u: v->u in D}`,
`d_v=|N^+(v)|`,
`C_v={v} union N^+(v)`,

and let

`H_v={K: v in K and K cap N^+(v) != emptyset}`.

Because every edge is oriented exactly once,

`A_G=union_v H_v`.                                                     (1)

For `0<p<1`, define

`h_v(p)=mu_p(H_v)=p[1-(1-p)^{d_v}]`,
`Lambda_D(p)=sum_v h_v(p)`.

Define the support-conflict graph `Gamma_D` on the active vertices by joining `v,w` iff

`C_v cap C_w != emptyset`.

Finally define the weighted independent-set fraction

`rho_D(p)=max_{I independent in Gamma_D} [sum_{v in I}h_v(p)]/Lambda_D(p)`.        (2)

### Theorem

If

`g(alpha):=-alpha log(1-alpha) <= rho_D(p)`,                          (3)

then for every `alpha p`-spread law `nu`,

`W_alpha(nu)(A_G) <= mu_p(A_G)`.                                      (4)

If `Gamma_D` is `chi`-colorable, then `rho_D(p)>=1/chi`, so the simpler sufficient condition

`g(alpha)<=1/chi`                                                      (5)

holds uniformly in `p`.

## Complete proof

Sample `J~nu` and condition on `J`. Under independent `alpha`-thinning, put

`D_v(J)=|N^+(v) cap J|`

and

`x_v(J)=P(H_v | J)
       =alpha 1_{v in J}[1-(1-alpha)^{D_v(J)}].`                       (6)

Every `H_v^c` is decreasing in the independent retention bits, so conditional Harris/FKG and (1) give

`P(K notin A_G | J) >= product_v(1-x_v(J)).`                          (7)

Let `phi(x)=-log(1-x)`. Since `0<=x_v<=alpha`, convexity of `phi` and `phi(0)=0` imply

`phi(x_v) <= [-log(1-alpha)/alpha]x_v`.                               (8)

We next bound `E x_v` using only spreadness. Put `r=alpha p` and

`q_v=P(v in J)<=r`.

If `q_v=0` there is nothing to prove. Otherwise two-point spreadness gives, for each `u in N^+(v)`,

`P(u,v in J)<=r^2`,

hence

`E[D_v(J)|v in J] <= d_v r^2/q_v`.                                   (9)

The real function

`f(t)=1-(1-alpha)^t`

is increasing and concave. Therefore

`E x_v
 <= alpha q_v f(d_v r^2/q_v).`                                   (10)

For fixed `C>0`, `q f(C/q)` is increasing in `q`: writing `a=-log(1-alpha)` and `z=aC/q`, its derivative is

`1-e^{-z}(1+z)>0`.

Since `q_v<=r`, (10) yields

`E x_v
 <= alpha r[1-(1-alpha)^{d_v r}]
 = alpha^2 p[1-(1-alpha)^{d_v alpha p}].`                             (11)

Assumption (3) implies `g(alpha)<=1`. Consequently

`alpha p[-log(1-alpha)]=p g(alpha)<=p<=-log(1-p)`,

so

`(1-alpha)^{alpha p} >= 1-p`.                                         (12)

Using (12) in (11),

`E x_v <= alpha^2 p[1-(1-p)^{d_v}]
          =alpha^2 h_v(p).`                                            (13)

Average (7). Since `y->e^{-y}` is convex, Jensen plus (8),(13) gives

`W_alpha(nu)(A_G^c)
 >= exp[-sum_v E phi(x_v)]
 >= exp[-g(alpha)Lambda_D(p)].`                                       (14)

Now let `I` be a maximum-weight independent set in `Gamma_D`. The support sets `C_v`, `v in I`, are pairwise disjoint, so the events `H_v`, `v in I`, are independent under product measure. Since `A_G` contains their union,

`mu_p(A_G^c)
 <= product_{v in I}(1-h_v(p))
 <= exp[-sum_{v in I}h_v(p)]
 = exp[-rho_D(p)Lambda_D(p)].`                                        (15)

Combining (3), (14), and (15),

`W_alpha(nu)(A_G^c)>=mu_p(A_G^c)`,

which is (4) after taking complements. If `Gamma_D` has `chi` color classes, their hazard weights sum to `Lambda_D(p)`, so one class has weight at least `Lambda_D(p)/chi`; hence `rho_D(p)>=1/chi`. This proves (5). `QED`

# B15 — break symmetry on purpose: double-stars

Take adjacent hubs `u,v`, with `a` pendant leaves at `u` and `b` pendant leaves at `v`. Orient `u->v`, all `u` leaves away from `u`, and all `v` leaves away from `v`.

The only two active hazards are

`h_u=p[1-(1-p)^{a+1}]`,
`h_v=p[1-(1-p)^b]`.

Their supports intersect at `v`, so

`rho_D(p)=max(h_u,h_v)/(h_u+h_v)`.                                    (16)

Therefore the new theorem proves domination whenever

`g(alpha)<=max(h_u,h_v)/(h_u+h_v)`.                                  (17)
