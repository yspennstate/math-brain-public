## 4. B11 — dualize: root barycenters and a positive second-moment energy

Embed `G_n` at lattice coordinates in `{0,...,n-1}^2`. For a connected component `C`, set

`b(C):=E_{mu_C}R_C`,

`V(C):=E_{mu_C}||R_C-b(C)||_2^2`.                                  (4.1)

For a singleton these mean its unique coordinate and zero variance.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact conductance moment formulas

For `|C|>=2`, writing `C_C=sum_e c_C(e)`,

`b(C)=[1/(2C_C)] sum_{ {x,y} in E(C)}c_C({x,y})(x+y)`,              (4.2)

and

`V(C)=[1/(2C_C)] sum_{ {x,y} in E(C)}c_C({x,y})
       (||x-b(C)||_2^2+||y-b(C)||_2^2)`.                            (4.3)

**Proof.** Equation (3.3) says `mu_C(x)=deg_c(x)/(2C_C)`. Therefore

`sum_x mu_C(x)x=[1/(2C_C)]sum_x sum_{y~x}c_C({x,y})x`.

Every undirected edge contributes its two endpoints once, proving (4.2). Replacing `x` by `||x-b(C)||_2^2` in the same degree-sum gives (4.3). QED.

Thus the first two moments of the peak-root law are exact positive edge geometry, not an alternating expression.

### Theorem 4.2 — Lipschitz-dual barycenter lower bound

For every first-merge state `S=A union B`,

`E d_1(R_A,R_B) >= ||b(A)-b(B)||_1`.                               (4.4)

**Proof.** If `||t||_infty<=1`, then `f_t(z)=t dot z` is 1-Lipschitz for `d_1`, so `d_1(x,y)>=t dot(x-y)`. Take expectations and then the supremum over the unit `l^infty` ball; the dual norm is `l^1`. QED.

### Theorem 4.3 — dispersion-energy lower bound

Define

`q_n(S):=||b(A)-b(B)||_2^2+V(A)+V(B)`.                              (4.5)

Then

`E d_1(R_A,R_B) >= q_n(S)/[2(n-1)]`.                               (4.6)

**Proof.** Independence gives the exact centered identity

`E||R_A-R_B||_2^2
 = ||b(A)-b(B)||_2^2+V(A)+V(B)=q_n(S)`.                            (4.7)

On `G_n`, every pair satisfies

`||x-y||_2^2 <= d_1(x,y)^2 <= 2(n-1)d_1(x,y)`,                     (4.8)

because `d_1(x,y)<=2(n-1)`. Take expectations and combine with (4.7). QED.

Insert (4.6) into the exact positive mixture (3.2):

`E[rho_n/n | two peaks]
 >= [sum_S Omega_n(S)q_n(S)]/[2n(n-1)sum_S Omega_n(S)]`.            (4.9)

Define only as an auxiliary progress coordinate

`Xi_n := [sum_S Omega_n(S)q_n(S)]/[n^2 sum_S Omega_n(S)]`.          (4.10)

Then

`D_* >= (1/2) limsup_n Xi_n`.                                      (4.11)

The frozen metric remains `D_*`; `Xi_n` is not a replacement metric.

### B11 obstruction found

A statewise barycenter-separation claim is false. On an odd square choose a centered inner square `B`, a one-lattice-layer unrevealed moat, and a centered thick outer annulus `A`. The two components have no edge, have common-boundary merge sites in the moat, and are invariant under the coordinate reflections. Since `h_C` and `mu_C` are automorphism invariant, `b(A)=b(B)` is the grid center, so (4.4) gives zero. Yet every annulus vertex lies a macroscopic distance from its center, hence `V(A)=Theta(n^2)`. This is why the variance terms in `q_n` are essential. It does **not** prove that such annular states have non-negligible `Omega_n` mass.

## 5. B12 — conserved two-front occupation and absorption flux

Let `T_2(omega)` be the rank of the second birth and `T_m(omega)` the rank of the unique first merge in a full descending order `omega` with exactly two births. Set

`I_s(omega)=1{T_2(omega)<=s<T_m(omega)}`.                            (5.1)

Trajectory by trajectory,

`I_s-I_{s-1}=1{T_2=s}-1{T_m=s}`.                                   (5.2)

For a two-component set `S=A union B` of size `s`, define

`P_G(S)=binom(s,|A|)R(A)R(B)`.                                     (5.3)

### Theorem 5.1 — active-state and merge-flux counts

`P_G(S)` is exactly the number of two-birth prefixes with revealed set `S`. Therefore

`Q_G(S):=P_G(S)F_G(S)`                                             (5.4)

is exactly the number of full exactly-two-birth orders active at state `S`, while

`Omega_G(S)=P_G(S)g_G(S)`                                         (5.5)

is exactly the number whose first merge occurs on the next step from `S`. In particular

`Omega_G(S)/Q_G(S)=g_G(S)/F_G(S)=lambda_G(S)`.                     (5.6)

**Proof.** Choose independently a one-birth successive order on `A` and on `B`, then interleave the two internal orders in `binom(s,|A|)` ways. Because there is no cross-edge, the root of whichever component appears second globally is the second birth and all later prefix vertices attach internally. Restriction to the two components reverses the construction uniquely. This proves (5.3). A birth-free suffix gives (5.4); choosing a common-boundary merge vertex and then a birth-free suffix gives (5.5). Division gives (5.6). QED.

Let

`A_s=sum_{|S|=s,kappa(G[S])=2}Q_G(S)`,

`J_s=# {omega:T_2(omega)=s}`,

`M_s=# {omega:T_m(omega)=s}`.

Summing (5.2) over all exactly-two-birth orders gives the exact conservation equation

`A_s-A_{s-1}=J_s-M_s`,                                            (5.7)

and the absorption slice is

`M_{s+1}=sum_{|S|=s,kappa(G[S])=2}Omega_G(S)`.                     (5.8)

Summing over time gives a Little-law identity

`sum_s A_s = sum_omega (T_m(omega)-T_2(omega))`,                   (5.9)

hence

`[sum_s A_s]/Z_2(G)=E[T_m-T_2 | exactly two peaks]`.               (5.10)

So `Omega/Z_2` is precisely the **absorption-flux law** of the positive active two-front measure `Q`, and `lambda=g/F` is its statewise merge hazard. This gives a second way to attack (4.11): prove high root energy under the active occupation law and show the merge hazard does not disproportionately suppress those states.

The flux identity remains valid after multiplying (5.2) by any fixed nonnegative symmetric function of the two peak roots, so root observables can be transported through the conservation law without returning to the signed independent-set formula.

`widehatOmega_n{S:||b(A)-b(B)||_1>=eps n}>=eta`,                    (6.3)
