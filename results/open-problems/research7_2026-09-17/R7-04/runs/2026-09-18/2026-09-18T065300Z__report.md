## B14 — exact symmetry reduction

Let `Gamma<=Sym(S)` preserve an up-class `A`. For any `r`-spread law `nu`, define

`bar(nu)=|Gamma|^{-1} sum_{g in Gamma} g_#nu`.

For every `I subseteq S`,

`bar(nu)(H_I)
 = |Gamma|^{-1} sum_g nu(H_{g^{-1}I})
 <= r^{|I|}`,

so `bar(nu)` is still `r`-spread. Since independent thinning commutes with coordinate permutations and `A` is `Gamma`-invariant,

`W_t(bar(nu))(A)=W_t(nu)(A)`.

Thus an invariant maximizer always exists.

For the threshold up-class

`A_{n,k}={K:|K|>=k}`,

an invariant law is determined by `x_j=P(|J|=j)` and the exact spread constraints become

`sum_{j=s}^n x_j binom(j,s)/binom(n,s) <= r^s`, `s=1,...,n`.

The objective is

`sum_j x_j P(Bin(j,t)>=k)`.

Hence fully symmetric source instances reduce exactly to a one-dimensional factorial-moment linear program. This introduces no exchangeability assumption: symmetry is obtained by averaging an optimizer.

## B24 — exact solution of the first dense non-sunflower pattern

Take

`S={1,2,3}`

and the minimal witnesses

`{1,2}`, `{1,3}`, `{2,3}`.

The up-class is

`A_triangle={K:|K|>=2}`.

It is the smallest connected witness pattern left by the previous run's reductions: all witness pairs overlap, but the three witnesses have no common core and therefore do not form a sunflower.

Write `r=alpha p`. By B14, average over `S_3` and set

`x_j=P(|J|=j)`.

The exact spread constraints are

`(x_1+2x_2+3x_3)/3 <= r`,

`x_2/3+x_3 <= r^2`,

`x_3<=r^3`,

`sum_jx_j=1`, `x_j>=0`.

The objective is

`alpha^2 x_2 + alpha^2(3-2alpha)x_3`.

Moving `x_1` to `x_0` preserves the objective and relaxes all constraints, so take `x_1=0`. For fixed `x_3`, maximize `x_2`:

`x_2 <= min{1-x_3, (3/2)(r-x_3), 3(r^2-x_3)}`.       (1)

The pair/single, pair/mass and single/mass intersections are respectively

`A=2r^2-r`,

`B=(3r^2-1)/2`,

`C=3r-2`,

and

`A-B=(r-1)^2/2>=0`,

`A-C=2(r-1)^2>=0`,

`r^3-A=r(r-1)^2>=0`.                                  (2)

### Exact optimizer when `alpha<=alpha_*`

If `r<=1/2`, the pair constraint in (1) is active throughout the feasible interval. Along it the coefficient of `x_3` in the objective is

`alpha^2[(3-2alpha)-3]=-2alpha^3<0`.

Therefore

`x_3=0`, `x_2=3r^2`, `x_0=1-3r^2`

is optimal, and

`sup W_alpha(nu)(A_triangle)=3alpha^2r^2`.             (3)

If `r>=1/2`, the active bound before the pair line is either the mass line and then the singleton line, or the singleton line alone. Along the mass line the objective slope is

`2alpha^2(1-alpha)>0`,

and along the singleton line it is

`alpha^2(3/2-2alpha)>=0`

because `alpha<=alpha_*<3/4`. After the pair/single intersection `x_3=A`, the slope is `-2alpha^3<0`. By (2), `A` is feasible. Hence the exact optimizer is

`x_3=2r^2-r`,

`x_2=3r(1-r)`,

`x_0=(1-r)^2`,

and

`sup W_alpha(nu)(A_triangle)
 = alpha^2 r(3r+2alpha-4alpha r)`.                     (4)

### Strict product domination

Under `mu_p`,

`mu_p(A_triangle)=p^2(3-2p)`.

From (3), in the `r<=1/2` regime the ratio is at most

`3alpha^4/(3-2p) <= 3alpha_*^4
 = 0.9032676147... <1`.

In the `r>=1/2` regime, `p>=1/(2alpha)` and (4) gives the exact ratio

`R(alpha,p)
 = alpha^4 [2+p(3-4alpha)]/[p(3-2p)]`.

Because `alpha<=alpha_*<3/4`,

`2+p(3-4alpha)<=5-4alpha`.

The concave denominator factor `p(3-2p)` is at least one on `p in [1/(2alpha),1]`: it equals one at `p=1`, and at the other endpoint it is

`(3alpha-1)/(2alpha^2)>=1`

for `alpha>=1/2`. Therefore

`R(alpha,p)
 <= alpha^4(5-4alpha)
 <= alpha_*^4(5-4alpha_*)
 = 0.6133143225... <1`.

Thus, for **every** `alpha<=alpha_*`, every `p`, and every `alpha p`-spread law,

`W_alpha(nu)(A_triangle) < mu_p(A_triangle)`.

This completely rules out the first dense non-sunflower witness complex in the entire parameter interval not already forbidden by the singleton obstruction.

It is a restricted theorem, not source closure.

## B5 — universal validity is downward closed

Let `P(a)` denote the exact universal source statement at parameter `a`.

### Theorem

If `P(beta)` holds for `0<beta<1`, then `P(alpha)` holds for every `0<alpha<beta`.

### Proof

Let `nu` be `alpha p`-spread. Put

`p'=alpha p/beta`.

Then `nu` is `beta p'`-spread. Assuming `P(beta)`,

`W_beta(nu) <=_st mu_{p'}`.

Thin both sides with retention `alpha/beta`. Thinning preserves stochastic order and composes, so

`W_alpha(nu)
 <=_st mu_{(alpha/beta)p'}
 = mu_{alpha^2p/beta^2}
 <=_st mu_p`.

Hence `P(alpha)` holds. `QED`

Therefore the universally valid source parameters form a lower interval, up to possible endpoint inclusion. In particular, a failure at one parameter forces failure at all larger parameters by contraposition.

## B5 — an exact Russo-flow formula

Fix one finite up-class `A`, one `r`-spread law `nu`, and define for `t in (r,1)`

`p(t)=r/t`,

`D(t)=mu_{p(t)}(A)-W_t(nu)(A)`.

The same `nu` remains exactly eligible along this curve because `tp(t)=r`.

Russo's formula on the full cube and on each conditional subcube gives

`D'(t)
 = -(r/t^2) sum_i Inf_i^{r/t}(A)
   - E_{J~nu} sum_{i in J} Inf_i^{J,t}(A)
 <=0`.                                                (5)

Thus every fixed-spread instance gap is nonincreasing. If the same `(r,nu,A)` fails at some `t_0`, it continues to fail for every larger `t` after adjusting the target product density to `r/t`.

The exact unresolved analytic step is now a barrier inequality: spreadness would need to control the second pivotal sum in (5), or the total negative derivative, strongly enough that a positive gap cannot cross zero before some universal `t>0`. No such dimension-free barrier is proved here.
