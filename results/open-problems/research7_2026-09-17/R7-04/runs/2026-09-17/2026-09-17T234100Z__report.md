## 4. B12 — exact relative flattening theorem for the disagreement field

Let

\[
Q=\sup_x q_x>0.
\]

The exact parent identity is

\[
A_x(\xi)-A_x(\eta)
=\frac1{n(n-1)}\sum_{y\ne z}(D_y\xi_z+\eta_yD_z).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
0\le
\mathbb E[(1-\xi_x)(A_x(\xi)-A_x(\eta))]
\le 2Q,
\]

while

\[
0\le \mathbb E[D_xA_x(\eta)]\le Q.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{-Q\le \Gamma_x\le 2Q.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The exact stationary disagreement equation from the preceding run is

\[
0=\varepsilon^{-2}\Delta_s q(x)-q_x+\beta\Gamma_x.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because `beta>4.5`, (4.2) gives

\[
\boxed{\|\Delta_s q\|_\infty\le2\beta\varepsilon^2Q.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Normalize `h=q/Q`.  Then

\[
0\le h\le1,
\qquad
\|\Delta_sh\|_\infty\le2\beta\varepsilon^2.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Let `P_t` be the continuous-time nearest-neighbor random-walk semigroup with generator `Delta_s`.  Duhamel yields

\[
|h(x)-P_th(x)|\le2\beta\varepsilon^2t.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus for every fixed lattice displacement `r`,

\[
\sup_x|h(x+r)-h(x)|
\le4\beta\varepsilon^2t+
\|P_t(r,\cdot)-P_t(0,\cdot)\|_{TV}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The total-variation term tends to zero as `t->infinity`.  A direct coupling proof is enough: represent the coordinates by independent one-dimensional continuous-time symmetric walks, reflection-couple each coordinate until meeting, then synchronize; every coordinate coupling time is almost surely finite.

Choose any `t(epsilon)->infinity` with `epsilon^2 t(epsilon)->0`, e.g. `t=epsilon^{-1}`.  Then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_x\frac{|q_{x+r}-q_x|}{Q}\to0
\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0)}
\tag{4.8}
\]

for every fixed `r`.

Consequently, at any approximate-maximal disagreement site `x_epsilon` with `q_x/Q->1`,

\[
\boxed{q_{x_\varepsilon+r}/q_{x_\varepsilon}\to1}
\tag{4.9}
\]

for all finitely many source-neighborhood offsets `r`.

**Outcome.**  One part of the Palm bridge is now rigorous in the required *relative* sense: all local `q_y/q_x` factors in the exact Palm reproduction identity converge to `1`; the old absolute-error-over-rare-`q` problem does not occur for the one-tag profile.

## 5. B3 — stronger tagged-moment hierarchy

Use the three ordered-coupling colors

\[
A_x=\eta_x,\qquad D_x=\xi_x-\eta_x,\qquad C_x=1-\xi_x.
\]

For pairwise distinct sites `x_1,...,x_m` and one-site labels `alpha_i in {A,D,C}` with at least one `D`, define

\[
H_\alpha(\mathbf x)
=\mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^m\alpha_i(x_i).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Pure stirring transports the colors by edge transpositions.  If `S_m` is the `m`-label stirring generator on injective tuples, then exactly

\[
\mathbb E S\left(\prod_i\alpha_i(x_i)\right)
=(S_mH_\alpha)(\mathbf x).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The nonstirring reaction contribution is also proportional to `Q`.  Reactions at displayed sites not carrying the chosen discrepancy factor contribute only while that discrepancy is present, hence `O(Q)`.  At a displayed discrepancy site, destruction is again `O(Q)`, while creation of a new disagreement has mean rate

\[
\beta\mathbb E[(1-\xi_x)(A_x(\xi)-A_x(\eta))]
\le2\beta Q.
\]

Thus, for a constant independent of `epsilon,Q` and positions,

\[
\left|\mathbb ER\left(\prod_i\alpha_i(x_i)\right)\right|
\le C_{m,\beta}Q;
\tag{5.3}
\]

`C_{m,beta}=4m(1+beta)` is a safe source-level choice.

Stationarity under `L_epsilon=epsilon^{-2}S+R` therefore gives the new hierarchy

\[
\boxed{
|(S_mH_\alpha)(\mathbf x)|
\le C_{m,\beta}\varepsilon^2Q.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

After dividing by `Q`,

\[
h_\alpha:=H_\alpha/Q
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{\|S_mh_\alpha\|_\infty\le C_{m,\beta}\varepsilon^2.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let `P_t^(m)` be the finite-label stirring semigroup.  Duhamel gives

\[
\|h_\alpha-P_t^{(m)}h_\alpha\|_\infty
\le C_{m,\beta}\varepsilon^2t,
\tag{5.6}
\]

and therefore

\[
|h_\alpha(\mathbf x)-h_\alpha(\mathbf x')|
\le2C_{m,\beta}\varepsilon^2t
+\|P_t^{(m)}(\mathbf x,\cdot)-P_t^{(m)}(\mathbf x',\cdot)\|_{TV}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is a stronger reduction than the previous block heuristic.  The exact two- and three-point numerators in the Palm identity now have an `O(epsilon^2)` *relative* stirring defect.  The unknown small disagreement density has disappeared from the analytic error term.

I also proved in the artifact a finite-state sector-Poisson lemma: for a finite connected color-interchange system, any observable vanishing on the zero-disagreement sector satisfies a relative perturbation estimate

\[
\frac{|\mu(f-\Pi f)|}{Q}
\le2\varepsilon^2M^2(1+\beta)\|g\|_\infty,
\tag{5.8}
\]

where `Pi` is uniform averaging inside a fixed color-count sector and `Sg=f-Pi f`.  This is not a finite-volume solution of the source problem — the absorbing finite source process has no positive stationary phase — but it proves that the `1/Q` loss is not intrinsic to fast stirring.

**Outcome.**  The remaining averaging question is a pure finite-label stirring/Poisson problem rather than a rare-event perturbation problem.
