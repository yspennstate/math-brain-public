## 3. B5 — add an interpolation parameter and differentiate

### 3.1 Exact interaction operator

For a dual configuration `xi`, let `P_y` be the Bernoulli(`q`) heat-bath operator that resamples membership at site `y`, and write

```text
D_y=I-P_y.
```

On a finite cycle the simultaneous-neighbor-refresh generator `Q` and the separate-clock BABP generator `B` are

```text
Q = sum_x xi_x (P_{x-1}P_{x+1}-I),                  (3.1)
B = sum_x xi_x[(P_{x-1}-I)+(P_{x+1}-I)].            (3.2)
```

Subtracting gives the exact identity

```text
Q-B
 = sum_x xi_x (I-P_{x-1})(I-P_{x+1})
 =: H.                                               (3.3)
```

Because `P_{x-1}` and `P_{x+1}` are commuting conditional-expectation projections and multiplication by `xi_x` commutes with them, each summand of `H` is positive semidefinite in the reversible finite-cycle Hilbert space. This algebra is the mechanism behind (2.3): BABP has the extra dissipation of the genuine two-neighbor interaction component.

The convex interpolation

```text
L_theta=(1-theta)Q+theta B=Q-theta H,
0<=theta<=1,                                         (3.4)
```

is a valid Markov-generator interpolation preserving the same reversible finite-cycle law. If `P_t^(theta)=exp(tL_theta)`, Duhamel's formula gives

```text
partial_theta P_t^(theta) f
 = - integral_0^t
       P_s^(theta) H P_{t-s}^(theta) f ds.           (3.5)
```

This is a sharper statement than the factor-two form comparison: a transfer from BABP to `Q` fails only through the spacetime accumulation of the explicit three-site interaction operator `H`.

### 3.2 One-site occupation does not see the interpolation until third order

Take `f(xi)=xi_0`. Directly,

```text
H f=0,                                               (3.6)
Q f=(xi_{-1}+xi_1)(q-xi_0),                         (3.7)
H Q f=0.                                             (3.8)
```

Equation (3.8) is not a cancellation accident: `Qf` is a sum of functions of adjacent coordinate pairs, whereas every `H` summand extracts dependence simultaneously on two sites at distance two around one occupied center.

Start from the singleton `A_0={0}`. Since the only initially enabled center is zero,

```text
L_theta f({0})=0.                                    (3.9)
```

Using (3.7), after the first simultaneous refresh of the two neighbors one obtains

```text
L_theta^2 f({0})=Q^2 f({0})=-2 p q.                 (3.10)
```

To compute the first interpolation-sensitive order, set

```text
b=xi_{-1}, c=xi_0, d=xi_1.
```

At `c=1` and `xi_{-2}=xi_2=0`, a direct application of `Q` to (3.7) gives

```text
Q^2 f
 = -2 p q + 2p(b+d)+2p b d.                         (3.11)
```

At the singleton state, the only nonzero term of `H Q^2 f` is the `x=0` mixed-neighbor projection. Since

```text
D_{-1}D_1 (b d)=(b-q)(d-q),
```

we get

```text
H Q^2 f({0})=2 p q^2.                                (3.12)
```

Also, averaging (3.11) over the first `Q` refresh and subtracting its singleton value gives

```text
Q^3 f({0})=2 p q(2+q).                               (3.13)
```

Therefore

```text
L_theta^3 f({0})
 = Q^3 f({0})-theta H Q^2 f({0})
 = 2 p q(2+q-theta q).                              (3.14)
```

The exact small-time expansion is consequently

```text
P_{ {0} }^(theta)(0 in A_t)
 = 1-pq t^2
   + [pq(2+q-theta q)/3] t^3
   + O(t^4).                                         (3.15)
```

In particular,

```text
P^Q_{ {0} }(0 in A_t)-P^B_{ {0} }(0 in A_t)
 = (p q^2/3)t^3+O(t^4).                              (3.16)
```

So the simultaneous-refresh dual and BABP have exactly the same first and second local Taylor orders, and first separate at third order through a true three-site correlation. Near time zero, `Q` retains the singleton origin slightly more strongly than BABP.

### B5 outcome

This kills the naive inference “factor-two Dirichlet comparison should transfer BABP one-site mixing.” Any successful interpolation proof needs a weighted/local estimate on the `H` term in (3.5). The precise blocker is no longer an unnamed process difference.

## 4. B14 — exploit translation/reflection symmetry: a perfect one-sided local refresh word

Translation and reflection reduce the elementary local problem to target `0` and a left or right occupied guard.

### Proposition 4.1 — exact one-sided guard regeneration

Assume at time `s` that `xi_{-1}(s)=1`. Fix `delta>0` and define the Poisson-clock event

```text
E^-_delta = {
  no clock rings at -2 during [s,s+delta],
  no clock rings at  0 during [s,s+delta],
  the clock at -1 rings at least once during [s,s+delta]
}.                                                   (4.1)
```

Then

```text
P(E^-_delta | F_s, xi_{-1}(s)=1)
 = e^{-2 delta}(1-e^{-delta})=:r(delta)>0,           (4.2)
```

and, conditional on `E^-_delta` and the entire history before `s`,

```text
xi_0(s+delta) ~ Bernoulli(q).                        (4.3)
```

Indeed, only rings at `-2` or `0` can refresh the membership of `-1`, so on `E^-_delta` the guard `-1` stays occupied throughout the block. Let `sigma` be its last ring in the block. At `sigma`, site `0` receives a fresh Bernoulli(`q`) coin. After `sigma`, there are no further `-1` rings. A possible ring at `+1` can again refresh `0`; because there are no rings at `0`, the evolution and enabledness of `+1` do not depend on the coin placed at `0`. Hence the final value at `0` is the last of one or more independent Bernoulli(`q`) refresh coins chosen at stopping times independent of their values. This proves (4.3). Reflection gives the right-guard version.

The same argument shows the terminal bit is decoupled from the pre-block history on the successful word, not merely that its mean equals `q`.

### Why this still does not close (2.2)

A one-site perfect refresh is not a persistent equilibrium shield. After the successful block, a later ring at the target can feed its current value into the neighbor configuration. Later enabled-neighbor refresh hazards can then depend on that value. Thus the exact local regeneration cannot simply be iterated as an iid scalar contraction.

The missing B14 theorem is now concrete: build a **growing-space regeneration shield** whose success both refreshes a target block from product Bernoulli and prevents exterior/self-feedback from recoupling that block before the observation time, with exponentially small failure. This is stronger than Proposition 4.1 and was not proved here.

### B14 outcome

There is a genuine all-q perfect-refresh mechanism with explicit block probability `r(delta)`, but the gluing failure is post-refresh feedback, not the absence of any local regeneration word.

## 5. B24 — find the extremal object: geometry prevents an unnormalized finite-set maximizer

Let

```text
u_A(t)=P_A(0 in A_t).
```

The exact initial derivative follows immediately from (3.7):

```text
u_A'(0)
 = (1_A(-1)+1_A(1))(q-1_A(0)).                      (5.1)
```

Thus among sets containing `0`, an isolated target has zero first derivative while an occupied neighbor causes immediate drift down at rate `p` per neighbor. Among sets omitting `0`, a set with no occupied neighbor of `0` also has zero first derivative. There is already no single shape extremizer independent of the relative geometry.

### Proposition 5.1 — exact shortest-path Taylor coefficient

For the singleton start `A={R}`, `R>=1`,

```text
P_{ {R} }(0 in A_t)
 = q^R t^R/R! + O(t^(R+1))                          (5.2)
```

as `t downarrow 0`.

Reason: before site `0` can be occupied, the dual must create `R-1,R-2,...,0` in this order. Each inward record birth requires the current nearest occupied site to ring and its inward Bernoulli refresh coin to equal one. No sequence of fewer than `R` rings can reach the origin, while the unique shortest inward sequence contributes `q^R` at generator order `R`. Extra rings contribute only at order `R+1` or higher.

### Proposition 5.2 — nonasymptotic distance obstruction

Before the origin is hit, every new leftward record of the nearest occupied site can occur only at a ring of that current record site whose inward Bernoulli coin is one. Its predictable intensity is at most `q`. Therefore, with `N~Poisson(qt)`,

```text
P_{ {R} }(0 in A_t)
 <= P(hit 0 by time t)
 <= P(N>=R).                                         (5.3)
```

For every fixed `t` and `epsilon>0`, the right side is below `epsilon` for all sufficiently large `R`.

Consequently an extremal search over **all** finite starts at fixed `t` is the wrong normalization: by sending the initial set far from the target one can keep the occupation probability arbitrarily close to zero. The constants in (2.2) are allowed to depend on `A`, so distance/span must be frozen or factored out before an extremal-object reduction can be useful.

### B24 outcome

The promising “singleton/interval is the worst finite dual start” formulation is false as a useful global reduction unless geometry is normalized. The correct extremal problem should condition on target distance/span and then optimize internal shape.
