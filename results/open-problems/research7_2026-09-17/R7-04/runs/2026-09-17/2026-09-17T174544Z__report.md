## 3. B1 — change category: exact Boolean update formulas and disagreement ancestry

### 3.1 Idea before calculation

Instead of regarding FA-1f as a constrained spin system, regard each Harris ring as a random Boolean gate. This makes the precise way information from the initial state can reach a terminal observable completely explicit.

Let the pre-ring values at a ring of `x` be

```text
X = eta_x,
L = eta_{x-1},
R = eta_{x+1},
```

and let the common Harris coin be `s`, with `P(s=0)=q` and `P(s=1)=p`.

### Proposition 3.1 — exact Boolean gate representation

At every FA-1f ring,

```text
F_0(X,L,R) = X L R,
F_1(X,L,R) = 1 - (1-X) L R.                         (3.1)
```

Equivalently, a `0`-coin is a three-input AND gate, while a `1`-coin is

```text
X OR (NOT L) OR (NOT R).
```

#### Proof

If `L=R=1`, the constraint fails and the site keeps its old value `X`. In (3.1), both formulas reduce to `X` in this case.

If at least one of `L,R` is zero, then `LR=0`; the ring is legal and (3.1) gives respectively `0` and `1`, exactly the Harris coin. QED.

This formula is elementary but it exposes the non-attractiveness algebraically: the `s=0` gate is increasing in all three bits, while the `s=1` gate is increasing in the center and decreasing in each neighbor.

### Proposition 3.2 — exact disagreement-creation law

Couple two FA-1f processes `eta,xi` with the same clocks and coins. Suppose they agree at `x` immediately before a ring, with common value `a in {0,1}`.

A disagreement can be created at `x` at that ring only if the two constraints at `x` differ. If the constraints differ, exactly one copy updates and the other keeps the common old value `a`. Therefore the conditional probability that the ring creates a disagreement is

```text
q, if a=1,
p, if a=0.                                           (3.2)
```

In particular, if either neighbor is a **common zero** in the two copies, then both constraints are legal, so a ring at `x` sets both copies to the same coin and makes `x` agree with probability one.

#### Proof

If the constraints agree, either both copies update to the common coin or neither updates, hence agreement is preserved. If they differ, one copy becomes `s` while the other remains `a`; disagreement is therefore exactly the event `s != a`, whose probabilities are `q` for `a=1` and `p` for `a=0`. A common-zero neighbor forces both constraints to be legal. QED.

The asymmetry in (3.2) is important at small `q`: a common healthy boundary bit is hard to damage (`q` hazard), whereas a common zero at the boundary can be damaged at `p` hazard unless another common zero protects it. Thus “a zero is always a shield” is false; **a zero one site behind the boundary** is the more robust local geometry.

### Proposition 3.3 — disagreement ancestry path

Fix a finite terminal time `t`. If the two same-Harris copies disagree at `(x,t)`, then almost surely there is a backward càdlàg space-time path from `(x,t)` to a site that disagreed at time zero such that:

1. the path is vertical between Harris rings;
2. every spatial jump is by one lattice edge;
3. a spatial jump occurs only at a ring where a disagreement was created from prior agreement, hence one of the two neighbors disagreed immediately before that ring;
4. no ring at which the updated site has a common-zero neighbor can carry the path through as a post-ring disagreement.

#### Proof

Trace backward from `(x,t)` through the locally finite graphical construction. At the last ring of the current site before the current backward time, if the site already disagreed just before the ring, keep the parent at the same site. If it agreed before the ring but disagrees after, Proposition 3.2 says the constraints differed. Since the FA-1f constraint depends only on the two neighbors, at least one of those neighbors disagreed immediately before the ring; choose such a neighbor as the parent and jump there. Repeating this rule cannot explode in finite time in the nearest-neighbor Poisson construction and eventually reaches time zero. Finally, if the updated site had a common-zero neighbor, both constraints were legal and both copies were refreshed to the same coin, so a disagreement cannot emerge after that ring. QED.

### B1 outcome

The blocker can now be restated as an **information-percolation / random Boolean-circuit problem**:

> show that all backward disagreement ancestries from a fixed terminal support are cut at exponentially high probability by common-facilitator events generated from the anchored zero.

This is sharper than treating the full disagreement set as a generic branching process. A single terminal disagreement only requires one surviving ancestry path; common-zero rings are exact path killers.

However, the initial disagreement set in the hard coupling is infinite, so finite-speed alone is insufficient. The cuts must be produced dynamically and with enough persistence.

## 4. B15 — break symmetry: an exact one-sided regenerating `01` shield

### 4.1 Why the boundary state `01` is the right asymmetric primitive

Consider the right edge of a region on which every process in a coupled family has the same state. If the final two common bits are

```text
site x-1 : 0
site x   : 1,
```

then `x` has a common-zero neighbor. Therefore every ring at `x` is legal for every member of the family, independent of the state at `x+1`. This is a one-sided shield against right-exterior uncertainty.

The next lemma quantifies exactly how such a shield can move one site to the right while preserving its shape.

### Proposition 4.1 — background-uniform one-step shield transport

Let `tau` be a stopping time for the Harris construction. Suppose that, for every process in an arbitrarily large coupled family using the same future Harris marks,

```text
eta_{x-1}(tau)=0,
eta_x(tau)=1.                                        (4.1)
```

No assumption is made on any other site; in particular all processes may disagree arbitrarily at `x+1` and beyond.

There is a future-mark event depending only on the clocks/coins at `x-1,x,x+1` whose conditional probability is exactly

```text
c_sh(q) = p q / ((1+q)(3-q)) > 0,                   (4.2)
```

and on that event there is a later stopping time `tau'` at which every process in the family satisfies

```text
eta_x(tau')=0,
eta_{x+1}(tau')=1.                                   (4.3)
```

Thus the common `01` shield has moved one lattice unit to the right, uniformly over every exterior background.

#### Proof

Because `x-1` is common zero, rings at `x` are certainly legal in every coupled process as long as `x-1` has not rung.

**Stage 1.** Ignore the harmless `1`-coin rings at `x`, which leave the common state `1` unchanged. The `0`-coin rings at `x` form a thinned Poisson process of rate `q`; the clock at `x-1` has rate `1`. Require the first of these two relevant processes to be a `0`-coin ring at `x`. This has probability

```text
q/(1+q).                                             (4.4)
```

On this event, the pair becomes common `00` at `x-1,x`.

**Stage 2.** From that moment, ignore further `0`-coin rings at `x`, which keep `x=0`. Consider the independent relevant clocks:

```text
x+1 : any ring, rate 1;
x-1 : any ring, rate 1;
x   : 1-coin ring, rate p.
```

Require the `x+1` clock to occur first. Its probability is

```text
1/(2+p) = 1/(3-q).                                  (4.5)
```

Until this event, `x-1` has not rung and no `1`-coin has occurred at `x`, so `x` remains a common zero. Therefore the ring at `x+1` is certainly legal in every coupled process, no matter what the old values at `x+1` were. Require its independent coin to be `1`, an event of probability `p`. Then every process has the shifted common pair `01` at `(x,x+1)`.

Multiplying (4.4), (4.5), and `p` gives (4.2). QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — exact finite-distance uniform shield word

By the strong Markov property, the preceding event can be concatenated. Starting from one common `01` pair, for every integer `d>=1` there is a background-uniform Harris event of probability

```text
c_sh(q)^d                                             (4.6)
```

that transports the same `01` shield `d` sites in the chosen direction.

This is a genuine all-`q` graphical certificate. It is stronger in quantifier structure than a two-process construction because the same event works simultaneously for **all** exterior configurations consistent with the starting `01` pair.

### Why this does not close the source

The price in (4.6) is exponential in distance, exactly the kind of spatial price already tolerated by the deterministic `H(a,b)` reduction. The unresolved issue is **time amplification and the wake behind the moving shield**. A failed stage can destroy the simple `01` certificate, and after a successful stage old sites behind the moving pair may later be affected from the other side. Thus (4.6) gives a uniform moving cut, not an exponentially high-probability persistent coupling of a fixed target.

The result nevertheless identifies a much smaller local object than an entire reset block: one oriented two-bit pattern is enough to transport a background-uniform coupling certificate.

## 5. B27 — swap the quantifiers: why a sitewise grand-coupling abstraction provably loses the needed information

### 5.1 Stronger target

The diagnosis suggested asking for one Harris event that erases **all** initial backgrounds sharing a common zero. That is stronger than coupling one deterministic state to `nu_x`, but if true with exponentially high probability it would immediately imply the desired worst-background estimate.

A natural implementation is a sitewise set-valued or “bounding chain” abstraction. Initially, for the family

```text
F_0 = {eta : eta_0=0},
```

set

```text
S_0(0)={0},
S_y(0)={0,1} for y!=0.
```

At every Harris ring, replace the site's possible-value set by the image of the Cartesian product of the three current possible-value sets under the Boolean gate (3.1). This is a rigorous rectangular over-approximation of the true coupled family: it may forget correlations, but it never discards a truly possible value.

### Proposition 5.1 — the rectangular abstraction has a permanent-information-loss event of probability `p/3`

Starting from the single fixed anchor described above, with probability exactly

```text
p/3,                                                  (5.1)
```

this rectangular abstraction reaches the completely unknown state

```text
S_y={0,1} for every y,
```

before either neighbor of the anchor has rung. The completely unknown rectangular state is absorbing. Consequently, a proof that tracks **only one-site possible-value sets** cannot give a vanishing all-background coupling failure probability from a single shared anchor.

#### Proof

Look at the first ring among sites `-1,0,1`. By symmetry of three independent rate-one clocks, the first ring is at the origin with probability `1/3`. Require also that its independent coin is `1`, which has probability `p`. This gives (5.1).

At that time neither neighboring possible-value set has changed, so both are `{0,1}`, while the center input set is `{0}`. For the `1`-gate,

```text
F_1(0,L,R)=1-LR.
```

As `(L,R)` range over `{0,1}^2`, this output takes both values: it is `0` at `(1,1)` and `1` whenever at least one neighbor is zero. Hence the new center set is `{0,1}`. Every other site was already `{0,1}`, so the abstraction is now completely unknown.

If all three input sets at a future ring are `{0,1}`, both Boolean gates in (3.1) attain both output values on the Cartesian product. Therefore that site's output set remains `{0,1}`. The all-unknown rectangular state is absorbing. QED.

### What Proposition 5.1 does and does not say

It does **not** prove that the actual grand coupling fails. The true family retains correlations discarded by rectangular projection. On the event used in the proof, the center value after the `1`-coin ring is not arbitrary: it satisfies the exact relation

```text
eta_0^+ = 1 - eta_{-1}^- eta_1^-.                   (5.2)
```

The one-site hull forgets (5.2), and that loss is irreversible in the abstraction. A correlation-aware Boolean formula, clause, or certificate representation can retain it.

Thus B27 has produced a precise negative conclusion about the *method*, not about Conjecture 2:

> a sitewise monotone bounding chain is too coarse for the all-`q` singular-start problem. Any successful quantifier-swapped argument must preserve logical correlations between sites.

This is consistent with the source's warning that non-attractiveness defeats standard monotone/censoring couplings, but the calculation above identifies the exact first local correlation that a successful grand coupling has to remember.

- **Generic branching-process domination of disagreements.** Too crude. Proposition 3.2 shows creation and killing depend on the common local state; a common-zero neighbor is a perfect killer, while an unprotected common zero is actually fragile at rate `p`.
- **Treat any common zero as an absorbing shield.** False. A common zero at the boundary may split when the two legality indicators differ and a `1` coin appears. Proposition 4.1 deliberately uses the oriented `01` geometry instead.
- **Use the `01` transport word as a full proof.** Invalid. Its success probability over distance is `c_sh(q)^d`; it gives spatially exponential minorization, not failure probability exponentially small in elapsed time.
- **Use a sitewise grand-coupling bounding chain.** Proposition 5.1 proves a permanent all-unknown event of probability `p/3` in the rectangular abstraction. This route cannot supply a vanishing error without retaining correlations.
- **Infer actual grand-coupling failure from Proposition 5.1.** Invalid. Equation (5.2) is precisely a surviving correlation hidden by the rectangular hull.
