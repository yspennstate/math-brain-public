## New theorem: arbitrary finite-support terminal-stretch invariance inside stabilized cells

Fix a finite prefix

\[
0<x_1<\cdots <x_{k-1}=b
\]

with fixed masses `p_1,...,p_{k-1}` and terminal mass `r`, and move only the last support point:

\[
X_d\in\{x_1,\ldots,x_{k-1},b+d\}.
\]

Keep `lambda` fixed and restrict to `rho(d)<1`. Define

\[
\mu=E[X_d\wedge b],\qquad
M_2(d)=E[X_d^2],\qquad
\beta=1-\lambda\mu.
\]

For every preterminal support interval, its SERPT record candidate has the form

\[
L_j(d)=c_j+\frac r{S_j}d,
\qquad S_j>r.
\]

Thus every preterminal slope is strictly below one, whereas the terminal candidate is `d`. M-SERPT therefore has only finitely many pairwise affine record contacts in the terminal-location parameter.

For Gittins before age `b`, every stopping candidate that stops by `b` is independent of `d`; the candidates using the terminal atom increase affinely in `d`. Hence beyond a finite prefix-stabilization threshold the whole Gittins rank function on `[0,b)` becomes independent of `d`.

Call an open stable interval a **terminal-stabilized cell** when: the M-SERPT record ordering is fixed; the terminal record strictly dominates every preterminal M-SERPT record; the Gittins prefix is fixed; and the terminal Gittins jump exceeds every prefix Gittins rank.

On every such cell, the SOAP work sets for every nonterminal tagged job are fixed. A terminal competitor's recycled Gittins tail below a fixed prefix threshold has fixed length independent of how much extra tail lies above it, while M-SERPT never recycles after the strict terminal record. Thus all nonterminal conditional responses are independent of `d`.

For the terminal tagged job, both policies have the common waiting term

\[
W_\infty(d)
=
\frac{\lambda M_2(d)}{2(1-\rho(d))\beta},
\]

and both terminal residence derivatives are `1/beta`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dd}\left(E[T_{MS}]-E[T_G]\right)=0.
}
\]

Writing `D=E[T_MS]-E[T_G]` and `F=2E[T_G]-E[T_MS]=E[T_G]-D`, exact differentiation yields

\[
\boxed{
F'(d)=
 r\left[
 \frac{\lambda}{2\beta}
 \left(
 \frac{2r(b+d)}{1-\rho(d)}
 +\frac{\lambda rM_2(d)}{(1-\rho(d))^2}
 \right)
 +\frac1\beta
 \right]>0.
}
\]

So the three-point tail-location cancellation was not special to three atoms. **For arbitrary finite support, every stabilized open terminal-stretch cell has constant additive M-SERPT penalty and strictly increasing factor-two slack.** Continuous optimization inside those cells is eliminated; each cell is controlled by its left one-sided boundary.

This is the main new structural handoff to P01-B.

## Exact four-atom obstruction: global tail monotonicity fails at an M-SERPT record contact

The remaining question is whether the one-sided boundary can be crossed without losing slack. The answer is not automatically yes.

Consider

\[
X_d\in\left\{1,\frac52,\frac72,\frac72+d\right\},
\]

with masses

\[
\left(\frac13,\frac1{12},\frac12,\frac1{12}\right)
\]

and `lambda=1/10`. The M-SERPT SERPT-start candidates are exactly

\[
\frac{d+31}{12},\quad
\frac{d+19}{8},\quad
\frac{d+7}{7},\quad d.
\]

The first two cross at `d=5` because their difference is `(d-5)/24`. At the contact, `E[X]=3` and `rho=3/10`, and exact SOAP evaluation gives

\[
E[T_G]=\frac{52145}{13706},\qquad
E[T_{MS}]=\frac{57497}{14952},
\]

so

\[
D(5)=\frac{961}{23496},\qquad
F(5)=\frac{619013}{164472}.
\]

Immediately to the right of the contact, the new topology is a terminal-stabilized open cell with

\[
D(5+)=\frac{1553}{11748}.
\]

The Gittins prefix topology is unchanged through the contact, so `E[T_G]` is continuous there. Hence

\[
F(5+)=\frac{301999}{82236}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
F(5)-F(5+)=\frac{65}{712}>0.
}
\]

Thus **global terminal-stretch monotonicity is false as a proof route**: factor-two slack can jump downward at an M-SERPT record contact even though it is strictly increasing on the open cells on either side. This is not a counterexample to Conjecture 8.1; both slacks are strongly positive.

The mechanism is exact and tie-sensitive. At `d=5` the first two M-SERPT plateau levels tie, so FCFS tie-breaking avoids the strict priority change that appears for `d>5`.

## Corrected four-atom bottleneck

The continuous transport problem is therefore solved in the stabilized regime. The next finite-support induction does **not** need another generic tail derivative. It needs a discrete record-contact charge.

Let `d_c` be an M-SERPT record contact and

\[
J_c:=D(d_c+)-D(d_c).
\]

The example proves that `J_c` can be positive, so the hoped-for `J_c<=0` is false. A sufficient replacement is

\[
\boxed{J_c\le F(d_c),}
\]

which would imply `F(d_c+)>=0`. If such an inequality can be proved at every contact, Theorem 5.1 then propagates the factor-two inequality across the entire stabilized cell until the next contact.

This converts the minimal four-atom problem from a continuous support-position optimization into **finite contact bookkeeping plus a jump inequality**. Because P01-B now has a finite-support reduction for a broad record-regular arbitrary-law class, a general contact-jump theorem would be directly useful to the arbitrary-law route rather than merely another special family.
