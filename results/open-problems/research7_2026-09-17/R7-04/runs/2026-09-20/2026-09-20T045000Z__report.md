# 1. Atomic plateau-contact theorem

Let

\[
S(x)=P(X>x),\quad N(x)=E[(X-x)^+],\quad
m_x=E[X\wedge x],\quad v_x=E[(X\wedge x)^2],
\]

and

\[
A_x=1-\lambda m_x,\qquad m=E[X],\qquad \lambda m<1.
\]

Consider a finite-support M-SERPT plateau with ages

\[
0\le u<c<b
\]

and level `L`, satisfying

\[
N(u)=LS(u),\qquad N(c)=LS(c),
\]

\[
N(x)\le LS(x)\quad(c\le x<b),
\]

and at the next record boundary

\[
N(b)\ge LS(b).
\]

The one-sided post-contact topology leaves `[u,c)` at the old plateau and makes `[c,b)` infinitesimally worse, with all ordering outside this block unchanged.

Put

\[
p=P(u<X\le c),\qquad q=P(c<X\le b),
\]

\[
M=E[X\,1_{\{c<X\le b\}}].
\]

The contact identities give

\[
\boxed{m_c-m_u=Lp.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Using the exact SOAP waiting/residence decomposition, only jobs in `(u,b]` change. Jobs in `(u,c]` change old-work cutoff from `b` to `c` while keeping new-work cutoff `u`; jobs in `(c,b]` change new-work cutoff from `u` to `c` while keeping old-work cutoff `b`.

Therefore the exact one-sided mean-response jump `J=T_+-T_0` is

\[
\boxed{
J
=p\left(\frac{\lambda v_c}{2A_cA_u}-\frac{\lambda v_b}{2A_bA_u}\right)
+q\left(\frac{\lambda v_b}{2A_bA_c}-\frac{\lambda v_b}{2A_bA_u}\right)
+M\left(\frac1{A_c}-\frac1{A_u}\right).
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

After inserting `(1.1)`, this factors as

\[
\boxed{
J=\frac{\lambda p}{2A_uA_cA_b}H_\lambda,
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
H_\lambda
=A_b(v_c+2LM)-v_b(A_c-\lambda Lq).
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The key structural fact is that `H_lambda` is **affine in the load parameter `lambda`**.

# 2. New theorem: atomic M-SERPT contact splitting is always nonnegative

Because the denominator in `(1.3)` is positive, it suffices to prove `H_lambda>=0` on `0<=lambda<1/m`. Affinity reduces the sign problem to two endpoints.

At zero load,

\[
H_0=v_c+2LM-v_b.
\]

A survival-function rearrangement gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{H_0}{2}
=\int_c^b(LS(x)-N(x))\,dx
+b\,[N(b)-LS(b)].
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Both terms are nonnegative: the first is the integrated SERPT deficit under the plateau, while the second is the next-record overshoot created by the atom/boundary at `b`. Hence `H_0>=0`.

At the algebraic stability boundary `lambda=1/m`,

\[
A_b=\frac{N(b)}m,
\]

and, because `q=S(c)-S(b)` and `N(c)=LS(c)`,

\[
A_c-\frac{Lq}{m}=\frac{LS(b)}m.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
mH_{1/m}
=N(b)H_0+v_b[N(b)-LS(b)]\ge0.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since `H_lambda` is affine and is nonnegative at both endpoints,

\[
\boxed{H_\lambda\ge0\quad(0\le\lambda\le1/m),}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{J\ge0\quad\text{for every stable load}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

This proves that the previous four-point positive jump was not an isolated artifact. Under the stated generic atomic plateau-contact topology, **breaking an M-SERPT rank tie can only increase M-SERPT mean response**.

The atomic formula differs materially from P01-B's smooth-valley formula: the discrete case contains the explicit boundary overshoot `b[N(b)-LS(b)]`, which vanishes in a smooth same-level valley endpoint.

# 3. Exact factor-two contact obligation

When the Gittins topology and `E[T_G]` are continuous through this M-SERPT-only contact,

\[
F^+=F^- -J.
\]

Hence the old abstract contact requirement `J<=F^-` becomes the explicit local inequality

\[
\boxed{
F^-
\ge
\frac{\lambda p}{2A_uA_cA_b}
\left[A_b(v_c+2LM)-v_b(A_c-\lambda Lq)\right].
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is now a concrete prefix-credit target for P01-B: every quantity on the right is a local M-SERPT/truncated-moment object. No unknown Gittins geometry remains inside the contact charge itself.

The theorem does **not** prove `(3.1)` globally; it identifies exactly what the pre-contact factor-two slack must pay.

# 4. Finite sawtooth reduction on a terminal-stabilized ray

Combine this run with the prior terminal-cell theorem. On a generic finite-support tail-location ray after Gittins-prefix stabilization and terminal dominance:

1. between simple M-SERPT record contacts, `D'(d)=0` and `F'(d)>0`;
2. at every simple M-SERPT plateau contact covered above, `D` has a nonnegative right jump `J`, while `F` has a downward jump `-J` if Gittins stays continuous.

Therefore `F(d)` has a **finite sawtooth structure**: strictly increasing on each open stabilized cell and dropping only at finitely many M-SERPT contacts. Consequently the infimum on such a ray is attained among a finite candidate set consisting of the initial stabilized left boundary and the immediate right limits after M-SERPT contacts.

This removes another continuous optimization layer. The remaining finite-support problem is now discrete: prove that each downward sawtooth jump, or an aggregate of jumps along a record chain, is paid by accumulated prefix/Gittins credit.
