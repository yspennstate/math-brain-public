## 1. New theorem: factor two for arbitrary finite support with at most one strict M-SERPT record jump

Let `X` have positive finite support, mean `mu`, and stable load `rho=lambda mu<1`. Write

\[
S(a)=P(X>a),\qquad N(a)=E[(X-a)^+],\qquad r_S(a)=N(a)/S(a).
\]

Assume the M-SERPT increasing envelope has exactly one strict internal record jump at age `b`. Equivalently,

\[
r_S(a)\le\mu\ (a<b),\qquad L:=r_S(b)>\mu,
\qquad r_S(c)\le L\ (c>b).
\]

This includes arbitrarily many support points on either side of `b`; it is not a four-point parameter family.

Define

\[
r=P(X>b),\quad p=1-r,
\]

\[
m=E[X\wedge b],\quad B=E[X1_{\{X>b\}}],
\]

\[
V_b=E[(X\wedge b)^2],\quad V=E[X^2],
\]

and

\[
A=1-\lambda m,\qquad \alpha=1-\rho.
\]

Then for every stable load,

\[
\boxed{E[T_{MS}]<2E[T_G].}
\]

The proof gives the explicit factor-two slack

\[
\boxed{
2E[T_G]-E[T_{MS}]
\ge
\frac{\mu(1-\rho)(1-\rho/2)}{A}>0.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

## 2. Structural lemma: the unique M-SERPT record must also be the unique Gittins hill

For a finite law, the Gittins stopping candidate between ages `a<c` is the integrated-tail chord

\[
R(a,c)=\frac{N(a)-N(c)}{S(a)-S(c)},
\]

with `c=infinity` recovering the SERPT candidate. Thus `r_G(a)<=r_S(a)`.

Before `b`, this gives `r_G(a)<=mu`. At `b`, set

\[
D_\mu(x)=N(x)-\mu S(x).
\]

We have

\[
D_\mu(b)=S(b)(L-\mu)>0.
\]

For every genuine stopping endpoint `c>b`, the one-record hypothesis implies

\[
D_\mu(c)=S(c)(r_S(c)-\mu)
\le S(c)(L-\mu)
< S(b)(L-\mu)=D_\mu(b),
\]

where the strict inequality uses `S(c)<S(b)` for a genuine finite-law stopping endpoint. Hence

\[
R(b,c)>\mu
\]

for every admissible endpoint, including the terminal candidate `L>mu`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{r_G(b)>\mu.}
\]

So `b` is a strict Gittins hill. Lemma 5.3 rules out any other Gittins hill because there is no other M-SERPT hill. Thus both policies have the same hill endpoints, namely the two blocks `(0,b]` and `(b,infinity)`.

This is the part that makes the theorem structural: it converts a condition on the easy SERPT record chain into an exact statement about the hard Gittins hill topology.

## 4. New response-slack inequality

Multiplying `Q_MS+2mu-R_MS` by `A` and using (3.1)-(3.2) gives

\[
K:=A(Q_{MS}+2\mu-R_{MS})
=
\mu-\lambda m(\mu+B)
+\frac\lambda2\left(pV_b+\frac{rV}{\alpha}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Use `m<=mu`, `B<=mu`, `pV_b>=0`, and tail Cauchy-Schwarz

\[
B^2\le rE[X^2 1_{\{X>b\}}]\le rV.
\]

Then

\[
K\ge \mu-\lambda\mu(\mu+B)+\frac{\lambda B^2}{2\alpha}.
\]

Writing `y=B/mu` and `rho=lambda mu`, the normalized lower bound is

\[
1-\rho(1+y)+\frac{\rho y^2}{2(1-\rho)}.
\]

The decisive identity is

\[
\boxed{
1-\rho(1+y)+\frac{\rho y^2}{2(1-\rho)}
=(1-\rho)(1-\rho/2)
+\frac{\rho}{2(1-\rho)}(y-(1-\rho))^2.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q_{MS}+2\mu-R_{MS}
\ge
\frac{\mu(1-\rho)(1-\rho/2)}A>0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (3.3), `R_G>=mu`, and (4.3) yields (1.1) and the factor-two theorem.

The important feature is that all detailed support geometry below the record age disappears into `m<=mu`, while the entire terminal tail is paid by one Cauchy second-moment charge. No Bernstein certificate or parameter-cell enumeration is needed.
