## 3. New analytic lemma: the final M-SERPT record can never be skipped by Gittins

Let `b` be the last strict internal M-SERPT record of any positive finite-support law, and write

\[
L=r_S(b)=\frac{N(b)}{S(b)},
\qquad
S(x)=P(X>x),\quad N(x)=E[(X-x)^+].
\]

Because `b` is the last M-SERPT record,

\[
r_S(c)\le L\qquad(c>b).
\]

For any later Gittins stopping endpoint `c`,

\[
\frac{N(b)-N(c)}{S(b)-S(c)}-L
=
\frac{-\{N(c)-LS(c)\}}{S(b)-S(c)}\ge0.
\]

The terminal stopping candidate equals `L`, so

\[
\boxed{r_G(b)=L=r_S(b).}
\]

Immediately before `b`, Gittins rank is at most SERPT rank and is below the previous M-SERPT record level, which is strictly below `L`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{b\text{ is a strict Gittins hill}.}
\tag{3.1}

This is the useful structural reduction for arbitrary finite record chains: **Gittins and M-SERPT always share the final record**. Rank mismatch is necessarily a prefix phenomenon.
