## Exact outside source and locator

Michel Talagrand, **“Are Many Small Sets Explicitly Small?”**, STOC 2010, pp. 13–36, DOI `10.1145/1806689.1806693`.

Current author-hosted source copy used for the source check:
`https://michel.talagrand.net/prizes/small.pdf`

The exact source endpoint is **Conjecture 7.9**, PDF p. 9 in the current `prizes/small.pdf` copy (the displayed statement is on zero-indexed PDF page 8), with its Hahn–Banach dual **Conjecture 7.12** on the following page.

Let `S` be finite, let `0<p<1`, and for `I⊆S` write `H_I={J⊆S:I⊆J}`. A probability measure `ν` on `2^S` is `αp`-spread when

`ν(H_I) ≤ (α p)^{|I|}`

for every `I⊆S`. Given `J`, let `θ_{J,α}` be the law obtained by retaining each element of `J` independently with probability `α`, and define

`W_α(ν)=∫ θ_{J,α} dν(J)`.

Talagrand’s Conjecture 7.9 asks whether there exists a universal `0<α<1` such that, for every finite `S`, every `0<p<1`, and every `αp`-spread `ν`, the product Bernoulli measure `μ_p` **stochastically dominates** `W_α(ν)`, i.e.

`W_α(ν)(A) ≤ μ_p(A)`

for every up-class `A⊆2^S`.

The current author-hosted preprint copy under `/preprints/small.pdf` has a numbering drift: the same statement is called Conjecture 7.8 there. The canonical intake `F0106` and `prizes/small.pdf` use Conjecture 7.9; this admission follows that exact source version and records the alias to prevent false duplication.

## Full-source closure criterion

A source-level positive solution is a complete proof that **one universal constant `α∈(0,1)`** works simultaneously for every finite ground set `S`, every `p∈(0,1)`, every `αp`-spread probability measure `ν`, and every up-class `A`.

A source-level refutation must negate the existential quantifier: it is not enough to produce a counterexample for one fixed `α`; one needs a parametric family or argument showing failure for **every** `α∈(0,1)`.

## Initial mathematical obstruction to test

After thinning, `κ=W_α(ν)` is `q`-spread with `q=α^2p`. Applying Li’s theorem gives `X~κ` covered by `R1∪R2`, where each `Rj~μ_q`. If `R1,R2` were independent, their union would be `μ_{2q-q^2}` and would be dominated by `μ_p` whenever `2α^2p-α^4p^2≤p`, in particular for `α≤1/√2`. But Li’s theorem does **not** give independence of `R1,R2`; the missing gluing inequality is precisely whether the two product marginals can be arranged, together with `X`, so their union is itself below one `μ_p` set.
