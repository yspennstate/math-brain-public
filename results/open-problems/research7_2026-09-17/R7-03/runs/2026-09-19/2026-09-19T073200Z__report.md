## 3. B29 — add the upper endpoint and identify the point-process limit

Fix an integer `r>=1` and put `k=n-1-r`. Use the equivalent smaller-score-is-preferred formulation. Every vertex rejects exactly its `r` largest incident edge scores.

For the root `0`, define

`Y_i = n(1-U_{0i})`.

Let `A_{n,r}` be the `r`th smallest `Y_i`. Then the root proposes to neighbor `i` exactly when `Y_i>A_{n,r}`. If `J_i` is the indicator that neighbor `i` rejects the root edge, then conditional on `Y_i=y`,

`P(J_i=1 | Y_i=y) = b_{n,r}(y) := P(Bin(n-2,y/n) <= r-1)`.

Writing `H_n=k-D_n`,

`H_n = sum_i 1_{Y_i>A_{n,r}} J_i`.

The root point process `sum_i delta_{Y_i}` converges to a rate-one Poisson point process on `(0,infinity)`, and

`A_{n,r} => A_r ~ Gamma(r,1)`.

The mark probability has the limit

`p_r(y)=e^{-y} sum_{j=0}^{r-1} y^j/j! = P(Gamma(r,1)>y)`.

This compactifies the only singular dense boundary into a finite upper-tail Poisson process.

## 4. B30 — localize the dependence and prove marked-Poisson convergence

The delicate point is that the rejection indicators are not independent: two candidate neighbors share their mutual edge. I localized that dependence rather than discarding it.

Fix a bounded upper-tail window `[0,L]` and finitely many candidate neighbors. Remove the finitely many candidate-candidate edges. Their remaining endpoint stars are disjoint, so the associated exceedance counts are independent `Bin(n-m-1,y_i/n)` variables and converge jointly to independent `Poisson(y_i)` variables.

A removed candidate-candidate edge can affect a mark only if its score exceeds at least one root-edge threshold. For candidate coordinates in `[0,L]`, this has probability at most `L/n` per internal edge. Thus the total probability that any internal candidate edge changes the finite mark vector is `O(m^2 L/n)=o(1)`.

Hence on every compact window the root Poisson process is asymptotically independently marked, with a point at location `y` receiving a rejection mark with probability `p_r(y)`.

To remove the compact cutoff, for fixed `r` I proved the uniform tail bound

`b_{n,r}(y) <= e^{-y/2} sum_{j=0}^{r-1} y^j/j!`

for all sufficiently large `n`. This integrable exponential-polynomial envelope makes the expected number of marked points beyond `L` tend uniformly to zero as `L→∞`.

For first-two-moment convergence, condition two endpoint decisions on their unique shared edge. Their remaining edge sets are then independent, and each rejection probability is bounded by the analogous `Bin(n-3,y/n)` lower-tail probability. The same integrable envelope controls the second factorial-moment tails. Therefore distribution, mean, and variance all converge.

## 5. Exact dense-boundary law

Conditional on the `r`th Poisson arrival `A_r=a`, points after `a` form an independent rate-one Poisson process. Thinning at location `y` with probability `p_r(y)` gives

`H_r | A_r=a ~ Poisson(Lambda_r(a))`,

where

`Lambda_r(a)=int_a^infinity p_r(y) dy
            = e^{-a} sum_{ell=0}^{r-1} (r-ell) a^ell/ell!`.

Thus

`H_n => H_r`

with convergence of the first two moments.

If

`mu_r=E[H_r]`, `nu_r=Var(H_r)`,

then the mean has the closed form

`mu_r = r * C(2r,r)/4^r = Gamma(r+1/2)/(sqrt(pi) Gamma(r))`.

The mixing second moment is the exact finite sum

`M2_r = 1/(r-1)! * sum_{ell,m=0}^{r-1}
        (r-ell)(r-m)(r+ell+m-1)! /
        (ell! m! 3^(r+ell+m))`,

and

`nu_r = mu_r + M2_r - mu_r^2 > 0`.

Since `D_n=k-H_n`,

`E[D_n]=k-mu_r+o(1)`,

`Var(D_n)=nu_r+o(1)`,

and the standardized degree converges to

`L_r := (mu_r-H_r)/sqrt(nu_r)`.

This is a discrete non-Gaussian law depending on the fixed complement `r`.

## 7. B7 — the boundary family glues exactly to the interior law

Let `A_r,G_r` be iid `Gamma(r,1)`. The mixed-Poisson mean satisfies

`Lambda_r(A_r)=E[(G_r-A_r)_+ | A_r]`.

After centering at `r` and scaling by `sqrt(r)`, the gamma CLT plus Wasserstein-1 convergence gives

`Lambda_r(A_r)/sqrt(r) => g(Z)`,

in distribution with the first two moments, where

`g(z)=phi(z)+z Phi(z)`.

The conditional Poisson noise is negligible on this scale because

`E[(H_r-Lambda_r(A_r))^2]/r = mu_r/r -> 0`.

Therefore

`H_r/sqrt(r) => g(Z)`,

`mu_r/sqrt(r) -> 1/sqrt(pi)`,

`nu_r/r -> v := 1/3 + (sqrt(3)-2)/(2*pi)`,

and

`L_r => (1/sqrt(pi)-g(Z))/sqrt(v)`.

This is exactly the non-Gaussian interior law from the preceding R7-03 theorem. The finite-complement family therefore joins continuously to the full interior-growing regime as `r→∞`.
