## 2. B12 — exact proposal-complement conservation law

Put independent continuous edge weights `U_e` on `K_n`. At a vertex `v`, let `P_k^U(v)` be its `k` incident edges of smallest weight. Write `N=n-1` and `r=N-k`. Under the transformed weights `U'_e=1-U_e`, the `r` smallest transformed incident weights are exactly the complement of the `k` smallest original weights, so pathwise

\[
1_{\{e\in P_r^{U'}(v)\}}=1-1_{\{e\in P_k^U(v)\}}. \tag{2.1}
\]

Let `A_k(U)` be the number of unordered edges proposed by exactly one endpoint and `M_k(U)` the number of mutual edges. Complementing both endpoint proposal indicators sends `(1,0)<->(0,1)` and `(1,1)<->(0,0)`, hence preserves unilateral status:

\[
\boxed{A_k(U)=A_r(1-U).} \tag{2.2}
\]

Every vertex makes exactly `k` proposals, hence

\[
nk=2M_k(U)+A_k(U),\qquad nr=2M_r(1-U)+A_r(1-U). \tag{2.3}
\]

Subtracting and using (2.2) gives the exact pathwise translate

\[
\boxed{M_k(U)-M_r(1-U)=\frac{n(k-r)}2.} \tag{2.4}
\]

Therefore the *entire centered total-edge-count law* is complement-invariant:

\[
\boxed{M_k(U)-E M_k=M_r(1-U)-E M_r.} \tag{2.5}
\]

For the random sample-average degree `\bar D_k=2M_k/n`,

\[
\boxed{\bar D_k(U)-\bar D_r(1-U)=k-r}, \tag{2.6}
\]

so centered laws and variances agree exactly under `k <-> n-1-k`.

There is also a local orientation refinement. Let `O_v^{(k)}` and `I_v^{(k)}` be outgoing/incoming unilateral counts. Then `O_v^{(k)}=k-D_v^{(k)}` and complementing reverses unilateral orientations:

\[
\boxed{O_v^{(r)}(1-U)=I_v^{(k)}(U).} \tag{2.7}
\]

Since `sum_v O_v=sum_v I_v=A_k` pathwise and vertices are exchangeable, `E O_v=E I_v`, which structurally recovers the exact mean-deficit symmetry. But local variances need not be symmetric; indeed

\[
Var(D_v^{(k)})-Var(D_v^{(r)})
=Var(O_v^{(k)})-Var(I_v^{(k)})
=Cov(O_v+I_v,O_v-I_v). \tag{2.8}
\]

## 3. B7 — fixed-budget finite-size corrections and boundary matching

The previously established exact mean deficit, with `r=n-1-k`, is

\[
\delta_{n,k}:=E(k-D)
=\frac{kr}{k+r}\frac{\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2r}{r}}{\binom{2(k+r)}{k+r}}. \tag{3.1}
\]

Fix an integer `q>=1`, put `N=n-1`, and define

\[
\mu_q:=q\frac{\binom{2q}{q}}{4^q}=\frac{\Gamma(q+1/2)}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma(q)}. \tag{3.2}
\]

At `k=q`,

\[
\frac{\delta_{n,q}}{\mu_q}=
\left(1-\frac qN\right)R_{N,q},\qquad
R_{N,q}=4^q\frac{\binom{2(N-q)}{N-q}}{\binom{2N}{N}}. \tag{3.3}
\]

The ratio has the exact product representation

\[
R_{N,q}=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}(1-j/N)^2}{\prod_{j=0}^{2q-1}(1-j/(2N))}. \tag{3.4}
\]

For fixed `q`, Taylor expansion gives

\[
\log R_{N,q}=\frac{q}{2N}+\frac{q(2q-1)}{8N^2}+O_q(N^{-3}), \tag{3.5}
\]

and therefore

\[
R_{N,q}=1+\frac{q}{2N}+\frac{q(3q-1)}{8N^2}+O_q(N^{-3}). \tag{3.6}
\]

Multiplying by `1-q/N` yields

\[
\boxed{\delta_{n,q}=\mu_q\left[1-\frac{q}{2N}-\frac{q(q+1)}{8N^2}+O_q(N^{-3})\right].} \tag{3.7}
\]

The exact complement symmetry of (3.1) gives the identical expansion for `k=N-q`. Thus sparse fixed-budget and dense fixed-complement limits match through two explicit finite-size corrections, not merely at leading order.

If instead `k/N->p in (0,1)`, and `A_k=sum_v(k-D_v)` is the total unilateral-edge count, then `E A_k=n delta_{n,k}` and Stirling in (3.1) gives

\[
\boxed{\frac{E A_k}{n^{3/2}}\to\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}\pi}.} \tag{3.8}
\]

The coefficient is maximized at `p=1/2`, so balanced proposal budget maximizes the leading expected *global unilateral asymmetry*.
