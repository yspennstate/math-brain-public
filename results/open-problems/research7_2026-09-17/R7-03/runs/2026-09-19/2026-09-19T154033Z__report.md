## 3. B2 — attack the conjecture on the smallest finite groups

### Theorem 3.1 — Conjecture 5.7 holds for every finite group of order at most three

Let `Gamma` be a finite group with `N=|Gamma| <= 3`, and let `Q` be a `Gamma`-equivariant positive contraction on `l^2(Gamma)`. Put

\[
p:=\operatorname{FK}(Q)=(\det Q)^{1/N},\qquad
q:=\operatorname{FK}(I-Q)=(\det(I-Q))^{1/N}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes\Gamma}\preccurlyeq P^Q
\preccurlyeq \operatorname{Ber}(1-q)^{\otimes\Gamma},}
\]

and both constants are optimal.

#### Proof

Let the eigenvalues of `Q` be `lambda_1,...,lambda_N` in `[0,1]`. Lyons Theorem 2.11 says that if `X~P^Q`, then

\[
|X|\ \stackrel d=\ S:=\sum_{i=1}^N \xi_i,
\qquad \xi_i\ \text{independent},\quad \xi_i\sim\operatorname{Bern}(\lambda_i).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Also, because `Q` is `Gamma`-equivariant, `P^Q` is invariant under left translation by `Gamma`.

For `N=1` the statement is immediate. For `N=2`, left translation is transitive on every layer of the Boolean lattice `2^Gamma`; for `N=3`, the only group is cyclic and left translation is again transitive on every layer: singletons form one orbit, and two-element sets form one orbit because they are complements of singletons. Therefore, for `N<=3`, conditional on `|X|=j`, `X` is uniform over all `j`-subsets. The same is true for a product Bernoulli process.

Hence, for these values of `N`, stochastic domination of the subset measures is equivalent to stochastic domination of their cardinalities. Indeed, once cardinalities `K>=L` are coupled, sample a uniform random permutation of `Gamma`, take the first `K` sites for the larger set and the first `L` for the smaller set; this is a monotone coupling with the correct uniform layer laws.

It remains to prove

\[
S\ \succeq_{\rm st}\ \operatorname{Bin}(N,p),
\qquad p=(\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)^{1/N}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For `N=2`,

\[
\Pr(S\ge2)=\lambda_1\lambda_2=p^2,
\]

and

\[
\Pr(S\ge1)
=\lambda_1+\lambda_2-\lambda_1\lambda_2
\ge 2p-p^2
=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(2,p)\ge1),
\]

by AM-GM.

For `N=3`, write

\[
e_2=\lambda_1\lambda_2+\lambda_1\lambda_3+\lambda_2\lambda_3,
\qquad e_3=\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3=p^3.
\]

Then

\[
\Pr(S\ge3)=p^3
=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(3,p)\ge3),
\]

while

\[
\Pr(S\ge2)=e_2-2e_3
\ge 3p^2-2p^3
=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(3,p)\ge2),
\tag{3.3}
\]

because AM-GM applied to the three pair products gives `e_2>=3p^2`.

For the remaining tail, generalized Holder gives

\[
(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3)^{1/3}
+
\bigl((1-\lambda_1)(1-\lambda_2)(1-\lambda_3)\bigr)^{1/3}
\le1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus

\[
\prod_i(1-\lambda_i)\le(1-p)^3
\]

and therefore

\[
\Pr(S\ge1)
=1-\prod_i(1-\lambda_i)
\ge1-(1-p)^3
=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(3,p)\ge1).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equations (3.2)–(3.5) prove the lower product domination.

For the upper bound, use the standard complement identity for finite determinantal measures: if `X~P^Q`, then `Gamma\X~P^{I-Q}`. Applying the just-proved lower bound to `I-Q` yields a monotone coupling in which a Bernoulli-`q` product set is contained in `Gamma\X`. Taking complements gives

\[
X\subseteq Y,\qquad Y\sim\operatorname{Ber}(1-q)^{\otimes\Gamma},
\]

which is the required upper stochastic domination.

Finally the constants are forced. If product density `r` is dominated by `P^Q`, the increasing event `X=Gamma` gives

\[
r^N\le\Pr_{P^Q}(X=\Gamma)=\det Q=p^N,
\]

so `r<=p`. If `P^Q` is dominated by product density `r`, the decreasing event `X=emptyset` gives

\[
\det(I-Q)=q^N\ge(1-r)^N,
\]

so `r>=1-q`. Hence both bounds are optimal. This completes the proof. `□`

**B2 outcome: SUCCESS on a strict finite source slice.** There is no counterexample for group order `<=3`; the full conjectured constants are exact there.

## 4. B6 — deform from the solved low-order case

The preceding proof reveals that the spectral part of the conjecture is easier than the configuration part.

For every finite `N`, Theorem 2.11 reduces `|X|` to a Poisson-binomial sum with eigenvalues of `Q`. At `N<=3`, translation invariance happens to promote that one-dimensional cardinality law to the entire configuration law because the regular group action is transitive on every fixed-cardinality layer.

The low-order proof therefore decomposes the conjecture into two logically separate ingredients:

1. **spectral/cardinality comparison:** compare the Poisson-binomial tails associated with the eigenvalues of `Q` to the binomial tails at the geometric mean `p=(det Q)^{1/N}`;
2. **orbit-sensitive gluing:** turn a cardinality coupling into a monotone coupling of configurations while respecting the non-exchangeable orbit structure forced by a general regular group action.

For `N<=3`, ingredient 2 is free. This is why the harmonic-mean worst-site mechanism can be bypassed entirely in these cases.

There is a useful exact inequality that survives for every `N` at the first tail:

\[
\left(\prod_i\lambda_i\right)^{1/N}
+
\left(\prod_i(1-\lambda_i)\right)^{1/N}\le1,
\tag{4.1}
\]

again by generalized Holder. Hence

\[
\Pr(S\ge1)\ge 1-(1-p)^N.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The top tail is always equality,

\[
\Pr(S=N)=\det Q=p^N.
\tag{4.3}
\]

So any finite-group obstruction to Conjecture 5.7 must live in intermediate configuration events, not the empty/full events and not the first occupancy tail. This is a sharper diagnosis than the campaign's existing harmonic-mean bound, but it is still only a restricted structural result.

**B6 outcome: SUCCESS as a deformation/structure theorem, not source closure.** It isolates the configuration-orbit problem as the first place where the small-group proof can fail to extend.

## 5. B30 — localize the exact gluing failure: order four is the first genuinely new finite case

The small-group argument stops for a precise group-theoretic reason at `N=4`.

For `Gamma=Z/4Z`, translation on two-element subsets has two orbits: adjacent pairs (difference `±1`) and antipodal pairs (difference `2`). For `Gamma=(Z/2Z)^2`, translation on two-element subsets has three orbits, one for each nonzero difference. Thus a `Gamma`-invariant determinantal measure need not be uniform conditional on cardinality two.

Consequently, even a complete proof that

\[
|X|\succeq_{\rm st}\operatorname{Bin}(N,p)
\]

for arbitrary eigenvalues would not by itself prove the configuration-level stochastic domination required by Conjecture 5.7 once `N>=4`. Increasing events can distinguish pair orbits while having the same cardinality profile.

This identifies the **first nontrivial finite source boundary**:

```text
|Gamma| <= 3: full Conjecture 5.7 proved above;
|Gamma| = 4: cardinality-to-configuration gluing already ceases to be automatic.
```

The natural next finite calculation would therefore parameterize `Gamma`-equivariant positive contractions for `Z/4Z` and `(Z/2Z)^2`, reduce increasing events by orbit, and test/prove the required inequalities orbit by orbit. That would still be a finite-group subproblem, not closure of the all-group source.

**B30 outcome: SUCCESS.** The failure of the low-order proof is localized exactly at the first split of a Boolean layer into multiple translation orbits.
