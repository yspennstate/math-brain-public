## 4. B18 — exact spectral-mixture language for the adjacent event

Let `F` be the unitary Fourier matrix of `Z/6Z` and let

\[
Q=F\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_5)F^*,
\qquad
p=(\lambda_0\cdots\lambda_5)^{1/6}.
\]

Use the finite-DPP spectral mixture: include each frequency `j` independently with probability `lambda_j`, obtaining `J`; conditional on `J`, sample the projection DPP with kernel `F_JF_J^*`.

Let `I=U_adj^c`, the event that the occupied coordinate set is an independent set of the six-cycle, and define

\[
f(J)=P^{F_JF_J^*}(I).
\]

Then

\[
P^Q(I)=\mathbb E_{\lambda}f(J).
\tag{4.1}
\]

When all six spectral parameters equal `p`, `Q=pI`, so the DPP is iid Bernoulli(`p`) and

\[
P_p(I)=\mathbb E_p f(J).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the entire adjacent-event inequality (3.1) is **exactly equivalent** to

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\lambda}f(J)\le \mathbb E_p f(J),
\qquad p=(\prod_i\lambda_i)^{1/6}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The function `f` has an exact finite table:

- `|J|=0,1`: `f=1`;
- `|J|>=4`: `f=0`, since the independence number of `C_6` is 3;
- `|J|=2`: if the cyclic frequency separation is `1,2,3`, then
  \[
  f=5/6,\ 1/2,\ 1/3
  \]
  respectively;
- `|J|=3`: `f=1/4` exactly when `J` contains one representative of every residue class modulo 3, and `f=0` otherwise.

The two-frequency formula follows because a rank-two Fourier projection selects an adjacent coordinate pair with probability six times

\[
\frac1{36}|1-e^{\pi i d/3}|^2,
\]

which is `1/36,1/12,1/9` for frequency distances `d=1,2,3`. The three-frequency formula follows because the only independent 3-subsets of `C_6` are `{0,2,4}` and `{1,3,5}`; the corresponding `3 x 3` Fourier minor vanishes unless the spectral frequencies are distinct modulo 3, and otherwise each alternating triple has weight `1/8`.

The table also gives the exact monotonicity

\[
J\subseteq J'\implies f(J)\ge f(J').
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a real change of language: the unresolved DPP inequality is now a six-coordinate Bernoulli product inequality for one explicit decreasing function. It is not yet a proof, because fixed geometric mean does not give a coordinatewise product-measure stochastic order.

## 5. B7 — strict local stability of the scalar spectrum

I next attacked the easiest possible counterexample mechanism: an arbitrarily small symmetry-breaking perturbation of `pI` at fixed FK determinant.

Fix `0<p<1` and write

\[
\lambda_i(\varepsilon)=p e^{\varepsilon x_i},
\qquad \sum_i x_i=0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then `prod lambda_i=p^6` exactly. Let

\[
G(\lambda)=P^Q(U_{\rm adj})-P_p(U_{\rm adj}).
\]

Cauchy--Binet gives for a coordinate set `S` of size `k`

\[
D_S(\lambda)=\sum_{|T|=k}|\det F_{S,T}|^2\prod_{j\in T}\lambda_j.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Substituting (5.1), the first derivative of `G` at zero vanishes by cyclic symmetry plus `sum x_i=0`, and

\[
G(\lambda(\varepsilon))
=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}x^TM_px+O(\varepsilon^3).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The exact first row of the symmetric circulant Hessian matrix is

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_0&=p^2(5p^3-2p^2-3p+2),\\
r_1&=\frac{p^2}{6}(24p^3-8p^2-5p+1),\\
r_2&=\frac{p^2}{2}(8p^3-2p^2-3p+1),\\
r_3&=\frac{2p^2}{3}(6p^3-2p^2-2p+1),\\
r_4&=r_2,\qquad r_5=r_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Diagonalizing the circulant matrix gives, on the zero-mean Fourier modes,

\[
\boxed{\mu_{1}=\mu_5=p^2(1-p)^2(1+p)>0,}
\tag{5.5}
\]

\[
\boxed{\mu_2=\mu_4=p^2(1-p)(2-p^2)>0,}
\tag{5.6}

\[
\boxed{\mu_3=p^2(1-p)^2(p+2)>0.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore the scalar spectrum is a **strict local minimum** of the adjacent-event gap on the fixed-product manifold for every `0<p<1`. ## 6. B27 — periodic-spectrum lifting and full two-sided restricted theorems

There is also a clean quantifier/product decomposition that proves much more than the adjacent event on large exact families.

Let `d|n` and let a `Z/nZ`-equivariant kernel have periodic Fourier spectrum

\[
\lambda_{j+d}=\lambda_j.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
Q(x,y)=\frac1n\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}\lambda_a e^{2\pi i a(x-y)/n}
\sum_{\ell=0}^{n/d-1}e^{2\pi i\ell d(x-y)/n}.
\]

The inner sum vanishes unless `n/d` divides `x-y`. Hence the coordinate space splits into `n/d` residue classes modulo `n/d`, with no matrix entries between distinct classes. On each class the induced kernel is exactly the `Z/dZ` circulant kernel `K` with Fourier eigenvalues `lambda_0,...,lambda_{d-1}`. Therefore

\[
P^Q=(P^K)^{\otimes n/d}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Also

\[
FK(Q)=FK(K)=\left(\prod_{a=0}^{d-1}\lambda_a\right)^{1/d},
\tag{6.3}
\]

and identically for `I-Q`. Independent products of monotone couplings then lift **both** sides of Lyons 5.7 from `K` to `Q`.

Since the cumulative R7-03 record already proves the full sharp two-sided theorem for groups of orders at most three, this gives the full source inequality for every `Z/6Z` spectrum of the forms
