## 3. Method B1 — change the category

Instead of treating `H=k-D` as merely a sum of locally dependent graph indicators, I recast it as a symmetric statistic of the independent non-root edge array after conditioning on the root order statistic.

Let `S` be the root's `k` proposed neighbors and `X` the `(k+1)`st smallest root-edge score. Conditional on `(X,S)`, the root scores to `S` are iid `Uniform(0,X)` and the non-root edge scores remain independent. Write

\[
H=\sum_{i\in S}R_i,
\]

where `R_i` records rejection of the root edge by endpoint `i`, and let

\[
q_n(x)=x^{-1}\mathbb E[(x-T)_+],
\qquad T\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,n-k-1).
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb E(H\mid X=x)=kq_n(x).
\]

The new category makes the non-root randomness amenable to an ANOVA/Hoeffding projection rather than a global graph comparison.

Outcome: **successful**. It yields a shorter dependence proof for the complete interior regime, not just the balanced test slice.

## 4. Method B11 — dualize via the shared-edge Hoeffding projection

Fix two selected neighbors `i != j` and let `W=U_{ij}` be their unique shared non-root edge. Given `(X=x,W=w)`, `R_i` and `R_j` depend on disjoint remaining edge sets and independent root scores, hence are conditionally independent. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j\mid X=x)
=
\operatorname{Var}(a_{n,x}(W)),
\]

where `a_{n,x}(w)=P(R_i=1|X=x,W=w)` is the first ANOVA projection onto the shared-edge sigma-field.

If `B_u~Bin(n-3,u)`, direct conditioning on the root score `u` gives

\[
a_{n,x}(w)
=c_{n,x}
+\frac1x\int_0^x\mathbf 1_{\{w<u\}}P(B_u=k-1)\,du.
\]

Let `F_n` be the cdf of `T~Beta(k,n-k-1)`. The exact density identity

\[
(n-2)P(B_u=k-1)=f_T(u)
\]

therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
 a_{n,x}(w)=c_{n,x}
 +\frac{[F_n(x)-F_n(w)]_+}{x(n-2)}.
}
\]

Consequently, for `W~Uniform(0,1)`,

\[
\boxed{
 b_n(x):=\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j\mid X=x)
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}_W([F_n(x)-F_n(W)]_+)}{x^2(n-2)^2}.
}
\tag{A}
\]

This is an exact identity, not just an estimate, and immediately implies

\[
0\le b_n(x)\le\frac{1}{4x^2(n-2)^2}.
\tag{B}
\]

Outcome: **successful**. It identifies the pair dependence as an exact squared `L^2` projection norm.

## 5. Method B14 — use the acting permutation group

Conditional on `X=x`, the vector `(R_i)_{i\in S}` is exchangeable under `S_k`. Therefore its covariance matrix has the exact two-eigenspace form

\[
\Sigma_x=(a_n(x)-b_n(x))I_k+b_n(x)J_k,
\qquad a_n(x)=q_n(x)(1-q_n(x)).
\]

The permutation representation splits as the trivial representation `span{1}` and its standard orthogonal complement. The corresponding eigenvalues are

\[
\lambda_{\rm triv}=a_n+(k-1)b_n,
\qquad
\lambda_{\rm std}=a_n-b_n.
\]

Since `H=1^T R`, only the trivial eigenspace contributes:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(H\mid X=x)
=kq_n(x)(1-q_n(x))+k(k-1)b_n(x).
}
\tag{C}
\]

Outcome: **successful**. The entire dependence relevant to the scalar degree is reduced to one ANOVA norm `b_n(x)`.

## 6. New finite-n covariance-budget theorem

The root cutoff has

\[
X\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k+1,n-k-1).
\]

For every `k>=2`,

\[
\mathbb E[X^{-2}]
=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{k(k-1)}.
\]

Integrating (B) exactly therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
0\le k(k-1)\mathbb E[b_n(X)]
\le\frac{n-1}{4(n-2)}<\frac12.
}
\tag{D}
\]

Thus the **entire ordered-pair covariance contribution is uniformly bounded by a constant for every finite `n` and `2<=k<=n-2`**. Combining (C), `q(1-q)<=q`, and the tower identity `E[kq_n(X)]=EH` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(H\mid X)
\le
\mathbb EH+\frac{n-1}{4(n-2)}.
}
\tag{E}
\]

The previous R7-03 exact mean theorem gives, with `r=n-1-k`,

\[
\mathbb EH
=\frac{2B(n-1,n-1)}{B(k,k)B(r,r)}
\sim\sqrt{\frac{kr}{\pi(k+r)}}
\sim\frac{s_n}{\sqrt\pi},
\]

where

\[
s_n^2=\frac{k(n-k)}n.

Hence for every interior sequence `k->infinity`, `n-k->infinity`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(H\mid X)}{s_n^2}
\longrightarrow0,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{H-\mathbb E(H\mid X)}{s_n}\to0
\quad\text{in }L^2.

\tag{F}
