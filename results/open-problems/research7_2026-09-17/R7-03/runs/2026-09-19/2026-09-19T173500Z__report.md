## 5. B15 — deliberately break the scalar symmetry

The exact gap formulas permit a rigorous local symmetry-breaking calculation. Fix `p in (0,1)` and take

\[
\lambda_i=p e^{\varepsilon x_i},\qquad \sum_i x_i=0.
\]

For either pair shape, let `w` be the Fourier-minor probability distribution on frequency pairs and write `S=x_i+x_j`. Its coordinate marginals imply `E_w S=0`. Then

\[
\frac{G_P(\varepsilon)}{p^2}
=E_w e^{\varepsilon S}-pE_w e^{-\varepsilon S}-(1-p),
\]

hence

\[
G_P(0)=G_P'(0)=0,
\qquad
\boxed{G_P''(0)=p^2(1-p)E_wS^2>0}
\]

in every nonzero zero-sum direction.

For the triple gap,

\[
\frac{G_T(\varepsilon)}{p^3}
=E_w e^{-\varepsilon S}-pE_u e^{-\varepsilon x}-(1-p),
\]

where `u` is uniform on frequencies. The quadratic form `E_w(x_i+x_j)^2` has spectrum

\[
\{4/5,1/5,1/5,2/5,2/5\},

so on the zero-sum subspace

\[
E_wS^2\ge E_u x^2.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{G_T''(0)\ge p^3(1-p)E_u x^2>0.}
\]

Thus the scalar spectrum is a **strict local minimum of all four order-five gaps** on each fixed-geometric-mean logarithmic slice. There is no sufficiently small symmetry-breaking counterexample near product kernels.
