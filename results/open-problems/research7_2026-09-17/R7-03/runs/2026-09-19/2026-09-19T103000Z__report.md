## 3. B17 — identify the degree deficit with a Beta Gini mean difference

Use the equivalent low-weight construction. Every edge has an iid `Uniform(0,1)` weight; each vertex proposes the `k` incident edges with smallest weight. Put

\[
r=n-1-k,
\]

so each vertex rejects exactly its `r` largest incident edges. Fix root `0`, let `D=D_{n,k}` be its degree, and let

\[
H=k-D
\]

be the number of root proposals rejected by the opposite endpoint.

Fix an edge `e={0,i}` with weight `U`. Among the `N=n-2` other edges incident to `0`, let `T_0` be the `r`th largest weight. Among the `N` other edges incident to `i`, let `T_i` be the `r`th largest weight. The two underlying edge sets are disjoint, so `T_0,T_i` are iid and independent of `U`.

The root proposes `e` iff `U<T_0`; endpoint `i` rejects `e` iff `U>T_i`. Thus

\[
\{e\text{ contributes to }H\}=\{T_i<U<T_0\}.
\]

Conditional on `T_0,T_i`, the probability is `(T_0-T_i)_+`. By iid symmetry,

\[
\mathbb E(T_0-T_i)_+=\frac12\mathbb E|T_0-T_i|.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The `r`th largest of `N=n-2` uniforms is the `k=n-1-r`th smallest and hence has law `Beta(k,r)`. Therefore, for iid `X,Y~Beta(k,r)`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EH=\frac{n-1}{2}\mathbb E|X-Y|.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is an exact finite-`n` representation: the degree deficit is controlled by a classical Gini mean difference of one beta order statistic. No approximation and no false independence between different root-neighbor indicators is used.

For a beta law the standard mean-absolute-difference identity is

\[
\mathbb E|X-Y|
=\frac{4}{k+r}
\frac{B(k+r,k+r)}{B(k,k)B(r,r)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since `k+r=n-1`, (3.2) and (3.3) immediately give the exact theorem below.

## 4. New exact finite-`n` theorem

For every `n>=3` and `1<=k<=n-2`, with `r=n-1-k`,

\[
\boxed{
 k-\mathbb ED_{n,k}
 =\frac{2B(n-1,n-1)}{B(k,k)B(r,r)}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Using `B(a,a)=(a-1)!^2/(2a-1)!`, this is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb ED_{n,k}
=k-
\frac{2(n-2)!^2(2k-1)!(2r-1)!}
{(2n-3)!(k-1)!^2(r-1)!^2}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Using instead

\[
B(a,a)=\frac{2}{a\binom{2a}{a}},
\]

and writing `m=n-1=k+r`, the deficit has the especially useful central-binomial form

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{n,k}:=k-\mathbb ED_{n,k}
=\frac{kr}{k+r}
\frac{\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2r}{r}}
{\binom{2(k+r)}{k+r}}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This formula is exact for every nondegenerate finite parameter pair, not merely asymptotic.

### Independent finite-sum representation

Let `q=1-U`. Conditional on `q`, the two counts of other endpoint edges larger than `U` are independent `Bin(N,q)` variables. Hence

\[
\mathbb EH
=(n-1)
\sum_{a=r}^{N}\sum_{b=0}^{r-1}
\binom Na\binom Nb
B(a+b+1,2N-a-b+1).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The companion exact rational checker compares (4.4) against both (4.1) and (4.3) for all `741` pairs with `3<=n<=40` and finds exact equality throughout. This is a self-check, not an independent audit.

## 5. B5 — exact complement duality and the shape of the mean deficit

Formula (4.3) is invariant under swapping

\[
k\longleftrightarrow r=n-1-k.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{n,k}=\delta_{n,n-1-k}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb ED_{n,k}-\mathbb ED_{n,r}=k-r,
\qquad r=n-1-k.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The adjacent ratio is also exact:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\delta_{n,k+1}}{\delta_{n,k}}
=\frac{(2k+1)(r-1)}{k(2r-1)}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Comparing with one gives

\[
(2k+1)(r-1)-k(2r-1)=r-k-1.
\]

Hence the deficit strictly increases while `r>k+1`, is flat across the two central indices when `r=k+1`, and strictly decreases after that. Thus the exact finite-`n` deficit is symmetric and unimodal, with its maximum at the balanced proposal/complement budget.

This duality was not visible in the source's one-sided sparse asymptotic formula and provides a single finite formula connecting sparse and dense boundary calculations.

## 7. Two-parameter asymptotics and a sharper source mean theorem

From

\[
\binom{2a}{a}

\left(1-\frac1{8a}+O(a^{-2})\right),

formula (4.3) yields whenever `k->infinity` and `r->infinity`,

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{n,k}
=\sqrt{\frac{kr}{\pi(k+r)}}
\left[
1+\frac1{8(k+r)}-\frac1{8k}-\frac1{8r}
+O(k^{-2}+r^{-2})
\right].
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
 k-\mathbb ED_{n,k}
\sim \sqrt{\frac{kr}{\pi(k+r)}}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Now take the sparse growing regime, `r=n-1-k`, and suppose `k=o(sqrt n)`. Expanding the exact expression gives

\[
\delta_{n,k}
=\sqrt{\frac{k}{\pi}}
\left(
1-\frac1{8k}-\frac{k}{2(n-1)}
+O(k^{-2}+k^2/n^2+1/n)

\tag{7.3}
\]

The finite-population correction has absolute size `k^(3/2)/n`. It is `o(k^(-1/2))` precisely when `k^2/n -> 0`. Consequently the source's Theorem-8 three-term expansion actually holds throughout

\[
\boxed{k\to\infty,\qquad k=o(\sqrt n),}

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb ED_{n,k}
=k-\sqrt{\frac{k}{\pi}}
+\frac1{8\sqrt{\pi k}}

\tag{7.4}

The source states (7.4) only for `k=o(n^(1/3))`. The exact formula therefore extends that particular three-term mean expansion to the full `o(sqrt n)` range.
