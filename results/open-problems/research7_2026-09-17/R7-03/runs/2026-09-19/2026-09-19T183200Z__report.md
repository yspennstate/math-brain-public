## 4. B18 — say the four inequalities in a different language: an excess chain

For either pair/triple orbit shape define

\[
E_2=D-p^2,
\qquad
E_3=T-p^3,
\qquad
E_4=D_4-p^4.
\]

Then (3.1) is exactly

\[
\boxed{E_2\ge E_3\ge E_4.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is more informative than the original subtraction form: the order-five obstruction asks whether the Cauchy--Binet excess above the geometric mean decreases under the matched `2 -> 3 -> 4` minor chain.

Each excess is separately nonnegative. The Cauchy--Binet weights on frequency `k`-sets sum to one and every frequency has marginal weight `k/5`. Therefore weighted AM--GM gives

\[
D\ge p^2,
\qquad
T\ge p^3,
\qquad
D_4\ge p^4.
\tag{4.2}
\]

So the hard part is not positivity of the excesses but their ordering.

**B18 outcome: SUCCESS.** The four nonlinear orbit inequalities become two excess-monotonicity inequalities per orbit, which exposes an additional deformation monotonicity used next.

## 5. B5 — add a common scaling parameter and differentiate / order it

Fix any positive spectral shape `r=(r_0,...,r_4)` and define

\[
\lambda_i(s)=s r_i,
\qquad
0<s\le s_{\max}:=\frac1{\max_i r_i}.
\]

Write

\[
p_0=(r_0r_1r_2r_3r_4)^{1/5}.
\]

By homogeneity of the relevant minors,

\[
p(s)=sp_0,
\quad D(s)=s^2D_0,
\quad T(s)=s^3T_0,
\quad D_4(s)=s^4D_{4,0}.
\]

Hence

\[
E_2(s)-E_3(s)
=s^2\big[(D_0-p_0^2)-s(T_0-p_0^3)\big],
\tag{5.1}
\]

\[
E_3(s)-E_4(s)
=s^3\big[(T_0-p_0^3)-s(D_{4,0}-p_0^4)\big].
\tag{5.2}
\]

By (4.2), the coefficients multiplied by `-s` are nonnegative. Therefore

\[
\frac{E_2(s)-E_3(s)}{s^2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{E_3(s)-E_4(s)}{s^3}
\]

are nonincreasing functions of the common scale `s`.

### Radial-boundary theorem

For every one of the four order-five orbit inequalities, the hardest point on a fixed positive spectral ray occurs at the largest admissible scale. Therefore **to prove the lower half of C0036 on `Z/5Z`, it is enough to prove the four orbit inequalities on the spectral boundary**

\[
\boxed{\max_i\lambda_i=1.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Zero-product boundary cases have `p=0` and follow by continuity / trivial lower Bernoulli density.

This theorem is global and does **not** assume two spectral levels.

**B5 outcome: SUCCESS.** The arbitrary order-five lower problem loses one radial degree of freedom; a counterexample, if one exists, can be scaled until some Fourier eigenvalue is exactly one without repairing the violated orbit gap.

## 6. B25 — count the same Cauchy--Binet weight mass twice: the missing 2+3 family

The one-spike theorem covers multiplicity pattern `1+4`. To finish all two-level spectra it is enough to prove the `2+3` pattern.

Assume the Fourier spectrum is

\[
(a,a,b,b,b),
\qquad a,b\in[0,1],
\tag{6.1}
\]

in arbitrary frequency locations. For either spatial pair orbit, let `c` be the Cauchy--Binet weight assigned to the unique exceptional-exceptional frequency pair. Depending on the two relative orbit types,

\[
c\in\{\alpha,\beta\},
\quad
\alpha=\frac{5-\sqrt5}{50},
\quad
\beta=\frac{5+\sqrt5}{50}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Total pair weight is one and every frequency has marginal pair weight `2/5`. Counting exceptional incidences two ways gives exactly:

\[
\text{AA weight}=c,
\]

\[
\text{AB total weight}=\frac45-2c,
\]

\[
\text{BB total weight}=\frac15+c.
\]

Therefore

\[
D
=c a^2+\left(\frac45-2c\right)ab
+\left(\frac15+c\right)b^2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Using complementary frequency pairs for the three-minor gives

\[
T
=c b^3+\left(\frac45-2c\right)ab^2
+\left(\frac15+c\right)a^2b,
\tag{6.4}
\]

and complementary one-frequency weights for the four-minor give

\[
D_4=\frac25ab^3+\frac35a^2b^2.
\tag{6.5}
\]

These formulas cover both spatial orbit shapes and both possible placements of the exceptional pair; only `c=alpha` or `beta` changes.

Take first `a,b>0` and write

\[
a=bt^5,
\qquad
p=(a^2b^3)^{1/5}=bt^2.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The contraction constraints are

\[
0<b\le1,
\qquad bt^5\le1.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Define

\[
S=t^8+2t^7+3t^6+4t^5+5t^4+4t^3+3t^2+2t+1,
\]

\[
P_c=cS+\frac{4t^3+3t^2+2t+1}{5},
\]

\[
Q_c=cS+\frac{t^8+2t^7+3t^6+4t^5}{5},
\]

\[
R=\frac{t^5(3t^3+6t^2+4t+2)}5.
\]

Direct algebra gives the two exact factorizations

\[
\boxed{
D-T-p^2(1-p)
=b^2(t-1)^2[P_c-bQ_c]
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
T-D_4-p^3(1-p)
=b^3(t-1)^2[Q_c-bR].
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

### Pair bracket, t<=1

Since `b<=1`,

\[
P_c-bQ_c\ge P_c-Q_c
=\frac{(1-t)(t+1)^3(t^4+3t^2+1)}5\ge0.
\tag{6.10}
\]

### Pair bracket, t>=1

Since `b<=t^{-5}`,

\[
P_c-bQ_c\ge \frac{t^5P_c-Q_c}{t^5},
\]

and

\[
t^5P_c-Q_c
=\frac{t-1}{5}\left[5cU(t)+t^5(3t^2+4t+3)\right],
\tag{6.11}
\]

where

\[
U(t)=t^{12}+3t^{11}+6t^{10}+10t^9+15t^8+18t^7+19t^6
+18t^5+15t^4+10t^3+6t^2+3t+1.
\]

Every coefficient is positive, proving the pair bracket.

### Triple bracket, t<=1

The only numerical property of the Fourier weights needed is

\[
\alpha>\frac1{25},\qquad \beta>\frac1{25}.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Again `b<=1`, so it is enough to prove `Q_c-R>=0`. Let

\[
h=-2t^8-4t^7-t^6+2t^5.
\]

Then `Q_c-R=cS+h/5`, and the exact identity

\[
S+5h
=(1-t)(9t^7+27t^6+29t^5+15t^4+10t^3+6t^2+3t+1)
\tag{6.13}
\]

gives

\[
Q_c-R
=\left(c-\frac1{25}\right)S+\frac{S+5h}{25}\ge0.
\tag{6.14}
\]

### Triple bracket, t>=1

Now `b<=t^{-5}`. Let

\[
h_2=t^8+2t^7+3t^6+4t^5-3t^3-6t^2-4t-2.
\]

Then

\[
t^5Q_c-R=t^5\left(cS+\frac{h_2}{5}\right),
\]

and

\[
S+5h_2
=(t-1)(6t^7+18t^6+36t^5+60t^4+65t^3+54t^2+27t+9).
\tag{6.15}
\]

Hence

\[
cS+\frac{h_2}{5}
=\left(c-\frac1{25}\right)S+\frac{S+5h_2}{25}\ge0.
\tag{6.16}
\]

This proves all four non-cardinality lower inequalities for the `2+3` family.

The previous all-finite cardinality theorem supplies the remaining seven invariant upper sets, so

\[
\operatorname{Ber}(\operatorname{FK}(Q))^{\otimes\Gamma}\preccurlyeq P^Q
\]

for every two-level `2+3` spectrum on `Z/5Z`. Applying the same lower theorem to `I-Q` (which is again two-level) and complementing configurations gives the sharp upper domination. Boundary values follow by continuity.

Together with the previous `1+4` theorem, this proves:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Lyons Conjecture 5.7 holds sharply on } Z/5Z
\text{ for every Fourier spectrum taking at most two distinct values.}
}
\tag{6.17}
\]

**B25 outcome: SUCCESS.** The missing nontrivial multiplicity pattern closes by exact incidence-weight bookkeeping and polynomial factorization, without an exhaustive minor table.
