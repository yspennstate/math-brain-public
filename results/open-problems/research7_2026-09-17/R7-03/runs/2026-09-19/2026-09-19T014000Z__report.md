## Method B22 — read the failure: sharpen Aldous's terminal-set step

For Aldous's iid return-block cover process, let `C` be the block cover time, `B_i` the set still uncovered after `i` blocks, `T=B_{C-1}`, and `c(B)` the expected fresh-block time to cover `B`. Aldous equation (24) gives, with his `eta`,

`P(B subset S_1) <= 4/[eta c(B)^2]`.

Instead of truncating the block index at a deterministic `K`, condition on the actual surviving uncovered set. Since block `S_i` is independent of the past,

`P(c(T)>=u)`

`= sum_{i>=1} E[1_{C>i-1}1_{c(B_{i-1})>=u} P(B_{i-1} subset S_i | F_{i-1})]`

`<= [4/(eta u^2)] sum_{i>=1}P(C>i-1)`

`= 4 EC/(eta u^2)`.

Integrating the tail and using `c(T)<=EC` yields the complete bound

`E c(T) <= 4 sqrt(EC/eta)`.

Combined with Aldous Theorem 1(b), this improves the corresponding block-level concentration estimate to

`Var(C)/(EC)^2 <= 4 kappa / sqrt(eta EC)`.

This is a valid sharpening of the old proof route, but it still controls the number of iid return blocks, not the accumulated random durations of those blocks. B24 shows that this distinction is exactly where the source claim can fail.

## Method B12 — conserved terminal-value budget

The block-value function obeys the dynamic-programming identity

`c(B)=1+E[c(B\S_1)]`.

Therefore

`M_i=(i wedge C)+c(B_{i wedge C})`

is a martingale from `M_0=EC` to `M_C=C`. Writing

`D_i=c(B_i)-c(B_{i+1})`

for `i<C`, one has pathwise

`sum_{i<C} D_i=EC`,

and conditionally `E[D_i|F_i]=1`. Hence

`C-EC=sum_{i<C}(1-D_i)`

and, by stopped martingale orthogonality,

`Var(C)=E sum_{i<C} E[(D_i-1)^2|F_i]`.

This identifies an exact conserved budget for **block count fluctuation**. It also makes the missing mechanism explicit: source-level cover-time fluctuation can be generated by random block *durations* after the relevant vertices in a block have already been covered.

## Method B24 — extremal object: star plus clique delay trap

For each `k>=2`, define `G_k` as an unweighted simple graph with a center `s`, `k` pendant leaves `a_1,...,a_k` adjacent to `s`, a clique `Q={g,q_2,...,q_k}` of size `k`, and one bridge edge `s-g`.

Let `h_k=t_hit(G_k)`. The graph has

`m_k=(k^2+k+2)/2`

edges and effective-resistance diameter at most `2+2/k`. The commute identity therefore gives

`h_k <= 2m_k(2+2/k) <= 6k^2`.

### Exact trap-excursion variance

Condition on a departure `s->g` and let `Y_k` be the complete time until the next return to `s`. Every visit to `g` exits to `s` with probability `1/k`. On failure it moves to a non-gateway clique vertex; the return time `G` to `g` is geometric with success probability `1/(k-1)`, so

`EG=k-1`,  `Var(G)=(k-2)(k-1)`.

If `F` is the number of failed gateway visits before exit, then

`EF=k-1`,  `Var(F)=k(k-1)`.

Thus

`T_{g->s}=1+sum_{j=1}^F(1+G_j)`,

and the random-sum formula gives

`Var(T_{g->s})=(k-1)^2(k-2)+k^3(k-1) >= k^4/2`.

Consequently

`EY_k=k^2-k+2`,  `Var(Y_k)>=k^4/2`.

### Positive-probability state with the clique covered and many leaves remaining

Start from an arbitrary vertex and first hit `s`; before then at most one ordinary leaf has been visited. From `s`, the number of ordinary-leaf excursions before the first trap excursion is geometric in the trap choice, so

`P(N<=floor(k/2)) -> 1-e^{-1/2}`.

The first trap excursion covers the clique before returning to `s` with probability `1-o(1)`: coupling its internal motion to random walk on `K_k`, after `t_0=ceil(4(k-1)log k)` internal steps the clique-cover failure probability is at most `k^{-3}`, while the probability of using the bridge exit before then is at most `(2+4log k)/k`.

Hence for all sufficiently large `k`, uniformly over every starting vertex `x`, with probability at least `1/4` there is a stopping time `sigma` at which the walk is back at `s`, the whole clique is covered, and at least `r>=k/3` ordinary leaves remain unvisited.

### Future coupon collector forces divergent normalized variance

After `sigma`, let `N_r` be the number of center departures required to see the remaining `r` target leaves. When `j` remain the success probability is `j/(k+1)`, so

`E N_r=(k+1)H_r`.

Let `J_r` count trap departures before the last target leaf is found. Wald's identity gives

`E J_r=H_r`.

Conditional on the departure-category sequence, the `J_r` trap excursion durations are iid copies of `Y_k`, independent of that sequence. Therefore if `R_r` is the remaining cover time after `sigma`,

`Var(R_r) >= H_r Var(Y_k) >= (1/2)k^4 H_r`.

The conditional-variance formula then yields, uniformly over every start,

`Var_x(tau_cov) >= (1/16) k^4 log k`

for all large `k`, hence

`SD_x(tau_cov) >= (1/4)k^2 sqrt(log k)`.

Since `h_k<=6k^2`,

`SD_x(tau_cov)/t_hit >= sqrt(log k)/24 -> infinity`.

This directly refutes a universal `SD=O(t_hit)` bound.

### The counterexample remains inside the Aldous/DLP concentration regime

On the same positive-probability event, expected future trap time is at least

`H_r (k^2-k+2)`,

so `t_cov(G_k)>=c k^2 log k`. Matthews' general upper bound gives `t_cov(G_k)<=h_k H_{2k}=O(h_k log k)`. Thus

`t_cov(G_k)/t_hit(G_k)=Theta(log k)->infinity`.

So the family does not evade the asymptotic regime stated immediately before DLP Question 5.2.

### Sharpness of the existing exponent

For every fixed `beta>1/2`,

`SD(tau_cov)/[t_hit^beta t_cov^(1-beta)] >= c_beta (log k)^(beta-1/2) -> infinity`.

Hence no universal interpolation exponent larger than `1/2` is possible. The geometric-mean scale is exponent-sharp.

### Tail clause

Choose, for each `G_k`, a start attaining the source worst-start mean `t_cov`. If universal constants `A,a>0` satisfied the standard two-sided form

`P(|tau_cov-t_cov|>=u t_hit)<=A e^{-au}`,

But the variance lower bound above makes the left side diverge at least like a positive constant times `log k`. Thus the standard universal constant-rate two-sided exponential-tail interpretation is also false.
