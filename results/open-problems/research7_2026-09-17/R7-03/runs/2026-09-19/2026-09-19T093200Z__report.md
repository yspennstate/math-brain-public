## 5. B3 — exact finite-`n` falling-factorial theorem

Write `x^{\underline m}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-m+1)`.

### Theorem 5.1

For every `n>=3` and every integer `1<=m<=n-1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[H_n^{\underline m}\right]
=
\frac{(n-1)^{\underline{m+1}}}
{(m+1)\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}
\left(n-\frac{j+1}{2}\right)}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[H_n^{\underline m}\right]
=
\frac1{m+1}
\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}
\frac{n-j}{n-(j+1)/2}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence, for every fixed `m`,

\[
\mathbb E[H_n^{\underline m}]
=
\frac1{m+1}-\frac{m}{4n}+O_m(n^{-2}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Fix an ordered `m`-tuple of distinct neighbors `i_1,...,i_m` and condition on their root-edge weights

\[
u_a=U_{0i_a},\qquad M=\max_a u_a.
\]

All `I_{i_a}=1` if and only if three disjoint-edge-set requirements hold.

First, at least one of the `n-1-m` remaining root edges exceeds `M`, giving the conditional factor

\[
1-M^{n-1-m}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Second, for each selected `i_a`, all `n-m-1` edges from `i_a` to non-root, non-selected vertices must lie below `u_a`, giving

\[
\prod_{a=1}^m u_a^{n-m-1}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Third, every selected-selected edge `(i_a,i_b)` must lie below both endpoint thresholds, giving

\[
\prod_{a<b}\min(u_a,u_b).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus the joint probability is the integral over `[0,1]^m` of the product (5.4)--(5.6). The integrand is symmetric, so restrict to

\[
0<u_1<\cdots<u_m<1
\]

and multiply by `m!`. On this simplex,

\[
\prod_{a<b}\min(u_a,u_b)=\prod_{a=1}^m u_a^{m-a},
\]

so the total exponent of `u_a` is `n-a-1`. Put

\[
\alpha_a=n-a,
\qquad
S_j=\sum_{a=1}^j\alpha_a
=jn-\frac{j(j+1)}2
=j\left(n-\frac{j+1}{2}\right).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Successive integration of `u_1,...,u_{m-1}` reduces the ordered-simplex integral to

\[
\frac1{\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}S_j}
\left(
\frac1{S_m}-\frac1{S_m+n-1-m}
\right).
\tag{5.8}
\]

But

\[
S_m+n-1-m=S_{m+1},
\]

so (5.8) equals

\[
\frac{n-m-1}{\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}S_j}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Finally, the falling-factorial expansion sums the joint event over `(n-1)^{\underline m}` ordered neighbor tuples. Multiplying (5.9) by `(n-1)^{\underline m}m!` and using

\[
(n-1)^{\underline m}(n-m-1)=(n-1)^{\underline{m+1}},
\]

\[
\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}S_j
=(m+1)!\prod_{j=1}^{m+1}\left(n-\frac{j+1}{2}\right)
\]

gives (5.1). Equation (5.2) is algebraic rearrangement. At fixed `m`, the logarithm of the product in (5.2) is

\[
-\frac{m(m+1)}{4n}+O_m(n^{-2}),
\]

giving (5.3). For `m=n-1`, both sides are zero because `H_n<=n-2`. This completes the proof.

Taking `m=1,2`,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[H_n(H_n-1)]
=\frac{2(n-3)}{3(2n-3)}},
\tag{5.11}
\]

and therefore

\[

\]

In particular,

## 6. The `r=1` limit is exactly uniform-mixed Poisson

\[

H_r\mid A_r=a\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda_r(a)),

with

\[

At `r=1`, `A_1~Exp(1)` and `Lambda_1(A_1)=e^{-A_1}`. Therefore

\[

\[
\boxed{H_1\mid U\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(U).}
\tag{6.1}

The complete limiting pmf is consequently

\[
\boxed{

## 7. General fixed-complement limit: all factorial moments

B3 also gives one useful exact extension of the existing mixed-Poisson theorem. For every fixed `r>=1` and integer `m>=1`, conditional Poisson factorial moments imply

\[
\mathbb E[H_r^{\underline m}]
=\mathbb E[\Lambda_r(A_r)^m].

Expanding `Lambda_r` and integrating term-by-term against the `Gamma(r,1)` density gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[H_r^{\underline m}]
=
\frac1{(r-1)!}
\sum_{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_m=0}^{r-1}
\frac{\prod_{a=1}^m(r-\ell_a)}{\prod_{a=1}^m\ell_a!}
\frac{(r+\ell_1+\cdots+\ell_m-1)!}
