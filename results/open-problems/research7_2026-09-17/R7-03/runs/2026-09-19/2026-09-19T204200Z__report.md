## 4. Normalize the arbitrary order-five boundary problem

Let the five Fourier eigenvalues be `lambda_0,...,lambda_4 in (0,1]` and

\[
p=(\lambda_0\cdots\lambda_4)^{1/5}.
\]

By the preceding radial theorem it is enough to assume

\[
\max_i\lambda_i=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define

\[
a_i:=\frac p{\lambda_i}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
\prod_i a_i=1,
\qquad
r:=\min_i a_i=p.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Cyclically rotate so `a_0=r`.

Put

\[
\alpha=\frac{5-\sqrt5}{50},\qquad
\beta=\frac{5+\sqrt5}{50}.
\]

The special order-five identities are

\[
\alpha+\beta=\frac15,\qquad
\alpha\beta=\frac1{125},\qquad
\beta-\alpha=\frac{\sqrt5}{25}=\sqrt{\alpha\beta}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For either pair-frequency orbit weight system `w`, let

\[
D_w(x)=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}x_ix_j,
\tag{4.5}
\]

where the first system places `alpha` on adjacent frequency pairs and `beta` on diagonals, and the second swaps them. Each system has total weight one and vertex marginal `2/5`.

The prior Cauchy--Binet formulas become

\[
D=p^2D_w(a^{-1}),\qquad
T=p^3D_w(a),\qquad
D_4=p^4\frac15\sum_i a_i.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus define

\[
F_T(a)=D_w(a)-1-r\left(\frac15\sum_i a_i-1\right),
\tag{4.7}
\]

\[
F_P(a)=D_w(a^{-1})-1-r(D_w(a)-1).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Then exactly

\[
T-D_4-p^3(1-p)=p^3F_T(a),
\tag{4.9}
\]

\[
D-T-p^2(1-p)=p^2F_P(a).
\tag{4.10}
\]

The order-five problem is therefore to prove `F_T,F_P>=0` for both weight systems.
