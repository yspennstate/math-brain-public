## 5. New theorem: index-two coset-union lift

Let `H <= Gamma` have index two and size `m`, and put

\[
p=(\det Q)^{1/(2m)}.
\]

Write `K=Q|_H`. Equivariance gives equal determinants on the two cosets, and Fischer's determinant inequality gives

\[
\det Q\le (\det K)^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{FK}(K)=(\det K)^{1/m}\ge p.
\]

Assume the lower FK product theorem is already known on `H`. For any increasing event `A` on the first coset and its translate `A'` on the second, both marginals satisfy

\[
P^Q(A),P^Q(A')\ge \operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes H}(A)=q.
\]

Lyons's Theorem 2.10 gives negative association for determinantal laws generated by positive contractions, so for these disjoint-coordinate increasing events

\[
P^Q(A\cap A')\le P^Q(A)P^Q(A').
\]

Consequently

\[
P^Q(A\cup A')
\ge1-(1-P^Q(A))(1-P^Q(A'))
\ge1-(1-q)^2,
\]

which is exactly the product-measure probability because its two cosets are independent.

For both order-six groups, the relevant index-two subgroup has order three (`{0,2,4}` in `Z/6Z`, and `A_3` in `S_3`), and the complete order-three theorem is already in the own R7-03 record. Hence this new lift is applicable without an unproved external hypothesis.

The same construction proves the corresponding two coset-local event inequalities in `S_3`.

\[

\[
\boxed{

\]

The proof is exact. If `lambda_0,...,lambda_5` are the Fourier eigenvalues, split them into even and odd triples `x` and `y`. For a triple `z` define

\[
A(z)=\frac{z_1+z_2+z_3}{3},\quad
B(z)=\frac{z_1z_2+z_1z_3+z_2z_3}{3},\quad
g(z)=(z_1z_2z_3)^{1/3}.

A new elementary lemma is

\[
\boxed{A(z)-g(z)\ge B(z)-g(z)^2\ge0.}
\]

For `g>0`, write `z_i=g u_i`, `u_1u_2u_3=1`, and `M=max u_i`. Since `z_i<=1`, `g<=1/M`. Relabelling so `u_3=M` gives the exact identity

\[
3\{M(U_1-1)-(U_2-1)\}
=\frac{(M-1)^3}{M}\ge0,
\]

where `U_1=(u_1+u_2+u_3)/3` and `U_2=(u_1u_2+u_1u_3+u_2u_3)/3`. This yields the displayed excess chain; the zero-product boundary follows directly/continuously.

\[

for a union of two antipodal pairs. If `a=g(x)g(y)=p^2`, inclusion--exclusion gives
