## 3. B1 — change mathematical category: exact Banach/Fredholm form

Define

\[
\mathcal D_p=
\left\{u:X\to\mathbb C:
|u(0)|+\sum_{n\ge1}p_n|u(n)|<\infty
\right\}
\tag{3.1}
\]

with norm

\[
\|u\|_{\mathcal D_p}
=|u(0)|+\sum_{n\ge1}p_n|u(n)|.
\]

Let `A=P-I`, and define weighted-gradient coordinates

\[
z_n=p_n(u(n)-u(0)).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — exact source domain coordinates

The map

\[
\Phi:\mathcal D_p\to\mathbb C\oplus\ell^1(\mathbb N),
\qquad
\Phi(u)=(u(0),z),
\tag{3.3}
\]

is a Banach-space isomorphism with inverse

\[
\Phi^{-1}(a,z)(0)=a,
\qquad
\Phi^{-1}(a,z)(n)=a+\frac{z_n}{p_n}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

For `u in D_p`,

\[
\sum_n|z_n|
\le \sum_np_n|u(n)|+|u(0)|\sum_np_n
=\sum_np_n|u(n)|+|u(0)|<\infty.
\]

Conversely, if `(a,z) in C\oplus ell^1`, then

\[
\sum_np_n\left|a+\frac{z_n}{p_n}\right|
\le |a|+\sum_n|z_n|<\infty.
\]

The same estimates give boundedness of `Phi` and `Phi^{-1}`; for example

\[
\|\Phi u\|\le2\|u\|_{\mathcal D_p},
\qquad
\|\Phi^{-1}(a,z)\|_{\mathcal D_p}
\le2|a|+\|z\|_1.
\]

Thus `Phi` is a Banach isomorphism. ∎

In these coordinates,

\[
A\Phi^{-1}(a,z)
=\left(\sum_{n\ge1}z_n,-z_1,-z_2,\ldots\right).
\tag{3.5}
\]

The first coordinate follows from

\[
(P-I)u(0)
=\sum_np_n(u(n)-u(0))
=\sum_nz_n,
\]

and the leaf coordinate is

\[
(P-I)u(n)=p_n(u(0)-u(n))=-z_n.
\]

Define

\[
Jz=\left(\sum_nz_n,-z_1,-z_2,\ldots\right).
\]

### Theorem 3.2 — exact Fredholm sequence

Let

\[
\Sigma(d)=\sum_{x\in X}d(x)
\qquad(d\in\ell^1(X)).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
0\to\mathbb C
\to\mathcal D_p
\xrightarrow{P-I}\ell^1(X)
\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\mathbb C
\to0
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

is exact. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\ker(P-I)=\mathbb C\mathbf1,}
\tag{3.7}
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Ran}(P-I)=\ker\Sigma,}
\tag{3.8}
\]

`Ran(P-I)` is closed and codimension one, and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ind}(P-I)=1-1=0.}
\tag{3.9}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (3.5) shows that `(P-I)u=0` iff `z=0`, i.e. iff `u` is constant. It also shows every range element has total sum zero. Conversely, if `d in ell^1(X)` and `Sigma(d)=0`, define `z_n=-d_n`. Then

\[
\sum_nz_n=-\sum_{n\ge1}d_n=d_0,
\]

so `Jz=d`; hence every balanced `ell^1` defect lies in the range. Since `Sigma` is a bounded nonzero functional, its kernel is a closed codimension-one hyperplane. ∎

The adjoint also becomes explicit. Under `D_p ~= C\oplus ell^1`, for `h in ell^infty(X)`,

\[
\langle h,Jz\rangle
=\sum_{n\ge1}(h_0-h_n)z_n,
\]

hence

\[
(P-I)^*h=(0,(h_0-h_n)_{n\ge1})
\tag{3.10}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\ker(P-I)^*=\mathbb C\mathbf1.}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Thus the source mechanism is a genuine index-zero Fredholm alternative: constants span the homogeneous kernel and total mass spans the cokernel. A nonzero constant defect `c*1` is not in `ell^1(X)` because `X` is infinite, hence cannot lie in the range; once `c=0`, the kernel calculation makes `u` constant.

**B1 outcome: SUCCESS.** The star proof is upgraded from an explicit coordinate calculation to a topologically exact Banach/Fredholm complex with a completely identified adjoint obstruction.

## 4. B12 — conserved quantity forced by every homogeneous-space walk

Let `(G,mu)` now be any countable measured group and `X=G/H` any countable homogeneous space. Let the left quotient kernel induced by `check(mu)` be

\[
P(x,y)=\check\mu\{s:sx=y\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — every quotient kernel is doubly stochastic

\[
\boxed{\sum_yP(x,y)=1,
\qquad
\sum_xP(x,y)=1.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

The first identity is ordinary stochasticity. For the second, Tonelli gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_xP(x,y)
&=\sum_x\int_G\mathbf1_{\{sx=y\}}\,d\check\mu(s)\\
&=\int_G\left(\sum_x\mathbf1_{\{sx=y\}}\right)d\check\mu(s).
\end{aligned}
\]

For fixed `s`, left multiplication is a bijection of `G/H`, so there is exactly one `x=s^{-1}y`. The inner sum is one, proving the second identity. ∎

Thus counting measure on `G/H` is a stationary sigma-finite measure for **every** quotient walk, including nonsymmetric `mu`.

### Theorem 4.2 — exact finite-set boundary flux

For finite `F subset X` and any `u` for which the relevant quotient convolutions are absolutely defined,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\in F}(Pu-u)(x)
=
\sum_{y\notin F}u(y)\sum_{x\in F}P(x,y)
-
\sum_{y\in F}u(y)\sum_{x\notin F}P(x,y).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Write

\[
\sum_{x\in F}(Pu-u)(x)
=
\sum_yu(y)
\left(\sum_{x\in F}P(x,y)-\mathbf1_F(y)\right).
\]

For `y in F`, column stochasticity converts the coefficient to

\[
-\sum_{x\notin F}P(x,y),
\]

and for `y notin F` it remains `sum_{x in F}P(x,y)`. This is exactly (4.3). ∎

If `Pu-u=c` is constant, then every finite `F` satisfies

\[
\boxed{|F|c=\operatorname{Flux}_F(u).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore any exhaustion satisfying

\[
\frac{|\operatorname{Flux}_{F_N}(u)|}{|F_N|}\to0
\]

forces `c=0`.

For the explicit star quotient with `F_N={0,1,...,N}`, (4.3) reduces to the prior exact identity

\[
(N+1)c
=
\sum_{n>N}p_n(u(n)-u(0)),
\tag{4.5}
\]

and source integrability makes the right side tend to zero. The one-dimensional cokernel in B1 is therefore the functional-analytic shadow of a conservation law intrinsic to group actions by permutations; it is not an artifact of choosing a symmetric ambient measure.

**B12 outcome: SUCCESS.** The conserved quantity has now been identified at the full homogeneous-space level: counting mass, with an exact finite-boundary flux formula.

## 5. B26 — hunt the sporadic case: there is no directed-star homogeneous-space exception

A natural attack on the proof is to ask whether a nonsymmetric measured group can yield a *directed* star quotient with different probabilities from root to leaf and leaf to root. Such a case could in principle break the earlier symmetric calculation.

### Theorem 5.1 — classification of every pure-star homogeneous-space kernel

Let `(G,mu)` be a countable measured group, let `H<G` have infinite index, write `X=G/H`, and let `o=H`. Assume the quotient kernel satisfies

\[
P(i,j)=0
\qquad
\text{for distinct leaves }i,j\in X\setminus\{o\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then there are `r in [0,1)` and strictly positive `(p_i)_{i ne o}` with

\[
r+\sum_{i\ne o}p_i=1
\tag{5.2}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
P(o,o)=r,
\quad P(o,i)=P(i,o)=p_i,
\quad P(i,i)=1-p_i.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Hence every pure-star homogeneous-space kernel is automatically reversible with respect to counting measure even when `mu` itself is nonsymmetric.

#### Proof

First, `supp(check(mu))` semigroup-generates `G`. Indeed, if `g^{-1}=s_1...s_m` with each `s_j in supp(mu)`, then

\[
g=s_m^{-1}\cdots s_1^{-1}
\]

is a positive word in `supp(check(mu))`. Since `G` acts transitively on `G/H`, the quotient chain is irreducible.

Under the star zero pattern, any path from `o` to leaf `i` must use the direct edge `o->i`, and any return path must use `i->o`; therefore

\[
P(o,i)>0,
\qquad
P(i,o)>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Set `a_i=P(o,i)` and `b_i=P(i,o)`. Row stochasticity at `i` gives

\[
P(i,i)=1-b_i.
\]

Column stochasticity from Theorem 4.1 gives

\[
1=P(o,i)+P(i,i)=a_i+1-b_i,
\]

so `a_i=b_i=:p_i>0`. Writing `r=P(o,o)`, the root row sum gives (5.2) and therefore all of (5.3).

One can see the same obstruction directly at the permutation level. The zero pattern (5.1) and positivity of the mixture imply that every supported permutation of `X` is either the identity or one transposition `(o i)`: if it fixes `o`, bijectivity and the prohibition on leaf-to-distinct-leaf moves force it to fix every leaf; if it moves `o` to `i`, bijectivity forces `i` back to `o` and every other leaf fixed. There is therefore no hidden directed-star mixture. ∎

### Theorem 5.2 — universal pure-star constant-defect rigidity

Under Theorem 5.1, let `u` be any quotient function whose convolution is absolutely defined at the root and suppose

\[
Pu=u+c.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Then `c=0` and `u` is constant.

#### Proof

The leaf equation is

\[
c=p_i(u(o)-u(i)),
\]

so

\[
u(i)=u(o)-\frac{c}{p_i}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

There are infinitely many leaves and `sum_i p_i=1-r<infty`, hence `p_i->0` along the leaves. If `c ne 0`,

\[
p_i|u(i)|=|p_iu(o)-c|\to|c|,
\tag{5.7}
\]

so

\[
\sum_{i\ne o}p_i|u(i)|=\infty.
\]

That contradicts absolute definition of the root convolution. Therefore `c=0`; (5.6) then gives `u(i)=u(o)` for every leaf. ∎

This also enlarges the explicit E0070 family. On the original finitary symmetric group, for arbitrary

\[
0\le r<1,
\qquad p_n>0,
\qquad r+\sum_np_n=1,
\]

the symmetric law

\[
\mu_{r,p}=r\delta_e+\sum_np_n\delta_{(0\,n)}
\tag{5.8}
\]

still semigroup-generates `G` and has the same rigidity. Thus even an arbitrary holding probability is harmless.

**B26 outcome: SUCCESS.** The main obvious sporadic quotient pathology is impossible: group-homogeneous star geometry itself forces the reversible kernel used by the construction.
