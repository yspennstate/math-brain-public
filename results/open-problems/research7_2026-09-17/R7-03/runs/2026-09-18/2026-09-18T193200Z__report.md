## 4. Counterexample/obstruction first: the previous “capacity balance” route is invalid

The prior canonical attempt `E0106-A001` compared customer input intensity with the asymptotic growth of Wiener-sausage volume and inferred a dimension-dependent service-capacity threshold, including low-dimensional instability.

That reasoning assumes service is consumable. It is not, under the source wording. The Brownian Boolean field is an **exogenous nonexclusive clearing field**: covering one customer does not delay a Brownian particle and does not reduce its ability to cover any number of other customers. Therefore there is no queue conservation law of the form

```text
arrival rate < volume of new space swept per unit time.
```

The correct local-backlog primitive is instead the service delay of **one fixed point**. Once its mean is finite, Campbell/Tonelli determines the stationary customer density exactly.

This is a material correction to the repository attempt, not a cosmetic reinterpretation.

## 5. B18 — translate stability into a stationary random-measure problem

Use the canonical positive-radius model: initial mobile centers are a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity `lambda>0`; every center carries an independent standard Brownian motion and a radius-`r` grain, `r>0`. Customer arrivals are an independent space-time Poisson process of finite intensity `nu`.

Extend the service field to two-sided stationary time. Let `T` be the waiting time from a stationary epoch until a fixed point is next covered. Define the time-zero backlog from all arrivals in the infinite past. For bounded Borel `A`, let `N_0(A)` be the number of customers in `A` that have not yet been cleared.

If `E T<infinity`, then Campbell's theorem gives

```text
E N_0(A)
 = nu integral_A dx integral_0^infinity P(T>u)du
 = nu |A| E T.                                         (5.1)
```

Thus local stability reduces completely to the integrability of one point-detection time.

## 6. New pathwise lemma: a one-coordinate range forces enough sausage volume

Let `gamma:[0,t]->R^d` be continuous and

```text
W_r(gamma,t)=union_{0<=s<=t}(gamma(s)+B_r).
```

Let `m=min_s gamma_1(s)` and `M=max_s gamma_1(s)`. For each `y in [m,M]`, continuity gives some `s_y` with `gamma_1(s_y)=y`. The slice of `W_r` in the hyperplane `{x_1=y}` then contains the `(d-1)`-ball of radius `r` centered at the transverse coordinate of `gamma(s_y)`. Therefore, by Fubini,

```text
vol_d(W_r(gamma,t))
 >= omega_{d-1} r^{d-1}(M-m).                          (6.1)
```

For standard Brownian motion,

```text
E(M-m)=2 sqrt(2t/pi),
```

so

```text
E vol_d(W_r(B,t))
 >= c_{d,r} sqrt(t),
 c_{d,r}=2 omega_{d-1}r^{d-1}sqrt(2/pi)>0.             (6.2)
```

Combining (3.1) and (6.2),

```text
P(T>t) <= exp(-lambda c_{d,r} sqrt(t)).                (6.3)
```

Consequently

```text
E T
 <= integral_0^infinity exp(-a sqrt(t))dt
 = 2/a^2 < infinity,
 a=lambda c_{d,r}.                                     (6.4)
```

This proof is dimension-free and does not need sharp Wiener-sausage asymptotics. It also explains precisely why the earlier claim of `d=1,2` instability was wrong.

## 7. B23 + B25 — the unexpected theorem and the exact double count

The source framing suggests a queueing threshold may depend delicately on dimension. Under the natural local-backlog notion, the opposite happens.

### Theorem proved this run

For every `d>=1`, `lambda>0`, positive sensing radius `r>0`, and every finite customer intensity `nu`, the exogenous Brownian Boolean service system admits a time-stationary, spatially translation-invariant backlog that is locally finite almost surely and has finite mean density

```text
rho_backlog = nu E T < infinity.                       (7.1)
```

Moreover, if the customer system is started empty at time `-L` and inspected at time zero, then for each bounded `A`, its local backlog `N_0^(L)(A)` increases monotonically to the stationary `N_0(A)` and the limit is finite almost surely.

### Proof

Equation (6.4) gives `E T<infinity`. Counting surviving customers by location and age yields (5.1), so `E N_0(A)<infinity`. Since `N_0(A)` is nonnegative integer-valued, it is finite almost surely. A countable exhaustion of space gives local finiteness. The two-sided construction is time-stationary. Finally, increasing the empty-start horizon from `-L` only adds older possible survivors; the monotone union over `L` is exactly the infinite-past stationary backlog, which is locally finite by the preceding argument. QED.

Thus there is **no finite traffic-capacity threshold** for this stability notion: because service is nonexclusive, every finite `nu` is stable whenever the positive-radius Brownian service field is present.

For boundary context: with `lambda=0` and positive `nu`, there is no service and local backlog diverges. If one degenerates the grain to a point, `d>=2` point-Brownian trajectories have zero `d`-dimensional Lebesgue sausage volume and a point customer is not detected under the Poisson-void formula; positive-radius grains remove this degeneracy. These boundary cases are not needed for the canonical theorem.
