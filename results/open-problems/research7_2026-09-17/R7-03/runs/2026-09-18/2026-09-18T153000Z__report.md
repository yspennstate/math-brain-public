## 3. B18 — exact translation to empirical measures

Define

\[
\mu_n:=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{y\in G_n} I_n(y)\,\delta_{y/n},
\qquad h_n:=a_n/n.
\]

Stable square-grid heights lie in `{0,1,2,3}`, so each atom has mass at most `3/n^2` and the total masses are uniformly bounded. The source average is exactly

\[
\boxed{f_n(x)=h_n^{-2}\mu_n(B(x,h_n)).}
\]

Thus F0132 contains a precise scale-selection problem: weak-* convergence acts at fixed macroscopic scales, whereas the source needs one radius `h_n ->0` with `n h_n -> infinity`.

## 4. B12 — new mesoscopic extraction theorem

### Theorem

Let `q_n:G_n->[0,M]` be uniformly bounded and

\[
\mu_n=n^{-2}\sum_{y\in G_n}q_n(y)\delta_{y/n}.
\]

If

\[
\mu_n\Rightarrow \rho(x)dx,
\qquad \rho\in L^\infty([0,1]^2),
\]

then there exists a single integer sequence `a_n` with

\[
a_n\to\infty,\qquad a_n/n\to0,
\]

such that for every interior Lebesgue point `x` of `rho`, simultaneously,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{a_n^2}\sum_{\|y-nx\|\le a_n}q_n(y)
\longrightarrow \pi\rho(x).
}
\]

For another norm, `pi` is replaced by the area of its unit ball.

### Proof

For a radius `r` and thickness `eta`, associate a square cell of side `1/n` to each lattice point. A cell whose center lies in the annulus

\[
B(x,r+\eta)\setminus B(x,(r-\eta)_+)
\]

lies in the same annulus enlarged by `sqrt(2)/n`. Disjointness of cell interiors gives uniformly in the center

\[
\mu_n(\text{annulus})
\le C M\left[r\left(\eta+\frac1n\right)
+\left(\eta+\frac1n\right)^2\right].
\tag{1}
\]

The limiting measure has the analogous bound

\[
\mu(\text{annulus})\le C\|\rho\|_\infty(r\eta+\eta^2).
\tag{2}
\]

Fix `r>0`. Because `rho dx` gives every circle zero mass, weak convergence gives `mu_n(B(x,r))->mu(B(x,r))` for each center. A finite `eta`-net of the square plus (1)--(2) upgrades this to

\[
\boxed{
\sup_x|\mu_n(B(x,r))-\mu(B(x,r))|\to0
}
\tag{3}
\]

for every fixed radius `r`.

Choose `r_m downarrow0`, for example `r_m=2^{-m}`. By (3), choose increasing `N_m` so that for all `n>=N_m`,

\[
\sup_x|\mu_n(B(x,r_m))-\mu(B(x,r_m))|\le r_m^2/m
\tag{4}
\]

and also `n r_m>=m^2`. For `N_m<=n<N_{m+1}`, set

\[
a_n=\lfloor nr_m\rfloor,
\qquad h_n=a_n/n.
\]

Then `h_n->0` while `a_n>=m^2-1->infinity`. Also `|h_n-r_m|<=1/n`; applying (1) with thickness `1/n` yields

\[
\sup_x\frac{|\mu_n(B(x,h_n))-\mu_n(B(x,r_m))|}{r_m^2}=O(m^{-2}).
\tag{5}
\]

Since `h_n/r_m->1`, equations (4)--(5) imply

\[
\sup_x\left|
 h_n^{-2}\mu_n(B(x,h_n))
-r_m^{-2}\mu(B(x,r_m))
\right|\to0.
\tag{6}
\]

At every interior Lebesgue point `x`, the differentiation theorem gives

\[
r_m^{-2}\mu(B(x,r_m))
= r_m^{-2}\int_{B(x,r_m)}\rho(z)dz
\to \pi\rho(x).
\tag{7}
\]

Combining (6) and (7) proves the theorem. The construction of `a_n` never depends on `x`, so one sequence works simultaneously at all interior Lebesgue points. `QED`.

### Corollary for the source conjecture

If the square-grid identity measures `mu_n` weak-* converge to a bounded density having a representative `rho_tilde` that is locally constant almost everywhere, then HLM+ Conjecture 6.4 follows. Set `f=pi rho_tilde` in the interior. Every continuity point of this representative is a Lebesgue point, so the theorem provides the source convergence on the same single sequence `a_n`.

This reduces the source's mesoscopic-scale issue completely to the macroscopic weak-* / patch-density theorem. It does **not** prove that the square-grid identities possess that limit.

## 5. B29 — boundary endpoints and why they are not a hidden obstruction

At a smooth edge of the closed square a small Euclidean ball sees asymptotically half a disk, and at a corner one quarter. If `rho` is continuous there, the corresponding normalized limits are

\[
(\pi/2)\rho(x)\quad\text{and}\quad(\pi/4)\rho(x).
\]

Boundary values do not affect local constancy almost everywhere because the boundary has area zero. They may be assigned their tangent-cone limits when continuity is desired, or chosen so those boundary points are discontinuities. The source asks for convergence only at continuity points of the selected `f`. Hence there is no extra boundary obstruction to the interior weak-* implication.
