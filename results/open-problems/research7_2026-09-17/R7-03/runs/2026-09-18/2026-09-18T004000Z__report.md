## 4. Construction

Let

\[
X=\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
\]

and let

\[
G=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathrm{fin}}(X)
\]

be the group of all finitely supported permutations of `X`. Put

\[
H=\operatorname{Stab}_G(0)=\{g\in G:g(0)=0\}.
\]

For each integer `n>=1`, let

\[
\tau_n=(0\ n).
\]

Choose any strictly positive probability vector `(p_n)_{n>=1}` and put

\[
\mu(\tau_n)=p_n,
\qquad
\sum_{n\ge1}p_n=1.
\]

For one completely explicit source answer take

\[
\boxed{p_n=2^{-n}.}
\]

The general proof below works for every strictly positive probability vector, but the source needs only one example.

### 4.1 Countability

Every finitary permutation has support contained in `{0,1,...,m}` for some finite `m`, hence

\[
G=\bigcup_{m\ge0}\operatorname{Sym}(\{0,1,\ldots,m\}),
\]

a countable union of finite sets. Thus `G` is countable.

### 4.2 Semigroup generation

Every `tau_n` is an involution. For distinct `i,j>=1`,

\[
\tau_i\tau_j\tau_i=(i\ j),
\]

because conjugating `(0 j)` by `(0 i)` sends the unordered pair `{0,j}` to `{i,j}`. Hence the star transpositions generate every transposition of `X`. Every finitary permutation is a finite product of transpositions, so

\[
\boxed{
\langle\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\rangle_{\rm semigroup}=G.
}
\]

The word `semigroup` matters: because each generator is its own inverse, no extra inverse symbols are needed. Therefore `(G,mu)` is a measured group in the exact source sense.

### 4.3 Symmetry

Since `tau_n^{-1}=tau_n` for every `n`,

\[
\boxed{\check\mu=\mu.}
\]

This removes any left/right reflection ambiguity in the source convention.

### 4.4 Infinite-index subgroup and exact quotient identification

Define

\[
\Psi:G/H\to X,
\qquad
\Psi(gH)=g(0).
\]

If `gH=g'H`, then `g'=gh` for some `h in H`, so `g'(0)=g(h(0))=g(0)`, hence `Psi` is well-defined. Conversely, if `g(0)=g'(0)`, then `g^{-1}g'` fixes `0`, so `g^{-1}g' in H` and `gH=g'H`. Surjectivity follows from `tau_n(0)=n` and the identity sending `0` to `0`.

Thus

\[
\boxed{G/H\cong\mathbb N_0},
\qquad
\boxed{[G:H]=\infty.}
\]

## 5. B18 — translate the source statement to an exact infinite-star kernel

Under the quotient identification, left multiplication by `tau_n` sends the state

\[
x=g(0)
\]

to

\[
\tau_n(g(0))=\tau_n(x).
\]

Since `check(mu)=mu`, the left convolution operator is

\[
(Pu)(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}p_nu(\tau_nx).
\]

At the center, each star transposition sends `0` to its own leaf:

\[
\boxed{(Pu)(0)=\sum_{n\ge1}p_nu(n).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

At a fixed leaf `k>=1`, only `tau_k` moves `k`, sending it to `0`; every `tau_n` with `n\ne k` fixes `k`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{(Pu)(k)=p_ku(0)+(1-p_k)u(k).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This translation is lossless: the group problem has become an infinite-star Markov kernel with one center row and one leaf-row formula.

**B18 outcome:** success. The source statement is reduced exactly, with no approximation or restricted subclass, to equations (5.1)–(5.2).

## 6. B2 — attack the negation and solve every possible defect profile

Assume there is a source-valid complex-valued function satisfying the more general constant-defect equation

\[
Pu=u+c
\tag{6.1}
\]

for some `c in C`. This is deliberately stronger than the literal source-normalized quasi-harmonic equation, whose defect is tied to the basepoint integral.

At a fixed leaf `k`, equations (5.2) and (6.1) give

\[
p_ku(0)+(1-p_k)u(k)=u(k)+c.
\]

Rearranging,

\[
p_k(u(0)-u(k))=c.
\]

Since `p_k>0`, every possible solution is forced to satisfy

\[
\boxed{u(k)=u(0)-\frac{c}{p_k}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

There is no remaining leafwise degree of freedom.

The source-valid convolution at the center is a complex Lebesgue integral; therefore it is absolutely defined:

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}p_n|u(n)|<\infty.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Suppose `c\ne0`. From (6.2),

\[
p_n|u(n)|
=
|p_nu(0)-c|.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Because `(p_n)` is a strictly positive summable sequence,

\[
p_n\to0.
\]

Consequently

\[
|p_nu(0)-c|\to |c|>0.
\]

Thus the terms of the nonnegative series (6.3) fail even to converge to zero, a contradiction. Hence

\[
\boxed{c=0.}
\]

Then (6.2) immediately gives

\[
u(k)=u(0)
\qquad\text{for every }k\ge1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{u\text{ is constant on }G/H.}
\]

This argument uses no boundedness assumption and no asymptotics beyond the elementary fact that summable positive masses tend to zero.

**B2 outcome:** success. The negation of the target is algebraically impossible once source integrability is imposed.

## 7. B25 — exact coset/orbit bijection and the repeated-defect obstruction

The decisive global mechanism can be stated without analytic decoration. Equation (6.2) implies

\[
p_nu(n)=p_nu(0)-c.

If `c\ne0`, then **each of infinitely many leaves contributes asymptotically the same nonzero defect `-c` to the center convolution**. The coset/orbit bijection guarantees these are genuinely distinct quotient states rather than duplicate representatives. ## 8. Exact match to Björklund's quasi-harmonic normalization

Let `u:G/H->C` and define its right-`H`-invariant pullback

\[
\widetilde u(g)=u(gH).
\]

The source-normalized left quasi-harmonic equation is

\[
\int_G\widetilde u(sg)\,d\check\mu(s)
=
\widetilde u(g)+
\int_G\widetilde u(s)\,d\check\mu(s).
\tag{8.1}
\]

In the present symmetric construction, define

\[
c:=\int_G\widetilde u(s)\,d\mu(s)
=
\sum_{n\ge1}p_nu(n).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Then (8.1) is exactly the constant-defect equation

\[
Pu=u+c
\]

already solved above. Moreover, putting `g=e` into (8.1) gives

\[
\int_G\widetilde u(s)\,d\mu(s)
=
\widetilde u(e)+
\int_G\widetilde u(s)\,d\mu(s),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{u(H)=\widetilde u(e)=0.}
\]

The stronger theorem above says any source-integrable constant-defect solution has `c=0` and is constant; with this source normalization the constant must therefore be zero. Thus every source-normalized left quasi-harmonic quotient function is the zero function. Ordinary left harmonic functions are the `c=0` case without the quasi normalization and are all constant.
