## 4. Dated primary-literature check and exact closure derivation

### Fu--Han--Wang--Yan--Zhang--Zhou (27 July 2026)

Primary source checked:

Weibo Fu, Yanjun Han, Guanyang Wang, Jun Yan, Peng Zhang, Zhengqing Zhou, *Sharp small-deviation inequalities for sums of independent nonnegative random variables*, arXiv:2607.23980.

`https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23980`

Their Theorem 1.1 says that for independent nonnegative variables with `E X_i<=1`, with `S=sum_i X_i`,

`P(S < E S + delta) >= b_{n,delta}`,

and for `delta>=1`,

`b_{n,delta}=(1-1/(n+delta))^n`.

The paper states the bound is optimal for every fixed `n` and `delta>=1`, and explicitly identifies the `delta=1` case as a proof of Feige's conjecture.

Under E0052's exact assumptions, `E S=n`. Put `delta=1`. Then

`P(S<n+1) >= (n/(n+1))^n`.                    (4.1)

Taking the exact complement gives

`P(S>=n+1) <= 1-(n/(n+1))^n`.                 (4.2)

For `x>=0`, `log(1+x)<=x`. Hence

`n log(1+1/n)<=1`,

so

`(n/(n+1))^n = exp(-n log(1+1/n)) >= e^{-1}`.

Substituting into (4.2),

`P(S>=n+1) <= 1-e^{-1}`,

which is exactly the corrected Bandeira/Feige source statement.

This is a positive source-matching theorem, not an inference from search failure.

### Independent confirmation

Zipei Nie and Jiaye Wei, *On Feige's conjecture*, arXiv:2607.24528, also submitted 27 July 2026, states directly

`P(S<n+1) >= (n/(n+1))^n >= 1/e`

for independent nonnegative mean-one variables.

Primary source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24528`.

A later stronger paper, Zhi Ling, *On Samuels' Conjecture*, arXiv:2608.18392 (18 August 2026), claims the full Samuels product formula and explicitly notes that Feige follows in the equal-means case. E0052's resolved status does not depend on validating that stronger later claim, because the two July Feige papers already match the source.

## 5. Exact finite-n strengthening and boundary-atom audit

The 2026 result is stronger than Bandeira's uniform constant. For every fixed `n`, (4.2) gives the sharp inequality

`P(S>=n+1) <= 1-(n/(n+1))^n`.

Sharpness is witnessed by independent

`X_i = n+1` with probability `1/(n+1)`,

`X_i = 0` with probability `n/(n+1)`.

Each variable has mean one. Here `{S<n+1}` occurs if and only if all variables vanish, so

`P(S<n+1)=(n/(n+1))^n`

and

`P(S>=n+1)=1-(n/(n+1))^n`.

As `n->infinity`, `(n/(n+1))^n -> e^{-1}`, proving that the source constant is asymptotically optimal uniformly in `n`.

The same example explains the intake mistake: `{S<=n+1}` additionally includes the configurations with exactly one nonzero coordinate, so replacing `<` by `<=` changes the probability precisely at the extremal boundary atom.
