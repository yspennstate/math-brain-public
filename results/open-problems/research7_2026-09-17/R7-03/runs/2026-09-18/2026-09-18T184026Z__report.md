## 4. B2 — obstruction first and exact quantifier boundary

A universal statement over literally every discrete law is false for a simple geometric reason. If `s=0` and

`P(X_e=0)>p_c(Z^2)=1/2`,

zero-weight edges percolate and point-to-point passage times can remain tight. Hence a diverging `sqrt(log n)` lower fluctuation scale is impossible. ## 5. B6 — exact deformation from smooth laws to atoms

The later Bates–Chatterjee coupling gives a particularly clean atomic calculation. For arbitrary `X` and independent copies `X^(1),...,X^(m)`, define

`X^max=max(X,X^(1),...,X^(m))`.

Then `X^max>=X` pointwise, eliminating the sign problem. For an ordered discrete law with atom masses `p_j` and cumulative masses `F_j`, one has

`P(X^max=a_j)=F_j^(m+1)-F_(j-1)^(m+1)`

and therefore

`P(X^max=a_j)/p_j = sum_{r=0}^m F_j^(m-r)F_(j-1)^r <= m+1`.

So `Law(X^max)` is absolutely continuous with respect to `Law(X)` with density at most `m+1`, despite the original law being purely atomic. Bernoulli-thinning this monotone replacement with probability `epsilon` produces one-coordinate Hellinger loss `O(epsilon^2)`, and tensorization yields product-law total variation bounded by

`C (sum_i epsilon_i^2)^(1/2)`.

For Bernoulli `{0,1}` weights with `P(X=0)=p`, the likelihood ratios are explicit: `p^m` at 0 and `1+p+...+p^m` at 1. The monotone increment occurs with probability `p(1-p^m)` before thinning. Thus atomicity causes no perturbative singularity at all.

Outcome B6: **the old smooth-density perturbation can be replaced by a monotone min/max deformation that works exactly on atoms.**
