## 3. Dated literature reconciliation

Current positive source-matching evidence, checked 2026-09-18 UTC:

**Matthew Kwan and Lisa Sauermann**, *Resolution of the quadratic Littlewood–Offord problem*, arXiv:2312.13826 (21 December 2023), now *Compositio Mathematica* 161(12) (2025), 3089–3139, DOI `10.1112/S0010437X25102789`; Cambridge lists online publication on 30 March 2026.

Their Theorem 1.1 states that if a degree-at-most-two polynomial in independent Rademacher variables **robustly depends on at least `m` variables**, in the precise sense that no specification of any `m-1` variables pins down the polynomial's value, then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}\Pr(Q(\xi)=z)\le C m^{-1/2}
}
\]

for an absolute constant `C`.

This is stronger than the old all-nonzero-quadratic-coefficient hypothesis. Current higher-degree follow-up work also confirms that the broad degree-`k` programme should not be silently called solved: Jin–Kwan–Sauermann–Wang (arXiv:2505.23335, 2025) studies algebraic structure in the polynomial Littlewood–Offord problem, while Grebennikov–Kwan (arXiv:2505.24699, 2025) proves robust `O(m^{-1/2})` bounds for bounded Chow rank and related classes. The present status correction therefore relies on a positive theorem for the quadratic endpoint and does not use a search miss as an openness or novelty certificate.

## 4. B2 — attack the negation and certify the sharp exponent

The first question is whether a stronger uniform endpoint could remain after the 2023/2025 theorem. It cannot.

Let

\[
Q_0(\xi)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i\right)^2
=n+2\sum_{i<j}\xi_i\xi_j.
\]

All off-diagonal quadratic coefficients are nonzero.

For even `n`,

\[
\Pr(Q_0=0)
=\Pr\left(\sum_i\xi_i=0\right)
=2^{-n}\binom{n}{n/2}
\sim \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi n}}.
\]

For odd `n`,

\[
\Pr(Q_0=1)
=\Pr\left(\sum_i\xi_i\in\{-1,1\}\right)
=2\,2^{-n}\binom{n}{(n-1)/2}
=\Theta(n^{-1/2}).
\]

Hence

\[
\sup_z\Pr(Q_0=z)=\Theta(n^{-1/2}),
\]

so no source-valid universal improvement `o(n^{-1/2})` is possible.

**B2 outcome:** the exponent `1/2` is the exact source endpoint, not an artifact of an old method.

## 5. B6 — deform Kwan–Sauermann's solved theorem to the Tao–Vu hypothesis

For functions on the Rademacher cube we first use the unique multilinear representation

\[
Q(\xi)=c+\sum_i b_i\xi_i+\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}\xi_i\xi_j,
\]

using `xi_i^2=1`. Under the standard symmetric quadratic-form convention, the source's nonzero quadratic coefficients give

\[
a_{ij}\ne0\qquad(i<j).
\]

If an ordered nonsymmetric coefficient array is written instead, one first symmetrizes it; the effective coefficient of `xi_i xi_j` is `c_ij+c_ji`. This removes a purely representational cancellation issue.

### Lemma

If every `a_ij` is nonzero, then `Q` robustly depends on at least `n-1` variables in the sense of Kwan–Sauermann.

### Proof

Fix arbitrarily any set of at most `n-2` variables. Choose two unfixed coordinates `u != v`. After the substitution, the restricted polynomial retains the Walsh/Fourier monomial

\[
a_{uv}\xi_u\xi_v
\]

with nonzero coefficient. Substitution into terms involving fixed variables can create constants and linear terms in the remaining variables, but it cannot alter the coefficient of the distinct degree-two monomial `xi_u xi_v`. A constant function on the remaining hypercube has every nonconstant Walsh coefficient equal to zero. Therefore the restricted polynomial is not constant.

So no assignment of `n-2` variables pins down `Q`. This is exactly robust dependence with `m=n-1`. `□`

Kwan–Sauermann Theorem 1.1 therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\sup_z\Pr(Q(\xi)=z)
\le \frac{C}{\sqrt{n-1}}
=O(n^{-1/2}).
}
\]

This is exactly Tao–Vu's explicit quadratic conjecture.

**B6 outcome:** the old source hypothesis is a direct special case of the published robust-dependence theorem. There is no missing quadratic lemma.
