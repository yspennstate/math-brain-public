## 4. B14 — find the group acting: exact elimination of the vertex-transitive subbranch

### Proposition 4.1

There is no vertex-transitive counterexample in the `z=0` order-19 branch.

### Proof reduction

Let `D` be vertex-transitive on 19 vertices. Its automorphism group is transitive, hence its order is divisible by 19. By Cauchy's theorem it contains a nonidentity element of order 19. On a 19-point set such a permutation is one 19-cycle. Therefore `D` has a regular cyclic automorphism and can be identified with a Cayley oriented graph on `Z/19Z`.

Because the missing graph is 2-regular and translation invariant, there is one missing opposite difference pair `+/-h`, where `h in {1,...,9}`. For each of the other eight opposite pairs `+/-d`, orientedness and 8-regularity choose exactly one member as an out-step. Thus the out-step set `S` has size eight and there are exactly

`9 * 2^8 = 2304`

possible oriented circulants.

For vertex zero,

`N_1^+(0)=S`,

and the exact second out-neighborhood is

`N_2^+(0)=(S+S) \ (S union {0})`.

The accompanying exact-integer checker exhausts all 2304 possibilities. It obtains

`|N_2^+(0)| = 8` in 18 cases,

`|N_2^+(0)| = 9` in 126 cases,

`|N_2^+(0)| = 10` in 2160 cases,

and never less than eight. Translation gives the same cardinality at every vertex. Hence no such graph is a counterexample. QED.

## 5. B19 — make it an algorithm: identify the triangle-free arc core

The second report had a `(19_3)` incidence structure but did not identify what that structure means directly inside `D`. The following routing lemma does so.

### Lemma 5.1 — a missing neighbor routes around every unreachable triangle

Assume `z=0`. Let `m` be a nonneighbor of `v`. Then

1. `m notin U(v)` and `m -> u` for every `u in U(v)`;
2. `m notin P(v)` and `p -> m` for every `p in P(v)`.

### Proof

Take `x in U(v)`. Then `v in P(x)`. Since `P(x)` is a directed 3-cycle and `m` is nonadjacent to `v`, we have `m notin P(x)`. Equality (3.2) at target `x` says that every outneighbor of `v` lies in `T(x)`, indeed

`N^+(v)=Q_x union {sigma_x(v)}`.

Because `m` is not an outneighbor of `v` and is not in `P(x)`, it is not in `Q_x` either. Hence `m notin T(x)`. If `x != m`, the definition of `T(x)` therefore forces `m -> x`.

If `m in U(v)`, let `x,y` be the other two members of the directed triangle `U(v)`. They are distinct from `m`, so the preceding paragraph gives both `m -> x` and `m -> y`. But a vertex of a directed 3-cycle has exactly one outneighbor inside that cycle, contradiction. Therefore `m notin U(v)`, and now `m -> x` for every `x in U(v)`.

Apply the same argument to the reverse digraph. The second report proved that the reverse is again a `z=0` counterexample and that

`U_{D^R}(v)=P_D(v)`.

The missing pairs are unchanged. Thus `m notin P_D(v)` and `m ->_{D^R} p` for every `p in P_D(v)`, equivalently `p ->_D m`. QED.

### Corollary 5.2

For every vertex `v`,

`P(v) subset N^+(v)`,    `U(v) subset N^-(v)`,             (5.1)

and if `H(v)` denotes the two missing neighbors, then

`P(v) -> H(v) -> U(v)` completely.                         (5.2)

### Definition of the core `J`

Put an arc

`x ->_J p  iff  p in P(x)`.

By (5.1), `J` is a spanning subdigraph of `D`. Every vertex has outdegree three in `J`; every vertex also has indegree three because

`#{x : v in P(x)}=|U(v)|=3`.

More importantly:

### Proposition 5.3 — `J` is exactly the directed-triangle-free arc set of `D`

For an arc `x -> p` of `D`,

`x ->_J p`

if and only if `x -> p` lies in no directed 3-cycle of `D`.

### Proof

Because `D` is oriented, `p` cannot return directly to `x`. Thus `p in P(x)` exactly when there is no two-step path `p -> y -> x`. Such a path exists exactly when `x -> p -> y -> x` is a directed triangle containing the arc `x -> p`. QED.

So `J` is 3-out/3-in regular and contains no directed 3-cycle.

Now let `C` be the union of the 19 directed triangles `P(x)`. Column linearity from the previous run implies distinct `P(x)` blocks share at most one vertex, so their arcs are disjoint. Every vertex lies in exactly three such blocks, because `v in P(x)` iff `x in U(v)` and `|U(v)|=3`. Therefore `C` is also 3-out/3-in regular and has 57 arcs.

Every arc of `C` lies in a directed triangle whereas every arc of `J` is triangle-free, hence `C` and `J` are arc-disjoint. Since `D` is 8-out/8-in regular, the residual

`E = D \ (J union C)`

is exactly 2-out/2-in regular.

Thus the old `3+5` decomposition sharpens to

`D = J (3-in/3-out, triangle-free)`

`    disjoint-union C (3-in/3-out, 19 edge-disjoint directed triangles)`

`    disjoint-union E (2-in/2-out)`,                       (5.3)

with the missing pairs forming a separate undirected 2-factor.

This is also an algorithmic compression: the `(19_3)` incidence matrix is, up to transpose convention, simply the adjacency matrix of the triangle-free core `J`. A future finite search need not introduce thousands of two-step conjunction variables; it can enumerate `J`, the cyclic orders giving `C`, the missing 2-factor and the 2-diregular residual, enforcing the local neighborhood identities.

## 6. B20 — find why the gluing algorithm fails: the zero-slack branch is impossible

The routing lemma lets the equality formulas glue strongly enough to produce a direct contradiction.

### Theorem 6.1

There is **no** hypothetical order-19, minimum-outdegree-eight SSNC counterexample in the branch `z=0`.

### Proof

Fix any vertex `v`. Let

`P(v)={p, sigma, tau}`

be its directed 3-cycle, labeled so that

`p -> sigma -> tau -> p`.

By Corollary 5.2, all three members of `P(v)` are outneighbors of `v`. Put

`A=N^+(v)\P(v)`.

Then `|A|=5`. Let `H(v)` be the two nonneighbors of `v`. Because

`T(v)=N^+(v) union H(v)`

and `P(v) subset N^+(v)`, equation (3.2) for the source `p in P(v)` becomes

`N^+(p)=A union H(v) union {sigma}`.                       (6.1)

Now `p in P(v)` means, by definition, that

`v in U(p)`.

The second-run reverse-digraph argument proved that `U(p)` is a directed 3-cycle of size three. In that directed triangle, besides `v`, exactly one vertex is an outneighbor of `v` and exactly one is an inneighbor of `v`.

Which outneighbor of `v` can lie in `U(p)`? The outneighbors of `v` are

`P(v) union A`.

Every member of `A` is a direct outneighbor of `p` by (6.1), so none can lie in `U(p)`. The vertex `sigma` is also a direct outneighbor of `p`. The vertex `p` itself is excluded from `U(p)`. Therefore the **only** possible outneighbor of `v` in `U(p)` is `tau`. Hence

`tau in U(p)`.

Let `w` be the third member of `U(p)`. Since `U(p)` is a directed cycle and `v -> tau`, its orientation must contain

`v -> tau -> w -> v`.                                     (6.2)

In particular `w in N^-(v)` and `tau -> w`.

But apply (6.1) to the source `tau in P(v)`. Its unique outneighbor inside the cycle `P(v)` is `p`, so

`N^+(tau)=A union H(v) union {p}`.                         (6.3)

The set on the right of (6.3) is disjoint from `N^-(v)`. Therefore `tau` cannot point to `w`, contradicting (6.2).

The contradiction was obtained from an arbitrary vertex `v`. Thus the entire `z=0` branch is impossible. QED.

### Corollary 6.2 — every order-19 counterexample must carry positive dense-case slack

Any order-19, minimum-outdegree-eight counterexample must satisfy

`z >= 1`.                                                   (6.4)

Equivalently, it must contain at least one vertex `x` with

`q_x=0`, hence

`d^+(x)=8`, `a_x=0`.

So `x` is adjacent to all other 18 vertices and has indegree ten. In addition, (3.1) gives

`z = h + 2E + L + C >= 1`,                                 (6.5)

so every remaining counterexample must realize genuine degree/source/target slack rather than the globally tight configuration studied in the first two runs.

This is the principal mathematical advance of the run. It eliminates a complete structural branch, but it does **not** prove the full order-19 case because `z>0` remains possible.
