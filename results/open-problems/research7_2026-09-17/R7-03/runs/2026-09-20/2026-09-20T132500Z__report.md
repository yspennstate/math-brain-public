## Exact source target

Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures* (ICM 2014), arXiv:1406.2707, Section 5.2, Conjecture 5.7. The source fixes a sofic group `Gamma` and asks, for every `Gamma`-equivariant positive contraction `0<=Q<=I` on `ell^2(Gamma)`, with `tr_Gamma(T)=<T delta_e,delta_e>` and `FK_Gamma(Q)=exp(tr_Gamma log|Q|)`, for the sharp two-sided stochastic domination on every increasing event

`P^{FK_Gamma(Q) I} <= P^Q <= P^{I-FK_Gamma(I-Q) I}`.

For a finite group of order `n`, `FK_Gamma(Q)=(det Q)^(1/n)`. The source states the conjecture is open even for finite groups. A bounded current-source search in this run did not locate a later exact resolution; absence from that search is not treated as proof of worldwide openness.

## Previous useful result

The preceding checkpoint proved singleton gauge-midpoint domination for every finite DPP: flipping one coordinate by a diagonal sign gauge and taking the matrix geometric midpoint lowers the entire DPP in stochastic order, preserves determinant, and decouples that coordinate. Iterating yields a nonuniform product lower bound

`mu_d = tensor_i Ber(d_i) <= P^Q`, with `prod_i d_i = det Q`,

and the analogous nonuniform upper product sandwich via `I-Q`. The unresolved issue was restoring the required *uniform* density `p=(det Q)^(1/n)` without invalid coordinatewise comparison.

## New mathematics

This run proves a symmetry-restoring theorem that removes that obstruction whenever the kernel has enough coordinate automorphisms.

### 1. Invariant-upper-set reduction

Let `H<=Sym(E)` preserve `Q`, and let `mu_d` be any peeling product lower bound. If

`mu_d(U) >= Ber(p)^E(U)`

for every `H`-invariant increasing event `U`, then `Ber(p)^E <= P^Q` on *all* increasing events. For an arbitrary increasing event `A`, average its indicator over `H`; the resulting increasing function is `H`-invariant, both `P^Q` and `Ber(p)^E` preserve its expectation, and layer-cake reduces it to invariant upper sets. This cleanly separates the determinantal step (already handled by peeling) from the symmetry-restoring product comparison.

### 2. Fixed-product Poisson-binomial lemma

For independent Bernoulli variables with parameters `d_1,...,d_n` and `p=(prod_i d_i)^(1/n)`,

`Bin(n,p) <=_st sum_i Ber(d_i)`.

The proof is pairwise geometric equalization. Replacing `(a,b)` by `(sqrt(ab),sqrt(ab))` keeps the two-success probability `ab` fixed and decreases the at-least-one-success probability because `a+b>=2sqrt(ab)`. Adding the remaining independent coordinates preserves stochastic order. Repeated log-coordinate averaging converges to the constant vector `(p,...,p)`.

### 3. Set-transitive automorphism theorem

If the coordinate automorphism group of a finite positive contraction `Q` contains a subgroup `H` that is transitive on the `k`-subsets for every `k`, then every `H`-invariant increasing event is a cardinality threshold `{|X|>=k}`. The Poisson-binomial lemma therefore verifies every invariant test required by the reduction above. Hence, with

`p=(det Q)^(1/n)` and `r=(det(I-Q))^(1/n)`, one obtains the complete sharp sandwich

`Ber(p)^E <= P^Q <= Ber(1-r)^E`.

The upper bound follows by applying the same lower theorem to the vacancy kernel `I-Q` and complementing. Full-set and empty-set probabilities force sharpness.

This is an arbitrary-cardinality theorem, not a small-case event check.

### 4. Source-valid finite-group class

For any finite group `Gamma`, the theorem applies to every `Gamma`-equivariant kernel whose coordinate automorphism group contains a set-transitive subgroup. In particular it applies, for every group order, to

`Q = a I + b (J-I)`,

whenever the two eigenvalues `a+(n-1)b` and `a-b` lie in `[0,1]`. The exact sharp lower source density is

`[(a+(n-1)b)(a-b)^(n-1)]^(1/n)`,

and the vacancy density is

`[(1-a-(n-1)b)(1-a+b)^(n-1)]^(1/n)`.

Thus this run proves the full source inequality on a nontrivial arbitrary-order family across all finite groups.

## What this does not prove

The full-source metric remains **PROVED PARTIAL**. A regular finite-group action is generally not set-transitive, so arbitrary finite groups, arbitrary `S_3`, and the original all-sofic-group conjecture remain open in this work.

The result nevertheless changes the bottleneck: after determinant-preserving peeling, one only needs product-measure inequalities on the finite collection of group-invariant upper sets. For regular `S_3`, that is the already enumerated family of 76 invariant upper sets. This is materially simpler than another determinant inclusion-exclusion expansion for `P^Q` itself.
