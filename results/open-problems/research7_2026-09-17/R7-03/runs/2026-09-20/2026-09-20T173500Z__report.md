## New mathematics: mirrored two-point symmetrization

For a negatively associated Bernoulli pair with atom table

`(00,10,01,11)=(e,u,v,q^2)`

and its coordinate mirror `(e,v,u,q^2)`, take the two pairs independently and add coordinates. The resulting law on `{0,1,2}^2` stochastically dominates two independent `Bin(2,q)` variables.

The proof is structural rather than an event-by-event expansion. Put `s=u+v` and `t=uv`. The summed count law has table

`[[e^2, es, t], [es, 2 e q^2+s^2-2t, q^2 s], [t,q^2 s,q^4]]`.

Every increasing-event probability is affine in `t`, while negative association and AM-GM force

`e q^2 <= t <= s^2/4`.

At `t=e q^2` the pair is independent; its marginal parameters have product `q^2`, so scalar geometric smoothing gives the desired binomial comparison. At `t=s^2/4` the pair is symmetric; feasibility implies its common marginal is at least `q`, and its `11` probability is exactly `q^2`, so the two-bit law itself dominates `Ber(q)^2`. Since both endpoints dominate the same target and the count law is affine in `t`, every interior point does as well.

## New structural theorem: generalized-dihedral two-coset count domination

Let `A` be any finite abelian group of order `m` and

`Gamma=Dih(A)=A rtimes C_2`, with the involution acting by inversion. For every arbitrary `Gamma`-equivariant positive contraction `Q`, split the ground set into the two `A` cosets and write

`N_0=|X cap A|`, `N_1=|X cap sA|`.

Fourier transform inside the two cosets. Because the count fugacity matrix `z I_A directsum w I_sA` is scalar on each coset, it commutes with this Fourier transform. The bivariate count generating function therefore factors into independent two-point DPP blocks `K_chi`, indexed by characters `chi in Ahat`.

Character inversion pairs the blocks: `K_bar(chi)` has the two diagonal entries of `K_chi` swapped and the same off-diagonal modulus. Self-inverse characters give symmetric blocks. Thus the mirrored-pair lemma applies to each nontrivial inversion orbit and the symmetric two-point lemma applies to each fixed character.

Writing

`r_chi=sqrt(det K_chi)`,

one obtains a product lower comparator in which the two coset counts are independent copies of the same Poisson-binomial sum with parameters `{r_chi}`. Moreover

`prod_chi r_chi=(det Q)^(1/2)`.

Hence the geometric mean of the `m` parameters is exactly

`p=(det Q)^(1/(2m))`.

Scalar geometric equalization then yields the full count-factor theorem

`Bin(m,p) x Bin(m,p) <= Law(N_0,N_1)`

on every increasing subset of `{0,...,m}^2`.

Applying the same theorem to the vacancy DPP `I-Q` gives the sharp upper factor

`Law(N_0,N_1) <= Bin(m,q) x Bin(m,q)`,

where

`q=1-(det(I-Q))^(1/(2m))`.

This is an arbitrary-order source family, not another six-point numerical example.
