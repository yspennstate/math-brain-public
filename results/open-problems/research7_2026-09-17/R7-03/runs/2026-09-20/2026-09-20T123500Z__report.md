## New theorem: singleton gauge midpoint

Let `E=F disjoint-union {e}` and let

\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}A&x\\x^*&b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D=\operatorname{diag}(I_F,-1).
\]

Then, for every finite positive contraction,

\[
\boxed{\mathbf P^{Q\#(DQD)}\preccurlyeq\mathbf P^Q,}
\qquad
\boxed{\det(Q\#(DQD))=\det Q.}
\]

The midpoint commutes with `D`, so it removes every coupling incident to `e` in one step.

For the strict case, define the conditional kernels

\[
S=A-xx^*/b,
\qquad
T=A+xx^*/(1-b).
\]

Writing `G=Q#(DQD)=diag(C,c)`, the Riccati characterization of the geometric mean gives

\[
C=S\#A.
\]

If `h=x^*S^{-1}x` and `s=sqrt(1+h/b)`, then

\[
C=S+\tau xx^*,
\qquad
\tau=\frac{s-1}{h}=\frac1{b(s+1)},
\qquad
c=\frac b s.
\]

The rank-one family `K_t=S+t xx^*` has DPP law

\[
\mu_t=\mu_0+t\sigma.
\]

For increasing `B`, `sigma(B)>=0` by determinantal stochastic monotonicity. The key new pointwise estimate is

\[
\sigma(X)\le h\mu_0(X)
\quad\text{for every exact configuration }X\subseteq F.
\]

It follows from the `L`-ensemble representation and Sherman--Morrison: the likelihood slope is

\[
r_X=v_X^*L_0[X]^{-1}v_X-m,
\]

and the principal inverse quadratic form is monotone under adding coordinates, so `r_X<=r_F=h`.

For an arbitrary increasing event `A` on `E`, let its sections be `B_0 subseteq B_1` according to whether `e` is absent or present. Exact subtraction gives

\[
P^Q(A)-P^G(A)
=(b-c)\mu_0(B_1\setminus B_0)
 +(1/b-\tau)\sigma(B_0)
 -c\tau\sigma(B_1\setminus B_0).
\]

Since `c tau h=b-c`, the pointwise slope bound cancels the potentially negative last term, while the middle term is nonnegative. Hence the difference is nonnegative for every increasing event. Boundary kernels follow by continuity.

## New corollary: every finite DPP has a determinant-preserving product sandwich

Apply the singleton midpoint successively to coordinates. Previously removed coordinates stay decoupled because geometric means preserve orthogonal block sums. After at most `n-1` steps the kernel is diagonal,

\[
D_- =\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n),
\]

with

\[
\mathbf P^{D_-}\preccurlyeq\mathbf P^Q,
\qquad
\prod_i d_i=\det Q.
\]

Applying the same construction to `I-Q` and complementing configurations gives parameters `r_i` with `prod_i r_i=det(I-Q)` and

\[
\boxed{
\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Ber}(d_i)
\preccurlyeq\mathbf P^Q
\preccurlyeq
\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Ber}(1-r_i).
}
\]

This is a full stochastic-order statement and holds for every finite DPP, not merely an equivariant one.

## What this changes for C0036

For a finite group of order `n`, the lower peeling parameters already have exactly the Lyons determinant invariant

\[
\prod_i d_i=\det Q=p^n,
\qquad p=(\det Q)^{1/n}.
\]

Thus the finite-group bottleneck has been reduced to **uniformization**: use equivariance to replace the nonuniform peeling product by the scalar product `Ber(p)^n` without losing stochastic domination.

Peeling alone cannot do this. Product-measure domination

\[
\operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes n}\preccurlyeq\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Ber}(d_i)
\]

forces `p<=d_i` for every coordinate; combined with `prod d_i=p^n`, this forces all `d_i=p`. Hence determinant preservation by itself is insufficient. This is the precise new obstruction, not a failed small case.
