## Method change in the same run: arbitrary `S_3` coset-factor theorem

Let `H=A_3={e,r,r^2}` and split `S_3` into the two cosets `H` and `sH`. In the `H`-Fourier decomposition write

`Q_0=[[a0,z0],[z0,a0]]`,

`Q_1=[[a1,z],[zbar,a2]]`,

`Q_2=[[a2,zbar],[z,a1]]`.

Put

`x=sqrt(a0^2-z0^2)`, `d=a1*a2-|z|^2`, and `p=(det Q)^(1/6)=(x*d)^(1/3)`.

For `X~P^Q`, define the two coset-hit indicators

`Z_H=1{X cap H nonempty}`, `Z_sH=1{X cap sH nonempty}`,

and set

`h=1-(1-p)^3`.

The new theorem proves, for **every arbitrary S_3-equivariant positive contraction Q**,

`Ber(h)^2 <= Law(Z_H,Z_sH)`.

Thus every increasing event depending only on which `A_3` cosets are hit satisfies the exact projected Lyons lower comparison. No mean-zero, centrality, hidden-`C_6`, or local-neighborhood condition is used.

### Nontrivial simultaneous-hit inequality

The hard two-bit event is

`U_x = {X hits both H and sH}`.

Its exact probability is

`1 - 2(1-a0)(1-a1)(1-a2) + (1-2a0+x^2)*(1-(a1+a2)+d)^2`.

Keeping `x,d` (hence `p`) fixed, the proof minimizes this expression in three monotone reductions:

1. For fixed `a1+a2`, it decreases as `a1*a2` increases, so the minimum occurs at `a1=a2=y`.
2. With `e=1-2y+d`, positivity gives `0<=e<=1-y`; the derivative in `a0` is `2[(1-y)^2-e^2]>=0`, so the minimum occurs at `a0=x`, i.e. the quotient coupling vanishes.
3. The resulting function

`F(y)=1-2(1-x)(1-y)^2+(1-x)^2(1-2y+d)^2`

has derivative

`4(1-x)[(1-y)-(1-x)(1-2y+d)]>=0`,

so its minimum occurs at `y=sqrt(d)`, where the standard-sector coupling also vanishes.

Hence

`P^Q(U_x) >= [1-(1-x)(1-sqrt(d))^2]^2`.

Generalized Holder on the triples `(x,sqrt(d),sqrt(d))` and `(1-x,1-sqrt(d),1-sqrt(d))` gives

`(x*d)^(1/3) + ((1-x)(1-sqrt(d))^2)^(1/3) <= 1`,

and therefore

`P^Q(U_x) >= [1-(1-p)^3]^2`.

The coordinate-hit inequalities follow from the same Holder inequality and `p^3<=a0*a1*a2`; the union inequality follows from

`(det Q)^(1/6)+(det(I-Q))^(1/6)<=1`.

Together these are exactly all increasing event types on two bits, proving the factor domination.

## Dual upper factor theorem

Apply the lower theorem to the vacancy DPP `P^{I-Q}`. If

`r=(det(I-Q))^(1/6)` and `q=1-r`,

and

`W_H=1{H subset X}`, `W_sH=1{sH subset X}`,

then complementing the vacancy-hit bits yields

`Law(W_H,W_sH) <= Ber(q^3)^2`.

In particular,

`P^Q(H subset X or sH subset X) <= 2 q^3 - q^6`,

which is exactly the upper Lyons comparator on that nontrivial coarse event.
