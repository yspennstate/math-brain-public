## Second new lead: random-order Schur pivots

For each coordinate order `pi`, the previous block theorem gives the explicit product lower law

`mu_pi=tensor_i Ber(d_i^pi) <= P^Q`,

where

`d_{pi_j}^pi=det Q_{P_j}/det Q_{P_{j-1}}`

and `product_i d_i^pi=det Q`.

Hence their uniform mixture `nu_Q=(1/n!) sum_pi mu_pi` is also below `P^Q`.

Let `g(S)=log det Q_S`. Hadamard--Fischer makes `g` submodular, and `x_i^pi=log d_i^pi` are its greedy/marginal vectors. Under a transitive equivariant action, the average greedy vector is constant:

`average_pi x_i^pi=(1/n)log det Q=log p`.

Therefore Jensen proves, for **every principal inclusion event** `T subset X`,

`nu_Q(T subset X)=average_pi exp(sum_{i in T}x_i^pi) >= p^{|T|}`.

This is not yet stochastic domination, so it is labelled honestly as partial. The missing candidate statement is a Shapley-product lemma: does the mixture of product Bernoulli laws at the greedy vectors of a submodular function dominate the product law at the exponential Shapley vector on all increasing events?

A useful structural fact was proved for that next attack: for every increasing event, its independent-product probability as a function of log-parameters is convex along every coordinate-transfer line `x_i+x_j=constant`, because every conditioned two-bit increasing section is one of `0,1,AND,i,j,OR`. Greedy vectors form the vertices of the generalized permutahedron/base polytope of `g`, whose edges are exactly such transfer directions. The missing step is a Jensen theorem from this edgewise convexity to the greedy-vertex barycenter. If it holds, it would settle the finite-group lower half immediately; applying it to `I-Q` would settle the upper half. No such conclusion is claimed yet.
