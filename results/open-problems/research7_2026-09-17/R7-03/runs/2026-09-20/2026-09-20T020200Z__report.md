## Exact source target

The outside source remains Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures* (ICM 2014), arXiv:1406.2707, §5.2, Conjecture 5.7. The section assumes a sofic group `Gamma` and a `Gamma`-equivariant positive contraction

\[
0\le Q\le I\quad\text{on }\ell^2(\Gamma),
\]

with group trace

\[
\operatorname{tr}_\Gamma(T)=\langle T\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle
\]

and Fuglede--Kadison quantity

\[
\operatorname{FK}_\Gamma(Q)=\exp\!\left(\operatorname{tr}_\Gamma\log|Q|\right).
\]

The source asks for stochastic domination on **all increasing events**:

\[
\operatorname{Ber}(\operatorname{FK}_\Gamma(Q))^{\otimes\Gamma}
\preccurlyeq P^Q
\preccurlyeq
\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(1-\operatorname{FK}_\Gamma(I-Q)\right)^{\otimes\Gamma},
\]

for every such `Q`, with optimal constants. For finite groups, the trace is normalized matrix trace and `FK(Q)=(det Q)^{1/|Gamma|}`.

## New theorem: reflection-compatible geometric averaging is globally monotone

Let `rho(i)=-i mod 6` and define

\[
(S_\rho\lambda)_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_{\rho(i)}}.
\]

This leaves frequencies `0,3` fixed and simultaneously averages reflected pairs `(1,5)` and `(2,4)`.

I proved

\[
\boxed{F(S_\rho\lambda)\ge F(\lambda)\qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in[0,1]^6.}
\]

The proof is exact and global, not a Hessian expansion. Write

\[
a=\lambda_0,\ b=\lambda_1,\ c=\lambda_2,\ d=\lambda_3,\ e=\lambda_4,\ f=\lambda_5,
\]

\[
x=\sqrt{bf},\qquad y=\sqrt{ce},
\]

\[
A=b+f-2x=(\sqrt b-\sqrt f)^2,
\qquad
B=c+e-2y=(\sqrt c-\sqrt e)^2,
\]

and

\[
W=(b-f)(c-e).
\]

For

\[
\Delta(a,d)=F(a,x,y,d,y,x)-F(a,b,c,d,e,f),
\]

multi-affinity makes `Delta` a convex combination of its four `(a,d) in {0,1}^2` corner values. Exact substitution of the already-proved projection table gives

\[
12\Delta_{00}=5AB+(10y-4y^2)A+(10x-4x^2)B-3W,
\]

\[
24\Delta_{10}=5AB+(4+10y-8y^2)A+(12+10x-16x^2)B-3W,
\]

\[
24\Delta_{01}=5AB+(12+10y-16y^2)A+(4+10x-8x^2)B-3W,
\]

and

\[
3\Delta_{11}=(1-y)^2A+(1-x)^2B-AB.
\]

For the first three corners, all `A` coefficients dominate `6y` and all `B` coefficients dominate `6x`. When `W>0`, setting `T=AB` and `Z=yA+xB` gives

\[
W^2=T(T+4Z+16xy),\qquad Z\ge2\sqrt{xyT},
\]

and the exact estimate

\[
5T+6Z\ge3W.
\]

Indeed

\[
(5T+6Z)^2-9W^2
=4(4T^2+6TZ+9Z^2-36xyT)\ge0,
\]

with the minimum over the admissible `Z` attained at `2 sqrt(xyT)`. If `W<=0` the result is immediate. For the fourth corner, the box constraints imply

\[
A\le(1-x)^2,\qquad B\le(1-y)^2,
\]

which proves `Delta_11>=0` directly. Hence every corner is nonnegative and so is `Delta(a,d)`.

## Globalization: two reflections force the scalar spectrum

Let

\[
\rho_0(i)=-i,\qquad \rho_1(i)=1-i\pmod6.
\]

For positive eigenvalues put `z_i=log lambda_i`. Geometric reflection symmetrization becomes the orthogonal projection

\[
z\mapsto (z+\rho_cz)/2.
\]

Alternating the two projections converges to the intersection of their fixed spaces. Since `rho_1 rho_0` is the one-step rotation, that intersection consists exactly of constant vectors. The log sum is preserved, so the limit is `(log p,...,log p)`.

Because `F` is nondecreasing under each reflection step and continuous,

\[
\boxed{F(\lambda)\le F(p,p,p,p,p,p).}
\]

If some eigenvalue is zero then `p=0` and the inequality is trivial. This completes the missing adjacent-event lower bound for every `Z/6Z`-equivariant positive contraction:

\[
\boxed{
P^Q(U_{\rm adj})
\ge
P_{\operatorname{Ber}(\operatorname{FK}(Q))^{\otimes6}}(U_{\rm adj}).
}
\]
