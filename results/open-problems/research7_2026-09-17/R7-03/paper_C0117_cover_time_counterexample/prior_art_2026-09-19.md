# C0117 bounded prior-art review — 2026-09-19

This file records the dated literature review for the source-level claim in `main.tex`. It is a bounded search record, **not** a novelty, priority, or completeness certificate.

## Exact source

Jian Ding, James R. Lee, Yuval Peres, *Cover times, blanket times, and majorizing measures*, Annals of Mathematics 175 (2012), 1409–1471, Section 5, Question 5.2, printed p. 1466.

- DOI: `10.4007/annals.2012.175.3.8`
- Official article page: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2012/175-3/p08
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4371

The official source was rechecked on 2026-09-19. Immediately after recalling Aldous's qualitative concentration theorem under `t_cov(G_n)/t_hit(G_n) -> infinity`, DLP ask whether the standard deviation is bounded by maximal hitting time and whether `(tau_cov-t_cov)/t_hit` exhibits exponential decay with constant rate.

## Closest later results checked

### Jian Ding — GFF asymptotics and tree concentration

Jian Ding, *Asymptotics of cover times via Gaussian free fields: Bounded-degree graphs and general trees*, arXiv:1103.4402.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4402

The paper derives exponential concentration for general trees and, in particular, a standard-deviation scale bounded by a geometric-mean expression involving cover and hitting times. This is broader than a single family but does not assert the unrestricted pure-`t_hit` bound asked in DLP Question 5.2.

### Alex Zhai — general GFF-based exponential concentration

Alex Zhai, *Exponential concentration of cover times*, arXiv:1407.7617; Electronic Journal of Probability (2018).

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7617

Zhai proves a general-graph exponential concentration bound in terms of the Gaussian free field and resolves a stochastic-domination question posed in Ding's later work. This is not a resolution of the literal DLP 2012 Question 5.2 at a universal pure-`t_hit` fluctuation scale. The abstract explicitly describes asymptotic sharpness as `t_hit/t_cov -> 0`, but the generic theorem is expressed through the broader GFF/geometric-mean scale.

### Jonathan Hermon — qualitative concentration characterization

Jonathan Hermon, *A spectral characterization for concentration of the cover time*, Journal of Theoretical Probability 33 (2020), 2167–2184; arXiv:1809.00145.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00145
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s10959-019-00946-5

Hermon proves an asymptotic concentration characterization for vertex-transitive graphs and, more generally, under a comparability assumption between maximal and average hitting times. It does not state the unrestricted universal `O(t_hit)` standard-deviation bound of DLP Question 5.2.

### Berestycki–Hermon–Teyssier — vertex-transitive Gumbel fluctuations

Nathanaël Berestycki, Jonathan Hermon, Lucas Teyssier, *On the universality of fluctuations for the cover time*, arXiv:2202.02255.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02255

For bounded-degree vertex-transitive graphs the paper characterizes when suitably normalized cover times have Gumbel fluctuations, using uncovered-set decorrelation, growth/heat-kernel input, and quantitative hitting-time approximation. The hypotheses exclude the highly inhomogeneous star-plus-clique-trap family used in `main.tex`.

Nathanaël Berestycki, Jonathan Hermon, Lucas Teyssier, *Stationary hitting times on vertex-transitive graphs*, arXiv:2601.03864, first posted 2026-01-07.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03864

This companion work refines Aldous–Brown exponential approximations for stationary hitting times and is used in the vertex-transitive fluctuation analysis. It does not claim the unrestricted DLP endpoint.
