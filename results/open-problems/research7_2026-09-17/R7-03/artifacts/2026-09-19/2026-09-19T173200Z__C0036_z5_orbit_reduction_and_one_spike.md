## 1. Exact source scope

Outside source: Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures*, Proceedings of the ICM 2014, Conjecture 5.7, section 5.2; published PDF `https://rdlyons.pages.iu.edu/pdf/icm-pub.pdf`.

For a group `Gamma` and a `Gamma`-equivariant positive contraction `Q` on `l^2(Gamma)`, Lyons asks for the sharp two-sided stochastic bounds

\[
\operatorname{Ber}(\operatorname{FK}(Q))^{\otimes\Gamma}
\preccurlyeq P^Q
\preccurlyeq
\operatorname{Ber}(1-\operatorname{FK}(I-Q))^{\otimes\Gamma}.
\]

The source explicitly says this is open even for finite groups. Lyons's errata, dated 30 December 2025, records resolutions/corrections to other conjectures but does not announce a resolution of Conjecture 5.7. The current author page also lists the later Lyons--Thom operator-order coupling result, which proves monotone coupling under `Q_1 <= Q_2` but does not supply the determinant comparison required here.

The previous R7-03 run proved the full sharp conjecture for every finite group of order at most four and proved all cardinality-threshold events for every finite group. This note attacks the first unresolved group order, `Gamma = Z/5Z`.

## 2. Setup for Z/5Z

Every equivariant Hermitian contraction on `Z/5Z` is circulant. In the Fourier basis write its eigenvalues

\[
\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_4\in[0,1],
\qquad
p=(\lambda_0\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3\lambda_4)^{1/5}
=\operatorname{FK}(Q).
\]

For the lower stochastic bound it is enough, by the translation-averaging reduction proved in the preceding R7-03 run, to check translation-invariant increasing events.

There are exactly eight translation orbits in `2^(Z/5Z)`:

- one orbit each in sizes `0,1,4,5`;
- two pair orbits, represented by `{0,1}` and `{0,2}`;
- two triple orbits, represented by `{0,1,2}` and `{0,1,3}`.

The quotient poset has exactly eleven upper sets. Seven are cardinality-only (`empty`, `whole`, and the five thresholds `|X| >= m`, `m=1,...,5`). Thus only **four** invariant increasing events are not already covered by the all-finite cardinality theorem from the previous run: the two events generated by a pair orbit and the two events generated by a triple orbit.

This gives the first exact order-five gluing reduction.

## 3. B30: the full order-five obstruction is four nested-minor inequalities

Let

- `D_1,D_2` be the inclusion probabilities of representatives of the two pair orbits;
- `T_1,T_2` the inclusion probabilities of representatives of the two triple orbits, indexed so that a triple of type `i` contains two pairs of type `i` and one pair of the other type;
- `D_4` the inclusion probability of a fixed four-set;
- `D_5=det(Q)=p^5`.

For the increasing event `E_{P_i}` that the random set contains a pair of orbit type `i`, exact incidence counting on the five-cycle gives

\[
\mathbf 1_{E_{P_i}}
=\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_i}\mathbf 1_{\{P\subseteq X\}}
-\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_i}\mathbf 1_{\{T\subseteq X\}}
+\mathbf 1_{\{\Gamma\subseteq X\}}.
\]

Therefore

\[
P^Q(E_{P_i})=5D_i-5T_i+p^5.
\]

For Bernoulli product measure of density `p`, the same event has probability

\[
5p^2-5p^3+p^5.
\]

Thus pair-orbit domination is **exactly**

\[
\boxed{D_i-T_i\ge p^2(1-p),\qquad i=1,2.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Similarly, for the event `E_{T_i}` generated by one triple orbit,

\[
P^Q(E_{T_i})=5T_i-5D_4+p^5,
\]

whereas product measure gives `5p^3-5p^4+p^5`. Hence triple-orbit domination is exactly

\[
\boxed{T_i-D_4\ge p^3(1-p),\qquad i=1,2.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently **every possible lower-bound counterexample on `Z/5Z` must violate one of the four inequalities (3.1)--(3.2)**. There is no remaining cardinality-only obstruction and no additional invariant event.

### Fourier formulas

Put

\[
\alpha=\frac{5-\sqrt5}{50},\qquad
\beta=\frac{5+\sqrt5}{50}.
\]

Define cyclic sums (indices mod five)

\[
A=\sum_i\lambda_i\lambda_{i+1},
\qquad
B=\sum_i\lambda_i\lambda_{i+2},
\]

and

\[
C_1=\sum_i\prod_{j\notin\{i,i+1\}}\lambda_j,
\qquad
C_2=\sum_i\prod_{j\notin\{i,i+2\}}\lambda_j,
\]

as well as

\[
e_4=\sum_i\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j.
\]

Cauchy--Binet for the `5 x 5` Fourier matrix gives

\[
\boxed{D_1=\alpha A+\beta B,\qquad D_2=\beta A+\alpha B.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed the squared `2 x 2` Fourier minor at spatial separation `d` and frequency separation `r` equals

\[
\frac4{25}\sin^2\frac{\pi dr}{5},
\]

which is `alpha` or `beta`. The complementary-minor identity for a unitary matrix then gives

\[
\boxed{T_1=\alpha C_1+\beta C_2,\qquad T_2=\beta C_1+\alpha C_2,}
\tag{3.4}
\]

and the `4 x 4` complementary minors give

\[
\boxed{D_4=\frac{e_4}{5}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equations (3.1)--(3.5) are a completely explicit four-inequality formulation of the order-five case.

## 4. B2: counterexample attack before proof

The negation was attacked at the four exact gaps

\[
G_{P_i}=D_i-T_i-p^2(1-p),
\qquad
G_{T_i}=T_i-D_4-p^3(1-p).
\]

A broad floating-point search (random spectra plus multistart constrained minimization) found no negative value; all optimized minima returned to the scalar-spectrum boundary within numerical precision. This numerical evidence is not used as proof.

After the analytic work below was complete, an exact finite self-check was run on the algebraic grid

\[
\lambda_i=(m_i/4)^5,\qquad m_i\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}.
\]

On this grid `p=(m_0m_1m_2m_3m_4)/4^5` is rational and all four gaps lie in `Q(sqrt(5))`, so their signs are decidable by exact rational arithmetic. All `5^5=3125` spectra pass all four inequalities. This is a finite self-check only, not a proof of the unrestricted order-five case.

## 5. B15: breaking symmetry near the scalar spectrum

The counterexample search suggests that equality spectra are locally extremal. This can be proved exactly and gives information unavailable from the finite grid.

Fix `p in (0,1)` and write a product-preserving logarithmic perturbation

\[
\lambda_i=p e^{\varepsilon x_i},
\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^4x_i=0,
\]

for sufficiently small `epsilon` so that all eigenvalues remain in `[0,1]`.

For either pair shape, let `w` be the probability distribution on the ten frequency pairs with weight `alpha` on one five-cycle of pairs and `beta` on the complementary five-cycle (or with `alpha,beta` swapped). If `S_{ij}=x_i+x_j`, then

\[
\mathbb E_w S=0
\]

because each frequency occurs with total incidence weight `2/5`.

The corresponding pair gap is

\[
\frac{G_P(\varepsilon)}{p^2}
=\mathbb E_w e^{\varepsilon S}
-p\,\mathbb E_w e^{-\varepsilon S}
-(1-p).
\]

Hence

\[
G_P(0)=G_P'(0)=0,
\qquad
\boxed{G_P''(0)=p^2(1-p)\,\mathbb E_w S^2>0}
\tag{5.1}
\]

for every nonzero zero-sum direction `x`.

For the triple gap, let `u` be the uniform law on the five frequencies. Then

\[
\frac{G_T(\varepsilon)}{p^3}
=\mathbb E_w e^{-\varepsilon S}
-p\,\mathbb E_u e^{-\varepsilon x}
-(1-p),
\]

so

\[
G_T''(0)
=p^3\left(\mathbb E_w S^2-p\,\mathbb E_u x^2\right).
\]

The circulant quadratic form `E_w(x_i+x_j)^2` has spectrum

\[
\left\{\frac45,\frac15,\frac15,\frac25,\frac25\right\};
\]

swapping `alpha` and `beta` only swaps the two nontrivial Fourier eigenspaces. Therefore on the zero-sum subspace

\[
\mathbb E_w S^2\ge\frac15\sum_i x_i^2=\mathbb E_u x^2.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
G_T''(0)\ge p^3(1-p)\,\mathbb E_u x^2>0
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

for every nonzero zero-sum direction.

**Conclusion.** For both pair shapes and both triple shapes, the scalar spectrum is a strict local minimum of the order-five domination gap on every fixed-geometric-mean logarithmic slice. Therefore no sufficiently small symmetry-breaking perturbation of a product kernel can refute the conjecture on `Z/5Z`.

This is a local theorem, not a global proof.

## 6. A global nontrivial order-five family: one exceptional Fourier eigenvalue

The same symmetry-breaking route does close a substantial global family.

### Theorem 6.1

Let `Gamma=Z/5Z`. Suppose the Fourier eigenvalues of an equivariant positive contraction are

\[
(a,b,b,b,b),\qquad a,b\in[0,1],
\]

up to cyclic permutation. Then the full sharp two-sided bounds in Lyons Conjecture 5.7 hold for `Q`.

### Proof of the lower bound

It suffices first to take `a,b` in the interior; boundary cases follow by continuity of all finitely many event probabilities and of the finite-dimensional Fuglede--Kadison determinant.

Set

\[
a=b t^5,\qquad t>0,
\qquad p=(ab^4)^{1/5}=bt.
\]

The parameter constraints are

\[
0<b\le1,\qquad bt^5\le1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a fixed spatial `k`-set, Cauchy--Binet weights on frequency `k`-sets sum to one and every frequency has marginal inclusion probability `k/5`. Since only one frequency has eigenvalue `a`, the two spatial orbit shapes coincide in this family and

\[
D_2=\frac25ab+\frac35b^2
=\frac{b^2}{5}(3+2t^5),
\tag{6.2}
\]

\[
T_3=\frac35ab^2+\frac25b^3
=\frac{b^3}{5}(2+3t^5),
\tag{6.3}
\]

\[
D_4=\frac45ab^3+\frac15b^4
=\frac{b^4}{5}(1+4t^5).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Define

\[
P(t)=2t^3+4t^2+6t+3,
\]
\[
Q(t)=3t^3+6t^2+4t+2,
\]
\[
R(t)=4t^3+3t^2+2t+1.
\]

Direct factorization gives the pair-orbit gap

\[
\boxed{
D_2-T_3-p^2(1-p)
=\frac{b^2(t-1)^2}{5}\,[P(t)-bQ(t)].
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

If `0<=t<=1`, then `b<=1` and

\[
P-bQ\ge P-Q
=(1-t)(t^2+3t+1)\ge0.
\]

If `t>=1`, then (6.1) gives `b<=t^{-5}`, and

\[
P-bQ\ge P-t^{-5}Q
=\frac{t^5P-Q}{t^5}\ge0,
\]

because

\[
t^5P-Q
=(t-1)(t+1)
(2t^6+4t^5+8t^4+7t^3+8t^2+4t+2).
\]

Therefore both pair-orbit inequalities (3.1) hold.

For the triple-orbit gap,

\[
\boxed{
T_3-D_4-p^3(1-p)
=\frac{b^3(t-1)^2}{5}\,[Q(t)-bR(t)].
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

If `0<=t<=1`, then

\[
Q-bR\ge Q-R
=-t^3+3t^2+2t+1
=(t^2-t^3)+2t^2+2t+1\ge0.
\]

If `t>=1`, then `b<=t^{-5}` and

\[
Q-bR\ge Q-t^{-5}R.
\]

Moreover

\[
t^5Q=3t^8+6t^7+4t^6+2t^5\ge15t^3,
\]

while

\[
R=4t^3+3t^2+2t+1\le10t^3.
\]

Hence `t^5Q-R>=5t^3>0`, proving (6.6). Thus both triple-orbit inequalities (3.2) hold.

The preceding all-finite cardinality theorem handles the seven cardinality-only invariant upper sets. Section 3 shows that (3.1)--(3.2) handle the only four remaining invariant upper sets. Translation averaging therefore gives

\[
\operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes\Gamma}\preccurlyeq P^Q.
\]

### Upper bound and sharpness

The spectrum of `I-Q` is again one-spike:

\[
(1-a,1-b,1-b,1-b,1-b).
\]

Applying the lower result to `I-Q` and complementing configurations gives

\[
P^Q\preccurlyeq
\operatorname{Ber}(1-\operatorname{FK}(I-Q))^{\otimes\Gamma}.
\]

For the lower density, the full-set event has probability `det Q=p^5`, so no larger product density can be dominated by `P^Q`. Applying the same argument to the complement shows optimality of the upper density. This proves the theorem. `QED`

## 7. What remains at order five

The full `Z/5Z` problem is now reduced to the four explicit algebraic gaps in (3.1)--(3.5). The present run proves:

1. no cardinality-threshold counterexample exists (previous all-finite theorem);
2. there are exactly four non-cardinality invariant upper-set inequalities;
3. the scalar spectrum is a strict local minimum for every one of those four gaps;
4. all four gaps are globally nonnegative for every one-spike Fourier spectrum `(a,b,b,b,b)`;
5. an exact `3125`-spectrum algebraic grid has no counterexample.

What is **not** proved is global nonnegativity of the four gaps for arbitrary five-tuples `lambda_i`. That is the simplest remaining finite case. The source itself is much broader still: arbitrary finite and infinite groups.
