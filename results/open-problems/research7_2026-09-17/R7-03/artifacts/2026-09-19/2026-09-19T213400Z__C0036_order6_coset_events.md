## 1. Source target and recovered state

For a finite group `Gamma` of order `N`, let `Q` be a `Gamma`-equivariant positive contraction on `ell^2(Gamma)`, let `P^Q` be its determinantal law, and write

\[
p=\operatorname{FK}(Q)=(\det Q)^{1/N}.
\]

The lower half of Lyons Conjecture 5.7 is

\[
\operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes\Gamma}\preccurlyeq P^Q.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The upper half follows from the corresponding lower statement for `I-Q` after complementing configurations, but a proof for only selected lower events does **not** automatically establish their upper analogues.

Earlier R7-03 work proved the full sharp two-sided statement for every finite group of order at most four and for `Z/5Z`, and proved all cardinality-threshold lower inequalities for every finite group. This note does not rederive those results. Its aim is the first unresolved finite order, six.

There are two groups of order six: `Z/6Z` and `S_3`. For invariant measures, stochastic domination can be tested only on invariant increasing functions: average an arbitrary increasing function over the group action; invariance preserves both expectations and averaging preserves monotonicity. On a finite quotient poset it is therefore enough to test invariant upper sets.

Exact enumeration gives:

- `Z/6Z`: 14 subset orbits and 44 invariant upper sets. Removing the empty event and the seven cardinality thresholds `|X|>=k`, `k=0,...,6`, leaves **36 genuinely orbit-sensitive upper sets**.
- `S_3` under its regular left action: 16 subset orbits and 76 invariant upper sets, leaving **68 genuinely orbit-sensitive upper sets** after the same eight trivial/cardinality cases are removed.

These counts are finite claims and are checked exactly by the companion script.

## 2. A general index-two coset-union lift

The first new mechanism uses negative association rather than Fourier algebra.

### Theorem 2.1 — two-coset union lift

Let `Gamma` be a finite group, `H <= Gamma` an index-two subgroup of size `m`, and `Q` a `Gamma`-equivariant positive contraction. Put

\[
p=(\det Q)^{1/(2m)}.
\]

Let `K=Q|_H` be the principal compression to `H`. Suppose the lower Fuglede--Kadison product domination is known for the `H`-equivariant determinantal law `P^K`. Then, for **every** increasing event `A` on `2^H`, if `A_0` is `A` on the first coset and `A_1` is its translate to the other coset,

\[
P^Q(A_0\cup A_1)
\ge
\operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes\Gamma}(A_0\cup A_1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Equivariance makes the two coset compressions unitarily equivalent, so

\[
d:=\det Q_H=\det Q_{\Gamma\setminus H}.
\]

Fischer's determinant inequality for the positive semidefinite block matrix `Q` gives

\[
\det Q\le d^2.
\]

Hence, if

\[
a=d^{1/m}=\operatorname{FK}(K),
\]

then

\[
a\ge (\det Q)^{1/(2m)}=p.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The marginal of `P^Q` on either coset is the determinantal law of the corresponding compression. By the assumed lower theorem on `H`, followed by monotonicity of Bernoulli product measures in their density,

\[
P^Q(A_i)\ge \operatorname{Ber}(a)^{\otimes H}(A)
\ge \operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes H}(A)=:q.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Determinantal probability measures generated by positive contractions are negatively associated. Since `A_0` and `A_1` are increasing and depend on disjoint coordinate sets,

\[
P^Q(A_0\cap A_1)\le P^Q(A_0)P^Q(A_1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
P^Q(A_0\cup A_1)
&=P^Q(A_0)+P^Q(A_1)-P^Q(A_0\cap A_1)\\
&\ge 1-(1-P^Q(A_0))(1-P^Q(A_1))\\
&\ge 1-(1-q)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Under `Ber(p)^{\otimes\Gamma}`, the two cosets are independent and each local event has probability `q`, so its union has probability exactly `1-(1-q)^2`. This proves (2.1). ∎

### Corollary 2.2 — two new order-six orbit inequalities

Both groups of order six have an index-two subgroup of order three: the parity subgroup in `Z/6Z` and `A_3` in `S_3`. The full order-three theorem was already proved earlier in R7-03, so Theorem 2.1 applies.

For `Z/6Z`, take

\[
H=\{0,2,4\},\qquad 1+H=\{1,3,5\}.
\]

Because the cyclic action on a three-point coset is transitive on every cardinality layer, the two non-cardinality local choices are:

1. `A={|Y|>=2}`. The union `A_0 union A_1` is exactly the event that `X` contains a pair at cyclic distance two. In the binary orbit notation of the exact checker, its minimal orbit is `101000`.
2. `A={|Y|=3}`. The union is the event that `X` contains one of the two alternating triples; its minimal orbit is `101010`.

Thus both events satisfy the sharp lower product inequality at density `p=FK(Q)` for **every** `Z/6Z`-equivariant positive contraction.

The same argument on `S_3` with `H=A_3` proves the corresponding two coset-local invariant event inequalities there as well.

This is not a full order-six theorem: it covers a structured family of invariant upper sets, not all 36 or 68 orbit-sensitive upper sets.

## 3. The third pair orbit on `Z/6Z`: antipodal pairs

The order-six cyclic group has three translation orbits of two-point subsets, represented by cyclic distances 1, 2, and 3. Section 2 settles distance 2. The distance-3 orbit is the three antipodal pairs

\[
H_0=\{0,3\},\quad H_1=\{1,4\},\quad H_2=\{2,5\}.
\]

We now prove the sharp lower bound for the event

\[
U_{\mathrm{opp}}=\{H_j\subseteq X\text{ for at least one }j\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 — a three-variable Maclaurin excess chain

For `z_1,z_2,z_3 in [0,1]`, define

\[
A(z)=\frac{z_1+z_2+z_3}{3},\qquad
B(z)=\frac{z_1z_2+z_1z_3+z_2z_3}{3},\qquad
g(z)=(z_1z_2z_3)^{1/3}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{A(z)-g(z)\ge B(z)-g(z)^2\ge0.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

The second inequality is AM--GM applied to the three pair products.

If `g=0`, then `A>=B` because `xy <= (x+y)/2` for numbers in `[0,1]`, and (3.2) follows. Assume `g>0` and write `z_i=g u_i`, so `u_1u_2u_3=1`. Let `M=max u_i>=1`; relabel so `u_3=M`. Since every `z_i<=1`,

\[
gM\le1,\qquad g\le M^{-1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Put

\[
U_1=\frac{u_1+u_2+u_3}{3},\qquad
U_2=\frac{u_1u_2+u_1u_3+u_2u_3}{3}.
\]

Because `u_1u_2=1/M`, direct cancellation yields

\[
3\big(M(U_1-1)-(U_2-1)\big)
=M^2-3M+3-M^{-1}
=\frac{(M-1)^3}{M}\ge0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus `U_1-1 >= M^{-1}(U_2-1) >= g(U_2-1)`. Multiplying by `g` gives

\[
A-g=g(U_1-1)\ge g^2(U_2-1)=B-g^2.
\]

This proves the lemma. ∎

### Theorem 3.2 — sharp lower product inequality for the antipodal-pair event

Let `Q` be an arbitrary `Z/6Z`-equivariant positive contraction and `p=FK(Q)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
P^Q(U_{\mathrm{opp}})
\ge 3p^2-3p^4+p^6
=\operatorname{Ber}(p)^{\otimes6}(U_{\mathrm{opp}}).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

First assume `Q` is invertible; the boundary follows by continuity. Let its Fourier eigenvalues be

\[
\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_5\in(0,1].
\]

Split them by parity:

\[
x=(\lambda_0,\lambda_2,\lambda_4),\qquad
y=(\lambda_1,\lambda_3,\lambda_5).
\]

Write `A_x=A(x)`, `B_x=B(x)`, `g_x=g(x)` and similarly for `y`.

For one antipodal pair, say `{0,3}`, the `2x2` compression is diagonalized by the symmetric and antisymmetric vectors on that pair. Its two eigenvalues are the arithmetic means of the even and odd Fourier triples, so

\[
d_1:=\det Q_{\{0,3\}}=A_xA_y.
\tag{3.6}
\]

A union of two antipodal pairs is the complement of the third. Jacobi's complementary-minor identity gives

\[
d_2:=\det Q_{\Gamma\setminus\{0,3\}}
=(\det Q)\det (Q^{-1})_{\{0,3\}}.
\]

Applying (3.6) to `Q^{-1}` and using

\[
(\lambda_0\lambda_2\lambda_4)
\frac{\lambda_0^{-1}+\lambda_2^{-1}+\lambda_4^{-1}}3
=B_x
\]

(and the analogous identity for `y`) yields

\[
d_2=B_xB_y.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Finally

\[
\det Q=(g_xg_y)^3=p^6.
\]

Set

\[
a=g_xg_y=p^2.
\]

Inclusion--exclusion over the three antipodal pairs gives

\[
P^Q(U_{\mathrm{opp}})=3d_1-3d_2+a^3.
\tag{3.8}
\]

For the Bernoulli product law, the three antipodal pair events are independent because the pairs are disjoint, so

\[
P_p(U_{\mathrm{opp}})=1-(1-p^2)^3=3a-3a^2+a^3.
\tag{3.9}
\]

It remains to show `d_1-d_2 >= a-a^2`.

Define

\[
\delta_x=A_x-g_x,\qquad \epsilon_x=B_x-g_x^2
\]

and similarly `delta_y,epsilon_y`. Lemma 3.1 says

\[
\delta_x\ge\epsilon_x\ge0,\qquad
\delta_y\ge\epsilon_y\ge0.
\]

Since `g_x,g_y<=1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_1-a
&=g_x\delta_y+g_y\delta_x+\delta_x\delta_y,\\
d_2-a^2
&=g_x^2\epsilon_y+g_y^2\epsilon_x+\epsilon_x\epsilon_y.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every term in the first line dominates the corresponding term in the second. Therefore

\[
d_1-a\ge d_2-a^2,
\]

which is the required inequality. ∎

## 4. B30 localization: the first surviving cyclic pair-orbit obstruction

The three two-point translation orbits on `Z/6Z` are now:

- distance 2 (`101000`): **proved** by the index-two coset-union lift;
- distance 3 (`100100`): **proved** by Theorem 3.2;
- distance 1 (`110000`): **still unresolved** by these mechanisms.

Thus the first unresolved principal pair-orbit event is

\[
U_{\mathrm{adj}}
=\{X\text{ contains an edge of the 6-cycle}\}.
\]

Let

\[
D_{01}=\det Q_{\{0,1\}},\quad
D_{012}=\det Q_{\{0,1,2\}},\quad
D_{0134}=\det Q_{\{0,1,3,4\}},\quad
D_5=\det Q_{\{0,1,2,3,4\}},\quad
\Delta=\det Q=p^6.
\]

Exact inclusion--exclusion on the six cycle edges gives

\[
\boxed{
P^Q(U_{\mathrm{adj}})
=6D_{01}-6D_{012}-3D_{0134}+6D_5-2\Delta.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the product law,

\[
P_p(U_{\mathrm{adj}})
=6p^2-6p^3-3p^4+6p^5-2p^6.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since `Delta=p^6`, the exact remaining pair-level inequality is

\[
\boxed{
6(D_{01}-p^2)
-6(D_{012}-p^3)
-3(D_{0134}-p^4)
+6(D_5-p^5)
\ge0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This formula identifies the gluing failure precisely. The distance-2 event is a union of local events on the two parity cosets and is handled by negative association. The antipodal event is a union over a three-coset partition and collapses to the three-variable excess chain. Adjacent pairs cross the parity cosets and do not respect either decomposition, so neither mechanism controls the alternating signs in (4.3).

The next mathematical attack should target (4.3) directly rather than re-enumerating the full 44-event quotient.

## 5. B2 exact negation search

After deriving the structural formulas, an exact finite search tested the geometric-mean lower domination on a nontrivial reflection-symmetric spectral grid

\[
(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_5)
=(t_0^6,t_1^6,t_2^6,t_3^6,t_2^6,t_1^6),
\qquad t_i\in\{1/3,2/3,1\}.
\]

This choice keeps the kernel rational and also makes

\[
p=t_0t_3t_1^2t_2^2
\]

rational, so no floating-point comparison is involved.

The checker evaluates the exact determinantal law by rational principal minors and Möbius inversion, enumerates all 44 invariant upper sets, and compares each with exact Bernoulli product probability. It checks

\[
81\times44=3564
\]

spectral-event pairs. It finds **zero negative gaps**. The smallest strictly positive gap on this grid is

\[
\frac{43}{68630377364883}.
\]

This is a bounded exact counterexample search, **not** a proof of the unrestricted `Z/6Z` case and not a source-resolution certificate.
