## Source question and corrections

F. den Hollander and J. E. Steif, *Random walk in random scenery: A survey of some recent results*, IMS Lecture Notes–Monograph Series 48 (2006), 53–65, DOI 10.1214/074921706000000077, [published-version preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608219). Read Section 3, printed pp. 61–62 (PDF pp. 9–10), and Section 4, printed p. 63 (PDF p. 11), in full. Section 1 explicitly assumes irreducibility and permits infinite increment support.

Section 3 requires a single measurable observation-to-scenery map succeeding with joint probability one, modulo translations/reflections. This is stronger than identifying which member of a known pair generated the record. Its Theorem 3.3 has a strict **superset** sign: F strictly contains {-1,0,1}. The proposed symmetric five-step binary example is not a new result. Section 4, question 7, is the general recurrence question; question 6 separately asks about an entropy inequality.

## Distinguishing results and false negative leads

- I. Benjamini and H. Kesten, *Distinguishing sceneries by observing the scenery along a random walk path*, J. Anal. Math. 69 (1996), 97–135, DOI 10.1007/BF02787104. Publisher metadata checked; later primary papers describe its fixed-versus-random i.i.d. binary distinction result (including dimension two). Full original theorem numbers not obtained. Pair-dependent tests do not supply a single universal reconstruction map.
- C. D. Howard, *Detecting defects in periodic scenery by random walks on Z*, Random Structures Algorithms 8 (1996), 59–74; *Orthogonality of Measures Induced by Random Walks with Scenery*, CPC 5 (1996), 247–256; *Distinguishing certain random sceneries on Z via random walks*, Statist. Probab. Lett. 34 (1997), 123–132, DOI 10.1016/S0167-7152(96)00173-3. Author bibliography and publisher abstract checked. The 1997 paper concerns renewal sceneries with heavy-tailed spacings, **not heavy-tailed walk jumps**. No original theorem numbers claimed here.
- H. Kesten, *Detecting a single defect in a scenery by observing the scenery along a random walk path*, in *Itô's Stochastic Calculus and Probability Theory* (1996), 171–183; *Distinguishing and reconstructing sceneries from observations along random walk paths*, DIMACS Series 41 (1998), 75–83. Located through primary bibliographies and the DIMACS meeting program; original texts not obtained. Later papers differ in their reported four-/five-color thresholds, so no threshold from that unverified original is used in our proof.
- E. Lindenstrauss, *Indistinguishable Sceneries*, Random Structures Algorithms 14(1) (1999), 71–86. Author publication list and web-readable preprint checked; preprint Theorem 2.1 constructs continuum many indistinguishable sceneries using a rapidly growing odometer structure. Direct PDF download failed, and journal-page correspondence was not verified. This is an exceptional deterministic family, not nonreconstruction for i.i.d. scenery. The survey mentions a countable subfamily; the paper gives uncountably many. Indistinguishability means failure of mutual singularity, not necessarily equality of laws.
- H. Matzinger and S. Popov, *Detecting a local perturbation in a continuous scenery*, EJP 12, paper 22 (2007), 637–660, Theorem 1.1, printed p. 641 / PDF p. 5: Brownian motion and Poisson-distributed bells, with a known reference outside a finite perturbation. Full text saved; this is a different model.

## 2006–2026 follow-through

- S. Popov and A. Pachon, *Scenery reconstruction with branching random walk*, Stochastics 83(2) (2011), 107–116, DOI 10.1080/17442508.2010.544973. Uses branching observations and a window of scenery at a particle, not one unlabelled trajectory.
- H. Matzinger, S. Popov, A. Pachon, *Reconstruction of a multidimensional scenery with a branching random walk*, Ann. Appl. Probab. 27(2) (2017), 877–897; arXiv 1511.00973. Theorem 2.1, arXiv PDF p. 4: at least 2d+1 uniform colors, all branching-particle records. Full text saved. Not a theorem for one recurrent walk.
- H. Finucane, O. Tamuz, Y. Yaari, *Scenery reconstruction on finite abelian groups*, SPA 124(8) (2014), 2754–2770; arXiv 1105.5569. Theorems 1.2–1.3, arXiv pp. 2–3, concern Fourier criteria on finite groups; negative examples cannot simply be lifted to typical i.i.d. scenery on Z. Full text saved.
- T. Lakrec, *Scenery reconstruction for random walk on random scenery systems*, Israel J. Math. 248 (2022), 149–200, DOI 10.1007/s11856-022-2301-y; arXiv 1909.07470. Full preprint saved. Explicitly supplies the walk channel as well as colors and allows adversarial corruptions. Its reconstruction statement does not resolve color-only F0126.
- Searches included the exact survey title and authors; all requested author/title combinations; “scenery reconstruction unbounded jumps”, “heavy-tailed jumps”, “binary jumps”, “arbitrary recurrent”, “counterexample”, and year-specific searches through 2026. No verifiable Popov–Pachon paper with the supplied “random walk with jumps” or “heavy-tailed jumps” titles was located; their identifiable papers above are branching models. No printed theorem resolving arbitrary irreducible recurrent increments and arbitrary finite i.i.d. scenery was located.

The conclusion is a bounded literature audit, not a claim that every paper through 2026 was accessible. In particular, no unverified bibliography entry is promoted to a full general theorem.
