# Opening assessment from PDF pages 1–2 alone

Line numbers throughout refer to `paper/main.tex` (the supplied 695-line source). The completed proof and source audit supports minor revision.

- Lines 49–76 (PDF p. 1): Virág’s Theorem 4.1 supplies a single random orthogonality measure for all beta-Hermite degrees, using reversed coefficients in common spectral units. Question 2 asks for other such families. The manuscript requires actual leading blocks of one positive-off-diagonal Jacobi matrix, a stronger requirement than interlacing alone.
- Lines 78–112 (PDF pp. 1–2): Theorem 1.1 asserts that independent `d_i = beta^(-1/2) chi_{beta(a+i)}` and `ell_i = beta^(-1/2) chi_{beta i}`, for beta > 0 and a > -1, give `L = BB^T` with all leading-block spectra having the fixed-power beta-Laguerre density. It also asserts strict interlacing, essential self-adjointness, nonnegativity, and one spectral measure whose orthogonal-polynomial zeros have those marginals. The fixed hard-edge exponent is `theta(a+1)-1`, and the effective column count is `n+a`.
- Lines 114–143 (PDF p. 2): The finite ingredients are Dumitriu–Edelman’s beta-Hermite/Laguerre models and Dumitriu–Forrester’s antisymmetric model. Killip–Nenciu and Edelman–Sutton supply finite Jacobi models. Full corners processes and projective arrays, as well as ordinary parameter-shifting intertwinings, are distinguished from a common Jacobi recurrence. Stochastic Airy and circular operator couplings concern limits and do not assert literal common finite prefixes.
- Lines 108–112, 145–150 (PDF p. 2): The other constructive claim is an antisymmetric Gaussian beta coupling. Later results are expressly restricted obstructions: dimensions, ordinary corners links, simple Jacobi reversal, unitary leading blocks, and Gaussian characterisations with additional assumptions.
- Lines 35–43, 103–112: The advertised size is appropriate: an elementary consequence of the finite model and an existential answer for these families. It is expressly not a classification of weights, a new finite beta-Laguerre model, or a full corners law. Fix: retain these qualifications; no stronger novelty claim is justified by pages 1–2.

## Independent proof audit

1. **Lines 181–230, Theorem 1.1: correct finite construction.** Reversing `B_n^T` makes a lower bidiagonal matrix with diagonal degrees `beta(a+n-j+1)` and subdiagonal degrees `beta(n-j)`. Multiplication by `sqrt(beta)` matches Dumitriu–Edelman with `A_n=theta(n+a)`. Their condition `A_n>theta(n-1)` is precisely `a>-1`; their `p_n=1+theta(n-1)` gives `A_n-p_n=theta(a+1)-1`. The effective column number `2A_n/beta=n+a` changes with n. If `t=beta x`, the exponential is `exp(-beta x/2)=exp(-theta x)`, and the full scale factor is `beta^[n+beta n(n-1)/2+n(theta(a+1)-1)]`, independent of x. Fix: none to the mathematics; keep the changing column count explicit.

2. **Lines 199–206: boundary identity is exact.** Row i of B uses columns i-1 and i only, so the leading block of `BB^T` is exactly `B_n B_n^T`. The leading block of `B^T B` has the extra `ell_n^2 e_n e_n^T`. This is the distinction that permits the construction. Positivity follows from the invertible triangular factor; positive off-diagonals and the three-term recurrence give strict interlacing. Fix: none.

3. **Line 94: replace “entrywise product” by “formal matrix product” (or “matrix product, defined entry by entry”).** “Entrywise product” conventionally means the Hadamard product, which here would be diagonal, contrary to equation (7). The displayed product and all subsequent calculations use ordinary matrix multiplication correctly.

4. **Lines 235–261, Lemma 3.1: correct density and Jacobian.** The triangular derivative has determinant `2^n d_n product_{i<n} d_i^2`. Dividing the primitive density and substituting `ell_i=c_i/d_i` leaves powers `beta a-2` for i<n and `beta(a+n)-2` at n. The exponential is `exp(-theta tr L_n)`. At n=1 this reduces to the Gamma(theta(a+1),theta) density in `t_1=d_1^2`, an independent boundary check. Fix: none.

5. **Lines 269–287, Lemma 3.2: Carleman argument is valid.** Explicitly, `P(d_i^2>Ki) <= exp(-beta Ki/4) 2^[beta(a+i)/2]`; the analogous bound for `ell_i^2` has a=0. Choose `K>2 log 2` and absorb the fixed factor involving a. Thus `c_i<=C i` eventually and `sum 1/c_i` diverges. The imaginary-part boundary identity excludes every nonzero square-summable deficiency solution: eventually `1/c_n <= C |u_n u_{n+1}|`, whose sum is finite by Cauchy–Schwarz. Nonnegativity passes from the core to the graph closure. Fix: none; an explicit K would be optional clarification.

6. **Lines 159–167, 289–290: spectral-measure conclusion is valid.** Every `J^k e_1` is finitely supported and in the operator domain. Nonnegative self-adjoint closure puts support in `[0,infinity)`. Fix: none.

8. **Lines 300–363, Corollary 4.1: both parities are correct.** Even N=2m yields the rate-one Laguerre factor after division by `sqrt(theta)`, with a=-1/2. For odd N=2m+1, `Q^T Q` has diagonal `u_j+v_j` and off-diagonal `sqrt(v_j u_{j+1})`. At each step `w_j~Gamma(theta/2,1)`, independent of unused variables and earlier Cholesky entries; `v_j~Gamma(theta j,1)`. Their sum D_j^2 is independent of their beta ratio. Splitting the independent `u_{j+1}~Gamma(theta(j+1/2),1)` gives mutually independent `w_{j+1}~Gamma(theta/2,1)` and `E_j^2~Gamma(theta j,1)`, also independent of D_j^2 and the past. The last step produces D_m^2 with the required shape. This proves the full joint law, not merely the individual shapes. Squaring eigenvalues gives hard-edge exponents theta/2-1 and 3theta/2-1; the Jacobian `2^m product lambda_j` gives beta/2-1 and 3beta/2-1. The size-three check is `lambda^2=r_1^2+r_2^2~Gamma(3beta/4,1)`. Fix: none to these formulas.
