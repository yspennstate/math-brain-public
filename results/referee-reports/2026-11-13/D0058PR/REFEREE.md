## Independent proof audit

The arguments in §§2–4 are valid as stated. The checks below specify the load-bearing steps and the hypotheses that prevent apparent counterexamples. “Fix: none” means that no mathematical repair is required.

1. Lines 267–301: positivity propagates because m positive integer children give sum at least m and hence positive output for m ≥ 2. Criticality excludes the zero law. The generating-function identity follows by separating child sum S = 0 from S ≥ 1, including the negative correction when 0 < s < 1. The mean identity is exact, not an asymptotic differentiation. Finite G_0(m) propagates by G_(n+1)(m) ≤ G_n(m)^m and dominates every fixed ordinary power. Finally G_n(m) − 1 = Σ_(k≥1)(m^k − 1)p_(n,k) ≥ (m − 1)q_n. The cited convergence theorem applies with the standing nonconstant-law exclusion. Fix: none.

2. Lines 310–339, path bound: number sibling subtrees by their root heights h = 0,…,t−1; their laws are X_(n+h). For a fixed selected leaf, these subtrees use mutually disjoint sets of independent leaves, also disjoint from the selected leaf. This proves the product formula for P(E_v). On E_v its ancestor at height h has value X_n(v) − h ≥ t + 1 − h, so the root is positive. For distinct v,w with lowest common ancestor at height H, the child subtree containing w has root value zero on E_v and at least t + 1 − (H−1) > 0 on E_w. Thus the events are mutually exclusive. Summing over m^t leaves is correct; the events E_v are not being assumed independent. For t = 0 this is equality. Fix: none; replacing the product-formula sentence’s height index i with h would avoid its reuse as an absolute-time index, but the displayed product is correct.

3. Lines 341–367, infinite product: summability forces q_i → 0, so eventually −log(1−q_i) ≤ 2q_i. Each of the finitely many earlier factors is strictly positive by q_i < 1. Thus P_N > 0. A finite interval product is at least P_N because all omitted factors lie in (0,1]. Dividing the path bound gives T_n(k) ≤ P_N^(-1)m^(-(k−1))q_(n+k−1). A zero atom before N is irrelevant; a factor q_i = 1 at or after N would invalidate this division and is expressly excluded. Fix: none.

4. Lines 370–415, conditional law: survival of the next step means child sum at least 2. The exact recursion (3.4) therefore determines p_(n,1)/q_n → (m−1)/m when q_n → 0 and q_(n+1)/q_n → 1. For output k ≥ 1, the one-positive-child contribution is m(1−q_n)^(m−1)p_(n,k+1); the remainder is bounded by the probability of two or more positive children, at most binom(m,2)q_n². Division by q_n makes this error vanish. The time-shift factor q_(n+1)/q_n transfers the known mass limit at k to time n+1, giving the next mass divided by m. No mass escapes: the limiting geometric masses sum to one, and the finite-head/tail argument proves total variation convergence. Fix: none.

5. Lines 417–431, reverse implication: weak convergence on the positive integers implies convergence of the singleton mass at 1. Substitution into the exact survival identity gives ratio limit m − (m−1) = 1. The assumption q_n → 0 is used and cannot be silently removed. In Theorem 1.2 it follows from criticality and the cited theorem. Fix: none.

6. Lines 434–487, uniform tail: q_n ∼ An^(−b), b > 1, gives eventual positive q_n < 1, summability, and q_(n+t)/q_n ≤ 4(n/(n+t))^b ≤ 4 simultaneously for every future t ≥ 0. Consequently ν_n([k,∞)) ≤ 4B_N m^(-(k−1)), uniformly for n ≥ N. This is also an atom bound. For each fixed r > 0, Σ k^r m^(-(k−1)) converges, so its tails tend to zero uniformly; this proves uniform integrability even for 0 < r < 1. Termwise limits are therefore legitimate. For each fixed 1 < s < m the analogous dominating series has ratio s/m < 1, giving (m−1)s/(m−s). For 0 < s ≤ 1 the test function is bounded. No unproved interchange of a limit and an unbounded observable is used. Fix: none.

7. Lines 490–513, equivalence: (i) ⇒ (iii) uses Theorem 1.1 with A = 4/(m−1)², b = 2; its limit is A M_r(m) = c(r,m). Taking r = 1 proves (iii) ⇒ (ii). Conversely, μ_n ∼ Dn^(−2) implies μ_(n+1)/μ_n → 1, so mμ_n − μ_(n+1) ∼ (m−1)Dn^(−2). There is no cancellation of its leading term because m−1 > 0. The exact mean recursion then gives q_n → 0 and mq_n ∼ (m−1)Dn^(−2); D = 4m/(m−1)³ produces precisely A. Fix: none. The first-moment equivalence itself is elementary and the source already supplies its forward direction; it should not be advertised separately as a new discovery.

8. Lines 515–527 and 602–621, scope of Conjectures 2 and 4: the below-m generating-function conclusion follows from G_n(s)−1 = q_n(E[s^X_n | X_n>0]−1) and the limit m(s−1)/(m−s). This constant is negative when 0 < s < 1, correctly matching G_n(s)−1 < 0. The point s = 1 is excluded from an asymptotic equivalence with a nonzero comparison function. Theorem 1.2 asserts neither that Conjecture 2 fixes the survival constant nor that the endpoint Conjecture 4(b) follows. At s = m the limiting geometric exponential moment is infinite; finite truncation gives liminf ≥ (m−1)K for every K. Under the third tilted moment and the survival prediction, Theorem 3’s order-1/n bounds divided by q_n of order n^(−2) give order n for the conditional endpoint moment, not its exact constant. Fix: none.

9. Lines 538–567, corollary and its external input: `posted/survival_constant_main.tex`, Theorem 1.1 (lines 61–79), supplies exactly the asserted nonconstant critical law and common survival subsequence. Its construction is independently checkable: for k_j = 2^j, a_j = j^(−2)k_j^(−ρ), 1 ≤ ρ < 2, set A = Σ((m−1)k_j−1)a_j, T = Σm^(−k_j)a_j, c = 1/(A+T), P(X_0=0)=cA, P(X_0=k_j)=cm^(−k_j)a_j. Both normalizing sums are finite and positive. The two critical expectations agree because A+Σa_j = (m−1)Σk_ja_j; Σk_ja_j < ∞. At n_j = k_j−1, any leaf of size k_j forces root survival, so independence of the m^n_j leaves gives q_(n_j) ≥ 1−exp(−c/(m j²k_j^ρ)). Hence n_j²q_(n_j) grows at least as (c/m)k_j^(2−ρ)/j² → ∞. This works for every m ≥ 2. For every r > 0 and integer x ≥ 0, x^r ≥ 1_{x>0}, so the same one subsequence gives simultaneous divergence of all n_j²E[X_(n_j)^r]. No diagonal choice over uncountably many r is needed. G_0(m) < ∞ ensures every finite-time ordinary moment is finite. The construction’s third tilted moment diverges (Σk_j^(3−ρ)/j² = ∞). The corollary therefore has exactly the stated scope. Fix: none.
