Theorem 1.1 is a conditional theorem for the deterministic, integer-valued, m-ary Derrida–Retaux recursion, without a separate criticality assumption. If q_n ~ A n^(-b), A > 0, b > 1, then the positive conditional law converges in total variation to π_k = (m−1)m^(−k), every positive conditional moment converges to (m−1) Σ k^r m^(−k), and each conditional exponential moment with 1 < s < m converges to (m−1)s/(m−s).

Theorem 1.2 gives, in the nonconstant critical setting of arXiv:1705.04787v2, equivalence of Conjecture 1, the specified first-moment prediction in Conjecture 3, and the collection of all positive-moment predictions in Conjecture 3. Each implies Conjecture 2 and Conjecture 4(a). Conjecture 2 is equivalent in this setting to q_(n+1)/q_n → 1; no converse from that statement to the exact survival constant is asserted. There is no claimed equivalence with an arbitrary single moment of order other than 1.

Corollary 1.3 uses only Theorem 1.1 of the posted survival-constant paper and x^r ≥ 1_{x>0} on the nonnegative integers. For every m ≥ 2, one nonconstant critical initial law has limsup n² E[X_n^r] = ∞ for every real r > 0, along a common subsequence. All its ordinary positive moments are finite at each finite time. The sparse construction is not repeated.

## Re-derivation of the proofs

1. **Tree bound (Lemma 3.1).** For a specified depth-t leaf, require its value to be at least t+1 and each sibling subtree root along its ancestral path to equal zero. The underlying leaf sets of these sibling subtrees are pairwise disjoint, including at different heights. The probability of the event is T_n(t+1) times the product of p_(i,0)^(m−1). Two different selected leaves give incompatible events at their lowest common ancestor. Summing the m^t disjoint events gives the lower bound on q_(n+t), hence the upper bound on T_n(k). Independence is used within each event; independence between the events is neither asserted nor needed.
2. **Positive product (Corollary 3.2).** Eventual summability of q_i and eventual q_i < 1 give a strictly positive infinite product P_N. Every relevant finite product is at least P_N. This proves a tail estimate uniformly in both n and k; no early zero-atom hypothesis or monotonicity of q_n is inserted.
3. **Conditional masses (Lemma 3.3).** The zero-output event is exactly child sum zero or one. Dividing its identity by q_n gives p_(n,1)/q_n → (m−1)/m. Contributions to a positive output with at least two positive children have probability at most binom(m,2)q_n². Division by q_n and induction identify every limiting mass. Their sum is one; the finite-set/tail argument proves total variation convergence.
4. **Uniform integrability (Theorem 1.1).** The power asymptotic gives Σq_n < ∞ and q_(n+t)/q_n ≤ 4 for all sufficiently large n and all t ≥ 0. Thus ν_n([k,∞)) ≤ 4 B_N m^(−(k−1)). In particular, for each fixed r > 0, the conditional rth-moment tail beyond K is bounded uniformly in n by 4 B_N Σ_(k>K) k^r m^(−(k−1)), which tends to zero. This proves uniform integrability for noninteger orders, including 0 < r < 1. The same argument works for s^k precisely when 1 < s < m. Weak or total variation convergence alone is not used to justify unbounded moments.
5. **Mean converse (Theorem 1.2).** Re-derive μ_(n+1) = m μ_n − 1 + (1−q_n)^m from (S−1)^+ = S−1+1_{S=0}. If μ_n ~ D/n², then mμ_n−μ_(n+1) ~ (m−1)D/n², without any subtraction of two equal leading terms. Since 1−(1−q_n)^m ~ mq_n, this yields q_n ~ (m−1)D/(mn²). At D = 4m/(m−1)³ the constant is 4/(m−1)².
6. **Normalization, second calculation.** Σ_(k≥1) (m−1)m^(−k) = 1. Differentiating the geometric series gives Σ k m^(−k) = m/(m−1)², hence M_1(m) = m/(m−1). Multiplying M_r(m) by 4/(m−1)² gives exactly c(r,m), with no extra factor 1/m. Also (m−1)s/(m−s)−1 = m(s−1)/(m−s), which checks the factor m in Conjecture 4(a).
7. **Common subsequence (Corollary 1.3).** Choose the subsequence using only n²q_n. The same pointwise inequality then holds for every r > 0; there is no countable diagonal argument over moment orders. Finiteness at finite times follows independently from G_(n+1)(m) ≤ G_n(m)^m and x^r ≤ K_(r,m)m^x.
8. **Endpoint remark.** Truncation to k ≤ K in the conditional m-exponential moment yields liminf at least (m−1)K. This shows divergence, rather than a finite endpoint limit. Under the third tilted moment and the predicted survival asymptotic, the existing two-sided 1/n bounds for G_n(m)−1 give a conditional m-exponential moment of order n. No endpoint constant is deduced.

These derivations agree with the substantive proof conclusions of both input reports. No numerical experiment, law iteration, or simulation was run for this paper.
