## Statement and sources

Let $\lambda$ be uniform Haar probability on $G=\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb N}$, with coordinatewise multiplication, and write

$$
\mu_a=\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\delta_1+\frac{1-a}{2}\delta_{-1}\right)^{\otimes\mathbb N},
\qquad T_af(x)=\int f(xy)\,d\mu_a(y),\qquad 0<a<1.
$$

The question asks for one function $\phi_a$, independent of the nonnegative density $f$, such that $\lambda\{T_af\ge t\}\le\phi_a(t)$ and $t\phi_a(t)\to0$. On the finite cube, $\lambda_n(x)=2^{-n}$ and the kernel is $\mu_a^{(n)}$; the same bound must hold for every $n$. The bias belongs to the convolution kernel; the reference measure remains uniform.

The three-page prize list matches the weak statement in Section 4, PDF pp. 2–3, and offers **USD 1,000**. Its actual title is *How to make $7000*, and its directory timestamp is 18 August 2012. The separate *Regularization from L1 by convolution* note is timestamped 12 March 2016; Chen cites it as [Tal16]. Thus “2016 list, Some open problems” conflates source versions/titles but identifies the right problem. The separate note gives the stronger conjecture as Conjecture 6, p. 4, with a finite-cube example on pp. 3–4. The old list’s final stronger formula contains a defective logarithm; the note supplies the unambiguous $1/(t\sqrt{\log t})$ rate. These are historical prize terms; current payout availability was not established. [Prize list](https://michel.talagrand.net/prizes/prizes.pdf), [2016 note](https://michel.talagrand.net/prizes/convolution.pdf), [directory](https://michel.talagrand.net/prizes/).

## Proof of the requested infinite-product statement

Take the finite-cube theorem above as the literature input and set $K_a=\sqrt{(1+a)/(1-a)}$. Here is the complete passage to the infinite product and non-strict level sets.

Convolution is a positive contraction on $L^1(\lambda)$: Tonelli’s theorem and Haar invariance give

$$
\int_G\int_G |h(xy)|\,d\mu_a(y)\,d\lambda(x)=\|h\|_1.
$$

This also shows that changing an $L^1$ representative changes its convolution only on a null set. Thus $g=T_af$ is well defined almost everywhere, nonnegative, and has integral one.

Let $\mathcal F_m$ be generated by the first $m$ coordinates and put $f_m=\mathbb E_\lambda[f\mid\mathcal F_m]$. These are nonnegative densities and $f_m\to f$ in $L^1$, since the coordinate sigma fields generate the product sigma field. Each $f_m$ is a density on the $m$-cube, and its convolution uses exactly $\mu_a^{(m)}$. Consequently,

$$
\|T_af_m-g\|_1\le\|f_m-f\|_1\longrightarrow0.
$$

Pass to a subsequence with almost sure convergence. Fix $t>1$ and $1<r<t$. Wherever $g\ge t$, eventually $T_af_m>r$. Fatou’s lemma and the finite theorem imply

$$
\lambda\{g\ge t\}
\le\liminf_m\lambda\{T_af_m>r\}
\le\frac{K_a}{r\sqrt{\log r}}.
$$

Letting $r\uparrow t$ gives

$$
\boxed{\lambda\{f*\mu_a\ge t\}\le\frac{K_a}{t\sqrt{\log t}},\qquad t>1.}
$$

For every threshold, choose

$$
\phi_a(t)=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le t\le1,\\[2pt]
\min\left\{1,\dfrac1t,\dfrac{K_a}{t\sqrt{\log t}}\right\},&t>1.
\end{cases}
$$

The other bounds are the probability bound and Markov’s inequality. This function is independent of $f$ and dimension, and $t\phi_a(t)\le K_a/\sqrt{\log t}\to0$. Conversely, the infinite-product bound applies to cylinder densities, giving the finite version uniformly in dimension.

For probability $p<1/2$ of $+1$, reflection reduces to $a=|2p-1|$: negative-bias convolution is positive-bias convolution followed by $x\mapsto-x$, preserving Haar measure. Thus an explicit sufficient constant is

$$
K_a=\sqrt{\frac{\max(p,1-p)}{\min(p,1-p)}}.
$$

For $p=1/2$, convolution is identically one. Deterministic coins $p=0,1$ are excluded: they give a translation, allowing equality in Markov’s bound on arbitrarily small cylinder sets. The displayed constant is sufficient, without asserting numerical optimality.

## Gaussian analogue and the former obstruction

For $s>0$, define

$$
Q_sf(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(e^{-s}x+\sqrt{1-e^{-2s}}y)\,d\gamma_n(y).
$$

The Gaussian question replaces $T_a,\lambda_n$ by $Q_s,\gamma_n$ and likewise requires dimension-free improved tails.

- Ball, Barthe, Bednorz, Oleszkiewicz and Wolff, *L1-smoothing for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup*, **Mathematika 59 (2013), 160–168**, DOI 10.1112/S0025579312001064: author draft, Theorem 4, p. 4, gives $C_{n,s}(\log\log t)/(t\sqrt{\log t})$. The constant depends on dimension. The saved seven-page draft does not have journal pagination. [Author draft](https://keithmball.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/12/2012-01-26-gaussian.pdf), [publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/abs/l1smoothing-for-the-ornsteinuhlenbeck-semigroup/3B03A74E7A46B0242A19009A76CFBC43).
- Eldan and Lee, *Regularization under diffusion and anticoncentration of the information content*, **Duke Math. J. 167(5) (2018), 969–993**, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2017-0048: final author version arXiv:1410.3887v3, Theorem 1.2, p. 4, and Corollary 1.4, p. 5, give $C_s\sqrt{\log\log t}/(t\sqrt{\log t})$. The fourth-power loss quoted in Lehec’s earlier abstract refers to an earlier version. [Final version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.3887v3), [author’s record](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jrl/pubs-entropy.html).
- Lehec, *Regularization in L1 for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup*, **Ann. Fac. Sci. Toulouse Math. (6) 25(1) (2016), 191–204**, DOI 10.5802/afst.1492: **Theorem 2.1, printed p. 193** (PDF page 4 including cover), gives $C\max(1,s^{-1})/(t\sqrt{\log t})$. Lemma 2.2 there provides the log-Hessian bound; Theorem 2.3, p. 194, is the semilog-convex density inequality. [Published article](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/afst.1492.pdf).

The old Gaussian route uses smooth log densities, a lower Hessian bound, Brownian/Föllmer drift, and perturbations controlled by stochastic change of measure. A cube walk jumps across edges, with

$$
L\log h(x)=\frac12\sum_i\log\frac{h(x^{(i)})}{h(x)}.
$$

Substituting finite differences into a diffusion chain rule does not give the same identity: a jump from $h=2$ to $h=1$ has log increment $-\log2$, whereas its relative linear increment is $-1/2$. Also, a Gaussian deviation theorem integrates against Gaussian measure, not the atomic measure on cube vertices. A continuous extension alone does not change that measure. These observations explain the lack of a direct transfer; they do not rule out discrete stochastic methods.

Chen perturbs reverse jump rates and controls terminal-law discrepancy. Lu–Guo–Fang use a power coupling; Shaposhnikov uses a stopped coordinate likelihood martingale and an edge identity. The obstruction has now been overcome, and the cube rate is the full $1/(t\sqrt{\log t})$. [Chen, §§1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19374v2), [Lu–Guo–Fang, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15515v1), [Shaposhnikov, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11290v2).

## Checks and stopping decision

The decisive rate was checked in the primary statements of Chen, Lu–Guo–Fang and Shaposhnikov, and against Talagrand’s updated account. Parameter matching, uniformity, normalization, nonnegative densities, strict/non-strict tails and the infinite-product passage were checked separately. Page markers and received-document hashes are in the source texts; the theorem-location audit is in data/literature_checks.json.

An independent magnitude check uses a normalized point mass. For $f=2^n\mathbf1_{\{(1,\ldots,1)\}}$, the convolution at a vertex with $k$ plus signs is $t_n=(1+a)^k(1-a)^{n-k}$. With $p=(1+a)/2$ and $k=\lfloor np\rfloor$,

$$
t_n\lambda_n\{T_af\ge t_n\}
\ge {n\choose k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\asymp_a n^{-1/2},
\qquad \log t_n=nD(p\Vert1/2)+O_a(1).
$$

Stirling’s formula gives this comparison and $D(p\Vert1/2)>0$. Thus along this sequence the order is $1/\sqrt{\log t_n}$, agreeing with the upper bound and Talagrand’s Proposition 5 in the 2016 note, pp. 3–4. This is an analytic check, not a numerical extremizer claim.
