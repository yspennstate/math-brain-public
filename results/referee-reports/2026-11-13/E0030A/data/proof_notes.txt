E0030: checked partial results, before computation

Notation: T_n is the minimum closed tour length, with T(empty)=T(singleton)=0
and T({x,y})=2 dist(x,y). Distances are either Euclidean on the unit square
or geodesic on the flat unit two-torus. All statements here use true optima.

1. Insertion: for any nonempty A, 0 <= T(A+x)-T(A) <= 2 dist(x,A).
Lower bound: remove x from a tour and shortcut by the triangle inequality.
Upper bound: insert x next to a nearest point y, replacing an incident edge yz
by yx,xz. The increase <= 2 dist(x,y). The singleton case follows directly.

2. Let R be the distance from an independent uniform x to m uniform points.
For the square, S=min_j ||x-X_j||_infinity satisfies
 P(S>r | x) <= (1-r^2)^m, 0<=r<=1,
because each coordinate interval cut out by an infinity ball has length >=r.
Thus E R^2 <= 2 E S^2 <= 2/(m+1).
For the torus, the infinity ball has area 4r^2, 0<=r<=1/2, so
 E S^2=1/[4(m+1)] and E R^2 <= 1/[2(m+1)].

3. Efron-Stein: delete point i, call the remaining set A, and write
 delta=T(A+X_i)-T(A), delta'=T(A+X_i')-T(A).
Conditional on A, these are iid and nonnegative. Hence
 E[(delta-delta')^2] <= 2 E[delta^2] <= 8 E R^2.
Applying the 1/2 factor in Efron-Stein gives Var(T_n)<=8 (square), <=2
(torus), n>=2; n=0,1 have zero variance.

4. Under the natural nested coupling T_{n+1}=T_n+Delta_n, insertion gives
 E Delta_n^2 <= K/(n+1), where K=8 (square) or 2 (torus).
Reverse triangle inequality in centered L2 gives
 |sqrt(Var T_{n+1})-sqrt(Var T_n)| <= sqrt(K/(n+1)),
so |Var T_{n+1}-Var T_n| <= 2K/sqrt(n+1).
The bounded sequence of variances has subsequences converging to its liminf
and limsup. Since consecutive differences tend to zero, its cluster set is the
whole closed interval between them (cross any interior value infinitely often;
at a crossing the distance from that value is at most the step size).
In the square the known fluctuation lower bound makes the liminf positive.
This neither proves the interval is a singleton nor asserts a torus lower bound.

5. Conditional Poisson block criterion (all d>=2). Let P be rate-one Poisson
in Q_L=[0,L]^d. Let H_L be the global TSP cost, with cube or torus distance.
For each integer r>=1 partition Q_L into floor(L/r)^d side-r cubes and the
remaining rectangular cells, and let B_{L,r} be the SUM of independent
Euclidean TSP costs inside these cells. (This is a surrogate, not a single tour.)
For D_x F(P)=F(P+x)-F(P), assume
 e_r^2 := limsup_{L->infinity} L^{-d} integral_{Q_L}
          E[(D_x H_L-D_x B_{L,r})^2] dx ->0 as r->infinity.
This is an explicit unproved L2 locality hypothesis for the true TSP.

Conclusion: lim_{L->infinity} Var(H_L)/L^d exists and is finite, and equals
 lim_{r->infinity} Var(T(P intersect [0,r]^d))/r^d.

Proof: Poisson Poincare gives
 Var(H_L-B_{L,r}) <= integral E[(D_x H_L-D_x B_{L,r})^2] dx.
For fixed r, independence of Poisson restrictions implies
 lim_{L->infinity} Var(B_{L,r})/L^d = a_r,
 a_r=Var(T(P intersect [0,r]^d))/r^d.
To check rectangular remainders, their total volume is O_d(r L^{d-1});
each has diameter <=sqrt(d)r. Its add-one cost is <=2 sqrt(d)r, so by
Poincare the total remainder variance is <=4 d r^2 times that volume,
which is o(L^d) for fixed r. Empty/singleton conventions preserve this bound.
Reverse triangle inequality now gives
 limsup_L |sqrt(Var H_L/L^d)-sqrt(a_r)| <= e_r.
Compare r,s through the same L: |sqrt(a_r)-sqrt(a_s)|<=e_r+e_s.
Thus sqrt(a_r) is Cauchy. Its finite limit and the preceding bound prove
the conclusion for all real L->infinity, not only a divisible subsequence.

Poisson Poincare used here can be proved from Efron-Stein without a
stabilization assumption. At fixed bounded Q divide it into m equal-volume
cells and independently replace one Poisson restriction at a time. In the
1/2 sum of squared replacements, the leading events have a single point
in exactly one of the old/new restrictions. Their two equal contributions
give integral E[(D_x F)^2]dx in the mesh limit. If |D_x F|<=C deterministically,
events with at least two old/new points have total contribution O(m*(|Q|/m)^2)
and vanish. Deleting a shrinking cell changes the Palm integrand by o(1),
by coupling and boundedness. This proves the inequality for every functional
used above, whose add-one costs are deterministically bounded at fixed L,r.

Scaling: H_L has law L*T(P_{L^d}) on the unit cube or unit torus. Therefore
 Var(H_L)/L^d = n^{2/d-1} Var(T(P_n)), n=L^d.
This conditional theorem addresses Poisson input only. Binomial variance
needs a de-Poissonization hypothesis as well; Poisson convergence alone does
not imply the binomial assertion.

6. Conditional de-Poissonization, stated precisely for the plane. Let
N_n~Poisson(n) independent of an iid point sequence, and suppose constants
a and sigma_P^2 satisfy Var(T_{N_n})->sigma_P^2 and
 || (T_{N_n}-T_n-a*(N_n-n)/sqrt(n)) -
    E[T_{N_n}-T_n-a*(N_n-n)/sqrt(n)] ||_2 ->0.
Then Var(T_n)->sigma_P^2-a^2.
Indeed T_n and N_n are independent. Center the displayed identity; the two
main terms are orthogonal, with variances Var(T_n) and a^2. The error has
L2 norm o(1). Var(T_n) is uniformly bounded by step 3, so all error
covariances vanish by Cauchy-Schwarz. No such approximation is proved here.

The same formula works in d dimensions with n^{1/d-1/2}T costs and a fixed
coefficient of (N_n-n)/sqrt(n), if the corresponding centered L2 remainder
vanishes and the scaled binomial variances are bounded. The plane statement
is all that is used in the report.

7. A sufficient stabilization condition for the block criterion is explicit.
For the cube model, suppose the rate-one whole-space Poisson process admits
an a.s. finite add-one stabilization radius R at the origin: once P inside
the Euclidean ball of radius R is fixed, D_0 T(S) has the same value for every
finite completion S of those interior points outside the ball. Translate this
condition to each deterministic x. Then global and block add-one costs agree
whenever x is at distance >=s from its cell boundary and the translated R<=s.

The requisite higher moments are automatic for ordinary TSP insertion costs.
For a rate-one Poisson process in a side-r cube, put S=min infinity-distance
from x to a point, assigning S=r if the process is empty. For 0<=t<r,
 P(S>t)<=exp(-t^d), because the intersection infinity ball has volume >=t^d.
For every p>0, E S^p <= Gamma(1+p/d), uniformly in r and x. Euclidean nearest
distance is <=sqrt(d) S, and |D_x T|<=2sqrt(d)S (also when the process is empty).
Thus E|D_x T|^p <= (2sqrt(d))^p Gamma(1+p/d), uniformly in r,x. The torus has
the analogous uniform bound, with infinity ball volume (2t)^d, t<r/2.

Take p>2 and theta=1-2/p. Holder and these moments imply that the average
gradient error in step 5 is <=C_{d,p} [s/r+P(R>s)^theta], apart from a remainder
that vanishes as L->infinity for fixed r,s. The boundary fraction estimate is
at most 2ds/r for s<=r/2. First let r->infinity with s=sqrt(r), then use R<infinity
a.s. This proves the block criterion from the stated stabilization property
for the Euclidean cube functional. No tail rate is needed for this implication.
For the periodic global functional one must additionally assume compatibility
with periodic completion: its add-one cost agrees with the same stabilized
local cost off the radius event. Euclidean strong stabilization alone is not
silently asserted to imply that compatibility.

Alternative remainder check in step 5: in a rectangular cell of diameter
<=sqrt(d)r and volume v<=r^d, T<=sqrt(d)r N, N~Poisson(v). Therefore
 Var(T)<=d r^2 (v^2+v)<=d r^2(r^d+1)v. Summing over independent remainder
cells gives o(L^d) directly, agreeing with the Poincare bound.

Failure of the tempting proof: a deterministic boundary patching error or a
relative error o(E T_n) does not control its centered L2 norm on the constant
fluctuation scale. Removing the boundary by periodic distance does not prove
locality of the optimizer. Short incident edges do not rule out global changes
of the Hamiltonian cycle. No claim of nonexistence of a stabilizing radius is
made; what is missing is a proof of a radius, or an adequate weaker L2 property.
