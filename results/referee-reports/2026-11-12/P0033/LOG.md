## 3. E, G, C and Lemma [lem:colour]

- CORRECT, definition line 117: E requires existence of the binary limit, and G imposes that requirement at every actual descendant. Taking u to be the empty word also imposes it at the root.
- CORRECT, (a), lines 121 and 128: each q_n is locally constant on T_23. Existence of a limit and membership of that limit in {0,1} are Borel conditions. Presence of a fixed word is clopen; taking its descendant tree is continuous on that domain. The countable intersection defining G is therefore Borel. The limit map is Borel on G. Permuting children changes neither q_n nor the existence of any of these descendant limits. Finally (t^u)^w=t^(uw) proves closure under all subtrees.
- CORRECT, (b), lines 122 and 130: bounded martingale convergence gives q_n -> Q almost surely. The displayed series estimate is sum_n d_n/2 <= (1/4)/(1-9/10)=5/2. Tonelli makes sum_n q_n(1-q_n) finite almost surely, hence its summands tend to zero and Q is binary. An alternative, requiring only d_n -> 0, is bounded convergence: E[2Q(1-Q)]=lim d_n=0. Nonnegativity then gives Q(1-Q)=0 almost surely. Thus there is no missing passage from mean disagreement to a binary limit.
- CORRECT, remaining proof of (b), line 130: membership u in T depends only on strict ancestors' offspring, independently of all offspring in T^u, so its conditional descendant law is GW. Each bad-descendant event has probability zero; countably many words suffice. Bounded convergence gives E Q=1/2 and therefore GW(C=1)=1/2.
- CORRECT, (c), lines 123 and 132: for every individual t in G, all finitely many child limits exist and are binary. Polynomial continuity permits passing to the limit in the recursion. Continuity includes the boundary values 0 and 1; this is a pointwise statement on G, not merely another almost-sure assertion.

## 4. Flow, [lem:cond], and [thm:main]

- CORRECT, line 137: S is nonempty in all four cases. AND=1 requires both children 1; AND=0 requires at least one 0; OR=1 requires at least one 1; OR=0 requires all three 0. Each t in G has only infinite descendant trees. Outside G an infinite locally finite rooted tree has an infinite child subtree, since a finite union of finite trees is finite.
- CORRECT, display lines 138–140 and conservation step line 141: positive flow only reaches infinite descendant trees, where sigma is defined and has total mass 1. Zero-flow vertices and their children have flow zero, so conservation holds there too. Hence this is a unit flow on every infinite tree, including trees with leaves and finite branches.
- CORRECT, consistency step line 141: for a positive-flow child x, the product of local sigma factors along xw is exactly Theta_t(x) times the product along w in t^x. This remains true if t is outside G but t^x is inside G: the same sigma_(t^x) is used by both products. No hereditary property of the complement of G is needed.
- CORRECT, equivariance step line 141: isomorphisms preserve G, its colours, and finiteness of descendant trees, hence S and sigma at every vertex. Induction gives equivariance of the whole flow, including the fallback.
- CORRECT, Borel step line 141: in addition to Lemma [lem:colour](a), one uses that an infinite descendant tree is a Borel condition (it has a vertex at every depth). The finite sums defining |S| and the finite path products are Borel on their domains. Coordinates for absent vertices may be set to zero. The paper omits these routine details but supplies enough for this step; no missing mathematical hypothesis is needed.
- CORRECT, [eq:kernel], lines 142–144: the first-step weights are exactly sigma_t.
- CORRECT, line 146: B and G_0 have mass 1/2, and conditioning on B^c or G_0 gives the same law since their difference is GW-null.
- CORRECT, [lem:cond], statement line 149 and proof line 153: conditional on K the child trees are independent GW. Each child's colour is a function of that child tree with probabilities 1/2. Product conditioning gives probability p_k 2^(-k) for each ordered colour vector and independent conditional laws GW_(c_i). One may disregard the null complement of G throughout.

Four explicit cases for stationarity, conditioning on the root colour r (N is the number of selected children):

| r | K | possible colour vectors | N and probability under GW_r | selected subtree law |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 11 | N=2: 3/10 | GW_1 |
| 1 | 3 | 001,010,100; 011,101,110; 111 | N=1: 3/10; N=2: 3/10; N=3: 1/10 | GW_1 |

These are joint conditional masses for (K,N), not probabilities additionally conditioned on K. The entries sum to 1 for each r. Their unconditional masses are half these numbers. For each fixed vector and each Borel A, the conditional expectation of the average of the N selected indicators is N*GW_r(A)/N=GW_r(A).

- CORRECT, theorem (i), line 157 and display lines 158–160: this conditional expectation is exactly as above; choosing among matching colours biases the colour but does not bias the subtree further once its colour is specified.
- CORRECT, display [eq:statr], lines 161–165: summing over all vectors producing r gives GW_r P=GW_r; averaging the two stationary laws gives GW P=GW. This proves stationarity on all Borel sets, including ordered-tree cylinder events.
- CORRECT, theorem (ii), line 167: positive transitions preserve G and its root colour, hence B. It has probability 1/2. Equivalence transfers invariance by mu<<GW and transfers positivity of both B and B^c by GW<<mu. Stationarity of mu then gives nonergodicity in the stationary sense.
- CORRECT, theorem (iii), line 169, restriction step: A'=A intersect G'_r agrees with A under GW_r; transitions from G'_r remain there, so A' is invariant modulo GW_r. A finite average of zero-one indicators with strictly positive weights can equal zero or one only when all the indicators equal that value. Therefore every selected child and its parent have the same A' indicator, almost surely.
- CORRECT, line 169, event D: for either r, AND(r,r)=r; given D both children are selected with weight 1/2. P_GW_r(D)=(3/5)(1/4)/(1/2)=3/10>0. GW_r-null exceptional trees remain null under this conditioning. Lemma [lem:cond] gives *independent* GW_r child trees under D.
- CORRECT, display lines 170–172: if alpha=GW_r(A'), independent children give probability alpha^2 that both belong and (1-alpha)^2 that neither belongs.
