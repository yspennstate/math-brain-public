The setting: the trace-reinforced ant process of Kious, Mailler and Schapira on a finite connected graph G with nest N and food F. Ant n+1 performs a random walk from N, weighted by the current edge weights W(n), until it hits F, and every edge it crossed gets one unit of weight. The paper addresses KMS Conjecture 1.1 (W_e(n)/n converges almost surely to a deterministic chi_e, and chi_e > 0 for every e when dist(N,F) >= 2). It claims:
- Theorem (Dead branches): if u is a neighbour of F and H, a subset of V minus {N,F,u}, is joined to V minus (H union {F}) by exactly one edge {u,y}, then W_f(n)/n -> 0 almost surely for every edge f with an endpoint in H (u may be N; no adjacency of N and F is assumed). Examples: the paw graph and the 4-cycle N,a,F,b plus y joined to a and F (2-connected, distance 2).
- Theorem (Shadowed neighbours): for a reduced triple (G - F connected) with N not adjacent to F, if some neighbour x of F has no neighbour in I_0, the component of N in G - F - Gamma(F), then the mean-field map p (p_e(w) = probability the w-walk from N crosses e before F) has no equilibrium with all coordinates positive, so W(n)/n converges to an all-positive limit with probability zero. Examples: K_{3,3} plus an edge (3-connected) and the 4-cycle plus c joined to a, b, F, with the edge {c,F} dying and {a,c}, {b,c} at 0.3209.
