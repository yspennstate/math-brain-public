Overall verdict: SOUND WITH GAPS

The two main theorems and the theta proposition survive this audit. In particular, the dead-branch proof is an almost-sure proof, not an argument about the mean-field flow. It refutes positivity in KMS Conjecture 1.1 even on reduced, 2-connected graphs at distance two. I found no counterexample to the corrected conjecture in 320 independently generated cases. This verdict does require corrections: the boundary-stability discussion makes unjustified generalizations from single-edge perturbations, some boundary notation is undefined, and the simulation discussion refers to limiting weights which the paper has not proved to be limits.

I used only the local manuscript, code and data. I did not browse, use other agents, push, or change files outside this directory. Programs below are independent implementations; none imports or executes the authors' programs. The bibliographic comparisons below consequently distinguish internal consistency from verification against unavailable sources.

## 1. Conjecture 1 and the reduction paragraph — SOUND as a quoted target; ERROR in the literal reduction sentence

Lines 60–63 state the target supplied for this audit: deterministic almost-sure convergence, followed by positivity at distance at least two. These are separate assertions. The paper disproves the second, and does not prove or disprove the first in general.

The reduction intended in lines 72–74 is correct. Let R be the component of N in G−F. An ant stays in R until its final step into F. The graph to retain is the subgraph induced by R∪{F}; all other edges are never reinforced, retain initial weight 1, and have normalized weight tending to zero. Excising them changes none of the transition probabilities at vertices visited before F. The retained triple is reduced.

The literal wording, “an edge with an endpoint outside the connected component of N in G−F is never crossed,” is false: F itself is outside that component. On the path N−a−F, the edge aF is crossed by every ant. Replace “outside [that component]” by “outside R∪{F}.” Also distinguish the conjecture as actually quoted from the additional reduced interpretation: without reduction the positivity assertion has immediate, uninteresting counterexamples. The paper's examples do not rely on this issue.

The assertion that the *original* authors implicitly intended reduction cannot be checked from the source files provided. State the reduction as the present paper's convention unless the original source explicitly imposes it.

## 2. Theorem 2, Dead branches — SOUND

I checked all three steps, including u=N, multiple excursions into H, and nonconnected choices of H.

**Step 0, lines 134–137.** To enter H before absorption an ant must cross uy. Edges from H to F cannot provide another entrance, because hitting F ends the walk. Thus the indicator for crossing any edge incident to H is bounded by the indicator for crossing uy. Starting all weights at 1 gives W_f≤X pathwise. Components of H accessible only through F are simply never visited; they cause no exception.

**Step 1, lines 139–166.** Conditional on the frozen weights, use the strong Markov property at successive returns to u. Write w_u=A+X+R and S=A+R(1−ρ′). Summing return excursions before the first crossing of e gives

    p_e = h X/(X+S).

For a, returns can occur both through H and through the other incident edges. Summing all such returns gives

    p_a = h A/[S+X(1−ρ)].

Thus the second formula, unlike the first, explicitly incorporates arbitrarily many entries into H. There is no assumption of at most one entry per ant. “Successive departures are independent” should be understood as the stopped sequence of excursions generated by the strong Markov property; the geometric calculation is valid. If R=0, the unused conditional probability ρ′ can be assigned arbitrarily, with R(1−ρ′)=0.

The displayed difference in line 159 is exact. Its lower bound follows from S+X(1−ρ)≤S+X. At y, every incident edge has weight at most X by Step 0, including yF if present. Therefore ρ≥X/w_y≥1/deg(y). Together with w_u≤deg(u)(n+1), this gives precisely the drift inequality displayed in lines 164–166. No positive lower bound on h is needed.

When u=N, h=1 and the first hitting time is time zero. All the excursion formulas and bounds remain valid.

**Step 2, lines 168–185.** This is a genuine convergent martingale argument. Put ΔΛ=D log(1+1/X)−D′ log(1+1/A). The logarithm inequalities give the stated conditional drift. Since the two logarithms are nonnegative,

    (ΔΛ)² ≤ D/X² + D′/A².

The pathwise sums of the right-hand terms are each at most Σk≥1 k⁻²: an increment consumes the current integer weight exactly once. Taking expectations proves summability of the martingale-difference variances and hence L²-boundedness and almost-sure convergence of M. Independence of D and D′ is unnecessary; they can both equal 1. Tonelli and the same increment-counting identity make Q finite almost surely. Equation (L) follows, with Λ₀=0.

**Step 3, lines 187–198.** The martingale with differences (D−p_e)/(n+1) has summable variance, proving the equivalence of divergent sums. The summation-by-parts identity is correct. Infinitely many times X_m≥δm yield infinitely many disjoint intervals [m,2m] with a uniformly positive contribution to the sum; choose successive m beyond the preceding doubled endpoint. Consequently, on {limsup X_n/n>0}, equation (L) implies X_n/A_n→0. Since A_n≤n+1, this implies X_n/n→0, contradicting that event. One can formalize the choice of δ using positive rational δ, a countable union. Step 0 gives the conclusion for all remaining edges.

Neither a stochastic-approximation theorem nor convergence of the full weight vector is needed here. I find no missing probabilistic step.

## 3. Example 3, paw — SOUND

The graph is reduced and dist(N,F)=2. With u=x and H={y}, Theorem 2 gives both asserted zeros. Excursions to the pendant nest can be erased without changing the trace on the remaining triangle, so the alternative reduction is valid at the level of the induced ant process. The precise identification with KMS Theorem 1.3 cannot be checked without that source, but the present theorem independently proves the example.

The natural attracting boundary equilibrium is Nx=xF=1 and xy=yF=0. This does not mean it is the only equilibrium: for example Nx=xy=yF=1, xF=0 is another, unstable, equilibrium. Throughout this report, numerical “equilibrium” values refer to the equilibria approached from the tested positive starts, not a classification of every boundary equilibrium.

## 4. Example 4, the 4-cycle with y — SOUND

The graph is reduced, has vertex connectivity 2, and has nest–food distance 2. Each edge lies on some simple N–F path. Deleting ay in G−F isolates y, so Theorem 2 applies exactly as stated. Both ay and yF have normalized weight tending to zero almost surely.

The boundary equilibrium on the remaining cycle has Na=Nb=1/√2 and aF=bF=1/2. The paper has not, by this observation alone, proved convergence of the surviving weights on the *augmented* graph. Theorem 2 only proves convergence of its dead coordinates. The reference to “their limits” at line 319 needs qualification or an additional robustness/convergence argument.

## 5. Theorem 5, Shadowed neighbours — SOUND

For an all-positive equilibrium on a reduced graph, every vertex other than F is reachable before F, V₁=Γ(F), and Proposition 8(a) fixes every food-neighbour voltage at 1. Proposition 8(c) supplies, for each such neighbour x, a support neighbour z with v(z)>1 and a path from z to N entirely in {v>1}. That set avoids both F and Γ(F), so z belongs to I₀. A shadowed neighbour has no such z: contradiction. This excludes *every* all-positive equilibrium, without symmetry assumptions or a restriction to attracting equilibria.

The second statement is also proved in the manuscript, at lines 256–260. A precise result giving the required implication is the strong law for bounded martingale differences. For each edge let

    ξ_{j+1} = 1{ant j+1 crosses e} − p_e(W(j)).

The series Σ ξ_{j+1}/(j+1) converges almost surely by L² martingale convergence; Kronecker's lemma then gives n⁻¹Σ_{j<n}ξ_{j+1}→0. If W(n)/n→χ>0, homogeneity and continuity imply p(W(j))=p(W(j)/j)→p(χ). Cesàro averaging of the reinforcement identity gives χ=p(χ), impossible. The j=0 term is irrelevant.

Here p is smooth on the positive orthant: the finite killed walk has an invertible transient linear system. On the proposed positive-limit event, the normalized vector is eventually in a compact neighbourhood of χ inside that orthant. Thus the support/domain concern does not invalidate this argument. There is no need to invoke a global Benaïm theorem or to assume global regularity across changing supports. The stochastic-approximation recursion is explicitly

    x_{n+1}−x_n = [p(x_n)−x_n+ξ_{n+1}]/(n+1),  x_n=W(n)/n,

with bounded noise and square-summable step sizes. The elementary martingale proof supplied is sufficient and more specific than the general citations.

The two named shadow examples have the stated connectivity and distance. In the K₃,₃ example, Γ(F)={a,b,c,x} and I₀={N}; x is indeed shadowed. In the five-vertex example, I₀={N} and c is shadowed. The theorem does **not** identify cF or xF as an almost-sure dead edge of the process; it excludes convergence to an all-positive vector. The manuscript correctly labels those specific zero sets as mean-field observations.

## 6. Remark 6, a triangle attached by two edges — SOUND; ERROR in the later general stability inference

For a concrete verification, take edges Nu and uF of weight 1 and a triangle uy, uz, yz of weight s. Exact calculation gives

    p_uy=p_uz=4s/(3+4s),   p_yz=s/(1+s),   p_uF=1.

Therefore p_uy/s→4/3>p_uF/1 as s↓0. The claimed failure of the one-edge bridge mechanism is real. The positive equilibrium has uy=uz=0.224744871391589 and yz=0.155051025721682.

However, lines 262–268 overstate what single-edge rates establish. At the equilibrium with only Nu=uF=1 positive, perturbing uy **alone** gives p_uy=s/(1+s), with linear rate 1; perturbing uz alone has the same rate, and perturbing yz alone gives rate 0. Perturbing all three together gives the formula above and outward drift s/3+O(s²) on the attachments. In particular p is not jointly differentiable at this boundary point. Individual coordinate derivatives do not constitute a linear stability analysis there.

The two first-order formulas in lines 263–264 are valid for the stated configurations, with the visited endpoint different from F when the other endpoint is unvisited. They do not cover all zero edges. On the dead paw equilibrium, adding weight s to yF alone gives p_yF=0, because y cannot be reached before absorption; the scalar flow is s′=−s. Edges with both endpoints unvisited similarly need separate treatment. The phrase “not repelled only when v(x)=1” must not be applied to these configurations, where v(x) may not even have been defined.

Finally, linear rate 1 does not by itself prove a 1/t decay law for the coupled flow. That requires a second-order calculation and a nonzero coefficient on the relevant invariant/centre manifold. Theorem 2 proves no rate. The rate discussion at lines 321–323 is explicitly heuristic and is acceptable as a heuristic; the categorical wording at lines 266–268 is not. These problems do not enter either theorem's proof.

## 7. Lemma 7, crossings and voltages — SOUND on defined endpoints; GAP in the boundary convention

For a nonfood vertex x, occupation counting gives E[#(x→z)]=g(x)w_xz/w_x=w_xz v(x). Summing the two directions gives the identity. The indicator of at least one crossing is bounded by the number of crossings. An edge incident to F can be crossed only once, in the final step, so its expected crossing count equals its crossing probability. These prove every assertion.

There is a small domain gap at lines 208–217: v(x) is defined only if w_x>0, but the lemma quantifies over edges with zero weight whose endpoints can have w_x=0. Define v=0 at all unvisited vertices, including isolated vertices, and restrict Kirchhoff equations as the text already does. Occupation identities then hold on every edge, with zero terms interpreted literally. On positive-weight components unreachable before F, g=0 and v=0 already. This fixes the displayed expressions without changing the theorem arguments.

The voltage is the grounded solution on the visited component, not an unqualified unique voltage on disconnected components: a network component disconnected from N and F admits arbitrary additive constants in Kirchhoff's equations. The occupation definition selects zero there.

## 8. Proposition 8, items (a)–(d) — SOUND with the preceding convention and exclusion of F

At an equilibrium, a positive edge cannot be unvisited: p_e=0 would contradict w_e=p_e>0. Thus all positive support edges lie in the visited component together with the terminal vertex F.

**(a), SOUND.** Divide w_xF=p_xF=w_xF v(x) by the positive weight. Every member of V₁ is visited.

**(b), SOUND.** On visited nonfood vertices outside V₁ and N, Kirchhoff's law is the weighted mean-value property. A minimum below 1 propagates along positive edges; it cannot terminate at V₁, where voltage is 1. At N, the source equation prevents a local minimum, since its weighted sum of differences is +1. This supplies the source-vertex detail suppressed by the minimum-propagation wording in lines 236–239.

**(c), SOUND.** For x∈V₁, x≠N,

    Σ_{z≠F} w_xz(v(z)−1) = w_xF > 0.

Some neighbour therefore has voltage greater than 1. For t>1, a connected component of the superlevel set {v≥t} cannot avoid N: it avoids V₁, is harmonic, and a maximum would propagate through the whole support component. That component would have no connection to its lower-voltage boundary, contradicting connectivity to the source and food. This justifies the path assertion; the path need not have strictly monotone voltage, only voltage everywhere greater than 1.

**(d), SOUND with a wording qualification.** The maximum principle gives v≤v(N)=1 on visited nonfood vertices, while (b) gives v≥1. Kirchhoff at N then gives w_NF=1, and Kirchhoff at any other food neighbour forces its food-edge weight to vanish. “v≡1 on the visited vertices” must mean vertices visited *before* absorption, excluding F, since v(F)=0. Say so explicitly.

## 9. Conjecture 9 and the mean-field census — SOUND as conjecture and numerical evidence, with the limits stated below

The definitions of I₀, the front A, shadow, D1 and D2 are unambiguous on a reduced graph. D2 extends beyond Theorem 2, since its attachment u may be a shadow vertex which is not adjacent to F. That extension is conjectural. D1 for nonadjacent nests is likewise not proved edge by edge by Theorem 5. The manuscript mostly maintains these distinctions.

For nonreduced triples, first retain R∪{F} and account separately for never-crossed edges. Apply D only on that reduced graph. This is what the numerical comparison in this report does.

I independently generated 320 connected marked graphs with NetworkX, using sizes 4–7, random edge probabilities in [0.17,0.85], seed 20261112, and no authors' census cases as inputs. These are distinct up to graph isomorphism preserving N and F, checked using marked node attributes and exact isomorphism tests. There are 22, 71, 103 and 124 cases of sizes 4, 5, 6 and 7, respectively; 288 are reduced before pruning. Of the reduced versions, 237 have nonempty D.

For each case, two independent positive starts were evolved without consulting D. An independent killed-edge linear solver evaluated p. Fixed-step flow approximations at t=800 and 1600 detected decaying coordinates; only then were surviving coordinates refined by a root solve. D was computed separately and compared afterward. All 640 runs agreed with D; there were **no mismatches**, maximum equilibrium residual 8.799×10⁻¹⁵, and maximum difference between the two refined equilibria 3.553×10⁻¹⁵. Raw flow values, decay ratios, inferred supports and refined vectors are saved, so the classification can be inspected rather than inferred from a small residual alone.

This supports the proposed rule, but finite-time support detection is not a proof. A tiny residual can coexist with a wrong support near a marginal zero. Nor do two starts establish uniqueness of an attractor. The authors' six starts and support-restricted eigenvalues have the same limitations. In particular support stability does not control all directions in the zero coordinates, as Remark 6 demonstrates.

I also independently enumerated marked-pair automorphism orbits in the NetworkX atlas and obtained exactly 22,828 cases. Separately, I reevaluated p at every equilibrium supplied in data/ex5.jsonl, data/ex6.jsonl and data/ex7.jsonl. Maximum residual was 1.001×10⁻¹³. The counts are 12,004 adjacent and 10,824 nonadjacent cases; 10,286 and 9,806 are reduced; 10,064 and 4,118 reduced cases have nonempty D. These match the table.

Independent combinatorial tests also reproduce 2,504 applications of Theorem 2, 3,294 applications of Theorem 5, and 1,643 and 247 cases with dead edges and connectivity at least 2 and 3, respectively, among the reduced nonadjacent cases. The smallest positive coordinate in the single-solution records is 0.001902628489419. The largest *stored* support eigenvalue is −0.015257982953657; a fresh central-difference Jacobian at that maximizing record, using two step sizes, checks the reported approximately −0.015 value. I did not independently recompute every stored spectrum.

The sole supplied-record support discrepancy is the disclosed case cid=3769: one of six starts retains edge {1,4}, weight about 1.74×10⁻⁷. Its residual is only 9.55×10⁻¹⁴. This is not a new counterexample to D and was not a mismatch in my independent 320-case sample. It illustrates why residual size alone is insufficient. The local rerun text reports resolution at longer times; I did not treat that reported rerun as an independent proof or rerun its very long integrations.

The census proves neither almost-sure convergence nor deterministic limits of the ant process. Line 319 is the material exception to the paper's otherwise appropriate separation: replace “their limits” by “the corresponding boundary-equilibrium values,” unless a further proof is added.

## 10. Proposition 10, theta graphs — SOUND

On path i, an edge at depth j is crossed exactly when depth j is first reached. From depth 1 the gambler's-ruin probability is

    [Σ_{m=0}^{j−1} α_i^(−m)]⁻¹.

Multiplying by the departure weight α_i gives the displayed a_{i,j}. The omitted normalization Σα_i is common to all excursion types and cancels in every ratio. The competition is between reaching depth j on path i and absorption along another path, giving p_{i,j}=a_{i,j}/(a_{i,j}+C−c_i). Excursions reaching depth j and returning can recur; the first-event calculation handles them.

Substitution yields 1−α_i=C−c_i and hence (1−α_i)/(1−α_i^{L_i})=C, with c_i=Cα_i^{L_i}. Summing gives the required normalization. Thus the displayed conditions really imply p(w)=w for every edge, not only the terminal edges.

For existence and uniqueness, f_L(α)=1/(1+α+⋯+α^{L−1}) is strictly decreasing from 1 to 1/L. Its inverse α_i(C) exists on the common interval (max_i 1/L_i,1). The sum Σα_i(C)^{L_i} strictly decreases from a limit greater than 1 to 0. At the lower endpoint, at least one shortest-path term tends to 1 and the remaining terms stay positive; if several paths are shortest, several terms tend to 1. The intermediate-value and monotonicity arguments prove exactly one solution. Equal lengths force equal α, and normalization gives α=k^(−1/L).

The proposition asserts uniqueness of this parameter tuple and constructs an equilibrium. It does not assert uniqueness of all equilibria on the theta graph, or convergence of the ant process for k>2. Those stronger assertions would require further arguments. The 39 flow experiments are appropriately numerical evidence.

## Relation to KMS, Armfield–Mailler and Aletti; scope of the refutation

Theorem 2 provides actual almost-sure zeros at nest–food distance two, so the claimed refutation of the second sentence of KMS Conjecture 1.1 is justified independently of full-vector convergence. The reduced paw already suffices, and Example 4 removes objections based on cut vertices or edges lying on no simple N–F path. Theorem 5 alone would establish only incompatibility of convergence and positivity on its graphs; the manuscript says this correctly at lines 116–117.

The paper does not establish the first sentence for general graphs, nor does a mean-field census establish it. Its corrected conjecture expressly retains that unproved sentence. The phrase “their limits” for surviving cycle edges is the one specific overstatement identified above.

The cited KMS families, the adjacent-nest assertions, and the k=2 theta comparison are internally consistent with the mathematics checked here and with the version of Conjecture 1.1 supplied for this task. Aletti is described as an open-problems source, not as proving convergence. Armfield–Mailler is attributed a result for a different reinforcement rule, not for the trace rule; no implication between those rules is used in the main proofs.

I cannot authenticate the exact statements or theorem numbering of KMS/KMS0, the Aletti restatement, the recent Armfield–Mailler preprint, or the cited distance-three counterexample, because those source texts are absent and browsing was prohibited. In particular lines 66–69 describe both loop-erasure rules simply as reinforcing “the loop-erasure”; that does not specify the direction in which loops are erased. The distinction is necessary when comparing forward and reverse constructions. Add precise definitions or source quotations before treating the associated geodesic claims as verified. No substantive error in those external attributions has been established by this local audit.
