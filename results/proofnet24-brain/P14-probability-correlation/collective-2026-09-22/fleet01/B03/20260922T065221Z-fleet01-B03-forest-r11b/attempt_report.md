## 2. Base-forest reduction

Fix distinct edges `e,f` of `G` and put

`B = G - {e,f}`.

Let `F(B)` be the set of **all** forests of `B` and

`N = |F(B)|`.

For `H in F(B)`, let

- `X(H)=1` iff `H+e` is a forest,
- `Y(H)=1` iff `H+f` is a forest.

Write `n_ij` for the number of base forests with `(X,Y)=(i,j)`. Inside the `11` state define

`Q = {H : H+e and H+f are forests, but H+e+f is cyclic}`

and write `q=|Q|`. Let `S=n_11-q` be the number for which both marked edges are jointly addable.

The four extension counts are

`Z = N + (n_10+n_11) + (n_01+n_11) + S`,

`Z_e = (n_10+n_11) + S`,

`Z_f = (n_01+n_11) + S`,

`Z_ef = S`.

A direct cancellation gives the exact source count identity

**(2.1)**

`Delta_G(e,f) := Z_e Z_f - Z Z_ef = n_10 n_01 + q N - n_00 n_11`.

Thus the whole marked-pair problem is the comparison

No connectivity of `H` is assumed anywhere.

## 3. New overlap-incidence bijection

For a state-`00` forest `H`, both marked endpoints are connected inside `H`, so there are unique forest paths `P_e(H)` and `P_f(H)` joining the endpoints of `e` and `f`.

Split the `00` forests into

- `D`: the two unique paths have no common **edge**;
- `O`: the two unique paths share at least one edge.

Write `d=|D|`, `o=|O|`; hence `n_00=d+o`.

For `H in O` define

`s(H)=|E(P_e(H)) intersect E(P_f(H))| >= 1`.

For `K in Q`, the quotient of `K` by its connected components sends `e` and `f` to two parallel edges between the same two distinct components. Call those components `A_K,B_K` and define

`c(K) = number of physical edges of B having one endpoint in A_K and the other in B_K`.

`c(K)` may be zero; the marked edges themselves are not edges of `B`.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact reconnection incidence

There is a bijection

`{(H,g): H in O, g in P_e(H) intersect P_f(H)}`

with

`{(K,g): K in Q, g in E(B) crosses A_K|B_K}`

under `K=H-g` and `H=K+g`. Consequently

**(3.1)**

`I := sum_{H in O} s(H) = sum_{K in Q} c(K)`.

**Proof.** If `g` lies on both unique marked paths in the forest `H`, deleting `g` separates its tree into two components across which both `e` and `f` cross. Thus each marked edge is individually addable to `K=H-g`; adding both gives two distinct links between the same two `K`-components and hence one cycle, so `K in Q`.

Conversely, for `K in Q`, individual addability says each marked edge connects two distinct `K`-components, while failure of joint addability forces the two unordered component pairs to be equal. If a base edge `g` crosses these same two components, then `K+g` is a forest. In `K+g`, both marked endpoint pairs are connected and the unique path for each marked pair uses `g`; hence `K+g in O` and `g` belongs to the intersection of the two paths. The constructions are inverse and preserve all other disconnected components untouched. QED.

This includes shared-endpoint marked edges, disjoint marked edges, arbitrary disconnected base forests, isolated components, and the cases `c(K)=0` or `s(H)>1`.

## 4. Exact geometric decomposition of the Rayleigh gap

Define the path-overlap excess and the signed reconnection-multiplicity excess

`E_O = sum_{H in O}(s(H)-1) = I-o >= 0`,

`E_Q = sum_{K in Q}(c(K)-1) = I-q`.

The second quantity is signed because `c(K)=0` is possible. From (3.1),

**(4.1)**

`o-q = E_Q-E_O`.

Insert `n_00=d+o` into (2.1) and use (4.1). The exact gap becomes

**(4.2)**

`Delta_G(e,f)`

`= [n_10 n_01 - d n_11]`

`  + q (N-n_11)`

`  + n_11 E_O`

`  - n_11 E_Q`.

This is the requested conditioning/reconnection accounting for overlapping cycles. It has four geometrically distinct terms:

1. `n_10 n_01-d n_11`: the edge-disjoint-path switching balance;
2. `q(N-n_11)`: free overlap-cycle credit paired with base forests outside the `11` state;
3. `n_11 E_O`: credit from extra common path edges, because one `00` overlap forest has multiple valid deletions into `Q`;
4. `n_11 E_Q`: debt from extra physical reconnection edges between the two relevant `Q` components.

The last item is the only genuinely new overlap multiplicity debt after the exact incidence cancellation.

## 5. Refuted candidate: the q-term does not pay the overlap sector by itself

A natural next attempt is to prove

**(BAD)** `o n_11 <= q N`,

which would let the `qN` term in (2.1) pay all overlap-path `00 x 11` pairs before dealing with the edge-disjoint sector. The incidence lemma shows why this is tempting, but it is false already on five vertices.

Take the simple graph with edges

`01,02,03,04,12,14,23`

and marks

`e=03`, `f=04`.

Then `B` has edges `01,02,12,14,23`. Exact enumeration gives

`N=28`, `d=1`, `o=2`, `q=1`, `n_10=n_01=5`, `n_11=15`,

and `I=2` on both sides of (3.1). Thus

`o n_11 = 30 > 28 = qN`.

The unique `Q` forest is

`K={12,14,23}`.

Its relevant components are `{0}` and `{1,2,3,4}`, and there are exactly two base reconnection edges across that component cut, `01` and `02`, so `c(K)=2`. The two inverse overlap forests are `K+01` and `K+02`, each with one common marked-path edge. Hence `E_O=0`, `E_Q=1`.

Nevertheless this graph is **not** a counterexample to FOREST. The full source gap is

`Delta = 5*5 + 1*28 - 3*15 = 8 > 0`.

In the new decomposition,

`Delta = (25-15) + (28-15) + 0 - 15 = 10+13-15=8`.

Thus the failed overlap-only payment is repaired by surplus from the edge-disjoint sector. Any successful switching proof must allow credit transfer between the disjoint-path and overlap-path sectors; treating them as two independently nonnegative pieces is invalid.

The same underlying graph also supplies a disjoint-mark version: choosing `e=03`, `f=14` gives the same aggregate counts and the same failure of (BAD). So the obstruction is not an artifact of the marked edges sharing an endpoint.
