# Credits

Every result here rests on other people's mathematics. This index lists the authors our public papers cite and the
prior work each open-problem result names in its own README. It is built mechanically by `build_credits.py` from
the papers' bibliographies (106 papers with a reference list, 1885 references in all) and from the result READMEs, so
it shows who is cited, not how much each contribution mattered; the papers' introductions and acknowledgment
sections say that. Surnames are merged across first names, so two authors with one surname share a row; the
papers' own authors are left out.

## Prior work named by each open-problem result

- `P0108_thin_cylinder_fpp_clt_2026_11` (First-passage percolation across thin cylinders: numerics for the CLT width): Auffinger, Damron and Hanson, *50 years of first-passage percolation* (AMS University Lecture Series 68, 2017; arXiv:1511.03262), Question 20: find the largest alpha such that the passage time across the cylinder [0, n] x [-n^alpha, n^alpha]^(d-1), centred and scaled, has a standard normal limit. Chatterjee and Dey, *Central limit theorem for first-passage percolation time across thin cylinders*, Probab. Theory Relat.
- `P0133_ust_torus_dual_theta_2026_11` (The dual theta graph of a uniform spanning tree on a torus (Schramm, Problem 6.2)): Oded Schramm, in his 2006 ICM address (*Conformally invariant scaling limits: an overview and a collection of problems*, Proceedings of the ICM, Madrid 2006, Vol. I, 513-543; arXiv:math/0602151), asked for the scaling limit of the topological data of a uniform spanning tree on a torus (Problem 6.2). For a fine discretization of the torus C / <1, tau>, the edges outside the tree, read in the dual graph, have a core that is almost always a theta graph, and the homology classes of its three cycles form a superbase {v1, v2, v3} of the lattice Z + tau Z with v1 + v2 + v3 = 0.
- `codex-rounds-2026-11-14` (Worker rounds on the top-200 open problems, 14-15 November 2026): The problems belong to the people who posed and developed them. The methods that recur most across these rows come from percolation and SLE (Smirnov, Lawler, Schramm and Werner), random nodal sets (Nazarov and Sodin), hard-sphere systems (Alder and Wainwright; Bernard and Krauth), the binary perceptron (Krauth and Mezard) and the low-degree method for planted problems.
- `famous-problems-checks-2026-11` (Checks of the 2025-2026 claimed solutions of well-known open problems, 12 November 2026): The problems are due to the people whose names they carry. The solutions are due to the authors in the table and to the earlier work they build on, which each check cites. Several of the sources state that a language model produced the first proof (OpenAI's models, GPT-6 Astra); the human authors who wrote, checked or formalised those proofs are named in the table.
- `fei-entropy-influence-2026-11` (Fourier entropy-influence conjecture: working record, 9-15 November 2026): The conjecture is due to Friedgut and Kalai. The methods used throughout come from the work of Kahn, Kalai and Linial and of Talagrand on influences; of Kelman, Kindler, Lifshitz, Minzer and Safra on the generalized influences that appear here as up(S); of O'Donnell, Wright and Zhou, of O'Donnell and Tan, and of Wan, Wright and Wu on FEI for special classes and composition; of Chakraborty, Kulkarni, Lokam and Saurabh on upper bounds for Fourier entropy; and of Keller, Mossel and Schlank on the chain-rule approach. The threshold-function argument uses the Kolmogorov-Rogozin concentration inequality.
- `fei-lower-bound-search-2026-11` (Fourier entropy-influence: exhaustive monotone censuses and structured searches, 10-12 November 2026): The conjecture is due to Friedgut and Kalai (1996). The lower bound 6.5218 is Numaro's (2026); earlier bounds are due to O'Donnell, Wright and Zhou (2011) and to Hod. The monotone counts are checked against the Dedekind numbers; the seven-variable value is due to Church (1965).
- `fei-papers-2026-11` (Fourier entropy-influence: the checks behind ten papers, November 2026): The mathematics the papers stand on belongs to the authors named above. The new statements, the check scripts and the audits were produced by AI agents (Claude and Codex) working under human direction; each paper's AI declaration says which system did what.
- `fei-solve-2026-11` (Fourier entropy-influence: working records of a solve campaign, 9-11 November 2026): The conjecture and every result cited above as known belong to the mathematicians named with it. The new statements were produced by AI agents (Claude and Codex) and have been checked by those agents and by the census scripts, not by a human referee. Agent and session labels have been removed from file names; worker folder names (FEIB01 and so on) are the brief numbers.
- `fei-step-constant-numerics-2026-09` (Fourier entropy-influence: numerics for the coordinate step, 20-21 September 2026): The conjecture is due to Friedgut and Kalai (1996). The read-once lower bound 6.4548 is Hod's, the record 6.5218 is Numaro's, and the first lower bound and the read-once upper bound are O'Donnell, Wright and Zhou's (2011). The scripts and their reading were produced by AI agents (Claude) and have not been reviewed by a person.
- `frontier-worker-drafts-2026` (Worker drafts and measurements from the open-problem frontier, October 2026): The T039 measurements and certificates use the linearisation and the radial Laguerre block method of Heilman (arXiv:2608.00333). Its range below 2/5 is Heilman's, and the new range extends his method. The T267 drafts use the ReLU kernel computations of Heckel and Yilmaz (arXiv:2007.10099) and the noise phases of Stephenson and Lee (arXiv:2108.12006).
- `frontier-worker-results-2026` (Worker folders from the open-problem frontier, October 2026): The problems and the arguments these workers extend belong to the authors cited in each row of the list and in each worker's `LITERATURE_AUDIT.md`. The checking discipline of a certificate rebuilt by an independent program, from the proof text alone, follows long practice in computer-assisted proof.
- `paper_af344_exact_flow_characterization` (Exact infimum-over-flows characterization of relaxation time): David Aldous and James Fill, *Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs* (2002 manuscript / 2014 recompilation), Chapter 3, Open Problem 3.44: > Does there exist a characterization of the relaxation time as exactly an inf over flows? Live source: https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch3.S8.html
- `paper_huw_problem_4_11_all_times` (Hasebe–Ueda–Wang Problem 4.11: all-time failure of strong log-unimodality): Takahiro Hasebe, Yuki Ueda and Jiun-Chau Wang, *Log-unimodality for free positive multiplicative Brownian motion*, arXiv:2009.13848 (2020), journal version *Colloquium Mathematicum* 169 (2022), 209–226, Problem 4.11. The source asks whether the free positive multiplicative Brownian law `sigma_t` fails free multiplicative strong log-unimodality for **every** `t>0`. The source's Theorem 4.9 proves failure only for sufficiently small positive time.
- `paper_romero_split8_n8_layer` (The first unresolved n=8 layer of uniform split reliability): Pablo Romero, *Characterization of locally most split reliable graphs*, Theoretical Computer Science 1047 (2025), 115327, DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2025.115327, studies uniformly most split reliable two-terminal graphs in `T_{n,m}`. Romero proves nonexistence for `n>=7` and `n <= m <= binom(n-3,2)+3`, while the previously known results settle the trivial complete/near-complete classes and all classes through `n<=7`. Thus the first complete unresolved vertex layer is `n=8, m=14,15,...,26`.
- `proof-checks-2026-11-12` (Checks of reported solutions, 11-13 November 2026): The solutions recorded here belong to their authors, named above and in each check with the paper they wrote. Where a proof is credited to an AI system, the credit is as the paper or the repository gives it, and the human authors who wrote up, checked or extended it are named. Our part is only the reading and the tests in these folders.
- `t104-alon-specht-gaps-2026-11` (Spectral gaps on Specht modules (Alon's 2/3 conjecture): working record, 11-13 November 2026): The conjecture and the framework are Alon's. The hook computations rest on Bacher's work and on Alon and Kozma's reading of it, and the comparison with the graph Laplacian on the theorem of Caputo, Liggett and Richthammer; the mixing-time motivation is Oliveira's. Young's orthogonal form is used for every numerical computation.
- `top200-probability-list-2026-10` (A working list of major open problems in probability and in the probability side of deep-learning theory): The problems are the work of the people who posed them and of the many who advanced them, and the cited sources are the record of that. The list leans especially on several surveys and problem collections: - G. Grimmett, *Percolation*; - I.
- `top200-vetting-2026-11` (Vetting the list of two hundred open problems, 11-14 November 2026): Every problem on the list belongs to the mathematicians who posed it, and every verdict of "solved" belongs to the authors of the paper that solved it; the tables give the arXiv identifiers so the credit can be followed. The pass itself was carried out by AI agents, with each verdict recorded next to the evidence. It has not been reviewed by a person, and a row marked "still open" means only that the searches recorded here found no solution.
