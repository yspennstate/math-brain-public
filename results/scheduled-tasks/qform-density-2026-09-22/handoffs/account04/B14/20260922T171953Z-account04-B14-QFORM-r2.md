## New theorem

For every cap-one probability density \(f\) and every \(R\), let
\[
m_R=\int_{-R}^R f,\qquad \delta_R=1-m_R,
\]
choose a bounded set \(F_R\) outside \([-R,R]\) with \(|F_R|=\delta_R\), and set
\[
g_R=f1_{[-R,R]}+1_{F_R}.
\]
Then \(g_R\) is a compactly supported cap-one probability density and
\[
d_{\rm TV}(g_R\,dx,f\,dx)\le\delta_R\to0.
\]
Products satisfy
\[
d_{\rm TV}\!\left(\bigotimes_i\mu_i,\bigotimes_i\nu_i\right)
\le\sum_i d_{\rm TV}(\mu_i,\nu_i),
\]
and measurable pushforward contracts total variation. Hence the optimal quadratic small-ball constant satisfies
\[
C_{\rm all}=C_{\rm compact}
\]
with no loss. Together with the earlier B14 bathtub/finite-fragment theorem,
\[
C_{\rm all}=C_{\rm compact}=C_{\rm finite\ interval\ unions}.
\]
Diameters, translations, gaps, and fragment counts remain arbitrary; no uniform bounded-diameter claim is made.
