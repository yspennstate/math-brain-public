## New proved result

For any probability density `0<=f<=1`, any `a!=0`, any `b`, and

`F(t)=int exp(i t (a x^2+b x)) f(x) dx`, every `q>2` obeys

`||F||_q <= C_q |a|^(-1/q)`,  
`C_q=(4*pi)^(1/q) ((q-1)/(q-2))^((q-1)/q)`.

In particular `||F||_3^3 <=16*pi/|a|`, hence

`Leb{|F|>eta} <=16*pi/(|a| eta^3)`.

Optimizing `q` gives the near-critical distributional estimate

`Leb{|F|>eta} <=16*pi*e |a|^(-1) eta^(-2) max(1,log(1/eta))`, `0<eta<1`.

The strong `L^2` endpoint genuinely fails already for `f=1_[0,1]`, because the square-law density `1/(2 sqrt y)` on `(0,1)` is not `L^2`.

Conditioning gives the full-coupled corollary: if an original-coordinate diagonal entry `a_ii!=0`, then for the complete quadratic form, with all outside couplings retained,

`||phi_Q||_q <= C_q |a_ii|^(-1/q)`.
