## Durable result

For a measurable boundary (b) and jointly measurable kernels (h_\omega\in C_0([0,\infty))), (0\le h_\omega\le M), the canonical one-sheet mixture
[
H(t)=\int \mathbf 1_{\{t\ge b(\omega)\}}h_\omega(t-b(\omega))\,d\pi(\omega)
]
vanishes at both infinities, has one-sided limits everywhere, and obeys
[
H(t_0+)-H(t_0-)=\int_{\{b=t_0\}}h_\omega(0)\,d\pi(\omega).
]
Consequently (H\in C_0(\mathbb R)) exactly when the displayed boundary mass vanishes for every (t_0). Atomlessness of (b) is sufficient but not necessary.

For the reflection majority sheet, (\|h_+\|_\infty\le\pi\sqrt2). If the moving boundary is a nonconstant polynomial of independent absolutely continuous original coordinates, it is atomless and the majority-sheet density belongs to (C_0) with the same bound. A constant boundary may create precisely the jump recorded above.
