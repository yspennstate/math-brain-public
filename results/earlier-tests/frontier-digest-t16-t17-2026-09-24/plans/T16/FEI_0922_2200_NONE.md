# FEI research plan

**Aim.** Open since 1996. I aim at a proof, bank partial results on the way, and believe nothing that has not survived a small-$n$ computation.

**What I build on.**
- Proved classes: symmetric functions and read-once decision trees (O'Donnell–Wright–Zhou), read-once formulas via a composition theorem (O'Donnell–Tan), random functions and random LTFs; entropy bounds from decision-tree depth and certificate complexity, with FEI read as a coding problem (Wan–Wright–Wu; Chakraborty et al.; Arunachalam et al.).
- Trivially $H=O(I\log n)$. Kelman–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer–Safra, extending Bourgain–Kalai, prove a weakened FEI giving concentration on $2^{O(I\log I)}$ characters; removing that $\log I$ would essentially settle FEI and Mansour's conjecture.
- Monotone FMEI follows from KKL ($\hat f(\{i\})=\mathrm{Inf}_i$), yet Hod's lower bound $C>6.45$, built from lexicographic functions, holds for monotone functions: the monotone case is not soft.

**Reformulations.**
1. Coding: $H(\hat f^2)$ is at most the expected length of any prefix code for the spectral sample $S\sim\hat f^2$; FEI says $S$ is describable in $O(1)$ bits per element on average, and every explicit code is a theorem.
2. Splitting: $f$'s coefficients are those of $E_if$ and $D_if$ together, so unnormalised entropy adds and $I(f)=I(E_if)+I(D_if)+\mathrm{Inf}_i(f)$; given $i\in S$, $S\setminus\{i\}$ is the spectral sample of $D_if$.
3. Set form: for $A\subseteq\{-1,1\}^n$ of density $\alpha$ let $q_A=\widehat{1_A}^{\,2}/\alpha$. FEI is equivalent, up to constants, to $H(q_A)\le C|\partial A|/|A|+C$ (embed $A$ as $f(x,y)=1_{A^c}(x)+y1_A(x)$; conversely take $A=\{f=-1\}$). Subcubes are tight: $H(q_A)=|\partial A|/|A|=\log_2(1/\alpha)$. For monotone $f$, $D_if$ is the indicator of $i$'s pivotal set, and these pivotal sets are exactly the order-convex sets (differences of nested up-sets). A second derivative brings signs back, which is where an induction has to work.

**The obstacle.** Naming $i\in S$ costs about $\log(1/\mathrm{Inf}_i)$; summed naively this is the influence entropy, order $I\log n$ for Majority, whose entropy is $\Theta(I)$. Hypercontractivity puts most of the weight through $i$ on sets of size $\gtrsim\log(1/\mathrm{Inf}_i)$, which bounds the naming cost only by $O(E|S|^2)$. The saving must come from density (Majority: $\log(n/|S|)$ per element) or clustering (Tribes: a block costs $\log n$, its elements then $O(1)$ each). Target lemma: a potential $\Phi$ on peeling states, $O(I)$ at the start, such that each step's conditional entropy is at most $C$ times the expected number of elements it reveals plus the expected drop in $\Phi$.

**Order of work.**
1. First concrete step (about an hour): a fast Walsh–Hadamard workbench giving $H$, $I$ and the exact chain-rule split of $H$ along min-peeling (given $\min S=i$, the rest is the spectrum of $E_{x_{<i}}D_if$), for all $2^{16}$ functions on 4 variables, all 7.8 million monotone functions on 6, and Majority, Tribes, lexicographic functions, random DNFs and LTFs up to $n\approx20$. It shows where entropy runs ahead of influence consumed and which sets extremise the set form, fixing the peeling order before any proof attempt.
2. Monotone case (hours two to three): derivatives are unsigned and the hard examples live there. Prove the set form for order-convex sets, using hypercontractivity on each pivotal set to pay for opening a new cluster, then bound the cost of choosing $i$.
3. In parallel: find exactly where KKLMS lose $\log I$; if it is a worst-case count over scales, replace the count by an entropy chain rule over multiscale random restrictions, charging each scale only the influence it carries.

Checkpoint at hour three: if no potential survives the workbench on monotone families, shift to step 3 and bank any special class proved.

**What I avoid, and why.**
- Level-by-level counting and junta bounds: Tribes spreads its weight near level $\log n$ over $\binom{n}{\log n}$ sets, so these lose a log factor by construction.
- Gaussian transfer and invariance principles: they see low-degree statistics, while the entropy lives in tiny high-degree coefficients.
- Induction on dimension with a local potential $E[\varphi(f)]$: the two-point inequality fails by about $v^2\log(u^2/v^2)$ when the local derivative $v$ is small against the local mean $u$, so the potential must be nonlocal.
- Real-valued relaxations: $(x_1+\dots+x_N)/N$ already breaks FEI, so Booleanity must stay in every inductive hypothesis.
- Composition counterexamples: the composition theorem limits them, and such constructions have reached only about $6.45$; a counterexample needs a new mechanism.
