# FEI: plan for the first hours

A proof in a few hours is unlikely, so the aim is narrower: a reduction that puts the whole difficulty into one checkable inequality, exact data on whether that inequality survives, and a proof of it in whatever regime the standard tools reach.

## First line: peel one coordinate

Let $f_\pm$ be the restrictions $x_i=\pm1$. A short computation (entropy in bits) gives

$$H(f)=\tfrac12[H(f_+)+H(f_-)]+D_i(f),\qquad I(f)=\tfrac12[I(f_+)+I(f_-)]+\mathrm{Inf}_i(f),$$

$$D_i(f)=\mathrm{JS}(\hat f_+^2,\hat f_-^2)+\sum_{S\not\ni i} m_S\,h\big(\hat f(S)^2/m_S\big),\qquad m_S=\hat f(S)^2+\hat f(S\cup i)^2,$$

with $h$ the binary entropy. Restrictions of Boolean functions are Boolean, so induction on $n$ gives FEI with constant $C$ from

**(L)** every non-constant Boolean $f$ has a coordinate $i$ with $D_i(f)\le C\,\mathrm{Inf}_i(f)$.

Why start here: each term of $D_i$ is controlled by $\mathrm{Inf}_i$, and only one carries a logarithm. Since $\sum(\hat f_+-\hat f_-)^2=4\,\mathrm{Inf}_i$, the Hellinger bound gives $\mathrm{JS}\le 4\log_2 e\cdot\mathrm{Inf}_i$. The splitting term is $O(\mathrm{Inf}_i)$ plus a leak $L_i=\sum \hat f(S\cup i)^2\log(\hat f(S)^2/\hat f(S\cup i)^2)$ over pairs whose upper coefficient is smaller, and the log-sum inequality gives $L_i\le \mathrm{Inf}_i\log(1/\mathrm{Inf}_i)+O(\mathrm{Inf}_i)$. So (L) holds with $C=K+O(1)$ unless every influence is below $2^{-K}$. In that regular case Talagrand gives $I\ge cK\,\mathrm{Var}$, and $D_if$ is $\{0,\pm1\}$-valued with measure $\mathrm{Inf}_i$: each of its coefficients is at most $\mathrm{Inf}_i$, and the level-$k$ inequality puts most of its weight at levels of order $\log(1/\mathrm{Inf}_i)$. Sanity check: odd and even functions have $D_i=0$ for every $i$, so majority peels for free.

## What I build on

- O'Donnell-Wright-Zhou: symmetric functions and read-once decision trees; $C\ge 60/13$.
- O'Donnell-Tan's composition theorem (the outer function needs biased FEI), Keller-Mossel-Schlank on the biased version, and recursive compositions (O'Donnell-Tan, Hod) that force $C$ above 6. The constant in (L) can be no smaller.
- Wan-Wright-Wu: read-$k$ decision trees, and entropy as the expected code length of $S\sim\hat f^2$.
- Kelman-Kindler-Lifshitz-Minzer-Safra: concentration on $2^{O(I\log I)}$ characters. The missing $\log I$ is the gap.
- KKL, Talagrand, Friedgut's junta theorem, level-$k$ inequalities, and edge-isoperimetry, which is FEI's small-variance case and must fall out of any proof.

## What I avoid

- Arguments valid for bounded real-valued $f$: FEI fails there ($n^{-1/2}\sum x_i$ has $I=1$, $H=\log n$).
- Entropy proxies: Rényi entropies of order below 1, $\ell_q$ spectral norms with $q<2$, $H\le 2\log\|\hat f\|_1$. For majority $H_\alpha\approx n$ for every fixed $\alpha<1$ while $I\approx\sqrt n$.
- Multiscale junta arguments, which lose $\log I$ (KKLMS is where that road ends), and level-by-level counting, which gives $I\log n$. Majority shows within-level entropy $k\log(n/k)$ is unavoidable at low levels, so a proof must trade across levels.
- Small-$n$ searches for counterexamples to FEI itself: $H\le n$ caps small cases and the worst known ratios come from compositions at large $n$. (L) is new and stronger, so a small-$n$ search against it is worth running.

## First concrete step (about 90 minutes)

Code the identity and check it to $10^{-12}$ on random functions. Then compute $\rho(f)=\min_i D_i/\mathrm{Inf}_i$, and $\Lambda(f)/I(f)$ with $\Lambda=\sum_i L_i$, over all 65,536 functions on 4 variables and all 7,828,354 monotone functions on 6; by hill-climbing on truth tables at $n=8$-$12$ to maximize $\rho$; and in closed form for $\mathrm{AND}_k$, tribes, thresholds with nonzero threshold, address functions and the O'Donnell-Tan and Hod compositions at large $n$. Then:

- $\rho$ bounded and flat in $n$: prove (L) for regular $f$. A leak near $\mathrm{Inf}_i\log(1/\mathrm{Inf}_i)$ is the equality case of log-sum, which needs $f$'s coefficients directly under $D_if$'s spectrum to be larger by a factor near $1/\mathrm{Inf}_i$. The first idea is to show, with Talagrand and the level-$k$ inequality, that this cannot hold for every $i$ at once.
- $\rho$ growing: the maximizing family shows what (L) misses, and I add a potential to the induction, as the inductive proof of edge-isoperimetry carries $\mathrm{Var}\log(1/\mathrm{Var})$. Candidates: that term, the entropy of the level distribution, and a $p$-biased form, which composition already required.

If neither branch has traction by hour four, I read the KKLMS argument for the step where $\log I$ enters, with the regular case as the target. Before calling the reduction new I check it against OWZ, O'Donnell-Tan and KKLMS.
