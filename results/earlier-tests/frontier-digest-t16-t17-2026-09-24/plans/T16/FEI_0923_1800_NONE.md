# FEI: plan for the first hours (working alone)

## Where it stands
FEI has been open for thirty years. It implies two statements that are also open: the (log n)^2 total-influence bound for graph properties (Bourgain-Kalai get (log n)^(2-eps)), and a weak form of Mansour's conjecture. The closest general result is KKLMS (Kelman-Kindler-Lifshitz-Minzer-Safra 2020), which falls short by a factor of log I: it gives concentration on 2^O(I log I) characters. So the plan is a real attempt, with a kill test at every step and a checkable partial result if it stalls. Nothing counts as proved until it has been derived a second way.

## Build on
- **Proved cases:** symmetric functions and read-once decision trees (O'Donnell-Wright-Zhou); read-once formulas, via the p-biased composition theorem (O'Donnell-Tan); decision-tree and protocol bounds (Wan-Wright-Wu).
- **Tools:** hypercontractivity on the derivatives D_i f ∈ {0,±1}, which drives KKL, Talagrand and Friedgut; random restrictions, E_z[f_{J|z}^(T)^2] = Pr[S∩J=T].
- **Sanity check:** C >= 6.278 (O'Donnell-Tan; Hod reports about 6.45). An argument giving a smaller C has a bug.

## First attack: entropy chain rule on an adaptive decision tree
Split f on coordinate i into f+ and f-, with spectra mu+, mu- and coefficients a, b. Since f^(S) = (a+b)/2 and f^(S+i) = (a-b)/2, the following holds exactly:

    H(f) = (H(f+)+H(f-))/2 + JS(mu+,mu-) + E_mubar[h(q)],  I(f) = (I(f+)+I(f-))/2 + Inf_i(f),

with mubar = (mu+ + mu-)/2, q(S) = Pr[i∈S | S\i], and E_mubar[q] = Inf_i.

A chi^2 bound should give JS <= 4Inf_i nats and E_mubar[h(q)] <= Inf_i log(e/Inf_i). A fixed order plus Jensen then gives a warm-up bound, H <= Σ_i Inf_i log(C0/Inf_i). This needs checking and may already be known.

In any adaptive order, FEI is equivalent to one statement: the surprise E[log 1/q | i∈S], averaged over tree nodes with influence weights, is O(1). Small influence alone does not force surprise. Majority is odd, so q ∈ {0,1} at its root and the surprise there is zero, although every influence is about n^(-1/2).

The plan is to split on the most influential coordinate first. Surprise at a low-influence node is charged to the excess influence that KKL/Talagrand force once all remaining influences are small.

Why this attack first:
- it uses only restrictions, which keep f Boolean;
- everything in it is explicit;
- the read-once proofs rest on the same root-split recursion.

Candidate strengthened hypotheses:
- p-biased FEI;
- C·I − H >= c·Var·log(1/Var);
- normalized FEI for the {0,±1}-valued derivatives.

## Second attack (if nothing survives by hour 5): the log I in KKLMS
Find where log I enters their proof. It may be a union over degree scales, or the count of characters at each level. Then test whether a separate restriction rate for each dyadic band of levels removes it.

## Fallbacks
- **Monotone functions:** D_i f is the indicator of the pivotal set and f^(i) = Inf_i. This is not easy, because it already gives the monotone graph-property bound.
- **Linear threshold functions.**
- **Collision entropy:** E[f(x)f(y)f(z)f(xyz)] >= 2^(-CI). FEI implies it, and it implies FMEI.

## Avoid
- **Per-level bounds:** level-k entropy can reach log C(n,k), since majority's level-1 weight is uniform over n coordinates. The levels have to trade off.
- **Non-Boolean relaxations:** FEI fails for maps into the sphere; x -> x/√n has H = log n and I = 1. A proof must use discreteness.
- **The bare potential C·I, node by node:** at small q, h(q) ≈ q log(1/q) exceeds Cq.
- **Junta-plus-counting (Friedgut):** it gives 2^O(I^2), and KKLMS have already taken that line to the log I gap.
- **Guessing C from small n:** extremal ratios come from deep recursive compositions, so small-n data only kills lemmas.

## First concrete step (hour 1)
A C harness at BelowNormal priority:
1. Walsh-Hadamard over all functions for n <= 5 (2^32 at n = 5), and hill-climbing for n = 6-12.
2. Verify the split identity to 1e-12 and test the warm-up bound.
3. Under the greedy order, tabulate per-node surprise and the worst functions for weighted surprise. Add tribes, recursive majority and O'Donnell-Tan compositions.

Proof time goes only to the hypotheses that survive.
