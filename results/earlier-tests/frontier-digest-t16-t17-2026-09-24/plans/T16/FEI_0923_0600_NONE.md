# FEI: plan for the first hours

## Scope

FEI implies Mansour's conjecture for polynomial-size DNFs (constant $\varepsilon$) and the $(\log n)^2$ lower bound on the total influence of graph properties of constant variance, beyond Bourgain–Kalai's $(\log n)^{2-\varepsilon}$. A proof has to contain both. I will aim at the conjecture, but each block of hours should end in a checked statement or a recorded counterexample, and every candidate lemma is first asked what it says about $s$-term DNFs and about graph properties.

## What I build on

Known cases: symmetric functions and read-once decision trees (O'Donnell–Wright–Zhou), read-once formulas through the FEI$^+$ composition theorem (O'Donnell–Tan), and bounds by average decision-tree depth (Wan–Wright–Wu) and certificate-type measures (Chakraborty et al.; Arunachalam et al.). The nearest general result is Kelman–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer–Safra (KKLMS): Bourgain–Kalai-style random restrictions put the spectrum on $2^{O(I\log I)}$ characters, and removing that $\log I$ is essentially the conjecture. Tools: the restriction identity $\mathbb E_z\hat f_z(A)^2=\sum_{T\subseteq B}\hat f(A\cup T)^2$, hypercontractivity and the level-$k$ inequality, KKL and Talagrand, Friedgut's junta theorem, and the Garban–Pete–Schramm picture of the percolation spectral sample as a clustered set.

## What I would try first

Let $\mathbf S$ be the spectral sample and $Z$ uniform on $\{\pm1\}^B$, coupled by $\Pr[Z=z,\ \mathbf S\setminus B=A]=2^{-|B|}\hat f_z(A)^2$. The restriction identity gives an exact chain rule

$$H(f^2)=\mathbb E_zH(f_z^2)+H(\mathbf S\cap B\mid\mathbf S\setminus B)+\mathrm{MI}(Z;\mathbf S\setminus B),\qquad I(f)=\mathbb E_zI(f_z)+\mathbb E|\mathbf S\cap B|,$$

with $0\le\mathrm{MI}\le H(\mathbf S\cap B\mid\mathbf S\setminus B)$ by the Walsh–Hadamard entropic uncertainty principle. Single coordinates give $H\le\sum_i\mathrm{Inf}_i\log(1/\mathrm{Inf}_i)+O(I)$, and a decision tree gives the average-depth bound (probably folklore; to be checked). For a fixed block the identity uses Booleanity only through $\|f_z\|_2=1$, so the step bound has to use more, as $|\hat f(i)|\le\mathrm{Inf}_i$ does (from $D_if\in\{0,\pm1\}$); that alone makes level 1 cost $O(I)$.

So FEI becomes a charging problem: the cost of locating a piece of the spectral sample must be paid by the influence of its coordinates. In Tribes, locating a selected tribe costs about $\log s\approx w$ bits and the tribe contributes about $w/2$ elements; small sets keep their Fourier weight above level of order $\log(1/\mu)$, and that is the mechanism to localize. Scheme: restrict a random half of the coordinates each round, so influence halves; if every round costs $O(\text{influence removed that round})$, the rounds sum to $O(I)$ and no $\log I$ appears. Tribes is roughly self-similar under halving (surviving tribes have half the width), so it should pass. Rounds stop at restricted influence $O(1)$: a real sub-problem, not a base case.

Why this first: it turns the conjecture into a local inequality testable on every known extremal family before any proof effort, and shows where a $\log I$ can enter.

## What I would avoid

- Coordinate-by-coordinate induction with FEI as the hypothesis. The step loses about $\mathrm{Inf}_i\log(1/\mathrm{Inf}_i)$ when the derivative's spectrum is spread, and nothing at one coordinate pays for it.
- Level-by-level bounds. Majority's level 1 has weight $2/\pi$ spread evenly over $n$ sets; the payment comes from high levels.
- Anything that would hold for bounded real functions: $(x_1+\dots+x_n)/\sqrt n$ has entropy $\log n$ and influence 1.
- Complexity-measure bounds (decision-tree depth, certificates). For majority they are of order $n$ against $I\approx\sqrt n$.
- DNFs and graph properties as warm-ups: each contains a named open problem.
- Small-$n$ numerics as evidence. They are for killing lemmas.

## First concrete step

Hour 1: code $H$, $I$ and every term of the block chain rule with the fast Walsh–Hadamard transform; check the identity and the uncertainty bound to machine precision on all 65,536 functions of four variables and on majority, Tribes, recursive majority-of-3 and iterated tribes up to $n\approx14$; tabulate, per function and block density $p\in\{1/2,1/4,1/I\}$, round cost against influence removed, and $H/I$ at the $O(1)$-influence leaves. Hours 2–3: find where $\log I$ enters KKLMS (my guess: a sum over about $\log I$ restriction scales) and whether conditioning on $\mathbf S\setminus B$ removes it. Hours 3–5: if the per-round charge survives the table, prove it for LTFs, which are closed under restriction, have the regular/critical-index structure, and to my knowledge remain open (random LTFs are done). If it fails, keep the smallest counterexample and move the charge to larger blocks.
