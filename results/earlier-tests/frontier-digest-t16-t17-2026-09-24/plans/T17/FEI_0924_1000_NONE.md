# FEI: research plan for the first hours

**Goal and expectations.** The owner's goal is to prove $H(\hat f^2)\le C\,I(f)$, working alone and continuously. The problem has been open since 1996 and implies Mansour's conjecture, so a complete proof in one session is unlikely. I'll push toward it the whole time but aim for checkable intermediate results. I'll label each claim *proved*, *numerically tested*, or *heuristic*, and keep a log that includes dead ends. Nothing gets called a proof until every step has been checked.

**Known results to build on**
- Kelman–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer–Safra (KKLMS, 2020): a weak form of FEI that loses a $\log I$ factor (spectrum concentrated on $2^{O(I\log I)}$ characters), via random restrictions and hypercontractivity. This is the best general result, and the $\log I$ is the gap.
- O'Donnell–Wright–Zhou (OWZ): symmetric functions and read-once decision trees. O'Donnell–Tan: composition theorem (needs FEI under biased measures) and the lower bound $C>6$.
- Wan–Wright–Wu, Chakraborty et al., Arunachalam et al.: entropy bounded by decision-tree and certificate measures, via chain rules and coding.
- Edge isoperimetry $I\ge 2\alpha\log(1/\alpha)$; KKL, Talagrand, Friedgut's junta theorem.

**What I'll try first: localize the conjecture by peeling one coordinate**
Let $S\sim\hat f^2$ and $f_\pm=f|_{x_i=\pm1}$. Exactly,
- $H(f)=\tfrac12[H(f_+)+H(f_-)]+\mathrm{JS}(\hat f_+^2,\hat f_-^2)+\mathrm{Er}_i$, where $\mathrm{Er}_i=H(\mathbf 1[i\in S]\mid S\setminus\{i\})$;
- $I(f)=\tfrac12[I(f_+)+I(f_-)]+\mathrm{Inf}_i$.

*Sketch, to verify first.*
- With $a=\hat f_+(T)$, $b=\hat f_-(T)$: $\mathrm{JS}\le\tfrac12\sum_T(a^2-b^2)^2/(a^2+b^2)\le\sum_T(a-b)^2=4\,\mathrm{Inf}_i$. So $\mathrm{Er}_i$ is the only dangerous term.
- FEI with $C=D+4$ then follows by induction on $n$ from an **erasure lemma**: $\sum_i\mathrm{Er}_i\le D\,I(f)$ for all Boolean $f$. (Pick an $i$ with $\mathrm{Er}_i\le D\,\mathrm{Inf}_i$.) Conversely, Han's inequality gives $\sum_i\mathrm{Er}_i\le H$.
- Also $\mathrm{Er}_i=\sum_T\widehat{D_if}(T)^2\log\bigl(1+\widehat{E_if}(T)^2/\widehat{D_if}(T)^2\bigr)+O(\mathrm{Inf}_i)$, where $E_if,D_if$ are $\{0,\pm1\}$-valued with complementary supports (non-pivotal and pivotal points for $i$).
- So FEI should be equivalent, up to constants, to $\mathbb E_{S}\sum_{i\in S}\log\bigl(1+\hat f(S\setminus i)^2/\hat f(S)^2\bigr)=O(I)$. In words: deleting an element of the spectral sample rarely raises its coefficient much. This may already be known (it is close to the chain-rule arguments above).

Why first:
- It turns a global entropy bound into a local statement about the pairs $(E_if,D_if)$, and Booleanity enters through their disjoint supports.
- Jensen gives only $\mathrm{Er}_i\le\mathrm{Inf}_i\log(e/\mathrm{Inf}_i)$, the familiar lossy bound. The task becomes beating Jensen *on average over coordinates*. For majority ($n$ odd) this holds exactly: $\mathrm{Er}_i=0$ by parity.
- Every candidate lemma is cheap to test by computer.

Then, in order:
1. Find the sharpest per-coordinate or averaged inequality the data support.
2. Prove it for monotone functions ($D_if\ge0$, $\hat f(\{i\})=\mathrm{Inf}_i$) and for symmetric functions. Both classes are closed under restriction, so the induction stays inside them, and the symmetric case recovers OWZ as a check.
3. Look for a global argument (hypercontractivity applied to $D_if$, KKLMS-style restrictions), and pin down exactly where KKLMS lose $\log I$.

Fallbacks if the general case stalls: min-entropy FEI (a coefficient $\ge2^{-O(I)}$), or new restriction-closed classes such as LTFs.

**Approaches I'll avoid**
- Arguments that use only $|f|\le1$ or $\ell_2$ structure. FEI fails for bounded real $f$: $\frac1n\sum x_i$ has $H/I=2\log n$. Booleanity has to enter.
- Spectral-norm, sparsity, or Rényi-($<1$) bounds. For majority $2\log\|\hat f\|_1=\Theta(n)$, but $I=\Theta(\sqrt n)$.
- Counting $d$-sets inside a Friedgut junta of size $2^{O(I)}$. That costs about $dI$ bits at level $d$ instead of $O(d)$, and KKLMS's refinement of this route still loses $\log I$.
- Counterexample hunting. No finite search can refute "some constant $C$ works", so computation is only for testing lemmas.

**First concrete step (about 1 hour)**
- Write a Python/numpy tool with a fast Walsh–Hadamard transform that computes $H$, $I$, $\mathrm{Inf}_i$, $\mathrm{JS}_i$ and $\mathrm{Er}_i$.
- Check the peeling identity and $\mathrm{JS}_i\le4\,\mathrm{Inf}_i$ to $10^{-12}$ on random $f$, and confirm $H/I\to4$ for AND (base-2 logs).
- Tabulate $\max_f\sum_i\mathrm{Er}_i/I$ and $\max_f\min_i\mathrm{Er}_i/\mathrm{Inf}_i$: exhaustively for $n\le4$, by hill-climbing for $n\le12$, and on AND, tribes, majority, recursive majority, lexicographic/threshold and address functions up to $n\approx20$.
- Meanwhile, write out the reduction proof line by line.
