# FEI research plan (next few hours)

**Framing.** FEI has been open since 1996, and the best general bound is off by a logarithmic factor. A full proof in one session is unlikely. I'll aim for verified progress: a loss-free reformulation, the exact place where the log is lost, and a proof in some new regime.

**Build on** (from memory, re-derived before use):
- Log-lossy baselines: H ≤ Σ_i h(Inf_i) (subadditivity; h is binary entropy) and H = O(I log n) (counting).
- Kelman–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer–Safra: random restrictions plus hypercontractivity concentrate the spectrum on 2^{O(I log I)} characters. Removing that log I is essentially the conjecture.
- Proven cases:
  - symmetric functions and read-once decision trees (O'Donnell–Wright–Zhou);
  - read-once formulas, via O'Donnell–Tan's composition theorem. That theorem needs FEI under biased measures, a hint for the inductive hypothesis.
- Hod's lexicographic functions give C > 6.45, so constants can be generous. These functions and recursive compositions are the stress tests.
- XOR tensorization: H and I are additive over copies on disjoint variables, so H ≤ C·I + o(n) already implies FEI with the same C.
- Booleanity input: D_i f takes values in {−1,0,1}, so Bonami gives Σ_{S∋i} ρ^{2|S|−2} f̂(S)² ≤ Inf_i^{2/(1+ρ²)}.

**Try first: self-predictability of the spectral sample.** Let S ~ f̂², and reveal S in the order of independent uniform times u_i. The chain rule gives exactly

H(f̂²) = Σ_i E H(1[i∈S] | S ∩ {j : u_j < u_i}).

Up to an additive O(I), FEI says a typical element of S costs O(1) bits given the already-revealed part of S.

The posterior is explicit:

Pr[i∈S | revealed part = T] = a/(a+b),

where a and b are the Fourier weights at T of the complementary partial functions D_i f and E_i f, averaged over restrictions of the unrevealed coordinates.

Dyadic form: it suffices that E_J H(S∩J | S∩J′) ≤ C·E|S∩J| at every density 2^{−t}, where J′ is a random half of J; then sum over t. Averaging over J is essential (a multiplexer's data bits break the worst case).

Why first:
- It's exact, with no built-in loss, and local in coordinate and scale.
- Known methods pay ≈log(n/I) bits (log I in KKLMS) per element named "from scratch." FEI needs such namings to be rare, O(1) per "cluster" of S, as in tribes, AND and majority.

Early times (small u) dominate, since conditioning only lowers entropy. Per scale:
- where little is revealed, use junta/KKLMS arguments;
- where much is revealed, look for a "prediction lemma," charging each scale only for new elements (chaining, not a one-shot union bound).

**Avoid:**
- Extending to [−1,1]-valued f. It's false ((x₁+…+xₙ)/n loses a factor of log n), so every step must use Booleanity.
- Coordinate-wise or level-wise subadditivity: it loses a factor of log n on tribes.
- Decision-tree and certificate-complexity bounds: they can be polynomially larger than I (majority: n versus √n).
- Global, one-shot hypercontractivity: it reproduces the Σ Inf_i log(1/Inf_i) and I log I losses.
- Large counterexample searches: well explored, and they don't advance a proof. Small ones only, to falsify lemmas.

**Workflow.**
- Build an exact harness (fast Walsh–Hadamard transform, n ≤ 18) computing H, I and per-scale surprisal profiles.
- Test each candidate lemma before trying to prove it, on tribes, majority, lexicographic functions, compositions, multiplexers and local-search maximizers of H/I.
- Label every claim as proved, tested or conjectured.
- Re-plan every ~2 hours.

**Milestones.**
1. (~1h) Write-ups: chain rule, posterior formula, dyadic reduction, tensorization.
2. (~1h) Surprisal profiles of the extremal examples, locating where the entropy is paid.
3. The top-scale step H(S | S∩J′) ≤ C·I, then its extension to marginals. Alternatively, FEI for a new class, e.g. monotone functions (FMEI follows there from KKL; FEI is open, to my knowledge).
4. Fallback: FMEI, which FEI implies. Up to a factor 2 it's equivalent to Σ_S f̂(S)⁴ = ‖f‖_{U²}⁴ ≥ 2^{−O(I)}.

**First concrete step.** Prove the chain-rule identity, the posterior formula and the dyadic reduction on paper. Then compute the surprisal-versus-u profile for tribes and majority, by hand asymptotically and then numerically.
