# FEI conjecture: research plan (session of 2026-09-25)

**Target.** Owner's goal: prove H(f̂²) ≤ C·I(f) for all Boolean f. The problem has been open for 30 years; I aim for a genuine advance and will label every claim as proved, heuristic or refuted. Nothing counts until written out and checked numerically.

## Known results I build on
- **KKLMS (Kelman–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer–Safra 2020).** Bourgain–Kalai random restrictions plus hypercontractivity give FEI with a log I loss; for example, constant-variance f is concentrated on 2^{O(I log I)} characters. No other general method comes this close.
- **Tools that turn "spread" into influence:** Friedgut's junta theorem, KKL/Talagrand, Bourgain's tail theorem, level-k inequalities.
- **Proven cases and test beds:** symmetric functions and read-once decision trees (O'Donnell–Wright–Zhou); the O'Donnell–Tan composition theorem, which needs biased FEI; random LTFs; compositions forcing C ≥ 6.278.
- **Exact identity:** the spectral sample S ∼ f̂² has I = E|S| and H = H(S). For J ⊆ [n], Pr[S∩J=U, S∖J=V] = F̂_U(V)², where F_U(z) = f̂_{J|z}(U) (coordinates outside J fixed to z).

## What I try first, and why
1. **Tensorization reductions (hour 1).** H and I add over products on disjoint variables, and the surprisal log(1/f̂(S)²) of f^{⊗k} is an i.i.d. sum, so the law of large numbers should make FEI equivalent to: for fixed A, B, c > 0, every f puts Fourier mass ≥ c on coefficients with f̂(S)² ≥ 2^{−A·I(f)−B}. Additive o(I) or o(n) errors should also cost nothing. If this holds, a version of KKLMS's concentration theorem without the log I would already imply FEI. The log I cannot tensorize away, because I(f^{⊗k}) = k·I(f). It also lets me discard bad spectral mass < 1−c.
2. **Main attack (hours 2–4): multiscale restrictions on the constant-mass version.** Reconstruct KKLMS at scale p ≈ 1/I and locate where log I enters (I expect: O(I) summed over ~log I dyadic level ranges). Then treat all scales at once via the exact random-order chain rule H(S) = ∫₀¹ Σ_i E[1_{i∉J_p}·h(Pr[i∈S | S∩J_p])] dp/(1−p), with J_p p-random, h the binary entropy, and Pr[i∈S | S∩J_p=U] = Inf_i(F_U)/‖F_U‖². Goal: bound the pathwise version (surprisal rather than entropy) by I·w(p) with ∫w < ∞, outside bad events of spectral mass < 1−c. Booleanity enters because each (F_U(z))_U is a Boolean function's spectrum.
3. **Fallback (no concrete mechanism by hour 4).** Prove the constant-mass version for a class where FEI is, to my knowledge, open, e.g. regular LTFs. Heuristic: level-k entropy ≈ k·H(w²/‖w‖²) − log k!, and Shannon ≤ Rényi-½ gives H(w²/‖w‖²) ≤ 2 log(‖w‖₁/‖w‖₂) ≈ 2 log I + O(1). This balances against I just as it does for majority.

## Approaches I avoid
- **Arguments valid for [−1,1]-valued f, and fixed-p Fourier ℓ_p/Rényi bounds.** (x₁+…+xₙ)/n has H/I ≈ 2 log n, and majority has ‖f̂‖₁ = 2^{Θ(n)} with I = Θ(√n). Booleanity must be used, and entropy handled at p → 2.
- **Decision-tree, certificate or protocol bounds.** Average depth can be far above I (majority: ≈ n versus √n).
- **Level-by-level or per-coordinate charging.** At bounded levels majority spends ≈ log n entropy per unit influence, paid for by its heavy tail; per-level sums recreate the log I loss. Quick check: per-coordinate FEI with the 1/|T| split fails for f = y·1[Σx ≥ t√n] + 1[Σx < t√n] as n → ∞. The budget has to move across levels and coordinates.
- **Counterexample hunting as the main goal.** The conjecture is widely believed, and extremal ratios come from deep compositions, not small n.

## First concrete step
Write out the tensorization/quantile reduction in full: the LLN step, the constants A, B, c, and why constant-variance hypotheses are harmless (Var f^{⊗k} → 1). In parallel, build a Walsh–Hadamard harness for n ≤ 20, exhaustive for n ≤ 4, that computes H, I and surprisal quantiles for majority, tribes, recursive majority, address functions, compositions, random DNFs and hill-climbed truth tables. Every candidate lemma is tested there before I try to prove it.
