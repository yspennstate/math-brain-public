# P0189: candidate counterexample to Brown-measure continuity under strong convergence

Scope: this addresses the literal general conjecture in van Handel, *The strong convergence phenomenon*, arXiv:2507.00346v2 (11 February 2026), Section 6.9, pp. 69-70. It does not address the narrower conjectures for independent Ginibre matrices or free limiting families.

Primary source for the question: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00346 . Primary source for the established limiting Brown measure: Fang, Jiang, Lin and Xu, *On generalized universal irrational rotation algebras and the operator u+v*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.4771 , Theorem12.2. Its introduction also states simplicity and uniqueness of trace of the irrational rotation algebra. These literature theorems are dependencies, not results proved here.

## Finite matrices

Fix an irrational theta in(0,1). Choose reduced rational approximants p_j/q_j tending to theta, with q_j tending to infinity. Let omega_j=exp(2*pi*i*p_j/q_j). On the standard basis indexed by k modulo q_j define

\[
 U_j e_k=\omega_j^k e_k,\qquad
 S_j e_k=e_{k-1},\qquad
 V_j=e^{\pi i/q_j}S_j.
\]

Then U_j and V_j are unitary and satisfy

\[
 V_jU_j=\omega_jU_jV_j,\qquad U_j^{q_j}=I,\qquad V_j^{q_j}=-I.
\]

Put A_j=U_j+V_j. Its characteristic polynomial is exactly z^{q_j}. Indeed, in the determinant of zI-U_j-V_j only the all-diagonal permutation and the full cyclic permutation contribute (for q_j>=2). The former contributes

\[
 \prod_{k=0}^{q_j-1}(z-\omega_j^k)=z^{q_j}-1,
\]

because omega_j is primitive. The latter contributes minus the product of the cyclic weights, namely -exp(pi*i)=+1. Thus det(zI-A_j)=z^{q_j}. Cayley-Hamilton gives A_j^{q_j}=0, and every empirical eigenvalue distribution is delta_0.

An equivalent algebraic check is the root-of-unity binomial identity (U_j+V_j)^{q_j}=U_j^{q_j}+V_j^{q_j}=0. The determinant calculation supplies the conclusion without relying on that identity.

## Strong convergence of the pair

Let A_theta be the universal unital irrational rotation C*-algebra generated by unitaries u,v satisfying vu=exp(2*pi*i*theta)uv. This algebra is simple and has a unique faithful tracial state tau, as recalled in the cited primary paper.

Consider the norm quotient

\[
 \mathcal Q=\prod_j M_{q_j}(\mathbb C)\big/\bigoplus_j M_{q_j}(\mathbb C),
\]

where the direct sum consists of sequences whose operator norms tend to zero. The classes of (U_j) and (V_j) are unitaries and satisfy the irrational relation exactly, since omega_j tends to exp(2*pi*i*theta). Universality therefore gives a unital *-homomorphism A_theta to Q. Simplicity makes it injective, hence isometric. Since the quotient norm of a bounded sequence is its limsup norm, for every noncommutative *-polynomial P,

\[
 \limsup_j\|P(U_j,V_j,U_j^*,V_j^*)\|
 =\|P(u,v,u^*,v^*)\|.
\]

The same argument applies to every subsequence. If the norms failed to converge to the displayed value, a subsequence uniformly separated from that value would contradict the same limsup identity. Consequently the pair converges strongly.

The normalized traces also converge to tau. Using the commutation relation, every fixed word is a phase times U_j^a V_j^b. When b is a fixed nonzero integer and j is large, the shift has no diagonal entries, so its trace is zero. If b=0 and a is fixed nonzero, the geometric sum of omega_j^{ak} is zero for all q_j>|a|. The identity word has trace one, and phases converge. These are exactly the canonical irrational-rotation trace values. Thus both norm and tracial versions of strong convergence are satisfied, with a faithful limiting trace.

In particular A_j converges strongly, including all *-polynomials in A_j alone, to a=u+v.

The survey Definition 1.1 also allows fixed finite-dimensional matrix coefficients. For each fixed D, amplify the quotient homomorphism to M_D(A_theta). An injective *-homomorphism is completely isometric, and M_D(Q) identifies with the quotient of the product of M_D(M_qj) by norm-null sequences. Therefore the same limsup identity and subsequence argument apply to every D-by-D matrix-valued *-polynomial. The identification follows entrywise in finite D: a matrix sequence tends to zero in norm exactly when all its entries do, and the quotient norm is the limsup of the block-matrix norms. Thus the precise matrix-coefficient definition is covered.

A separate norm-convergence proof uses the trace computation above for the lower bound and needs no appeal to simplicity. Write P_j for a fixed matrix-valued polynomial and P for its limit. For every positive integer m, with normalized traces on the matrix factors,
\[
 \|P_j\|\ge\big[(\mathrm{tr}_D\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{q_j})((P_j^*P_j)^m)\big]^{1/(2m)}.
\]
Taking the lower limit in j and then m to infinity gives liminf ||P_j|| >= ||P||, because the limiting trace tr_D tensor tau is faithful. Complete contractivity of the universal quotient homomorphism gives limsup ||P_j|| <= ||P||. This independently proves the required full norm convergence. The parent suggested these two explicit extensions during its first bounded review on 6 September 2026; the author checked and incorporated them. The remaining review and novelty status is pending.

## The Brown-measure mismatch

The cited Theorem12.2 states that the Brown measure of u+v, in the tracial von Neumann algebra generated by the irrational rotation pair, is Haar probability measure on the unit circle. It is therefore different from delta_0. The finite empirical measures are all delta_0 and do not converge to the Brown measure of a, despite strong convergence.

The same example also prevents a universal lower spectral-radius conclusion: every A_j has spectral radius zero, whereas the source Theorem11.1 gives spectrum of u+v equal to the closed unit disk and hence spectral radius one.

To make the matrices random rather than deterministic, take Haar unitary Q_j and replace the pair by (Q_j U_j Q_j*,Q_j V_j Q_j*). For every outcome, norms, normalized traces, nilpotency, and eigenvalue distributions are unchanged. This supplies genuinely random families satisfying the same convergence. The two members are dependent; the construction does not claim otherwise. Equivalently, the single random matrix Q_j A_j Q_j* is already a counterexample for a one-variable polynomial.

## Optional realization in every integer dimension

The dimensions q_j suffice for a counterexample along a sequence tending to infinity. If an all-integer-dimension formulation is required, take consecutive continued-fraction denominators, which are coprime. For each sufficiently large N, choose the largest j with q_j*q_{j+1}<=N. The elementary coin theorem gives N=a*q_j+b*q_{j+1} for nonnegative integers a,b. Define the N-dimensional pair as the block direct sum of a copies of the j-th pair and b copies of the(j+1)-st pair. Both block dimensions tend to infinity as N does. Polynomial norms are the maximum of their block norms, traces are convex combinations of block traces, and the sum remains nilpotent. Thus the same counterexample exists with dimension exactly N for every sufficiently large integer N.

## Scope and remaining checks

This is a candidate refutation of the survey's literal implication from arbitrary strong convergence to empirical Brown-measure convergence. It is not a refutation for independent-entry ensembles, free limits, or holomorphic polynomials in independent Ginibre matrices. Yi Han's June2026 paper, arXiv2606.01664, claims the latter case and is compatible with this construction.

Load-bearing obligations are the precise survey scope, the primitive-root determinant sign, isometry of the quotient homomorphism, faithful-trace interpretation, and the cited Brown-measure theorem. All are explicit here. It remains possible that the construction or the general failure of continuity is already known; current-literature novelty is unverified. Numerical eigenvalues of these nilpotent matrices would be an unreliable check because of their extreme nonnormality.
