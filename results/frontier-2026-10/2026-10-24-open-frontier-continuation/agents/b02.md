# B02 — T268, product-only spectral control with a fixed smooth activation

Date: 2026-10-23 UTC. Status: **two rigorous scoped results**: a negative product-only result by reduction to a published ReLU theorem, and a positive depth/width/dimension-independent Rademacher theorem when every individual layer has norm at most 1, for one fixed entire analytic activation. Neither is a general result for all smooth activations or arbitrary individual budgets. External novelty has not been certified.

## Provenance and target selection

The live catalog `06_math_research/top200_probability_2026_10_14/list.jsonl` row T268 asks if spectral norms of all layers, with a bound on their product, can give sample complexity independent of width and depth. Its `frontier/rows/T268/DOSSIER.md` says the ReLU status needs checking and identifies smooth activations as a possible remaining gap. The row's `moves.jsonl` only records that broad preliminary triage. In fact Vardi–Shamir–Srebro, *The Sample Complexity of One-Hidden-Layer Neural Networks*, [arXiv:2202.06233](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.06233), Theorem 1 and discussion on pp. 4–5, explicitly include **ReLU** with their parameter alpha=1, with width-dependent fat-shattering under two separate spectral-norm budgets. Thus the unrestricted T268 yes/no question is already **no** in a dimension-free formulation; do not report that as a new solution. Golowich–Rakhlin–Shamir, [arXiv:1712.06541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.06541), section 5 and introduction, also give spectral-norm width lower bounds.

The narrower target here: Does *smoothness alone* rescue a **dimension-free, width-independent bound expressed only through the product of spectral budgets**, even at depth two? Vardi–Shamir–Srebro Theorem 4 gives a positive bound for an analytic activation with the first layer norm bounded by a fixed number `B` and output vector norm by a fixed `b`, with a dependence on `B` beyond `bB`; Theorem 5 treats power activations at deeper networks. Their theorem does not imply a bound depending on `bB` alone. A targeted 23 Oct web check found no primary source asserting such a product-only positive theorem. The following elementary reduction closes that precisely stated product-only question negatively. It also explains why individual spectral budgets cannot silently be collapsed into their product for nonhomogeneous smooth activations.

T272 was not duplicated: its recent `moves.jsonl` includes w714-T272-C002, a bounded smooth finite-horizon depth-three SGD claim, even though its DOSSIER still highlights trained depth three as open. T279's `moves.jsonl` already contains the orbit-minimized Hessian trace identity; literature also contains flat-but-memorizing counterexamples. The `codex30_frontier_2026_10_23/CLAIMS.json` and `DIRECTIONS.md` distinguish completed T228 and scoped T336 work from unresolved goals. No repository write was made.

## Exact derivation

Define one **fixed** activation, independent of width, input dimension and budget,

\[
 \rho(t)=\tfrac12\left(t+\int_0^t \operatorname{erf}(s)\,ds\right).
\]

It is entire analytic, `rho(0)=0`, and `rho'(t)=(1+erf(t))/2` belongs to `(0,1)`, hence it is 1-Lipschitz. It is exactly the smooth ReLU-type activation of Vardi–Shamir–Srebro Example 3 with `r=1`, so their positive theorem for each fixed individual budget applies (with finite, potentially very large bound). Put `C=1/(2 sqrt(pi))`. Since `integral_0^infty erfc(s) ds=1/sqrt(pi)`, direct integration for positive and negative `t` gives

\[
 \sup_{t\in\mathbb R}|\rho(t)-[t]_+|\le C,
 \qquad
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}|K^{-1}\rho(Kz)-[z]_+|\le C/K
 \quad(K>0).                                      \tag{1}
\]

Fix any width `h`, matrix `W in R^{h x d}` with `||W||_op <= B`, and output vector `a in R^h` with `||a||_2 <= b`. Let `f(x)=a^T [Wx]_+` and construct a **smooth** two-layer network

\[
 f_K(x)= (a/K)^T\rho(KWx).
\]

The two operator norms obey `||KW||_op <= KB` and `||a/K||_2 <= b/K`, whose **product is exactly bounded by `bB` for every K**. By (1) and Cauchy–Schwarz, uniformly for all `x in R^d`,

\[
 |f_K(x)-f(x)|\le C\|a\|_1/K\le Cb\sqrt h/K.   \tag{2}
\]

In particular, if an `h`-wide ReLU network realizes any dichotomy on `N` inputs with threshold margins `gamma>0`, choose the same `K >= 2 C b sqrt(h)/gamma` for all dichotomies. Then its smooth counterpart has margins at least `gamma/2`, while its product of spectral budgets remains `bB`. The thresholds do not change. Thus, writing `F_rho(h,P)` for the union over *all* layer budgets `B_1,B_2` with `B_1 B_2 <= P`,

\[
 \mathrm{fat}_{\gamma/2}(F_\rho(h,bB))
 \ge \mathrm{fat}_{\gamma}(F_{\mathrm{ReLU}}(h;b,B)). \tag{3}
\]

Vardi–Shamir–Srebro Theorem 1 gives, for `b=B=R=1`, a sufficiently small fixed `gamma_0>0` and a sufficiently large input dimension `d(h)` (their dimension threshold can grow polynomially in `h`), `fat_{gamma_0}(F_ReLU(h;1,1)) >= c h` for some universal positive `c`. Its technical lower-bound-size condition is met by first choosing `gamma_0` small enough and then `h` large. Apply (3): `fat_{gamma_0/2}(F_rho(h,1)) >= c h`, already with **one hidden layer and one fixed analytic 1-Lipschitz activation**, despite spectral-norm product at most 1. Equivalently the empirical Rademacher complexity on a corresponding shattered sample of size `N >= ch` is at least `gamma_0/2`: choose the network for each Rademacher sign vector, sum its margin inequalities, and observe that the expectation of the threshold term vanishes.

Therefore no dimension-free uniform convergence/sample-complexity guarantee depending only on the product `P` can be independent of width for this smooth activation. The individual budgets `B_1=K`, `B_2=1/K` vary with width; this is exactly the distinction from Vardi–Shamir–Srebro's fixed-`b`, fixed-`B` smooth positive theorem. The proof establishes no impossibility when every layer norm is separately bounded by a fixed constant (e.g. both at most 1), and no statement about an algorithm's implicit bias or average-case generalization. A fixed input dimension may admit a dimension-dependent bound by compactness and the global Lipschitz property; the counterexample uses growing `d(h)`. It refutes a *dimension-free* width/depth claim, which is the notion in the cited spectral-norm theorems.

## A positive all-depth theorem under **separate** spectral budgets

Here is a genuinely different, stricter regime. Suppose every hidden weight matrix individually satisfies `||W_l||_op <= 1`, the final scalar readout vector satisfies `||a||_2 <= 1`, the input norm is at most 1, there are no biases, and every hidden layer uses the **same** `rho` above. The class is the union over all finite widths and all finite depths `L >= 1`. Then its empirical Rademacher complexity on **any** `n` sample inputs is at most

\[
   \frac{S}{(1-S)\sqrt n}, \quad
   S=\tfrac12+\frac1{\sqrt\pi}
     \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{j!(2j+1)(2j+2)}<1.       \tag{4}
\]

If depth zero (a linear readout on input) is included, add `1/sqrt(n)` to the RHS. This is genuinely independent of input dimension, width **and depth**. The proof is constructive and uses a universal Hilbert feature lift rather than a bound on the number of parameters.

First, the absolutely summed coefficients of the Maclaurin series are `S`: 

\[
  \rho(t)=\tfrac12t+\frac1{\sqrt\pi}
       \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^j t^{2j+2}}
       {j!(2j+1)(2j+2)}.
\]

In particular, `S < 1/2+(e-2)/sqrt(pi) < 1`, since the summation equals `integral_0^1 (1-u)e^{u^2}du`, which is less than `integral_0^1 (1-u)e^u du=e-2`. The last strict inequality follows, for example, from `e<11/4` and `pi>3`.

Let `rho(t)=sum_{k>=1} c_k t^k`, so `sum |c_k|=S`. For an arbitrary Hilbert space `H`, define another Hilbert space `H' = direct_sum_{k>=1} H^{tensor k}`. Define the feature lift

\[
 \Phi'(x)=\bigoplus_{k\ge1}\sqrt{|c_k|}\,\Phi(x)^{\otimes k}.
\]

Starting with `H_0=R^d` and `Phi_0(x)=x`, repeat this lift to construct `H_l,Phi_l` for each `l`, **without reference to any trained weights**. If `||Phi_{l-1}(x)||<=1`, then

\[
  \|\Phi_l(x)\|^2=\sum_{k\ge1}|c_k|
              \|\Phi_{l-1}(x)\|^{2k}
     \le S\|\Phi_{l-1}(x)\|^2\le S^l.        \tag{5}
\]

For every network, its hidden vector `h_l(x)` can be expressed as `A_l Phi_l(x)`, with a bounded linear operator `A_l:H_l -> R^{m_l}` satisfying `||A_l|| <= S^{l/2}`. Set `A_0=I`. To prove the induction, for each `k` let `D_k:(R^{m_l})^{tensor k}->R^{m_l}` select the diagonal tensor coordinates, `D_k(e_{i_1} tensor ... tensor e_{i_k})=e_i` if all indices are `i`, and zero otherwise. It is an orthogonal-coordinate projection of operator norm 1. With `W_l` the next layer's matrix, define `A_l` on the `k`-th direct-sum block by

\[
 A_{l,k}=\operatorname{sgn}(c_k)\sqrt{|c_k|}\,
       D_k(W_l A_{l-1})^{\otimes k}.           \tag{6}
\]

Then `A_l Phi_l(x) = sum_k c_k D_k (W_l h_{l-1}(x))^{tensor k} = rho(W_l h_{l-1}(x))` coordinatewise, and the horizontal block-operator estimate gives

\[
 \|A_l\|^2\le\sum_{k\ge1}|c_k|\|W_l A_{l-1}\|^{2k}
      \le S\|A_{l-1}\|^2\le S^l.            \tag{7}
\]

For the final output `f(x)=a^T h_L(x)=<A_L^*a,Phi_L(x)>`, the coefficient norm is at most `S^{L/2}` and each sample feature norm at most `S^{L/2}`. Therefore, for the class `F_L` at each **fixed** depth, the usual Hilbert-ball calculation yields

\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak R}_n(F_L)
 \le\frac{S^{L/2}}n
        \left(\sum_{i=1}^n\|\Phi_L(x_i)\|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le S^L/\sqrt n.                              \tag{8}
\]

One cannot merely take the supremum of these expected bounds when depth is allowed to vary: the supremum over `L` lies **inside** the Rademacher expectation. Instead, all depth-specific suprema are nonnegative (the zero output vector is allowed), so the supremum over the union is at most their **sum**, and Tonelli plus the geometric series in (8) proves (4). This handles the infinite union over depths without a hidden `log L` penalty. Standard contraction transfers the bound to any fixed 1-Lipschitz bounded loss; standard uniform-deviation terms also include confidence/sample terms. This theorem concerns uniform convergence, not SGD dynamics.

The condition `S<1` is sufficient rather than necessary. More generally the same proof works for any fixed analytic `sigma(t)=sum_{k>=1}c_k t^k` with `sum |c_k|=S<1`, inputs of norm at most 1, no biases and each individual spectral norm at most 1. It fails to cover arbitrary products of layer bounds because (7) then contains `sum |c_k| ||W_l A_{l-1}||^{2k}`. The negative result above exploits precisely an unbounded first-layer norm balanced by a tiny output norm. At input radius greater than 1 or for activations whose coefficient majorant at radius 1 is at least 1, a different scaling argument is required.

## What to investigate next

Extend the Hilbert lift to explicit nonunit individual budgets or smooth functions with `sum |c_k|>=1`, while retaining a summable depth tail. A result whose constant depends solely on the product of budgets is impossible by (1)–(3). **Priority caveat:** Shilton–Gupta–Rana–Venkatesh, [NeurIPS 2025, *Reproducing Kernel Banach Space Models for Neural Networks with Application to Rademacher Complexity Analysis*](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/652efa5ba15928e48577f76a786faf27-Paper-Conference.pdf), Corollary 5, already asserts width- and depth-independent bounds under sufficiently small individual spectral budgets, by a different feature representation and a broader activation class. This may subsume the *phenomenon* of (4), though the exact `S` criterion, infinite-depth union quantifier, and constant have not been compared line by line. Treat (4) as a self-contained scoped proof, **not a priority or publication claim**. Vardi–Shamir–Srebro Theorem 5 gives a deep result for power activations, whereas their Theorem 4 is shallow. The present all-depth union theorem is a mathematical derivation here, not an externally peer-reviewed fact.
