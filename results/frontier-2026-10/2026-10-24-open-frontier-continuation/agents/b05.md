## Rank 1: T262, unshaped ReLU angle law — OPEN, unassigned

**Exact model/target.** Width `n`, depth `L=⌊tn⌋`, independent He Gaussian weights, no biases, two fixed inputs with initial angle `θ₀∈(0,π)`; prove convergence of `φ_L=n θ_L` jointly with the log output norm. The dossier's candidate is

```
d log φ_s = (−1−φ_s/(3π)) ds + √2 dB₁(s),   φ_0=+∞ (entrance),
d log ||h_s||² = −5/2 ds + √5 dB₂(s),
d⟨B₁,B₂⟩_s = −2/√10 ds.
```

Equivalently `u_s=1/φ_s` starts at zero and solves `du_s=(2u_s+1/(3π))ds−√2 u_s dB₁(s)`. Thus `L θ_L ⇒ 3π/A_t`, where `A_t=t⁻¹ ∫₀ᵗ exp(s+√2 W_s)ds`. In the row's TARGETS this is target 1; the scalar angle law is still a proposed law, and the joint SDE is identified numerically, not proved. Target 2, the `p≥3` tangent Gram matrix diffusion, is also open despite a measured one-step covariance/drift; target 3 critical tanh remains a numerical prediction. The precise **unpaid lemma** is the uniform one-layer conditional generator, including rare ReLU sign flips and the entrance from ordinary fixed angle into `θ~1/n`. Proving this plus a martingale-problem tightness argument would settle a real row target, not merely a special case.

**My initial attack and a concrete repair.** Let `Θ_n(θ)` be the exact angle after one Gaussian/ReLU layer, `u=1/(nθ)` and `u'=1/(nΘ_n(θ))`. First prove, for fixed `K`, sufficiently small `ε`, and `1/(nε)≤u≤K`,

```
|n E(u'−u)−(2u+1/(3π))| ≤ C_K ε+o_n(1),
|n E((u'−u)²)−2u²| ≤ C_K ε+o_n(1),
n E|u'−u|³=o_n(1),
```

with a stopping/truncation for the exponentially rare zero-output layer. Split into the active tangent Gaussian term and flip residual as T244 does. Conditional on `k` active coordinates, the tangent ratio `R=√(χ²_{k−1}/χ²_k)` has **exact** independent-chi-square moments `E R=1` for `k≥2` and `E R⁻¹=(k−1)/(k−2)` for `k≥3`; hence the reciprocal's `2u` drift comes from an exact identity, while the `1/(3π)` drift must be isolated entirely from flip geometry. This is a useful analytic starting identity, not a proof of the full lemma. For three inputs the same active-set split yields a matrix Wishart fluctuation and the pairwise cubic-distance drift; after the scalar lemma, prove joint centered moment bounds for a fixed number of tangent coordinates.

**Gap in the written outline to avoid inheriting.** T244 “Step 4 written,” part (b), invokes `E|log(φ'/φ)|³=O(n⁻³ᐟ²)` *uniformly for `θ≤ε`*. At a fixed small `θ>0`, the deterministic ReLU kernel map has `log(F(θ)/θ)≈−θ/(3π)`, so the raw third absolute moment is order `θ³`, not `n⁻³ᐟ²`. The plausible correct estimate is `C(n⁻³ᐟ²+θ³)` (or a centered third moment of order `n⁻³ᐟ²`); multiplying by `u³` in the reciprocal increment changes `u³θ³` to `n⁻³`, sufficient for the displayed generator condition on compact `u`. Separately verify the no-return/entrance bound rather than importing the unproved supermartingale claim wholesale. A proof that leaves this uniformity uncorrected is not referee-ready.

## Rank 2: T253, minimal-width first-layer NTK hard edge — OPEN, unassigned

**Exact target.** Inputs `x_i` uniform on radius-`√d` sphere, `n=d²`, two-layer ReLU with `m` hidden units and fixed output signs; `K_ij=(x_i·x_j/d)m⁻¹Σ_a 1_{w_a·x_i>0}1_{w_a·x_j>0}`, `md/n→γ>1`. Prove a nonzero deterministic limit of `λ_min(K)/λ_min(K_∞)` without a polylog width and identify the function of `γ`, including its hard-edge exponent. The dossier predicts a generalized Marchenko–Pastur lower edge with population law `H₂=(1/2)δ_a+(1/2)Law(a+X/π)`, `a=1/4−1/(2π)`, `X~MP(1/2)`, so `r₂(γ)~0.3833(γ−1)²`; these are computations/conjectures, not established network limits. The original `k=1` (`n=qd`) fixed-`γ` assertion is **false**: bounded `m` leaves zero rows with high probability. Work only on `k=2`, not the refuted portion.

**Unpaid lemma.** Conditional on Gaussian weights `W`, rows of the Jacobian are iid structured vectors `φ_W(x)` with population covariance `Σ_W`. Need (i) a no-eigenvalue-below-the-edge theorem at `md/n→γ>1` for these discontinuous, non-Gaussian rows (quadratic-form concentration and lower-tail control at the hard edge), and (ii) convergence of the empirical spectrum and its bottom for `Σ_W` to the proposed `H₂` edge. The existing data-side surrogate agrees within 1–3% at finite sizes; this does not imply universality. An attack would begin with a resolvent leave-one-out quadratic-form variance bound conditional on `W`, separating low-degree spherical harmonics from the isotropic high-degree part and proving the bound uniformly as the spectral parameter approaches the proposed lower edge.

## Rank 3: T267, nonlinear epoch-wise double descent — OPEN, unassigned

**Exact target.** In a two-layer network with a genuinely nonlinear activation, both layers trained from standard initialization on a noisy teacher model, prove the test-risk curve has two descents in training time and locate the intervening peak as a function of sample size/noise/width. For the row's numerical candidate: a two-scale ReLU teacher, `d_A=60`, `d_B=5`, slow input scale `s=0.05`, `m=8`, `n=2000`, noise SD `σ=0.5`, full-batch GD; six-seed averaged risk is `0.406` (step 181) → `0.453` (step 1115) → `0.329` (~step 5300). This is a finite simulation, not a theorem. `σ=1` did not show the second descent, and symmetric/overparameterized arms had single-U behavior; the linear models and layer-wise-speed network in Heckel–Yilmaz are already solved and cannot count.

**Unpaid lemma.** A two-timescale trajectory approximation precise enough to preserve the *sign* of risk change on three time windows, including the label-noise fitting term and the nonlinear feature drift simultaneously. A leading-order risk upper bound alone is insufficient. The best first theorem could use the asymmetry in `d_A,d_B` and a singular slow scale to compare with a fast conditional least-squares process, then control feature drift/remainder uniformly until the peak; nevertheless this is substantially farther from proof than T262.
