## Preliminary derivation for T084: exact low-fugacity coefficient

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph with `n=|V|`. For distinct edges e,f, let `L` be the length of the shortest simple cycle containing both, and `N_L` the number of such cycles. If none exists, the corresponding edge indicators are independent at all fugacities (graphic-matroid direct-sum factorization). Write `R_ef(Z)=(partial_e Z)(partial_f Z)-Z partial_e partial_f Z`, taking the multivariate derivatives **before** setting all edge variables to lambda.

For the forest polynomial `Z_F(z)=sum_{A forest} prod_{g in A} z_g`, I obtain the formal expansion

`R_ef(Z_F)(lambda 1) = N_L lambda^(L-2) + O(lambda^(L-1))`.

For the random-cluster polynomial `Z_RC(z)=sum_{A subset E} q^{k(A)} prod_{g in A} z_g`, at each fixed `0<q<1`, the corresponding expansion is

`R_ef(Z_RC)(lambda 1) = (1-q) q^(2n-L) N_L lambda^(L-2) + O_{G,q}(lambda^(L-1))`.

**Proof sketch with all cancellations:** `R_ef(Z)=-Z^2 partial_e partial_f log Z`. The coefficient of a monomial supported on S in the logarithm depends only on the restriction to S. For a graphic-matroid restriction, the partition function factorizes over matroid-connected components, since `k(A)=n-|A|+c(A)` and cycle rank c is additive; so a mixed logarithmic coefficient involving e and f vanishes unless the restriction contains a circuit through both. (The standard circuit-elimination characterization says two elements are in the same matroid component iff some circuit contains both.) Therefore a mixed term below total degree L is zero, even if variables are repeated. At total degree L, the only possible support is a shortest common circuit C, with every variable used once. The forest restriction to C has `Z_F|C=prod_{g in C}(1+z_g)-prod_{g in C}z_g`, hence `[z_C] log Z_F=-1`. The random-cluster restriction is `q^n{prod_{g in C}(1+z_g/q)-(1-q)prod_{g in C}(z_g/q)}`, hence `[z_C]log Z_RC=-(1-q)q^{-L}`. Multiply by the zero-fugacity constant `Z_F(0)^2=1` or `Z_RC(0)^2=q^{2n}` after the two derivatives. This proves the expansions and strict negative covariance for each fixed pair at sufficiently small positive lambda. It does **not** give a uniform interval across growing graphs or settle lambda=1.

An explicit, deliberately crude window follows without assuming coefficientwise positivity. If `m=|E|`, the sum of absolute coefficients of either Rayleigh polynomial is at most `2*4^m`, because each partition function and each first/second derivative has coefficient 1-norm at most `2^m` for `0<q<=1`. After the leading term `c lambda^(L-2)`, the remainder has magnitude at most `2*4^m lambda^(L-1)` for `0<lambda<=1`. Thus `R_F>0` when `lambda<=1/(4*4^m)`, and `R_RC>0` when `lambda<=(1-q)q^(2n-3)/(4*4^m)`; in both cases the leading coefficient dominates by at least a factor two. These exponential windows are far from the K_n window of order `1/n` where the existing proof gap lies.

Direct exact checks: an independent Python enumeration in `t084_probe.py` confirms this leading-coefficient formula for all pairs in K3–K6 and 15 random graphs at each n=3–6, plus 30 seven-vertex, 40 eight-vertex, and 15 nine-vertex sparse graphs. For K4, an adjacent pair has `R=lambda+8lambda^2+19lambda^3+16lambda^4`; a disjoint pair has `R=2lambda^2+4lambda^3`, matching one shortest triangle and two shortest four-cycles. The same enumeration found no negative *ordinary* coefficient in these samples, but that stronger empirical observation is **not a theorem** and overlaps the dossier's stronger unreviewed Bernstein experiments. The derivation above is an endpoint lemma, not a new solution or a priority claim.

One possible next attack: use the exact first nonzero coefficient as an anchor for an interval certificate in `q` and `lambda` on K_n, joining it to C022's `lambda >= (1-q)+sqrt(1-q)` region. The dossier already reports positivity of a Bernstein-in-`1-q` expansion through n=16; the missing step is a uniform-in-n sign argument, not another finite-n scan. For arbitrary graphs, a proof that all Bernstein coefficients remain nonnegative under a valid graph operation would be stronger than the row itself, so it must be checked against the known non-graphic Seymour–Welsh matroid counterexample and should not be assumed. A simple low-lambda asymptotic cannot bridge the size-dependent critical window `lambda=Theta(1/n)`.
