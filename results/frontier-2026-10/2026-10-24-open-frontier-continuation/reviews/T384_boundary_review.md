## Marked law and independence

The tail measure for `W/m^(1/α)` is `αc_α|z|^(−1−α) dz` on **each** half-axis; multiplying by the unscaled independent mark law `P_U` gives the stated marked PPP. The product-jump one-sided tail is `αc_α² t^(−α)log t`; since `log((m log m)^(1/α))/log m→1/α`, the normalized product-jump tail is `c_α² x^(−α)` on each sign. This yields the symmetric output exponent `c_α|t|^α`. The square tail is `c_α x^(−α/2)` and gives the displayed exponent `c_α Γ(1−α/2)s^(α/2)`.

Independence of the *whole marked process* `N`, product sum `F`, and square sum `C` follows from the one-sample mixed-transform argument, not just from their marginal limits. On `|W|>ηm^(1/α)`, a retained product jump requires a mark of order `(log m)^(1/α)` unless `|W|/m^(1/α)` itself escapes a subsequently removable `L`; a retained square jump requires `U` of order `m^(1/α)`. Retained product and square jumps likewise have vanishing overlap after the `U/m^(1/α)` split. Thus all mixed terms vanish at fixed cutoffs. The discarded product jumps are independent, mean zero (including for `α≥1`, because symmetric truncation makes each truncated summand integrable and centered), with variance at most `O(ε^(2−α))`; discarded positive-square jumps have expectation `O(δ^(1−α/2))`. Removing cutoffs preserves factorization. The mark escape estimate is enough to upgrade compact-mark point-process convergence for functionals retaining all marks above a positive `|z|` threshold.

## Right continuity, infinite small jumps, and `J₁`

Conditional on finite `r₀=y−F≠0`, the total initial first-layer mass `M=Σ_{(z,u)∈N}z²1_{u>0}` is finite almost surely: there are finitely many atoms at `|z|>1` and the expected mass at `|z|≤1` is `2αc_α/(2−α)`. Each active atom contributes a right-continuous nonincreasing gate with a strictly positive death time if opposed, or survives forever if supporting. The entire sum is right-continuous even at **zero** by dominated convergence against the summable weights `z²1_{u>0}`. At every other time it is right-continuous by the same argument; monotonicity supplies left limits. Equivalently, discard `|z|≤ε`: the supremum path error is at most `Σ_{|z|≤ε}z²`, which tends to zero almost surely and in mean. Hence the limit is a well-defined `D([0,S])` element even when death times accumulate at zero or are dense.

The phrase “pure-jump process (possibly with infinitely many small jumps)” in the current boundary note is apt. It should **not** be replaced with “finite step process.” Indeed, for `α≥1`, every nonempty positive time interval has infinitely many death times almost surely, conditional on `r₀≠0`: the stable mark density is positive at zero, and for small opposing `|z|` the intensity of death times in `(a,b)` is comparable to `∫₀^ε z^(−α)dz=∞`. Their summed jump sizes remain finite. This observation poses no `J₁` obstruction because the truncated paths are uniformly close in kernel value.

For fixed `ε>0`, make the existing finite-atom alignment statement fully formal by first retaining `ε<|z|<R` and `|u|<M`, with deterministic continuity cutoffs. On that compact set, vague point-process convergence matches a finite collection of atoms and the joint convergence includes `F_m,C_m`. Conditional on `F`, stable `U` marks have no atoms, so almost surely there is no mark at `u=0`, no death at `0` or `S`, and no two retained opposed death times coincide. The random threshold is continuous here: `r_{0m}=y−F_m→r₀≠0` under the joint-limit coupling, giving eventually constant sign. The scaled clocks satisfy `sup_{s≤S}|θ_m(s)−|r_{0m}|s|→0` in probability, while `m^(1/α)tanh q_m(s/L_m)−θ_m(s)→0` uniformly. More explicitly, `θ_m` is continuous and strictly increasing whenever `r_{0m}≠0`; uniform convergence to the positive-slope line makes each retained opposing gate's unique hitting time converge to `u/(|r₀z|)`. The matched jump sizes converge, and a homeomorphism aligning finitely many hits proves finite-atom `J₁` convergence. The exceptional finite-width event `r_{0m}=0` (possible for `y=0` when all neurons are inactive) has vanishing probability and is harmless.
