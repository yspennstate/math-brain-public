# Independent review: T384 standard-coordinate multi-example dynamics

**Disposition: pass under the stated assumptions.** I independently derived and checked `research/T384_orthogonal_dynamics.md` for fixed `k=d`, exact inputs `x_j=e_j` in the iid stable coordinate basis, loss `||y-f||²/2`, the paper's width and learning-rate scales, and an explicitly absorbing ReLU solution at every reached zero. This review addresses the pathwise dynamics and conditional composition with `research/T384_orthogonal_initialization.md`; it does not assert the result for arbitrary rotated orthogonal data or review the latter note's full triangular-array proof.

The exact tangent kernel is `K_m=diag(A_{j,m})+C_m`, with `C_{jℓ,m}=m^(−2/α)Σ_i(U_{ij})_+(U_{iℓ})_+`. The first-layer cross entries vanish **exactly** because the training inputs are coordinate vectors. If `z_i=(W_i,(U_{ij})_+)_j`, its active-coordinate matrix is a symmetric star matrix with norm at most `||r(t)||₂`. Positivity of `K_m` gives `||r(t)||₂≤||r(0)||₂`, so the Grönwall bound in equation (4) follows, including the per-neuron uniform displacement. Tightness of `S_m=m^(−2/α)Σ_i(W_i(0)²+Σ_jU_{ij}(0)²)` and of `||r(0)||₂` makes the total squared-weight and second-layer Gram drift `o_P(1)` on every finite horizon. On `S_m≤K,||r(0)||≤R`, it also gives `||K_m(t)||op≤2K` for large `m`.

The off-diagonal estimate is valid for the entire `0<α<2` range. For `α/2<p<min(α,1)`, the product of independent positive stable coordinates has a finite `p`-moment, and the concavity inequality bounds `P(C_{jℓ,m}(0)>ε)` by a constant times `m^(1−2p/α)→0`. Uniform Gram drift then makes `sup_{t≤T}||C_m(t)−diag C_m(t)||op=o_P(1)`. The variation-of-constants formula for each residual coordinate has diagonal factor at least `c e^(−2KT)` whenever `|r_j(0)|≥c`; its off-diagonal forcing is at most `RTζ_m=o_P(1)`. Thus **that coordinate** retains its sign and a positive lower magnitude throughout `[0,T]`, even if some other residual coordinate starts close to zero.

For fixed `ε>0`, equation (4) yields `sup_t max_i|W_i(t)−W_i(0)|≤β_mRT e^(β_mRT)m^(1/α)√K=o(m^(1/α))` on the bounded event. Every `|W_i(0)|≥εm^(1/α)` therefore keeps its sign, regardless of the smaller neurons' interactions. A supporting active gate remains open. An opposing one that survives to `δ` needs `U_{ij}(0)≥dε(log m)^(1/α)` by integrating its negative velocity, and independence of initialized `W_i,U_{ij}` makes the probability that *any* extreme neuron satisfies this `O(1/log m)`. The normalized mass of `|W_i(0)|<εm^(1/α)` has expectation `O(ε^(2−α))`. These facts justify the uniform positive-time comparison of each first-layer diagonal with its selected initial mass, followed by `ε↓0` and then removal of the bounded-event cutoffs.

For kernel convergence, marginal atomlessness of each limiting `F_j` and finite `k` remove the simultaneous sign-separation cutoff. For the stronger uniform output comparison, the proof correctly avoids that assumption: coordinates with `|r_j(0)|<c` contribute at most `2c+RTζ_m`; every other coordinate admits the diagonal rate comparison, with initial interval cost `O(Kδ)`. Taking `m→∞`, `δ↓0`, and `c↓0` gives equation (1). No joint initialization limit is silently used in these pathwise comparisons. Combining them with the independently stated joint limit of `(F_m,Q_m)` gives the `C([0,T];R^k)×D([δ,T];R^{k×k})` path-law corollary; the second factor uses `J_1` and has a constant limit even though finite-width kernels jump.

**Kink and scope qualification.** For multiple coordinates sharing `W_i`, a small neuron can hit zero and its output weight can subsequently reverse. An a.e. discontinuous ODE can then admit delayed reactivation from zero. The theorem explicitly selects the absorbing solution for *all* neurons; its claims are valid for that selected flow and should not be advertised as uniqueness of every Carathéodory solution. Likewise, iid non-Gaussian stable coordinates are not invariant under arbitrary rotations; the exact first-layer diagonality and off-diagonal second-layer estimate are justified for `x_j=e_j` in the initialization basis, not every orthonormal family. Both qualifications are correctly stated in the draft.

No mathematical change is required for the stated theorem. For editorial precision, the phrase “on the fixed-bound good event ... `→_P0`” in (14) means `P(G_m∩{sup|A_{j,m}−P_{j,m}|>h})→0` for each `h>0`; the subsequent cutoff argument makes this unambiguous in context.
