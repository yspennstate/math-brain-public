Checked during this run on 23 October 2026 UTC / 24 October in the owner's timezone. Searches used both available search engines, followed by full primary-source pages or PDFs. This was a bounded check, not an exhaustive novelty search. No external priority is claimed.

1. Stefano Favaro, Sandra Fortini and Stefano Peluchetti, *Large-width asymptotics and training dynamics of alpha-Stable ReLU neural networks*, TMLR, November 2024. [Publisher-linked institutional PDF](https://iris.unibocconi.it/retrieve/1d4101f5-ffcd-480d-8966-6c0f01de8f2b/2651_Large_width_asymptotics_a.pdf). Retrieved refs turn234view1 and turn235view3, pp.9–10, Discussion. The paper establishes the ReLU logarithmic normalization and random initial kernel framework; its discussion leaves first-layer training-kernel fluctuations and general deep training open. This provides context for T384, not a proof that every specific extension is new. Our finite-grid theorem supplies a particular boundary-scale law, not the paper's broad missing macroscopic equivalence.

2. Etienne Boursier, Loucas Pillaud-Vivien and Nicolas Flammarion, *Gradient flow dynamics of shallow ReLU networks for square loss and orthogonal inputs*, NeurIPS 2022. [Primary conference PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2022/file/7eeb9af3eb1f48e29c05e8dd3342b286-Paper-Conference.pdf). Retrieved refs turn234view0 and turn235view2, Section 2, Assumption 1 and gradient-flow discussion. Their orthogonality assumption is explicit; their zero-at-kink convention and existence discussion cannot be silently extended to arbitrary correlated-input regression. Their small-initialization model is also distinct from this package's heavy-tail scaling.

3. Hancheng Min, Enrique Mallada and Rene Vidal, *Early Neuron Alignment in Two-layer ReLU Networks with Small Initialization*, ICLR 2024. [Primary author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.12851v2), [author-hosted conference PDF](https://mallada.ece.jhu.edu/pubs/2024-ICLR-MVM.pdf). Retrieved refs turn235view0, turn236view0, turn236view1 and turn237view0. Appendix E.2 explicitly explains that inward fields on both sides of a ReLU boundary can obstruct Caratheodory continuation, then uses the paper's separated-label data condition to rule it out. E.3 discusses fixed nonzero boundary derivatives; E.4 discusses nonuniqueness. Thus the *general mechanism* behind e01 is already described in primary literature. The present package supplies an explicit evolving-residual squared-loss example at the stable scales, with an open fixed-width positive-probability event; it makes no priority claim for the obstruction. Opposite labels on positively correlated inputs violate that paper's separation condition.
