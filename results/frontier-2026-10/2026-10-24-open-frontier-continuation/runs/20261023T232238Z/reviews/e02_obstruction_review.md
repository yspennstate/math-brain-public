# Independent audit of `agents/e01_obstruction.md`

**Verdict: the finite-width positive-probability obstruction and its local Filippov/Clarke slide are correct in the stated fixed-\(m\) scope.** I checked the exact coupled equations rather than substituting a frozen residual. No width-uniform probability or nonorthogonal network limit follows.

For the open event, every row after row 1 has both projections negative, hence contributes zero output and has zero parameter velocity. In row 1's \((-+)\) sector, \(f_1=0\), \(f_2=Wv/b\), and the displayed equations

\[
r_1=-1,\quad r_2=1-Wv/b,\quad
W'=\beta r_2v,\quad v'=\beta Wr_2,\quad q'=\rho v'
\]

are exact. Differentiating gives

\[
r_2'=-(\beta/b)(v^2+W^2)r_2.
\]

Thus \(r_2>0\) at finite times. Since \(W,v\) start positive and increase and \(Wv=b(1-r_2)<b\), each stays bounded by a finite constant depending on the initial state; this also prevents finite-time blowup. The monotone increase \(v(T)-v(0)>0\), uniform over a sufficiently small compact initial rectangle, makes a suitably small negative \(q(0)\) hit zero before \(T\). The projected-mark map from \(U\) is invertible; all imposed strict inequalities define a nonempty open set. Independent symmetric stable coordinates and weights have full support, so it has positive probability for each fixed \(m\ge2\).

At the first hit, \(W,v,r_2>0\), \(r_2<1\), and \(r_1=-1\). Hence the inactive-side normal speed is \(\beta W\rho r_2>0\), while the active-side speed is \(\beta W(-1+\rho r_2)<0\) for \(\rho=1/2\). Continuity keeps both signs strict locally for any hypothetical full-state continuation. For \(q\ne0\), \(q q'<0\); for \(q=0\), \(q q'=0\). Therefore an AC continuation from \(q=0\) must have \((q^2)'\le0\) and so \(q\equiv0\). The prescribed binary value \(D(0)=0\) then demands \(q'>0\) almost everywhere, a contradiction. The same argument excludes \(D(0)=1\). This proves nonexistence under those pointwise binary conventions, not merely nonuniqueness.

The Filippov set at this isolated face is the segment between the two smooth limiting full-state vectors; the only coefficient tangent to \(q=0\) is \(\theta_*=-\rho r_2/r_1=\rho r_2\in(0,1)\). The same \(q^2\) argument forces any local Filippov continuation to remain on the face. Substitution then gives

\[
v'=\beta W(1-\rho^2)r_2,\qquad
W'=\beta r_2v,\qquad
r_2'=-(\beta/b)[v^2+(1-\rho^2)W^2]r_2.
\]

This is a smooth locally unique ODE with \(r_2>0\) for finite time. At the boundary, the full loss is locally piecewise \(C^1\) across one smooth hyperplane, so its Clarke subdifferential is the convex hull of the two limiting gradients. The only varying part of its Euclidean norm is \(\|\theta r_1x_1+r_2x_2\|^2\); its unique minimum gives the same \(\theta_*\). The \(W\) component of the gradient is independent of \(\theta\) because \(q_+=0\). Thus the stated Clarke and Filippov local continuations agree.

**Output derivative / kernel precision.** Along the viable exact slide, \(f_1=0\) and \(r_1=-1\) remain fixed. Direct differentiation of \(f_2=Wv/b\) gives

\[
f_1'=0,\qquad
f_2'=(\beta/b)[v^2+(1-\rho^2)W^2]r_2.
\]

Since \(\beta/b=1/a^2\), the boundary output law is generated by the Schur-complement operator

\[
K_{\rm slide}=a^{-2}
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&v^2+(1-\rho^2)W^2\end{pmatrix}
\]

on this constrained face. The formal fractional-gate matrix \(a^{-2}[hh^T+W^2D_{\theta_*}GD_{\theta_*}]\), with \(h=(0,v)\), happens to have the **same action on the current residual** because \(\theta_* r_1+\rho r_2=0\); it is not a residual-independent boundary kernel law. A time-averaged binary Gram factor \(\mathbb E[DGD]\) has \((1,1)\) entry \(\theta_*\), versus \(\theta_*^2\) for \(D_{\theta_*}GD_{\theta_*}\), and generally fails even to annihilate the first output derivative. It is not an output-generating kernel for the exact slide. Any discrete overshoot argument needs ReLU secants, not that naive Gram average. This distinction should accompany any use of this example in a residual-coupled limit.

Minor wording repair only: the phrase that \(W,v\) are "bounded by their positive initial values and \(b\)" should say \(W\le b/v(0)\), \(v\le b/W(0)\) (alongside monotone lower bounds). These are the actual upper bounds; the proof's conclusion is unaffected.
