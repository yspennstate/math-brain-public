# Independent review of `agents/e02_endpoint.md`

**Verdict: passes within its stated deterministic frozen-residual, generic-interior-mark scope.** I found no gap in forward uniqueness, finite event count, or the eventual-ray estimate. This review does not turn the result into a residual-coupled network theorem or an external priority claim.

1. **Filippov map and faces.** The four sector fields are the images under invertible `G` of the four corners of `[0,a] x [0,b]` (with signed endpoints understood), so their essential convex hull is exactly equation (1), including at the origin. At `v_1=0,v_2>0`, the normal interval has endpoints `rho b` and `a+rho b`. Assumption (2) makes both nonzero. Equal signs force a crossing; inward opposite signs force `theta_1=-rho b/a` and the tangential speed `(1-rho^2)b`; outward opposite signs cannot be reached from either adjacent open sector. The analogous formulas at `v_2=0` check. On a negative half-axis the neighboring `--` field is zero, and the only incoming active-side field points into the axis. The zero-normal convex combination is then the zero vector, so sticking is forced.

2. **Origin and uniqueness.** An interior straight segment can reach the origin only when its initial point lies on one sector-specific ray, at most three rays in total. These are explicitly excluded. An attracting positive-face slide points toward the origin only if its tangential coefficient is negative. On the first face this means `b<0`; attraction requires `rho b>0>a+rho b`, hence `rho<0` and `a<0`. The second face gives the same conclusion. For `a,b<0`, `Phi=a(v_1)_++b(v_2)_+` is concave; its supergradient is anti-monotone. The displayed `G^{-1}` contraction then proves uniqueness from the origin, and the zero path is a solution. Thus the only non-excluded route to the origin has a unique continuation.

3. **Finite number of events.** In a sector the velocity is constant. Each positive half-axis has fixed strict normal signs, so any crossing of it has one fixed direction; a later recrossing would require the forbidden reverse direction. An attracting slide either runs away forever or reaches the origin once. A negative half-axis is terminal. These observations exclude cycling and infinite face events. After a finite transient the trajectory is stationary or affine, `v(s)=ps+c`. Taking `C=sup_{s>=0}||v(s)-sp||<infinity` proves (3) uniformly for all `L>=1` and all finite `T`. It may help the final note to state this directed-face argument explicitly rather than merely saying each face is crossed at most once.

4. **Potential and example checks.** On the zero set of any absolutely continuous coordinate, that coordinate has derivative zero almost everywhere. Therefore arbitrary boundary fractions do not alter the chain rule for `Phi`, and `Phi'=g^TGg=v'^TG^{-1}v'>=0` is correct. For `rho=1/2,a=-1,b=1`, the four velocities, both displayed sample paths, the forced fraction `1/2`, and the slide speed `3/4` all check. The binary-gate Carathéodory obstruction is real: at the attracting face a squared-normal-coordinate argument forces the normal coordinate to remain zero, while either binary point value gives nonzero normal derivative. The metric-minimal face fraction calculation is correct. The note correctly warns that its frozen residual and first-order velocity do not determine a training NTK or a discrete secant limit.

**Scope distinction.** The counterexample uses opposite labels on a positively correlated pair. Literature restricted to orthogonal inputs or well-separated binary data does not settle this general regression boundary example; no novelty or priority follows from this review.
