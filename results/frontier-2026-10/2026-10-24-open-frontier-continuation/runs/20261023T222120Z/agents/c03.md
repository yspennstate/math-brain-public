# T384 rotated two-input gate audit

## Verdict

For strictly **mixed-sign** initial residuals, the requested later cumulative-integral sign crossing is impossible: the off-diagonal kernel is nonnegative and the mixed-sign orthant is forward invariant, even for the finite-width time-dependent kernel. However, the proposed **constant post-boundary kernel for arbitrary labels/residuals is false**. A same-sign but imbalanced residual produces a second, positive-time macroscopic loss of first-layer kernel mass. The example below lies in the open support of the rotated iid-stable initialization.

## Exact finite-width sign protection for mixed signs

Write `v_i=(U_i·x_1,U_i·x_2)` for `x_1=(1,1)/sqrt(2)`, `x_2=(1,-1)/sqrt(2)`. Orthogonality still makes the first-layer kernel diagonal. At every width and time,

`K_m(t)=diag(A_{1,m}(t),A_{2,m}(t))+C_m(t)`,

`C_{12,m}(t)=m^(-2/alpha) sum_i (v_{i1}(t))_+(v_{i2}(t))_+ >= 0`.

Thus if `r_1(0)>0>r_2(0)` and `z=(r_1,-r_2)`, then

`z'=[[-D_1,C_12],[C_12,-D_2]] z`, `D_j=A_j+C_jj>=0`.

This is a Metzler system. In particular `z_j(t)>=z_j(0)exp(-int_0^t D_j)>0` on every finite interval where the kernel is bounded. Hence `J_1(t)=int_0^t r_1(s)ds>0` and `J_2(t)<0` for all `t>0`. The reverse mixed-sign arrangement is identical. This conclusion survives gate hits and the absorbing-at-zero selection by applying the integral equation. Therefore no positive-entry `C` and diagonal `A` can furnish the proposed *mixed-sign* crossing. It also shows that an initial mixed-sign class of large supporting `W` gates has no later cumulative-displacement death on fixed horizons, since large `W` signs are stable.

## Attainable second switch from same-sign residuals

Take the post-initial-boundary quantities

`C=[[3/2,1],[1,3/2]]`, `A=diag(1/2,1/2)`, `K=A+C=[[2,1],[1,2]]`, `r(0)=(1,3)`.

`C` is SPD (determinant `5/4`) with positive entries. Before the next switch, the exact residual and accumulated residual are

`r_1(t)=2e^(-3t)-e^(-t)`, `r_2(t)=2e^(-3t)+e^(-t)`;

`J_1(t)=-1/3+e^(-t)-(2/3)e^(-3t)`,

`J_2(t)=5/3-e^(-t)-(2/3)e^(-3t)`.

The residual `r_1` changes sign at `(log 2)/2`, but `J_1` stays positive until its unique positive zero

`t_*=log(1+sqrt(3)) ~= 1.00505`.

At this crossing,

`r(t_*)=(sqrt(3)-2, 2sqrt(3)-3)` and `J_2(t_*)=3-sqrt(3)>0`.

The crossing is transversal: `J_1'(t_*)=sqrt(3)-2<0`. Equivalently the hypothetical constant-kernel asymptote is `K^(-1)r(0)=(-1/3,5/3)`, forcing `J_1` to cross after initially increasing. Large positive-`W` gates initially active for input 1 stay active while `J_1>0`, then all hit zero within a shrinking window after `t_*` (their individual initial `v_{i1}/(log m)^(1/alpha)` vanish). Under absorption the new kernel is

`K'=[[3/2,1],[1,2]]`:

its `(1,1)` entry has lost the macroscopic mass `A_1=1/2`, while `A_2=1/2` survives. From `t_*` onward `r_1<0<r_2` is an invariant mixed-sign orthant for `K'`, so `J_1` decreases and `J_2` increases; no reactivation or additional switch occurs. The limiting kernel has a genuine jump at positive time `t_*`, and the limiting output follows `exp(-Kt)r(0)` up to `t_*`, then `exp(-K'(t-t_*))r(t_*)`.

## Why these values are feasible under rotated stable initialization

With `b=alpha/2`, a single extreme original hidden coordinate contributes to the normalized second-layer Gram matrix in one of three directions:

* `U_1` large positive: `(U_1^2/2)[[1,1],[1,1]]`;
* `U_2` large positive: `(U_2^2/2)[[1,0],[0,0]]`;
* `U_2` large negative: `(U_2^2/2)[[0,0],[0,1]]`.

The corresponding limit is `C=[[S+B_1,S],[S,S+B_2]]`, where `S,B_1,B_2` are independent positive `b`-stable variables, each with Laplace exponent `c_alpha Gamma(1-b)2^(-b)s^b`. The target `S=1`, `B_1=B_2=1/2` is inside its support; a small neighborhood has positive probability. Notice the structural inequalities `C_jj>C_12` almost surely. These do **not** prevent the same-sign example, because its initial residual ratio is `3:1`.

For `r(0)>0` componentwise, the selected first-layer masses are `P_j=int_{z>0} z^2 1{v_j>0}N(dz,dv)`. Positive-`W` extreme marks fall with probability `1/4` each into the four activation patterns `(++),(+0),(0+),(00)` (the two original hidden coordinates are iid symmetric). Poisson thinning gives `P_1=D_0+D_1`, `P_2=D_0+D_2`, where `D_0,D_1,D_2` are independent positive `b`-stable variables with full support on `(0,infinity)`. Thus `(P_1,P_2)` can lie arbitrarily close to `(1/2,1/2)` with positive probability.

The product-normalized initial output also has full support in `R^2`: its stable limit decomposes along `(1,1)`, `e_1`, and `e_2`, with independent symmetric nondegenerate stable components. For deterministic labels `y=(1,3)`, the event `r(0)` near `(1,3)` therefore has positive probability. Endpoint separation as in the coordinate initialization proof makes this product limit independent of the hidden-coordinate endpoint masses and output-weight endpoint Poisson process. A full rotated joint-initialization proof would need to write that multivariate endpoint-separation argument; the deterministic switch and open-support obstruction do not rely on an exact value of its probability.

## Finite-width implications and proof obligations

The orthogonal dynamical energy bound in `T384_orthogonal_dynamics.md` survives rotation because the row norm is invariant and the first-layer kernel remains diagonal. Hence `C_m(t)` freezes uniformly on compact time intervals and large initial `W_i/m^(1/alpha)` keep their signs. For such a weight with limit `z>0`, while its input-1 gate is active,

`v_{i1}(t)/(log m)^(1/alpha) = z J_{1,m}(t)+o_P(1)`

uniformly away from gate hits; its initial term vanishes because conditional marks are `O_P(1)`. Negative-`W` opposing gates die in the initial vanishing layer. A stopping-time/bootstrap argument then gives first-phase convergence on `[delta,t_*-epsilon]`, and all macroscopic positive-`W` input-1 gates die in `(t_*-epsilon,t_*+epsilon)`; after that the second-phase kernel is `K'` on `[t_*+epsilon,T]`. Truncating `W` extremes and controlling their small-weight square mass exactly as in the coordinate proof removes the cutoff. Transversality of `J_1` makes the switch robust under small perturbations of `r(0),C,P`.

One technical caution: uniform kernel convergence through `t_*` is impossible because the limit jumps; formulate kernel convergence away from `0,t_*`, or use `J1`/Skorokhod convergence after proving that the shrinking gate-death window carries all mass. The finite-width local proof and rotated joint initialization remain to be written in full before claiming a complete path-space theorem. The example already disproves a universally constant positive-time kernel, while the exact Metzler argument proves the requested mixed-sign protection.
