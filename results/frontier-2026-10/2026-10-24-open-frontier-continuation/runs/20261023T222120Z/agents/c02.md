# T384: an exact Euler gate-crossing law at the scalar switching scale

**Status:** New one-step proposition proved from the established joint marked-initialization lemma in `research/T384_marked_PPP.md`. It covers one scalar input, one explicit full-batch gradient-descent update, and every fixed `gamma>0`. It also gives a counterexample to carrying the *continuous-time path* boundary law over to piecewise-constant Euler interpolation at this step size. The mesh-point kernel and first-step output increment do have precise continuous-boundary counterparts. No multi-input or macroscopic discrete-training theorem is claimed here.

## Setup and proposition

Use precisely the scalar model of `research/T384_SCALAR_LIMIT.md`: iid independent-coordinate standard symmetric `alpha`-stable `(W_i,U_i)`, `0<alpha<2`, one input `1`, fixed label `y`, `a_m=(m log m)^(-1/alpha)`, `eta_m=(log m)^(2/alpha)`, and the ReLU derivative `1_{U>0}`. Put `L_m=(log m)^(1/alpha)`, `h_m=gamma/L_m`, `beta_m=eta_m a_m=m^(-1/alpha)L_m`, `F_m=a_m sum_i W_i(U_i)_+`, `r_m=y-F_m`, and

`H_m(0)=m^(-2/alpha) sum_i [W_i^2 1_{U_i>0}+(U_i)_+^2]`.

One simultaneous explicit-GD step is

`U_i^+=U_i+d_m W_i 1_{U_i>0}`, `W_i^+=W_i+d_m(U_i)_+`, `d_m=h_m beta_m r_m=gamma m^(-1/alpha)r_m`.

Define `f_m^+=a_m sum_i W_i^+(U_i^+)_+`, `r_m^+=y-f_m^+`, and `H_m^+=m^(-2/alpha)sum_i[(W_i^+)^2 1_{U_i^+>0}+(U_i^+)_+^2]`. If the update puts `U_i^+<0`, all future scalar GD updates of that neuron are exactly zero: its negative hidden coordinate does **not** reactivate. In particular one must retain the signed overshoot in the update before applying the next gate.

Let `(F,C,N)` be the joint limit proved in `research/T384_marked_PPP.md`, where `F,C,N` are mutually independent and `N` is the marked Poisson process of initial pairs `(z,u)=(W_i/m^(1/alpha),U_i)` with intensity `alpha c_alpha |z|^(-1-alpha) dz P_U(du)` on each half-axis. Set `r=y-F`, nonzero almost surely, and

`K(s)=C+sum_{(z,u) in N} z^2 1_{u>0, u+r z s>0}`, `s>=0`.

This is exactly the continuous-flow boundary kernel in `research/T384_boundary_layer.md`. For each fixed `gamma>0`, the following convergence is joint:

`(F_m, H_m(0), H_m^+, L_m(f_m^+-F_m), L_m[r_m^+-(r_m-h_m H_m(0)r_m)])`

`=> (F, K(0), K(gamma), r int_0^gamma K(s) ds, r int_0^gamma [K(0)-K(s)] ds).` **(1)**

The last coordinate is the order-`L_m^(-1)` correction to the naive tangent-kernel Euler residual step. In particular it is not generally zero. Conditional on `r`, the crossing process has intensity

`Lambda_gamma(r)=c_alpha (gamma |r|)^alpha E[U^(-alpha) 1_{U>0}]` **(2)**

where the right side is interpreted as infinity for `alpha>=1`. Thus, for `alpha<1`, the last coordinate is nonzero with conditional probability `1-exp[-Lambda_gamma(r)]`; for `alpha>=1` it is nonzero almost surely. (The expectation in (2) is finite and positive precisely when `alpha<1` because the stable density is continuous and positive at zero.) Its sign is the sign of `r`.

For a zero-order-hold Euler boundary path, `H_m^E(s)=H_m(floor(s/gamma))` at physical time `h_m floor(s/gamma)`, any fixed `0<s<gamma` satisfies `H_m^E(s)=H_m(0) => K(0)`. The continuous-flow value is `K(s)`, strictly less than `K(0)` with the same positive-probability/a.s. dichotomy just stated with `gamma` replaced by `s`. Hence the continuous `J_1` boundary law does **not** describe the held Euler path at fixed positive `gamma`. At the first mesh point the Euler kernel does converge to `K(gamma)`, and its rescaled output increment equals the continuous boundary-layer area `r int_0^gamma K(s)ds`.

## Proof

Write `z_i=W_i/m^(1/alpha)` and `u_i=U_i`. The first step obeys the **exact** identities

`U_i^+=u_i+gamma r_m z_i` and `W_i^+=W_i+gamma m^(-1/alpha)r_m u_i` for `u_i>0`; for `u_i<=0`, both coordinates are unchanged. Since `L_m a_m=m^(-1/alpha)`, expansion of the updated output gives

`L_m(f_m^+-F_m)`

`=sum_{u_i>0} z_i[(u_i+gamma r_m z_i)_+-u_i]`

`  +gamma r_m m^(-2/alpha) sum_{u_i>0} u_i(u_i+gamma r_m z_i)_+`. **(3)**

Let `C_m=m^(-2/alpha)sum_i(u_i)_+^2` and `S_m=m^(-2/alpha)sum_i(W_i^2+U_i^2)`. Both `r_m` and `S_m` are tight by the previous scalar results and the stable-square sum law. Define `D_m=m^(-2/alpha) sum_{u_i>0}u_i(u_i+gamma r_m z_i)_+`, the unprefactored second sum in (3). ReLU is 1-Lipschitz, so `D_m` differs from `C_m` in absolute value by at most

`gamma |r_m| m^(-2/alpha)sum_{u_i>0}u_i|z_i| <= gamma |r_m| m^(-1/alpha)S_m/2=o_P(1)`. **(4)**

The weighted squared-coordinate drift bound for this one step is at most `(2|d_m|+d_m^2)S_m=o_P(1)`. Initially inactive neurons do not move. Consequently

`H_m^+=C_m+sum_i z_i^2 1_{u_i>0,u_i+gamma r_m z_i>0}+o_P(1)`. **(5)**

Here (5) uses the **post-update** hidden coordinate, including a negative overshoot when a gate is crossed. A crossed neuron is absent from the sum and remains absent at later steps because its gradient is zero for every negative hidden value.

For the first sum in (3), define `G_b(z,u)=1_{u>0} z[(u+bz)_+-u]`. The Lipschitz inequality gives `|G_b(z,u)|<=|b|z^2 1_{u>0}`. At any fixed `epsilon>0`, the marked endpoint process convergence and joint convergence of `(F_m,C_m)` apply to the finitely many atoms `|z|>epsilon`, after a harmless large-`|z|` and mark truncation. The limiting process almost surely has no atoms at `u=0` or `u+gamma r z=0`, because `r` is independent of `N` and `U` has a density. Thus the finite-atom maps in (3) and (5) are continuity maps. For the omitted `|z|<=epsilon` atoms, their contribution to (5) is bounded by `sum z_i^2`, and that to the first sum of (3) by `gamma |r_m| sum z_i^2`. The established regular-variation truncation bound is

`limsup_m E[m^(-2/alpha)sum_i W_i^2 1_{|W_i|<=epsilon m^(1/alpha)}] <= const epsilon^(2-alpha)`.

The analogous Poisson expectation has the same vanishing order. Tightness of `r_m` removes its random multiplier. Letting `epsilon` decrease to zero, and using (4), proves joint convergence of the first four coordinates in (1); `H_m(0)` is the same functional with no endpoint gate shift.

For each Poisson atom with `u>0`, the fundamental theorem of calculus for ReLU gives

`z[(u+gamma r z)_+-u]=r z^2 int_0^gamma 1_{u+r z s>0} ds`.

The absolute value is at most `gamma |r|z^2 1_{u>0}`, whose Poisson sum is finite almost surely. Summing and adding the limit `gamma r C` from (4) yields the output coordinate in (1). Since `r_m^+=r_m-(f_m^+-F_m)`, the residual-correction coordinate follows by subtracting the initial-tangent prediction `h_m r_mH_m(0)` and multiplying by `L_m`.

To see its nondegeneracy, write the positive quantity

`M_gamma= int_0^gamma[K(0)-K(s)]ds`

`=sum_{(z,u) in N: u>0,rz<0} z^2 (gamma-u/(|r||z|))_+`. **(6)**

It is positive exactly when at least one opposing marked atom satisfies `0<u<gamma |r||z|`. Conditional on `r`, the PPP count of those atoms has mean

`alpha c_alpha int_0^infty v^(-1-alpha)P(0<U<gamma |r|v)dv`

`=c_alpha(gamma |r|)^alpha E[U^(-alpha)1_{U>0}]`.

This proves (2), including the infinite-count case. The sum in (6) still converges absolutely because it is bounded by `gamma sum_{N,u>0}z^2<infinity`. Finally, the held-path statement for `0<s<gamma` follows directly from its definition, while the continuous kernel has `K(0)-K(s)>0` exactly on the corresponding crossing event.

## What this does and does not say

The first-step **output** agrees to leading order with the continuous boundary-area law even though the Euler kernel path waits until the grid endpoint to drop. The cancellation is a consequence of using the actual ReLU value after crossing: a crossed endpoint atom contributes `|z|u` to the output change, the same as its pre-hit kernel area. Replacing the true output step by `h_m H_m(0)r_m` misses the random amount `r M_gamma/L_m`. Replacing a negative post-step hidden coordinate by a fictitious positive reactivation would also change all subsequent steps.

This note does not prove the macroscopic finite-time GD limit, a full all-grid `D([0,S])` Euler boundary theorem, or any result for multiple training inputs. It isolates the exact one-step scaling at `h_m=gamma/L_m` and the interpolation-dependent obstruction to reusing the continuous path law.
