Stacking A=[X;Z] gives a joint initial ray law with the same signed-ray variables for train and test coordinates. The finite-ray approximation of (AU)_+ and its outer product only uses that A is a fixed finite matrix: the first remainder is bounded by the second-largest original coordinate, and the Gram remainder by pairwise coordinate products. Neither estimate requires A to have full row rank. Full rank is needed only for training X to retain the earlier density of R and positivity of C. Thus F_* shares the signed product-ray variables with F, C_* shares the square-ray variables with C, and the large-W marked process N remains jointly independent of those ray families. Test-only large-U rays appear in F_* even when they contribute no training feature.

The exact finite-width projection identity is correct. Since every hidden update lies in the range of Xᵀ and K=ZXᵀ(XXᵀ)⁻¹, ZU_n=KXU_n+(Z−KX)U_0. For a retained large-W Poisson atom, its original-coordinate mark converges to a finite u, so the second term divided by L vanishes. Therefore the scaled test **preactivation** tends to Kq_train, and the feature tends to (Kq_train)_+. It would be wrong to use K(q_train)_+ or omit the coordinatewise ReLU when K has negative entries.

The test output integration-by-parts formula uses W_n−W_0 from the training update and the test feature at the final node; it yields the rectangular cross-time Gram A_*(n,j)=a⁻²∑g_test,n h_train,jᵀ exactly. The row-displacement estimate from MACRO_COMPACTNESS bounds both feature changes by a constant times ε_mR_i,0, while the features themselves are bounded by a constant times R_i,0. Expanding the two-factor difference therefore gives sup_n,j||A_*(n,j)−C_*,m,0||≤const ε_mS_0=o_P(1), without requiring this rectangular matrix to be symmetric. The resulting contribution is C_*∫r.

For the endpoint term, the displayed small-initial-|z| estimate is decisive. ReLU Lipschitzness controls the **change** in a test feature by the hidden update, independently of the size or direction of its initial U. Summing |z_i,0| times that change gives const·(∑_{|z_i,0|≤ε}z_i,0²+ε_mS_0), and the initial square mass vanishes as ε↓0. Thus a row with a very large test-only initial projection cannot create an omitted increment simply because its output weight is small. That row's initial contribution is already in F_*. For |z|>ε there are finitely many atoms after an original-mark cutoff; the probability of a retained large-W atom having |u|>B vanishes as B↑∞. On each retained atom the orthogonal offset U_0/L vanishes. These two cutoff routes exhaust the possible large-U cases and avoid double counting between F_* and the evolving endpoint term.

The limiting Poisson sum ∫z(Kq_z)_+N converges absolutely and uniformly because ||q_z||≤|z|||G||∫||r|| and ∫z²N<∞. The same localized energy bound controls the maximum one-step test-output jump by O_P(h), so the polygonal and held paths share the identified limit. These facts establish joint test/training path convergence rather than only fixed-time convergence.
