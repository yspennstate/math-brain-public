## Finite-ray joint initialization

For the `d` original hidden coordinates define `v_ell=(x_{1,ell},...,x_{k,ell})` and `h_ell^±=(±v_ell)_+`. A single large positive or negative `U_ell` contributes its **positive magnitude** times the corresponding nonnegative ray, so the proposed formula

`F=sum_(ell,sign) h_ell^sign Z_ell^sign`,

`C=sum_(ell,sign) S_ell^sign h_ell^sign(h_ell^sign)^T`

has the correct signs and scale constants. For fixed `d,k`, `||H(u)-Psi(u)||<=C M_2(u)` follows by choosing the largest `|u_ell|` first and using ReLU's Lipschitz property on the remainder. Since `P(M_2(U)>t)=O(t^(-2alpha))`, `E M_2^alpha<infinity`. Conditional on all U rows, each test projection of the normalized W-weighted error is exactly symmetric alpha-stable with conditional exponent at most `const (m log m)^(-1)sum_i M_2(U_i)^alpha`; its expectation is `O(1/log m)`. Thus the output-vector error vanishes in probability. For `d=1`, define `M_2=0`; the ray reduction is exact.

For the Gram matrix, the deterministic error is bounded by `C sum_(ell<q)|u_ell u_q|`. Choose `alpha/2<p<min(alpha,1)`; stable p-moments and subadditivity give

`P[a_m^(-2)sum_i |U_iell U_iq|>epsilon] <= const epsilon^(-p)m^(1-2p/alpha)->0`.

The finite signed-ray product and square categories have the same axis separation as the coordinate-input proof. Opposite signs of one U coordinate are rowwise disjoint. Distinct coordinates' product categories share W, but the conditional-U bound makes their **m-multiplied** one-row large-jump overlap `O(1/log m)` at each fixed cutoff. Product versus same-coordinate U-square endpoints and product versus marked W endpoints have `o(1/m)` one-row overlap by splitting the endpoint magnitude at an auxiliary multiple of `a_m`; cross-coordinate product/square endpoints are independent at one row; distinct U-square endpoints are disjoint or independent. With only finitely many categories, pairwise overlap bounds eliminate all mixed terms in the one-row transform. Centered product small jumps have vanishing `O(epsilon^(2-alpha))` variance, and positive-square small jumps have vanishing `O(delta^(1-alpha/2))` mean. This proves the stated mutual independence of all primitive ray limits and the **entire marked** W-endpoint process, after mark truncation. Slutsky then transfers it from the ray approximation to `(F_m,C_m,N_m)`.

The deterministic ray vectors span `R^k`: `h_ell^+-h_ell^-=v_ell`, and the coordinate rows `v_ell` of the orthonormal-input matrix span `R^k`. Since every positive-stable `S_ell^sign>0` almost surely, `C` is positive definite. The F characteristic exponent is bounded below by `c_*||t||^alpha`, so F has a bounded continuous full-dimensional density. Each projected mark `u dot x_j` has a continuous symmetric stable law; residual sign ties and gate-mark boundaries are null. For fixed sign vector, the selected PPP mass `P_j` is finite because small z squares are integrable, and positive because its eligible marked half-axis has infinite small-z intensity. The same `O(xi^(2-alpha))` truncated-square bound and finite-atom mark cutoffs give the joint convergence of the **random-sign** `P_j,m`; no false independence of selected P and F is used.

## Finite-width energy and compactness

The projected equations and diagonal first-layer block in equation (9) of `d01_k_input_extension.md` are exact for orthonormal inputs. At each row, the active `(W,h_1,...,h_k)` motion is a symmetric star matrix whose operator norm is at most `gamma_m||r_m||`. The rate-scaled kernel is PSD, so `||r_m(t)||<=||r_m(0)||`; initial energy `S_m=a_m^(-2)sum_i(W_i(0)^2+sum_j V_ij(0)^2)` is tight for fixed k. On bounded initial residual/energy events this yields uniform kernel and residual-Lipschitz bounds, as well as `o(1)` total W-square drift and frozen C. The bound remains valid at gate hits because absorption only removes a star-matrix edge.

Each `B_j,m(t)=a_m^(-2)sum_i W_i(0)^2 1_{V_ij(t)>0}` is bounded and nonincreasing, including after a negative or zero gate hit. Helly compactness in `L^1(0,T)` and Arzela-Ascoli for r and J give joint subsequential tightness. The drift estimates and `L^1` convergence allow passage through the integral residual equation (11), without claiming pointwise convergence at deletion times. For maximum rigor, the coupled tuple should explicitly contain `S_m` and the marked endpoint process (or each finite truncated endpoint configuration), along with `(F_m,C_m,B_m,r_m,J_m)`. Then every localized pre-hit inequality is a relation among jointly coupled finite-width variables; alternatively it can be transported as an unconditional probability inequality. This is a coupling-presentation repair, not a missing estimate.

The uniform pre-hit error is correctly **unscaled**: `max_i |W_i(t)-W_i(0)|=O_{K,R,T}(L_m)` and hence

`sup_{i,j,t<=T}|e_ij,m(t)|<=gamma_m RT max_i sup_t|W_i(t)-W_i(0)|=O_{K,R,T}(L_m^2/a_m)=o(1)`.

This is strong enough to protect a fixed positive gate mark for a supporting W-endpoint atom. The integrated formula is used only while that gate survives or at its first hit. Opposing endpoint atoms die after an initial positive cumulative excursion; supporting atoms survive while the cumulative clock has remained strictly positive and die after a prior strict negative excursion. An atom that dies stays dead, even if a later k-dimensional residual motion makes its cumulative clock positive again. Finite-atom truncation and unconditional large-mark/large-z probability bounds, followed by the `O(xi^(2-alpha))` small-W-square estimate, identify the subsequential initial-square clock exactly on the strict-sign regions stated in (13), almost everywhere in time.

## Induction across multiple returns

The sensitivity/Sard result supplies a probability-one environment/residual set where every candidate deletion on each finite horizon is unique, transverse, and distinct from prior deletions. It applies here because the primitive F is **independent of** `(C,N)` and has a full-dimensional density; on each fixed residual-sign orthant, the selected P is a function of `(C,N)` only. Conditioning on the sign does not assert that the assembled selected Q is independent of r0. The global identity `J'=r0-Q_S J` is valid across a deletion because the dropped term is `P_j e_j J_j=0` at its switch. The continuous vector field makes the saltation of `D_(r0)J` the identity, exactly the hypothesis used by the reviewed sensitivity lemma.

I checked the first-exit use of (13) carefully. Since `r_0j!=0`, every subsequential signed clock starts strictly positive for a short interval, so (13) fixes all A_j=P_j there and ODE uniqueness matches the explicit first phase. Suppose the paths agree up to a time strictly before the next **candidate** return. Every still-active candidate signed clock is strictly positive at that time and on a neighborhood (away from t=0), so (13) again fixes the same A and ODE uniqueness extends agreement. Thus equality cannot first fail before the candidate return. At a unique transverse return, continuity gives the same J and r at the event; its `sigma_j r_j<0` forces a strict negative excursion immediately afterward in **any** subsequential solution. Equation (13) and absorption then set that coordinate's A_j=0 permanently. All other active candidate clocks have positive margins at the event, so their masses stay P_j. The next constant-matrix phase is therefore forced. Induction repeats at most k times. If there is no further candidate return before T, the same first-exit argument covers the rest of `[0,T]`. This does not assume a two-dimensional sign cone or monotonicity of a surviving clock between events; it uses historical absorption after each strict negative excursion.

Consequently every subsequential `r,J,A` equals the same regular explicit hybrid, and the kernel converges in `L^1` while the output converges uniformly. No `J_1` kernel convergence follows when several endpoint deaths coalesce at a macroscopic switch. The theorem is finite T only; it does not identify a width-then-infinite-training-time interchange.
