# A15 independent adversarial audit: all positive nonlinearities

**Verdict.** With **\(\tau>0\)** (a nondegenerate Gaussian label), the claim is true for every fixed finite \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\eta>0\) satisfying \(\gamma=\mathbb E\log M<0\). Theorem 2.3(b) and Proposition 8.6(b) of Alsmeyer–Brofferio–Buraczewski, *Asymptotically linear iterated function systems on the real line* (2023), apply to the proposed shifted map. They prove the finite, strictly positive exact tail constant, **not uniqueness**. Uniqueness follows separately from equivalence of the one-step transition laws to Lebesgue measure. If the notation \(Y\sim N(0,\tau^2)\) permits \(\tau=0\), the unconditional claim is **false**: \(0\) is absorbing and \(\delta_0\) has tail constant zero. Primary source: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.02299), §§2, 8–9, especially lines 244–259 and 1185–1196; [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.02299v2), pp. 8–9, 44–45. Web retrieval refs `turn93view1`, `turn93view3`, `turn116view0`.

## Concrete model and the uniform error

Write \(X=(Z,U)\), \(H=\|U\|\), \(\rho=\|X\|\), and \(g(z,y)=(\phi_\varepsilon(z)-y)\phi_\varepsilon'(z)\). The exact radial random map \(\Psi(r)=\|r e_1-\eta g(rZ,Y)X\|\) uses iid copies of \((X,Y)\) because the Gaussian vector transition is orthogonally equivariant. Its homogeneous comparator is \(M r=\|r(e_1-\eta ZX)\|\), where
\[
M^2=(1-\eta Z^2)^2+\eta^2Z^2 H^2,\qquad
0<M\le 1+\eta\rho^2\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
With \(t(z)=\tanh z\) and \(s(z)=\operatorname{sech}^2 z\),
\[
g(z,y)-z=-(1+\varepsilon s(z))y
 +\varepsilon[t(z)+z s(z)]+\varepsilon^2t(z)s(z).
\]
Since \(|z|s(z)\le1\), the reverse triangle inequality gives, **uniformly over all \(r\ge0\)**,
\[
|\Psi(r)-Mr|\le Q:=\eta\rho[(1+\varepsilon)|Y|+2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2].
\]
For every fixed finite \(\varepsilon>0\), \(Q\) has moments of all orders; no small-\(\varepsilon\) Lipschitz estimate is needed. Dependence among \(M,Q,\Psi\) is permitted in the paper's iid *tuples* assumption.

The lower bound \(M\ge\eta|Z|H\), with \(H>0\) a.s. for \(d\ge2\), proves \(\mathbb E|\log M|<\infty\); the upper bound handles the positive part. Also \(\Pr(M>1)>0\), and \(M\) is nonconstant. Thus \(h(p)=\mathbb E M^p\) is finite at every \(p>0\), \(h(0)=1\), \(h'(0)=\gamma<0\), and strict log-convexity plus \(h(p)\to\infty\) give a **unique** positive root \(\alpha\), with \(h(p)<1\) for \(0<p<\alpha\) and \(h'(\alpha)=\mathbb E[M^\alpha\log M]>0\). Its absolute tilted log moment is finite: \(x^\alpha\log^-x\) is bounded, and \(M\) has arbitrarily high moments. Conditional on any \(Z\ne0\), the continuous \(H\)-law makes \(M\) non-atomic, so \(\log M\) is nonarithmetic. These facts hold for **all** \(\varepsilon\) because \(M\) is independent of \(\varepsilon\).

## Exact source-hypothesis map, including zero left slope

Define on **all** of \(\mathbb R\)
\[
F(s)=1+\Psi((s-1)_+),\qquad
\Lambda(s)=\begin{cases}Ms,&s>0,\\0,&s\le0.\end{cases}
\]
The sample map \(F\) is continuous at \(s=1\) as well as everywhere else, always takes values in \([1,\infty)\), and
\[
\sup_{s\in\mathbb R}|F(s)-\Lambda(s)|\le B:=1+M+Q.
\]
Indeed for \(s\ge1\), \(F(s)-Ms=1-M+[\Psi(s-1)-M(s-1)]\); for \(0<s<1\), \(F(s)=1+\Psi(0)\le1+Q\) and \(Ms<M\); for \(s\le0\), \(\Lambda(s)=0\). This checks the paper's (1), (4) with \(B\ge1\), and \(\mathbb E B^\alpha<\infty\). **The shift matters:** the stationary law of \(S=R+1\) is supported in \([1,\infty)\subset\mathbb R_>\), precisely the positive-support restriction in Theorem 2.3(b), without having to investigate a possible atom of \(R\) at zero.

Here the paper's signed asymptotic coefficients are \({}^+A=M>0\), \({}^-A=0\). Its formal **separated case** requires \(p_{-+}=p_{+-}=0\), which holds; it does *not* require \(p_{--}>0\) or a nonzero left coefficient. The paper expressly allows zero endpoint slopes (HTML lines 133–136). For \(p>0\), its tilted matrix is \(P(p)=\operatorname{diag}(0,h(p))\); the right entry satisfies \(p_{++}(\alpha)=1\), and \(p'_{++}(0)=\mathbb E\log M=\gamma<0\). Condition (19) is exactly the checked finite \(\mathbb E[M^\alpha\log M]\) and \(\mathbb E B^\alpha\); conditional non-atomicity checks nonarithmetic. Theorem 2.3(b) therefore gives a finite limit \(C\ge0\) for \(s^\alpha\Pr(S>s)\). Proposition 8.6(b) makes it **strictly** positive once unbounded support is verified. No negative-mean logarithm of the *global Lipschitz constant of \(F\)* is in these hypotheses.

For \(\tau>0\), \(\phi_\varepsilon'(z)=1+\varepsilon\operatorname{sech}^2z>0\). Conditional on any \(X\ne0\), varying the full-support label \(Y\) makes \(\Psi(r)\) arbitrarily large for every fixed \(r\). Consequently every invariant radial law \(\pi\) satisfies \(\pi((T,\infty))=\int\Pr(\Psi(r)>T)\pi(dr)>0\) for every \(T\); the stationary law of \(S\) is unbounded to the right. The proposition yields \(C\in(0,\infty)\), and
\[
t^\alpha\Pr(\|W\|>t)
=t^\alpha\Pr(S>t+1)\longrightarrow C.
\]
The corrected positive-side Goldie numerator, if a constant formula is desired, is the **single joint expectation**
\(\mathbb E[F(S)^\alpha-(MS)^\alpha]/(\alpha\mathbb E[M^\alpha\log M])\), with \(S\) independent of fresh \((X,Y)\). Its absolute integrability follows from the bounded error and subcritical moments, not from finite separate \(\alpha\)-moments of \(F(S)\) and \(MS\).

## Existence and uniqueness, which the tail theorem does not supply

Pick \(p\in(0,\min\{1,\alpha\})\); then \(h(p)<1\). The bound \(\Psi(r)\le Mr+Q\) gives, for the radial chain started at zero,
\[
\mathbb E R_n^p\le h(p)\mathbb E R_{n-1}^p+\mathbb E Q^p,
\]
so its laws are tight. The kernel is Feller because sample maps are continuous; Cesàro limits provide an invariant radius law. This is also Proposition 9.1 of the paper, with \(\rho(p)=h(p)<1\), but §9 explicitly says it **does not address uniqueness** (HTML lines 1191–1196).

For later use, **every** stationary radius has finite moments of every order \(p<\alpha\). The shifted affine upper bound \(F(s)\le Ms+B\) iterated for \(n\) steps dominates a stationary \(S_n\) by \(\prod_{j=1}^nM_j\,S_0\) plus a sum distributed as the first \(n\) terms of the positive perpetuity \(H_\infty=\sum_{k\ge1}(\prod_{j<k}M_j)B_k\). The product term tends to zero a.s. by \(\gamma<0\), without assuming any moment of \(S_0\). The perpetuity has finite \(p\)-moment whenever \(h(p)<1\), by subadditivity for \(p\le1\) and Minkowski for \(p>1\). Taking distributional limits gives stochastic domination and the claimed stationary subcritical moments. Consequently the positive-side Goldie perturbation is integrable: \(|F(S)^\alpha-(MS)^\alpha|\le B^\alpha\) if \(\alpha\le1\), and is bounded by a constant times \(B^\alpha+B(MS)^{\alpha-1}\) if \(\alpha>1\). Fresh \((M,B)\) is independent of \(S\), \(\mathbb E S^{\alpha-1}<\infty\), and all moments of \(M,B\) are finite. This independently checks the integrability behind the tail-limit assertion despite the source's printed constant typo.

Uniqueness has a direct, non-perturbative proof when \(\tau>0\). For \(r>0\), conditional on \(X\), put \(G=\eta(\phi_\varepsilon(rZ)-Y)\phi_\varepsilon'(rZ)\), a Gaussian variable with everywhere-positive density. Completing the square,
\[
\Psi(r)^2=\rho^2\left(G-\frac{rZ}{\rho^2}\right)^2
                 +\left(\frac{rH}{\rho}\right)^2.
\]
Thus its conditional law has positive density above \(m(X)=rH/\rho\), and has no atom at \(m(X)\). For **every** \(s>0\), Gaussian directions sufficiently near \(\pm e_1\) have \(m(X)<s\) with positive probability. Hence the unconditional one-step radial law \(P_r\) is *equivalent to Lebesgue measure on* \((0,\infty)\). At \(r=0\), \(\Psi(0)=\eta(1+\varepsilon)|Y|\rho\), giving the same equivalence.

For completeness, this also establishes **vector** uniqueness. In polar coordinates \(X=\rho U\), conditional on \((\rho,U)\), the displacement \(W'-w=T U\), where \(T=-\eta\rho(\phi_\varepsilon(w\cdot\rho U)-Y)\phi_\varepsilon'(w\cdot\rho U)\), has a positive Gaussian density on the entire real line. Integrating over uniform \(U\) gives \(P_w\) a positive Lebesgue density on \(\mathbb R^d\setminus\{w\}\), with no atom at \(w\). Every nonzero positive measure \(\mu\) therefore has \(\mu P\) equivalent to Lebesgue measure. Were two distinct invariant probabilities to exist, the positive and negative Jordan parts of their difference would be mutually singular, whereas their one-step images would be equivalent; strict decrease of total variation contradicts invariance. First construct an invariant vector law by taking an invariant radius with independent uniform direction; orthogonal equivariance and uniqueness of the isotropic law with that radius prove invariance. The same Jordan argument proves uniqueness of the radius law.

## Corrections and boundary

1. **Do not infer uniqueness from Theorem 2.3(b) or Proposition 9.1.** The kernel-density argument above supplies it even for large \(\varepsilon\), where the samplewise global radial Lipschitz exponent might be nonnegative.
2. The displayed definition of \(C_\delta\) immediately before Theorem 2.3 in both HTML and arXiv PDF p. 9 has \(\mathbf1_{\{\Psi(R)<0\}}\) even for \(\delta=+\). Read literally for our strictly positive \(F(S)\), this is wrong. The theorem's **tail-limit** and Proposition 8.6(b)'s **positivity** are the relevant statements; for the positive constant replace that indicator with the positive-side event. The paper's §7 phrase “homeomorphism \(\mathbb R\to[1,\infty)\)” is likewise topologically impossible as printed. The explicit continuous extension above avoids any dependence on that phrase.
3. If \(\tau=0\), then \(Y=0\), \(\phi_\varepsilon(0)=0\), and \(w=0\) is absorbing. For example any \(0<\eta<2/(d+2)\) has \(\mathbb E M^2=1-2\eta+(d+2)\eta^2<1\), hence \(\gamma<0\) by Jensen, yet \(\delta_0\) has \(C=0\). State \(\tau>0\) explicitly.
