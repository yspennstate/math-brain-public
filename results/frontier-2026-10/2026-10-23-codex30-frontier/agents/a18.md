# A18 — universal radial tail for bounded perturbations of a linear activation

This is a rederivation and slope-\(a>0\) reformulation of the existing T228 class-(A) result in [`brain_t228_T3_CLAIM.md`](brain_t228_T3_CLAIM.md), not a novelty claim. That earlier result already treats all dimensions, minibatches, and noisy teacher labels; this note isolates the one-sample independent-label case and a separate explicit average-contraction criterion.

Let \(d\ge2\), \(X\sim N(0,I_d)\), \(Y\sim N(0,\tau^2)\) with \(\tau>0\), and let fresh independent pairs be used at successive squared-loss SGD steps. Fix \(a,\eta>0\) and
\[
 \phi(z)=az+b(z),\qquad b\in C^1(\mathbb R),\qquad
 B_0=\|b\|_\infty<\infty,\quad B_1=\|b'\|_\infty<\infty,\quad
 D_1=\|z b'(z)\|_\infty<\infty. \tag{A}
\]
In fact, for bounded \(C^1\) functions \(b\), the condition \(D_1<\infty\) already implies \(B_1<\infty\): use continuity on \([-1,1]\) and \(|b'(z)|\le D_1/|z|\) outside it. Put
\[
 \beta=\eta a^2,\quad Z=X_1,\quad U=(X_2,\ldots,X_d),\quad
 M_\beta=\|(I-\beta XX^\top)e_1\|=\|e_1-\beta ZX\|,
 \quad h_\beta(p)=\mathbb E M_\beta^p.
\]
Assume the *linear radial stability* condition
\[
 \gamma_\beta:=\mathbb E\log M_\beta<0. \tag{B}
\]
A simple, though not necessary, sufficient condition is \(0<\beta<2/(d+2)\), since \(\mathbb E M_\beta^2=1-2\beta+(d+2)\beta^2<1\).

**Theorem.** Under (A)–(B), the exact radius process has an invariant probability. There is a unique \(\alpha=\alpha(d,\beta)>0\) such that \(h_\beta(\alpha)=1\). Every invariant radius law \(\pi\) with unbounded support satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}t^\alpha\pi((t,\infty))=C_\pi\in(0,\infty).\quad} \tag{1}
\]
If \(\phi'(0)\ne0\), every invariant radius law has full support \([0,\infty)\), so (1) applies to *every* such law. The convenient stronger condition
\[
 \inf_{z\in\mathbb R}\phi'(z)>0 \tag{C}
\]
ensures this radial open-set irreducibility; \(B_1<a\) is a simple sufficient condition for (C). Neither a small bound on \(b\) nor control of \(b''\) is needed for (1). The index \(\alpha\) depends on the activation only through its linear slope \(a\), via the product \(\eta a^2\), and not on \(b\) or the positive Gaussian label variance. The positive constant can depend on \(b,\tau,\pi\).

## Algebraic condition and the exact radial recursion

Write \(g_Y(z)=(\phi(z)-Y)\phi'(z)\). The exact identity
\[
 g_Y(z)-a^2z
 =azb'(z)+ab(z)+b(z)b'(z)-(a+b'(z))Y \tag{2}
\]
gives, *simultaneously for all (z,Y)*,
\[
 |g_Y(z)-a^2z|\le D_0+D_Y|Y|,
 \quad D_0=aD_1+aB_0+B_0B_1,
 \quad D_Y=a+B_1. \tag{3}
\]
Thus it is at most \(K_b(1+|Y|)\), where \(K_b=\max(D_0,D_Y)\). Within the class of bounded \(C^1\) functions \(b\), finite \(B_1,D_1\) are also **necessary** for a deterministic bound \(K(1+|Y|)\) valid for all (z,Y): letting \(|Y|\to\infty\) in (2) bounds \(|a+b'(z)|\) uniformly; then setting \(Y=0\) and using bounded (b,b') bounds (azb'). This characterizes exactly the stated uniform-error criterion, rather than just supplying a sufficient smoothness heuristic.

For the vector update \(F_{X,Y}(w)=w-\eta g_Y(X\cdot w)X\), fix \(w=re_1\) and set \(\Psi(r)=\|F_{X,Y}(re_1)\|\). Equation (2) says
\[
 F_{X,Y}(re_1)=(I-\beta XX^\top)re_1-\eta X\{g_Y(rZ)-a^2rZ\}.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality therefore gives the **uniform-in-\(r\)** bound
\[
 |\Psi(r)-M_\beta r|\le Q,
 \quad Q:=\eta\|X\|(D_0+D_Y|Y|),\qquad r\ge0. \tag{4}
\]
Every positive moment of \(Q\) is finite, and dependence between \(Q\) and \(M_\beta\) is allowed. In particular \(\Psi(r)/r\to M_\beta\) almost surely as \(r\to\infty\). By rotational invariance, conditional on \(\|w_n\|=r\) the next norm has precisely the law of \(\Psi(r)\); independent copies of this scalar random map give the *exact* radius Markov kernel, even though the original vector direction changes over time.

## Stability, nonarithmeticity, existence, and the positive tail

The Gaussian multiplier obeys
\[
 M_\beta^2=(1-\beta Z^2)^2+\beta^2 Z^2\|U\|^2,
 \quad \beta |Z|\|U\|\le M_\beta\le1+\beta|Z|\|X\|. \tag{5}
\]
Consequently \(M_\beta>0\) almost surely and \(\mathbb E|\log M_\beta|<\infty\), also for \(d=2\), because the logarithms of \(|Z|\) and \(\|U\|\) are integrable near zero. The multiplier has moments of every positive order. Conditional on \(Z\ne0\), \(M_\beta^2\) is a strictly increasing affine function of the continuously distributed \(\|U\|^2\); hence \(\log M_\beta\) is nonarithmetic. Also \(\Pr(M_\beta>1)>0\). The strictly convex function \(h_\beta\) has \(h_\beta(0)=1\), right derivative \(\gamma_\beta<0\), and grows to infinity. It therefore has exactly one positive root \(\alpha\), and \(h_\beta(s)<1\) for \(0<s<\alpha\). The tilted log moment \(\mathbb E[M_\beta^\alpha\log M_\beta]\) is finite (the negative integrand is harmless at zero; the positive part is controlled by Gaussian moments).

Choose \(s\in(0,\min\{1,\alpha\})\). The inequality \(\Psi(r)\le M_\beta r+Q\) and subadditivity of \(x^s\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E[R_{n+1}^s]\le h_\beta(s)\mathbb E[R_n^s]+\mathbb E Q^s.
\]
Starting at zero gives uniformly bounded \(s\)-moments and tight Cesàro occupation laws. The kernel is Feller because every sample map \(\Psi\) is continuous. A weak Cesàro limit is thus invariant. This proves existence without a global Lipschitz or uniqueness assumption.

To check the *positive* constant in (1), first establish unbounded support. For each fixed \(r>0\), \(\phi'\) cannot vanish identically (that would make \(az+b(z)\) constant, contradicting bounded \(b\)), and the variable \(rZ\) has full support. Thus \(\Pr(\phi'\(rZ\)\ne0)>0\). Conditional on such an \(X\ne0\), \(Y\mapsto\Psi(r)\) is the norm of a nonconstant affine line and is unbounded; Gaussian \(Y\) has full support. The same holds at \(r=0\) when \(\phi'(0)\ne0\). Hence \(P(r,(T,\infty))>0\) for all \(r,T\) in that case, and stationarity implies \(\pi((T,\infty))>0\). Under (C), the stronger radial open-set statement holds: for any \(r\ge0\) and \(t>0\), choose \(X\) in a small cone about a nonzero vector parallel to \(e_1\); the line traced by varying \(Y\) has distance \(r\|U\|/\|X\|<t\) from the origin and therefore attains radii near \(t\). This cone and a suitable interval of \(Y\) both have positive Gaussian probability. Thus every positive-radius open interval has positive one-step transition probability, and every invariant radius law has full support. Indeed \(\phi'(0)\ne0\) alone also gives this statement: for each \(r>0\), choose a nonzero \(z\) with \(\phi'(z)\ne0\) and the cone near \(X=(z/r)e_1\).

Apply Alsmeyer–Brofferio–Buraczewski, *Asymptotically linear iterated function systems on the real line*, Theorem 2.3(b) **and** Proposition 8.6(b), [primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.02299) ([published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/22-AAP1812)). For its strictly positive half-line formulation, let \(S=1+R\) and extend the sample map to the whole real line by
\[
 \widetilde\Psi(u)=1+\Psi((u-1)_+),\qquad
 \Lambda(u)=\begin{cases}M_\beta u,&u>0,\\0,&u\le0.\end{cases}
\]
Both are continuous and \(\sup_{u\in\mathbb R}|\widetilde\Psi(u)-\Lambda(u)|\le1+M_\beta+Q\): this follows directly from (4) for \(u\ge1\), and \(\Psi(0)\le Q\) for \(u<1\). The right coefficient is \(M_\beta>0\), the left coefficient is zero, so this is the paper's separated case. Its error has all moments; its positive-tail moment, tilted-log, and nonarithmetic hypotheses follow above; and its positivity hypothesis \(p'_{++}(0)<0\) is exactly (B). Theorem 2.3(b) gives a finite tail limit for the stationary law of \(S\), and Proposition 8.6(b) makes it strictly positive when support is unbounded. Finally \(\Pr(R>t)=\Pr(S>t+1)\), proving (1). The theorem alone does *not* guarantee a positive constant, and bounded error alone does *not* establish uniqueness.

## Separate sufficient condition for average contraction

Let \(b=\delta q\), \(0\le\delta\le1\), with \(q\in C^2(\mathbb R)\) satisfying
\[
 q_0=\|q\|_\infty<\infty,\quad q_1=\|q'\|_\infty<\infty,\quad
 q_2=\|q''\|_\infty<\infty,\quad
 q_{12}=\|zq''(z)\|_\infty<\infty,\quad
 \|zq'(z)\|_\infty<\infty. \tag{6}
\]
Now \(g_Y'(z)=\phi'(z)^2+(\phi(z)-Y)\phi''(z)\), and direct expansion yields
\[
 |g_Y'(z)-a^2|
 \le\delta\{2aq_1+aq_{12}+q_2|Y|\}
       +\delta^2\{q_1^2+q_0q_2\}
 \le\delta H(Y),
 \quad H(Y)=2aq_1+aq_{12}+q_1^2+q_0q_2+q_2|Y|. \tag{7}
\]
Differentiate the vector *before* taking its norm; no differentiability of the norm at its zeros is needed:
\[
 V_\delta'(r)=e_1-\eta g_Y'(rZ)ZX,
 \quad V_\delta(r)=re_1-\eta g_Y(rZ)X.
\]
It follows that for every sample and \(r,u\ge0\),
\[
 |\Psi_\delta(r)-\Psi_\delta(u)|\le L_\delta|r-u|,
 \quad L_\delta=M_\beta+\delta K,
 \quad K=\eta|Z|\|X\|H(Y). \tag{8}
\]
Pick \(s\in(0,\min\{1,\alpha\})\), write \(m_s=\mathbb E M_\beta^s<1\), \(k_s=\mathbb E K^s<\infty\), and require
\[
 \delta^s k_s<1-m_s. \tag{9}
\]
Then \(\mathbb E L_\delta^s\le m_s+\delta^s k_s<1\). Synchronous coupling contracts the radius transition operator in the Wasserstein metric for \(d_s(r,u)=|r-u|^s\), giving a unique invariant radius law among finite-\(s\)-moment laws and geometric convergence in that metric. In fact it is unique among **all** invariant radius laws: by (5), \(\log^-L_\delta\le\log^-M_\beta\) is integrable; the Gaussian upper bound in (8) makes \(\log^+L_\delta\) integrable; Jensen and (9) give \(\mathbb E\log L_\delta<0\). Couple any invariant starting radius and the constructed invariant radius with the same iid maps; their distance is at most its finite initial value times \(\prod_{j=1}^nL_{\delta,j}\to0\) almost surely. Equality of their stationary marginals follows by bounded continuous test functions. If also \(\delta q_1<a\), then \(\phi_\delta'(z)\ge a-\delta q_1>0\) and this unique law has the positive exact tail (1). Condition (9) certifies recurrence and uniqueness through a *stronger* average-contraction mechanism; it is not a premise of the tail theorem above.

For an especially explicit interval, if \(0<\beta<2/(d+2)\), one may choose \(s=1\) because \(m_1\le\sqrt{1-2\beta+(d+2)\beta^2}<1\), and impose \(\delta\mathbb E K<1-m_1\), along with \(\delta q_1<a\). For more general stable \(\beta\), an \(s\in(0,1]\) exists by (B), even if \(\mathbb E M_\beta\ge1\).

## Examples and boundaries

* \(b(z)=\delta\tanh z\) and \(b(z)=\delta\arctan z\) satisfy (A), and their \(q=\tanh,\arctan\) satisfy (6). For \(\delta>0\), \(\phi'(z)\ge a\) regardless of the size of \(\delta\); (1) holds throughout the stable linear regime, while (9) is only guaranteed for sufficiently small \(\delta\). For either sign, \(|\delta|<a\) is an easy sufficient derivative-positivity test because \(\|q'\|_\infty=1\).
* Boundedness and positivity of the activation derivative are **not** enough: \(b(z)=\delta\sin z\) with \(0<\delta<a\) has \(\phi'\ge a-\delta>0\), but at \(z=2\pi n\), \(g_Y(z)-a^2z=a\delta z-(a+\delta)Y\), which is unbounded. Its oscillating leading derivative destroys (4), so the claimed universal exponent is not established by this argument.
* The tail assumptions do **not** imply the derivative estimate (7). For instance \(q(z)=\sin(z^2)/(1+z^2)\) is bounded and smooth with bounded \(q'\) and \(zq'\), hence qualifies for (1) for small \(\delta\) (and then \(\phi'>0\)), but \(zq''(z)\) is unbounded. Its exact-tail conclusion remains valid; the specific global Lipschitz and contraction certificate (8)–(9) is unavailable.
* If \(\phi'(0)=0\), zero is absorbing: \(F_{X,Y}(0)=0\) and \(\delta_0\) is invariant with zero tail. Any *other* invariant law that has unbounded support still satisfies (1); the support condition cannot be omitted in this case. If (B) fails, this note asserts neither a stationary heavy-tail regime nor the root \(\alpha>0\).
