
It needs NumPy and SciPy and uses deterministic RNG seed `271828`. The script passed on 2026-10-23. It checks the algebra in `agents/a21.md`, the one-sided minimax and recurrence in `agents/a22.md`, and the RBF finite-feature/KL composition in `agents/a23.md`. It does **not** execute attention heads, construct exact raw-input feature embeddings, analyze pretraining sample complexity, or prove the literal T336 request.

## What is checked

For a spectral interval `[a,b]`, set `ρ=(√(b/a)−1)/(√(b/a)+1)`, `δ_D=2ρ^D/(1+ρ^{2D})`, and

`r_D(λ)=[T_D((a+b−2λ)/(b−a))+1]/[T_D((a+b)/(b−a))+1]`,
`P_{D−1}(λ)=(1−r_D(λ))/λ`,
`ε_D=4ρ^D/(1+ρ^D)^2`.

The checker samples each residual at 10,001 eigenvalues on three intervals for `D=1,2,3,4,5,10,20,40`, verifies `0≤r_D≤ε_D` to roundoff, and independently verifies `r_{2L}=q_L²` for the even degrees. It also handles the singleton `[a,a]` directly as `P=1/a`. The recurrence is the ordinary Chebyshev semi-iteration with `X₀=0`, `X₁=B/h`, coefficients from bounded `w_j=t_{j−1}/t_j`, and final division by `1+δ_D`. The independent comparison evaluates `P_{D−1}(A)B` with an eigendecomposition and the scalar polynomial at each eigenvalue. It does not compare two calls to the same recurrence.

Five GP configurations use `n=1,2,5,8,12`, `m=1,3,2,4,5`, noise `s=1,0.2,0.01,0.001,10⁻⁵`, respectively; selected feature tails are `p=1,2,5,5,20`. Every configuration runs the above eight degrees, so in particular it checks requested squared-residual depths `L=1,2,5,20` at `D=2L`. The largest actual spectral condition number was `1,200,001.00023` for 12 duplicate training points. For each configuration the feature kernel comes from explicit multi-index RBF coordinates of total degree `<p` in two dimensions; the script also computes the direct truncated Taylor expression. It compares the exact RBF noisy joint GP law, exact finite-feature GP law, and finite-feature polynomial GP law. Its forward KL uses the actual matrix Gaussian expression, not a reverse-KL surrogate.

Across all checks, the largest relative recurrence/spectral-polynomial discrepancy was `9.58×10⁻¹¹`; the smallest observed eigenvalue of `Ĉ−C_p` was `−1.78×10⁻¹⁶`; and every reported finite-depth covariance had its smallest eigenvalue above `s` (minimum measured margin `0.02217`). The largest observed ratio of true-to-approximate forward KL to the derived composition upper bound was `2.13×10⁻⁵`; the bounds are intentionally uniform and often loose. Each row in the JSON records the actual mean/covariance/KL discrepancies and its corresponding one-sided solver or RBF-plus-solver upper bound.

For each prompt, the RBF test compares `K−K_{p−1}` and successive increments `K_{p′−1}−K_{p−1}` as matrices, checks positive semidefiniteness numerically, and compares the diagonal difference with the Poisson tail `P{Poisson(||x||²/ell²)≥p}` from `scipy.special.gammainc`. It verifies the uniform tail bound `δ_p=(R²/ell²)^p/p!` with `R=1,ell=0.75`. At `p=20`, `δ_p=4.0872×10⁻¹⁴`; a measured entrywise difference as small as `2×10⁻¹⁶` cannot meaningfully distinguish the actual mathematical tail from roundoff. The most negative eigenvalues of tail and increment matrices were `−2.50×10⁻¹⁵` and `−2.97×10⁻¹⁵`, respectively. Direct use of `K−K_p` is especially cancellation-prone at high degree; these are diagnostics, not PSD certificates.

## Exact rational edge checks

For `A=[[2,1],[1,2]]` and `[a,b]=[1,3]`, the `D=2` one-sided polynomial is exactly `P₁(A)=I−A/4=[[1/2,−1/4],[−1/4,1/2]]`. Direct rational inversion gives `A⁻¹−P₁(A)=[[1/6,−1/12],[−1/12,1/6]]`. Both `P₁(A)` and this difference are positive definite by their principal minors, with no floating-point tolerance.

For one training point and two identical queries, use the rank-one PSD joint Gram of all ones, `s=1`, loose interval `[1,3]`, and label `y=3`. The `D=1` polynomial is `P₀=1/3` and the exact inverse is `1/2`. The exact and finite-depth means are `(3/2,3/2)` and `(1,1)`. The exact and finite-depth covariance matrices have diagonal/off-diagonal entries `(3/2,1/2)` and `(5/3,2/3)`, respectively; `Ĉ−C` is the all-`1/6` PSD matrix. The covariance eigenvalues are `(1,2)` and `(1,7/3)`, so both preserve the noise floor. The exact forward KL is `log(7/6)/2+1/28≈0.1127896256`, independently matched by the numerical Gaussian KL routine.

Ordinary unshifted Chebyshev can fail much more sharply. Take one training and one query point with joint Gram `[[1,1],[1,1]]⪰0`, `s=1/10`, and spectral interval `[a,b]=[1/10,11/10]`. At the shallowest depth `D=1`, the ordinary inverse iterate is `1/h=5/3`; the true inverse at `A=11/10` is `10/11`. The ordinary predictive variance is **`−17/30`**, despite a consistent PSD full Gram. The one-sided division by `1+δ₁` produces exactly `10/11`, hence predictive variance `21/110≥s`. All values are `Fraction` identities in the checker.

## Scope and error limits

The analytical reasons for one-sided Loewner ordering and the forward KL inequalities are in `a21/a22/a23`; empirical sampling cannot establish them uniformly. Numerical assertions admit small absolute and scale-relative tolerances, because eigenvalue subtraction, a nearly singular Gram, matrix solves, and `log det` all accrue rounding error. In particular a slightly negative `~10⁻¹⁶` eigenvalue in a computed PSD difference is not a counterexample, while this script cannot certify PSD for an arbitrary implementation or deep finite-precision network. The spectral-polynomial comparison uses an eigendecomposition only as an independent reference; the recurrence under test remains matrix-free. The construction presupposes exact finite RBF features and unnormalized signed-ReLU kernel attention if interpreted as the architecture in `a21`. Nothing here measures transformer size for computing those raw-input features or the number of pretraining datasets needed for Bayes convergence.
