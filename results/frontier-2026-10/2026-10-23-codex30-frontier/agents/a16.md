# T228: full-vector stationarity for every \(\varepsilon>0\) when \(\mathbb E\log M<0\)

Let \(d\ge2\), \(\eta,\varepsilon,\tau>0\), and let independent iid \(X_n\sim N(0,I_d)\), \(Y_n\sim N(0,\tau^2)\) drive
\[
W_{n+1}=F(W_n,X_{n+1},Y_{n+1}),\qquad
F(w,x,y)=w-\eta[\phi(w\cdot x)-y]\phi'(w\cdot x)x,\qquad
\phi(z)=z+\varepsilon\tanh z.
\]
Put \(A_x=I_d-\eta xx^\top\), \(M=\|A_Xe_1\|\), \(h(s)=\mathbb EM^s\), and \(\gamma=\mathbb E\log M\).

**Theorem.** If \(\gamma<0\), then \(h(s)<1\) for some \(s\in(0,1]\). For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the **full-vector** kernel has one and only one invariant probability \(\pi\), with \(\pi(\|w\|^s)<\infty\). It is Lebesgue-irreducible, aperiodic and positive Harris recurrent. The measure \(\pi\) is equivalent to Lebesgue measure, hence has full unbounded support. For \(V(w)=1+\|w\|^s\), some \(C<\infty,\rho<1\) satisfy
\[
\sup_{|f|\le V}|P^nf(w)-\pi(f)|\le CV(w)\rho^n
\quad(w\in\mathbb R^d,\ n\ge0).
\tag{T}
\]
In particular, the same bound holds in total variation. Nondegeneracy \(\tau>0\) matters for one-step density. Neither global contraction of \(F\) nor \(\mathbb E\log\operatorname{Lip}(\Psi)<0\) is assumed.

## A small contracting moment and uniform radial error

Write \(X=(Z,U)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{d-1}\). Then
\[
M^2=(1-\eta Z^2)^2+\eta^2Z^2\|U\|^2,\qquad
\eta|Z|\|U\|\le M\le1+\eta\|X\|^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(M>0\) almost surely. The negative logarithms of a one-dimensional Gaussian absolute value and a \(\chi_{d-1}\) variable are integrable near zero, even when \(d=2\), so (1) proves \(\mathbb E|\log M|<\infty\). The upper bound gives finite positive moments of every order. Dominated differentiation of \(h(s)\) at \(0+\) is justified by \(|\log M|\) for \(M\le1\), and \(M\log M\) for \(M>1\). Therefore \(h(0)=1\), \(h'(0+)=\gamma<0\), and \(h(s)<1\) for some \(s\in(0,1]\).

For \(z=w\cdot x\), put
\[
H(z)=\phi(z)\phi'(z)-z
=\varepsilon\tanh z+\varepsilon z\operatorname{sech}^2z+
\varepsilon^2\tanh z\operatorname{sech}^2z.
\]
Since \(\phi'\in[1,1+\varepsilon]\) and \(\sup_z|z|\operatorname{sech}^2z\le1\),
\[
F(w,x,y)=A_xw-\eta[H(z)-y\phi'(z)]x,\qquad
\|F(w,X,Y)-A_Xw\|\le Q
:=\eta\|X\|[2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2+(1+\varepsilon)|Y|].
\tag{2}
\]
This bound is uniform in \(w\), and \(Q\) has all positive moments for *every* finite \(\varepsilon>0\). For the radial map \(\Psi(r)=\|F(re_1,X,Y)\|\), the reverse triangle inequality yields \(|\Psi(r)-Mr|\le Q\) for all \(r\ge0\). Dependence between \(M,Q\) is harmless.

For \(w\ne0\), isotropy gives \(\|A_Xw\|/\|w\|\stackrel d=M\). The triangle inequality and \((a+b)^s\le a^s+b^s\) imply, including at \(w=0\),
\[
\mathbb E_w\|W_1\|^s\le h(s)\|w\|^s+K,\qquad K=\mathbb EQ^s<\infty.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(PV\le h(s)V+1-h(s)+K\). For any \(\lambda\in(h(s),1)\), choose a sufficiently large closed ball \(C=\overline B(0,R)\) and finite \(b\) so that
\[
PV\le\lambda V+b\mathbf1_C.
\tag{4}
\]

## Positive density and compact one-small sets in the full state space

Conditional on \(X=ru\), \(r>0,u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\), write \(a=rw\cdot u\). Then \(F(w,ru,Y)=w+Tu\), where \(T=\eta r\phi'(a)[Y-\phi(a)]\) has the positive density
\[
g_{w,r,u}(t)=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\eta r\tau\phi'(a)}
\exp\left\{-\frac1{2\tau^2}
\left[\frac{t}{\eta r\phi'(a)}+\phi(a)\right]^2\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(f_X\) be the standard Gaussian density. For \(v\ne w\), set \(H=\|v-w\|\), \(u_v=(v-w)/H\). Polar coordinates in \(x=ru\) and the two branches \(t=\pm H\) give the transition density
\[
q(w,v)=H^{1-d}
\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\int_0^\infty
r^{d-1}f_X(\sigma r u_v)\,
g_{w,r,\sigma u_v}(\sigma H)\,dr>0.
\tag{6}
\]
There is no atom at \(v=w\): conditional on \(X\ne0\), this equality forces the continuously distributed \(Y\) to equal \(\phi(w\cdot X)\). Formula (6) describes the entire kernel. The integral is finite off the diagonal: near \(r=0\), its integrand is bounded by \(Cr^{d-2}e^{-cH^2/r^2}\), and for large \(r\) by \(Cr^{d-2}e^{-r^2/2}\). In particular \(P(w,A)>0\) for every \(w\) and every Lebesgue-positive Borel \(A\).

Every closed ball \(C=\overline B(0,R)\), \(R>0\), is one-small. If \(w,v\in C,v\ne w\), restrict the positive \(\sigma=+1\) integral to \(r\in[1,2]\). The factor \(r^{d-1}f_X(ru)g_{w,r,u}(H)\) has a positive minimum over the compact parameter set \(w\in C,u\in\mathbb S^{d-1},r\in[1,2],H\in[0,2R]\). Because \(H^{1-d}\ge(2R)^{1-d}\), some \(c_R>0\) satisfies \(q(w,v)\ge c_R\) off the diagonal on \(C\times C\). Hence
\[
P(w,A)\ge c_R\operatorname{Leb}(A\cap C)
=\alpha_R\nu_C(A),\quad
\nu_C=\frac{\operatorname{Leb}(\cdot\cap C)}{\operatorname{Leb}(C)},\quad
\alpha_R=c_R\operatorname{Leb}(C)>0,\quad w\in C.
\tag{7}
\]
The diagonal is Lebesgue-null. Formula (6) gives Lebesgue irreducibility, and \(\nu_C(C)=1\) gives strong aperiodicity. The same lower-bound argument works for \(w\) in one bounded ball and \(v\) in another.

## Existence, uniqueness, convergence and support

For bounded continuous \(f\), \(Pf(w)=\mathbb Ef(F(w,X,Y))\) is continuous by dominated convergence: \(P\) is Feller. Iteration of (3) shows
\[
\mathbb E_w\|W_n\|^s
\le h(s)^n\|w\|^s+
K\frac{1-h(s)^n}{1-h(s)}.
\tag{8}
\]
The Cesàro laws are tight. A weak subsequential limit \(\pi\) exists and is invariant: Feller continuity handles \(Pf\), while the difference between consecutive Cesàro averages is \([P^Nf(w)-f(w)]/N\to0\) for bounded continuous \(f\). Portmanteau and (8) give \(\pi(\|w\|^s)<\infty\).

Now (4), (7), irreducibility and aperiodicity meet the usual geometric drift/minorization theorem, giving positive Harris recurrence and (T). A precise primary-source formulation is Roberts and Rosenthal, *General State Space Markov Chains and MCMC Algorithms*, Probability Surveys **1** (2004), Theorem 9 and Fact 10, pp. 22–23, <https://www.probability.ca/jeff/ftpdir/probsurv.pdf>. Theorem 9's invariant-law hypothesis was proved above; Fact 10 gives the all-state \(V\)-uniform bound. In particular \(P^nf(w)\to\pi(f)\) for every \(w\) and bounded \(f\). If \(\mu\) is *any* other invariant probability, integrating this convergence against \(\mu\) and using dominated convergence gives \(\mu(f)=\pi(f)\). This proves uniqueness of the full-vector law without an assumed moment, rotation symmetry, or a radial-only argument.

For a direct Harris check, outside \(C\), (4) gives \(\Pr_w(\tau_C>n)\le\lambda^nV(w)\) for the first hit \(\tau_C\), hence returns to \(C\) occur infinitely often almost surely. Given a Lebesgue-positive \(A\), choose a bounded positive-measure \(D\subseteq A\). The bounded-ball version of (7) gives \(\inf_{w\in C}P(w,D)>0\). Trials at successive visits to \(C\), spaced at least two steps apart, show that \(A\) is visited almost surely from every state.

Because \(P(w,\cdot)\ll\operatorname{Leb}\) for every \(w\), invariance gives \(\pi\ll\operatorname{Leb}\). Positivity in (6) gives \(P(w,A)>0\) for every positive-Lebesgue \(A\), hence \(\pi(A)>0\). Thus \(\pi\) is equivalent to Lebesgue measure and has full unbounded support. Rotation covariance \(F(Ow,Ox,y)=OF(w,x,y)\) and uniqueness imply rotational invariance.

## Why this goes beyond \(\eta<2/(d+2)\)

One familiar sufficient condition is \(\mathbb EM^2=1-2\eta+(d+2)\eta^2<1\), i.e. \(0<\eta<2/(d+2)\). At the endpoint \(\eta_0=2/(d+2)\), \(\mathbb EM^2=1\) but \(M^2\) is nonconstant, so strict Jensen gives
\[
\gamma(\eta_0)=\tfrac12\mathbb E\log M^2
<\tfrac12\log\mathbb EM^2=0.
\]
On a compact interval of positive \(\eta\)'s, (1) bounds negative logarithms with the *lower endpoint* and positive logarithms with the *upper endpoint*, making \(\eta\mapsto\gamma(\eta)\) continuous by dominated convergence. Hence \(\gamma<0\) for an interval strictly beyond \(2/(d+2)\). Arbitrarily large fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) changes \(K\) and the drift ball, but neither \(h(s)\) nor the validity of the proof.
