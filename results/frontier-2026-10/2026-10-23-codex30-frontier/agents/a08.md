# A08: multidimensional leaky-ReLU SGD, exact Pareto stationary tails

Let (d\ge1, 0<a\le1, X_n\overset{iid}\sim N(0,I_d)), and (Y_n\overset{iid}\sim N(0,\sigma^2)) independent, **with \(\sigma>0\)**. For the half-square loss \(\frac12(\phi_a(w\cdot X)-Y)^2\), \(\phi_a(u)=\max(u,au)\), and \(\eta>0\), the update at \(w\ne0\) is
\[
W'=\left(I-\eta c(w,X)^2 XX^\top\right)w+\eta c(w,X)YX,
\quad c(w,X)=1_{w\cdot X>0}+a1_{w\cdot X<0}.
\]
Under the involution \((X,Y)\mapsto(-X,-Y)\), the pair \((XX^\top,YX)\) is fixed and \(\operatorname{sgn}(w\cdot X)\) flips. Since \(w\cdot X\ne0\) a.s., the sign is a fair independent coin relative to that pair **for each fixed nonzero \(w\)**. Thus the *entire transition kernel* outside zero equals that of an affine recursion with i.i.d. pairs
\[
A=I-\eta C^2XX^\top,\qquad B=\eta CYX,
\quad C\text{ is an independent fair draw of the two values }1,a.
\]
This is kernel equality, not pathwise identification of the endogenous gates. At \(w=0\) the activation kink demands a convention; any derivative/subgradient \(g\in[a,1]\) produces \(W'=\eta gYX\ne0\) a.s. Since every state goes to zero with probability zero, any invariant probability has zero mass there; its law is exactly the affine perpetuity's law. For nonzero deterministic starts, all finite-step laws agree with the affine process.

An even cleaner match to Damek–Mentemeier's **(Rank1) model with batch size \(b=1\)** is \(\widetilde X=CX\): then \(A=I-\eta\widetilde X\widetilde X^\top\), \(B=\eta\widetilde X Y\). The input \(\widetilde X\) is a two-scale mixture of centered isotropic Gaussians, independent of \(Y\); its density is radial, strictly positive everywhere, bounded, and decays faster than any polynomial because \(a>0\). Their §3.4.2 explicitly discusses such rotationally invariant full-support continuous inputs, so the application needs no new random-matrix theorem.

Define independent \(Z^2\sim\chi^2_1\), \(V\sim\chi^2_{d-1}\) (\(V=0\) for \(d=1\)), and \(C\) as above. For any unit vector \(e\),
\[
M^2:=\lVert Ae\rVert^2
=1-2\eta C^2Z^2+\eta^2 C^4Z^2(Z^2+V),\quad
h(s):=E M^s,\quad \gamma:=E\log M.
\]
Conjugation invariance of \(A\) gives \(E|Av|^s=h(s)|v|^s\). Conditioning inductively on previous products and comparing the operator norm with the images of a fixed basis shows the random-matrix pressure \(k(s)=\lim_n(E\|A_n\cdots A_1\|^s)^{1/n}\) equals \(h(s)\), **not merely bounds it**. Likewise the successive log-norm increments along any fixed nonzero vector have the same conditional distribution \(\log M\), hence are iid, and finite-basis comparison gives top Lyapunov exponent \(\gamma=E\log M\).

If \(\gamma<0\), then \(h(0)=1\), \(h'(0)=\gamma<0\), strict log convexity (nonconstant \(M\)), and \(P(M>1)>0\) imply a **unique** \(\alpha>0\) such that \(h(\alpha)=1\). All moments of \(A,B\) exist; the stationary perpetuity is unique. Under the geometric and moment conditions below, the corrected Damek–Mentemeier rendition of Guivarc'h–Le Page Theorem 5.2 yields
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^\alpha P(\|W_\infty\|>t, W_\infty/\|W_\infty\|\in D)
=c\,\operatorname{Unif}(S^{d-1})(D),\qquad c>0
\]
for sphere continuity sets \(D\). In particular \(P(\|W_\infty\|>t)\sim c t^{-\alpha}\), with moments finite for \(p<\alpha\) and infinite for \(p\ge\alpha\). Orthogonal equivariance also independently proves the stationary distribution is spherically symmetric. For \(d=1\), the unit-sphere uniform distribution means equal tails on \(\{+1,-1\}\); signed scalar Goldie's theorem supplies an alternate route.

**Conditions audit, all dimensions.** Almost-sure invertibility follows from \(\det A=1-\eta C^2\|X\|^2\), whose zero event has probability zero. Interpret the semigroup support **relative to \(GL(d)\)** (the ordinary ambient matrix closure includes singular matrices at \(\eta C^2\|x\|^2=1\)). For any unit \(u\) and \(t\ge0\), matrices \(I-tuu^\top\) belong to the ambient support. Those with \(t>2\) have a simple dominant negative eigenvalue \(1-t\), so are proximal, including \(d=2\). To see strong irreducibility for \(d\ge2\), suppose a finite invariant union of nonzero proper linear subspaces exists. Choose a maximal one \(L\) and \(x\in L\) lying in no other subspace. For fixed \(u\), \((I-tuu^\top)x\) stays in \(L\) for all sufficiently small positive \(t\), by continuity and the finite union's invariance; therefore \(uu^\top x\in L\). Every \(u\) with \(u\cdot x\ne0\) then lies in \(L\), a contradiction. There is no invariant proper closed convex cone: for any nonzero \(x\) in such a cone and \(u\cdot x\ne0\), normalized even and odd powers of \(I-tuu^\top\), \(t>2\), converge respectively to \(\pm u\); as these \(u\)'s are dense on the sphere, the cone would be all of \(\mathbb R^d\). In \(d=1\), irreducibility/proximality are automatic and the support includes negative coefficients; also \(\log|A|\) has a continuous, hence nonarithmetic, component. In higher dimensions \(\log\rho(I-tuu^\top)=\log(t-1)\) ranges over an interval for \(t>2\), ruling out a lattice obstruction in the matrix renewal cocycle.

For any \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), \(Ax+B=x\iff Y=C X\cdot x\) (a.s. \(X\ne0\)), which has probability zero conditional on \((C,X)\) because \(\sigma>0\). For every \(q\ge0\), \(E\|A\|^q<\infty\) and \(E|B|^q<\infty\). Near the potential singularity, \(U=\|X\|^2\) has smooth bounded density in a neighborhood of each positive \(1/(\eta c^2)\); consequently \(E|\det A|^{-\delta}<\infty\) for every \(\delta<1\). Since \(\|A^{-1}\|\le \max(1,|\det A|^{-1})\), and \(\|A\|\) grows only polynomially in \(\|X\|\), the theorem's mixed inverse-moment requirement \(E\|A\|^\alpha N(A)^\epsilon<\infty\), \(N(A)=\max(\|A\|,\|A^{-1}\|)\), holds for sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\). This is an especially simple direct b=1 verification of the 2026 paper's Theorem 3.6. **Its Corollary 3.2 explicitly assumes \(b>d+1\), so don't cite that corollary for this one-sample setup.** Use Theorems 3.1 and 3.4 plus these checks (or their Theorem 3.6). Do not use the 2021 ICML proof's matrix-density assumption, which Damek–Mentemeier identify as false for symmetric rank-one coefficients.

**Explicit finite/infinite-variance phase boundary.** Compute
\[
h(2)=1-\eta(1+a^2)+\frac{\eta^2(d+2)(1+a^4)}2,
\qquad \eta_2=\frac{2(1+a^2)}{(d+2)(1+a^4)}.
\]
At \(\eta=\eta_2\), \(h(2)=1\) and strict Jensen gives \(\gamma=E\log M<\frac12\log E M^2=0\), hence \(\alpha=2\). For \(\eta\) just above \(\eta_2\), pick \(s\in(0,2)\) with \(h(s;\eta_2)<1\); continuity preserves \(h(s;\eta)<1\), hence \(\gamma<0\), whereas \(h(2;\eta)>1\); thus \(\alpha\in(s,2)\) and the stationary solution has exact Pareto tails and infinite variance. For \(0<\eta<\eta_2\), \(h(2)<1\), so the stationary variance is finite; at or above the threshold **within the contractive \(\gamma<0\) regime**, stationarity cannot have finite variance: if it did, its mean is zero and
\((1-h(2))E\|W_\infty\|^2=E\|B\|^2=\eta^2 d\sigma^2(1+a^2)/2>0\), impossible. The threshold and tail index do not depend on \(\sigma>0\), although the tail scale does. For \(Y\equiv0\), the unique contractive invariant law is \(\delta_0\), so nondegeneracy is essential.
