# Smooth genuinely nonlinear SGD: stationary heavy-tailed law

Fix \(d\ge2,\epsilon>0,\tau>0,\eta>0\), with independent iid \(X_n\sim N(0,I_d)\), \(Y_n\sim N(0,\tau^2)\). Define
\[
\phi(u)=u+\epsilon\tanh u,\qquad
F(w,x,y)=w-\eta\bigl(\phi(w\cdot x)-y\bigr)\phi'(w\cdot x)x,\qquad
W_{n+1}=F(W_n,X_{n+1},Y_{n+1}).
\]
This is SGD for sample loss \(\frac12(\phi(w\cdot x)-y)^2\); \(\phi\) is \(C^\infty\), genuinely nonlinear, and has positive derivative.

**Theorem.** Let \(M_\eta=\|(I_d-\eta XX^\top)e_1\|\). If \(\rho=\mathbb E M_\eta^s<1\) for some \(0<s\le1\), there is a unique invariant probability \(\pi\). The chain is positive Harris recurrent and aperiodic, \(\pi\|w\|^s<\infty\), and constants \(C<\infty,r<1\) satisfy
\[
\|P^n(w,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}\le C(1+\|w\|^s)r^n
\quad\text{for every }w\in\mathbb R^d,\ n\ge0.
\]
The measure \(\pi\) is rotationally invariant and equivalent to Lebesgue measure, hence has full unbounded support. In fact some finite \(\kappa>0,c>0,t_0<\infty\) satisfy
\[
\pi\{\|w\|>t\}\ge c\,t^{-\kappa}\quad(t\ge t_0),
\qquad \pi\|w\|^q=\infty\quad(q\ge\kappa).
\]
The condition is explicit for **every** \(0<\eta<2/(d+2)\): \(\mathbb E M_\eta^2=1-2\eta+(d+2)\eta^2<1\), so \(\mathbb E M_\eta\le(\mathbb E M_\eta^2)^{1/2}<1\), allowing \(s=1\). The polynomial bound is a lower bound, not a claim of regular variation or an exact tail exponent.

## Proof

**Uniform linearization and drift.** Put \(A_x=I_d-\eta xx^\top\) and \(u=w\cdot x\). Then
\[
F(w,x,y)=A_xw+R(w,x,y),\qquad
R=-\eta[\phi(u)\phi'(u)-u-y\phi'(u)]x.
\]
Here \(\phi'(u)=1+\epsilon\operatorname{sech}^2u\in[1,1+\epsilon]\) and
\[
\phi(u)\phi'(u)-u
=\epsilon\tanh u+\epsilon u\operatorname{sech}^2u
+\epsilon^2\tanh u\operatorname{sech}^2u.
\]
Since \(\sup_u|u|\operatorname{sech}^2u<1\), uniformly in \(w\),
\[
\|R(w,x,y)\|\le B(x,y):=
\eta\|x\|[(2\epsilon+\epsilon^2)+(1+\epsilon)|y|].
\tag{1}
\]
All moments of \(B(X,Y)\) are finite. For every nonzero \(w\), isotropy gives \(\|A_Xw\|/\|w\|\stackrel d=M_\eta\). The triangle inequality and \((a+b)^s\le a^s+b^s\) imply, for \(V(w)=1+\|w\|^s\),
\[
PV(w)\le1+\rho\|w\|^s+K,\qquad K=\mathbb E B(X,Y)^s<\infty.
\tag{2}
\]
Choose \(\lambda\in(\rho,1)\); a sufficiently large nontrivial closed ball \(C=\{\|w\|\le R_0\}\) and finite \(b\) then give \(PV\le\lambda V+b1_C\). Globally, \(PV\le\rho V+(1+K-\rho)\), so \(\sup_n\mathbb E_w V(W_n)<\infty\) for each deterministic start. For the explicit range, expand \(M_\eta^2=1-2\eta X_1^2+\eta^2X_1^2\|X\|^2\) and use \(\mathbb E[X_1^2\|X\|^2]=3+(d-1)=d+2\).

**Kernel positivity and a one-step small ball.** Condition on \(X=ru\), \(r>0\), \(u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\). The signed displacement \(T\) in \(W'=w+Tu\) has the positive Gaussian density
\[
g_{w,r,u}(t)=
\frac{\exp\!\left[-\frac1{2\tau^2}
\left(\frac{t}{\eta r\phi'(rw\cdot u)}+\phi(rw\cdot u)\right)^2\right]}
{\sqrt{2\pi}\eta r\tau\phi'(rw\cdot u)}.
\]
For \(z\ne w\), set \(h=\|z-w\|\), \(u_v=(z-w)/h\), and let \(f_X\) be the standard Gaussian density. Polar change of variables yields
\[
q(w,z)=h^{1-d}\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\int_0^\infty
r^{d-1}f_X(\sigma r u_v)\,g_{w,r,\sigma u_v}(\sigma h)\,dr>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Also \(P(w,\{w\})=0\), since conditional on \(X\ne0\), the continuous \(Y\) must equal the single value \(\phi(w\cdot X)\) for zero displacement. Thus \(P(w,dz)=q(w,z)\,dz\). Formula (3) is continuous away from \(z=w\): on compact sets with \(h\ge h_0>0\), its integrand near \(r=0\) is dominated by \(Cr^{d-2}e^{-c/r^2}\), and as \(r\to\infty\) by \(Cr^{d-2}e^{-r^2/2}\).

Every nontrivial closed ball \(C\) is **one-small with its own normalized Lebesgue measure**. For \(w,z\in C,z\ne w\), restrict the plus integral in (3) to \(1\le r\le2\). The factor \(r^{d-1}f_X(ru)g_{w,r,u}(h)\) has a strictly positive uniform lower bound on the compact domain \(w\in C,u\in\mathbb S^{d-1},r\in[1,2],h\in[0,\operatorname{diam}C]\). Because \(d\ge2\), \(h^{1-d}\ge(\operatorname{diam}C)^{1-d}\). Hence \(q(w,z)\ge c_C>0\) off the diagonal on \(C\times C\), and
\[
P(w,A)\ge c_C\operatorname{Leb}(C)\,\nu_C(A),\qquad
\nu_C(A)=\frac{\operatorname{Leb}(A\cap C)}{\operatorname{Leb}(C)},\quad w\in C.
\tag{4}
\]
The diagonal has Lebesgue measure zero. The drift ball can be used in (4). One-step positivity makes the chain Lebesgue-irreducible; \(\nu_C(C)=1\) makes it strongly aperiodic.

**Stationarity and convergence.** For bounded continuous \(f\), \(Pf(w)=\mathbb E f(F(w,X,Y))\) is continuous by dominated convergence: the chain is Feller. Bound (2) makes the Cesàro laws \(N^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}P^n(w,\cdot)\) tight. Any weak subsequential limit \(\pi\) is invariant, using Feller continuity and the telescoping difference \((P^Nf(w)-f(w))/N\to0\). Portmanteau and the bounded moments yield \(\pi(V)<\infty\).

Apply the geometric drift plus small-set theorem to (2)–(4), now that invariance, irreducibility and aperiodicity have been checked. It yields uniqueness, positive Harris recurrence, and the displayed all-state \(V\)-weighted geometric convergence. A primary-source formulation is Roberts and Rosenthal, *General State Space Markov Chains and MCMC Algorithms* (2004), **Theorem 9 and Fact 10**, pp. 22–23, https://www.probability.ca/jeff/ftpdir/probsurv.pdf. Fact 10 points to Meyn and Tweedie, *Markov Chains and Stochastic Stability*, Theorems 15.0.1, 16.0.1 and 14.3.7. In this proof existence was established independently, meeting Theorem 9's invariant-law hypothesis.

Harris recurrence can also be seen directly. With \(\tau_C=\inf\{n\ge0:W_n\in C\}\), the outside-\(C\) drift gives \(\mathbb P_w(\tau_C>n)\le\lambda^nV(w)\), so \(C\) is hit and revisited infinitely often almost surely from every state. Given any Lebesgue-positive \(A\), choose a bounded positive-measure subset \(D\subset A\). Restricting (3) to \(r\in[1,2]\) now gives \(\inf_{w\in C}P(w,D)>0\), since \(C\cup D\) is bounded. At successive visits to \(C\) (taking trials at least two steps apart), the conditional chance of moving into \(D\) is uniformly positive. Thus every Lebesgue-positive \(A\) is visited infinitely often almost surely from every start.

Since \(P(w,\cdot)\ll\operatorname{Leb}\) and \(P(w,A)>0\) for every Lebesgue-positive \(A\), invariance implies \(\pi\ll\operatorname{Leb}\) and \(\pi(A)>0\) for every such \(A\). Thus \(\pi\) is equivalent to Lebesgue, non-atomic and has full unbounded support. For any orthogonal \(Q\), \(F(Qw,Qx,y)=QF(w,x,y)\) and \(QX\stackrel d=X\); uniqueness forces \(Q_\#\pi=\pi\).

**Polynomial lower tail from a repeatable expansion event.** Let \(e=e_1,\ x_\star=2e/\sqrt\eta\), \(A_\star=I_d-4ee^\top\), and write \(w=w_1e+w_\perp\). On the closed double cone \(\mathcal K=\{w:\|w_\perp\|\le|w_1|\}\),
\[
\|A_\star w\|\ge\sqrt5\,\|w\|,\qquad
|(A_\star w)_1|-\|(A_\star w)_\perp\|
=3|w_1|-\|w_\perp\|\ge\sqrt2\,\|w\|.
\tag{5}
\]
Choose \(\delta>0\) so \(\|A_x-A_\star\|_{\rm op}\le1/4\) whenever \(\|x-x_\star\|<\delta\). The event \(E=\{\|X-x_\star\|<\delta,\ |Y|\le1\}\) has probability \(p\in(0,1)\). By (1), a finite \(B_\star\) bounds \(\|R(w,X,Y)\|\) on \(E\), uniformly in \(w\). Pick \(R_\star>\max\{1,4B_\star\}\). For \(w\in\mathcal K,\|w\|\ge R_\star\), on \(E\), the distance from \(F(w,X,Y)\) to \(A_\star w\) is at most \(\|w\|/2\). By (5), the cone persists and
\[
\|F(w,X,Y)\|\ge(\sqrt5-1/2)\|w\|>(3/2)\|w\|.
\tag{6}
\]
The open annular cone \(S=\{R_\star<\|w\|<2R_\star,\ \|w_\perp\|<|w_1|/2\}\) has \(\pi(S)>0\). Start in stationarity, require \(W_0\in S\), then \(n\) independent occurrences of \(E\). This event has probability \(\pi(S)p^n\) and entails \(\|W_n\|>(3/2)^nR_\star\). Let \(a=3/2\), \(\kappa=-\log p/\log a\). For \(t\ge R_\star\), choose \(n=\lceil\log(t/R_\star)/\log a\rceil\); stationarity gives
\[
\pi\{\|w\|>t\}\ge\pi(S)p^n\ge\pi(S)p\,(t/R_\star)^{-\kappa}.
\]
The tail-integral moment identity then gives \(\pi\|w\|^q=\infty\) for every \(q\ge\kappa\), despite Gaussian \(X,Y\) and smooth nonlinear \(\phi\). No exact power-law claim is used.
