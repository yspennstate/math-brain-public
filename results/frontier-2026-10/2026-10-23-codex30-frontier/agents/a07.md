# A07 — exact sign-symmetry reduction for one-neuron leaky-ReLU SGD

## Verdict

**The proposed transition-kernel identity is true at every nonzero state.** Under iid fresh data invariant under `(X,Y) -> (-X,-Y)` and `P(w·X=0)=0` for every deterministic `w != 0`, the *actual* gate is independent of the simultaneous-sign-invariant pair `(XXᵀ,YX)` and has values `1,a` with probability `1/2` each. Consequently the nonzero-state SGD kernel is exactly that of ordinary linear least-squares SGD on iid artificial features `Z=CX`, where `C` is independent of the raw `(X,Y)`. For `d >= 2`, a nonzero iterate cannot hit zero under the stated no-hyperplane-mass condition, so this is equality in law of **entire trajectories from every nonzero initialization**, not just one step. At `d=1`, zero may be reached and a fixed derivative convention there generally destroys the trajectory identity. Nothing here asserts that the original gate is independent of the *raw* `(X,Y)`; it is not.

This yields a concrete non-scalar, nonlinear *sample-loss* model with exact multivariate Pareto stationary tails (Gaussian example below). Its Markov chain and population risk nevertheless reduce to linear/quadratic objects, so it should **not** be described as resolving T228 for genuinely non-affine SGD maps.

## Exact theorem and proof

Let `d >= 1`, `η>0`, `a in R` (usually `0<=a<1`), and let `(X_n,Y_n)` be iid, independent of `W_0`, with distribution invariant under `T(x,y)=(-x,-y)`. Assume `P(w·X=0)=0` for each nonzero deterministic `w`. Define `U=(H,J)=(XXᵀ,YX)` and `G_w=1_{w·X>0}+a 1_{w·X<0}`. For any Borel set `D` in the range of `U`, the involution preserves `U` and exchanges the positive/negative gate events. Thus

`P(U in D, w·X>0) = P(U in D, w·X<0) = (1/2)P(U in D)`.

The last equality uses absence of ties. Therefore `G_w` is independent of `U` and its law is the `1/a` fair mixture. In fact the sign is independent of **every** statistic invariant under simultaneous reversal, e.g. also `Y²`. The actual `G_w` is determined by `X,w` and is emphatically *not* independent of raw `(X,Y)`.

At a nonzero `w`, `σ_a(w·X)=G_w(w·X)` and `σ'_a(w·X)=G_w` almost surely, whence

`W'=(I-η G_w² H)w+η G_w J`.

For an independent coin `C` with `P(C=1)=P(C=a)=1/2`, `(G_w,H,J)` and `(C,H,J)` have the same law. Hence for every bounded measurable `f`,

`E[f(W') | W=w] = E[f((I-η C²XXᵀ)w+η C YX)]`, `w != 0`.

Writing `Z=CX`, the right-hand side is exactly the ordinary squared-loss linear SGD step

`w - η(w·Z-Y)Z`.

Let `F_n=σ(W_0,(X_j,Y_j):j<n)`. On `{W_n != 0}`, the conditional law of `(G_{W_n},H_n,J_n)` given `F_n` is the same *fixed* product measure `Law(C)⊗Law(H,J)`, regardless of the random direction `W_n`. By induction the pairs `(G_{W_n},H_n,J_n)` are iid as long as the path avoids zero. This is stronger than equality of a single transition kernel. One can use the actual gates to construct iid affine coefficients `(I-η G_n²H_n,ηG_n J_n)` along this path; these gates remain dependent on the signed raw observations at their own steps. Equivalently, resample independent fresh `(C_n,X_n,Y_n)` and obtain the same state trajectory in distribution.

When `d>=2`, the no-hyperplane condition makes the avoidance of zero automatic. A step has the form `w+sX`, so `w+sX=0` implies `X` is collinear with `w`. Choose any fixed nonzero `v⊥w`. Then `{X collinear with w} ⊂ {v·X=0}`, an event of probability zero. Conditional on every nonzero `W_n=w`, the next iterate is nonzero almost surely; countable induction proves `P(W_n != 0 for all n)=1` if `P(W_0 != 0)=1`. The same applies to the surrogate. This uses `d>=2` and is independent of `a`, labels, and step size.

### Origin and one-dimensional counterexample

If `a!=1`, `σ'_a(0)` is mathematically undefined; a program must specify a value `b`. Its original update at zero is `W'=η b YX`, while the proposed surrogate update is `W'=η C YX`. These kernels generally differ even if `b=(1+a)/2` makes their means agree. The no-ties assumption explicitly excludes `w=0`.

For a reachable-zero example in `d=1`, take `a=0`, `η=1`, `X=±1` fairly, an independent fair Bernoulli `R`, and `Y=RX`. The pair `(X,Y)` has the required joint sign symmetry and `P(wX=0)=0` for every `w!=0`. From `W_0=1`, the event `{X=1,R=0}` has probability `1/4` and sends the true ReLU chain to zero. With the conventional derivative `σ'_0(0)=0`, it stays there forever. The surrogate instead leaves zero with positive probability because `C=1,R=1` occurs with probability `1/4`. Its two-step law thus differs. With derivative `b=1`, the original departure probability is `1/2`, again different. For `0<a<1` the same construction hits zero on `{X=1,R=0}` and a fixed `b` still fails to reproduce the two-point random `C` law. A randomized derivative at zero drawn independently as `C` would restore the identity, but that is a different algorithm. Alternatively, a one-dimensional data law that makes zero inaccessible permits full-path reduction from nonzero starts.

At `d>=2`, any affine stationary law `π` with `π({0})=0` is also stationary for the original chain (regardless of its specified origin derivative), because the kernels agree on its support. A zero-derivative convention may create an additional absorbing stationary state `δ_0` for the original chain, even when the affine recursion has a unique nontrivial invariant law. Therefore do not claim global uniqueness for the unqualified ReLU algorithm.

The condition `P(w·X=0)=0` for **every** nonzero `w` rules out a finite-support dataset in `d>=2`: for any supported nonzero `x`, some nonzero `w` is perpendicular to `x`. One can weaken this condition for a particular trajectory if ties are otherwise controlled.

## Population risk, moments, and labels

Assume `E||X||²+EY²<∞`, let `Σ=E[XXᵀ]` and `μ=E[YX]`, and set `q_j=E[C^j]=(1+a^j)/2`. Simultaneous reversal makes the positive and negative halves contribute equally to `E[(w·X)²]` and to `E[Y(w·X)]`. Thus **for every `w`, including zero**,

`L(w)=(1/2)E[(σ_a(w·X)-Y)²]=(q_2/2) wᵀΣw-q_1 μᵀw+(1/2)EY²`.

The population loss is exactly a convex quadratic, and its gradient is `q_2Σw-q_1μ`. The no-hyperplane assumption and finite second moments make `Σ` positive definite; its unique minimizer is `w_*=(q_1/q_2)Σ⁻¹μ`. A stationary law with an integrable mean (and enough first moments to justify the drift) has mean `w_*`. This generally differs from the usual least-squares coefficient `Σ⁻¹μ` whenever `0<a<1`; ReLU (`a=0`) and linear (`a=1`) happen to give `q_1=q_2`. At the origin, a fixed sample derivative can be biased relative to this smooth population gradient.

For general `a`, the exact second-moment propagation operator for the homogeneous affine part is

`M ↦ E[AMAᵀ] = M-ηq_2(ΣM+MΣ)+η²q_4 E[(XᵀMX)XXᵀ]`,

where `A=I-ηC²XXᵀ` and `q_4=(1+a⁴)/2`. This is a matrix operator, **not** the scalar equation `E|A|^α=1` from one-dimensional Kesten theory. When the affine Lyapunov exponent is negative and standard moment/nondegeneracy/geometric conditions hold, multivariate random-affine results apply to its stationary law; none of those tail conclusions follows from sign symmetry alone.

For ReLU `a=0`, the independent gate is zero half the time and one half the time. The surrogate transition kernel is exactly `P_ReLU=(I+P_linear)/2`, with the same original `(X,Y)` used on active updates. At deterministic time `n`, its law equals that of ordinary linear SGD after an independent `N_n~Binomial(n,1/2)` number of active steps. Its affine stationary measures and tail exponents (when defined) are exactly those of ordinary linear SGD, not merely approximately equal. For the original ReLU chain on `R^d\{0}` this holds under the preceding assumptions; an absorbing origin remains an extra global issue.

Joint sign symmetry accepts an odd linear teacher `Y=θ·X+ε` provided `(X,ε)` is jointly sign symmetric (independent centered symmetric noise is a simple case). It **usually excludes** the realizable leaky-ReLU teacher `Y=σ_b(θ·X)+ε` with `b!=1`: for symmetric `X`, `σ_b(-t) != -σ_b(t)` whenever `t!=0`. In particular, one must not silently transfer this reduction to conventional ReLU-teacher learning.

## Concrete non-scalar Pareto-tail example and exact threshold

Let `d>=2`, `0<a<1`, `X~N(0,σ²I_d)`, and `Y~N(0,τ²)` independently with `σ,τ>0`. This meets both symmetry and no-ties assumptions. The original one-neuron leaky-ReLU sample loss is piecewise quadratic and non-linear in `w`, yet from any nonzero start its trajectory is exactly linear SGD in law with iid `Z=CX`, `A=I-ηZZᵀ`, `B=ηYZ`. Here `Z` is a smooth, full-support, rotationally invariant two-Gaussian scale mixture, independent of `Y`; `B` supplies nondegenerate persistent label noise. In particular, no common fixed point exists: for any fixed `v`, `Av+B=v` requires `Y=Z·v` because `Z!=0`, which has conditional probability zero.

Rotational symmetry reduces the multivariate matrix-product pressure to `k(s)=E||Ae_1||^s`, and the top Lyapunov exponent to `γ=E log||Ae_1||`. [The 2026 corrected random-affine treatment, Theorems 3.1 and 3.4](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/analysing-heavytail-properties-of-stochastic-gradient-descent-by-means-of-stochastic-recurrence-equations/2C941823AF7FD751744E302B362191FF) gives this formula and positive multivariate Pareto tails when its hypotheses hold. Direct Gaussian moments give

`k(2)=1-2η q_2 σ²+η² q_4 (d+2)σ⁴`.

Consequently the positive variance boundary is

`η_2 = 2 q_2/[q_4 σ²(d+2)] = 2(1+a²)/[(1+a⁴)σ²(d+2)]`.

At `η=η_2`, `k(2)=1`; since `||Ae_1||` is nonconstant, strict Jensen yields `γ=(1/2)E log||Ae_1||² < (1/2)log E||Ae_1||²=0`. Thus there is a unique nondegenerate affine stationary law and its Kesten-type root is **exactly `α=2`**. For `η` just above `η_2`, continuity gives `γ<0` while `k(2)>1`, hence the positive root satisfies **`α<2`**. This is a nonempty open interval of contracting-on-average, infinite-variance, multivariate Pareto-tail SGD dynamics. For `η<η_2`, `k(2)<1` and `γ<0`, and the root is `α>2` in this Gaussian setting.

To check the heavy-tail theorem rather than assuming it: `A` is invertible almost surely because `det A=1-η||Z||²` and `||Z||²` has a continuous density; small inverse moments exist since the radial density is locally bounded near `1/η`. Gaussian-mixture and Gaussian-label tails give the required positive moments of `A,B`. The full support of `Z` makes the matrix semigroup strongly irreducible (for any fixed nonzero `v`, `z↦v-η(z·v)z` has locally full-rank Jacobian when `z·v!=0`), proximal (choose `η||z||²>2`), and without an invariant proper cone (choose `z` parallel to any cone vector with `η||z||²>1`, forcing its negative into the cone). Continuous radii also remove arithmetic lattice behavior. Independent continuous `Y` verifies the no-common-fixed-point condition. These are the standard rank-one rotational hypotheses of the cited theorem; the stationary tail constant is positive and the angular tail measure is uniform. At the threshold, `P(||W_∞||>t)~c t^{-2}` with `c>0`; just above, `~c_η t^{-α(η)}` with `0<α(η)<2`. The invariant heavy-tailed law has zero atom at the origin (conditional on `Z`, continuous `Y` cannot map a state exactly to zero), so it is stationary for the original nonzero-state leaky-ReLU chain too.

There is also an elementary variance check: the isotropic stationary covariance, when finite, must be `vI_d`, where `v=η²q_2τ²σ²/(1-k(2)) = η q_2τ²/[2q_2-ηq_4(d+2)σ²]`. At and above `η_2`, the stationarity equation has no finite nonnegative solution because its forcing is strictly positive. This proves the variance transition independently of the full Pareto theorem; the theorem supplies the **exact** tail and positive constant.

At the ReLU endpoint `a=0`, the feature mixture has an atom at zero, so the smooth-density hypothesis just used should **not** be invoked verbatim. Instead the exact lazy-chain identity transports any valid Gaussian linear-SGD Pareto theorem and its exponent unchanged. At the linear endpoint `a=1`, this is ordinary Gaussian least-squares SGD.

It also discusses nonlinear random recursions, but the present symmetry reduction lands squarely in its affine linear-regression class.

Use the corrected 2026 framework for a formal `d>=2` power-law citation. The elementary sign-orbit reduction and Gaussian threshold calculation above are deductions here; no claim is made that the symmetry identity is unpublished.
3. T228 asks for a concrete genuinely nonlinear/asymptotically linear **non-affine** recursion. This model is nonlinear pointwise but affine *in distribution* after symmetry. It is a strong exactly soluble boundary case, not the broad non-affine result requested there.
