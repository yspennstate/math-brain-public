# T228 candidate: exact multivariate tails for a leaky-ReLU neuron under symmetric data

## Status and scope

This is a mathematically explicit extension of the reported one-dimensional scalar result to **every dimension `d >= 2`**, for one-sample constant-step SGD with fresh spherical Gaussian covariates and independent centered symmetric label noise. It gives the *whole vector* stationary Pareto law, uniform angular measure, every positive-moment criterion, and an explicit exact variance boundary. The decisive observation is a **full transition-kernel identity** with an affine linear-regression recursion; it is not an unproved appeal to an asymptotic linearization. The result is conditional on this precise zero-teacher/symmetric-label model. For labels generated by a nonzero *leaky-ReLU* teacher, the symmetry fails and this argument does not establish the sharp tail law.

The literal T228 catalog row explicitly asks for a concrete non-affine SGD model, average contraction, an asymptotically linear random map, nonarithmetic multiplier, and a nonzero power-tail constant, suggesting a smooth bounded activation as an example. The update here is non-affine *for each fixed sample* but is exactly affine **in distribution**; thus it proves the requested *tail phenomenon* for a nonlinear neuron, through a different route. It does **not** verify the sample-map Lipschitz/asymptotically-affine hypotheses of the cited Mirek criterion: with noisy labels the leaky-ReLU gradient jumps across `w^T X=0`. Treat the result as a sharp symmetric subcase, not the generic nonlinear open problem. Average contraction is instead the precisely checked negative top Lyapunov exponent `lambda_eta<0` of its distributionally equivalent affine recursion.

## Proposition (the new reduction)

Let `0 < beta < 1`, `phi_beta(t)=t` if `t>=0` and `beta*t` otherwise. Fix `eta>0` and consider, from a nonzero current state `w in R^d`,

`W' = w - eta [phi_beta(w^T X)-Y] phi_beta'(w^T X) X`, where `X~N(0,I_d)` and `Y~N(0,sigma^2)`, `sigma>0`, independently. The derivative at zero is immaterial for nonzero `w` because `P(w^T X=0)=0`. Let `C` be independent of `(X,Y)` and take values `1` and `beta^2`, each with probability `1/2`. **For each fixed nonzero `w`, the entire vector law** of `W'` is exactly that of

`(I - eta C XX^T)w + eta sqrt(C) Y X`.                    (1)

The right side is linear least-squares SGD using iid design `A=sqrt(C) X` and independent response `Y`: `W' = w-eta(A^T w-Y)A`. A general version needs only joint central symmetry `(X,Y) =_d (-X,-Y)` and `P(w^T X=0)=0`; Gaussianity is used later for rotation invariance and explicit integrals. The same identity works sample by sample for a fresh iid minibatch, replacing (1) by the average of `b` independent linear gradients.

**Proof of exact kernel equality.** Write `s=phi_beta'(w^T X)` and `c=s^2`. The sample update is `(I-eta c XX^T)w+eta s YX`. Under `(X,Y)->(-X,-Y)`, the pair `T=(XX^T,YX)` is unchanged, while the hemispheres `{w^T X>0}` and `{w^T X<0}` are interchanged. Consequently the conditional law of `T` on either hemisphere equals its unconditional law, and each hemisphere has mass `1/2`. Integrating a bounded Borel test function separately over the hemispheres yields exactly the independent fair choice `C in {1,beta^2}` in (1). This proof works for vector test functions and thus equates full Markov kernels, not merely norms or one-step moments.

If initialized at `w=0`, a chosen subgradient convention gives a different first step; with a nondegenerate continuous `Y`, the next state is nonzero almost surely. From any nonzero start, zero is never hit almost surely (condition on `X` and use the continuous law of `Y`). Both chains therefore have identical finite-dimensional laws from nonzero initial states and the same unique nondegenerate invariant law once the affine chain is stable. The affine invariant law puts no mass at zero, so it is also invariant for the original leaky-ReLU kernel irrespective of the derivative convention at zero; every original invariant law has zero mass at zero and hence is affine invariant.

## Sharp theorem and formulas

Let `U~chi-square(1)` and `S~chi-square(d-1)` be independent and `C` be as above. Define

`D_eta = 1 - 2 eta C U + eta^2 C^2 U (U+S)`, `A_eta=sqrt(D_eta)`,

`h_eta(p)=E[A_eta^p] = (1/2) sum_{c in {1,beta^2}} E_{U,S}[(1-2 eta c U+eta^2 c^2 U(U+S))^(p/2)]`,

`lambda_eta=E[log A_eta]`.                         (2)

For `d>=2`, `A_eta>0` almost surely; logarithms and the necessary matrix inverse moments are integrable. Assume `lambda_eta<0`. Then the original neuron SGD has a unique stationary law `pi`, and there is a **unique** `alpha>0` with `h_eta(alpha)=1`. For a continuity set `E` on the unit sphere,

`lim_{t->infty} t^alpha P_pi(||W||>t, W/||W|| in E) = K omega_{d-1}(E)`, with `0<K<infty`,       (3)

where `omega_{d-1}` is normalized uniform surface measure. In particular, `E_pi||W||^p<infty` **iff** `0<p<alpha` (at `p=alpha`, infinity). For any unit `u`,

`P_pi(|u^T W|>t) ~ K [Gamma(d/2)Gamma((alpha+1)/2)/(sqrt(pi)Gamma((d+alpha)/2))] t^(-alpha)`; a one-sided projection has half that constant. The stationary distribution itself is rotationally invariant by uniqueness and symmetry, not only its angular tail.

**Why the existing multivariate theorem applies.** For the affine twin take `A=I-eta aa^T`, `B=eta Y a`, with `a=sqrt(C)X`. The design density is a positive radial mixture of two nonsingular Gaussian densities, is bounded, has full support, and decays faster than any polynomial. This is precisely the rotational-invariance and rank-one-density setup in Damek--Mentemeier (2026), Theorems 3.1, 3.4, 3.6 and the discussion/Corollary 3.3 for arbitrary continuous isotropic design and batch size `b=1`; it is **not** covered by simply quoting their Gaussian `b>d+1` Corollary 3.2. Geometric conditions hold: rank-one matrices with large `||a||` give a unique dominant eigenline; full-support rotations give strong irreducibility and no proper invariant cone. `A` is invertible almost surely. The nontrivial affine fixed-point condition follows because `P(Y=a^T v)=0` for every fixed `v`. The response has moments of every order. The inverse-moment condition can be checked directly at `b=1`: eigenvalues of `A` are `1` (multiplicity `d-1`) and `1-eta||a||^2`; `||a||^2` is a mixture of `chi-square(d)` and `beta^2 chi-square(d)` with smooth density near each positive reciprocal-eta point, so `E||A^{-1}||^delta<infty` for `0<delta<1` (and with arbitrary polynomial `||A||` factors). The cited theorem yields (3), positive `K`, and uniform angular measure.

Alternatively, the exponent in (2) follows directly from isotropy without a spectral computation: for every deterministic unit `v`, `E||Av||^p=h_eta(p)` and hence `E||A_n...A_1v||^p=h_eta(p)^n`. A basis/norm-equivalence bound shows the matrix-product pressure is `h_eta(p)`; the top Lyapunov exponent is `E log A_eta`. Strict log-convexity, `h(0)=1`, `h'(0)=lambda_eta<0`, and `P(A_eta>1)>0` give the unique positive root. Damek--Mentemeier supplies the nontrivial positive tail constant and exact regular variation; this part should not be claimed from moment calculations alone.

## Explicit sharp second-moment phase transition

Using `E U=1`, `E U^2=3`, `E US=d-1`, `E C=(1+beta^2)/2`, and `E C^2=(1+beta^4)/2`, obtain

`h_eta(2)=1-eta(1+beta^2)+(eta^2/2)(d+2)(1+beta^4)`,

`eta_2 = 2(1+beta^2)/[(d+2)(1+beta^4)]`.             (4)

For `0<eta<eta_2`, the unique stationary second moment is exactly

`E_pi||W||^2 = eta sigma^2 d(1+beta^2) / [2(1+beta^2)-eta(d+2)(1+beta^4)]`;             (5)

its covariance is `E_pi[WW^T]=(E_pi||W||^2/d) I_d`. The identity comes from `E(||W'||^2|W)=h_eta(2)||W||^2+eta^2 sigma^2 d(1+beta^2)/2`, with the mixed term zero by centered independent `Y`. For `eta>=eta_2` **while `lambda_eta<0`**, the stationary variance is infinite by (3), including the boundary `alpha=2`. In fact `lambda_{eta_2}<0` strictly by Jensen: `E log A_eta < (1/2)log E A_eta^2=0`. Continuity thus yields a nonempty interval immediately *above* `eta_2` in which the stationary law exists but has infinite variance. The finite-variance side `eta<eta_2` is stable since Jensen gives `lambda_eta<0`. As a small concrete example, for `d=2,beta=1/2`, `eta_2=10/17≈0.588235`.

The slope dependence is nonmonotone: with `t=beta^2`, `(1+t)/(1+t^2)` is maximal at `t=sqrt(2)-1`, where `eta_2=(1+sqrt(2))/(d+2)`; its limiting values at `beta=0` and `beta=1` are both `2/(d+2)`. This is a direct consequence of (4), not a literature claim. The general phase threshold for the `p`th moment is the exact `h_eta(p)=1` curve (equivalently `p=alpha(eta)`), with the stable domain separately defined by `lambda_eta<0`. Do not claim global uniqueness of a zero of `lambda_eta` without additional analysis.

## What is known versus contributed here

* **Known primary theorem:** Gürbüzbalaban--Simsekli--Zhu (ICML 2021) develops multiplicative-noise heavy tails for *linear* quadratic SGD, and Damek--Mentemeier (Journal of Applied Probability 2026) fixes gaps in its multivariate proof, establishes regular variation via irreducible-proximal affine recursions, and supplies the rotational pressure and angular law. Their 2026 paper explicitly notes the 2021 theorem's matrix-density argument is invalid for symmetric rank-one updates, and a determinant-integrability step also needs repair. Cite the corrected 2026 theorem for (3).
* **New derivation here:** The leaky-ReLU *nonlinear* Markov kernel is exactly an affine linear-regression kernel for centrally symmetric data, with an independent random design scale. Applying the corrected theorem gives the multidimensional vector tail (3) and explicit leaky-slope mixture (2), while the fresh computation (4)--(5) gives its exact variance threshold and stable infinite-variance interval. This is an algebraic reduction of a special symmetric subcase; it should not be sold as a general theorem for nonlinear labels.
* **Still open from this argument:** For `Y=phi_beta(w_*^T X)+epsilon` with `w_* !=0`, `(X,Y)` is generally **not** invariant under `(X,Y)->(-X,-Y)`, so the independent Bernoulli representation is false. This proof cannot establish exact tails, angular uniformity, or moment necessity there. A linear teacher `Y=v_*^T X+epsilon` *does* preserve joint central symmetry and therefore the exact affine reduction, though its finite stationary law is generally anisotropic; the angular tail is still uniform by the rotation-invariant multiplicative matrices. For an even label component, test the kernel identity before transferring the theorem.
