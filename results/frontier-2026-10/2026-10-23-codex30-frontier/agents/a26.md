# Evidence weights for finite hierarchical GP mixtures: a partial theorem

**Source boundary.** Kang–Lee–Cheng (2026), [arXiv HTML, Remark 3.2 and §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26713v1), also [PMLR 306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/kang26h.html), suggest parallel componentwise Richardson recursions and an additional mixture-weight readout; they explicitly leave a full hierarchical-GP theorem open. Their §E contains finite-hyperparameter-mixture experiments. Their Theorem 3.1 assumes *unnormalized PSD-kernel attention*, not standard ReLU dot-product attention. The result here proves a numerical evidence and mixture-error lemma; it does not construct that missing attention architecture or claim to resolve an unidentified T336 benchmark statement.

## Uniform evidence-weight theorem

Fix $H<\infty$ components with $\pi_h>0$ and $\sum_h\pi_h=1$. In component $h$, a zero-mean GP gives $A_h=K_h+s_hI_n$, $s_h>0$, for training inputs and labels $Y$. Assume uniformly over components and admissible prompts that $n\le N$, $aI_n\preceq A_h\preceq bI_n$ with $0<a<b$, and $\|Y\|_2\le B\sqrt N$. All bounds and the finite prior are fixed before observing the prompt. Set
$$
\kappa=b/a,\quad q=\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}\in(0,1),\quad
c=(a+b)/2,\quad d=(b-a)/2,\quad
\gamma=(\sqrt b+\sqrt a)^2/4,\quad t(x)=(x-c)/d .
$$
The evidence logits and posterior component weights are
$$
\ell_h=\log\pi_h-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\log A_h
                -\tfrac12Y^\top A_h^{-1}Y,\qquad
w_h=\frac{e^{\ell_h}}{\sum_j e^{\ell_j}} .
$$
The common $-n\log(2\pi)/2$ likelihood term cancels in the softmax.

Let $T_j$ be Chebyshev polynomials. Define, for $m\ge1$ and $r\ge0$,
$$
R_m(x)=\frac{T_m((c-x)/d)}{T_m(c/d)},\qquad
P_m(x)=\frac{1-R_m(x)^2}{x},\qquad
L_r(x)=\log\gamma+
       2\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{(-1)^{j+1}q^j}{j}T_j(t(x)).
$$
Since $R_m(0)=1$, $P_m$ is genuinely a polynomial of degree at most $2m-1$; its evaluation uses no inverse. Form
$$
\widehat\ell_h=\log\pi_h-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}L_r(A_h)
                         -\tfrac12Y^\top P_m(A_h)Y,\qquad
\widehat w=\operatorname{softmax}(\widehat\ell).
$$
Then the following deterministic bounds hold *uniformly* over $h$ and prompts:
$$
\begin{aligned}
0&\preceq P_m(A_h)\preceq A_h^{-1},&
0&\preceq A_h^{-1}-P_m(A_h)\preceq D_m I_n,&
D_m&=\frac{4q^{2m}}a,\\
|\ell_h-\widehat\ell_h|
&\le\varepsilon_{m,r}:=
\frac{Nq^{r+1}}{(r+1)(1-q)}
+\frac{2B^2N}{a}q^{2m},\\
\mathrm{KL}(w\|\widehat w)&\le\varepsilon_{m,r}^2/2,&
\mathrm{TV}(w,\widehat w)&\le\min\{1,\varepsilon_{m,r}/2\}. \tag{1}
\end{aligned}
$$
The weight bounds require neither a lower bound on posterior weights nor a factor depending on $H$. If the implementation incurs additional uniform logit error $e_{\rm impl}$, replace $\varepsilon_{m,r}$ by $\varepsilon_{m,r}+e_{\rm impl}$. Separate absolute trace and quadratic errors $e_{\rm tr},e_{\rm quad}$ contribute $(e_{\rm tr}+e_{\rm quad})/2$ to this implementation error, along with any error in $\log\pi_h$.

For any $0<\delta<1$, the explicit sufficient choices
$$
r+1\ge\max\!\left\{\frac1{1-q},
                  \frac{\log(2N/\delta)}{\log(1/q)}\right\},\qquad
m\ge\max\!\left\{1,
        \frac{\log_+(4B^2N/(a\delta))}{2\log(1/q)}\right\} \tag{2}
$$
give $\varepsilon_{m,r}\le\delta$; the second logarithmic condition is vacuous for $B=0$. Thus the degrees $r$ and $2m-1$ are
$O(\sqrt\kappa[1+\log(N(1+B^2/a)/\delta)])$, in particular $O(\sqrt\kappa\log(N/\delta))$ when $B^2/a$ is fixed and the accuracy is small. Here $\log(1/q)=2\operatorname{arctanh}(1/\sqrt\kappa)\ge2/\sqrt\kappa$ and $(1-q)^{-1}=(\sqrt\kappa+1)/2$. When $a=b$, each $A_h=aI_n$, so constant inverse and logarithm polynomials give exact results.

**Proof.** $T_m(c/d)=(q^{-m}+q^m)/2>1$, while $|T_m((c-x)/d)|\le1$ on $[a,b]$. Hence $|R_m(x)|\le2q^m/(1+q^{2m})<1$. Spectral calculus applied to
$A_h^{-1}-P_m(A_h)=A_h^{-1}R_m(A_h)^2$
proves the first line of (1), including its one-sided sign. Next $c=\gamma(1+q^2)$ and $d=2\gamma q$. For $t(x)=\cos\theta$, the convergent Fourier–Chebyshev expansion is
$$
\log x=\log\gamma+\log(1+qe^{i\theta})+\log(1+qe^{-i\theta})
=\log\gamma+2\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}q^j}{j}T_j(t(x)).
$$
Its uniform tail is at most
$\eta_r=2q^{r+1}/((r+1)(1-q))$, so the log-determinant error is at most $n\eta_r\le N\eta_r$. The quadratic error is one-sided and at most $D_m\|Y\|^2\le4B^2Nq^{2m}/a$. Halving these errors proves the logit bound. The two constraints (2) make the determinant and quadratic contributions at most $\delta/2$ each.

For the weights, let $e_h=\widehat\ell_h-\ell_h\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]$. Then $\widehat w_h=w_he^{e_h}/\mathbb E_w[e^e]$ and
$$
\mathrm{KL}(w\|\widehat w)
=\log\mathbb E_w[e^e]-\mathbb E_w[e]
\le\frac{(\max e_h-\min e_h)^2}{8}
\le\varepsilon^2/2
$$
by the bounded-variable Hoeffding lemma. Pinsker yields the TV bound. These arguments apply to arbitrary positive prior weights.

## Combining evidence and Gaussian components

For one **noisy** query $x_*$, set $v_h=k_h(X,x_*)$, $u_h=k_h(x_*,x_*)+s_h$, and assume additionally that $s_h\ge s_0>0$ and $\|v_h\|_2\le V$ uniformly. The component predictive laws are
$$
G_h=\mathcal N(\mu_h,C_h),\quad
\mu_h=v_h^\top A_h^{-1}Y,\quad
C_h=u_h-v_h^\top A_h^{-1}v_h\ge s_0 ,
$$
and use the same polynomial for $\widehat\mu_h=v_h^\top P_m(A_h)Y$ and
$\widehat C_h=u_h-v_h^\top P_m(A_h)v_h\ge C_h$, yielding
$\widehat G_h=\mathcal N(\widehat\mu_h,\widehat C_h)$. Then
$$
|\widehat\mu_h-\mu_h|\le D_mVB\sqrt N,\qquad
0\le\widehat C_h-C_h\le D_mV^2,
$$
and direct univariate Gaussian KL calculation gives
$$
\mathrm{KL}(G_h\|\widehat G_h)
\le\xi_m:=
D_m^2\left(\frac{V^2B^2N}{2s_0}
          +\frac{V^4}{4s_0^2}\right). \tag{3}
$$
Indeed, writing $t=(\widehat C_h-C_h)/C_h\ge0$, its covariance contribution is
$\frac12[\log(1+t)-t/(1+t)]\le t^2/4$; the mean contribution is at most
$(\widehat\mu_h-\mu_h)^2/(2s_0)$.

For the exact and approximate *mixture laws*
$P=\sum_h w_hG_h$ and $\widehat P=\sum_h\widehat w_h\widehat G_h$,
the chain rule for the labeled joint law followed by data processing (forgetting $h$) proves
$$
\mathrm{KL}(P\|\widehat P)
\le\mathrm{KL}(w\|\widehat w)
       +\sum_h w_h\mathrm{KL}(G_h\|\widehat G_h)
\le\varepsilon_{m,r}^2/2+\xi_m,\qquad
\mathrm{TV}(P,\widehat P)
\le\sqrt{\varepsilon_{m,r}^2/4+\xi_m/2}. \tag{4}
$$
For example, choose (2) and increase $m$ until $\xi_m\le\delta^2$; then TV is at most $\sqrt3\,\delta/2$.

For a fixed number $r_*$ of jointly queried noisy observations, replace $v_h$ by its $n\times r_*$ cross-covariance, $V$ by an operator-norm bound, and the $V^4/(4s_0^2)$ term of (3) by $r_*V^4/(4s_0^2)$. For a single query with $k_h(x_*,x_*)\le k_{\max}$, positive semidefiniteness of the joint prior kernel matrix implies the sufficient $V^2\le b k_{\max}$; for $r_*$ queries, $\|K_{XQ}\|_{\rm op}^2\le b\|K_{QQ}\|_{\rm op}\le br_*k_{\max}$. The training-spectrum assumptions alone do not bound query cross-covariances. For a noiseless/latent query, a separate lower bound on predictive covariance is needed for (3)–(4).

## Matrix operations and unresolved architecture

An exact deterministic trace needs $n$ basis-vector channels per component. Let $D_h=(A_h-cI)/d$, initialize $U_0=I_n$, $U_1=D_h$, and iterate $U_{j+1}=2D_hU_j-U_{j-1}$. Then
$\operatorname{tr}L_r(A_h)=n\log\gamma+
2\sum_{j=1}^r(-1)^{j+1}q^j\operatorname{tr}U_j/j$.
This uses $O(HN)$ vector channels across the fixed components, $O(HN^2)$ scalar state over $n$ tokens, and $r$ matrix applications, plus positional diagonal extraction and summation. $P_m(A_h)Y$ is a degree-$(2m-1)$ polynomial application requiring at most $2m-1$ additional matrix applications, not an inverse oracle. Randomized trace estimation would instead require its own probabilistic error theorem.

Kang et al.'s kernel-attention primitive can multiply by $K_h$, with the $s_hI$ part in a local linear skip, **if** each component kernel is available as its attention similarity. A full ReLU-attention existence theorem still must realize or uniformly approximate those kernel products in the specified primitive; route $N$ positional channels; extract and sum diagonal entries; propagate finite arithmetic errors through the polynomial recurrences; and specify width/depth and readout for bounded-domain products and softmax. ReLU MLPs can approximate the scalar maps on the resulting compact domains, but that alone is not an explicit attention construction. Equations (1)–(4) establish the evidence and mixture analysis conditional on these computational primitives.
