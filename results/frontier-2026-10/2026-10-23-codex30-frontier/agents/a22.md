# One-sided Chebyshev inverse for coherent finite-depth GP prediction

**Scope.** This is a self-contained algebraic extension of the kernel-attention GP construction in Kang, Lee, and Cheng, [*Transformers Can Learn Posterior Predictive Distributions In-Context*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/kang26h.html), ICML 2026. Their published single-query theorem uses Richardson iteration and does not state the joint covariance or this Chebyshev construction. The classical Chebyshev semi-iteration and its polynomial preconditioning interpretation predate the paper: see Golub and Varga, [*Chebyshev semi-iterative methods, successive overrelaxation iterative methods, and second order Richardson iterative methods*](https://www.math.kent.edu/~varga/pub/paper_17.pdf), *Numerische Mathematik* 3 (1961), and Wathen and Rees, [*Chebyshev semi-iteration in preconditioning for problems including the mass matrix*](https://etna.ricam.oeaw.ac.at/volumes/2001-2010/vol34/abstract.php?pages=125-135), *Electronic Transactions on Numerical Analysis* 34 (2009). Earlier Brain w727 work, as checked by the coordinator, **already covered scalar GP prediction with fixed features and a Chebyshev feature-form variance that is PSD above the noise floor at every finite depth**; its stated `δ≤1/2` threshold entered the KL estimate, not positivity. The present candidate's incremental target is a **data-space conservative Loewner approximation** `C_P≽C` for arbitrary PSD kernels, the full joint multi-query covariance, and a uniform forward KL without a small-error threshold. It must not be described as inventing Chebyshev GP prediction or finite-depth positivity. The one-sided derivation and joint bounds below are proved here, with no novelty claim.

## Algebraic theorem and the exact one-sided minimax residual

Let `A=Aᵀ ≻ 0` have spectrum in a known interval `[a,b]`, `0<a<b`. Write

`h=(a+b)/2`, `d=(b−a)/2`, `z(x)=(h−x)/d`, `z₀=h/d>1`, `κ=b/a`, and `ρ=(√κ−1)/(√κ+1)∈(0,1)`.

With `T_j` the first-kind Chebyshev polynomial, define

`q_j(x)=T_j(z(x))/T_j(z₀)`, `δ_j=1/T_j(z₀)=2ρ^j/(1+ρ^{2j})`.

Then `q_j(0)=1`, `|q_j(x)|≤δ_j<1` on `[a,b]`, and the requested degree-`2L−1` polynomial is

`P_{2L−1}(x)=(1−q_L(x)^2)/x`.

The apparent quotient is a polynomial because its numerator vanishes at `x=0`. For all eigenvalues in `[a,b]`,

`0 < P_{2L−1}(x) ≤ 1/x`, `0 ≤ 1−xP_{2L−1}(x)=q_L(x)^2 ≤ δ_L² ≤4ρ^{2L}`.

Consequently `0 ≺ P_{2L−1}(A) ≼ A⁻¹`, with the exact error identity

`A⁻¹−P_{2L−1}(A)=A⁻¹q_L(A)^2 ≽0`.

No threshold on `L` is necessary: every integer `L≥1` works. More generally, for *any* degree `D≥1`, set

`r_D(x)=[T_D(z(x))+1]/[T_D(z₀)+1]=[q_D(x)+δ_D]/[1+δ_D]`,

`\widehat P_{D−1}(x)=[1−r_D(x)]/x=[1−q_D(x)]/[(1+δ_D)x]`,

`ε_D=max_{x∈[a,b]}r_D(x)=2δ_D/(1+δ_D)=4ρ^D/(1+ρ^D)^2`.

Thus `0≤r_D≤ε_D<1` and `0≺\widehat P_{D−1}(A)≼A⁻¹`. The Chebyshev identity `T_{2L}=2T_L²−1` gives **`r_{2L}=q_L²` and `\widehat P_{2L−1}=P_{2L−1}`**, so the squared construction is exactly the even-degree member of this family. These are minimax among all *one-sided* inverse polynomials in the residual sense: if `p` has degree at most `D−1`, `r(x)=1−xp(x)≥0` on `[a,b]`, and `E=max r(x)`, then `r(0)=1`; the Chebyshev extremal property applied to `2r/E−1` gives `2/E−1≤T_D(z₀)`, hence `E≥ε_D`. Equality is attained by `r_D`. This is a minimax statement for `max|1−xp(x)|` subject to `p≤1/x`, not for unweighted `max|p(x)−1/x|`.

When `a=b`, take `P(x)=1/a` on the singleton spectrum (zero residual), avoiding the undefined affine mapping. For a uniform prompt class with `a=s`, `b=s+NR²`, the nondegenerate case holds if `NR²>0`.

## Matrix-free recurrence; fixed, data-independent coefficients

Let `B` be an arbitrary matrix of right-hand sides. The ordinary degree-`D−1` Chebyshev iterate `F_D(A)B=[1−q_D(A)]A⁻¹B` is computed without an inverse or an eigendecomposition. Define `t_j=T_j(z₀)` and, for `j≥1`, `β_j=t_{j−1}/t_{j+1}`, `α_j=2t_j/(d t_{j+1})`. Initialize

`X₀=0`, `X₁=B/h`, and iterate for `j=1,…,D−1`:

`X_{j+1}=X_j+β_j(X_j−X_{j−1})+α_j(B−A X_j)`.

Then `X_D=F_D(A)B`, and **the one-sided result** is `\widehat P_{D−1}(A)B=X_D/(1+δ_D)`. It costs `D−1` applications of `A` to all right-hand sides (and `O(D)` vector additions/scalings), plus an optional final application if one explicitly wants its residual. In particular, the requested `P_{2L−1}` costs `2L−1` `A`-matvecs. The recurrence follows by normalizing `T_{j+1}(z(A))=2z(A)T_j(z(A))−T_{j−1}(z(A))` and replacing `q_j(A)B` by `B−A X_j`.

At every intermediate `j≥1`, the output `X_j/(1+δ_j)` uses the degree-`j−1` one-sided polynomial `\widehat P_{j−1}`. Thus an anytime implementation can report a PSD predictive covariance after every recurrence stage, not only after the terminal stage.

For numerical coefficient generation without growing `t_j`, use `w₁=1/z₀`, `w_{j+1}=1/(2z₀−w_j)`, `β_j=w_jw_{j+1}`, `α_j=2w_{j+1}/d`; evaluate `δ_D=2ρ^D/(1+ρ^{2D})`. All scalars depend only on the fixed spectral bounds and `D`, not on the training data or labels. Exact Loewner positivity is an exact-arithmetic claim; rounding error can slightly break it in a finite-precision implementation.

For comparison, two `L`-stage ordinary Chebyshev solves also construct the squared residual. Compute `U=F_L(A)B`, `V=B−AU=q_L(A)B`, and `X=U+F_L(A)V`. Then `X=P_{2L−1}(A)B` and again only `2L−1` matvecs are needed (the `L`th first-cycle matvec forms `V`, while each `F_L` costs `L−1`). The single `D=2L` recurrence above is simpler.

## Joint noisy GP covariance and forward KL

For any PSD kernel and training inputs `X=(x_i)_{i=1}^n`, query inputs `Q=(q_j)_{j=1}^m`, let `G=K_XX`, `H=K_XQ`, `J=K_QQ`, `A=G+sI_n` with `s>0`. The exact *noisy-test-observation* predictive law is `\mathcal N(μ,C)` where

`μ=HᵀA⁻¹Y`, `C=J+sI_m−HᵀA⁻¹H`.

Use any one-sided polynomial above, with residual `r(A)=I−AP(A)` satisfying `0≼r(A)≼ε I`. Define finite-depth predictions

`μ_P=HᵀP(A)Y`, `C_P=J+sI_m−HᵀP(A)H`.

The block Gram matrix `[[G,H],[Hᵀ,J]]` is PSD, so `[[A,H],[Hᵀ,J]]` is PSD and its Schur complement yields `S:=HᵀA⁻¹H≼J`. Therefore

`C≽sI_m`, `Δ:=C_P−C=HᵀA⁻¹r(A)H≽0`, `Δ≼ε S≼ε J`, and `C_P≽C≽sI_m`.

For *every* finite collection of query inputs, `J−HᵀP(A)H` is PSD. Indeed it is the sum of `J−S≽0` and `Δ≽0`. The formula thus defines an exact PSD posterior covariance **kernel**, including all query cross-covariances, at every finite depth. Its noisy version is uniformly positive definite. Positive definiteness of the kernel alone should not be confused with proving `μ_P,C_P` are the exact posterior moments of a different GP prior.

For a general class with `n≤N`, `m≤M`, `|y_i|≤B`, and `k(x,x)≤R²` for all training and query inputs, one can fix `a=s`, `b=s+NR²`: `G≽0`, `λ_max(G)≤tr G≤NR²`, so the same polynomial coefficients work for *every prompt*. The Schur bound gives `tr S≤tr J≤mR²`, avoiding the cruder `||H||²≤nmR⁴`. The mean error `e:=μ−μ_P=HᵀA⁻¹r(A)Y` obeys

`eeᵀ≼ ε² (||Y||²/s) S`, hence `||e||²≤ε² NB²mR²/s`.

To bound **forward** `KL(\mathcal N(μ,C) || \mathcal N(μ_P,C_P))`, set `T=C⁻¹/²ΔC⁻¹/²≽0`. The covariance contribution is

`(1/2) Σ_i [log(1+t_i)−t_i/(1+t_i)] ≤ ||T||_F²/4 ≤ (tr T)²/4 ≤ ε²m²R⁴/(4s²)`;

the inequality uses `log(1+t)−t/(1+t)≤t²/2` for `t≥0`. The mean contribution is at most `||e||²/(2s)`. Therefore, uniformly over this prompt class,

`KL(\mathcal N(μ,C) || \mathcal N(μ_P,C_P)) ≤ [ε²/(4s²)](2NB²mR²+m²R⁴) ≤ [ε²/(4s²)](2NB²MR²+M²R⁴).`

For the *requested squared construction*, `ε=δ_L²=4ρ^{2L}/(1+ρ^{2L})²≤4ρ^{2L}`, so forward KL is at most

`[4ρ^{4L}/s²](2NB²mR²+m²R⁴)`.

For a desired tolerance `ζ` in forward KL it is enough to choose `L` such that the last displayed expression is `≤ζ` (when `ρ=0`, the exact inverse is immediate). When `A_*:=2NB²mR²+m²R⁴>0` and `0<ρ<1`, a sufficient even degree `D=2L` satisfies `D≥log(4A_*/(s²ζ))/[2log(1/ρ)]`, from `ε_D≤4ρ^D`; take `D≥2` and round upward to even, while a nonpositive logarithm imposes no additional depth. Since `log(1/ρ)∼2/√κ` for large `κ`, the matvec depth is `O(√κ log(1/ζ))` for fixed accuracy constants. The Richardson residual with step `1/b` is `(1−x/b)^D`, with worst-case `(1−1/κ)^D`, requiring `O(κ log(1/ζ))` matvec depth under the same style of bound. Pinsker also gives `TV≤sqrt(KL/2)`. The `s>0` noise floor is essential to a uniform Gaussian KL guarantee; the PSD latent covariance alone can be singular. These bounds are deterministic in the labels, not expectation bounds under a GP draw.

## Architectural and novelty boundary

The linear-algebra statement needs only a train-kernel matvec `A V=G V+sV`, local linear updates, and a final cross-kernel readout `HᵀV`. All columns of `[Y,H]` can be carried as simultaneous right-hand sides, with the query Gram `J` read in separately. Under the **unnormalized kernel-attention** and source-mask primitive of Kang–Lee–Cheng, this suggests `D+O(1)` attention blocks and fixed width linear in the number of query slots, provided train tokens store the actual dual coefficients `X_j(i)` and preserve the original RHS. A subtlety: storing only primal values `k(z,X)X_j` does **not** give direct access to `A X_j` at training tokens; the coefficient state must be routed as such. The exact token wiring and any restrictions imposed by signed values or normalized attention require an explicit architectural proof. This note does not establish an implementation in standard ReLU/softmax attention.

The classical Chebyshev residual and polynomial preconditioning are established numerical methods; prior Brain w727 also contains finite-depth positive scalar GP variance via a different feature-space formula. The novelty search for the precise one-sided data-space GP posterior interpretation and architectural realization is incomplete; do not claim that the minimax polynomial, Chebyshev GP prediction, or mere finite-depth positivity is new, or that this solves an unspecified benchmark T336 without its literal statement.
