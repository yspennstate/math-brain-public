# A19: minibatch extension of smooth nonlinear T228 with a positive exact tail

Let \(d\ge2\), \(b\in\mathbb N\), \(\tau>0\), and let each fresh batch consist of independent \(X_i\sim N(0,I_d)\), \(Y_i\sim N(0,\tau^2)\), mutually independent across examples and times. Use the half-squared loss and the *average* batch gradient, with \(\phi(z)=z+\varepsilon\tanh z\) for an **arbitrary fixed** \(\varepsilon>0\):

\[
 W^+=W-\frac{\eta}{b}\sum_{i=1}^b
     (\phi(W\cdot X_i)-Y_i)\phi'(W\cdot X_i)X_i.
\]

**Conclusion.** Put \(\eta_2=2b/(d+b+1)\). For every \(b,d\) as above there is \(\delta_{b,d}>0\) such that for each \(\eta\in(\eta_2,\eta_2+\delta_{b,d})\), and for **every** \(\varepsilon>0\), the exact radius chain has at least one invariant probability law. Every one of its invariant laws \(\nu\) satisfies

\[
 \Pr_\nu(R>t)\sim C_\nu t^{-\alpha},\qquad
 0<C_\nu<\infty,\qquad 0<\alpha<2,
\]

where \(\alpha\) is the unique positive solution of

\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
  \left\{\left(1-\frac{\eta S}{b}\right)^2
         +\frac{\eta^2 S Q}{b^2}\right\}^{\alpha/2}\right]=1,
 \quad S\sim\chi_b^2,\quad Q\sim\chi_{d-1}^2,\quad S\perp Q.
 \tag{1}
\]

In particular, the exponent is independent of \(\varepsilon\), though the invariant law and constant generally are not. Isotropically lifting any invariant radius yields a stationary vector-SGD law with the same norm tail. No claim of uniqueness for arbitrary \(\varepsilon\) is needed: large \(\varepsilon\) need not satisfy a global average-Lipschitz contraction.

## Exact radial kernel and uniform nonlinear remainder

Fix a deterministic unit vector \(e_1\), and build from one batch the random continuous function

\[
 \Psi_b(r)=\left\|r e_1-\frac{\eta}{b}\sum_i
       (\phi(rZ_i)-Y_i)\phi'(rZ_i)X_i\right\|,\quad
 Z_i=e_1\cdot X_i,\quad r\ge0.
\]

For any current nonzero \(w=rv\), choose an orthogonal \(O\) with \(Oe_1=v\). Since \((O^TX_i,Y_i)_i\) has the original batch law, \(\|W^+\|\), conditional on \(W=w\), has exactly the distribution \(\Psi_b(r)\); the same holds at \(r=0\). Thus the radius process has the transition kernel of the iid random-function recursion \(R_{n+1}=\Psi_{b,n+1}(R_n)\), not just an asymptotic radial approximation. An isotropic measure with invariant radius is invariant for vector SGD by orthogonal equivariance.

Let \(A_b=I-\eta b^{-1}\sum_iX_iX_i^T\), \(M_b=\|A_be_1\|\), and write \(t(z)=\tanh z\), \(s(z)=\operatorname{sech}^2z\). Since \(|t|,s\le1\) and \(|z|s(z)\le1\), the identity

\[
 (\phi(z)-Y)\phi'(z)-(z-Y)
  =\varepsilon t(z)+\varepsilon z s(z)
     +\varepsilon^2t(z)s(z)-\varepsilon Y s(z)
\]

implies, simultaneously for **all** \(r\ge0\),

\[
 \left|\Psi_b(r)-M_b r\right|\le Q_b,
 \qquad Q_b:=\frac{\eta}{b}\sum_{i=1}^b\|X_i\|
   \big[(1+\varepsilon)|Y_i|+2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\big].
 \tag{2}
\]

This follows by comparing the vectors, then using the reverse triangle inequality. All positive moments of \(Q_b\) and \(M_b\) are finite for every fixed finite \(b,\varepsilon,\eta\). The pair \((M_b,Q_b)\) may be dependent; the cited scalar theorem permits that. Notice \(\Psi_b(0)\le Q_b\).

## Chi-square slope and the stable \(\alpha<2\) interval

Decompose \(X_i=(Z_i,U_i)\), \(U_i\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\). With \(S=\sum_i Z_i^2\), conditionally on the entire \((Z_i)_i\), the vector \(\sum_i Z_iU_i\) is \(N(0,S I_{d-1})\). Hence its squared norm has the joint law \(S Q\), for \(Q\sim\chi_{d-1}^2\) independent of \(S\sim\chi_b^2\), and

\[
 M_b^2=\left(1-\frac{\eta S}{b}\right)^2+
                \frac{\eta^2 S Q}{b^2}. \tag{3}
\]

There is **one** \(S\) in the perpendicular term, not \(S^2\): the conditional covariance of \(\sum Z_iU_i\) is \(S I_{d-1}\). Since \(\mathbb E S=b\), \(\mathbb E S^2=b(b+2)\), and \(\mathbb E Q=d-1\),

\[
 h_b(2):=\mathbb E M_b^2
 =1-2\eta+\eta^2\frac{d+b+1}{b},\qquad
 h_b(2)=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \eta=\eta_2
 \quad(\eta>0). \tag{4}
\]

For every \(\eta>0\), (3) gives \(M_b\ge(\eta/b)\sqrt{S Q}>0\) almost surely. Both chi-square logarithms are integrable even when \(b=1,d=2\). Together with the polynomial upper bound \(M_b\le1+(\eta/b)\sum_i\|X_i\|^2\), this proves \(\mathbb E|\log M_b|<\infty\). These bounds locally uniformly dominate \(|\log M_b(\eta)|\) as \(\eta\) ranges over a compact interval away from zero, so

\[
 \gamma_b(\eta):=\mathbb E\log M_b(\eta)
\]

is continuous on \((0,\infty)\). **Only logarithmic integrability is used; no inverse moment of \(S\), \(Q\), or \(M_b\) is assumed.** At \(\eta_2\), strict Jensen, with \(M_b\) nonconstant, gives

\[
 \gamma_b(\eta_2)<\tfrac12\log\mathbb E M_b(\eta_2)^2=0.
\]

Consequently some \(\delta_{b,d}>0\) has \(\gamma_b(\eta)<0\) throughout \([\eta_2,\eta_2+\delta_{b,d})\), whereas (4) gives \(h_b(2)>1\) for \(\eta>\eta_2\). The function \(p\mapsto\log h_b(p)\), \(h_b(p)=\mathbb E M_b^p\), has derivative \(\gamma_b<0\) at zero, is strictly convex (the slope has a nonconstant continuous law), and has \(\log h_b(2)>0\). Hence it has one and only one positive zero \(\alpha\in(0,2)\), as in (1), and \(h_b(s)<1\) for every \(s\in(0,\alpha)\). At the boundary \(\eta=\eta_2\), the same argument gives \(\alpha=2\).

The conditional distribution of \(M_b\) given \(S>0\) is a strictly increasing continuous function of \(Q\); therefore \(\log M_b\) is nonarithmetic. Gaussian polynomial bounds give every positive moment of \(M_b\), and \(x^\alpha|\log x|\) is bounded near zero, so \(\mathbb E[M_b^\alpha|\log M_b|]<\infty\). The continuous conditional law also proves the strict Jensen/convexity claims above.

## Existence, unbounded support, and exact positive tail

Choose \(s\in(0,\min\{1,\alpha\})\). From (2), subadditivity and fresh batches give

\[
 \mathbb E[\Psi_b(r)^s]\le h_b(s)r^s+\mathbb E Q_b^s,
 \qquad h_b(s)<1.
\]

Iterating from any finite radius makes \(\sup_n\mathbb E R_n^s<\infty\). The radius kernel is Feller because each sample map is continuous. Tightness of its Cesàro laws and the Krylov–Bogoliubov argument produce an invariant \(\nu\). This avoids assuming any global contraction of the nonlinear maps. There is no dependence on a smallness restriction for \(\varepsilon\).

Every invariant \(\nu\) has unbounded right support. Indeed, for fixed \(r\) and conditionally on the \(X_i\), the update vector is a deterministic vector plus the centered Gaussian

\[
  \frac{\eta}{b}\sum_i (1+\varepsilon\operatorname{sech}^2(rZ_i))Y_iX_i.
\]

Its covariance \(\tau^2\eta^2b^{-2}\sum_i(1+\varepsilon\operatorname{sech}^2(rZ_i))^2X_iX_i^T\) is nonzero almost surely. Thus \(\Pr(\Psi_b(r)>T)>0\) for every \(r,T\), and invariance implies \(\nu((T,\infty))>0\). Full-dimensional Gaussian noise is unnecessary; a nonzero one-dimensional Gaussian projection suffices even if \(b<d\).

For precise application of Alsmeyer–Brofferio–Buraczewski (2023), define on all real \(x\)

\[
 F(x)=1+\Psi_b((x-1)_+),\qquad
 \Lambda(x)=\begin{cases}M_bx,&x>0,\\0,&x\le0.\end{cases}
\]

If \(S_*=R+1\), its law is invariant under \(F\) and supported on \([1,\infty)\). The maps \(F\) are continuous, and checking \(x\le0\), \(0<x<1\), and \(x\ge1\) in turn gives

\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|F(x)-\Lambda(x)|\le B_b:=1+Q_b+M_b,
 \qquad \mathbb E B_b^p<\infty\quad(p>0). \tag{5}
\]

This is the paper's separated case: \({}^{+}A=M_b>0\), \({}^{-}A=0\), and \(p_{++}(p)=h_b(p)\), while both sign-switch entries vanish. Its Theorem 2.3(b) applies because \(h_b(\alpha)=1\), \(\mathbb E M_b^\alpha\log M_b<\infty\), \(\mathbb E B_b^\alpha<\infty\), and \(\log M_b\) is nonarithmetic. It gives \(t^\alpha\Pr(S_*>t)\to C_\nu<\infty\). Its **separate Proposition 8.6(b)** makes \(C_\nu>0\), because \(p'_{++}(0)=\gamma_b<0\) and the invariant support is unbounded. Shifting by one yields the asserted exact tail of \(R\). This theorem is about *any* invariant radial law, with no average nonlinear Lipschitz hypothesis. At \(\eta=\eta_2\) the identical checks also give a positive exact \(t^{-2}\) tail, so an invariant law then has infinite second moment.

Primary theorem text: [Alsmeyer, Brofferio, Buraczewski, *Asymptotically linear iterated function systems on the real line*, Annals of Applied Probability 33 (2023), Theorem 2.3(b), Proposition 8.6(b), Proposition 9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.02299). Existence and positivity are logically separate from the theorem giving a possibly zero limiting constant.
