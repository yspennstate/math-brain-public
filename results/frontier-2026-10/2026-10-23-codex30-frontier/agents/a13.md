# T228: a genuinely nonlinear activation with an exactly contracting radial recursion

Let \(d\ge2\), \(X=(Z,X_2,\ldots,X_d)\sim N(0,I_d)\), and \(Y\sim N(0,\tau^2)\) be independent. Fix \(\eta>0\), \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), and
\[
\phi_\varepsilon(z)=z+\varepsilon\tanh z,\qquad
V_\varepsilon(r)=r e_1-\eta\bigl(\phi_\varepsilon(rZ)-Y\bigr)\phi_\varepsilon'(rZ)X,
\qquad \Psi_\varepsilon(r)=\|V_\varepsilon(r)\|,\quad r\ge0.
\]
Set
\[
M=\|(I-\eta XX^\top)e_1\|=\|e_1-\eta ZX\|,
\qquad K=\eta |Z|\|X\|(7+2|Y|),
\qquad L_\varepsilon=M+\varepsilon K.
\]
Then \(L_\varepsilon\) is a *samplewise Lipschitz bound* for the scalar map \(\Psi_\varepsilon\). It is positive almost surely, has \(\mathbb E|\log L_\varepsilon|<\infty\), and \(\mathbb E\log L_\varepsilon<0\) for every sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\) whenever \(\mathbb E\log M<0\).

## Uniform asymptotic error and Lipschitz bound

Write \(s(z)=\operatorname{sech}^2z\) and \(t(z)=\tanh z\). The elementary estimate \(|z|s(z)\le1\) holds for every real \(z\) (indeed, \(\cosh^2z\ge1+z^2\)). Expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\phi_\varepsilon(z)-Y)\phi_\varepsilon'(z)
&=z-Y+\varepsilon\{t(z)+(z-Y)s(z)\}
  +\varepsilon^2t(z)s(z),\\
\left|(\phi_\varepsilon(z)-Y)\phi_\varepsilon'(z)-(z-Y)\right|
&\le\varepsilon(2+|Y|+\varepsilon).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, for *every* \(r\ge0\), not only asymptotically,
\[
\boxed{\quad|\Psi_\varepsilon(r)-Mr|\le Q_\varepsilon
:=\eta\|X\|\bigl[(1+\varepsilon)|Y|+2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\bigr].\quad}
\]
In particular \(\Psi_\varepsilon(r)/r\to M\) almost surely as \(r\to\infty\). The variables \(K,Q_\varepsilon,M\), and \(\Psi_\varepsilon(0)=\eta(1+\varepsilon)|Y|\|X\|\) have finite moments of every positive order.

For the Lipschitz claim define
\[
c_\varepsilon(z,Y)=\phi_\varepsilon'(z)^2+
                         (\phi_\varepsilon(z)-Y)\phi_\varepsilon''(z).
\]
Since \(\phi_\varepsilon'=1+\varepsilon s\) and \(\phi_\varepsilon''=-2\varepsilon st\),
\[
|c_\varepsilon(z,Y)-1|
\le 2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2+
2\varepsilon(|z|s(z)+\varepsilon+|Y|)
\le\varepsilon(4+3\varepsilon+2|Y|)
\le\varepsilon(7+2|Y|).
\]
Differentiate the *vector* map, which stays differentiable even at a zero of its norm:
\[
V_\varepsilon'(r)=e_1-\eta c_\varepsilon(rZ,Y)ZX,
\qquad \|V_\varepsilon'(r)\|\le M+\varepsilon K=L_\varepsilon.
\]
The mean-value bound on \(V_\varepsilon\) and the reverse triangle inequality now prove
\[
|\Psi_\varepsilon(r)-\Psi_\varepsilon(u)|\le L_\varepsilon|r-u|
\qquad (r,u\ge0).
\]

## Integrability and a quantitative contraction criterion

The perpendicular coordinates supply a useful lower bound:
\[
M\ge\eta |Z|\|(X_2,\ldots,X_d)\|>0\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Both factors on the right have integrable negative logarithms, including for \(d=2\); also \(M\le1+\eta|Z|\|X\|\). Hence \(\mathbb E|\log M|<\infty\). Since \(M\le L_\varepsilon\le M+K\), the same holds for \(\log L_\varepsilon\). As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), \(\log L_\varepsilon\downarrow\log M\); dominated convergence applies to \(\log L_\varepsilon-\log M\), bounded above by the integrable \(\log(M+K)-\log M\). Thus \(\mathbb E\log L_\varepsilon\to\mathbb E\log M<0\). This is the justified version of the monotone-convergence argument; \(\log L_\varepsilon\) itself need not be nonnegative.

There is also an explicit sufficient smallness condition. Because \(M\) has every positive moment and an integrable negative logarithm,
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\mathbb EM^s\right|_{s=0+}=\mathbb E\log M<0.
\]
Choose \(s\in(0,1]\) with \(m_s:=\mathbb EM^s<1\), and let \(k_s:=\mathbb EK^s\in(0,\infty)\). By subadditivity of \(x^s\),
\[
\rho_s:=\mathbb E L_\varepsilon^s
\le m_s+\varepsilon^s k_s<1
\quad\text{if}\quad
0<\varepsilon\le1,\qquad
\varepsilon^s k_s<1-m_s.
\tag{1}
\]
For a non-strict, explicit upper endpoint one can take \(0<\varepsilon\le\min\{1,[(1-m_s)/(2k_s)]^{1/s}\}\); then \(\rho_s\le(1+m_s)/2<1\). Jensen also gives \(\mathbb E\log L_\varepsilon\le s^{-1}\log\rho_s<0\).

One concrete parameter range needs no implicit \(s\). Gaussian fourth moments give
\[
\mathbb E M^2=1-2\eta+(d+2)\eta^2.
\]
If \(0<\eta<2/(d+2)\), then \(\mathbb EM\le\sqrt{1-2\eta+(d+2)\eta^2}<1\), and
\[
\mathbb E K\le\eta\sqrt d\left(7+2\tau\sqrt{2/\pi}\right).
\]
Thus the completely explicit condition
\[
0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{1,
\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2\eta+(d+2)\eta^2}}
{\eta\sqrt d\left(7+2\tau\sqrt{2/\pi}\right)}\right\}
\tag{2}
\]
implies \(\mathbb E L_\varepsilon<1\). It yields contraction in ordinary \(W_1\) as well as negative mean logarithmic Lipschitz constant. Formula (1) covers any \(\eta\) for which \(\mathbb E\log M<0\), even when \(\mathbb EM\ge1\).

## Exact radial dynamics and stationary law

The squared-loss SGD update for a vector \(w\in\mathbb R^d\) is
\[
F_{X,Y}(w)=w-\eta\bigl(\phi_\varepsilon(w\cdot X)-Y\bigr)
                 \phi_\varepsilon'(w\cdot X)X.
\]
Rotational invariance of \(X\) shows that, conditional on any past and on \(\|w_n\|=r\), the law of \(\|F_{X_n,Y_n}(w_n)\|\) is exactly that of \(\Psi_{\varepsilon,n}(r)\), with fresh independent \(X_n,Y_n\). Thus the norm process has precisely the kernel of the iid scalar iterated-function recursion \(R_{n+1}=\Psi_{\varepsilon,n+1}(R_n)\); no approximation is involved.

Under (1), endow \([0,\infty)\) with the complete metric \(d_s(r,u)=|r-u|^s\). Synchronous coupling gives
\[
\mathbb E d_s(\Psi_\varepsilon(r),\Psi_\varepsilon(u))
\le\rho_s d_s(r,u).
\]
The associated law operator preserves measures of finite \(s\)-th moment because \(\mathbb E\Psi_\varepsilon(0)^s<\infty\), and is a strict contraction in \(W_1\) for the metric \(d_s\). It therefore has a unique invariant radial law \(\pi_\varepsilon\) with finite \(s\)-th moment, and laws started from such initial data converge geometrically in that metric. The invariant law is in fact unique *among all* probability laws on \([0,\infty)\): if another invariant \(R_0\) is coupled to an independent \(S_0\sim\pi_\varepsilon\) and both receive the same iid maps, then
\[
|R_n-S_n|\le |R_0-S_0|\prod_{j=1}^n L_{\varepsilon,j}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely},
\]
by the strong law for the integrable \(\log L_\varepsilon\). Their stationary marginals must therefore agree, using bounded Lipschitz test functions. An isotropic vector law obtained by sampling radius from \(\pi_\varepsilon\) and an independent uniform direction is also invariant under vector SGD.

For every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(\phi_\varepsilon''(z)=-2\varepsilon\operatorname{sech}^2z\tanh z\) is nonzero for \(z\ne0\), so the activation is genuinely nonlinear. Nevertheless, each full-vector sample map has global Lipschitz constant at least one: for any nonzero \(h\perp X\), \(F_{X,Y}(w+h)-F_{X,Y}(w)=h\). The scalar radial contraction is therefore a consequence of isotropy and the exact norm kernel, not a full-vector samplewise contraction. Finite moments of \(Q_\varepsilon\) do not by themselves assert that the invariant law has moments beyond the \(s\) certified above.
