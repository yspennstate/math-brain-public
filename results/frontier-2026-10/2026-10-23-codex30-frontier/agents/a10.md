# A10: a genuinely smooth, non-affine Gaussian-label SGD model with exact positive Pareto tail

## Result and scope

**This resolves the concrete smooth-activation subcase in the literal T228 row, for sufficiently small positive nonlinearity.** Fix an integer `d>=2` and `tau>0`. There exist `delta>0` and `epsilon_0>0` (depending on `d,tau`) such that for every

`eta in (eta_2,eta_2+delta)`, `epsilon in (0,epsilon_0)`, `eta_2=2/(d+2)`,

one-sample constant-step SGD for

`phi_epsilon(z)=z+epsilon tanh(z)`, `ell(w;X,Y)=1/2(phi_epsilon(w·X)-Y)^2`,

with iid `X~N(0,I_d)` and independent `Y~N(0,tau^2)`, has a unique invariant vector law `Pi`. It is spherical, and for some **strictly positive, finite** `c_eta,epsilon`,

`P_Pi(||W||>t) ~ c_eta,epsilon t^(-alpha_eta)`, `0<alpha_eta<2`,

where the exponent, **independent of epsilon and tau**, is the unique positive solution of

`E[(1-2eta Z²+eta² Z²(Z²+H²))^(alpha_eta/2)]=1`,

`Z~N(0,1)`, `H²~chi-square(d-1)`, independent. In fact `Pi` has uniform angular direction independently of its radius, so for a sphere continuity set `D`, `t^alpha P_Pi(||W||>t, W/||W|| in D) -> c_eta,epsilon Unif(D)`. All positive moments below `alpha_eta` are finite and all moments at or above it infinite. The sample update and the population objective really are non-affine/nonquadratic; no leaky-ReLU symmetry reduction is used. This says nothing about arbitrary epsilon, non-Gaussian covariates, nonlinear teacher labels, or minibatch `b>1`.

**Primary theorem actually checked:** M. Mirek, *Heavy tail phenomenon and convergence to stable laws for iterated Lipschitz maps*, arXiv:0907.2261v2, [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2261), Assumptions 1.6 (H1–H2), 1.7 (H3–H7), Theorem 1.8 and its *separate* positivity criterion (1.12), PDF pp. 2–3. Note that the arXiv HTML renderer numbers the analogous theorem 1.13. Gürbüzbalaban–Şimşekli–Zhu, [PMLR PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/gurbuzbalaban21a/gurbuzbalaban21a.pdf), Theorem 1, is a **compressed quotation** of Mirek that says `c_0>0` without stating Mirek's unbounded-support/moment-ratio positivity hypothesis; the present proof verifies that hypothesis explicitly. See web refs `turn72view0`/`turn72view1` for the complete original conditions and `turn56view0` for the compressed statement.

## Exact radial iterated random functions

At a state of norm `r`, rotate it to `r e_1`; the Gaussian input becomes `(Z,X_perp)`, with independent `Z~N(0,1)`, `H=||X_perp||`, and label `Y`. Set

`g_e(z,y)=(phi_e(z)-y)phi_e'(z)`, `rho=sqrt(Z²+H²)`,

`F_e(r;Z,H,Y)=sqrt((r-eta g_e(rZ,Y) Z)^2 + eta² g_e(rZ,Y)^2 H²)`, `r>=0`.

Rotational equivariance makes `R_{n+1}=F_e(R_n;theta_{n+1})`, with iid `theta=(Z,H,Y)`, an **exact scalar Markov recursion**, even though the original vector gradient is nonlinear. Extend it to all real inputs as `psi_theta(x)=F_e(|x|;theta)`; after a single step it lives on `[0,infinity)`. At `epsilon=0` its homogeneous multiplier is

`M_eta=sqrt((1-eta Z²)^2+eta² Z² H²)>0` almost surely.

For `epsilon>0`, put `s(z)=sech²(z)`, `t(z)=tanh(z)`. Direct expansion gives

`g_e(z,y)-z=e[t(z)+z s(z)]+e² t(z)s(z)-y[1+e s(z)]`.

Since `sup_z |z|sech²(z)<infinity`, for `0<e<=1` one may fix an absolute constant `C_0` and take

`Q_e=eta rho[(1+e)|Y|+C_0 e]` (enlarge `C_0` if necessary),

so the reverse triangle inequality proves **for every** `r>=0`, `|F_e(r)-M_eta r|<=Q_e`. Consequently `Q_e` has all positive moments; `psi_theta,t(x)=t psi_theta(x/t)` converges for every real `x` to `M_eta |x|`, equal to `M_eta x` on the invariant support `[0,infinity)`. These are precisely Mirek H1–H2; the vector map's asymptotic matrix is not falsely claimed to be a scalar similarity.

## Average contraction and stationary existence

For `r>=0`, let `v_e(r)=(r-eta g_e(rZ,Y)Z,-eta g_e(rZ,Y)H)`. At points where its norm is differentiable, the norm's Lipschitz constant is bounded by that of `v_e`. The derivative is

`v_e'(r)=(1,0)-eta Z (Z,H) partial_z g_e(rZ,Y)`.

Since `partial_z g_e=(phi_e')²+(phi_e-Y)phi_e''`, `phi_e''=-2e tanh(z)sech²(z)`, and `sup_z |z phi_e''(z)|<=C e`, there is an absolute `C_1` with

`sup_z |partial_z g_e(z,Y)-1| <= C_1 e(1+|Y|)` for `0<e<=1`.

Hence the **actual global scalar map** obeys

`Lip(psi_theta) <= L_e:=M_eta+C_1 eta e |Z|rho(1+|Y|)`.

All log-plus moments are finite. Also `E|log M_eta|<infinity`: its negative part is dominated by the integrable negative part of `log|1-eta Z²|`, and its positive part by `log(1+eta rho²)`. At `eta_2=2/(d+2)`, Gaussian moments give

`E M_eta²=1-2eta+(d+2)eta²=1`,

so strict Jensen yields `gamma_eta2:=E log M_eta2 < (1/2)log E M_eta2²=0`. The logarithmic expectation is continuous in `eta` locally: on a compact positive `eta` interval the Gaussian density gives `sup_eta P(|1-eta Z²|<u)<=C u` for sufficiently small `u`, which uniformly controls the logarithmic negative tails. Thus `gamma_eta<0` on an interval above `eta_2`. For each such `eta`, dominated convergence as `e downarrow 0` gives `E log L_e -> gamma_eta<0`: bound the positive part by `log(1+M_eta+C_1 eta |Z|rho(1+|Y|))`, and the negative part by `(log M_eta)^-`. The preceding uniform logarithmic bound and a polynomial Gaussian envelope for the positive part also give joint continuity at `(eta_2,0)`, hence a **uniform open rectangle** `eta in (eta_2,eta_2+delta), 0<e<epsilon_0` with `E log L_e<0`. Finally `psi_theta(0)=eta(1+e)|Y|rho` has integrable `log+`. Mirek's averaged-Lipschitz contraction conditions therefore give a unique radial stationary solution `S>=0`, with convergence from every initial radius. No `E log ||I-eta XX^T||<0` assertion is made (indeed this operator norm is at least one when `d>=2`).

For the full vector chain, take `W=SU`, where `U` is an independent uniform direction. Orthogonal equivariance implies its one-step image is again isotropic and has the stationary radius `S`; hence this constructs an invariant vector law. For each fixed `w`, the transition has an everywhere-positive Lebesgue density away from `w`: conditional on any nonzero Gaussian `X=s v`, the scalar step along `v` is an affine function of the full-support Gaussian `Y` with nonzero slope `eta phi_e'(w·X)s`, while `v` itself has full spherical support. Thus the vector chain is Lebesgue-irreducible (indeed its one-step laws are equivalent to Lebesgue measure). Standard irreducibility uniqueness of an invariant probability gives the asserted **unique** `Pi`; rotational equivariance then forces spherical symmetry. This also verifies full/unbounded support of the radius.

## Tail hypotheses, including strict positivity

Let `h_eta(p)=E M_eta^p`. Its log is strictly convex, `h_eta(0)=1`, `h_eta'(0)=gamma_eta<0`, and `P(M_eta>1)>0` (take `|Z|` large). Its Gaussian moments exist at every order; it has a unique positive root `alpha_eta`, and `E M_eta^alpha |log M_eta|<infinity` with `m_alpha:=E M_eta^alpha log M_eta>0`. Moreover `h_eta(2)>1` for `eta>eta_2`, so `alpha_eta<2` in the stated rectangle. The law of `M_eta` is nonatomic with support `(0,infinity)` relative to the multiplicative group: approach zero with `H↓0, Z²→1/eta`, and obtain arbitrarily large values with `|Z|→infinity`; continuity of this Gaussian image fills the intermediate values. Thus `log M_eta` is nonarithmetic and its support generates the whole group `R_+^*`. This checks H3–H6. The all-order moments of `Q_e` check H7.

For **nonzero tail constant**, the radial invariant support is unbounded: from any `r` and almost every `(Z,H)` with `rho>0`, letting `|Y|→infinity` sends `F_e(r)` to infinity, and the Gaussian label has positive density at every value. Mirek's additional condition (1.12), needed because his theorem otherwise permits a *zero* tail constant, is

`lim_{p→infinity} (E Q_e^p / E M_eta^p)^(1/p) < infinity`.

The limit actually exists. Gaussian chi moments and Stirling give `(E rho^p)^(1/p)/sqrt(p) -> 1/sqrt(e)`, `(E|Y|^p)^(1/p)/sqrt(p) -> tau/sqrt(e)`, and `(E(Z²)^p)^(1/p)/p -> 2/e`. Independence, the triangle inequality, and the lower/upper bounds

`eta (E(Z²)^p)^(1/p)-1 <= (E M_eta^p)^(1/p) <= 1+eta (E rho^(2p))^(1/p)`

therefore yield `(E M_eta^p)^(1/p)/p -> 2eta/e`. Similarly, since `Q_e=eta rho[(1+e)|Y|+C_0 e]`, the triangle inequality and positivity give `(E Q_e^p)^(1/p)/p -> eta(1+e)tau/e`. Hence the exact ratio limit in Mirek (1.12) is `(1+e)tau/2<infinity`. Mirek Theorem 1.8 now yields

`lim_{t→infinity} t^alpha P(S>t) = [E(F_e(S;theta)^alpha - (M_eta S)^alpha)]/(alpha m_alpha) =:c_eta,e in (0,infinity)`, 

with `S` independent of fresh `theta`; the displayed difference means the **joint expectation**, not separate expectations (which may each be infinite). It is integrable: `|F_e(S)-M_eta S|<=Q_e`; for `alpha<=1` use `|a^alpha-b^alpha|<=|a-b|^alpha`, and for `1<alpha<2` use `|a^alpha-b^alpha|<=C_alpha(Q_e^alpha+Q_e S^(alpha-1) M_eta^(alpha-1))`, with `E S^(alpha-1)<infinity` from the subcritical moment bound. This validates the strict-positivity point missing from the compressed SGD citation. Since `Pi` is spherical, the corresponding vector/angular assertion follows directly.

## Genuine nonlinearity and limitations

For `e>0`, `g_e(z,Y)` is not affine in `z` for generic `Y`, so the fixed-sample gradient map is genuinely nonlinear and smooth. The population risk is also nonquadratic: for `r=||w||`,

`E ell(w;X,Y)=r²/2+e E[rZ tanh(rZ)]+(e²/2)E[tanh²(rZ)]+tau²/2`.

The middle term grows as `e r E|Z|` for large `r`; it cannot be absorbed into a quadratic. The tail multiplier is nevertheless the **linear leading radial multiplier**, not the sample map's global Lipschitz constant. Perturbative smallness of `e` is used only for averaged radial contraction; all other asymptotic, nonarithmetic, moment, and positivity checks work for any fixed finite `e>0`. The proof does not provide a published novelty check beyond these primary sources or explicit numerical `delta,epsilon_0` values.
