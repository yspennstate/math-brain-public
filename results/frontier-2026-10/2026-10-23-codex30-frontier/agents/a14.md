# A14 — exact contraction margins and quadrature for T228 candidates

The files [`research/code/a14_checks.py`](../research/code/a14_checks.py) and [`research/code/a14_results.json`](../research/code/a14_results.json) give a reproducible `d=2`, one-sample calculation. Run `python research/code/a14_checks.py` from the workspace root. SciPy's adaptive quadrature supplies descriptive values; the strict inequalities below use exact rational Gaussian moments and Cauchy–Schwarz, so do not depend on the quadrature error estimates.

## Explicit models and certified interval

Let `X~N(0,I_2)` and `Y~N(0,1)` be independent and freshly sampled each step. For the half-square loss, take the smooth activation `φ(z)=z+ε tanh z` with **`η=13/25`, `ε=1/1000`**. Its update is `F(w)=w−η(φ(w·X)−Y)φ'(w·X)X`. The linear asymptote is `A=I−ηXXᵀ`, `B=ηYX`. For `e` a fixed unit vector, `Z=e·X` and `V=||X−Ze||²` are independent chi-square coordinates (`Z²,V~χ²_1`) and

`M²=||Ae||²=(1−ηZ²)²+η²Z²V`, `h(p)=E M^p`.

Exact arithmetic gives `h(2)=651/625=1.0416`, while the moment certificate below gives `h(1)≤7941802637/8025437500≈0.9895787784<1`. Thus strict log convexity yields a unique root `α∈(1,2)` and a negative top radial Lyapunov exponent. Numerical quadrature gives `h(1)=0.9134095660` and `α=1.8403225889`. The latter is a numerical index for the linear asymptote; proving the smooth model has a nonzero exact Pareto constant still requires the nonlinear renewal theorem's remaining hypotheses and positivity argument. The calculation alone makes no such theorem claim.

The **mean two-point contraction** of the genuinely nonlinear smooth update is also certified. Write `s=sech² z`, `g(z)=(φ(z)−Y)φ'(z)`. Since `|z|s≤1`, `|tanh z|≤1`, and `ε≤1`,

`|g'(z)−1|=|(1+εs)²−1−2ε(z+ε tanh z−Y)tanh(z)s|≤ε(7+2|Y|)`.

For deterministic distinct `w,v`, set `e=(w−v)/||w−v||`. The fundamental theorem of calculus along their line segment gives, samplewise,

`||F(w)−F(v)||/||w−v|| ≤ ||Ae||+ε K_e`, `K_e=η|X·e| ||X|| (7+2|Y|)`.

By Cauchy–Schwarz, `E|X·e| ||X||≤√(d+2)=2`, while `E|Y|≤1`. Hence `EK_e≤η·2·9=234/25`, and **for every pair**

`E||F(w)−F(v)|| ≤ κ||w−v||`, where `κ≤2004230183/2006359375≈0.9989387784<1`.

The certified margin is `2129192/2006359375≈0.00106122`. This is a bound on expected distance for each fixed pair, **not** a claim that `E sup_w ||DF(w)||<1`: in dimension two, the latter would fail even for the rank-one linear map. Since `E||F(0)||<∞`, the bound gives a unique stationary law by Wasserstein-1 contraction. Moreover `F(w)=Aw+B+R(w)` with the samplewise uniform estimate `sup_w||R(w)||≤ηε||X||(3+|Y|)`, so `R(w)=o(||w||)` a.s. as `||w||→∞`.

For the leaky activation `φ_a(z)=max(z,az)`, choose **`a=1/2`, `η=3/5`** with the same input and noise. The sign-symmetry reduction makes its entire nonzero-state transition kernel that of the affine update `(I−ηC²XXᵀ)w+ηCYX`, with an independent fair gate `C∈{1,a}`. Replace `η` by `ηC²` in `M`. Exact arithmetic gives `h(2)=203/200=1.015` and the same certificate gives `h(1)≤8377768883/8523860000≈0.9828609202<1`; quadrature gives `h(1)=0.9179564029`, `α=1.9360311075`. The threshold is `η₂=10/17≈0.5882353`, so `3/5` is strictly on the `α<2` side. Coupling the affine surrogate with shared `(A,B)` proves its `W₁` contraction, and the original kernel agrees at every nonzero state. The exact-Fraction tests pair `(X,Y)` with `(-X,-Y)`: their unordered full-vector outputs equal those from the two independent-gate values. These checks illustrate the algebra; the symmetry involution supplies the proof for Gaussian data. An arbitrary definition of the derivative at the kink `w=0` need not give this kernel there, but stationary noise-driven laws charge zero mass to that point.

## Rational moment certificate

For `S=M²`, set `m₂=ES`, `m₄=ES²`, `m₆=ES³`, `Q=m₄−2m₂+1=E(S−1)²`, and `T=m₆−m₄−m₂+1=E[(S−1)²(S+1)]`. Both are positive. The pointwise identity and denominator bound

`√S=(1+S)/2−(S−1)²/[2(√S+1)²] ≤(1+S)/2−(S−1)²/[4(S+1)]`

together with Cauchy–Schwarz imply

`h(1)≤(1+m₂)/2−Q²/(4T)`.

All moments here are rational because `R²=||X||²~χ²_d`, `U=Z²/R²~Beta(1/2,(d−1)/2)` independently, and `S=1−(2βR²−β²R⁴)U`, with `β=η` or `ηC²`. In particular, writing `μ_j=E C^{2j}`,

`m₂=1−2ημ₁+(d+2)η²μ₂`,

`m₄=1−4ημ₁+2(d+8)η²μ₂−12(d+4)η³μ₃+3(d+4)(d+6)η⁴μ₄`.

The script computes `m₆` by finite binomial sums of exact chi-square and beta moments. For the smooth asymptote `(m₂,m₄,m₆)=(651/625,1848809/390625,1088264059/9765625)`; for the leaky mixture they are `(203/200,95903/20000,11892023/80000)`.

## Quadrature method and limits

For `d=2`, set `t=β||X||²`, so `t` is exponential with scale `2β`. Conditional on `t`, `U` is arcsine distributed and

`E[M^p|t] = ₂F₁(−p/2,1/2;1;2t−t²)`.

The script integrates this one-dimensional expression by SciPy QUADPACK, splitting at `t=1` (the integrable `M=0` cusp), and truncating at `t=100`. For `1≤p≤2`, the omitted tail is bounded by `e^(−100/(2β))[(101)²+4β(101)+8β²]`, below `10⁻³²` for both selected models. It independently checks `h(1)` through the complete elliptic integral `(2/π)E(2t−t²)`, checks quadrature at `p=2,4` against exact rational moments, and solves `h(α)=1` by Brent's method. Reported QUADPACK error estimates are **not rigorous interval bounds**; the ten displayed decimal digits are numerical, whereas the strict bracket `1<α<2`, contraction, and moment identities are analytic.
