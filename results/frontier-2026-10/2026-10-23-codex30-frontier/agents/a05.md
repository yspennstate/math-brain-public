# T364 quartic sphere: source check and partial results

## Source and status

The October 22 email says T364 concerns spurious local maxima of a quartic on the sphere. It reports one \(d=250\) instance with coordinate-concentrated numerical values \(1.13\)–\(1.26\) and spread-out values near \(1.05\). The email gives neither the formula nor a citation. The root agent separately located the catalog row at Brain 06_math_research/top200_probability_2026_10_14/list.jsonl and says it uses independent sphere-uniform vectors, asking for \(n\le c d^2\) to exhibit spurious local maxima or show all local maxima are close to components. I did not independently fetch the row and do not quote its exact wording.

The closest verified primary source is Rong Ge and Tengyu Ma, *On the Optimization Landscape of Tensor Decompositions*, NeurIPS 2017: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/a48564053b3c7b54800246348c7fa4a0-Paper.pdf . It studies \(f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n\langle a_j,x\rangle^4\) on \(S^{d-1}\), **with Gaussian** \(a_j\sim N(0,I_d)\). The introduction and conclusion explicitly ask whether other spurious local maxima exist. Theorem 1.1 rules them out only in a high superlevel set for \(d^{1+\epsilon}<n<d^{2-\epsilon}\); Theorem 3.1 gives a polylogarithmic refinement. It does not resolve the full sphere or the \(n\asymp d^2\) edge. It is a plausible source match, not a verified citation for T364, and its Gaussian sampling is distinct from the catalog's sphere-uniform sampling. Relevant primary-source refs: turn51view1 and turn51view2.

## Exact sphere-uniform height result

Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) be independent uniform vectors on \(S^{d-1}\), \(F_d(x)=\sum_j\langle a_j,x\rangle^4\), and \(n/d^2\to\rho\in(0,\infty)\). Put \(\mu_d=3/[d(d+2)]\). Then simultaneously over all \(n\) components,
\[
\max_i\left|F_d(a_i)-\{1+(n-1)\mu_d\}\right|\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]
For an independent uniform \(U\), \(F_d(U)-n\mu_d\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0\). Consequently
\[
\min_i F_d(a_i)-F_d(U)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}1,\qquad
\max_i\left|\frac{F_d(a_i)}{n\mu_d}-\left(1+\frac1{3\rho}\right)\right|\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0,\qquad
\frac{F_d(U)}{n\mu_d}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}1.
\]

**Proof.** Conditional on \(a_i\), the \(Z_{ij}=\langle a_j,a_i\rangle\), \(j\ne i\), are independent copies of the first coordinate \(Z\) of a uniform spherical vector. Its moments are
\[
\mathbb E Z^{2k}=\frac{(2k-1)!!}{d(d+2)\cdots(d+2k-2)}.
\]
Thus \(\mathbb E Z^4=\mu_d\), \(\mathbb E(Z^4-\mu_d)^2=O(d^{-4})\), and \(\mathbb E(Z^4-\mu_d)^4=O(d^{-8})\). Independence and the fourth-moment expansion give, for \(S_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(Z_{ij}^4-\mu_d)\),
\(\mathbb E S_i^4\le C(n+n^2)d^{-8}\).
Markov and a union bound (no independence across different \(i\) required) yield, for fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
\mathbb P(\max_i|S_i|>\delta)
\le C_\delta n(n+n^2)d^{-8}=O(d^{-2})\to0.
\]
Since \(\langle a_i,a_i\rangle^4=1\), this proves the first assertion. Conditional on independent \(U\), the summands of \(F_d(U)\) have the same coordinate law; the same estimate proves the second. Finally \(n\mu_d\to3\rho\). \(\square\)

This proves a gap between **specified directions**, not critical points. A random \(U\) is almost surely not a local maximum; the numerical spread-out optimizer requires a separate certificate. If the experiment normalized by the random-direction mean and used this exact model, ratios \(1.13\)–\(1.26\) would correspond to \(\rho\) about \(2.56\)–\(1.28\). The email lacks \(n\), normalization, and its actual formula, so that comparison is conditional.

## Restricted deterministic spurious-maximum certificate

Let
\[
H(x)=\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_i x_i^4+E[x,x,x,x],\quad x\in S^{d-1},\quad
m\le\lambda_i\le2m,
\]
where \(E\) is symmetric 4-linear and
\(\|E\|_\times:=\sup_{\|v_k\|=1}|E[v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4]|\le\varepsilon\le m/32\).
Each cap \(C_i=\{x:x_i\ge\sqrt{15}/4\}\) contains exactly one critical point \(x_i^*\), a strict local maximum, and
\[
H(e_i)\le H(x_i^*)\le H(e_i)+\frac{128\varepsilon^2}{13m}.
\]
Thus \(x_i^*\) is spurious whenever some \(j\) has
\(\lambda_j+E[e_j^4]>\lambda_i+E[e_i^4]+128\varepsilon^2/(13m)\).
In particular, \(\lambda_j-\lambda_i>2\varepsilon+128\varepsilon^2/(13m)\) suffices. Antipodal caps have symmetric maxima.

**Proof.** Write \(x=\cos\theta\,e_i+\sin\theta\,w\) in the cap, \(w\perp e_i\), \(\sin\theta\le1/4\). The spherical Hessian of a homogeneous quartic \(Q\) is \(D^2Q(x)[v,v]-4Q(x)\|v\|^2\) for tangent \(v\). Here \(v_i^2\le\sin^2\theta\|v\|^2\) and \(\sum_{k\ne i}\lambda_kx_k^2v_k^2\le2m\sin^2\theta\|v\|^2\), so
\[
\operatorname{Hess}_S H_0[v,v]\le
[-4\lambda_i\cos^2\theta(1-4\sin^2\theta)+24m\sin^2\theta]\|v\|^2
\le-\frac{21m}{16}\|v\|^2.
\]
The perturbation Hessian norm is at most \(16\varepsilon\le m/2\), hence \(H\) is \(\mu=13m/16\)-strongly geodesically concave on \(C_i\). At the boundary its outward derivative is at most
\(-15\sqrt{15}m/64+2\sqrt{15}m/64+4\varepsilon<0\).
Compactness and cap convexity yield a unique interior strict maximum. The initial derivative on the geodesic from \(e_i\) is at most \(4\varepsilon\), so strong concavity bounds its value gain by \((4\varepsilon)^2/(2\mu)=128\varepsilon^2/(13m)\). Comparing \(H(e_j)\) gives the criterion. \(\square\)

This last theorem requires a globally small multilinear perturbation of an **orthogonal diagonal** tensor. It does **not** automatically apply to the sphere-uniform ensemble with \(n\asymp d^2\), which has many nonorthogonal rank-one terms. It is a certificate template, not a claim to solve T364; no novelty claim relative to perturbation literature. The hard remaining task is to certify the existence or exclusion of spread-out local maxima, not merely compare their candidate heights.
