# A23 — uniform RBF finite-feature approximation and forward predictive KL

This is a self-contained mathematical bridge from the infinite-rank Gaussian/RBF kernel to a finite feature kernel, conditional on **exact preprocessing of the features** and exact real arithmetic for the stated polynomial solver. It is not, by itself, a realization from raw inputs by a standard normalized ReLU-attention transformer. The relevant primary paper [Kang–Lee–Cheng, *Transformers Can Learn Posterior Predictive Distributions In-Context* (ICML 2026)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/kang26h.html) analyzes an unnormalized kernel-attention GP predictive construction. The finite-feature and KL estimates below are derived here, not claimed as that paper's theorem.

## Kernel theorem and error

Fix `d≥1`, length scale `ell>0`, input radius `R≥0`, noise variance `s>0`, integer `1≤n≤N`, a fixed number `m≥1` of ordered queries, and labels `|y_i|≤B`. Every training/query input belongs to the ball `||x||₂≤R` in `R^d`. Let

`k(x,z)=exp(−||x−z||₂²/(2ell²))`, `a=R²/ell²`, and `p=k_degree+1`.

For each multi-index `α∈N^d` with `|α|≤k_degree`, use the **exact** coordinate

`φ_α(x)=exp(−||x||₂²/(2ell²)) x^α/(ell^{|α|} sqrt(α!))`.

Then `D=binom(d+k_degree,k_degree)` features give the PSD kernel

`k_{k_degree}(x,z)=<φ_{≤k_degree}(x),φ_{≤k_degree}(z)> = exp(−(||x||²+||z||²)/(2ell²)) Σ_{j=0}^{k_degree}(x·z/ell²)^j/j!`.

The omitted infinite coordinates are also a feature map. Their diagonal squared norm at `x`, writing `u=||x||²/ell²≤a`, is exactly

`r_p(x)=exp(−u) Σ_{j=p}^∞ u^j/j! = P{Poisson(u)≥p} ≤ u^p/p! ≤ a^p/p!`.

The inequality follows from `(p+h)!≥p!h!`. Cauchy–Schwarz on the omitted coordinates yields the **uniform** bound

`|k(x,z)−k_{k_degree}(x,z)| ≤ δ_p := a^p/p!` for `||x||,||z||≤R`.

The direct Taylor remainder gives the weaker estimate `|k(x,z)−k_{k_degree}(x,z)|≤exp(a)a^p/p!`; the tail-feature argument removes `exp(a)`. The entire difference kernel `k−k_{k_degree}` is PSD, not just entrywise small. If `a=0`, already `k_degree=0` is exact. Both `k` and `k_{k_degree}` have diagonal at most one, hence `|k(x,z)|,|k_{k_degree}(x,z)|≤1`. For **even** `k_degree`, its scalar entries are strictly positive: the even Taylor polynomial of `exp(t)` exceeds `exp(t)>0` for negative `t`, and has positive coefficients for nonnegative `t`. Even degree can be chosen at an extra cost of at most one degree if entrywise nonnegativity is needed; PSD holds for every degree.

## Predictive means, covariance, and forward KL

Define `G=K_XX ∈R^{n×n}`, `H=K_XQ ∈R^{n×m}`, `J=K_QQ ∈R^{m×m}`, `A=G+sI_n`; subscript `k` denotes the same matrices for `k_{k_degree}`, and `A_k=G_k+sI_n`. The *noisy* joint GP predictive distributions are

`P=N(μ,C)`, `μ=HᵀA^{−1}y`, `C=J+sI_m−HᵀA^{−1}H`,

`P_k=N(μ_k,C_k)`, `μ_k=H_kᵀA_k^{−1}y`, `C_k=J_k+sI_m−H_kᵀA_k^{−1}H_k`.

Schur complements of the two full train/query PSD kernel matrices give `C,C_k⪰sI_m`. The elementary norms are

`||G−G_k||op≤nδ_p`, `||H−H_k||op≤sqrt(nm)δ_p`, `||J−J_k||op≤mδ_p`,

`||H||op,||H_k||op≤sqrt(nm)`, `||y||₂≤sqrt(n)B`, and `||A^{−1}||op,||A_k^{−1}||op≤1/s`.

Using `A^{−1}−A_k^{−1}=A^{−1}(A_k−A)A_k^{−1}`, these give

`||μ−μ_k||₂ ≤ M_n δ_p`, `M_n := n sqrt(m) B/s ·(1+n/s)`,

`||C−C_k||op ≤ V_n δ_p`, `V_n := m(1+n/s)²`, and `||C−C_k||F ≤sqrt(m)V_nδ_p`.

**Forward KL (true RBF first, finite features second):**

`D_KL(P || P_k) ≤ [M_n²/(2s) + m V_n²/(4s²)] δ_p².                 (1)`

Indeed, the mean term of Gaussian KL is at most `||μ−μ_k||²/(2s)`. For `F(X)=−log det X`, the Gaussian covariance term is half the Bregman divergence `D_F(C,C_k)`. Every point of the line segment joining `C` and `C_k` has minimum eigenvalue at least `s`, so `D_F(C,C_k)≤||C−C_k||F²/(2s²)` by the Hessian formula `∇²F(X)[E,E]=tr(X^{−1}EX^{−1}E)≤||E||F²/s²`. Thus (1) does not require a small-error assumption or a KL triangle inequality. It holds uniformly after replacing every `n` in the constants by `N`.

## Composition with a positivity-preserving accelerated solver

There is an explicit Chebyshev polynomial approximating the inverse of **the truncated** matrix `A_k` while preserving the covariance floor. Set `b=s+N`, `κ=b/s`, `q=(sqrt(κ)−1)/(sqrt(κ)+1)∈(0,1)`. Because `G_k⪰0` and `tr G_k≤n≤N`, `spec(A_k)⊆[s,b]`. Let `T_L` be the degree-`L` Chebyshev polynomial and define

`r_L(t)=[T_L((b+s−2t)/(b−s))/T_L((b+s)/(b−s))]²`,

`P_L(t)=(1−r_L(t))/t` (a polynomial of degree at most `2L−1`, since `r_L(0)=1`),

`e_L=T_L((b+s)/(b−s))^{−2}=4q^{2L}/(1+q^{2L})²≤4q^{2L}`.

For `t∈[s,b]`, `0≤r_L(t)≤e_L`, hence `0⪯P_L(A_k)⪯A_k^{−1}` and `||A_k^{−1}−P_L(A_k)||op≤e_L/s`. In exact arithmetic, `P_L(A_k)` can be applied to the `m+1` right-hand sides `[y,H_k]` with `O(L)` fixed-coefficient `A_k` matrix-vector stages (degree `2L−1`); this is an algebraic solver statement, not a specified attention-network implementation.

Take `μ̂=H_kᵀP_L(A_k)y` and `Ĉ=J_k+sI_m−H_kᵀP_L(A_k)H_k`. Then `Ĉ⪰C_k⪰sI_m`, and putting `S_n=n sqrt(m) B/s` and `U_n=nm/s` gives

`||μ−μ̂||₂≤M_nδ_p+S_n e_L`,

`||C−Ĉ||F≤sqrt(m)(V_nδ_p+U_n e_L)`,

`D_KL(N(μ,C) || N(μ̂,Ĉ)) ≤ (M_nδ_p+S_n e_L)²/(2s) + m(V_nδ_p+U_n e_L)²/(4s²).    (2)`

This is the composition bound; in particular, it directly addresses the forward orientation of KL. More generally (2) holds for any symmetric polynomial solver `P(A_k)` with `0⪯P(A_k)⪯A_k^{−1}` and relative spectral residual `||I−A_k P(A_k)||op≤e`, using `e` in place of `e_L`. If a proposed accelerated inverse polynomial overshoots `A_k^{−1}`, its approximate predictive covariance need not retain the `sI_m` floor, so the above proof cannot be quoted unchanged.

## Fully explicit accuracy and feature count

Use the constants with `n=N` and set

`C_*=(M_N+S_N)²/(2s) + m(V_N+U_N)²/(4s²)`, `h=min{1,sqrt(ε/C_*)}`.

If `a>0`, choose integer `p≥max{1,2 e a, log₂(1/h)}`, and set `k_degree=p−1`; if desired, increase `k_degree` by at most one to make it even. From `p!≥(p/e)^p`, we obtain `δ_p≤(ea/p)^p≤2^{−p}≤h`. Choose

`L≥ceil[log(4/h)/(2 log(1/q))]`.

Then `e_L≤h` and (2) is at most `C_* h²≤ε` for every allowed prompt. The feature dimension is exactly `D=binom(d+k_degree,k_degree)`; with fixed `d,R/ell,s,N,m,B`, it is `O((log(1/ε))^d)`, while the accelerated matrix-vector depth is `O(log(1/ε)/log(1/q))`. For kernel truncation alone, use `C_0=M_N²/(2s)+mV_N²/(4s²)`, `h=min{1,sqrt(ε/C_0)}`, and the same degree rule; (1) is then at most `ε`.

The exact preprocessing assumption is substantive: `exp(−||x||²/(2ell²))` and all monomials `x^α` are given as exact token coordinates. A finite raw-input ReLU MLP can only approximate these on a compact ball; proving its width/depth, preserving a joint PSD Gram matrix and covariance floor, and incorporating its errors into (2) require an additional construction. Even if an even-degree truncation makes its pairwise kernel values positive, normalized ReLU attention also divides by data-dependent row sums. Neither raw-input feature realization nor cancellation of that normalization follows from this note.
