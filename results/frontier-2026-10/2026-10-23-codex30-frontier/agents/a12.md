# A12: positive exact radial tail, source and hypothesis audit

**Conclusion (for the stated Gaussian radial SGD, provided the invariant radius has unbounded support):** Yes. The clean exact result is Alsmeyer–Brofferio–Buraczewski, *Asymptotically linear iterated function systems on the real line*, Ann. Appl. Probab. **33** (2023), Theorem 2.3(b) **together with** Proposition 8.6(b). It yields
\[
   \Pr_\nu(R>t)\sim C t^{-\alpha},\qquad 0<C<\infty.
\]
The theorem of existence alone permits \(C=0\); its separate positivity proposition is essential. Author-hosted primary full text: <https://arxiv.org/html/2102.02299> (theorem at lines 244–259, positivity at 1185–1190; web refs `turn78view2`, `turn78view3`). Published primary DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1214/22-AAP1812>.

## Direct fit, including the half-line technicality

Let the radial kernel be represented by iid random continuous functions \(r\mapsto\Psi(r)\ge0\), \(r\ge0\), with \(|\Psi(r)-Mr|\le Q\), \(M>0\), \(Q\ge0\). A radius law \(\nu\) is stationary for this scalar IFS by rotational invariance. Set \(S=R+1\), and define a random function on **all** of \(\mathbb R\) by
\[
 F(s)=1+\Psi((s-1)_+),\qquad
 \Lambda(s)=\begin{cases}Ms,&s>0,\\0,&s\le0.\end{cases}
\]
The law of \(S\) is stationary and supported on \([1,\infty)\), as required by Theorem 2.3(b)'s phrase “stationary distribution on \(\mathbb R_>\).” For every real \(s\),
\[
 |F(s)-\Lambda(s)|\le B:=1+Q+M.
\]
Proof: for \(s\ge1\), write \(F(s)-Ms=1-M+[\Psi(s-1)-M(s-1)]\); for \(0<s<1\), use \(\Psi(0)\le Q\) and \(Ms\le M\); for \(s\le0\), \(\Lambda=0\). Thus this is the paper's separated case with \({}^{+}A=M\), \({}^{-}A=0\), \(p_{++}(u)=\mathbb E M^u\), \(p_{-+}=p_{+-}=0\). This shift also avoids proving \(\Pr(R=0)=0\), though in the usual \(\epsilon>0\) model it is true.

Precisely map the hypotheses:

| Source hypothesis | Here |
|---|---|
| a.s. continuous real random maps, uniform bounded-error asymptote (paper (1), (4)) | \(\Psi\) continuous; the extension \(F\) continuous and the displayed \(B\) bound holds |
| \(p_{++}(\alpha)=1\) | \(\mathbb E M^\alpha=1\) |
| paper (19): \(\mathbb E[M^\alpha\log M]<\infty\), \(\mathbb E B^\alpha<\infty\) | Gaussian \(M\le1+\eta\|X\|^2\), so every positive power and positive log moment is finite; \(x^\alpha|\log x|\) is bounded near zero; \(Q\) has all moments |
| nonarithmetic \(\log M\) | given (and Gaussian model has a continuous nonconstant law) |
| Prop. 8.6(b): \(p'_{++}(0)<0\), unbounded right support | \(\mathbb E\log M<0\), and \(\operatorname{supp}(S)=1+\operatorname{supp}(R)\) is unbounded |

Consequently \(t^\alpha\Pr(S>t)\to C\in(0,\infty)\), hence \(t^\alpha\Pr(R>t)=t^\alpha\Pr(S>t+1)\to C\). No global average contraction of \(F\) is needed. Existence of a stationary law also follows from the paper's Proposition 9.1, because \(\mathbb E M^s<1\) for some \(s\in(0,\alpha)\) and \(\mathbb E B^s<\infty\); but the assignment already supplies existence. Source `turn65view2` / `turn78view3`.

For the usual squared-loss update \(w'=w-\eta(\phi(X\cdot w)-Y)\phi'(X\cdot w)X\), one can check the asserted \(Q\) explicitly. Put \(z=X\cdot w\), \(h(z)=\phi(z)\phi'(z)-z=\epsilon\tanh z+\epsilon z\operatorname{sech}^2z+\epsilon^2\tanh z\operatorname{sech}^2z\), which is bounded. Then
\[
 w'=(I-\eta XX^\top)w+\eta YX-\eta X\{h(z)-\epsilon Y\operatorname{sech}^2z\},
\]
so \(Q\le\eta\|X\|[(1+|\epsilon|)|Y|+\|h\|_\infty]\). The reverse triangle inequality gives \(|\Psi(r)-Mr|\le Q\). Dependence of \(M,Q\) through \(X\) is allowed by both papers. In \(d\ge2\), writing \(X=(Z,U)\) in coordinates along the radius,
\(M^2=(1-\eta Z^2)^2+\eta^2Z^2\|U\|^2>0\) a.s.; positive moments are finite. The stationary support is unbounded when \(\phi'(rZ)\ne0\) with positive probability for each \(r\), since conditional on such an \(X\), the coefficient of the independent full-support Gaussian \(Y\) in \(w'\) is nonzero, making \(\Pr(\Psi(r)>T)>0\) for every \(T\); integrate against a stationary law. For \(\epsilon>0\), \(\phi'>0\) everywhere. **Caveat:** at \(\epsilon=-1\), \(\phi'(0)=0\), so \(R\equiv0\) is absorbing and Gaussian \(Y\) does not force *every* stationary law to have unbounded support; unbounded support must then be stipulated for the particular \(\nu\).

## Goldie / Buraczewski–Damek comparison

Goldie's 1991 implicit renewal theorem supplies the tail **limit** if its perturbation integrability holds, but not positivity merely from unbounded support in arbitrary nonlinear recursions. The 2023 paper's Section 7 explicitly derives Theorem 2.3 from Goldie Theorem 2.3/Corollary 2.4, and its Section 8 separately proves positivity (`turn87view0`). For an independent check, from \(\mathbb EM^s<1\), \(s<\alpha\), and \(Q\)'s moments, the positive perpetuity \(\sum_{k\ge1}(\prod_{i<k}M_i)Q_k\) has all \(s<\alpha\) moments. Stationarity and the upper affine bound imply \(\mathbb E R^s<\infty\) for all \(s<\alpha\). Therefore \(\mathbb E|\Psi(R)^\alpha-(MR)^\alpha|<\infty\): for \(\alpha\le1\) bound it by \(Q^\alpha\); for \(\alpha>1\) by \(C_\alpha Q[(MR)^{\alpha-1}+Q^{\alpha-1}]\). This checks the perturbation integral without assuming \(\mathbb ER^\alpha<\infty\).

Buraczewski–Damek, *A simple proof of heavy tail estimates for affine type Lipschitz recursions*, SPA **127** (2017), Lemma 2.6, does prove a \(c t^{-\alpha}\) **lower bound** from \(\Phi(x)\ge Ax+B\) and unbounded support, but **also assumes** \(\mathbb E\log L(\Phi)<0\) and \(\mathbb E\log^+|\Phi(0)|<\infty\). Its affine comparator can be \(A=M,B=-Q\) (extend \(\Psi\) constantly for negative \(x\)); yet \(\mathbb E\log M<0\) does **not** imply \(\mathbb E\log L(\Psi)<0\), especially for a nonlinear SGD map. Do not invoke the lemma without proving this extra condition. Primary text <https://arxiv.org/html/1604.06943v1>, Lemma 2.6 at lines 126–149 (`turn60view1`). Its proof uses the average Lipschitz contraction to construct a backward stationary limit (lines 178–193, `turn65view4`).

## Elementary positivity alternative avoiding a Lipschitz condition

Here is a self-contained argument modulo standard affine/Cramér tail estimates. Let \(\Pi_n=\prod_{j=1}^nM_j\), \(H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\Pi_{k-1}Q_k\uparrow H_\infty\), \(T_t=\inf\{n\ge1:\Pi_n>t\}\), and let \(\nu\) be any invariant law of the nonnegative radial IFS. By monotonicity of the affine lower bounds (not of \(\Psi\) itself),
\[
 \Psi_1\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_n(x)\ge\Pi_nx-H_n\quad(x\ge0).
\]
For a fixed \(t\), \(Z_n=\int \mathbf1_{\{\Psi_1\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_n(x)>t\}}\nu(dx)\) is a bounded martingale relative to the iid maps (invariance supplies the conditional mean). Optional stopping at \(T_t\wedge n\), then Fatou, yields for any \(K>1\)
\[
 \nu((t,\infty))\ge \nu([K,\infty))\{\Pr(T_t<\infty)-\Pr(H_\infty>(K-1)t)\}.
\]
Cramér's nonlattice random-walk maximum estimate gives \(\Pr(T_t<\infty)\ge c_1t^{-\alpha}\) for large \(t\). Kesten–Goldie applied to the positive affine perpetuity \(H_\infty\stackrel d=Q+MH_\infty'\) gives \(\Pr(H_\infty>u)\le c_2u^{-\alpha}\). Both apply with dependent \((M,Q)\) pairs, \(\mathbb E M^\alpha\log M<\infty\), \(\mathbb E Q^\alpha<\infty\), and \(\mathbb E\log M<0\); see Buraczewski–Damek Theorem 1.3 and equations (2.4)–(2.5), `turn60view1` lines 62–70, 126–139. Choose \(K\) with \(c_2/(K-1)^\alpha<c_1/2\); unbounded support ensures \(\nu([K,\infty))>0\). Thus \(\liminf t^\alpha\Pr(R>t)>0\). Combine with Goldie's limit or Theorem 2.3(b). This is essentially the scalar form of Alsmeyer et al.'s Lemma 8.2 (`turn87view1`, lines 948–958), and it does not assume \(\mathbb E\log L(\Psi)<0\).

## Boundary of the claim

The abstract list \(\mathbb EM^\alpha=1\), \(\mathbb E\log M<0\), nonarithmetic \(\log M\), \(Q\) all moments, unbounded invariant support **by itself** omits \(\mathbb E M^\alpha\log^+M<\infty\). This is automatic for the concrete Gaussian \(M\), but not an abstract consequence of \(\mathbb EM^\alpha=1\). Infinite tilted-mean affine examples have a slowly varying correction instead of a positive constant: Kevei, *A note on the Kesten–Grincevičius–Goldie theorem*, Theorem 1, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.07262>, `turn90view0` lines 4–12 and 138–173. Thus phrase the theorem application with the Gaussian moment check, and separately verify unbounded support (the \(\epsilon=-1\) exception). Also, the 2023 HTML/PDF introductory displayed definition of \(C_\delta\) before Theorem 2.3 appears to have a typo, showing \(\mathbf1_{\{\Psi(R)<0\}}\) for \(\delta=+\); avoid copying it. The exact positive-tail result and Proposition 8.6(b) are unambiguous.
