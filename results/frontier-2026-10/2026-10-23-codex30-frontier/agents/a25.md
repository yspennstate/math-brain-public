## What the 21 October construction does and does not cover

`C002` uses the **feature-space** identity `A=sI_p+Σ_i z_i z_iᵀ`, `b=Σ_i z_i y_i`, query `q`, predictive `m=qᵀA⁻¹b`, `v=s+s qᵀA⁻¹q`. Its Chebyshev residual on `[s,L]`, `L=s+max(1,n)R²`, is `r_T(x)=T_T((c−x)/d)/T_T(c/d)` with `c=(L+s)/2`, `d=(L−s)/2`; its inverse polynomial is `P_T=(1−r_T(A))A⁻¹`. At any `T≥1`, `|r_T|≤1/T_T(c/d)<1`, so `P_T≻0` and the scalar `v_T=s+s qᵀP_Tq≥s` **already holds at every such depth**, not just at the final tolerance. The proof deliberately excludes joint multiple queries, infinite-rank truncation, finite-bit weight cost, pretraining/ERM, and SGD convergence. `C005` only treats **finite-rank** continuous kernels. `C003` says a discontinuous bounded rank-one raw-input kernel obstructs arbitrarily small uniform KL for continuous predictors.

The earlier local `agents/a04.md` gives multiple-query cross-covariance for **unnormalized kernel attention** using Richardson. That is not C002's normalized dot-product ReLU architecture and its query count is a genuine architectural distinction, but multi-RHS parallelization is conceptually close. Kang–Lee–Cheng already treat a scalar query's mean and variance for arbitrary exact PSD kernel attention, including RBF; do not conflate that primitive with ordinary Q/K/V ReLU attention.

## Candidate increments, with attribution boundaries

1. **Fixed `m` joint noisy GP prediction in normalized ReLU attention:** For `Q=[q_1,…,q_m]∈R^{p×m}`, exact `μ=QᵀA⁻¹b`, `C=sI_m+sQᵀA⁻¹Q`. C002's five-head scalar solver can be parallelized: broadcast query vectors in separate `p`-coordinate slots; run one `w` recurrence and `m` independent `u_a` recurrences, each with a signed pair of heads; one constant-score head updates all previous-iterate registers. Pairwise signed readout at query token `a` gives `μ_a` and row `C_{a,:}`, initializing its covariance register to `s e_a`. A candidate accounting with role-separated input query blocks is `D=(4m+3)p+(m+3)`, `≤2m+3` heads, `T+2` layers, and `N=n+m` padded tokens. Check all Q/K/V matrices and flag gating before calling this a theorem. With `δ=1/T_T(c/d)`, `||Q||≤√m R`, `||b||≤nRB`, errors obey `||Δμ||≤√m nR²B δ/s`, `||ΔC||op≤mR²δ`; `C_T=sI+sQᵀP_TQ⪰sI` even without a one-sided modification. If `||ΔC||op≤s/2`, Gaussian KL is at most `||Δμ||²/(2s)+||ΔC||F²/(2s²)`. This is an extension, **not** the already-closed scalar target, and the `a04` kernel-attention corollary overlaps its posterior algebra.

2. **One-sided Chebyshev and conservative covariance:** Let `F_T=T_T(c/d)>1`. The nonnegative residual `r_T^+(x)=(1+T_T((c−x)/d))/(1+F_T)` satisfies `0≤r_T^+≤2/(1+F_T)` and `r_T^+(0)=1`. Its inverse polynomial is just `P_T^+=[F_T/(F_T+1)]P_T`, so C002's readout can be rescaled; in feature space `sI+sQᵀP_T^+Q⪯C` (a **lower** covariance, albeit PSD). For an **upper** feature-space covariance use `r_T^-(x)=(T_T((c−x)/d)−1)/(F_T−1)∈[-2/(F_T−1),0]`, `P_T^-=[F_T/(F_T−1)]P_T⪰A⁻¹`, giving `sI+sQᵀP_T^-Q⪰C`. `T≥1`; at `T=1`, `P_T^-=I/s`. In **data-space Schur complement** `C=J+sI−HᵀA_data⁻¹H`, the lower inverse `P_T^+` instead gives an upper covariance. The bounds `2/(F_T±1)` are minimax within the respective one-sided degree-`T` residual class: transform an admissible residual interval `[0,M]` or `[-M,0]` to a polynomial bounded by 1 on `[-1,1]`, then use Chebyshev's external-point extremality at `c/d`. This is a small calibration refinement of C002, not a new accelerated solver or basic every-depth PSD result.

3. **Explicit RBF truncation is still absent in Brain T336 and can be formulated concretely.** For `k(x,x')=τ² exp(-||x−x'||²/(2ℓ²))` on `[-a,a]^d`, truncate the PSD monomial feature expansion at total degree `M`: `φ_α(x)=τ exp(-||x||²/(2ℓ²))(x/ℓ)^α/√(α!)`, `|α|≤M`, rank `p=binom(d+M,d)`. With `D=da²/ℓ²` and `h=M+1>D`, diagonal feature-tail squared is `≤τ²(eD/h)^h`; by Cauchy-Schwarz the uniform kernel entry error is `δ_k≤τ²(eD/h)^h`. A simple explicit choice is `h≥max(2eD,log₂(τ²/δ_k))`. Both exact and truncated Gram matrices plus `sI` have inverse norm at most `1/s`. For `n` labels `|y|≤B`, a direct resolvent calculation gives `|m−m_M|≤nBδ_k/s·(1+nτ²/s)` and `|v−v_M|≤δ_k·(1+nτ²/s)²` for one noisy query. The truncated feature has norm `≤τ` and, after rescaling the cube to `[0,1]^d`, coordinate Lipschitz constant `≤2aτ/ℓ` by the full RBF feature metric. Thus reviewed C004 can be composed with this deterministic truncation budget. This appears to be a **straightforward corollary**, and exact-kernel-attention Kang et al. already handle RBF scalar moments by a different primitive; an end-to-end explicit ReLU raw-input RBF KL statement would still need its constants and independent review.

4. **Pretraining remains a separate substantive gap.** C002/C005 explicitly claim no ERM bound. Bai et al. Theorem 20 proves a parameter/norm-controlled pretraining generalization theorem for **square-loss point prediction** under bounded labels, not GP Gaussian log-loss. A true GP prior produces unbounded Gaussian context and query labels, whereas C002's uniform approximation assumes `|y_i|≤B`. A GP-prior average argument must handle Gaussian tails, positive/clipped predicted variance, a parameter-bounded architecture class and loss envelope (or subexponential concentration), and the population cross-entropy identity `risk(θ)−risk(Bayes)=E_context KL(PPD||q_θ)`. Only an approximate/global ERM, not SGD dynamics, follows from uniform convergence. No such combined T336 theorem was located in the inspected internal files or the named papers; absence of a hit is **not** a novelty proof.

## Primary literature checked

- [Bai, Chen, Wang, Xiong, Mei, arXiv:2306.04637v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.04637v2): normalized ReLU-attention architecture and ridge ICL; Theorem 20's pretraining result is square loss with bounded labels.
- [Mahfoozi et al., arXiv:2610.08578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08578): random Fourier feature GP-attention **module** with posterior mean/variance and a feature-space inverse; related RBF approximation prior art, not a finite-layer Q/K/V ReLU inverse solver.
