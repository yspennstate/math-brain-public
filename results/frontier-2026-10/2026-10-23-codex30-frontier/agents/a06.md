# Assignment 06 — leaky-ReLU SGD in dimension \(d\): exact affine reduction and moment thresholds

Assume \(d\ge2\), \(0<a\le1\), \(\eta>0\), \(X\sim N(0,I_d)\), and independent symmetric \(Y\) with \(\mathbb P(Y\ne0)>0\). For \(w\ne0\), write \(c(w,X)=1\) if \(w^\top X>0\) and \(a\) otherwise. The update is
\[
 w'=(I-\eta c^2XX^\top)w+\eta cYX.
\]

## Stronger than radial equivalence: exact affine transition kernel

Let \(T=(XX^\top,YX)\). The joint law of \((X,Y)\) is invariant under \((X,Y)\mapsto(-X,-Y)\), which leaves \(T\) unchanged and exchanges \(w^\top X>0\) with \(w^\top X<0\). Thus, **conditional on \(T\), the gate is a fair coin**, for every fixed \(w\ne0\). In particular the *full vector transition law*, not just its radius, is identical to
\[
 V'=(I-\eta C^2XX^\top)V+\eta CYX,
 \qquad \mathbb P(C=1)=\mathbb P(C=a)=\tfrac12,
\]
where \(C\) is independent of \((X,Y)\). This is an iid affine stochastic recurrence, with effective design \(G=CX\) and label \(Y\). For a minibatch of size \(b\), the same argument is independent sample by sample and gives \(A=I-(\eta/b)\sum_i C_i^2X_iX_i^\top\), \(B=(\eta/b)\sum_i C_iY_iX_i\). The convention for \(\sigma'_a(0)\) can only alter a transition *from* \(w=0\); from a nonzero state the process almost surely never hits zero, and the nondegenerate invariant law gives zero mass to zero.

This reduction lets one apply the corrected multivariate affine-recursion theorem of Damek and Mentemeier (2026), [Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 3.4](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/analysing-heavytail-properties-of-stochastic-gradient-descent-by-means-of-stochastic-recurrence-equations/2C941823AF7FD751744E302B362191FF). Their hypotheses hold for \(G=CX\): its density is a positive radial two-Gaussian mixture with full support and rapid decay; the induced rank-one matrix law is rotationally invariant and proximal; \(A\) is a.s. invertible and has a small inverse moment. If \(Y\) has all moments (e.g. Rademacher or Gaussian), \(\gamma<0\) gives a unique affine invariant law and an exact Pareto radial tail \(\mathbb P(\|W_\infty\|>t)\sim Kt^{-\alpha}\), \(K>0\). The exponent solves the one-step equation below. The affine invariant law is also an invariant law of the original SGD, and their transition kernels agree off zero. Uniqueness of the affine invariant law transfers to the original chain for stationary laws without mass at zero.

## Multiplicative factor and Lyapunov exponent

For a unit \(u\), put \(Z=u^\top X\sim N(0,1)\), \(S=\|X-Zu\|^2\sim\chi^2_{d-1}\), independent. Given \(C=c\), with \(\beta=\eta c^2\),
\[
 M_\beta^2=\|(I-\beta XX^\top)u\|^2
 =(1-\beta Z^2)^2+\beta^2 Z^2S
 =1-2\beta Z^2+\beta^2Z^2(Z^2+S).
\]
Let \(m_p=\tfrac12\mathbb E M_\eta^p+\tfrac12\mathbb E M_{\eta a^2}^p\). At \(Y=0\), the radial multipliers are iid and \(\log\|W_n\|=\log\|W_0\|+\sum_{j=1}^n\log M_j\) exactly. The top Lyapunov exponent for the affine model is
\[
 \gamma=\mathbb E\log M=\tfrac14\mathbb E\log M_\eta^2
                         +\tfrac14\mathbb E\log M_{\eta a^2}^2,
 \qquad \gamma\le\tfrac12\log m_2,
\]
with strict inequality at \(m_2=1\) because \(M\) is nonconstant. If \(\gamma<0\), strict log convexity and unbounded Gaussian excursions give one positive root \(m_\alpha=1\); for light-tailed labels the corrected theorem identifies \(\alpha\) as the stationary Pareto exponent. As \(\eta\to\infty\), \(\gamma=\log\eta+\log a+\frac12[\psi(1/2)+\psi(d/2)+2\log2]+o(1)\), so stable step sizes are bounded, while the exact \(m_2=1\) boundary is still strictly within the stable regime.

## Exact second and fourth moments, one sample

Set \(\mu_k=\mathbb E C^{2k}=(1+a^{2k})/2\), \(v_2=\mathbb EY^2>0\), and assume required label moments exist. Gaussian moments give
\[
 m_2=1-2\eta\mu_1+(d+2)\eta^2\mu_2,
 \quad \boxed{\eta_2=\frac{2\mu_1}{(d+2)\mu_2}
 =\frac{2(1+a^2)}{(d+2)(1+a^4)}}.
\]
The exact stationary variance, when \(\eta<\eta_2\), is
\[
 \mathbb E\|W_\infty\|^2
 =\frac{\eta^2d\mu_1v_2}{2\eta\mu_1-(d+2)\eta^2\mu_2}.
\]
For \(\eta\ge\eta_2\), any invariant law has infinite second moment. This follows directly from \(\mathbb E(\|W'\|^2\mid W=w)=m_2\|w\|^2+\eta^2d\mu_1v_2\), not merely from a heuristic tail formula.

For the fourth moment,
\[
 \boxed{m_4=1-4\eta\mu_1+2(d+8)\eta^2\mu_2
       -12(d+4)\eta^3\mu_3+3(d+4)(d+6)\eta^4\mu_4.}
\]
The polynomial \(m_4-1\) has exactly one positive nonzero root \(\eta_4\). Indeed \(\eta\mapsto m_4\) is strictly convex as an expectation of the fourth power of a norm of an affine function of \(\eta\), starts at 1 with negative derivative, and diverges; moreover \(m_4>m_2^2\), hence \(0<\eta_4<\eta_2\). More explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(\|W'\|^4\mid W=w)
={}&m_4\|w\|^4
 +2\eta^2(d+2)v_2[\mu_1-6\eta\mu_2+3(d+4)\eta^2\mu_3]\|w\|^2\\
 &+\eta^4d(d+2)\mu_2\mathbb EY^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bracket is strictly positive: conditional on a fixed \(c\) it equals \(c^2[1-6\beta+3(d+4)\beta^2]\), whose minimum is \(c^2(1-3/(d+4))>0\). Thus a stationary fourth moment is finite exactly for \(\eta<\eta_4\) if \(\mathbb EY^4<\infty\), and is infinite at and above \(\eta_4\) whenever a stationary law exists. For \(\eta\in[\eta_4,\eta_2)\), the invariant law has **finite variance and infinite fourth moment**, with Pareto index \(\alpha\in(2,4]\); at \(\eta=\eta_2\), \(\alpha=2\), and by \(\gamma(\eta_2)<0\) there is a nonempty open range beyond \(\eta_2\) with a stationary law of infinite variance.

For \(p>1\), \(m_p<1\) and \(\mathbb E|Y|^p<\infty\) imply existence of a stationary law with finite \(p\)-moment by a norm-power drift bound. Conversely, symmetry and strict convexity give \(\mathbb E_Y\|Aw+\eta CYX\|^p>\|Aw\|^p\); hence a stationary finite \(p\)-moment requires \(m_p<1\). With sufficiently light-tailed \(Y\), the Pareto theorem also covers \(p\le1\) and gives all thresholds. Do not claim an exact multiplicative recurrence for the *nonzero-label* radius: the full affine reduction is the correct statement.

### Slope dependence and high-dimensional asymptotics

Writing \(q=a^2\), \(\eta_2=2(1+q)/[(d+2)(1+q^2)]\) is maximized at \(q=\sqrt2-1\), where \(\eta_2=(1+\sqrt2)/(d+2)\). At both endpoints \(a=1\) and \(a\downarrow0\), \(\eta_2=2/(d+2)\); the intermediate slope raises the mean-square threshold by at most \((\sqrt2-1)/2\approx20.7\%\). In fact at the excluded endpoint \(a=0\), the transition kernel is exactly \(K_0=(I+K_1)/2\): the ReLU chain is a lazy version of the linear one and has the same stationary laws and Pareto exponents, only slower time evolution.

For fixed \(0<a<1\) and \(d\to\infty\), expanding \(m_4\) around \(\eta_2\) gives
\[
 \eta_2-\eta_4
 \sim \frac{24a^4(1-a^2)^2(1+a^2)}{(1+a^4)^4}\,d^{-2}.
\]
The endpoints are singular: at \(a=1\), the leading coefficient vanishes and \(\eta_2-\eta_4\sim24d^{-3}\). The two dimensions of the \((\eta_4,\eta_2)\) window are numerically, for \((d,a)=(2,0.5)\), \((0.3632812,0.5882353)\), and for \((10,1)\), \((0.1535615,0.1666667)\). These are exact polynomial roots rounded to seven places.

## Minibatch \(b\): exact moment formulas

Put \(t=\eta/b\), \(H=\sum_{i=1}^b C_i^2X_iX_i^\top\), \(u=e_1\), \(U=u^\top Hu\), and \(V=u^\top H^2u\). Then \(\|Au\|^2=1-2tU+t^2V\), and
\[
 m_{2,b}=1-2\eta\mu_1+\frac{\eta^2}{b}[(d+2)\mu_2+(b-1)\mu_1^2],
\quad
 \boxed{\eta_{2,b}=\frac{2b\mu_1}{(d+2)\mu_2+(b-1)\mu_1^2}}.
\]
The label-noise term in \(\mathbb E(\|W'\|^2\mid W=w)\) is \(\eta^2d\mu_1v_2/b\).

Here is an explicit quartic for batches, using quantities that keep the combinatorics legible:
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_1&=\mathbb EU=b\mu_1,\\
g_{11}&=\mathbb EU^2=b[3\mu_2+(b-1)\mu_1^2],\\
g_2&=\mathbb EV=b[(d+2)\mu_2+(b-1)\mu_1^2],\\
g_{12}&=\mathbb E(UV)=b[3(d+4)\mu_3+(b-1)(d+8)\mu_1\mu_2+(b-1)(b-2)\mu_1^3],\\
g_{22}&=\mathbb EV^2=b[3(d+4)(d+6)\mu_4+12(b-1)(d+4)\mu_1\mu_3\\
&\hspace{47mm}+(b-1)(d^2+6d+20)\mu_2^2\\
&\hspace{47mm}+2(b-1)(b-2)(d+8)\mu_1^2\mu_2+(b-1)(b-2)(b-3)\mu_1^4],\\
m_{4,b}&=1-4tg_1+t^2(4g_{11}+2g_2)-4t^3g_{12}+t^4g_{22}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Again \(\eta_{4,b}\) is its unique positive nonzero crossing of 1 and \(\eta_{4,b}<\eta_{2,b}\). As a sanity check, the formula gave \(m_{4,2}=0.69305254\) for \((d,a,\eta,b)=(3,0.5,0.2,2)\), versus direct Monte Carlo \(0.69296379\) with 500,000 iid draws. For \(b=1\) it reduces to the boxed one-sample polynomial.

## Caveats and provenance

The exact reduction uses **joint sign symmetry**; \(\mathbb EY=0\) without \(Y\overset d=-Y\) does not suffice. Heavy-tailed labels can dominate the tail, so state the Pareto exponent only with \(\mathbb E|Y|^{\alpha+\epsilon}<\infty\). The scalar \(d=1\) case follows the kernel reduction too, but the cited multivariate proximal theorem is for \(d\ge2\); handle scalar via the one-dimensional perpetuity theorem separately. The 2021 linear-SGD paper introduced heavy-tail phenomena, but the [2026 corrected paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/analysing-heavytail-properties-of-stochastic-gradient-descent-by-means-of-stochastic-recurrence-equations/2C941823AF7FD751744E302B362191FF) explicitly identifies a gap in the older proof and supplies the applicable irreducible-proximal theorem.
