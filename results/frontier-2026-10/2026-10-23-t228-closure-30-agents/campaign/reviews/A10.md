## 2. Algebra, contraction, and tail exponent

The sample gradient is correctly differentiated. With `z=Ax`, `t=tanh(z)`, `u=sech^2(z)`,

\[
H-1=2du+d^2u^2-2dutz-2d^2ut^2+2dutE.
\]

The absolute values are bounded by `2d + d^2 + 2d + 2d^2 + 2d|E|`, giving the stated `4d+3d^2+2d|E|`. The elementary bound `|z|sech^2(z)<=1` is sufficient. This is a bound on the derivative at every state, so the resulting `L` controls the actual global Lipschitz constant, not just the asymptotic multiplier.

The remainder expansion is also correct:

\[
\Psi(x)-(1-\eta A^2)x
=-\eta A\{dzu+(1+du)(dt-E)\}.
\]

Its uniform absolute bound is precisely the stated `B`. Both `B` and `L` have all positive moments. No independence between `B` and the multiplier is needed or claimed here.

The identity `E|1-A^2|=4 phi(1)` and the uniform triangle bound in eta are correct. The arithmetic is

\[
0.98+1.01(0.006003)=0.98606303<1.
\]

Independence of the two original Gaussian coordinates is used correctly when computing the expectation involving `A^2|E|`. Thus the average contraction is stronger than the logarithmic condition requested in the registry.

For `xi=|1-eta A^2|`, all nonnegative moments exist. Strict log-convexity, `h(0)=1`, `h(1)<1`, and `h(2)>=1.9603>1` prove exactly one positive root in `(1,2)`. The derivative `m_alpha` is positive by strict convexity and finite by Gaussian integrability. The logarithmic singularities at the two zeros of the multiplier are integrable. The continuous law of `xi`, with no atom at zero, makes `log xi` nonarithmetic. These claims hold throughout the entire closed eta interval.

## 3. Stationarity, uniqueness, moments, and support

The Markov operator is a contraction on the complete space of probability laws with finite first moment. The finite moment of `Psi(0)` is immediate from `Psi(0)=eta(1+d)AE`. Banach's theorem yields the invariant law and the geometric Wasserstein-one convergence of the law from every deterministic start.

The extension to uniqueness among all invariant probability measures is sound. One may choose any proposed stationary initial variable independently of future samples. The iid Lipschitz bounds give `E product L_j <= q^n`; Markov's inequality and Borel-Cantelli force that product to zero almost surely. Multiplication by any finite initial difference preserves this convergence, even when the initial law has no first moment. Bounded Lipschitz test functions then identify its stationary law with the constructed one.

The moment drift uses `E xi^p<1`, not `E L^p<1`; that distinction is correct because the uniform affine remainder bound is available. The inequalities for the cases `p<=1` and `p>1` give a genuine coefficient below one, without any factorization of `xi` and `B`. The passage from uniformly bounded moments to the limiting law follows from lower semicontinuity/Portmanteau, equivalently a suitable Fatou argument.

The compact minorization is valid: conditioning on `A in [1,2]` gives a normal transition with bounded mean and a standard deviation bounded above and away from zero when the starting point lies in a compact interval. Its density has a positive infimum over the required compact sets. Every nonempty interval therefore has positive invariant mass. In particular, the compact set used in the final excursion argument has positive mass, and an annulus at a fixed finite distance can be reached uniformly from it in one step.

## 4. Exact tail limit and constant formula

I checked the PDF of Jelenkovic–Olvera-Cravioto, arXiv:1006.3295v5, Theorem 3.1, printed page 5. The corresponding HTML renumbers it as Theorem 2. Its scalar `N=1` specialization permits an arbitrary nonnegative `R` independent of the coefficient, subject to the stated moment and tail-discrepancy hypotheses. It does not require an affine stochastic fixed-point equation. Version 2 explicitly supplies the extra coefficient moment with `gamma=0`.

Consequently, the fact that `|X|` need not itself be an autonomous scalar iterated-map recursion is not an obstacle. Take a fresh stationary `X` independent of the next data pair; then `R=|X|` is independent of `xi`, and stationarity identifies the other tail with that of `|Psi(X)|`.

The weighted discrepancy estimate follows from the indicator-integral identity for two coupled nonnegative variables. The pointwise power difference is bounded by a constant times

\[
B^\alpha+B\xi^{\alpha-1}|X|^{\alpha-1}.
\]

Here independence is only between `X` and the new sample. The mixed coefficient moment is `E[B xi^(alpha-1)]`, which is finite; it is not incorrectly split into a product of coefficient moments. This verifies absolute integrability and justifies signed Fubini. The formula therefore has denominator `alpha m_alpha`, with no missing factor of two. It is an expectation of an integrable difference; writing it as a difference of separate alpha-moments would be invalid, and the proof correctly avoids that.

This theorem alone supplies only a finite nonnegative constant. The proof does not use the positive-constant wording in the SGD paper's abbreviated Mirek statement as a substitute for proving nondegeneracy.

## 5. Adversarial check of the positive-constant proof

### The joint tilt and stopping time

For fixed eta and its root alpha, the one-step tilted probability is

\[
dQ(a,e)=|1-\eta a^2|^\alpha\,dP(a,e).
\]

It is normalized because `h(alpha)=1`. Taking iid steps preserves independence across time and preserves the actual within-step dependence between `xi` and `B`. The full joint law may be used, or its pushforward to `(xi,B)`; the path event used in the proof is measurable from these pairs and the pathwise lower bound holds for every full sample realization.

For a stopped event `F`, summation over `{tau=n}` gives

\[
P(F,\tau<\infty)
=\sum_{n\ge1}E_Q[e^{-\alpha S_n};F,\tau=n].
\]

This is the correct argument even though the hitting time can be infinite under `P`. It does not invoke an unjustified optional-stopping equality or uniform integrability of the likelihood martingale. Under `Q`, the positive drift makes the level-hitting time finite almost surely. Version 2 states this stopped change-of-measure convention correctly.

### Positive survival probability and tight overshoots

Positive finite drift gives an almost surely finite future minimum. There is a positive-probability finite run of increments bounded below by some positive number. An independent future walk stays above the negative of a sufficiently large accumulated buffer with positive probability. This proves `p_+>0`, including the requirement of strict positivity at each preceding step.

The overshoot estimate also works. Write `Y=log xi` and `rho=E_Q exp(-theta Y)=h(alpha-theta)<1`. Chernoff gives

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}Q(S_n\le1)\le e^\theta/(1-\rho)=K_U.
\]

For an arbitrary interval of length one, stop at its first entrance. Starting anywhere inside that interval, every later visit implies that the subsequent increment sum is at most one. Strong Markov therefore bounds the expected total visits by `K_U`, uniformly over the interval's location.

Partition the positions immediately before a crossing of level `u` into `(u-k-1,u-k]`. An overshoot above `z` from such a position requires the fresh increment to exceed `z+k`. Independence of this increment from the past, followed by the uniform visit bound, yields the displayed sum. It is permissible to drop the first-crossing restriction in this upper bound. The sum tends to zero because `E_Q Y^+<infinity`. This is a real proof of uniform tightness, not a renewal-theoretic assertion assumed without hypotheses.

### Discounted errors and absence of hidden independence

The essential factorization is

\[
E_Q[e^{-\theta S_j}B_j^\theta]
=\rho^{j-1}E[\xi^{\alpha-\theta}B^\theta].
\]

Only the previous steps are factored from the current **joint** pair. The same-step `xi_j` and `B_j` remain inside one expectation. Since `0<theta<min(1,alpha)`, the coefficient exponent `alpha-theta` is positive and the mixed moment is finite. Summing and using subadditivity proves `E_Q V_infinity^theta<infinity`. There is no substitution of `E_Q B^theta E_Q xi^(-theta)`.

Choose the overshoot cutoff and the error cutoff so the sum of the two bad-event probabilities is at most `p_+/2`. A union bound then gives the good stopped event probability at least `p_+/2`; independence of survival, overshoot, and accumulated error is neither available nor needed.

For `2K<=|y|<=4K`, on that event all intermediate times have

\[
|X_j|\ge e^{S_j}(2K-K/2)>K,
\]

and the final time has `|X_tau|>(3/2)t>t`. The bounded overshoot supplies the lower bound on the stopped likelihood. Thus the claimed uniform excursion hitting probability proportional to `t^(-alpha)` is valid.

### Conversion to a stationary tail

The one-step annulus entrance uses the original compact minorization. Future samples are fresh after that entrance, so the uniform bound over entrance points applies by the Markov property.

The last-exit inequality is correct and does not require reversing time or assuming independent excursions. For every `N`, disjoint last-visit events give

\[
\pi(D)\ge\sum_{k=1}^N
\int_A\pi(dx)P_x(X_k\in D,\tau_A^+>k).
\]

Letting `N` increase gives the expected number of visits to `D` before return, which dominates the probability of at least one such visit. In combination with the compact mass and annulus entrance probability, this proves a strictly positive `liminf t^alpha pi(|x|>t)`. The finite limit established in Section 4 must therefore be strictly positive.
