## 3. Invariant laws, moments, and compact minorization

The exact zero-input value is `Ψ(0)=ηA(1+d)E`, which is integrable. Thus the Markov operator preserves `P₁(ℝ)`. An optimal input coupling, independent of the fresh pair `(A,E)`, gives

`W₁(μP,νP)≤EL·W₁(μ,ν)≤q W₁(μ,ν)`.

Completeness and Banach yield the unique fixed law in `P₁` and the explicit geometric bound `W₁(δₓPⁿ,π)≤qⁿ W₁(δₓ,π)`.

The extension to all invariant laws is sound and does not assume `E|Z|<∞`. Start any finite real random variable `Z`, independently of future maps, and couple with the zero-start chain. The iid Lipschitz bounds satisfy `E∏Lⱼ=(EL)^n≤qⁿ`. For each positive rational ε, Markov and Borel–Cantelli show `∏Lⱼ≤ε` eventually almost surely. Hence the product tends to zero almost surely, and multiplying it by the finite random number `|Z|` preserves convergence. Bounded Lipschitz test functions and dominated convergence identify any invariant initial law with π.

For `0<p≤1`, subadditivity gives a drift inequality with `r_p=h(p)<1` and `C_p=EB^p`. For `1<p<α`, the stated inequality follows from convexity with `λ=(1+ε)^(-1/(p-1))`:

`(a+b)^p≤(1+ε)a^p+(1-λ)^(1-p)b^p`.

One explicit choice is `ε=(1-h(p))/(2h(p))`, giving `r_p=(1+h(p))/2<1`. Independence between `ξ` and `B` is not needed. Starting from zero, iteration gives `sup_n E|X_n|^p≤C_p/(1-r_p)`. Weak convergence, applied first to the bounded continuous functions `min(|x|^p,N)` and then followed by monotone convergence in N, gives the same bound under π. The source's reference to Fatou is standard shorthand for this valid lower-semicontinuity step.

The minorization argument also works, and can be made uniform in η. On `a∈[1,2]` and `|x|≤R`, put

`σ₋=99/100`, `σ₊=101101/50000`,

`M_R=(126101/25000)R+101101/50000000`.

`ε_R = P(A∈[1,2])/(σ₊ sqrt(2π)) · exp(-(R+M_R)²/(2σ₋²)) > 0`.

The checker verifies all rational scale and mean coefficients. The mixture has a density because `A=0` has probability zero. For any positive-length interval, choose a bounded positive-length subinterval and then R large enough to enclose it and satisfy `π([-R,R])>0`. Invariance and the minorization give positive π-mass to that interval, so the support conclusion is not circular.
