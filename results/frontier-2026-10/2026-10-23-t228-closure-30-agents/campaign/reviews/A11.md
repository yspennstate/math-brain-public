## 1. Exact substitution and independence

Fix \(\eta\in[0.99,1.01]\). Take a stationary input \(X\sim\pi\) independent of the current Gaussian pair \((A,E)\), and set

\[
M=1-\eta A^2,\qquad \xi=|M|,\qquad
R_0=|X|,\qquad N\equiv1,\qquad C_1=\xi.
\]

Thus \(R_0\) is independent of \(\xi\). Independent future copies can be appended on a product space if one follows the tree proof literally.

The stationary output \(U=|\Psi(X)|\) is a coupled variable with the same marginal law as \(R_0\). The application does **not** claim that \(U\) is independent of the current \(\xi\); this generally fails and is unnecessary. Independence is imposed on the input \(R_0\).

## 3. Lower moments precede renewal

The expansion in Section 1 and \(|z|\operatorname{sech}^2z\le1\) imply

\[
|\Psi(x)-Mx|\le B,\qquad
B=\eta|A|\{d+(1+d)(d+|E|)\}.
\]

Both \(B\) and \(\xi\) have all positive moments. For \(0<p<\alpha\), subadditivity when \(p\le1\), or the weighted power inequality with \((1+\varepsilon)h(p)<1\) when \(p>1\), gives

\[
\mathbb E|\Psi(x)|^p\le r_p|x|^p+D_p,\qquad r_p<1,\quad D_p<\infty.
\]

Independence of \(B\) and \(\xi\) is unnecessary. Iteration from zero bounds \(\sup_n\mathbb E|X_n|^p\). The already established Wasserstein-one convergence implies weak convergence to \(\pi\), and Portmanteau gives \(\mathbb E_\pi|X|^p<\infty\).

This proves the needed moments without tail regular variation, a finite \(\alpha\)-moment, or a positive tail constant. Existence of \(\pi\) uses the earlier average Lipschitz contraction and finite first moment of \(\Psi(0)\).

## 4. Absolute discrepancy: no critical moment is needed

Put \(U=|\Psi(X)|\), \(V=|MX|=\xi|X|\). Stationarity gives \(U\overset d=R_0\), while \(|U-V|\le B\). Pointwise indicator comparison and Tonelli give

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}
|\mathbb P(R_0>t)-\mathbb P(\xi R_0>t)|\,dt\\
&\qquad\le
\mathbb E\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}
|\mathbf1_{\{U>t\}}-\mathbf1_{\{V>t\}}|\,dt
=\frac1\alpha\mathbb E|U^\alpha-V^\alpha|.
\end{aligned}
\]

There is no subtraction of divergent integrals. Since \(0<\alpha-1<1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
|U^\alpha-V^\alpha|
&\le\alpha |U-V|\max(U,V)^{\alpha-1}\\
&\le\alpha B(V+B)^{\alpha-1}\\
&\le\alpha B\xi^{\alpha-1}|X|^{\alpha-1}+\alpha B^\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second term is integrable. For the first, independence of the stationary input from the step gives

\[
\mathbb E[B\xi^{\alpha-1}|X|^{\alpha-1}]
=\mathbb E[B\xi^{\alpha-1}]\,\mathbb E|X|^{\alpha-1}<\infty.
\]

Indeed \(\xi^{\alpha-1}\le1+\xi\), and \(\mathbb E[B(1+\xi)]<\infty\) by Gaussian polynomial moments. Correlation of \(B\) and \(\xi\) is allowed. The state moment was established above; for this discrepancy alone, even the finite first moment from contraction suffices.

Thus the full absolute discrepancy integral is finite, including both improper endpoints, before renewal is invoked.

## 5. Formula and signed-to-radial reduction

The same absolute integrability permits Fubini for the signed difference:

\[
\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}
\{\mathbb P(R_0>t)-\mathbb P(\xi R_0>t)\}\,dt
=\frac1\alpha\mathbb E[U^\alpha-V^\alpha].
\]

Consequently the constant is exactly

\[
C_\eta=
\frac{\mathbb E[|\Psi(X)|^\alpha-|MX|^\alpha]}
{\alpha\,\mathbb E[\xi^\alpha\log\xi]}.
\]

The bracketed difference is integrable. Neither individual power is asserted integrable. Splitting its expectation into two expectations would be invalid after positivity is proved; the candidate expressly avoids that operation.

Sign changes of \(M\) cause no obstruction because \(|MX|=\xi|X|\) exactly in this scalar model. It is unnecessary to represent the radial chain as an autonomous affine recursion or an autonomous iterated random map. The comparison concerns marginal tails. No angular renewal theorem enters.

The later symmetry argument is independent: \(\Psi_{A,-E}(-x)=-\Psi_{A,E}(x)\), symmetry of \(E\), and uniqueness of \(\pi\) imply

\[
\pi(X>t)=\pi(X<-t)=\tfrac12\pi(|X|>t)\qquad(t>0).
\]

Thus splitting the radial limit requires no unproved angular convergence.

## 6. Positivity boundary and corrections

Renewal establishes \(\lim_{t\to\infty}t^\alpha\pi(|X|>t)=C_\eta\in[0,\infty)\). It cannot alone establish strict positivity, nor may infinite \(\alpha\)-moment be assumed as input. The candidate respects both restrictions.

At the interface relevant to this audit, Section 5's tilted excursion argument uses only the established \(h(\alpha)=1\), \(h(\alpha-\theta)<1\), finite positive tilted drift, joint Gaussian moments, and positive compact/annulus masses. Its lower bound \(\pi(|X|>t)\ge c t^{-\alpha}\) uses no preexisting positive tail constant. Combining it with the independently obtained limit forces positivity. There is no logical cycle across Sections 3–6.
