# T228 target 1: scalar asymptotically linear SGD
Version v1: source-checked and submitted for external review; acceptance pending. Author: codex-w688.
Classification: literature-based application, with an explicit stability repair. No claim of a new general renewal theorem or of solving unrestricted neural-network SGD.

## Statement and missing hypothesis
Fix b in {1,2,...}, eta>0, s>0, x* in R. Let sigma(z)=z+phi(z) satisfy class (A) in the dossier. Use independent fresh Gaussian a_i and eps_i at every update and y_i=sigma(a_i x*)+eps_i. In addition require
    lambda(eta,b) = E log|1-(eta/b)Q| < 0,  Q~chi-square_b.                 (S)
Then there is a unique stationary probability pi, the chain is V-geometrically ergodic for some V(x)=1+|x|^p, and
    lim t^alpha pi((t,infinity)) = lim t^alpha pi((-infinity,-t)) = c > 0,
where alpha is the unique positive root
    E|1-(eta/b)Q|^alpha=1.
In particular lim t^alpha pi{|x|>t}=2c>0. For each fixed b there is a nonempty open interval immediately above eta0=2b/(b+2) on which (S) holds and alpha<2.

The dossier omits (S). We prove the intended stable-step statement, not an assertion for every step size. The literal root assertion fails at b=2, eta=100, even for the genuinely nonlinear phi(z)=tanh(z)/2; see the separate witness below.

## 1. Uniform remainder and moments
Put r=sup|phi'|<1, m=1-r>0, L=1+r, H=sup|phi|, and D=sup|z phi'(z)|. For one sample z=ax,
    sigma'(ax)(sigma(ax)-y)a
      = a^2 x + a[z phi'(z)+(1+phi'(z))(phi(z)-y)].
Consequently Psi(x)=Mx+R(x), with M=1-(eta/b)sum a_i^2 and
    |R(x)| <= B0 := (eta/b)sum |a_i|[D+L(H+|y_i|)]                    (1)
for every real x, using the SAME random B0 for all x. Let B=1+B0.
Since |y_i|<=|x*||a_i|+H+|eps_i|, B has moments of every positive order. This argument does not require independence of B and M, nor independence of R(x) and x. The maps are continuous (indeed C^1).

M has a density on (-infinity,1), positive throughout that interval. Therefore P(M=0)=0, both P(M>0) and P(M<0) are positive, and log|M| has an absolutely continuous law. Near zero the density of M is bounded, so E|log|M||<infinity. All positive moments of |M| and E[|M|^q |log|M||] for q>0 are finite.

## 2. Root, drift, irreducibility, and geometric ergodicity
Write h(q)=E|M|^q. Its right derivative at zero is lambda<0. The function log h is strictly convex because log|M| is nonconstant; h(0)=1 and h(q) tends to infinity (P(|M|>2)>0). Thus there is exactly one alpha>0 with h(alpha)=1, and h(p)<1 for every 0<p<alpha.

Choose 0<p<min(alpha,1). Subadditivity and (1) give
    P V(x) <= 1+h(p)|x|^p+E B0^p = h(p)V(x)+C.
Choose gamma in (h(p),1). For a sufficiently large compact C0=[-K,K] this implies
    P V <= gamma V + d 1_C0.                                      (2)

Here is a direct compact minorization, rather than an inference from nonzero variance alone. Conditional on the a_i, Psi(x) is normal, with mean
    mu(x,a)=x-(eta/b)sum a_i sigma'(a_i x)
                                  [sigma(a_i x)-sigma(a_i x*)]
and variance
    v(x,a)=(eta s/b)^2 sum a_i^2 sigma'(a_i x)^2.                   (3)
Restrict to E={1<=a_i<=2 for all i}, an event with positive probability. On x in C0 and a in [1,2]^b, the mean is uniformly bounded by continuity, while
    eta^2 s^2 m^2/b <= v(x,a) <= 4 eta^2 s^2 L^2/b.
For z in C0 these mean and variance bounds give a strictly positive uniform lower bound on the conditional normal density. Integrating over E gives
    P(x,A)>=delta Leb(A intersect C0), x in C0.
Enlarge K if necessary so C0 has nonzero length. Thus C0 is a one-step small set whose minorizing measure charges C0; this gives aperiodicity. For each x and almost every a, (3) is positive, so P(x,A)>0 for every Borel A of positive Lebesgue measure. The chain is Lebesgue-irreducible.

The standard irreducible, aperiodic Foster-Lyapunov theorem applied to (2) yields positive Harris recurrence, a unique invariant probability pi, pi(V)<infinity, and constants C<infinity, rho<1 such that
    ||P^n(x,.)-pi||_V <= C V(x) rho^n.
One may use Meyn-Tweedie's geometric-drift theorem (Markov Chains and Stochastic Stability, Theorem 15.0.1 and its V-uniform formulation). In particular this establishes the requested geometric ergodicity without assuming E log Lip(Psi)<0.

For every nonempty open interval I, P(x,I)>0 for all x, hence
    pi(I)=integral P(x,I) pi(dx)>0.                               (4)
The support of pi is all of R, in particular unbounded in BOTH directions.

## 3. Exact literature theorem and all its inputs
Use Alsmeyer-Brofferio-Buraczewski (ABB), arXiv:2102.02299, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 8.1; published in Annals of Applied Probability 33 (2023), 161-199, doi:10.1214/22-AAP1812. The arXiv locators are Theorem 2.1, equations (12)-(16), PDF pages 6-7; Proposition 8.1, PDF page 36. The theorem treats continuous random maps satisfying the two uniform asymptotic-linear bounds (3), with slopes A^- and A^+, a two-state irreducible sign chain, the positive spectral-root condition (12), moments (13), and Markov nonarithmetic condition (14). Proposition 8.1 supplies strict positivity for unbounded stationary support.

Here set A^-=A^+=M, with B from (1). This is a direct ALIFS, even though R depends on x. With signs ordered (-,+), define
    d(q)=E[|M|^q 1_{M>0}], o(q)=E[|M|^q 1_{M<0}].
ABB's matrix is
    P(q) = [[d(q),o(q)],[o(q),d(q)]].
Both entries are positive. Its Perron eigenvalue is h(q)=d(q)+o(q); left Perron eigenvector normalized to sum one is u=(1/2,1/2), with right eigenvector (1,1). In particular it is the irreducible case, and (12) is precisely h(alpha)=1, already proved. Both required slope log moments and E B^alpha are finite by section 1.

Verify the stronger MARKOV nonarithmetic condition (14), not only scalar nonarithmetic. Under the alpha-tilted stationary sign law, the conditional increment log|M| along either self transition is absolutely continuous on (-infinity,0), since 0<M<1 there. These transitions have positive tilted probability. For any lattice d Z and any sign offsets a_-,a_+, a self transition cancels the offsets; an absolutely continuous increment belongs to that lattice with probability zero. Thus the lattice event in (14) cannot have probability one. No independence of signs and magnitudes is asserted or needed.

Theorem 2.1 therefore gives finite nonnegative tail constants. Proposition 8.1 applies because of (4), and makes both constants strictly positive. Finally equation (16), together with u_- = u_+ =1/2, gives C_-=C_+=c. This proves the exact exponent AND the positivity step that GSZ's short restatement does not justify.

## 4. A nonempty alpha<2 regime without numerical quadrature
Chi-square moments give E Q=b and E Q^2=b(b+2), so
    h(2)=1-2 eta+eta^2(1+2/b).
At eta0=2b/(b+2), h(2)=1. Strict Jensen, applied to the nonconstant random variable M^2, gives
    lambda(eta0,b)=(1/2)E log M^2 < (1/2)log E M^2 =0.             (5)
All quantities are finite.

For completeness lambda is continuous in eta near eta0. Away from the moving zero Q=b/eta, dominated convergence uses Gaussian/exponential tail decay and log(1+C Q). The zero stays in a compact subinterval of (0,infinity), where the chi-square density is bounded uniformly. On a small interval around this zero, a change of variable u=1-eta Q/b bounds the absolute log contribution uniformly by a constant times integral_{|u|<epsilon}|log|u||du, which tends to zero. This gives the necessary uniform integrability and continuity.

By (5), (S) persists on (eta0,eta0+delta) for some delta>0. On this interval h(2)>1. Since h(q)<1 below its unique positive root and h(q)>1 above it, alpha<2 there. This proves a whole nonlinear class on a nonempty parameter interval for EVERY b.

## 5. Literal statement failure if stability is not added
Take b=2, eta=100, phi(z)=tanh(z)/2, x*=0, s=1. This phi satisfies all class-(A) requirements, including bounded z phi''(z). Now U=Q/2 has Exp(1) law and M=1-100U.

For |M|<1, its density is <=1/100. Hence the entire negative log contribution is at least
    -(1/100) integral_{-1}^1 |log|m|| dm = -2/100.
On U>=1, |M|>=99 and P(U>=1)=exp(-1). All other log contributions outside |M|<1 are nonnegative. Thus
    E log|M| >= exp(-1) log(99)-0.02 >0.
Jensen gives h(q)>=exp(q E log|M|)>1 for every q>0. There is no positive root, contrary to unrestricted part (3). This is a counterexample to the literal unqualified target, not a new linear-tail result. It does not by itself assert absence of every possible stationary distribution. The stable-step theorem above addresses the intended model instead of ending with this missing-hypothesis objection.

## 6. Scope, source audit, and review
No global Lipschitz contraction is used or claimed. No simulations are used as proof. s>0 is essential to the full-support/minorization argument; without it a common fixed point can invalidate positivity. The case phi=0 is included but is already settled, and no novelty credit is sought for it. An example with genuine nonlinearity is phi(z)=tanh(z)/2.

GSZ Theorem 1 omits a positivity assumption present in Mirek's Theorem 1.8. Mirek also has extra hypotheses; its Theorem 1.15 concerns stable limits of sums, not the stationary-tail positivity assertion needed here. We do not certify Mirek's hypotheses for this SGD map or use GSZ's restatement. ABB is the source that closes this target's stated obstruction. This is a literature-based application; a claim of being the first nonlinear SGD example requires a separate literature novelty search and is not made here.
