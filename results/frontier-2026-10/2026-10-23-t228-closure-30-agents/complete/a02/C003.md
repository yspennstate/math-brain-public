# Raw-input obstruction for Target 1

Claim T336-w727-C003 v1. Status: proof (unaudited). This is a scope obstruction to the literal bounded-kernel raw-input statement; it does not contradict C002's supplied-feature theorem. No novelty or Lean certification is claimed.

## Statement

There is a rank-one bounded positive-semidefinite kernel on the compact raw-input domain [-1,1], observation-noise variance s=1, and bounded context labels, such that every predictor with continuous Gaussian output parameters (mu(x),v(x)), v(x)>0, has worst-case noisy predictive forward KL at least

    c0=(1/4) log(9/8)>1/36.

The conclusion applies in particular to every finite transformer whose raw-input encoding and subsequent operations are continuous and whose output is a Gaussian with positive continuous variance. It applies to normalized ReLU attention and softmax attention under these conditions. Therefore boundedness, compactness, finite rank, and positive observation noise alone cannot imply the raw-input version of Target 1 for arbitrarily small eps.

## Kernel and posterior

Define phi(x)=0 for x<0 and phi(x)=1 for x>=0. Set k(x,x')=phi(x)phi(x'). For any finite coefficients a_i,

    sum_{i,j} a_i a_j k(x_i,x_j)=(sum_i a_i phi(x_i))^2>=0.

Thus k is positive semidefinite, bounded by 1, and of rank exactly one (because k(0,0)=1). The GP can be represented as f(x)=Z phi(x), with Z~N(0,1). Observations add independent N(0,1) noise.

Use the empty context if allowed. Alternatively, for any n>=1 use one observation at x=-1 with label y=0, which satisfies every B>=0. This observation is pure noise independent of Z, so the posterior law of Z remains N(0,1). For either context, the new noisy predictive distribution is

    P_x=N(0,1) for x<0; P_x=N(0,2) for x>=0.

Hence the example does not rely on whether a context of length zero is included.

## Continuity forces two incompatible approximation requirements

Fix this context and any continuous output mu(x),v(x)>0. Let E=sup_x KL(P_x || N(mu(x),v(x))). If E is infinite the bound is immediate. Otherwise put mu0=mu(0),v0=v(0)>0. At x=0,

    D_2:=KL(N(0,2)||N(mu0,v0))<=E.

Take x_j=-1/j tending to zero from below. Since mu,v are continuous and v0>0, the explicit Gaussian KL formula is continuous along this sequence; all denominators remain positive near the limit. Therefore

    D_1:=KL(N(0,1)||N(mu0,v0))<=E.

No dominated convergence, probabilistic conditioning argument, or exchange of a supremum with a limit is used: every term in this deterministic convergent sequence is <=E.

For a>0 define

    D_a=(1/2)(log(v0/a)+(a+mu0^2)/v0-1).

Writing w=3/(2v0)>0 and combining logarithms gives the exact identity

    (D_1+D_2)/2
      = (1/4)log(9/8)+(1/2)(w-1-log w)+mu0^2/(2v0).

The inequality log w<=w-1 follows by integrating 1/t from 1 to w, separately for w>=1 and 0<w<=1, or by differentiating w-1-log w. Both additional terms are nonnegative. Consequently

    E >= (D_1+D_2)/2 >= (1/4)log(9/8).

Finally log(9/8)=integral_1^{9/8} dt/t > (1/8)(8/9)=1/9, since 1/t>8/9 except at the right endpoint. Thus c0>1/36. Every denominator and logarithm argument above is strictly positive.

## Interpretation for the dossier

C002 supplies phi as an input, so its input encoding itself is discontinuous as a function of raw x in this example; the continuity hypothesis here then fails. There is no contradiction. To close Target 1, the architecture must explicitly permit the fixed feature map in the input representation, or the theorem must add assumptions that make raw-input feature computation possible and account for its cost. Merely calling the kernel finite rank does not settle this issue.

The example is an obstruction to a named target interpretation, not a claimed solution of the entire T336 problem. The remaining feature-input theorem C002 and this obstruction both await independent review.
