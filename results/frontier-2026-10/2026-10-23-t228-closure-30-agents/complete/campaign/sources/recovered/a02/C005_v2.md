# Target 1 for continuous finite-rank kernels on compact raw domains

Claim T336-w727-C005 v2. Status: proof (unaudited), dependent on C002 v1 and the deterministic perturbation/encoder constructions in C004 v1. The entire result is not Lean-checked. No independent review or novelty certification is claimed.

The hardest step is removing the supplied-feature oracle without adding a hidden feature-computation cost. A finite Gram factorization fixes the encoder weights; a kernel modulus controls its error. The size explicitly includes the resulting encoder.

## 1. Statement and all parameters

Let X be a nonempty compact subset of R^d, d>=1. Let k:X times X -> R be a continuous real symmetric positive-semidefinite kernel of rank at most p, p>=1, with k(x,x)<=R^2 for R>=0. The prior is the zero-mean GP with kernel k. Observation noise is independent N(0,s), s>0. Labels are bounded by B>=0, contexts have length <=n with n>=0 an integer, and eps>0. The prediction concerns a new noisy observation.

Define the canonical kernel modulus in the ambient L1 metric by

    Omega(r)=sup_{x,y in X, ||x-y||_1<=r}
                sqrt(k(x,x)+k(y,y)-2k(x,y)).

The expression is nonnegative by positive semidefiniteness. Omega(r) tends to zero as r decreases to zero, as proved below.

Retain the following explicit constants from C004's deterministic perturbation bound:

    S=R+1, C_A=n(2R+1),
    C_m=nB[(R+S)/s+S C_A R/s^2],
    C_v=R+S+S^2 C_A/s>0,
    eta=min(1,sqrt(s eps)/(2(1+C_m)),s sqrt(eps)/(2C_v),s/(4C_v)),
    eps0=min(eps/4,1/16), e=eta/sqrt(p)>0.

Choose any **fixed admissible** r>0 with Omega(r)<=e/2. An explicit upper modulus for k can be used to choose r; continuity guarantees existence, not an algorithm for computing a modulus of an arbitrary black-box kernel. Put

    A_cone=(2R+e)/r>0, h=e/(2A_cone)>0.

Choose a finite h-net G contained in X, of cardinality J>=1. The size bound is explicit in J (or in a known upper bound for the covering number). For X=[0,1]^d one may take the regular grid with

    m=max(1,ceil(d A_cone/e)), J=(m+1)^d.

For the attention solver define

    kappa=1+max(1,n)S^2/s, M=nS^2B/s,
    delta0=min(1/2,s/(2S^2),sqrt(s eps0)/(1+M),s sqrt(eps0)/S^2),
    T=ceil(sqrt(kappa) log(2/delta0)/2).

There is a transformer, using normalized residual ReLU attention and tokenwise residual ReLU MLPs, with

    D=7p+6+d+Jd+pJ,
    H=2Jd+pJ+4p+1,
    L_total=T+5+ceil(log_2 J),
    at most five attention heads per layer,
    at most L_total(15D^2+2DH)+2D dense scalar parameters,

whose positive-variance Gaussian output has forward KL<=eps from the exact noisy GP predictive, uniformly over all admissible raw-input contexts and queries. Its input contains only raw coordinates, labels, fixed context/query flags, and constants. The encoder is counted. These are arbitrary-real-weight size bounds; no finite-bit, kernel-evaluation, offline factorization-time, SGD, or ERM-generalization bound is asserted.

## 2. Canonical finite-dimensional features and the kernel modulus

Form the vector space of finite formal combinations of symbols delta_x with bilinear form

    <sum_i a_i delta_{x_i},sum_j b_j delta_{y_j}>
       =sum_{i,j}a_i b_j k(x_i,y_j).

Positive semidefiniteness makes this a nonnegative quadratic form. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality follows by applying nonnegativity to u+t v for all real t; in particular a zero-norm vector is orthogonal to every vector. Quotient by the zero-norm subspace. For any finite list of symbols, the dimension of their quotient span is the rank of their Gram matrix. Every finite list of quotient vectors is contained in the span of finitely many symbols. Thus the dimension of the resulting inner-product space is the supremum of these Gram ranks, which is the kernel rank and is at most p. It is finite dimensional, so no completion issue remains.

Choose an orthonormal basis and pad with zeros in R^p. The coordinates z(x) of delta_x then satisfy

    k(x,y)=z(x)^T z(y), ||z(x)||<=R,
    ||z(x)-z(y)||^2=k(x,x)+k(y,y)-2k(x,y).

Thus z is continuous and ||z(x)-z(y)||<=Omega(r) whenever ||x-y||_1<=r. To justify Omega(r)->0 directly, suppose the contrary. There are sequences r_j->0 and x_j,y_j in X at distance <=r_j with feature distance bounded away from zero. Compactness supplies a subsequence x_j->x; then y_j->x as well. Continuity of k makes the squared feature distance tend to k(x,x)+k(x,x)-2k(x,x)=0, a contradiction. The supremum is finite because the kernel is continuous on the compact product. In particular every fixed positive e admits the required r.

The factorization at the finite net does not require evaluating any feature map on runtime inputs. Form the fixed J-by-J Gram matrix K_G=(k(a,b))_{a,b in G}. It is PSD of rank <=p, so a finite spectral factorization gives vectors v_a in R^p with v_a^T v_b=k(a,b). There is an isometry between the spans of z(a) and v_a: the map sum c_a z(a) -> sum c_a v_a is well defined and norm preserving because both squared norms equal c^T K_G c. Extend orthonormal bases of the two spans to R^p. The isometry extends to an orthogonal map on R^p. Applying it to all z(x) gives a representation, still called z, with z(a)=v_a for every a in G and with the same norm and modulus bounds.

Consequently **all feature values used as weights below can be obtained from the finite prior Gram matrix on G**. Computing this fixed factorization is preprocessing of the fixed prior, not an input-dependent inverse hidden in the predictive head. Its real entries are included in the encoder's parameter count.

## 3. Cone envelope for a general continuous feature map

Define, using the finite Gram feature values,

    ztilde_j(x)=min_{a in G}[v_{a,j}+A_cone ||x-a||_1].

This formula is valid even though z_j need not be Lipschitz. We claim |ztilde_j(x)-z_j(x)|<=e for every j,x.

For a with ||x-a||_1<=r, the modulus gives v_{a,j}>=z_j(x)-Omega(r)>=z_j(x)-e/2. Adding a nonnegative cone term preserves this lower bound. For a with ||x-a||_1>r, the coordinate bound |z_j|<=R gives

    v_{a,j}+A_cone ||x-a||_1
      >z_j(x)-2R+A_cone r=z_j(x)+e.

So every candidate is >=z_j(x)-e/2, and the finite minimum is too.

For the upper bound choose a net point with ||x-a||_1<=h. Since h=e r/(2(2R+e))<=r/2, the modulus bound applies. Therefore

    ztilde_j(x)<=z_j(x)+Omega(r)+A_cone h
                  <=z_j(x)+e/2+e/2=z_j(x)+e.

It follows that sup_x ||ztilde(x)-z(x)||<=sqrt(p)e=eta and ||ztilde(x)||<=R+eta<=S. All denominators above are positive, including R=0. For a cube grid, coordinate rounding gives L1 distance <=d/(2m)<=e/(2A_cone)=h, which verifies the displayed grid size.

## 4. Exact network realization and original-posterior KL

Use C004 Section 4's explicit register construction, with the J centers now the net points a and with the slope K in the cone layer replaced by A_cone. Layer 1 computes all Jd absolute differences with 2Jd ReLUs. Layer 2 forms the pJ affine cone values using these nonnegative absolute registers and the constant register. A balanced residual-min tournament takes ceil(log_2 J) layers, including zero layers when J=1. The last gating layer, using the binary context/query flags and |ztilde_j|<=S, writes the context/query feature registers exactly. For padding choose a fixed anchor point in X as its raw coordinates, rather than assuming the ambient origin belongs to X. Its flags are zero, so the computed features are gated to zero.

This uses E=3+ceil(log_2 J) encoder layers and precisely the width and hidden-width bounds in Section 1. This invokes the exact algebraic encoder construction, **not the Lipschitz-error assertion** of C004 Section 3. Section 3 above supplies the replacement uniform approximation guarantee.

C004 Section 2's perturbation proof uses only ||ztilde-z||<=eta<=1, not feature Lipschitzness. It therefore gives original-versus-encoded predictive errors

    |mu-mutilde|<=sqrt(s eps)/2,
    |v-vtilde|<=min(s/4,s sqrt(eps)/2).

Apply C002 v1 to the encoded features with bound S and accuracy eps0. The one-head query broadcast, T five-head Chebyshev layers, and four-head readout produce muhat,vhat with vhat>=s and errors

    |mutilde-muhat|<=sqrt(s eps)/2,
    |vtilde-vhat|<=min(s/4,s sqrt(eps)/2).

The original predictive variance is also >=s. Adding scalar errors gives |mu-muhat|<=sqrt(s eps) and |v-vhat|<=min(s/2,s sqrt(eps)). The Gaussian calculation in C002 Section 5 then yields forward KL<=eps from the **original kernel** predictive distribution to the transformer output. No triangle inequality for KL is used.

Combining the E encoder layers with the T+2 solver layers gives the depth. Count three D-by-D matrices for each of at most five heads, two MLP matrices of dimensions H-by-D and D-by-H per layer (even counting zeros), and a two-coordinate linear readout. This proves the displayed parameter bound.

## 5. Exact scope

C005 covers every continuous bounded finite-rank kernel on a nonempty compact Euclidean domain, with an explicit bound in terms of a kernel modulus and a finite covering number. It includes continuous kernels whose features are not Lipschitz. C004 gives the simpler closed grid formula when a coordinatewise Lipschitz feature bound is available. C002 is smaller when features are supplied directly.

C003 proves that the bounded-kernel statement without continuity can fail for continuous raw-input Gaussian-output architectures. Thus these results distinguish the feature-input interpretation from the regularity needed for a raw-input theorem. They do not claim that a raw-input size bound can ignore the fixed kernel's modulus or its feature-computation complexity. All three constructive claims remain independently unaudited.
