## 2. Independent derivative and remainder reconstruction

With `z=Ax`, `t=tanh z`, and `u=sech² z`, differentiation at fixed realized data gives

```text
Ψθ'(x)=1−ηA²[(1+δu)²−2δut(z+δt−Y)].
```

Although `Y` depends on `A`, it does not depend on the variable being differentiated, `x`. It is therefore correct that no derivative of the teacher term appears. Expanding the bracket minus one gives

```text
2δu + δ²u² − 2δzut − 2δ²ut² + 2δutY.
```

The inequalities `|t|≤1`, `u≤1`, and `|z|u≤1` bound its absolute value by

```text
4δ+3δ²+2δ|Y|.
```

Consequently the actual global Lipschitz constant, not just the slope at infinity, satisfies

```text
Lip(Ψθ) ≤ Lθ := |1−ηA²|+ηA²(4δ+3δ²+2δ|Y|).
```

The identity

```text
(1+δu)(z+δt−Y) = z+δzu+(1+δu)(δt−Y)
```

also gives, with `M=1−ηA²`,

```text
Ψθ(x)=Mx+Rθ(x),
|Rθ(x)|≤Bθ:=η|A|[δ+(1+δ)(δ+|Y|)]  for every x.
```

Since `|Y|≤(1+δ)|A|+|E|`, both `Lθ` and `Bθ` have all positive moments. Every mixed moment used later is controlled by a polynomial in the Gaussian absolute values. There is no claim or need that `M`, `Y`, and `Bθ` be mutually independent.

The nonlinearity assertion is also genuine: for `A≠0`, the derivative at zero is `1−ηA²(1+δ)²`, whereas its limit as `|x|→∞` is `M`. These differ with probability one, independently of the value of the label.

**Derivative, global contraction envelope, and uniform remainder: PASS.**

## 3. Teacher dependence and exact contraction slack

The mixed expectation must not be replaced by `EA² E|Y|`. The valid computation is

```text
E[A²|Y|]
≤ (1+δ)|θ| E|A|³ + E[A²|E|]
= [2(1+δ)|θ|+1]√(2/π)
≤ (3+2δ)√(2/π) < 3.002.
```

Only the noise summand factors, using the stated independence of `A` and `E`. This verifies the exact conservative input used by the author. An optional independent strengthening, not needed for the theorem, follows from `|tanh|≤1` instead:

```text
E[A²|Y|] ≤ |θ| E|A|³ + δ EA² + E[A²|E|]
          ≤ 3√(2/π)+δ < 3.001.
```

The Gaussian identity `E|1−A²|=4/√(2πe)` follows by integrating `(aφ(a))'=(1−a²)φ(a)` on `[0,1]`. The conservative estimate `<97/100`, together with the pointwise triangle inequality, yields

```text
E|1−ηA²| ≤ E|1−A²|+|η−1|EA² < 98/100.
```

Using the requested `3.002` mixed-label bound gives the following exact rational calculation, independently executed with Python's `fractions.Fraction`:

```text
4δ+3δ²+2δ(3.002) = 10007/1000000,
q = 98/100+(101/100)(10007/1000000)
  = 99010707/100000000 = 0.99010707,
1−q = 989293/100000000 = 0.00989293.
```

Thus `E Lip(Ψθ)≤ELθ<q<1`, uniformly over the entire stated parameter rectangle. At `η=1`, the same computation gives `<980007/1000000=0.980007`, as claimed. The stronger optional `3.001` estimate would give uniform bound `0.99010505`; retaining the advertised conservative bound causes no problem.

I also independently checked the author's rational normal-bound margin and second-moment endpoint:

The logarithmic contraction is legitimate. The positive logarithm of the actual Lipschitz constant is integrable because it is bounded by `Lθ`. In fact `Lip(Ψθ)≥|M|` by the limiting derivative, and `E|log|M||<∞`, so the negative logarithm is also controlled. The author's envelope lower bound `Lθ≥4δ+3δ²>0` is separately correct.

**Requested contraction and exact slack: PASS.**

## 4. Uniqueness and the unchanged exponent

The teacher term changes the remainder, not the asymptotic coefficient. Thus

```text
hη(p)=E|1−ηA²|^p
```

is exactly the same function for all fixed teachers. It is finite for nonnegative `p`, has strictly convex logarithm, and satisfies `hη(0)=1`, `hη(1)<1`, and

```text
hη(2)=1−2η+3η²≥1.9603>1.
```

The intermediate value theorem and strict log-convexity give precisely one positive root `α∈(1,2)`, with `hη(p)<1` on `(0,α)`. The derivative at that root is positive: the derivative of `log hη` at `α` is at least the positive secant slope from `1` to `α`. It is finite by the Gaussian tails and local logarithmic integrability at `A=±η^(−1/2)`. These same local facts imply that `log|M|` is integrable and nonarithmetic; its law is absolutely continuous.

The Markov operator preserves the complete Wasserstein-one space because `E|Ψθ(0)|=E|η(1+δ)AY|<∞`. Synchronous fresh-step coupling contracts that metric by at most `q`, establishing a unique invariant law in that space and the claimed geometric bound.

There is no concealed first-moment restriction on global uniqueness. For any real-valued initial random variable `Z`, independent of future steps,

```text
|X_n^Z−X_n^0|≤|Z|∏_(j=1)^n Lθ,j,
E[∏_(j=1)^n Lθ,j]=(ELθ)^n≤q^n.
```

Markov's inequality and Borel–Cantelli, applied at countably many positive thresholds, make the product tend to zero almost surely. Since `|Z|<∞` almost surely for every probability law on `R`, bounded Lipschitz tests identify any invariant law with the Wasserstein-one invariant law. No moment of `Z` is required.

For `0<p<α`, the uniform remainder and `hη(p)<1` yield the displayed subadditive or `(1+ε)` drift. Uniform moments from the zero start, followed by weak convergence and lower semicontinuity, establish all stationary moments strictly below `α` without assuming the tail result.

**Global uniqueness, geometric convergence, and teacher-independent exponent: PASS.**

```text
μθ(x,a)=x−ηaσ'(ax)[σ(ax)−σ(aθ)],
s(x,a)=η|a|σ'(ax).
```

Restricting to `a∈[1,2]` gives `s_min=0.99` and `s_max=2(1.01)(1+δ)`. For `|x|≤R`, the author's

```text
[s_max√(2π)]^(−1) exp(−(T+D_R)²/(2s_min²)).
```

Integrating over `A∈[1,2]` proves the stated compact minorization. Every nonempty open interval therefore has positive transition probability from every point and positive mass under the invariant law. ## 6. Exact tail limit: dependence-safe renewal input

Let `X` have the invariant law and be independent of the complete next step. Set `U=|Ψθ(X)|` and `W=|MX|`. Then `U` has the law of `|X|` and `|U−W|≤Bθ`. The deterministic identity

```text
∫₀∞ t^(α−1)|1{U>t}−1{W>t}|dt = |U^α−W^α|/α
```

and Tonelli prove the required weighted discrepancy bound. Because `1<α<2`,

```text
|U^α−W^α|
≤ αBθ(W+Bθ)^(α−1)
≤ α[Bθ^α+Bθ ξ^(α−1)|X|^(α−1)].
```

The relevant factorization is only

```text
E[Bθ ξ^(α−1)|X|^(α−1)]
=E[Bθ ξ^(α−1)] E|X|^(α−1)<∞.
```

The coefficient–remainder factor remains a joint moment. Every other renewal condition is established before the application, including the auxiliary coefficient moment with exponent zero. The theorem thus supplies a finite nonnegative radial tail limit. Signed Fubini applies to the integrable coupled difference and gives the displayed formula with denominator `α mα`.

This reasoning never subtracts two infinite α-moments and does not smuggle positivity into the renewal application. The stationary input is independent of the fresh coefficient even though the fresh label is dependent on that coefficient.

**Exact limit and integrable-difference formula: PASS.**

## 7. Independent reconstruction of strict positivity

The full-step tilt has density `ξ^α=|1−ηA²|^α`. It changes the complete data-step law and leaves the algebraic relation `Y=σ(Aθ)+E` intact. Independent steps remain independent; no within-step independence of `ξ,Bθ` is imposed. Write `Z=log ξ` under the tilted law, `S_n=Σ Z_j`, and take `β=1/2`. Then

```text
E_Q Z=mα>0,
r:=E_Q e^(−βZ)=hη(α−β)<1.
```

The six potential failure points in the positivity proof were checked as follows.

1. **Survival above zero.** `e^(−βS_n)` is a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one. At the first hit of `(-∞,−1]`, its value is at least `e^β`; bounded stopping and then monotone convergence give a hitting probability at most `e^(−β)`. A first increment above one, followed by an independent walk that never hits `(-∞,−1]`, therefore has probability at least `Q(Z>1)(1−e^(−β))=p₀>0` and keeps every positive-time partial sum above zero. The positive drift already guarantees that the walk's infimum is attained, so there is no endpoint issue in the displayed infimum formulation.

2. **Overshoots uniform in the crossing level.** Chernoff gives `Σ_n Q(S_n≤1)≤e^β/(1−r)=K_U`. After the first entry into any translated interval of length one, a later visit to that interval requires a fresh displacement at most one. Strong Markov therefore bounds its total expected occupation by `K_U`, uniformly over all translations. Partitioning `(-∞,u]` into `(u−k−1,u−k]` gives `Q(O_u>z)≤K_U Σ_(k≥0)Q(Z>z+k)`, uniformly in `u`. The sum tends to zero because `E_Q Z⁺<∞`. This is the required uniform overshoot estimate, not a fixed-level estimate.

3. **Discounted error with the joint law preserved.** Reverse triangle inequalities give `|X_n|≥e^(S_n)(|X_0|−V_n)`, where `V_n=Σ_(j≤n)e^(−S_j)Bθ,j`. Independence of a step from its past, together with subadditivity, yields

   ```text
   E_Q V_∞^β
   ≤ Σ_(j≥1) r^(j−1) E_P[ξ^(α−β)Bθ^β]
   = E_P[ξ^(α−β)Bθ^β]/(1−r)<∞.
   ```

   Here `α−β>0`, so no inverse-power singularity has been introduced near a zero of `M`. The numerator is a joint Gaussian-polynomial moment, not an invalid product of moments.

4. **A good excursion event with fixed constants.** Choose `z` so that every overshoot failure probability is at most `p₀/4`, and then choose `K` so that `Q(V_∞>K/2)≤p₀/4`. Both choices precede the variable threshold `t`. With `u=log(t/K)`, the stopped good event has probability at least `p₀/2` by a union bound. It implies, for every `2K≤|X_0|≤4K`, that `|X_j|>(3K/2)>K` up to crossing and `|X_(τ_u)|>3t/2`. The coefficient's sign never enters the radial inequality.

5. **Stopping-time change of measure.** For the good event, summing the finite-time likelihood identities over `{τ_u=n}` gives

   ```text
   P(F_u)=E_Q[e^(−αS_(τ_u))1_(F_u)]
         ≥ e^(−α(u+z))p₀/2.
   ```

   This uses only stopped, finite-crossing events. It does not require that `τ_u` be almost surely finite under the original law, global absolute continuity on infinite paths, or an optional-stopping equality for a likelihood martingale.

6. **Transfer to stationary tail mass.** For disjoint sets `C,D`, splitting a stationary trajectory according to its last visit to `C` before time `N` gives

   ```text
   π(D)≥∫_C π(dx) Σ_(j=1)^N P_x(X_j∈D,j<τ_C^+).
   ```

   The events in that finite-horizon sum are disjoint after stationarity translates them to the common endpoint. Monotone convergence bounds the expected excursion occupation, which in turn bounds the probability of ever hitting `D` before returning to `C`. This is an unnormalized integral against `π|_C`; no missing return-time or `π(C)` normalization is present. Compact minorization gives a positive first-step probability into the annulus, and full support gives `π(C)>0`. Hence the author's strictly positive constant

   ```text
   c_(η,θ)=π(C)δ_K(p₀/2)e^(−αz)K^α
   ```

   satisfies `π(|x|>t)≥c_(η,θ)t^(−α)` for all `t>4K`.

The previously proved tail limit must therefore be strictly positive. These arguments extend to the teacher-dependent remainder exactly as written. They claim positivity for each fixed parameter pair, not a uniform positive lower constant over the rectangle.

**Strict tail positivity: PASS.**

## 8. Symmetry, estimation error, and nonzero signal

The valid reflection identity changes the teacher:

```text
Ψ_(−θ,A,−E)(−x)=−Ψ_(θ,A,E)(x).
```

It yields reflection of `π_(η,θ)` into `π_(η,−θ)` and equality of their radial constants. It yields fixed-model symmetry only when `θ=0`.

The extension's stronger nonsymmetry assertion is also correct. Finite first moment and stationarity allow the expected gradient identity

```text
E[Aσ'(AX)σ(AX)] = E[Aσ'(AX)σ(Aθ)].
```

If the stationary law were symmetric about zero, the left side would vanish by oddness in `X`, conditional on each `A`. The integrand on the right has the strict sign of `θ` for `A≠0`. Both sides are integrable because `σ'≤1+δ`, `|σ(v)|≤(1+δ)|v|`, and the stationary first moment is finite. Thus the law cannot be symmetric about zero when `θ≠0`.

The theorem correctly avoids asserting equal one-sided tail constants for nonzero teachers. Such equality is not disproved by nonsymmetry, but it would require an additional signed-tail argument and is not an output of the scalar absolute-value renewal application audited here.

Finally, bounded translation gives

```text
P(|X|>t+|θ|)≤P(|X−θ|>t)≤P(|X|>t−|θ|),
```

so the absolute estimation error has the same positive tail constant and the same exact moment threshold. The population-risk identity and inequality

```text
Fθ(x)=1/2+(1/2)E[σ(Ax)−σ(Aθ)]²
     ≥1/2+(x−θ)²/2
```

follow from independence and centering of the additive noise and `σ'≥1`. They establish a unique population minimizer at the stated teacher. In particular `θ=1,η=1` is a genuine nonzero-signal example.

**Symmetry handling, centered error, and teacher interpretation: PASS.**
