## 2. Batch-two update, contraction, and exponent: PASS

The simultaneous gradient of the average of two losses is exactly

\[
\Psi(x)=x-\frac\eta2\sum_{i=1}^2 A_i\sigma'(A_ix)
 [\sigma(A_ix)-\sigma(A_i\theta)-E_i].
\]

The multiplier is therefore `M=1−η(A₁²+A₂²)/2`, rather than a product of two sample multipliers. Polar integration of the independent standard-normal input density gives `T=(A₁²+A₂²)/2 ~ Exp(1)` exactly. Consequently

\[
h(1)=1-\eta+2\eta e^{-1/\eta},\qquad
h(2)=1-2\eta+2\eta^2.
\]

I independently differentiated the actual random map. With `t=tanh(z)` and `u=sech²(z)`,

\[
H(z,Y)-1=2\delta u+\delta^2u^2
 -2\delta uzt-2\delta^2ut^2+2\delta utY.
\]

The inequalities `|t|≤1`, `0<u≤1`, and `|z|u≤1` give `|H−1|≤4δ+3δ²+2δ|Y|`. Thus the claimed global Lipschitz envelope follows from the derivative, not merely from the asymptotic slope. At zero the derivative is `1−η(1+δ)²T`; at infinity it tends to `1−ηT`. Since `T>0` almost surely, the actual batch map is nonaffine almost surely and its Lipschitz constant is at least `|M|`.

Input-label dependence is handled correctly:

\[
E[A_i^2|Y_i|]
\le (1+\delta)|\theta|E|A_i|^3+E[A_i^2|E_i|]
=[2(1+\delta)|\theta|+1]\sqrt{2/\pi}<3+2\delta.
\]

Only the input and its independent additive noise are factored. The two pairs `(A_i,Y_i)` are iid within a fresh batch; `A_i` and `Y_i` need not be independent. The factor `1/2` in the averaged gradient cancels the two equal marginal expectations, yielding `E Lip(Ψ)<h(1)+η·10007/10⁶`.

On `η∈[11/10,6/5]`, direct differentiation gives

\[
h'(1;\eta)=-1+2e^{-1/\eta}(1+1/\eta)>2/9.
\]

The notation here means the derivative of `h_η(1)` with respect to `η`. Using the displayed exact Taylor bound in the proof gives `h(1)<107/125`. Hence the actual-map bound is strictly below `q₂=2170021/2500000=0.8680084`. Also `h(2)≥61/50=1.22>1`, with the minimum at `η=11/10`.

The stated density of `ξ=|M|` is correct:

\[
g_\eta(x)=\eta^{-1}e^{-(1+x)/\eta}
 +1_{(0,1)}(x)\eta^{-1}e^{-(1-x)/\eta}.
\]

It gives no atom at zero, logarithmic integrability, and an absolutely continuous logarithmic law. Strict log-convexity, `h(0)=1`, `h(1)<1`, and `h(2)>1` prove exactly one positive root `α∈(1,2)`, with `h(p)<1` for `0<p<α` and `0<E[ξ^α log ξ]<∞`. These quantities do not depend on the teacher.

The remainder identity is also exact. It gives

\[
\sup_x|\Psi(x)-Mx|
\le B=\frac\eta2\sum_i |A_i|
 [\delta+(1+\delta)(\delta+|Y_i|)].
\]

Both this envelope and the Lipschitz envelope have all positive moments under a common polynomial bound in the Gaussian inputs and noises. Since `|M|≤Lip(Ψ)≤L`, the actual logarithmic Lipschitz constant has a finite negative expectation, including a finite negative part.

## 3. Stationarity, conditional variance, and compact minorization: PASS

The first-moment Wasserstein contraction is legitimate: `E|Ψ(0)|<∞`, and a coupling using a common fresh batch contracts expected distance by at most `q₂`. For uniqueness without a moment restriction, `E∏_{j≤n}L_j≤q₂ⁿ` and Borel–Cantelli imply that the product tends to zero almost surely. Multiplication by any almost surely finite starting value preserves convergence to zero. Bounded Lipschitz test functions then identify any invariant probability with the constructed first-moment law. The subcritical moment drift uses `h(p)<1` and does not require independence of `M` and `B`.

Conditional on the two inputs, the noise coefficients are `ηa_iσ'(a_ix)/2`. Independence of the two fresh standard-normal noises therefore gives exactly

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Psi(x)\mid A_1=a_1,A_2=a_2)
=\frac{\eta^2}{4}\sum_{i=1}^2a_i^2\sigma'(a_ix)^2.
\]

There is no cross term, and neither a factor `η²/2` multiplying the sum nor a variance formed by squaring the sum would be correct. The conditional mean in the proof retains both input-dependent teacher signals.

For `a₁,a₂∈[1,2]`, the bounds `2≤a₁²+a₂²≤8` and `1≤σ'≤1+δ` yield

\[
s_{\min}=\frac{11}{10\sqrt2},\qquad
s_{\max}=\frac{6\sqrt2}{5}(1+\delta),\qquad
|\mu|\le R+\frac{24}{5}(1+\delta)^2(R+1).
\]

Thus the Gaussian density lower bound in (17) is valid uniformly on each specified pair of compact sets. Its multiplicative input probability is `P(1≤A≤2)²`, as required by independence of the two inputs. The resulting transition minorization proves full support of the invariant law and the stated compact-to-annulus launch probability. All constants may be extremely small, which does not affect positivity.

## 4. Batch-two renewal and strict positivity: PASS

**Primary source checked directly:** Jelenković–Olvera-Cravioto, [arXiv:1006.3295v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1006.3295v5), Theorem 3.1 and its preceding paragraph, printed p. 5 / PDF page 5, especially condition (5) and the formula for `H`. This is the general discrepancy theorem: it does not require the proposed stationary variable to solve an affine recursion.

For `N=1`, take `R=|X|` independent of `C₁=ξ`. The positive nonarithmetic coefficient, root, finite positive tilted logarithmic mean, coefficient moment with `γ=0`, and all stationary moments below `α` have been verified. Set `U=|Ψ(X)|`, `V=ξ|X|`. Stationarity identifies the law of `U`, while `|U−V|≤B` gives

\[
E|U^\alpha-V^\alpha|
\le\alpha E\{B^\alpha+B\xi^{\alpha-1}|X|^{\alpha-1}\}<\infty.
\]

The only factorization is between the independent stationary state and the fresh complete batch. The mixed batch moment `E[Bξ^{α−1}]` remains joint. Tonelli gives the absolute weighted discrepancy condition; signed Fubini gives the claimed coupled-difference constant. Its two terms must not be integrated separately.

I checked the separate positivity proof as follows.

1. The recursion `|X_n|≥ξ_n|X_{n−1}|−B_n` iterates to `|X_n|≥e^{S_n}(|X₀|−V_n)`. Tilting the complete batch by `ξ^α` preserves independence between different batches. It generally changes the joint law inside a batch; the argument never factors that joint law.
2. The stopped likelihood identity follows by summing finite-time likelihood identities on `{τ=n}`. It requires no infinite-path absolute continuity, no uniform integrability of likelihood ratios, and no almost sure crossing under the original measure.
3. For `β=1/2`, the tilted contraction is `r=h(α−β)<1`. The nonnegative exponential supermartingale yields the stated positive survival bound. Unbounded multiplier support gives `Q(Z>1)>0`.
4. The translated occupation bound is sound: after the first entry into `(a,a+1]`, any later visit entails a fresh displacement at most `1`. Summing `Q(S_n≤1)≤e^βr^n` bounds the expected occupation by `e^β/(1−r)`, independently of `a`. Partitioning the pre-crossing half-line then gives the uniform overshoot bound (23).
5. The discounted-remainder estimate correctly uses the joint quantity `E_P[ξ^{α−β}B^β]`. Its exponent on `ξ` is positive, so no singular inverse-multiplier moment is hidden. The common Gaussian polynomial envelope makes it finite.
6. The event `F_u` is measurable at `τ_u`. The stronger infinite survival and bounded-`V_∞` events are used only to lower-bound its tilted probability. On `F_u`, all preceding `V_j≤V_{τ_u}≤K/2`; therefore the whole path avoids `C=[−K,K]` before reaching the large-tail set. The stopped change of measure gives (25) uniformly over the initial annulus.
7. For the stationary transfer, define at a fixed horizon `N` the events that `N−j` is the last visit to `C` before the endpoint in `D`, for `1≤j≤N`. These events are disjoint. Stationarity makes their probabilities exactly the terms of (26)'s finite sum. Thus the inequality is correct, and monotone convergence yields the occupation integral. There is no omitted return-time normalization.
8. The annulus `J={2K≤|y|≤4K}` has Lebesgue measure `4K`. The compact minorization launches it with probability at least `4Kε_(K,4K)>0`; full support supplies `π(C)>0`. The displayed strictly positive `t^(−α)` lower bound follows.

The existing nonnegative tail limit and this lower bound imply a finite strictly positive radial constant. Tail integration gives the exact moment threshold, including divergence at `p=α`; a fixed translation preserves the constant and threshold.

## 5. Signed-tail primary call and specialization: PASS

**Primary source read directly:** Alsmeyer–Brofferio–Buraczewski, [arXiv:2102.02299v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.02299v2), dated 6 October 2021. Relevant locators are standing assumptions (1), (4), printed pp. 2–3; sign matrix (8), p. 5; Perron normalization and (9), p. 6; Theorem 2.1, pp. 6–7, conditions (12)–(14) and conclusion (16); Section 4.3, pp. 22–23; Proposition 8.1, p. 36. Printed and PDF page numbers coincide.

In the irreducible case, Theorem 2.1 supplies finite nonnegative one-sided limits under its root, moment, and phase-offset conditions, with `u_+C_−=u_−C_+`. Proposition 8.1 makes both constants positive for an unbounded-support stationary measure. No additional independence between slopes and the remainder is required. The model also has a stochastic `P(0)` and strict intermediate-moment contraction, as used in that proposition's proof.

The complete model-specific checks are:

| Obligation | Independent verification |
|---|---|

| Moments and zero slopes | The density `exp(−(1−m)/(2η))/sqrt(2πη(1−m))`, `m<1`, is the correct change of variables from `A²`. It gives `P(M=0)=0`, both signs positive, and `E[|M|^α|log|M||]<∞`. The Gaussian polynomial bound gives `EB^α<∞`. |
| Perron root | `P(s)=[[d_s,o_s],[o_s,d_s]]`, with `d_s,o_s>0`. Its eigenvalues are `d_s+o_s` and `d_s−o_s`; the first is strictly dominant and equals `E|M|^s`. Thus `ρ(α)=1`. |

The Perron relation now gives `C_−=C_+`. Summing the two disjoint tail events identifies their sum with the source's radial constant, so each equals `C_(η,θ)/2∈(0,∞)`. This also independently checks positivity using the established radial constant. The formula with denominator `2αmα` follows, retaining the expectation of one coupled integrable difference. The fixed translation `X−θ` preserves each one-sided constant.

The nonsymmetry claim remains consistent and valid. If a fixed nonzero-teacher invariant law were symmetric about zero, finite-first-moment stationarity would give

\[
0=E[A\sigma'(AX)\sigma(AX)]
 -E[A\sigma'(AX)\sigma(A\theta)].
\]

The first term would vanish by oddness in `X`, whereas the second has the strict sign of `θ`. This contradiction does not conflict with equality of the asymptotic tails. The Perron sign distribution is not the sign distribution of the nonlinear stationary state.
