## A14: independent checks

### Smoothness with the moving cusp

The substitution `y=η A²` gives the correct density factor

```text
h(η,p) = (2π)^(-1/2) ∫_0^∞ |1-y|^p y^(-1/2) η^(-1/2) exp(-y/(2η)) dy.
```

On any compact rectangle with `η∈[a,b]⊂(0,∞)` and `p∈[p0,p1]⊂(-1,∞)`, every mixed derivative has an integrable envelope. At zero the only necessary singular factor is `y^(-1/2)`; at one it is `|1-y|^p0` times a finite logarithmic power; at infinity exponential decay dominates all the powers introduced by differentiation. Consequently `h∈C∞({η>0,p>-1})` is correct. The proof does not incorrectly differentiate the original cusp to arbitrarily high order.

Differentiating the rescaled density gives the correct score identity

```text
∂η h = [E(A² ξ_η^p) - h]/(2η).
```

It remains integrable for every `p>-1`. The original-variable first derivative for `p>1` has the stated minus sign and a Gaussian-integrable envelope. Both formulas agree. The displayed logarithmic moment formulas and strictly positive `∂pp h` are justified by the same domination.

### Root, derivative sign, and quantitative bounds

The bounds `h(η,1)<0.99` on `J=(0.98,1.02)` and `h(η,2)>1` there yield the unique positive root in `(1,2)`. Strict convexity in `p` gives

```text
m_η > [1-h(η,1)]/(α-1) > 0,
```

and gives `m_η>1/50` on `I`. For a fixed root exponent `p=α>1`, the function `g(u)=E|1-uA²|^p` is strictly convex and differentiable, including at zero. Since `g(0)=g(η)=1`, its strict tangent inequality at `η` forces `g'(η)>0`. Thus the implicit-function derivative is strictly negative. This argument uses convexity of the expectation, not a false pointwise monotonicity claim about the multiplier.

The uniform lower bound for `N_η` is valid: the half-step quadratic moment is at most `30203/40000<49/64`; the `L^p` versus `L²` inequality gives `g(η/2)<7/8`; the secant slope then gives `N_η>1/(4η)≥25/101`. Discarding the negative derivative contribution on `ηA²<1` and bounding the remainder by Gaussian fourth moments gives `N_η<6η≤303/50`. Discarding the negative logarithmic contribution on `ξ≤1` and using the sixth Gaussian moment gives `m_η<15η³<16`. Hence


and its integrated strict inequality are correct, including the closed-interval endpoints through the smooth extension on `J`.

## A15: independent checks

### Finite times, lower moments, and entire-sequence divergence

The new state is independent of the fresh multiplier, but the multiplier and remainder envelope can be dependent. The subadditivity and Minkowski estimates require exactly this independence and no more. The all-orders moments of the envelope therefore give finite moments of every positive order at every finite time, including time zero.

For each subcritical `r`, the drift factor can be made strictly less than one. Choosing `p<r<α` gives a uniform `r`-moment and uniform integrability of `|X_n|^p`, which correctly strengthens weak convergence to convergence of the `p`-th moment. For `p≥α`, bounded continuous truncations and the infinite stationary moment force `liminf m_p(n)=∞`. Thus the conclusion concerns the entire sequence; it does not assume monotone moments or exchange an unbounded function with a weak limit.

### Exact supercritical normalization

Writing `u_n=||X_n||_p`, fresh-step independence and reverse Minkowski give

```text
|u_(n+1) - a u_n| ≤ b,       a=h(p)^(1/p)>1.
```

The normalized increments are bounded by the summable sequence `b a^(-n-1)`. More strongly, summing from `n` to infinity gives precisely

```text
|u_n - ℓ a^n| ≤ b/(a-1).
```

This is valid for every `n≥0`, even though the actual remainder depends on the state and on the new step. If `ℓ=0`, the norms would be uniformly bounded, so the already proved divergence forces `ℓ>0`. Raising the normalized norm to the `p`-th power proves the exact positive limit. Both iterated one-sided estimates in the supplement also have the correct signs. The stated `p=2` error bound follows by squaring the bounded norm error. The formula and upper bound for `||B||_2` are correct.

### Direct nonlinear second-moment forcing and all-time claims

The decomposition

```text
Ψ(x) = (1-ηA²)x - ηAd[zu+(1+du)t] + ηA(1+du)E
```

is exact. Conditional on `A,x`, the final term has mean zero and variance `η²A²(1+du)²`. Bounding the cross term by its absolute value, dropping the nonnegative squared perturbation, and using `1+du≥1` gives the claimed lower drift

```text
E Ψ(x)² ≥ h_2 x² - c_η|x| + η².
```

Cauchy–Schwarz gives `c_η<0.006` without requiring independence of `M` and `A`. At zero, the correct absolute first moment is `η(1+d)2/π<13/20`. Iterating contraction yields the stated first-moment bound and, after the defined finite time `N`, the strict bound `E|X_n|<97`. Hence the additive forcing exceeds `0.3981`, and retaining `0.39` is safe.

Iteration proves the displayed bound for every integer `k≥0`. Its replacement of `h_2` by `1.9603` is valid because `(h_2^k-1)/(h_2-1)=Σ_{j=0}^{k-1}h_2^j` is increasing in `h_2` for integer `k`; the `k=0` case is zero and also holds. `N` depends on `x_0` and the fixed universal `q`, not on the stepsize. The positive lower bound for `K_2` follows after division by `h_2^(N+k)` and passage to the established norm-lemma limit.

For the independent invariant-law argument, contraction first gives `E_π|X|<47`. If its second moment were finite, integrating the lower drift would give `v_π>h_2 v_π+0.6981`, impossible for `v_π≥0` and `h_2>1`. This proof uses no tail theorem.

### Critical slope and mean/variance statements

The actual one-step difference defining `g` is absolutely integrable, and the asserted estimate

```text
|g(x)| ≤ C0 + C1 |x|^(α-1)
```

follows from the mean-value inequality and subadditivity at exponent `α-1∈(0,1)`. Compact domination proves continuity. Any `r∈(α-1,α)` supplies uniform control of the removed tails, with the displayed orders `T^(-r)` and `T^(α-1-r)`. Therefore weak convergence legitimately implies `E g(X_n)→∫g dπ`.

Absolute integrability also justifies identifying the latter integral with the base formula's numerator. The resulting constant is exactly `α μ_α C_η`, with no missing factor of two. At each finite time the identity `m_α(n+1)-m_α(n)=E g(X_n)` subtracts finite quantities. Cesàro averaging gives the claimed slope without subtracting two infinite stationary moments.

The synchronous coupling proves the stated geometric `W1` estimate. Symmetry plus the independently finite stationary first moment gives `E_π X=0`; thus the nonstationary mean decays at the stated geometric rate and subtracting its square leaves the same `K_2 h_2^n` variance asymptotic. Finally, finite-time finite second moments and the stationary infinite second moment make every quadratic coupling cost infinite, exactly as argued.
