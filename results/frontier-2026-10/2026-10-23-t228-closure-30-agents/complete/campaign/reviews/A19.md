## Finding and precise correction

The original Section 1 said kernel continuity was “necessary for a continuous raw-input architecture.” That is too broad if read as a necessity claim for every individual kernel and parameter regime. For example, with `n=0`, the discontinuous rank-one kernel `k(x,y)=phi(x)phi(y)` on `[-1,1]`, where `phi=-1` on the negative half and `phi=1` otherwise, has constant diagonal one. Its noisy predictive is the constant `N(0,1+s)`, which a continuous predictor can reproduce exactly.

C003 correctly establishes that boundedness alone cannot support a theorem over *all* bounded kernels. It does not establish continuity as an individual necessary condition in every regime. This was a scope wording issue, not a defect in the continuous-kernel theorem.

The parent has resolved it by stating that boundedness alone is insufficient for a theorem covering all kernels, and explicitly declining a universal individual-kernel necessity claim. No further correction is required for this review's PASS.

## Mathematical audit

### 1. Projection and finite prior preprocessing — PASS

The canonical Hilbert-space construction works for an arbitrary real symmetric PSD kernel. Quotienting zero-norm combinations and completing is legitimate, and the norm identity implies continuity of `Phi` from continuity of `k`. Compactness supplies uniform continuity and finite nets in the ambient L1 metric. No sample-path continuity, Mercer measure, eigenfunction expansion, or design distribution is needed.

For the span of net sections, orthogonal projection gives a PSD residual kernel. The best-approximation property yields `||(I-P0)Phi(x)|| <= Omega(r0)` uniformly, and Hilbert-space Cauchy–Schwarz gives the off-diagonal residual bound `|r(x,y)| <= Omega(r0)^2`. Thus the first half of the entrywise kernel-error budget is valid.

The positive-eigenspace formula `Lambda^(-1/2) U^T k(G0,x)` correctly represents the projection coordinates, including singular `K00`: every null vector of `K00` represents the zero Hilbert vector and is orthogonal to every `Phi(x)`. The formula is used only at the finitely many fixed encoder centers. No runtime inverse, kernel evaluation, or free feature oracle is hidden in the input encoding.

C005's finite-rank feature reconstruction is also valid: equal finite Gram matrices induce an isometry on the sampled spans, which extends to an orthogonal transformation of the padded `R^p`. The unsampled global coordinates need only exist for the proof; they are not assumed available to the network.

### 2. General continuous feature encoder — PASS

For each coordinate, the two-case lower bound on the cone envelope is correct. At distance at most `r1`, the modulus bounds the sample value below by `z_j(x)-e/2`; farther away, the cone slope dominates the full `2R` coordinate oscillation. The h-net supplies an upper bound `z_j(x)+e`, because `h <= r1/2` and `Acone h=e/2`. Therefore the Euclidean feature error is at most `sqrt(p)e=eta` and the encoded norm is at most `S=R+1`.

The resulting kernel is PSD by construction, and

`|k0-ktilde| <= (2R+eta)eta <= (2R+1)eta = theta/2`.

Adding the projected residual gives the advertised total entrywise error `theta`. It is important, and correctly enforced here, that the approximate kernel is PSD rather than just an arbitrary entrywise approximation.

All encoder operations are genuine residual ReLU MLP operations. Absolute values use `2Jd` hidden units; the cone-writing layer uses `Jd+1`; each signed minimum tournament level uses at most `p floor(J/2)`; role routing uses `4p`. The tournament handles odd J and J=1. The routing identity is valid at both boundaries `f=+S` and `f=-S`. Padding raw coordinates are a fixed point of X, so the feature bound used by the gate also holds on padding. No assumption that the ambient origin belongs to X is needed.

### 3. Posterior perturbation, conditioning, and KL — PASS

The regularized context covariance matrices have inverse norm at most `1/s`, including repeated observations and singular unregularized Gram matrices. Entrywise kernel error `a` implies operator error at most `ta`, query-vector error at most `sqrt(t)a`, and inverse error at most `ta/s^2`.

The resulting bounds are exactly

`|m-mtilde| <= (tB/s)(1+tS^2/s)a`

and

`|v-vtilde| <= (1+2tS^2/s+t^2 S^4/s^2)a`.

Replacing t by n yields the displayed `Cm` and `Cv`. The mean comparison includes both the query-vector and inverse changes; the variance comparison includes both query factors, the inverse, and the query diagonal. There is no missing factor of t or noise variance.

The Schur-complement argument proves each exact noisy predictive variance is at least s. It also yields the upper bound `s+S^2`. Empty contexts are correctly handled by the stated empty-vector convention. The theta budget and `eps0=min(eps/4,1/16)` allocate half the scalar mean and variance error to each stage. The denominator `1+Cm` avoids the n=0 or B=0 degeneracy.

After adding scalar errors, the Gaussian identity with `h=(v-vhat)/vhat` and `|h|<=1/2` gives the claimed forward-KL bound. The inequality `h-log(1+h)<=h^2` is valid over the whole stated interval, including negative h. No KL triangle inequality or conversion from an averaged loss to a uniform guarantee is used.

### 4. Exact solver, positivity, and full size — PASS

The seven p-registers plus four solver scalars in C002 are sufficient. The broadcast sets the query feature identically on all tokens. The first two solver heads implement the signed residual `sum z_i(y_i-z_i^T w)`; the next pair implements `-sum z_i z_i^T u`; the fifth head implements ridge, forcing, momentum, and previous-iterate updates. Its lack of an N factor is correct because the working registers are identical across tokens. The readout also correctly cancels its normalized sum using identical iterate registers.

The recurrence starts at `x_0=x_{-1}=0`, has first residual `1-lambda/c`, and satisfies the stated Chebyshev residual identity. All denominators are positive. The use of `max(1,n)` makes the enclosing spectral interval nondegenerate even at n=0. The stated T bounds the residual by delta. Since `delta<=1/2`, the polynomial approximate inverse is positive on the entire spectral interval, so the network's variance itself is at least s.

The count checks are:

| Token width | `D=7p+6+d+Jd+pJ` |
| Maximum MLP hidden width | `H=2Jd+pJ+4p+1` |

The count includes all nonlinear raw-feature computation. Its stated exclusion of fixed affine input placement is explicit and consistent. The model uses bidirectional normalized ReLU attention, not softmax attention or a causal mask. The attention and residual-MLP conventions match Definitions 1–3 of Bai et al.; the primary NeurIPS paper was checked directly: [Transformers as Statisticians, Section 2.1](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/b2e63e36c57e153b9015fece2352a9f9-Paper-Conference.pdf). This check concerns the architecture definitions, not novelty.

### 5. RBF truncation and explicit encoder — PASS

The centered Gaussian Taylor features have the correct normalization. The total-degree feature count is `binom(d+r-1,d)`. The omitted feature coordinates form a PSD residual kernel. With `t=||u||^2<=U`, the diagonal tail satisfies

`R^2 exp(-t) sum_{j>=r} t^j/j! <= R^2 t^r/r! <= R^2 U^r/r!`.

The factorial inequality used to cancel the exponential is correct. The choice `r>=euler^2 U` gives `(euler U/r)^r<=exp(-r)`, while `r>=log(2R^2/theta)` makes the diagonal residual at most `theta/2`. The max with 1 handles small or negative logarithmic thresholds. PSD gives the same off-diagonal absolute bound.

The full feature distance is at most `(R/ell)||x-y||_2`; coordinate truncation cannot enlarge it. Thus `K=R/ell` is a valid coordinatewise L1 Lipschitz constant, and the grid size in the corollary gives the required total feature error. The specialization removes the unspecified kernel modulus and covering-number choices. The stated polynomial dependence of p on `log(1/theta)` is correctly qualified by fixed d, ell, and R.

### 6. Edge cases and bounded-output interface — PASS

The proof covers R=0 by a constant predictor and avoids division by R in that case. The main nonzero branch also permits the zero kernel to have a loose positive bound R. The degenerate sampled positive eigenspace is harmless. n=0, B=0, singleton/disconnected compact X, repeated observations, and J=1 cause no missing operation or invalid denominator.

The target mean bound `B sqrt(nR^2/s)` follows from Cauchy–Schwarz in the inverse covariance metric and the Schur complement. Projection of the two output coordinates to intervals containing their targets preserves the required scalar error bounds. The one-layer, four-unit clipping implementation is correct. The note explicitly avoids the false claim that variance clipping always decreases total Gaussian KL. The unbounded-label/ERM statement is correctly left as a separate obligation.

## Mandatory scope for preservation
