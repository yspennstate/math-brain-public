## 2. Actual nonlinear map and contraction

I independently differentiated the update. With `z=Ax`, `u=sech^2(z)`, and `t=tanh(z)`, its derivative is

`1-eta A^2 [(1+delta u)^2-2 delta u t (z+delta t-Y)]`.

The bounds `|t|<=1`, `0<=u<=1`, and `|z|u<=1` give exactly the stated global derivative envelope. This controls the actual random maps uniformly over all states, not just their linear behavior at infinity. The gradient expansion also gives the stated state-independent remainder bound in `Psi(x)=Mx+R(x)`, with `M=1-eta A^2`.

The teacher-dependent label cannot be treated as independent of the input. The manuscript correctly retains

## 3. Exponent, invariance, and all subcritical moments

The two simple zeros of `1-eta A^2` give local logarithmic integrability; Gaussian decay controls the other tails. The law of `log|1-eta A^2|` is absolutely continuous, hence nonarithmetic. Strict log-convexity, `h(0)=1`, `h(1)<0.98`, and `h(2)>=1.9603` prove the unique positive root lies strictly between one and two. They also prove `h(p)<1` for every `0<p<alpha`. Differentiation at the root is justified by the stated logarithmic integrability, and the secant from one to alpha gives a strictly positive finite `m_alpha`.

The Wasserstein contraction acts on the complete space of probability laws with finite first moment because `E|Psi(0)|<infinity`. This yields existence, first-moment finiteness, and the stated geometric convergence rate. Uniqueness is not restricted to that space: the common-noise distance is bounded by `|Z|` times the product of iid Lipschitz envelopes; its product tends to zero almost surely by summable Markov bounds and Borel--Cantelli. Every real-valued initial `Z` is finite almost surely, even without a moment. Bounded Lipschitz test functions therefore identify any invariant probability with the constructed law.

The subcritical drift argument is not circular. For `p<=1`, subadditivity uses `h(p)<1`; for `1<p<alpha`, a sufficiently small epsilon preserves a drift coefficient below one. Starting at zero gives uniformly bounded pth moments, and weak convergence plus lower semicontinuity transfers them to the stationary law before renewal theory is invoked.

The conditional Gaussian minorization is valid for a nonzero teacher: conditioning on `A=a` leaves the noise standard normal, with the stated teacher-dependent conditional mean. Restriction to `a in [1,2]` gives the displayed uniform mean and variance bounds and a positive density lower bound on every compact. Thus the transition and stationary laws have full support. No independence of label and input is silently inserted here.

## 4. Primary renewal theorem and coupled difference

The manuscript supplies all these, including the auxiliary moment at gamma zero. The scaled-limit formulation includes a zero constant; the proof's smoothed limit and monotonicity give that interpretation. Strict positivity is proved separately.

For the actual application, `R0=|X|` is independent of the fresh complete step and hence of `xi`. Stationarity makes `U=|Psi(X)|` have law `R0`; `W=xi|X|` is the comparison variable. The pathwise bound `|U-W|<=B` implies

`|U^alpha-W^alpha| <= alpha [B^alpha+B xi^(alpha-1)|X|^(alpha-1)]`.

This is integrable using the already established `(alpha-1)` moment of `X` and the joint step moment `E[B xi^(alpha-1)]`. Indicator integration gives the weighted absolute discrepancy bound; signed Fubini then gives the claimed numerator divided by `alpha m_alpha`. There is no subtraction of two separately finite alpha moments, and no unjustified factorization of the dependent step variables.

## 5. Strict positivity: independent reconstruction

I checked the full chain rather than assuming positivity from unbounded support.

1. The reverse triangle inequality telescopes to `|X_n|>=exp(S_n)(|X_0|-V_n)`, with the remainder discounted through the current multiplier. The `xi=0` null events cause no pathwise issue after taking the countable intersection of their complements.
2. Tilting the complete iid steps by `xi^alpha` produces a probability law. The likelihood identity at any finite stopped event follows by summing deterministic-time identities over `{tau=n}`. It does not require infinite-path equivalence or uniform integrability.
3. Under the tilt, the walk has finite absolute increment mean and positive drift. For beta one half, `r=E_Q exp(-beta Z)=h(alpha-beta)<1`. Stopping the nonnegative exponential supermartingale at the first descent below minus one proves a ruin probability at most `exp(-beta)`. A first increment above one followed by independent survival gives the stated positive `p0`.
4. Chernoff bounds give finite occupation of `(-infinity,1]`. After first entrance into any interval of length one, each later visit requires a fresh displacement at most one. Strong Markov conditioning therefore gives a uniform translated occupation bound. Partitioning the pre-crossing position into these intervals bounds the overshoot by `K_U` times a tail sum, which tends uniformly to zero because the tilted positive increment is integrable.
5. For the remainder, independence is used only between a complete step and earlier steps. Subadditivity gives `E_Q V_infinity^beta <= E_P[xi^(alpha-beta)B^beta]/(1-h(alpha-beta))`. Since `alpha-beta>0`, there is no inverse-multiplier singularity near a zero, and polynomial Gaussian envelopes make the joint numerator finite.
6. The choices of overshoot cutoff and starting radius precede the tail threshold. The event using the partial remainder at the crossing is stopped-measurable. Positive survival minus the two exceptional events leaves tilted probability at least `p0/2`. Starting in the annulus, this event avoids the central interval until it exceeds the tail threshold. The stopped likelihood lower bound yields the uniform order `t^(-alpha)` excursion probability under the original law.
7. The last-exit transfer is correct with its unnormalized stationary measure. At a finite horizon, events classified by their last visit to the center are disjoint; stationarity converts their sum to the stated integral. Monotone convergence then bounds the probability of reaching the distant set before return. There is no missing expected-return-time factor.
8. Compact minorization supplies a positive one-step probability from the center into the annulus; full support gives positive stationary center mass. The previous two steps therefore give a positive lower bound for `t^alpha pi(|X|>t)` uniformly for all sufficiently large thresholds. Combining with the independently established finite limit proves `C>0`.
