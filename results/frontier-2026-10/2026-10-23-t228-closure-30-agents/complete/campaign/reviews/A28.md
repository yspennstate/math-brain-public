## 2. Model and literal parameter domain

Fix the deterministic teacher `θ` and step size `η` in the stated rectangle.
Let `δ=1/1000`, `σ(z)=z+δ tanh(z)`, and let `(A,E)` be independent standard
normal variables. Define

```text
Y = σ(Aθ)+E,
Ψ(x) = x−ηAσ'(Ax)[σ(Ax)−Y],
M = 1−ηA²,   ξ=|M|,   N=2(1+A²+E²).
```

To distinguish the deterministic teacher from Mirek's random map parameter,
write the latter as `w=(a,e)`. One may take the metric parameter space to be

```text
Θη = (R \ {0, η^(−1/2), −η^(−1/2)}) × R,
```

with the restricted Gaussian product law, which still has total mass one.
This deletes only a null event and leaves the Markov kernel unchanged. It
makes `M(w)≠0` for every parameter in the chosen space; removing `a=0` is
optional. Thus the theorem's pointwise requirements cause no exceptional-set
problem. The maps and coefficient functions are jointly continuous, hence
measurable, on this parameter space. Fresh parameters are iid.

The state space remains all of `R`; the parameter-space deletion does not
restrict possible states. Section 6 below proves `supp π=R`, so the
support-dependent parts of (H1-H2) are checked on their entire required
domain.

## 4. Actual Lipschitz constant, bounded error and dilation

Put `z=Ax`, `u=sech²(z)`, `t=tanh(z)`. Differentiation gives

```text
Ψ'(x)=1−ηA²H(z,Y),
H(z,Y)=(1+δu)²−2δut(z+δt−Y).
```

Using `|t|≤1`, `u≤1` and `|z|u≤1` gives

```text
|H−1|≤4δ+3δ²+2δ|Y|,
K≤L:=ξ+ηA²(4δ+3δ²+2δ|Y|).
```

The derivative converges to `M` as `|x|→∞`. Since a differentiable globally
Lipschitz function has `|Ψ'(x)|≤K`, this also proves `K≥ξ`. Measurability
of `K` follows, for example, by taking its defining supremum over pairs of
distinct rational states, using continuity. On `Θη`, `0<ξ≤K<∞`.

The label bound retains the dependence on `A`:

```text
|Y|≤(1+δ)|A|+|E|,
E[A²|Y|]≤(1+δ)E|A|³+E[A²]E|E|
          =(3+2δ)√(2/π)<3+2δ.
```

The exact identity `E|1−A²|=4/√(2πe)<0.97`, together with
`|1−ηA²|≤|1−A²|+|η−1|A²`, gives `Eξ<0.98`. The strict numerical
inequality can be certified using `π>157/50`, `e>1359/500`, as in the
pinned proof. Consequently

```text
E K≤E L
 < 98/100+(101/100)[4/1000+3/10⁶+(2/1000)(1501/500)]
 = 99010707/100000000 < 1.
```

All positive moments of `L` are finite, by its polynomial Gaussian bound.
The logarithmic checks needed for the original standing assumption are

```text
log⁺K≤K≤L,
log⁻K≤log⁻ξ,
E|log K|<∞,
E log K≤log E K<log(99010707/100000000)<0.
```

Finiteness of `E log⁻ξ` is verified in Section 5. Also,
`Ψ(0)=η(1+δ)AY` has all positive moments, so
`E log⁺|Ψ(0)|<∞`. These checks apply to the actual Lipschitz constant, not
merely to the asymptotic multiplier or to an arbitrary upper bound.
They give a unique stationary law by the standing contraction result.
Equivalently, the direct Wasserstein construction in Section 4 of the
pinned model proof gives the same law.

For the remainder, the identity

```text
σ'(z)[σ(z)−Y] = z+δzu+(1+δu)(δt−Y)
```

gives

```text
sup_x |Ψ(x)−Mx|≤B:=η|A|[δ+(1+δ)(δ+|Y|)]
 ≤η[(2δ+δ²)|A|+(1+δ)²A²+(1+δ)|AE|].
```

Using `|A|≤(1+A²)/2`, `|AE|≤(A²+E²)/2`, and `η≤101/100` proves the
following exact coefficient bound:

```text
B ≤ 0.001010505 + 1.518536515 A² + 0.505505 E²
  ≤ 2(1+A²+E²)=N.
```

The three coefficients are respectively
`202101/200000000`, `303707303/200000000`, and `101101/200000`.
They were independently recomputed using rational arithmetic.
In particular, A17's largest coefficient `1.518536515` is correct.

For every `t>0`,

```text
tΨ(x/t)=Mx+tR(x/t),    sup_x |R(x)|≤N,
sup_(x∈R)|tΨ(x/t)−Mx|≤tN→0.
```

Thus the limiting map is exactly `x↦Mx`; convergence is even uniform over
`R`. This verifies both the dilation statement and its equality to the
similarity on `supp π`.

## 5. Similarity group, logarithms, exponent and multiplier moments

The multiplier has density

```text
f_M(m) = exp[−(1−m)/(2η)] / sqrt[2πη(1−m)],   m<1,
```

and zero density for `m>1`. This follows from the `χ²₁` density by the
change of variables `m=1−ηa²`. In particular `P(M=0)=0`.

Use `G=R*`, identified with the one-dimensional similarity group
`R₊*×O(1)`, where `O(1)={−1,1}` is closed. The support **relative to
this group**, which excludes zero, is `(-∞,0)∪(0,1]`. Every negative
number is already in that support, and every `r>0` is the product
`(-r)(-1)` of two support elements. Hence the generated semigroup itself
is `G`; taking its closure introduces no restriction. Both signs and every
positive dilation are present.

Near `m=0`, `f_M` is bounded, so `∫_0^ε|log r|dr<∞` handles the only
logarithmic singularity of `log|M|`. The density's square-root singularity
at `m=1` is integrable and `log|m|` is bounded there. At negative infinity,
the density decays exponentially in `|m|`. Therefore

```text
E|log ξ|<∞,
E[ξ^p|log ξ|]<∞ and Eξ^p<∞ for every p>0,
s∞=∞.
```

The change of variables `z=log|m|` gives an absolutely continuous law for
`log ξ`, with density `e^z[f_M(e^z)+f_M(−e^z)]` wherever defined. An
absolutely continuous probability cannot be concentrated on a lattice,
which verifies the conditional nonarithmetic condition as `P(M≠0)=1`.

Let `h(p)=Eξ^p`. Its logarithm is strictly convex because `ξ` is
nonconstant. The checked values are

```text
h(0)=1,    h(1)<0.98<1,
h(2)=1−2η+3η²≥19603/10000>1.
```

These, continuity and strict log-convexity give a unique positive root
`α∈(1,2)`, and `h(p)<1` for `0<p<α`. The positive mass on `ξ>1`
also gives `h(p)→∞`. Differentiation under the expectation is justified
by the logarithmic moment bounds. At the root,

```text
0<mα:=E[ξ^α log ξ]<∞.
```

Indeed the derivative of `log h` at `α` exceeds its strictly positive
secant slope from `p=1` to `p=α`. Jensen also gives `E log ξ<0`.
No multiplier moment or logarithmic condition is left unchecked.

## 6. Supporting domain and unbounded stationary support

For every fixed state `x`, condition on `A=a≠0`. The next state is
Gaussian with mean

```text
x−ηaσ'(ax)[σ(ax)−σ(aθ)]
```

and standard deviation `η|a|σ'(ax)>0`. Its conditional density is positive
on all of `R`. Thus every nonempty open interval `I` has `P(x,I)>0`
for every `x`. Invariance yields

```text
π(I)=∫ P(x,I) π(dx)>0.
```

Hence `supp π=R`, in particular it is unbounded. This reasoning uses only
independence of the fresh `E` from `A` and the state. It does not require
independence of `A` and `Y` or symmetry of the stationary law.

## 7. Rigorous moment-growth check, including a bounded supremum

Let `W=A²+E²~χ²₂`. For real `s>0`, direct Gaussian integration gives

```text
E(A²)^s = 2^s Γ(s+1/2)/Γ(1/2),
E W^s   = 2^s Γ(s+1).
```

Both are finite. Since `N=2(1+W)`, all positive moments of `N` are finite
as well, proving (H7) at the required `α` and providing all higher moments
used below.

For fixed `a>0`, the logarithmic form of Stirling's formula gives

```text
log Γ(s+a)
 = s log s−s+(a−1/2)log s+(1/2)log(2π)+O(1/s),
[Γ(s+a)/Γ(a)]^(1/s) = (s/e)[1+o(1)].
```

This applies for real `s→∞`, not only for integer moments. It yields

```text
||A²||_s ~ 2s/e,        ||W||_s ~ 2s/e.
```

For `s≥1`, the triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities in `L^s` give

```text
| ||1+W||_s − ||W||_s | ≤ 1,
| ||1−ηA²||_s − η||A²||_s | ≤ 1.
```

Consequently

```text
||N||_s ~ 4s/e,    ||M||_s ~ 2ηs/e,
lim_(s→∞) (E N^s / E|M|^s)^(1/s)
 = lim_(s→∞) ||N||_s/||M||_s
 = 2/η < ∞.                                      (A28.1)
```

This is an actual finite limit, exactly as required in (1.12). The limit
lies between `200/101` and `200/99` over the parameter rectangle. The
finite-endpoint alternative (1.11) is inapplicable because `s∞=∞`.

The stronger supremum statement also holds for each fixed `η`:

```text
sup_(s>0) (E N^s / E|M|^s)^(1/s) < ∞.             (A28.2)
```

To see this without mistaking a pointwise finite ratio for a bounded one,
the ratio is continuous and positive on every compact subinterval of
`(0,∞)`, by dominated convergence and the finite higher moments. Its
large-`s` behavior is (A28.1). At the remaining endpoint,

```text
lim_(s↓0) (E N^s / Eξ^s)^(1/s)
 = exp(E log N−E log ξ) < ∞.
```

For example, differentiability at zero follows from the bound
`|(v^s−1)/s|≤|log v|(1+v^ε)` for `0<s≤ε`, applied to positive `v=N,ξ`.
The dominating expectations are finite by Sections 5 and 7. Compactness
now proves (A28.2). This stronger fact is not needed to replace or weaken
the original limit requirement.

## 8. The constant is the same, and its expectation is legitimate

For `X~π` independent of the fresh pair `(A,E)`, the model's formula is

```text
C_(η,θ) = E[|Ψ(X)|^α−|MX|^α] / (α mα).           (A28.3)
```

Mirek's displayed equation (1.10), printed page 3, explicitly gives this
radial normalization; equation (4.6), printed page 9, repeats it. There is
no extra factor of two or unstated one-sided normalization in A17's claim.
The positive tail measure from (1.12) has positive angular mass in the
polar decomposition (4.5), so its radial constant is strictly positive.

For completeness, absolute integrability of the coupled difference can
be proved directly, without invoking JOC. The drift bound following from
`|Ψ(x)|≤ξ|x|+N` and `h(p)<1` gives `Eπ|X|^p<∞` for each `0<p<α`:
use subadditivity when `p≤1`, and
`(a+b)^p≤(1+ε)a^p+C_(p,ε)b^p` with `(1+ε)h(p)<1` when `p>1`.
Iterate from zero, then pass to the stationary weak limit by lower
semicontinuity.

Since `1<α<2`, the mean value theorem gives

```text
| |Ψ(X)|^α−|MX|^α |
 ≤ αN(ξ|X|+N)^(α−1)
 ≤ α[N^α+Nξ^(α−1)|X|^(α−1)].
```

The first expectation is finite. For the second, independence of the
stationary input from the fresh step permits factorization of the state
moment, and the step's joint moment is finite because

```text
Nξ^(α−1)≤2(101/100)^(α−1)(1+A²+E²)^α.
```

No independence of `N` and `M` is used. Formula (A28.3) is the expectation
of one absolutely integrable difference; it is not the subtraction of
two finite expectations. In fact strict positivity of the tail implies
that the individual `α`th moments are infinite. The checks above and
Mirek's theorem give `0<C_(η,θ)<∞` independently of the JOC-plus-excursion
route in the pinned model proof.

## 9. Why the stable-sum condition is not a missing tail assumption

The requirement `K≤|M|` is (L1) of Assumption 1.14, printed page 4, used
in the later Theorem 1.15. It is not among (H1-H7) or the standing
conditions for Theorem 1.8.

Indeed, for this model it fails on an event of positive probability.
The derivative at zero is

```text
Ψ'(0)=1−ηA²(1+δ)².
```

On `{ηA²>1}`,

```text
K≥|Ψ'(0)|=ηA²(1+δ)²−1 > ηA²−1=|M|.
```

That Gaussian event has positive probability. Thus this audit certifies
the stationary-tail application; it does not certify the hypotheses of
Mirek's stable-sum theorem for these actual maps. A17's distinction is
necessary and correct.
