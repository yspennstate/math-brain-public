## 1. Exact object and the verified application

The object is fixed-step, batch-one, streaming SGD with independent standard Gaussian A,E,

    σ(z)=z+0.001 tanh(z),  Y=σ(Aθ)+E,
    Ψθ(x)=x−ηAσ'(Ax)[σ(Ax)−Y],
    |θ|≤1,  0.99≤η≤1.01.

The explicit proof supplies the following useful inputs. I checked their algebra against the displayed model:

* M=1−ηA² and Ψθ(x)=Mx+Rθ(x), with a single uniform random bound |Rθ(x)|≤Bθ.
* The actual global Lipschitz constant K obeys |M|≤K≤Lθ and E Lθ<0.99010707. The lower bound follows from the limiting derivative at infinity. The upper bound retains dependence between A and Y.
* h(1)<1<h(2), where h(p)=E|M|^p, gives the unique root α∈(1,2). The logarithmic multiplier is integrable and nonarithmetic, and all positive multiplier and remainder moments exist.
* Fresh Gaussian E has a nonzero conditional coefficient for A≠0. Every open interval has positive transition probability; every invariant law therefore has full support.

Contraction gives existence, uniqueness and W₁ convergence. These elementary model checks, followed by either route below, yield the exact positive stationary power tail. Neither route requires A and Y to be independent. The sample map is nonaffine almost surely, so this is an actual nonlinear SGD example rather than a linear model described with nonlinear terminology.

## 2. Original Mirek theorem already covers this example

Primary source: Mariusz Mirek, [arXiv:0907.2261v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.2261v2), printed page 3, Assumptions 1.6–1.7 and Theorem 1.8, especially (1.10) and positivity condition (1.12); standing contraction/integrability conditions are on printed page 1. The PDF was downloaded and the original statements read. The arXiv HTML currently numbers the corresponding tail result differently; the locators here refer to the PDF.

The theorem requires the asymptotic similarity and bounded error, a generated group, nonarithmetic logarithms, the moment root and moments. Its positive-tail conclusion additionally uses unbounded stationary support and a remainder-to-multiplier moment-growth condition. These are real assumptions; the abbreviated SGD restatement is insufficient by itself.

Here is an independent verification of the extra condition that was not checked in the supplied proofs. Put δ=0.001. Their remainder bound, using |θ|≤1, gives

    Bθ ≤ η[(2δ+δ²)|A|+(1+δ)²A²+(1+δ)|AE|]
       ≤ N := 2(1+A²+E²).

For the last inequality use |A|≤(1+A²)/2 and |AE|≤(A²+E²)/2. The largest coefficient after η≤1.01 is 1.518536515<2.

For s→∞, Gaussian moment formulas and Stirling give

    ||A²||s ~ 2s/e,       ||A²+E²||s ~ 2s/e,
    ||N||s ~ 4s/e,        ||1−ηA²||s ~ 2ηs/e.

Indeed E(A²)^s=2^s Γ(s+1/2)/Γ(1/2), whereas E(A²+E²)^s=2^s Γ(s+1). Adding or subtracting the bounded constant 1 changes the relevant Ls norms by at most 1. Consequently

    lim_(s→∞) [E N^s / E|M|^s]^(1/s)=2/η<∞.

This is precisely the required moment-ratio check with s∞=∞. The support is unbounded by the Gaussian conditional law. The support of nonzero M contains every negative real and the positive interval (0,1), so its closed generated semigroup in R* is R*=R⁺×{−1,1}. Uniform bounded remainder implies the dilation limit. All moment and nonarithmetic assumptions follow from the preceding inputs. Finally E|log K|<∞: its negative part is bounded by that of log|M|, and its positive part by Lθ. Thus Mirek's standing contraction conditions also hold.

This provides an older, direct general-theorem route to the exact positive radial tail and the integrable-difference constant. The condition K≤|M| appearing in Mirek's subsequent Theorem 1.15 concerns stable limits of sums and is not a hypothesis of the stationary-tail theorem used here.

## 3. ABB gives another complete route and a signed-tail strengthening

Primary source: Gerold Alsmeyer, Sara Brofferio and Dariusz Buraczewski, [arXiv:2102.02299v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.02299v2), printed page 2, (1)–(3); pages 6–7, Theorem 2.1, (12)–(16); page 36, Proposition 8.1. These original statements and surrounding hypotheses were read.

The continuous asymptotically linear maps may have dependent slopes and remainders. The irreducible case gives two one-sided limits; the positivity proposition applies when stationary support is unbounded. No extra global mean-Lipschitz condition is hidden in that proposition: its following paragraph obtains the needed spectral contraction from the theorem's hypotheses.

For this model the two slopes coincide: A⁻=A⁺=M. Define

    d(p)=E[|M|^p 1_(M>0)],  o(p)=E[|M|^p 1_(M<0)].
    P(p)=[[d(p),o(p)],[o(p),d(p)]].

Both entries are positive. Its dominant eigenvalue is h(p), and its normalized left eigenvector is (1/2,1/2). Thus the spectral-root condition is the already verified equation h(α)=1. The requisite moments hold. For the full Markov nonarithmetic condition, consider either self-transition: its log M has an absolutely continuous conditional distribution, and sign offsets cancel. Such transitions have positive tilted probability, so no lattice and sign offsets can make the lattice event have probability one.

Full support supplies positivity. In particular, my application of ABB yields

    lim t^α π((t,∞)) = lim t^α π((−∞,−t)) = C_(η,θ)/2 > 0

even when θ≠0. Equal asymptotic constants do not imply symmetry of the whole stationary law. The nonzero-teacher proof's decision to assert only the radial tail is conservative and valid; its lack-of-symmetry discussion is compatible with this optional strengthening.

That is a bounded search result, not evidence sufficient for a first-example priority claim. No exhaustive citation graph, multilingual literature review, or all unpublished manuscripts audit was performed.

A suitable claim is: **“We verify a concrete nonlinear Gaussian regression SGD model satisfying the existing renewal-theorem hypotheses, including actual average contraction and a strictly positive tail constant, on an explicit parameter rectangle.”** Avoid “new general nonlinear SGD theorem,” “first nonlinear SGD heavy-tail example,” and “solves heavy tails for general neural networks.”

The registry asks for a concrete nonlinear model and gives examples rather than requiring a specific bounded activation. The present activation is unbounded but a smooth bounded perturbation of the identity; this distinction should remain explicit. Under that literal concrete-model reading, the new contraction certificate completes the requested verification. A broader interpretation requiring arbitrary networks or bounded activations would remain unresolved.
