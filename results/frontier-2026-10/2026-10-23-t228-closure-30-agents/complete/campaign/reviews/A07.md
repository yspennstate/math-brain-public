For the scalar specialization, the theorem takes an independent nonnegative pair consisting of a random variable R and a multiplier ξ. Its assumptions include nonarithmetic logarithmic weights; Eξ^α=1; finite positive E[ξ^α log ξ]; Eξ^γ<∞ for some 0≤γ<α; all positive moments of R below α; and absolute integrability of the weighted difference between the tails of R and ξR. It supplies the finite nonnegative tail coefficient and the signed-tail integral formula. No affine distributional fixed-point equation is an extra hypothesis. This paraphrase is based on the theorem itself, rather than its surrounding applications.

Here the substitutions are N=1, C₁=ξ=|1−ηA²|, and R=|X| with X having the stationary law and independent of the fresh step. The negative values of the signed multiplier M do not obstruct this application: the theorem is applied to the nonnegative absolute-value multiplier and to |X|.

The candidate's list of hypotheses at the start of Section 4 omits the auxiliary Eξ^γ<∞ condition. It is automatically satisfied with γ=0, because ξ>0 almost surely and N=1; it is also satisfied with any 0<γ<α by Section 2. This is an optional completeness edit, not a missing assumption or a gap.

## 2. Section 4: direct verification of the discrepancy and formula

Write U=|Ψ(X)| and W=|MX|=ξ|X|. Stationarity gives U equal in distribution to |X|, and the uniform remainder bound gives |U−W|≤B. For every realization of U,W,

```text
∫₀∞ t^(α−1) |1{U>t}−1{W>t}| dt = |U^α−W^α|/α.
```

Taking expectations and applying the triangle inequality to the expected indicators yields exactly the candidate's weighted-discrepancy bound. This use of Tonelli needs no α-moment of U or W separately.

The integrable envelope can be made particularly explicit. Since 1<α<2 and U∨W≤W+B,

```text
|U^α−W^α|
≤ α B(W+B)^(α−1)
≤ α[B^α + B ξ^(α−1)|X|^(α−1)].
```

The last step uses subadditivity of the exponent α−1∈(0,1). The expectation of the mixed term factors only between X and the fresh step:

```text
E[B ξ^(α−1)|X|^(α−1)]
= E[B ξ^(α−1)] E|X|^(α−1) < ∞.
```

It does **not** require independence of B and ξ. Their nonnegative powers are controlled by Gaussian polynomial moments, and the required stationary moment is strictly below α. The E B^α term is finite for the same Gaussian reason.

Consequently the signed integrand is absolutely integrable as well. Fubini may therefore be used on the **coupled difference** to obtain

```text
∫₀∞ t^(α−1) [P(U>t)−P(W>t)] dt
= E[U^α−W^α]/α.
```

Combining this with the theorem's denominator m_α=E[ξ^α log ξ] gives

```text
C_η = E[|Ψ(X)|^α−|MX|^α]/(α m_α).
```

There is no illegal subtraction of two infinite expectations. There is also no circular use of tail positivity: at this stage the theorem supplies C_η≥0, with finiteness, and Section 5 is separately needed to rule out zero.

**Section 4 status: PASS.**

## 3. Section 5: construction of the tilted law and stopping identity

Let Y_j=log ξ_j, S_n=Y₁+⋯+Y_n, S₀=0, and let F_n be the sigma field of the first n complete steps. Define the tilted law on a complete step by dQ/dP=ξ^α. This is a probability tilt because Eξ^α=1. Its iid product law satisfies, for every E∈F_n,

```text
P(E) = E_Q[e^(−αS_n) 1_E].
```

Tilting the complete steps also removes any ambiguity about residual randomness in Ψ not determined by (ξ,B); equivalently, the candidate's successful event uses only the projected pair process.

For a stopping time τ and a stopped event E, the precise identity needed is

```text
P(E, τ<∞)
= Σₙ P(E, τ=n)
= Σₙ E_Q[e^(−αS_n) 1{E,τ=n}]
= E_Q[e^(−αS_τ) 1{E,τ<∞}].
```

All summands are nonnegative, so no uniform integrability or optional-stopping theorem is required. Under Q, E_QY=m_α>0 and E_Q|Y|<∞; the strong law gives S_n→∞ and therefore τ_u<∞ Q-almost surely.

Under P, τ_u need not be almost surely finite. This is not a defect in the resulting probability bound: the event used for the lower bound is a finite-crossing event, and the displayed identity applies to it. However, the candidate's phrase “At each finite stopping time” is terse enough that I recommend explicitly stating this identity on {τ<∞}. It would be incorrect to infer global absolute continuity of P and Q on all infinite paths, or to assume E_Q e^(−αS_τu)=1. Neither claim is needed or actually used.

For 0<θ<α, put

```text
r_θ = E_Q e^(−θY) = E_P ξ^(α−θ) = h_η(α−θ) < 1.
```

This exact exponential bound is available in this model and supports the subsequent elementary arguments.

**Tilted stopping-time status: PASS, with a recommended precision edit.**

## 4. Positive probability of remaining above zero

The positive-drift claim implies that inf_{n≥0}S_n is finite Q-almost surely. Choose b>0 such that

```text
q_b := Q(inf_{n≥0}S_n > −b) > 0.
```

Since E_QY>0, there is δ>0 with a:=Q(Y≥δ)>0. Choose an integer n₀ satisfying n₀δ>b. The event that the first n₀ increments are at least δ has probability a^n₀>0 and makes each initial partial sum positive. By independent increments, intersecting it with an independent future walk whose infimum is greater than −b has probability a^n₀q_b>0. The full walk then stays strictly positive at every positive time. Thus

```text
p₊ := Q(S_j>0 for every j≥1) ≥ a^n₀ q_b > 0.
```

No unstated one-sided increment assumption is involved. The negative increments allowed by ξ<1 are fully compatible with this argument.

**Part (a) status: PASS.**

## 5. Overshoot uniformity from a translated occupation bound

For a fixed permissible θ, Chernoff gives

```text
Q(S_n≤1) ≤ e^θ r_θ^n,
Σ_{n≥0} Q(S_n≤1) ≤ K_U := e^θ/(1−r_θ).
```

The n=0 term is harmless because the bound e^θ is at least one. To justify the translation-uniform claim, let I=(a,a+1] be any interval and σ its first entry time. Before σ there are no visits. Conditional on σ<∞ and S_σ=s∈I, the post-entry walk has independent increments and

```text
Q(s+S_m∈I) ≤ Q(S_m≤1).
```

The inclusion is true because every point of I is at most one above s. Summing over m≥0 and then over σ yields

```text
U(I) := Σ_{n≥0} Q(S_n∈I) ≤ K_U,
```

uniformly in the location a. Negative jumps, skipped intervals, and later re-entries do not invalidate the argument: the first-entry decomposition includes all future visits and multiplies by an entry probability at most one.

For u≥0, define τ_u=inf{n≥1:S_n>u}, which is Q-almost surely finite, and O_u=S_τu−u. For z≥0, drop the first-crossing restriction in the upper bound to obtain

```text
Q(O_u>z)
≤ Σ_{n≥1} E_Q[1{S_(n−1)≤u} Q(Y>u+z−S_(n−1))].
```

Partition (−∞,u] into the half-open intervals

```text
I_k=(u−k−1,u−k],  k=0,1,2,… .
```

For a point s in I_k, u−s≥k. Hence, with no endpoint gap or missing shift,

```text
Q(O_u>z)
≤ Σ_{k≥0} U(I_k) Q(Y>z+k)
≤ K_U Σ_{k≥0} Q(Y>z+k).
```

Finally,

```text
Σ_{k≥0} Q(Y>z+k)
≤ Q(Y>z) + ∫_z∞ Q(Y>x) dx → 0,
```

because E_QY⁺<∞. The proof therefore establishes exactly the required uniform tightness over all u≥0. This is a fixed-η statement; the theorem does not claim a common positive tail constant over the whole η interval, so uniformity in η is unnecessary here.

**Part (b) status: PASS.**

## 6. Discounted error with the dependence preserved

The reverse-triangle recursion gives, by induction,

```text
|X_n| ≥ ξ_n|X_(n−1)|−B_n
       ≥ e^S_n [|X₀|−Σ_{j=1}^n e^(−S_j)B_j].
```

There is no sign assumption on M in this induction. Since ξ>0 almost surely, multiplication by ξ preserves the inequality.

For 0<θ<min(1,α), apply subadditivity to finite partial sums of V and then monotone convergence. For each j≥1, independence is used between step j and its **past**, not between ξ_j and B_j:

```text
E_Q[e^(−θS_j) B_j^θ]
= E_Q[e^(−θS_(j−1))] E_Q[ξ_j^(−θ) B_j^θ]
= r_θ^(j−1) E_P[ξ^(α−θ)B^θ].
```

It follows that

```text
E_Q[V_∞^θ]
≤ E_P[ξ^(α−θ)B^θ] Σ_{j≥1}r_θ^(j−1)
= E_P[ξ^(α−θ)B^θ]/(1−r_θ) < ∞.
```

The numerator uses the correct joint moment. Because α−θ>0, it introduces no inverse-multiplier singularity near A=±η^(−1/2), and Gaussian polynomial moments control infinity. In particular V_∞ is finite Q-almost surely, and its upper-tail probability tends to zero. A mistaken replacement of the numerator by a product of moments would not be justified in this proof; the candidate does not make that mistake.

**Part (c) status: PASS.**

## 7. Uniform excursion probability and transfer back to P

Choose z≥0 so that sup_u Q(O_u>z)≤p₊/4, then choose K>0 large enough that Q(V_∞>K/2)≤p₊/4. These choices precede t and therefore do not introduce a hidden t-dependent constant.

For t>4K and u=log(t/K)>0, define the finite-crossing event

```text
F_u = {τ_u<∞, S_j>0 for 1≤j≤τ_u,
       V_τu≤K/2, O_u≤z}.
```

Each truncated condition is F_τu-measurable. The event that the walk is always positive, V_∞≤K/2, and O_u≤z is contained in F_u. The union bound therefore gives Q(F_u)≥p₊/2 without requiring any independence between the three conditions.

For every deterministic starting point with 2K≤|y|≤4K, and on F_u, monotonicity of V_n implies for all 1≤j≤τ_u that

```text
|X_j| ≥ e^S_j (|y|−V_j) ≥ (3K/2)e^S_j > K.
```

At crossing,

```text
|X_τu| ≥ (3K/2)e^S_τu > (3K/2)(t/K) = 3t/2 > t.
```

Thus F_u forces a hit of D_t={|x|>t} before the central interval A=[−K,K]. The lower bound remains valid if the chain hits D_t even earlier than τ_u. On F_u, S_τu=u+O_u≤u+z, so the stopped identity gives

```text
P_y(τ_Dt<τ_A)
≥ P(F_u)
= E_Q[e^(−αS_τu)1_Fu]
≥ e^(−α(u+z)) Q(F_u)
≥ (p₊/2)e^(−αz)(K/t)^α.
```

This holds uniformly over the whole starting annulus, with constants independent of t. The successful event depends on the noise sequence and implies the needed chain behavior simultaneously for every allowed y.

**Excursion and change-of-measure status: PASS.**

## 8. Stationary last-exit inequality: explicit finite-horizon proof

This is the most consequential potential source of a direction or normalization error. The candidate's direction and normalization are both correct.

Let the chain be stationary, let A and D be disjoint Borel sets, and put τ_A⁺=inf{n≥1:X_n∈A}. For a fixed N≥1, the following N events are disjoint, indexed by k=0,…,N−1:

```text
E_(k,N)={X_k∈A, X_(k+1),…,X_(N−1)∉A, X_N∈D}.
```

They are the possible last visits to A before the endpoint, among paths ending in D. Paths that have no visit to A in the window supply a nonnegative remainder. Therefore stationarity and the Markov property give

```text
π(D)
≥ Σ_{k=0}^{N−1} P_π(E_(k,N))
= ∫_A π(dx) Σ_{j=1}^N P_x(X_j∈D, τ_A⁺>j)
= ∫_A π(dx) E_x[Σ_{j=1}^N 1{X_j∈D, j<τ_A⁺}].
```

The summands are nonnegative. Letting N increase gives

```text
π(D)
≥ ∫_A π(dx) E_x[Σ_{j=1}^{τ_A⁺−1}1{X_j∈D}]
≥ ∫_A π(dx) P_x(τ_D<τ_A⁺).
```

The last sum is interpreted over all positive j if τ_A⁺=∞. This proof requires neither positive Harris recurrence nor an a priori finite expected return time. It is an unnormalized integral against π restricted to A, so no additional division by π(A), return-time factor, or regeneration factor is missing.

The minorization needed to concatenate the excursion is also sufficient as written. Use the compact minorization with radius 4K, and let C={2K≤|y|≤4K}. It gives a number δ_K>0 such that

```text
inf_{x∈A} P(x,C) ≥ δ_K > 0.
```

The set C lies outside A and, for t>4K, inside the complement of D_t. After this one step, the Markov property and the uniform annulus bound imply

```text
P_x(τ_Dt<τ_A⁺)
≥ δ_K (p₊/2)e^(−αz)K^α t^(−α),  x∈A.
```

Section 3's full-support conclusion gives π(A)>0. Applying the stationary inequality now yields the explicit strictly positive lower bound

```text
π(D_t) ≥ c_η t^(−α),
c_η := π(A)δ_K(p₊/2)e^(−αz)K^α > 0,
```

for all t>4K. Section 4 already supplies convergence of t^απ(D_t), so C_η≥c_η>0.

**Last-exit and final strict-positivity status: PASS.**

## 9. Failure modes tested and conclusion

| Attempted objection | Result |
|---|---|
| Nonlinear Ψ falls outside the cited theorem | Rejected: Theorem 3.1 imposes a discrepancy condition, not affine recursion. |
| Negative M invalidates a positive-weight theorem | Rejected: the application is to ξ=|M| and R=|X|. |
| Infinite α-moments invalidate the numerator | Rejected: the coupled difference is absolutely integrable. |
| Overshoots are tight only at fixed levels | Rejected: the translated unit-interval occupation argument is genuinely uniform in u. |
| ξ and B are correlated | Accounted for in E_P[ξ^(α−θ)B^θ]. |
| The good events need to be independent | Rejected: a union bound against p₊ suffices. |
| τ_u is infinite with positive P-probability | Accounted for by summing the stopped identity over {τ_u=n}; no optional stopping is needed. |
| K or z might depend on t | Rejected: both are chosen before t using uniform overshoot tightness. |
| Stationary hitting probability does not control stationary mass | It does here by the explicit last-exit occupation inequality, in the stated direction. |
| A return-time or π(A) normalization is missing | Rejected: the unnormalized stationary integral is exactly correct. |

Recommended nonblocking edits for a final polished proof:

2. Replace the short stopping-time sentence by the explicit identity on {τ<∞}, and explicitly include τ_u<∞ in the successful event. This prevents a reader from interpreting the argument as an unsupported infinite-horizon likelihood-ratio or optional-stopping claim.
3. Optionally display the finite-horizon last-exit sum. The existing prose is correct; the formula above makes the missing-window remainder and normalization transparent.

None of these changes requires a new lemma, an extra model assumption, a change in constants, or a change to the conclusion. **No substantive FIX or FAIL was found in Sections 4–5, and this audit presents no mathematical blocker to closure from those sections.**
