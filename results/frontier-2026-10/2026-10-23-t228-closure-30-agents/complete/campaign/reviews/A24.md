## Defect found, demonstrated, and resolved

The original executable used `assert` for all validity and sign checks. Running

```bash
python -O campaign/checks/A13_tail_index_certificate.py \
  --grid-denominator 10 \
  --output campaign/checks/A24_optimized_falsepass.json
```

before the fix returned exit code zero, printed all three `Certified` messages, and wrote `"status": "PASS"`. All six resulting endpoint intervals contained one. For example, the supposed lower endpoint for eta 0.99 had the interval

\[
[0.8241917946603861673758271563768423750602,
  1.174191794660386168412297981306454259293].
\]

Thus the program falsely reported that a sign certificate had passed, even though its own interval data did not establish either required sign. This does not show the claimed mathematical brackets were false; it shows the original `PASS` control flow was unsound under optimized execution. Python's primary documentation explicitly says optimization removes assertion code: [Python 3.12, the assert statement](https://docs.python.org/3.12/reference/simple_stmts.html#the-assert-statement).

The parent reviewer preserved the original and added, before imports and computation, an unconditional `if not __debug__: raise RuntimeError(...)`. A24 verified that `-O`, `-OO`, `PYTHONOPTIMIZE=1`, and `PYTHONOPTIMIZE=2` now all exit with code 1 and empty stdout, explaining that no certificate was produced. Ordinary execution at the deliberately inadequate denominator 10 still exits unsuccessfully on its endpoint sign assertion. The original false-PASS JSON is retained solely as counterexample evidence, not as an accepted certificate.

## Independent analytic audit of the cusp and quadrature error

Put \(v=1-\eta x^2\), \(u=|v|\), \(g=u^p\), and \(f=g e^{-x^2/2}\). Away from \(r=\eta^{-1/2}\), direct differentiation gives

\[
g'=-2p\eta x\operatorname{sgn}(v)u^{p-1},\qquad
g''=4p(p-1)\eta^2x^2u^{p-2}
      -2p\eta\operatorname{sgn}(v)u^{p-1}.
\]

Since \(f''=e^{-x^2/2}[g''-2xg'+(x^2-1)g]\), the four-term majorant in A13 is correct:

\[
|f''|\le e^{-x^2/2}\left[
4p(p-1)\eta^2x^2u^{p-2}
+2p\eta u^{p-1}+4p\eta x^2u^{p-1}
+(x^2+1)u^p\right].
\]

For every fixed \(1<p\le2\), \(u\) has a simple zero at \(r\), so \(g'\), and hence \(f'\), tends to zero there. The only potentially unbounded term in \(f''\) is locally of order \(|x-r|^{p-2}\), which is integrable because \(p-2>-1\). Patching the absolutely continuous derivative on each side across its matching limit proves \(f'\) is absolutely continuous on compact intervals. There is no hidden jump or Dirac mass at the cusp. This is precisely the regularity needed for the trapezoidal kernel identity; twice continuous differentiability is unnecessary.

For the singular term, set \(t=\eta x^2\). On \(0\le t\le2\), dropping the Gaussian and using \(\sqrt t\le\sqrt2\) gives

\[
2p(p-1)\sqrt\eta\sqrt2\int_0^2|1-t|^{p-2}dt
=4p\sqrt{2\eta}\le8\sqrt{2(101/100)}<12.
\]

The factor \(p-1\) correctly cancels the integral's \(1/(p-1)\); the bound therefore remains uniform as \(p\downarrow1\). On \(t\ge2\), \(u^{p-2}\le1\), so the remainder of this term is at most \(8b^2c_G\), where \(b=101/100\) and \(c_G=\sqrt{\pi/2}<13/10\).

For the other terms, \(u^{p-1}\le1+u\le2+bx^2\), and \(u^p\le1+u^2\le2+2bx^2+b^2x^4\). Integrating with the half-Gaussian moments \(c_G,c_G,3c_G,15c_G\) at orders 0, 2, 4, 6 gives the exact majorants

\[
12,\quad \frac{132613}{12500},\quad
\frac{395213}{25000},\quad\frac{660439}{12500},\quad
\frac{1978717}{50000}.
\]

Their sum is \(6541351/50000=130.82702<140\). No underestimated coefficient or omitted derivative term was found.

On each mesh cell \([a,b]\), \(b-a=\Delta\), the exact identity is

\[
\frac\Delta2[f(a)+f(b)]-\int_a^b f(x)dx
=\frac12\int_a^b(x-a)(b-x)f''(x)dx.
\]

The kernel is nonnegative and at most \(\Delta^2/8\). Absolute continuity makes the identity valid on a cell crossing the cusp. Summing and taking absolute values therefore gives \(140\Delta^2/8\). For \(\Delta=10^{-4}\) this is \(1.75\times10^{-7}\). Multiplication by the exact normalizer \(\sqrt{2/\pi}<1\) can only decrease this allowance. The error constant and the direction of both final bounds are correct.

## Independent tail and normalizer audit

For \(x\ge10\), \(\eta x^2-1\ge98>1\), hence

\[
u^p\le u^2\le\eta^2x^4<2x^4.

Writing \(I_k(R)=\int_R^\infty x^ke^{-x^2/2}dx\), integration by parts gives \(I_4=R^3e^{-R^2/2}+3I_2\) and \(I_2=Re^{-R^2/2}+I_0\). The elementary Mills bound \(I_0<e^{-R^2/2}/R\) proves the proposed normalized tail majorant. Since \(e>8/3\), its exact rational value is

## Decimal operations, sign logic, and trust boundary

The official [Python 3.12 Decimal documentation](https://docs.python.org/3.12/library/decimal.html) specifies nearest-even correct rounding for `ln` and `exp`, adjacent representable results for `next_minus`/`next_plus`, and directed floor/ceiling rounding. Its context methods have the corresponding Decimal-method semantics. These documented guarantees suffice for the one-neighbor widening used here; assuming that `ln` or `exp` honors directed context rounding directly would not suffice, but the code does not make that mistake.

All actual abscissas, \(u\), \(x^2/2\), exponents, and learning rates are exact finite decimals, with inexact initial divisions trapped. Multiplication of a log interval by positive \(p\) preserves endpoint order, including for negative logarithms. Directed subtraction then encloses the exponent argument, and monotonicity of the exponential plus adjacent-number widening encloses its value. The exact \(u=0\) and \(u=1\) cases are handled without undefined logarithms. Positive weights, directed sums, and positive-normalizer multiplication preserve enclosure. No arithmetic operator in the numerical accumulation silently uses the default Decimal context. The general Decimal power function is not used.

Dominated convergence gives continuity of \(h(p)=E Y^p\), and strict convexity follows by integrating the strict convexity of \(p\mapsto Y^p\) on the event \(Y\ne1\). Independently of quadrature,

The strict first inequality is verified using \(\pi>157/50\), \(e>1359/500\), and rational squaring; the second is the Gaussian polynomial moment. With \(h(0)=1\), continuity gives a positive root and strict convexity precludes two positive roots. Thus the six certified signs place that root in exactly the asserted three intervals of width \(10^{-5}\). The coarser calibration also encloses the independent exact moment \(h_1(2)=2\).

Acceptance relies on the analytic derivations above, Python's ordinary exact integer/Fraction behavior, its documented Decimal rounding contract, and a correctly functioning interpreter/runtime/hardware.
