## 1. Independent check of the scalar proof

Write `b=beta1`, `q=beta2`, and assume `v_t -> V < infinity` for a scalar eigenvalue `lambda>0`.

The identity

\[
\lambda^2x_t^2=\frac{v_{t+1}-qv_t}{1-q}\longrightarrow V
\]

is exact, including at `q=0`. It implies `|x_t| -> a=sqrt(V)/lambda`; it does not require any rate of convergence. If `V=0`, the coordinate tends to zero and its denominator tends to `epsilon`, proving the inactive alternative. Its momentum also tends to zero because the stable moving-average recursion has a vanishing input.

If `V>0`, define the actual coefficient in the update by

\[
\alpha_t=\frac{\eta}{(1-b^{t+1})d_t},\qquad
d_t=\sqrt{\frac{v_{t+1}}{1-q^{t+1}}}+\epsilon.
\]

Then `alpha_t -> alpha=eta/(sqrt(V)+epsilon)>0`, and hence `alpha_t/alpha_{t-1}->1`. Both bias-correction factors are handled here. Eliminating `m_t` gives exactly

\[
x_{t+1}=
\left(1+b\frac{\alpha_t}{\alpha_{t-1}}-(1-b)\lambda\alpha_t\right)x_t
-b\frac{\alpha_t}{\alpha_{t-1}}x_{t-1}.
\]

There is no indexing error: `m_t=-(x_t-x_{t-1})/alpha_{t-1}` is the identity from the preceding position update.

Eventually `x_t` is nonzero. Put `r_t=sign(x_t)sign(x_{t-1})`. Because successive absolute-value ratios tend to one, division of the preceding recurrence by `x_t` yields

\[
r_{t+1}+br_t\longrightarrow 1+b-k,
\qquad k=(1-b)\lambda\alpha>0.
\]

For `0<b<1`, the four possible values of the left side are distinct. Convergence of this finite-valued sequence makes it eventually constant. Each value fixes the ordered pair `(r_t,r_{t+1})`, and overlapping pairs then force both entries to be the same. The choice `+1` would give `k=0`. Thus `r_t=-1` eventually and `k=2(1+b)`. Therefore

\[
\sqrt V+\epsilon=\frac{\eta(1-b)\lambda}{2(1+b)},\qquad
a=\frac{\eta(1-b)}{2(1+b)}-\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda}>0.
\]

This proves eventual strict sign alternation and convergence of the two parity subsequences to opposite nonzero values. It proves more than an assertion about accumulation points. For completeness, the full limiting state has

\[
x_t=(-1)^t\sigma a+o(1),\quad
m_t=-c\lambda(-1)^t\sigma a+o(1),\quad
v_t\to\lambda^2a^2,\qquad c=\frac{1-b}{1+b}.
\]

The same proof works without bias correction. It also works for the displayed recurrence with arbitrary finite `m_0` and nonnegative finite `v_0`; the name “bias correction” is then only a convention for the displayed factors and does not mean that they remove the initial moments' bias. No assertion about the probability or basin of these initial states is implicit.

**No core proof gap was found in lines 65–96 of the reviewed note.**

## 2. Parameter domains and the exact epsilon threshold

The theorem does **not** need `beta2>beta1^2`. Such an inequality appears in other, unconditional dynamical results, but this proof already assumes convergence of the second moments. Inserting that extra restriction would unnecessarily weaken this conditional lemma.

The stated endpoint choices are coherent:

- `q=0` is included and valid. `q=1` is excluded: the inversion of the moment recurrence fails and the displayed bias correction is undefined.
- `0<b<1` makes the finite-set argument injective. The theorem as written makes no RMSProp claim at `b=0`. There is a valid separate extension at `b=0`: the first-order recurrence gives `x_{t+1}/x_t -> 1-lambda*alpha`, whose nonzero-amplitude limit must be `-1`, hence `lambda*alpha=2` and `K=2`. The existing proof should not be called verbatim valid at that endpoint.
- `b=1` is excluded correctly; the update and threshold degenerate.
- `epsilon>0` is essential for the inactive-coordinate denominators and the curvature cap. Nothing here licenses substituting `epsilon=0`, putting epsilon inside the square root, allowing negative second moments, or using varying beta parameters.
- The diagonal proof is for a fixed finite-dimensional positive definite quadratic. An arbitrary rotation of a diagonal quadratic is not automatically covered, because Adam's coordinatewise moments are not rotation invariant. The original row allows a diagonal model, so this is a legitimate partial setting.

Set `K=2(1+b)/(1-b)`. A nonzero limiting cycle is possible under this classification only if

\[
\frac{\eta\lambda}{\epsilon}>K.
\]

This is a **strict necessary condition**, not a sufficient criterion for a given standard-start trajectory. If `eta*lambda/epsilon<=K`, the moment-convergence hypothesis forces the inactive alternative. At equality, the amplitude formula is zero and does not define a nontrivial cycle. If the inequality is strict, the coordinate can still be inactive; the lemma deliberately does not rule out exceptional or identically zero coordinates.

One clarification is needed in the epsilon-power section (reviewed lines 104–110): the earlier setup allows general `b`, but the later amplitude `eta/38-epsilon/lambda` and the requirement `lambda>38` at `p=1` specialize to `b=0.9`. Add explicitly:

> In the remainder of this section set `beta1=0.9`, so `K=38`; `beta2` remains in `[0,1)` and may in particular equal `0.999`.

Alternatively replace 38 throughout that section with `K`. This is a parameter-scope clarification, not a defect in the general theorem. Under its declared general setup, the literal claim that every `p=1` oscillatory coordinate has `lambda>38` would otherwise be false for other beta1 values.

## 3. Vector classification and curvature conclusions

For the finite coordinate set, the scalar results give a vector `z` with `z_i=sigma_i*a_i` on oscillatory coordinates and zero elsewhere, such that `x_t-(-1)^t z -> 0`. Different coordinate phases cause no longer period: the limiting position cycle is the pair `(z,-z)`.

The definition in the note agrees with the unnormalized version of Fong–Yang's active curvature in equation (5):

\[
C_{\rm act,t}=
\frac{g_t^TD_t^{-1}\Lambda D_t^{-1}g_t}
{g_t^TD_t^{-1}g_t}
=\frac{\sum_i (g_{i,t}^2/d_{i,t})(\lambda_i/d_{i,t})}
{\sum_i g_{i,t}^2/d_{i,t}}.
\]

The weights of inactive coordinates tend to zero, and each oscillatory coordinate has a strictly positive limiting weight and curvature `K/eta`. If the oscillatory set `A` is nonempty, the denominator is bounded away from zero eventually. Consequently both the active-curvature limit and the maximum formula in the note follow without dividing by a vanishing quantity:

\[
C_{\rm act,t}\to K/\eta,\qquad
S_t\to\max\left\{K/\eta,\max_{i\in I}\lambda_i/\epsilon\right\}.
\]

The strict limiting-separation criterion is correct. It characterizes

\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}S_t>K/\eta
\]

by the existence of an inactive coordinate satisfying `eta*lambda_i/epsilon>K`, conditional on the preceding hypotheses. In that case there is an eventual positive gap. This must not be silently restated as an equivalence for mere eventual pointwise inequalities `S_t>K/eta`, whose gap could tend to zero, or as exact finite-time equality `Cact_t=K/eta`.

The condition `A nonempty` cannot be deleted. When all coordinates are inactive, the weights all tend to zero. The proof gives no universal active-curvature limit in that case. In one dimension, whenever the active curvature is defined, it is simply `lambda/d_t` and tends to `lambda/epsilon`, which need not be `K/eta`. The identically zero standard-start trajectory makes active curvature undefined at every time. At the critical scalar value `eta*lambda/epsilon=K`, an inactive trajectory, if its moments converge and it remains nonzero at the times under consideration, would have the threshold as its curvature limit without a positive-amplitude cycle.

For standard `m_0=v_0=0`, an initially zero coordinate remains identically zero. Thus supercritical inactive coordinates are compatible with standard initialization at least in this trivial support-restricted sense. An absolutely continuous random initialization assigns zero probability to any prescribed coordinate being exactly zero; this observation does not settle whether nonzero initial coordinates can converge to zero or with what probability. The theorem's limiting support `A` is an outcome, not a computed function of the eigenvalues and initial alignment.

## 4. Epsilon-power regimes and order of limits

The unconditional bound

\[
0\leq\eta C_{\rm act,t}\leq\eta S_t
\leq\lambda_{\max}\eta^{1-p}
\]

is correct for every valid iterate at which active curvature is defined. It does not require moment convergence, special momentum parameters, or control of the initial moments beyond validity of the denominators. Its uniformity is for a **fixed Hessian** (or at least a uniform eigenvalue bound); it does not apply unchanged to eigenvalues diverging with eta.

With `beta1=0.9`, the conclusions are:

| Regime | What is actually established |
| --- | --- |
| `p<1` | Both normalized observables tend to zero uniformly over time and valid initializations; attraction to 38 in an `o(1/eta)` band is impossible. For the registry's additional requirement `epsilon(eta)->0`, the applicable power-law subrange is `0<p<1`. |
| `p=1`, `lambda_max<38` | The normalized observables stay at least `38-lambda_max` below 38, uniformly. |
| `p=1`, `lambda_i=38` | A positive limiting amplitude in that coordinate is excluded by the conditional theorem; the epsilon cap alone does not create a strict gap below 38. |
| `p=1`, `lambda_i>38` | The positive amplitude formula is algebraically admissible. Actual attraction, moment convergence, and its probability remain unproved here. |
| `p>1` | For each fixed positive eigenvalue, the amplitude formula becomes positive for sufficiently small eta. The cap is no obstruction; actual dynamical attraction still does not follow. |

For a family of trajectories for which all second moments converge and `A(eta)` is nonempty for every sufficiently small eta, the claimed eventual band follows by the definition of convergence. Given any positive `delta(eta)->0`, choose a finite trajectory-dependent `T(eta)` such that

\[
\sup_{t\geq T(\eta)}|\eta C_{\rm act,t}-38|<\delta(\eta).
\]

This is a legitimate conditional `o(1/eta)` band. It supplies no quantitative estimate for `T`, no uniformity over initialization, no specified probability, and no permission to exchange the small-eta and long-time limits. The reviewed note explicitly acknowledges these limitations.

The loss formula is also correct, but a limiting loss does not imply that the loss approaches it from above, decreases overall from its initial value, or has any required nonmonotonic behavior. A nonzero exact two-cycle in the uncorrected recurrence has constant loss. The conditional classification cannot supply the registry's loss requirement without another argument.

## 5. Standard initialization, Proposition F.8, and numerical fast tails

I inspected the saved primary-source HTML for [Fong–Yang, arXiv:2608.20638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20638v2), specifically Section 2, equation (5), Assumption D.2, and Proposition F.8 with its proof. The saved document identifies v2 as dated 1 October 2026 and uses the title *Adam at the Edge of Stability: Adaptive Feedback, Provable Oscillation, and Gradient Reversal*.

Section 2 uses **uncorrected** Adam with an arbitrary nonnegative initial second moment. Proposition F.8 fixes a strictly positive scalar initial second moment, takes a nonzero initial scalar position, and selects an initial momentum through a positive shooting slope. In its notation,

\[
m_0^{\parallel}=-c\lambda(1+\zeta_0)x_0^{\parallel},\qquad \zeta_0>0.
\]

Thus the constructed initial momentum is nonzero. Assumption D.2 permits these initial states, but standard initialization is only a special case of D.2, not the case used in this construction. The source proposition therefore does not establish the existence of a nonzero fast-tail trajectory from `m_0=v_0=0`, does not give its probability under random `x_0`, and does not by itself transfer to the bias-corrected system. The A06 note handles the initialization distinction correctly; adding that the source system is uncorrected would make the warning even more precise.

There is no numerical candidate, precision log, validated enclosure, or proof of a standard-start fast-tail certificate in the supplied A06 files. `ROUTES.jsonl` explicitly records that route as unfinished. I therefore cannot validate or refute a particular reported floating-point initial value from these inputs. Any numerical fast tail mentioned by the campaign must remain an **uncertified candidate**, not a rigorous counterexample to a standard-start statement.

There is a concrete reason why long numerical decay is insufficient. If a scalar coordinate tends to zero in the supercritical regime, the limiting position recurrence has characteristic polynomial

\[
p(r)=r^2-Ar+b,\qquad
A=1+b-(1-b)\eta\lambda/\epsilon<-(1+b).
\]

Since `p(-1)=2(1+b)-(1-b)*eta*lambda/epsilon<0` and `p(0)=b>0`, it has one root in `(-1,0)` and one root below `-1`. A stable direction coexists with an unstable direction. Finite-precision shooting can shadow a decaying tail for a long interval without proving exact cancellation of the unstable component. Conversely, roundoff can destroy a genuine exceptional trajectory. This calculation is a diagnostic, not a stable-manifold/intersection proof for the nonlinear, bias-corrected system.

Two exact checks are useful when evaluating such logs:

1. For `b>0`, a nonzero standard-start scalar trajectory cannot reach `x_t=m_t=0` at a finite time. Indeed the position update then implies `x_{t-1}=0`, the momentum update implies `m_{t-1}=0`, and backward induction gives `x_0=0`. A reported absorbing exact zero reached from nonzero `x_0` would be a numerical artifact, whereas a single isolated `x_t=0` can occur and does not imply absorption.
2. For `0<q<1` and nonzero `x_0`, exact arithmetic gives `v_t>=q^(t-1)(1-q)lambda^2*x_0^2>0` for every integer `t>=1`. A finite-time machine zero in that second moment reflects underflow or changed arithmetic, not an exact equality.

A rigorous standard-start counterexample would need an existence argument placing the standard-initialization curve on the exceptional stable set, or a validated boundary-value/shooting construction with a justified infinite-time tail. More floating-point digits or more forward steps alone do not prove either. No such certificate is supplied here.

The saved `moves.jsonl` reports a separate claimed local standard-start theorem and a cycle-stability interval under claim `T421-W706-006`. Its proof is absent from the inspected handoff. A21 does not validate that external claim. Even a valid local attracting-cycle theorem would not, without its basin and initialization argument, establish the global or probabilistic hypotheses of this conditional classification.

## Recommended corrections and retained gaps

1. **Make the beta1 specialization explicit** before the epsilon-power section, or retain `K` instead of 38 throughout its general-beta formulas.
2. **Retain the logical qualifiers** “all second moments have finite limits” and “at least one nonzero limiting amplitude” in every reuse of the active-curvature conclusion. Strict supercriticality alone does not imply either.
3. **Keep the separation claim asymptotic with a strict limiting gap.** Do not equate it with mere eventual strict inequalities or finite-time equality of active curvature.
4. **Record both source mismatches for F.8:** its construction uses selected nonzero initial momentum and positive initial second moment, and the paper's recursion is uncorrected.
5. **Keep numerical fast tails and the unavailable W706 proof uncertified.** Establish standard-initialization existence/probability and infinite-time control before using them as counterexamples or as the missing hypothesis.
6. **Do not close T421.** Moment convergence, active-set probabilities, the requested initialization/eta/time quantifiers, favorable-regime burn-in control when required, and the loss behavior remain genuine gaps to the actual registry row. Novelty relative to the literature or uninspected worker proofs is also unestablished.

**Final review disposition:** accept the side theorem and elementary obstruction at their existing limited statuses; apply the beta1 wording clarification before general reuse; leave the broad T421 row unresolved in this handoff.
