## 1. Statistical experiment and clipping — PASS

The experiment is specified strongly enough for all uses of Gaussianity and independence. Each task has a fresh GP/noise draw; the entire covariate/query/length configuration is independent of that draw. Covariates inside a task may otherwise be dependent, and context labels need not be independent. Conditioning on all covariates fixes the GP covariance matrices. With `A=G+sI`, the posterior mean has variance `k_*^T A^{-1}k_* <= K`, the query label has variance at most `V0=s+K`, and the noisy posterior variance lies in `[s,V0]`.

The model receives clipped context labels, while its target and the Bayes comparator retain the original GP law. On `E_B`, clipping leaves the context unchanged. On its complement the proof bounds risk directly; it never treats the clipped-label distribution as if it were the original posterior experiment. Final mean/variance clipping is used only to preserve scalar errors and create an integrable envelope. The report correctly avoids the false general assertion that variance clipping monotonically improves full Gaussian KL when the means differ.

The bounded-context mean bound follows from Cauchy–Schwarz in the `A^{-1}` inner product and the Schur complement:

```text
|k_*^T A^{-1}y|^2 <= (k_*^T A^{-1}k_*) (y^T A^{-1}y)
                   <= K n B^2/s.
```

For `a,b>=s`, integrating `(b-a)/(2b^2)` gives the stated global variance-only KL bound `(a-b)^2/(4s^2)`. This is valid in both orders of `a,b`; no small-relative-error hypothesis is needed. Hence equation (1) is valid.

## 2. Correlated Gaussian bad-event control — PASS

The delicate estimate is (3), and its proof handles the dependence correctly. For a fixed coordinate and conditioned covariates, write the jointly Gaussian pair as

```text
m = sqrt(w) [r U + sqrt(1-r^2) V],
Y_i = sqrt(a_i) U,
```

with independent standard normals `U,V`, `w<=K`, and `a_i<=V0`; degenerate `w=0` is immediate. Conditional second moments give exactly the two-term expression in the report. The threshold is at least `sqrt(2t0)>1`, so the truncated second moment dominates the corresponding tail probability. Monotonicity in the threshold then permits replacement by `sqrt(2t0)`. Integration by parts and the usual elementary Gaussian tail bound yield the constant `3/sqrt(pi)` for `t0>=1`. Summing over at most `n` coordinates uses only a union bound, not label independence.

On the bad event, `(m-m_q)^2/(2s) <= m^2/s+M^2/s`, while both variances lie in an interval of width `K`. Multiplying the bounded terms by `P(E_B^c)<=2n exp(-t0)` and substituting `M^2=2nKV0 t0/s` reproduces every coefficient of `T_tail(t0)` in (4), including the `n^2` contribution. There is no missing covariance or tail moment term.

Equation (5) is also valid for all positive tail budgets, including the branch where `t0=1`: use `sqrt(t0)<=t0`, `1<=t0`, and `t exp(-t)<=(2/e)exp(-t/2)`. `K=0` is separately handled by the exact constant predictor.

## 3. Finite-class concentration with unbounded targets — PASS

The pointwise nonnegative envelope

```text
0 <= ell_h <= a0 + Y^2/s
```

is enough even though `m_h(Z)` is correlated with `Y` through the context. For the stated `A0`, `a0/A0<=log(2)/2` and `1/(sA0)<=1/(4V0)`. The centered Gaussian conditional-on-covariates moment generating function bounds `E exp(Y^2/(4V0))` by `sqrt(2)`. Therefore (7) is uniform over the class and does not assume that target labels are bounded or independent of predictions.

From (7), `E ell_h^k<=2 k! A0^k`. For positive lambda the centered exponential series gives the displayed subexponential MGF bound. For negative lambda, `exp(-x)-1+x<=x^2/2` for nonnegative `x`, followed by `log u<=u-1`, gives a bound no larger than the positive-side expression. Thus both tails in (8) have variance factor `4A0^2` and scale `A0`; the constants `sqrt(8u/J)` and `u/J` are correct.

The union bound and two empirical/population deviations produce (9). The shifted Bayes NLL has finite expectation because `s<=v<=V0`; subtraction yields exactly the average conditional forward KL. Membership of the Bayes predictor in the finite class is unnecessary. The two requirements in (10) make each of the square-root and linear contributions at most half the assigned budget.

The finite hypothesis class and fixed architecture/box must be specified independently of the training tasks, as the report's fixed-construction setup does. `J` counts independent whole tasks, not context labels. Reusing a single sampled GP across all tasks would require a different argument and is outside this theorem.

## 4. Parameter nets and full-box ERM — PASS

The covering bound `(2+2W/r)^P` is conservative and valid. The output loss derivatives satisfy

```text
|partial_m ell| <= (M+|Y|)/s,
|partial_v ell| <= 1/(2s)+(M+|Y|)^2/(2s^2).
```

Interpolating the output pairs within their common rectangle, then using the output Lipschitz bounds, proves (13). Expectations satisfy `E|Y|<=sqrt(V0)` and `EY^2<=V0`. This needs no differentiability of the network in parameters. Grid ERM therefore has exactly one comparator discretization term `r C_pop`, as in (14).

For full-box ERM, independence across tasks gives

```text
E exp(sum_j Y_j^2/(4V0)) <= 2^(J/2).
```

Markov's inequality yields the displayed `Q_J` with failure probability `delta/2`. On that event, empirical Cauchy–Schwarz supplies `P_J|Y|<=sqrt(Q_J)`, giving `C_emp`. The finite-net event uses the other `delta/2`; equation (15) follows by extending each net deviation with one empirical and one population Lipschitz error. Applying the usual two-deviation ERM comparison to this uniform event gives (16), with the stated factor two and no omitted comparator-net penalty.

Compactness and samplewise continuity prove existence of a global empirical minimizer. The same integrable envelope proves continuity of population risk by dominated convergence. These are existence and statistical statements; they do not furnish an algorithm for finding a global minimizer. The stated leading rate with `r=1/J` is an asymptotic statement for a fixed construction, whose constants may be very large.

## 5. Explicit architecture constants and correction — PASS AFTER FIX

For all core coefficients bounded by `W`, the attention amplitude is bounded by `hD^4W^3R_l^3`: `Qz` and `Kz` each have coordinate magnitude at most `DWR_l`, the dot product has magnitude at most `D^3W^2R_l^2`, and the value vector at most `DWR_l`. A three-factor telescoping bound gives respectively

```text
3hD^4W^3R_l^2   for state changes,
3hD^4W^2R_l^3   for parameter changes at fixed states.
```

ReLU is 1-Lipschitz, and the fixed `1/N_tok` normalization cancels the number of summands. The residual MLP has state Lipschitz bound `1+FDW^2`. Its fixed-input parameter contribution is `2FW(D A_l+1)+1`, by separately varying the two affine maps and their biases. This proves (17). Varying the affine readout contributes `DW C_L+D R_L+1`, and output clipping does not increase that bound. The compact input domain and fixed context clip furnish a finite `R0`; `C0=0` uses fixed input placement. Thus (18) follows without any appeal to parameter count alone.

**Issue in the original hash.** Section 6 originally bounded only trainable matrix entries/biases and then said the same constants also covered coefficients kept fixed. Those statements are not sufficient if fixed coefficients can exceed `W`. The original section 4 prescription for `W` likewise mentioned only trainable coefficients.

A concrete counterexample to that literal reading uses `D=F=h=R0=W=1`, zero attention, zero biases, fixed `U2=100`, and the only trainable entry `U1=theta` in `[-1,1]`. With input 1, the block output is `1+100 ReLU(theta)`. The stated old recursion would give `R1=6`, `C1=13`, and affine-readout bound `L_m=20`, whereas the state can be 101 and the actual output sensitivity is 100 on positive theta. Fixed clipping to `[-200,200]` leaves this example unchanged. Exact rational evaluation at `theta=1/2` and `51/100` confirms the violation. An embedding in larger width gives the same issue by making the fixed coefficient sufficiently large.

**Applied correction.** The parent amended sections 4 and 6 so that `W` bounds every fixed or trainable core matrix entry/bias and affine-readout coefficient; the external fixed clips are treated separately. The final fixed-parameter sentence now repeats that requirement. I reread the three changed paragraphs and inspected the complete diff against the preserved original. This resolves the issue: all coefficients in A02's finite real-weight construction are finite, so such a `W` exists, whether they are trained or frozen. The recursion and probability estimates need no alteration.

**Polynomial lower bound.** The residual-polynomial minimax proof is correct. The lower bound concerns instance-independent polynomial coefficients and uniform relative residual/operator accuracy. The text explicitly excludes scalar-readout lower bounds, adaptive Krylov methods, matrix-memory algorithms, and arbitrary transformers. No universal transformer depth lower bound is inferred. The ancillary Newton-method bibliography was not re-audited here and is unnecessary for the stated minimax proof.
