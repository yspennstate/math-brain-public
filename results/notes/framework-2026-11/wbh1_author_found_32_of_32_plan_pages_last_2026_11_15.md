A writer gets 600 to 800 words of a published novel (authors born 1868 to 1900, off the canon lists) and writes the
next page; a judge gets the same passage and seven next pages, one of them the author's, and ranks them by how likely
each is to be the author's. Eight passages, three plain writers, three writers using `turn.py plan`, four judges. Rule
registered before any page was written.

- The author's page was ranked first in 32 of 32 cells. In 27 the judge named the book, so the count is partly
  memory; in the 5 cells with no book named it was still first.
- Plan pages were ranked further from the author than plain pages: 5.78 against 3.22 of seven, a plan page last in
  30 of 32 cells, 86 quoted tells against 43.
- The tells. The author's page moves on: it settles an interruption in a line, skips a finished transaction, changes
  place, brings in a named third person, carries debts, dates and streets. A machine page stays in the scene it was
  handed and plays it out; a plan page adds an arranged close (a closing gesture, a question raised and withheld, a
  reversal). None of the tells is about period vocabulary.

How to apply: this is the measure for any mechanism that claims a page reads like an author's; it has to beat the
plain arm's 3.22. A scene shaped by the plan has to be read for the arranged close. Compare
[[wbab4_plan_ranks_far_better_not_a_win_by_rule_2026_11_15]], where the same plan won against other machine pages:
better than the plain machine page and further from the author are both true.

Limits: one model family wrote and judged; the next version needs passages the judges cannot name.
