Does writing five lines before a one-page scene, and making one cutting pass after, give a better page? The rule was
registered before any page was written. Six writers an arm (one OpenAI model at normal settings), three new one-page
tasks, 36 pages, four blind judges.

- Pages written with `turn.py plan` ranked 4.78 against 8.22 without it (1 best of 12), one-sided p 0.0004. A second
  blind reader from another model family, ranks saved before the key was opened, gave 4.06 against 8.94. Four of the
  six plan writers are ahead of every control writer.
- By the rule it is not a win: the rule also required no more misfit flags than the control, and one plan page
  carries a word that three judges flagged (3 flags against 0).

What weakens it: plan pages are 25 words shorter (322 against 347), though the pages of the next test
([[wbab5_random_draw_on_plan_gave_nothing_2026_11_15]]) are as short and did not win; one model family wrote and
judged; the plan was built from reading earlier judged pages; one-page scenes only; no person ranked a page. Two of
four judges did not prefer the plan on task 1. The repeat with new tasks is
[[wbab6_plan_wins_by_rule_second_reader_sees_no_gap_2026_11_16]].

How to apply: use the plan as a provisional step when drafting a scene, labelled as not having met its rule. A separate
test found the same plan makes a page less like a published author's next page (the closing beat is arranged rather than
found), so the gain is against other machine pages, not toward published prose.
