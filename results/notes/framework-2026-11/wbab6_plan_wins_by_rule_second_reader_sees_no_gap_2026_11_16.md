Question this answers: does `turn.py plan` (five lines written before a scene is drafted, one cutting pass after) make a better page, or was the WBAB4 gap made by shorter pages and familiar tasks?

Answer, 16 Nov 2026 (WBAB6, registered before any page; rule unchanged from WBAB4): by the judges, yes. Three new tasks, both arms held to 320-340 words, six Codex writers an arm, four blind Codex judges.

- Mean rank of a page (1 best of 12): plan 4.76, no tool 8.24; 271 of 324 pairs; p 0.0001. Misfit flags 2 against 10. Words 326 against 332. Both clauses of the rule met: a win.
- Every judge alone puts the plan ahead. By writer, 35 of 36 pairs (exact p 0.002), so it does not rest on treating a writer's three pages as independent. The gap is there on both machines.
- WBAB4 had 4.78 against 8.22. With length held the gap did not shrink (3.47 against 3.44), so length was not the cause.
- Failed predictions: the plan cost 1.67 times the tokens (limit set at 1.5); two plan writers needed a second run.

The doubt to carry: I ranked the same 36 pages blind before reading the key and saw NO gap (plan 6.39, no tool 6.61); my order agreed with the judges' at -0.12, 0.10, 0.10 while the judges agree with each other at 0.55-0.73. The judges are one model, the model that wrote the pages. Either one reader's order over 36 near-alike pages is noise, or the judging model rewards what the plan produces and another reader does not. No person has ranked the pages.

What would change it: a person's blind ranking of ten or twelve of the pages; judges of a different model; a test that removes one plan line at a time.

How to apply: for a drafted scene, run the plan once and write its five lines first; do not claim more than "one model's judges prefer it". Not evidence about a chapter.
