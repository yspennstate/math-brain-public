The writing brain has three wings, separate but linked: literature, script and mathematics.

Rule cards. Each card states one rule, with the editor's words it came from, the date, what to look for and, where a
line pattern can catch it, a bad and a better example. A pattern is compiled case-blind and must fire on the card's
bad example and stay silent on its better one; a self-test runs those controls both ways. Repeats of a rule are
folded into one card. A careful reading found that a long run of editorial notes holds a few hundred distinct rules,
not thousands: about 250 cards after every note had been read. Ten cards carry a check counted over a whole passage
(turns inward, sense words, speech with no named speaker, whether the hour holds through a scene), with limits
measured on 523 novels and stories.

Craft cards, the second kind. These are counted from the books, not taken from anyone's notes, and are kept apart.
Each carries a figure (measured), a reading (an agent's judgement), limits and a check. Examples by era, using the
middle book of each era (before 1850, 1850 to 1899, 1900 and after, the last mostly before 1925):
- a chapter's last sentence: 28, 19, 15 words;
- chapters ending on a sentence of eight words or fewer: 9, 18, 27 in a hundred;
- a spoken line: 15, 12, 11 words;
- an adverb beside the speech tag: 4.8, 11.8, 16.7 in a hundred.
The same direction holds on books with a catalogue year and on books with an estimated year. A ratio of sentences
that state (open on There was or It was, hand the point over after a colon) to sentences that hold back (open on a
when or if clause, not-this-but-that, main clause last) has a middle value of 0.49 per 9,000-word stretch on the
whole shelf and 0.67 from 1900 on; one stretch in a hundred from 1900 on is above 2.58. Ten more craft cards were
read by hand rather than counted (six ways a chapter begins, five ways it ends, six ways a description is entered,
four ways talk is carried, and on the script side six ways a play opens, seven ways it ends, five things a stage
direction does); each names three passages to read and says that it is a reading, not a count.

The shelf. Public-domain texts only, taken from a project release in batches; films and in-copyright books are
catalogue rows, never text (and Project Gutenberg's main site bars bulk fetching). Works are cut into passage,
sentence-pattern and shape nodes, tens of thousands of them, with roughly 2,900 novels and stories carrying passages
at the last count.

The hook. On a Write or Edit to a chapter, script or .tex file it runs every pattern card over the new text and prints
the cards that fire with the matched words; on a chapter of 80 sentences or more it also shows the craft cards the
page sits furthest outside, one a page. It never blocks: a card is a hint to weigh. Tools around it: recall before a
scene, an after-page check, a viewer for what the masters did at a given role (opening, ending), and a draft laid
beside the shelf.

Lessons from building it.
- Measure like against like. A shelf mixing verse, philosophy and scripture with novels stretches every range;
  measure a novel against novels and stories.
- Measure a chapter against stretches of its own size. A 9,000-word chapter swings far more than a 150,000-word book;
  against whole books a test chapter was marked on nine patterns, against 9,000-word stretches on four.
- Catalogue years are often edition or e-text years (2021 for an eighteenth-century satire), so a year is believed
  only inside the author's life or up to 1930; other years are estimated from the author's dates and marked as such.
- Read a sample by hand before a count becomes a card. Every first sampling found faults: headings and epigraphs taken
  as first sentences, "while" counted as a time word, "began" as a speech tag. A figure pushed before a filter fix
  (first sentence 23, 21, 18 words) became 21, 21, 18 after it, and its card was withdrawn by the builder's own rule.
- Cut at the source. About a third of chapter-end nodes carried the back matter of the volume and a fifth of opening
  nodes the front matter; the cutter now stops at the last THE END or FINIS, cuts at a back heading and skips a
  preface. Re-cutting 3,028 books changed 569 openings and 662 endings. A third of sampled play nodes were editors'
  notes, the same fault in the play cutter.
- A complaint about tone may not be found by the first count tried. In one case named feelings sat at the middle of
  the shelf; the state-to-hold-back ratio, a sum of shapes already on the shelf and read by hand first, was the count
  that matched.
- Reading a .tar.gz: one pass with a dict of wanted members; per-member extraction seeks and never finishes.
