Three questions a reviser asks of a draft chapter, each answered by counting the same thing in published fiction
first. The tools are `turn/numbers_sheet.py`, `creative/sentence_repeat_rate.py` and `creative/paragraph_openers.py`
in `framework/writing_brain/`. The drafts are chapters of one manuscript written with language models.

**Numbers (15 Nov).** `numbers_sheet.py` lists every number in a chapter by the thing it counts, the clock times, and
each number that counts four or more different things. On 401 openings of published fiction (authors born 1880 to
1945, 2,000 to 13,000 words) the median opening states 1.8 counted numbers above three per thousand words (95th
percentile 4.5, highest 10.1), and its most used number counts 3 different things (95th percentile 6; 4 of 401 reach
ten). Two draft first chapters stated 9.8 and 9.3 per thousand, with one number counting 10 and 13 different things.
A second count with a single raw pattern agreed (10 and 14). After one pass that cut the figures the story does not
turn on, the first draft read 7.0 per thousand with its favourite number on 7 things. Reading: keep a figure the story
turns on; cut a figure that only says a thing is big when the sentence already shows it; say a repeated figure once.
No blind reader has compared the text before and after the pass, and the published sample holds little science
fiction, where a world-building chapter may sit high for good reason.

**A sentence written twice (16 Nov).** The paper check's repeat rule moved to prose: a sentence of 14 or more words
sharing 70 per cent of its word pairs with a sentence three or more back in the same chapter. In 21 draft chapters
none of 2,801 sentences repeats one. In 25 published novels cut into pieces of the drafts' median chapter length,
5 of 33,708 do (a refrain in *Tristram Shandy* four times, an echoed line in *The Brothers Karamazov*), so the rule
fires on real repeats. At the novels' rate the drafts would show 0.4, so this rules out only a rate six times the
novels' or more. The drafts are not repetitive in this way, and the check is not printed on a writer's page.

**Paragraphs opening on the same word (16 Nov).** Runs of three or more prose paragraphs (eight words or more, not
dialogue) with the same first word. Drafts: 4.7 paragraphs in such runs per 100. Novels: median 3.3, and 7 of 25 at
or above the drafts. Inside the published range, so not printed. In 15 of 21 draft chapters the commonest paragraph
opener is "The"; draft paragraphs run about 59 words against about 120 in these mostly nineteenth-century novels,
which may be the era rather than the writer.

What would change these readings: a blind reader who prefers the cut chapter (numbers), or a published sample from
the writer's own decade and genre (all three).
