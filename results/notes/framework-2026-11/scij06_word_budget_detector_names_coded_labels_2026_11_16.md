Follow-up to [[scij04_distinct_topics_no_effect_judge_keys_on_full_fact_coverage_2026_11_16]]. Writers turn a
synthetic data task into a short science piece; a blind judge of another model family says which pieces a machine
wrote, against pieces by people.

This round gave each writer a budget of 40 to 70 words per fact kept. The pieces were 34 to 53 percent shorter, and
the detector still called about 96 percent of them machine-written (human pieces 4 to 14 percent). Three rounds each
removed the tell the judge named (using every fact handed over, then padding per fact), and each time it named the
next. In this round 43 of its 72 stated reasons point at the task's coded labels (site S07, cohort C3). That is a
property of the test material, not of the prose: no human control piece carries such codes. Print-ready ratings fell
with the shorter pieces (4.17 to 3.92 plain, 4.06 to 3.64 with the writing brain). Writing brain against plain: no
effect (permutation p = 292/924).

How to apply: before any further writer arm, map the codes to natural names deterministically per task and apply the
map to the briefs, the data and the grading facts alike; until then this test measures the material, not the writer.
A blind judge's stated reason is a lead to check, and when removing it only brings the next one, look at what every
machine piece shares that no human piece could.
