Does the reflex a_refused_mesh_claim_did_not_stop_the_write fire on the hazard it names (a claim whose refusal does not stop the write that follows)? Mostly not.

Replayed its live trigger (instincts.tsv) over every Bash call with a mesh claim or assert in the second machine transcripts of 25 Oct to 8 Nov: 8,665 calls, 2,018 fires. A quote-aware read of the first claim statement splits them:

- gated by `|| exit N` or `|| { ...; exit N; }`: 58
- inside an `if`/`until` test: 55
- followed by `rc=$?; [ $rc -eq 0 ] && ...`: 100
- followed by `&&`: 1,290. A seeded sample of 12, judged by hand: 0 on-domain. In every one the write the claim guards sits inside the `&&` chain; what comes after the chain is a release, a log line or a readback.
- followed by `;` or a line break (the named hazard): 492. A sample of 12 had 2 test-gated (the class above, added after it), and 5 of the other 10 were on-domain (remember, embedding build, PREDICTIONS write run with the claim code only printed). The rest wrote nothing on the claimed resource.
- other: 23

Why it misfires: the trigger is a regex, not a shell reader. A `printf '%s\n'` format reads as a line break, a `;` inside a quoted status line reads as a separator, and a heredoc's lines read as statements, so `claim ... || exit 3; assert ... && printf '%s\n' "...; ..." >> file` fires (W111 round 0801, case 11).

Rough corpus precision is about 0.12 (246 of 2,018), against 0.49 on its 65 hand-judged fires in reflex_precision.py. Some of that gap is sampling: the sheet draws from the top-10 reflexes of a day. Some is judging: earlier verdicts may have counted chains as on-domain. Both numbers come from small samples.

Proposal (queue W112 (k), the author's word before it goes live): a Python post-check beside _writes_shipped_code in instinct_prime.py that drops this reflex's fire unless the first claim statement is truly ungated. classify() in the check script is the reference. Expected: 2,018 fires become 492 (-76%) at about 0.5 precision, losing 0 of 12 sampled on-domain fires (95% upper bound 26% of the chain class). The reflex is in the REFILL_AB trial, which would then compare arms on the narrower set. mesh_claim_never_chained_with_mutate and denied_claim_gates_the_next_command name the same hazard and co-fire on the same commands, so the post-check could serve all three.

Probe for the one-command question (8 Nov 08:18, two throwaway agents on topic:probe/5f485802/*, released): a refused claim exits 3; a two-resource claim with one held exits 3 and acquires neither (all or nothing); so `claim ... || exit 3; mutate` cannot run the mutation on a refusal, and the separate-command rule of mesh_claim_never_chained_with_mutate is stricter than the hazard needs. Neither form catches two things: a pipe between the claim and the `|| exit 3` (the gate tests the pipe's last command), and a resource typed without its namespace (a bare word is claimed as a path under the working directory and exits 0).
