R9, 16 November 2026: a sentence check for the narrator's verdict (the narrator rates or sums up what the page
just showed: a sentence that says the memory was worth keeping, that something was a pity or a miracle,
or what the book is, and hindsight forms). Six regular expressions written from the eight CH1S readers' notes on pass 59,
registered in R9_PREREG.md (710fc426) before scoring.

Result: NOT SHOWN. On the current chapter one (4 readings) it flagged 3 of 439 sentences and 0 were faulted by a
reader; on pass 45 (16 readings) 3 of 560 and 2 faulted (corrected 16 Nov: 1 was scored against four of the sixteen readings). Bars were 2 and 3 faulted at lift 2. On the text it was
written from it scored 8 of 8, which is what fitting looks like.

Why: the fault is a relation between a sentence and the paragraph before it (the scene already showed it), and
the sentence alone seldom carries a mark. A regex written from eight sentences finds those eight.

Do instead: a reader's question that sees the preceding paragraph ("list the sentences where the narrator rates
or sums up what the paragraph before showed"), scored the same way against blind readers asked nothing of the
kind. R7 (narrator's maxim) is still the only sentence check whose flags fall on what blind readers fault.

Also seen: three of the author's lines that eight readers quoted as weakest in the second chapter (5, 2 and 6 of
8) are in the current chapter too, and none of its four readers listed them there. A sentence in both texts of a
pair is listed under one at most, so weak-sentence counts in a paired read are lower bounds and depend on where
the reader files the line.

Size of the doubt: one model's readings; thin bars. What would change it: the shelves run through the forms, or
a reading by a second model family.
