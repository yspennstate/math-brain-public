The follow-up to the word-palette test, restricted to stories and to a small offer.

Design. Forty story briefs, Codex writers. Arm A plain; arm S given three word-walk words, each with its sense and a
line from a master writer using it, and told to use none if a plain word is better. Judged in both orders by fresh
Opus 5.5 and fresh Codex sessions, no tools, no hooks; the order key kept off the repository with its sha256
published. The rule fixed in advance: S helps if it wins at least 26 of 40.

Result: no detectable effect in either family. Opus S 18, A 7, ties 15, sense S minus A +0.18 (p 0.16). Codex S 19,
A 7, ties 14, +0.11 (p 0.15). A sign test on decided pairs alone would give p 0.04 and 0.03, but that was not the
rule, and the lean has a plain explanation:
- S pages were longer than A in 39 of 40 pairs (median 354 against 332 words), and the longer page won 18 of 25 and
  18 of 26 decided pairs.
- S used an offered word on only 11 of 40 pages (13 uses of 120 words offered). Twelve of the 18 Opus wins for S, and
  15 of the 19 Codex wins, were pages that used none of the offered words.
- The families agree weakly: 49 of 80 items (kappa 0.19); the same pair winner on 18 of 40, opposite on 7.

How to apply: a word palette adds length, not better words, so it is not a quality mechanism. To test the words
themselves, hold length equal (a word cap on both arms, or length-matched pairs). Before reading any A/B preference on
a writing prompt, check the length ratio across arms.
