The tool. A figures sheet counts habits of narration per 10,000 words (speech left out) and prints each beside the
same count on a reference shelf; where the draft stands above three quarters of the shelf it lists every place.
The first reference was the first 11,000 words of forty published novels. Quartiles on it (quarter, median, three
quarters, highest):
- a clause hung on a sentence with ", which": 8.2, 11.3, 18.3, 59.5;
- "like" before a noun: 3.7, 5.4, 10.2, 27.6;
- "as if" or "as though": 1.2, 6.2, 9.5, 30.4;
- a comparison with "the way" or "as you": 0.0, 0.0, 1.1, 7.6 (an idiom younger than most of the forty);
- the comparison forms together: 5.5, 13.3, 20.1, 58.9.

One draft chapter had 30.1 ", which" clauses per 10,000 words, above 37 of the 40. Twenty readings had not shown it,
because no single clause is a fault. A pass recast 13 of 29 (keeping the dry asides and those carrying a needed fact)
and brought it to 16.6.

Why a second shelf. Two new rows on how a paragraph lands (ending on a short sentence; a paragraph of one short
sentence) put twenty draft chapters at four times the forty older openings. Before reporting that as a fault, every
row was counted again on the openings (words 2,000 to 13,000) of 387 novels and stories in the corpus by authors born
1880 to 1945 (2,975,499 words of narration). There the chapters were ordinary (short closer 7.5 against a later median
of 8.6). The rows would have been a false charge.

Works of the 387 above the drafts' pooled rate, per 10,000 words of narration:
- habits of the drafts on both shelves: "the way" / "as you" comparison 19.3, no work above; a denial followed by the
  same subject 7.2, none above; negation written out 80.5, 4 above; ", which" 17.7, 12 above; comparisons together
  38.6, 18 above;
- between: a sentence opening on Not 1.8, 23 above; a short "It is." sentence 9.1, 47; "had" 158.6, 48;
- habits of the period, not faults: three short sentences in a row 22.7, 87 above (later median 10.8; the forty older
  openings 2.4); one-line paragraph 14.2, 93; short closer 7.5, 108; "like" 11.2, 121; "as if" 8.1, 136.

The tool now prints both shelves beside each count and lists places only when the draft is above the three-quarter
mark of both. On the chapter summary, rows starred for short runs fell from 20 of 20 chapters to 8. Notes already
sent that rested on short runs against the older shelf were withdrawn; those on "the way", the denial pair,
negation and ", which" stand.

How to apply. Before calling a counted habit a fault, count it on a shelf of the draft's own period. A reference a
century old measures the date as well as the draft. A rate is never a reason to cut a particular sentence: read every
instance and keep the ones that earn their place.

Limits. Both shelves are public domain, mostly before 1970 and much of the later one magazine fiction; whole books
instead of openings might move every row. A modern reference would move the "the way" row most.
