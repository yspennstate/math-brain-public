The agent mesh (`~/.agents/coordination/agent_mesh.sqlite3`, 605 MB) runs a rollback journal on purpose (PROTOCOL.md; every `Mesh()` sets `journal_mode=DELETE`). In that mode a writer that is ready to commit takes PENDING and waits for every current reader to finish, and no new reader gets in meanwhile. One slow reader (a cold full scan of the 416 MB messages table, e.g. `inbox --include-acked`, or the checklist's `session_key LIKE '%sid%'` scan) therefore shuts out every hook in every session for as long as it runs. On 11 Nov a 90 s probe at 19:56 saw the file locked 0.3 pct of the time, yet a watcher caught spans of 26.2, 17.6 and 8.4 s at 19:59 with ordinary `claim`/`release`/`send` CLI calls 10-27 s old queued behind them; the live inbox hook's log had 284 `read failed: database is locked` and 53 `register failed` between 16:00Z and 18:00Z, in bursts of 10-41 a minute.

What a hook should do about it: wait briefly and skip, never stack waits. Since 11 Nov 20:03 `live_inbox_hook.py` waits 0.3 s per mesh step (`LIVE_INBOX_MESH_WAIT_S`, was 0.6), reads before it writes, and after a locked read skips both the registration pulse (retried a minute later) and the unacknowledged-mail read. On a temp mesh held under `BEGIN EXCLUSIVE` a prompt cost 0.83 s against 3.86 s before; unlocked delivery, the delivered stamp and the next look after the lock were unchanged. Nothing is lost by skipping: a failed look is retried at the next tool call, and mail shown but not stamped is stamped then. The checklist hook got the same treatment the same evening (cached lookup, 0.25 s wait): [[checklist_mesh_lookup_cached_ten_minutes_1800_to_0_ms_2026_11_11]].

The fix at the source is WAL mode (readers and writers stop blocking each other), but it cannot be switched on from one place. Measured on a temp file: an old `Mesh()` init running `PRAGMA journal_mode=DELETE` returns 'delete' even while a WAL connection is open, and turns the file back to DELETE as soon as it is the only connection. Every code path that opens the mesh (agent_mesh.py, the hooks' read-only connections, long-lived waiters) has to change in one landing, and read-only WAL readers on Windows need the -shm file present. Until then, shrinking the hot tables (`purge`, outside a busy hour) and keeping full scans out of hot paths is what helps.

Status: the hook change is verified on a temp mesh and live (md5 c47d57014445, brain a0d6e4cd); the convoy mechanism is the standard SQLite rollback-journal behaviour and fits every observation, but I did not catch the individual slow reader by name.

Measured after it landed (11 Nov 20:06-21:00, 409 prompts, hook_profile): the in-process prompt chain fell from a median of 1,885 ms to 882 (p90 3,845 to 1,965); the live inbox hook's p90 from 1,467 ms to 69.

What the long reader is NOT (11 Nov 20:40-21:15, a second watcher recording every young python process at each span): the agent desk (its agents scan is 47 ms, its mail query 12 ms, and it is itself locked out at times), release() and the handoff check (all indexed, under 7 ms), slow commits (30 small commits in the mesh folder, max 29 ms even with the box in ui_freeze distress), the plan-index build at Mesh() init (both indexes exist), the four live inbox waiters (indexed, one look a second), the checklist's flush child (sleeps its 60 s window, never opens the mesh). The events table helps read a span: a writer's event row carries the time inside its transaction, so a claim stamped 19:02:15Z that committed at 19:02:28 waited 13 s for a reader; one 17 s span (19:08:48Z) had no event at all, so its writer was one that logs none (a delivered stamp). Read events with the indexed `happened_at` range only.

A busy timeout is not a wall-clock wait on this machine (found 11 Nov 21:2x, after the first landing). On a copy of the mesh under a held `BEGIN EXCLUSIVE`, one locked read with Python's `sqlite3.connect(..., timeout=W)` cost 0.32-0.36 s at W=0.05, 0.46-0.49 s at 0.1, 0.81-0.91 s at 0.3 and 1.23 s at 0.6; at W=0 it cost 30 ms and at 0.001 it cost 79 ms (measured with the box loaded: commit charge 88-91 pct, every core busy, a bare PowerShell start 12 s; a quiet box may overshoot less, the shape is the same). SQLite's busy handler counts its retry sleeps at their nominal 1, 2, 5, 10 ... ms, each real sleep rounds up to the Windows timer tick, and a locked read runs the wait twice. That is why the live inbox hook's tool-result rows clustered at 800-900 ms (39 of 278 since 20:15) even after its wait went to 0.3 s. The fix: connect with `timeout=0` and retry the statement every 20 ms until a `time.monotonic()` deadline. Landed 21:31 in `live_inbox_hook.py` (reads; the Mesh() writes take busy 50 ms, about 0.3 s real; live md5 d54a616ca6a3) and 21:34 in `task_checklist_hook.py`'s mesh lookup (md5 ad44f7728bee). Tested on the copy: the same rows as the old query unlocked, 0.27-0.32 s under a held lock (was 0.76-0.88), a lock lifted at 0.1-0.15 s still read through, and a non-lock error raised at once. Not reached: `frontier_digest.py` mesh_task (timeout 0.5, so about 1.1 s real when locked; read at most hourly). Any short SQLite wait on this box, on any database, has the same overshoot.

First live read (19:31-19:48Z, against 18:00-19:31Z): no inbox-hook row over 600 ms (was 39 tool results, 45 prompts, 5 Stops), max 380 ms, p90 811 to 325 ms on tool results. The medians rose (prompts 6.8 to 55 ms), but not from the reads: a live A/B of the old and new read, alternating 60 times, gave medians 8.6 and 7.0 ms with no retry needed, and the hook's parts cost 2 ms each. The window held the 19:44Z brain-high broadcast to every agent, so most looks were deliveries with a stamp write, and the shorter write wait raised the stamp failure rate from 9 to 31 pct (a failed stamp is not shown again; the next look retries it). Expected write cost per stamp is about the same as before (0.3 s at 31 pct against 0.85 s at 9 pct). Re-measure on a window without a broadcast before touching MESH_WRITE_BUSY_MS.

Scar from the hunt: a diagnostic `GROUP BY recipient` over messages on the live mesh took 35 s and made a 29.8 s span of its own. Never run a full scan of messages (or a count of events) on the live file; copy it or use an indexed range.
