Does the brain still beat no brain when a fault needs two scar memories joined? Only a little, and not past the bar: at five tool calls, every arm sits near the floor (DIAG15, 8 Nov 2026).

Items: a Codex writer paired 28 of 48 freshly drawn scars into 14 two-cause symptoms. 11 pairs survived the overlap and clue checks; 9 of those needed a rewrite after the first clue read. Plus DIAG4's 6 controls. Arms and budget as DIAG14: bare, each and grep, on Codex gpt-6.1-sol high and Claude opus-5-5 high, 3 runs each, 5 tool calls. Store brain-graph-full-2026-11-07. A pair scores 2 when both causes are named, 1 for one.

Points of 66, valid runs:
- Codex: bare 0, each 6, grep 6.
- Claude: bare 0, each 7, grep 9 (one valid run, scaled).
- The bar was +11; no arm clears it.
- No run named both causes of any pair. Every control in the brain arms was right; Codex bare got 16 of 18.

Part A agrees: plain recall held both memories of a pair in its top 40 for 0 of 14 contexts.

Exploratory: the 2 pairs that needed no rewrite earned 14 of the brain arms' 22 points, and the 9 rewritten pairs earned 8. Rewriting a symptom to strip its clue also seems to cut its tie to the memory. Next time, drop flagged items and write more, rather than rewriting them.

What it means: the brain's edge on single-cause faults (DIAG13 and DIAG14, [[diag14_second_fresh_draw_brain_beats_bare_lines_no_agent_lift_2026_11_08]]) does not carry over to joins at this budget. A fairer join test gives more calls, or asks for the second cause after the first is named.
