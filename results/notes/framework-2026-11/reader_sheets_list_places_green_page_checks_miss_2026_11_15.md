The measurement. On one chapter of about 10,700 words, the 28 pattern checks of the writing brain's literature wing
all passed. A read from the first line to the last then changed 22 sentences:
- a sentence whose meaning arrives only on the second reading, 9;
- a fact told twice some pages apart, 4;
- a pointer word with its noun out of sight, 3;
- a connective doubled in one sentence, 2;
- the same spelled-out quantity twice in one paragraph, 2;
- two spans of years that cannot both stand, 2.
The largest group has no program check; only a reader finds it.

What was built from it: reading sheets. Each prints places for a reader to hold against each other; none of them
says a place is wrong.
- R1 a doubled connective; R2 a quantity twice in a paragraph; R3 a short sentence ending on a bare pointer word (a
  list to read down: 3 of 15 were faults); R4 two far-apart sentences sharing four rare words (catches a fact told
  twice in the same words, misses it in different words). A `--sheet` mode prints every quantity in the draft by
  unit, which caught a number given two ways in two chapters.
- Totals (`--totals`): every sentence that speaks for all or none of a group (everybody, nobody, not one, she
  alone, the only one, of the forty of us). Reading the rows together found a contradiction that eight passes of
  reading had let stand (a crowd that "all" did a thing, and eight lines on one member who "alone had not"), and a
  writer's default beat: 28 of 51 rows turned on one word, six of them opening sentences within thirty lines.
  Rule: a word that carries half the rows of a sheet is a habit.
- R5 the repeated opener: a word other than an article, pronoun or name opening three or more sentences within
  twelve lines. A name is any word the draft never writes in lower case; "capitalised mid-sentence three times"
  let names through. Across twenty other chapters: 0 to 7 rows each, mostly Then and That, often meant.
- `--speeches N`: every stretch of more than N spoken words (55 by default, about four printed lines) with nothing
  told inside it. About half of published pages carry a speech over 40 words, so this checks a draft against its
  own plan, not against the shelf. The repair that read best was one thing happening inside the speech that carries
  something of its own; an adverb added to the speech tag does not count.
- R8 the echo: a sentence opening on the word or two words the last one ended on ("...wherever there was. There was
  a lamp on the table."). The eye takes the second sentence for a repeat and goes back. A repeated cry and a join on
  a pronoun or on then or there alone are not counted. On forty published openings (11,000 words each) the rate is
  0.5 in 10,000 words, more than half have none, and about half of the published places are the figure used on
  purpose. In a later-period shelf (below) it is 0.87, a habit of that period. A place is a fault only where the
  second sentence was written as an explanation of the last word of the first; fold it into the first sentence or
  open the second on its own subject.

Size of the doubt. One chapter, one reader who was also its writer, and small counts; the reference shelf is public
domain. What would change this: a second reader's count on the same page, or the same sheets on another writer's
chapter finding nothing a read had not already found.

How to apply: after the page checks pass, run the sheets, read each list in one sitting, and then read the page from
start to finish anyway.
