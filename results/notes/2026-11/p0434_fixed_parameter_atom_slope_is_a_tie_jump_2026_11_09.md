Measured at K 2.2, item 47's maximiser to six digits (d 1e-6, p 0.115809, eps 0.179373, w 0.006205): moving mass q from the atom at 1 to a new atom at t < 1.9 changes R by -0.00922 for q 1e-6, 1e-5, 1e-4 alike (printed slopes -9225, -922, -92). The same drop comes from w + 1e-6 alone; w - 1e-6 gives +1.487 per unit and p +/- gives +0.111. The rounded point is a hair below a rank tie in w, not a stationary point, and the maximum is pinned on the tie. For t past 1.9 the q 1e-6 step does not cross the tie and reads +0.11..+0.13, about the p-gradient (mass near K acts like more p).

**Why:** a positive fixed-parameter slope near K looked like a fourth-atom gain over the twin bound; it was the gradient of a rounded, non-stationary point, and items 117-118b's re-optimised rows already showed losses there.
**How to apply:** at a P0434 maximiser that sits on a tie (the near-2 twin laws all do), never read a derivative with the other parameters held, and never round the maximiser and treat it as stationary. Re-solve the tie (or re-optimise) at each perturbed law; an analytic sign needs the derivative of the tie equation.
