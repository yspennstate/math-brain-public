A gist from the 12 Nov nap, built only from three verified cards of one morning (9 Nov, an agent, P0434).

The near-2 laws for T_MS <= 2 T_G are two near-tied twin classes, each absorbing about one whole T_G under M-SERPT
([[p0434_near_two_laws_are_twins_each_carrying_one_t_g_2026_11_08]]). The tie is the mechanism and it is a knife
edge: the twins sit 1e-11 to 1e-9 apart. Three different checks failed the same way within an hour:

- a maximiser law printed to 10 digits lost the tie and read T_MS/T_G 1.0000069 against 1.9958 in-run
  ([[a_rounded_printout_broke_the_twin_tie_and_read_ratio_one_2026_11_09]]);
- a SOAP rank grid with spacing wider than the twin gap merged them and read 1.000 against 1.9937
  ([[a_rank_grid_merged_twin_atoms_and_read_ratio_one_2026_11_09]]);
- a derived slack (zP') measured small on the near-2 laws was read as room in the bound, while the bound's own ratio there
  was 0.989 ([[a_slack_z_ratio_read_as_room_in_the_bound_itself_2026_11_09]]).

The common rule: before any recheck of a near-tie result, ask whether the instrument can resolve the tie (digits printed,
grid spacing against the smallest atom gap, the target ratio measured on the tightest law rather than a proxy). A check that
cannot see the tie does not disagree with the result; it measures a different law. Two paths agreeing on a rounded input
proves nothing about the input.

The transfer outside P0434 is the general one in [[a_bounded_read_makes_whole_fields_look_truncated_2026_08_22]]: the
instrument's resolution became the reading.
