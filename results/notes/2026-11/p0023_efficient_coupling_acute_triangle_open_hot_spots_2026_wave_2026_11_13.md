P0023, rank 48 on the 200 list. Source: Krzysztof Burdzy, "My favorite open problems", Problem 4 "Efficient couplings in
acute triangles", https://sites.math.washington.edu/~burdzy/open_mathjax.php (text read 13 Nov). D is a triangle with
all angles below pi/2, and mu_2 > 0 is the second Neumann eigenvalue of the Laplacian. Can one construct reflected
Brownian motions X, Y in D from different starting points so that the meeting time tau satisfies
P(tau > t) <= exp(-(mu_2 - eps) t) for large t, for every eps > 0? Background: Burdzy and Kendall, "Efficient Markovian
couplings: examples and counterexamples", Ann. Appl. Probab. 2000.

Status 13 Nov 2026: open.
- Burdzy's page still lists Problem 4.
- Convention caveat (200-list note, and our 6 Sep pass): the page calls mu_2 a Laplacian eigenvalue. If reflected
  Brownian motion has generator Delta/2, the spectral rate of the process is mu_2/2. A factor-two mismatch in the
  convention is not a solution, so read the rate in Burdzy-Kendall's convention.
- Lower half, our 6 Sep pass P0023-A001 (codex_20h): for a bounded Neumann eigenfunction phi with P_t phi =
  e^{-lambda t} phi and any coupling with meeting time T, |P_t phi(x) - P_t phi(y)| <= 2 ||phi||_inf P(T > t). Taking
  phi(x) != phi(y) gives P(T > t) >= c e^{-lambda t}, with lambda the generator's eigenvalue. So no coupling beats the
  spectral rate. The open part is a coupling that comes within eps of it in every acute triangle.
- My arXiv check on 13 Nov: "efficient coupling" with reflect*, "Markovian coupling" with Neumann, coupling with
  "reflected Brownian" and triangle, mirror coupling, maximal coupling with reflected Brownian. Nothing on efficient
  couplings in triangles. Nearby work on the eigenfunction side, which the mirror-coupling approach uses:
  - Judge and Mondal, "Euclidean triangles have no hot spots", arXiv 1802.01800 (Ann. Math. 2020), and Siudeja, "Hot
    spots conjecture for a class of acute triangles", Math. Z. 280 (2015), arXiv 1308.3005.
  - An October 2026 wave on hot spots. Burdzy, arXiv 2610.12622, and Banuelos, arXiv 2610.32920, extend a no-interior-
    extremum theorem for smooth simply connected planar domains to rough simply connected domains with a simple first
    positive eigenvalue. Both take the smooth-domain theorem as an external input credited to OpenAI, and Burdzy's
    abstract says the paper was generated by ChatGPT. Atar and Burdzy, arXiv 2610.19908, give a starlike Lipschitz
    counterexample in 3D. None of the three mentions efficient couplings. I grepped 2610.12622 and 2610.32920 for
    "efficient coupl", "acute triangle" and Burdzy-Kendall; the only hit was the Siudeja reference.
- My guess at the route, not a result: Burdzy-Kendall tie efficient couplings to how the second eigenfunction separates
  the domain. In a triangle the eigenfunction has no interior hot spots (Judge-Mondal), which is the kind of
  structure a mirror or reflection coupling can follow. But the equilateral triangle has a double mu_2, and a coupling
  rate argument has to work along the whole family of acute triangles.
