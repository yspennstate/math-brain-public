# Referee audit, 2026-10-11

This is an independent analytic audit, not external peer review. The proof uses the general Biane parametrization as stated and referenced in the fetched published source. Each potential failure below was checked.

1. **Wrong source hypothesis or time normalization.** Published PDF p.15/journal p.367: exponential quadratic moment (4.13). Both N(0,t) and S(0,t) have variance t. Source equations (2.9)--(2.14) hold for arbitrary probability measures. For delta_m the parametrization gives R=t-(u-m)^2, psi(u)=2u-m and p(x)=sqrt(4t-(x-m)^2)/(2 pi t), so the free variance really is t. No compact-support theorem is used to extend the global-mode result.
2. **Unimodality smuggled into the free proof.** None is used. Source Proposition 3.4 explicitly permits failure of free unimodality for every time, even with the exponential moment. Only uniqueness of the highest peak is proved. Smaller distant peaks remain possible.
3. **Global maximum fails to exist or escapes.** The dominated-convergence estimate F(u,delta^2)->0 yields eta(u)->0. Jensen provides a positive comparison height. Attainment then follows by continuity on a compact interval, and the Chebyshev estimate confines every maximizer to [-B,B] uniformly in time.
4. **Incorrect curvature sign.** The exact polynomial numerator is z^2(15+17z+6z^2); it is nonnegative for z>=0. This is the finite algebraic step in Algebra.lean. It is applied to F at the FIXED maximal height R*, so no derivatives of R(u) enter F_uu. E q^2=u^2+v<=A, and t>7A ensures R*>6A, giving strict negativity. Strict concavity on the full interval rules out two stationary points at the same maximal height.
5. **Rational derivatives or tails unjustified.** For every fixed R>0, the needed u and R derivatives are bounded rational functions of q=u-X, giving differentiation under a finite measure locally uniformly in positive R. The implicit equation has F_R=-E(R+q^2)^-2<0. All global maxima have positive height, so they are interior stationary points.
6. **Coordinate/physical-mode confusion.** The free subordination coordinate moves first by -2mu_3/t; transport by psi supplies the final -3mu_3/t. The explicit bound controls the transport term separately. Atomic roots check both equations and use the spatial map only at the end.
7. **Illegal analytic continuation for unbounded laws.** No continuation and no analytic implicit function theorem is used. Taylor remainder inequalities hold for ALL z>=0. A finite seventh absolute moment dominates the remainders uniformly since u=O(1/t) and r>=1/2. The source hypothesis supplies that moment.
8. **Local critical point mistaken for global classical mode.** The centered source theorem gives unimodality at T_c. The source supplies a mode in [-sqrt(t)/2,sqrt(t)/2], throughout which H_h is strictly negative; any plateau of global maxima would contradict that negativity at the point. Thus the identified classical mode is unique and global.
9. **Remainder merely little-o.** Explicit Taylor bounds give O(s^3) before coefficient extraction. The sequential substitutions use u=O(s), h=O(s), r=1+O(s); each discarded term is bounded by a law-dependent constant times s^3. No third derivative of a branch is assumed.
10. **Degenerate variance and zero skewness.** v=0 is treated exactly. Symmetry forces exact modes m at large time. The zero-third-moment asymmetric four-atom law uses weights (4,5,10,1)/20 at (-2,-1,1,3), with mean=mu_3=0, mu_5=6; direct computations verify the nonzero second-order coefficients 3/4 and 30 respectively.
11. **Universal sharpness falsely asserted.** The thresholds and constants are sufficient, not optimal. The order t^-1 cannot improve for the full class because the two-atom centered example has mu_3=2. The factor six applies to the leading coefficients, not to all times or all higher orders.

## Numerical verification

`check_numeric.py`, 55-digit mpmath root-finding; output `numerics_atomic.json`. Five laws at t=10^3,10^4,10^5,10^7: point mass, symmetric Bernoulli, asymmetric Bernoulli, a rare distant outlier, and an asymmetric zero-skewness law. Both normalized errors t^3(mode - displayed second-order expansion) settle to finite values. For the asymmetric Bernoulli, they approach -2.375000133 and -137.9999322 at t=10^7. For the zero-skewness law they approach 0.5000000047 and -119.9999478. These are checks on the asymptotic remainder, not estimates of the universal error constant.

`check_unbounded.py`, two-component Gaussian mixture with unit component variance, component means -1 and 2, weights 2/3 and 1/3. This law has unbounded support and finite exponential quadratic moment for epsilon<1/2. Classical convolution is independently evaluated as a Gaussian mixture of variance t+1. Free expectations use 80 and 160 Gauss--Hermite nodes per component. Root residuals are below 3e-15. Doubling node count changes the unscaled free mode by less than 8e-16 at t=10^4. Scaled errors are sensitive to floating-point cancellation at that time; they are numerical evidence only. Both methods confirm the second-order coefficients on this unbounded law.
