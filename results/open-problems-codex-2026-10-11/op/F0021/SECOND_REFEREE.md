# Additional attempt: hostile referee report

The verdict is PARTIAL. No KPZ distribution or Airy process limit is established.

1. The chain count is for distinct sites at strictly increasing times. Independence is used only for the markings of those sites; no independence between dyadic layers is asserted. Extending the spatial count to all integer increments enlarges it. The lattice term is essential: the exact enumeration finds chains of zero entropy at the even-time sites with spatial position zero.
2. Every selected subchain has entropy no larger than its parent. This is needed both in the union bound for chains of length at least ell and in the estimates for each weight layer. It is proved by an explicit squared-difference identity and tested using exact rational arithmetic on 45,044 coarsenings.
3. A nearest-neighbour-compatible chain has entropy at most n/2, which justifies the finite dyadic entropy range. Chains that cannot be visited have zero contribution to the partition expansion. The initial-point travel cost is also bounded by the full chain entropy.
4. The infinite high weight layers are controlled by one tail threshold at each entropy level. There is no presumed deterministic maximum of the disorder. In the low layers the integer threshold is floor(h)+1, including atoms at layer boundaries and the case that this threshold exceeds n.
5. The two low-layer exponents are negative because q*kappa>5/3 and kappa<1/3. Logarithmic factors and the O((log n)^2) number of pairs do not affect this conclusion. The high-layer exponents require q>5; no alpha=5 boundary claim is made.
6. The partition expansion is exactly over subsets of large sites. A compatible subset has a unique time order and a product of bare-walk transition probabilities. That product is what makes the quadratic entropy bound valid. The argument cannot replace these probabilities by capped-polymer visit probabilities.
7. The count of all large sites is o_P(n^(1/3)). On the common high-probability event each nonempty subset contributes at most exp(-n^(1/3)/2); bounding their number by 2^N therefore proves the stated exponential bound for Z_tail-1. The result is in probability, not in expectation: the disorder still has no positive exponential moment.
8. The slowly growing-temperature corollary explicitly tracks D_n=O(n^r) in both the high-layer and low-layer bounds. The strict condition kappa+r<1/3 is used; merely keeping beta fixed in the constants would not justify its use with Hölder conjugates tending to infinity.
9. Hölder is applied inside a finite path sum, so all partition functions are finite for each realized environment without an annealed exponential moment. The decomposition is omega=min(omega,u)+(omega-u)_+, and the excess is bounded by the rare-only positive field. This remains true with an unrestricted negative tail.
10. The temperature-comparison identity uses the same fixed capped environment at both temperatures. Its entropy is relative to the uniform law on all 2^n paths; hence it is between zero and n log 2. It does not involve disorder averaging or independence of the cap and tail.
11. The resulting unconditional truncation bound is of order n^(1-r). It is not o(n^(1/3)). For 5<alpha<=6 this argument allows only r<1/18. A weaker sublinear error does not justify transferring a KPZ distribution.
12. Two deterministic examples refute proposed shortcuts: multiplicative subadditivity fails for two rewards at one site, and a bounded favourable diagonal background amplifies a terminal spike that is invisible under the bare walk. Neither example is an iid counterexample to F0021.
13. The admissible positive/negative Pareto mixture has a finite second moment and an infinite third absolute moment. It shows that a routine third-moment replacement theorem does not follow from the stated assumptions; it does not rule out a more refined method or disprove universality.
14. Exhaustive finite checks validate the subset expansion, the entropy comparison and Hölder inequalities. The Pareto experiments at alpha=5.5 show slow finite-size behaviour and are not presented as asymptotic confirmation. The proof, rather than extrapolation from those experiments, supplies the special-case conclusion.

The finite Holder check passed 200 cases, and the relative-entropy identity had maximum error 2.67e-14. The subset expansion was compared with all 256 paths in 50 environments, with maximum log-partition error 1.34e-15. These floating-point checks corroborate the algebra; the complete proofs do not depend on their tolerances.

The extended sparse experiment stored only the large sites, even at n=2^30, rather than an array of all cone sites. Its runtime was 330.64 seconds and its site counts were small (maximum mean 73.87). At alpha=5.5, kappa=0.32, the fraction failing the stated exponential bound fell from 0.265 at n=2^18 to 0.11 at n=2^30, with 200 repetitions at each size. Such finite samples still do not establish an asymptotic law; the large-n binomial generator and log-kernel computations also use floating-point arithmetic.

The remaining requirements for the actual open question are a truncation/perturbation comparison at the n^(1/3) scale in the random background and a fixed-temperature KPZ/Airy universality theorem for the resulting model. Neither is proved here.
