    <title>Transience of percolation clusters on wedges</title>
    <updated>2002-06-12T17:59:31Z</updated>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206130v1" rel="alternate" type="text/html"/>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0206130v1" rel="related" type="application/pdf" title="pdf"/>
    <summary>  We study random walks on supercritical percolation clusters on wedges in $\Z^3$, and show that the infinite percolation cluster is (a.s.) transient whenever the wedge is transient. This solves a question raised by O. Haggstrom and E. Mossel. We also show that for convex gauge functions satisfying a mild regularity condition, the existence of a finite energy flow on $Z^2$ is equivalent to the (a.s.) existence of a finite energy flow on the supercritical percolation cluster. This solves a question of C. Hoffman.</summary>

    <id>http://arxiv.org/abs/math/0110296v3</id>
    <title>Transience, Recurrence and Critical Behavior for Long-Range Percolation</title>
    <updated>2014-03-03T08:55:14Z</updated>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0110296v3" rel="alternate" type="text/html"/>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110296v3" rel="related" type="application/pdf" title="pdf"/>
    <summary>We study the behavior of the random walk on the infinite cluster of independent long range percolation in dimensions $d=1,2$, where $x$ and $y$ a re connected with probability $\simβ/\|x-y\|^{-s}$. We show that when $d&lt;s&lt;2d$ the walk is transient, and when $s\geq 2d$, the walk is recurrent. The proof of transience is based on a renormalization argument. As a corollary of this renormalization argument, we get that for every dimension $d$, if $d&lt;s&lt;2d$, then critical percolation has no infinite clusters. This result is extended to the free random cluster model. A second corollary is that when $d\geq 2$ and $d&lt;s&lt;2d$ we can erase all long enough bonds and still have an infinite cluster. The proof of recurrence in two dimensions is based on general stability results for recurrence in random electrical networks. In particular, we show that i.i.d. conductances on a recurrent graph of bounded degree yield a recurrent electrical network.</summary>
