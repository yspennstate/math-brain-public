## Two approaches not previously tried

**A. Endpoint conditional expectation and conditional Hellinger affinity (selected).** Rather than estimate the original bridge chaos expansion, average only the bulk environment, keeping the first and last time slabs. The resulting conditional partition function has an explicit Gaussian joining kernel. Its algebra and a negative control will be checked, rather than asserted to solve the problem.

## A1. Averaging the bulk: an unconditional endpoint factorization (proved)

Use the notation of ATTEMPT.md, with E denoting environment expectation and P Brownian expectation. Fix t>2l>0, and let F_(t,l) be generated by the environment in the two time slabs (0,l] and (t-l,t]. Define

    M_(t,l) = E[U_t(0) | F_(t,l)],
    Y_(t,l) = W_l(omega) W_l(omega reversed about time t).

The reversed W_l uses only the terminal slab and starts spatially at zero. Both M and Y are strictly positive, finite almost surely, and have expectation one. The positivity of M follows from positivity of U; its finiteness follows from its mean one. The factors in Y are independent because the slabs are disjoint.

Let B,C be independent standard Brownian motions starting at zero, run up to l. Let e_l(B) and e_l^back(C) be their normalized weights in the first and reversed last slab. Then

    M_(t,l) = P_B P_C [e_l(B)e_l^back(C) g_(t,l)(B_l,C_l)],
    Y_(t,l) = P_B P_C [e_l(B)e_l^back(C)],
    g_(t,l)(z,w) = rho_(t-2l)(w-z)/rho_t(0).              (A1)

Proof of (A1): under a bridge from zero to zero, the joint law of its initial segment and its reversed terminal segment is absolutely continuous relative to these independent Brownian segments. The Radon–Nikodym density is the heat kernel for joining their terminal positions through the missing interval of length t-2l, divided by the full bridge endpoint density rho_t(0). This can be proved first for cylinder functions by the Brownian Markov property and heat-kernel disintegration, and then for nonnegative measurable functions by the monotone-class theorem. Conditional on the full path, the normalized weight contributed by the middle slab has environment expectation one: its tube has measure r^d(t-2l), and the Poisson Laplace functional cancels its compensator. The middle environment is independent of both endpoint slabs. Apply Tonelli to average this weight. This proves the displayed formula without any moment assumption beyond annealed mean one. Deterministic slab boundaries have no environment atoms almost surely.

Write s=t-2l and A=(t/s)^(d/2). Then

    g=A exp(-|w-z|^2/(2s)).

For each pair of Brownian paths, the two endpoint weights have environment expectation one, because their environments are independent and each tube's annealed weight is one. Thus the triangle inequality and Tonelli give

    E|M_(t,l)-Y_(t,l)| <= P_B P_C |g(B_l,C_l)-1|.

For z>=0 and A>=1, |A exp(-z)-1| <= A-1+A(1-exp(-z)) <= A-1+Az. Since B_l-C_l has covariance 2l I, its mean squared norm is 2dl. Therefore

    E|M_(t,l)-Y_(t,l)| <= A-1 + A dl/(t-2l) =: eps_(t,l). (A2)

In particular this tends to zero for ANY deterministic l=l_t with l_t/t->0. There is no l_t=o(sqrt(t)) restriction and no restriction on beta, nu, r, or disorder phase. This is factorization of the conditional expectation M, NOT of the original U. Confusing them would falsely solve the problem.

As a normalization check, P_B P_C g=1: integration of a Gaussian with covariance 2l I gives P exp(-|B_l-C_l|^2/(2s))=(s/t)^(d/2)=1/A. Equivalently the joining density changes this difference Gaussian's covariance from 2l I to 2l(s/t)I.

## A2. A sharp universal upper bound for the half-moment (proved)

Let l_t->infinity with l_t/t->0. Put m_t=E sqrt(M_(t,l_t)). Conditional Jensen gives a_t<=m_t. The square-root stability inequality from the first attempt and (A2) give

    |m_t - E sqrt(Y_(t,l_t))| <= sqrt(eps_(t,l_t)).

Independence gives E sqrt(Y)=b_(l_t)^2. Since b_l->b_infinity:=E sqrt(W_infinity),

    m_t -> c:=b_infinity^2,
    limsup_(t->infinity) a_t <= (E sqrt(W_infinity))^2.    (A3)

This holds in every dimension and every disorder phase. In strong disorder c=0, recovering a_t->0. In the L2 regime the first attempt shows equality. Outside L2 the new bound establishes the largest possible limiting half-moment, but supplies no positive lower bound.

## A3. The exact conditional Hellinger deficit (proved)

For brevity set U=U_t(0), M=M_(t,l), F=F_(t,l), and L=U/M. Since M>0, E[L|F]=1. Set h=E[sqrt(L)|F], so 0<=h<=1 by conditional Cauchy–Schwarz. Conditional expectations here can first be localized on {1/n<=M<=n} and then extended by monotone limits, avoiding divisions by an unbounded random variable inside an unjustified integrability argument.

The following identities are exact:

    E[sqrt(U)|F]=sqrt(M) h,
    E[(sqrt(L)-1)^2|F]=2(1-h),
    E[(sqrt(U)-sqrt(M))^2|F]=2M(1-h).

Indeed expand each square and use E[L|F]=1. Hence

    Delta_(t,l):=E sqrt(M)-E sqrt(U)
       = E[sqrt(M)(1-h)]
       = (1/2) E[sqrt(M) E[(sqrt(L)-1)^2|F]].             (A4)

Interpretation: conditional on endpoint environment, L is the likelihood ratio of the bridge-tilted conditional bulk environment against the original conditional bulk environment. The conditional Hellinger affinity is h, and its squared Hellinger distance under the convention integral(sqrt(dQ)-sqrt(dP))^2 is 2(1-h). This interpretation is not used to assume a bound.

There is also a useful truncated error bound. For K,eta>0, on {M<=K} one has M<=sqrt(K)sqrt(M). Conditional on F, integrate the last squared-error identity above, then apply Markov's inequality. Since E M=1, P(M>K)<=1/K. Therefore

    P(|sqrt(U)-sqrt(M)|>eta)
       <= 1/K + 2 sqrt(K) Delta_(t,l)/eta^2.             (A5)

This bound requires no second moment of U. It shows that if Delta_(t,l_t)->0, then sqrt(U_t)-sqrt(M_t)->0 in probability: first fix K and let t grow, then let K grow. Since E U_t=E M_t=1, sqrt(U_t)+sqrt(M_t) is tight (its squared expectation is at most 4), and multiplying by the square-root difference shows U_t-M_t->0 in probability.

Combining (A2)–(A5), for any l_t->infinity with l_t=o(t), the following three assertions are equivalent:

1. U_t(0)-Y_(t,l_t)->0 in probability.
2. Delta_(t,l_t)->0.
3. a_t->c=(E sqrt(W_infinity))^2.

For 2=>1 use (A5), tightness, and M-Y->0 in L1. For 1=>3 use the mean-one square-root bound established in ATTEMPT.md section 3a. For 3=>2 use m_t->c and Delta=m_t-a_t. All three concern the same chosen l_t, and (A2) holds for every such l_t.

These equivalences are a reduction for the full maximal half-moment identity, which is stronger than the source's positivity-only threshold equality. They do NOT show that weak disorder forces any of the three assertions.

## A4. Trying to obtain a positive lower bound

The source's first conjecture does not require Delta->0. In weak disorder c>0, (A4) gives a_t=m_t-Delta_t. Thus if Delta_t has a limit delta<c, the directional half-moment has the positive limit c-delta. A sufficient but unproved quantitative version is a limiting conditional affinity bounded below after weighting by sqrt(M). Merely a lower bound on liminf a_t would still leave existence of the limit open.

The tempting next step is to invoke weak-disorder uniform integrability of W_t to bound Delta. That would be invalid: W_t is an average over endpoint bridge densities, whereas Delta measures the extra fluctuations in a single conditioned endpoint. The conditional expectation M only controls endpoint slabs; its mean one and factorization do not control U/M in the bulk. An ordinary endpoint central limit theorem also gives no such control. The next sections exhibit explicit obstructions to those two proposed shortcuts.

## A5. Pursuing the deficit by Poisson intensity interpolation (proved)

Keep the endpoint-slab intensity equal to nu. In the middle slab (l,t-l), give the Poisson environment intensity h nu, for 0<=h<=1. Normalize each path's weight with the corresponding intensities. Thus U_h has the bridge path weight

    exp{beta omega_end(V_end)+beta omega_h(V_bulk)
        -nu lambda r^d [2l+h(t-2l)]}.

Write E_h for the expectation over this environment and let pi_h(s,x) be the bridge polymer probability that x belongs to the tube at time s, computed with this weight. Let f(h)=E_h sqrt(U_h). Then f(0)=E sqrt(M_(t,l)), f(1)=a_t, and

    Delta_(t,l) = (nu/2) integral_0^1 dh integral_l^(t-l) ds integral_Rd dx
        E_h[ sqrt(U_h) (sqrt(1+lambda pi_h(s,x))-1)^2 ].  (A6)

This is an exact identity, not a small-beta expansion.

### Proof, including unbounded-space justification

First restrict the bulk point process to a bounded spatial box Q. In the bulk weight, use the path-dependent compensator

    -h nu lambda integral_l^(t-l) |(B_s+U) intersect Q| ds,

so that every fixed path still has annealed bulk weight one. Endpoint weights are unchanged. Call the resulting partition function U_h^Q and occupation pi_h^Q. There are finitely many bulk points in (l,t-l)xQ. Differentiating its Poisson count series with respect to h, including its explicit compensator, gives

    f_Q'(h)=nu integral_(bulk x Q) E_h[
       sqrt(U_h^Q)(sqrt(1+lambda pi_h^Q)-1-lambda pi_h^Q/2)] ds dx.

The inserted-point rule is U_h^Q(omega+delta_(s,x))=U_h^Q(omega)(1+lambda pi_h^Q(s,x)); it holds because the tube indicator is zero or one. The compensator derivative is -(nu lambda/2)sqrt(U_h^Q) integral pi_h^Q. On a finite box the differentiations can be justified by its Poisson series: for fixed finite t all positive moments of the partition function are bounded uniformly in 0<=h<=1 by Jensen on path space and the fixed-path Poisson exponential formula; each compensator derivative is bounded by a fixed multiple of sqrt(U_h^Q). This supplies domination for the series and its derivative. The endpoint environment can be integrated using the same finite-time moment bound. At h=0 use the right derivative, sufficient for the integrated identity.

If z=sqrt(1+lambda p), then z^2=1+lambda p and

    z-1-lambda p/2 = -(z-1)^2/2.

This proves f_Q'<=0 and gives (A6) with Q in place of R^d. Integrate over h before removing Q.

Now let Q increase through centered boxes. For a fixed bridge path, once its compact tube lies inside Q, its restricted and full weights coincide. For every h their annealed weights are one. Thus

    E_h|U_h^Q-U_h| <= 2 P_bridge{the tube leaves Q}->0,

uniformly in h. The same argument holds for the unnormalized occupied mass X_h(s,x)=U_h pi_h(s,x), since the tube indicator is at most one. The square-root inequality implies f_Q(h)->f(h), including h=0 and h=1.

To pass the integral on the right to the limit, put

    F(u,x)=sqrt(u)(sqrt(1+lambda x/u)-1)^2, 0<=x<=u,

with F(0,0)=0. Since lambda>-1, there is a finite constant C_beta such that

    F(u,x)<=C_beta sqrt(u)(x/u)<=C_beta sqrt(x).

For example use (sqrt(1+lambda p)-1)^2=lambda^2 p^2/(sqrt(1+lambda p)+1)^2 and p^2<=p. Moreover E_h X_h^Q(s,x)=E_h X_h(s,x)=P_bridge{x in B_s+U}. This annealed equality follows from the path-dependent compensation, and is independent of h and Q. Hence the random variables sqrt(X_h^Q(s,x)) have uniformly bounded second moments at each point; they are uniformly integrable. Convergence of U_h^Q and X_h^Q in L1 implies convergence in probability, and F is continuous on u>0. The full U_h is strictly positive. Thus Vitali gives convergence of the expectations of F at every fixed h,s,x.

Their expectations are bounded by C_beta sqrt(P_bridge{x in B_s+U}). This is integrable over h in [0,1], s in [l,t-l], and x in R^d: for fixed t the bridge B_s is centered Gaussian with covariance s(1-s/t)I<=tI/4; the probability of its being in the fixed-radius ball around x has a Gaussian tail uniformly in s when |x| exceeds twice the tube radius. On bounded x the square root is at most one. Dominated convergence therefore applies to the entire right side, including the indicator 1_Q. This proves (A6) on unbounded space.

### The weighted bulk overlap criterion

Let

    I_(t,l) = integral_0^1 dh integral_(bulk) ds dx
        E_h[sqrt(U_h) pi_h(s,x)^2].

For beta!=0 put z_min=min(1,exp(beta/2)), z_max=max(1,exp(beta/2)). Then (A6) implies the two-sided comparison

    nu lambda^2 I_(t,l) / [2(1+z_max)^2]
       <= Delta_(t,l)
       <= nu lambda^2 I_(t,l) / [2(1+z_min)^2].           (A7)

Proof: pi lies in [0,1], so sqrt(1+lambda pi) lies between z_min and z_max. Insert the exact rational-square expression into (A6). The constants are strictly positive and finite for every fixed finite beta!=0. For beta=0, Delta=0 identically.

Thus I_(t,l_t)->0 is necessary and sufficient for the maximal half-moment identity/factorization in A3, for any l_t->infinity, l_t=o(t). This is a concrete overlap reduction rather than an assumed local limit theorem.

## A6. Endpoint Hellinger identity and a CLT obstruction (proved)

Let q_t^omega(x)=rho_t(x)U_t(x)/W_t be the normalized quenched endpoint density. Its Hellinger affinity with the free endpoint density is

    H_t(omega)=integral_Rd sqrt(q_t^omega(x) rho_t(x)) dx,
    0<H_t<=1.

Endpoint disintegration and Tonelli, followed by shear invariance, give the exact identity

    a_t = E[sqrt(W_t) H_t].                              (A8)

Indeed the integrand sqrt(W_t)sqrt(q_t rho_t) is rho_t(x)sqrt(U_t(x)), whose environment expectation does not depend on x. This identity is an alternative direct target for positivity: a suitable nonvanishing limit of H_t could suffice. It is not provided by weak convergence of the quenched endpoint laws.

Here is a complete counterexample to that proposed CLT shortcut, not to E0112. Let phi and Phi be the standard normal density and CDF on R. Choose n>=2 and eps_n=1/n. In the uniform quantile coordinate u in (0,1), let

    A_n = union_(j=0)^(n-1) [j/n,(j+eps_n)/n),
    q_n(z)=phi(z) 1{Phi(z) in A_n}/eps_n.

Each q_n is a probability density. Each full quantile cell has exactly the same q_n mass 1/n as the normal law. Within one cell the CDFs differ by at most 1/n, so their Kolmogorov distance is at most 1/n and q_n dz converges weakly to the standard normal law. But

    integral_R sqrt(q_n(z) phi(z)) dz
       = |A_n|/sqrt(eps_n)=sqrt(eps_n)->0.

Thus even quantitative convergence of CDFs and absolute continuity of endpoint laws do not ensure nonzero Hellinger affinity. Additional microscopic or mesoscopic density control is essential. The q_n are not polymer endpoint laws, so this does not refute the source conjecture. Nor does it prove that such densities can arise in a weak-disorder Brownian Poisson polymer.

## B. The Poisson-thinning route: exact identity and a tested obstruction

Let T_rho F(omega) be the conditional expectation of F evaluated at the environment obtained by independently retaining each original point with probability rho and adding an independent Poisson process of intensity (1-rho)nu. For any deterministic finite-volume tube A,

    T_rho exp{beta_2 omega(A)-nu lambda_2 |A|}
      = (1+rho lambda_2)^(omega(A)) exp{-nu rho lambda_2 |A|}.

Proof: retained count on A is Binomial(omega(A),rho), and immigrated count is Poisson((1-rho)nu|A|), independently. Their exponential moments give respectively (1-rho+rho exp(beta_2))^(omega(A)) and exp((1-rho)nu lambda_2|A|); cancel the original compensator. Since lambda_2>-1, beta_1=log(1+rho lambda_2) is finite and lies between beta_2 and zero, on either sign branch. Tonelli then proves

    T_rho W_t^(beta_2)=W_t^(beta_1),
    T_rho U_t^(beta_2)(x)=U_t^(beta_1)(x).                (B1)

The affine temperature conversion is exact in this unmarked Poisson model. Conditional Jensen yields E sqrt(U_t^(beta_1))>=E sqrt(U_t^(beta_2)), but this monotonicity does not turn a zero directional half-moment at beta_2 into a positive one at beta_1.

The tempting generic Lp-to-Lq hypercontractivity step fails. Fix a Poisson count N of mean mu>0 and f_k=exp{kN-mu(exp(k)-1)}. For 1<p<q<infinity and 0<rho<=1,

    log ||f_k||_p = mu[(exp(pk)-1)/p-(exp(k)-1)],
    log ||T_rho f_k||_q
      = mu[((1+rho(exp(k)-1))^q-1)/q-rho(exp(k)-1)].

Consequently log(||T_rho f_k||_q/||f_k||_p)->infinity as k->infinity: the leading positive term is mu rho^q exp(qk)/q, which dominates all exp(pk) and exp(k) terms. Each f_k is positive, mean one, and in every finite Lp. Therefore there is no finite universal operator bound ||T_rho f||_q<=C||f||_p for any fixed rho>0 and q>p on Poisson space. This is a proved obstruction to that particular imported theorem, not a refutation of modified log-Sobolev or more specialized Poisson inequalities.

The count example lets k vary while E0112 fixes beta. Thus it does not rule out a fixed-beta, geometry-sensitive smoothing estimate for Brownian tube functionals. That specialized estimate is still unproved here. Route B supplies an exact identity and a precise failed general shortcut, not a solution.
