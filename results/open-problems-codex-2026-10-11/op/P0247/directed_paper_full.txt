Abstract
Consider a simple graph in which a random walk begins at a given vertex. It moves at each step with equal probability to
any neighbor of its current vertex, and ends when it has visited every vertex. We call such a random walk a random cover
tour. It is well known that cycles and complete graphs have the property that a random cover tour starting at any vertex
is equally likely to end at any other vertex. Ronald Graham asked whether there are any other graphs with this property.
L´aszlo Lov´asz and Peter Winkler showed that cycles and complete graphs are the only undirected graphs with this property.
We strengthen this result by showing that cycles and complete graphs (with all edges considered bidirected) are the only
directed graphs with this property.
Keywords: random walks; directed graphs; cover tours.
2020 Mathematics Subject Classiﬁcation: 05C81, 05C20.
Let G be a connected digraph. A cover tour of G from a vertex u is a directed walk which begins at u and ends when it
has visited every vertex of G. Lov´asz and Winkler showed that cycles and complete graphs are the only undirected graphs
with the property that a random cover tour beginning at any given vertex is equally likely to end at any other vertex [1].
We address a question posed by Winkler of whether there are any further directed graphs with this property.
We will borrow the notation used in [1]; let L(u, v) be the event that v is the last vertex visited by a random cover tour
beginning at u. In the case of a bidirected cycle, one might be tempted to believe that a neighbor of the starting vertex
should be less likely to be the last visited than one further from the start. While this intuition does not hold for cycles,
it does hold for many other connected graphs. It is also shown in [1] that, for any two nonadjacent vertices u and v of a
bidirected graph G, there is a neighbor x of u such that P(L(x, v)) ≤P(L(u, v)), and this inequality is strict if the induced
subgraph G −{u, v} is connected. From the proof, it follows that non-neighbors of a given vertex u are at least as likely as
neighbors to be last visited by a random cover tour from u. We generalize this notion in the following lemma (the proof is
nearly identical to that of Theorem 2 in [1]).
Lemma 1. Let G be a connected digraph, and let u be a vertex in G. If u does not have an edge to a vertex v, then there is an
out-neighbor x of u such that P(L(x, v)) ≤P(L(u, v)). Furthermore, this inequality is strict if there is a directed walk from u
to v which visits all vertices in G, but does not revisit u.

and let L(xi; v, u) denote the event that u is the last new vertex, and v the next-to-last new vertex, visited by a random cover
tour of G beginning at xi. The event L(u, v) is the disjoint union of the events L(xi, v) and L(xi; v, u); either the random
cover tour from u to v visits u more than once, or it does not. Thus,

Proof. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists a pair of vertices u and v in a digraph G = (V, E), as
described, such that vu ∈E and uv ̸∈E. By Lemma 1, any cover tour from u to v in G revisits u. Let T denote a shortest
such cover tour, and let T ′ denote the closed walk T + vu.
We begin by considering the cover tour which starts at v, ﬁrst takes the edge vu, and continues along T until the last
seen vertex, v1. The vertex v appears only once in this cover tour, since v appears only once in T. By Lemma 1, we have
vv1 ∈E. Notice also that v1 appears only once in T. Otherwise, the walk that starts at u, follows T to the ﬁrst copy of v1
(visiting every vertex but v), and then follows T from the last copy of v1 back to v is a cover tour that is shorter than T,
which contradicts its minimality.

denote the closed walk that visits them in order. Note that W visits the vertices in U in opposite order to T. (See Figure 1.)
We note that any vertex x in V \ U has a copy in T, and the minimality of T ensures that no two copies of x appear in
