# C0119 literature and source verification — 2026-10-12

## Source fidelity

Sourav Chatterjee, *Chaos, concentration, and multiple valleys*, arXiv:0810.4221v2 (2008/2009), https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.4221. Downloaded as source_pdf.pdf; page-numbered extraction source.txt.

Section 12, item (2), printed/PDF page 59 says verbatim:

> (2) Prove chaos in directed polymers in dimensions ≥ 3. Certain parts
> of the current proof do not work in higher than 2 dimensions, but
> other parts are okay. Since the Benjamini-Kalai-Schramm approach
> is inherently dimension-free, this seems to be a more doable open
> problem.

The words "at low temperature" and "polymer measure" are intake paraphrases, not in this question. Section 8, pp. 37–38, treats the **zero-temperature ground state**, iid standard Gaussian vertex weights, length-n paths with n vertices starting at 0, positive-coordinate steps, and free endpoint. Perturbation: g^t=e^{-t}g+sqrt(1-e^{-2t})g'. Chaos is normalized expected vertex overlap tending to zero for some t_n→0. Section 8's two-dimensional theorem is Var(M)≤Cn/log n and E|P∩P^t|≤Cn/((1-e^{-t})log n).

## Relevant verified papers: leads, not an exact printed chaos theorem

1. B. T. Graham, *Sublinear variance for directed last-passage percolation*, Journal of Theoretical Probability **25** (2012), 687–702; arXiv:0909.1352v3, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.1352. Download graham.pdf, extraction graham.txt. Theorem 6.1, PDF page 14, says (equations transcribed, all words retained):

> Theorem 6.1. Let d ≥ 2, and let ω = (ω(x) : x ∈ Z^d) be a collection of
> independent, standard normal random variables;
> var[T(0, S_N)] = O(N/log N)
> and
> var[\hat T(0, \hat S_N)] = O(N/log N).

S_N={x∈Z_+^d:|x|=N}; T excludes the starting vertex. The hat version is nearest-neighbor space-time paths in d−1 spatial dimensions. This is precisely the needed ground-state variance estimate for both conventions; adding the independent origin weight changes variance by 1. It states variance, not chaos. The implication to chaos is proved in ATTEMPT.md, so this is **not** tagged SOLVED_IN_LITERATURE under the user's strict rule.

2. Sourav Chatterjee, *Superconcentration in surface growth*, arXiv:2103.09199v3 (7 May 2022), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.09199. Download surface_growth.pdf, extraction surface_growth.txt. Theorem 2.4(3), PDF page 9:

> (3) For any t ≥ 2 and x ∈ Z^d, Var(f(t, x)) ≤ Ct/log t.

The full theorem begins: "Consider the surface f generated by the directed polymer model defined above. Let C denote any constant that depends only on the dimension d, the inverse temperature β, and the law of the environment." Section 2.4, pp. 8–9, explicitly allows every d≥1, defines nearest-neighbor paths in Z^d, free endpoint, iid weights that are a Lipschitz image of Gaussians, and f has the same law as β^{-1}log Z_t. Standard Gaussian weights satisfy this with the identity map. Again a variance result, not a printed finite-temperature chaos theorem. Only the Gaussian specialization is used in our OU overlap proof.

3. Kenneth S. Alexander and Nikolaos Zygouras, *Subgaussian concentration and rates of convergence in directed polymers*, Electronic Journal of Probability **18** (2013), 1–28; arXiv:1204.1819v2. The fetched arXiv abstract establishes subgaussian concentration for nearly gamma iid disorder in (d+1) dimensions. Not used as a black box: Chatterjee's theorem above is enough.

4. Shirshendu Ganguly, Victor Ginsburg, and Zoe Himwich, *Temperature chaos in directed polymers*, arXiv:2607.18194v1 (20 July 2026). Search abstract concerns continuum directed random polymers in the KPZ class and β_2≫β_1≫1; different object and different perturbation. Not a solution of C0119.

5. Francesco Caravenna and Anna Donadini, *Enhanced noise sensitivity, 2D directed polymers and Stochastic Heat Flow*, arXiv:2507.10379v2 (first posted 14 July 2025). Search abstract: critical two-dimensional weak-disorder scaling and partition functions, not the present low-temperature fixed-β overlap question.

6. Daniel Ahlberg, Malo Hillairet, and Ekaterina Toropova, *Noise sensitivity in last-passage percolation*, arXiv:2601.05361v1 (8 January 2026). Search abstract: geometric last-passage percolation under Bernoulli encoding; different disorder and observable.

7. Yu Gu and Tomasz Komorowski, *Gaussian fluctuations of replica overlap in directed polymers*, arXiv:2201.07097v2 (first posted 18 January 2022). Search abstract: Brownian polymers in spatially mollified Gaussian noise, all dimensions, low temperature; different object.
