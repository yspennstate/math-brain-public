 Abstract 
    
 
 We prove that for supercritical percolation on every infinite transitive graph, the probability that the origin belongs to a finite cluster of size at least  n n  decays exponentially in  Φ ⁡ ( n ) \Phi(n) , where  Φ \Phi  is the isoperimetric function of the graph. 
 
  
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Let  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  be an infinite connected (locally-finite, vertex-)transitive graph. Let  P p \mathrm{P}_{p}  be the standard percolation measure, under which each edge is open with probability  p p  independently of all others. Fix a vertex  o o , and write  p c p_{c}  for the critical point given by 
 

 
 (2) 
 
 p c ≔ inf { p ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] : P p ​ ( o ↔ ∞ ) > 0 } . \displaystyle p_{c}\coloneqq\inf\{p\in[0,1]\colon\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\infty)>0\}. 
 
 
 
 
 A fundamental result in percolation theory is subcritical sharpness ( [ 28 ,  1 ] , see also  [ 3 ,  15 ,  14 ,  31 ] ), which says that for all  p < p c p<p_{c} , there exists  c > 0 c>0  such that for all  n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , the cluster  𝒞 o \mathcal{C}_{o}  of the origin satisfies 
 

 
 (3) 
 
 P p ​ ( | 𝒞 o | ≥ n ) ≤ e − c ​ n and P p ​ ( o ↔ ∂ B n ) ≤ e − c ​ n , \displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}(|\mathcal{C}_{o}|\geq n)\leq e^{-cn}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{n})\leq e^{-cn}, 
 
 
 where  B n \mathrm{B}_{n}  is the ball of radius  n n  centred at  o o , and  ∂ S \partial S  is the set of all edges with exactly one endpoint in a given set of vertices  S S . 
 
 
 In this paper, we will prove the following supercritical counterpart to subcritical sharpness. Write  Φ : ℕ → ℕ \Phi\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}  for the isoperimetric function given by 
 

 
 (4) 
 
 Φ ⁡ ( n ) ≔ min ⁡ { | ∂ S | : S ⊂ V ​  and  ​ n ≤ | S | < ∞ } . \displaystyle\Phi(n)\coloneqq\min\left\{|\partial S|\colon S\subset V\text{ and }n\leq|S|<\infty\right\}. 
 
 
 
 
 Theorem 1 . 
 
 For all  p > p c p>p_{c}  there exists  c > 0 c>0  such that for all  n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , 
 

 
 (5) 
 
 P p ( n ≤ | 𝒞 o | < ∞ ) ≤ e − c ​ Φ ​ ( n ) and P p ( o ↔ ∂ B n  but  o   ／ ↔ ∞ ) ≤ e − c ​ n . \displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}(n\leq|\mathcal{C}_{o}|<\infty)\leq e^{-c\,\Phi(n)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{n}\text{ but }o\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.98611pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.40282pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}\infty)\leq e^{-cn}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 We will deduce Theorem  1  from the following more general result. For each finite set of vertices  W W , define 
 

 
 (6) 
 
 Φ ⁡ ( W ) := min ⁡ { | ∂ S | : W ⊂ S ⊂ V ​  and  ​ | S | < ∞ } . \Phi(W):=\min\left\{|\partial S|\colon W\subset S\subset V\text{ and }|S|<\infty\right\}. 
 
 
 Equivalently,  Φ ⁡ ( W ) \Phi(W)  is the size of the smallest edge cutset from  W W  to  ∞ \infty . Also write  𝒞 S \mathcal{C}_{S}  for the set of vertices connected to  S S . 
 
 
 Theorem 2 . 
 
 For all  p > p c p>p_{c}  there exists  c > 0 c>0  such that for every finite set of vertices  S S  and for all  n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , 
 

 
 (7) 
 
 P p ​ ( S   ／ ↔ ∞ ​  and  ​ Φ ​ ( 𝒞 S ) ≥ n ) ≤ e − c ​ n . \displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{p}(S\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.98611pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.40282pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}\infty\text{ and }\Phi(\mathcal{C}_{S})\geq n)\leq e^{-cn}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 1.1  Comments 

 
 Historical context. 

 
 In the first decades after its introduction by Broadbent and Hammersley  [ 10 ] , percolation was primarily studied on the hypercubic lattice  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . Both subcritical and supercritical sharpness of the percolation phase transition were ultimately well understood in this context. For relevant textbooks, see   [ 21 ,  9 ] . In 1996, Benjamini and Schramm launched the study of percolation on arbitrary infinite transitive graphs in their seminal paper,  Percolation beyond  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}   [ 8 ] . See  [ 27 , Chapters 7 and 8]  for a survey of the rich research area that has since emerged. The proofs of certain results, such as subcritical sharpness, were easily extended to this more general setting. In our paper, we focus on supercritical sharpness, a result that is well-known in the context of the hypercubic lattice but whose proofs have remained restricted to specific geometries. 
 
 
 
 Previous results on supercritical sharpness. 

 
 In the classical case that  G = ℤ d G=\mathbb{Z}^{d} , supercritical sharpness was deduced in  [ 11 ]  from the celebrated Grimmett–Marstrand slab theorem  [ 20 ]  that for all  d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , 
 

 
 (8) 
 
 lim m → ∞ p c ​ ( ℤ 2 × { − m , … , m } d − 2 ) = p c ​ ( ℤ d ) . \lim_{m\to\infty}p_{c}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\{-m,\ldots,m\}^{d-2}\big)=p_{c}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big). 
 
 
 At the end of our paper, we will explain why conversely, this slab theorem is easily implied by supercritical sharpness. 
 
 
 More recently, supercritical sharpness was extended from  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}  to every infinite transitive graph of  polynomial growth   [ 13 ] . At this level of generality, one cannot use renormalisation schemes that rely on the symmetries of  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . However, the geometry of transitive graphs of polynomial growth is nevertheless known to be highly rigid following the foundational works of Gromov and Trofimov  [ 22 ,  30 ] . In particular,  [ 13 ]  uses the special fact that minimal cutsets in such graphs are coarsely connected. By a different argument, supercritical sharpness was also established for every infinite transitive graph that is nonamenable  [ 23 ] , i.e.​ such that  inf n ≥ 1 Φ ⁡ ( n ) / n > 0 \inf_{n\geq 1}\Phi(n)/n>0 . 
 
 
 The problem of establishing supercritical sharpness for general infinite transitive graphs has been around as folklore for some time and was recently recorded in  [ 23 , Conjectures 5.1–5.3] . 
 
 
 
 Tightness. 

 
 Theorem  1  is sharp in the sense that for all  p > p c p>p_{c} , there exists  C < ∞ C<\infty  such that for all  n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 
 

 
 (9) 
 
 P p ( n ≤ | 𝒞 o | < ∞ ) ≥ e − C ​ Φ ​ ( n ) and P p ( o ↔ ∂ B n  but  o   ／ ↔ ∞ ) ≥ e − C ​ n . \mathrm{P}_{p}(n\leq|\mathcal{C}_{o}|<\infty)\geq e^{-C\Phi(n)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{P}_{p}(o\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{n}\text{ but }o\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 5.0pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.98611pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.40282pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\leftrightarrow}}}\infty)\geq e^{-Cn}. 
 
 
 Indeed, the first of these inequalities was established in  [ 16 , Comment 9] , while the second follows by considering the event that  𝒞 o \mathcal{C}_{o}  is a fixed geodesic path of length  n n . 
 
 
 
 Transience. 

 
 Following  [ 6 ] , there has been much work investigating which properties of the random walk on a transitive graph are stable under percolation. (See e.g.​  [ 7 ,  12 ,  19 ,  29 ,  4 ] , and in the context of finite transitive graphs of high degree,  [ 18 ,  5 ,  17 ,  2 ] ). Conjecture 1.7 from  [ 6 ]  is that for every infinite transitive graph 1 1

 (10) 
 
 P p ​ (  every infinite cluster is transient  ) = 1 . \mathrm{P}_{p}\left(\text{ every infinite cluster is transient\,}\right)=1. 
 
 
 Note that ( 10 ) holds vacuously when  p < p c p<p_{c} , and if one believes the well-known conjecture from  [ 8 ]  that  P p c ​ ( o ↔ ∞ ) = 0 \mathrm{P}_{p_{c}}(o\leftrightarrow\infty)=0 , then ( 10 ) should also hold vacuously when  p = p c p=p_{c} .
By an argument from  [ 24 , Section 3] , adapted from  [ 29 ] , our Theorem  1  has the following corollary. 
 
 
 Corollary 1.1 . 
 
 For every infinite connected transitive graph that is transient, ( 10 ) holds for all  p > p c p>p_{c} . 
 
 
 
 
 
 1.2  Notation 

 
 We use the convention that  ℕ \mathbb{N}  contains  0 0 . Let  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  be a graph. Throughout the paper, we assume that every graph is locally finite. 
 
 
 Graphs.  Given a set of vertices  A A , we define the following:

 ∂ A \partial A 
 
 ∂ + A \partial^{+}\!A

 B r ​ ( A ) \mathrm{B}_{r}(A) 
 vertices at distance at most  r r  from  A A 
 
 G ⁡ [ A ] G[A] 
 the subgraph induced by  A A 
 
 
 
 
 Cutsets.   Given sets of vertices  L L  and  R R , we say that a set of edges  Π \Pi  is an edge cutset from  L L  to  R R  if  L L  and  R R  are not connected to each other in the graph  ( V , E ∖ Π ) (V,E\setminus\Pi) . We say that a set of vertices  A A  is a vertex cutset from  L L  to  R R  if  L ∖ A L\setminus A  is not connected to  R ∖ A R\setminus A  in  ( V ∖ A , E ∖ A ¯ ) (V\setminus A,E\setminus\overline{A}) . 
 
 
 Percolation. 
For each  p ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] p\in[0,1] , let  P p G \mathrm{P}_{p}^{G} , or simply  P p \mathrm{P}_{p} , be the standard percolation measure of parameter  p p  on  G G . Given a set of vertices  A A , we may write  P p A \mathrm{P}_{p}^{A}  as shorthand for  P p G ⁡ [ A ] \mathrm{P}_{p}^{G[A]} . When working with random variables that are not defined in terms of  P p \mathrm{P}_{p} , we think of them as all living in one big probability space called  ( Ω , ℱ , ℙ ) (\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}) . 
 
 
 Connectivity.  Given sets of vertices  L L  and  R R  and given a set of edges  F F , we write  L ↔ 𝐹 R L\xleftrightarrow{F}R  for the event that  L L  is connected to  R R  in the graph  ( V , F ) (V,F) . As usual, we identify each configuration  ζ ∈ { 0 , 1 } E \zeta\in\{0,1\}^{E}  with its support, which is a set of edges. In particular, we may use expressions such as  ζ ∩ F \zeta\cap F  and  L ↔ ζ ∩ F R L\xleftrightarrow{\zeta\cap F}R . We write  L ↔ 𝐹 ∞ L\xleftrightarrow{F}\infty  to mean that the set of vertices connected to  L L  in the graph  ( V , F ) (V,F)  is infinite. We use the same definitions when  L L  or  R R  are sets of edges, in which case we naturally identify these sets with the set of vertices contained in at least one of these edges. When working with  P p \mathrm{P}_{p}  specifically, given a set of vertices  A A , we write  L ↔ 𝐴 R L\xleftrightarrow{A}R  to mean that  L ↔ ω ∩ A ∘ R L\xleftrightarrow{\omega\cap A^{\circ}}R , and we simply write  L ↔ R L\leftrightarrow R  to mean that  L ↔ 𝜔 R L\xleftrightarrow{\omega}R , where  ω \omega  is the underlying percolation configuration. 
 
 
 
 1.3  Outline of the paper 

 
 In Section  2 , we will introduce our key technical result, Proposition  2.1 , and we will explain why this result implies Theorem  2 . In Section  3 , we will present a sketch of the proof of Proposition  2.1 . In preparation for the proof of Proposition  2.1 , in Section  4 , we will establish a few self-contained lemmas. In the epilogue, Sections  7  and  8 , we will return to the classical setting of  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}  and explain how to recover Grimmett–Marstrand from supercritical sharpness.

 3  Sketch of proof 

 
 In this section, let us present a sketch of the proof of Proposition  2.1 . Fix a connected finite set  S S  in the graph  G G  and consider percolation restricted to the complement of  S S .
Call an edge  e ∈ ∂ S e\in\partial S  a ‘touch’ if it is connected to infinity (in this restricted configuration). Our proof relies on an exploration algorithm that witnesses these touches one after another. An important feature of our algorithm is that the set of revealed edges of our  p p -percolation remains connected throughout the process. We will first describe a toy version of this algorithm and then explain how to modify it for the final construction. 
 
 
 Toy algorithm. 

 
 Pick a vertex that is midway between  S S  and infinity, and explore its cluster  𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1} . Here  midway  means that the vertex has constant probability to connect to both  S S  and  ∞ \infty . Then pick a vertex that is midway between  S S  and  𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1} , and explore its cluster  𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}_{2} . Note that at this stage,  𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}_{2}  can at best reach the external vertex boundary of  𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1}  because the boundary of  𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1}  has been revealed to be closed. Consider all edges where  𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1}  and  𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}_{2}  touch, and sprinkle here to glue these two clusters together with constant probability. Continue in this manner  t t  times, obtaining sets  𝒞 1 , … , 𝒞 t \mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{t} , at each step choosing a point that is midway between  S S  and the previously explored sets. This construction guarantees that each exploration has at least constant probability  α > 0 \alpha>0  to connect to the previous explorations and witness a new touch. It follows that 
 

 
 (23) 
 
 P q ​ ( Ψ ⁡ ( S ) ≥ t ) ≥ α t . \mathrm{P}_{q}(\Psi(S)\geq t)\geq\alpha^{t}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Improved algorithm. 

 
 To improve the probability of creating a new touch from  α \alpha  to  1 − ε 1-\varepsilon , we split each iteration into two: First, by revealing one large ball at a time, we search for a  seed  (a fully open ball) that is midway between  S S  and the previous explorations. Then we explore the cluster of this seed. To ensure that the search for seeds does not interact with future cluster explorations, we look for seeds in a  seed layer , which is simply a low-density percolation that is independent of the original  p p -percolation. 
 
 
 
 Where does  Φ \Phi  come into play? 

 
 Let us conclude this sketch with an important observation. In the toy algorithm, the set of all revealed edges is always connected. In contrast, in the improved algorithm, the search for seeds generates many  bad  balls in the seed layer that are disconnected from each other yet contain negative information. Consequently, while the toy algorithm can always be run until it witnesses all possible touches (i.e.​ all edges in the exposed boundary of  S S ), the improved algorithm may halt after finding only order  Φ ⁡ ( S ) \Phi(S)  touches if the union of the bad balls forms a cutset from  S S  to infinity.

 4  Finding mid-points and mid-balls 

 
 A standard observation in percolation theory is that for any sets of vertices  L L  and  R R  in a connected graph  G G , if every vertex is connected to  L ∪ R L\cup R  with constant probability, then there exists a  mid-point  vertex that has both constant probability to connect to  L L  and to  R R . (See  [ 16 ]  for a recent example.) 
 
 
 Now suppose that our graph contains not just one but many disjoint paths from  L L  to  R R . Then we can find not just one but many mid-points.
