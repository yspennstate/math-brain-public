# G0001 — attempt and referee record (2026-10-11)

## Exact question and verdict

Independent nearest-neighbour Bernoulli **site** percolation on Z^d, integer d>=3, parameter 0<p<1. Write C(0) for the open cluster of the origin (empty if the origin is closed), theta(p)=P_p(|C(0)|=infinity), and

F_p(n)=P_p(n<=|C(0)|<infinity),
L(p)=liminf_{n->infinity} log(log(1/F_p(n)))/log n.

Cerf, arXiv:2608.23661v1, Conjecture 1.4, PDF page 3 asks whether theta(p)>0 implies L(p)>0. The conjecture is **not solved here**. Proved independently below: an explicit high-p special case with the optimal exponent, and a lower bound at every parameter with theta(p)>0. Also proved: the precise endpoint reduction using the source's strict-supercritical result. The lower bound is in the opposite direction to the missing critical upper bound.

## 1. Source-based reduction, with the dependencies explicit

Cerf's Theorem 1.2, PDF page 2, states that either theta(pc)=0 or L(pc)=0. Equation (1.5), on the same page, states for every p>pc that the limit defining L exists and equals 1-1/d. These are invoked as results stated in the source, not reproved here. The source explicitly defines the site model on page 1. Equation (1.5) cites Kesten–Zhang and Grimmett–Marstrand. No bond-to-site model identification is made here.

Proposition. Given those two source results, Conjecture 1.4 for a fixed dimension d is equivalent to theta(pc)=0 for SITE percolation in that dimension.

Proof. If Conjecture 1.4 holds and theta(pc)>0, its application at p=pc gives L(pc)>0. Here 0<pc<1, as stated on page 1, so the conjecture's parameter hypothesis applies. Theorem 1.2 instead gives L(pc)=0, a contradiction. Thus theta(pc)=0. Conversely, suppose theta(pc)=0. Monotonicity of theta and the definition of pc give theta(p)=0 for p<pc, so theta(p)>0 implies p>pc. Equation (1.5) then gives L(p)=1-1/d>0. This proves both directions. QED.

Consequently the exact unsolved case is p=pc with theta(pc)>0. Establishing an upper bound F_pc(n)<=exp(-c n^a) for any c,a>0 under that hypothesis would contradict Theorem 1.2 and prove critical extinction. The reduction itself is not a new solution.

## 2. Independent high-p proof

Theorem. Let d>=2, Delta=3^d-1, q=1-p, and rho=Delta^2 q. If 0<rho<1, then with alpha=(d-1)/d and M=ceil(n^alpha),

F_p(n) <= Delta^(-2) rho^M [ M/(1-rho) + rho/(1-rho)^2 ].                 (2.1)

In particular F_p(n)<=A exp(-a n^alpha) for some A,a>0 and all n>=1, and L(p)>=alpha. For d=3 the explicit condition is p>675/676. The condition is sufficient and very conservative, not a proposed critical value. The proof does not assume theta(p)>0.

### 2.1 Filling holes and boundary connectivity

Let K be any finite nearest-neighbour connected set containing 0. The complement of K has exactly one infinite component U: outside a box containing K the lattice is connected when d>=2, and every infinite component must meet that exterior. Define H=Z^d\U, the set obtained by adding every finite complementary component to K. It is finite: every point outside the enclosing box belongs to U. It is connected: every finite complementary component has a neighbour in K, since its nonempty boundary cannot be in another component of Z^d\K. Both H and U are connected.

Let E be the nearest-neighbour edges with one endpoint in H and one in U; let B be their U endpoints. If K is an open cluster, all vertices in B are closed. Indeed, the H endpoint of each edge of E actually belongs to K: a point in H\K lies in a finite component of the complement of K and cannot be adjacent to U. A neighbour outside K of an open cluster must be closed.

We prove B is star-connected, where star adjacency means distinct lattice vertices of sup-norm distance 1. On E impose the equivalence relation generated by declaring two edges equivalent if they occur in the same elementary unit square. Every square contains an even number of edges of E, because traversing it changes membership in H an even number of times. All its E edges belong to a single equivalence class. Therefore each equivalence class meets every elementary square in an even number of edges.

A class that meets every square evenly meets every finite closed walk evenly (counting edge traversals mod 2). To verify this without a topological assumption, encode a walk as a word in steps +/-e_i. A closed word has net exponent zero for each i. Commute adjacent steps in distinct coordinate directions to group the coordinates; each such swap changes the edge multiset modulo 2 by the four edges of an elementary square. Within each coordinate group cancel adjacent opposite steps, which traverses an edge twice and changes no parity. All steps eventually cancel. Thus square parity implies closed-walk parity.

Take arbitrary edges e=(h,u), f=(h',u') of E, with h,h' in H and u,u' in U. Join h to h' inside H and u to u' inside U. Together with e and f these paths form a closed walk whose only cut-edge traversals are e and f, once each if e!=f. The class of e has even intersection with this walk, so it contains f. Hence E has only one class. In each square, the U endpoints of its edges are either equal or star-adjacent. Chains of equivalent cut edges therefore give star paths in B. This proves the claimed connectivity.

### 2.2 Surface-size bound, including the finite inequality used

For a finite nonempty set H, denote by pi_i H its projection deleting coordinate i. Every coordinate-i line meeting H has a lowest and highest point in H. The two edges immediately beyond these extrema are distinct edges of E. (Here complement H=U.) Hence

|E| >= 2 sum_i |pi_i H|.

Each vertex of B is incident to at most 2d edges of E, and so

|B| >= (1/d) sum_i |pi_i H| >= (product_i |pi_i H|)^(1/d) >= |H|^((d-1)/d). (2.2)

The middle inequality is arithmetic-geometric mean. For completeness the last inequality, the finite Loomis–Whitney inequality, follows as follows. Let X be uniform on H and let Ent denote Shannon entropy with natural logarithms. The chain rule gives Ent(X)=sum_j Ent(X_j | X_1,...,X_{j-1}). Apply the chain rule to each vector X_{-i}. In its j term, conditioning has omitted at most X_i, so conditional entropy is at least Ent(X_j | X_1,...,X_{j-1}). Each j is present in d-1 such vectors. Thus sum_i Ent(X_{-i}) >= (d-1)Ent(X). Since Ent(X)=log|H| and Ent(X_{-i})<=log|pi_i H|, exponentiation gives |H|^(d-1)<=product_i |pi_i H|.

The entropy facts here are elementary finite inequalities: chain rule follows by factoring joint probabilities into successive conditional probabilities. Nonnegativity of relative entropy follows from log u<=u-1 applied to u=Q/P on the support of P; averaging conditional relative entropy gives the decrease of conditional entropy under additional conditioning. Relative entropy with respect to a uniform measure gives the upper bound by log of the support size. Zero masses are handled by restricting to the support (or by a limit). This supplies the finite inequality rather than assuming an unproved geometric surface estimate.

Since H contains K, |K|>=n implies m=|B|>=n^alpha.

### 2.3 Anchoring the boundary; avoiding an infinite translation count

Let t>=1 be the first integer such that t e_1 is not in H. Then t e_1 is in B. If a and b are the minimum and maximum first coordinates of H, B contains vertices with first coordinates a-1 and b+1, respectively. Since B is star-connected with m vertices its first-coordinate span is at most m-1: a simple star path between two extrema has at most m-1 steps. Also a<=0 and b>=t-1. Therefore m-1>=b-a+2>=t+1, in particular 1<=t<=m. This places a boundary vertex among the m deterministic anchors e_1,...,m e_1.

### 2.4 Counting and the probability estimate

In the star lattice, every vertex has Delta neighbours. For a fixed anchor v, there are at most Delta^(2(m-1)) connected m-vertex sets containing v. Fix a total order of the Delta step directions. For each set, choose a spanning tree by a deterministic search and traverse the rooted tree in depth-first order, giving a walk of length 2(m-1). Its step word uniquely recovers the visited vertex set, so this encoding is injective into the Delta^(2(m-1)) possible words. The choices need not all encode trees; overcounting is harmless.

For each deterministic set B of m sites, P_p(all B closed)=q^m by site independence. A union bound over its possible anchors and shapes yields

F_p(n) <= sum_{m>=M} m Delta^(2(m-1)) q^m
       = Delta^(-2) sum_{m>=M} m rho^m.

The standard geometric sum (differentiate the finite geometric identity and pass to its convergent limit, or sum m=M+j) gives (2.1). Polynomial factors are absorbed in rho^(M/2), since sup_{M>=1}(M+1)rho^(M/2)<infinity. Thus F_p(n)<=A exp[-(-log rho)n^alpha/2]. Taking two logarithms and dividing by log n gives L(p)>=alpha. All bounds refer to deterministic sets before taking the union bound; no independence of random boundary vertices is asserted. QED.

## 3. Independent lower bound throughout theta>0

Theorem. For any d>=2 and 0<p<1 with theta=theta(p)>0, there are constants c,C>0 depending on p,d such that for every integer n>=1,

F_p(n) >= c exp(-C n^((d-1)/d)).                                      (3.1)

In particular limsup log(log(1/F_p(n)))/log n <=(d-1)/d. Combined with section 2 this gives the exact limit (d-1)/d in its explicit high-p range whenever theta>0.

### 3.1 Positive association used here

For completeness, increasing events under independent Bernoulli sites are positively associated (Harris inequality). For one Bernoulli variable the covariance of two increasing functions is p(1-p)(f(1)-f(0))(g(1)-g(0))>=0. Induct on the number of sites, conditioning on the last site: the expected conditional covariance is nonnegative by induction and the covariance of the conditional expectations is nonnegative by the one-variable identity. This proves the finite-variable result. Events of connection to infinity used below are decreasing limits of finite connection events; finite intersections and finite counts commute with this limit. Taking limits gives the required positive association for these events too.

### 3.2 Density event and origin connectivity

Let Q_R=[-R,R]^d intersect Z^d, I=Q_{R-1}, S=Q_R\Q_{R-1}, T=Q_{R+1}\Q_R, and N=|I|=(2R-1)^d. Let X count vertices in I belonging to an infinite open cluster. Translation invariance gives E[X]=theta N, whether or not the infinite cluster is unique. Put D={X>=theta N/2}. If a=P(D), the pointwise bounds X<=N on D and X<=theta N/2 on its complement imply

theta <= a+(1-a)theta/2,

hence a>=theta/(2-theta)>=theta/2. The origin-infinite event O is increasing, as is D. Harris gives P(O intersect D)>=theta P(D)>=theta^2/2.

Choose R=ceil(((2n/theta)^(1/d)+1)/2), so R>=1 and theta N/2>=n. Every infinite-cluster site in I has an open path inside Q_R to S: take the part of an infinite path up to its first hit on S. On O intersect D, the origin also has such a path. Thus the following **finite**, increasing event A has probability at least theta^2/2:

0 is connected to S within Q_R, and at least n vertices of I are connected to S within Q_R.

This event involves only sites in Q_R. We do not try to preserve the infinity event when modifying the shell.

### 3.3 Force two shells

The nearest-neighbour graph on S is connected for d>=2. To see this, from a vertex on a face x_i=+/-R, keep coordinate i fixed and move the other coordinates to +R one at a time. At least one other coordinate is now +R; hold it fixed while moving coordinate i to +R. The whole path stays on S and reaches the corner (R,...,R). Therefore any two shell vertices connect.

Let B={all S open}. Harris for the two finite increasing events A and B gives P(A intersect B)>=P(A)p^|S|. The event E={all T closed} is independent of A intersect B, since T is disjoint from Q_R. On A intersect B intersect E the origin and at least n vertices belong to a single open cluster inside Q_R. It cannot exit Q_R because every path out must visit T. Consequently

F_p(n) >= (theta^2/2) p^|S| (1-p)^|T|.                              (3.2)

The shell sizes are |S|=(2R+1)^d-(2R-1)^d and |T|=(2R+3)^d-(2R+1)^d. The identity a^d-b^d=(a-b)sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a^(d-1-j)b^j gives each <=2d(2R+3)^(d-1). The chosen R satisfies R<=((2n/theta)^(1/d)+3)/2=O(n^(1/d)), with a constant depending on theta,d. Thus the logarithmic cost in (3.2) is at most C n^((d-1)/d), giving (3.1) with c=theta^2/2. Taking logarithms proves the claimed limsup bound. QED.

## 4. Positivity, equivalence with stretched-exponential notation, and endpoint checks

For every 0<p<1 and every n there is a positive-probability configuration where an n-vertex straight path starting at 0 is open and all of its exterior neighbours are closed. Thus F_p(n)>0. Also F_p(n)->0: the events {n<=|C(0)|<infinity} decrease to the empty event. Hence both logarithms in L(p) are well-defined for all sufficiently large n.

L(p)>0 is equivalent to the existence of beta>0 and n0 such that F_p(n)<=exp(-n^beta) for all n>=n0. In the forward direction choose 0<beta<L(p); the definition of liminf gives log log(1/F_p(n))>=beta log n eventually and exponentiate twice. In the reverse direction that inequality gives L(p)>=beta. Allowing a constant c in exp(-c n^beta) does not change positivity, since the added log c vanishes after division by log n (or decrease beta).

Boundary parameters p=0 and p=1 are excluded by the question; no zero-probability logarithm convention is needed. The proofs of sections 2 and 3 use d>=2. In dimension 1 shell connectivity fails and the filled-complement argument has two infinite components, so it must not be extended there. No evidence from d=1 or finite boxes settles d>=3 critical percolation.

## 8. Check of an earlier shape reduction and a failed comparison route

Scope correction: FULL boundary density is not the same as exterior boundary density or geometric compactness. The phrase "compact/low-boundary" below must refer to the small-full-boundary condition, not all geometrically compact clusters. BOUNDARY_OBSTRUCTION.md proves this distinction explicitly, with complete periodic cell counts and connected finite examples.

The earlier report research7_2026-09-17/R7-06/artifacts/2026-09-18/2026-09-18T191000Z__G0001_boundary_score_local_uniqueness_reduction.md contains a useful exact reduction that we independently checked, rather than assuming truncated finite-cluster tails are monotone in p.

Let partial K be the FULL exterior vertex boundary of a finite connected K (including boundaries of holes). For 0<p<r<1 define

s_*(p,r)=log(r/p)/log((1-p)/(1-r)).

If 0<=s<s_*(p,r), let kappa=log(r/p)-s log((1-p)/(1-r))>0. Then

P_p(n<=|C(0)|<infinity, |partial C(0)|<=s|C(0)|)<=exp(-kappa n).

Complete proof: The exact-cluster event C(0)=K fixes all k=|K| sites of K open and all b=|partial K| boundary sites closed; it leaves every other site free. Its probability is p^k(1-p)^b. Dividing by the corresponding probability at r gives exp[-k log(r/p)+b log((1-p)/(1-r))]. For b<=sk this is at most exp(-kappa k)<=exp(-kappa n). Sum this inequality over the countably many eligible finite connected K containing 0. The corresponding exact-cluster events at r are pairwise disjoint, so their total probability is at most one. This proves the bound without any monotonicity or critical hypothesis. Differentiating numerator and denominator at r=p (both vanish with positive denominator derivative) yields lim_{r downarrow p}s_*(p,r)=(1-p)/p. Thus every fixed s<(1-p)/p admits such a comparison parameter r.

If theta(pc)>0, Theorem 1.2 says L(pc)=0. For every s<(1-pc)/pc, the high-full-boundary part of the tail must also have logarithmic liminf zero. To check this conclusion carefully: the low-boundary part is exponentially small by the previous bound. If the high-boundary part had positive logarithmic liminf, both parts would be eventually bounded by a stretched exponential with a common positive exponent, and their sum would too (absorb the factor two by decreasing the exponent). That contradicts L(pc)=0. The high-boundary tail tends to zero; hence its logarithmic liminf is at least zero, so its failure to be positive means it is zero. Its positivity along the relevant subsequence follows from the same comparison with the strictly positive total tail.

This explains a failed route: directly comparing F_p(n) with F_r(n) cannot work by monotonicity. Even the singleton probability p(1-p)^(2d) rises and then falls, with derivative (1-p)^(2d-1)[1-(2d+1)p]. The event {n<=|C(0)|<infinity} is not increasing. The exact probability ratio controls compact/low-boundary clusters but leaves highly exposed clusters; it does not supply the missing critical upper bound. This verified reduction is prior work, not a new endpoint proof.
