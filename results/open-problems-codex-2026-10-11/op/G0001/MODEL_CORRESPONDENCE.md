1. **Vertices and adjacency.** `Site d` is `Fin d → ℤ`. The comparator's graph is the undirected Hasse graph of coordinatewise order. In this order, a cover `x ⋖ y` holds exactly when `y=x+e_i` for one coordinate `i`. Here is the complete order argument: if `x<y`, some coordinate increases by an integer at least one. If its increase is at least two, `x+e_i` is a strict intermediate point. If any second coordinate increases, `x+e_i` is again strict intermediate. A cover therefore has just one coordinate increasing, by exactly one. Conversely, between such `x` and `y`, every other coordinate is fixed and the remaining integer coordinate has only its two endpoints, so no strict intermediate point exists. Symmetrizing covers gives exactly nearest-neighbour adjacency. This applies to every positive integer `d`, hence to `d≥3`.

2. **Configurations and independence.** Configurations are `Set (Site d)`, equivalently functions from sites to propositions. The map used in `prodBernoulli`, `s ↦ (i ↦ i∈s)`, is the membership encoding of that same predicate. It is a bijection, with inverse `f ↦ {i | f i}`; both compositions are the identity by extensionality. Coordinate events generate the product sigma algebra. Pulling back the infinite product of measures `p•dirac True+(1-p)•dirac False` consequently gives independent membership indicators with success probability `p`. In particular the labels are vertices, not edges. No bond-to-site identity is asserted.

3. **Cluster convention.** `openSiteGraph G ω` keeps precisely graph edges with both endpoints in `ω`. The cluster is `{y | x∈ω ∧ Reachable x y}` in that open graph. If the root is closed the cluster is empty; if it is open this is precisely its open connected component, including the root. The `.Infinite` predicate is infinity of its vertex set. Thus the event in `thetaSite` is exactly the event `|C(0)|=∞` in G0001.

4. **Probability is not a nonmeasurable or infinite-measure shorthand.** On the lattice the event that the cluster is infinite equals the intersection, over positive integer radii, of the events that the root has an open path to outside the radius box. Indeed an infinite subset of the lattice cannot lie in a finite box, and a set meeting outside every box is infinite. Each radius event can be tested in the next larger finite box: truncate an open path at its first exit. It is a finite cylinder event. The infinity event is therefore measurable. The underlying Bernoulli product is a probability measure, so its measure is at most one; taking `Measure.real` is its usual finite real probability here.

5. **Critical parameter.** The external definition takes the infimum of the parameters in `[0,1]` where this probability is positive, and adds `{1}`. On `ℤ^d`, `d≥1`, the all-open model at parameter one has an infinite cluster, so adding `{1}` does not change that set. Monotone coupling makes the percolation probability nondecreasing. Its zero-parameter probability is zero and its one-parameter probability is one. Consequently its zero set is a nonempty downward set, its positive set a nonempty upward set, and the supremum of the former equals the infimum of the latter. To prove equality explicitly, let `a` be that infimum. For every `p<a`, positivity would make `p` an element of the positive set smaller than its infimum, impossible. For every `p>a`, the definition of an infimum supplies a positive-set parameter `r<p` (otherwise `p` would be a lower bound exceeding `a`); monotonicity gives positivity at `p`. Thus zeros reach arbitrarily close below `a` and none lie above it. This matches Cerf's critical parameter. The unit-interval subtype merely records membership, adding no mathematical hypothesis.
