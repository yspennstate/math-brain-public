 Non-perturbative finite connectivities in supercritical Bernoulli percolation on  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}  with  d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 
 
 
 Yacine Aoun
 
    
 Kamil Khettabi
 † † thanks:  
Section de mathématiques, Université de Genève, Switzerland.
Email:  kamil.khettabi@unige.ch 
 

 Abstract 
    
 We solve a longstanding problem in Bernoulli percolation: we derive sharp Ornstein–Zernike asymptotics for the probability that two distant points are connected by a finite cluster in the whole supercritical regime. Our proof has two main ingredients. First, the renewal structure of  [ 2 ]  yields an  a priori  bound on cluster volume. Second, we adapt the coarse-graining and irreducible-cluster framework of  [ 10 ]  to finite supercritical connections. The main distinction from the subcritical analysis is that our argument  does not require  a separation of masses between irreducible and unrestricted finite connections. The volume bound and a modified coarse-graining construction instead provide stretched-exponential tails for the effective random-walk increments, which suffice to obtain the sharp asymptotics. 
  
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 1.1  Context 

 
 The problem of establishing a sharp control on general two-point functions (covariance, connectivity functions, Green functions) has a long history in mathematics and physics. It can be traced back to the original work of Ornstein and Zernike on density fluctuations in fluids  [ 14 ] . The resulting Ornstein–Zernike (OZ) asymptotics describe exponential decay with a polynomial prefactor of order  ∥ x ∥ − ( d − 1 ) / 2 \|x\|^{-(d-1)/2} , where  ∥ ⋅ ∥ \|\cdot\|  is the Euclidean norm. Since then, the OZ asymptotics became a cornerstone of the study of two-point functions in statistical mechanics: see for instance  [ 19 ,  20 ,  21 ,  22 ] . 
 
 
 For Bernoulli percolation, the first non-perturbative OZ asymptotics for the probability of two distant points to be in the same cluster were derived for the subcritical regime by Campanino, Chayes and Chayes  [ 8 ]  for axial directions, and later extended to arbitrary directions by Campanino and Ioffe  [ 9 ] . The theory was then extended for the finite-range Ising model above the critical temperature  [ 15 ]  and for the subcritical finite-range FK percolation model by Campanino, Ioffe and Velenik  [ 10 ] . For the infinite-range Ising model with exponentially decaying interactions, the OZ asymptotics can fail  [ 18 ,  27 ] . The OZ asymptotics were established under appropriate assumptions in the subcritical and non-saturated regime by Aoun, Ott and Velenik  [ 16 ] . At the saturated point (where the transition between the saturated and unsaturated regime occurs), we established  [ 17 ]  the OZ asymptotics up to constants on  ℤ \mathbb{Z}  under appropriate assumptions. 
 
 
 For the supercritical counterpart, Bricmont and Fröhlich  [ 6 ]  established the OZ asymptotics for spin covariances in the Ising model at very low temperatures on  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}  with  d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . For Bernoulli percolation, the two-point function is the probability of two points of being connected by a finite cluster. On  ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} , Campanino, Ioffe and Louidor  [ 13 ]  derived a different type of asymptotics (of order  ‖ x ‖ − 2 \|x\|^{-2}  instead of  ∥ x ∥ − 1 / 2 \|x\|^{-1/2}  that would be given by the OZ formula) in the whole supercritical phase. In dimension  d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , the OZ asymptotics were derived for  p p  sufficiently close to  1 1  by Braga, Procacci and Sanchis  [ 26 ]  for the axial directions and by Campanino and Gianfelice  [ 11 ]  for an arbitrary direction (and later extended to FK percolation  [ 12 ] ). More recently, Fribergh and Hammond  [ 2 ]  proved sharp point-to-plane asymptotics in the whole supercritical phase using a renewal structure with stretched-exponential tails on  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}  with  d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . In the present work, we prove the last remaining asymptotics for nearest neighbor Bernoulli percolation on  ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} : we establish the OZ asymptotics in the whole supercritical regime in dimension  d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 .

 Then, we develop a OZ theory for the supercritical finite connections. The main skeleton of the proof stays the same : to every cluster, we associate a skeleton and we derive an appropriate energy estimate for every skeleton. The standard problem when deriving asymptotics in this way is that it might happen that some parts of the cluster are very densely connected, and hence on a local level it might be impossible to extract any energy. To circumvent this problem, we notice that the a priori control on the volume of the connected component  prevents  this from happening too often, yielding overall a ’missing’ exponential cost that can be made (on an exponential scale) arbitrarily small, hence removing the problem altogether. We establish that, up to an exponential error, there is a positive density of cone-points. In a sense, this result establishes a  mass-gap  of some particular connections. Combined with the first result above, this allows us to construct a directed random walk with stretched exponential tails, which are enough to sharply derive the wanted asymptotics. 
 
 
 
 1.5  Extensions 

 
 Our results suggest several directions for further research. A natural extension would be to prove the OZ asymptotics in the supercritical phase for a general finite-range Bernoulli percolation model. This would require an extension of the renewal theory from  [ 2 ]  as well as extending the OZ theory for this setting (the latter seems to be a more direct adaptation). 
 
 
 Another natural direction would be extending our results for dependent percolation models such as FK percolation. This would extend the perturbative results of  [ 12 ]  in the whole supercritical regime. 
 
 
 Another (potentially more challenging) extension would be to apply our framework to study the low-temperature covariances in Ising and Potts models. For the Ising model, possible approaches include the random current representation  [ 24 ]  and the recent double cluster swapping representation introduced by van Engelenburg, Heeney and Lis  [ 25 ] , which provides a percolation representation of truncated correlations.
