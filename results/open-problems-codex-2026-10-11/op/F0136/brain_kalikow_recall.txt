    Let f be continuous on the closed interval [a,b]. Part 1: the function F(x) = integral from a to x of f(t) dt is differentiable on [a,b] and F'(x) = f(x) for every x in [a,b]. Part 2 (evaluation): if G is any antiderivative of f on [a,b], meaning G'(x) = f(x) for all x in [a,b], then the integral from a to b of f(x) dx equals G(b) - G(a). Together the two parts show differentiation and (Riemann) integration are inverse processes: every continuous function has an antiderivative, and definite integrals are computed from any one of them.

    The Gamma function Gamma(z) = integral from 0 to infinity of t^(z-1) e^(-t) dt, convergent for Re(z) > 0, extends the factorial to the complex plane. It satisfies the functional equation Gamma(z+1) = z Gamma(z) (integration by parts), so Gamma(n) = (n-1)! for positive integers n; it continues meromorphically to all of C with simple poles at the non-positive integers, and Gamma(1/2) = sqrt(pi). By the Bohr-Mollerup theorem it is the unique log-convex function on the positive reals with value 1 at 1 that satisfies the functional equation. Stirling's approximation is the asymptotics of Gamma (equivalently of the factorial) for large argument.
