## B1. Path-law transfer theorem (proved)

Let R_i and X_i be Levy processes on R^{d_i}, i=1,2. Make the reference pair independent and the target pair independent. Assume for every finite T>0 and starting point x_i that the target law on the RAW canonical path sigma-field F_T^0 is absolutely continuous with respect to the corresponding reference law. If R=(R_1,R_2) satisfies (H), then X=(X_1,X_2) satisfies (H). If the local absolute continuity is mutual for both coordinates, their products satisfy (H) simultaneously.

Proof. Fix T and a starting pair. On D([0,T],R^{d_1}) and D([0,T],R^{d_2}), write the Radon-Nikodym derivatives as L_1 and L_2. Independence identifies the path-pair law as the tensor product of its coordinate laws. For measurable rectangles A_1 x A_2,

Q(A_1 x A_2) = (int_{A_1} L_1 dP_1)(int_{A_2} L_2 dP_2)
             = int_{A_1 x A_2} L_1(w_1)L_2(w_2) d(P_1 tensor P_2).

Both sides define probability measures, since each L_i has integral one. The pi-lambda theorem extends equality to the product sigma-field. The identification of a cadlag path pair with its R^{d_1+d_2}-valued path preserves the raw coordinate sigma-fields: both are generated by evaluations at times <=T. Thus the target product is locally absolutely continuous to the reference product for EVERY starting pair. Standard Levy processes can be realized on the common canonical path space with their own standard completed filtrations. Apply Hu-Sun-Wang Theorem 1.1(i), PDF p.3 of arXiv:1903.00050v3, which explicitly handles those completions. No assertion about absolute continuity of completed filtrations is needed. All processes are conservative, so its lifetime condition {T<zeta} is automatic. This proves the one-way result. Applying the same argument in the reverse direction proves the equivalence under mutual local absolute continuity. QED.

No resolvent-density hypothesis occurs in B1. HOWEVER the reference PRODUCT satisfying (H) is a hypothesis, not a consequence inferred from factor (H). This avoids circular use of the very open question being attempted.

## B2. A checkable triplet version (proved special case)

Use the source's exponent convention (a,Q,mu). For each reference R_i with triplet (a_i,Q_i,mu_i), suppose the target X_i has triplet (a_i',Q_i,mu_i') with mu_i'=K_i mu_i, K_i>=0 measurable,

I_i = int (1-sqrt(K_i))^2 dmu_i < infinity,

a_i' - a_i + int_{|x|<1} x(K_i(x)-1) mu_i(dx) in Range(Q_i).

If the reference product satisfies (H), the target product satisfies (H). Theorem 4.5, PDF p.14, of Hu-Sun-Wang is exactly the equivalence between these conditions and finite-horizon local absolute continuity. Translations give its condition at all starting points. Therefore B1 applies.

The correction integral is absolutely convergent. Indeed Cauchy-Schwarz and (sqrt(K)+1)^2 <= 2(K+1) give

int_{|x|<1} |x| |K-1| dmu
 <= sqrt(I) * sqrt(2 int_{|x|<1} |x|^2 (K+1) dmu) < infinity,

because mu and K mu are both Levy measures. Gaussian covariance equality is essential; a drift change outside Range(Q_i) is NOT absorbed by a jump tilt.

These conditions tensorize exactly at the triplet level too. The coordinate-axis embeddings of the two Levy measures have disjoint supports, since neither charges zero. The product tilt equals K_1 on the first axis and K_2 on the second, so its Hellinger integral is I_1+I_2. The drift correction is the pair of the individual corrections; Range(diag(Q_1,Q_2))=Range(Q_1) x Range(Q_2), since the block matrix acts coordinate by coordinate. This independently verifies the product law test.

Example of the special case: independent symmetric alpha_i-stable references, 0<alpha_i<1, have transition densities and a common constant sector bound (zero imaginary part); their product satisfies (H) by the first attempt's Theorem 3.1. Set K_i(x)=1+theta_i x on |x|<1 on the real line, K_i=1 outside, |theta_i|<1/2, and choose a_i'=-int_{|x|<1} x(K_i-1)mu_i(dx). Then

(sqrt(1+theta_i x)-1)^2
 = theta_i^2 x^2/(sqrt(1+theta_i x)+1)^2 <= theta_i^2 x^2,

so I_i is finite by the Levy-measure condition. The target pair satisfies (H) by B2. These perturbations have infinitely many small jumps and unequal positive/negative intensities; they are not merely finite compound-Poisson additions. This is a proved special case, without a novelty claim or a claim that it surpasses every known sector theorem.

## B3. Why symmetric references cannot cover all (H) factors (proved obstruction)

For arbitrary real p,q,s,

2[(p-s)^2+(q-s)^2]-(p-q)^2 = (p+q-2s)^2 >= 0.       (B3.1)

The minimum of (p-s)^2+(q-s)^2 over s is (p-q)^2/2, attained at s=(p+q)/2. For nonnegative p,q this minimizing s is nonnegative, so the same optimum is admissible for square roots of intensities. This elementary identity, inequality and optimum are formalized in HellingerObstruction.lean. The final Lean run exited 0; all three named lemmas have only [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound] as axioms, with no sorryAx. The integral and path-law arguments are written proofs, not claimed to be Lean-formalized.

Let 0<alpha<1 and let X be the driftless stable process with Levy density

nu(dx)=c_+ x^{-1-alpha} dx on x>0,
       =c_- |x|^{-1-alpha} dx on x<0,

where c_+,c_->=0 and c_+ != c_-. Such X satisfies (H), including c_-=0 (a stable subordinator): it is one of the stable classes covered in the source. In particular the original (H) assumption permits it.

Claim: X is NOT locally absolutely continuous on every finite horizon to ANY symmetric Levy reference law.

Proof. Suppose such a reference existed. By the necessary conditions of Hu-Sun-Wang Theorem 4.5, it must have the same zero Gaussian covariance, a symmetric Levy measure rho dominating nu, and finite Hellinger integral

I=int (1-sqrt(dnu/drho))^2 drho.

Decompose rho into its Lebesgue absolutely continuous and singular parts. Symmetry makes its Lebesgue density g satisfy g(x)=g(-x) almost everywhere, by uniqueness of Lebesgue decomposition. The singular part contributes its full nonnegative mass to I because nu has no singular part. On the absolutely continuous part of rho, Hellinger energy is

int (sqrt(dnu/dx)-sqrt(g(x)))^2 dx,

including zero-density regions by the usual common dominating measure interpretation. Pair x and -x for 0<x<1. Apply (B3.1) to

p=sqrt(c_+) x^{-(1+alpha)/2},
q=sqrt(c_-) x^{-(1+alpha)/2},
s=sqrt(g(x)).

The contribution of this pair is at least

(1/2)(sqrt(c_+)-sqrt(c_-))^2 x^{-1-alpha}.

Its integral over (0,1) is infinite because alpha>0 and the coefficient is strictly positive. This contradicts I<infinity. Extra singular mass of rho, or a different drift, cannot cancel this nonnegative divergence. QED.

For c_-=0,c_+=1 the lower bound is x^{-1-alpha}/2. This is a concrete obstruction in a very familiar (H) factor, not a hypothetical pathology. It disproves the proposed universal representation by locally absolutely continuous symmetric reference laws. It is NOT a counterexample to E0024: independent stable products themselves satisfy (H).

Thus approach B reaches a genuine reduction and infinite-activity special case, but cannot turn the bare hypotheses of E0024 into its reference-law hypotheses. Nor may the known (H_m) conclusion for singular symmetric references silently be promoted to (H).


## B4. Exact optimal symmetric-reference test for arbitrary Levy measures (proved)

This extends B3 beyond densities and stable processes. It is a characterization of the reference-law hypothesis, NOT a characterization of (H).

Let nu be any Levy measure on R^d, let nu^- be its reflection, and put Lambda=nu+nu^-. Lambda is symmetric and sigma-finite off zero. Write g=dnu/dLambda. Then 0<=g<=1 and dnu^-/dLambda=1-g; choose versions such that g(-x)=1-g(x) almost everywhere, as follows from uniqueness of Radon-Nikodym derivatives. Define

J(nu) = (1/4) int (sqrt(g)-sqrt(1-g))^2 dLambda,
h(x) = (1/4)(sqrt(g(x))+sqrt(1-g(x)))^2,
rho_* = h Lambda.

Then rho_* is symmetric, is a Levy measure, and dominates nu: h(-x)=h(x), 1/4<=h<=1/2, so nu<<rho_*. The bounds follow from (sqrt(g)+sqrt(1-g))^2=1+2sqrt(g(1-g)) and 0<=2sqrt(g(1-g))<=g+(1-g)=1. Thus 1/4<=h<=1/2, proving Levy integrability.

For two sigma-finite Levy measures, write their unnormalized Hellinger integral as

I(nu,rho)=int (sqrt(dnu/dM)-sqrt(drho/dM))^2 dM,

where M=nu+rho; this is independent of the dominating measure. If nu<<rho, it equals int(1-sqrt(dnu/drho))^2 drho, including regions where the target density is zero.

### Optimality

For EVERY symmetric Levy measure rho, even one not dominating nu,

I(nu,rho)>=J(nu),

and equality holds for rho=rho_*. Thus J is exactly the minimum Hellinger cost of replacing the jump measure by a symmetric one.

Proof. Use the symmetric dominating measure M=Lambda+rho. Set f=dnu/dM, f^-=dnu^-/dM, s=drho/dM. Reflection gives f^-(x)=f(-x) and s(x)=s(-x) almost everywhere. Hence

2 I(nu,rho)=int [(sqrt(f)-sqrt(s))^2+(sqrt(f^-)-sqrt(s))^2] dM
 >= (1/2) int (sqrt(f)-sqrt(f^-))^2 dM
 = 2 J(nu).

The inequality is exactly B3.1. For the last equality, on {dLambda/dM>0} write f=g*dLambda/dM and f^-=(1-g)*dLambda/dM; outside it both are zero. This change of dominating measure proves the equality also for infinite integrals. For rho_*, sqrt(h)=(sqrt(g)+sqrt(1-g))/2, so

I(nu,rho_*)=int [sqrt(g)-(sqrt(g)+sqrt(1-g))/2]^2 dLambda
           =(1/4) int (sqrt(g)-sqrt(1-g))^2 dLambda=J(nu).

This proves optimality. QED.

### Exact path-law symmetrization criterion

For a Levy process with triplet (a,Q,nu), a symmetric Levy reference to which its finite-horizon laws are absolutely continuous exists IF AND ONLY IF

J(nu)<infinity
and v:=a+int_{|x|<1} x(nu-rho_*)(dx) belongs to Range(Q).       (B4.1)

When these hold, (0,Q,rho_*) is such a reference.

Proof. If J is finite, the small-jump vector integral defining v is absolutely convergent by the Cauchy-Schwarz argument in B2, applied to nu<<rho_* and I=J. If v belongs to Range(Q), source Theorem 4.5 with reference (0,Q,rho_*) gives the asserted local absolute continuity.

Conversely suppose a symmetric reference (0,Q_0,rho) exists. Source Theorem 4.5 forces Q_0=Q, I(nu,rho)<infinity and a+int_{|x|<1}x(nu-rho)(dx) in Range(Q). Optimality implies J<infinity. Using a common dominating measure and (u+w)^2<=2u^2+2w^2 gives

I(rho,rho_*)<=2 I(rho,nu)+2 I(nu,rho_*)<infinity.

For ANY two Levy measures A,B with finite Hellinger integral, Cauchy-Schwarz proves

int_{|x|<1}|x| |A-B|(dx)
 <= sqrt(I(A,B)) * sqrt(2 int_{|x|<1}|x|^2(A+B)(dx))<infinity.

Apply it to rho and rho_*. Their signed difference is symmetric; its absolutely convergent small-jump vector integral is zero by the substitution x -> -x. Therefore

int_{|x|<1}x(nu-rho)(dx)=int_{|x|<1}x(nu-rho_*)(dx),

so v belongs to Range(Q). QED.

Notice also that Lambda/2-rho_* is a finite symmetric measure when J<infinity: its density relative to Lambda is (sqrt(g)-sqrt(1-g))^2/4, so its total mass is J. This is another check on the drift correction.

B3's skew stable process has J=infinity, even though it satisfies (H). Thus (B4.1) cannot be inferred from the original (H) hypotheses. B4 nonetheless gives an exact criterion, valid for atomic and singular jump measures as well, for how far the selected path-law reduction can go. It does not infer full (H) for a singular symmetric reference from (H_m), and does not establish an unproved symmetric-product theorem.


## A1. Smoothing closes the limit at each fixed scale (proved)

Assume X,Y,(X,Y) have resolvent densities, X satisfies (H), and Y is symmetric. Let nu be a finite positive compactly supported measure of finite product 1-energy. Write nu_1 for its first marginal. Survey Proposition 4.5 (PDF pp.21-23) gives E_X^1(nu_1)<infinity. The original Kanda paper has now been fetched: *Notes on energy for space-time processes over Levy processes*, Nagoya Math. J. 122 (1991), 63-74, DOI 10.1017/S0027763000003536. Its Corollary of Lemma 2.1, printed p.66/PDF p.4, proves BOTH monotonicity in lambda for positive finite-energy measures and monotonicity under positive-measure domination. Its hypotheses are resolvent densities, explicitly stated at printed p.65. This confirms the precise imported positive-measure result used here; it is not used for complex measures.

Let gamma_epsilon be the centered Gaussian measure on R^m with Fourier transform exp(-epsilon |y|^2/2), and let

nu_epsilon=nu*(delta_0 tensor gamma_epsilon).

For the complex partial Fourier measure sigma_y from ATTEMPT.md Section 7, write sigma_y=A_y-B_y+i(C_y-D_y), where all four measures are positive and <=nu_1. Specifically they integrate the positive/negative parts of cos(<y,v>) and sin(<y,v>) against nu(du,dv). Thus each has finite X 1-energy by positive domination. Fourier Cauchy-Schwarz gives, for rho>=lambda>=1,

E_X^rho(sigma_y)<=4(E_X^rho(A_y)+E_X^rho(B_y)+E_X^rho(C_y)+E_X^rho(D_y))
               <=16 E_X^lambda(nu_1).

The second inequality applies Kanda's positive domination and positive-measure monotonicity separately to the four positive measures. It is NOT a claim that E_X^rho(sigma_y) is itself monotone.

The exact sliced-energy identity and Gaussian multiplier yield

E_Z^lambda(nu_epsilon)
 = int exp(-epsilon |y|^2) E_X^{lambda+r_Y(y)}(sigma_y) dy
 <=16 (pi/epsilon)^{m/2} E_X^lambda(nu_1) -> 0

for every fixed epsilon>0, by (H) for X and survey Theorem 4.2. All integrals here are nonnegative Fourier integrals; Tonelli applies. Although nu_epsilon is no longer compactly supported, it is a finite positive measure and the energy criterion allows such measures.

Also

E_Z^1(nu-nu_epsilon)
 = int w_1(x,y)|nu_hat(x,y)|^2(1-exp(-epsilon|y|^2/2))^2 dxdy ->0

by dominated convergence, since the last squared multiplier is between 0 and 1 and original nu has finite 1-energy. This is energy-norm approximation, different from and not ruled out by the earlier diagonal domination obstruction.

## A2. Exact uniformity criterion for removing smoothing (proved)

For EACH such fixed nu, the following are equivalent:

(i) E_Z^lambda(nu)->0 as lambda->infinity;
(ii) D_epsilon(nu):=sup_{lambda>=1} E_Z^lambda(nu-nu_epsilon)->0 as epsilon decreases to zero.

Proof. The multiplier gives E_Z^lambda(nu-nu_epsilon)<=E_Z^lambda(nu) at every lambda. Also the elementary kernel comparison in ATTEMPT.md (7.2) gives E_Z^lambda(tau)<=lambda E_Z^1(tau) for any finite complex measure tau of finite 1-energy.

Suppose (i). Given eta>0, choose M with E_Z^lambda(nu)<eta for every lambda>=M. This bounds the residual energy by eta on that tail. For 1<=lambda<=M it is bounded by M E_Z^1(nu-nu_epsilon), which tends to zero by A1. Therefore limsup_epsilon D_epsilon(nu)<=eta. Since eta is arbitrary, (ii) follows.

Conversely, Fourier |a+b|^2<=2|a|^2+2|b|^2 gives

E_Z^lambda(nu)<=2 E_Z^lambda(nu-nu_epsilon)+2 E_Z^lambda(nu_epsilon).

At fixed epsilon the second term tends to zero by A1. Thus limsup_lambda E_Z^lambda(nu)<=2D_epsilon(nu); letting epsilon decrease to zero proves (i). QED.

This is an exact per-measure reformulation of the symmetric-partner gap. It identifies precisely the needed exchange of two limits. It does not prove uniformity from energy-norm approximation alone.

## A3. A fixed, already-(H) product defeats a uniform energy-norm shortcut (proved)

One might try to remove smoothing by a bound

E_Z^lambda(nu-nu_epsilon)<=C E_Z^1(nu-nu_epsilon)  for all lambda>=1,

with C depending only on Z and applying to all positive nu. This bound is false, even for a fixed product whose (H) is already known, and even at epsilon=1 with smooth positive probability measures.

Take X with exponent Phi(x)=|x|+i kappa x log|x|, kappa=2/pi, and independent Brownian Y with exponent Psi(y)=y^2. X is a skew 1-stable process satisfying (H); both factors have transition densities. They satisfy a common Rao bound f(A)=2+kappa log A (use f for Brownian as well), so their product Z satisfies (H) by the first attempt's Theorem 3.1. Its Fourier energy kernel is

w_lambda(x,y)=(lambda+|x|+y^2)/[(lambda+|x|+y^2)^2+(kappa x log|x|)^2].

For T>1 set delta=T^{-1/2}, b_T=kappa T log T, and let G_delta(u) be the centered real Gaussian density of variance delta^{-2}. Define the positive probability measure

nu_T(du,dv)=Z_T^{-1} G_delta(u)G_delta(v)[1+cos(Tu+v)] du dv,
Z_T=1+exp[-(T^2+1)/(2delta^2)].

Positivity follows from 1+cos>=0; Gaussian integration gives the displayed normalizer. All these densities are square integrable, so every E_Z^lambda is finite since w_lambda<=1/lambda. Their Fourier transforms are

nu_T_hat(x,y)=Z_T^{-1}[g_delta(x,y)+(1/2)g_delta(x-T,y-1)+(1/2)g_delta(x+T,y+1)],

g_delta(x,y)=exp[-(x^2+y^2)/(2delta^2)].

Let tau_T=nu_T-nu_T*(delta_0 tensor gamma_1). Its Fourier transform is the above multiplied by q(y)=1-exp(-y^2/2). Put q_1=1-exp(-1/2)>0. As T->infinity,

E_Z^1(tau_T) ~ pi q_1^2/[2 kappa^2 T^2(log T)^2],
E_Z^{b_T}(tau_T) ~ pi q_1^2/[4 kappa T^2 log T].       (A3.1)

Consequently E_Z^{b_T}(tau_T)/E_Z^1(tau_T) ~ (kappa/2)log T ->infinity, disproving the bound.

Complete verification of the asymptotics. Expanding the squared Fourier transform gives three squared Gaussian packets and their cross terms. For packet centers c_i,c_j, completion of squares gives exactly

g_delta(z-c_i)g_delta(z-c_j)
 =exp[-|c_i-c_j|^2/(4delta^2)] exp[-|z-(c_i+c_j)/2|^2/delta^2].

Distinct centers here have distance at least T. Each cross integral is at most pi delta^2 exp[-T^2/(4delta^2)], times its fixed coefficient, since 0<=q^2<=1 and w_1<=1; at b_T it is bounded even more strongly by 1/b_T times this quantity. These are negligible relative to either leading expression. Z_T tends to 1 with error exponentially small in T^3.

For the packet centered at zero, q(y)<=y^2/2, so its weighted integral at level 1 is O(delta^6)=O(T^{-3}), using the Gaussian fourth moment in y and zeroth moment in x. At level b_T it is O(T^{-3}/b_T), because w_{b_T}<=1/b_T. These too are negligible in (A3.1).

For either packet centered at (T,1) or (-T,-1), take a ball of radius delta R_T where R_T=sqrt(20 log T). On it, q(y)^2=q_1^2(1+o(1)) uniformly, since delta R_T->0. Also |x|=T+o(1), y^2=1+o(1), and |kappa x log|x||=b_T(1+o(1)) uniformly. Hence

w_1(x,y)=(kappa^2 T(log T)^2)^{-1}(1+o(1)),
w_{b_T}(x,y)=(2kappa T log T)^{-1}(1+o(1)),

uniformly on each ball. The integral of the squared Gaussian packet over that ball is pi delta^2(1+o(1)); the omitted Gaussian mass is pi delta^2 exp(-R_T^2)=pi T^{-21}. Bounding kernels by 1 at level 1 and by 1/b_T at the other level shows the omitted tails are negligible relative to (A3.1). Finally there are two such packets, each with coefficient (1/2)^2=1/4, so their combined leading factor is pi delta^2 q_1^2/2. Substitution of delta^2=1/T proves both formulas in (A3.1). QED.

All measures in this example are finite positive and smooth before taking the signed smoothing residual. The theorem disproves a bound uniform over positive input measures, not A2's possible per-measure uniformity for an arbitrary (H) product. The family nu_T changes with T and is not a single non-(H) witness. The Gaussian family is initially noncompact. The compact truncation argument below proves the same failure for compactly supported positive probability measures as well.

### A3 addendum: compact positive input measures also defeat the bound

This checks the compact-support restriction in the remaining gap. Let f_T be the probability density used in A3; its Gaussian variance is T in each physical coordinate. Put R_T=sqrt(20 T log T), and let f_T^c be the restriction of f_T to [-R_T,R_T]^2, normalized to mass one. It is a positive compactly supported probability density. For large T its normalizer is bounded away from zero.

The two-dimensional Gaussian product density is P_T(u,v)=(2pi T)^{-1} exp[-(u^2+v^2)/(2T)], and 0<=f_T<=2P_T because Z_T>=1. The one-dimensional Gaussian tail bound P(|N(0,T)|>R)<=2exp[-R^2/(2T)] and a union bound give omitted f_T mass O(T^{-10}). Also, by squaring P_T and integrating Gaussian tails,

||f_T 1_{outside square}||_2^2=O(T^{-1}exp[-R_T^2/T])=O(T^{-21}).

Normalization changes its density by O(T^{-10}) times ||f_T||_2=O(T^{-1/2}). Thus

||f_T^c-f_T||_2=O(T^{-21/2}).

Convolution by a probability density is an L2 contraction (apply Jensen to the pointwise convolution, then Tonelli). Hence the corresponding residual densities
h_T=f_T-f_T*(delta_0 tensor gamma_1)
and h_T^c=f_T^c-f_T^c*(delta_0 tensor gamma_1)
have L2 difference O(T^{-21/2}), and each has L2 norm O(T^{-1/2}).

Plancherel and w_lambda<=1/lambda give the following energy continuity estimate for all lambda>=1, where C is only the fixed Fourier-convention constant:

|E_Z^lambda(h_T^c)-E_Z^lambda(h_T)|
 <= (C/lambda) ||h_T^c-h_T||_2 (||h_T^c||_2+||h_T||_2)
 = O(T^{-11}/lambda).

To verify it, use | |F|^2-|G|^2 |<=|F-G|(|F|+|G|), bound the kernel by 1/lambda, apply Fourier Cauchy-Schwarz, and then Plancherel. The error at lambda=1 and at lambda=b_T is negligible compared with the two leading energies in A3.1. Consequently the compactly supported inputs have the same ratio asymptotic (kappa/2)log T. This rules out the uniform bound within the compact positive measure class actually used in A1/A2. It still does NOT refute per-measure uniform smoothing approximation or (H); the input measure changes with T.
