Neurobiological theories of consciousness look primarily to synaptic firing and \u201cspike codes\u201d as the physical substrate of consciousness, although the specific mechanisms of consciousness remain unknown. Synaptic firing results from electrochemical processes in neuron axons and dendrites. All neurons also produce electromagnetic (EM) fields due to various mechanisms, including the electric potential created by transmembrane ion flows, known as \u201clocal field potentials,\u201d but there are also more meso-scale and macro-scale EM fields present in the brain. We call this assertion the \u201celectromagnetic field hypothesis.\u201d The neuroanatomy of the brain produces the local and global EM fields but these fields are not identical with the anatomy of the brain. As such, the EM fields represent the more granular, both spatially and temporally, aspects of the brain\u2019s structure and functioning than the neuroanatomy of the brain. The brain\u2019s various EM fields seem to be more sensitive to small changes than the neuroanatomy of the brain. We discuss issues with the spike code approach as well as the various lines of evidence supporting our argument that the brain\u2019s EM fields may be the primary seat of consciousness.
