## 1. Exact arrow-stack reduction (proved)
Let E={+/-e_1,...,+/-e_d}. On an enlarged probability space, given the cookie environment omega, sample independent arrows A(x,i) in E with conditional law P(A(x,i)=e | omega)=omega(x,e,i). The walk at its i-th departure from x follows A(x,i). This is the standard coin construction, and its conditional walk law given omega is exactly the original quenched law.

Additional hypothesis (AE): the infinite arrow sequence (A(x,i))_{i>=1} at a site is exchangeable in its visit indices under the averaged law. It is the infinite sequence that must be exchangeable, not just a finite prefix. Spatial IID of cookie stacks and independence of the arrow coins imply spatial IID of the whole arrow stacks.

Standard input: the infinite de Finetti theorem for a finite alphabet. An infinite exchangeable E-valued sequence has a random probability vector q on E such that, conditionally on q, its entries are independent with common law q. Moreover q(e) is the almost-sure limit of empirical frequencies of e. The finite-alphabet formulation is a special case of the classical de Finetti/Hewitt-Savage representation. We use the exact representation, not a finite-sample approximation.

Apply this at each site. Because q_x is measurable from the local arrow sequence via empirical frequencies, the field (q_x) is IID. The joint conditional law of the arrow stacks given (q_x) is the product across sites of IID arrow sequences with respective laws q_x: independence of the site pairs (q_x,A(x,*)) gives this first on finite site/cylinder sets, and then on the product sigma-algebra. Thus, conditional on q, following these arrows has the law of a Markovian nearest-neighbor RWRE with transition q_x(e) at x.

To verify this without an informal memory argument, fix a finite valid nearest-neighbor path x_0=0,x_1,...,x_n and let i_k=#{0<=j<=k:x_j=x_k}. The departure pairs (x_k,i_k), 0<=k<n, are all distinct. The probability of this path conditional on q is therefore
product_{k=0}^{n-1} q_{x_k}(x_{k+1}-x_k),
which is exactly the quenched Markov path probability. Equality on all finite cylinders implies equality of probability measures on the full path space, since the countable product sigma-algebra is generated by cylinders. Therefore spatial transience, its complement, and zero-speed events can be transferred. No equality of the original quenched cookie law with a fixed RWRE law is asserted: the reduction is for the averaged path law.

## 2. Ellipticity survives even under the weaker arrow hypothesis (proved)
Fix a site x and direction e, and write p_i=omega(x,e,i). Let O be the event A(x,1)=e and A(x,i)!=e for every i>=2; let Z be the event A(x,i)!=e for all i>=1.

By the de Finetti representation, P(O)=0. Indeed, conditionally on q_x, if q_x(e)=0 the first required e is impossible; if q_x(e)>0, the probability that infinitely many subsequent independent trials all avoid e is zero. Also P(Z)=P(q_x(e)=0), by the same dichotomy.

On the other hand, conditionally on omega, arrow independence gives
P(O | omega)=p_1 T, where T=lim_{m->infinity} product_{i=2}^m(1-p_i) in [0,1].
Since (EL) gives p_1>0 almost surely and E[p_1 T]=P(O)=0, nonnegativity forces T=0 almost surely. Consequently P(Z)=E[(1-p_1)T]=0. Hence P(q_x(e)=0)=0. Taking the countable intersection over all sites and finitely many directions proves every transition q_x(e) is strictly positive almost surely.

This argument does not assume uniform ellipticity, nor that original cookies are exchangeable. The arrow-law exchangeability assumption is essential to P(O)=0. It also resolves a possible hidden gap: an exchangeable sequence with positive one-index marginals by itself can have a directing vector on the boundary; the original conditional product of strictly elliptic cookies rules that out here.

## 3. Easily verifiable sufficient classes for (AE) (proved)
(a) Infinite exchangeability of the cookie probability vectors within each site. Permuting cookie indices preserves their law, and using independent arrow coins preserves that permutation symmetry. Thus their arrow sequence is exchangeable. This includes arbitrarily signed projected drifts and infinitely many non-placebo cookies.

(b) Conditional independent cookie noise around a latent transition vector. Suppose at each site there is a random probability vector Q_x, with spatially IID whole site data. Given Q_x, the cookie vectors are independent across visit indices, but need not have identical distributions, and satisfy E[omega(x,e,i) | Q_x]=Q_x(e) for every e and i. For any finite collection of directions and distinct indices, integrate the independent cookie vectors to obtain the joint arrow probability product_i Q_x(e_i). Thus, conditionally on Q_x, the arrows are IID with law Q_x and (AE) follows. In particular, visit-index-dependent cookie variances are allowed. This is a strictly broader sufficient formulation than exchangeability of the cookie vectors themselves.

(c) Independent cookie vectors with a common mean transition vector q, across all sites and visit indices (their higher moments may depend on the visit index). Then the averaged walk is exactly an ordinary walk with IID increments of distribution q. Its strong law immediately gives zero speed on the non-transient event, since any nonzero mean velocity forces escape to infinity. When its mean is zero it has zero speed on the whole path space. This is a signed, infinite-cookie special case not covered by requiring every cookie to have zero drift.

The exact RWRE input has now been verified and the special-case application completed in Section 8 below.


## 4. Supplementary approach A: an exact discovery-order LLN (proved)
The warning about adaptive site selection in the first attempt can be made precise and overcome for discovery-order sums. Sample IID full stacks W_1,W_2,... with the source stack law, independently of a sequence of IID uniform variables driving transitions. On discovering a new site assign it the next unused W_j; at previously discovered sites use the already assigned stack. The walk cannot consult the stack at a new site before reaching it, so the label chosen for a new site is a function only of previously revealed stacks and driving uniforms. Induction on finite time shows that this lazy construction has the same joint law of the walk and its revealed stacks as the usual IID environment: conditional on the revealed past, the next untouched site has an independent stack of the original law. Finite-time joint laws determine the full observable path/revealed-stack law.

Let r_n be the number of distinct departure sites through time n-1. These are the first r_n discovered sites; the possible new arrival at time n is not counted until it is a departure site. Let F be any real integrable function of a stack, and f=E[F(W_1)]. The ordinary IID strong law gives sum_{j<=m}F(W_j)-fm=o(m) almost surely. On the event r_n->infinity, substitute m=r_n and use r_n<=n to obtain
(sum_{x in R_n}F(omega_x)-f r_n)/n -> 0.
If r_n stays finite, the sum and r_n are eventually constant, so the same conclusion holds. This is a pathwise almost-sure result on the whole space, and can be restricted to the non-transient event without conditioning the IID sequence on that event. The discovered stacks need not remain IID after conditioning on an event of recurrence; that conditional claim is not used.

Apply this with B(x)=sum_i |b(x,i)| and assume E[B(0)]<infinity. Then
|D_n|/n <= E[B(0)] r_n/n + o(1)
almost surely. Therefore sublinear range on an event implies zero projected speed there even for unbounded random cookie budgets with integrable total absolute drift. No uniform budget bound or uniform ellipticity is needed. This corrects the earlier incomplete caution: adaptive discovery does allow an IID budget law, because the newly selected stack has not yet been inspected.

For an exact signed version put delta(x)=sum_i b(x,i), mu=E[delta(0)], and
Q_n=sum_{x in R_n} sum_{i>N_n(x)} b(x,i).
Absolute summability makes these expressions well-defined. Grouping the consumed terms gives
D_n=sum_{x in R_n}delta(x)-Q_n,
so, using the martingale law from the first attempt,
X_n dot ell/n = mu r_n/n - Q_n/n + o(1).
This is a sharper reservoir reduction: under zero expected total drift, the remaining question is precisely whether outstanding signed drift Q_n=o(n).

The integrable budgets also control any sparse, even adversarially selected subset of discovered sites. If F_n is a subset of the first r_n discoveries and |F_n|/n->0, then for every fixed K>0,
sum_{x in F_n}B(x)/n <= K |F_n|/n + (1/n)sum_{j<=r_n} B(W_j)1_{B(W_j)>K}.
The discovery-order strong law makes the limsup at most E[B(0)1_{B(0)>K}], because r_n/n<=1. Integrability makes this expectation tend to zero as K->infinity. Thus the weighted sparse sum is o(n), despite the subset possibly depending on every revealed budget.

In particular, if the drifting cookies at each site are eventually exhausted after a finite random number M(x), define H_n={x in R_n:N_n(x)<M(x)}. If |H_n|/n->0 then |Q_n|<=sum_{x in H_n}B(x)=o(n). If also mu=0, zero speed follows. This is a proved conditional special case, not a proof that the unfinished-site count is sublinear on the non-transient event. Even when every fixed site is eventually exhausted, that conclusion is a separate density claim about a moving set of sites.

New remaining obstacle in approach A: control r_n/n (arbitrary mu) or outstanding signed cookie mass Q_n/n (zero mu). Eventual depletion at fixed sites alone does not justify either. Approach B does not need a finite drift budget, but does need infinite arrow exchangeability.


## 5. Full finite-alphabet representation argument
For clarity, the exact representation needed above can be derived directly from finite sampling. Let (A_i) be an infinite exchangeable sequence in the finite alphabet E, and let Q_N(e)=N^{-1}#{1<=i<=N:A_i=e}. Fix k<=N and directions e_1,...,e_k. By exchangeability, sampling k distinct ordered indices uniformly from {1,...,N}, independently of A, gives the same joint law as (A_1,...,A_k).

Couple these distinct indices J with k independently sampled uniform indices I as follows: if the I's are distinct, set J=I; otherwise draw J independently and uniformly among distinct ordered k-tuples. Both branches give the same uniform conditional law for J, so this is a valid coupling. The probability of a collision among I is at most sum_{r<s}P(I_r=I_s)=k(k-1)/(2N). Conditional on the sequence A_1,...,A_N, sampling I with replacement gives probability product_j Q_N(e_j). Therefore
|P(A_1=e_1,...,A_k=e_k)-E[product_j Q_N(e_j)]| <= k(k-1)/(2N).

The probability simplex Delta_E is a compact metric space. Standard weak compactness of probability measures on a compact metric space gives a subsequence N_m along which the laws of Q_{N_m} converge weakly to a probability measure rho on Delta_E. The monomial product_j q(e_j) is continuous and bounded there. Send m to infinity in the displayed estimate to obtain
P(A_1=e_1,...,A_k=e_k)=integral product_j q(e_j) rho(dq).
The same subsequence works for every k and every choice of directions because weak convergence tests every continuous function. Thus every finite-cylinder probability agrees with the law obtained by sampling q~rho and then drawing all A_i independently with law q. Equality on cylinders yields equality of the full infinite-sequence laws. Under this mixture construction the ordinary IID strong law, conditionally on q, implies Q_N(e)->q(e) for every e almost surely; integrate over rho and intersect the finitely many directions. Consequently the directing q is a measurable empirical-frequency limit, so the conditional representation used in Section 1 is available in the original sequence as well.

External standard inputs here are only weak compactness on a compact metric space, the countable product construction, and the ordinary IID strong law. Finite exchangeability without an infinite extension would leave a nonzero sampling error and would not give this exact conclusion. This is why a two-cookie exchangeable prefix is insufficient.

## 6. Exact obstruction to extending the reduction to all IID elliptic stacks
In d=2 set q=1/4 and 0<a<1/4. At each site choose an independent fair sign S_x. The first cookie assigns probabilities q+S_x a and q-S_x a to +e_1 and -e_1, and probability q to each vertical direction. The second cookie has the opposite horizontal biases, and every subsequent cookie is placebo. This is an IID uniformly elliptic, bounded-stack environment. Every individual cookie's averaged transition vector is the fair vector, and even the first two cookie vectors are exchangeable as a pair (replace S by -S). Their infinite sequence is not exchangeable.

For the finite path 0,e_1,0,e_1, independence of the origin's sign and the first cookie at e_1 gives exactly
P(path)=q E[(q+S_0 a)(q-S_0 a)]=q^3-q a^2.
For a=1/8 this is 3/256, while the fair walk gives q^3=4/256. The pair of origin departure indicators for +e_1 has covariance -a^2.

This path law cannot equal that of ANY IID Markovian RWRE with the same averaged first-step distribution. Such a RWRE would have E[Q_x(+e_1)]=E[Q_x(-e_1)]=q, and its same path probability would be
E[Q_0(+e_1)^2] E[Q_{e_1}(-e_1)] >= q^2 q=q^3,
because E[(Q_0(+e_1)-q)^2]>=0. Thus the exact RWRE representation proved under (AE) fails within the original hypotheses, even with UEL and two non-placebo cookies. This is a counterexample to a proposed universal representation, NOT a counterexample to zero velocity on the non-transient event.

In contrast, using the same sign at every cookie (a genuinely exchangeable infinite stack) gives P(path)=q^3+q a^2=5/256, exactly the persistent-sign RWRE formula. The extra correlation is retained by the latent transition vector, not averaged away.

## 8. Completed exchangeable-arrow special case

The retrieved original text of Zerner (2002), Theorem 1, printed p.192, assumes spatial IID strictly positive Markovian transition vectors and P_0[A_e union A_{-e}]=0 for a signed coordinate vector e. It concludes X_n dot e/n -> 0 almost surely. Its strict ellipticity is exactly what Section 2 proves for the directing vector. The paper is not a theorem for arbitrary cookie stacks.

For the represented RWRE, Kosygina--Zerner Theorem 3.16 applies because a constant cookie stack is a special excited environment with (IID), (EL). For each coordinate its two-sided directional escape probability is therefore zero or one. If any such probability is one, spatial escape has probability one and the target complement is null. Otherwise all these probabilities are zero, and Zerner's Theorem 1 applies to every coordinate. Their normalized values tend to zero almost surely. Equality of full averaged path laws from Section 1 transfers this result back to the original excited walk satisfying (AE). Thus the desired conclusion is proved for the entire exchangeable-arrow special class, without a stack-length, drift-sign, moment or uniform-ellipticity assumption.

Referee check: the directing vector is an IID field of transition vectors, not merely a collection of identically distributed one-coordinate means; its strict positivity has been proved separately. Each escape and limit event is a full-path measurable event, so equality on cylinders suffices for the transfer. This completes the previously pending special case.
