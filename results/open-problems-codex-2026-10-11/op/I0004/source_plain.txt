 The randomly oriented Manhattan lattice in 2D is transient 
 
 
 Ahmed Bou-Rabee
 
  and  
 Yuval Peres
 

 Abstract. 
    
 Independently orient each horizontal and vertical line of  ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2}  by a fair
coin. A walker chooses one of the two lines through its current position
with equal probability and takes one step in the direction of that line.
Redner  [ 1989 ]  introduced this walk as a model of transport in
an isotropic random velocity field and predicted that its root-mean-square
displacement grows like  n 2 / 3 n^{2/3} . We prove that the walk is transient
almost surely.
The proof is a short variational argument. 
  
 
 
 Figure 1.  One fair coin directs each line, so every edge of a line points the
same way. The two blue arrows are the possible steps from the marked site. 
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 The  randomly oriented Manhattan lattice  is the square lattice with
every horizontal and vertical line given a direction by an independent fair
coin. We study the random walk that at each step chooses one of the two
lines through its position with equal probability and moves one unit in that
line’s direction (Figure  1 ). Once a line is oriented, every later step along it has the same direction.
Figure  2  compares the walk with simple random walk. 
 
 
 Figure 2.  The randomly oriented Manhattan walk on the left and simple random
walk on the right, after  400 400  steps in the top row and  4000 4000  steps in the
bottom row, colored by time from blue to red. The two panels of each row are
drawn to the same scale. 
 
 
 Redner [1989]  introduced this walk and predicted a
non-Gaussian displacement profile on the scale  n 2 / 3 n^{2/3} . In particular,
this prediction implies transience of the walk.

 1.3.  Proof overview 

 
 For reversible walks, the usual technique to show transience is to exhibit a
unit flow from a vertex to infinity with finite energy, see, for example,
 [ Lyons and Peres, 2016 , Theorem 2.11] . In our nonreversible setting, we instead use a
variational formula for the resolvent, given, for example, in
 [ Komorowski, Landim, and Olla, 2012 , Theorem 4.1] . Variational principles for operators that are
not self-adjoint are standard in homogenization; see
 Armstrong, Kuusi, and Mourrat [2019, Chapter 10] ,  Armstrong, Bou-Rabee, and Kuusi [2024, (2.4)] , and
 Armstrong, Bou-Rabee, and Kuusi [2026, (2.32)] . 
 
 
 The variational formula bounds the annealed Green function using a test
function at each spatial frequency. We build the test function from the
line orientations. It is not a flow and satisfies no additional constraint:
every square-integrable function of the orientations can be used.
The difficulty is to choose one that makes the bound small. 
 
 
 The zero test function in this variational principle gives the same bound as for simple random walk, which is too weak: its
frequency integral diverges logarithmically. To improve on this, we must exploit the enhancement of the drift by careful choice of test function.
This choice is made from a family of functions formed from products of one and three line signs, indexed by one even function of a single
frequency. As we will see, a careful optimization of the even function will make the frequency integral converge.

 1.4.  Related results 

 
 1.4.1.  Randomly oriented Manhattan lattices 

 
 As discussed above, the displacement scale  n 2 / 3 n^{2/3}  predicted by  Redner [1989] 
would imply transience of the walk if accompanied by an annealed local limit: the return
probability would then have order  n − 4 / 3 n^{-4/3} . Simulations support this decay
 [ Mejía-Monasterio, Nechaev,
Oshanin, and Vasilyev, 2020 , Section 3.2] ; further discussions of the displacement
prediction appear in  Bouchaud and Georges [1990]  and
 Pène [2020, Section 3.5] . The transience conjecture is attributed
to Guillotin-Plantard in  Bosi [2019, Appendix A] . 
 
 
 In the mathematics literature, the Laplace transform of the annealed
mean-square displacement has been bounded rigorously by
 Ledger, Tóth, and Valkó [2018, Theorem 1.1]  in dimensions two and three. In dimension three,
 Nguyen [2026, Theorem 1.1]  obtained order
 λ − 2 ​ | log ⁡ λ | \lambda^{-2}\sqrt{\lvert\log\lambda\rvert} , up to multiplicative factors
 ( log ⁡ | log ⁡ λ | ) ± ( 2 + ε ) (\log\lvert\log\lambda\rvert)^{\pm(2+\varepsilon)}  for every
 ε > 0 \varepsilon>0  and all sufficiently small  λ \lambda .
These estimates establish superdiffusivity in the Laplace-transform sense, but do not immediately yield bounds on mean-square displacement. Such bounds alone do not control the probability of returning to the origin. 
 
 
 When the horizontal orientations are independent and uniform on
 { − 1 , 1 } \{-1,1\}  and the vertical orientations alternate deterministically,
the walk is almost surely transient  [ Bosi, 2019 , Theorem 9] .
On the randomly oriented Manhattan lattice, following both orientations at every
step gives the deterministic update
 z ↦ z + ω ⁡ ( z , 1 ) ​ e 1 + ω ⁡ ( z , 2 ) ​ e 2 z\mapsto z+\omega(z,1)e_{1}+\omega(z,2)e_{2} ; this walk is eventually
two-periodic for  ℙ \mathbb{P} -almost every environment
 [ Collevecchio, Hamza, and Tournier, 2019 , Theorem 1] . Quantum and classical network models on
the alternating deterministic lattice are studied in
 Beamond, Owczarek, and Cardy [2003] . 
 
 
 Our proof uses the nonreversible resolvent variational formula
 [ Komorowski, Landim, and Olla, 2012 , Theorem 4.1]  that  Ledger, Tóth, and Valkó [2018, Section 2]  use for displacement bounds.
Polynomial test functions similar to ours also appear in the study of asymmetric exclusion
 [ Landim, Quastel, Salmhofer, and Yau, 2004 , Sections 3–4]   [ Yau, 2004 , Section 2]  and the
stationary one-dimensional self-repelling Brownian polymer
 [ Tarrès, Tóth, and Valkó, 2012 ] . Variational formulas for
resolvents are discussed in  Tóth [2018, (39)] , and their connection
with capacities and transience in  Gaudillière and Landim [2014, Theorem 2.4] . 
 
 
 
 1.4.2.  Partially oriented lattices 

 
 If only the horizontal lines are oriented, the walker can move up,
down, or in the direction of its current horizontal line. Choosing
uniformly among these three moves makes the vertical coordinate a
lazy simple random walk independent of the orientations. One can
therefore describe the visits to horizontal lines before using their
directions to determine the horizontal displacement. This is a
discrete version of the Matheron–de Marsily model
 [ Matheron and de Marsily, 1980 ] . For the walk studied here, each coordinate
depends on the orientations encountered by the other, so the same
separation is unavailable. 
 
 
 With independent horizontal orientations uniform on  { − 1 , 1 } \{-1,1\} ,
the partially oriented walk is almost surely transient
 [ Campanino and Petritis, 2003 , Theorem 1.8]  and has zero speed
 [ Campanino and Petritis, 2004 , Theorem 2.11] . The horizontal displacement grows on
a different scale from the vertical displacement:
 Guillotin-Plantard and Le Ny [2008a, Theorem 2]  proved an annealed joint functional limit
theorem with scales  n 3 / 4 n^{3/4}  and  n 1 / 2 n^{1/2} , respectively.
The limiting coordinates are dependent, even though the vertical
motion is independent of the orientations. The annealed return
probability has order  n − 5 / 4 n^{-5/4} 
 [ Castell, Guillotin-Plantard, Pène, and
Schapira, 2011 , Theorem 20] . 
 
 
 For the partially oriented walk,  Guillotin-Plantard and Le Ny [2008b, Theorem 1]  extend almost-sure transience from independent
horizontal orientations uniform on  { − 1 , 1 } \{-1,1\}  to orientations whose
probabilities are sampled along a stationary orbit of a dynamical system. The orientations
are independent conditional on the orbit, but can be dependent after
averaging over it. Their theorem assumes that each orientation has
mean zero and that the probabilities satisfy the integrability
condition (2.3).  Brémont [2017]  allows all transition
probabilities to depend on the layer. In such models, changing only
departures from the horizontal axis can turn a transient walk into
a recurrent one  [ Bosi, Hu, and Peres, 2022 , Corollary 2.6] ; see
also  Brémont [2017, Proposition 7.8] . 
 
 
 
 1.4.3.  Diffusion in incompressible random flows 

 
 Our walk has divergence-free drift, making it a discrete analogue of a
Brownian particle transported by a stationary incompressible random flow
 [ Bouchaud, Georges, Koplik, Provata, and
Redner, 1990 ] . For a class of incompressible random drifts with covariance
decay  R − 2 ​ ( 1 − γ ) R^{-2(1-\gamma)}  at distance  R R ,  Armstrong, Bou-Rabee, and Kuusi [2026]  obtained moment
estimates over the environment for the quenched mean-square displacement
at scale  t 2 / ( 2 − γ ) t^{2/(2-\gamma)} , for sufficiently small positive  γ \gamma .
For the  d d -dimensional version of our walk, the covariance of each drift
component is constant along its coordinate axis and zero elsewhere.
Its average over a box of side  R R  centered at the origin therefore has
order  R 1 − d R^{1-d} .
Matching these exponents gives the heuristic correspondence 
 

 
 (4) 
 
 γ = 3 − d 2 . \gamma=\frac{3-d}{2}\,. 
 
 
 The renormalization-group predictions of  Bouchaud and Georges [1990] , as recalled in
 Armstrong, Bou-Rabee, and Kuusi [2026] , then give mean-square displacement of order  t 4 / 3 t^{4/3}  in
dimension two,  t ​ ( log ⁡ t ) 1 / 2 t(\log t)^{1/2}  in dimension three, and  t t  in higher
dimensions. In dimension two this recovers Redner’s displacement exponent.
It remains unclear how to adapt the methods of  Armstrong, Bou-Rabee, and Kuusi [2026]  to our
setting, since their argument requires sufficiently small positive  γ \gamma .

 7.  Open questions 

 
 Problem 1   (Mean-square displacement) . 
 
 Is the annealed mean-square displacement of order  t 4 / 3 t^{4/3}  as
 t → ∞ t\to\infty ?
Can the renormalization scheme of  Armstrong, Bou-Rabee, and Kuusi [2026, Section 3]  be
adapted to this setting? 
 
 
 
 Problem 2   (A decay rate) . 
 
 Can one prove that  p ¯ t ​ ( 0 , 0 ) = O ⁡ ( t − β ) \overline{p}_{t}(0,0)=O(t^{-\beta})  as  t → ∞ t\to\infty 
for some  β > 1 \beta>1 ? Redner’s prediction  [ Redner, 1989 , (3)]  suggests return probabilities of order  t − 4 / 3 t^{-4/3} . 
 
 
 
 Problem 3   (Which orientations are transient) . 
 
 Which orientation fields give a recurrent walk, and which give a transient
one? Theorem  1.1  gives transience for  ℙ \mathbb{P} -almost every
environment. A uniform random shift by a vector in  { 0 , 1 } 2 \{0,1\}^{2}  of the
alternating environment of Section  1.1  gives a stationary
ergodic law with mean-zero line orientations under which the walk is
recurrent almost surely. 
 
 
 For the partially oriented walk,  Guillotin-Plantard and Le Ny [2008b, Section 4]  ask a related
question when an irrational rotation determines the horizontal orientations.
Fix an irrational  α \alpha . Direct the line at height  y ∈ ℤ y\in\mathbb{Z}  to the right
if the fractional part of  y ​ α y\alpha  is less than  1 / 2 1/2 , and to the left
otherwise; vertical edges can be crossed in either direction.
Is this walk recurrent or transient?
