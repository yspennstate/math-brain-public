    <title>A Sharp Phase Transition for Killed Branching Random Walks with Heavy-Tailed Displacements</title>
    <updated>2026-09-26T11:18:08Z</updated>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32480v1" rel="alternate" type="text/html"/>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32480v1" rel="related" type="application/pdf" title="pdf"/>
    <summary>We study the survival of a branching random walk in a supercritical Galton-Watson tree subject to a deterministic, superlinear killing barrier with heavy-tailed displacements. For a fixed $m&gt;0$, we consider a family of such barriers and kill particles whose ancestral paths fall below the barrier. Under some standing assumptions, we establish a sharp phase transition at $m=4$: the process becomes extinct almost surely for $m&lt;4$, while survives with positive probability for $m&gt;4$. Notably, a natural first-moment heuristic suggests the threshold $m=2$; the true critical value $4$ arises from a deterministic constraint along infinite surviving rays. At the critical value $m=4$, we provide examples showing that the same standing assumptions do not determine the survival behavior. The proofs are based on analysis of infinite surviving rays and embedded trees.</summary>
