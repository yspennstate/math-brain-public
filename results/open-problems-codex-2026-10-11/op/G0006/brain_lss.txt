    Let {X_{m,n} : 0 <= m < n} satisfy: (L1) X_{0,n} <= X_{0,m} + X_{m,n} for every 0 < m < n; (L2) for each m >= 1 the sequence {X_{nm, (n+1)m} : n >= 1} is STATIONARY; (L3) the distribution of the row {X_{m, m+k} : k >= 1} does not depend on m; (L4) E[X_{0,1}^+] < infinity and E[X_{0,n}] >= -c_0 n for every n, for some finite c_0. Then: (a) gamma = lim_n E[X_{0,n}]/n = inf_n E[X_{0,n}]/n exists in [-c_0, infinity); (b) X = lim_n X_{0,n}/n exists almost surely and in L^1, with E[X] = gamma; (c) if in addition all the stationary sequences in (L2) are ERGODIC then X = gamma almost surely. The difference from Kingman is that (L2) and (L3) together are strictly WEAKER than joint stationarity of the whole array: they demand stationarity only along each fixed block length and equality of the row laws, not invariance of the full joint distribution. That weakening is what lets the theorem apply to models where the array is not generated by a single measure-preserving transformation. Note also that (L4) asks only for the POSITIVE part of X_{0,1} to be integrable, weaker than Kingman's E|X_{0,1}| < infinity. Quoting Kingman's hypotheses when using Liggett's conclusion, or the reverse, is the standard misquotation here. [Liggett (1985), 'An improved subadditive ergodic theorem', Ann. Probab. 13, 1279-1285; textbook form in Durrett, Probability: Theory and Examples, 5th ed., Theorem 6.6.1] (L1) subadditivity (L2) stationarity of the sequence of blocks of each fixed length (L3) the row law does not depend on the starting index (L4) E[X_{0,1}^+] finite and a linear lower bound on E[X_{0,n}] ergodicity of the block sequences is needed only for a constant limit

The smoothing transform concerns fixed points of the distributional equation W =_d sum_i T_i W_i, where (T_i) is a given, typically random, sequence of nonnegative weights and (W_i) are i.i.d. copies of W independent of (T_i); such fixed points arise as limits of branching random walk martingales, including derivative martingale limits, or of Mandelbrot multiplicative cascades, and their tail behavior, characterized by a power-law index alpha solving E[sum_i T_i^alpha]=1 or a related equation, governs the limit's regular variation. Durrett and Liggett characterized the solution set of the smoothing transform equation, establishing existence, uniqueness up to a scaling constant, and the precise tail index under moment conditions on (T_i), a result foundational for analyzing branching random walk boundary and derivative martingale limits and related models such as the random energy model. Separately, Poisson-Dirichlet distributions PD(alpha,theta) arise as the limiting law of ranked, normalized jump sizes of a subordinator, of ranked cycle lengths in random permutations, or of ranked masses in fragmentation and coalescent processes, characterized via the stick-breaking (GEM) construction or via a Poisson process of jumps of a stable subordinator normalized by its total mass. Neither result gives an explicit closed-form density for the general fixed point or limit; the smoothing transform's fixed point is typically characterized only implicitly, through its tail index and functional equation, and both results require specific moment or regular-variation conditions on the input weights or subordinator to hold. Typical failure: Assuming the smoothing transform has a unique nontrivial fixed point up to scale without checking that the characteristic equation E[sum T_i^alpha]=1 actually has a solution in the admissible range, or without the Durrett-Liggett/Biggins regularity conditions, particularly in boundary cases such as the critical branching random walk where the derivative martingale, rather than the ordinary martingale, is the relevant object; applying the generic smoothing-transform tail behavior in a boundary or degenerate parameter regime without re-deriving it is a common source of incorrect tail-index claims. Canonical reference: Durrett and Liggett, 1983, Zeitschrift fur Wahrscheinlichkeitstheorie und verwandte Gebiete; Biggins and Kyprianou, 2005, Electronic Journal of Probability; Pitman and Yor, 1997, Annals of Probability. for the smoothing transform: existence of alpha solving E[sum_i T_i^alpha]=1 in the admissible range, plus regularity conditions such as E[sum_i T_i^alpha log^+ T_i]<infinity from Durrett-Liggett/Biggins theory for Poisson-Dirichlet: a well-defined subordinator, or fragmentation/coalescent structure, whose ranked, normalized jump sizes are being studied independence of (T_i) from the i.i.d. copies (W_i) in the smoothing transform equation non-degeneracy conditions ruling out the trivial Dirac-mass-at-0 solution
