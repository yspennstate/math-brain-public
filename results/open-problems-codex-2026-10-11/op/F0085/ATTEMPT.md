# F0085 — rigorous partial answer (2026-10-11)

## Exact target and verdict
Let Ξ be intensity-one homogeneous Poisson centres in R^d, and let 0<α<1 be a constant appetite. The target asks whether unclaimed sites percolate for all sufficiently small α and fail to percolate for all sufficiently large α<1; it asks analogous questions for claimed sites. Percolation means existence of an unbounded connected component, rather than infinitely many points in a bounded component.

VERDICT: PARTIAL. The literature already proves the two small-appetite assertions for d≥2 (FPV Theorem 1.1), and high-dimensional near-one percolation of CLOSED claimed sites (FPV Theorem 1.2). We have not proved near-one absence of unclaimed percolation in any general d≥2, or near-one closed-claimed percolation for all low dimensions. Literature search is bounded and partly rate limited; we do not assert these remain open as a universally verified fact.

This file proves topology/version identification, order consequences, d=1 boundary cases, and a rigorous obstruction to the prior attempted proof. These are elementary consequences and corrections, not claimed as novel solutions of the unresolved regimes.

## Proposition A: the canonical unclaimed set is exactly R^d minus C_α
Proof. If x∈U_α, openness gives a ball B(x,r)⊆U_α. This ball is disjoint from D_α, so x∉closure(D_α). Thus U_α⊆R^d\C_α.
Conversely if x∉C_α, some ball B(x,r) has empty intersection with D_α. Throughout this ball ψ_α takes only the values ∞ and ∆. Since E_α is null, this ball is essentially a subset of ψ_α^{-1}(∞). The defining canonicalization therefore assigns ∞ to x. Canonicalizing a canonical allocation leaves it unchanged: for a non-∆ point its open constant neighbourhood supplies that value; if a ∆ point had an essentially constant neighbourhood the original canonical construction would already assign that value, since the original and canonical versions agree a.e. Thus x∈U_α. Both inclusions prove equality. ∎

This makes FPV Theorem 1.1(ii) applicable to canonical unclaimed sites, with an explicitly proved version-identification step. No a.e. equality of arbitrary sets is being treated as a topological equality.

## Proposition B: monotonicity and one-sided threshold reductions
Fix 0≤α₁≤α₂. Almost surely C_{α₁}⊆C_{α₂} and U_{α₂}⊆U_{α₁}.
Proof. Outside a single null exceptional set, a site claimed at α₁ has finite distance to its assigned centre. The Proposition 22 distance inequality makes its distance at α₂ finite, hence it is claimed at α₂. This proves D_{α₁}\D_{α₂} is null.
If x∈D_{α₁}, it lies in an open territory T_{α₁}(ξ). Every ball about x intersects that territory in a nonempty open set, which has strictly positive Lebesgue measure. Since D_{α₁}\D_{α₂} is null, every such ball intersects D_{α₂}. Therefore x∈C_{α₂}. Taking closure and using closedness of C_{α₂} proves C_{α₁}⊆C_{α₂}. Taking complements and invoking Proposition A proves U_{α₂}⊆U_{α₁}. ∎

Any unbounded connected subset of a subset A of B remains an unbounded connected subset of B. It lies in a connected component of B, which is therefore unbounded. Consequently vacant percolation at α₂ implies vacant percolation at α₁, while claimed-closure percolation at α₁ implies claimed-closure percolation at α₂. These implications hold also for probabilities under the common-centre coupling. The set of α for which vacant percolation has positive probability is downward closed; the analogous claimed set is upward closed. FPV gives a nonempty small interval in the former and a nonempty small interval of nonpercolation in the latter. It remains to prove that the vacant percolation interval has supremum strictly less than one. Monotonicity by itself supplies no such strictness.

## Proposition C: raw canonical claimed sites never percolate
For every fixed finite α in any d≥1 under the HHP geometry hypotheses, every connected component of D_α is bounded a.s.
Proof. Work on the probability-one event on which all territories are open and bounded. Let K⊆D_α be nonempty and connected; choose x∈K and its centre ξ. The subsets K∩T_α(ξ) and K∩⋃_{ζ≠ξ}T_α(ζ) are disjoint, relatively open in K, and together cover K. The first is nonempty. If the second were nonempty these two sets would separate K, contradicting connectedness. Thus K⊆T_α(ξ), a bounded set. Apply this to every component. ∎

The closed union C_α can percolate because it includes interfaces between territories. The original source's a.e. allocation language is not enough to define percolation independently of null-set choices. Proposition C is about the raw canonical version ONLY and is not a refutation of FPV, whose definition explicitly takes closure. The unresolved claimed regime in this report uses C_α.

## Proposition D: one dimension and endpoints
For d=1 and each fixed 0<α<1, both C_α and U_α have only bounded connected components a.s. At α=0, C_0=∅ and U_0=R^d. At α=1, C_1=R^d and canonical U_1=∅ (all d).
Proof in d=1. Define Y_n=Leb(U_α∩[n,n+1)). Translation equivariance makes (Y_n) stationary. Unit translation on a one-dimensional Poisson process is ergodic: its restrictions to half-open unit intervals, translated into [0,1), are independent identically distributed random point configurations, and unit translation is the bilateral iid shift. The allocation is a measurable factor of this shift and therefore ergodic as well. The usual pointwise ergodic theorem applies to bounded variables 0≤Y_n≤1. Fubini and stationarity give E Y_0=∫_0^1 P(x∈U_α)dx=1−α.
Applying the ergodic theorem to the forward and inverse shifts yields, almost surely,
lim_{N→∞} Leb(U_α∩[0,N))/N=1−α,
lim_{N→∞} Leb(U_α∩[−N,0))/N=1−α.
By Proposition A the complementary C_α averages are α>0. Consequently neither U_α nor C_α can contain a half-line: a U_α half-line would force the corresponding C_α average to zero, and a C_α half-line would force the corresponding U_α average to zero.
Every connected subset of R is an interval. Indeed if a<b lie in a connected set and a<t<b does not, its intersections with (−∞,t) and (t,∞) give a separation. An unbounded interval contains a half-line in at least one direction. Thus neither set can have an unbounded connected component.
At α=0 every territory has measure zero. Canonical territories are open, so are empty. The canonical rule assigns every point to ∞; C_0=∅. At α=1 the phase theorem says D_1 has full measure, hence it is dense (every nonempty ball has positive measure). Thus C_1=R^d, and Proposition A gives U_1=∅. ∎

The d=1 conclusion is outside FPV's d≥2 question, so it is recorded only as a boundary special case. The α=1 conclusion does not imply disappearance of vacant percolation strictly below one: nested percolating sets may have empty intersection.

## Proposition E: no deterministic common bound on territory radii
Fix d≥1, 0<α<1, and r_α=(α/v_d)^{1/d}, where v_d is unit-ball volume. Put R_ξ=ess sup{|x−ξ|:x∈T_α(ξ)}. Almost surely sup_{ξ∈Ξ} R_ξ/r_α=∞.
Proof. Fix any positive integer M and choose an integer k>(M+1)^d. Select infinitely many deterministic pairwise disjoint balls B(z_n,r_α), e.g. along one axis with centre spacing 3r_α. Their centre counts are independent Poisson variables with mean α. The event A_n that a ball contains at least k centres has a common probability p>0. The probability that none of the first N balls has A_n is (1−p)^N, tending to zero. Thus almost surely at least one such ball contains k centres.
On the probability-one event that all centres are sated, choose k of those centres ξ₁,…,ξ_k. Suppose every R_{ξ_i}≤M r_α. Outside a null set each corresponding territory lies inside B(ξ_i,M r_α), hence inside the ball with centre z_n and radius (M+1)r_α, allowing closed balls without changing their volume. The k territories are disjoint and each has volume α. Their union therefore has volume kα. But the containing ball has volume v_d((M+1)r_α)^d=(M+1)^d α<kα, a contradiction. At least one radius exceeds M r_α. Intersecting these probability-one events over all positive integers M proves the asserted infinite supremum. ∎

Every individual territory is still bounded a.s.; the supremum across all centres need not be bounded. The proof disproves deterministic-radius domination centred at the same Poisson centres. It does not disprove the dependent random-radius domination in Gouéré or FPV. It also does not supply the prior attempt's missing estimates for exceptional clusters.

## Proposition F: density, monotonicity and an empty endpoint are insufficient
This is a counterexample to a general inference, NOT to the stable-marriage question. In any d≥2 there exists a translation-stationary, translation-ergodic family of nested open sets W_ε, 0<ε<1, with volume fraction ε, all percolating, and with intersection over ε>0 empty.
Proof. Let V be uniform on [0,1), and let
W_ε={x∈R^d : the fractional part of x_d−V lies in (0,ε)}.
This is the disjoint union over n∈Z of open slabs R^{d−1}×(V+n,V+n+ε). Each slab is connected and unbounded, since d−1≥1. Thus every W_ε percolates. If ε₁≤ε₂ then W_{ε₁}⊆W_{ε₂}. For a fixed fractional part t∈[0,1), membership for every ε>0 is impossible: t=0 is excluded, and if t>0 choose ε<t. The intersection is therefore empty.
Uniform V gives P(x∈W_ε)=ε for every fixed x. In expanding axis-parallel boxes the volume fraction tends to ε, since the last-coordinate occupied intervals are periodic of period one and total occupied length per period ε, with an O(1) endpoint discrepancy. Translations along the last coordinate rotate V modulo one, preserving its uniform law, while other translations leave the set unchanged; the law is stationary under the full translation group. Any event invariant under all spatial translations lifts to an event for V invariant under all circle rotations. Such an event has probability zero or one: averaging its indicator over the rotations gives its constant Lebesgue mean, whereas invariance makes that average equal the original indicator a.e. Thus the family is ergodic under the full group. ∎

This proves precisely why the source's decreasing vacant volume and the α=1 endpoint cannot alone close the near-one gap. Dependence or geometry special to stable allocation must enter. The slab process is not a Poisson stable allocation and is not reported as a counterexample to F0085.

## New proved deductions: G–L
These are deductions from the verified HHP inputs, not claimed novel literature theorems. All allocations to a general centre configuration below are site-optimal Gale–Shapley allocations; canonical versions are used for the Poisson process. All parameter sequences are countable. Lebesgue measure is denoted by |·|.

### G. Exact occupied-ball representation
For 0<α≤1 let R_α(ξ)=ess sup{|x−ξ|:x∈T_α(ξ)} and O_α=⋃_{ξ∈Ξ}B(ξ,R_α(ξ)), with OPEN balls. Almost surely
D_α⊆O_α⊆C_α, C_α=closure(O_α), and U_α=R^d\closure(O_α).
Proof. Every territory is open and bounded. Its ordinary distance supremum therefore equals its essential supremum: a point at a larger distance would have an open positive-measure neighbourhood in the territory at larger distances. In fact every point of the territory is at distance strictly less than R_α(ξ); equality at a point of an open territory would give points farther out by moving slightly away from ξ. Thus D_α⊆O_α. If an unclaimed x lay in B(ξ,R_α(ξ)), the definition of essential supremum supplies a y∈T_α(ξ) farther from ξ than x. Then ξ covets x and x desires ξ, contradicting stability. Hence O_α∩U_α=∅ and Proposition A gives O_α⊆C_α. Taking closures in both inclusions gives C_α=closure(O_α); taking complements proves the last equality. ∎
These balls have nonlocal, dependent radii. This is not an iid Boolean model.

### H. Continuity of outer radii from below: a complete limit-allocation proof
Assume a locally finite countable centre set, appetites α_n↑α_*>0, stable site-optimal allocations ψ_n and ψ_*, all centres sated at these appetites, each R_*(ξ) finite, the HHP site/appetite monotonicity inequalities, and a.e. uniqueness at α_*. Then R_n(ξ)↑R_*(ξ) for every centre. The hypotheses hold a.s. for the Poisson process when α_*≤1. They also hold for any finite centre configuration at every finite positive α_*.
Proof. Write γ_n(ξ,r)=|ψ_n^{-1}(ξ)∩B(ξ,r)|. Saturation means
R_n(ξ)>r iff α_n−γ_n(ξ,r)>0.
HHP Proposition 22(ii) makes this residual nondecreasing with appetite. Therefore the radii are nondecreasing and R_n(ξ)≤R_*(ξ): if a rational r lay strictly between R_*(ξ) and R_n(ξ), the residual at n would be positive and the target residual zero, violating that inequality.
Discard the union of the null exceptional sets for site monotonicity, the allocations' ∆ sets, all centre-pair bisectors, and all territory points outside the bounds B(ξ,R_*(ξ)). This union N is null because both the centres and indices are countable. At x outside N the distance to its assignment is nonincreasing in n. If x is ever claimed, all subsequent candidate centres lie in the compact ball centred at x whose radius is its first finite assignment distance. There are finitely many such centres by local finiteness. Their distances are distinct because bisectors were discarded. A nonincreasing sequence in this finite list is eventually constant, so the assignment eventually stabilizes. If x is never claimed, its assignment is always ∞. Define ψ_lim(x) by this eventual value outside N and put ψ_lim(x)=∆ on N. This is measurable: eventual assignment events and the event of being unclaimed at every stage are countable measurable operations.
For any fixed centre ξ, the indicators of its territories converge a.e. to that of ψ_lim^{-1}(ξ). All these indicators are supported, up to null sets, in the fixed finite-volume ball B(ξ,R_*(ξ)). Dominated convergence therefore gives
|ψ_lim^{-1}(ξ)|=lim_n |ψ_n^{-1}(ξ)|=α_*.
Thus the limit is an allocation and every centre is sated. There is no loss of territory mass at infinity.
It is stable. If (x,ξ) were unstable for ψ_lim, then x is not ∆, ξ is sated, and there is y assigned to ξ in ψ_lim with |y−ξ|>|x−ξ|. At all sufficiently large stages y is assigned to ξ. If x has a finite limit assignment ζ, it too has that assignment at all sufficiently large stages, preserving the strict desire inequality |x−ξ|<|x−ζ|. If x is unclaimed in the limit, it is unclaimed at every stage. In either case (x,ξ) would be unstable for ψ_n at all sufficiently large n, a contradiction. A.e. uniqueness at α_* identifies ψ_lim with ψ_* a.e.
Dominated convergence now also gives γ_n(ξ,r)→γ_*(ξ,r) for each r. Whenever r<R_*(ξ), the limit α_*−γ_*(ξ,r) is strictly positive; hence α_n−γ_n(ξ,r)>0 eventually and R_n(ξ)>r eventually. This proves convergence to R_*(ξ), together with the monotonicity already established. ∎
For completeness, the finite-configuration hypotheses are justified as follows. If there are k finite centres, the claimed volume is at most kα and there is infinite unclaimed volume. Stability therefore rules out any unsated centre. For r with |B(ξ,r)|>kα there must be an unclaimed point in that ball outside the null ∆ set. If the territory radius exceeded r, that point and ξ would form an unstable pair. Consequently R(ξ)≤(kα/v_d)^{1/d}. A.e. uniqueness follows from HHP Theorem 2: every distance-threshold graph on a finite centre set is recurrent on each of its finite components. No translation-invariance hypothesis is being applied to a finite truncated process.

### I. Unconditional compact coverage as α approaches one
For any deterministic compact K⊂R^d and any deterministic α_n↑1, almost surely there is a finite random N such that K⊆O_{α_n}⊆C_{α_n} for all n≥N. In particular
lim_{α↑1} P(U_α∩K≠∅)=0.
Proof. The verified source result, HHP Theorem 6(ii), states: "A.s. every site is coveted by infinitely many centers." At critical appetite every centre is sated. For the canonical version, G identifies ordinary and essential territory-radius suprema. Hence a site coveted by a centre is strictly inside its radius ball. It follows that O_1=R^d a.s., not merely that its closure is all space.
By compactness select finitely many critical balls B(ξ_i,R_1(ξ_i)) covering K. The continuous function f(x)=max_i(R_1(ξ_i)−|x−ξ_i|) is positive everywhere on K, so its minimum δ on K is positive. H gives R_{α_n}(ξ_i)>R_1(ξ_i)−δ/2 for every selected i once n is sufficiently large. For every x∈K choose an index attaining f(x); then |x−ξ_i|<R_{α_n}(ξ_i). Thus these finitely many subcritical balls cover K eventually. The empty compact set is trivial.
The corresponding probabilities along the sequence tend to zero by bounded convergence applied to the indicator of K meeting U_{α_n}. Proposition B orders these probabilities for each fixed appetite pair, so the same limit holds for arbitrary real α↑1. This last probability statement does not require a simultaneous probability-one event over uncountably many appetites. ∎
This strengthens the earlier density result. It does NOT imply absence of an unbounded vacant component: the dependence scale remains uncontrolled.

### J. Finite-buffer critical certificates and their limits
Fix a block side a>0, Q=[−a/2,a/2]^d, and an integer M≥1. Retain only Ξ∩[−aM,aM]^d and allocate the whole of R^d to these finitely many centres with appetite α, using the site-optimal algorithm. Let their essential territory radii be R^{(M)}_α(ξ). Define the LOCAL certificate
G_{α,M}={Q⊆⋃_{ξ in the buffer}B(ξ,R^{(M)}_α(ξ))},
and p_{α,M}=P(G_{α,M}^c). The union is empty if the buffer has no centres. The radii here are computed for the FINITE input, so the certificate depends on no centres outside the buffer.
Claims: G_{α,M} implies Q⊆C_α; its probability increases with both α and M; p_{α,M}↓p_{1,M} as α↑1 for fixed M; and p_{1,M}↓0 as M→∞. More generally, for fixed α≤1, G_{α,M} increases to {Q⊆O_α} a.s.
Proof of certification. Each finite centre is sated by H's finite-configuration argument. At any site inside one of its radius balls, the finite allocation cannot be unclaimed, by the instability argument of G. Thus almost every site inside the ball is claimed in the finite allocation. HHP Proposition 21(i) gives a.e. claimed inclusion after the omitted centres are added. Every neighbourhood of any point of the finite open ball consequently meets global claimed sites, so the entire open ball lies in C_α. This proof does not presume openness or stationarity of the finite allocation.
Proof of truncation convergence. For a fixed α≤1, let ψ_M denote the finite-input allocations. For each centre ξ, once it is retained, Proposition 21(ii) and saturation give R^{(M)}_α(ξ) increasing with M and bounded above by the global R_α(ξ). Outside a countable union of null exceptional sets and bisectors, assignments stabilize if the site is ever claimed: Proposition 21(i) decreases its assignment distance and local finiteness gives finitely many candidates within its first finite distance. Otherwise the site remains unclaimed. Define the limit as in H, putting ∆ on the null exceptional set. Each fixed centre's territories have a fixed finite support bound R_α(ξ); dominated convergence makes that centre sated in the limit with volume α. Stability passes exactly as in H, since a farther limit territory point is assigned at all sufficiently large stages, and a desired site is either eventually assigned to its limiting centre or unclaimed throughout. Global Poisson a.e. uniqueness then identifies the limit with ψ_α. Dominated convergence of γ at each radius, followed by the positive residual argument, proves R^{(M)}_α(ξ)↑R_α(ξ).
It follows that each finite open ball is contained in the corresponding global open ball, so G_{α,M} implies Q⊆O_α. Conversely, if Q⊆O_α, compactness supplies finitely many balls with a positive cover margin as in I. Their centres are all retained for large M, and convergence of their radii preserves the cover. Hence G_{α,M} eventually holds. This proves the asserted increasing-event equality. At α=1, I's critical O_1=R^d gives p_{1,M}↓0.
For fixed M, the centre configuration is finite and unique. H applied to this finite configuration gives appetite continuity of all its radii. If G_{1,M} holds, a positive compact cover margin preserves it for α_n sufficiently near one. Radius monotonicity gives the opposite inclusion, so the increasing union of G_{α_n,M} is G_{1,M}, proving p_{α,M}↓p_{1,M}. These events are measurable: the finite Gale–Shapley construction and its territory-volume integrals are measurable, and finite open-ball cover of a compact cube is the positivity of the minimum of a continuous distance-margin function. ∎

### K. A quantitative finite-range sufficient condition for the missing vacant assertion
Let d≥2, A=3^d−1. If for some a>0 and integer M≥1
p_{1,M}< A^{−2^d}/(2M+1)^d,                                       (K1)
then there exists α_0<1 such that canonical U_α has no unbounded connected component a.s. for every α_0<α<1.
Proof. By J choose a fixed α_0<1 with p=p_{α_0,M} below the right side of (K1). Translate Q and its buffer by az, z∈Z^d, to obtain local good certificates G_z. They certify the corresponding closed small cubes as claimed globally. Write b=2M+1. A macro cube of side ab centred at abw, w∈Z^d, is the union of the b^d small cubes with indices bw+j, j∈{−M,…,M}^d. Declare the macro cube good if every one of these small cubes has its local certificate. Its failure probability is at most q=b^d p by the union bound.
All input buffers for one macro event are contained in the cube of radius 2aM in each coordinate about abw. Macro indices having the same coordinatewise residues modulo 2 have disjoint such input cubes: distinct indices of one colour differ in some coordinate by at least 2, giving physical separation at least 2ab=(4M+2)a>4aM. Poisson restrictions to disjoint Borel regions are mutually independent. Consequently the bad events within each of the 2^d colours are mutually independent. No independence of the global allocation is asserted.
A fixed self-avoiding star-neighbour path with N vertices has at least ceil(N/2^d) vertices of one colour. If every vertex of the path is bad, those same-colour independent events all occur, an event of probability at most q^{ceil(N/2^d)}≤q^{N/2^d}. The number of such paths from any fixed starting vertex is at most A^{N−1}. Thus the probability of a bad path with N vertices starting there is at most
A^{N−1}q^{N/2^d}=A^{-1}(Aq^{1/2^d})^N,
which tends to zero by (K1). The q=0 case is immediate by a countable union over vertices. An infinite connected component of a locally finite graph supplies self-avoiding paths of every finite length, so no starting vertex lies in an infinite star-connected bad component a.s. A countable union over starting vertices rules out all such components.
An unbounded component of U_{α_0} would give one: an open connected subset of R^d is path-connected (the points reachable by polygonal paths form a relatively open-and-closed class), and a continuous compact path visits finitely many closed macro cubes whose indices connect by star steps. Every cube met by U_{α_0} is bad because a good cube is entirely contained in C_{α_0}. An unbounded component meets infinitely many cubes, hence yields an infinite star-connected bad component. This contradiction rules out vacant percolation at α_0. Proposition B gives absence for every fixed larger α<1, which has the required quantifier "for every α, a.s."; no uncountable intersection is needed. ∎
The factor b^d is essential. A first, uncoarsened colour count would require exponentially small p in M^d; grouping into macro cubes improves the criterion to a fixed constant times M^{−d}. In d=2, (K1) is p_{1,M}<1/[4096(2M+1)^2]. Neither mere p_{1,M}→0 nor the existence of high-probability certificates with an unspecified buffer establishes this inequality for even one M.

### L. Critical certificates have a rigorous heavy-tail obstruction
Let M_*(a) be the smallest integer M≥1 for which G_{1,M} holds. It is finite a.s. by J. With κ=d/2 for d=1,2 and κ=d for d≥3,
E M_*(a)^κ=∞.
Proof. Let X be the global critical assignment distance at a deterministic origin. If G_{1,M} holds, a.e. x∈Q is claimed in the finite allocation: the covering radius balls contain no finite unclaimed sites and the ∆ set is null. All finite centres lie in [−aM,aM]^d, so their distance from any x∈Q is at most c(M+1/2), where c=√d a. Site monotonicity on adding the omitted centres implies the global assignment distance at those x is at most the same bound, for a.e. x. Therefore
|Q|^{-1}∫_Q 1{global distance at x>c(M+1/2)}dx ≤ 1{G_{1,M}^c}.
Take expectations and use stationarity and Fubini on the left. Monotonicity of the certificates on the right gives
P(X>c(M+1/2))≤p_{1,M}=P(M_*(a)>M).                              (L1)
For t≥3c choose M=floor(t/c−1/2)≥t/(2c). Since c(M+1/2)≤t, (L1) gives P(X>t)≤P(M_*(a)>t/(2c)). Integrating this tail inequality yields
E X^κ ≤ (3c)^κ+(2c)^κ E M_*(a)^κ,
with the inequality understood in the extended nonnegative reals. HHP Theorem 7, PDF page 6, gives E X^κ=∞, so the certificate moment must be infinite. ∎
In d=2 this implies ∑_{M≥1}p_{1,M}=∞ by the tail-sum formula for an integer random variable. Thus for any fixed a the bound p_{1,M}≤constant/M^2 cannot hold for all sufficiently large M: otherwise the sum would be finite. There are infinitely many buffers that fail K's test. This does not rule out an isolated M and a that satisfy (K1). In higher dimensions the divergent d-th moment is compatible with M^{−d} tails and does not by itself exclude K's criterion. The obstruction is proved from the source's critical transport moment, not from the provisional brain card.

## Hostile referee checks and residual gap after the new attempt
- Essential radii versus individual null points: G uses canonical openness to identify ordinary and essential suprema. The finite-input argument in J uses only a.e. claimed support, so it does not presume the same property for arbitrary finite versions.
- Limit allocation: radii are bounded separately for EACH centre by the target radius; dominated convergence is applied separately. No nonexistent deterministic uniform radius bound is used. Limit stability handles both eventually claimed and never-claimed sites, and all centres are sated so there is no unsated case left over.
- Finite truncation: the truncated process is not declared stationary; saturation, radius bounds and finite uniqueness are justified independently. Uniqueness of the global Poisson allocation applies to the limit even though the truncation boxes break equivariance.
- Compact coverage: the proof uses simultaneous critical coveting for every site and a finite cover with strictly positive margin. It does not use an invalid limit of merely full-volume or closed-ball covers.
- Dependence: K applies independence only to local finite-input events in disjoint buffers, and its union bound retains the full macro-block factor (2M+1)^d.
- Tail obstruction: L averages assignment distances over Q before using stationarity; it does not assume the finite allocation's value at a deterministic point avoids ∆ with probability one. The divergence only forbids an eventual M^{-2} bound in d=2, not every isolated successful scale.
- Quantifiers: a high-probability certificate can be obtained for any requested error, but its buffer size depends on that error. No interchange turns this into (K1). Lean certification scope is limited to the two finite lemmas and will be reported from the compiler log.

The new unconditional progress is compact coverage near one, radius continuity, finite-buffer convergence, and the critical certificate-moment obstruction. The principal sufficient-condition gap is now concrete: find a>0 and an integer M with p_{1,M}< (3^d−1)^{−2^d}/(2M+1)^d, or supply a more effective dependence estimate that avoids this finite-range bound. No such inequality has been proved, and the experiment supplies no continuum certification. This route has not settled near-one vacant nonpercolation, and it does not settle low-dimensional closed-claimed percolation. VERDICT remains PARTIAL.
