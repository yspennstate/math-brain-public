When is a random walk on a subgraph of G G also fast mixing? More precisely, what kinds of subgraph restrictions preserve good expansion? This paper studies the case of the hypercube graph H n H_{n} , where vertices x , y ∈ { 0 , 1 } n x,y\in\{0,1\}^{n} are connected by an edge if and only if they differ in exactly one coordinate. Recall that the lazy random walk on H n H_{n} has mixing time Θ ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) \Theta(n\log n) . Given a subset A ⊆ { 0 , 1 } n A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n} of vertices, we consider the random walk on { 0 , 1 } n \{0,1\}^{n} censored to A A . Definition 1.1 (Censored random walk, [ 17 ] ) . Given A ⊆ { 0 , 1 } n A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n} , the random walk on { 0 , 1 } n \{0,1\}^{n} censored to A A is defined as follows. On state x ∈ A x\in A , sample i ∈ [ n ] i\in[n] uniformly at random and let y y be the vertex obtained by flipping the i i -th bit of x x . Then 1. If y ∈ A y\in A , flip a coin and either stay at x x or move to y y (each with probability 1 / 2 1/2 ). 2. If y ∉ A y\not\in A , stay at x x (in which case we call this a censored step). Without further guarantees on A A , the censored random walk may mix well or extremely poorly even when A A is large and connected, as the following two examples illustrate: Example 1 (Subcube) . Let S ⊂ [ n ] S\subset[n] be a set of indices, and let A A be the subcube given by the vertices x ∈ { 0 , 1 } n x\in\{0,1\}^{n} satisfying x i = 0 x_{i}=0 for all i ∈ S i\in S . Then the censored random walk is essentially a random walk on the smaller cube { 0 , 1 } n ′ \{0,1\}^{n^{\prime}} , where n ′ := n − | S | n^{\prime}\vcentcolon=n-|S| , except that only an O ⁡ ( n ′ / n ) O(n^{\prime}/n) -fraction of the transitions are not censored. Thus the censored random walk has mixing time O ⁡ ( n n ′ ⋅ n ′ ​ log ⁡ n ′ ) = O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(\tfrac{n}{n^{\prime}}\cdot n^{\prime}\log n^{\prime})=O(n\log n) . Example 2 (Middle slice bridge) . Let x ∗ ∈ { 0 , 1 } n x^{*}\in\{0,1\}^{n} be an arbitrary vertex with Hamming weight | x ∗ | = ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ |x^{*}|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor , and consider the set A := { x ∈ { 0 , 1 } n : | x | ≠ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ } ∪ { x ∗ } A\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:|x|\neq\lfloor n/2\rfloor\right\}\cup\{x^{*}\} . A spectral argument shows that the mixing time of the censored random walk is exponential in n n . Thus, it is natural to ask: what properties of A A ensure fast mixing? The Poincaré inequality (see e.g. [ 40 ] ) for the hypercube states that the spectral expansion of the (lazy) hypercube H n H_{n} is exactly 1 / n 1/n , which implies the (non-tight) mixing time bound O ⁡ ( n 2 ) O(n^{2}) for the lazy random walk on H n H_{n} . Thus, a natural question related to mixing under censoring is the robustness of the classical Poincaré inequality under vertex removal from H n H_{n} . Specifically, does the spectral expansion of H n H_{n} remain on the order of 1 / n 1/n if only a small fraction of the vertices are removed, or does it exhibit a significant deviation? Example 2 demonstrates that the spectral expansion can shrink to exponentially small values if the removed set is arbitrary. Our goal is to show that when the removed set of vertices is monotone (and not too large), the spectral expansion remains at least on the order of 1 / n 1/n . For the purpose of clearer comparison with the classical Poincaré inequality, we introduce the Dirichlet form of a function on A A . The proof in [ 17 ] also analyzes the spectral expansion of H A H_{A} , achieving the lower bound γ ⁡ ( H A ) ≳ μ ​ ( A ) 2 / n 2 \gamma(H_{A})\gtrsim\mu(A)^{2}/n^{2} . By Cheeger’s inequality, to obtain a lower bound on γ ⁡ ( H A ) \gamma(H_{A}) , it suffices to lower bound the bottleneck ratio ϕ ⁡ ( H A ) := min S ⊆ A ⁡ | E ⁡ ( S , A ∖ S ) | min ⁡ { | S | , | A ∖ S | } , \phi(H_{A})\vcentcolon=\min_{S\subseteq A}\frac{|E(S,A\setminus S)|}{\min\{|S|,|A\setminus S|\}}, where E ⁡ ( S , A ∖ S ) E(S,A\setminus S) denotes the set of edges { x , y } \{x,y\} of H n H_{n} with x ∈ S x\in S and y ∈ A ∖ S y\in A\setminus S . By an isoperimetric inequality of the hypercube, there is good lower bound on the number of boundary edges connecting a vertex in S S to a vertex in { 0 , 1 } n ∖ S \{0,1\}^{n}\setminus S . However, it is not immediately clear how many of these edges actually lead to A ∖ S A\setminus S . The crucial observation of [ 17 ] is that if an edge from a vertex x ∈ S x\in S goes “upward”—that is, if its other endpoint y ∈ { 0 , 1 } n ∖ S y\in\{0,1\}^{n}\setminus S satisfies y ⪰ x y\succeq x —then by the monotonicity of A A , we must have y ∈ A ∖ S y\in A\setminus S . Coincidentally, there is a “directed isopetrimetric inequality” [ 26 , Theorem 2] , developed by the property testing community, which provides a lower bound on exactly the number of such upward boundary edges. Specifically, it gives a lower bound on the number of edges connecting a vertex x ∈ S x\in S to a vertex y ∈ { 0 , 1 } n ∖ S y\in\{0,1\}^{n}\setminus S with y ⪰ x y\succeq x in terms of the distance of S S to monotonicity. The precise notion of distance is less important than the key fact that, when | A | |A| is not too small, at least one of S S and A ∖ S A\setminus S must be far from any monotone set—i.e., it must have a large distance to monotonicity—due to the FKG inequality [ 23 ] . As a result, we can lower bound the number of upward boundary edges from either S S or A ∖ S A\setminus S . Since both sets of upward edges are subsets E ⁡ ( S , A ∖ S ) E(S,A\setminus S) , we can thus arrive at a lower bound on | E ⁡ ( S , A ∖ S ) | |E(S,A\setminus S)| and hence ϕ ⁡ ( H A ) \phi(H_{A}) . The work [ 17 ] actually achieves the optimal bound ϕ ⁡ ( H A ) ≳ μ ⁡ ( A ) / n \phi(H_{A})\gtrsim\mu(A)/n on the bottleneck ratio. However, this only translates to a quadratically worse bound γ ⁡ ( H A ) ≳ μ ​ ( A ) 2 / n 2 \gamma(H_{A})\gtrsim\mu(A)^{2}/n^{2} for the spectral expansion, due to the loss incurred by applying Cheeger’s inequality. One natural idea is to avoid using Cheeger’s inequality by directly bounding the spectral gap γ ⁡ ( H A ) \gamma(H_{A}) . In this direction, [ 15 ] used a canonical path argument to show the bound γ ⁡ ( H A ) ≳ μ ⁡ ( A ) / n 2 \gamma(H_{A})\gtrsim\mu(A)/n^{2} , which improves upon Theorem 1.2 by a factor of μ ⁡ ( A ) \mu(A) . However, this improvement is only effective when μ ⁡ ( A ) ≪ 1 \mu(A)\ll 1 and the dependence on n n remains suboptimal. 1.3 Proof overview: our work At first glance, the proof in [ 17 ] , as described in the previous subsection, appears to heavily depend on the discrete nature of the bottleneck ratio. In our view, a key conceptual contribution of this work is that the arguments in [ 17 ] can be adapted to the L 2 L^{2} setting. While the discrete setting leads to the bottleneck ratio, in the L 2 L^{2} setting, the corresponding arguments directly lead to the spectral expansion as stated in Theorem 1.6 . Approximate FKG inequality. The classical FKG inequality of [ 23 ] states that if f , g : { 0 , 1 } n → ℝ f,g:\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} are monotone increasing functions and x x is a uniformly random element of { 0 , 1 } n \{0,1\}^{n} , then the random variables f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) and g ⁡ ( x ) g(x) are nonnegatively correlated. It is well-known that this statement holds for increasing functions over a broader class of partially ordered sets (posets). In our proof, we crucially need a lower bound on the correlation ratio of any two increasing functions A → ℝ A\rightarrow\mathbb{R} , where the set A A is partially ordered by the natural partial order of the hypercube. However, it is easy to see that the FKG inequality does not generally hold on this poset. Thus, we seek an “approximate” version of the FKG inequality, where we are content with a correlation ratio bounded away from − 1 -1 , rather than necessarily nonnegative. Definition 1.11 (Approximate FKG ratio) . Fix a monotone set A ⊆ { 0 , 1 } n A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n} with at least 2 elements. We define the approximate FKG ratio of the poset A A to be δ ⁡ ( A ) := min ⁡ { 0 , inf f , g ∈ 𝗆𝗈𝗇𝗈 A ∖ 𝖼𝗈𝗇𝗌𝗍 A Cov A ​ [ f , g ] Var A ​ [ f ] ⋅ Var A ​ [ g ] } , \delta(A):=\min\left\{0,\inf_{f,g\in\mathsf{mono}_{A}\setminus\mathsf{const}_{A}}\frac{\mathrm{Cov}_{A}\left[f,g\right]}{\sqrt{{\mathrm{Var}}_{A}\left[f\right]\cdot{\mathrm{Var}}_{A}\left[g\right]}}\right\}, where f f and g g range over all non-constant monotone increasing functions from A A to ℝ \mathbb{R} . Here, Cov A ​ [ f , g ] \mathrm{Cov}_{A}\left[f,g\right] stands for the covariance of the random variable pair ( f ⁡ ( x ) , g ⁡ ( x ) ) (f(x),g(x)) where x x is a uniformly random element of A A . Theorem 1.12 (Approximate FKG inequality) . For any monotone set A ⊆ { 0 , 1 } n A\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n} with at least 2 elements, we have δ ⁡ ( A ) ≥ − 1 − μ ⁡ ( A ) \delta(A)\geq-\sqrt{1-\mu(A)} . 1.4 The case of small A A : fast mixing requires good FKG ratio The bound in Theorem 1.6 gives only a spectral gap bound γ ⁡ ( H A ) ≳ μ ⁡ ( A ) / n \gamma(H_{A})\gtrsim\mu(A)/n for the random walk censored to A A . The dependence on n n is clearly optimal: even in the case A = H n A=H_{n} , the spectral gap is exactly 1 / n 1/n . The next example shows that the asymptotic dependence on μ ⁡ ( A ) \mu(A) is also close to optimal. Example 3 ( [ 17 , Example 1.3] ) . Assume n / 4 ≤ m ≤ n / 2 n/4\leq m\leq n/2 and consider A = { x ∈ { 0 , 1 } n : x 1 = ⋯ = x m = 1 } ∪ { x ∈ { 0 , 1 } n : x m + 1 = ⋯ = x 2 ​ m = 1 } A=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:x_{1}=\dots=x_{m}=1\}\cup\{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}:x_{m+1}=\dots=x_{2m}=1\} , the union of two subcubes. In this case, μ ⁡ ( A ) ∼ 2 − m + 1 \mu(A)\sim 2^{-m+1} . Let f : A → ℝ f:A\rightarrow\mathbb{R} be defined by f ⁡ ( x ) = 1 f(x)=1 if x 1 = ⋯ = x m = 1 x_{1}=\dots=x_{m}=1 and f ⁡ ( x ) = − 1 f(x)=-1 otherwise. Then ℰ A ​ ( f ) ∼ m ⋅ 2 − m \mathcal{E}_{A}(f)\sim m\cdot 2^{-m} and Var A ​ [ f ] ∼ 1 {\mathrm{Var}}_{A}\left[f\right]\sim 1 , so γ ⁡ ( H A ) ≲ μ ⁡ ( A ) \gamma(H_{A})\lesssim\mu(A) . Standard Markov chain theory (e.g. [ 36 , Theorem 7.4] ) shows that the mixing time of the random walk censored to A A is exponentially large in n n . Remarkably, Example 3 is also where the approximate FKG inequality fails badly—if we let A A be the union of two subcubes as in Example 3 and consider the indicator functions of the two subcubes, it is easy to see that they are both increasing functions on the poset A A but are very anti-correlated (i. e. the approximate FKG ratio δ ⁡ ( A ) \delta(A) is very close to − 1 -1 ). Prior work on spectral theory for directed graphs. There has been extensive prior work developing spectral graph theory beyond the classical setting of undirected graphs, toward capturing directed graphs and hypergraphs. Early work of [ 22 , 14 ] associated a certain Hermitian matrix with each directed graph, and showed a Cheeger-type inequality based on the eigenvalues of that matrix, and many subsequent works have built upon that foundation; we refer to the recent thesis [ 46 ] for a thorough review, and here we mention two recent lines of work that are closest to our setting. One line of works [ 34 , 35 , 46 ] has developed a theory of reweighted eigenvalues capturing expansion properties of directed graphs and hypergraphs, proved Cheeger inequalities for these settings, and devised efficient algorithms for graph partitioning. Another line of works [ 48 , 49 , 24 , 29 ] has pursued similar goals by analyzing a nonlinear Laplacian operator and the heat equation associated with it. While our interest in a spectral theory for directed graphs is related to these previous works (and indeed we will build upon the approach of [ 48 ] ), our focus is slightly different. In a nutshell, while prior works have focused on spectral characterizations of good expansion of a directed graph G G as captured by directed versions of Cheeger inequalities (for edge conductance or vertex expansion) and mixing time of random walks, our focus will be on the quality of G G as the “substrate” for a dynamical process; we will consider G G a good directed spectral expander if it affords fast convergence for that process. In particular, our perspective allows for directed acyclic graphs to be considered good expanders, which is a stark departure from prior perspectives – as we briefly explain next. Indeed, a central focus of prior works has been to establish Cheeger inequalities of the type λ → 2 ≲ ϕ → ​ ( G ) ≲ λ → 2 , \vec{\lambda}_{2}\lesssim\vec{\phi}(G)\lesssim\sqrt{\vec{\lambda}_{2}}\,, (14) where λ → 2 \vec{\lambda}_{2} denotes a relevant second eigenvalue related to the directed weighted graph G G , and the edge conductance ϕ → ​ ( G ) \vec{\phi}(G) of G G is ϕ → ​ ( G ) := min ∅ ≠ S ⊊ V ⁡ min ⁡ { w ⁡ ( δ + ​ ( S ) ) , w ⁡ ( δ + ​ ( V ∖ S ) ) } min ⁡ { vol w ⁡ ( S ) , vol w ⁡ ( V ∖ S ) } , \vec{\phi}(G)\vcentcolon=\min_{\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq V}\frac{\min\left\{w(\delta^{+}(S)),w(\delta^{+}(V\setminus S))\right\}}{\min\left\{\vol_{w}(S),\vol_{w}(V\setminus S)\right\}}\,, where δ + ​ ( S ) \delta^{+}(S) denotes the outgoing edge boundary of S S and vol w ⁡ ( S ) \vol_{w}(S) denotes the total weighted degree of all vertices in S S . Now, if G G is not strongly connected, then in general there exists a set S S with positive volume but no outgoing edges, which makes ϕ → ​ ( G ) \vec{\phi}(G) and thus λ → 2 \vec{\lambda}_{2} zero. Our approach. The type of spectral theory for directed graphs we study in this section was first developed by [ 48 ] in the context of network analysis. In that work, [ 48 ] defined a nonlinear Laplacian operator acting on real-valued functions defined on the vertices of a directed graph, showed that this operator induces a dynamical process that is a directed analogue of the classical heat flow on graphs, proved that this operator has nontrivial eigenvalues, and established a Cheeger inequality like ( 14 ) for this setting. While [ 48 ] focused on the implications of directed spectral theory for graph partitioning and related problems in network analysis, we focus on the dynamical properties of the heat flow on directed graphs – namely its convergence to a monotone limit, and its connection to the directed Poincaré inequality. As mentioned in the introduction, recent work of [ 21 ] also proved a directed Poincaré inequality – for functions defined on the continuous cube [ 0 , 1 ] n [0,1]^{n} – using a dynamical argument. Indeed, that work also took as its starting point the connections between the heat flow and the Poincaré inequality, and showed that the natural directed version of the heat flow in continuous space enjoys exponential energy decay, which implies a directed Poincaré inequality for that setting. Our proof is conceptually similar to the proof of [ 21 ] , but our techniques differ in at least two ways: 1) [ 21 ] required analytical arguments from the theory of partial differential equations (PDEs), while we are able to study our dynamical process as an ordinary differential equation (ODE) thanks to the finite-dimensional nature of our problem; and 2) [ 21 ] used tools from optimal transport theory to tensorize their one-dimensional result, while we obtain a multidimensional inequality directly by studying the directed heat flow as a gradient system. In this last regard, our proof also bears resemblance to, and is inspired by prior work of [ 33 ] on the so-called Paulsen problem from operator theory, where a “movement decay” property of a suitable dynamical system was used to bound the distance between the initial and equilibrium states of that system.
