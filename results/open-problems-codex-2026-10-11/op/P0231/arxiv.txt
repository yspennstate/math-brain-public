    <title>Fractal properties of the frontier in Poissonian coloring</title>
    <updated>2023-11-27T14:45:53Z</updated>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07254v2" rel="alternate" type="text/html"/>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.07254v2" rel="related" type="application/pdf" title="pdf"/>
    <summary>We study a model of random partitioning by nearest-neighbor coloring from Poisson rain, introduced independently by Aldous and Preater. Given two initial points in $[0,1]^d$ respectively colored in red and blue, we let independent uniformly random points fall in $[0,1]^d$, and upon arrival, each point takes the color of the nearest point fallen so far. We prove that the colored regions converge in the Hausdorff sense towards two random closed subsets whose intersection, the frontier, has Hausdorff dimension strictly between $d-1$ and $d$, thus answering a conjecture raised by Aldous. However, several topological properties of the frontier remain elusive.</summary>

    <title>Random partitions of the plane via Poissonian coloring, and a self-similar process of coalescing planar partitions</title>
    <updated>2016-12-31T16:21:31Z</updated>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.00131v1" rel="alternate" type="text/html"/>
    <link href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.00131v1" rel="related" type="application/pdf" title="pdf"/>
    <summary>Plant differently colored points in the plane, then let random points ("Poisson rain") fall, and give each new point the color of the nearest existing point. Previous investigation and simulations strongly suggest that the colored regions converge (in some sense) to a random partition of the plane. We prove a weak version of this, showing that normalized empirical measures converge to Lebesgue measures on a random partition into measurable sets. Topological properties remain an open problem. In the course of the proof, which heavily exploits time-reversals, we encounter a novel self-similar process of coalescing planar partitions. In this process, sets $A(z)$ in the partition are associated with Poisson random points $z$, and the dynamics are as follows. Points are deleted randomly at rate $1$, when $z$ is deleted, its set $A(z)$ is adjoined to the set $A(z^\prime)$ of the nearest other point $z^\prime$.</summary>
