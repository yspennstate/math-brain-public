## Step-by-step verification

1. **First environment and initial law: lines 13–25.** The uniform law on the two alternating environments is invariant and ergodic under the full translation group: translation by one interchanges its atoms. For each initial component, the pairs (eta(2n),eta(2n+1)) are iid, so translation by two is ergodic on that component. A translation-by-one invariant event has probability zero or one in each component, and shifting by one equates these probabilities. Its probability under their mixture is therefore zero or one. This is a valid spatially ergodic law even though translation by two is not ergodic on the mixture. Cylinder approximation gives the stated bound of 4 epsilon: the intersection approximation costs at most 2 epsilon and replacing the squared probability costs at most 2 epsilon. The pair strong law gives density (1/2+2)/2=5/4.

2. **Stationarity: lines 29–33.** Each parity is a homogeneous totally asymmetric zero-range process after relabeling, with its own constant site rate. On a finite ring, a jump preserves the geometric product weight. The number of incoming moves to a state equals the number of occupied arrival sites, and the number of outgoing moves equals the number of occupied departure sites; these counts agree. Hence weighted global balance holds. The total rate is bounded by lambda times the ring size, so stationarity follows for the countable-state ring chain. I independently enumerated and accumulated every directed transition for ring sizes 2 through 6 and total particle counts 0 through 8, using exact rational weights and rates: all 4,995 states passed. This computation supports, but does not replace, the all-state balance identity.

   The infinite-volume passage is also valid. Source Lemma 5.1, pp.10–11, supplies finite graphical dependency components on sufficiently short time intervals. Coupling a large ring to the infinite line transfers finite-set distributions once its boundary lies outside the relevant component. The component is determined by bounded-rate clocks independently of occupations. Its radius is finite almost surely, so the coupling error vanishes as ring size increases. The Markov property extends short-time invariance to all times. Independence of the parity dynamics then gives invariance of both initial components and their mixture.

3. **Failure of every product limit: lines 37–43.** I recalculated the zero-indicator covariance as ((1-u)^2+(1-v)^2)/2 - (((1-u)+(1-v))/2)^2 = (u-v)^2/4 = 1/36. Every specified equilibrium is a product measure, whereas the stationary initial law has this strictly positive covariance. The zero indicators and their product are bounded continuous cylinder functions in the usual product topology. The constant law therefore cannot converge weakly to any specified equilibrium. The statement that this environment has infinite critical density follows from its positive atom at c and source equation (2.9).

4. **Phase transition and supercritical initial law: lines 47–63.** For constant nonzero occupation rate, the one-site mean is phi/(a-phi). I independently obtained integral f(a) da = 16(1/4)^2 = 1 and critical density integral [c/(a-c)]f(a) da = 4. The initial uniform two-phase law is translation invariant and ergodic because translation acts transitively on its two atoms; both configurations have spatial density 5. The environment is iid, genuinely disordered, bounded below by c=1/2, and has no site attaining c almost surely. The critical geometric product is well defined. Taking subcritical fugacities up to c gives weak convergence to it; graphical locality makes the cylinder semigroup continuous with a uniform vanishing localization error, which justifies passing stationarity to the limit. This checks line 56 rather than assuming endpoint invariance from the source's open parameter interval.

5. **Persistent empty parity: lines 65–73.** No arrow leaves one parity for the other. An initially empty parity remains empty, so P(eta(0,t)=0) is at least 1/2 for every t. The critical equilibrium has zero probability 1-c/alpha_0 strictly below 1/3 because alpha_0<3/4. This strict inequality is in the correct direction. The bounded continuous one-site observable contradicts critical weak convergence. No conservation-of-expectation argument, moment uniform integrability, or assumed actual limit is involved. This already settles the second question negatively without later literature.

6. **Actual iid limit: lines 87–106.** I independently downloaded arXiv:1804.06148 and read its model, environment assumptions, Theorem 2.5 and its proof section. The model on p.7 is one-dimensional nearest-neighbor with p(1)>1/2, including p(1)=1. Assumptions 2.1–2.2 are on pp.9–10; Example 2.1 and the following remark cover almost every iid environment. Theorem 2.5 on p.18 states unscaled convergence for a deterministic configuration with left density rho>0 to the equilibrium indexed by min(rho,rho_c). For each relabeled parity, the present iid environment satisfies the assumptions almost surely, has infimum c attained nowhere, and makes the fixed observation sequence typical under Definition 2.1 on p.15. The active configuration has left density 10>4. Intersecting the two full-probability environment sets permits application on both parities. The continuous embedding and finite mixture preserve weak convergence. I independently expanded the limiting covariance and checked exact rational sample rates: it is c^2/(4 alpha_0 alpha_2)>0. The joint ergodicity argument is valid for this iid example. The final quenched density statement uses an integrable iid annealed sequence followed by Fubini, as required; it does not assert an unjustified nonidentical-variable strong law.

7. **Positive specialization: lines 110–116.** Birkhoff gives the requisite left density almost surely for an integrable spatially ergodic initial law. The same published equilibrium limit applies to each such deterministic start, and dominated convergence integrates bounded continuous observables over the initial law. Density zero implies the empty configuration almost surely. Rate normalization and spatial reflection have the stated effects. This is a valid literature-based nearest-neighbor specialization for typical environments satisfying the cited assumptions, not a proof for arbitrary finite-range kernels or exceptional fixed environments.
