## Step-by-step independent verification

**First environment and dynamics, ATTEMPT.md:13-15.** The two alternating rate sequences, each of probability 1/2, form a transitive orbit under one-site translation. Their law is invariant and ergodic, although not mixing. Its essential infimum is 1/2. With p(2)=1, jumps have finite range and positive mean 2, and preserve parity. Each relabelled parity chain has a constant rate, either 1/2 or 1. The occupation rate is bounded and monotone. All these are allowed standing hypotheses.

**Initial ergodicity and density, lines 19-25.** Conditional on a phase, the site pairs are iid. The two-site shift is therefore ergodic on each phase. A one-site-invariant event is also two-site-invariant, so its probability in each phase is zero or one; the one-site shift exchanges the phases and makes the two probabilities equal. This proves ergodicity of the mixture under the full spatial translation group. It is not an inadmissible mixture of two one-site-invariant ergodic laws. The pair strong law gives the same density in both phases: geometric means are 1/2 and 2, giving density 5/4. Both half-line and symmetric spatial averages have this value.

**Stationarity, lines 29-33.** On an m-site cycle, the geometric product weight is (1-q)^m q to the total occupation. Every move preserves this weight. Outgoing weighted rate equals lambda times the weight times the number of occupied sites. A predecessor for the edge j to j+1 exists precisely when the target state's site j+1 is occupied; it has the same weight and rate lambda. Summing incoming rates gives the identical result. Since the total rate is at most lambda m, there is no explosion and global balance proves invariance of the probability measure.

I independently enumerated all cycle states for m=2,...,6 and total occupation 0,...,8: 4,995 states. All predecessor checks and balance identities passed. This is supporting verification, not a proof of infinite-volume stationarity. The latter follows from the source's Lemma 5.1, which I checked on page 10: on a fixed sufficiently short interval, the dependency component of any finite set is almost surely finite and depends only on the clocks, not occupations. A cycle coupled in a growing common region agrees on this set unless the component reaches the boundary. That probability tends to zero. Consequently stationary cylinder marginals pass to the infinite chain. Iterating the short interval by the Markov property proves invariance for all times. Conditional parity chains are independent, so each pi_i and their mixture are invariant for either fixed alternating environment.

**Obstruction to every specified equilibrium, lines 37-43.** Independently expanding the two phase expectations gives Cov(1{eta(0)=0},1{eta(2)=0})=(u-v)^2/4=1/36. All source nu_phi^alpha are products, so this stationary law is none of them. The zero indicators and their product are bounded continuous cylinder functions on N^Z. Their expectations obstruct weak convergence to any product law. Positive probability of alpha_0=c makes rho*=infinity by source equation (2.9); this example correctly claims only failure of the first clause.

**Second environment and critical equilibrium, lines 47-56.** The density 32(a-1/2) on (1/2,3/4) integrates to 1. Its iid law is spatially ergodic, with essential infimum c=1/2 and no site attaining c almost surely. For r(k)=1 at positive k, M(q)=q/(1-q), and source equations (2.9) and (2.14) give rho*=E[c/(alpha_0-c)]=(1/2)*32*(1/4)=4. The endpoint product has geometric parameters c/alpha_x<1, hence finite occupation at every site. Subcritical products converge weakly to it: convergence of all finite marginals also gives tightness on this countable product, by choosing coordinate cutoffs with summable tail bounds. Bounded rates give uniform finite-dependency estimates for cylinder observables, so their semigroup expectations are bounded continuous and invariance passes to the endpoint. This justifies the endpoint use, rather than assuming the source's strictly subcritical Proposition 1 already states it.

**Initial law and contradiction, lines 58-73.** The two deterministic configurations with alternating 0 and 10 again form a transitive translation orbit. Their uniform law is spatially ergodic, independent of alpha, and each configuration has density 5>4. Conditional on the phase that makes the even parity empty, all even occupations remain zero. No permitted arrow crosses parity and no particle is created at an empty site. Thus P(eta(0,t)=0)>=1/2 at every time. The critical product instead has zero probability 1-c/alpha_0<1/3, since alpha_0<3/4. A bounded continuous one-site test function proves the contradiction. The event that every site is in the displayed open interval has Q-probability one. The supercritical refutation is therefore quenched on a full-probability set, not just an exceptional environment. This argument is self-contained and requires no later convergence theorem.

**Actual limit and joint ergodicity, lines 85-106.** I independently downloaded arXiv:1804.06148 (65 pages). Its section 2.1, page 7, restricts p to {-1,1} with p(1)>1/2, including total asymmetry. Assumptions 2.1-2.2 on pages 9-10 concern environment averaging and sufficiently dense slow sites; Example 2.1 and the following remark explicitly cover almost every ergodic environment. Theorem 2.5 on page 18 states convergence from deterministic starts with a positive asymptotic left density. The fixed observation site is typical under Definition 2.1 on page 15 in the chosen iid environment: all rates exceed c and the left liminf is c.

Each parity restriction is iid with the chosen marginal. Intersecting their full-probability environment sets is legitimate. Relabelling gives exactly the totally asymmetric nearest-neighbor process, with left initial density 10>4 on the occupied parity. The empty parity remains zero. The embeddings into the full configuration space are continuous; their pushforwards and the half-half mixture therefore converge as claimed. Expanding the limiting cylinder moments independently gives covariance c^2/(4 alpha_0 alpha_2)>0, excluding every product nu_phi^alpha.

There is also an independent earlier verification of this particular limit: I downloaded arXiv:math/9911205v2 (19 pages). Its Theorems 2.3-2.4, page 7, prove supercritical convergence for the totally asymmetric constant-occupation-rate nearest-neighbor model under precisely the relevant environment and left-density conditions. They apply on both relabelled parities. This verifies the specific strengthening without relying solely on the later general-rate theorem.

Conditional on either initial phase, the two-site shift on the iid environment is an iid-pair Bernoulli shift. It is ergodic. One-site translation exchanges phases, so the same invariant-event argument proves joint initial/environment ergodicity. Under the annealed critical product, (alpha_x,eta_x) are iid with finite mean occupation 4. The strong law followed by Fubini proves the stated quenched density 4 for almost every environment. These steps are correct.

**Positive specialization, lines 110-116.** For a finite-density spatially ergodic initial law, Birkhoff gives its asymptotic left density almost surely. Applying the verified deterministic-start theorem on its stated admissible environments and integrating bounded continuous test-function expectations by dominated convergence gives weak convergence of the random-start law. Density zero forces the empty configuration. Dividing r by r(infinity) rescales time by r(infinity) and fugacity by its reciprocal; reflecting the lattice handles leftward drift. This establishes the stated nearest-neighbor specialization under the expressly retained environment hypotheses. It is not an arbitrary-kernel theorem.
