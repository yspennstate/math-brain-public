## Exact claim and scope

The main claim (lines 13–21) is this sufficient-condition theorem. Let P be a transient irreducible chain on a countable state space S, with its compact metrizable Martin compactification K. Use spatially homogeneous independent offspring with finite mean m>1 and E[N log N]<infinity. Write W for the almost-sure weak limit of m^{-n}B_n supplied by Candellero–Hutchcroft. If there is a countable basis (V_i) of K\S such that, for every i and every boundary point xi in V_i,

    liminf_{x in S, x -> xi} P_x(X_infinity in V_i) > 0,

then supp(W) equals the boundary accumulation set L almost surely, for each starting state. The lower bound may depend on i and xi. On extinction both sets are empty. Dirichlet regularity implies this hypothesis. The claimant also proves applications to simple random walk on every regular tree of degree at least three and to symmetric finitely supported generating walks on non-elementary word-hyperbolic groups with spectral radius less than one. All homogeneous supercritical L log L offspring distributions are allowed; no second moment or BRW transience restriction is imposed.

I downloaded https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15287 independently. PDF page 2 confirms the hypotheses and the expectation identity for every Borel set; PDF page 9 states Problem 4.2 exactly as quoted: “Give conditions under which the support of W coincides almost surely with the set of accumulation points of B.” That page explicitly expects equality in sufficiently nice examples such as hyperbolic groups and already gives a lamplighter failure of universal equality. Thus a nonvacuous sufficient criterion with these applications answers the question as actually stated. It is not a necessary-and-sufficient classification, and lines 46, 86 and 94 correctly disclaim that stronger result.

## Independent examination of every main proof step

1. **Support inclusion, line 24.** For K-open U, Portmanteau gives liminf m^{-n}B_n(U)>=W(U). Positive limiting lower bound forces particles in U at every sufficiently large generation. For xi in the support, apply this pathwise to shrinking metric balls and choose increasing generations. No uncountable union of probabilistic exceptional sets is needed because weak convergence already holds as convergence of measures. W(S)=0 and S open imply supp(W) lies in the closed boundary. This proves supp(W) subset L without (R).

2. **Uniform success probability, lines 26–28.** Testing weak convergence against the constant function 1 identifies the total mass W_x(K) with the Galton–Watson martingale limit Z. Its law is independent of x, and E[Z]=1 by Kesten–Stigum under precisely the retained moment hypothesis. For Y=W_x(V), 0<=Y<=Z. Splitting according to Z<=K gives E[Y]<=K P(Y>0)+E[Z;Z>K]. Integrability permits a positive finite K with the last term below c/2. Since E[Y]=h_V(x)>=c, P(Y>0)>=c/(2K). This works uniformly over all such x and requires no variance bound. Thresholds above 1 are vacuous; choosing K positive is harmless. I independently checked the underlying pointwise truncation inequality by exact rational arithmetic, in addition to deriving it analytically.

3. **Subtree decomposition and conditioning, lines 30–34.** Finite mean implies finite offspring almost surely and hence finite generations. At each fixed n, the generation n+k measure is the sum of the descendant generation-k measures; multiplying by m^{-(n+k)} and taking weak limits in this finite sum proves W=m^{-n}sum W^u as an equality of measures. A countable intersection over possible genealogical labels gives all required subtree limits. Consequently W(V)=0 iff every W^u(V)=0, for any Borel V, without assuming V is a continuity set. Conditional independence of the still-unrevealed descendant processes gives the displayed product formula, including the empty-generation case. Here F_n is the usual filtration through the birth and location of generation n; reproduction of generation-n particles belongs to the next step. The natural interpretation of “through generation n” in line 30 is consistent with that filtration.

4. **Repeated visits, line 36.** A={W(V)=0} is measurable in the terminal genealogical sigma-algebra because W is the limit of measurable finite-generation measures. Levy's upward theorem gives E[1_A|F_n]->1_A. On A this eventually exceeds 1-p_c, whereas any generation with a particle satisfying h_V>=c has conditional probability at most 1-p_c. Such generations must therefore be finite on A. This argument does not assume independence between successive visits, nor survival of the subtree of a chosen visiting particle.

5. **Reverse inclusion and quantifiers, line 38.** A point outside the support has an open zero-mass neighborhood U. Choose one countable-basis element V_i containing it and contained in U intersect boundary. Condition (R) supplies a state-space neighborhood where h_{V_i}>=1/j for some integer j. Accumulation entails visits there at arbitrarily large generations, contradicting step 4 on {W(V_i)=0}. All exceptional events were intersected only over the countable pairs (i,j), so choosing xi after observing the process is legitimate. The boundary is closed, so these relative-open sets are Borel in K. This completes equality.

I found no failing step or substantive missing hypothesis in this theorem.

## Applications and additional claims

**Regular tree, lines 55–69.** I re-derived the radial recurrence with downward probability 1/(q+1), verified its solution and endpoints, and checked the exit argument and monotone passage to eventual hitting. Return probability 1/q gives G(a,a)=q/(q-1); first-hit decomposition gives the stated Green function and Martin kernel. Finite visits to each vertex imply permanent exit from finite balls and convergence to an end. Finite branching, subsequence extraction and separation of distinct ends by the kernels justify the topology identification. Leaving a cylinder's forward subtree requires visiting its parent, giving the stated lower bound, which tends to one along that cylinder. All steps are valid, including in the BRW local-survival regime.

Independent exact checks in exact_checks.py/exact_checks.txt passed: 10,890 radial recurrence/interior checks and 968 endpoint checks for q=2,...,12 and L=2,...,45; 550 cylinder lower bounds; 13,284 pointwise truncation inequalities. For the cylinder check I independently used the exact probability h_C(x)=1-1/((q+1)q^d), where d is depth below the cylinder root, and compared it with 1-q^{-(d+1)}. These finite checks supplement the analytic derivations; they do not establish infinite-time convergence.

**Hyperbolic groups, lines 74–80.** I downloaded https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13615v2. Proposition 3.38 on PDF page 24 states the cited Dirichlet-regularity result; item (ii) on page 25 states the Martin/hyperbolic identification. The graph and chain assumptions described on page 22 are met: a finite symmetric generating set gives a connected locally finite Cayley graph; the walk is bounded range and uniformly irreducible; left translations preserve P and act transitively; the non-elementary boundary is infinite and has no global fixed point. Spectral radius below one ensures transience. Proposition 3.19 on page 18 also directly confirms concentration of exit measures at each regular boundary point. Independently, the continuous bump-function argument in line 80 follows from harmonic extension, bounded martingale convergence and boundary convergence, and yields h_V(x)->1. These cited inputs legitimately prove the claimed application. They are established results, not newly proved boundary-identification theorems.

**Moment counterexample, line 89.** The offspring probabilities normalize correctly. Integral comparison proves finite mean and infinite E[N log N]; N>=2 ensures survival and the mean is greater than two. Kesten–Stigum as stated on source page 2 gives zero normalized total mass. The bounded-test-function inequality then gives weak convergence to zero. The first-child ray has ordinary simple-random-walk law and converges to a tree end. Thus L is nonempty while supp(W) is empty. This is correctly described only as a counterexample to deleting the moment assumption, outside the main theorem.

**Computational claims, lines 71 and 92–94.** My checks above are independent of the claimant's computational logs. I did not rerun its Lean environment or certify its historical exit code. Formal verification of the algebra alone would not establish the probabilistic theorem; the claimant explicitly makes that limitation, and the verdict rests on the mathematical proof checked here.

I independently downloaded Woess (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.07301) and Geldbach (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.06730v3) to check the issue at ATTEMPT.md line 83. Woess's PDF page 26 really attributes dimension min{2lambda,1} to the topological support and infers strict inclusion. Geldbach's Theorem 1.1 on PDF page 4 is about dimension of the measure; Corollary 3.2 on page 11 obtains its upper bound from a full-measure countable union of typical-particle endpoint sets. It does not bound the dimension of their closure, and hence does not bound topological support dimension. A countable dense atomic probability measure, for example, has dimension zero and full interval support. The claimant's distinction is correct: the quoted attribution is not a demonstrated counterexample to the theorem. I am not certifying any Brownian support-equality theorem here.
