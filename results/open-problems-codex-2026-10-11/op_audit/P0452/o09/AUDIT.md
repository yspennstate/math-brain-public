## Independent mathematical checks

1. **Upper bound and concentration — ATTEMPT.md:14-48, 91-101.** I re-derived the one-edge mass change bound 2(k-1), the at-most-four retained-edge changes under one cost resampling, and the pivotal union bound 2(d_i+d_j)/(n-1). Their Efron–Stein calculation gives the stated conditional variance bound 64(k-1)^2 sum_i d_i. Weak empirical convergence indeed implies sum_i d_i=o(n^2): budgets at most M contribute at most M/n after division by n^2, and the remainder contributes at most the empirical fraction above M. For IID budgets, the truncated-square bound follows from min(X,n)^2/n<=X and dominated convergence with EX<infinity; no second moment is being assumed. Fixed-k component membership is determined by a finite unmarked ball. Thus F_k/n converges to the corresponding tree probability. The inequality L1/n<=F_k/n+(k-1)/n proves the desired upper bound as k increases. The IID clipping convention is harmless because finite mean implies n Pr(D>=n-1)->0. The claimant correctly avoids treating the source's informal O(1) wording as a rigorous bounded-largest-component conclusion.

2. **Other first-attempt statements — ATTEMPT.md:50-83, 105-142, 176-189.** The coalescence identity Q=S^2-sum_C a_C^2 and inequalities S(S-M)<=Q<=S^2-M^2 are correct. They give the stated equivalence, with limits in the specified order. The mean-at-most-two extinction argument treats the deterministic-one-offspring exception D=2 separately; its disjoint rejection events along each ray have positive probability and are independent. The path-count proof for budgets at most two uses genuinely disjoint outside-edge sets. I re-evaluated its integrals: the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is 1/(2 alpha^2), and the squared endpoint-function norm is 5/(4 alpha^3). The resulting exponent requires exactly c>2/log(2). The displayed bounded-budget collision estimate follows by substituting the budget bound into the cited conditional inequality, applying Bernoulli's inequality, and summing the arithmetic progression. These auxiliary statements do not substitute for the final supercritical argument.

3. **Arrival process and augmented local limit — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:9-23, referenced by ATTEMPT.md:214, 221.** An edge arriving at time w is preferred by an endpoint exactly when fewer than its budget incident edges arrived earlier. Counting rejected arrivals is essential and is done correctly. Accepted edges persist. For fixed t, the all-arrival graph is ER with parameter 1-exp(-t/n), independent of budgets. Finite-radius exploration has bounded expected size; truncating that size bounds repeated targets by O(M^2/n). Sampling deterministic marks without replacement then converges to IID P. Rank, activity, and the covered-path events need only a finite-radius enlargement of this graph, so their use of local convergence is justified. A non-root accepting the parent reserves one budget slot, hence has Gamma(D) threshold and D-1 other candidate costs; the root has Gamma(D+1) threshold and D candidates. This matches the source. The cutoff variance bound is valid: a cost resampling cannot affect G_n(t) if both values exceed t, giving pivotal probability at most 2t/n. Budget resampling changes at most the all-arrival degree in incident edges.

4. **Active vertex in an infinite cutoff component — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:25-40.** I independently changed variables u=z-t in the displayed fresh-child integral. It becomes

       exp(-t) integral_0^infinity exp(-u) u^(k-1)/(k-1)! du = exp(-t).

The child is active and accepted on this event when k>=2: its only arrival by t is the parent. Testing occurs before conditioning on acceptance, so the fresh candidate's unconditioned variables can legitimately be used. An infinite component forces infinitely many eligible candidate tests, and the conditional probability of this motif at each test is the fixed positive number P(D>=2) exp(-t). The geometric avoidance bound therefore holds. This proves existence somewhere in the component; the subsequent finite-distance argument uses local finiteness and does not assume a density law along an infinite component.

5. **Simultaneous coverage — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:42-52.** On infinite cutoff survival, some finite retained path reaches an active vertex and its finitely many all-arrival degrees have a finite maximum. Increasing R,B gives coverage with probability tending to one on survival. Large finite components have vanishing mass as k increases. The resulting expectation bound and Markov inequality control total uncovered mass. A degree-at-most-B path endpoint covers at most 1+B+...+B^R roots. Consequently every macroscopic union of old large components has the asserted linear active count, simultaneously. No independence or concentration within an individual large component is needed here.

6. **Sprinkling and partition argument — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:54-79, 221.** Conditional on the complete past, unseen edges have independent residual exponential clocks. The number of new arrivals is dominated by a Poisson variable of mean delta n/2; its Chernoff exponent delta n(log 2-1/2) is correct. Each arrival deactivates at most two vertices. With delta=a/4, both initially macroscopic sides retain at least an/2 active vertices before the arrival cap. A seen edge between currently active vertices would have been accepted earlier, so such a cross pair must be unseen while the no-cross event persists. Its total cross rate is therefore at least a^2 n/4. Absorbing the finite-state process at either a cross acceptance or excess arrivals gives q'(u)<=-(a^2 n/4)q(u). This avoids invalid conditioning on the cap. Counting its failure once outside the partition union bound is correct. There are at most 2^(n/k) old-component partitions, and the specified increase of k makes the exponential bound summable over them. The final-block partition argument and the weaker bound L1>=S-3 eta n are valid. Constants may depend on zeta, but the lower-bound event in the final graph does not; letting zeta decrease is legitimate.

7. **Outside-type contraction and return process — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:83-129.** In the ordinary slot law the acceptance kernel is h(x,z)=min(x,z)/x. Finite mean permits a compact fertile set K with epsilon=integral_{K^c} m dmu<1. Expected outside potential contracts by epsilon each generation; births are bounded by the preceding potential. Thus every family killed at its first K hits has finite expected size and is almost surely finite, including its terminal hits. Zero-potential types cause no problem. Return offspring are finite almost surely; their descendants are independent conditional on types, because first-hit selection reveals their incoming acceptance and type, not their outgoing subtree variables.

For the return mean density, paths with j outside intermediates contribute at most B epsilon^j. This yields the claimed bounded, continuous, strictly positive kernel. I re-derived path reversal: with alpha=m/x and pi=dmu/alpha on K, the density relative to pi has the symmetric endpoint factor alpha(s)alpha(u), the internal alpha factors, and the product of symmetric min kernels. Intermediate integrations remain against mu. Therefore the operator really is compact self-adjoint. The absolute-value Rayleigh inequality gives a positive eigenfunction for its norm; strict kernel positivity and compactness bound that eigenfunction above and away from zero. There is a uniform positive zero-offspring probability. Repeated visits to a bounded population then force eventual extinction, so population diverges on survival. If the eigenvalue were at most one, the bounded expected weighted population and Fatou's lemma would contradict positive survival. Original survival is equivalent to survival of the finite-first-hit forest's return process, because all stopped outside families are finite. Thus positive original survival implies return eigenvalue greater than one.

8. **Bounded-cost survival — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:131-156.** I checked both omitted-path bounds: long excursions lose at most B epsilon^(N+1)/(1-epsilon) in endpoint density; a first excluded type outside L loses at most B tau_L/(1-epsilon)^2. Their sum gives the claimed uniform operator approximation. This is an approximation of the return kernel, not an unjustified spectral truncation of the original infinite-type process. Choosing the error small gives a supercritical truncated return operator. Path reversal survives both restrictions. Bounded excursion depth and budgets give bounded offspring, so the displayed L2 martingale variance series converges geometrically and proves positive survival. Every edge on a retained return path costs at most its parent's bounded threshold. The root can reach K with positive probability at a sufficiently small cost. Hence a finite cutoff has positive survival.

9. **Full cutoff-survival continuity — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:158-183.** The extinction equation uses the correct cutoff kernel min(t,x,z)/x. Dominated convergence makes e_* a full fixed point. Bounded-cost survival ensures e_*<1 on K, and the fixed-point equation upgrades this to a uniform gap below one. Iterating through the almost surely finite outside family gives the first-K-hit product identity. In return generations, products tend to one on extinction and to zero on survival, because the return population diverges and every factor is uniformly below one. Thus e_* equals full extinction, rather than an unrelated fixed point. Integration against the distinct root law finishes rho_t increasing to rho. This step addresses the genuine infinite-type escape obstruction rather than assuming continuity of survival under increasing cutoffs.

10. **Conclusion — SECOND_ATTEMPT.md:185-187, 229-233; ATTEMPT.md:221-225.** The finite-cutoff lower bound plus the established continuity gives the full lower bound for every tolerance. Combining it with item 1 gives convergence in probability; bounded fractions give expectation convergence. For deterministic arrays, the finite-time graph exploration samples budgets uniformly independently of edge arrivals, so weak empirical convergence suffices even when empirical means fail to converge.
