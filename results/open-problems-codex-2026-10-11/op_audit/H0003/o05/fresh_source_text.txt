manuscript, August 2026 Abstract. We study systems of coupled Ornstein–Uhlenbeck diffusions with an absorbing (failure) state, indexed by the vertices of a sequence of finite graphs G n G_{n} that converge locally weakly (Benjamini–Schramm) to a unimodular Galton–Watson tree T ∼ UGW ⁡ ( g ) T\sim\mathrm{UGW}(g) . A single failure can trigger a cascade of threshold crossings, and the central question is whether that cascade is subcritical (a.s. finite) or supercritical, and what its cluster-size law and cluster geometry look like at and near criticality. Our main structural result is a reduction: under an explicitly stated front-decoupling hypothesis (Hypothesis 2.4 , the saturated-coupling regime), which we verify exactly on the Bethe lattice and prove in part in general, the failed cluster of the root converges in law to the genealogical tree of a finite-type ( | D | |D| -type) Galton–Watson process whose mean matrix M M is computed from a single (matrix-valued) many-to-one formula. Conditional on this reduction we prove: (A) criticality occurs exactly at ρ ⁡ ( M ) = 1 \rho(M)=1 , where ρ ⁡ ( M ) \rho(M) is the annealed Perron root, and the harmonic-measure-dimension expression one is tempted to write for ρ \rho equals the quenched (geometric-mean) growth rate, which is ≤ ρ ⁡ ( M ) \leq\rho(M) with equality iff the boundary weights are a.s. constant; (B) the quenched and annealed rates are respectively the tangent at 0 0 and the value at 1 1 of a single convex, real-analytic annealed pressure φ ⁡ ( β ) = log ⁡ ρ ⁡ ( M ( β ) ) \varphi(\beta)=\log\rho(M^{(\beta)}) built from tilted mean matrices, so that the quenched/annealed gap is a chord–tangent inequality, and — extending the reduction beyond boundary-blind observables — in the supercritical regime the end boundary ∂ S \partial S of the failed cluster has, a.s. on survival, Hausdorff dimension exactly log ⁡ ρ ⁡ ( M ) \log\rho(M) , of strictly positive codimension in ∂ T \partial T unless transmission is perfect; (C) at criticality the total progeny obeys ℙ ( | S | = n ) ∼ C n − 3 / 2 \mathbb{P}(|S|=n)\sim Cn^{-3/2} with the universal mean-field exponent via the Otter–Dwass formula and a local CLT, and, under an additional moment hypothesis, the conditioned cluster rescaled by n − 1 / 2 n^{-1/2} converges in the Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov sense to Aldous’ Continuum Random Tree, upgrading the universal exponent to a universal shape; (D) the subcritical domain is open and contractible (but not convex; the supercritical domain is the convex object), the resolvent ( I − M ) − 1 (I-M)^{-1} is a bounded nonnegative matrix, and the spectral gap 1 − ρ ⁡ ( M ) 1-\rho(M) controls quantitative stability. A secondary aim is cautionary. Several statements that are natural to write down in this setting are false, and we prove that they are false: (i) the infinite-dimensional Hilbert–Schmidt / Krein–Rutman programme for an operator K ∞ K_{\infty} on L 2 ​ ( ∂ T , ν h ) L^{2}(\partial T,\nu_{h}) is both unnecessary for (A)–(D) and, for the energy reason we make precise, generically impossible; (ii) the small-noise first-passage probability carries an algebraic prefactor Λ − 1 / 2 \Lambda^{-1/2} rather than a multiplicative ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) (1+o(1)) ; (iii) the scalar many-to-one identity fails because consecutive edge weights along a ray share a vertex type — the correct identity is a matrix product whose growth rate is ρ ⁡ ( M ) \rho(M) ; and (iv) the spectral-radius/dimension identity and the convexity of the subcritical domain both fail, in the directions we identify. The load-bearing input is the front-decoupling hypothesis itself. We prove it unconditionally in an explicit dissipative regime: when the dissipation ratio κ = γ max ​ L σ ​ Δ max / α min \kappa=\gamma_{\max}L_{\sigma}\Delta_{\max}/\alpha_{\min} is below 1 1 , a pathwise front-tracking argument (synchronous coupling, a cone-of-influence estimate showing that back-reaction “echoes” decay geometrically in graph distance, and an exploration telescoping in which the relevant volume is the failed cluster rather than the ambient tree) shows that echo decay strictly beats volume growth, yielding a quantitative total-variation bound and making the main theorems unconditional on an open parameter region. The naive Girsanov change of measure provably cannot do this: the back-reaction drift is O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) and the Radon–Nikodym density is not tight. Outside the dissipative and saturated regimes the reduction remains open — we state the precise conjecture — and outside saturation the natural object is a continuous-type branching process, for which we nevertheless prove finite-dimensional two-sided criticality certificates by a kernel-sandwich argument. The architecture Let ( G n ) (G_{n}) be finite connected graphs with uniformly bounded average degree converging in the Benjamini–Schramm local weak sense [ 5 , 3 ] to a unimodular random rooted tree ( T , o ) (T,o) ; we take T ∼ UGW ⁡ ( g ) T\sim\mathrm{UGW}(g) , the unimodular Galton–Watson tree with offspring generating function g g , finite mean μ = g ′ ​ ( 1 ) > 1 \mu=g^{\prime}(1)>1 and finite variance σ g 2 = g ′′ ​ ( 1 ) + g ′ ​ ( 1 ) − g ′ ​ ( 1 ) 2 < ∞ \sigma_{g}^{2}=g^{\prime\prime}(1)+g^{\prime}(1)-g^{\prime}(1)^{2}<\infty . On each G n G_{n} we place a system of interacting diffusions (Definition 2.1 ); a vertex may cross a threshold and enter an absorbing failed state, and a failed vertex stresses its neighbours, which may then fail in turn. The cascade initiated by failing the root has a random cluster S ⊂ V ⁡ ( T ) S\subset V(T) of eventually-failed vertices. The governing heuristic is the next-generation operator of mathematical epidemiology [ 21 ] : on a finite graph the matrix K n K_{n} whose ( u , v ) (u,v) entry is the expected number of secondary failures at v v caused by u u has a spectral radius ρ ⁡ ( K n ) \rho(K_{n}) that separates subcritical from supercritical behaviour. A natural programme suggested by this heuristic is to pass to the limit and replace K n K_{n} by a bounded integral operator K ∞ K_{\infty} on the Hilbert bundle ℋ ≅ L 2 ​ ( ∂ T , ν h ) ⊗ ℝ m \mathcal{H}\cong L^{2}(\partial T,\nu_{h})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m} over the Martin boundary of T T , and then to run an infinite-dimensional Perron–Frobenius (Krein–Rutman) theory. Part of the thesis of this paper is that this programme is both unnecessary for the central questions and, generically, impossible (Proposition 7.3 ). 1.2. The thesis of this paper The cluster size | S | |S| , the survival event, and the subcritical stability are all boundary-blind : they do not depend on where on ∂ T \partial T the failed vertices lie. This single observation reorganises everything. Conditional on the reduction (Hypothesis 2.4 ), the failed cluster is the family tree of a Galton–Watson process whose type is only the hazard domain d ∈ D d\in D , | D | = m < ∞ |D|=m<\infty ; the Martin boundary integrates out of every quantity that the main theorems concern. Consequently: • The relevant spectral object is the finite m × m m\times m annealed mean matrix M M , not an operator on L 2 ​ ( ∂ T ) L^{2}(\partial T) . Its Perron root is computed by one (matrix-valued) many-to-one identity (Theorem 3.1 , ( 4 ), and Proposition 5.1 ). • Simplicity of the leading eigenvalue, monotonicity, real-analyticity, and the operator van den Driessche–Watmough lift — the delicate-looking spectral issues — become elementary finite-dimensional Perron–Frobenius facts (Section 7 ). The Hilbert–Schmidt property of K ∞ K_{\infty} is never used. • The n − 3 / 2 n^{-3/2} law follows from the Otter–Dwass formula and a one-dimensional local CLT (Section 6 ); no general-state-space Kesten–Stigum theory is needed for the size law. Under an additional moment hypothesis the full shape of the critical cluster is universal: the Continuum Random Tree (Theorem 6.4 ). • The finite-dimensional collapse extends one step past boundary-blindness. The tilted matrices M ( β ) M^{(\beta)} and their pressure φ ⁡ ( β ) = log ⁡ ρ ⁡ ( M ( β ) ) \varphi(\beta)=\log\rho(M^{(\beta)}) organise the quenched and annealed rates as the tangent at 0 0 and the value at 1 1 of a single convex analytic function (Theorem 5.3 ), and the Hausdorff dimension of the failed boundary ∂ S \partial S — a boundary-resolved quantity — equals log ⁡ ρ ⁡ ( M ) \log\rho(M) on survival (Theorem 5.5 ). The operator K ∞ K_{\infty} is needed only for boundary fluctuations, not for boundary geometry at the level of dimension. • The reduction itself is a theorem, not merely a hypothesis, in the dissipative regime: when dissipation dominates total coupling ( κ < 1 \kappa<1 ), a pathwise cone-of-influence estimate shows that back-reaction echoes decay geometrically in graph distance while their sources live only on the failed cluster, whose volume is spectrally controlled; the resulting exploration telescoping proves Hypothesis 2.4 with a quantitative rate (Theorem 11.5 ). 1.3. Warnings: natural claims that are false Five statements that are natural to formulate in this setting are false. Identifying them precisely, and proving the true statements that replace them, is part of the content of this paper. (F1) The spectral-radius/dimension identity is false in general. Writing ρ = p min − dim H \rho=p_{\min}^{-\dim_{\mathrm{H}}} equates ρ \rho with the harmonic-measure dimension expression, which is the quenched , geometric-mean growth rate e ℓ e^{\ell} . The criticality of the cascade is governed by the annealed branching rate m ¯ = ρ ⁡ ( M ) \bar{m}=\rho(M) . One has m ¯ ≥ e ℓ \bar{m}\geq e^{\ell} , with equality iff the boundary weights are ν h \nu_{h} -a.s. constant (Theorem 4.2 ); the sharpest form is the chord–tangent inequality of the pressure (Theorem 5.3 ). The identity holds on the Bethe lattice precisely because the boundary there is homogeneous; off it, p min − dim H p_{\min}^{-\dim_{\mathrm{H}}} undercounts criticality. (F2) The subcritical domain is not convex; convexity belongs to the supercritical domain. One might try to deduce convexity of ℳ − = { ρ < 1 } \mathcal{M}_{-}=\{\rho<1\} from log-concavity of the first-passage probability, “because sublevel sets of a concave function are convex.” That implication is false (superlevel sets of a concave function are convex). The true statement: the supercritical domain ℳ + = { ρ > 1 } \mathcal{M}_{+}=\{\rho>1\} is the convex object (a superlevel set of a log-concave function), and ℳ − \mathcal{M}_{-} is its non-convex complement, but ℳ − \mathcal{M}_{-} is contractible by a coupling-retraction (Theorem 8.1 ). (F3) The small-noise first-passage asymptotic carries an algebraic prefactor. The formula p ex = e − Λ ⁡ ( 1 + O ⁡ ( Λ − 1 ​ log ⁡ Λ ) ) p^{\mathrm{ex}}=e^{-\Lambda(1+O(\Lambda^{-1}\log\Lambda))} conflates an exponential rate with a probability; Laplace’s method on the exact Wald representation gives p ex ≍ Λ − 1 / 2 e − Λ p^{\mathrm{ex}}\asymp\Lambda^{-1/2}e^{-\Lambda} (Section 10 ). The exponent Λ \Lambda is correct; the multiplicative-correction form is not. The main results use the exact Wald formula and are unaffected, but the constant C C in Theorem 6.2 inherits the prefactor. (F4) The Hilbert–Schmidt property of K ∞ K_{\infty} is generically false. The energy integral of | k ∞ | 2 |k_{\infty}|^{2} against ν h ⊗ ν h \nu_{h}\otimes\nu_{h} diverges for essentially all supercritical g g , because the tree Martin kernel is singular on the diagonal and ν h ​ ( B n ) ≍ br ⁡ ( T ) − n \nu_{h}(B_{n})\asymp\operatorname{br}(T)^{-n} (Proposition 7.3 ). This is not a hard lemma awaiting proof; it is a false target to be abandoned. (F5) The scalar many-to-one identity is false. The identity 𝔼 ⁡ [ ∑ | v | = n ∏ e F ⁡ ( e ) ] = ( m ∗ ​ 𝔼 q ⊗ q ​ [ F ] ) n \mathbb{E}[\sum_{|v|=n}\prod_{e}F(e)]=(m_{*}\mathbb{E}_{q\otimes q}[F])^{n} , and with it the identification m ¯ = m ∗ ​ 𝔼 q ⊗ q ​ [ p e ​ w e ] \bar{m}=m_{*}\mathbb{E}_{q\otimes q}[p_{e}w_{e}] , silently assumes that consecutive edge weights along a ray are independent; they share a vertex type and are not. The correct annealed object is the matrix product of Proposition 5.1 , with growth rate ρ ⁡ ( M ) \rho(M) ; the scalar m ∗ ​ 𝔼 ​ [ p ] m_{*}\mathbb{E}[p] is only the n = 1 n=1 factor and is neither an upper nor a lower bound for ρ ⁡ ( M ) \rho(M) in general. A related normalisation point: the growth rate of UGW ⁡ ( g ) \mathrm{UGW}(g) is m ∗ = g ′′ ​ ( 1 ) / g ′ ​ ( 1 ) m_{*}=g^{\prime\prime}(1)/g^{\prime}(1) , not μ \mu (Section 2.1 ). 1.4. The load-bearing input Hypothesis 2.4 is the load-bearing reduction. Its naive justification by a Girsanov comparison between the full and directed systems fails: the back-reaction drift is O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) , so the Radon–Nikodym density is not tight toward 1 1 (Proposition 11.1 ). The correct mechanism is event-based and pathwise, and Section 11 carries it out in a regime: it dissects the obstruction — a back-reaction echo whose per-vertex size is O ⁡ ( ε ⁡ ( δ ) ) O(\varepsilon(\delta)) but which threatens to compound over the exponential volume of the tree — and proves (Theorem 11.5 ) that when the dissipation ratio κ = γ max ​ L σ ​ Δ max / α min \kappa=\gamma_{\max}L_{\sigma}\Delta_{\max}/\alpha_{\min} is below 1 1 , echo decay strictly beats volume growth: influences decay geometrically in graph distance (a cone-of-influence estimate), echo sources are supported on the failed cluster whose expected volume is ( 1 − ρ ⁡ ( M ) ) − 1 (1-\rho(M))^{-1} -bounded rather than m ∗ n m_{*}^{n} , and an exploration telescoping converts these into a quantitative total-variation bound. On this open region Hypothesis 2.4 is a theorem and everything below is unconditional. In the strong-coupling regime κ ≥ 1 \kappa\geq 1 the reduction remains open (Conjecture 11.8 ). Outside the saturated regime the natural object is a continuous-type branching process whose type is the failure strength of a vertex; that analysis — which is the general-state-space programme one would have to build — is open and is the honest home of the remaining difficulty. We do, however, show that its criticality question (though not its limit theory) dissolves into finite linear algebra: two-sided finite-matrix certificates squeeze the Perron eigenvalue of the continuous-type mean operator (Proposition 12.1 ). 2. The anatomy of the obstruction: the echo The cascade spreads through the tree as a wave, and the hoped-for reduction asserts that this wave is a Markovian front: the future depends only on the present state of the front, and the children of a failed vertex fail conditionally independently. Diffusions on a graph resist this in a way that classical epidemic models (SIR, directed percolation) do not, because the edges are continuous, two-way channels. The elementary obstruction event is the following four-step echo: (1) The event. A parent u u fails and begins forcing its children v v and w w . (2) The back-reaction. v v fails first; its state s t ( v ) s^{(v)}_{t} rises into B fail B_{\mathrm{fail}} , and, the coupling being undirected, v v now pushes back on u u . (3) The sibling correlation. The extra push drives u u deeper into the failure basin, altering the trajectory of u u , hence the forcing that u u exerts on the remaining child w w : the transmission indicators χ u ​ v \chi_{uv} and χ u ​ w \chi_{uw} are correlated through the shared parent trajectory. (4) The ancestral ripple. The perturbation of u u does not stop at u u : it propagates to u u ’s own parent and onward, subtly shifting the state — and hence the timing — of the entire active front. Proposition 11.2 (2) shows that in the saturated regime a single echo is small: the logistic coupling is flat on B fail B_{\mathrm{fail}} , so one back-reaction perturbs one outgoing forcing by O ⁡ ( ε ⁡ ( δ ) ) O(\varepsilon(\delta)) .
