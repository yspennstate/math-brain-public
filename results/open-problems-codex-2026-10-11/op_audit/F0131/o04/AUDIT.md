## Exact claim and scope

Lines 3, 123 and 127 claim that, for the ordinary conservative four-neighbour sandpile on Z² with initial height -H and n additional chips at the origin, the fired sets cannot have a nondegenerate regular (4H+12)-gon as a subsequential Hausdorff scaling limit for any integer H>=-1. In particular, H=0 cannot have a regular dodecagon limit. The H=-2 square remains the known exception. Lines 129 and 158 explain the normalization and limitations.

This answers Question 6.2 negatively. It rules out the proposed limit itself, rather than just exhibiting a nonregular finite aggregate. It uses the fired set {u>0}, the correct background sign, and fixed H as n tends to infinity. Disproving the H=0 assertion already suffices to disprove the proposed universal regular-polygon answer. The stronger all-H assertion also passes. Existence and identification of the actual fired-set limit, and the proposed extra dihedral symmetry, are not proved. A curved domain with that symmetry is not excluded.

## Step-by-step checks and potential failure points

1. **Finite facts, lines 11-21.** I independently checked the first-exceedance least-action argument. At the first attempted firing beyond a stabilizing comparison function, its own count is equal and all neighbour counts are smaller, so its height is at most three. This proves the required comparison and uniqueness without assuming a lower bound for the comparison configuration. Transport by signed coordinate permutations gives D4 symmetry. Increasing initial heights gives odometer monotonicity. A first firing away from the origin requires a previous neighbouring firing because the initial height is at most two, proving connectivity. Summing the finitely supported discrete Laplacian gives conservation. A fired site's height is nonnegative after each firing, while an unfired site's height stays at least its background. These statements, including the warning about negative shifted heights for H<0, are correct.

2. **Reflection monotonicity, lines 43-47.** The pasted minimum is stabilizing. I checked separately a retained site, a far-side site choosing the original function, a far-side site choosing the reflection, an even-k plane edge, and an odd-k crossing edge. On an odd crossing edge the two endpoints are exchanged, and choosing the smaller reflected value on the far side makes the retained neighbour no larger than the reflected comparison neighbour. The reflected source contributes -n to the Laplacian and therefore improves, rather than spoils, its upper bound. Least action forces equality with the pasted function. Taking k=2x_1-1 then proves coordinate monotonicity and downward closure in absolute coordinates. No erroneous global monotonicity of heights is needed.

3. **Interior filling, lines 51-53.** A point strictly inside the hypothetical convex limit can be moved a small distance outward in both signed coordinates while staying inside. Hausdorff approximation supplies a fired point whose absolute coordinates strictly dominate those of an entire neighbourhood of the original point. Downward closure fills the lattice points of that neighbourhood. A finite cover gives the compact-set assertion. Thus the proof does not confuse Hausdorff convergence with automatic interior occupancy. Here the compact-set wording means all rescaled lattice points in the compact set; it does not assert that a finite lattice equals a continuum set.

4. **Density lower bound, lines 57-68.** Order fired sites by their last legal firings. Each internal edge delivers at least one chip to its earlier endpoint after that endpoint's last firing. Its post-firing height was nonnegative. This counts every internal edge exactly once and proves the unweighted inequality. With nonnegative weights, an edge contributes the earlier endpoint's weight, which is at least the minimum of its endpoint weights. The identity

   sum_edges min(phi(x),phi(y)) = 2 sum_x phi(x) - (1/2) sum_edges |phi(x)-phi(y)|

   follows from degree four, including edges leaving the weight support. For a smooth compactly supported test the rescaled variation term is O(epsilon), so the weak limiting height is at least two in the filled interior. This is an averaged bound; final microscopic heights need not individually be at least two. That distinction is respected.

5. **Continuum passage, lines 72-78.** I fetched and read Pegden-Smart, arXiv:1105.0111. Theorem 4.2, PDF page 9, gives local uniform convergence of the regularized odometer. Section 2, PDF pages 5-6, gives convergence of its discrete fundamental solution away from zero; the end of Lemma 3.1, PDF page 8, explicitly identifies the resulting odometer convergence away from zero. The paper uses the unnormalized neighbour-sum Laplacian, matching the attempt: there is no missing factor of four. For epsilon=n^(-1/2), the rescaled finite-difference Laplacian of u/n is eta away from the source. Summation by parts and uniform test-function approximation therefore give Delta U=g. Stability bounds g above by three; the previous weighted inequality bounds it below by two inside the assumed polygon. Outside the polygon U vanishes by outer Hausdorff containment. The weak limit is bounded on the entire vertex neighbourhood, so no boundary line or point measure is present. D4 symmetry centers a regular polygon at the origin, keeping each vertex away from the singular source; this is stated explicitly at line 136.

6. **Corner growth estimate, lines 82-99.** For a nonnegative continuous solution with 0<=g<=b and u(0)=0, the circle-mean identity yields M(r)<=br²/4. Subharmonicity gives

   u(x) <= (pi r²)^(-1) integral_{B(x,r)} u <= 2br², for |x|<=r.

   I checked the integral constant using circle averages over B(0,2r). Convolution justifies these identities for distributional solutions; the bounded forcing controls passage through r=0. Thus the needed O(r²) growth does not rely on an unproved C^{1,1} boundary theorem.

7. **Fourier obstruction, lines 101-119.** Testing on full-circle annuli gives F''+F'/r-4F/r²=G with no angular boundary term. For F=r²f this is r²f''+5rf'=G, hence (r^5 f')'=r³G. I independently differentiated both identities. The integral of cos(2theta) over the central interval (-pi/4,pi/4) is one; its integral over the whole sector is sin(alpha). Thus G>=a-b(1-sin(alpha)). Bounded G and F=O(r²) eliminate the integration constant at zero: a nonzero constant would produce an r^(-4) term in f, with only an O(|log r|) correction. A strictly positive lower bound for G then gives f'>=kappa/(4r), contradicting bounded f logarithmically. The claimed necessary inequality a<=b(1-sin(alpha)) is valid.

8. **H=0 contradiction, line 123.** A dodecagon has alpha=5pi/6 and sin(alpha)=1/2. With a=2 and b=3, the necessary inequality becomes 2<=3/2. This is a contradiction, and applies to any orientation or positive dilation of the regular polygon.

9. **All-H extension and normalization, lines 127-129.** I fetched Bou-Rabee, arXiv:1909.07849. Theorem 9.2, PDF page 28, expressly covers a stationary ergodic integer background bounded below and above by 2d-2 and gives n^(-2/d) odometer convergence locally uniformly away from the origin. A constant -H background is a one-point stationary ergodic law satisfying the assumptions. The earlier high-density assumption for the paper's other model is not an extra assumption in this single-source theorem. The continuum equation is Delta U=eta_limit+H, so the interior bounds are H+2 and H+3. At H=-1 microscopic shifted heights can be negative, but outside the polygon Delta U=0, inside it the lower bound is positive, and its boundary has zero area. This supplies precisely the nonnegative forcing required by the corner lemma. With k=H+3>=2, the forbidden inequality is sin(pi/(2k))<=1/k; strict sine concavity gives the reverse strict inequality. Source Theorem 6.3, PDF page 30, has both inner and outer sqrt(n) radius bounds for H>=-1. A scalar scaling with a nondegenerate compact polygon limit must therefore be comparable to n^(-1/2); taking a convergent scale-ratio subsequence just changes the dilation. This normalization argument is valid.

For the remaining angles the exterior angle is at most arcsin(1/k); summing exterior angles proves the stated side bound. At H=0 the bound is 18.488825470..., and D4 rotation orbits make the vertex count a multiple of four, giving at least 20.
