For a centered node and a derivative row `E_i` with `q_i=mu(E_i)` that is relative-`eta` close to a physical coordinate subcube `Q_i={y_F=a}`, `epsilon_i<=eta q_i`, choose a canonical source-measurable trigger set `F`. The robust-row theorem gives `p_ik>=2(q_i-2epsilon_i)` for every `k in F`; the exact co-pair identity gives `r_ik=p_ik/4+o_ik`, hence `r_ik>=(q_i-2epsilon_i)/2`, so every selected trigger is backed by genuine source co-pair mass.

Partition `F` into disjoint physical blocks of size two and at most one singleton. Query blocks sequentially, continuing only on the unique child matching the target subcube pattern and stopping the macro at the first mismatch. The stage reach probabilities are geometric. Using the source physical-block inequality `Delta_A F<=U_A<=|A|`, the whole macro has

`F(root)-E F(leaf) < 8/3`.

The row's weighted terminal folded influence is at most

`mu(E_i\Q_i)+mu(Q_i\E_i)=epsilon_i`,

so the exact physical `J` ledger yields

`J(root)-E J(leaf) >= q_i-epsilon_i >= (1-eta)q_i`.

Therefore for any finite `K`, every such row with

`q_i > 8/[3K(1-eta)]`

is strictly `K`-affordable by a realizable nonreusing physical macro. Repeating the macro telescopes and leaves a terminal class with no relative-`eta` robust row above that threshold. At `eta=1/16`, the threshold is `128/(45K)`. A singleton-trigger variant improves `8/3` to `2` and the threshold to `2/[K(1-eta)]`.
