\title{A Boolean arithmetic refinement of level-wise entropy conversion}
\author{A067, stream B14}\date{12 October 2026}\maketitle
All cubes are uniform, outputs are $\{-1,1\}$, and entropies use bits.
Write $q_S=\wh f(S)^2$, $I=\sum_S |S|q_S$, and $v=1-q_\varnothing$.
For every nonzero nonempty coefficient put
\[
d_f(S)=\max_{T\supseteq S,\ \wh f(T)\ne0}|T|,
\qquad U(f)=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}q_S(d_f(S)-|S|).
\]
Zero terms are omitted, and constants have $U=0$.
\begin{lemma}
For every nonempty $S$ with nonzero coefficient,
$\wh f(S)\in2^{1-d_f(S)}\mathbb Z$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An integer-valued cube polynomial of real degree $m$ has integer
multilinear coefficients in the $y_i=(1-x_i)/2$ variables, by finite
differences of vertex values. Its Fourier coefficients are therefore
multiples of $2^{-m}$. If $s=|S|$, the mixed derivative
\[
D_S f=2^{-s}\sum_{z\in\{-1,1\}^S}\chi_S(z)f(z,x_{S^c})
\]
takes values in $2^{1-s}\mathbb Z$ and has degree $d_f(S)-s$.
Apply the integer-polynomial observation to $2^{s-1}D_S f$ and its
empty Fourier coefficient $2^{s-1}\wh f(S)$.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}
Every Boolean sign satisfies
\[
H(f)\le2I(f)+2U(f)-(2-\log_2 e)v.
\]
Thus $U(f)\le KI(f)$ implies $H(f)\le(2+2K)I(f)$ in every dimension.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The lemma bounds nonempty-coefficient surprisal by $2(d_f(S)-1)$.
Their weighted sum is $2I+2U-2v$. The empty contribution is at most
$(\log_2 e)v$ by $-x\ln x\le1-x$.
\end{proof}
This replaces the level-support estimate
$H(q\mid |S|=k)\le\log_2\binom nk\le k\log_2(en/k)$
on a stated Boolean regime. It is not an improvement of the general
Bonami--Beckner norm inequality. In the recovered threshold paper,
Section 5.1, Lemma 5.3 and equation (29), hypercontractivity bounds
a ternary pivotal fiber's noise norm before paying rarity and rank
costs. The entropy conversion still needs additional structure.

For the point defect $f_n=1-2\mathbf1_{\{x=\mathbf1\}}$,
put $p=2^{-n}$, $n\ge2$. Direct computation gives
\[
I=2np,\quad v=4p(1-p),\quad U=2np-4np^2\le I.
\]
Hence the theorem gives $H\le4I-(2-\log_2 e)v$, whereas the
generic coordinate-marginal upper bound has ratio
$nh_2(2p)/I=n-1+\log_2 e+O(p)$. Its Shannon ratio tends to four,
while every fixed-order spectral R\'enyi ratio below order one diverges.
The level-count bound is tight on point defects. For a strict gain
over level counting, the existing addressing baseline on $a+2^a$
variables has $d_f(S)=a+1$, $v=1$, $I=1+a/2$, and $U=a/2$.
The refined bound is exactly $H\le2a$, whereas generic full-cube
level counting costs $\Theta(a^2)$. Indeed its level law is
$W_{t+1}=2^{-a}\binom at$; at least half the mass has $t\ge a/4$
for $a\ge8$, and
$\log_2\binom{a+2^a}{t+1}\ge(t+1)[a-\log_2(a+1)]$.
This evaluates a new bound on a recovered baseline, rather than
claiming the addressing theorem as new.

The Boolean hypothesis is essential: the real function
$n^{-1/2}\sum_i x_i$ has singleton support, $U=0$, $I=1$, and
$H=\log_2 n$.

Universal payment of $U$ is false. A one-point dictator surgery
$g_n=x_1-2\mathbf1_{\{x=\mathbf1\}}$ has
\[
I(g_n)=1+(2n-4)2^{-n},\quad
U(g_n)=n-1-(2n-4)2^{-n}-4n2^{-2n}.
\]
Its $U/I$ diverges although its entropy tends to zero.

Even optimizing one character anchor fails. For
$F_n=\operatorname{Maj}_3(z)f_n(y)$ on disjoint variables, define
\[
E_A=(\log_2 e)(1-\wh F_n(A)^2)+
2\sum_{R\ne\varnothing}\wh{(F_n\chi_A)}(R)^2(d_{F_n\chi_A}(R)-1).
\]
One has $H(F_n)\le E_A$ for every $A$. At least $3/4$ of the
translated majority spectrum has nonempty $z$-support. The point
defect has full support on its $n$ variables; thus those coefficients
have upper degree at least $n+1$. It follows that
$E_A\ge3n/2$ for all anchors, although
$I(F_n)=3/2+2n2^{-n}\le5/2$ and $H(F_n)=2+H(f_n)$ is bounded.

Full FEI remains open. A tail-stable arithmetic replacement or a
completely paid partition is required; a universal maximal-support
envelope, even optimized over one anchor, cannot supply it.
