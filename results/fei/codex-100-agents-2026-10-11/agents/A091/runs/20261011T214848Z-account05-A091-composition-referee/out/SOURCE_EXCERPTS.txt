## Research and execution

The main objectives are a complete expected whole-set witness cost and a nonlocal physical restriction payment. Signed Booleanity, conditional phase entropy, arbitrary weighted thresholds under product biases, and low-energy/shared-variable composition provide distinct routes and substantial class targets. All constants, normalizers, rare-event logarithms and Boolean realizability requirements remain explicit.

The independently audited revision removes targets already proved, adds known counterexamples with their exact scopes, and gives every A-packet a concrete deliverable, acceptance test, bounded initial cycle, intended consumers and independent reviewers. Baseline imports finish once; subsequent runs advance their next unresolved implication. Three stagnant substantive attempts on the same stable gap prompt a change of method or a justified narrower theorem.

- [Packet coverage](PACKET_STATUS.json) separates artifact completion from mathematical resolution.

### B05 and independent B19 review — first matching deliverables adopted

It proves that no finite fixed nonnegative K,A,B make `L <= K*d + A*V_post + B*M_inf` valid at every actual Boolean split. The sparse bent point-defect family has `d=2^(-3k)`, `L/d=k+log2(e)+o(1)`, `V_post/d<=4*2^(-k)`, and `M_inf/d=12*k*2^(-3k)`. This refutes the local all-coordinate helper; it does not refute existential selection, suitable stopping trees, nonlocal transport or FEI.

B19 independently accepted that scope at commit `7955d427222392911dc6af2e4ee3aa8aab67ac8c`, run `runs/B19/20261011T195038Z-account01-B19-independent-review/`; Brain mirror `b57496edc22cb5b0dc058fb183bde2b787dc9d42`. Decimal entropy values are numerical support; the analytic argument supplies the all-k theorem. Recovery's mathematical auditor also reconstructed the analytic no-go independently.

The matching first deliverables of A025 and A092 are accepted in the revised packet index. They remain useful continuing research/review roles. **Next B05 step:** compare control-first and selector-first physical restriction trees, including terminal entropy and nonreusing creation/spending of logarithmic credit.

# FEI campaign counterexamples and mandatory scope tests

**Full FEI remains OPEN.** These examples refute specified intermediate assertions, or show that a proposed restriction retains full FEI. None is a counterexample to full FEI. Use the F01–F20 roster in `TEAMS.json`. Source aliases and log conventions are defined in `CLAIMS.md`; explicit natural-log exceptions are noted below.

## X001 — Parity lift hides all entropy from local erasure

**Owners:** F05–F07,F15,F17. **Related:** C015,C027. **Source:** ES §3.

For any Boolean g on n sign coordinates, let f(x,z)=g(x₁z,…,x_nz). A Fourier set S becomes S if |S| is even and S∪{z} otherwise. Therefore H(f)=H(g), every occupied set has even size, B(f)=0, and I(f)=I(g)+P(|S_g| odd)≤2I(g). Restricting the physical input z=+1 recovers arbitrary g.

**Defeats:** treating B=0, absence of spectral edges, or even spectral support as a certified easy leaf. The class is not closed under physical restriction. A universal bound for its entropy is full-FEI strength up to a factor two. A bound for aggregate erasure on *all restrictions* is not refuted.

## X002 — One physical selector creates unbounded erasure synergy

**Owners:** F05–F07,F17. **Related:** C027. **Source:** ES §§1–2.

For odd m≥3 put b(t)=(-1)^(t₁t₂+…+t_(m−2)t_(m−1)) on m−1 binary bits. Set g₀(y)=b(y₁+y_m,…,y_(m−1)+y_m), g₁(y)=(-1)^y_m g₀(y), with addition mod 2. Their squared spectra are uniform on even and odd subsets respectively: H(g_j)=m−1, B(g_j)=0, I(g_j)=m/2.

The actual Boolean splice F(0,y)=g₀(y), F(1,y)=g₁(y) has uniform spectrum on m+1 bits: H(F)=B(F)=m+1 and I(F)=(m+1)/2. Its selector influence is 1/2. The mixture of child spectra has B=m although its selector-label mutual information is only one bit.

**Defeats:** B(mixture)≤average B+C·Inf_selector for fixed C. Also defeats restriction stability of P_λ=B+λ(H−B) for every fixed 0≤λ<1: it would require C≥2[(1−λ)m+1+λ]. Does not refute nonlinear potentials or the true entropy restriction identity.

## X003 — Sparse activation leaves a necessary local logarithm

**Owners:** F03,F05,F15,F17. **Related:** C028. **Source:** LA §4.

Take even k≥4, δ=2^−k, r=2k, p=2^−r, b(x)=(-1)^(x₁x₂+…+x_(k−1)x_k), b'=b−2·1_{x=0}, and A(z)=1_{z=0} on r fresh bits. Boolean children G=1+A(b−1), H=1+A(b'−1) are spliced at a physical selector y.

The exact minority influence is j_y=pδ. Both child influences are at most 5k·2^−2k→0, while B_y/j_y≥(1−δ)(k−2)→∞. In this family log₂(1+κ_min/(2j_y))=k+o(1), where κ(u)=1−max_S uhat(S)².

**Defeats:** B_y≤j_y[C+K·min(I(G),I(H))], and the same with max or sum, for any fixed C,K. More generally any multiplier bounded near zero child influences fails. Does not refute global non-reusing amortization that retains the logarithm or the exact mismatch/KL rebate.

## X004 — Overlapping leaf supports invalidate the mixture identities

**Owners:** F11,F14,F16,F19. **Related:** C029. **Source:** direct two-bit substitution in P, `prop:selector-identities`.

Use cells a_±(z)=(1±z)/2 and balanced leaves g_+=y, g_−=−y. They share the same nonempty Fourier support {y}. The actual selector is F(z,y)=zy, so H(F)=0 and I(F)=2. If one wrongly applies the disjoint-support formula, p_±=1/2, q_± are uniform on the two control subsets, d_±=1/2, and it predicts H=2 and I=3/2.

**Defeats:** the *identities* C010 after removing spectral-support disjointness. Physical selector cells remain disjoint; leaf-variable overlap alone is not the issue. Correct Fourier coefficients must retain cross terms and their signs. This is not a counterexample to an unspecified upper bound with an explicit interference penalty.

Date: 2026-09-30. Full FEI remains OPEN. This is an exact arithmetic endpoint for the collective wringing route, not an approximate stability theorem. Publication priority is unverified.

## 1. A norm-arithmetic lemma

Let a be a nonzero finite vector of dyadic rational amplitudes indexed by a Boolean cube. Suppose its normalized squared law is a product law. Then its squared norm w is dyadic and its reduced odd numerator is congruent to 1 modulo 4.

**Proof.** Remove all coordinates deterministic under the product law. On the remaining r active coordinates every amplitude is nonzero. Since ratios of nonzero amplitudes are rational, the ratio of their magnitudes along coordinate i can be written B_i/A_i, where A_i,B_i are positive coprime integers. Product magnitudes then have the form

    |a_s|=c product_i A_i^(1-s_i) B_i^s_i.

The gcd of the integer products is one: for any prime, choose in each coordinate a factor not divisible by that prime. Thus a finite integer combination of those products equals one. Since all |a_s| are dyadic, the same combination shows that c is dyadic.

Consequently

    w=c² product_i (A_i²+B_i²).

If exactly one of A_i,B_i is odd, the corresponding sum of squares is odd and congruent to 1 modulo 4. If both are odd, its 2-adic valuation is exactly one and its odd part is again 1 modulo 4, since odd squares are 1 modulo 8. The odd part of the square factor c² is also 1 modulo 4. Their product proves the claim. Deterministic or one-atom components are included by an empty product. QED.

### Corollary 1.1

If two nonzero dyadic product-amplitude vectors have squared norms w0,w1 summing to one, then w0=w1=1/2.

Indeed, write wj=uj/2^dj in reduced form. Both dj>=1. Unequal denominators give an odd numerator when brought to the larger denominator, which cannot equal its even denominator. Thus d0=d1=d. Since u0 and u1 are each 1 modulo 4, their sum is 2 modulo 4; hence 2^d has d=1, forcing u0=u1=1.

If a dyadic product-amplitude vector has norm squared 1/2, every nondegenerate marginal of its normalized squared law is 1/2. In the factorization above the odd part of w is one, so each primitive A_i²+B_i² is a power of two. This forces A_i=B_i=1. The number r of active fair coordinates is odd, since c²2^r=1/2 and c is dyadic. At norm squared one the same argument gives an even number of fair coordinates, recovering the prior exact-product endpoint.

## 2. One address bit is paid with constant two

### Theorem 2.1

Let f be Boolean, and suppose that, conditional on either positive-probability value of one spectral coordinate X_j, the squared Fourier law of all other coordinates is a product law. Then

    H(f)<=2 I(f).

In fact the theorem holds for any unit vector of dyadic amplitudes with that conditional-product property; full Booleanity is used only to guarantee dyadic amplitudes and norm one.

**Proof.** The two coefficient slices across j are dyadic product-amplitude vectors. If both have positive mass, Corollary 1.1 makes both masses 1/2. Let r0,r1 be their respective fair-coordinate counts and k0,k1 their fixed-one counts. Their conditional entropies are r0,r1, and conditional expected remaining sizes are k0+r0/2 and k1+r1/2.     H(f)=1+(r0+r1)/2,

\section{Product measures and obstructions to threshold normalization}
\label{biasfull:section}

The generalized FEI inequality of O'Donnell and Tan~\cite{ODT13}
uses a corrected spectral entropy under product measures. Symmetric
threshold gates satisfy this inequality under arbitrary product inputs
in~\cite{Threshold}. The normalization estimates used for those gates
cannot be extended to weighted thresholds without a different argument.
We give explicit counterexamples to both a conditional-total-correlation
estimate and its proposed pivotal-credit replacement. Their genuine
corrected entropy-to-deficit ratios tend to \(2\).

\subsection{Normalization and exact composition}

Let \(Y_i\in\{0,1\}\) be independent with
\(\P(Y_i=1)=q_i\in(0,1)\), and let
\[
 \phi_i(Y_i)=\frac{Y_i-q_i}{\sqrt{q_i(1-q_i)}},
 \qquad v_i=4q_i(1-q_i),\qquad v_S=\prod_{i\in S}v_i.
\]
For a Boolean sign function \(f\), put
\[
 P_f^\mu(S)=\wh f_\mu(S)^2,\qquad
 \I_\mu(f)=\sum_S|S|P_f^\mu(S),\qquad
 V_\mu(f)=\Var_\mu(f),
 \qquad \Delta_\mu(f)=\I_\mu(f)-V_\mu(f).
\]
Its corrected entropy is
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:hplus}
 \HH_\mu^+(f)=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}P_f^\mu(S)
                      \log\frac{v_S}{P_f^\mu(S)}.
\end{equation}
Every summand is nonnegative, since
\[
 |\wh f_\mu(S)|\le\E_\mu|\phi_S|=\sqrt{v_S}.
\]
Write
\[
 h_0(f)=(\E_\mu f)^2\log\frac1{(\E_\mu f)^2}.
\]
If \(\HH_\mu(f)\) denotes the ordinary Shannon entropy of
\(P_f^\mu\), then
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:rawcorrected}
 \HH_\mu^+(f)=\HH_\mu(f)-h_0(f)
                    +\sum_i\I_i^\mu(f)\log v_i.
\end{equation}
In particular, on a uniform cube
\(\HH^+(f)=\HH(f)-h_0(f)\).

We use the following exact form of disjoint composition.

\begin{lemma}[Product-measure composition]\label{biasfull:composition}
Let \(g_i\) be Boolean functions on disjoint variable blocks with product
input laws \(\mu_i\). Let \(p_i=\P(g_i=1)\), \(V_i=4p_i(1-p_i)>0\), and
let \(\nu\) be the product law with these output probabilities.
For \(F=G(g_1,\ldots,g_k)\), set \(a_i=\I_i^\nu(G)/V_i\).
Then
\begin{align}
 \HH_\mu^+(F)&=\HH_\nu^+(G)+\sum_i a_i\HH_{\mu_i}^+(g_i),
       \label{biasfull:comph}\\
 \I_\mu(F)&=\sum_i a_i\I_{\mu_i}(g_i),
       \label{biasfull:compi}\\
 \Delta_\mu(F)&=\Delta_\nu(G)+\sum_i a_i\Delta_{\mu_i}(g_i).
       \label{biasfull:compdelta}
\end{align}
Constant children may be frozen and deleted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a nonempty spectral set, its occupied blocks form a unique set \(A\),
and its nonempty intersections with those blocks are \(T_i\), \(i\in A\).
The coefficient is
\[
 \wh F\Bigl(\bigcup_{i\in A}T_i\Bigr)
   =\wh G_\nu(A)\prod_{i\in A}\frac{\wh g_i(T_i)}{\sqrt{V_i}}.
\]
The squared coefficient is therefore the outer spectral mass times the
conditional nonempty spectral masses of the occupied children.
Taking the logarithm in~\eqref{biasfull:hplus} proves
\eqref{biasfull:comph}; summing degrees proves~\eqref{biasfull:compi}.
Finally \(V_\mu(F)=V_\nu(G)\), so subtraction proves
\eqref{biasfull:compdelta}.
\end{proof}

For an increasing event \(A\), write \(f=2\mathbf1_A-1\),
\(\alpha=\mu(A)\), and let \(\rho_i\) be its pivotal probability.
Then

\begin{proposition}\label{biasfull:tribes}
Let

 A_m=\bigvee_{j=1}^m(X_j\wedge Z_j),\qquad f_m=2\mathbf1_{A_m}-1,

\begin{align}

     &\sim\frac{2\gamma-1}{8}\ln m,\label{biasfull:tribestc}\\
 \frac{V\ln(1/V)-\sum_i\I_i^\mu(f_m)\ln^+(4\rho_i/V)}
           {\I_\mu(f_m)}
     &\sim\frac{2\gamma-1}{2}\ln m,\label{biasfull:tribescredit}\\
 \frac{\HH_\mu^+(f_m)}{\Delta_\mu(f_m)}
     &\longrightarrow2.\label{biasfull:tribesratio}
\end{align}
For all sufficiently large \(m\), every logarithmic credit in
\eqref{biasfull:tribescredit} is zero.

 \I_\mu(f_m)=8mq^2(1-q)r,\qquad V=4\alpha a.
\end{equation}

 \alpha\sim mq^2,\qquad
 \I_\mu(f_m)\sim8\alpha,\qquad
 V\sim4\alpha,\qquad \Delta_\mu(f_m)\sim4\alpha.

 \frac{4\rho_i}{V}=\frac{q}{\alpha(1-q^2)}
       \sim\frac1{mq}\longrightarrow0.

This proves~\eqref{biasfull:tribescredit} and its final assertion.

\[
 d=\frac{q^2(1-q)(1-q^2)^{m-1}}\alpha\sim\frac1m.
\]
Equation~\eqref{biasfull:tcdecomp} gives

 \TC(Q)=\ln(1/\alpha)
       -2m\KL\bigl(\operatorname{Ber}(q+d)\|
                         \operatorname{Ber}(q)\bigr).
\]
The inequality \(\ln t\le t-1\) implies

                         \operatorname{Ber}(q)\bigr)
       \le\frac{d^2}{q(1-q)}.
\]
Thus the subtracted term is \(O((mq)^{-1})=o(1)\).

\subsection{An asymmetric weighted threshold}

\begin{lemma}[Two-branch conditional law]\label{biasfull:mixture}
Fix \(m\ge1\), \(q,\eta\in(0,1)\), and let
\[
 a=q^m,\qquad
 q_0=\frac{(1-\eta)a}{\eta+(1-\eta)a}.
\]
Take independent \(X_0\sim\operatorname{Ber}(q_0)\) and
\(X_1,\ldots,X_m\sim\operatorname{Ber}(q)\), and set
\[
 A=\left\{mX_0+\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\ge m\right\},
 \qquad f=2\mathbf1_A-1,\qquad \alpha=\P(A).
\]
Then
\begin{align}
 \alpha&=\frac{a}{\eta+(1-\eta)a},\label{biasfull:alphamix}\\
 \TC(\mu(\cdot\mid A))
   &=m\{h(\eta+(1-\eta)q)-(1-\eta)h(q)\},
       \label{biasfull:tcmix}\\
 \frac{\I_\mu(f)}{4\alpha}
   &=1-\eta-q_0+m\eta(1-q).
       \label{biasfull:Imix}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The event is the disjoint union of \(\{X_0=1\}\) and
\(\{X_0=0,X_1=\cdots=X_m=1\}\).
Its probability gives~\eqref{biasfull:alphamix}.
Under the conditional law \(Q=\mu(\cdot\mid A)\),
\(\P_Q(X_0=0)=\eta\). Given \(X_0=1\), the light variables are
independent Bernoulli \(q\); given \(X_0=0\), they are all one.
Hence their marginal means are
\[
 \E_QX_0=1-\eta,\qquad
 \E_QX_j=\eta+(1-\eta)q\quad(1\le j\le m).
\]
The joint entropy is \(h(\eta)+(1-\eta)mh(q)\), while the sum of
marginal entropies is \(h(\eta)+mh(\eta+(1-\eta)q)\).
This proves~\eqref{biasfull:tcmix}.
Summing~\eqref{biasfull:pivotal} proves~\eqref{biasfull:Imix}.
\end{proof}

\end{align}
Here \(a=m^{-2m}\), \(q_0=O(ma)\), and \(\alpha=O(ma)\).
All terms containing \(aL\), \(q_0L\), or \(\alpha L\) tend to zero.
It follows that
\[
 D_m=\frac Lm-(1-q)\ln m-\ln4+o(1)
      =\ln m-\ln4+O((\ln m)/m).
\]
Dividing by~\eqref{biasfull:weightedinfluence} proves
\eqref{biasfull:weightedcredit}.

To prove~\eqref{biasfull:weightedratio}, let \(g\) be the sign version
of the conjunction of the \(m\) light variables. For \(S\ne\varnothing\),
\[
 P_g^\mu(S)=4a^2\left(\frac{1-q}{q}\right)^{|S|}.
\]
Summing its degree and its corrected entropy gives
\begin{align}
 \I_\mu(g)&=4ma(1-q),\notag\\
 \Delta_\mu(g)&=4a[m(1-q)-1+a],\notag\\
 \HH_\mu^+(g)&=2\Delta_\mu(g)
                 +8ma(q-a)\log(1/q).
                    \label{biasfull:andmany}
\end{align}
Consequently \(\Delta_\mu(g)\sim4ma\) and
\(\HH_\mu^+(g)\sim8ma\).
The outer OR gate has input probabilities \(q_0\) and \(a\).
Its deficit is
\[
 \Delta_o=4q_0a(1-q_0)(1-a)=O(ma^2).
\]
For an OR gate with probabilities \(x,y\), its corrected entropy is
\begin{align*}
 &8x(1-x)(1-y)^2\log\frac1{1-y}\\
 &\quad+8y(1-y)(1-x)^2\log\frac1{1-x}
       +8xy(1-x)(1-y).
\end{align*}
The inequality \(-\ln(1-u)\le u/(1-u)\) bounds this by
\((2+4/\ln2)\,4xy(1-x)(1-y)\).
Thus \(\HH_o^+=O(ma^2)\).
The heavy child is a dictator and has zero corrected entropy and
zero deficit. The light-block multiplier in
Lemma~\ref{biasfull:composition} is \(1-q_0\), so
\[
 \Delta_\mu(f)=\Delta_o+(1-q_0)\Delta_\mu(g)\sim4ma,\qquad
 \HH_\mu^+(f)=\HH_o^++(1-q_0)\HH_\mu^+(g)\sim8ma.
\]
This proves~\eqref{biasfull:weightedratio}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{biasfull:creditremark}
Equation~\eqref{biasfull:weightedcredit} refutes precisely an estimate
with coefficient one on the pivotal credits and an additional
\(O(\I_\mu(f))\) term. It does not refute an inequality with a larger
multiplier on the credits. Replacing the fixed factor \(4\) inside
all logarithms by another fixed positive factor changes the residual
by only a bounded multiple of \(\I_\mu(f)\) for this family, and
therefore does not repair the coefficient-one estimate.
Neither~\eqref{biasfull:weightedTC} nor~\eqref{biasfull:weightedcredit}
is a counterexample to generalized FEI.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A local total-correlation obstruction]
\label{biasfull:localTC}
There is no universal \(K\) such that every increasing weighted
threshold event \(A\) has a coordinate \(j\) satisfying
\[
 \alpha\sum_{k\ne j} I_Q(X_j;X_k)\le K\I_j^\mu(2\mathbf1_A-1),
 \qquad Q=\mu(\cdot\mid A).
\]
Here the mutual informations are measured in nats.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In Lemma~\ref{biasfull:mixture}, take \(\eta=1/2\) and let
\(q\downarrow0\) with \(m\) fixed.
The conditional law converges to the equal mixture of
\((1,0,\ldots,0)\) and \((0,1,\ldots,1)\).
Hence every pairwise mutual information tends to \(\ln2\),
while~\eqref{biasfull:pivotal} gives
\(\I_j^\mu(f)/\alpha\to2\) for every \(j\).
Every proposed coordinate ratio therefore tends to \(m\ln2/2\).
Letting \(m\) increase proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The exact recursion behind this failed proposal is
\[
 \TC(Q)=\E_{X_j}\TC(Q_{-j}\mid X_j)
                  +\sum_{k\ne j}I_Q(X_j;X_k).
\]
It follows by expanding the marginal and joint entropies.
The obstruction concerns the proposed payment of this recursion,
not the identity itself.

\subsection{What a uniform encoding of biased inputs transfers}

Let \(p_i\) be dyadic probabilities and choose arbitrary uniform-input
Boolean encoders \(g_i\) with \(\P(g_i=1)=p_i\), on disjoint blocks.
Let \(V_i=4p_i(1-p_i)\).
If ordinary uniform FEI is assumed with a constant \(C\), then
Lemma~\ref{biasfull:composition} applied to \(F=G(g_1,\ldots,g_k)\)
gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:encoding}
 \HH_\nu^+(G)+h_0(F)
   \le\sum_i\I_i^\nu(G)R_C(g_i),\qquad
 R_C(g)=\frac{C\I(g)-\HH^+(g)}{\Var(g)}.
\end{equation}
The relevant encoder quantity is its entropy-influence slack per
unit variance, rather than its influence alone.

\begin{proposition}[Encoder slack]\label{biasfull:encoderslack}
For the sign version \(g\) of the uniform conjunction on \(k\) bits,
with \(p=2^{-k}\),
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:andencoder}
 R_C(g)=2+\left(\frac{C}{2(1-p)}-2\right)\log\frac1p.
\end{equation}
More generally, if uniform FEI holds with \(C_0\), then for every
\(C>C_0\) and every uniform Boolean encoder of probability
\(p\in(0,1/2]\),
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:encodergap}
 R_C(g)\ge\frac{C-C_0}{2(1-p)}\log\frac1p.
\end{equation}
Thus no choice of encoders makes the coefficients in
\eqref{biasfull:encoding} uniformly bounded when the constant used
has a fixed positive slack over a valid FEI constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the conjunction, the nonempty squared coefficients are all
\(4p^2\). Thus
\[
 \I(g)=2kp,\qquad V=4p(1-p),\qquad
 \HH^+(g)=V(2k-2),
\]
which proves~\eqref{biasfull:andencoder}.
For an arbitrary encoder,
\[
 C\I(g)-\HH^+(g)
   =C\I(g)-\HH(g)+h_0(g)\ge(C-C_0)\I(g).
\]
The uniform edge-isoperimetric inequality gives
\(\I(g)\ge2p\log(1/p)\), proving~\eqref{biasfull:encodergap}.

For completeness, this form of edge isoperimetry follows by induction.
If a coordinate's two sections have probabilities \(p_0\le p_1\),
their other influences average, and the coordinate influence is at
least \(p_1-p_0\). Writing \(p_0=p(1-t)\), \(p_1=p(1+t)\), the
induction reduces to
\[
 (1-t)\log(1-t)+(1+t)\log(1+t)\le2t,\qquad0\le t\le1.
\]
The left side is convex, with endpoint values \(0,2\), and is
therefore below the chord \(2t\).
\end{proof}

This proposition is a limitation of the inference
\eqref{biasfull:encoding}. It does not rule out a different reduction,
additional structural information about the outer gate, or the
generalized FEI conjecture itself.

\subsection{A sharp loss in a monotone dual-rail embedding}

The following calculation makes explicit the product-measure cost of the monotone encoding considered in~\cite{BrainMonotone}.

Every Boolean function on \(\{0,1\}^n\) is the restriction of a
monotone function to the antichain
\[
 x\longmapsto(x_1,1-x_1,\ldots,x_n,1-x_n).
\]
For example, take the upward closure of the encoded inputs on
which the function equals one. The following bound quantifies the
loss when the ambient law is required to be a product law.

\begin{proposition}\label{biasfull:dualrail}
Let \(Y,Z\) be independent Bernoulli bits with
\[
 \P(Y=1\mid Y\ne Z)=p,\qquad t=\P(Y\ne Z).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:railone}
 t\le\frac1{1+2\sqrt{p(1-p)}}.
\end{equation}
The bound is sharp. For independent pairs encoding independent
bits of probabilities \(p_1,\ldots,p_n\), the event \(E\) that every
pair is valid satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:railall}
 \P(E)\le\prod_{i=1}^n
       \bigl(1+2\sqrt{p_i(1-p_i)}\bigr)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
In particular, balanced decoding forces \(\P(E)\le2^{-n}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The valid cell probabilities are \(pt\) and \((1-p)t\).
If the invalid cell probabilities are \(u,v\), independence implies
\[
 uv=p(1-p)t^2,\qquad u+v=1-t.
\]
Thus \((1-t)^2\ge4p(1-p)t^2\), proving~\eqref{biasfull:railone}.
Equality is attained by
\[
 \P(Y=1)=\frac{\sqrt p}{\sqrt p+\sqrt{1-p}},
 \qquad \P(Z=1)=1-\P(Y=1).
\]
Independent pairs give~\eqref{biasfull:railall} by multiplication.
\end{proof}

Conditioning on all valid pairs preserves independence of the decoded
bits, but its relative-entropy cost is
\[
 \KL(\mu(\cdot\mid E)\|\mu)=\ln\frac1{\mu(E)}
 \ge\sum_i\ln\bigl(1+2\sqrt{p_i(1-p_i)}\bigr).
\]
This is at least \(n\ln2\) for balanced decoding. The proposition
concerns this embedding; it asserts no obstruction to all conceivable
monotone embeddings.

\subsection{The gate-potential formulation on its finite-cube domain}

Let \(\mathcal Q_2\) be the dyadic probabilities in \([0,1]\).
The following formulation of the gate potential in~\cite{BrainGate}
uses only probabilities realized by finite uniform Boolean functions.
Put \(V_p=4p(1-p)\), and let \(h_2\) denote binary entropy in bits.

\begin{proposition}\label{biasfull:gatepotential}
Uniform FEI holds with constant \(C\) if and only if there is a
symmetric function \(\Gamma:\mathcal Q_2\to[0,\infty)\), with
\(\Gamma(1/2)\le C\), such that every finite Boolean gate \(G\)
and every vector of interior dyadic input probabilities satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:gatecondition}
 \Gamma(p_{\rm out})+\HH_\nu(G)
 \le\sum_i\I_i^\nu(G)
         \frac{\Gamma(p_i)+h_2(V_{p_i})}{V_{p_i}}.
\end{equation}
Here \(p_{\rm out}=\P_\nu(G=1)\), which is dyadic. Deterministic
inputs are frozen and deleted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For sufficiency, apply~\eqref{biasfull:gatecondition} to an arbitrary
Boolean function regarded as one gate with all input probabilities
\(1/2\). Since \(V_{1/2}=1\) and \(h_2(1)=0\),
\[
 \HH(G)+\Gamma(p_{\rm out})
      \le\Gamma(1/2)\I(G)\le C\I(G).
\]

Conversely, assume uniform FEI with \(C\) and define
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:gammaslack}
 \Gamma_C(p)=\inf_{\substack{g\ {\rm finite\ uniform\ Boolean}\\
                              \P(g=1)=p}}
                  \{C\I(g)-\HH(g)\}.
\end{equation}
The admissible set is nonempty for dyadic \(p\).
FEI makes the infimum nonnegative; a single example makes it finite.
Output negation gives symmetry, constants give the endpoint values
zero, and a dictator gives \(\Gamma_C(1/2)\le C\).

Choose disjoint child functions of the prescribed probabilities
whose slacks approach the infima. Their raw entropy composition is
\[
 \HH(F)=\HH_\nu(G)+
    \sum_i\frac{\I_i^\nu(G)}{V_{p_i}}
                  [\HH(g_i)-h_2(V_{p_i})].
\]
This follows either from the same spectral factorization as
Lemma~\ref{biasfull:composition}, or by conditioning each child
spectrum on being nonempty. Consequently
\[
 C\I(F)-\HH(F)=
 \sum_i\frac{\I_i^\nu(G)}{V_{p_i}}
      [C\I(g_i)-\HH(g_i)+h_2(V_{p_i})]-\HH_\nu(G).
\]
The left side is at least \(\Gamma_C(p_{\rm out})\).
Letting the finitely many child slacks tend to their infima proves
\eqref{biasfull:gatecondition}.
\end{proof}

No finite uniform Boolean function has a nondyadic output probability.
Thus the infimum in~\eqref{biasfull:gammaslack} does not directly
define an all-real-bias potential; that extension would need an
additional argument. The dyadic formulation already suffices for
equivalence to FEI on all finite uniform cubes.

For comparison with the encoder bound,
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:encoderpotential}
 \inf_{\P(g=1)=p}R_C(g)
   =\frac{\Gamma_C(p)+h_0(p)}{V_p},
 \qquad
 h_0(p)=(1-V_p)\log\frac1{1-V_p}.
\end{equation}
This identity follows from \(\HH^+(g)=\HH(g)-h_0(p)\).
If FEI holds with \(C_1<C_2\), edge isoperimetry also gives
\[
 \Gamma_{C_2}(p)\ge\Gamma_{C_1}(p)
                 +2(C_2-C_1)p\log(1/p),\qquad p\le\tfrac12.
\]

Finally, balanced gates retain the complete optimal-constant question:
\begin{equation}\label{biasfull:balancedoptimal}
 C_{\rm FEI}
  =\sup_{\substack{g\ {\rm balanced}\\\I(g)>1}}
                       \frac{\HH(g)}{\I(g)-1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, balanced self-composition of a gate with influence \(a>1\)
and entropy \(h\) gives ratios tending to \(h/(a-1)\).
Conversely, for any nonconstant \(f\), the balanced function
\(g=z\prod_{j=1}^r f(x^{(j)})\), with a fresh uniform sign \(z\),
satisfies \(\HH(g)=r\HH(f)\) and
\(\I(g)-1=r\I(f)\). A balanced function of influence one is a
signed dictator and has zero entropy.
In particular, whenever FEI holds with \(C\),
\(\Gamma_C(1/2)=C\).

The quantifier over every gate in~\eqref{biasfull:gatecondition}
already contains unrestricted FEI at uniform input. A proof for a
restricted family of gates supplies a composition theorem for that
family; it does not remove this universal quantifier.

## exact target, quantifiers, hypotheses and units

### Balanced coefficient identity

Write g_i=sum_{U subset B_i} c_i(U) chi_U and h=sum_{T subset [k]} a(T) chi_T. Balance means c_i(empty)=0. For a physical Fourier set S, put S_i=S intersect B_i and T(S)={i:S_i is nonempty}. Expanding h after substitution, a term indexed by T can contribute to S only if T=T(S): unused blocks contribute the constant 1, and every used block has a nonempty set. Disjoint blocks make the union unique. Hence, including S=empty,

    Fhat(S) = a(T(S)) product_{i in T(S)} c_i(S_i).                  (1)

Parseval gives sum_{U nonempty} c_i(U)^2=1. Thus the spectral law is exactly the following experiment: sample T with probability a(T)^2; independently for each i in T sample a nonempty U_i with probability c_i(U_i)^2; output S=union U_i. Conversely S recovers T and every U_i. The experiment is bijective on positive-probability outcomes, so its entropy is H(h)+sum_i P(i in T)H(g_i). Since P(i in T)=Inf_i(h), this proves the entropy formula, with no latent-label correction or omitted empty atom. Its expected size is sum_i Inf_i(h)I(g_i). The probability a given x in B_i is included is Inf_i(h)Inf_x(g_i). Finally (1) at empty proves E F=E h and therefore the variance formula. Zero coefficients are simply omitted; 0 log(1/0)=0.

### Arbitrary means: reexpanded outer gate

Let m_i=E g_i and v_i=1-m_i^2. First absorb all deterministic children (v_i=0) into h by fixing those output coordinates to m_i. This is exact because a Boolean uniform-cube function of mean +/-1 is constant at every input. Reindex the remaining children; now v_i>0. Their independent output law is mu, with P(Y_i=+1)=(1+m_i)/2. In its orthonormal basis psi_i(y)=(y_i-m_i)/sqrt(v_i), write

    h(y)=sum_T a_mu(T) product_{i in T} psi_i(y).

Define P_mu(T)=a_mu(T)^2, w_i=sum_{T containing i} P_mu(T), H_mu(h)=H(P_mu), and I_mu(h)=sum_T |T|P_mu(T). These biased spectral influences w_i are not the ordinary unweighted pivotal probabilities. Indeed w_i=v_i E_mu[(D_i h)^2], where D_i h=(h(y_i=+1)-h(y_i=-1))/2 on the other outputs.

On the physical block,

    (g_i-m_i)/sqrt(v_i)=sum_{U nonempty} c_i(U)/sqrt(v_i) chi_U.

This again has zero empty coefficient and squared mass one. The same unique union argument gives

    Fhat(S)=a_mu(T(S)) product_{i in T(S)} c_i(S_i)/sqrt(v_i).       (2)

The child's nonempty spectral law is q_i(U)=c_i(U)^2/v_i. Its entropy K_i satisfies

    H(g_i)=h2(v_i)+v_i K_i,                                        (3)

because the empty atom has mass m_i^2=1-v_i. The exact full identities are consequently

    E F = E_mu h = a_mu(empty),       Var F=Var_mu h,
    H(F)=H_mu(h)+sum_i (w_i/v_i)[H(g_i)-h2(v_i)],                   (4)
    I(F)=sum_i (w_i/v_i) I(g_i),
    Inf_x(F)=(w_i/v_i)Inf_x(g_i), x in B_i.                        (5)

Equations (2)-(5) are valid at every finite dimension, for arbitrary nonconstant child means. All entropy terms are finite; no limiting assertion is needed. If every child was deterministic, F is constant and all entropies and influences are zero. Outer irrelevant coordinates have w_i=0 and require no child contribution. Balanced children give v_i=1, h2(v_i)=0 and the uniform outer law, recovering the first formulas.

For comparison with Proposition 7.1, the strong-entropy convention must be distinguished from conditional nonempty entropy. The convention that actually composes is Eplus(g)=sum_{U nonempty} c(U)^2 log2(1/c(U)^2) on uniform physical inputs, and Eplus_mu(h)=sum_{T nonempty} P_mu(T)log2(product v_i/P_mu(T)) on outputs. Then exactly

    Eplus(F)=Eplus_mu(h)+sum_i (w_i/v_i) Eplus(g_i).                (6)

It follows immediately from (2), with no Var(F) numerator. The conditional nonempty entropy in (3), namely H(g)-h2(Var(g)), is a different quantity. This run's main identities (1)-(5) deliberately use full H or conditional K, so do not depend on a strong-entropy naming convention. Define deficit delta=I-Var. Since sum_i w_i=I_mu(h), and Var(F)=Var_mu(h),

### Physical restrictions: what survives, and what changes

Fix actual physical coordinates W to a deterministic assignment rho. In each block replace g_i by g_i^rho=g_i restricted to W intersect B_i. Remaining inputs retain the independent uniform law and disjoint supports. Therefore (2)-(5) hold with the **new** means m_i^rho, variances v_i^rho, absorbed deterministic children, and the Fourier expansion of the reduced outer gate under the **new** output product law. They do not generally hold with old a_mu or old w_i. The balanced formulas are available at a reached branch when every remaining nonconstant child is balanced after absorption of constants. If only one child is restricted, all untouched initially balanced children remain balanced, but the changed child must be checked. Balance of that child is sufficient; it is not claimed logically necessary for accidental numerical equality in a special gate.

For a single initially balanced child j, write the uniform outer polynomial as h=A(y_-j)+y_j B(y_-j). Let a_T=Ahat(T), b_T=Bhat(T), and let the changed child's mean be m, variance v=1-m^2>0. Other children stay balanced. Recenter:

    h=A+mB+sqrt(v) psi_j B.

The new outer coefficients are a_T+m b_T on T not containing j, and sqrt(v)b_T on T union {j}. If v=0, fix outer y_j=m, giving the actual reduced polynomial A+mB. Do not divide by v. If m=0, the original outer coefficients remain valid. ### Exact witness against retaining the old balanced formulas

Let h(y,z)=AND_2(y,z) in sign notation: +1 only when both inputs are +1. Its uniform spectrum has four coefficients of magnitude 1/2, so E h=-1/2, H(h)=2, and Inf_y(h)=Inf_z(h)=1/2. Let the first initially balanced child be majority on three signs (x,a,b), and the second be a fresh dictator z. Physically fixing x=+1 changes the first child to OR_2(a,b), with mean 1/2, variance 3/4, H=2 and I=1. The restricted F=AND_2(OR_2(a,b),z) has constant coefficient -1/4, z coefficient 3/4, and the other six coefficients of magnitude 1/4. Its spectrum is seven atoms 1/16 and one atom 9/16. Hence

    E F=-1/4,     I(F)=5/4,
    H(F)=4-(9/16)log2(9)=4-(9/8)log2(3).

Retaining old balanced formulas predicts E F=-1/2, I(F)=1, H(F)=3. All three predictions are false. For the entropy comparison, 3 > H(F) follows exactly from 3^9>2^8, with no floating point. The new output-biased gate weights are 1/16, 3/16, 9/16, 3/16 on empty, {y}, {z}, {y,z}. Thus w_y=3/8 and w_z=3/4, and (5) gives (3/8)/(3/4)+(3/4)=5/4. This is an actual Boolean physical restriction of an initially balanced substitution, not an arbitrary spectral law.

### Scope of consequences
