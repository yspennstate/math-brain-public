\maketitle
All entropy is in bits. Physical binary controls $x\in\F^n$ and sign
data $y\in\{-1,1\}^{2^a}$ are independent and uniform. The binary
Walsh convention is $\chi_u(x)=(-1)^{u\cdot x}$.

\begin{theorem}[Rank and fiber-phase ledger]
Let $M:\F^n\to\F^a$ be linear and let
\[
 q(x)=q_0+\sum_i\ell_i x_i+\sum_{i<j}Q_{ij}x_i x_j
\]
be a quadratic polynomial over $\F$. Write $Q$ also for its symmetric,
zero-diagonal polar matrix. Define the Boolean function
\[
 F_{M,q}(x,y)=(-1)^{q(x)}y_{Mx}.
\]
Let $r=\rk M$, $K=\ker M$, and $k=\rk(Q|_{K\times K})$.
Let $c$ count nonzero columns of $M$, let $b$ count indices $i$ whose
$M$ column is zero and whose $Q$ row is nonzero, and let $d$ count
indices whose $M$ column and $Q$ row are zero but $\ell_i=1$.
Then the squared Fourier spectrum has exactly $2^{2r+k}$ nonzero
atoms, all of mass $2^{-2r-k}$, and
\[
 H(F_{M,q})=2r+k,\qquad \I(F_{M,q})=1+\frac{c+b}{2}+d.
\]
Every active-address control has influence $1/2$. Every inactive-address
control has influence $1/2$ if its polar row is nonzero, and otherwise
has influence $\ell_i$. Each reachable data bit has influence $2^{-r}$;
unreachable data bits have influence zero. In particular,
\[
 H(F_{M,q})\le4\bigl(\I(F_{M,q})-1-d\bigr)
 \le4\bigl(\I(F_{M,q})-1\bigr).
\]
No selector-freshness hypothesis is imposed on a program computing $Mx$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Quadratic Walsh law]
A quadratic sign on a $D$-dimensional binary vector space whose polar
form has rank $k$ has $2^k$ nonzero Walsh coefficients, each of squared
magnitude $2^{-k}$. The rank $k$ is even.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $g(t)=(-1)^{q(t)}$, its autocorrelation at $h$ is
\[
 \mathbb E_t g(t)g(t+h)
 =(-1)^{q(h)+q(0)}\mathbb E_t(-1)^{B(t,h)}.
\]
This vanishes off the radical $R$ of the polar form. On $R$,
$q(h)+q(0)$ is linear. Consequently
\[
 |\widehat g(\lambda)|^2
 =2^{-D}\sum_{h\in R}(-1)^{q(h)+q(0)+\lambda(h)}
\]
is either zero or $|R|/2^D=2^{-k}$. Parseval gives the support count.
An alternating bilinear form has even rank: if nonzero, choose a pair
with pairing one, split off their nondegenerate two-dimensional span,
and repeat on its orthogonal complement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the theorem]
For a reachable $z$, choose $x_z$ with $Mx_z=z$ and put
$a_z(x)=\mathbf1_{Mx=z}(-1)^{q(x)}$. The function $q(x_z+t)$ on $K$
has the same polar form $Q|_K$ for every $z$; translating affects only
linear and constant terms. For an ambient frequency $u$,
\[
 \widehat a_z(u)=2^{-r}(-1)^{u\cdot x_z}
 \mathbb E_{t\in K}(-1)^{q(x_z+t)+u\cdot t}.
\]
The restriction map $(\F^n)^*\to K^*$ is onto with fibers of size
$2^r$. The lemma gives $2^{r+k}$ nonzero ambient coefficients per
reachable label, each of squared magnitude $2^{-2r-k}$.
The only data frequencies of $F$ are singletons $\{y_z\}$, with
coefficient $\widehat a_z(u)$. Different labels are distinct physical
data coordinates, so there are no cross-label coefficient collisions.
There are $2^r$ reachable labels. This proves the exact flat law and
the entropy formula, including $r=0$, $k=0$, and constant $q$.

Flipping $x_i$ changes $Mx$ by its $i$th column. If this column is
nonzero, the old and new data bits are distinct independent signs;
the output disagreement probability is $1/2$, regardless of $q$.
If it is zero, disagreement is exactly the event
\[
 q(x+e_i)+q(x)=\ell_i+\sum_jQ_{ij}x_j=1.
\]
A nonconstant linear polynomial is balanced; a constant one equals
$\ell_i$. Flipping $y_z$ is pivotal exactly on $Mx=z$, whose probability
is $2^{-r}$ for reachable $z$. Their total influence is one.

For the bound, let $V$ be the coordinate span of the nonzero $M$ columns
and the nonzero polar rows outside those columns. Its dimension is
$c+b$ and $M|_V$ has rank $r$. On all other coordinate directions
both $M$ and $Q$ vanish. Thus the restricted polar form on $K$ has rank
at most $\dim(K\cap V)=c+b-r$. Therefore
\[
 2r+k\le r+c+b\le2(c+b)=4(\I(F)-1-d),
\]
where $r\le c$. Pure linear phase directions counted by $d$ cost
influence but no entropy. This proof uses no FEI assumption.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact recursive addressing program.}
Fix a state width $a$, and initialize $s_0(x)=0$, $M_0=0$, $q_0=0$.
At stage $t$ allow arbitrary fixed binary matrices $A_t\in\F^{a\times a}$,
$B_t\in\F^{a\times n}$, and a fixed quadratic phase increment $p_t$:
\[
 s_t=A_ts_{t-1}+B_tx,\quad M_t=A_tM_{t-1}+B_t,\quad
 q_t=q_{t-1}+p_t,\quad F_t=(-1)^{q_t(x)}y_{s_t(x)}.
\]
The same physical control may occur at arbitrarily many stages.
Let $r_t,k_t,c_t,b_t,d_t$ be the theorem's parameters for $(M_t,q_t)$.
For every $t$ the exact recurrence is
\[
 H_t-H_{t-1}=2(r_t-r_{t-1})+(k_t-k_{t-1}),\quad
 \I_t-\I_{t-1}=\tfrac12(c_t+b_t-c_{t-1}-b_{t-1})+d_t-d_{t-1}.
\]
The changing kernel is recomputed; its quadratic rank is not assumed
additive. Influence increments can be negative. These are identities
for the final Boolean functions, not a conserved ancestral budget for
arbitrary stopping trees.

\paragraph{A cancellation tower.}
Take $n=a=m$, $A_t$ the identity, and at stage $t$ add $x_j$ to state
coordinate $j$, for $j=1+((t-1)\bmod m)$. Set every phase increment
to zero. On the first cycle $r_t=c_t=t$; on the second
$r_t=c_t=2m-t$. The program can be iterated indefinitely. At every
even complete cycle $F_t=y_0$, $H_t=0$, and $\I_t=1$. A ledger charging
fresh selectors at every syntactic level incorrectly keeps increasing.

\paragraph{Nontrivial quadratic fibers.}
Let $n=2m$, $q=\sum_{j=1}^m x_{2j-1}x_{2j}$. Address the first $h$
complete pairs, with state width $2h$, for $0\le h\le m$. Then
$r=c=2h$, $k=2(m-h)$, $b=2(m-h)$, $d=0$, so
\[
 H=2m+2h,\qquad \I=1+m.
\]
For $h<m$ there is genuine quadratic Fourier entropy inside address
fibers. This is not an application of the unsigned affine-cell formula.
The state map and phase can each be computed by unbounded-depth reuse
programs of the displayed type; their final ranks completely specify
the ledger.

\paragraph{Sharing labels and coefficient collisions.}
The final data singleton identifies the terminal label $J\in\operatorname{im}M$,
which is uniform and costs $r$ bits once. Conditional on that label,
the control-frequency support is uniform on a set of size $2^{r+k}$.
Choose fixed bases to encode it by $k$ bits for an allowed restriction
in $K^*$ and $r$ bits for its ambient lift. Total code length is exactly
$2r+k$. This is a normalized law, not a partial cost with an omitted
reference normalizer. The terminal label is shared by the entire Fourier
set and all program levels. The uniform physical transcript
$(s_1,\ldots,s_t)$ costs $R_t=\rk [M_1;\ldots;M_t]$ bits; given the
terminal label it retains $R_t-r_t$ bits. Terminal addressing discards
this history, and coherent Fourier cancellation must be performed
before squaring. The signed fiber calculation performs that cancellation.
Independent stage-label costs are not justified.

\paragraph{General fiber identity and physical-budget comparison.}
For any Boolean sign $g(x)$ in place of $(-1)^{q(x)}$, restrict it to
each reachable affine fiber $x_z+K$ and call the resulting sign on $K$
$g_z$. Choose any fixed linear coordinates on $K$ to define its Walsh
entropy; this entropy is invariant under changing those linear coordinates,
since the frequency labels are permuted. The same ambient-frequency
restriction calculation gives the exact identity
\[
 H(g(x)y_{Mx})=2r+2^{-r}\sum_{z\in\operatorname{im}M}H(g_z).
\]
For $M e_i=0$, the physical control influence is $\Pr[g(x)\ne g(x+e_i)]$;
for $M e_i\ne0$ it is $1/2$. Consequently
\[
 \I(g(x)y_{Mx})=1+\frac c2+
 \sum_{i:M e_i=0}\Pr[g(x)\ne g(x+e_i)].
\]
The ordinary coordinate influence of $g_z$ in an arbitrary kernel basis
is not this physical sum. An oblique basis change can greatly increase
or decrease influence and is not a free FEI reduction. For quadratic
phases the preceding rank argument pays exactly the residual
$H(g_z)=k$ without making that substitution.

There is also a valid, generally weaker physical reorganization. Query
all $c$ controls with nonzero $M$ column first. Their neighboring branches
have distinct data labels and hence are orthogonal, irrespective of the
phase. The remaining phase restrictions are ordinary Boolean signs on
the inactive physical controls. If those signs satisfy FEI with constant
$C$, baseline Corollary 8.7 pays the whole function with $\max\{4,C\}$.
For quadratic phase restrictions, the lemma gives $H\le2\I$ directly:
their polar rank is at most the number of nonzero polar rows, each
with influence $1/2$, and pure linear directions only increase influence.
Thus the present constant-four closure can also be recovered from the
baseline plus this elementary quadratic-leaf bound. The new artifact is
the exact rank ledger and reuse accounting, not a new universal closure
mechanism. The sharper $2r+k$ ledger records cancellation hidden by the
physical reorganization's $2c$ bound.

\paragraph{Scope and remaining universal gap.}
When $q$ is affine, $k=0$, and the entropy identity is an application
of the established affine-cell baseline (with shifted control spectra).
The unsigned tower alone is not a new FEI class. Quadratic fibers add
a closed exact ledger and explicit influence payment, not a novelty
claim or a universal FEI theorem. An arbitrary phase on a fiber loses
the flat quadratic law. If $M=0$ and $q$ is replaced by an arbitrary
Boolean phase $g(x)$, then $F=g(x)y_0$, $H(F)=H(g)$ and
$\I(F)=\I(g)+1$. A strong bound for this unrestricted class is full FEI;
an ordinary bound also implies full FEI by tensor amplification. Thus
unrestricted phase entropy cannot be declared paid by label compression.
The first unsupported universal line would be a constant bound on
$2^{-r}\sum_zH(g_z)$ in the displayed physical budget, for arbitrary
$g$ and $M$. At $M=0$ that line is exactly FEI. The next universal
obligation is to bound the complete conditional fiber spectrum for
nonquadratic, genuinely Boolean phases without spending previously
charged physical influence a second time.
