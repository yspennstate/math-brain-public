1: \section{Conditioned witness forests}\label{sec:forests}
2: 
3: We refine the queue reference of~\cite{Threshold} by retaining whole-forest costs, conditioning on nonempty roots, and subtracting the entropy of interchangeable witnesses. The forest bound itself uses ordered covers of gate supports; the subsequent fractional estimate uses the signed noise bounds for regular gates from~\cite{Threshold}.
4: 
5: \subsection{Ordered covers and a reference law}
6: 
7: Write $f=h_1\vee\cdots\vee h_N$, with a union true when at least one component equals $1$. Remove constant-false gates. A constant-true gate makes $f$ constant, so suppose every remaining gate is nonconstant. Let $R_l$ be its relevant support. Cover $R_l$ by $M_l\ge1$ ordered subsets $R_{l,t}\subseteq R_l$. For $i\in R_{l,t}$ let $r_{l,t}(i)$ be its rank and let $U_i^{l,t}$ be its strict predecessors. Put
8: \[
9:  \gamma=\frac65,\quad p_l=\P(h_l=1),\quad
10:  \pi_l=\min(p_l,1/2),\quad b_l=\log(1/\pi_l).
11: \]
12: Choose positive weights satisfying the fractional incidence constraints
13: \begin{equation}\label{eq:forestpacking}
14:  \sum_{l:j\in R_l}\lambda_l\le1\qquad(j\in[n]),
15: \end{equation}
16: and write
17: \begin{equation}\label{eq:forestrootconstants}
18:  L_l=\log(1/\lambda_l),\qquad
19:  \Lambda=\sum_l\log(1+\lambda_l\pi_l),\qquad
20:  Z=1-2^{-\Lambda}.
21: \end{equation}
22: Each $\lambda_l\le1$. For $s\ge1$ and $i\in R_l$, define
23: \[
24:  \theta_s^l(i)=\max_{t:i\in R_{l,t}}
25:        \min\{1,(s/r_{l,t}(i))^\gamma\},\qquad
26:  Z_s^l=e_s\bigl((\theta_s^l(i))_{i\in R_l}\bigr),
27: \]
28: where $e_s$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $s$.
29: 
30: \begin{lemma}[Subset normalizer]\label{lem:forestnormalizer}
31: For $1\le s\le|R_l|$,
32: \[
33:  \sum_{i\in R_l}\theta_s^l(i)\le6M_ls,
34:  \qquad Z_s^l\le(6eM_l)^s.
35: \]
36: \end{lemma}
37: \begin{proof}
38: For one ordered subset, the sum of its weights is at most
39: \[
40:  s+s^\gamma\int_s^\infty t^{-\gamma}\,dt
41:  =\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}s=6s.
42: \]
43: Bound the maximum defining $\theta$ by the sum over its $M_l$ orders. Finally $e_s(w)\le(\sum_iw_i)^s/s!$ and $s!\ge(s/e)^s$, proving the claim.
44: \end{proof}
45: 
46: Define the following random queue.
47: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
48: \item Independently activate gate $l$ as a root with probability $\lambda_l\pi_l/(1+\lambda_l\pi_l)$, and queue the roots in a fixed order.
49: \item At each visit to $l$, choose $s\ge1$ with probability $1/[s(s+1)]$. An invalid size fails. Otherwise choose an $s$-subset $B\subseteq R_l$ with probability $\prod_{i\in B}\theta_s^l(i)/Z_s^l$.
50: \item Choose $c\ge0$ with probability $1/[(c+1)(c+2)]$, then choose a multiset of $c$ elements of $B$ uniformly. Order its occurrences deterministically. For each occurrence of $j$, select gate $l'$ with probability $\lambda_{l'}$ whenever $j\in R_{l'}$, and append it to the queue. The remaining probability is failure; it is nonnegative by~\eqref{eq:forestpacking}.
51: \item If the queue empties, output the union of the selected subsets. Failed or infinite runs contribute no output mass.
52: \end{enumerate}
53: Let $Q_1(A)$ be the output subprobability. No root is activated with probability $2^{-\Lambda}$. Every finite run with a root outputs a nonempty set. Consequently
54: \begin{equation}\label{eq:forestconditioning}
55:  \sum_{A\ne\varnothing}Q_1(A)\le Z,
56:  \qquad Q_*(A)=Q_1(A)/Z\quad(A\ne\varnothing)
57: \end{equation}
58: is a subprobability law. Conditioning on a nonempty initial root set therefore remains valid without any assumption of almost sure termination.
59: 
60: \subsection{Partition forests and their costs}
61: 
62: Fix a nonempty Fourier set $A$ with $P_f(A)>0$. Every $j\in A$ belongs to some $R_l$. Choose an assignment $\tau(j)$ to such a gate, and put
63: \[
64:  A_l=\{j\in A:\tau(j)=l\},\quad s_l=|A_l|,
65:  \quad\mathcal F=\{l:s_l>0\},\quad\kappa_l=|A\cap R_l|.
66: \]
67: On $\mathcal F$ draw an edge $l\to l'$ if $l\ne l'$ and $A_l\cap R_{l'}\ne\varnothing$. Choose one root in each source strongly connected component and a directed spanning forest from these roots. Choose a connecting variable in $A_l\cap R_{l'}$ for each parent--child edge. These choices prescribe a finite queue run that visits every gate in $\mathcal F$ once, selects $B_l=A_l$, and outputs $A$. We call such a run a \emph{partition forest}. Its selected subsets partition $A$; arbitrary once-per-gate runs with overlapping selected subsets are not included in this definition.
68: 
69: For $j\in A\cap R_l$, define
70: \[
71:  F_l^j(A)=\min_{t:j\in R_{l,t}}
72:  \ln\frac{r_{l,t}(j)}{|A\cap U_j^{l,t}|+1}.
73: \]
74: These quantities are nonnegative. For a partition forest $r$ with root set $\mathcal R$, set
75: \begin{align}
76:  B_\lambda(A;r)={}&
77:  \sum_{l\in\mathcal F}\left[
78:  L_l+s_l\log M_l+\gamma s_l\log\frac{\kappa_l}{s_l}\right]
79:  +\sum_{l\in\mathcal R}b_l\notag\\
80:  &+\frac{\gamma}{\ln2}\sum_{j\in A}F_{\tau(j)}^j(A).\label{eq:forestcost}
81: \end{align}
82: Put $B_\lambda(A)=\min_r B_\lambda(A;r)$ and $B_\lambda(\varnothing)=0$. There are finitely many partition forests for each $A$.
83: 
84: \begin{lemma}[Root disjointness]\label{lem:forestrootdisjoint}
85: The sets $A\cap R_l$ for distinct roots of a partition forest are pairwise disjoint.
86: \end{lemma}
87: \begin{proof}
88: If $j$ lies in two such intersections, its assigned gate $\tau(j)$ has an edge to both roots, except for a possible edge within its own component. A source component cannot receive an edge from a different component. Thus $\tau(j)$ would belong to both source components, a contradiction.
89: \end{proof}
90: 
91: \begin{theorem}[Conditioned posterior forest bound]\label{thm:posteriorforest}
92: Put $v=\Var(f)$ and
93: \[
94:  K_{\rm ref}=3+2\log3+\log(6e).
95: \]
96: For every nonempty spectral $A$, let $\mathcal P_A$ be a probability distribution on distinct actual partition-forest runs producing $A$. Then
97: \begin{align}
98:  \HH(f)\le{}&h_2(v)+v(\Lambda+\log Z)+K_{\rm ref}\I(f)\notag\\
99:  &+\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}P_f(A)
100:  \left[\E_{r\sim\mathcal P_A}B_\lambda(A;r)
101:        -\HH(\mathcal P_A)\right].\label{eq:posteriorforest}
102: \end{align}
103: In particular,
104: \begin{equation}\label{eq:minforest}
105:  \HH(f)\le h_2(v)+v(\Lambda+\log Z)
106:              +K_{\rm ref}\I(f)+\E B_\lambda(A).
107: \end{equation}
108: Here $h_2$ is binary entropy, and the expectation in~\eqref{eq:minforest} is under $P_f$.
109: \end{theorem}
110: \begin{proof}
111: For a specified root set, its selection probability is
112: \[
113:  2^{-\Lambda}\prod_{l\in\mathcal R}\lambda_l\pi_l.
114: \]
115: Root and child gate names together therefore cost $\sum_{l\in\mathcal F}L_l$: every visited gate is a root or exactly one child. The root rarity cost is $\sum_{l\in\mathcal R}b_l$.
116: 
117: For $i\in A_l$, choose an inner order attaining $F_l^i(A)$. Since $|A\cap U_i^{l,t}|+1\le\kappa_l$,
118: \[
119:  \ln^+\frac{r_{l,t}(i)}{s_l}
120:  \le F_l^i(A)+\ln\frac{\kappa_l}{s_l}.
121: \]
122: Lemma~\ref{lem:forestnormalizer} bounds the subset-choice costs by
123: \[
124:  |A|\log(6e)+\sum_l s_l\log M_l
125:  +\gamma\sum_l s_l\log(\kappa_l/s_l)
126:  +\frac{\gamma}{\ln2}\sum_{j\in A}F_{\tau(j)}^j(A).
127: \]
128: The size choices cost at most $|A|\log3$, using $s(s+1)\le3^s$. If $c_l$ is the number of children, then
129: \[
130:  |\mathcal R|+\sum_lc_l=|\mathcal F|\le|A|.
131: \]
132: The choices of $c_l$ cost at most $|A|(1+\log3)$, since $(c+1)(c+2)\le2\,3^c$. The multiset choices cost at most $2|A|$, using $\binom{s+c-1}{c}\le2^{s+c}$. Thus the probability $q_1(r)$ of this actual run satisfies
133: \[
134:  -\log q_1(r)\le\Lambda+K_{\rm ref}|A|+B_\lambda(A;r).
135: \]
136: After conditioning on a nonempty root set, $q(r)=q_1(r)/Z$, so its negative logarithm gains $\log Z$.
137: 
138: For a probability law $\mathcal P_A$ on the indicated runs, log-sum gives
139: \[
140:  -\log Q_*(A)\le
141:  \E_{\mathcal P_A}\log\frac1{q(r)}-\HH(\mathcal P_A).
142: \]
143: Indeed, normalizing $q$ within this finite collection gives the same inequality with its total mass in place of $Q_*(A)$, and that mass is at most $Q_*(A)$. Count an actual run only once if it has several abstract descriptions. Finally use the subprobability reference with mass $1-v$ at the empty set and mass $vQ_*(A)$ at nonempty $A$. Gibbs' inequality proves~\eqref{eq:posteriorforest}. A point mass at a minimizing forest proves~\eqref{eq:minforest}.
144: \end{proof}
145: 
146: \begin{lemma}[Weighted activation bound]\label{lem:forestpath}
147: The quantities above satisfy
148: \[
149:  v\Lambda\le(\log e)\I(f).
150: \]
151: \end{lemma}
152: \begin{proof}
153: Put $p_0=\P(f=-1)$. Independently resample all coordinates of $R_l$ in a fixed order. The initial event $f=-1$ and the final event $h_l=1$ are independent and have joint probability $p_0p_l$. A path between them contains a false-to-true transition. Resampling coordinate $j$ makes such a transition with probability $\I_j(f)/4$, since every intermediate input is uniform. Weight the resulting union bound by $\lambda_l$ and sum:
154: \[
155:  p_0\sum_l\lambda_lp_l
156:  \le\frac14\sum_j\I_j(f)\sum_{l:j\in R_l}\lambda_l
157:  \le\I(f)/4.
158: \]
159: Now $v\le4p_0$, $\pi_l\le p_l$, and $\log(1+x)\le x\log e$ give the result.
160: \end{proof}
161: 
162: The standard level-entropy estimate, obtained from the edge-isoperimetric inequality, gives $h_2(v)\le2\I(f)$; see~\cite{OWZ11,Threshold}. The conditioning term has a distinct role. For one conjunction of width $n$ with $\lambda=1$, one has
163: \[
164:  \Lambda+\log Z=\log\pi=-n,
165: \]
166: which cancels its root rarity cost exactly.

168: \subsection{A fractional bound using pivotal witness distributions}
169: 
170: For this subsection suppose $h_l$ is a Boolean function of $M_l$ regular gates, and take their relevant supports and desirability orders as the ordered cover. A regular gate here means a gate which, after complementing some input coordinates, is monotone and admits a total desirability order: replacing $(x_i,x_j)=(-1,1)$ by $(1,-1)$ cannot decrease its value whenever $i$ precedes $j$. Different gates may use different orders and input orientations.
171: 
172: We use two signed-piece estimates from~\cite{Threshold}. Put $\beta=120/11$. If $d\in\{0,\pm1\}$ is supported on the $j$-pivotal set of one regular inner gate, its normalized Fourier law $\nu$ satisfies
173: \begin{equation}\label{eq:forestrankinput}
174:  \nu(F_l^j>u)\le\sqrt{2e}\,e^{-u/2}\qquad(u\ge0).
175: \end{equation}
176: If $d$ is supported on the $j$-pivotal set of $h_l$, then
177: \begin{equation}\label{eq:forestrareinput}
178:  \nu(\kappa_l<b_l/\beta)
179:  \le2^{3/5}2^{-5b_l/12}.
180: \end{equation}
181: Both statistics are evaluated at $A=T\cup\{j\}$ under the Fourier law of $d$. Arbitrary signs of $d$ are permitted. In~\eqref{eq:forestrareinput}, the density estimate is applied on fibers fixing only coordinates outside $R_l$; it does not assume that the original gate probability survives restrictions inside $R_l$.
182: 
183: For every $j$ with $\alpha_j:=\I_j(f)>0$, split $\D_jf$ pointwise among pivotal outer gates and then among pivotal regular inner gates, with fixed tie rules. Let $d_{j,l,t}$ be the resulting disjoint signed pieces, and put
184: \[
185:  q_{j,l,t}=\frac{\|d_{j,l,t}\|_2^2}{\alpha_j},\qquad
186:  \sum_{l,t}q_{j,l,t}=1,
187:  \qquad
188:  S_j(\lambda)=\sum_{l,t}\sqrt{q_{j,l,t}}\,
189:  M_l^{5/48}\lambda_l^{-11/48}.
190: \]
191: Zero pieces are omitted. A pivotal outer gate has a pivotal inner gate, so this decomposition exists.
192: 
193: \begin{theorem}[Fractional pivotal-witness bound]\label{thm:fractionalforest}
194: With the preceding hypotheses,
195: \begin{equation}\label{eq:fractionalforest}
196:  \HH(f)\le C_0\I(f)+\frac{48}{5}
197:        \sum_j\alpha_j\log S_j(\lambda)+v\log Z,
198: \end{equation}
199: where
200: \begin{align*}
201:  C_0={}&2+\log e+K_{\rm ref}+\frac{120}{11}\\
202:  &+\frac{24}{5}\left[\log(\sqrt{2e}+2^{3/5})+\log e\right]
203:  =40.80487605924\ldots<41.
204: \end{align*}
205: \end{theorem}
206: \begin{proof}
207: Call $l$ dense at $A$ if $\kappa_l\ge b_l/\beta$, and set
208: \[
209:  G_j(l)=\frac{\gamma}{\ln2}F_l^j(A)
210:        +b_l1_{\{l\text{ not dense}\}},\qquad
211:  C_j(A)=\min_{l:j\in R_l}\left[G_j(l)+\frac{11}{5}L_l+\log M_l\right].
212: \]
213: Choose $\tau$ attaining these minima. Lemma~\ref{lem:forestrootdisjoint} pays the rarity of dense roots by $\beta|A|$. Each nondense root owns at least one coordinate, so its rarity can be charged to the corresponding sum of $G_j$'s. Also
214: \[
215:  \sum_l\lambda_l\kappa_l\le|A|.
216: \]
217: The log-sum inequality therefore gives
218: \[
219:  \sum_l s_l\log(\kappa_l/s_l)\le\sum_l s_lL_l.
220: \]
221: Since $\sum_{l\in\mathcal F}L_l\le\sum_ls_lL_l$, the forest cost satisfies
222: \begin{equation}\label{eq:forestcoarsening}
223:  B_\lambda(A)\le\beta|A|+\sum_{j\in A}C_j(A).
224: \end{equation}
225: 
226: Let $\nu_{j,l,t}$ be a piece's normalized Fourier law. Splitting the rank and rarity costs at $u/2$ in~\eqref{eq:forestrankinput}--\eqref{eq:forestrareinput} gives
227: \[
228:  \nu_{j,l,t}(G_j(l)>u)\le C_*2^{-\delta u},\qquad
229:  C_*=\sqrt{2e}+2^{3/5},\quad\delta=5/24.
230: \]
231: For $u<0$ the right side exceeds $1$, so the inequality remains valid. For any Fourier event $E$, Minkowski's inequality gives
232: \[
233:  \nu_j(E)^{1/2}\le\sum_{l,t}\sqrt{q_{j,l,t}}\,
234:                                   \nu_{j,l,t}(E)^{1/2},
235: \]
236: where $\nu_j(T)=\wh{\D_jf}(T)^2/\alpha_j$. On $E=\{C_j>u\}$, each piece satisfies $G_j(l)>u-(11/5)L_l-\log M_l$. Hence
237: \[
238:  \nu_j(C_j>u)\le\min\{1,C_*S_j(\lambda)^2 2^{-\delta u}\}.
239: \]
240: The exponents are $\delta/2=5/48$ and $\delta(11/5)/2=11/48$. Since $S_j\ge1$, integration yields
241: \[
242:  \E_{\nu_j}C_j\le\frac{24}{5}(\log C_*+\log e)
243:                        +\frac{48}{5}\log S_j.
244: \]
245: Finally $P_f(A)=\alpha_j\nu_j(A\setminus\{j\})$ for $j\in A$. Sum, then apply~\eqref{eq:minforest},~\eqref{eq:forestcoarsening}, Lemma~\ref{lem:forestpath}, and $h_2(v)\le2\I(f)$.
246: \end{proof}
247: 
248: If the read number is $k$ and $M_l\le M$, choose $\lambda_l=1/k$. There are at most $kM$ nonzero pieces at each coordinate, so $S_j\le k^{35/48}M^{29/48}$. Dropping the nonpositive conditioning term recovers
249: \[
250:  \HH(f)\le\left(41+7\log k+\frac{29}{5}\log M\right)\I(f).
251: \]
252: Keeping only the outer witness probabilities $q_{j,l}=\sum_tq_{j,l,t}$ gives the intermediate bound
253: \begin{equation}\label{eq:outerfractional}
254:  \HH(f)\le\left(41+\frac{29}{5}\log M\right)\I(f)
255:  +\frac{48}{5}\sum_j\alpha_j
256:        \log\sum_l\sqrt{q_{j,l}}\lambda_l^{-11/48}.
257: \end{equation}
258: For uniform weights, its last term is $(11/5)\I(f)\log k+(24/5)\sum_j\alpha_j\HH_{1/2}(q_j)$, where $\HH_{1/2}(q)=2\log\sum_l\sqrt{q_l}$.

260: \subsection{Addressing and the cost of repeated labels}
261: 
262: Let $a\ge1$, $N=2^a$, and $f(x,y)=y_x$, with one data bit $y_b$ per selector word $b$. Its canonical DNF has the gates $h_b=[x=b\text{ and }y_b=1]$. Each has probability $1/(2N)$ and support consisting of all selectors and its own data bit. The expansion
263: \[
264:  f=\sum_b1_{\{x=b\}}y_b
265: \]
266: has $N^2$ coefficients of magnitude $1/N$, giving
267: \begin{equation}\label{eq:addressingHI}
268:  \HH(f)=2a,\qquad\I(f)=1+a/2.
269: \end{equation}
270: Each selector has influence $1/2$; each data bit has influence $1/N$.
271: 
272: \begin{proposition}[Coordinatewise obstruction and one-root repair]\label{prop:addressforest}
273: For this DNF, the nonnegative pressure term in~\eqref{eq:outerfractional}, minimized over fractional incidence weights, is exactly
274: \begin{equation}\label{eq:addresspressure}
275:  \frac72a^2+\frac{11}{5}a.
276: \end{equation}
277: Nevertheless, the conditioned whole-forest bound admits witnesses satisfying
278: \begin{equation}\label{eq:addressforestpayment}
279:  \E B_\lambda(A)+\log Z\le a+\frac65\I(f).
280: \end{equation}
281: \end{proposition}
282: \begin{proof}
283: For a selector derivative, a pivotal input has one true endpoint and one false endpoint. The true endpoint satisfies exactly one gate, so its witness is forced. Each gate's piece has density $1/(2N)$, giving $q_{j,b}=1/N$. A data derivative has a deterministic witness. All selector incidence constraints reduce to $\sum_b\lambda_b\le1$.
284: 
285: For a selector, the sum in~\eqref{eq:outerfractional} is $N^{-1/2}\sum_b\lambda_b^{-11/48}$. Convexity makes it minimal at $\lambda_b=1/N$, with value $N^{35/48}$. The data contribution is minimized at the same weights by concavity of $\log$. The $a$ selectors, each of influence $1/2$, contribute $(7/2)a^2$, and the data coordinates contribute $(11/5)a$. This proves~\eqref{eq:addresspressure}.
286: 
287: For a spectral set $A=\{y_b\}\cup S$, assign every coordinate to gate $b$. There is one root, no children, and $s_b=\kappa_b=|S|+1$, so the allocation cost vanishes. Choose $\lambda_b=1/N$, and order each clause's selectors first in their common order, with its data variable last. The label costs $a$ and the root rarity costs $a+1$.
288: 
289: Conditional on a selector of rank $r$ being selected, its predecessor count is $\operatorname{Bin}(r-1,1/2)$. The exact identity
290: \[
291:  \E\frac1{1+\operatorname{Bin}(r-1,1/2)}
292:  =\frac{2(1-2^{-r})}{r}\le\frac2r
293: \]
294: and Jensen's inequality bound its expected $F$-cost by $\ln2$. The same argument with $r=a+1$ applies to the always-present data coordinate. Consequently
295: \[
296:  \E\sum_{j\in A}F_b^j(A)\le\I(f)\ln2,
297:  \qquad \E B_\lambda(A)\le2a+1+\frac65\I(f).
298: \]
299: Finally $Z\le\sum_b\lambda_b\pi_b=1/(2N)$, proving~\eqref{eq:addressforestpayment}.
300: \end{proof}
301: 
302: Thus the quadratic loss in~\eqref{eq:addresspressure} is caused by charging a shared label separately at many Fourier coordinates. It is absent from the whole-forest construction.

304: \subsection{Posterior label erasure and the remaining condition}
305: 
306: A simple identity illustrates the entropy subtraction in Theorem~\ref{thm:posteriorforest}. Choose uniformly a $w$-subset of $[n]$, and then uniformly an $s$-subset of it, with $s\le w$. The probability of any specified $s$-set $A$ is
307: \[
308:  \frac{\binom{n-s}{w-s}}{\binom nw\binom ws}
309:  =\frac1{\binom ns}.
310: \]
311: The posterior root entropy is $\log\binom{n-s}{w-s}$; it subtracts the latent label cost exactly. A single chosen witness would miss this saving.
312: 
313: Theorem~\ref{thm:posteriorforest} leaves the following concrete sufficient condition for unrestricted FEI: a universal finite $K$ and, for every Boolean $f$, ordered gate representations, fractional weights, and realizable partition-forest distributions satisfying
314: \begin{equation}\label{eq:foresttarget}
315:  \boxed{\quad
316:  v(\Lambda+\log Z)+
317:  \sum_{A\ne\varnothing}P_f(A)
318:  \left[\E_{r\sim\mathcal P_A}B_\lambda(A;r)-\HH(\mathcal P_A)\right]
319:  \le K\I(f).
320:  \quad}
321: \end{equation}
322: This would give the FEI constant $2+K_{\rm ref}+K$. The condition remains open. If all successful runs are permitted, successively larger finite collections identify the variational infimum with the cross-entropy of the output reference. This does not bound that cross-entropy. Nor does a generic minterm representation suffice: the uniform reference on nonempty subsets gives only
323: \[
324:  \HH(f)\le h_2(v)+v\log(2^n-1)\le2\I(f)+nv.
325: \]
326: For balanced odd majority, $v=1$ while $\I(f)\le\sqrt n$, so this dimension term cannot be absorbed universally. An estimate such as~\eqref{eq:foresttarget} must account for the sharing and erasure of witnesses using additional structure of Boolean spectra.
