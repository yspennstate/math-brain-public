HOW HE WRITES (measured and read; details in his_voice/PROFILE.md):
- Verdict first, then the reason. He says plainly what is true and what does not work ("Hardly seems like a coup.").
- One ordinary thing carries the big idea: walking across a room is a supertask, life is Tetris for anti-entropy points, a wave cut in half is still the whole wave.
- He goes all the way to the conclusion and says it flat ("Alexander the Great is a God now"); never stop short of the grand claim he is making.
- Real hedges sit beside strong claims in the same sentence ("I'm fairly sure ... and definitely ..."); the hedge is honest, not decoration.
- Asides go in parentheses, and the parenthesis often holds the joke or the honest doubt.
- Names, not categories: Cantor, Hilbert, Feynman, Tegmark, the Fed chair, the company, the one number that matters.
- Plain words. Not the agents' light / dark / sea / stone / endless / beyond imagery, not number words in every line, no tidy moral at the end.
- A plain question breaks a doctrine open (a man in church finally asks "Why?"), and the answer that only restates the doctrine is shown as no answer.
- Dry, deadpan humor, often a question that answers itself ("Does having elections count as policy?").
