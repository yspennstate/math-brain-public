\section*{Two-factor signed refund for $q_a\otimes q_b$}

All logarithms are base two.  For a Boolean child $h$ and a constant
branch $c\in\{-1,1\}$, let $p=\Pr[h\ne c]$.  Let $\mathscr D_c(h)$ be the
complete physical selector packet $L+Q$ of the selector between $h$ and
$c$.  Then
\[
 \mathscr D_c(h)=F(p),
\]
where, with $0\log(1/0)=0$,
\[
 F(p)=4p(1-p)-2(1-p)^2\log(1-p)-2p^2\log p
 +(1-2p)^2\log|1-2p|.                                      \tag{1}
\]
In particular $F(p)=F(1-p)$ and $F(0)=F(1)=0$.

Indeed, for every nonempty Fourier cell the midpoint and half-difference
coefficients are both $\widehat h(S)/2$, and its $L+Q$ contribution is
$\widehat h(S)^2$.  Their sum is $\operatorname{Var}(h)=4p(1-p)$.
At the empty cell the two coefficients are, up to signs, $1-p,p$.
Writing $q=(1-p)^2+p^2$ and $r=(1-2p)^2=2q-1$, the phase term is
\[
 Q=-q\log q+\frac r2\log r.
\]
It cancels the $q\log q$ term in $L$, proving (1).

Let $q_n$ be $+1$ only at the origin and $-1$ elsewhere.  Put
\[
 x=2^{-a},\qquad y=2^{-b},\qquad p=x+y-2xy,
\]
and let
\[
 D_a=F(x),\quad D_b=F(y),\quad R_{a,b}=F(p).
\]
Here $R_{a,b}$ is the complete packet for $q_aq_b$ against the constant
$+1$ branch.  Define the cross term
\[
 \Xi_{a,b}=R_{a,b}-D_a-D_b.                                  \tag{2}
\]

When an $a$-factor coordinate is revealed at the state $q_mq_b$, one
child is $-q_b$ and the other is $q_{m-1}q_b$.  Both inherit the same
constant branch.  With $R_{0,b}:=D_b$, the signed first-factor credit is
\[
 \Gamma_{m\mid b}=R_{m,b}-\frac12R_{m-1,b}-\frac12D_b.       \tag{3}
\]
The state $q_mq_b$ is reached with probability $2^{m-a}$.  Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{m=1}^a2^{m-a}\Gamma_{m\mid b}=R_{a,b}-D_b
 =D_a+\Xi_{a,b}.}                                             \tag{4}
\]
After the first factor is exhausted, every leaf has packet $D_b$, since
$F(y)=F(1-y)$.  Reversing the factor order gives
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{m=1}^b2^{m-b}\Gamma_{m\mid a}=R_{a,b}-D_a
 =D_b+\Xi_{a,b}.}                                             \tag{5}
\]

For either order, split the reached credits into
\[
 P_{a\mid b}=\sum_m2^{m-a}(\Gamma_{m\mid b})_+,
 \qquad
 N_{a\mid b}=\sum_m2^{m-a}(-\Gamma_{m\mid b})_+.
\]
Equation (4) is exactly
\[
 \boxed{R_{a,b}+N_{a\mid b}=D_b+P_{a\mid b}.}                \tag{6}
\]
Thus every negative credit is paid by positive credits and the one
terminal $D_b$ packet, with every node and terminal used once.  Reversing
the order yields the analogous identity with $a,b$ exchanged.

The cross term is not zero and has no fixed sign.  Rigorous intervals give
\[
 \Xi_{4,4}<-0.01038,
 \qquad
 \Xi_{5,5}>0.00239.                                          \tag{7}
\]
Consequently a ledger that simply sums the two isolated one-factor
payments can overpay or underpay.  The exact factor-first telescope does
not double-spend: it assigns $\Xi_{a,b}$ once, to the first phase.

For equal factors, put $\delta=2^{-n}$.  Expanding (1) at zero gives
\[
 F(p)=4p+p^2\left(2\log\frac1p-4+\frac3{\ln2}\right)
 +O\!\left(p^3\log\frac1p\right).
\]
Since the tensor disagreement probability is $2\delta(1-\delta)$,
\[
 \boxed{\Xi_{n,n}=\delta^2\left(4n-24+\frac6{\ln2}\right)
 +O(n\delta^3).}                                              \tag{8}
\]
Hence $\Xi_{n,n}>0$ for all sufficiently large $n$.  It is a genuine
positive underpayment of naive additivity, but it is lower order relative
to $D_n+D_n\sim8\delta$.
