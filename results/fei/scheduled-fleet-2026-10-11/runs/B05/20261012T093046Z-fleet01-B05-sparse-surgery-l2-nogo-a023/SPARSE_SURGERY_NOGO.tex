\section*{A sparse odd-surgery obstruction to a selector-only averaged payment}

All logarithms in $U$ are base two.  Natural logarithms are used inside the
local scalar $\Phi$.  This note continues the independently accepted
odd-surgery identity and proves that its edit-count dependence cannot be
removed by bounding the retained charge solely by fresh selector influence.

\subsection*{Setup and the frozen proposed improvement}

Let $k=2r$ and take the standard quadratic bent function
\[
 g(x,y)=(-1)^{x\cdot y},\qquad x,y\in\mathbb F_2^r.
\]
Put $\delta=2^{-2r}$ and $\alpha=\sqrt\delta=2^{-r}$.  If $h$ is obtained
from $g$ by flipping an odd set $E$ of $m$ inputs, write
\[
 a_T=\widehat g(T),\quad e_T=\widehat h(T)-\widehat g(T),
 \quad \rho_T={e_T\over a_T}.
\]
Bentness gives $|a_T|=\alpha$ and
\[
 \mathbb E_T\rho_T^2=\sum_Te_T^2=4m\delta.               \tag{1}
\]
Here and below $T$ is uniform on the $2^{2r}$ Fourier sets.

The previous odd-surgery proof bounded its coherent scalar pointwise and got
a constant $O(m)$.  The proposed next step was an averaged replacement
proportional to (1), with an absolute constant.  We show that no such bound
exists.  More strongly, for a one-bit fresh selector, the complete retained
child charge divided by physical selector influence is unbounded.

\subsection*{The upper-cap averaging operator}

Define
\[
 (Pz)(T)=2^{|T|-2r}\sum_{Q\supseteq T}z(Q).              \tag{2}
\]
Thus $P$ is positive, $P1=1$, and it contracts the sup norm.  In the notation
of the accepted scalar identity,
\[
 v=2\rho+\rho^2,\qquad w=2p\rho+p^2\rho^2,\qquad u=Pv.  \tag{3}
\]

For a single coordinate pair, let the truth-input state be
$0=(0,0),1=(1,0),2=(0,1),3=(1,1)$.  In the orthonormal bent-modulated
character basis $\varphi_z(Q)=\sigma_Q\chi_Q(z)$, the Gram matrix of $P$ is
\[
 G={1\over16}\begin{pmatrix}
 5&-3&-3&3\\
 -3&9&5&-9\\
 -3&5&9&-9\\
 3&-9&-9&13
 \end{pmatrix}.                                         \tag{4}
\]
Consequently a fixed state $3$ has amplification $13/16$, whereas the
signed free pair supported on states $\{1,3\}$ with coefficients $(1,-1)$
has amplification
\[
 {(0,1,0,-1)G(0,1,0,-1)^t\over2}={5\over4}.             \tag{5}
\]

\subsection*{The growing sparse edit family}

Let $s=\lceil r/3\rceil$.  Form the product edit set $E_r^0$ by fixing the
first $s$ input pairs to state $3$ and allowing each remaining pair to be
state $1$ or state $3$.  Remove the all-state-$3$ point and call the result
$E_r$.  Then
\[
 M:=|E_r^0|=2^{r-s},\qquad m:=|E_r|=M-1
\]
is odd, and
\[
 m\alpha<2^{-s}\longrightarrow0.                        \tag{6}
\]
Thus the family lies eventually far inside the accepted sparse regime
$m2^{-k/2}\le1/32$, and every relative coefficient perturbation tends
uniformly to zero:
\[
 \|\rho\|_\infty\le2m\alpha<2^{1-s}.                    \tag{7}
\]

Before removing the point, tensorization of (4)--(5) gives the exact
amplification
\[
 A_r=\left({13\over16}\right)^s
     \left({5\over4}\right)^{r-s}.                      \tag{8}
\]
The removed point has amplification $(13/16)^r$.  The reverse triangle
inequality therefore gives, for the actual odd edit set,
\[
 {\|P\rho\|_2^2\over\|\rho\|_2^2}
 \ge L_r:=
 {\left(\sqrt{MA_r}-\sqrt{(13/16)^r}\right)^2\over M-1}.\tag{9}
\]
Since $s/r\to1/3$,
\[
 {1\over r}\log A_r\longrightarrow
 {1\over3}\log{13\over16}+{2\over3}\log{5\over4}>0.  \tag{10}
\]
Hence $L_r\to\infty$.  In particular
\[
 D_r:={\|P\rho-\rho\|_2^2\over\|\rho\|_2^2}
 \ge(\sqrt{L_r}-1)^2\longrightarrow\infty.              \tag{11}
\]

\subsection*{From amplification to the complete retained charge}

Use a one-bit selector, so $p=1/2$, and let $J_r$ be the complete charge
\[
 J_r=U(F)-\tfrac12U(g)-\tfrac12U(h).
\]
The accepted exact decomposition is
\[
 J_r={1\over\ln2}\mathbb E_T\Phi_{1/2}(u_T,v_T,w_T)
       +\tfrac14K_r,                                    \tag{12}
\]
because the $(1-2p)I_{\rm ctl}$ term vanishes.  The selector influence is
\[
 I_{\rm ctl}=m\delta.                                   \tag{13}
\]

The exact Taylor expansion at the origin has linear term
$pv-w=p(1-p)\rho^2$ and quadratic term
\[
 {p(1-p)\over2}(u-2\rho)^2.
\]
Equations (3), (7), and sup-norm contraction of $P$ make all arguments tend
uniformly to zero.  More explicitly, on a fixed neighbourhood
$|u|,|v|,|w|\leq\eta<1$ all third derivatives of the logarithmic summands
in $\Phi_p$ are bounded.  Hence the pointwise remainder is at most
$C\varepsilon_r(u^2+v^2+w^2)$, where
$\varepsilon_r=\max_T|\rho_T|\to0$.  Since
$u=2P\rho+O(\varepsilon_r^2)$, $v=2\rho+O(\varepsilon_r^2)$ and
$w=\rho+O(\varepsilon_r^2)$, averaging the remainder is
$o(\|\rho\|_2^2+\|P\rho-\rho\|_2^2)$.  Taylor's theorem therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb E\Phi_{1/2}(u,v,w)
 ={1\over4}\left[\|\rho\|_2^2
          +2\|P\rho-\rho\|_2^2\right]
 +o\!\left(\|\rho\|_2^2+\|P\rho-\rho\|_2^2\right).     \tag{14}
\]
By (1), one quarter of $\|\rho\|_2^2$ equals $m\delta=I_{\rm ctl}$.

Finally, $U(e)\ge0$ and $0\le2r-|T|\le2r$ imply
\[
 K_r=U(e)-\sum_Te_T^2(2r-|T|)\ge-2r\sum_Te_T^2.
\]
Thus $(1/4)K_r\ge-2rI_{\rm ctl}$.  Combining this with
(11)--(14) proves
\[
 {J_r\over I_{\rm ctl}}
 \ge {1+2D_r+o(1+D_r)\over\ln2}-2r\longrightarrow\infty.\tag{15}
\]

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
There is no absolute $C$ such that every odd sparse bent-core point surgery
activated by a fresh selector satisfies $J\le C I_{\rm ctl}$.  Equivalently,
the proposed $L_2$ scalar payment by $p\sum_Te_T^2$ is false even while
$m2^{-k/2}\to0$.  The earlier $O(m)$ theorem remains correct; this result
shows that some edit-count or data-core currency is genuinely necessary for
this mechanism.

This is not a counterexample to FEI or GU'.  The edited bent cores carry
physical data-coordinate influence that (15) deliberately does not charge.
The next viable route must debit a single-use data-edit/terminal packet, or
prove a global cancellation across descendants; selector influence alone
cannot pay arbitrary changing coherent cores.
