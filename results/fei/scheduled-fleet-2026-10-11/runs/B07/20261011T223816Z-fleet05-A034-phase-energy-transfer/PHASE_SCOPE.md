# Admission and scope

The theorem uses independent uniform physical X,Y cubes. Spectral Y coordinates may be correlated because each conditional spectrum is a uniform affine coset. The X-degree bound is an integer d>=1 and every distinct label has size at most d. Empty X labels are allowed; no balance assumption is silently added.

Admission is concrete and algebraic: each nonzero partial X-Fourier row is the unit signed quadratic phase on one affine physical Y cell; the cells partition Y. This includes unbounded, heterogeneous cell counts and codimensions, reused physical Y coordinates, and nonlinear degree-two phases on the cell. Merely having boundedly many conditional spectral laws or claiming that they are Boolean restriction laws is insufficient: h(X)g(Y) preserves those properties while retaining arbitrary H(g).

For an admitted tensor h(X,Y)g(Z), select one occupied row and one y value in its support. Its quadratic physical phase restricted to that y is exactly ±g(Z). Hence g must be quadratic. This excludes, for example, a cubic monomial phase (-1)^(z1*z2*z3); it permits quadratic factors, whose full conditional entropy the theorem explicitly pays.

Stability is proved for every physical Y coordinate restriction and for X restrictions preserving distinct projected labels. Full physical restriction closure is deliberately not asserted: ordinary addressing produces arbitrary g(Y) when its data bits are fixed to g's truth table. Requiring full closure therefore recreates universal FEI. The obstruction is proved, not inferred from a failed search.

The recovered H(p_X)<=(2/ln2)I_Y+2(d-1) leaves the conditional term. This stage supplies the stronger exact H(p_X)=Σalpha k and H(S_Y|S_X)=Σalpha(k+r) on its admitted class, hence pays H jointly by 4I_Y without double counting two independently charged budgets. For general Boolean f both the arbitrary-tensor and the label-collision residuals remain unpaid.
