# The finite bound genuinely needs Boolean realizability

On six uniform input bits consider the normalized real multilinear polynomial

psi(x)=product_{j=1}^6[(sqrt(15)+x_j)/4].

Each one-bit factor has squared Fourier masses15/16 and1/16, so Parseval gives ||psi||_2=1. Its full squared spectrum is a product law. In particular every conditional law given any two spectral address bits is product, exactly matching the conditional-product hypothesis in the Boolean theorem.

Its spectral influence is I=6/16. Its entropy is6 h2(1/16), and h2(1/16)=1/4+(15/16)log2(16/15)>1/4. Therefore H/I>4. The Boolean six-variable constant Cstar=16/3-(3/2)log2(3) is less than4 (indeed log2(3)>1 gives Cstar<23/6<4). Thus the new finite theorem fails for this normalized non-Boolean vector. At the all-positive physical input, psi=[(sqrt15+1)/4]^6>1 because sqrt15>3, so psi is explicitly not Boolean.

The exclusion is the full pointwise address-Walsh admission: a Boolean four-address table at each data point must give a pure +/-1 slice or four +/-1/2 slices with negative phase product. Normalization and product conditional spectra do not supply these common five-valued phase constraints. This control is not an FEI counterexample.
