# Checks against overstatement

The strongest accounting objection is that TC+M after reset might have been paid by renewing M. The proof instead subtracts the actual incoming K_H, leaving J_H=2M_h-m-log2(r_H), strictly less than the already allocated m+2. Root allocation stays exactly m+2. It is not m+2 plus a descendant M, nor is any spent debit recovered.

A second objection is a hidden cut between carrier reveal and reset. Lift erasure has E=0, O=0 and each transported child KL equals the entry core KL. The explicit max(K,TC+M) boundary and its positive pending liability retain that cut. Exact certificates check BOTH actual inherited child KLs before replacing their references.

A third objection is hidden terminal entropy. Every actual terminal keeps H+K or H+TC. The inequality for cross entropy adds expected terminal H to both sides; it does not declare that H is covered by the extra reserve, so this is not full FEI. The initial root K is also separately booked.

The general envelope is audited by physical edge partition and KL chain rules, separately from the field splice. Generic constant and parity examples exercise deterministic marginals and reference-only rows. Actual field certificates at l2,l3 include every first ordinary query after an actual reset in both carrier outcomes and both selector branches, using the unused physical u coordinate throughout. All signs use rigorous rational enclosures, not floating-point entropy.

The tempting generalization to a reset after an arbitrary adaptive prefix is unsupported: the actual K at that prefix need not equal m-H_h+log(r_H), so equation(4) is unavailable. The modes may be chosen at selector-child entry but not switched later without a new guard. A second intermediate reset is likewise not supplied. Finally, a positive original Sbar does not establish availability: unrelated spending and the caller's original-reference funding remain explicit hypotheses.
