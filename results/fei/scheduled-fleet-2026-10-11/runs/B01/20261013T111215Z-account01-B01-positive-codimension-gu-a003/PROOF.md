# Canonical bilinear point-defect restriction-closure theorem

All vector spaces are over \(\mathbb F_2\).  For \(t,k\ge0\), let
\(x,y\in\mathbb F_2^t\), \(z\in\mathbb F_2^k\), and define
\[
 F_{t,k}^{a,b,c}(x,y,z)=
 (-1)^{x\cdot y+a\cdot x+b\cdot y+c\cdot z}
 (1-2\mathbf1_{\{(y,z)=0\}}).                       \tag{1}
\]
Write \(U\) for the upper-set potential and
\[
G(f)=\sum_i\left(U(f)-\mathbb E_{\epsilon}U(f|_{v_i=\epsilon})\right).
\]

## Theorem

Let \(\mathcal C_{\rm cb}\) be generated by (1), pure affine-quadratic
phases, global signs, physical restrictions, and finite disjoint products.
Then \(\mathcal C_{\rm cb}\) is restriction closed and every
\(f\in\mathcal C_{\rm cb}\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{G(f)\le64I(f).}                               \tag{2}
\]
Consequently restriction induction gives
\[
 U(f)\le64I(f),\qquad H(f)\le130I(f).                 \tag{3}
\]
In fact the class has the sharper direct endpoint
\[
 \boxed{H(f)\le4I(f),}                                \tag{4}
\]
and the constant four is sharp along the raw point defects
\(F_{0,k}^{0,0,0}\) as \(k\to\infty\).

The constants are independent of rank \(t\), positive codimension \(k\),
affine phase, restriction depth, and number of disjoint blocks.

## 1. Exact exceptional-layer law

The \(y\)-frequency block is independent and uniform.  After removing it,
the law \(\nu\) on \((\alpha,\gamma)\in\mathbb F_2^t\times\mathbb F_2^k\)
has uniform \(\alpha\)-marginal.  For \(\alpha\ne a\), \(\gamma=c\)
deterministically.  At \(\alpha=a\), its conditional law \(\mu\) is
\[
 \mu(c)=(1-2^{1-k})^2,qquad
 \mu(g)=2^{2-2k}\quad(g\ne c).                        \tag{5}
\]
The endpoint \(k=0\) means the unit mass at the empty word.  Hence
\[
 U(F_{t,k})=\frac t2+U(\nu).                           \tag{6}
\]

Put
\[
 \delta=2^{-t},\quad \varepsilon=2^{-k},\quad
 h=|a|,\quad l=|c|,\quad m=t-h,
\]
and, for \(k\ge1\),
\[
 d=4\varepsilon^2,quad
 \mu_c=(1-2\varepsilon)^2,quad
 D=4\varepsilon(1-2^{-l}),\quad A=2^m.               \tag{7}
\]
Direct upper-fiber counting gives
\[
 U(F_{t,k})=t+\delta W_{t,k,h,l},                      \tag{8}
\]
where
\[
 W=-m+L+E,                                             \tag{9}
\]
\[
 L=\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}{h\choose j}
 \log_2(1-D2^{-(t-j)}),                               \tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
E={}&\mu_c\log_2\frac{A-D}{\mu_c}\\
 &+d\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}{l\choose j}
       \log_2\frac{A-4\varepsilon(1-2^{-j})}{d}\\
 &+d\sum_{j=0}^k\left({k\choose j}-{l\choose j}\right)(k-j).
                                                               \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]
As usual \({l\choose j}=0\) for \(j>l\), and zero-mass terms are omitted.
Formula (11) is also valid by continuity at the degenerate endpoints.

## 2. Uniform deviation lemma

Define
\[
 B_0=0,\qquad B_1=2,qquad
 B_k=(8k+22)2^{-k}\quad(k\ge2).                       \tag{12}
\]
Then, for every admissible \(t,k,h,l\),
\[
 \boxed{|U(F_{t,k})-t|\le B_k.}                       \tag{13}
\]

For \(k=0\), the squared spectrum is uniform and equality holds with zero
deviation.  For \(k=1\), \(\nu\) is a uniform graph with one flipped value.
The two exact graph formulas give either
\[
 U-t=-2^{-t}(t-h)
\]
or
\[
 U-t=2^{-t}\sum_{s\subsetneq a}
       \log_2(1-2^{-(t-|s|)}),
\]
whose absolute values are at most \(2\).

Suppose \(k\ge2\).  The total nonspecial mass in (5) is
\[
 q=1-\mu_c=4\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\le4\varepsilon. \tag{14}
\]
Its entropy obeys
\[
 H(\mu)=h_2(q)+q\log_2(2^k-1)\le8k\varepsilon.        \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, \(-(1-q)\log_2(1-q)\le q/\ln2\), while
\[
 \log_2(1/q)=k-2-\log_2(1-\varepsilon),
\]
and for \(\varepsilon\le1/4\) we may use
\(-\log_2(1-\varepsilon)\le1/2\) and \(1/\ln2<3/2\).
Thus \(h_2(q)\le qk\), and the second term is also below \(qk\).

For (10), every logarithm has argument \(1-u\) with
\(0\le u\le3/8\).  Hence \(-\log_2(1-u)\le2u\), and
\[
 \delta|L|
 \le 2D\,4^{-t}(3^h-2^h)
 \le8\varepsilon.                                     \tag{16}
\]

To bound \(E-m\), split the dust points into proper subsets of \(c\)
and points not below \(c\).  The coefficients of \(m=\log_2 A\) cancel
except for the latter mass, costing at most \(qm\).  Since
\(D/A\le3/4\), the inequality
\(-\log_2(1-u)\le3u\) costs at most
\(24\varepsilon\) across the special and dust terms.  The entropy terms
cost at most (15), and the points not below \(c\) contribute at most
\(qk\).  Therefore
\[
 |E-m|\le4\varepsilon m+12k\varepsilon+24\varepsilon. \tag{17}
\]
If \(t\ge1\), then \(2^{-t}t\le1/2\).  Combining
(8)--(10), (16), and (17) yields
\[
 |U-t|\le(6k+22)2^{-k}\le B_k.                        \tag{18}
\]
If \(t=0\), then \(U\le H(\mu)\le8k2^{-k}\le B_k\).
This proves (13).

## 3. Aggregate GU bound

The physical restrictions remain in the same scalar family:

- fixing \(x_i\) sends \((t,k)\) to \((t-1,k+1)\), and the two children
  append the two possible affine bits to \(c\);
- fixing \(y_i=0\) sends \((t,k)\) to \((t-1,k)\), whereas \(y_i=1\)
  removes the defect and leaves a pure quadratic phase of potential \(t-1\);
- fixing \(z_j=0\) sends \((t,k)\) to \((t,k-1)\), whereas \(z_j=1\)
  leaves a pure quadratic phase of potential \(t\).

Write \(e_{t,k}=U(F_{t,k})-t\).  Applying (13) only after summing all
coordinates gives
\[
 G(F_{t,k})
 \le 2t+t(2.5B_k+B_{k+1})
       +k(B_k+\tfrac12B_{k-1}).                        \tag{19}
\]
This is deliberately an aggregate estimate: individual rare \(z\)-charges
need not be bounded by their own influences.

For the numbers (12),
\[
 2.5B_k+B_{k+1}\le30,qquad
 k(B_k+\tfrac12B_{k-1})\le32.                          \tag{20}
\]
The first maximum occurs at \(k=2\) and equals \(29.5\); the second occurs
at \(k=3\) and equals \(31.5\).  For \(k\ge4\), the latter is
\(4k(4k+9)/2^k\) and decreases.  Since \(I(F_{t,k})\ge t\) for \(t\ge1\),
(19)--(20) give
\[
 G(F_{t,k})\le32t+32\le64t\le64I(F_{t,k}).            \tag{21}
\]
At \(k=0\), the sharper accepted root calculation gives
\(G\le(5/2)I\).  At \(t=0\), the parity-shifted point defects lie in the
independently accepted partial-star class and satisfy \(G\le14I\).
Pure affine-quadratic phases satisfy \(G\le3I\).  This proves (2) on every
generator.  Exact tensor factorization makes \(G\) and \(I\) additive on
disjoint products.

The proof explains why a coordinatewise attempt fails.  For example, with
\(h=t\), \(l=k-1\), the single remaining zero-phase \(z\)-coordinate can
have \(\Delta_zU/\operatorname{Inf}_z\) growing rapidly with \(t\).
Equation (19) keeps its signed compensation before charging the aggregate.

## 4. Restriction induction and sharp direct FEI

The class is restriction closed.  Applying (2) and induction on the number
of physical variables, together with
\[
 \sum_i\mathbb E I(f|_{v_i})=(n-1)I(f),
\]
gives \(U(f)\le64I(f)\).  The established bridge
\(H(f)\le2I(f)+2U(f)\) yields (3).

There is a much sharper direct entropy calculation.  From (5),
\[
 H(F_{t,k})=2t+2^{-t}H(\mu).                           \tag{22}
\]
For \(t+k\ge2\), every matched \(x_i,y_i\) pair contributes total influence
one, and the minimum over affine \(c\) occurs at \(c=0\):
\[
 I(F_{t,k})\ge t+k2^{1-t-k}.                           \tag{23}
\]
Equations (15), (22), and (23) give
\[
 H(F_{t,k})\le2t+8k2^{-t-k}
             \le4\bigl(t+k2^{1-t-k}\bigr)
             \le4I(F_{t,k}).                          \tag{24}
\]
The one-bit endpoints are immediate.  Additivity proves (4) on disjoint
products.  For \(t=0,c=0\), the entropy/influence ratio tends to four, so
this direct class constant is sharp.

## Exact scope

This closes the rank/codimension parameter for one canonical matched
bilinear block sharing one point-defect cylinder.  It does not cover an
arbitrary invertible physical bilinear matrix with the same GU constant,
two point-defect cylinders, changing nonflat coherent cores, or general
Boolean spectra.  The universal upper-set asymmetry mismatch remains open;
full FEI is not proved.
