1: \section{Restriction increments and coordinate selection}\label{sec:restrictions}
2: 
3: For a coordinate $i$, let $f_i^+$ and $f_i^-$ denote the two restrictions
4: of $f$, and put
5: \[
6:  \Delta_i\HH(f)=\HH(f)-\frac{\HH(f_i^+)+\HH(f_i^-)}2.
7: \]
8: The restriction framework of Gonz\'alez, MacManus and
9: Pereyra~\cite{GMP26} asks for a coordinate at which this entropy
10: increment can be charged to the influence lost in the split.
11: We first give an exact scalar description of the increment, then
12: identify several limitations of possible coordinate rules.
13: 
14: \subsection{The exact increment and its scalar profile}
15: 
16: Write $h_2(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)$, with its continuous endpoint
17: values, and define
18: \begin{equation}\label{eq:kappadef}
19:  \kappa(u)=1+h_2(u)
20:  -h_2\!\left(\frac12+\sqrt{u(1-u)}\right),
21:  \qquad 0\le u\le1.
22: \end{equation}
23: For $S\subseteq[n]\setminus\{i\}$ set
24: \[
25:  c_S=\wh f(S),\qquad d_S=\wh f(S\cup\{i\}),\qquad
26:  m_S=c_S^2+d_S^2,\qquad u_S=\frac{d_S^2}{m_S},
27: \]
28: omitting terms with $m_S=0$.
29: 
30: \begin{proposition}[Exact restriction increment]\label{prop:splitexact}
31: For every Boolean $f$ and every coordinate $i$,
32: \begin{equation}\label{eq:splitexact}
33:  \Delta_i\HH(f)=\sum_{S\not\ni i}m_S\kappa(u_S).
34: \end{equation}
35: In particular, $0\le\Delta_i\HH(f)\le2$.
36: 
37: If $\mathcal S$ has law $P_f$, define
38: \[
39:  B_i(f)=H\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{i\in\mathcal S\}}\mid
40:                          \mathcal S\setminus\{i\}\bigr).
41: \]
42: Then $\Delta_i\HH(f)=B_i(f)+M_i(f)$, where
43: \begin{equation}\label{eq:mixingbound}
44:  0\le M_i(f)\le
45:  \frac2{\ln2}\sum_{S\not\ni i}\min(c_S^2,d_S^2)
46:  \le\frac2{\ln2}\I_i(f).
47: \end{equation}
48: \end{proposition}
49: 
50: \begin{proof}
51: The two restricted coefficients indexed by $S$ are $c_S+d_S$ and
52: $c_S-d_S$. Suppressing subscripts and putting
53: $v=(c+d)^2/(2m)=1/2+cd/m$, the parent pair contributes
54: $-m\log m+mh_2(u)$ to entropy, whereas the average contribution
55: of the two branches is $-m\log(2m)+mh_2(v)$.
56: Their difference is $m[1+h_2(u)-h_2(v)]$.
57: Changing the sign of $cd$ does not affect $h_2(v)$, proving
58: \eqref{eq:splitexact}. Both $h_2(u)$ and $1-h_2(v)$ lie in $[0,1]$,
59: and $\sum_Sm_S=1$.
60: 
61: The term $\sum_Sm_Sh_2(u_S)$ is exactly $B_i(f)$, leaving
62: \[
63:  M_i(f)=\sum_Sm_S
64:  \left[1-h_2\!\left(\frac12+\frac{c_Sd_S}{m_S}\right)\right].
65: \]
66: To bound it, let
67: \[
68:  g(t)=\frac{(1+t)\ln(1+t)+(1-t)\ln(1-t)}2,
69:  \qquad 0\le t\le1.
70: \]
71: For $0\le t<1$, the inequality
72: $\atanh t\le t/\sqrt{1-t^2}$ follows by comparing derivatives
73: from zero. Since $g'(t)=\atanh t$, integration gives
74: $g(t)\le1-\sqrt{1-t^2}$. Apply this with
75: $t=2\sqrt{u(1-u)}$, for which
76: $1-\sqrt{1-t^2}=2\min(u,1-u)$, and divide by $\ln2$.
77: Continuity handles the endpoints. Multiplication by $m_S$ and
78: summation yield \eqref{eq:mixingbound}.
79: \end{proof}
80: 
81: \begin{lemma}[Concavity of the scalar profile]\label{lem:kappaconcavity}
82: The function $\kappa$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, symmetric about
83: $1/2$, and strictly concave on $(0,1)$. It satisfies
84: $\kappa(0)=\kappa(1)=0$ and $\kappa(1/2)=2$.
85: Consequently,
86: \begin{equation}\label{eq:kappabound}
87:  0\le\Delta_i\HH(f)\le\kappa(\I_i(f)).
88: \end{equation}
89: As $u\downarrow0$,
90: \begin{equation}\label{eq:kappasmall}
91:  \kappa(u)=u\log(1/u)+\frac{3u}{\ln2}+O(u^2).
92: \end{equation}
93: \end{lemma}
94: 
95: \begin{proof}
96: Put $t=2\sqrt{u(1-u)}$ and
97: $G(t)=1-h_2((1+t)/2)$. Away from the midpoint,
98: \[
99:  h_2''(u)=-\frac4{(\ln2)t^2},\qquad
100:  G'(t)=\frac{\atanh t}{\ln2},\qquad
101:  G''(t)=\frac1{(\ln2)(1-t^2)},
102: \]
103: \[
104:  t'=\frac{2(1-2u)}t,\qquad t''=-\frac4{t^3}.
105: \]
106: The contributions $h_2''(u)$ and $G''(t)(t')^2$ cancel, leaving
107: \begin{equation}\label{eq:kappasecond}
108:  \kappa''(u)=-
109:  \frac{4\atanh(2\sqrt{u(1-u)})}
110:  {(\ln2)(2\sqrt{u(1-u)})^3}<0.
111: \end{equation}
112: At the midpoint, with $u=1/2+x$, the expansion is
113: \[
114:  \kappa(1/2+x)
115:  =2-x^2\log(1/x^2)-\frac{3x^2}{\ln2}
116:        +O\bigl(x^4\log(1/|x|)\bigr).
117: \]
118: Thus the first derivative tends to zero on both sides; the
119: concavity on the two half-intervals joins across $1/2$.
120: Continuity and the endpoint values follow from
121: \eqref{eq:kappadef}. Jensen's inequality, together with
122: $\sum_Sm_S=1$ and $\sum_Sm_Su_S=\I_i(f)$, proves
123: \eqref{eq:kappabound}. Finally,
124: $h_2(u)=u\log(1/u)+u/\ln2+O(u^2)$ and
125: $G(2\sqrt{u(1-u)})=2u/\ln2+O(u^2)$ give
126: \eqref{eq:kappasmall}.
127: \end{proof}
128: 
129: Since a concave function vanishing at zero has a nonincreasing
130: ratio to its argument, any coordinate with $\I_i(f)\ge\tau>0$
131: satisfies
132: \begin{equation}\label{eq:kappacutoff}
133:  \Delta_i\HH(f)\le\frac{\kappa(\tau)}{\tau}\I_i(f).
134: \end{equation}
135: For example, $\kappa(1/16)/(1/16)=8.21872678758653\ldots$.
136: The logarithm in \eqref{eq:kappasmall} cannot be replaced by a
137: uniform constant for every coordinate, as
138: Proposition~\ref{prop:bentsplice} below shows.
139: 
140: \subsection{Valid induction and block exposure}
141: 
142: The exact influence identity is
143: \begin{equation}\label{eq:restrictionI}
144:  \I(f)=\I_i(f)+\frac{\I(f_i^+)+\I(f_i^-)}2.
145: \end{equation}
146: Indeed, restriction averages preserve the influence of every
147: other coordinate, while coordinate $i$ disappears.
148: 
149: \begin{proposition}[A suitable-coordinate induction]\label{prop:restrictioninduction}
150: Suppose a class of Boolean functions is closed under restrictions
151: and each nonconstant member has a relevant coordinate $i$ with
152: $\Delta_i\HH(f)\le C\I_i(f)$. Then every member satisfies
153: $\HH(f)\le C\I(f)$.
154: 
155: Existence of a finite global FEI constant is also equivalent to
156: existence of a finite $K$ such that every nonconstant Boolean
157: function has a relevant coordinate satisfying $B_i(f)\le K\I_i(f)$.
158: A global FEI constant $C$ permits $K=C$; the latter erasure
159: inequality implies FEI with constant $K+2/\ln2$.
160: \end{proposition}
161: 
162: \begin{proof}
163: The first assertion follows by induction on the number of
164: coordinates, using \eqref{eq:restrictionI}.
165: For the second, the entropy chain rule gives
166: \begin{equation}\label{eq:hanerasure}
167:  \sum_i B_i(f)\le\HH(f),
168: \end{equation}
169: because conditioning on all other coordinates can only decrease
170: each summand of a fixed chain rule. Under $\HH(f)\le C\I(f)$,
171: some positive-influence coordinate must have $B_i(f)\le C\I_i(f)$.
172: Conversely, \eqref{eq:mixingbound} turns the proposed erasure
173: charge into a suitable-coordinate entropy charge, to which the
174: first assertion applies.
175: \end{proof}
176: 
177: The quantifier is existential at every restricted function.
178: Neither an arbitrary coordinate nor a preferred coordinate rule
179: is justified by Proposition~\ref{prop:restrictioninduction}.
180: The implication is hereditary: it is not the pointwise
181: inequality
182: \[
183:  \HH(f)\le\sum_iB_i(f)+\frac2{\ln2}\I(f),
184: \]
185: which is false for the coded examples in~\cite{BrainErasure}.
186: Theorem~\ref{thm:simultaneous} and Lemma~\ref{lem:plateau}
187: also show how finite depths of restrictions can preserve entropy
188: while revealing no local entropy cost.
189: 
190: More generally, consider a finite adaptive restriction tree, and
191: choose its branches by the uniform input. If $f_v$ is the function
192: at a visited internal node and $i(v)$ its split coordinate, then
193: \begin{align}
194:  \HH(f)&=\E\sum_{v\ {\rm internal\ on\ the\ path}}
195:                \Delta_{i(v)}\HH(f_v)
196:               +\E\HH(f_{\rm leaf}),\label{eq:treetelescopeH}\\
197:  \I(f)&=\E\sum_{v\ {\rm internal\ on\ the\ path}}
198:                \I_{i(v)}(f_v)
199:               +\E\I(f_{\rm leaf}).\label{eq:treetelescopeI}
200: \end{align}
201: These identities permit amortized charges and stopping at classes
202: with established FEI bounds, as in~\cite{GMP26}. They require the
203: cost along the whole path to be controlled.
204: 
205: \begin{proposition}[Exact block increment]\label{prop:blockincrement}
206: Fix a block $W\subseteq[n]$. For $T\subseteq W^c$ put
207: \[
208:  q_T=\sum_{U\subseteq W}\wh f(T\cup U)^2,\qquad
209:  \phi_T(y)=q_T^{-1/2}
210:        \sum_{U\subseteq W}\wh f(T\cup U)\chi_U(y),
211: \]
212: omitting $q_T=0$. Then
213: \begin{equation}\label{eq:blockincrement}
214:  \HH(f)-\E_y\HH(f|_{W=y})
215:  =H(\mathcal S\cap W\mid\mathcal S\cap W^c)
216:    +\sum_Tq_T\operatorname{Ent}_2(\phi_T^2),
217: \end{equation}
218: where
219: $\operatorname{Ent}_2 Z=\E[Z\log(Z/\E Z)]$. Moreover,
220: \begin{equation}\label{eq:blockmixing}
221:  0\le\sum_Tq_T\operatorname{Ent}_2(\phi_T^2)
222:  \le\frac2{\ln2}\sum_{i\in W}\I_i(f).
223: \end{equation}
224: \end{proposition}
225: 
226: \begin{proof}
227: Let $p_T(U)=\wh f(T\cup U)^2/q_T$. Then
228: $\E\phi_T^2=1$, and the original entropy is
229: $H(q)+\sum_Tq_TH(p_T)$. The restricted coefficient indexed by
230: $T$ is $\sqrt{q_T}\phi_T(y)$, so its entropy averaged over $y$
231: is
232: \[
233:  H(q)-\sum_Tq_T\operatorname{Ent}_2(\phi_T^2).
234: \]
235: Subtracting proves \eqref{eq:blockincrement}. The uniform-cube
236: logarithmic Sobolev inequality~\cite{OD14}, in the normalization
237: $D_jg=(g|_{x_j=1}-g|_{x_j=-1})/2$, states
238: \[
239:  \operatorname{Ent}_2(g^2)
240:  \le\frac2{\ln2}\sum_j\E(D_jg)^2.
241: \]
242: Applying it to each $\phi_T$ and using Parseval gives
243: \[
244:  \sum_Tq_T\sum_{i\in W}\E(D_i\phi_T)^2
245:  =\sum_{T,U}|U|\wh f(T\cup U)^2
246:  =\sum_{i\in W}\I_i(f),
247: \]
248: which proves \eqref{eq:blockmixing}.
249: \end{proof}
250: 
251: Block exposure therefore retains a conditional erasure charge.
252: Taking $W=[n]$ makes that charge $\HH(f)$ itself. Small erasure
253: for bounded blocks at the initial function does not replace the
254: hereditary requirement, because the separated supports in
255: Lemma~\ref{lem:plateau} have zero entropy increment under all
256: sufficiently shallow restrictions.

258: \subsection{Character-centered budgets}
259: 
260: Define
261: \[
262:  \D(f)=\sum_j t(\I_j(f)),\qquad t(u)=\min(u,1-u).
263: \]
264: Character multiplication preserves entropy and complements the
265: influences on its selected coordinates. Hence
266: $\D(f)=\min_U\I(\chi_Uf)$ and $\D$ is character invariant.
267: For a split define
268: \[
269:  d_i^{\D}(f)=\D(f)
270:             -\frac{\D(f_i^+)+\D(f_i^-)}2.
271: \]
272: 
273: \begin{proposition}[Centered budget and its collapse]\label{prop:centerbudget}
274: For every $f,i$,
275: \begin{align}
276:  d_i^{\D}(f)
277:  ={}&t(\I_i(f))\notag\\
278:  &+\sum_{j\ne i}
279:  \left[t(\I_j(f))
280:        -\frac{t(\I_j(f_i^+))+t(\I_j(f_i^-))}2\right]
281:  \ge t(\I_i(f)).\label{eq:centerbudget}
282: \end{align}
283: Both $d_i^{\D}$ and $\Delta_i\HH$ are character invariant.
284: If $\max_j\I_j(f)\le1/4$, then
285: \begin{equation}\label{eq:centerbudgetcollapse}
286:  d_i^{\D}(f)=\I_i(f)\qquad\text{for every }i.
287: \end{equation}
288: \end{proposition}
289: 
290: \begin{proof}
291: For $j\ne i$, the two child influences average to $\I_j(f)$.
292: Concavity of $t$ proves \eqref{eq:centerbudget}.
293: Restriction commutes with character multiplication up to a
294: character and an output sign, proving the invariances.
295: Each child influence is at most twice its parent's influence.
296: Under the stated upper bound, all parent and child influences
297: are at most $1/2$, so $\D=\I$ at these three nodes.
298: Equation~\eqref{eq:restrictionI} now gives
299: \eqref{eq:centerbudgetcollapse}.
300: \end{proof}
301: 
302: A suitable-coordinate inequality
303: $\Delta_i\HH\le C d_i^{\D}$ would telescope to
304: $\HH\le C\D$, stopping at characters, whose entropy is zero.
305: The centered budget is sometimes larger than the original
306: budget: for a two-input conjunction it reduces the local ratio
307: from four to two. It supplies no improvement in
308: \eqref{eq:centerbudgetcollapse}. More generally, if
309: $\max_j\I_j(f)\le2^{-(r+1)}$, every restriction fixing at most
310: $r$ coordinates has influences at most $1/2$, so the first
311: $r$ levels have no centering gain. This follows by iterating
312: the factor-two bound. The low-influence reductions
313: in~\cite{BrainCritical} and Theorem~\ref{thm:simultaneous}
314: therefore remain applicable.
315: 
316: \subsection{Bad coordinates with arbitrarily small influences}
317: 
318: The following bent-splice mechanism also underlies the local
319: allocation obstruction in~\cite{BrainAllocation}.
320: 
321: \begin{proposition}[Bent splice]\label{prop:bentsplice}
322: For each even $m\ge4$, there is a Boolean function $F$ on $m+1$
323: coordinates, with distinguished coordinate $z$, such that
324: \[
325:  \I_z(F)=2^{-m},\qquad
326:  \I_j(F)=\frac12+2^{-m}\quad(1\le j\le m),
327: \]
328: and
329: \begin{equation}\label{eq:bentspliceratio}
330:  \frac{\Delta_z\HH(F)}{\I_z(F)}
331:  \ge\log\!\left(2^m-2\cdot2^{m/2}+2\right)\ge m-1.
332: \end{equation}
333: \end{proposition}
334: 
335: \begin{proof}
336: Use binary coordinates for the $m$ base variables, with characters
337: $(-1)^{S\cdot x}$, and put
338: \[
339:  g(x)=(-1)^{x_1x_2+x_3x_4+\cdots+x_{m-1}x_m},\qquad
340:  h(x)=g(x)-2\mathbf1_{\{x=0\}}.
341: \]
342: Both are Boolean. Define $F(x,1)=g(x)$ and $F(x,-1)=h(x)$.
343: Set $N=2^m$, $\alpha=N^{-1}$ and $R=\sqrt N$.
344: The two-bit quadratic factor has four Fourier coefficients of
345: magnitude $1/2$; tensorization gives
346: $\wh g(S)\in\{-1/R,1/R\}$ for every $S$.
347: Each base influence of $g$ is $1/2$.
348: Since $g(0)=g(e_j)=1$, flipping the value at zero changes the
349: edge $\{0,e_j\}$ from agreeing to disagreeing, increasing
350: $\I_j(h)$ by $2/N$. Averaging the branches proves the displayed
351: influence identities.
352: 
353: For the $z$-split, the coefficient pairs are
354: $c_S=\wh g(S)-\alpha$ and $d_S=\alpha$. Thus
355: \[
356:  \frac{m_S}{d_S^2}=(\pm R-1)^2+1
357:                    \ge N-2\sqrt N+2.
358: \]
359: The $d_S^2\log(m_S/d_S^2)$ term alone gives
360: \[
361:  \Delta_z\HH(F)\ge B_z(F)
362:  \ge N\alpha^2\log(N-2\sqrt N+2).
363: \]
364: Since $\I_z(F)=\alpha$, the first inequality in
365: \eqref{eq:bentspliceratio} follows. The second follows from
366: $N-2\sqrt N+2-N/2=(\sqrt N-2)^2/2\ge0$.
367: \end{proof}
368: 
369: \begin{corollary}[Failure of two maximum-budget rules]\label{cor:badmaximum}
370: There are Boolean functions $\widetilde F_m$ with
371: $\max_i\I_i(\widetilde F_m)\le2^{-m}$ for which the unique
372: maximum-influence coordinate has local ratio at least $m-1$.
373: It is also the unique coordinate maximizing $d_i^{\D}$, with
374: the same ratio $\Delta_i\HH/d_i^{\D}$.
375: \end{corollary}
376: 
377: \begin{proof}
378: In Proposition~\ref{prop:bentsplice}, replace each base coordinate
379: by a majority on a disjoint block of
380: $K=2N^2+1$ bits, keeping $z$ unchanged. Its leaf influence is
381: \[
382:  \beta_K=\binom{K-1}{(K-1)/2}2^{-(K-1)}
383:  \le\frac1{\sqrt{(K+1)/2}}<\alpha.
384: \]
385: For completeness, the bound
386: $\binom{2t}{t}/4^t\le1/\sqrt{t+1}$ follows by induction:
387: the consecutive ratio is $(2t+1)/(2t+2)$, and the squared
388: comparison reduces to $3t+2\ge0$.
389: 
390: Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced} gives each new leaf influence
391: $(1/2+\alpha)\beta_K<\alpha$ and preserves $\I_z=\alpha$.
392: It also preserves $\Delta_z\HH$: the additional entropy
393: in the parent is $\sum_j\I_j(F)\HH(\Maj_K)$, and the
394: additional branch entropies average to the same quantity
395: by \eqref{eq:restrictionI} applied to the other coordinates.
396: Thus $z$ is the unique maximum-influence coordinate, and
397: \eqref{eq:bentspliceratio} persists.
398: All influences are at most $\alpha\le1/16$, so
399: Proposition~\ref{prop:centerbudget} gives
400: $d_i^{\D}=\I_i$ at every coordinate, proving the second assertion.
401: \end{proof}
402: 
403: These examples reject the two specified rules, not the
404: existential hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:restrictioninduction}.
405: The cost may still be paid by choosing a different coordinate
406: or by an amortized argument.

408: \subsection{Separable influence potentials}
409: 
410: \begin{proposition}[An obstruction to charging every split]\label{prop:separablepotential}
411: Let $\phi:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ satisfy $\phi(0)=0$,
412: $\phi(t)=O(t)$ as $t\downarrow0$, and have a finite left derivative
413: at $1/2$. Put $P_\phi(f)=\sum_j\phi(\I_j(f))$.
414: There is no finite $C>0$ such that
415: \begin{equation}\label{eq:falsepotentialcharge}
416:  \Delta_i\HH(f)\le C\left[
417:  P_\phi(f)-\frac{P_\phi(f_i^+)+P_\phi(f_i^-)}2\right]
418: \end{equation}
419: for every Boolean $f$ and every coordinate $i$.
420: The assertion remains false after adding to $P_\phi(f)$ any
421: real-valued function of $\E f$.
422: \end{proposition}
423: 
424: \begin{proof}
425: Before flipping zero in Proposition~\ref{prop:bentsplice},
426: multiply $g$ by the character on all its base coordinates.
427: Its spectrum remains flat, and its base influences remain
428: $1/2$. Now the edges from zero to $e_j$ disagree, so flipping
429: zero makes the other branch's influences $1/2-2\alpha$ and
430: the parent influences $1/2-\alpha$.
431: The selector still has influence $\alpha$, and the lower bound
432: $\Delta_z\HH\ge(m-1)\alpha$ is unchanged.
433: The potential drop is exactly
434: \[
435:  \phi(\alpha)+m\left[
436:  \phi(1/2-\alpha)
437:  -\frac{\phi(1/2)+\phi(1/2-2\alpha)}2\right].
438: \]
439: The bracket is $o(\alpha)$ by the one-sided derivative
440: assumption. Dividing the displayed expression by $m\alpha$
441: therefore gives a quantity tending to zero, whereas
442: $\Delta_z\HH/(m\alpha)\ge1-1/m$.
443: This contradicts \eqref{eq:falsepotentialcharge}.
444: 
445: Finally multiply the entire splice by a fresh independent sign.
446: Parent and both selector branches then have mean zero, so any
447: mean-only correction cancels. The new coordinate has influence
448: one at all three nodes, and its contribution to the potential
449: drop also cancels. Entropy and the selector increment are
450: unchanged.
451: \end{proof}
452: 
453: The assumptions include $\phi(t)=t$ and
454: $\phi(t)=\min(t,1-t)$. The proposition concerns a charge at
455: every coordinate; it does not rule out a suitable-coordinate
456: inequality, a nonseparable potential, or a global tree budget.

506: $\{0,1\}^4$ equal to $-1$ precisely at
507: $0000,0001,0010$. Its squared coefficients are $25/64$ once,
508: $9/64$ three times, and $1/64$ twelve times. At either of its
509: coordinates of influence $1/8$, the local ratio is
510: \[
511:  24-\frac{25}{4}\log 5-\frac94\log 3
512:  =5.92178378033138391753959094180872425614\ldots .

514: The exact formulas establish these witness values independently
515: of the numerical ordering. Larger self-composition examples
516: in~\cite{BrainCritical} require larger suitable-coordinate
517: constants, so the finite table cannot be used as a uniform
518: restriction bound.
