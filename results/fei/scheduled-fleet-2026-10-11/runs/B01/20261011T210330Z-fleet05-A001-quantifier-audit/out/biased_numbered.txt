1: \section{Product measures and obstructions to threshold normalization}
2: \label{biasfull:section}
3: 
4: The generalized FEI inequality of O'Donnell and Tan~\cite{ODT13}
5: uses a corrected spectral entropy under product measures. Symmetric
6: threshold gates satisfy this inequality under arbitrary product inputs
7: in~\cite{Threshold}. The normalization estimates used for those gates
8: cannot be extended to weighted thresholds without a different argument.
9: We give explicit counterexamples to both a conditional-total-correlation
10: estimate and its proposed pivotal-credit replacement. Their genuine
11: corrected entropy-to-deficit ratios tend to \(2\).
12: 
13: \subsection{Normalization and exact composition}
14: 
15: Let \(Y_i\in\{0,1\}\) be independent with
16: \(\P(Y_i=1)=q_i\in(0,1)\), and let
17: \[
18:  \phi_i(Y_i)=\frac{Y_i-q_i}{\sqrt{q_i(1-q_i)}},
19:  \qquad v_i=4q_i(1-q_i),\qquad v_S=\prod_{i\in S}v_i.
20: \]
21: For a Boolean sign function \(f\), put
22: \[
23:  P_f^\mu(S)=\wh f_\mu(S)^2,\qquad
24:  \I_\mu(f)=\sum_S|S|P_f^\mu(S),\qquad
25:  V_\mu(f)=\Var_\mu(f),
26:  \qquad \Delta_\mu(f)=\I_\mu(f)-V_\mu(f).
27: \]
28: Its corrected entropy is
29: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:hplus}
30:  \HH_\mu^+(f)=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}P_f^\mu(S)
31:                       \log\frac{v_S}{P_f^\mu(S)}.
32: \end{equation}
33: Every summand is nonnegative, since
34: \[
35:  |\wh f_\mu(S)|\le\E_\mu|\phi_S|=\sqrt{v_S}.
36: \]
37: Write
38: \[
39:  h_0(f)=(\E_\mu f)^2\log\frac1{(\E_\mu f)^2}.
40: \]
41: If \(\HH_\mu(f)\) denotes the ordinary Shannon entropy of
42: \(P_f^\mu\), then
43: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:rawcorrected}
44:  \HH_\mu^+(f)=\HH_\mu(f)-h_0(f)
45:                     +\sum_i\I_i^\mu(f)\log v_i.
46: \end{equation}
47: In particular, on a uniform cube
48: \(\HH^+(f)=\HH(f)-h_0(f)\).
49: 
50: We use the following exact form of disjoint composition.
51: 
52: \begin{lemma}[Product-measure composition]\label{biasfull:composition}
53: Let \(g_i\) be Boolean functions on disjoint variable blocks with product
54: input laws \(\mu_i\). Let \(p_i=\P(g_i=1)\), \(V_i=4p_i(1-p_i)>0\), and
55: let \(\nu\) be the product law with these output probabilities.
56: For \(F=G(g_1,\ldots,g_k)\), set \(a_i=\I_i^\nu(G)/V_i\).
57: Then
58: \begin{align}
59:  \HH_\mu^+(F)&=\HH_\nu^+(G)+\sum_i a_i\HH_{\mu_i}^+(g_i),
60:        \label{biasfull:comph}\\
61:  \I_\mu(F)&=\sum_i a_i\I_{\mu_i}(g_i),
62:        \label{biasfull:compi}\\
63:  \Delta_\mu(F)&=\Delta_\nu(G)+\sum_i a_i\Delta_{\mu_i}(g_i).
64:        \label{biasfull:compdelta}
65: \end{align}
66: Constant children may be frozen and deleted.
67: \end{lemma}
68: \begin{proof}
69: For a nonempty spectral set, its occupied blocks form a unique set \(A\),
70: and its nonempty intersections with those blocks are \(T_i\), \(i\in A\).
71: The coefficient is
72: \[
73:  \wh F\Bigl(\bigcup_{i\in A}T_i\Bigr)
74:    =\wh G_\nu(A)\prod_{i\in A}\frac{\wh g_i(T_i)}{\sqrt{V_i}}.
75: \]
76: The squared coefficient is therefore the outer spectral mass times the
77: conditional nonempty spectral masses of the occupied children.
78: Taking the logarithm in~\eqref{biasfull:hplus} proves
79: \eqref{biasfull:comph}; summing degrees proves~\eqref{biasfull:compi}.
80: Finally \(V_\mu(F)=V_\nu(G)\), so subtraction proves
81: \eqref{biasfull:compdelta}.
82: \end{proof}
83: 
84: For an increasing event \(A\), write \(f=2\mathbf1_A-1\),
85: \(\alpha=\mu(A)\), and let \(\rho_i\) be its pivotal probability.
86: Then
87: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:pivotal}
88:  \I_i^\mu(f)=4q_i(1-q_i)\rho_i,\qquad
89:  \E_{\mu(\cdot\mid A)}Y_i-q_i
90:        =\frac{\I_i^\mu(f)}{4\alpha}.
91: \end{equation}
92: Both identities follow by conditioning on \(Y_i\).
93: For a distribution \(Q\) on a finite product set, its total correlation,
94: in nats, is
95: \[
96:  \TC(Q)=\KL\left(Q\,\middle\|\,\bigotimes_iQ_i\right)
97:        =\sum_i H_{\rm nat}(Q_i)-H_{\rm nat}(Q).
98: \]
99: If \(Q=\mu(\cdot\mid A)\), the product structure of \(\mu\) gives
100: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:tcdecomp}
101:  \ln\frac1\alpha
102:    =\TC(Q)+\sum_i\KL(Q_i\|\mu_i).
103: \end{equation}

105: \subsection{Rare disjoint tribes}
106: 
107: \begin{proposition}\label{biasfull:tribes}
108: Let
109: \[
110:  A_m=\bigvee_{j=1}^m(X_j\wedge Z_j),\qquad f_m=2\mathbf1_{A_m}-1,
111: \]
112: where the \(2m\) inputs are independent Bernoulli \(q=m^{-\gamma}\)
113: bits and \(1/2<\gamma<1\).
114: Writing \(\alpha=\P(A_m)\), \(V=V_\mu(f_m)\), and \(Q=\mu(\cdot\mid A_m)\),
115: one has
116: \begin{align}
117:  \frac{\alpha\TC(Q)}{\I_\mu(f_m)}
118:      &\sim\frac{2\gamma-1}{8}\ln m,\label{biasfull:tribestc}\\
119:  \frac{V\ln(1/V)-\sum_i\I_i^\mu(f_m)\ln^+(4\rho_i/V)}
120:            {\I_\mu(f_m)}
121:      &\sim\frac{2\gamma-1}{2}\ln m,\label{biasfull:tribescredit}\\
122:  \frac{\HH_\mu^+(f_m)}{\Delta_\mu(f_m)}
123:      &\longrightarrow2.\label{biasfull:tribesratio}
124: \end{align}
125: For all sufficiently large \(m\), every logarithmic credit in
126: \eqref{biasfull:tribescredit} is zero.
127: \end{proposition}
128: \begin{proof}
129: Put \(b=q^2\), \(a=(1-b)^m\), \(r=(1-b)^{m-1}\), and
130: \(\alpha=1-a\). Each pivotal probability is \(qr\), whence
131: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:tribesIV}
132:  \I_\mu(f_m)=8mq^2(1-q)r,\qquad V=4\alpha a.
133: \end{equation}
134: Since \(mq^2\to0\) and \(mq\to\infty\),
135: \[
136:  \alpha\sim mq^2,\qquad
137:  \I_\mu(f_m)\sim8\alpha,\qquad
138:  V\sim4\alpha,\qquad \Delta_\mu(f_m)\sim4\alpha.
139: \]
140: The logarithm arguments are exactly
141: \[
142:  \frac{4\rho_i}{V}=\frac{q}{\alpha(1-q^2)}
143:        \sim\frac1{mq}\longrightarrow0.
144: \]
145: This proves~\eqref{biasfull:tribescredit} and its final assertion.
146: 
147: Every conditional marginal under \(Q\) has mean \(q+d\), where
148: \[
149:  d=\frac{q^2(1-q)(1-q^2)^{m-1}}\alpha\sim\frac1m.
150: \]
151: Equation~\eqref{biasfull:tcdecomp} gives
152: \[
153:  \TC(Q)=\ln(1/\alpha)
154:        -2m\KL\bigl(\operatorname{Ber}(q+d)\|
155:                          \operatorname{Ber}(q)\bigr).
156: \]
157: The inequality \(\ln t\le t-1\) implies
158: \[
159:  \KL\bigl(\operatorname{Ber}(q+d)\|
160:                          \operatorname{Ber}(q)\bigr)
161:        \le\frac{d^2}{q(1-q)}.
162: \]
163: Thus the subtracted term is \(O((mq)^{-1})=o(1)\).
164: This proves~\eqref{biasfull:tribestc}.
165: 
166: For~\eqref{biasfull:tribesratio}, the sign version \(g\) of a single
167: two-input conjunction has
168: \begin{align}
169:  \Delta_\mu(g)&=4q^2(1-q)^2,\notag\\
170:  \HH_\mu^+(g)&=8q^2(1-q)^2
171:                   +16q^3(1-q)\log(1/q).
172:                     \label{biasfull:andpair}
173: \end{align}
174: These follow by listing its two singleton masses
175: \(4q^3(1-q)\) and its two-coordinate mass \(4q^2(1-q)^2\).
176: The outer OR gate has \(m\) inputs of probability \(b\).
177: Its variance and influence are \(V_o=4a\alpha\) and
178: \(\I_o=4mba\), and direct summation of its spectrum gives
179: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:orentropy}
180:  \HH_o^+=2(\I_o-V_o)
181:            +8ma(\alpha-b)\log\frac1{1-b}.
182: \end{equation}
183: Indeed its nonempty spectral mass at a set of size \(k\) is
184: \(4a^2[b/(1-b)]^k\).
185: The union bound and the first two Bonferroni terms give
186: \[
187:  0\le mb-\alpha\le\binom m2b^2,\qquad
188:  0\le\alpha-b\le mb.
189: \]
190: Using \(-\ln(1-b)\le b/(1-b)\) in~\eqref{biasfull:orentropy}
191: yields \(\HH_o^+=O(m^2b^2)=O(\alpha^2)\).
192: Every child multiplier in~\eqref{biasfull:comph} is \(r\).
193: Consequently
194: \[
195:  \HH_\mu^+(f_m)=\HH_o^++mr\HH_\mu^+(g)
196:        \sim8mq^2\sim8\alpha,
197: \]
198: which proves~\eqref{biasfull:tribesratio}.
199: \end{proof}

201: \subsection{An asymmetric weighted threshold}
202: 
203: The preceding example is monotone but not generally a weighted threshold.
204: The following calculation gives a counterexample within that smaller class.
205: Let
206: \[
207:  h(u)=-u\ln u-(1-u)\ln(1-u)
208: \]
209: denote binary entropy in nats.
210: 
211: \begin{lemma}[Two-branch conditional law]\label{biasfull:mixture}
212: Fix \(m\ge1\), \(q,\eta\in(0,1)\), and let
213: \[
214:  a=q^m,\qquad
215:  q_0=\frac{(1-\eta)a}{\eta+(1-\eta)a}.
216: \]
217: Take independent \(X_0\sim\operatorname{Ber}(q_0)\) and
218: \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\sim\operatorname{Ber}(q)\), and set
219: \[
220:  A=\left\{mX_0+\sum_{j=1}^mX_j\ge m\right\},
221:  \qquad f=2\mathbf1_A-1,\qquad \alpha=\P(A).
222: \]
223: Then
224: \begin{align}
225:  \alpha&=\frac{a}{\eta+(1-\eta)a},\label{biasfull:alphamix}\\
226:  \TC(\mu(\cdot\mid A))
227:    &=m\{h(\eta+(1-\eta)q)-(1-\eta)h(q)\},
228:        \label{biasfull:tcmix}\\
229:  \frac{\I_\mu(f)}{4\alpha}
230:    &=1-\eta-q_0+m\eta(1-q).
231:        \label{biasfull:Imix}
232: \end{align}
233: \end{lemma}
234: \begin{proof}
235: The event is the disjoint union of \(\{X_0=1\}\) and
236: \(\{X_0=0,X_1=\cdots=X_m=1\}\).
237: Its probability gives~\eqref{biasfull:alphamix}.
238: Under the conditional law \(Q=\mu(\cdot\mid A)\),
239: \(\P_Q(X_0=0)=\eta\). Given \(X_0=1\), the light variables are
240: independent Bernoulli \(q\); given \(X_0=0\), they are all one.
241: Hence their marginal means are
242: \[
243:  \E_QX_0=1-\eta,\qquad
244:  \E_QX_j=\eta+(1-\eta)q\quad(1\le j\le m).
245: \]
246: The joint entropy is \(h(\eta)+(1-\eta)mh(q)\), while the sum of
247: marginal entropies is \(h(\eta)+mh(\eta+(1-\eta)q)\).
248: This proves~\eqref{biasfull:tcmix}.
249: Summing~\eqref{biasfull:pivotal} proves~\eqref{biasfull:Imix}.
250: \end{proof}
251: 
252: \begin{theorem}[Failure of weighted-threshold normalization estimates]
253: \label{biasfull:weightedcounter}
254: For every \(m\ge2\), specialize Lemma~\ref{biasfull:mixture} to
255: \[
256:  q=m^{-2},\qquad \eta=\frac1m,\qquad
257:  a=q^m,\qquad d=1+(m-1)a.
258: \]
259: Thus \(q_0=(m-1)a/d\) and \(\alpha=ma/d\).
260: For these weighted thresholds,
261: \begin{align}
262:  \frac{\alpha\TC(\mu(\cdot\mid A))}{\I_\mu(f)}
263:    &=\frac{\ln m+1}{8}
264:         +O\left(\frac{\ln m}{m}\right),\label{biasfull:weightedTC}\\
265:  \frac{V_\mu(f)\ln(1/V_\mu(f))
266:             -\sum_i\I_i^\mu(f)\ln^+(4\rho_i/V_\mu(f))}
267:         {\I_\mu(f)}
268:    &=\frac12\ln m-\ln2
269:         +O\left(\frac{\ln m}{m}\right),\label{biasfull:weightedcredit}\\
270:  \frac{\HH_\mu^+(f)}{\Delta_\mu(f)}
271:    &\longrightarrow2.\label{biasfull:weightedratio}
272: \end{align}
273: In particular, neither of the first two numerators is bounded above
274: by a universal constant times \(\I_\mu(f)\).
275: \end{theorem}
276: \begin{proof}
277: The light conditional mean is
278: \[
279:  r=\frac1m+\left(1-\frac1m\right)m^{-2}
280:      =m^{-1}+m^{-2}-m^{-3}.
281: \]
282: By~\eqref{biasfull:tcmix},
283: \[
284:  \TC(\mu(\cdot\mid A))=mh(r)-(m-1)h(q).
285: \]
286: The expansion
287: \(h(u)=u\ln(1/u)+u+O(u^2)\) as \(u\downarrow0\) gives
288: \[
289:  mh(r)=\ln m+1+O((\ln m)/m),\qquad
290:  (m-1)h(q)=O((\ln m)/m).
291: \]
292: Equation~\eqref{biasfull:Imix} is now
293: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:weightedinfluence}
294:  \frac{\I_\mu(f)}{4\alpha}
295:       =2-\frac1m-q-q_0.
296: \end{equation}
297: Together these prove~\eqref{biasfull:weightedTC}.
298: 
299: We compute the credit residual, since the large heavy-coordinate
300: credit must be subtracted before the remaining cost is assessed.
301: The pivotal probabilities and influences satisfy
302: \[
303:  \rho_0=1-a,\qquad
304:  \rho_{\rm light}=(1-q_0)q^{m-1},
305: \]
306: \[
307:  \frac{\I_0^\mu(f)}{4\alpha}=1-\frac1m-q_0,\qquad
308:  \frac{\sum_{j=1}^m\I_j^\mu(f)}{4\alpha}=1-q.
309: \]
310: Since \(V:=V_\mu(f)=4\alpha(1-\alpha)\), the logarithm arguments are
311: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:weightcreditargs}
312:  \frac{4\rho_0}{V}=\frac{1-a}{\alpha(1-\alpha)},\qquad
313:  \frac{4\rho_{\rm light}}{V}
314:       =\frac{1}{mq(1-\alpha)}=\frac{m}{1-\alpha}.
315: \end{equation}
316: Both exceed one for \(m\ge2\). Let
317: \[
318:  L=\ln(1/\alpha)=2m\ln m-\ln m+\ln d.
319: \]
320: Dividing the residual by \(4\alpha\) yields exactly
321: \begin{align}
322:  D_m:={}&\frac{V\ln(1/V)-\sum_i\I_i^\mu(f)\ln^+(4\rho_i/V)}
323:                     {4\alpha}\notag\\
324:  ={}&(1-\alpha)[L-\ln4-\ln(1-\alpha)]\notag\\
325:  &-\left(1-\frac1m-q_0\right)
326:                  [L+\ln(1-a)-\ln(1-\alpha)]\notag\\
327:  &-(1-q)[\ln m-\ln(1-\alpha)].
328:                        \label{biasfull:residualexact}
329: \end{align}
330: Here \(a=m^{-2m}\), \(q_0=O(ma)\), and \(\alpha=O(ma)\).
331: All terms containing \(aL\), \(q_0L\), or \(\alpha L\) tend to zero.
332: It follows that
333: \[
334:  D_m=\frac Lm-(1-q)\ln m-\ln4+o(1)
335:       =\ln m-\ln4+O((\ln m)/m).
336: \]
337: Dividing by~\eqref{biasfull:weightedinfluence} proves
338: \eqref{biasfull:weightedcredit}.
339: 
340: To prove~\eqref{biasfull:weightedratio}, let \(g\) be the sign version
341: of the conjunction of the \(m\) light variables. For \(S\ne\varnothing\),
342: \[
343:  P_g^\mu(S)=4a^2\left(\frac{1-q}{q}\right)^{|S|}.
344: \]
345: Summing its degree and its corrected entropy gives
346: \begin{align}
347:  \I_\mu(g)&=4ma(1-q),\notag\\
348:  \Delta_\mu(g)&=4a[m(1-q)-1+a],\notag\\
349:  \HH_\mu^+(g)&=2\Delta_\mu(g)
350:                  +8ma(q-a)\log(1/q).
351:                     \label{biasfull:andmany}
352: \end{align}
353: Consequently \(\Delta_\mu(g)\sim4ma\) and
354: \(\HH_\mu^+(g)\sim8ma\).
355: The outer OR gate has input probabilities \(q_0\) and \(a\).
356: Its deficit is
357: \[
358:  \Delta_o=4q_0a(1-q_0)(1-a)=O(ma^2).
359: \]
360: For an OR gate with probabilities \(x,y\), its corrected entropy is
361: \begin{align*}
362:  &8x(1-x)(1-y)^2\log\frac1{1-y}\\
363:  &\quad+8y(1-y)(1-x)^2\log\frac1{1-x}
364:        +8xy(1-x)(1-y).
365: \end{align*}
366: The inequality \(-\ln(1-u)\le u/(1-u)\) bounds this by
367: \((2+4/\ln2)\,4xy(1-x)(1-y)\).
368: Thus \(\HH_o^+=O(ma^2)\).
369: The heavy child is a dictator and has zero corrected entropy and
370: zero deficit. The light-block multiplier in
371: Lemma~\ref{biasfull:composition} is \(1-q_0\), so
372: \[
373:  \Delta_\mu(f)=\Delta_o+(1-q_0)\Delta_\mu(g)\sim4ma,\qquad
374:  \HH_\mu^+(f)=\HH_o^++(1-q_0)\HH_\mu^+(g)\sim8ma.
375: \]
376: This proves~\eqref{biasfull:weightedratio}.
377: \end{proof}
378: 
379: \begin{remark}\label{biasfull:creditremark}
380: Equation~\eqref{biasfull:weightedcredit} refutes precisely an estimate
381: with coefficient one on the pivotal credits and an additional
382: \(O(\I_\mu(f))\) term. It does not refute an inequality with a larger
383: multiplier on the credits. Replacing the fixed factor \(4\) inside
384: all logarithms by another fixed positive factor changes the residual
385: by only a bounded multiple of \(\I_\mu(f)\) for this family, and
386: therefore does not repair the coefficient-one estimate.
387: Neither~\eqref{biasfull:weightedTC} nor~\eqref{biasfull:weightedcredit}
388: is a counterexample to generalized FEI.
389: \end{remark}

391: \begin{proposition}[A local total-correlation obstruction]
392: \label{biasfull:localTC}
393: There is no universal \(K\) such that every increasing weighted
394: threshold event \(A\) has a coordinate \(j\) satisfying
395: \[
396:  \alpha\sum_{k\ne j} I_Q(X_j;X_k)\le K\I_j^\mu(2\mathbf1_A-1),
397:  \qquad Q=\mu(\cdot\mid A).
398: \]
399: Here the mutual informations are measured in nats.
400: \end{proposition}
401: \begin{proof}
402: In Lemma~\ref{biasfull:mixture}, take \(\eta=1/2\) and let
403: \(q\downarrow0\) with \(m\) fixed.
404: The conditional law converges to the equal mixture of
405: \((1,0,\ldots,0)\) and \((0,1,\ldots,1)\).
406: Hence every pairwise mutual information tends to \(\ln2\),
407: while~\eqref{biasfull:pivotal} gives
408: \(\I_j^\mu(f)/\alpha\to2\) for every \(j\).
409: Every proposed coordinate ratio therefore tends to \(m\ln2/2\).
410: Letting \(m\) increase proves the assertion.
411: \end{proof}
412: 
413: The exact recursion behind this failed proposal is
414: \[
415:  \TC(Q)=\E_{X_j}\TC(Q_{-j}\mid X_j)
416:                   +\sum_{k\ne j}I_Q(X_j;X_k).
417: \]
418: It follows by expanding the marginal and joint entropies.
419: The obstruction concerns the proposed payment of this recursion,
420: not the identity itself.

422: \subsection{What a uniform encoding of biased inputs transfers}
423: 
424: Let \(p_i\) be dyadic probabilities and choose arbitrary uniform-input
425: Boolean encoders \(g_i\) with \(\P(g_i=1)=p_i\), on disjoint blocks.
426: Let \(V_i=4p_i(1-p_i)\).
427: If ordinary uniform FEI is assumed with a constant \(C\), then
428: Lemma~\ref{biasfull:composition} applied to \(F=G(g_1,\ldots,g_k)\)
429: gives exactly
430: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:encoding}
431:  \HH_\nu^+(G)+h_0(F)
432:    \le\sum_i\I_i^\nu(G)R_C(g_i),\qquad
433:  R_C(g)=\frac{C\I(g)-\HH^+(g)}{\Var(g)}.
434: \end{equation}
435: The relevant encoder quantity is its entropy-influence slack per
436: unit variance, rather than its influence alone.
437: 
438: \begin{proposition}[Encoder slack]\label{biasfull:encoderslack}
439: For the sign version \(g\) of the uniform conjunction on \(k\) bits,
440: with \(p=2^{-k}\),
441: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:andencoder}
442:  R_C(g)=2+\left(\frac{C}{2(1-p)}-2\right)\log\frac1p.
443: \end{equation}
444: More generally, if uniform FEI holds with \(C_0\), then for every
445: \(C>C_0\) and every uniform Boolean encoder of probability
446: \(p\in(0,1/2]\),
447: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:encodergap}
448:  R_C(g)\ge\frac{C-C_0}{2(1-p)}\log\frac1p.
449: \end{equation}
450: Thus no choice of encoders makes the coefficients in
451: \eqref{biasfull:encoding} uniformly bounded when the constant used
452: has a fixed positive slack over a valid FEI constant.
453: \end{proposition}
454: \begin{proof}
455: For the conjunction, the nonempty squared coefficients are all
456: \(4p^2\). Thus
457: \[
458:  \I(g)=2kp,\qquad V=4p(1-p),\qquad
459:  \HH^+(g)=V(2k-2),
460: \]
461: which proves~\eqref{biasfull:andencoder}.
462: For an arbitrary encoder,
463: \[
464:  C\I(g)-\HH^+(g)
465:    =C\I(g)-\HH(g)+h_0(g)\ge(C-C_0)\I(g).
466: \]
467: The uniform edge-isoperimetric inequality gives
468: \(\I(g)\ge2p\log(1/p)\), proving~\eqref{biasfull:encodergap}.
469: 
470: For completeness, this form of edge isoperimetry follows by induction.
471: If a coordinate's two sections have probabilities \(p_0\le p_1\),
472: their other influences average, and the coordinate influence is at
473: least \(p_1-p_0\). Writing \(p_0=p(1-t)\), \(p_1=p(1+t)\), the
474: induction reduces to
475: \[
476:  (1-t)\log(1-t)+(1+t)\log(1+t)\le2t,\qquad0\le t\le1.
477: \]
478: The left side is convex, with endpoint values \(0,2\), and is
479: therefore below the chord \(2t\).
480: \end{proof}
481: 
482: This proposition is a limitation of the inference
483: \eqref{biasfull:encoding}. It does not rule out a different reduction,
484: additional structural information about the outer gate, or the
485: generalized FEI conjecture itself.

487: \subsection{A sharp loss in a monotone dual-rail embedding}
488: 
489: The following calculation makes explicit the product-measure cost of the monotone encoding considered in~\cite{BrainMonotone}.
490: 
491: Every Boolean function on \(\{0,1\}^n\) is the restriction of a
492: monotone function to the antichain
493: \[
494:  x\longmapsto(x_1,1-x_1,\ldots,x_n,1-x_n).
495: \]
496: For example, take the upward closure of the encoded inputs on
497: which the function equals one. The following bound quantifies the
498: loss when the ambient law is required to be a product law.
499: 
500: \begin{proposition}\label{biasfull:dualrail}
501: Let \(Y,Z\) be independent Bernoulli bits with
502: \[
503:  \P(Y=1\mid Y\ne Z)=p,\qquad t=\P(Y\ne Z).
504: \]
505: Then
506: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:railone}
507:  t\le\frac1{1+2\sqrt{p(1-p)}}.
508: \end{equation}
509: The bound is sharp. For independent pairs encoding independent
510: bits of probabilities \(p_1,\ldots,p_n\), the event \(E\) that every
511: pair is valid satisfies
512: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:railall}
513:  \P(E)\le\prod_{i=1}^n
514:        \bigl(1+2\sqrt{p_i(1-p_i)}\bigr)^{-1}.
515: \end{equation}
516: In particular, balanced decoding forces \(\P(E)\le2^{-n}\).
517: \end{proposition}
518: \begin{proof}
519: The valid cell probabilities are \(pt\) and \((1-p)t\).
520: If the invalid cell probabilities are \(u,v\), independence implies
521: \[
522:  uv=p(1-p)t^2,\qquad u+v=1-t.
523: \]
524: Thus \((1-t)^2\ge4p(1-p)t^2\), proving~\eqref{biasfull:railone}.
525: Equality is attained by
526: \[
527:  \P(Y=1)=\frac{\sqrt p}{\sqrt p+\sqrt{1-p}},
528:  \qquad \P(Z=1)=1-\P(Y=1).
529: \]
530: Independent pairs give~\eqref{biasfull:railall} by multiplication.
531: \end{proof}
532: 
533: Conditioning on all valid pairs preserves independence of the decoded
534: bits, but its relative-entropy cost is
535: \[
536:  \KL(\mu(\cdot\mid E)\|\mu)=\ln\frac1{\mu(E)}
537:  \ge\sum_i\ln\bigl(1+2\sqrt{p_i(1-p_i)}\bigr).
538: \]
539: This is at least \(n\ln2\) for balanced decoding. The proposition
540: concerns this embedding; it asserts no obstruction to all conceivable
541: monotone embeddings.
542: 
543: \subsection{The gate-potential formulation on its finite-cube domain}
544: 
545: Let \(\mathcal Q_2\) be the dyadic probabilities in \([0,1]\).
546: The following formulation of the gate potential in~\cite{BrainGate}
547: uses only probabilities realized by finite uniform Boolean functions.
548: Put \(V_p=4p(1-p)\), and let \(h_2\) denote binary entropy in bits.
549: 
550: \begin{proposition}\label{biasfull:gatepotential}
551: Uniform FEI holds with constant \(C\) if and only if there is a
552: symmetric function \(\Gamma:\mathcal Q_2\to[0,\infty)\), with
553: \(\Gamma(1/2)\le C\), such that every finite Boolean gate \(G\)
554: and every vector of interior dyadic input probabilities satisfy
555: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:gatecondition}
556:  \Gamma(p_{\rm out})+\HH_\nu(G)
557:  \le\sum_i\I_i^\nu(G)
558:          \frac{\Gamma(p_i)+h_2(V_{p_i})}{V_{p_i}}.
559: \end{equation}
560: Here \(p_{\rm out}=\P_\nu(G=1)\), which is dyadic. Deterministic
561: inputs are frozen and deleted.
562: \end{proposition}
563: \begin{proof}
564: For sufficiency, apply~\eqref{biasfull:gatecondition} to an arbitrary
565: Boolean function regarded as one gate with all input probabilities
566: \(1/2\). Since \(V_{1/2}=1\) and \(h_2(1)=0\),
567: \[
568:  \HH(G)+\Gamma(p_{\rm out})
569:       \le\Gamma(1/2)\I(G)\le C\I(G).
570: \]
571: 
572: Conversely, assume uniform FEI with \(C\) and define
573: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:gammaslack}
574:  \Gamma_C(p)=\inf_{\substack{g\ {\rm finite\ uniform\ Boolean}\\
575:                               \P(g=1)=p}}
576:                   \{C\I(g)-\HH(g)\}.
577: \end{equation}
578: The admissible set is nonempty for dyadic \(p\).
579: FEI makes the infimum nonnegative; a single example makes it finite.
580: Output negation gives symmetry, constants give the endpoint values
581: zero, and a dictator gives \(\Gamma_C(1/2)\le C\).
582: 
583: Choose disjoint child functions of the prescribed probabilities
584: whose slacks approach the infima. Their raw entropy composition is
585: \[
586:  \HH(F)=\HH_\nu(G)+
587:     \sum_i\frac{\I_i^\nu(G)}{V_{p_i}}
588:                   [\HH(g_i)-h_2(V_{p_i})].
589: \]
590: This follows either from the same spectral factorization as
591: Lemma~\ref{biasfull:composition}, or by conditioning each child
592: spectrum on being nonempty. Consequently
593: \[
594:  C\I(F)-\HH(F)=
595:  \sum_i\frac{\I_i^\nu(G)}{V_{p_i}}
596:       [C\I(g_i)-\HH(g_i)+h_2(V_{p_i})]-\HH_\nu(G).
597: \]
598: The left side is at least \(\Gamma_C(p_{\rm out})\).
599: Letting the finitely many child slacks tend to their infima proves
600: \eqref{biasfull:gatecondition}.
601: \end{proof}
602: 
603: No finite uniform Boolean function has a nondyadic output probability.
604: Thus the infimum in~\eqref{biasfull:gammaslack} does not directly
605: define an all-real-bias potential; that extension would need an
606: additional argument. The dyadic formulation already suffices for
607: equivalence to FEI on all finite uniform cubes.
608: 
609: For comparison with the encoder bound,
610: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:encoderpotential}
611:  \inf_{\P(g=1)=p}R_C(g)
612:    =\frac{\Gamma_C(p)+h_0(p)}{V_p},
613:  \qquad
614:  h_0(p)=(1-V_p)\log\frac1{1-V_p}.
615: \end{equation}
616: This identity follows from \(\HH^+(g)=\HH(g)-h_0(p)\).
617: If FEI holds with \(C_1<C_2\), edge isoperimetry also gives
618: \[
619:  \Gamma_{C_2}(p)\ge\Gamma_{C_1}(p)
620:                  +2(C_2-C_1)p\log(1/p),\qquad p\le\tfrac12.
621: \]
622: 
623: Finally, balanced gates retain the complete optimal-constant question:
624: \begin{equation}\label{biasfull:balancedoptimal}
625:  C_{\rm FEI}
626:   =\sup_{\substack{g\ {\rm balanced}\\\I(g)>1}}
627:                        \frac{\HH(g)}{\I(g)-1}.
628: \end{equation}
629: Indeed, balanced self-composition of a gate with influence \(a>1\)
630: and entropy \(h\) gives ratios tending to \(h/(a-1)\).
631: Conversely, for any nonconstant \(f\), the balanced function
632: \(g=z\prod_{j=1}^r f(x^{(j)})\), with a fresh uniform sign \(z\),
633: satisfies \(\HH(g)=r\HH(f)\) and
634: \(\I(g)-1=r\I(f)\). A balanced function of influence one is a
635: signed dictator and has zero entropy.
636: In particular, whenever FEI holds with \(C\),
637: \(\Gamma_C(1/2)=C\).
638: 
639: The quantifier over every gate in~\eqref{biasfull:gatecondition}
640: already contains unrestricted FEI at uniform input. A proof for a
641: restricted family of gates supplies a composition theorem for that
642: family; it does not remove this universal quantifier.
