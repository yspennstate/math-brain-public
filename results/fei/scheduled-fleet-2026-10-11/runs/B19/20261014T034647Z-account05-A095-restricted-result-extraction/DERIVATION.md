# Support-completed inheritance across an arbitrary-core point edit

All costs and KL are bits. This is an explicit scoped C020 continuation, not a universal arbitrary-core reference construction. The new mechanism changes the reference after the edit rather than assuming that an inherited reference also pays newly created Fourier support.

## 1. Actual Boolean objects, support and one physical budget

Fix k>=1, a Boolean core g on k uniform bits, and ANY prescribed normalized reference R positive on the actual squared Fourier support S, with finite cost c=CE(P_g,R). Append m>=0 dummy bits, n=k+m, and flip the full actual vertex a of the n-cube: h_a=g-2g(a_x)1_a. Let M_a=(g+h_a)/2+z(g-h_a)/2, with z fresh. Put delta=2^-n and s=|S|. Let j(a_x) be the number of originally pivotal incident core edges. The exact physical identities are

    I(h_a)=I(g)+2delta[n-2j],
    I(M_a)=I(g)+delta[n+1-2j], Inf_z(M_a)=delta.             (1)

Thus destroyed edges enter with their actual negative sign. No slack is regenerated by relabeling them as newly available influence. Core mean, entropy and Fourier magnitudes need not be flat or balanced.

Let A_T=ghat(T), eta_T=g(a_x)chi_T(a_x), and let L consist of selector-absent, padding-absent subsets T in S. On L, Mhat=A_T-delta eta_T. On every other parent Fourier atom its magnitude is delta: selector-present atoms always have this magnitude; selector-absent padding-containing atoms and old zero-core atoms do too. Fourier inversion gives sum_S A_T eta_T=1. Therefore

    P_M(L)=1-2delta+s delta^2=1-w,
    w=(2^(n+1)-s)delta^2=2delta-s delta^2 <=2delta.          (2)

The coefficients include all new support, even when the original R assigned those atoms zero mass. The selector halves the point-edit shift; Boolean Fourier granularity prevents cancellation of an old nonzero coefficient, so M has full Fourier support.

## 2. One normalized full-output reference

First calibrate R to S: R_S(T)=R(T)/R(S), T in S, and zero otherwise. This is categorical calibration to the true support, chosen from fixed core instance data before spectral sampling. Its cost c_S=c+log2 R(S)<=c; its saving is exactly -log2 R(S), not an ignored conditional normalizer.

Define the complete reference

    Q_M(T)=(1-w)R_S(T) on L,
    Q_M(T)=delta^2 on the complement of L.                (3)

There are exactly 2^(n+1)-s complementary atoms. Equations (2)-(3) prove sum Q_M=1 and positivity on every true parent atom. At n=1 the old mass is still positive; 1-w=sum_S(A_T-delta eta_T)^2>0 because a nonzero cube coefficient has magnitude at least 2^(1-k)>delta. The original off-support core mass is accounted for by the exact categorical saving above. Q is a single reference, not a subprobability or a zero-injection assumption. Its construction uses only the original support S, prescribed R and dimensions; it does not substitute the arbitrary edited spectrum as a supposedly influence-paid reference.

Writing ell_T=log2(1/R_S(T))>=0, the exact complete cost and debt are

    CE_M=sum_S(A_T-delta eta_T)^2 ell_T
          -(1-w)log2(1-w)+2n w,
    D(P_M||Q_M)=(1-w) D(P_M(.|L)||R_S).                 (4)

The new sector is matched exactly; the entire old conditional divergence remains. No arbitrary terminal bill is declared zero. Both old squared masses and their signed perturbation are retained in (4).

## 3. A certified source shock, valid for arbitrary nonuniform R

Every nonzero Fourier coefficient of a k-bit Boolean sign has magnitude at least 2^(1-k). This follows directly from the integer character sum: for k>=1 the sum of 2^k signs is even. Consequently

    sum_S |A_T|ell_T <=2^(k-1)c_S,
    sum_S ell_T <=2^(2k-2)c_S.

Expanding the square in (4), without assuming a favorable sign, proves

    sum_S(A_T-delta eta_T)^2 ell_T
      <=[1+2^k delta+2^(2k-2)delta^2]c_S.

The remaining sector term obeys -(1-w)log2(1-w)<=w/ln2. Since delta<=2^-k and 2/ln2<3, we obtain the fully explicit bound

    CE_M <=c_S+delta[(5/4)2^k c_S+4n+3]
         <=c+delta[(5/4)2^k c+4n+3].                    (5)

This controls the nonuniform reference shock rather than assuming the original R also has finite or small cost on the edited child's new support. Calibration savings remain available but are not needed in the final upper bound.

## 4. Exactly one named source bank and a complete class theorem

For a target price K>=4 let S_K=K I(g)-c. Combining (1) and (5) gives the sufficient source-owned payment certificate

    K I(M_a)-CE_M >= S_K
      -delta[(5/4)2^k c+(4-K)n+K(2j-1)+3].             (6)

The source cost appears once, the lost physical edges appear once, and the reference shock is quantitatively paid once. This is an inequality with a proved surcharge, not the identity S=KI-H renamed as a theorem. It is uniform over edit vertices and all padding widths.

As a useful unconditional consequence GIVEN a supplied core certificate c<=K0 I(g) with K0>=0, choose K>=4 with K>K0. For nonconstant g, every nonzero coordinate influence is at least 2^(1-k), so I(g)>=2^(1-k). Using j<=k and dropping the favorable (K-4)n term in (6), the total requested reserve is at most

    2^-m[(5/4)K0+kK+(3-K)/2] I(g).

Thus, for every core and prescribed reference satisfying c<=K0 I(g), ALL edit vertices and ALL m satisfying

    2^-m[(5/4)K0+kK+(3-K)/2] <=K-K0                  (7)

have the normalized complete reference CE_M<=K I(M_a). This is an explicit arbitrary-core class theorem, uniform in padding dimension and nonuniform reference shape. At fixed admitted K0 and target K>K0, condition (7) needs only O(log k) padding, while the output price K is absolute across growing core dimensions. It supplies no universal core admission. For constant cores R_S is a point mass, c_S=0,j=0, and (5) already pays CE_M<4I(M_a) at every m without (7).

Concrete corollary: if c<=4I(g), K=6 is paid whenever 2^m>=3k+7/4. Thus ANY product g of disjoint point-defect signs (arbitrary widths and number, k the total physical core width) has a full-support one-point-selector perturbation at EVERY vertex with CE<=6I after this logarithmic padding. Its admitted core reference is the explicit occupied-block/uniform-local-subset product from A095@2, with c=H(g)<4I(g). Width-one character factors have H=0 and also satisfy the bound. The product reference retains all occupied-block selection costs; it is not the failed global-order rank code. The new point defect overlaps every original core block, so this is a genuine extra shared-core edit of the earlier disjoint class. Tensoring any independent supplied terminal and fresh activation preserves the entire terminal CE under Section 5.

## 5. Arbitrary independent terminal and fresh activation

For ANY Boolean q on an independent cube and prescribed normalized positive-support finite-cost R_q, use R_q tensor Q_M. Its complete cost is c_q+CE_M, exact debt is D_q+(1-w)D(P_M(.|L)||R_S), and physical influence is I(q)+I(M_a). The terminal c_q is retained, not spent to pay (6). If (6) or (7) pays M at price K and c_q<=C I(q), this closes max(C,K)I for the product.

Under r>=1 fresh point controls, p=2^-r, mu=E(qM_a), empty-calibrate this product reference to its actual empty squared coefficient and use the A095@3 biased activation reference. The full output cost is

    CE_F<=p c_q+p K I(M_a)+6rp(1-mu),
    I(F)=p[I(q)+I(M_a)+r(1-mu)],
    D_F=p[D_q+D_M-D_Ber(P_qM(empty)||R_qM(empty))].

Every complement normalizer and conditional debt remains; the Bernoulli subtraction is the actual calibration saving. The core bank in (6) has already been spent inside M; the new controller budget is fresh. Conditional terminal admission closes max(C,K,6)I(F). Ideal code costs have separate integer rounding.

## 6. Boundary and shortest universal gap

For a sparse original spectrum, the unmodified inherited full reference may assign arbitrarily small mass to a newly created atom. Its edited-child cost can then be arbitrarily large even when the original core CE certificate is fixed. An exact fixed-function witness is g(x)=(-1)^x, h the flip at x=0 (constant -1), R(empty)=2^-b, R({x})=1-2^-b, b>=1. Then c_g=-log2(1-2^-b)<=1, I(g)=1, and c_h=b. The same-R selector lift has cost 1+(c_g+b)/2 although its actual H=2,I=1. At b=8 its complete cost exceeds5 and defeats a price4 source bank; as b grows it diverges with unchanged Boolean functions. This is a prescribed-reference shock obstruction, not FEI or an entropy counterfamily. Equation (3) instead yields the uniform exact four-atom reference of cost2 on this example.

Equation (3) explicitly completes the support and prevents that failure, but (5)'s coefficient 2^k still depends on the core dimension. The first unsupported universal line is replacing this support-granularity shock (or its exact signed ell-profile in (4)) by a dimension-independent payment from a nonreused child reserve for arbitrary growing cores. Our theorem pays that surcharge under the explicit padding/slack hypothesis (7); it does not prove a universal supplied-core FEI bound or preserve a zero-margin sharp price under every edit.
