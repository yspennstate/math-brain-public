# Independently selected challenge regimes

1. **Arbitrary core signs.** Direct Walsh reconstruction used all Boolean
   cores in the exhaustive small cases.  The squared law remained uniform on
   \((a+\operatorname{im}M)\times\mathbb F_2^m\).
2. **Physical basis changes.** Every full-column-rank matrix through
   \((s,m)=(4,3)\) and every affine \(x\)-shift was checked through the exact
   support formula.  No coordinate-invariance assumption was used.
3. **Rectangular redundancy.** Duplicate and dependent nonzero physical rows,
   as well as zero rows with both affine states, were included.  Their true
   physical influence—not matrix rank alone—was charged.
4. **Intersecting rare cylinders.** These are absorbed into arbitrary
   \(D(y)\).  The proof never assigns separate credits to their upper filters.
5. **Reveal order and pruning.** One-step restrictions and branch-dependent
   two-level adaptive trees were reconstructed directly.  Signed local
   charges telescoped to the same endpoint difference.
6. **Terminal factor.** Direct spectra verified
   \(U(P_vh)=U(P_v)+U(h)\) at roots and children for arbitrary sampled
   two-bit Boolean \(h\).
7. **Sharp endpoint.** Complete revelation of square invertible examples has
   loss \(m=2I_x(P)\), proving constant \(2\) cannot be improved in this
   payment format.
8. **Excluded embedding.** Rank-deficient matrices were deliberately not
   admitted: their Fourier fibers have more than one point, so an arbitrary
   \(D\) creates coherent sums.  This is the precise unpaid leaf, not a
   harmless boundary case.

No counterexample to the author's v1 theorem was found.  The challenge did
identify a stronger rectangular constant, separated as an unreviewed
reviewer-derived claim.
