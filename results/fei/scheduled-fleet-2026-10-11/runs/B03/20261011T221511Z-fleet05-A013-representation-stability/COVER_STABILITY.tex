\section{Ordered gate covers and physical restrictions}
A cover represents $\{f=1\}=\bigcup_{l=1}^N E_l$, where $E_l=\{h_l=1\}$.
Each $h_l$ is a Boolean combination of $M_l$ regular functions with fixed
monotone orientations and desirability orders. Constants are allowed and
are removed when starting a queue reference. Write $R_l$ for relevant
physical support, $p_l=\Pr(E_l)$, $\pi_l=\min(p_l,1/2)$, and
$d(j)=|\{l:j\in R_l\}|$. Reference root odds are $o_l=\lambda_l\pi_l$,
root Bernoulli probabilities $q_l=o_l/(1+o_l)$, and
$\Lambda=\sum_l\log(1+o_l)$. These are reference probabilities, not the
physical probabilities of a Fourier witness root.

\begin{theorem}[Actual restriction ledger]
Fix a physical coordinate block $A$, $|A|=k$, to $a$. Replacing every
gate and every inner regular function by its actual restriction represents
$f_a$ exactly. No gates or inner functions are added. Relevant supports
shrink to subsets of $R_l\setminus A$; deleting coordinates from each
order gives the inherited order, and ranks never increase.
For any surviving positive gate,
\[
 p'_l=\Pr(E_l\mid X_A=a),\quad 0\le p'_l\le\min(1,2^kp_l),\quad
 \pi'_l=\min(p'_l,1/2)\le2^k\pi_l.
\]
Inherited incidence-feasible weights remain feasible. At fixed weights,
\[
 q'_l=\frac{\lambda_l\pi'_l}{1+\lambda_l\pi'_l},\qquad
 \Lambda'\le\sum_l\log(1+2^k\lambda_l\pi_l).
\]
There is no positive uniform lower bound on $p'_l/p_l$.
For a fixed surviving root set $U$, its negative log reference probability
changes by exactly
\[
 \Lambda'-\Lambda+\sum_{l\in U}\log\frac{\pi_l}{\pi'_l}.
\]
If there are weights on all gates and $L$ denotes the artificial label law
$t_l=\lambda_l\pi_l/W$, $W=\sum_l\lambda_l\pi_l>0$, and $W'>0$, then
\[
 t'_l=\frac{t_l(\pi'_l/\pi_l)}{W'/W},\qquad
 D(t'\Vert t)\le\log(2^kW/W').
\]
The actual law for uniform attachment names at a retained coordinate $j$
has entropy $\log d'(j)\le\log d(j)$.
The independent reference root-vector entropy is exactly
$\sum_l h_2(q_l)$ before and $\sum_l h_2(q'_l)$ after restriction;
there is no implied monotonicity of these sums.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restriction commutes with a Boolean combination and union. The defining
desirability inequality remains valid for every assignment after fixing
other coordinates, so regularity and the inherited orders survive.
Deleting now irrelevant coordinates preserves that inequality. Conditional
probability on an event of probability $2^{-k}$ gives the bound on $p'$.
Clipping at $1/2$ gives the asserted bound on $\pi'$. Incidence constraints
only lose terms. Independent root selection has probability
$2^{-\Lambda}\prod_{l\in U}\lambda_l\pi_l$, proving the difference.
For the label divergence, every nonzero likelihood ratio is at most
$2^kW/W'$. Average its logarithm under $t'$. Zero weights are omitted;
zero-probability gates cannot become positive after restriction.
Constants true make the union constant, and constants false are deleted.
Deleting their odds also removes their normalizer terms. All formulas for
surviving roots use the retained gate index set.
\end{proof}
This is not a pointwise monotonicity theorem for rank logarithms:
both $r(j)$ and $|T\cap U_j|+1$ shrink, so their ratio can increase.
For example $r=3$ and $T$ containing exactly one predecessor gives ratio
$3/2$. Restricting and deleting that occupied predecessor gives new ratio
$2/1$, which is larger. Every child subset reference must recompute
its weights and elementary symmetric normalizers. No claimed universal
payment for that recomputation is used below.

\section{Tensor and multiplexer covers}
To avoid silently complementing an OR cover, suppose both signs have
covers $E_{i,+,l},E_{i,-,l}$, with counts $N_{i,+},N_{i,-}$.
For $t$ functions on disjoint physical blocks, cover the positive event
of $\prod_i f_i$ by all intersections
\[
 \bigcap_i E_{i,\epsilon_i,l_i},\qquad\prod_i\epsilon_i=+1.
\]
It is exact even if the covers overlap within a block. Its count is
\[
 N_+=\tfrac12\left[\prod_i(N_{i,+}+N_{i,-})+
                         \prod_i(N_{i,+}-N_{i,-})\right].
\]
Each gate needs at most $\sum_i M_{i,\epsilon_i,l_i}$ regular inner
functions, all original orders survive on their separate blocks, and its
event probability is exactly $\prod_i p_{i,\epsilon_i,l_i}$.
If no negative cover was supplied, a safe construction for $f_i=-1$
is the single intersection $\bigcap_l\{h_{i,l}=-1\}$, using at most
$\sum_l M_{i,l}$ inner functions. This can greatly worsen $M$.
Gate counts can grow exponentially despite exact spectral tensor
additivity. For example $t$ independent literals have $2^{t-1}$ positive
minterms although their product has deterministic spectrum and entropy
zero. Representation size is not itself a paid Fourier cost.
For a coordinate in block $i$, the transformed attachment degree obeys
$d'(j)\le d_i^{\pm}(j)\prod_{k\ne i}(N_{k,+}+N_{k,-})$, where
$d_i^{\pm}$ counts both signed covers. The attachment-name cost increase
is therefore at most $\sum_{k\ne i}\log(N_{k,+}+N_{k,-})$ relative to
that two-sign incidence. Each tensor gate root uses
$\pi'=\min(\prod_i p_i,1/2)$ and its chosen feasible $\lambda'$;
the normalizer and root-vector entropy are exactly
$\sum\log(1+\lambda'\pi')$ and $\sum h_2(\lambda'\pi'/(1+\lambda'\pi'))$.

An artificial independent tuple-label law on all signs and names has
entropy $\sum_i H(L_i)$. Writing $B$ for its sign-product indicator and
$b=\Pr(B=1)$, the always valid identity is
\[
 bH(L\mid B=1)+(1-b)H(L\mid B=0)=\sum_i H(L_i)-h_2(b).
\]
Thus $H(L\mid B=1)\le\sum_iH(L_i)/b$ for $b>0$. Uniform
attachment names still cost exactly $\log d'(j)$ and must be counted
with the transformed incidence.

For a fresh selector block of $a$ bits and branches $g_b$, covers of
$g_b=1$ lift to $\{x=b\}\cap E_{b,l}$, with count $\sum_bN_b$,
inner count $M_{b,l}+a$, probability $2^{-a}p_{b,l}$ and
$\pi'_{b,l}=2^{-a}p_{b,l}$. When $a\ge1$ and $p_{b,l}>0$,
\[
 2^{-a}\le\pi'_{b,l}/\pi_{b,l}\le2^{1-a}.
\]
In a decision tree, replace $a$ by the actual leaf depth $d_l$.
For an artificial physical leaf label $B$ followed by conditional gate
name $L$, $H(B,L)=H(B)+\sum_b\Pr(B=b)H(L\mid B=b)$.
For dyadic tree cells $H(B)=D=\sum_l2^{-d_l}d_l$.
It is a latent label cost, not automatically the entropy of the output
Fourier set. Selector incidence is the total number of gates whose
path fixes that selector, so old feasible $\lambda$ need not remain
feasible. Scale all inherited weights by
\[
 c=\min\{1,\min_{j\text{ selector}}(
                  \sum_{l:j\in R'_l}\lambda_l)^{-1}\}.
\]
An empty sum imposes no constraint. Root odds are then
$c\lambda_l2^{-d_l}p_l$ and their independent-root normalizer is
exactly $\sum_l\log(1+c\lambda_l2^{-d_l}p_l)$.
The root log-odds increase per retained gate lies between
$d_l-1+\log(1/c)$ and $d_l+\log(1/c)$.

If selectors already occur in a branch, the actual branch is
$g_b|_{x=b}$. First restrict it, then gate by the selector event.
The probability is $2^{-a}\Pr(E_{b,l}\mid x=b)$, not $2^{-a}p_{b,l}$.
The formal example $g_+(x)=x$, $g_-(x)=-x$, selecting on that same $x$,
gives the constant $1$. Treating the branch $x$ as an independent data
literal wrongly predicts nonconstant spectrum and positive data
influence. Shared data alone is harmless to the cover identities;
shared physical selectors demand actual restriction first.

\section{A repaired complete cost on rare balanced-core activation}
The next theorem consumes the activated-literal obstruction to the
uncompressed physical transcript. It pays a complete projected reference,
not just one entropy component. It supplies a different reference from
the original incidence queue; no estimate of that queue's infimum is made.

\begin{theorem}[Vacuum-separated projection]
Let $g$ be any balanced Boolean function. Let $R_g(T)$ be a subprobability
reference on nonempty Fourier sets with
$c_g=\sum_{T\ne\varnothing}\widehat g(T)^2\log(1/R_g(T))<\infty$.
On $r\ge1$ fresh point controls let $p=2^{-r}$ and
$F=1+A_r(g-1)$. There is an explicit complete probability reference for
the full spectrum of $F$ with cross entropy $C_F$ satisfying
\[
 H(F)\le C_F\le h_2(2p-p^2)+2r(2p-p^2)+pc_g
              \le p c_g+(6+2/\ln2)pr.
\]
If $c_g\le K I(g)$ its complete cost is at most
$\max\{K,6+2/\ln2\}I(F)$. The arbitrary core cost is retained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Stop at the first control failure. Its constant leaf has depth $j$,
$1\le j\le r$; the last leaf is $g$ at depth $r$. The old transcript
Kraft masses are $4^{-j}$ times the leaf reference for each selector
frequency subset along the known path. Sum masses with equal decoded
Fourier sets. After relabeling the controls in their query order, this
gives, for a control-only set $S$, with $m=\max S$ and $m=1$ for
$S=\varnothing$,
\[
 Q(S,\varnothing)=\sum_{j=m}^r4^{-j};\qquad
 Q(S,T)=p^2R_g(T)\quad(T\ne\varnothing).
\]
The first formula for the empty set also starts at $j=1$.
Its total constant-leaf mass is $\sum_j2^j4^{-j}=1-p$; terminal
mass is $p\sum_TR_g(T)\le p$. Thus $Q$ is a subprobability law.
For nonempty $S$, $Q(S,\varnothing)\ge4^{-\max S}\ge p^2$.
The exact Boolean Fourier law is
\[
 P(\varnothing,\varnothing)=(1-p)^2,\quad
 P(S,\varnothing)=p^2\ (S\ne\varnothing),\quad
 P(S,T)=p^2\widehat g(T)^2\ (T\ne\varnothing).
\]
Let $v=2p-p^2$, $q_*=\sum_{(S,T)\ne(\varnothing,\varnothing)}Q(S,T)
\le1$. Define the full reference to give mass $1-v$ to the empty
set and mass $vQ(S,T)/q_*$ to every nonempty set. Here $q_*>0$.
Its exact cross entropy is
\[
 C_F=h_2(v)+\sum_{(S,T)\ne\varnothing}P(S,T)
                         \log(1/Q(S,T))+v\log q_*.
\]
The last term is nonpositive and is retained in the exact construction.
Control-only nonempty mass is $p-p^2$ and terminal mass is $p$.
Consequently the sum is at most $2rv+pc_g$. The core term follows
from $2^rp^2=p$. Since $p\le v\le2p$,
$h_2(v)\le v\log(1/v)+v/\ln2\le2pr+2p/\ln2$.
Using $r\ge1$ proves the stated coefficient. Gibbs' inequality gives
$H(F)\le C_F$. Finally physical edges give $I(F)=pI(g)+rp$:
each control has influence $p$ and data influence scales by $p$.
\end{proof}
This erases the unnecessary physical stopping label before charging
cost. An unchanged transcript costs $4(1-p)+pc_g$ and diverges relative
to $I(F)$ on $g=y$. Its projected empty atom has probability roughly
$1/3$; merely summing witnesses still pays a constant on that atom.
The separate vacuum reference is essential to the repair.

\section{Iteration and explicit nonclosure}
Disjoint complete references tensor by multiplication, with cross entropy
$\sum_i c_i$ and influence $\sum_iI_i$. Character balancing with a fresh
literal XOR-translates the spectrum and its reference, changing no cost
and adding influence one. Thus disjoint products, balancing, and balanced
core activations can be iterated with constant at most
$\max(K_0,6+2/\ln2)$ independent of depth. At each activation,
\[
 c_{\rm new}\le p c_{\rm core}+(6+2/\ln2)pr,
\quad I_{\rm new}=pI_{\rm core}+pr;
\]
expanding these recurrences weights each local activation charge by the
product of subsequent activation probabilities. No ancestral influence
is spent twice. Activating an unbalanced output again is outside this
theorem unless it is first balanced; no silent closure is asserted.

Restriction is not stability of A011's boundary admission. On two
selectors choose leaves $y_2$ only in cell $(+,+)$ and $y_1$ elsewhere.
The full two-level tree has $D=2$, label-flip budget $B=1$, so $B\ge D/2$.
Fix the first selector to $-$. Keeping the inherited second query gives
$D'=1$, $B'=0$. Pruning the equal child leaves repairs this example but
is a further representation change. Orthogonal neighboring branches
also need not remain orthogonal after data restriction: $y_1,y_2$ have
zero inner product, while fixing both to $+$ makes them identical.
These are failures of stated admission/certificates, not of FEI.
The ranks/root laws above transport the raw ordered cover; a universal
paid reference on every physically restricted arbitrary core remains
unproved. The activation theorem does not supply such a core code.

\section{Four exact levels with a reused physical selector}
Let $F_0=y$ and $F_d=\operatorname{MUX}_x(F_{d-1},z_d)$, $d=1,2,3,4$,
always using the same physical selector $x$, with distinct data $z_d$.
The expanded positive OR cover has $d+1$ clauses and formal selector read
$d+1$: $x^d\wedge y$ and $x^{d-k}\wedge(-x)\wedge z_k$, $1\le k\le d$.
Literal idempotence and contradiction eliminate every intermediate clause.
The actual represented function is
\[
 F_d=\operatorname{MUX}_x(y,z_d),\quad
 \text{gates }(x\wedge y),\ ((-x)\wedge z_d).
\]
At every listed depth the reduced cover has two regular conjunction gates,
read two at $x$ and read one at relevant data coordinates. Gate probability
is $1/4$. Feasible weights $1/2$ give root odds $1/8$, root probabilities
$1/9$, and $\Lambda=2\log(9/8)$. Root-vector entropy is $2h_2(1/9)$;
selector attachment-name entropy is one bit, data-name entropy zero.
All four spectral atoms have mass $1/4$, hence $H=2$, $I=3/2$:
influences are $1/2$ on $x,y,z_d$, zero on all intermediate data.
Reduced orders can be $(x,y)$ and $(-x,z_d)$ in each gate's orientation.
Raw read growth is syntactic; treating each reused selector as a new
independent physical coordinate gives the wrong ledger. Exact tables
