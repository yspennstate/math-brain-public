\section{A095: linear shared-collision tax with sharp leading coefficient}
\label{sec:lineartax}
This section independently reconstructs only the new shared-selector
application in \path{A095-RANK-KL-EQUIVALENCE}, version 3~\cite{A095d}.
The older rank-equivalence and activation clauses of that bundled ID are
not independently accepted here. Let $G(s,X),H(s,Y)$ share $t\ge2$
uniform bits, with arbitrary disjoint private blocks and a fresh selector $z$.
Let $F=(G+H)/2+z(G-H)/2$, $d=\Pr(G\ne H)$ and $d_* =\min(d,1-d)$.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lineartax}
For all such actual Boolean children,
\[
 \Delta=H(F)-\tfrac12(H(G)+H(H))
 \le (t+2+3/\ln2)d_*.
\]
Calibrating every shared-only reference atom and lifting through the
actual selector kernel gives a single complete normalized reference with
\[
 \CE(P_F,Q)=\tfrac12(c_G^c+c_H^c)+\Delta,
 \qquad D_2(P_F\Vert Q)=\tfrac12(D_2(P_G\Vert R_G^c)+D_2(P_H\Vert R_H^c)).
\]
Thus supplied child influence bounds transfer with constant
$\max(K_G,K_H,t+2+3/\ln2)$. If $\kappa_t$ is the supremum of
$\Delta/d_*$ over all such children and private dimensions, then
\[
 t-1-\log_2(16/9)\le\kappa_t\le t+2+3/\ln2\quad(t\ge4),
 \qquad \kappa_t/t\longrightarrow1.
\]
No arbitrary terminal reference payment is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Negating $H$ preserves its spectrum and interchanges the two selector
frequency masses in every fiber. It preserves $\Delta$, changing $d$ to
$1-d$. We may therefore fold to $d=d_*\le1/2$. If $d_*=0$, the children
are equal or opposite on the union cube and $\Delta=0$.
Let $C$ be the $N=2^t$ shared-only frequencies, and $a_T,b_T$ the
embedded child coefficients. Conditional means $u(s),v(s)$ belong to
$[-1,1]$, and private conditional independence gives $2d_*=\E(1-uv)$.
As $|u-v|\le1-uv$, each $T\in C$ satisfies $|a_T-b_T|\le2d_*$.
Put $m_T=(a_T^2+b_T^2)/2$, $e_T=(a_T-b_T)^2/4$, and
$\delta_C=\sum_{T\in C}e_T$. Parseval and the coefficient cap give
\[
 0\le\delta_C\le\min(d_*,N d_*^2),\qquad\sum_C m_T\le1.
\]
Add a dummy fiber of mass $1-\sum_C m_T$ and zero minority. Concavity
of binary entropy then pays the complete collision cost
$B_C=\sum_C m_T h_2(e_T/m_T)\le h_2(\delta_C)$.
The terminology minority denotes the chosen selector sector; the
inequality does not require it to be smaller in every individual fiber.
Outside $C$, true nonzero fibers identify their child and the selector
membership is fair. Hence
\[
 B_{\rm out}=\tfrac12(2-\E u^2-\E v^2)\le2d_*.
\]

For $d_*\ge1/N$, monotonicity of $h_2$ on $[0,1/2]$ and
$h_2(x)\le x\log_2(e/x)$ give
$B_C\le d_*(t+1/\ln2)$.
For $0<d_*\le1/N$, write $x=N d_*\le1$. Since $N\ge4$,
$\delta_C\le N d_*^2\le1/4$, and
\[
 B_C/d_*\le x\log_2(eN/x^2)\le\log_2(eN)=t+1/\ln2.
\]
For the last inequality the derivative, after multiplying by $\ln2$,
is $\ln N-1-2\ln x>0$ on $(0,1]$, and the limit at zero is zero.
Lemma~\ref{lem:single} gives $J\le2d_*/\ln2$, proving the tax.

The calibration and reference proof in Section~\ref{sec:shared} applies
to all shared-only atoms separately: it decreases each cost by exactly
the categorical KL of those singleton atoms and the complement.
The actual kernel preserves reference mass even at zero true fibers.
On $C$ true and reference child posteriors agree; outside $C$ the active
child is unique. Thus the displayed full KL and CE identities follow.
Sign folding permutes the selector kernel and does not change the
calibrated references or terminal bills. Physical influence remains
$I(F)=\tfrac12(I(G)+I(H))+d$, counting shared coordinates once, so
$d_*\le d$ proves the conditional child-bound consequence.

For even $t\ge4$, the actual bent/point-flip boundary in
Section~\ref{sec:shared} gives $\kappa_t\ge t-\log_2(16/9)$.
For odd $t\ge5$ use that construction on $t-1$ bits and one dummy shared
bit. This gives the lower bound for all $t\ge4$. Divide both bounds by $t$.
\end{proof}
The old exponential estimate was valid and is not refuted. This sharper
linear estimate still cannot become absolute using selector influence
alone; the bent example has the matching leading coefficient.

\section{Complete conditional KL: what compression must retain}
\label{sec:compression}
For any deterministic quotient $C_i$ of a complete derivative row,
the KL chain rule gives the exact influence-weighted lost refund
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotientloss}
 L_i(C_i)=d_i[D_2(\mu_i\Vert r_i)-D_2(C_i\mu_i\Vert C_i r_i)]
 =d_i\sum_c\mu_i(c)D_2(\mu_i(\cdot\mid c)\Vert r_i(\cdot\mid c)).
\end{equation}
The coarsened downward cost is $\mathcal L_i+L_i(C_i)$.
Marginalizing the complete reference preserves normalization but may
discard an unbounded conditional divergence. It is distinct from
conditioning the reference on derivative support, which also drops its
support mass and invalidates the complete-reference identity.

\begin{proposition}[A001 fixed-degree obstruction]\label{prop:degree}
Fix $L\ge0$ and keep each residual subset of size at most $L$ individually,
merging all larger subsets into one tail. For the one-point sign $f_n$,
let $e=2^{1-n}$, $m=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ and $G_n=f_n^{\otimes m}$,
with $n\ge\max(6,2L+2)$. Every Fourier coefficient is nonzero, every
coordinate has influence $e$, and $I(G_n)=mn e\to0$. Yet
\begin{align*}
 H(G_n)/I(G_n)&<4,\qquad\mathcal L(G_n)/I(G_n)\le2,\\
 \mathcal L^{L}(G_n)/I(G_n)
 &\ge\lfloor n/2\rfloor-2-\beta(n-1)\longrightarrow\infty,\\
 \beta&=e\sum_{j=0}^L\binom{n-1}{j}.
\end{align*}
This is \path{A001-BOUNDED-DEGREE-REFUND-LOSS}, version 1~\cite{A001d},
assembled at author scope, not an FEI counterexample.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The point sign has coefficient $1-e$ at empty and magnitude $e$ elsewhere.
Every influence is $e$. Its row downward price is
$e-e^2+e^2\log_2(((1-e)^2+e^2)/e^2)$, giving ratio at most
$1+2(n-1)e\le2$. With $a=(1-e)^2$, $W=1-a=2e-e^2$, its entropy is
$-a\log_2 a+2(n-1)W\le2e/\ln2+4(n-1)e<4ne$.
Product spectra add $H,I,\mathcal L$, proving the genuine tensor bounds.
There are no auxiliary parity factors or dummy coordinates.

In a row of $G_n$, the seed derivative is uniform on $2^{n-1}$ atoms,
each of mass $e$. Both row laws have the same independent spectator
factor. The individually kept low-atom derivative mass is at most $\beta$.
Every likelihood ratio is at most $1/e$, so their total signed KL
contribution is at most $\beta(n-1)$.
The reference tail contains the event that exactly one spectator block
has degree greater than $L$ and all other spectators are empty.
This has mass $(m-1)z a^{m-2}$ with
$z=e^2\sum_{j=L+1}^n\binom nj\ge e$ by binomial symmetry.
Since $me\le1/4$, $m-1\ge m/2$ and Bernoulli's inequality gives
$a^{m-2}\ge1-2e(m-2)\ge1/2$, the tail reference mass is at least
$me/4=2^{-(\lceil n/2\rceil+1)}$.
Its KL term is at most $\log_2(1/r_{\rm tail})\le\lceil n/2\rceil+1$,
including when the signed term is negative. Subtract these divergence
bounds from $\log_2(1/e)=n-1$. For fixed $L$, $\beta(n-1)$ is an
exponentially decaying polynomial, proving divergence.
Equation~\eqref{eq:quotientloss} shows that all the loss is the
within-tail conditional KL. Discarding only the independent spectators
instead preserves the seed likelihood ratio and loses zero KL.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A096 informative partner]\label{prop:partner}
Replace each physical coordinate $i$ in $J$ by a fresh disjoint parity pair
$u_i v_i$. Then
\[
 H(F_J)=H(f),\quad I(F_J)=I(f)+\sum_{i\in J}d_i,
 \quad\mathcal L(F_J)=\mathcal L(f)-\sum_{i\in J}\mathcal L_i(f).
\]
Each duplicated row has zero erasure and zero downward price.
Deleting its partner loses exactly $\mathcal L_i(f)$ of weighted KL;
retaining only its partner loses zero. These are the full scoped identities
of \path{A096-SINGLE-SPECTATOR-REFUND-OWNERSHIP}, version 1~\cite{A096d},
independently reconstructed by A073~\cite{A073d}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Character expansion embeds every selected frequency into both fresh bits,
injectively. The $H,I$ identities follow; unchanged rows retain their pairs.
For a duplicated row write $A_T=p_T$, $B_T=p_{T+i}$.
Its complete reference atoms are $r(T,0)=A_T$, $r(T,1)=B_T$ and its
derivative atoms are $\mu(T,0)=0$, $\mu(T,1)=B_T/d_i$.
The likelihood ratio is $1/d_i$ on the entire derivative support, giving
$D=\log_2(1/d_i)$, zero downward price, and no adjacent occupied pair.
Deleting the partner yields the original laws $A+B$, $B/d_i$ and thus
loses $\mathcal L_i(f)$ by~\eqref{eq:quotientloss}.
Retaining it yields $(0,1)$ against $(1-d_i,d_i)$; given partner one,
both remaining laws are $B_T/d_i$, so the loss is zero.
An arbitrary common independent spectator factor cancels conditionally.
\end{proof}
The associated local-payment no-go is an actual correlated Boolean row.
Let $b$ be the even-$d$ quadratic bent sign, $\delta=2^{-d}$, $t=\sqrt\delta$,
and $g(s,y)=b(y)-\mathbf1_0+s\mathbf1_0$. Its selector row has masses
$A_T=(\sigma_Tt-\delta)^2$, $B_T=\delta^2$, with influence $\delta$.
As $A_T+B_T=\delta(1-2\sigma_Tt+2\delta)\ge\delta/2$,
$\delta(d-1)\le\mathcal L_s(g)\le\delta d$, the upper bound following
from complete KL nonnegativity. Both signs $\sigma_T$ occur, so the partner
and core are genuinely correlated. Duplicating only $s$ gives two
influences $\delta$ whose deletion losses each lie in this interval.
Every core influence is $1/2+\delta$: the point flip adds a pivotal edge
in each direction. Thus $I=d/2+(d+2)\delta$ and $H/I\le3$.
No absolute allowance using only the queried/erased pair pays the loss.
This excludes that local reserve, not a source-owned terminal budget.

\section{A073: positive-erasure binary remainder with dedicated reserve}
\label{sec:binary}
This is \path{A073-NONZERO-ERASURE-BINARY-REMAINDER}, version 1,
with the exact source-bound A098 review~\cite{A073d,A098d}.
For even $d\ge2$, set $\delta=2^{-d}$, $t=\sqrt\delta$, let $b$ be
the quadratic bent sign and let the two-bit gate $q(u,v)$ be $-1$ only
at the binary input $(1,1)$. The actual Boolean function is
$F_d=b(y)+\mathbf1_{y=0}(q(u,v)-1)$.
Its four amplitudes over each core frequency are
\[
 \sigma_Tt-\delta/2,\quad\delta/2,\quad\delta/2,\quad-\delta/2.
\]
They are all nonzero, so both queried erasures are positive. The signs
occur $N_\pm=(2^d\pm2^{d/2})/2$ times, by summing coefficients at zero.
For query $u$ put $e=\delta/2$ and retain only partner occupancy $V$.
The complete laws are
\[
 \mu(T,0)=\mu(T,1)=\delta/2,\quad
 r(T,0)=\delta c_\sigma,\quad r(T,1)=\delta^2/2,
 \qquad c_\sigma=1-\sigma t+\delta/2.
\]
Hence $\mu_V=(1/2,1/2)$ and $r_V=(1-e,e)$.
Conditional on $V=1$, both core laws are uniform. Conditional on $V=0$,
the derivative is uniform and reference masses are $\delta c_\sigma/(1-e)$.
The KL chain rule gives exactly
\[
 D_{\rm full}=D_2((1/2,1/2)\Vert(1-e,e))+D_0/2,
 \quad D_0=\sum_{\sigma=\pm}N_\sigma\delta
       \log_2\frac{1-e}{c_\sigma},\quad L_u=eD_0/2.
\]
The same holds for $v$. Both sign counts are positive and $c_+\ne c_-$,
so strict KL nonnegativity gives $D_0>0$.
For $0<t\le1/2$,
$7(1-t+t^2/2)-5(1-t^2/2)=(2-3t)(1-2t)\ge0$,
so each conditional likelihood ratio is at most $7/5$ and
\[
 0<L_u/e\le\tfrac12\log_2(7/5)<63/256=1/4-1/256.
\]
The strict last inequality is the exact integer certificate
$7^{128}<2^{63}5^{128}$, rechecked in this run.
The two losses together are less than $\delta/4$.
Every core influence is $(1+\delta)/2$, as the gate flips zero on one
quarter of its assignments, adding $\delta/2$ to each bent influence.
Since $\delta\le1/4$, $\delta/4\le(1+\delta)/20=d_{y_1}/10$.
Thus a dedicated tenth of one physical core influence pays these two
losses once, if it is reserved exclusively for them. No other entropy,
terminal or query charge is allocated to this fraction.

The retained price remains large:
\[
 \mathcal L_u/e=1+\tfrac12\log_2((1-e)/e)-D_0/2>d/2+3/4.
\]
Here $(1-e)/e=2^{d+1}-1\ge2^d$ and $D_0/2<1/4$.
Total influence is $d/2+(d/2+1)\delta$ and $H\le d+2$,
giving $H/I\le2+4/d\le4$. Paying a quotient remainder does not pay
this retained cost or construct a globally available terminal reserve.
The universal normalized KL lower bound in Section~\ref{sec:refund}
therefore remains the first unpaid C018 line.
