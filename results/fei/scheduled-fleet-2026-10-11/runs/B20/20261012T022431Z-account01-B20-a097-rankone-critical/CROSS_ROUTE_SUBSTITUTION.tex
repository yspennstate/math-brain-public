\section*{Critical-endpoint classification for rank-one selector overlap}

Let $A=\{-1,1\}^t$ and $X=\{-1,1\}^m$, both with the uniform
measure.  Suppose $F(a,x)=g_a(x)$ has balanced genuine Boolean branches and
that the branch-by-nonconstant-frequency Fourier matrix has rank one.  The
A014 rank-one overlap theorem gives genuine Boolean functions
$\sigma:A\to\{-1,1\}$ and $g:X\to\{-1,1\}$ such that
\[
        F(a,x)=\sigma(a)g(x),
        \qquad H(F)=H(\sigma)+H(g),
        \qquad I(F)=I(\sigma)+I(g).
\]
The variables are physically disjoint.  Put $R(h)=H(h)/I(h)$ for every
nonconstant $h$ and
\[
 w_\sigma=\frac{I(\sigma)}{I(\sigma)+I(g)},\qquad
 w_g=\frac{I(g)}{I(\sigma)+I(g)}.
\]

Call $h$ $\eta$-descendant-critical if every nonconstant physical
descendant $u$ satisfies $R(u)\le R(h)+\eta$.

\paragraph{Theorem (exact cross-route transfer).}
Assume that both factors are nonconstant.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $F$ is descendant-critical ($\eta=0$), then
\[
 R(\sigma)=R(g)=R(F),
\]
and both $\sigma$ and $g$ are descendant-critical.
\item Conversely, if $\sigma$ and $g$ are descendant-critical and have the
same ratio, then $F$ is descendant-critical.
\item More generally, if $F$ is $\eta$-descendant-critical, then
\[
 |R(\sigma)-R(g)|\le
 \frac{\eta}{\min\{w_\sigma,w_g\}},
\]
and each physical descendant $u\preceq\sigma$ satisfies
\[
 R(u)\le R(\sigma)+\frac{\eta}{w_\sigma},
\]
with the symmetric estimate for descendants of $g$.
\item No nontrivial rank-one selector product is a strict critical core (a
function strictly dominating every proper nonconstant physical descendant).
Consequently, a strict critical core in the A014 rank-one class has constant
controller after irrelevant address variables are deleted.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.}
Tensor additivity gives the exact weighted average
\[
 R(F)=w_\sigma R(\sigma)+w_g R(g).                 \tag{1}
\]
Fixing the $x$ block at any input produces $\pm\sigma$ as an actual
physical descendant, and fixing the $a$ block produces $\pm g$.  If $F$ is
critical, both factor ratios are at most $R(F)$; (1) forces equality.
Any descendant $u\preceq\sigma$ is also a descendant of $F$ after fixing
the $g$ block, so $R(u)\le R(F)=R(\sigma)$.  This proves (1).

For the converse, every physical descendant of $F$ is a disjoint product
$u(a')v(x')$, where $u\preceq\sigma$ and $v\preceq g$; either factor may be
constant.  Its ratio is the influence-weighted average of the ratios of its
nonconstant factors, hence at most their common root ratio.

For the quantitative statement, A052's physical tensor-purity calculation
applied after the A014 factorization gives
\[
 R(F)-\frac{w_g}{w_\sigma}\eta\le R(\sigma)\le R(F)+\eta
\]
and its symmetric counterpart.  Equivalently, if
$R(\sigma)\ge R(g)$, then
$R(\sigma)-R(F)=w_g(R(\sigma)-R(g))\le\eta$;
the other ordering is symmetric.  This yields the displayed gap bound.  If
$u\preceq\sigma$, then $u$ is a physical descendant of $F$, so
\[
 R(u)\le R(F)+\eta
       \le R(\sigma)+\eta/w_\sigma.
\]
Finally, (1) implies that at least one factor has ratio at least $R(F)$.
That factor is a proper physical descendant, contradicting strict
criticality. \qed

\paragraph{Explicit A014 family.}
For $F_t(a,x)=h_t(a)\operatorname{Maj}_3(x)$, where $h_t$ is negative at
one address only,
\[
 I(h_t)=t2^{1-t},\qquad R(\operatorname{Maj}_3)=\frac43,
 \qquad R(h_t)\longrightarrow4.
\]
For $t\ge2$, $R(h_t)>4/3$, and any $\eta$ for which $F_t$ is
$\eta$-descendant-critical must satisfy
\[
 \eta\ge
 \frac{3/2}{t2^{1-t}+3/2}\left(R(h_t)-\frac43\right)
 \longrightarrow\frac83.
\]
Thus the unbounded-read, fractional-congestion-one, spectral-rank-one family
is not merely non-strict: asymptotically it stays a constant distance from
descendant-criticality.

\paragraph{Debt paid and remaining debt.}
This substitution pays the complete critical-endpoint classification for
the exact rank-one balanced-selector regime.  It does not bound rank at least
two, distance from rank one, unbalanced branches, or prime critical sources.
Full FEI remains open; the surviving structural target is a quantitative
rank-$r$/near-rank-one decomposition with its interference paid, or a bound
on the critical step ratio in the resulting prime remainder.
