\section*{Independent reconstruction and repair of the A047 half-tax certificate}

The audited claim is the two-bit Boolean inequality
\[
 J_X\le M_X+\frac12\delta(p),\qquad
 \delta(p)=p\log_2\frac{1-p}{p},                       \tag{1}
\]
for $0<p\le 1/2$.  The certified scope under review is
\[
 \frac1{64}\le p\le\frac{49}{100},\qquad 0\le q\le1. \tag{2}
\]
This is a local biased-restriction inequality.  It does not by itself imply
the universal GU inequality: a separate hierarchy theorem must pay the
resulting $M_X+\delta(p)/2$ costs without reuse and retain all terminal terms.

\subsection*{Truth-table reconstruction}

For $g(x,y)=(-1)^{xy}$ under independent biases
$\Pr(x=1)=p$, $\Pr(y=1)=q$, direct expansion in the normalized biased basis
gives squared Fourier masses
\[
 (1-2pq)^2,\quad 4q^2p(1-p),\quad
 4p^2q(1-q),\quad4p(1-p)q(1-q).                       \tag{3}
\]
Put $s=4q(1-q)$, $c=4q^2$, and $b=1-sp$.  The residual spectral bit has
mass $sp$.  Given that bit is zero, the revealed spectral bit has mass
$cp(1-p)/b$; given it is one, it has mass $1-p$.  Hence
\[
 M_X=b h_2\!\left(\frac{cp(1-p)}b\right)+sp h_2(p).   \tag{4}
\]
The $x=0$ child is constant and the $x=1$ child is the biased $y$ character,
so
\[
 J_X=h_2(sp)-p h_2(s).                                \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3)--(5) independently recover the frozen scalar margin
\[
 D=b h_2\!\left(\frac{cp(1-p)}b\right)+sp h_2(p)
   -h_2(sp)+p h_2(s)+\frac p2\log_2\frac{1-p}{p}.     \tag{6}
\]
Thus (1) is exactly $D\ge0$.  The accompanying checker reconstructs
(4)--(5) from (3) at 450 rational bias pairs; the maximum 90-digit Decimal
disagreement is $1.3\cdot10^{-89}$.

\subsection*{First unsupported source line}

Both source certificates declare 70-digit directed Decimal arithmetic, but
their functions \texttt{h\_point} contain
\[
 \texttt{l1 = log2\_point(ONE - x)}.                  \tag{7}
\]
The subtraction in (7) is evaluated before \texttt{log2\_point} enters a
local directed context.  It therefore uses Python's process-wide default
Decimal context, whose precision is 28.  The rounded value is then treated
as an exact point.  It need not enclose the true complement and so the
published scripts do not prove their claimed interval enclosures as written.
This occurs at line 70 of the compact-core checker and line 71 of the
endpoint-collar checker.

\subsection*{Minimal repair and result}

The audit replaces (7) by the directed interval
\[
 [\operatorname{fl}_{\downarrow}(1-x),
   \operatorname{fl}_{\uparrow}(1-x)]                 \tag{8}
\]
and propagates both endpoints through the logarithm and product.  No domain,
bisection rule, candidate, or acceptance threshold is changed.

The repaired compact certificate visits 7,571 boxes, accepts 3,786 leaves,
reaches depth 20, and has minimum lower endpoint
\[
 1.7580671042893135\cdot10^{-6}>0.                    \tag{9}
\]
The repaired lower-$q$ and upper-$q$ collars retain 45 and 39 leaves, with
minimum lower endpoints respectively
\[
 9.3029871479133499\cdot10^{-5},\qquad
 1.0426165977229429\cdot10^{-4}.                      \tag{10}
\]
There are no unresolved boxes.  Consequently (1) does hold on (2), but the
original certificate is accepted only after this material implementation
repair.  The reviewer is therefore a contributor to the repaired proof and
a fresh independent review is required.

The strips $49/100<p\le1/2$, the explicit join to the rare tail
$p<1/64$, higher-arity gates, and the nonreusing GU hierarchy payment remain
open.
