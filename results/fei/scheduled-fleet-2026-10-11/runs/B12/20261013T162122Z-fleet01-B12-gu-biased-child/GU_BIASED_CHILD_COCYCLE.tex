\section*{Exact upper-mass cocycle for one biased child}

All logarithms in this note are base two.  For a Boolean function $f$ write
\[
 p_f(S)=\widehat f(S)^2,\qquad
 \pi_f(S)=\sum_{R\supseteq S}p_f(R),\qquad
 U(f)=\sum_{S:p_f(S)>0}p_f(S)\log\frac{\pi_f(S)}{p_f(S)}.
\]
Put $\psi(t)=t^2\log(1/t^2)$, with $\psi(0)=0$.

\subsection*{1. Exact one-biased-child formula}

Fix a Boolean gate $g:\{-1,1\}\times\{-1,1\}^k\to\{-1,1\}$ and
decompose it in its first input as
\[
 g(y,z)=a(z)+y b(z).
\]
Thus $a=(g_++g_-)/2$, $b=(g_+-g_-)/2$, and pointwise
$a^2+b^2=1$, $ab=0$.  Let $a_T,b_T$ be their Fourier coefficients on
the $z$ block.  Define
\[
 B_T=\sum_{R\supseteq T}b_R^2,
 \qquad \beta=\sum_Tb_T^2=\operatorname{Inf}_y(g),
\]
and, for $-1\le\mu\le1$,
\[
 C_T(\mu)=\sum_{R\supseteq T}
 \bigl((a_R+\mu b_R)^2+(1-\mu^2)b_R^2\bigr).
\]
The quadratic terms cancel in the cap:
\[
 C_T(\mu)=\sum_{R\supseteq T}(a_R^2+b_R^2+2\mu a_Rb_R),       \tag{1}
\]
so every $C_T$ is affine in $\mu$.  Set
\begin{align*}
 E_b&=\sum_{T:b_T\ne0}b_T^2\log\frac{B_T}{b_T^2},\\
 \Phi_{g,y}(\mu)
 &=\sum_{T:a_T+\mu b_T\ne0}(a_T+\mu b_T)^2
       \log\frac{C_T(\mu)}{(a_T+\mu b_T)^2}
   +(1-\mu^2)E_b,                                               \tag{2}\\
 \Theta_{g,y}(\mu)&=\Phi_{g,y}(\mu)-\beta\psi(\mu).            \tag{3}
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Theorem 1.}
Let $h:\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}$ be any genuine Boolean function on a
block disjoint from the $z$ variables, let $\mu=\mathbb Eh$, and put
\[
 F(x,z)=g(h(x),z).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\ U(F)=\beta U(h)+\Theta_{g,y}(\mu)\ }.                 \tag{4}
\]
The full dependence on the nonconstant spectrum of $h$ is the single term
$\beta U(h)$; the composition defect depends on $h$ only through its mean.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For $S\subseteq[n]$ and $T\subseteq[k]$, Fourier expansion gives
\[
 \widehat F(\varnothing,T)=a_T+\mu b_T,
 \qquad
 \widehat F(S,T)=b_T\widehat h(S)\quad(S\ne\varnothing).        \tag{5}
\]
For $S\ne\varnothing$, the upper cap factors as
\[
 \pi_F(S,T)=B_T\pi_h(S).                                       \tag{6}
\]
For $S=\varnothing$, summing both the empty and nonempty child branches
gives exactly
\[
 \pi_F(\varnothing,T)=C_T(\mu).                                \tag{7}
\]
The nonempty-child terms in $U(F)$ therefore sum to
\[
 (1-\mu^2)E_b
 +\beta\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}p_h(S)
     \log\frac{\pi_h(S)}{p_h(S)}.                              \tag{8}
\]
The omitted empty atom of $U(h)$ is $\psi(\mu)$.  Adding the empty-child
terms from (7) proves (4). \qed

The influence also has the exact decomposition
\[
 I(F)=\beta I(h)+
 \sum_T |T|\bigl((a_T+\mu b_T)^2+(1-\mu^2)b_T^2\bigr).          \tag{9}
\]

\subsection*{2. The signed one-coordinate cocycle}

For a physical child coordinate $i$, write
\[
 \mu_-=\mathbb E h_{i,-},\qquad
 \mu_+=\mathbb E h_{i,+},\qquad
 \mu=\frac{\mu_-+\mu_+}{2}.
\]
Subtracting (4) for the two restrictions from (4) at the parent gives the
exact identity
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_iU(F)=\beta\Delta_iU(h)
 +\Theta_{g,y}(\mu)
 -\frac{\Theta_{g,y}(\mu_-)+\Theta_{g,y}(\mu_+)}2.}             \tag{10}
\]
No sign, separation, regularity, or nonzero-bias assumption occurs in (10).
In particular it remains valid at exact coefficient collisions and at
$\mu=0$.

Define the coherent composition defect at a child-query node $v$ by
\[
 R_v=\Delta_{i(v)}U(g(h_v,z))-\beta\Delta_{i(v)}U(h_v).          \tag{11}
\]
Equation (10) says precisely that $R_v$ is the Doob decrement of the scalar
potential $\Theta_{g,y}(\mathbb Eh_v)$.

\subsection*{3. Complete adaptive hierarchy}

\paragraph{Theorem 2.}
For every complete adaptive physical restriction tree on the child block,
with node reach probabilities $w_v$,
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{v\ {\rm internal}}w_vR_v
  ={}&\Theta_{g,y}(\mu)
  -\frac{1+\mu}{2}\Theta_{g,y}(1)
  -\frac{1-\mu}{2}\Theta_{g,y}(-1).                            \tag{12}
\end{align*}
The right side is independent of the child dimension, Fourier shape, tree
depth, and adaptive query order.  It vanishes on every unchanged cylinder.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Multiply (10)'s last three terms by $w_v$ and sum.  Every internal potential
cancels against its parent occurrence.  At a complete leaf $h=\pm1$.  The
total weights of the positive and negative leaves are $(1+\mu)/2$ and
$(1-\mu)/2$, respectively.  This proves (12). \qed

It is useful to name the endpoint-centered reserve
\[
 \overline\Theta_{g,y}(\mu)=\Theta_{g,y}(\mu)
  -\frac{1+\mu}{2}\Theta_{g,y}(1)
  -\frac{1-\mu}{2}\Theta_{g,y}(-1).                            \tag{13}
\]
Theorem 2 reduces the complete hierarchy problem for one biased child to the
single scalar quantity $\overline\Theta$.

\subsection*{4. Candidate universal payment and exact scope}

The natural next lemma is
\[
 \overline\Theta_{g,y}(\mu)
 \stackrel{?}{\le}3\beta(1-\mu^2),                              \tag{14}
\]
or the stronger local form
\[
 \Theta(\mu)-\frac{\Theta(\mu_-)+\Theta(\mu_+)}2
 \stackrel{?}{\le}3\beta\operatorname{Inf}_i(h).               \tag{15}
\]
Since $1-\mu^2\le I(h)$ for Boolean $h$, (14) would pay the entire terminal
reserve from the child-channel influence with a universal constant.  This
note does not claim (14) or (15) as proved.

The deterministic search accompanying this note reconstructed (4) in
$205{,}344$ cases and tested (15) in $555{,}024$ nontrivial coordinate cases
covering every outer Boolean gate through arity three and every child Boolean
function through dimension three.  The largest observed normalized defect was
\[
 2.280249404938720832\ldots<3.
\]
It also verified (12) on $37{,}728$ complete trees.  These are finite numerical
checks, not an all-dimension proof.

The exact result covers one arbitrary biased Boolean child and an arbitrary
outer Boolean gate, with the other outer inputs realized as fresh physical
bits (equivalently, nonempty disjoint parity characters).  Arbitrary balanced
non-character children in the other slots, overlapping physical inputs, and
a universal bound for (13) remain open.  The rare-bias lower bound accepted in
the B19/A091 review rules out discarding bias taxes without a ledger; here the
ledger is retained exactly through the endpoint-centered cocycle.
